# Kepler-Encoder-v0.1: Towards a Multimodal Embedding Model for Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Kepler-Encoder-v0.1 introduces a multimodal embedding model for robots that integrates vision, proprioception, and end-effector signals into a shared latent space. This model allows the vision-only latent to recover physical robot states, notably force, more effectively than vision-only baselines and generalizes across diverse robot embodiments using a single encoder.

## Problem

- Robotic systems relying solely on visual input have an incomplete understanding of their internal physical state, missing crucial parameters like force, contact, and joint torque that are not visible in RGB images.
- Traditional robot learning paradigms often append raw numerical robot state downstream of vision-only backbones, inheriting inherent 'blind spots' in visual perception.
- The need for a unified, embodiment-agnostic encoder capable of processing heterogeneous, multi-rate, and variable sensor sets across diverse robot platforms efficiently.

## Method

- A multimodal encoder, **Kepler-Encoder-v0.1**, integrates tokenized vision, proprioception, and end-effector signals through a ==latent-query cross-attention== mechanism.
- The architecture uses a single ==asymmetric cross-attention== block to summarize variable-sized input token sets into a fixed-size latent, suitable for embodiment-agnostic fusion via ==masking==.
- Self-supervised training employs a ==masked cross-modal latent prediction== objective, under the ==LeJEPA/SIGReg== framework, predicting the ==EMA== target latent of one modality from the others without explicit decoders.

## Results

- Kepler-Encoder-v0.1's vision-only latent achieved an average R^2 of **0.282** for end-effector state recovery, outperforming a vision-only control (R^2 **0.142**) and raw frozen-ViT features, with concentrated gains in force prediction.
- A single, unified Kepler-Encoder instance matched per-robot specialists' performance across **seven** RH20T configurations, demonstrating effective generalization across diverse robot embodiments.
- The frozen encoder's cross-modal prediction error serves as a training-free invalid-state monitor, achieving an AUROC of **0.90** for detecting out-of-range robot states and **0.69** for scene-swapped states.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fusing state modalities during training significantly enriches the vision-only latent, enabling it to capture and recover physical states (e.g., force) that are inherently difficult or impossible for a camera to perceive alone.
> - A single encoder can achieve embodiment agnosticism, matching or surpassing per-robot specialists across different robot configurations by learning from diverse robot data rather than simply larger data volumes.
> - The self-supervised masked latent prediction objective, combined with SIGReg regularization, encourages the encoder to learn robust, world-relevant structure that is reflected in the latent's organization and health.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic systems require a comprehensive understanding of their internal body state and external environment to perform complex tasks. Traditional approaches often rely heavily on visual input, yet a camera provides only a partial view of a robot's state. Crucial physical parameters such as force, contact, and joint torque leave minimal or no trace in a single RGB image. Existing robot learning paradigms frequently append raw numerical robot state downstream of a vision-only backbone (Kim et al., 2024), thereby inheriting this inherent "blind spot" in visual perception.

This work positions itself within the growing field of self-supervised learning, specifically leveraging Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) (Assran et al., 2023). JEPA models learn representations by predicting latent targets rather than pixel-level reconstructions, which is posited to encourage the learning of world-relevant structure over superficial texture. The specific training objective adopted, LeJEPA/SIGReg (Balestriero and LeCun, 2025), is a variation that replaces architectural anti-collapse heuristics with a distributional constraint. This self-supervised, latent-space predictive approach has been applied across various domains, including video (Assran et al., 2025), functional brain signals (Choi et al., 2024), and robotic motor control (Yang et al., 2024). Multimodal masked prediction, where one modality's representation is predicted from others, is a core component, drawing parallels with M3-JEPA (Lei et al., 2024), 4M (Mizrahi et al., 2023), MultiMAE (Bachmann et al., 2022), and data2vec (Baevski et al., 2022), but adapted here for robot sensor streams.

In the context of robot multi-sensor representation, current methods include vision-language-action models that are vision-centric, treating proprioception as an auxiliary input, and heterogeneous pre-trained transformers that align proprioceptive-visual streams across embodiments (Wang et al., 2024). Other research focuses on tactile and multisensory touch representations (Higuera et al., 2024, 2025; Li et al., 2022; Sferrazza et al., 2023). A related work, MSDP (Krohn et al., 2025), pre-trains a multi-sensory dynamics model but typically assumes synchronized observations. Kepler-Encoder-v0.1 differentiates itself by explicitly addressing the challenge of variable sensor sets and irregular, multi-rate sampling inherent in real robotic systems, aiming for an embodiment-agnostic encoder.

Architecturally, the model employs a latent-query cross-attention mechanism, derived from the Set Transformer (Lee et al., 2019) and Perceiver family (Jaegle et al., 2021b,a). This mechanism is designed to efficiently summarize a variable-size token set into a fixed-size latent, which is particularly suitable for heterogeneous and dynamically changing sensor inputs. The continuous-time embedding of each sensor token's timestamp builds upon techniques from irregular time-series analysis (Kazemi et al., 2019; Shukla and Marlin, 2021; Horn et al., 2019; Chen et al., 2024).

The work consciously departs from reconstruction-based objectives common in autoencoders, which are prone to "pixel-memorization." Instead, decoders are reserved for evaluating what information the *frozen* latent retains rather than for driving the learning process. The training data utilized is the RH20T corpus (Fang et al., 2023), a large-scale real-robot manipulation dataset, which facilitates generalization across diverse robot embodiments and tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The fundamental motivation for this research stems from a recognized limitation in current robotic perception: a robot's visual input, typically from an external camera, provides an incomplete understanding of its own physical state. Specifically, states like contact, force, and joint torque are often imperceptible in a single RGB frame. Empirical evidence presented in the paper supports this, showing that raw vision features achieve an R^2 score of 0.10 or lower for force recovery on all tested robots. This deficiency implies that models relying solely on visual information, or those that merely append raw state data to visual features, will have significant blind spots regarding crucial physical interactions.

The core hypothesis underpinning this work is that vision and robot state are intrinsically linked, representing "two views of one underlying body state." On a robot, the images produced by a camera are not arbitrary but are directly constrained by the robot's joint configuration, gripper aperture, and contact with the environment. Therefore, encoding these modalities jointly should allow them to mutually inform and enrich each other's representations.

The key objectives of the Kepler-Encoder-v0.1 project are:

1. **Develop a Robot-First Multimodal Encoder**: Create an architecture that integrates vision, proprioception (joint angles, velocities, gripper aperture), and end-effector signals (force/torque, TCP pose) as primary input modalities, fusing them into a single, shared latent representation. This design prioritizes the robot's physical state from the outset.
2. **Enable State Recovery from Vision-Only Input**: Design the encoder such that, at evaluation time, the robot's physical state, particularly components not directly visible (e.g., force), can be robustly recovered from the vision-only latent (i.e., when proprioception and force/torque inputs are withheld).
3. **Self-Supervised Training**: Train the encoder without explicit task labels, utilizing a masked cross-modal latent prediction objective under the LeJEPA/SIGReg framework. This involves holding out one modality and predicting its latent representation from the others, which is intended to force the encoder to learn robust, world-relevant structure rather than pixel-level details.
4. **Embodiment Agnosticism**: Engineer a single, unified architecture capable of ingesting diverse sensor sets and configurations across multiple robot embodiments (e.g., robots with differing degrees of freedom, gripper types, or presence/absence of force sensors) through a flexible masking mechanism rather than requiring separate models or truncated inputs.
5. **Validate Cross-Modal Learning Gains**: Empirically demonstrate that the joint training with state modalities confers a significant advantage to the vision-only latent in recovering physical body state, especially for parameters like force that are inherently difficult for a camera to perceive. This validation aims to answer the central research question: "Does fusing state into training make the vision-only latent carry anything the pixels do not already contain?"
6. **Assess Downstream Utility**: Evaluate the direct applicability of the frozen, learned latent representation for practical downstream tasks, such as training-free invalid-state monitoring (safety) and scene reconstruction, without requiring further fine-tuning or training.

By addressing these objectives, the research aims to move beyond vision-centric robot perception to a more holistic, embodied understanding, where internal physical states are integrated into a robot's representation of itself and its environment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed system, Kepler-Encoder-v0.1, is designed as a multimodal encoder that processes heterogeneous sensor streams into a unified latent representation. The methodology encompasses specific tokenization strategies, a cross-attention fusion architecture, a self-supervised training objective, and a comprehensive evaluation protocol.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The encoder, denoted as $f_\theta$, takes vision ($x_v$), motor state ($x_m$), and end-effector signals ($x_e$) as inputs to produce a fused latent $z$. Critically, at evaluation, the system is designed to recover a vision-only latent $z_v = f_\theta(x_v, \emptyset, \emptyset)$, where motor and end-effector streams are dropped. This setup tests whether the cross-modal training imbued the vision-only path with information about the other modalities.

#### 3.2. Token Representation

Every sensor reading is transformed into a token incorporating its value, a learned modality embedding, a spatial position (where applicable), and a continuous-time embedding (constant for the single-timestep model presented).

- **Vision**: Processed by a frozen LeJEPA ViT-B/16, yielding 196 patch tokens, each 768-dimensional. The backbone is frozen based on empirical evidence that finetuning on robot video does not improve robot-relevant probes.
- **Proprioception (Motor)**: Represented as a robot-agnostic 8x3 grid. Rows 0-6 correspond to individual joints, and row 7 represents the gripper. Each joint's data includes `[sin q, cos q, symlog ˙q]` to handle angle wrap-around and heavy-tailed velocity distributions. The gripper uses `symlog` for its width. Robot-agnosticism is achieved by masking rows or channels for sensors not present on a specific embodiment (e.g., a 6-DOF UR5 masks the 7th joint row).
- **End-effector (EE)**: A 13x15 block of native-rate samples. This includes `symlog` force/torque (6 dimensions), `symlog` TCP translation (3 dimensions), and 6-D rotation (6 dimensions) for TCP orientation (converted from quaternions to ensure continuity). This block is zero-padded to a fixed window. Franka robots, lacking a physical force/torque sensor, have these channels masked, providing a built-in test for hardware-absent sensors.

#### 3.3. Architecture: The Cross-Attention Fuser

The fuser implements a single asymmetric cross-attention block. It concatenates the projected vision, motor, and end-effector tokens into a single context set $C$ of $N = N_v + N_m + N_e$ tokens, all of a shared width $d$. A fixed set of $M$ learned query vectors ($Q^{(0)}$) then cross-attends over this context set. This process occurs across $L$ depth blocks, where queries attend to the context and pass through a feed-forward network. Finally, the queries are mean-pooled into a single fixed-size latent $z \in \mathbb{R}^d$.

- **Key Properties**: This architecture offers compute complexity of $O(NM)$, which scales linearly with input tokens, unlike the $O(N^2)$ of self-attention. It produces a fixed-size latent regardless of input variability, making it suitable for embodiment-agnostic fusion via masking. Its set-structured fusion allows position and modality information to be embedded within tokens, facilitating ingestion of arbitrary token sets.
- **Formal Specification**: Each stream is linearly projected and tagged with learned modality and position embeddings. Attention masks are used both for marking invalid entries (padding, hardware absence) and for the modality masking required by the training objective.

#### 3.4. Training Objective

The training loss combines three components:

1. **Masked Cross-Modal Latent Prediction**: The primary learning signal involves masking one modality (vision, motor, or end-effector) and predicting its exponential-moving-average (EMA) target latent from the remaining modalities. A per-modality predictor head $h_k$ maps the fused latent (with modality $k$ held out, $z_{(\setminus k)}$) to the target embedding from the EMA encoder $sg[\bar{f}_{\bar{\theta}}(x_k)]$. This objective encourages the encoder to learn inter-modal structure, enabling, for instance, vision to encode force-relevant cues.
2. **Per-Modality SIGReg (SlicedEppsPulley)**: This regularizer is applied to each modality's embeddings *before* fusion. It prevents collapse within individual modalities and standardizes their magnitudes, making them commensurate for fusion.
3. **Joint SIGReg**: Applied to the final fused latent $z$ to ensure it remains high-rank and expressive. Its empirical effect is noted to be smaller at the current scale but maintained as a safeguard against collapse with wider bottlenecks or larger data.

A decoder is explicitly excluded from the training objective to prevent the latent from being pulled towards pixel memorization, aligning with the "predict-do-not-equate" principle of JEPA. Only the fusion head and per-modality projections are trained, keeping the vision backbone frozen.

#### 3.5. Data

The model is trained on the RH20T corpus (Fang et al., 2023), a large-scale real-robot manipulation dataset consisting of approximately 54.3 million frames across 12,776 scenes, covering 7 configurations and 4 distinct robot embodiments (Flexiv Rizon, UR5, Franka, KUKA iiwa) with heterogeneous sensor suites.
Data streams include RGB frames, joint positions/velocities, TCP pose, and force/torque. Special care is taken for encodings (sin/cos for angles, symlog for unbounded quantities, 6-D rotation for TCP orientation) to handle numerical pathologies. Data is split by (config, task, user) groups for evaluation on held-out scenes, preventing near-copy leakage.

#### 3.6. Evaluation Metrics

A suite of predictor-free metrics is used to evaluate the *frozen* encoder's performance, avoiding confounding effects from trained decoders:

- **Latent Health**: RankMe (Garrido et al., 2023) measures the effective rank of the latent, indicating its expressiveness and detecting collapse.
- **Latent Organization**: Distance correlation measures Spearman correlation between pairwise latent distances and true robot-state distances, assessing if latent geometry reflects state structure. R_LDA (Zheng et al., 2026) measures how many latent directions stably separate samples.
- **Robot-State Recovery**: Linear (ridge regression) and MLP probes are fit on train data and evaluated (R^2) on held-out data to quantify how much of the robot's state (motor, end-effector) can be extracted from the vision-only latent.
- **Invalid-State/Safety Monitor**: Cross-modal prediction error is used to detect out-of-range or scene-swapped states, measured by AUROC.
- **Qualitative Analysis**: PixNerd (Wang et al., 2025a) diffusion decoder reconstructs camera frames from the latent, and patch-feature PCA and attention maps analyze how the vision path is transformed.

#### 3.7. Baselines

Comparison baselines include:
1. **Pretrained ViT (PT-ViT)**: Mean-pooled patch tokens from the frozen LeJEPA ViT-B/16 backbone.
2. **Pretrained ViT (PCA 256)**: The top 256 principal components of the PT-ViT features, serving as a compression control.
3. **Pretrained ViT + Finetuned Linear Head**: A trained 768->256 projection on the frozen backbone, adapted to the robot domain with a vision-only objective.
4. **Vision-Only Control (z_vo_v)**: An encoder with identical architecture and size, trained on the same data but with state streams permanently masked, isolating the effect of cross-modal training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research validates Kepler-Encoder-v0.1 through a series of quantitative and qualitative experiments, focusing on the ability of its vision-only latent representation ($z_v$) to capture robot physical state.

#### 4.1. Overall State Recovery and Cross-Modal Gain

- **End-Effector State and Force**: Kepler-Encoder-v0.1's vision-only latent demonstrates significantly superior recovery of end-effector state, and force in particular, compared to raw frozen-ViT features and a compute-matched vision-only control. The average R^2 for end-effector state was 0.282 for KEv0.1, compared to 0.142 for the vision-only control, 0.206 for a finetuned linear head, and 0.234 for PCA 256. This gain is concentrated where the camera is inherently weakest.
- **Motor State**: For motor state (joint angles, gripper aperture), which is largely visible to the camera, KEv0.1's performance (R^2 0.304) is statistically tied with the strongest vision baselines (PCA 256: 0.308; PT-ViT: 0.315). This indicates that while it performs comparably on visible states, its unique advantage lies in inferring non-visible states.
- **Source of Gain**: Comparison with the compute-matched vision-only control (same architecture, data, compute, but no state streams during training) confirms that the performance increase, especially for end-effector states, is attributable to cross-modal learning, not merely in-domain training or dimensionality reduction.

#### 4.2. Latent Health and Organization

- **Latent Health**: The fused latent ($z_v$) maintains a healthy RankMe score (169.7 out of a 256 ceiling), indicating a non-collapsed, expressive representation. The vision-only control, despite a higher RankMe (189.9), exhibited weaker probes, emphasizing that rank alone does not predict downstream performance.
- **Latent Organization**: KEv0.1's latent is the only feature whose geometry consistently tracks physical state, as measured by distance correlation (0.190 for motor, 0.221 for end-effector). This is notably higher than baselines (|ρ| ≤ 0.03 for frozen vision baselines, 0.050/0.015 for vision-only control). This finding suggests cross-modal training reshapes the vision path to align with underlying body state structure.

#### 4.3. Embodiment Agnosticism and Transfer

- **Single Encoder for Multiple Robots**: A single Kepler-Encoder-v0.1 instance, trained on all seven RH20T configurations, demonstrates the ability to match per-robot specialists on their own robot. It maintains strong performance across all training robots, while specialists degrade sharply when evaluated off their training embodiment (e.g., Kuka specialist R^2 for Flexiv motor state is 0.121 vs. KEv0.1's 0.252).
- **Embodiment Diversity vs. Data Volume**: A data-budget-matched control (KEv0.1 trained on the average specialist's data volume) confirms that the breadth of competence is primarily due to exposure to embodiment diversity rather than simply increased data volume.
- **Zero-Shot Transfer Limitations**: The model does not exhibit strong zero-shot transfer to *unseen* robot embodiments (i.e., when one robot is held out entirely from training). In such leave-one-robot-out tests, the zero-shot latent typically trails PT-ViT on motor probes, suggesting some visual domain tuning to training embodiments.

#### 4.4. Downstream Utility of the Frozen Encoder

- **Invalid-State/Safety Monitor**: The encoder's cross-modal prediction error, without any additional training, serves as an effective anomaly detector. It achieved an AUROC of 0.90 for detecting out-of-range robot states and 0.69 for scene-swapped states on held-out UR5 data.
- **Latent Content Preservation**: A generative diffusion decoder (PixNerd) conditioned on the vision-only latent ($z_v$) can reconstruct the camera frame, confirming that the spatial compression preserves world-state layout (though deliberately abstracting fine texture). Similarly, a state-only latent (vision hidden) can reconstruct arm pose from proprioception, indicating that the latent encodes the robot's body.
- **Latent Storage**: When all modalities are visible at encoding, a ridge probe can recover motor R^2 0.57 and end-effector R^2 0.67 from the full-context latent ($z$), demonstrating that a large share of input information is retained within the 256-D bottleneck.
- **Read-Out Transfer Across Configurations**: Probes fitted on the full-context latent ($z$) do not transfer effectively to the vision-only latent ($z_v$), collapsing to negative R^2 scores. This indicates that the two latents organize state information differently, requiring read-outs to be fitted for the specific sensor configuration deployed.

#### 4.5. Force Isolation

- When force/torque is isolated from TCP pose, KEv0.1's vision-only latent shows modest but consistent R^2 values (0.05, -0.00, 0.19 on flexiv, ur5, kuka respectively) compared to near-zero values for PT-ViT (-0.005, -0.115, 0.103). The gain is significant on all three sensored robots. This suggests that the recoverable force at a single timestep is largely the component entangled with visible joint configuration and contact geometry, pointing to a need for temporal fusion for full dynamic understanding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Kepler-Encoder-v0.1 represents a significant step towards enabling robots to form a more complete understanding of their own body state, particularly regarding aspects not directly observable through vision. Its contributions have several implications for robotics research and application:

- **Enhanced Robot Self-Awareness**: By fusing proprioception and force/torque with vision into a shared latent space, the model allows robots to "perceive" previously invisible internal states, such as contact forces and precise joint torques. This richer, more physical understanding of self is critical for complex manipulation tasks, adaptive control, and safer human-robot interaction.
- **Towards Embodied Foundation Models**: The development of a single, embodiment-agnostic multimodal encoder for heterogeneous robot sensor streams represents progress towards a general-purpose "foundation model" for robotics. This reduces the need for developing and training specialized models for each robot embodiment, thereby streamlining development and deployment across diverse robotic platforms.
- **Efficiency and Scalability**: The self-supervised training paradigm, based on masked cross-modal latent prediction, reduces the reliance on extensive, costly, and often noisy task-specific labels. The efficient latent-query cross-attention mechanism allows for linear scaling with input tokens (O(NM)), making it suitable for integrating numerous and varied sensor inputs without incurring prohibitive computational costs.
- **Improved Vision-Based Control**: The finding that the vision-only latent carries significantly more physical state information, especially force, than traditional vision features implies that policies built on this enhanced visual representation could be more robust and capable, particularly in contact-rich tasks where precise force control is paramount.
- **Inherent Safety Mechanisms**: The ability of the frozen encoder's cross-modal prediction error to act as a training-free invalid-state monitor is a direct, practical safety feature. This allows for early detection of anomalous or out-of-range robot states, enabling timely intervention and potentially preventing damage to the robot or its environment.
- **Structured Representation Learning**: The demonstration that the latent geometry of Kepler-Encoder-v0.1 directly tracks robot state, unlike baselines, suggests it learns a more semantically organized and interpretable representation. This structured latent space could facilitate downstream tasks like planning and reasoning.
- **Foundation for Temporal Understanding**: While the current work focuses on single-timestep fusion, it explicitly lays the groundwork for future extensions incorporating continuous-time, multi-rate temporal fusion. This is expected to unlock the ability to reason about true time-derivatives (velocities, accelerations) and complex contact dynamics, which are crucial for advanced robotic behaviors and for further improving force prediction.
- **Broader Applicability**: The principles and architectural choices for fusing heterogeneous, irregular sensor streams are not limited to robotics. They could potentially be applied to other domains dealing with complex, multi-modal time-series data, such as wearable health monitoring (Narayanswamy et al., 2026) or industrial process control.

The research acknowledges limitations, such as the single-timestep nature constraining dynamic inference and the lack of zero-shot transfer to entirely unseen robot embodiments. However, it provides a validated framework for a more holistic robot perception, addressing critical gaps in current approaches and opening avenues for more capable, robust, and safer autonomous systems.
