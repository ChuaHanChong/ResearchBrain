# VespaSeg: A Resource-Aware Ground-then-Segment Pipeline for Referring Expression Segmentation

> [!summary] Summary
> VespaSeg introduces a resource-aware ground-then-segment pipeline for Referring Expression Segmentation, utilizing compact vision-language models for grounding and MobileSAM for mask generation. This modular approach, adapted via techniques like LoRA, achieves 73.64% mIoU and 84.60% P@0.5 on a specific RefCOCO validation protocol, demonstrating improved computational efficiency for deployment.

## Problem

- Large, monolithic end-to-end Referring Expression Segmentation (RES) systems face deployment challenges due to high computational demands (latency, memory).
- Existing RES models are often impractical for resource-constrained environments or real-time interactive applications.
- Traditional monolithic architectures limit component interchangeability and independent adaptation for specific tasks or hardware.

## Method

- **VespaSeg** implements a two-stage ==`ground-then-segment`== pipeline that explicitly separates semantic localization (bounding box prediction) from pixel-accurate mask refinement.
- It employs compact VLMs (fine-tuned Florence-2 or Moondream2) for the grounding stage to predict bounding boxes, then ==MobileSAM== for segmentation, generating pixel masks with cached, reused image embeddings for multi-query efficiency.
- Parameter-efficient fine-tuning techniques like ==Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)== are applied to Florence-2 and MobileSAM's mask decoder to adapt them to the RefCOCO dataset, while Moondream2 undergoes full fine-tuning.

## Results

- The adapted Florence-2-base grounder combined with LoRA-adapted MobileSAM achieved **73.64%** mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) and **84.60%** Precision at IoU 0.5 (P@0.5) on a repository-specific RefCOCO validation protocol.
- Florence-2-base demonstrated **1.70 times** faster cached-image query throughput (**22.2 queries/second**) and used **1.17 GB** less allocated GPU memory compared to Florence-2-large, with matched accuracy.
- LoRA adaptation of MobileSAM's mask decoder, tested with ground-truth bounding boxes, improved its mIoU from **82.22%** to **86.61%**, highlighting the benefit of targeted mask refinement.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A modular `ground-then-segment` architecture provides a viable pathway for resource-aware RES systems, and parameter-efficient adaptation (e.g., LoRA) effectively improves compact VLM grounders and MobileSAM on RES tasks without extensive training costs.
> - Larger grounder models do not always yield higher accuracy and can incur higher resource costs; Florence-2-base outperformed Florence-2-large in resource efficiency with comparable accuracy.
> - Caching image embeddings from the segmentation model significantly improves throughput in scenarios with multiple queries on the same image.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Referring Expression Segmentation (RES) is a task that involves mapping a natural language expression and an image to the pixel-accurate mask of the described object. This capability is relevant for various applications, including robotic manipulation and assistive interactive systems, where connecting language to visual entities is necessary.

The field of RES has seen rapid advancements, particularly with the development of sophisticated end-to-end systems. However, the scale and complexity of many modern RES models can introduce challenges in deployment, especially in environments with strict latency or memory constraints.

This work addresses these deployment challenges by exploring a modular, ground-then-segment pipeline as an alternative to monolithic end-to-end RES systems. Earlier transformer-based RES approaches, such as LAVT, CRIS, and ReSTR, integrated linguistic and visual representations directly. More recent advancements, including LISA and EVF-SAM, combine large language or multimodal models with segmentation decoders, often leveraging the Segment Anything Model (SAM) for reasoning segmentation.

VespaSeg differentiates itself by maintaining an explicit bounding box interface between the grounding and segmentation stages. This architectural choice prioritizes component interchangeability, independent adaptation of each stage, and the ability to cache image embeddings for efficiency in multi-query scenarios.

The approach utilizes recent developments in promptable and efficient segmentation, notably MobileSAM, which offers a lightweight alternative to SAM's heavy image encoder while retaining its prompt encoder and mask decoder. This makes box-prompted segmentation a practical second stage, as the image embedding can be computed once and reused.

For the grounding stage, the research leverages compact visual grounding models such as Florence-2, which unifies various vision tasks through a prompt-based sequence-to-sequence interface, and Moondream2, an open model designed for compact visual grounding. To adapt these components, the work employs Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), a parameter-efficient fine-tuning technique suitable for large models where full weight updates are computationally expensive. This positions VespaSeg within the landscape of efforts to develop efficient and deployable vision-language models for fine-grained understanding tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and evaluate a resource-aware pipeline, named VespaSeg, for Referring Expression Segmentation. This pipeline aims to provide a balance between segmentation accuracy and the computational resources required for deployment.

The motivation for this work stems from the inherent challenges associated with deploying large, monolithic end-to-end RES systems. While these systems have demonstrated high performance, their computational demands in terms of latency and memory often render them impractical for resource-constrained environments or real-time interactive applications.

To address these limitations, the research proposes and investigates a modular "ground-then-segment" design. This design explicitly separates the task of semantic localization (identifying a bounding box based on a language query) from the task of pixel-accurate mask refinement (generating a mask from the predicted box). This modularity is motivated by several considerations:
- **Deployment Feasibility:** By breaking down the complex RES task into two distinct stages, the pipeline can potentially reduce the overall computational footprint, making it more suitable for deployment in scenarios with limited computational resources, such as edge devices.
- **Component Interchangeability:** The clear interface between the grounding and segmentation stages allows for the independent selection and exchange of different grounder models or segmentation models. This flexibility could facilitate future improvements or adaptations to specific application requirements without overhauling the entire system.
- **Independent Adaptation:** Each component of the pipeline can be adapted or fine-tuned independently. This enables targeted optimization, for example, improving grounding accuracy without affecting segmentation capabilities, or vice-versa, potentially reducing the complexity and cost of training.
- **Resource Efficiency through Caching:** For scenarios involving multiple language queries referring to the same image, the modular design enables caching of the image embedding generated by the segmentation model's image encoder. This reduces redundant computation, enhancing throughput for interactive or multi-query use cases.

The research specifically aims to evaluate the performance of compact Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like Florence-2 and Moondream2 as grounders when integrated with MobileSAM for segmentation. It also focuses on quantifying the impact of parameter-efficient adaptation techniques (e.g., LoRA) on both the accuracy and resource consumption (cached-query throughput and allocated GPU memory) of the pipeline. Ultimately, the work seeks to provide evidence supporting the utility of compact, modular designs for RES.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VespaSeg implements a two-stage inference pipeline: ground-then-segment. Given an image (𝐼) and a natural language expression (𝑞), a compact Vision-Language Model (VLM) grounder, denoted as $G_\theta$, first predicts a bounding box $\hat{b} = (x_1, y_1, x_2, y_2)$. Subsequently, MobileSAM, denoted by its encoder $E_\phi$ and decoder $D_\psi$, processes this predicted bounding box to generate a pixel mask $\hat{M}$. A key aspect of this design is that MobileSAM's image embedding, $z = E_\phi(I)$, is computed once per image and then reused for consecutive queries on the same image, optimizing for multi-query scenarios.

For the grounding stage, two distinct VLM families are employed:
- **Florence-2:** This model receives a specific task token, `<CAPTION_TO_PHRASE_GROUNDING>`, followed by the expression $q$. It then returns coordinates that are decoded to the input image frame.
- **Moondream2:** This model processes a pointing query and returns normalized coordinates, which are similarly converted into a bounding box. Invalid or improperly ordered coordinates are clamped to ensure valid inputs for MobileSAM.

The adaptation strategy involves targeted fine-tuning for improved performance on the RefCOCO dataset:
- **Florence-2 Grounder Adaptation:** Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is applied to specific linear projections within Florence-2 (q_proj, k_proj, v_proj, out_proj, fc1, and fc2). The LoRA configuration used $r=8$ and $\alpha=16$. Training utilized 120,624 expression pairs, generated by expanding every sentence in the 42,404 RefCOCO training records. Training parameters included a batch size of 4, gradient accumulation of 4, the AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of $10^{-4}$, and ran for three epochs.
- **MobileSAM Mask Decoder Adaptation:** LoRA is also applied to eligible linear layers within MobileSAM's mask decoder, using $r=8$ and $\alpha=16$. This stage was trained on 42,404 RefCOCO training records for 10 epochs with a batch size of 4 and a learning rate of $10^{-4}$. The loss function used for this adaptation was $\mathcal{L}_{\text{mask}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{Dice}} + 0.5 \mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}}$. Critically, this adaptation was performed using ground-truth bounding boxes to isolate the mask adaptation from potential grounding errors. This process updated 167,936 parameters out of MobileSAM's 10,298,028, representing 1.63% of the total.
- **Moondream2 Grounder Adaptation:** In contrast to LoRA for Florence-2, the Moondream2 checkpoint was fully fine-tuned (not LoRA-adapted) on the first expression of each of the 42,404 training records. This training ran for three epochs with a batch size of 2 and a learning rate of $10^{-5}$.

The experimental setup utilized the RefCOCO dataset, specifically its Hugging Face representation `jxu124/refcoco`. The evaluation protocol employed in this work is repository-specific. It selects only the first expression (sentence index zero) from each validation record, resulting in 3,811 instance–query pairs for evaluation. The authors explicitly state that this protocol is not directly comparable to the standard RefCOCO validation split, which contains 10,834 expressions, and that no results are claimed for the testA or testB splits. Performance is measured using mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) and Precision at IoU 0.5 (P@0.5), both reported as percentages.

Runtime measurements were conducted on an NVIDIA RTX 6000 Ada Generation GPU, using half-precision inference and PyTorch scaled dot-product attention. For Florence-2, images were resized to a maximum side of 320 pixels, and a maximum of 32 tokens were generated by default. The timing protocol included VLM preprocessing, VLM generation, and box-prompted MobileSAM decoding. A significant detail is that MobileSAM's image encoder runs outside the per-query timer when the input image changes, and its embedding is reused for subsequent queries on the same image. Consequently, the reported metric is "cached-image query throughput" (queries per second, q/s), not a direct video frame rate. Memory usage is reported as the mean PyTorch allocated GPU memory, sampled after each query, distinct from total board consumption.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the VespaSeg pipeline's accuracy, resource utilization, and the impact of component-level adaptations and inference budget adjustments.

**Repository-Protocol Accuracy:**
On the 3,811-pair repository-specific RefCOCO validation protocol, the adapted Florence-2-base grounder combined with LoRA-adapted MobileSAM achieved the highest reported performance, with a mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) of 73.64% and Precision at IoU 0.5 (P@0.5) of 84.60%. Unadapted Florence-2 variants, despite using the adapted MobileSAM, showed substantially lower mIoU scores (e.g., 40.40% for Florence-2-base), indicating that RefCOCO-specific grounding adaptation contributed significantly to the overall pipeline improvement. The fully fine-tuned Moondream2 grounder, integrated with adapted MobileSAM, yielded 63.22% mIoU and 71.37% P@0.5.

**Matched Florence-2 Resource Comparison:**
A comparative study on a matched subset of 500 validation pairs between the adapted Florence-2-base and Florence-2-large variants (both with adapted MobileSAM) revealed that the base model performed comparably or slightly better in terms of accuracy (73.73% mIoU for base vs. 72.82% mIoU for large). Concurrently, Florence-2-base demonstrated superior resource efficiency: it processed cached-image queries 1.70 times faster (22.2 q/s vs. 13.0 q/s) and utilized 1.17 GB less allocated GPU memory (2.21 GB vs. 3.38 GB). This suggests that for the evaluated operating point, the larger grounder model did not provide a performance advantage and incurred higher resource costs.

**Component and Inference-Budget Ablations:**
- **MobileSAM Mask Adaptation:** An ablation study using ground-truth bounding boxes showed that applying LoRA to the MobileSAM mask decoder improved its mIoU from 82.22% to 86.61% on a fixed 1,000-record validation subset, isolating the benefit of mask refinement adaptation.
- **Moondream2 Box Adaptation:** Fully fine-tuning Moondream2 independently improved its box IoU from 69.33% to 70.09% on a 500-record validation subset.
- **Florence-2-base Input and Generation Budget:** Varying the input image resolution and token generation limit for the adapted Florence-2-base pipeline demonstrated that reducing the output token budget from 64 to 32 tokens had no measurable impact on accuracy or memory usage, while slightly increasing throughput. Reducing the maximum image side to 256 or 192 pixels yielded only marginal throughput gains at the cost of progressively lower mIoU. Based on these results, the chosen operating point for the Florence-2-base pipeline was set at 320 pixels maximum image side and 32 output tokens.

**Qualitative Observations:**
Qualitative examples from a 200-pair diagnostic subset were presented to illustrate significant improvements. These instances included cases where the adapted model successfully recovered from initial complete grounding failures (0.000 IoU) to high IoU values (e.g., 0.970, 0.965) by correctly interpreting spatial and descriptive language. Another example showed improvement from a partial error (0.114 IoU) to accurate segmentation (0.921 IoU). These observations are illustrative of the pipeline's capabilities in specific instances, but are not intended to be statistically representative.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research demonstrates the viability of a modular ground-then-segment pipeline, VespaSeg, as an approach to Referring Expression Segmentation that can balance accuracy with resource efficiency. The explicit interface between visual grounding and promptable segmentation offers practical advantages for deployment in resource-constrained environments.

The findings indicate that compact, adapted Vision-Language Models, such as Florence-2-base, when integrated with an adapted MobileSAM, can achieve competitive accuracy while maintaining a relatively high cached-image query throughput and lower allocated GPU memory. The comparative study between Florence-2-base and Florence-2-large specifically highlights a resource advantage for the smaller model in the evaluated setup, suggesting that increasing grounder capacity does not universally translate to improved performance in this pipeline configuration and may increase resource consumption. This has implications for selecting appropriate base models for similar modular architectures.

The successful application of LoRA for both the grounding and segmentation stages underscores the efficacy of parameter-efficient fine-tuning techniques for adapting pre-trained models to specific RES tasks. This independent adaptation capability provides flexibility in optimizing individual components, potentially reducing the overall training complexity and cost. The identification of a no-loss reduction in the generation token budget for Florence-2 also contributes to optimizing inference efficiency without compromising accuracy.

The modular design inherently supports scenarios where image embeddings can be cached, such as interactive systems or applications involving multiple queries on the same visual content. This could lead to more responsive and efficient human-computer interfaces.

However, the authors acknowledge several limitations, which temper broader claims but also delineate critical areas for future work. The evaluation protocol, which uses only the first expression of each RefCOCO validation record, means that the reported accuracy is not directly comparable to standard RefCOCO leaderboard results and might be an optimistic estimate due to checkpoint selection based on validation behavior. Consequently, the research underscores the necessity for comprehensive evaluation on the complete standard RefCOCO expression splits (including testA and testB) using held-out checkpoints and multiple seeds to establish generalizability and robustness.

Furthermore, the resource measurements, conducted on a high-end desktop GPU with cached image embeddings, do not directly translate to video frame throughput, embedded-device latency, or total energy consumption. Future work should focus on performing independent end-to-end latency and peak-memory measurements on edge hardware to fully assess deployability. A direct comparison against current end-to-end RES systems under consistent protocols is also required to benchmark the proposed pipeline comprehensively.

Despite these limitations, VespaSeg represents a step towards developing efficient and deployable RES solutions, particularly for interactive and resource-sensitive applications. The release of code, training scripts, and result artifacts enhances reproducibility and facilitates further research in modular vision-language pipelines.
