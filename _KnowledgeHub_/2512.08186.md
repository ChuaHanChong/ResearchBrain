# Ground Slow, Move Fast: A Dual-System Foundation Model for Generalizable Vision-and-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> The DualVLN framework addresses Vision-Language Navigation by asynchronously integrating a slow VLM for high-level reasoning and a fast diffusion policy for real-time local control. This "Ground Slow, Move Fast" approach yields enhanced generalization and robust performance, including dynamic obstacle avoidance, across various simulated and real-world robotic platforms.

## Problem

- Existing VLM-based Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) systems generate fragmented, unnatural motions with high latency due to frequent, synchronous calls to large models for short-horizon actions.
- Current end-to-end VLM-based VLN methods tightly couple high-level reasoning, global planning, and local control, hindering agile decision-making and robust adaptation in dynamic, human-centric environments.
- Most VLN benchmarks focus on static environments, lacking evaluation for dynamic obstacle avoidance and social awareness, which are critical for real-world robot deployment.

## Method

- ==Introduces DualVLN==, a "**Ground Slow, Move Fast**" dual-system architecture that explicitly decouples a VLM-based global planner (System 2) from a diffusion transformer local controller (System 1).
- System 2 (Ground Slow) utilizes a finetuned QwenVL-2.5 model to predict the farthest visible pixel goal and performs self-directed view adjustments, operating at a slower frequency of 2 Hz.
- System 1 (Move Fast) is a lightweight Diffusion Transformer policy that generates smooth, continuous trajectories at a faster frequency (30 Hz), conditioned on high-frequency RGB inputs and implicit latent goal embeddings from System 2.

## Results

- DualVLN achieved state-of-the-art performance on VLN-CE (e.g., **64.3%** SR on R2R Val-Unseen) and VLN-PE benchmarks, substantially outperforming prior RGB-based and VLM-based approaches.
- The system demonstrated robust zero-shot long-horizon planning and real-time dynamic obstacle avoidance across diverse real-world scenarios and multiple robot platforms (wheeled, quadruped, humanoid).
- On the newly introduced Social-VLN benchmark, DualVLN showed improved task completion (**37.2%** SR) and a lower Human Collision Rate (**35.4%**) compared to baselines, indicating enhanced human-aware navigation capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly decoupling high-level VLM reasoning from low-level local control enables both robust long-horizon planning and agile, real-time action execution, overcoming limitations of monolithic end-to-end VLM systems.
> - Combining explicit pixel goals with implicit learned latent goal representations from the VLM provides richer, more adaptive guidance for the local controller, effectively bridging the semantic understanding and low-level control gap.
> - A decoupled sequential training strategy allows the VLM (System 2) to leverage vast data for generalizable reasoning, while the lightweight local policy (System 1) efficiently learns control on smaller datasets, preserving VLM generalization and improving scalability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) is a fundamental problem in robotics, requiring an agent to interpret natural language instructions and navigate through an environment based on visual observations. The field has evolved significantly, moving from early benchmarks focused on discrete goal planning to continuous action spaces and, more recently, to physically realistic simulations incorporating locomotion controllers. The advent of large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has further propelled VLN research, promising unprecedented generalization capabilities through transferred prior knowledge.

However, despite these advancements, existing VLM-based VLN methods, often referred to as Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, largely adopt an end-to-end paradigm. These models typically map high-level vision and language inputs directly to short-horizon discrete actions (e.g., move forward 0.25m). While effective in some scenarios, this design introduces critical limitations for real-world deployment. Such systems often produce fragmented and unnatural motions, leading to high execution latency due to frequent calls to large, computationally expensive VLMs. Furthermore, by tightly coupling vision-language reasoning, global planning, and local control into a single pipeline, these models struggle with agile control and dynamic obstacle avoidance, essential requirements in complex, human-centric environments.

DualVLN addresses these limitations by introducing a novel, *asynchronous dual-system architecture*. While some recent dual-system or hierarchical approaches have been explored in robotics, they often focus on tabletop manipulation tasks or lack the asynchronous "slow-fast" design necessary for robust, long-horizon, and real-time navigation in complex 3D environments. DualVLN differentiates itself by being the *first dual-system VLN foundation model* specifically designed for generalizable, long-horizon instruction following, accurate planning, and navigation in unseen environments, crucially bridging the gap between VLM reasoning capabilities and the agility required for real-time robotic control.

Moreover, the paper contributes to the research landscape by introducing the **Social-VLN benchmark**. This benchmark specifically targets the evaluation of a navigation model's ability to handle dynamic obstacles and recover tasks in human-centric environments. This is a significant addition, as most existing VLN benchmarks, including VLN-CE, primarily focus on static environments, leaving a critical gap in assessing social awareness and real-time adaptability—abilities vital for practical robotic deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind DualVLN stems from the inherent limitations of current end-to-end VLM-based VLN systems when applied to real-world robotics. These systems often exhibit:
1. **Fragmented and Unnatural Motions:** Direct mapping to short-horizon discrete actions results in jerky, inefficient movements.
2. **High Latency:** Frequent, synchronous calls to large VLMs for every action decision lead to slow overall execution.
3. **Lack of Hierarchical Coordination:** Entangling high-level reasoning, global planning, and local control hinders agile decision-making and robust adaptation in dynamic scenarios.
4. **Poor Real-world Adaptability:** Inability to effectively handle challenges like dynamic obstacle avoidance, a critical aspect of navigation in human-populated spaces.

To overcome these challenges, the authors set forth the following key objectives for DualVLN:
- **Decouple High-Level Reasoning from Low-Level Control:** Create a dual-system architecture that explicitly separates the VLM's robust reasoning capabilities (for global planning) from the agility required for real-time action execution (for local control).
- **Enable Robust Real-time Control:** Design a system capable of generating smooth, continuous trajectories and adaptively making local decisions in complex, dynamic environments.
- **Preserve VLM Generalization:** Ensure that the VLM's strong prior knowledge and generalization ability are retained when adapted for navigation tasks, particularly in downstream low-level planning.
- **Achieve Interpretable and Effective Local Navigation:** Develop a local policy that can effectively translate high-level goals into traversable paths while also being transparent in its decision-making.
- **Demonstrate Superior Performance:** Outperform existing state-of-the-art VLN methods across various benchmarks and prove robustness in challenging real-world deployments, including dynamic obstacle avoidance.
- **Introduce a Socially-Aware Benchmark:** Develop a new benchmark, Social-VLN, to specifically evaluate a model's capacity for social awareness and task recovery in dynamic environments, thereby pushing the frontier of VLN research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DualVLN introduces a novel "Ground Slow, Move Fast" dual-system framework that synergistically integrates high-level reasoning with low-level action execution. This architecture comprises two distinct, yet interconnected, systems:

**System 2: VLM-Based Pixel-Goal Grounding with Self-Directed View Adjustment (Ground Slow)**
- **Role:** This system acts as the global planner, performing slow but robust high-level reasoning.
- **Core Component:** It is built upon QwenVL-2.5 (7B), a powerful open-source Vision-Language Model.
- **Functionality:**
 - **Farthest Pixel Goal Grounding:** System 2 receives a sequence of egocentric RGB images and a natural language instruction. It formulates high-level planning as predicting 2D pixel coordinates within the image that correspond to the *farthest visible* preferred navigation waypoint. This approach provides spatially grounded mid-term targets.
 - **Self-Directed View Adjustment:** To address issues like occlusions or misaligned viewpoints, System 2 autonomously decides when to adjust its camera angle (e.g., Turn Left/Right 15°, Look Up/Down 15°) to gain a more informative perspective before making a pixel-goal prediction. This mimics human navigation behavior of looking around.
- **Output:** The system outputs an explicit pixel goal (2D coordinates) and generates a set of compact *latent goal representations* for System 1.
- **Frequency:** Operates at a slower frequency of 2 Hz, allowing for deeper reasoning.
- **Training:** QwenVL-2.5 is fully finetuned for one epoch on a pixel-goal grounding task, including self-directed view adjustments and STOP actions.

**System 1: A Diffusion Transformer Policy with Multimodal Conditioning (Move Fast)**
- **Role:** This system serves as the agile local controller, translating high-level goals into smooth, continuous trajectories.
- **Core Component:** It is implemented as a lightweight Diffusion Transformer (DiT) policy.
- **Functionality:**
 - **Multimodal Conditioning:** System 1 generates continuous trajectories (32 dense waypoints) conditioned on two crucial sources: (1) low-frequency *latent goal embeddings* (Z') extracted from System 2, and (2) high-frequency RGB inputs from the robot's current observation.
 - **Latent Goal Representation:** Learnable latent queries (special tokens) are appended to System 2's context features. These queries are optimized via prompt tuning, allowing them to attend to and extract task-relevant semantic information from System 2's hidden states, providing implicit, adaptive guidance to System 1.
 - **Asynchronous Inference:** System 1 operates at a much faster frequency (30 Hz) than System 2. It handles the asynchronous nature by encoding both the last RGB frame from System 2 (at time *t*) and the current observation (at time *t+k*), fusing them to adapt to dynamic changes and estimate distance traveled. A Q-Former compresses visual features for efficiency.
- **Output:** A sequence of smooth, obstacle-aware waypoints that a low-level robot controller (200 Hz) can follow.
- **Training:** System 2's weights are frozen during System 1's training. Only the latent queries and the DiT-based diffusion policy are trained using a flow matching objective, which minimizes the mean squared error between predicted and true trajectory velocities.

**Key Design Principles:**
- **Decoupled Sequential Training:** This two-stage training strategy allows System 2 to specialize in generalized pixel goal grounding using large-scale reasoning data, while System 1 can then be efficiently trained on a smaller dataset of low-level goal-reaching trajectories, preserving the VLM's generalization and enabling efficient learning.
- **Combined Explicit and Implicit Goals:** The explicit pixel goal provides clear, interpretable guidance, while the implicit latent features from the VLM offer richer, more adaptive, and task-relevant information, creating a deeper connection between reasoning and local planning.
- **Asynchronous "Ground Slow, Move Fast" Paradigm:** This core design enables real-time adaptability and smooth motion by allowing the VLM to perform complex reasoning at a lower frequency, while a specialized, lightweight policy rapidly executes actions, making the system robust to dynamic environments.

**Social-VLN Benchmark:** To rigorously evaluate socially-aware navigation, the authors introduce the Social-VLN benchmark. This benchmark extends R2R-CE by incorporating multiple dynamic humanoid agents (from Habitat 3.0) strategically placed along ground-truth VLN trajectories. This setup increases interaction likelihood and creates challenging scenarios. A new metric, Human Collision Rate (HCR), is introduced alongside standard VLN metrics. Training data for social navigation is collected using a modified A* algorithm that replans collision-free trajectories when human detection exceeds a threshold.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DualVLN demonstrates significant advancements in vision-language navigation, outperforming prior state-of-the-art methods across various benchmarks and real-world scenarios.

**Simulation Experiments:**
- **VLN-CE (R2R and RxR Val-Unseen splits):** DualVLN achieves substantial gains over all prior RGB-based approaches. For example, on R2R Val-Unseen, it achieves an SR of 64.3% and SPL of 58.5%, significantly higher than StreamVLN (56.9% SR, 51.9% SPL) and NaVILA (54.0% SR, 49.0% SPL), while using only first-person RGB inputs. This highlights the effectiveness of its dual-system design in leveraging VLM reasoning.
- **VLN-PE (Physically Realistic Environments):** On the VLN-PE benchmark, which simulates robot dynamics and control errors, DualVLN surpasses all baselines, including those explicitly trained on VLN-PE data and other VLM-based methods. It achieves a significantly lower Navigation Error (NE) of 4.66m (compared to NaVid's 5.94m) and a higher Success Rate (SR) of 51.60% (compared to NaVid's 22.42%) on R2R Validation Unseen, demonstrating superior robustness and effectiveness in continuous, physically realistic navigation.
- **Social-VLN Benchmark:** Evaluation on the newly introduced Social-VLN benchmark reveals the inherent difficulty of dynamic environments. Both DualVLN and StreamVLN show a considerable performance drop compared to static VLN tasks (e.g., DualVLN's SR decreases by ~27%). However, DualVLN still achieves better task completion (37.2% SR) and lower Human Collision Rate (HCR) (35.4%) than StreamVLN, proving its enhanced capability in human-aware obstacle avoidance.

**Real-World Cross-Embodiment Experiments:**
- DualVLN demonstrates robust long-horizon planning, real-time trajectory execution, and dynamic obstacle avoidance across diverse real-world scenarios (office, canteen, street, convenience store) and multiple robot platforms (wheeled Turtlebot4, quadruped Unitree Go2, humanoid Unitree G1). This is achieved in a zero-shot setting, without scene-specific finetuning.
- Compared to other VLM-based baselines in real-world tests, DualVLN consistently achieves high Success Rate and low Navigation Error across varying difficulty levels (hallway, bedroom, office scenarios). Other methods struggled with complex tasks, often missing goals or encountering collisions, whereas DualVLN successfully navigates cluttered environments, handles staircases, and avoids dynamic pedestrians.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Impact of Decoupled Training:** Joint end-to-end training of System 1 and System 2 without explicit intermediate pixel goals ("w/o Sys.2 Train") resulted in significantly slower convergence and degraded VLM generalization, affirming the crucial role of decoupled sequential training.
- **Role of Explicit Pixel Goal:** Removing the explicit pixel-goal text from System 1's conditioning ("w/o Pixel Goal") led to a clear performance drop, highlighting the value of explicit spatial guidance for the diffusion policy, which also enhances interpretability.
- **Role of Implicit Latent Goal:** When only the last-layer VLM hidden states of the pixel-goal text were used instead of learned latent queries ("w/o Latent Goal"), performance was weaker. This shows that adaptive information flow from System 2 to System 1 through learned latent queries is essential for extracting task-relevant representations.
- **System 1 vs. SOTA Point-Goal Nav Policies:** Replacing System 1 with state-of-the-art point-goal navigation policies (iPlanner, NavDP) yielded worse performance, even with oracle depth. This is attributed to the trajectory distribution gap and System 1's inherent robustness to minor pixel-goal deviations while maintaining obstacle avoidance, a robustness that point-goal planners lack.
- **Data Scaling for System 1:** System 1's performance saturates with a relatively small amount of training data (around 10% of System 2's data), indicating its lightweight nature and the relatively simpler nature of its low-level control task compared to the VLM's high-level reasoning.
- **Consistency:** Analysis confirms a strong correlation between predicted pixel goals and generated trajectories, with trajectories consistently oriented toward and reaching areas near the pixel goals.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DualVLN represents a significant leap forward in the field of Vision-Language Navigation, offering a robust and generalizable solution for embodied agents in complex, dynamic environments. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Novel Architectural Paradigm:** The "Ground Slow, Move Fast" dual-system design is a novel and highly effective paradigm for embodied AI. By explicitly decoupling high-level semantic reasoning from agile, low-level action execution, DualVLN overcomes fundamental limitations of prior end-to-end approaches, particularly fragmented motion, high latency, and poor real-world robustness. This architectural innovation provides a blueprint for future embodied agents.

- **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** DualVLN's ability to achieve state-of-the-art performance across diverse VLN benchmarks and, critically, demonstrate robust long-horizon planning and real-time adaptability in unseen real-world scenarios (including dynamic obstacle avoidance and cross-embodiment transfer) is groundbreaking. This level of generalization, achieved without scene-specific finetuning, is crucial for deploying robots in varied and unpredictable human environments.

- **Bridging the Semantic-Control Gap:** The work effectively bridges the longstanding gap between high-level semantic understanding provided by VLMs and the precise, agile motion control required for robotic execution. The use of both explicit pixel goals and implicit latent features for conditioning System 1 creates a deep and adaptive connection, allowing the robot to leverage VLM intelligence for practical navigation.

- **Practical Real-World Applicability:** The asynchronous inference pipeline, where the slow VLM generates targets and the fast diffusion policy executes them, ensures smooth, near real-time navigation. This, coupled with proven dynamic obstacle avoidance capabilities on various robot platforms, makes DualVLN highly relevant for practical deployment in applications such as service robotics, logistics, and assistive robotics, where interaction with humans and dynamic environments is common.

- **Efficient and Scalable Learning:** The decoupled sequential training strategy is efficient, allowing each system to specialize and scale independently. System 2 leverages vast VLM data for reasoning, while System 1 requires relatively less data for its specialized control task, making the overall training process more manageable and scalable.

- **New Benchmark for Socially-Aware AI:** The introduction of the Social-VLN benchmark is a critical contribution to the research community. By focusing on dynamic obstacles and social awareness, it pushes the frontier of VLN research beyond static environments, encouraging the development of more sophisticated and human-safe navigation behaviors essential for future robots.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** As a "foundation model" and part of the "InternVLA-N1" framework, DualVLN offers a flexible and scalable base. It is likely to inspire and facilitate further research towards more general-purpose, multimodal, and truly real-world-ready embodied agents, accelerating progress in the broader field of embodied AI and robotics. The associated open-source initiatives (InternNav) will further amplify its impact by making these advancements accessible to the wider research community.
