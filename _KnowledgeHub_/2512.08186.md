# Ground Slow, Move Fast: A Dual-System Foundation Model for Generalizable Vision-and-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> The DualVLN framework addresses Vision-Language Navigation by asynchronously integrating a slow VLM for high-level reasoning and a fast diffusion policy for real-time local control. This "Ground Slow, Move Fast" approach yields enhanced generalization and robust performance, including dynamic obstacle avoidance, across various simulated and real-world robotic platforms.

## Problem

- Existing VLM-based Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) systems generate fragmented, unnatural motions with high latency due to frequent, synchronous calls to large models for short-horizon actions.
- Current end-to-end VLM-based VLN methods tightly couple high-level reasoning, global planning, and local control, hindering agile decision-making and robust adaptation in dynamic, human-centric environments.
- Most VLN benchmarks focus on static environments, lacking evaluation for dynamic obstacle avoidance and social awareness, which are critical for real-world robot deployment.

## Method

- ==Introduces DualVLN==, a "**Ground Slow, Move Fast**" dual-system architecture that explicitly decouples a VLM-based global planner (System 2) from a diffusion transformer local controller (System 1).
- System 2 (Ground Slow) utilizes a finetuned QwenVL-2.5 model to predict the farthest visible pixel goal and performs self-directed view adjustments, operating at a slower frequency of 2 Hz.
- System 1 (Move Fast) is a lightweight Diffusion Transformer policy that generates smooth, continuous trajectories at a faster frequency (30 Hz), conditioned on high-frequency RGB inputs and implicit latent goal embeddings from System 2.

## Results

- DualVLN achieved state-of-the-art performance on VLN-CE (e.g., **64.3%** SR on R2R Val-Unseen) and VLN-PE benchmarks, substantially outperforming prior RGB-based and VLM-based approaches.
- The system demonstrated robust zero-shot long-horizon planning and real-time dynamic obstacle avoidance across diverse real-world scenarios and multiple robot platforms (wheeled, quadruped, humanoid).
- On the newly introduced Social-VLN benchmark, DualVLN showed improved task completion (**37.2%** SR) and a lower Human Collision Rate (**35.4%**) compared to baselines, indicating enhanced human-aware navigation capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly decoupling high-level VLM reasoning from low-level local control enables both robust long-horizon planning and agile, real-time action execution, overcoming limitations of monolithic end-to-end VLM systems.
> - Combining explicit pixel goals with implicit learned latent goal representations from the VLM provides richer, more adaptive guidance for the local controller, effectively bridging the semantic understanding and low-level control gap.
> - A decoupled sequential training strategy allows the VLM (System 2) to leverage vast data for generalizable reasoning, while the lightweight local policy (System 1) efficiently learns control on smaller datasets, preserving VLM generalization and improving scalability.
