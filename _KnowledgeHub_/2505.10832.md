# Learning When to Think: Shaping Adaptive Reasoning in R1-Style Models via Multi-Stage RL

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Institute of Automation, Chinese Academy of Sciences, Pengcheng Laboratory, and the University of Chinese Academy of Sciences developed AutoThink, a multi-stage reinforcement learning framework that teaches R1-style Large Language Models to adaptively determine when to employ explicit reasoning. This approach reduced token usage by up to 52% while maintaining or improving accuracy on mathematical and general benchmarks.

## Problem

- Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) generate verbose, step-by-step reasoning for all problems, leading to significant computational overhead, increased latency, and excessive token usage, even for simple tasks.
- Prior methods for efficient reasoning often rely on manual mode selection, uniform pruning, or hard-coded prompt designs, lacking the ability for LLMs to intrinsically adapt to problem complexity.
- Current RL-based pruning techniques primarily aim to shorten outputs by penalizing length, rather than enabling adaptive, difficulty-aware reasoning.

## Method

- **AutoThink**, a ==multi-stage Reinforcement Learning== (RL) framework, was developed using the GRPO algorithm.
- A foundational "ellipsis prompt" (\n...\n) was identified to stochastically trigger latent "thinking" or "no-thinking" modes in R1-style models.
- Reward shaping across three stages: ensuring mode stability, reinforcing accuracy in both modes, and pruning unnecessary reasoning with length-aware rewards.

## Results

- AutoThink-trained models on Distill-R1-**1.5B** achieved a **52%** reduction in token usage with a **6.4%** relative accuracy improvement over standard prompt baselines.
- The framework successfully fostered adaptive and difficulty-aware reasoning, with models engaging in explicit thinking more frequently for harder problems.
- Adaptive reasoning capabilities generalized to diverse non-mathematical benchmarks (e.g., GPQA, MMLU), often surpassing baseline accuracy with reduced response length.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A minimal ellipsis prompt (\n...\n) can unlock latent "thinking" or "no-thinking" behaviors in R1-style LLMs, offering a new control signal.
> - A multi-stage reinforcement learning approach with carefully designed reward functions is essential to stabilize dual reasoning modes and progressively optimize for both accuracy and efficiency.
> - LLMs can be taught to intrinsically adapt their reasoning depth based on problem complexity, enabling a form of metacognitive ability beyond manual or uniform strategies.
