# MSRL: Scaling Generative Multimodal Reward Modeling via Multi-Stage Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A Multi-Stage Reinforcement Learning (MSRL) method scales the training of generative multimodal reward models (MRMs) by sequentially leveraging large-scale textual preference data before adapting to multimodal inputs. This approach yielded substantial performance improvements across various visual understanding and generation tasks, including an average accuracy increase from 66.6% to 75.9% on VL-RewardBench for an 8B model.

## Problem

- Scaling Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for generative Multimodal Reward Models (MRMs) is bottlenecked by the scarcity and high cost of human-annotated multimodal preference data.
- Traditional approaches to address multimodal data scarcity, such as generating reward signals from confidence estimation, often suffer from error propagation and lead to performance plateaus.
- Developing a scalable curriculum for MRM training without an unscalable budget for multimodal data annotation remains a key challenge.

## Method

- A ==Multi-Stage== Reinforcement Learning (**MSRL**) framework is proposed, starting with Reinforcement Learning on large-scale textual preference data to build foundational reward reasoning capabilities.
- Preference generalization is achieved in a second stage by training with caption-based data and employing Cross-Modal Knowledge Distillation (CMKD) to bridge the task and modality gaps between text and vision.
- The final stage involves targeted Reinforcement Learning on a limited amount of original multimodal preference data, leveraging the robust reasoning abilities developed in preceding stages.

## Results

- MSRL consistently improved MRM performance across tasks: on VL-RewardBench, average accuracy increased from **66.6%** to **75.9%**; on Multimodal RewardBench, it improved from **76.2%** to **80.5%** (for an **8B** InternVL3.5 backbone).
- The method achieved substantial gains in video tasks, reaching **85.5%** on ShareGPT and **81.4%** on GenAI-Bench (video generation) for the **8B** backbone, outperforming vanilla generative baselines by +**4.9%** and +**13.1%** respectively.
- Ablation studies demonstrated the crucial contribution of textual RL (Stage 1), which accounted for a **6.9%** accuracy improvement on VL-RewardBench, confirming its importance for scaling MRMs and showing strong low-data efficiency for later multimodal stages.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Core preference reasoning abilities can be effectively learned from abundant, readily available text-only data and subsequently transferred to multimodal contexts.
> - Cross-modal knowledge distillation, combined with training on caption-based data, serves as an effective mechanism to bridge task and modality gaps for transferring textual reasoning to visual tasks.
> - Scaling RL on textual data significantly reduces the need for extensive and costly multimodal preference annotations, making generative MRM training more scalable and efficient.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancement of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) is significantly influenced by human preference alignment, a process often facilitated by multimodal reward models (MRMs). Initially, MRMs were primarily discriminative, classifying outputs as preferred or not. The field has since transitioned to generative reward modeling, which frames reward prediction as a chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning and text generation task. This generative approach allows MRMs to articulate *why* an output is preferred, mirroring human evaluative processes.

A further development in this area is the integration of reinforcement learning (RL) into MRM training, specifically Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). RLVR utilizes explicit preference labels to construct reward signals, enabling MRMs to refine their reasoning capabilities. However, a significant limitation of RLVR-based MRM training is its reliance on large volumes of human-annotated multimodal preference data. Such data is expensive and labor-intensive to acquire, creating a scalability bottleneck that impedes the further enhancement of MRMs and, consequently, MLLMs.

Existing approaches to address this data scarcity, such as generating reward signals from confidence estimation or self-verification, have shown limitations, often leading to error propagation and performance plateaus. This research addresses the fundamental challenge of scaling RL for MRMs without an unscalable budget for multimodal data annotation. It builds on the hypothesis that core preference reasoning abilities can be learned from abundant, text-only data and subsequently transferred to multimodal contexts, aligning with observations that preferences can generalize across modalities. This work seeks to provide a scalable curriculum for MRM training, distinguishing itself by systematically leveraging textual data before progressively incorporating multimodal information.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a scalable approach for training generative multimodal reward models (MRMs) that can effectively overcome the bottleneck of limited multimodal preference data. The authors propose a Multi-Stage Reinforcement Learning (MSRL) method to achieve this.

The motivation for this work stems from several key observations and challenges in the field of MLLMs and MRMs:

- **Human Preference Alignment is Critical for MLLMs:** MLLMs increasingly rely on human preference alignment to generate outputs that are helpful, safe, and factually accurate. MRMs are instrumental in this process, either by providing reward signals for fine-tuning MLLMs or by re-ranking candidate outputs during inference.
- **Shift to Generative MRMs:** The evolution from discriminative to generative MRMs, which produce chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning alongside preference predictions, represents a significant improvement in capturing complex human judgments. This approach leverages the inherent generative capabilities of MLLMs for more robust reward modeling.
- **Effectiveness of RLVR:** Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has been shown to enhance the reasoning capabilities of generative MRMs. By optimizing MRMs with explicit reward signals derived from preference labels, models can learn to generate more accurate and well-justified preference predictions.
- **The Multimodal Data Bottleneck:** Despite the benefits of generative MRMs and RLVR, their scalability is severely constrained by the scarcity of high-quality, human-annotated multimodal preference data. Obtaining such data is costly, time-consuming, and labor-intensive, making it impractical to scale MRM training using conventional RLVR methods directly on multimodal data.
- **Unexploited Potential of Textual Data:** The authors posit that the core reasoning abilities required for preference modeling might be learned from abundant, readily available text-only data and then transferred across modalities. This perspective challenges the conventional assumption that the multimodal data bottleneck must be addressed solely with more multimodal annotations.
- **Need for Cross-Modal Generalization:** Bridging the gap between textual and multimodal reasoning is crucial for enabling effective knowledge transfer. This involves addressing both task structure differences and inherent modality differences.

In summary, the core motivation is to enable scalable RL-based training for generative MRMs by strategically leveraging large-scale textual preference data, thereby mitigating the need for extensive and costly multimodal annotations. This approach aims to enhance MRM performance across diverse visual understanding and generation tasks without requiring additional multimodal preference annotations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is Multi-Stage Reinforcement Learning (MSRL), a curriculum-based approach designed to scale generative multimodal reward modeling with limited multimodal data. MSRL comprises three progressive stages, coupled with a cross-modal knowledge distillation technique. The base model for the MRM is an MLLM, such as InternVL3.5.

#### 3.1. Reinforcement Learning on Textual Data (Stage 1)

- **Objective:** To establish a robust and generalizable reward reasoning capability from abundant textual preference data. This addresses the data scarcity bottleneck by leveraging readily available text-only resources.
- **Process:**
 1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** The MRM is initially fine-tuned to generate well-structured chain-of-thought (CoT) outputs, including `<think>` and `<answer>` tags, and to follow the required output format. This SFT is performed on publicly available datasets that provide complete rationales (e.g., HelpSteer3).
 2. **RLVR on Textual Preference Data:** Following SFT, Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) is applied using the objective function:
 $L_{RLVR} = -E_{(p,x,y_a,y_b,l)\sim D_r, o\sim \pi_{\theta}} [r_v (s, o)] - \beta D_{KL}(\pi_{\theta} ||\pi_{\theta_{old}})$
 where $r_v(s, o) = r_{format}(s, o) + r_{accuracy}(s, o)$. The format reward $r_{format}$ is 1 if the output structure is correct, 0 otherwise. The accuracy reward $r_{accuracy}$ is 1 if the predicted preference matches the ground truth, 0 otherwise. This stage utilizes a large-scale textual preference dataset (e.g., GRAM-R2) with approximately 1M labeled examples.
 3. **Model Configuration:** During this text-only training, all parameters of the vision encoder and projector are frozen, as supervision signals are exclusively textual.
- **Outcome:** A text-only trained MRM ($\pi_{\theta_{text}}$) with strong reward reasoning capability derived from large-scale data.

#### 3.2. Preference Generalization (Stage 2)

This stage aims to transfer the textual reward reasoning capability to multimodal tasks by addressing both task and modality gaps.

- **Reinforcement Learning on Caption-Based Data:**
 - **Objective:** To bridge the "task gap" by transferring textual knowledge to visual tasks while preserving semantic grounding.
 - **Process:** A caption-based dataset is constructed by replacing images or videos in multimodal preference data with their corresponding textual captions (e.g., generated by GPT-5). RLVR is then applied using the same objective as Stage 1.
 - **Task Recognition Reward:** A new reward, $r_{task}$, is introduced. The model is prompted to output a task tag (e.g., `<type>Image Understanding</type>`) before reasoning. If the predicted type matches the ground truth, a reward (e.g., 0.2) is given; otherwise, 0. This encourages the MRM to differentiate among multimodal task types.
 - **Catastrophic Forgetting Mitigation:** To prevent forgetting the textual reasoning learned in Stage 1, an experience replay strategy is employed, mixing a subset of high-quality textual preference samples from Stage 1 into each training batch.
- **Cross-Modal Knowledge Distillation (CMKD):**
 - **Objective:** To address the "modality gap" by aligning textual and multimodal reward reasoning.
 - **Process:**
 1. Given a multimodal preference sample $s$ and its caption $c$, the caption-trained MRM ($\pi_{\theta_{text}}$) generates $n$ sampled rationales: $\{o_1, o_2..., o_n\} \sim \pi_{\theta_{text}}(\cdot | s, c)$.
 2. These rationales are distilled into a single high-quality supervisory signal $o^*$ through a three-step selection process:
 - Filter rationales whose predicted label differs from the majority vote (pseudo-label $\hat{w}$).
 - Filter out rationales that do not conform to the required output format.
 - Select the remaining rationale with the highest confidence.
 3. SFT is then performed on pairs $[c, o^*]$, encouraging the MRM to reproduce the distilled reasoning and preference prediction conditioned solely on the visual input, i.e., $- \log \pi_{\theta}([c, o^*] | s)$. The model also generates the visual caption (tagged as `<caption>`) before performing reward reasoning in subsequent RL.
- **Model Configuration:** The vision encoder and projector are unfrozen in this stage to enable multimodal adaptation.

#### 3.3. Reinforcement Learning on Multimodal Data (Stage 3)

- **Objective:** To fully adapt the model to handle genuine multimodal tasks using limited multimodal data.
- **Process:** RL is performed directly on the original multimodal preference data (containing visual inputs rather than captions) using the objective from Eq. (2) and incorporating the task recognition reward from Stage 2.
- **Data Efficiency:** Since the preceding stages have already endowed the model with strong reward reasoning and partial multimodal generalization, this final stage requires significantly less multimodal data. This makes the overall process scalable.
- **Scaling Principle:** The method emphasizes that scaling RL on abundant textual data can continually strengthen the model’s reward reasoning capability, thereby improving multimodal reward modeling without requiring additional costly multimodal data.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Model Backbones:** InternVL3.5 models (1B, 4B, 8B, 14B parameters) were used.
- **Training Datasets:**
 - Stage 1: 40k rationale-based preference examples from HelpSteer3 (SFT), followed by 400k preference examples from GRAM-R2 (RLVR).
 - Stage 2: 20k caption-based preference examples constructed from various sources spanning image understanding, image generation, video understanding, and video generation.
 - Stage 3: 20k multimodal examples from the same sources as Stage 2, but with original visual inputs.
- **Optimization:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) was used for all RL training.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:**
 - Image Understanding: VL-RewardBench, Multimodal RewardBench.
 - Image/Video Generation: GenAI-Bench, VideoGen-RewardBench.
 - Video Understanding: ShareGPTVideo.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrated that MSRL enhances the performance of generative multimodal reward models across various visual understanding and generation tasks, particularly in scenarios with limited multimodal data.

#### 4.1. Performance on Image Understanding and Generation Tasks

- **Consistent Improvements:** MSRL consistently and substantially improved performance. For instance, on VL-RewardBench, the 8B InternVL3.5 backbone saw an average accuracy increase from 66.6% (Generative MRM baseline) to 75.9% with MSRL. On Multimodal RewardBench, it improved from 76.2% to 80.5%.
- **Outperformance of Baselines:** MSRL significantly outperformed both discriminative and vanilla generative MRM baselines trained on the same limited multimodal preference data. This highlights the effectiveness of leveraging large-scale textual data for scaling multimodal reward modeling.
- **Comparison with CoT-based MRMs:** MSRL achieved stronger performance compared to CoT-based MRMs that rely solely on limited multimodal preference data. The method demonstrated a more direct and scalable approach by extending RL to abundant textual preferences without requiring additional costly multimodal annotations.
- **Impact of Majority Voting:** A simple majority voting strategy (e.g., +voting@16) further boosted performance, yielding average gains of 1.0-1.5% (e.g., 80.5% to 81.3% on Multimodal RewardBench for the 8B model).
- **Generalization to Image Generation:** Similar performance trends were observed on the GenAI-Bench for image generation, confirming MSRL's effectiveness across understanding and generation tasks. For the 8B backbone, MSRL achieved 75.7% on GenAI-Bench, compared to 70.2% for the generative baseline.

#### 4.2. Performance on Video Understanding and Generation Tasks

- **Addressing Challenges in Video Data:** MSRL effectively navigated the inherent challenges of rich, temporal-visual data, noise, and scarcity in video preference data.
- **Substantial Gains:** On video understanding (ShareGPT) and video generation (GenAI-Bench), MSRL achieved significant improvements. For the 8B backbone, MSRL reached 85.5% on ShareGPT and 81.4% on GenAI-Bench, outperforming the vanilla generative MRM by +4.9% and +13.1%, respectively.
- **Robust Generalization:** These consistent gains across video tasks demonstrated MSRL's efficiency in scaling multimodal reward modeling and its robust generalization capability across diverse multimodal tasks.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study using the InternVL3.5-8B backbone on VL-RewardBench investigated the contribution of each MSRL stage:

- **Importance of Stage 1 (Textual RL):** Removing Stage 1 (textual reinforcement learning) resulted in a substantial performance drop of +6.9% compared to the full MSRL, yielding 68.8% accuracy versus MSRL's 75.9%. This indicates that leveraging abundant textual preferences is crucial for scaling MRMs.
- **Indispensability of Stage 2 (Preference Generalization):** Removing Stage 2 (caption-based data training and CMKD) led to a clear performance drop (-1.6% to -2.6%), underscoring its role in bridging textual and multimodal domains. Specifically, removing caption-based data training resulted in 74.3% accuracy, and removing CMKD resulted in 73.4%.
- **Contribution of Stage 3 (Multimodal RL):** While multimodal adaptation (Stage 3) further refined reward reasoning and enhanced generalization, MSRL still surpassed the generative baseline by a considerable margin even without this stage (72.6% vs. 66.6%). This implies that a significant portion of the model's preference reasoning capability is acquired in the earlier stages.

#### 4.4. Scaling Behavior Across Model Sizes

- **Consistent Scaling Trend:** MSRL exhibited a clear and robust scaling trend, delivering consistent performance gains across model sizes ranging from 1B to 14B parameters. Larger models benefited even more substantially from textual RL, highlighting the robustness and practicality of the approach.
- **Low-Data Efficiency:** The comparison between using 5k and 20k multimodal samples in Stage 3 demonstrated MSRL’s strong low-data efficiency. Even with only 5k multimodal examples, MSRL achieved substantial improvements. This suggests that once large-scale textual reward reasoning is established, the marginal returns from additional multimodal learning signals diminish, requiring only a small amount of multimodal data for strong performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents MSRL as a method to address a critical bottleneck in the advancement of multimodal large language models (MLLMs): the scarcity and high cost of human-annotated multimodal preference data required for training robust reward models.

The significance of this work lies in several areas:

- **Scalability of Multimodal Reward Modeling:** MSRL demonstrates that generative multimodal reward models (MRMs) can be effectively scaled by strategically leveraging readily available, large-scale textual preference data. This approach mitigates the reliance on expensive multimodal annotations, offering a practical pathway to develop more capable MRMs.
- **Enhanced Reasoning Capabilities:** By first grounding reward reasoning in abundant textual data, MSRL enables MRMs to acquire a strong and generalizable understanding of preferences before adapting to multimodal contexts. This structured curriculum facilitates the transfer of complex reasoning abilities across modalities.
- **Improved MLLM Alignment:** More robust and scalable MRMs directly translate to better human preference alignment for MLLMs. This can lead to MLLMs that generate outputs that are more helpful, safe, factually grounded, and creatively aligned with human intent across both visual understanding and generation tasks.
- **Cross-Modal Generalization Techniques:** The introduction of caption-based reinforcement learning and cross-modal knowledge distillation (CMKD) provides concrete mechanisms for bridging the "task gap" and "modality gap" between textual and multimodal data. These techniques contribute to the broader understanding of how to effectively transfer knowledge between different data modalities.
- **Practical Implications for Research and Development:** The findings suggest that researchers and developers can allocate resources more efficiently by prioritizing the collection of diverse textual preference data and relatively smaller, targeted multimodal datasets. This could accelerate the development of advanced MLLMs by lowering the data acquisition barrier for reward model training.
- **Foundation for Future Work:** MSRL's multi-stage approach, particularly its emphasis on scaling textual RL, opens new avenues for exploring how foundational reasoning capabilities can be cultivated and transferred across complex AI systems. The observation that multimodal learning signals exhibit diminishing returns after strong textual reasoning is established provides valuable insights into future data collection and training strategies.

Overall, MSRL offers a scalable and efficient paradigm for training generative MRMs, contributing to the development of more sophisticated and human-aligned MLLMs that can operate effectively across diverse multimodal applications.
