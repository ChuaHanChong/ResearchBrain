# Hierarchical Reactive Grasping via Task-Space Velocity Fields and Joint-Space Quadratic Programming

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at MIT developed a hierarchical control framework enabling high-Degrees-of-Freedom arm-hand systems to perform real-time, collision-free grasping in dynamic and cluttered environments. The system achieved 100% grasp success for complex concave objects in simulation and demonstrated robust reactivity to external perturbations and moving obstacles on a 15-DoF robot.

## Problem

- Generating real-time, collision-free global motion plans for high-Degrees-of-Freedom (DoF) robotic arm-hand systems is computationally prohibitive.
- Existing reinforcement learning (RL) approaches struggle with generalizability in cluttered or diverse environments.
- Model-based methods often get trapped in local minima and struggle with complex object geometries like concave shapes.

## Method

- A two-layer hierarchical control framework is proposed, separating global guidance in task space from local feasibility in joint space.
- The higher layer generates desired velocities for fingertips (via 3D collision-free path optimization), fingertip orientations, and gripper joints, prioritizing objectives dynamically.
- The lower layer utilizes a Joint-Space Quadratic Program (QP) to track these desired velocities in real-time, while satisfying full-body collision avoidance, joint limits, and target/obstacle avoidance constraints.

## Results

- Achieved 100% grasp success for concave objects like mugs and wine glasses in simulation, vastly outperforming a linear baseline that frequently failed (e.g., 30.7% for mug).
- Demonstrated robust real-time reactivity to significant external physical pushes on the robot arm and dynamic changes in target object positions in both simulation and real-world.
- Successfully navigated cluttered environments with multiple moving obstacles, achieving high grasp success rates (e.g., 7/10 for 3 obstacles) on a 15-DoF real robot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling global motion guidance in a lower-dimensional task space from local feasibility in the full joint space significantly improves real-time reactivity for high-DoF robots.
> - Employing dynamically weighted task-space velocity fields for fingertip positions, orientations, and gripper joints enables robust guidance and adaptation to task progress.
> - Lightweight 3D collision-free path optimization for fingertip targets allows the system to escape local minima, particularly for grasping highly concave objects.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical and long-standing challenge in robotics: **real-time, collision-free global motion planning for high-Degrees-of-Freedom (DoF) systems**, specifically in the context of reactive grasping in dynamic and unstructured environments. As robotic systems transition from controlled industrial settings to more complex real-world scenarios, the ability to adapt in real-time, known as reactive control, becomes paramount. Grasping, a fundamental manipulation skill, is particularly difficult for high-DoF arm-hand systems due to the inherent complexity of coordinating many joints while simultaneously avoiding collisions and adapting to environmental changes.

The authors effectively position their work by highlighting the limitations of existing approaches:

- **Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based methods,:** While showing promise, these approaches are often constrained to single-object, obstacle-free, or top-down reach scenarios, limiting their generalizability to out-of-distribution or cluttered environments. Their reliance on extensive training data can also be a bottleneck.
- **Artificial Potential Fields (APF), and Dynamical Systems (DS),:** These model-based approaches offer local modulation for obstacle avoidance. However, they face challenges in task space (e.g., fingertip positions) due to tedious weight tuning, poor representation of highly concave obstacles, and susceptibility to local minima without formal convergence guarantees for complex geometries. In joint space, collision constraints are typically highly non-convex, making these methods difficult to apply directly.
- **Iterative Motion Optimization methods, such as Model Predictive Control (MPC),:** MPC can generate collision-free motions in a closed-loop manner and, with long planning horizons, can escape local minima. However, its computational cost scales cubically with both state dimensionality and planning horizon. This makes it too slow for truly reactive global planning for high-DoF arm-hand systems (which can have 15+ DoF, more than double a typical 7-DoF arm), often limiting its use to "quasi-reactive" scenarios requiring strong warm-start heuristics or problem-specific sampling.

This paper's contribution lies in proposing a novel **hierarchical control framework** that aims to bypass the "coupled growth of state and time-horizon dimensionality" that plagues MPC for high-DoF systems. By strategically decoupling global planning in a lower-dimensional task space from local tracking in the full joint space, the authors seek to achieve real-time reactivity, generalizability across clutter, and robustness to dynamic disturbances, thereby advancing the state-of-the-art in model-based reactive manipulation. The work also explicitly leverages recent advancements in deep visual perception models, such as FoundationPose, acknowledging the crucial role of accurate, real-time perception for reactive control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a **fast and reactive grasping framework** for high-dimensional arm-hand robotic systems operating in unstructured and dynamic environments. The authors are motivated by the inherent difficulty of performing collision-free motion planning in real-time for systems with many degrees of freedom, especially when dealing with complex object geometries and unforeseen environmental changes.

More specifically, the key objectives include:

- **Real-time Collision-Free Motion Generation:** To enable high-DoF arm-hand systems to perform reaching motions towards target grasp poses without colliding with the environment, themselves, or obstacles, and to do so within strict real-time constraints (e.g., 50 Hz or faster).
- **Adaptability to Dynamic Environments and External Disturbances:** To ensure the robot can dynamically adjust its trajectory in response to sudden changes in the target object's position, external forces applied to the robot, or the movement of obstacles.
- **Overcoming High-DoF Planning Challenges:** To address the combinatorial explosion of the search space that makes traditional real-time global planning intractable for high-DoF systems, particularly when planning horizons are long.
- **Robust Grasping of Complex Objects:** To facilitate stable grasping of objects with challenging, highly concave geometries (e.g., wine glasses, bowls) that often trap simpler linear planning methods in local minima.
- **Generalizability Across Clutter and Diverse Grasping Directions:** To propose a model-based framework that can generalize naturally beyond simple, obstacle-free, or top-down scenarios, allowing for varied approach vectors and cluttered scenes.

The motivation stems from the growing need for robots that can operate autonomously in human-centric environments, which are inherently dynamic and unpredictable. Grasping is a foundational skill, and improving its reactivity and robustness for complex robotic hands is a crucial step towards more capable and versatile robotic assistants.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology is a **two-layer hierarchical control framework** that strategically decouples global guidance from local feasibility, enabling real-time reactive grasping for high-DoF systems.

**Assumptions:**
The framework operates under the assumption that real-time perception modules are available. These modules provide:
- Accurate recognition of object geometries and target fingertip positions.
- Dynamic tracking of object poses (e.g., using FoundationPose).
- Signed Distance Functions (SDFs) for target objects and obstacles.
- Predefined candidate fingertip target positions.

**The Two Layers:**

1. **Higher Layer: Global Guidance via Task-Space Velocity Fields**
 This layer is responsible for generating high-level velocity targets that guide the robot towards a desired grasp pose, primarily focusing on global paths without fully accounting for complex joint-space constraints. It constructs time-invariant velocity fields in multiple coordinates:

 - **Fingertip Position Velocity Fields:** To overcome local minima often encountered by simple linear attractors, the desired fingertip velocity ($\dot{x}_{i,des}$) for each fingertip is derived from a lightweight **3D collision-free path optimization** problem in R3. This optimization minimizes path curvature while respecting signed distance constraints to the target object and other obstacles. Similar to MPC, only the *initial velocity direction* from this globally optimized path is extracted and used as the desired velocity vector. A scalar speed function ensures constant speed when far from the target and a smooth, quadratic decay as it approaches. This allows for globally informed guidance in a low-dimensional space (3D for each fingertip).

 - **Auxiliary Velocity Fields for Grasp Stability:**
 - **Fingertip Orientations:** An angular velocity field ($\omega_{i,des}$) is defined for each fingertip to guide its orientation towards satisfying a "stable grasping criterion" $g(\cdot)$. This field is derived from the gradient of the stability score, effectively applying a torque to correct undesirable orientations. A sigmoid-like weight parameter $\alpha(g)$ ensures that this correction is prioritized only when the grasp stability criterion is not met, smoothly decreasing its influence as stability improves.
 - **Gripper Joints:** An auxiliary velocity field ($\dot{q}_{gr,des}$) is introduced for the gripper's own joints to drive it towards a nominal, open configuration when the fingertips are far from the target. This ensures the gripper is in a suitable state for grasping. A weight parameter $\beta(h)$, dependent on the fingertip position error $h(x,x^*)$, downweights this objective when the fingertips are already close to their targets.

2. **Lower Layer: Local Feasibility via Joint-Space Quadratic Programming (QP)**
 This layer computes feasible joint velocities ($\dot{q}_{des}$) by locally tracking the high-level velocity targets generated by the higher layer. It uses a **Quadratic Program (QP)** formulation that linearizes all relevant constraints around the current robot configuration over a short planning horizon $H$.

 - **Objective Function:** The QP minimizes a weighted sum of squared errors between the desired velocities from the higher layer and the actual joint velocities. The weights $\alpha(g)$ and $\beta(h)$ from the auxiliary fields play a crucial role in prioritizing objectives dynamically.
 - Primary objective: Tracking fingertip linear velocities.
 - Secondary objectives (weighted): Tracking fingertip angular velocities and gripper joint velocities.
 - **Constraints:**
 - **Full-Body Collision Avoidance:** Includes robot-environment collisions and self-collisions, defined by the minimum Euclidean distances $\Gamma(q)$ and their gradients.
 - **Target Object/Obstacle Avoidance:** Uses signed distance functions $d(X(q), O)$ between gripper geometry points and obstacles/target.
 - **Joint Limits:** Enforces joint position and velocity limits ($\dot{q}_{min} \le \dot{q} \le \dot{q}_{max}$ and $q_{min} \le q + \dot{q}H \le q_{max}$).
 - **Solution:** The QP is solved efficiently using modern solvers like OSQP. The planning horizon $H$ and safety margins $\epsilon$ allow for tuning the conservativeness of the tracking behavior. The resulting desired joint velocity is then integrated and applied via PD control with gravity compensation.

**Implementation Details:**
The framework utilizes standard robotics tools: Pinocchio for forward kinematics and Jacobians, FCL for collision/proximity queries. For representing object geometries, superquadric models are used for convex shapes, and GPU-accelerated nearest-neighbor queries on point clouds are employed for concave or thin-surface objects. The 3D path optimization is solved using Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP) with a few iterations, initialized by a GPU-accelerated sampling-based search for a via point.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research validated its framework through extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world settings, using a 15-DoF arm-hand manipulation system.

**A. Simulation Studies (Mujoco Physics Engine):**
- **Superior Grasp Success for Concave Objects:** The proposed method achieved a **100% grasp success rate** across a diverse set of concave objects (Bowl, Dish, Mug, Wine Glass) under varying initial conditions. This significantly outperformed a baseline linear fingertip velocity field (used in), which showed very low success rates for concave objects (e.g., 30.7% for Mug, 32.0% for Wine Glass), consistently getting trapped in local minima.
- **Reduced Fingertip Position Errors:** The hierarchical method maintained consistently low fingertip position errors at convergence (mostly below 1 cm, with a maximum of ~2 cm for the thin "Dish" due to table collision constraints). The linear method, in contrast, exhibited large errors for concave shapes, indicating failed convergence.
- **Robustness to External Perturbations:** The system demonstrated strong real-time reactive capabilities. It successfully recovered and re-converged to the target grasp despite significant external physical pushes to the robot arm and sudden, interactive changes in the target object's position. This highlights the closed-loop nature and adaptability of the control strategy.
- **Collision Avoidance in Cluttered Dynamic Environments:** The method successfully navigated around multiple moving, non-target obstacles (modeled as superellipsoids) to reach and grasp a target wine glass without collisions, showcasing its ability to handle dynamic clutter.

**B. Real Robot Experiments:**
- **Robustness to Real-World Disturbances:** Experiments on a physical 15-DoF robot confirmed the framework's real-time responsiveness to strong disturbances (large object position deviations, strong robot pushes). High success rates were observed (5/5 for bowl, cup, wine glass, 4/5 for tape), even with occasional, recoverable finger-object collisions due to tracking errors. Failures were mainly attributed to perception module limitations (e.g., object loss due to occlusion) or specific challenges like objects still rolling (tape), which the system, lacking prediction, could not preempt.
- **Navigation in Obstacle-Rich Scenes:** The robot successfully performed grasping tasks in the presence of randomly placed static obstacles. Success rates for grasping a bowl were encouraging (8/10 for 1 obstacle, 8/10 for 2, 7/10 for 3 obstacles), demonstrating its practical ability to navigate complex environments.
- **Perception Integration:** The successful real-world trials underscore the effective integration of state-of-the-art perception modules like FoundationPose for real-time object pose tracking and BundleSDF for mesh reconstruction.
- **Demonstrated Limitations:** The study candidly highlighted a limitation: while fingertip paths may appear collision-free from a task-space perspective, the independent planning in the higher layer can occasionally lead the entire arm into sub-optimal or problematic joint configurations. These include situations where the arm faces an obstacle, or gets trapped in local minima in narrow passages that are physically impassable for the full robot, even if the fingertip path is clear. This occurs because the high-level planning doesn't fully consider the entire robot's geometry in complex, tight spaces.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of reactive robotic manipulation, particularly for high-DoF arm-hand systems. Its impact is multifaceted:

- **Enabling Robust Reactive Grasping:** The proposed hierarchical framework provides a highly effective solution for real-time, collision-free grasping in dynamic and cluttered environments. This capability is crucial for deploying robots in various unstructured settings, from manufacturing and logistics to domestic assistance and disaster response.
- **Addressing Computational Bottlenecks:** By decoupling global guidance into a lower-dimensional task space and handling local feasibility with a constrained joint-space QP, the method effectively mitigates the combinatorial explosion issue inherent in high-DoF planning. This allows for truly reactive control rates (e.g., 100 Hz planning) that were previously challenging for MPC-like approaches in such high-dimensional systems.
- **Enhanced Grasping of Complex Geometries:** The method's ability to robustly grasp highly concave objects (like wine glasses and bowls) is a notable achievement. This overcomes a common failure mode of simpler planning strategies that get trapped in local minima around intricate object shapes.
- **Practical Applicability and Real-World Validation:** The extensive validation in both high-fidelity simulations and real-world experiments using a complex 15-DoF arm-hand system lends strong credibility to the framework. The integration with cutting-edge perception technologies like FoundationPose demonstrates its readiness for practical deployment.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper identifies clear avenues for future work, such as integrating learning-based grasp point generation for novel objects and incorporating higher-level, lower-frequency joint-level optimization to address the limitations observed in extremely cluttered environments. This open-ended discussion fosters further development and refinement of reactive manipulation strategies.
- **Inspiration for Control Architectures:** The hierarchical structure, which prioritizes and weights different task-space and joint-space objectives within a real-time QP, offers a versatile paradigm for control system design. This approach can inspire similar solutions for other complex manipulation tasks requiring a balance between global objectives and local constraint satisfaction.

In conclusion, this work contributes a robust and efficient framework that pushes the boundaries of what high-DoF robotic systems can achieve in dynamic and challenging manipulation tasks. Its emphasis on reactivity, collision avoidance, and handling complex object geometries makes it a valuable contribution to the field of robotics, paving the way for more autonomous and adaptable robotic agents.
