# From Seeing to Experiencing: Scaling Navigation Foundation Models with Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UCLA developed the Seeing-to-Experiencing (S2E) framework, which scales navigation foundation models by combining large-scale offline video pre-training with reinforcement learning, leading to policies with enhanced interactive capabilities and zero-shot generalization across diverse robotic platforms. The work also introduces NavBench-GS, a photorealistic 3D benchmark for evaluating navigation policies in dynamic environments.

## Problem

- Current navigation foundation models, pre-trained on passive offline data, lack causal reasoning, counterfactual understanding, and real-time reactivity to dynamic elements in complex environments.
- These models primarily 'see' the world without truly 'experiencing' it through active interaction, leading to limitations in adapting to unforeseen circumstances.
- Existing reinforcement learning approaches for generalizable navigation suffer from poor sampling efficiency and lack strong inductive priors when trained in constrained synthetic environments, hindering real-world transfer.

## Method

- The ==Seeing-to-Experiencing== (S2E) framework was introduced, integrating offline pre-training on real-world videos with online reinforcement learning in simulated environments.
- Anchor-Guided Distribution Matching (AGDM) was employed during pre-training to robustly learn and represent the complex, multimodal nature of navigation trajectories.
- A Residual-Attention Module (RAM) was designed for reinforcement learning post-training, allowing the model to acquire reactive behaviors by adaptively tuning interaction-centric cross-attention layers while freezing generalizable perceptual components.

## Results

- S2E-Full (pre-training + RL) achieved a **21%** improvement in success rate over the pre-trained S2E-BC model on the NavBench-GS benchmark, particularly in obstacle and obstacle-pedestrian scenarios.
- The framework demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization and performance when deployed on two distinct physical robotic platforms (wheeled and quadruped robots) in real-world scenarios.
- S2E-Full consistently outperformed state-of-the-art navigation foundation models across all four NavBench-GS task categories, showing superior interactive and reactive capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning is a more effective mechanism than solely scaling offline data for enhancing navigation foundation models, overcoming the diminishing returns observed in pure behavior cloning approaches.
> - The targeted adaptation facilitated by the Residual-Attention Module (RAM) enables efficient injection of interactive capabilities without catastrophic forgetting of pre-trained knowledge or significant representation drift to synthetic data.
> - Modeling multimodal action distributions through Anchor-Guided Distribution Matching (AGDM) during pre-training provides a robust and structured backbone, improving the model's ability to capture diverse navigation behaviors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses challenges in applying large-scale foundation models, which have demonstrated capabilities in areas such as language understanding, generation, and visual recognition, to the domain of robot navigation. While foundation models often exhibit enhanced generalizability and adaptability due to training on massive datasets, their application to sequential decision-making in dynamic real-world environments, such as urban navigation, presents specific difficulties.

Existing navigation foundation models, exemplified by works like Shah et al. (2023a;b) and Sridhar et al. (2024), typically rely on pretraining with large-scale web videos and human demonstrations. These approaches primarily involve passive visual learning, where models are trained to imitate observed behaviors. A key limitation of such methods is their reliance on data that captures visual correlations but lacks explicit information on physics or cause-and-effect relationships. Consequently, policies derived solely from offline data often exhibit limited reactivity to dynamic surroundings and may struggle to adapt to diverse objects and motions, such as avoiding obstacles or moving pedestrians. The paper characterizes this gap as a transition from "seeing" to "experiencing."

Conversely, while reinforcement learning (RL) offers a paradigm for interactive learning and bridging the gap between observations and actions, its standalone application to generalizable navigation has shown limited success (Shen et al., 2019; Putta et al., 2024). Prior RL approaches frequently train agents in narrow synthetic environments, which can lead to poor sampling efficiency and a lack of inductive priors necessary for scalable and generalizable navigation in the real world.

The paper positions its contribution within a hybrid learning paradigm, which combines large-scale pretraining with RL fine-tuning. This approach has gained traction in other fields, such as superhuman performance in games (Silver et al., 2016) and alignment of large language models (LLMs) with human preferences through Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) (Touvron et al., 2023). However, the paper notes that the systematic comparison and application of RL versus Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for scaling foundation models in robot learning remain underexplored compared to their discussion in LLM contexts (Kumar et al., 2025).

Furthermore, the work addresses limitations in current evaluation benchmarks. Previous evaluations for navigation foundation models often rely on offline 2D video-based testing (Shah et al., 2023a;b; Sridhar et al., 2024). This paper introduces a new benchmark, NavBench-GS, which leverages photorealistic 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstructions and incorporates physical interactions to facilitate reactive, closed-loop policy assessment. This aims to provide a more comprehensive and realistic evaluation of navigation models in terms of generalizability and safety.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the capabilities of navigation foundation models by enabling them to perform interactive and safe behaviors in dynamic real-world environments, while retaining their generalization capacity. This involves bridging the performance gap between models trained on passive observations and those requiring active interaction and adaptation.

The motivations for this work stem from several identified limitations of existing approaches:
- **Addressing Deficiencies of Offline-Trained Models:** Current navigation foundation models, trained predominantly on offline datasets, often lack the ability to reason about the consequences of their actions or to adapt to unforeseen circumstances through counterfactual understanding. This limitation is particularly pronounced in urban navigation, where real-time obstacle and pedestrian avoidance, as well as safe maneuvering, are critical. The authors motivate the necessity for agents to transition from merely "seeing" to "experiencing" the world, learning from interactive feedback.
- **Overcoming Generalization Challenges of Pure RL:** While reinforcement learning offers a mechanism for interactive learning, its direct application to generalizable navigation has been limited. RL agents often struggle to transfer skills learned in narrow simulated environments to the diverse and complex real world, due to issues like poor sampling efficiency and a lack of robust inductive priors. The motivation is to combine the strengths of large-scale visual pretraining with interactive RL to overcome these individual limitations.
- **Preserving Generalizability while Enhancing Interactivity:** A core motivation is to develop a framework that allows navigation models to acquire new interactive skills through RL in simulation without degrading the broad visual representations and generalizable knowledge gained from pretraining on extensive real-world video data. This requires a selective fine-tuning approach.
- **Developing Robust Technical Innovations:** To achieve the above, the authors are motivated to introduce specific technical components:
 - **Anchor-Guided Distribution Matching (AGDM):** This is motivated by the challenge of modeling multimodal action distributions inherent in robot navigation trajectories. Standard methods (e.g., discrete actions, unimodal Gaussians, overly flexible diffusion models) often fall short. AGDM aims to provide a structured, multimodal representation that is expressive, stable during training, and suitable for subsequent RL fine-tuning.
 - **Residual-Attention Module (RAM):** This module is motivated by the problems of "catastrophic forgetting" and "domain shift" that arise when fine-tuning pre-trained models with RL. Full parameter fine-tuning can lead to the loss of previously acquired capabilities and overfitting to simulator-specific visual statistics. RAM aims to enable targeted adaptation of interaction-related components while preserving the pre-trained visual backbone.
- **Establishing a Comprehensive Evaluation Standard:** The motivation for introducing NavBench-GS is to address the shortcomings of existing 2D video-based evaluation benchmarks, which cannot fully assess reactive, closed-loop navigation policies. The new benchmark aims to provide a photorealistic, physically interactive, and reproducible environment for systematically evaluating the generalizability and safety of navigation models in diverse and challenging scenarios.
- **Investigating Scaling Laws in Robot Learning:** The research is also motivated by a broader interest in understanding how different post-training paradigms (RL vs. SFT) impact the scaling of robot learning capabilities, particularly in the context of diminishing returns observed with offline data alone.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces the **Seeing-to-Experiencing (S2E) learning framework**, a hybrid approach that integrates pretraining on large-scale offline videos with post-training through reinforcement learning in simulated environments. The framework aims to train a visual navigation policy that directs a robot from a source to a target waypoint, emphasizing local navigation that can be chained for long-distance tasks.

#### 3.1. Pretraining with Anchor-Guided Distribution Matching (AGDM)

The initial phase involves pretraining a robust visual navigation backbone on diverse, real-world navigation videos. This addresses the challenge of modeling multimodal data distributions, where multiple valid actions might exist for a given observation.
- **Limitations of Prior Action Representations:** The paper notes that discrete actions or unimodal Gaussian representations often lack the expressiveness required for multimodal behaviors. Diffusion models, while expressive, can be overly flexible and challenging to control, potentially leading to unsafe or unstable trajectories.
- **Anchor-Guided Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM):** S2E proposes representing robot actions (short-term relative waypoints) using an anchor-guided GMM. This involves defining `M` "anchor points," which are representative intention points generated by K-Means clustering on the dataset and uniformly sampled in the robot's forward direction. Each anchor corresponds to a distinct Gaussian mode within the mixture, allowing the policy to predict diverse yet goal-aligned trajectories. The model also predicts a velocity scale for each mode.
- **Model Architecture:** The backbone employs a DINOv3-based visual encoder for processing continuous RGB frames (past `k` frames and current frame). Goal information (target coordinates or target image) is also encoded. These tokens, along with anchor representations, are fed into a Transformer-based architecture comprising a 6-layer encoder and a 6-layer decoder. The decoder uses anchor features as queries to generate action-relevant representations, which are then decoded by MLP heads to predict trajectory parameters (mean, standard deviation, correlation for each Gaussian mode) and mode scores. Stochastic masking is applied to goal signals during training to prevent shortcut learning.
- **Training Objective:** The model is trained end-to-end using a combination of Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL) loss for supervising classification and trajectory heads, and an L2 regression loss for optimizing the velocity scale. For NLL, an assignment strategy is used, selecting the mode whose predicted direction best aligns with the ground-truth trajectory.

#### 3.2. Reinforcement Learning with Residual-Attention Module (RAM)

To imbue the pre-trained model with interactive capabilities and address limitations of full fine-tuning, S2E incorporates a reinforcement learning phase using a Residual-Attention Module.
- **Challenges of Naive Fine-Tuning:**
 - **Forgetting of Pre-trained Capabilities:** Full-parameter fine-tuning can lead to a loss of previously acquired behaviors due to interference and covariate shift, particularly in infrequently visited states.
 - **Domain Shift:** Updating the visual encoder on simulator-only observations can cause it to overfit to simulated visual statistics, resulting in a degradation of real-world performance when deployed.
- **Residual-Attention Module (RAM) Design:** RAM addresses these challenges by enabling selective fine-tuning. It freezes the parameters of the original pre-trained cross-attention layers (which model agent-environment relationships and are robust to visual appearance changes) and introduces a parallel, trainable copy. This copy is gated by zero-initialized linear layers. This design ensures that the residual branch initially contributes nothing, allowing a gradual, controlled injection of interaction dynamics as training progresses. This method is also more parameter- and computation-efficient than full-model fine-tuning.
- **Reward Function Design:** The RL agent's behavior is shaped by a composite reward function `R = R_G + R_R + R_H`:
 - `R_G` (Global Goal): Comprises dense and sparse goal-reaching rewards, and dense and sparse collision penalties.
 - `R_R` (Rule Regularization): Enforces navigation rules, such as staying within walkable areas.
 - `R_H` (Human Likeness): Encourages smooth and natural behaviors through trajectory similarity and heading smoothness penalties.
- **Training Objective:** The policy is optimized using a Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)-based objective with entropy regularization. Only the parameters of the RAM branches are fine-tuned, with gradients from the visual encoder and context features being truncated. A differentiable controller translates the predicted waypoint trajectory into robot-specific velocity commands. To ensure sufficient exploration, the standard deviation of the action distribution is reinitialized.

#### 3.3. NavBench-GS Evaluation Benchmark

To systematically evaluate the framework, the authors introduce **NavBench-GS**, an end-to-end evaluation benchmark.
- **Composition:** Built upon photorealistic 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) reconstructions of real-world scenes, which provide accurate physics and interactive dynamics. This enables reactive simulation for closed-loop policy assessment.
- **Scenarios:** The benchmark includes 26 scenes, each instantiated with four tasks: empty environments, environments with static obstacles, environments with moving pedestrians, and environments with both obstacles and pedestrians. This combinatorial approach increases complexity and realism.
- **Metrics:** Performance is measured using Success Rate (SR), Route Completion (RC), and Collision Times (CT). An episode is considered successful if the robot reaches the destination within 1 meter and experiences fewer than 3 collisions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to validate the S2E framework, its components, and its performance in various scenarios, including comparisons with existing foundation models and real-world deployments.

#### 4.1. Scaling Model Performance via Reinforcement Learning

- **Diminishing Returns of Offline Data:** Experiments with S2E-BC (behavior cloning) showed that increasing the offline training data volume from 250k to 750k samples resulted in a marginal 2% increase in success rate. This indicates that solely scaling offline data eventually yields diminishing returns for navigation tasks.
- **RL Overcomes Limitations:** In contrast, RL fine-tuning (S2E-Full) significantly boosted performance, achieving a 15% improvement in success rate over the pretrained S2E-BC model, without the need for additional offline data. This suggests that RL can provide performance gains beyond what can be achieved by merely scaling offline data.
- **RL vs. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** A comparison between RL and SFT showed that RL maintained or improved success rates with increasing training costs, while SFT, even when trained on curated collision-free data, suffered from overfitting. This indicates that RL is more sample-efficient and robust for post-training in robot learning.
- **RAM Effectiveness:** Ablation studies demonstrated that the Residual-Attention Module (RAM) enabled stable RL training, consuming less GPU memory, whereas direct RL fine-tuning of the action decoder layers (DecFT-RL) experienced gradient explosion and higher memory usage.

#### 4.2. NavBench-GS Benchmark Results

- **Superior Performance:** S2E consistently outperformed several state-of-the-art navigation foundation models, including GNM, ViNT, NoMaD, MBRA, and CityWalker, across all four task categories (empty, static obstacles, moving pedestrians, and combined obstacles + pedestrians). S2E achieved higher success rates and route completion, and generally lower collision times.
- **Efficiency of RL-based Scaling:** S2E, trained with 100 hours of data, surpassed methods like CityWalker trained with over 2000 hours of data. This highlights the efficiency of RL in scaling navigation performance compared to extensive reliance on offline data volume.
- **Qualitative Assessment:** Visualizations from the NavBench-GS benchmark demonstrated S2E-Full's ability to navigate complex scenes, successfully avoiding obstacles and reaching goals, in situations where baseline models (e.g., S2E-PPO, S2E-BC, NoMaD, CityWalker) either failed or exhibited suboptimal behaviors.

#### 4.3. Real-World Evaluations

- **Zero-Shot Transfer:** S2E demonstrated zero-shot generalization and effective deployment on real-world wheeled and Unitree GO2 quadrupedal robots in diverse urban environments, including scenarios with static obstacles and moving pedestrians.
- **Collision Avoidance:** Qualitative and quantitative results indicated S2E-Full's superior collision avoidance capabilities in real-world settings compared to baseline methods like NoMaD and CityWalker. For the wheeled robot, S2E-Full achieved a 0.51 success rate and 0.60 collision times, outperforming baselines. On the quadruped robot, it achieved a 0.55 success rate and 0.62 collision times.
- **Interactive Skills Transfer:** The effective transfer of interactive capabilities learned in simulation to the real world validates the framework's design, indicating the policies can adapt to real-world dynamics.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Effectiveness of Anchor Guidance (AGDM):** S2E-BC, utilizing anchor-guided distribution matching, significantly outperformed S2E-BC-Single (single-mode matching) on NavBench-GS-Obstacle by achieving an 11% higher success rate and 0.64 fewer collisions. This confirms that AGDM effectively models multimodal distributions, improving obstacle interaction. Further, increasing the number of anchors from 1 to 64 improved trajectory prediction metrics (minADE and mAP) on the RECON dataset.
- **Effectiveness of Residual-Attention Module (RAM):** The RAM-based fine-tuning approach (S2E-Full) achieved superior performance on NavBench-GS-Obstacle compared to alternative fine-tuning strategies such as pure PPO, SFT, or fine-tuning only the action decoder layers (DecFT-RL).
- **Cross-Embodiment Generality:** The trained S2E model demonstrated cross-embodiment generalization, maintaining high success rates, route completion, and SPL scores across wheeled and quadruped robots in both URBAN-SIM and NavBench-GS environments. Performance on a humanoid robot was comparatively lower but still reasonable, indicating embodiment-agnostic properties.
- **Simulation Environment Design:** Redesigning the procedural generation rules for URBAN-SIM environments, leading to more structured and realistic urban layouts, resulted in notable improvements in success rate and reduced collision times compared to environments with randomly sampled layouts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The S2E framework represents a contribution to the field of robot navigation by addressing several limitations of current foundation models. Its significance and potential impact can be outlined as follows:

- **Advancement in Navigation Foundation Models:** The research provides a method for enhancing navigation foundation models beyond passive observation. By integrating reinforcement learning, S2E enables these models to acquire crucial interactive and adaptive behaviors, such as dynamic obstacle and pedestrian avoidance, which are essential for real-world deployment in complex urban environments. This moves robot learning closer to "experiencing" the world rather than just "seeing" it.

- **Efficient Scaling of Robot Learning:** The findings suggest that incorporating interactive learning through RL can overcome the diminishing returns typically observed when scaling robot learning solely by increasing offline data. This implies a more efficient and effective pathway to developing advanced, robust robotic navigation capabilities, potentially reducing the massive data collection efforts required for purely imitation-based methods.

- **Improved Real-World Generalization and Deployment:** The demonstrated zero-shot transferability of S2E to different real-world robotic platforms (wheeled and quadruped robots) in diverse, unseen urban scenarios is a significant step towards practical robot deployment. This capability can reduce the time and resources required for real-world fine-tuning, making robust robot navigation more accessible and scalable.

- **Methodological Innovations:**
 - **Anchor-Guided Distribution Matching (AGDM):** This strategy offers a stable and expressive mechanism for modeling multimodal action distributions from diverse datasets, which is a common challenge in learning complex behaviors in robotics. This approach could be relevant for other control tasks requiring multimodal policy outputs.
 - **Residual-Attention Module (RAM):** RAM provides a principled and parameter-efficient method for fine-tuning pre-trained models with reinforcement learning. Its design effectively mitigates issues of catastrophic forgetting and simulator-to-real domain shift, which are critical considerations for transferring policies from simulation to reality. This module's design could inspire similar selective adaptation strategies in other applications of foundation models.

- **Standardized and Realistic Evaluation:** The introduction of NavBench-GS, a comprehensive evaluation benchmark based on photorealistic 3D Gaussian Splatting with accurate physics and interactive dynamics, provides a valuable resource for the robotics community. This benchmark facilitates standardized, reproducible, and realistic assessment of navigation models, addressing a previous gap in evaluating reactive, closed-loop policies.

- **Insights into Post-Training Paradigms:** The comparative analysis between RL and SFT for post-training in robot learning contributes to a deeper understanding of effective strategies for scaling foundation models in this domain. These insights can guide future research and development efforts in robot learning.

The acknowledged limitation concerning the current system's lack of 3D perception and the potential for integrating depth or occupancy prediction indicates clear avenues for future research. Addressing this could further enhance collision avoidance and robustness in visually ambiguous or geometrically complex environments, building upon the interactive capabilities demonstrated by S2E.
