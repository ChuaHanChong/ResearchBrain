# A Simulation Evaluation Suite for Robust Adaptive Quadcopter Control

> [!summary] Summary
> A modular simulation testbed, built on the RotorPy simulator, enables systematic evaluation of robust adaptive quadcopter control methods against diverse disturbances and model uncertainties. The framework provides a common platform for benchmarking controller performance, demonstrating that adaptive controllers generally maintain higher success rates under significant perturbations compared to non-adaptive baselines.

## Problem

- Quadcopter trajectory tracking is challenged by external disturbances (e.g., wind, payload shifts, rotor degradation, control latency) and inherent model uncertainties.
- The evaluation of robust adaptive control strategies is fragmented across bespoke simulation environments, hindering systematic comparisons and leading to redundant development efforts.
- Existing simulation benchmarks often rely on simplified dynamics or are tightly coupled with specific applications, limiting their utility for comprehensive and realistic assessment of advanced controllers.

## Method

- Developed a ==modular simulation testbed==, extending the lightweight **RotorPy** simulator with enhanced quadcopter dynamics relevant for adaptive control evaluation.
- Integrated a comprehensive library of ==non-adaptive and adaptive controllers==, alongside ==configurable disturbance models== for external disturbances (e.g., gusty wind, off-center payload, rotor effectiveness loss) and model uncertainties (uniform, scaled).
- Implemented ==automated stress testing== capabilities and a suite of performance metrics (e.g., ==position RMSE==, ==success rate==) to systematically assess controller robustness limits.

## Results

- Under ideal, uncertainty-free conditions, all controllers achieved high tracking precision (**0.030m to 0.074m** position RMSE) and **100%** success rates.
- Automated stress testing revealed that non-adaptive controllers significantly degraded under increasing off-center payload mass ratios (**0% to 200%**) and succumbed to instability with latency increases (e.g., `geo` failed at **0.07s** delay).
- Adaptive controllers, such as `indi-a` and `xadap`, demonstrated superior robustness by maintaining higher success rates and better tracking performance against gusty wind, dynamically attached payloads, and various model uncertainties.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adaptive and learning-based controllers generally exhibit greater robustness and maintain higher performance than non-adaptive baselines when confronted with significant, time-varying disturbances and model uncertainties.
> - A unified, modular, and easy-to-deploy simulation framework is crucial for enabling systematic, reproducible research and reducing redundant development in quadcopter control.
> - Realistic dynamic models and a diverse set of configurable disturbances are essential for accurately assessing the robustness margins of adaptive control algorithms under real-world conditions.
