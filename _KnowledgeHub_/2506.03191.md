# Multimodal Generative AI with Autoregressive LLMs for Human Motion Understanding and Generation: A Way Forward

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey systematically reviews 250+ papers on text-conditioned human motion generation, comparing GAN, VAE, diffusion, and autoregressive-LLM approaches alongside their visual tokenization schemes (VQ-VAE, VQ-GAN, VQ-Diffuse). It proposes a unified model architecture that couples autoregressive LLMs with diffusion via shared multimodal transformers, concluding this hybridization is the field's most promising direction despite a persistent lack of standardized evaluation metrics.

## Problem

- Prior surveys on human motion generation cover only single modalities or subtasks, such as prediction or video generation, while omitting multimodal LLM integration entirely.
- Existing generative-model surveys explain GAN, VAE, and diffusion methodology generically without addressing motion-specific tokenization, backbone architectures, or unified text-to-motion frameworks.
- No prior work jointly covers datasets, benchmarks, evaluation metrics, architectures, and applications for text-to-motion generation within a single unified taxonomy.

## Method

- Systematic literature review of 250+ papers from CVPR, ICCV, ECCV, NeurIPS, TPAMI, and 3DV, organized into a structured taxonomy spanning data representation, ==MLLM==, ==diffusion==, and unified frameworks.
- Formalizes visual tokenization mechanisms, ==VQ-VAE==, ==VQ-GAN==, and ==VQ-Diffuse==, with encoder-codebook-decoder equations that convert continuous motion sequences into discrete tokens processable by ==autoregressive LLMs==.
- Proposes a unified architecture that couples ==autoregressive LLMs== and ==diffusion models== through dense or ==mixture-of-experts== multimodal transformers, jointly processing semantic and pixel-level motion tokens.

## Results

- Compiled comparisons across **20-plus** methods on HumanML3D and KIT-ML show VQ-VAE-based motion reconstruction reaching an FID of **0.017**, far below diffusion baseline Tevet's **4.569**.
- Catalogues **10+** standard datasets, including BABEL's **28,000** motion sequences across **250** categories, and formalizes **8** evaluation metrics (FID, Precision, Coverage, Recall, MMS, MPJPE) with explicit bounds.
- Unified AR-diffusion models such as MotionLLM's rotation-based variant report FID as low as **0.045** on HumanML3D, outperforming the standalone diffusion baseline MotionDiffuse's **0.630**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Discretizing continuous motion into tokens enables LLM-style autoregressive modeling but trades away fine-grained motion detail, a tension that recurs across every reviewed architecture.
> - Hybridizing autoregressive and diffusion paradigms, via shared embedding spaces or mixture-of-experts, tends to beat either paradigm alone, suggesting complementary rather than competing strengths.
> - Despite dozens of proposed metrics, the field still lacks a unified evaluation standard, so cross-paper benchmark numbers should be read as only approximately comparable.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper presents a comprehensive survey situated at the intersection of multimodal Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI), autoregressive Large Language Models (LLMs), and human motion understanding and generation (HMUG). It positions itself within the broader context of advancing Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) through the development of more sophisticated AI systems capable of synthesizing realistic and versatile human movements from diverse inputs. Recent high-profile generative models such as SORA, GPT-4V, and Dall-E are cited as examples of progress in conditioned generation of motion, video, and images, underscoring the increasing demand for human-like movements across various applications.

The survey distinguishes itself from existing literature by offering a focused and integrated perspective. Prior surveys are noted as being either too broad (covering general video or image generation with motion) or too narrow (focusing on specific subtopics like motion prediction or general generative methodologies without explicit attention to multimodal LLMs and transformers). This work aims to fill that gap by exclusively concentrating on text and motion modalities, providing an in-depth analysis of architectural variants, backbone models, and the emerging role of LLMs in the specific domain of HMUG. Its scope includes both 2D and 3D motion representations, incorporating fundamental physics and mathematical principles, and examining keypoint and joint rotation sequences.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this survey is to furnish a comprehensive and insightful review of the advancements in human motion generation, specifically through the integration of multimodal GenAI and autoregressive LLMs. The authors seek to map the emerging methods and architectures, evaluate their capabilities, and outline their potential to drive more realistic and versatile motion synthesis.

The specific objectives that guide this extensive review include:
- **Analysis of Generative Approaches:** To explore and critically analyze various generative models, including autoregressive models, diffusion models, Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), and transformer-based models. This analysis focuses on their respective strengths and limitations concerning motion quality, computational efficiency, and adaptability in the context of human motion generation.
- **Text-Conditioned Motion Generation:** To highlight recent progress in text-conditioned motion generation, examining how natural language descriptions can be effectively utilized to guide and refine the synthesis of complex, human-like motion sequences with greater precision.
- **Integration of LLMs:** To investigate how the integration of LLMs enhances existing motion generation models by enabling semantic alignment between textual instructions and motion outputs, thereby improving the coherence and contextual relevance of the generated movements.
- **Identification of Applications:** To underscore the transformative potential of text-to-motion GenAI and LLM architectures across a spectrum of applications, including healthcare, robotics (humanoids), gaming, animation, and assistive technologies.
- **Addressing Challenges:** To address the ongoing technical and practical challenges inherent in generating efficient, realistic, and robust human motion, providing a roadmap for future research endeavors.

The motivation behind this survey is multi-faceted. The authors identify a crucial need to synthesize the rapid advancements in GenAI and LLMs that are pushing the boundaries of human motion generation, contributing to the broader goal of AGI. The increasing demand for realistic and natural human-like movements in various industries—such as film production, video games, robotics, virtual training, and healthcare—underscores the practical relevance of this field. Furthermore, the survey is motivated by the observation that existing literature lacks a unified and dedicated focus on multimodal LLMs and transformers within the domain of human motion understanding and generation, making this work a valuable resource for researchers and practitioners navigating this evolving landscape.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This paper employs a systematic survey methodology to analyze the current state of multimodal Generative AI for human motion understanding and generation. The authors conducted an extensive literature review, examining over 250 research articles published in prominent journals and conferences such as CVPR, ICCV, ECCV, SigIR, NIPS, TIPS, and TPAMI over recent years.

The approach is structured into several key components:

- **Preliminary of Human Motion Data:** The survey begins by detailing how human pose and motion data are represented and collected. This includes:
 - **Physical Representation:** Discussing kinematic chains, joint positions (2D/3D coordinates of key points, e.g., knees, elbows), and joint rotations (angular motion using Euler angles, axis angles, quaternions, particularly in models like SMPL and SMPL-X).
 - **Mathematical Representation:** Exploring abstract descriptions derived from motion capture data, mapping poses to mathematical spaces using algebra, differential encoding, graphs, and motion trajectories.
 - **Text Data Representation:** Explaining how textual descriptions are converted into numerical vectors using traditional (Word2Vec, GloVe) and contextualized (BERT, GPT) word embeddings for semantic understanding.
 - **Data Collection Approaches:** Categorizing methods into marker-based motion capture (high precision, often optical or IMU-based), markerless motion capture (computer vision-based, more flexible but less accurate), pseudo-labeling (estimating 2D/3D key points from images/videos), and manual annotation (labor-intensive but high quality).

- **Foundational Multimodal LLM:** This section delves into the integration of LLMs with visual information:
 - **Autoregressive Probabilistic LLMs:** Describes the autoregressive nature of LLMs (predicting the next word based on previous sequence) and how visual information is integrated, often through Vision Language Modeling (VLM) for aligning textual and visual encoders (e.g., CLIP).
 - **Visual Tokenizers (VT):** Explains how motion sequences are converted into discrete visual tokens. This involves models like VQ-VAEs, VQ-GANs, and VQ-Diffuse, with detailed mathematical formulations of their training loss functions to optimize motion reconstruction and codebook learning.
 - **Multimodal LLMs in HMUG:** Discusses specific MLLM architectures (e.g., MotionLLM, AvatarGPT, MotionGPT, MotionChain, WalkLLM) that leverage text and motion, highlighting techniques like adapter-based fine-tuning (LoRA) and strategies for synchronizing heterogeneous data representations (cross-attention, contrastive learning).

- **Preliminaries in Multimodal Diffusion:** The survey reviews core generative models fundamental to motion synthesis:
 - **Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs):** Explains the adversarial training process between a generator and discriminator, outlining the min-max game objective function, and discussing variants (e.g., Temporal GAN, Deep Convolutional GAN, StyleGAN) while noting challenges like mode collapse.
 - **Variational Autoencoders (VAEs):** Describes the encoder-decoder architecture for learning a probabilistic latent space, detailing the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) loss function, and mentioning variants like Conditional VAE (CVAE) and Vector Quantized VAE (VQ-VAE).
 - **Diffusion Probabilistic Modeling:** Introduces the forward (noise addition) and reverse (denoising) processes, formalizing the gradual transformation of data to noise and back, and highlighting the efficiency of Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs).

- **Text-Conditioned Motion Generation:** This section categorizes and discusses methods based on their conditioning and generative model type:
 - **Action-to-Motion:** Generating motion from predefined action categories (e.g., "Walk," "Jump").
 - **Text-Description-to-Motion:** Generating dynamic motion from free-form natural language descriptions.
 - **T2M using GAN, VAE, and Diffusion variants:** Provides examples of models within each generative paradigm that address text-to-motion tasks, such as Text2Motion (GAN), MotVAE (VAE), and MotionDiffuse (Diffusion).

- **Unified Human Motion Model:** Explores efforts to integrate different generative paradigms for AGI:
 - **Multimodal Probabilistic Modelling:** Discusses combining autoregressive models (for understanding) and diffusion models (for high-quality generation).
 - **Unified Model Architectures:** Examines frameworks like MotionLLaMA, MotionGPT-2, and MotionLLM, which discretize motion data into tokens for LLM processing, often utilizing multimodal transformers and shared embedding spaces, while also considering dense models versus mixture-of-experts strategies.

- **Datasets and Evaluation Metrics:** The paper systematically lists common datasets for HMUG, distinguishing between text-motion paired data (KIT-ML, BABEL, HumanML3D), motion-only data (UESTC, NTU-RGB+D 120, HumanAct12), and unified multimodal datasets (Human3.6M, AMASS, HUMANISE, BEAT). It then details various evaluation metrics categorized into:
 - **Fidelity Metrics:** Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), Precision (R-Precision).
 - **Diversity Metrics:** Coverage, Recall, Multimodality Score (MMS).
 - **Accuracy and Consistency Metrics:** Condition Consistency, Text-Motion Consistency, R-Precision.
 - **Motion Quality Metrics:** Motion Quality (subjective/objective), Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE). The importance of unified evaluation metrics is also discussed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

As a survey paper, the "findings" are a synthesis of the current research landscape, identifying established methods, emerging trends, and persistent challenges in multimodal human motion understanding and generation (HMUG).

**Key Architectural and Methodological Trends:**
- **Dominance of Generative Models:** The survey confirms that GANs, VAEs, and especially Diffusion Models and Autoregressive LLMs are the predominant generative paradigms for HMUG. Diffusion models are recognized for generating high-quality, diverse outputs and stable training, while autoregressive LLMs excel in text understanding and structured sequence generation.
- **Visual Tokenization as a Bridge:** Visual tokenizers (e.g., VQ-VAEs, VQ-GANs, VQ-Diffuse) are identified as critical components for discretizing continuous motion data, thereby enabling its seamless integration and processing by LLMs. This effectively treats motion as a "foreign language" comprehensible to language models.
- **Text-Conditioned Generation Advances:** Significant progress has been made in generating human motion directly from natural language descriptions. LLMs enhance this by providing rich semantic and contextual understanding, leading to more coherent and precisely controlled motion outputs compared to simpler action-to-motion approaches.
- **Emergence of Unified Frameworks:** A key trend is the development of unified models that combine the strengths of autoregressive and diffusion architectures. These frameworks (e.g., MotionLLM, MotionLLaMA, MotionGPT-2, Motion-Verse) aim to achieve both comprehensive understanding and high-quality generation of human motion, often utilizing multimodal transformers and shared embedding spaces. Techniques like adapter-based fine-tuning (LoRA) and contrastive learning are frequently employed to manage computational complexity and align disparate modalities.
- **Importance of Multimodal Inputs:** The shift towards integrating multiple modalities beyond just text and motion (e.g., audio, scene context, images, speech, emotion) is noted, enabling more nuanced and contextually aware motion generation. Datasets like HUMANISE, TED-Gesture++, and BEAT exemplify this trend.

**Identified Challenges and Limitations:**
- **Computational Efficiency:** A persistent challenge across generative models, particularly diffusion models (due to iterative denoising) and large transformer-based unified models (due to high parameter counts), is their significant computational cost and inference latency, hindering real-time applications and long-sequence generation.
- **Fine-Grained Detail Preservation:** Autoregressive models, while effective, can lose fine-grained motion details during visual tokenization and rely on causal attention mechanisms that complicate token sequencing and require large datasets for generalization.
- **Training Stability and Mode Collapse:** GANs continue to face issues with training instability and mode collapse, leading to limited diversity in generated motions. While VAEs are more stable, they can suffer from posterior collapse.
- **Data Scarcity and Quality:** There is a significant lack of large-scale, unified, and high-quality multimodal datasets specifically designed for HMUG that support comprehensive cross-validation and simultaneous understanding and generation tasks. Issues include privacy concerns, inconsistent annotations, and high collection costs.
- **Evaluation Standardization:** The absence of a unified, standardized evaluation framework for human motion generation is a critical concern. Existing metrics (FID, Precision, Coverage, Recall, MPJPE) provide partial assessments, but a cohesive framework that aligns with human judgment for holistic quality assessment is still needed.
- **Photorealism and Control:** Achieving photorealistic, natural, and fluid human motion, especially for long durations and multi-person interactions, remains challenging. Refining control mechanisms to translate nuanced textual descriptions into precise physical movements is an ongoing area of development.
- **Ethical and Adversarial Concerns:** The rise of digital humanoids and virtual humans generated by these models raises significant ethical issues regarding privacy, responsible data use, and the potential for malicious exploitation through adversarial inputs (e.g., generating harmful content).

**Quantitative Comparisons (from survey tables):**
The survey presents quantitative comparisons (Table 6, Figures 9 and 10) of various multimodal LLM and diffusion-based methods on benchmark datasets like HumanML3D and KIT-ML, using metrics such as FID, Precision, Diversity, and Multimodality Score. The results indicate varying performance across models, reflecting trade-offs between fidelity, diversity, and other attributes. For instance, MotionLLM (a unified multimodal LLM) shows competitive FID scores (0.491 on HumanML3D, 0.781 on KIT-ML) and diversity metrics, while MotionDiffuse (a multimodal diffusion model) also demonstrates strong performance (0.630 FID on HumanML3D). The comparisons highlight that no single model consistently outperforms all others across all metrics, emphasizing the ongoing research towards achieving balanced performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey holds substantial significance for the field of artificial intelligence, particularly within the domains of computer vision, natural language processing, and robotics. Its detailed analysis provides a structured overview of the advancements in multimodal generative AI for human motion understanding and generation, serving as a critical resource for researchers and practitioners.

The potential impacts of this work are far-reaching and transformative:

- **Advancing Human Motion Generation:** By systematically categorizing and analyzing diverse methodologies, architectures, and evaluation metrics, the survey clarifies the current state-of-the-art and identifies key research gaps. This guidance can accelerate the development of more realistic, versatile, and efficient human motion synthesis models.
- **Bridging Modality Gaps:** The emphasis on integrating text and motion modalities, particularly through autoregressive LLMs and diffusion models, fosters a deeper understanding of how machines can semantically interpret and translate complex natural language instructions into dynamic physical movements. This bridging capability is a fundamental step toward more human-like AI interaction.
- **Driving Towards AGI:** The paper positions the advancements in HMUG as crucial components in the broader pursuit of Artificial General Intelligence. Developing models that can not only generate but also comprehend and reason about human motion from multimodal inputs represents a significant stride towards creating more intelligent and adaptable AI systems.
- **Enabling Transformative Applications:** The survey highlights a wide array of practical applications where these technologies can create substantial impact:
 - **Healthcare and Rehabilitation:** Customizing therapeutic exercises, enhancing gait analysis for patients, and developing AI-driven assistive technologies for individuals with motor disabilities, leading to personalized and effective treatment.
 - **Virtual and Augmented Reality (VR/AR) & Metaverse:** Creating highly immersive and interactive digital experiences through realistic character movements in games, virtual training environments (e.g., medical, military), and general metaverse applications, thus enhancing user engagement and training efficacy.
 - **Entertainment and Fashion Industry:** Revolutionizing film animation by automating realistic human movements, generating diverse motion styles for video games, and enabling dynamic virtual models for fashion shows and try-on applications, boosting creativity and efficiency.
 - **Robotics and Human-Robot Interaction (HRI):** Improving robotic motion planning and execution, allowing humanoid robots to perform tasks more naturally, and facilitating seamless, intuitive collaboration between humans and robots.
 - **Security and Surveillance:** Enhancing systems by enabling accurate prediction of abnormal human behavior for real-time detection and aiding forensic analysis through sophisticated motion reconstruction.
 - **Autonomous Vehicles:** Significantly improving the safety and reliability of autonomous driving by allowing vehicles to predict and simulate diverse pedestrian movements, leading to better decision-making and collision avoidance in complex urban environments.
- **Guiding Future Research:** By explicitly outlining persistent challenges in computational efficiency, data quality, evaluation metrics, and ethical considerations, the survey provides a clear roadmap for future research directions. This includes calls for unified evaluation frameworks, larger and more diverse multimodal datasets, and robust ethical guidelines for responsible AI development.

In conclusion, this research report underscores that multimodal generative AI with autoregressive LLMs for HMUG is not merely an academic pursuit but a foundational technology with the potential to reshape numerous industries and human-computer interactions, moving closer to the vision of truly intelligent and adaptive systems.
