# WholeBodyVLA: Towards Unified Latent VLA for Whole-Body Loco-Manipulation Control

> [!summary] Summary
> WholeBodyVLA introduces a unified latent Vision-Language-Action framework for humanoid robots to perform large-space loco-manipulation by integrating data-efficient learning from action-free videos with a specialized low-level control policy. The framework achieved a 78.0% success rate on complex real-robot tasks and reduced the need for extensive teleoperation data.

## Problem

- Scarcity of integrated loco-manipulation knowledge and data for humanoids, making robust policy learning difficult and hindering training of policies coordinating movement and interaction.
- Existing VLA systems typically focus on dexterous in-place manipulation, lacking integrated humanoid locomotion and thereby confining robots to limited workspaces.
- Low-level Reinforcement Learning (RL) controllers, often using continuous velocity-tracking objectives, struggle with precision and stability for fine-grained loco-manipulation commands, leading to "decision-execution misalignment."

## Method

- Developed a Unified Latent Learning approach with two separate ==VQ-VAE==-based ==Latent Action Models== (LAMs) for manipulation and locomotion, trained on diverse, low-cost action-free videos including human egocentric data.
- Introduced a ==Loco-Manipulation-Oriented== (LMO) RL Policy that employs a discrete command interface and a two-stage curriculum to ensure precise and stable execution of fundamental movements like advancing, turning, and squatting.
- Integrated the LAMs and LMO policy into a unified Vision-Language-Action (VLA) framework that jointly predicts both manipulation and locomotion latent actions, which are then grounded into robot-specific commands.

## Results

- WholeBodyVLA achieved an average success rate of **78.0%** across challenging Bag Packing, Box Loading, and Cart Pushing tasks, outperforming modular and end-to-end VLA baselines.
- Unified latent learning, utilizing pretraining from action-free human egocentric videos, improved performance by **38.7%** compared to variants without latent pretraining and reduced the quantity of teleoperation data required for fine-tuning.
- The Loco-Manipulation-Oriented (LMO) RL policy surpassed a conventional velocity-based RL controller by **24.0%**, particularly demonstrating superior precision and stability in locomotion-heavy subgoals and extended locomotion tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning separate latent action models for distinct modalities like manipulation and locomotion is effective due to their differing visual dynamics, outperforming a single shared model.
> - Low-cost, action-free human egocentric videos can effectively provide valuable latent priors for robot loco-manipulation, substantially reducing reliance on expensive teleoperation or motion capture data.
> - Explicitly tailoring low-level control with a discrete, task-oriented command interface and a structured two-stage curriculum (LMO policy) is critical for achieving precision and stability in whole-body loco-manipulation for humanoids.
