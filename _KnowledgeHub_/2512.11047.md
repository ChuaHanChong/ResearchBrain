# WholeBodyVLA: Towards Unified Latent VLA for Whole-Body Loco-Manipulation Control

> [!summary] Summary
> WholeBodyVLA introduces a unified latent Vision-Language-Action framework for humanoid robots to perform large-space loco-manipulation by integrating data-efficient learning from action-free videos with a specialized low-level control policy. The framework achieved a 78.0% success rate on complex real-robot tasks and reduced the need for extensive teleoperation data.

## Problem

- Scarcity of integrated loco-manipulation knowledge and data for humanoids, making robust policy learning difficult and hindering training of policies coordinating movement and interaction.
- Existing VLA systems typically focus on dexterous in-place manipulation, lacking integrated humanoid locomotion and thereby confining robots to limited workspaces.
- Low-level Reinforcement Learning (RL) controllers, often using continuous velocity-tracking objectives, struggle with precision and stability for fine-grained loco-manipulation commands, leading to "decision-execution misalignment."

## Method

![[2512.11047-method.png]]
*The WholeBodyVLA architecture combines unified latent learning from action-free videos with a loco-manipulation-oriented RL policy for precise whole-body control.*

- Developed a Unified Latent Learning approach with two separate ==VQ-VAE==-based ==Latent Action Models== (LAMs) for manipulation and locomotion, trained on diverse, low-cost action-free videos including human egocentric data.
- Introduced a ==Loco-Manipulation-Oriented== (LMO) RL Policy that employs a discrete command interface and a two-stage curriculum to ensure precise and stable execution of fundamental movements like advancing, turning, and squatting.
- Integrated the LAMs and LMO policy into a unified Vision-Language-Action (VLA) framework that jointly predicts both manipulation and locomotion latent actions, which are then grounded into robot-specific commands.

## Results

- WholeBodyVLA achieved an average success rate of **78.0%** across challenging Bag Packing, Box Loading, and Cart Pushing tasks, outperforming modular and end-to-end VLA baselines.
- Unified latent learning, utilizing pretraining from action-free human egocentric videos, improved performance by **38.7%** compared to variants without latent pretraining and reduced the quantity of teleoperation data required for fine-tuning.
- The Loco-Manipulation-Oriented (LMO) RL policy surpassed a conventional velocity-based RL controller by **24.0%**, particularly demonstrating superior precision and stability in locomotion-heavy subgoals and extended locomotion tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning separate latent action models for distinct modalities like manipulation and locomotion is effective due to their differing visual dynamics, outperforming a single shared model.
> - Low-cost, action-free human egocentric videos can effectively provide valuable latent priors for robot loco-manipulation, substantially reducing reliance on expensive teleoperation or motion capture data.
> - Explicitly tailoring low-level control with a discrete, task-oriented command interface and a structured two-stage curriculum (LMO policy) is critical for achieving precision and stability in whole-body loco-manipulation for humanoids.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics aims to create general-purpose embodied agents capable of perceiving, reasoning, and acting in human-centric environments. A central challenge is the seamless coordination of dexterous manipulation with agile locomotion, collectively known as "loco-manipulation." WholeBodyVLA positions itself at the forefront of addressing this challenge by synthesizing and extending prior work in several key areas.

Existing research has made significant strides in:
- **RL-based Whole-Body Control and Imitation:** Efforts have focused on learning whole-body behaviors through imitation learning or reinforcement learning (e.g., Ji et al., 2024; He et al., 2025b; Truong et al., 2025). However, many of these approaches often model upper-body influence simplistically or rely on velocity-tracking objectives that are insufficient for the precise and stable control required during manipulation-aware locomotion. They struggle with complex inertial couplings and maintaining stability under external loads.
- **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Systems:** The advent of large-scale multimodal foundation models has spurred the development of VLA systems (e.g., RT-2, OpenVLA, Pi0.5) that demonstrate impressive generalization and dexterous *in-place* manipulation capabilities (Brohan et al., 2023; Kim et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025). Crucially, these systems typically emphasize upper-body manipulation and do not provide an integrated solution for humanoid locomotion, thus confining robots to limited workspaces.
- **High-Level Planners for Humanoids:** Some approaches employ modular pipelines, using Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for high-level planning that sequences discrete locomotion and manipulation skills (e.g., Yuan et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025b; Chen et al., 2025a). While conceptually sound, these systems suffer from "brittle skill boundaries," error accumulation due to decoupled feedback, and reliance on cloud-based perception, hindering real-time control and often leading to suboptimal robot configurations.
- **End-to-End Whole-Body Control:** While a promising direction to alleviate handoff issues (e.g., Boston Dynamics' Atlas, Bjorck et al., 2025), end-to-end training typically requires prohibitively large datasets of whole-body teleoperation or motion capture (MoCap) data, which are extremely expensive and time-consuming to collect.
- **Latent Action Learning:** This paradigm addresses data scarcity by compressing visual changes in action-free videos into compact, discrete latent tokens to supervise policy learning (e.g., Genie, LAPA, UniVLA). This approach has primarily been explored in tabletop manipulation, demonstrating its potential for data-efficient learning.

WholeBodyVLA aims to bridge these gaps by proposing a *unified* framework that integrates VLA capabilities with whole-body control for large-space loco-manipulation. It directly tackles the limitations of existing methods by:
1. **Overcoming Data Scarcity:** Extending latent action learning to humanoid loco-manipulation by leveraging low-cost, action-free human egocentric videos, thereby reducing the reliance on expensive teleoperation data for large-scale whole-body trajectories.
2. **Enhancing Execution Precision and Stability:** Introducing a novel Loco-Manipulation-Oriented (LMO) RL policy that explicitly focuses on stable and precise execution of fundamental loco-manipulation movements, addressing the "decision-execution misalignment" prevalent in controllers using generic velocity-tracking objectives.
3. **Providing an End-to-End Unified Solution:** Unlike modular systems, WholeBodyVLA offers a single, cohesive framework that couples vision and language with robust whole-body control, eliminating brittle skill boundaries and enabling manipulation-aware locomotion for the first time in large-space scenarios.

In essence, WholeBodyVLA is a significant step towards realizing truly general-purpose humanoid robots by enabling them to autonomously perform complex, integrated loco-manipulation tasks in diverse, large-scale environments, an ability that previous systems lacked or achieved only partially.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enable humanoid robots to perform complex, large-space loco-manipulation tasks autonomously and end-to-end, moving beyond confined workspaces and decoupled control paradigms. The authors identify two fundamental concerns that motivate their work:

1. **Scarcity of Loco-Manipulation Knowledge and Data:** Humanoid robots require a vast amount of experience to learn effective loco-manipulation behaviors. However, collecting large-scale, integrated datasets of humanoid locomotion *and* manipulation (e.g., via teleoperation or Motion Capture) is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming. This data scarcity prevents the acquisition of comprehensive loco-manipulation knowledge, hindering the training of robust policies that can coordinate movement and interaction. The problem is exacerbated because existing datasets typically treat manipulation and navigation as separate tasks, failing to capture the intricate interplay required for integrated loco-manipulation.

2. **Limitations in Executing Locomotion Commands with Precision and Stability:** Even with high-level decisions, existing low-level Reinforcement Learning (RL) controllers often struggle to faithfully and reliably execute locomotion commands, particularly for fine-grained control crucial in manipulation contexts. These controllers frequently employ continuous velocity-tracking objectives, which, while suitable for general cruising, are deficient for precise start-stop semantics, consistent gait patterns across different speeds, braking precision, and maintaining heading fidelity. This "decision-execution misalignment" means that even if a high-level VLA predicts the correct action, the robot may fail to execute it accurately or stably, leading to errors like stumbling, path deviation, or instability under load.

To address these motivations, the paper sets out to achieve the following key objectives:

- **Develop a unified latent learning framework:** This framework aims to acquire richer loco-manipulation knowledge by learning from abundant, low-cost, action-free egocentric videos. This approach seeks to alleviate the data scarcity bottleneck by transforming observation-only data into useful supervisory signals.
- **Devise an efficient human data collection pipeline:** To augment the dataset for latent learning, a cost-effective method for gathering human egocentric videos that captures manipulation-aware locomotion is proposed.
- **Present a loco–manipulation–oriented (LMO) RL policy:** This policy is specifically tailored for accurate and stable execution of fundamental loco-manipulation movements, such as advancing, turning, and squatting, by employing a simplified discrete command interface. The goal is to improve the reliability and precision of low-level control, mitigating decision-execution misalignment.
- **Introduce WholeBodyVLA:** Integrate the unified latent learning and LMO RL policy into a single, unified framework for end-to-end humanoid loco-manipulation, enabling real-world, large-space tasks for the first time.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The WholeBodyVLA framework is designed to enable efficient training and deployment of autonomous control policies on bipedal humanoids by combining two main innovations: **Unified Latent Learning** and a **Loco-Manipulation-Oriented (LMO) RL Policy**.

#### 3.1. Unified Latent Learning

The core idea is to leverage low-cost, action-free videos to acquire manipulation and locomotion primitives, which then serve as latent supervision for training a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model.
- **Challenge of Unified LAM:** Initially, the authors found that training a single Latent Action Model (LAM) on mixed manipulation and locomotion data yields suboptimal performance. This is attributed to the fundamentally different visual dynamics: manipulation videos often have a near-static camera observing arm motion, while locomotion videos involve continuous camera movement and environmental changes. These conflicting visual patterns bias the model's attention and can lead to ambiguous latent encodings.
- **Two Separate LAMs:** To overcome this, WholeBodyVLA trains two distinct LAMs:
 - **Manipulation LAM:** Trained using the AgiBot World dataset, a large real-robot manipulation dataset.
 - **Locomotion LAM:** Trained on a newly collected dataset of *human egocentric manipulation-aware locomotion videos*.
- **LAM Architecture:** Both LAMs employ a VQ-VAE (Vector Quantized Variational Autoencoder) architecture, with the encoder built on top of DINOv2 features. Given consecutive frames (o_t, o_t+k), the LAM encoder (E_i) extracts a continuous latent vector (z_t), which is then quantized to the nearest entry in a learned codebook (C_i) to obtain a discrete latent action (c_t). The LAM decoder (D_i) is trained to reconstruct the future frame (o_t+k) from the current frame (o_t) and the quantized latent action (c_t), minimizing a VQ-VAE loss.
- **VLA Training with Joint Supervision:** After pre-training the individual LAMs, the VLA policy (π_θ) is trained to *jointly predict* both manipulation (c_mani_t) and locomotion (c_loco_t) latent actions based on visual observations (o_t) and language instructions (ℓ). This joint prediction using a Cross-Entropy loss compels the VLA to learn how locomotion and manipulation interact cohesively to achieve task goals.
- **Low-Cost Human Data Collection:** An efficient pipeline is devised for collecting manipulation-aware locomotion videos. It requires only a single operator with a head-mounted camera, avoiding expensive MoCap or teleoperation setups. Operators perform diverse motions (advancing, turning, squatting) and actively approach potential manipulation goals, ensuring the collected locomotion data is directly relevant to loco-manipulation tasks.
- **Execution on Humanoids:** A lightweight action decoder (f) grounds the VLA's predicted latent actions into robot-specific commands: (i) upper-body joint angles and (ii) a locomotion command for the low-level RL controller. This modular design allows the VLA to provide high-level decisions while the RL policy ensures stable, low-level execution.

#### 3.2. Loco-Manipulation-Oriented (LMO) RL Policy

The LMO RL policy addresses the issue of decision-execution misalignment by providing precise and stable low-level control, tailored for loco-manipulation tasks.
- **Discrete Command Interface:** Unlike traditional RL controllers that use continuous velocity-tracking objectives, the LMO policy employs a discrete command interface. At each time step, the high-level planner generates a command u_t = [s_x, s_y, s_ψ, h*], where s_x, s_y, s_ψ are ternary flags (e.g., -1 for backward/left/turn-left, 0 for stop, 1 for forward/right/turn-right) for movement along x, y, and yaw axes, and h* specifies the desired stance height (for squatting). This explicit command structure enforces clear start-stop semantics and reduces trajectory variance, simplifying training for both the RL controller and the high-level VLA.
- **Proprioceptive Observation Space:** The policy relies solely on proprioceptive egocentric states (base angular velocity, gravity vector, joint states, previous action) with a short history stack, ensuring closed-loop stability without privileged simulator information.
- **Smooth Reference Shaping:** Discrete intent flags are passed through a smooth gating function (e.g., tanh) to generate smooth velocity references, preventing abrupt accelerations and oscillations.
- **Two-Stage Curriculum:**
 - **Stage I (Basic Gait Acquisition):** The policy first learns a minimal gait that prevents falls and responds to discrete commands. Random goal speeds are sampled, and upper-body movements are introduced as gradually increasing disturbances to develop a stable foundation.
 - **Stage II (Precision and Stability):** This stage refines the policy for loco-manipulation-level precision and robustness. Cruising speeds are fixed to standardize gaits, and a "directional accuracy" reward (J_dir) penalizes yaw drift at episode termination, enforcing precise initiation and consistent braking. Realistic manipulation-induced perturbations (sampled arm motions from Agibot-World) are injected to train the legs to compensate for structured inertial couplings. A "stand-still penalty" (J_stand) discourages unnecessary leg actions during stationary episodes.
- **Domain Randomization:** Extensive domain randomization is applied to dynamics, contact properties, controller gains, and external disturbances to enhance the policy's robustness to real-world variations.

In summary, WholeBodyVLA integrates data-efficient learning from diverse video sources with a robust, task-specific low-level controller to achieve unprecedented capabilities in end-to-end, large-space loco-manipulation for humanoids.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the Agibot X2 humanoid robot demonstrate the superior performance, strong generalization, and extensibility of WholeBodyVLA across a range of challenging loco-manipulation tasks.

- **Overall Performance and Baseline Comparison (Table 2, Section 4.2):**
 - WholeBodyVLA significantly outperforms both modular and end-to-end VLA baselines across three complex task suites: Bag Packing, Box Loading, and Cart Pushing.
 - Achieved an average success rate of **78.0%**, surpassing Modular Design (64.0%), GR00T w/ LMO (42.0%), and OpenVLA-OFT w/ LMO (56.7%). This indicates a substantial improvement in handling integrated loco-manipulation behaviors.

- **Contribution of Unified Latent Learning from Action-Free Videos (Table 2, Fig. 3(a,b), Section 4.3):**
 - **Effectiveness of Latent Pretraining:** WholeBodyVLA with full latent pretraining (78.0%) dramatically outperforms the variant without latent pretraining (WholeBodyVLA w/o LAM, 39.3%), showcasing a **38.7% improvement**. This confirms that unified latent learning effectively extracts valuable priors from action-free human videos, enhancing downstream policy learning.
 - **Modality Separation:** Training separate LAMs for manipulation and locomotion (78.0%) slightly outperforms a single shared LAM (66.0%), supporting the decision to decouple them due to distinct visual patterns.
 - **Comprehensive Pretraining:** The full pretraining (with both locomotion and manipulation LAMs) yielded a 14.7% higher success rate than latent learning based only on in-place manipulation (WholeBodyVLA w/ manip. lam, 63.3%), with the largest gains observed in tasks requiring substantial locomotion.
 - **Reduced Teleoperation Data Reliance:** Data scaling experiments (Fig. 3(a,b)) demonstrate that stronger latent pretraining from human egocentric videos reduces the amount of expensive teleoperation fine-tuning data required. Models pretrained with more human videos consistently achieved higher success rates and needed significantly fewer teleoperation trajectories to reach comparable performance levels.

- **Contribution of Loco-Manipulation-Oriented (LMO) RL Policy (Table 2, Table 3, Fig. 3(c), Section 4.4):**
 - **Superiority over Velocity-Based RL:** WholeBodyVLA (with LMO) (78.0%) outperforms a variant using a conventional velocity-based RL controller (WholeBodyVLA w/ vel.-based RL, 54.0%) by **24.0%**. Notably, 91.7% of this performance gap stemmed from failures in locomotion-heavy subgoals, highlighting LMO's critical role in precise and stable locomotion.
 - **Robustness in Extended Locomotion:** On challenging extended tasks (uneven terrain traversal, long multi-step sequences, visual navigation), the LMO policy consistently outperformed the velocity-based RL variant, which often failed due to suboptimal behaviors like stumbling, path deviation, or turning while advancing.
 - **Ablation Studies in Simulation (Table 3):** Controlled experiments in MuJoCo confirmed the importance of each LMO component: removing the directional accuracy reward, disabling Stage II (precision and stability refinement), or Stage I (basic gait acquisition) led to significant degradation in locomotion accuracy (position/orientation error) and manipulation stability (CoM sway).

- **Generalization and Extensibility (Fig. 3(c), Section 4.5):**
 - WholeBodyVLA exhibits strong generalization capabilities across diverse start-pose variations (distance, orientation, height) and scene appearance changes (unseen objects, tables, object positions).
 - It also demonstrates remarkable extensibility to more complex, long-horizon tasks beyond the benchmark suites, including terrain traversal, multi-step manipulation sequences, visual navigation, vacuum cleaning, and wiping stains, maintaining superior performance across all settings.

- **Failure Analysis (Fig. 7, Section C.3):**
 - A post-hoc analysis revealed that the dominant failure modes for WholeBodyVLA in generalization settings were not catastrophic stumbles or collisions, but rather small yet systematic stance and orientation errors during the approach phase. These errors often led to the object or basket being unreachable for subsequent manipulation, propagating into task failures. This suggests that future improvements should focus on refining approach precision, especially for turning, lateral stepping, and squatting.

- **Task Execution Time (Table 8, Section C.4):**
 - WholeBodyVLA achieves competitive, and in many cases, faster average execution times compared to baseline methods, indicating that its enhanced robustness does not come at the cost of efficiency.

- **Shared Latent Action Space Across Embodiments (Fig. 8, Table 9, Section C.5, C.6):**
 - The learned latent action space exhibits cross-domain transferability, with human and robot video clips retrieved for the same latent action showing consistent semantics (e.g., "Go Forward," "Turn Left," "Squat"). This validates the design principle of learning embodiment-agnostic latent representations.
 - The Relative Reconstruction Gain (RRG) metric further confirmed the quality of the learned latent codes, with separately trained LAMs outperforming a shared LAM in reconstructing future frames from task videos.

In summary, WholeBodyVLA sets a new benchmark for humanoid loco-manipulation by demonstrating superior performance, data efficiency through latent learning, robust low-level control, and strong generalization across a wide array of challenging real-world tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The WholeBodyVLA framework represents a significant advancement in the field of humanoid robotics, addressing critical limitations that have historically hindered the development of truly versatile embodied agents. Its potential impact spans both research and practical applications:

- **Enabling Large-Space, End-to-End Loco-Manipulation:** For the first time, WholeBodyVLA enables humanoid robots to perform complex, integrated locomotion and manipulation tasks autonomously across large physical spaces. This moves beyond the constrained tabletop environments or decoupled locomotion/manipulation tasks prevalent in previous research, unlocking new possibilities for humanoids to operate in unstructured, human-centric environments. This is a crucial step towards the vision of general-purpose robots that can seamlessly navigate and interact within our world.

- **Overcoming the Data Scarcity Bottleneck:** The introduction of unified latent learning, leveraging abundant and low-cost action-free egocentric videos, provides a transformative solution to the pervasive problem of data scarcity in robotics. By drastically reducing the reliance on expensive and time-consuming teleoperation or MoCap data, WholeBodyVLA makes advanced humanoid capabilities more accessible for research and scalable for development. This approach could accelerate progress across various embodied AI domains.

- **Enhancing Robot Reliability and Precision:** The Loco-Manipulation-Oriented (LMO) RL policy fundamentally improves the precision and stability of low-level humanoid control. By replacing generic velocity-tracking with a discrete, task-aligned command interface and a specialized two-stage curriculum, the LMO policy mitigates the "decision-execution misalignment." This leads to more robust and dependable execution of crucial movements like advancing, turning, and squatting, which are vital for successful manipulation in dynamic environments. Enhanced reliability is paramount for real-world deployment.

- **Strong Generalization and Extensibility:** The demonstrated ability of WholeBodyVLA to generalize across varied start poses, scene appearances, manipulated objects, and external loads, along with its extensibility to complex, long-horizon tasks (e.g., terrain traversal, vacuum cleaning), indicates a robust and adaptable framework. This suggests that the learned policies are not merely memorizing specific trajectories but acquiring a deeper understanding of loco-manipulation principles, making them applicable to a broader spectrum of real-world scenarios.

- **Paving the Way for Future Humanoid Capabilities:** This work lays a solid foundation for future research in several promising directions mentioned by the authors. Integrating lightweight mapping and memory could enable more sophisticated long-term planning, while active perception strategies would enhance robustness in cluttered or dynamic environments. These advancements will further improve autonomy and versatility.

In conclusion, WholeBodyVLA represents a significant leap towards developing autonomous humanoid robots capable of truly general-purpose loco-manipulation. By innovatively addressing data limitations and control precision, it sets a new standard for intelligent embodied systems and opens exciting avenues for future advancements in robotics.
