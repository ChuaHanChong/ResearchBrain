# Reinforcement Learning Finetunes Small Subnetworks in Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This research from UIUC reveals that reinforcement learning (RL) finetuning of large language models (LLMs) consistently updates only a small, sparse subnetwork (5-30% of parameters) across diverse models and algorithms. Finetuning solely this subnetwork achieves performance equivalent to or better than full finetuning, converging to nearly identical parameter values, and this sparsity is primarily driven by training on in-distribution data rather than explicit regularization.

## Problem

- The high computational cost and resource demands associated with finetuning large language models, particularly with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
- A lack of mechanistic understanding of how RL finetuning modifies LLMs, specifically whether significant behavioral changes necessitate updates across the entire parameter space.
- The prevailing assumption that achieving sophisticated aligned behaviors in LLMs requires extensive, dense modifications throughout the network, leading to full finetuning practices.

## Method

- Empirically measuring ==parameter update sparsity== across a wide array of **10** LLMs and **7** RL algorithms (e.g., PPO, DPO, PRIME) to quantify the extent and location of modifications.
- Developing a method to identify the ==active subnetwork== during full RL finetuning and then performing 'masked finetuning' on a fresh model, updating only these parameters, to test if it recovers full model performance and parameter values.
- Analyzing the consistency of the identified subnetworks under varying training conditions (seeds, data, algorithms) and investigating factors (regularization, prior SFT, data distribution) that contribute to the observed sparsity.

## Results

- RL finetuning consistently updates only **5% to 30%** of LLM parameters, exhibiting **6**8.5%** to 96.0%** sparsity across diverse models and algorithms, in contrast to SFT's much lower **6% to 15%** sparsity.
- Finetuning only the identified sparse subnetwork recovers or even slightly improves performance (e.g., **+1.6%** for DPO, **+2.4%** for PRIME) compared to full finetuning, and leads to nearly identical final parameter values (**90.5-94.0%** identical with bfloat16 precision).
- RL-updated subnetworks show substantial consistency, with significantly higher overlap than random chance across different random seeds (**~60.5%**), training data (**~67.1%**), and even different RL algorithms (up to **~59.1%** across multiple changes).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning naturally induces strong parameter update sparsity (68.5% to 96.0%) in LLMs, a pervasive phenomenon distinct from the dense updates seen in supervised finetuning.
> - The active subnetwork is distributed across nearly all transformer layers and parameter matrices, making sparse yet almost always full-rank modifications, not confined to low-rank subspaces like LoRA.
> - Training on 'in-distribution' data—where the input data is close to the model's current policy distribution—is the primary driver for RL-induced sparsity, while traditional regularization techniques have limited impact.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper makes a significant contribution to the rapidly evolving fields of large language model (LLM) finetuning, reinforcement learning (RL) for AI alignment, and model efficiency. It directly addresses the computational challenges and theoretical understanding gaps prevalent in adapting massive neural networks.

**LLM Finetuning and Alignment:** The post-pretraining stage, particularly Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and its variants (like DPO, PPO, KTO), has become indispensable for aligning LLMs with human values, safety protocols, and for enhancing their performance on complex reasoning tasks. This work scrutinizes the fundamental assumption that achieving these sophisticated behaviors necessitates substantial, dense updates across the entire model. By revealing that only a small fraction of parameters are actively modified, the paper offers a novel perspective on *how* LLMs learn during this crucial alignment phase.

**Efficiency in Large Models:** As LLMs continue to grow in size, the computational cost of training and finetuning becomes prohibitive. This has spurred extensive research into parameter-efficient finetuning (PEFT) methods like Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) and its derivatives. These methods explicitly constrain updates to a small number of parameters or a low-rank subspace to reduce memory and computational overhead. This paper, however, presents a distinct finding: a high degree of parameter update sparsity *emerges naturally* in RL finetuning, without any explicit regularization or architectural constraints. This discovery is highly significant because it points to an intrinsic property of the optimization process, rather than an imposed one, thereby opening new avenues for efficiency beyond existing PEFT techniques. The finding that updates are sparse but *full-rank* further differentiates this work from LoRA-like methods, which focus on low-rank approximations.

**The Lottery Ticket Hypothesis (LTH):** The research directly builds upon and extends the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis (Frankle and Carbin, 2019), which posits that dense neural networks contain sparse subnetworks capable of achieving performance comparable to the full network when trained in isolation from scratch. This paper extends LTH in three key ways: (1) it observes *naturally emerging* subnetworks during RL finetuning of *pretrained* LLMs, rather than identifying them through pruning; (2) it demonstrates that finetuning this subnetwork not only reproduces performance but also converges to *nearly identical parameter values* as full finetuning; and (3) it shifts the focus from training from scratch to the critical finetuning stage of LLMs.

**Preservation of Pretrained Capabilities:** Recent studies have suggested that RL finetuning may preserve the pretrained capabilities of LLMs more effectively than supervised finetuning (SFT). This paper provides a concrete mechanism for this observation: by updating substantially fewer parameters, RL inherently retains more of the original pretrained model's knowledge, offering a compelling explanation for its ability to avoid catastrophic forgetting compared to SFT, which induces much denser updates.

Overall, this research reshapes our understanding of the RL finetuning process in LLMs. It shifts the paradigm from assuming dense, comprehensive updates to recognizing a powerful, naturally occurring sparsity. This insight has profound implications for designing more efficient, effective, and theoretically informed training strategies for the next generation of AI models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to systematically investigate a surprising phenomenon observed during the Reinforcement Learning (RL) finetuning of Large Language Models (LLMs): the substantial improvements in downstream task performance and alignment with human values result from updating only a small subnetwork, typically comprising a mere 5% to 30% of the total model parameters, while the vast majority remain effectively unchanged. This phenomenon is termed "parameter update sparsity induced by RL."

The motivation for this investigation stems from several critical points:

1. **Challenging a Core Assumption:** It is widely assumed that achieving significant behavioral changes and alignment in LLMs—behaviors often markedly different from those of the pretrained model—requires substantial modifications across the entire parameter space. This assumption leads to the common practice of full finetuning during RL. The authors, however, empirically observe that this might not be the case, prompting a deeper inquiry into the actual mechanics of RL finetuning.
2. **Uncovering an Unexplained Phenomenon:** The observed update sparsity occurs *without* any explicit sparsity-promoting regularizations, architectural constraints, or parameter-efficient training methods. This suggests an intrinsic property of the RL optimization landscape for LLMs, which demands explanation.
3. **Efficiency Implications:** If RL naturally focuses on a small subnetwork, it implies that much of the computational effort in full finetuning might be redundant. Understanding this inherent sparsity could unlock significant efficiencies in training time, memory usage, and energy consumption, which are critical concerns for ever-growing LLMs.
4. **Deeper Mechanistic Understanding:** The research seeks to answer fundamental questions about *how* RL updates LLMs. Where in the model do these updates occur? Are they low-rank or full-rank? Can the identified subnetwork truly reproduce the full model's capabilities in isolation? Is this subnetwork consistent across different training conditions? And crucially, what factors contribute to its emergence?
5. **Extending Prior Theories:** The work is motivated to explore connections to and distinctions from existing theories like the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis (LTH), which focuses on finding trainable sparse subnetworks, but primarily in models trained from scratch or identified via pruning. This paper aims to see if a similar phenomenon emerges naturally during finetuning.

Specifically, the authors structure their investigation around five key research questions (RQs):
- **RQ1:** To what extent does RL induce sparse parameter updates, where do these updates occur in the model, and how does this compare to supervised finetuning (SFT)?
- **RQ2 & RQ3:** Can finetuning *only* the identified subnetwork recover both the performance and the *exact parameter values* of the full-finetuned model (Conjecture 1)?
- **RQ4:** How consistent is the RL-updated subnetwork under varying training conditions (e.g., random seed, training data, different RL algorithms)?
- **RQ5:** What factors contribute to the observed update sparsity in RL finetuning?

By addressing these questions, the authors aim to provide a comprehensive characterization of RL-induced sparsity, understand its underlying causes, and highlight its profound implications for the future of LLM research and development.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors employ a comprehensive empirical methodology to investigate the phenomenon of reinforcement learning (RL) induced parameter update sparsity in large language models (LLMs). Their approach involves extensive experimentation across a wide range of LLMs and RL algorithms, coupled with detailed quantitative analysis.

### 4. Experimental Setup

- **Models:** The study analyzes publicly released checkpoints of 10 diverse LLMs from different families, including Tulu 8B/70B, Eurus 7B, DeepSeek Math 7B, Llama 3 8B, and Qwen2.5-Math-7B. This variety ensures that the observed phenomena are not model-specific artifacts.
- **RL Algorithms:** To ensure broad applicability, the authors investigate 7 widely-used RL algorithms: Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), Generalized Reinforcement Policy Optimization (GRPO), Only Reference Preference Optimization (ORPO), Keras Transformation Operator (KTO), Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), Simple Preference Optimization (SimPO), and Process Reinforcement through Implicit Rewards (PRIME). They also include supervised finetuning with rejection sampling (RAFT++), which has RL-like characteristics.
- **Sparsity Measurement:** The core metric is "update sparsity," defined as `1 - ||θ_1 - θ_0||_0 / n`, where `θ_0` and `θ_1` are model parameters before and after finetuning, `||.||_0` counts non-zero elements, and `n` is the total number of parameters. A numerical tolerance of `10^-5` (default for bfloat16) is used to account for floating-point precision, considering values below this threshold as effectively zero.

### 5. Investigating Sparsity Extent and Location (RQ1)

- **Quantitative Sparsity:** The authors directly compute the update sparsity for each RL-finetuned model by comparing its parameters with its preceding supervised finetuned (SFT) or base model checkpoint.
- **Comparison with SFT:** They perform a similar sparsity analysis for the SFT stage to contrast update patterns.
- **Layer-wise and Per-Matrix Analysis:** To understand where updates concentrate, they break down sparsity by transformer layer and individual parameter matrices (e.g., Q, K, V projections).
- **Rank Analysis:** They calculate the average rank of individual update matrices across all layers to determine if sparse updates are also low-rank, distinguishing their findings from methods like LoRA.

### 6. Subnetwork Finetuning and Reproduction (RQ2 & RQ3)

- **Subnetwork Identification:** From a full RL finetuning run (resulting in `θ_full` from `θ_init`), a binary mask `m` is created. `m_i = 1` if `(θ_init - θ_full)_i ≠ 0`, and `0` otherwise.
- **Masked Finetuning:** A second model (`θ_sub`) is finetuned from `θ_init` using the exact same data and hyperparameters as `θ_full`, but gradients are masked (`m ⊙ ∇_θ L(θ)`) at each step, ensuring only the identified subnetwork is updated.
- **Evaluation:** `θ_sub` and `θ_full` are compared on:
 - **Performance:** Test accuracy on standard benchmarks relevant to the models (e.g., AGIEval, MMLU Pro Math for DPO; MATH500 for PRIME).
 - **Parameter Identity:** The percentage of identical parameter values between `θ_sub` and `θ_full` is quantified using different numerical tolerances.
 - **Training Loss:** Training loss curves are compared to observe optimization dynamics.
- **Algorithm Diversity:** This experiment is conducted for both DPO (an off-policy algorithm) and PRIME (an on-policy algorithm) to demonstrate generalizability.

### 7. Subnetwork Consistency (RQ4)

- **Overlap Metric:** The authors define one-sided overlap `o_1 = |I_1 ∩ I_2| / |I_1|` (proportion of updated parameters in `I_1` that are also updated in `I_2`) and `o_2 = |I_1 ∩ I_2| / |I_2|`.
- **Controlled Ablations:** They perform experiments to vary one factor at a time:
 - **Random Seed:** Comparing subnetworks from two runs with different random seeds.
 - **Training Data:** Comparing subnetworks trained on different preference datasets (Tulu vs. PRIME rollout data).
 - **RL Algorithm:** Comparing subnetworks from DPO and PRIME using the same base model.
- **Random Baseline:** Observed overlaps are compared against a theoretical random guessing baseline for context.

### 8. Factors Contributing to Sparsity (RQ5)

- **Gradient Clipping and KL Regularization:** They analyze GRPO variants trained with and without these common regularization techniques, and examine SimPO which lacks a KL term.
- **Prior SFT:** The impact of pre-RL SFT is assessed by looking at models like DeepSeek-R1-Zero, which forgo SFT entirely.
- **Training Duration:** Intermediate checkpoints of a PRIME training run are analyzed to observe how sparsity evolves over time.
- **In-distribution Data:** The role of training data distribution is investigated by comparing:
 - SFT on rejection-sampled, "in-distribution" data (RFT, RAFT++).
 - DPO on "out-of-distribution" data (by skipping prior SFT).

By employing this rigorous multi-faceted approach, the authors aim to provide robust empirical evidence and insights into the mechanisms underlying RL-induced sparsity in LLMs.

### 9. Main Findings and Results

The research uncovers several significant findings that challenge conventional wisdom regarding Reinforcement Learning (RL) finetuning of Large Language Models (LLMs).

### 10. Pervasive and Strong RL-Induced Parameter Update Sparsity (RQ1)

- **High Sparsity:** Across all 7 widely-used RL algorithms (PPO, GRPO, ORPO, KTO, DPO, SimPO, PRIME) and 10 different LLM families, RL consistently updates only a small subnetwork of parameters. The update sparsity ranges from **68.5% to 96.0%**, meaning a substantial majority of parameters remain unchanged (Table 1). This sparsity emerges *naturally*, without any explicit regularization or architectural constraints.
- **Contrast with SFT:** In stark contrast, supervised finetuning (SFT) induces much denser updates, exhibiting only 6% to 15% sparsity (Figure 1). This suggests a fundamental difference in how RL and SFT modify LLMs.
- **Distributed Sparsity, Not Localized:** The sparse updates are not concentrated in specific layers or modules. Instead, nearly all transformer layers and individual parameter matrices (e.g., Q, K, V projections) receive similarly sparse updates (Figure 3). The only exception is layer normalization layers, which receive very little to no updates. This implies that the 'active' subnetwork is distributed throughout the model architecture.
- **Sparse but Full-Rank Updates:** Despite the sparsity, the updates are found to be almost always **full-rank** (Table 2). For instance, models like Tulu 8B (DPO) and Eurus 7B (PRIME) show update ranks of 99.8% and 99.5% of the maximum possible rank, respectively. This distinguishes the natural sparsity from low-rank adaptation techniques (like LoRA), which explicitly constrain updates to low-rank subspaces.

### 11. Subnetwork Finetuning Fully Recovers Performance and Parameter Values (RQ2 & RQ3 - Conjecture 1 Supported)

- **Performance Equivalence/Superiority:** Finetuning only the small subnetwork identified at the end of full RL training (denoted `θ_sub`) not only matches but often *outperforms* the test set performance of the full-finetuned model (`θ_full`). For DPO, `θ_sub` achieved an average +1.6% improvement across various AGIEval and MMLU Pro tasks (Table 3a). For PRIME, `θ_sub` showed an average +2.4% improvement on MATH500 across difficulty levels (Table 3b).
- **Near-Identical Models:** Crucially, `θ_sub` and `θ_full` converge to **nearly identical parameter values**. With a standard bfloat16 tolerance of `10^-5`, 94.0% of DPO parameters and 90.5% of PRIME parameters were identical. When the tolerance was slightly relaxed to `10^-4`, the models became **100% identical**. This strongly supports Conjecture 1, demonstrating that the parameters outside the subnetwork play a negligible role in the final model state and performance.
- **Lower Training Loss:** The training loss for subnetwork finetuning is consistently lower than that for full finetuning (Figure 4), suggesting a more efficient and stable optimization process.

### 12. Consistency of Subnetworks Across Conditions (RQ4)

- **Substantial Overlap:** The RL-updated subnetworks exhibit **substantially greater overlap than expected by random chance** across different random seeds, training data, and even different RL algorithms (Table 4).
 - Varying random seeds for DPO showed ~60.5% overlap, significantly higher than the 36.7% random baseline.
 - Varying training data (Tulu vs. PRIME) yielded overlaps up to ~67.1%, well above the random baseline of 14.6%-36.7%.
 - Even under a "stress test" where seed, data, and RL algorithm were all changed, notable overlaps of 59.1% and 33.2% were observed, exceeding the random baseline of 12.9%-23.0%.
- This consistency suggests the existence of a partially transferable subnetwork structure within the pretrained model that is robust to various training conditions.

### 13. Primary Driver of Sparsity: Training on In-Distribution Data (RQ5)

- **Limited Impact of Regularization:** Common techniques like KL-divergence regularization and gradient clipping, which are designed to constrain policy shifts, were found to have **limited impact** on update sparsity. GRPO variants with and without KL regularization showed comparable sparsity levels (69.8% vs. 68.8%). SimPO, which removes the KL term, also exhibited high sparsity (Table 1).
- **SFT Before RL Not Crucial:** While often performed, prior SFT is not a prerequisite for sparsity. DeepSeek-R1-Zero, which skips SFT, still showed 86.0% sparsity after 8K training steps (Table 1).
- **Training Duration:** Sparsity gradually decreases with more training steps but eventually converges to a high, non-zero asymptote (e.g., ~80% for PRIME, Figure 5). This suggests that not all parameters will eventually be updated. Analysis also showed that some parameters outside the final subnetwork receive transient, canceling-out gradients (Figure 6).
- **Dominant Factor: In-Distribution Data:** The most significant factor identified is **training on data that is near the policy distribution**.
 - SFT on in-distribution data (e.g., rejection sampling in RFT/RAFT++) produced high sparsity (~90% for Qwen2.5-Math-7B, Table 5).
 - Conversely, DPO applied to *out-of-distribution* data (without prior SFT) resulted in **dense updates** (e.g., 6.8%-7.7% sparsity for Llama-3.1-8B-SFT DPO and Zephyr-7b-Beta DPO, Table 5). This strongly suggests that when the model encounters data closely matching its current policy, only minor, sparse adjustments are needed.

In summary, the paper reveals an inherent and powerful sparsity in RL finetuning, demonstrating that LLMs achieve significant improvements by making highly targeted, full-rank modifications to a small, consistent, and distributed subnetwork, primarily when learning from in-distribution data.

### 14. Significance and Potential Impact

The findings presented in this paper carry substantial significance for the fields of large language models, reinforcement learning, and computational efficiency in AI. They offer novel insights into the fundamental learning dynamics of LLMs during their critical post-pretraining phase and open up several avenues for future research and practical applications.

### 15. Revolutionizing LLM Finetuning Efficiency

The most immediate and impactful implication is the potential for drastically more efficient RL finetuning. If 70-95% of LLM parameters remain effectively unchanged during RL, then current full finetuning practices are highly inefficient. This discovery suggests that future RL training methods could explicitly leverage this natural sparsity, leading to:
- **Reduced Computational Cost:** Less memory, fewer FLOPs, and faster training times by only updating the active subnetwork. This is crucial for democratizing access to LLM research and development, especially for larger models.
- **Novel PEFT Methods:** Beyond existing methods like LoRA (which constrain to low-rank subspaces), this work inspires new parameter-efficient finetuning techniques that leverage *sparse, full-rank* updates, potentially offering a different trade-off between expressivity and efficiency.
- **Greener AI:** Lower computational demands directly translate to reduced energy consumption, contributing to more environmentally sustainable AI development.

### 16. Deepening Understanding of LLM Learning and Alignment

The paper provides a mechanistic explanation for how LLMs adapt during RL. It suggests that RL primarily involves refining a specific, pre-existing "competent" subnetwork within the pretrained model, rather than fundamentally restructuring the entire network. This insight helps to:
- **Explain RL's Preservation of Capabilities:** The finding that RL makes sparse updates aligns with recent evidence that RL better preserves the broad capabilities of pretrained models compared to SFT, which induces dense updates. By changing fewer parameters, RL inherently retains more of the original knowledge, reducing catastrophic forgetting.
- **Inform Alignment Strategies:** Understanding *which* parameters are modified and *why* could lead to more targeted and effective alignment strategies, allowing researchers to focus computational and analytical resources on the most critical parts of the model.

### 17. Advancing the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis (LTH)

This work significantly extends the LTH by demonstrating that "winning tickets" (trainable subnetworks) not only exist but also *naturally emerge* during RL finetuning of pretrained LLMs. Furthermore, it shows that these subnetworks, when finetuned in isolation, can recover not just the performance, but *nearly identical parameter values* of the full model. This is a stronger claim than LTH and opens new theoretical questions about the nature of plasticity and redundancy in pretrained networks.

### 18. Potential for Subnetwork Transferability

The observed consistency of subnetworks across different random seeds, training data, and even RL algorithms suggests that parts of this active subnetwork might be transferable. This could lead to:
- **Warm-starting RL:** Reusing pre-identified sparse masks or subnetworks for new tasks or different RL algorithms (e.g., using a mask from a cheaper DPO run to accelerate a more expensive PPO run) could dramatically reduce training costs.
- **Optimized Hyperparameter Tuning:** In iterative development cycles or hyperparameter sweeps, partially reusing subnetworks could save substantial computational resources.

### 19. Guiding Future Research Directions

The paper explicitly highlights several avenues for future work:
- **Theoretical Analysis:** The empirical findings invite rigorous theoretical investigations into why this sparsity emerges, particularly the role of in-distribution data and the interaction between model architecture and optimization dynamics.
- **Early Subnetwork Identification:** Developing methods to identify the sparse subnetwork early in the training process, or even before training, could further enhance efficiency.
- **Generalized Sparsity:** Exploring whether similar sparsity phenomena exist in other foundation models, such as multimodal LLMs or diffusion models, could extend the impact of these findings beyond text-only LLMs.
- **Controlled Confounders:** Addressing potential confounders and exploring more complex interactions between different sparsity-contributing factors.

In conclusion, this research is a pivotal step towards understanding the intrinsic learning mechanisms of LLMs under RL. By uncovering the ubiquitous and effective nature of update sparsity, it not only promises to significantly enhance the efficiency of LLM finetuning but also deepens our theoretical grasp of how these complex models adapt and align, paving the way for a new generation of more interpretable, efficient, and capable AI systems.
