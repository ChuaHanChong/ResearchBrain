# Reinforcement Learning Finetunes Small Subnetworks in Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This research from UIUC reveals that reinforcement learning (RL) finetuning of large language models (LLMs) consistently updates only a small, sparse subnetwork (5-30% of parameters) across diverse models and algorithms. Finetuning solely this subnetwork achieves performance equivalent to or better than full finetuning, converging to nearly identical parameter values, and this sparsity is primarily driven by training on in-distribution data rather than explicit regularization.

## Problem

- The high computational cost and resource demands associated with finetuning large language models, particularly with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).
- A lack of mechanistic understanding of how RL finetuning modifies LLMs, specifically whether significant behavioral changes necessitate updates across the entire parameter space.
- The prevailing assumption that achieving sophisticated aligned behaviors in LLMs requires extensive, dense modifications throughout the network, leading to full finetuning practices.

## Method

- Empirically measuring ==parameter update sparsity== across a wide array of **10** LLMs and **7** RL algorithms (e.g., PPO, DPO, PRIME) to quantify the extent and location of modifications.
- Developing a method to identify the ==active subnetwork== during full RL finetuning and then performing 'masked finetuning' on a fresh model, updating only these parameters, to test if it recovers full model performance and parameter values.
- Analyzing the consistency of the identified subnetworks under varying training conditions (seeds, data, algorithms) and investigating factors (regularization, prior SFT, data distribution) that contribute to the observed sparsity.

## Results

- RL finetuning consistently updates only **5% to 30%** of LLM parameters, exhibiting **6**8.5%** to 96.0%** sparsity across diverse models and algorithms, in contrast to SFT's much lower **6% to 15%** sparsity.
- Finetuning only the identified sparse subnetwork recovers or even slightly improves performance (e.g., **+1.6%** for DPO, **+2.4%** for PRIME) compared to full finetuning, and leads to nearly identical final parameter values (**90.5-94.0%** identical with bfloat16 precision).
- RL-updated subnetworks show substantial consistency, with significantly higher overlap than random chance across different random seeds (**~60.5%**), training data (**~67.1%**), and even different RL algorithms (up to **~59.1%** across multiple changes).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning naturally induces strong parameter update sparsity (68.5% to 96.0%) in LLMs, a pervasive phenomenon distinct from the dense updates seen in supervised finetuning.
> - The active subnetwork is distributed across nearly all transformer layers and parameter matrices, making sparse yet almost always full-rank modifications, not confined to low-rank subspaces like LoRA.
> - Training on 'in-distribution' data—where the input data is close to the model's current policy distribution—is the primary driver for RL-induced sparsity, while traditional regularization techniques have limited impact.
