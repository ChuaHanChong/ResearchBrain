# X-Capture: An Open-Source Portable Device for Multi-Sensory Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Stanford University researchers introduce X-Capture, a portable open-source device integrating RGBD camera, tactile sensors, and microphones for collecting aligned multi-sensory data from real-world objects, along with a 3,000-point dataset across 500 everyday items that enables improved cross-modal learning when fine-tuning foundation models.

## Problem

- Limited availability of high-quality, real-world multi-sensory datasets for training AI systems
- Existing datasets focus on scenes rather than objects and often rely on controlled environments or simulated data
- High cost and complexity of current multi-sensory data collection setups limit broader research participation

## Method

- Developed a low-cost portable device (**X-Capture**) combining ==RGBD, tactile, and audio sensors== with ==explicit input-output measurements==
- Created user interface for consistent data collection and released open-source hardware designs
- Curated diverse dataset of real-world objects with ==aligned multi-modal measurements==

## Results

- Fine-tuning foundation models on X-Capture data improved cross-sensory retrieval and reconstruction, and enhanced performance when used as pre-training for smaller specialized datasets
- Demonstrated generative applications including image synthesis from audio/tactile inputs
- Achieved object localization in scenes using impact sound embeddings

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct alignment of multiple sensory modalities leads to stronger cross-sensory representations
> - Real-world data collection is crucial for bridging the sim-to-real gap in multi-modal learning
> - Audio embeddings can effectively replace text prompts for object detection tasks

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

- **Multi-Sensory Learning:** This paper fits squarely within the rapidly growing field of multi-sensory learning. The work acknowledges the importance of integrating multiple sensory modalities (vision, touch, audio) for AI and robotics systems to achieve human-like perception and understanding of the world. The work specifically recognizes that research into multi-sensory learning has been hampered by the limited availability of high-quality, real-world multi-sensory datasets.
- **Existing Datasets and Their Limitations:** The paper identifies key deficiencies in existing multi-sensory datasets. These include:
 - Focus on scenes rather than objects, limiting relevance for object-centric tasks like robotic manipulation.
 - Reliance on simulated objects or data collected in controlled environments, leading to a domain gap when applied to real-world scenarios.
 - High cost and limited accessibility of data collection setups.
 - Lack of breadth in sensory modality pairings, often linking only two modalities.
- **Foundation Models:** The work connects to research on large foundation models that aim to capture implicit knowledge of the physical world through their representations, such as CLIP, ImageBind, and ULIP.
- **Handheld Devices:** The paper builds upon previous work developing handheld devices for data collection. The authors acknowledge the advantages of the inter-sensor measurement consistency of sophisticated setups with the portability needed to collect data in situ that a handheld device provides.
- **The X-Capture Device as a Solution:** The X-Capture device directly addresses these limitations by providing a low-cost, portable, and open-source solution for capturing correlated RGBD images, tactile readings, and impact audio samples from objects in real-world environments.
- **Democratization of Multi-Sensory Data Collection:** By providing an accessible and affordable device, the authors aim to democratize the creation of multi-sensory datasets, enabling broader participation in this research area.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

- **Overcome Data Scarcity:** The primary motivation is to address the scarcity of diverse, high-quality, real-world multi-sensory data, which is crucial for training robust and generalizable AI and robotics systems.
- **Bridge the Sim2Real Gap:** The authors aim to reduce the domain gap between simulated/controlled data and real-world data by enabling data collection in "in-the-wild" environments.
- **Enable Object-Centric Learning:** The paper emphasizes object-centric learning by focusing on collecting data from individual objects rather than entire scenes, making the data more suitable for robotic manipulation and object understanding tasks.
- **Promote Multi-Modal Integration:** The X-Capture device and dataset are designed to facilitate the direct alignment of multiple sensory modalities (RGBD, touch, audio) to enable learning of richer cross-sensory representations.
- **Accessibility and Scalability:** The low cost and open-source nature of the X-Capture device are intended to make multi-sensory data collection more accessible to researchers and facilitate the creation of larger datasets.
- **Improve AI and Robotics Systems:** Ultimately, the goal is to advance human-like sensory representations in AI and robotics systems, leading to better performance in tasks such as object recognition, manipulation, and interaction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

- **Hardware Design:** The authors designed and built the X-Capture device, a portable and low-cost multi-sensory data collection device. The device integrates a RealSense D405 RGBD camera, a DIGIT tactile sensor, an impact hammer with a force sensor, and a microphone.
- **Explicit Input-Output Measurement:** A novel design feature is the explicit measurement of input-output relationships for both touch and audio. This is achieved by measuring the force applied by the DIGIT sensor using a load cell and by measuring the impact force of the hammer using a piezo sensor.
- **Software and User Interface:** The authors developed a user interface (UI) to visualize data during the collection process. The UI displays real-time sensor readings and provides feedback to the user, ensuring consistent data collection.
- **Dataset Creation:** The authors curated a sample dataset of 3,000 points on 500 everyday objects from diverse, real-world environments using the X-Capture device.
- **Benchmark Experiments:** The authors conducted experiments to evaluate the value of the X-Capture dataset for multi-modal representation learning. These experiments included cross-sensory retrieval, contact point localization, and generation.
- **Fine-Tuning and Pre-training:** The authors fine-tuned existing multi-sensory representation models (ImageBind, CLIP) on the X-Capture dataset and evaluated their performance on object-centric benchmark tasks. The authors also evaluated whether the X-Capture data set could be used as a pretraining dataset.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- **Feasibility of Low-Cost Multi-Sensory Data Collection:** The authors demonstrated the feasibility of building a low-cost, portable device for capturing high-quality multi-sensory data in real-world environments.
- **Value of Multi-Sensory Data:** The experiments showed that fine-tuning multi-sensory representation models on the X-Capture dataset improved their performance on object-centric tasks such as cross-sensory retrieval and reconstruction.
- **Direct Multi-Modal Alignment:** The results suggested that training with data that directly aligns multiple sensory modalities can lead to stronger cross-sensory representations.
- **Importance of Real-World Data:** The authors highlighted the importance of real-world data for training robust and generalizable models that can perform well in uncontrolled environments.
- **Generalization Gap:** Experiments indicated that the model had difficulty generalizing to datasets with a significant domain gap. However, using the X-capture data as a pre-training step helped bridge the generalization gap when using datasets with a smaller sample size.
- **2D/3D Generative Potential:** The audio and tactile embeddings from the trained encoder showed potential for generative applications such as generating images and 3D neural radiance fields.
- **Audio as Modality for Object Detection:** The trained CLIP-aligned audio embedding was used to replace text prompts for the Detic object detection model and it was able to localize objects in scenes using the sound of their impact against surfaces.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

- **Democratization of Multi-Sensory Research:** The X-Capture device has the potential to democratize multi-sensory research by providing a low-cost and accessible tool for data collection.
- **Advancement of AI and Robotics:** The X-Capture dataset and device can facilitate the development of more robust and generalizable AI and robotics systems that can better understand and interact with the real world.
- **Enabling New Applications:** The work can enable new applications in areas such as robotic manipulation, human-robot interaction, assistive technology, and augmented reality.
- **Community Resource:** By releasing the hardware designs and dataset as open-source resources, the authors encourage community contributions and further development in this area.
- **Further Research:** The limitations acknowledged by the authors regarding static object configurations suggest avenues for future work, such as developing automated data collection methods and exploring dynamic object interactions.
