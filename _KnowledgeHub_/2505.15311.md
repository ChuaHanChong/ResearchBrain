# Trajectory Bellman Residual Minimization: A Simple Value-Based Method for LLM Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UW-Madison and MIT present Trajectory Bellman Residual Minimization (TBRM), a value-based reinforcement learning algorithm for enhancing Large Language Model reasoning. TBRM achieves competitive or superior performance on mathematical reasoning benchmarks compared to policy-based methods, while offering improved computational efficiency and simpler implementation.

## Problem

- Policy-based reinforcement learning methods, commonly used for LLM reasoning, incur high computational costs due to the requirement for on-policy rollouts.
- Current policy-based approaches demand complex architectures, including auxiliary critic models, advantage estimation, and clipping mechanisms, increasing implementation complexity.
- Assigning credit for sparse, end-of-trajectory rewards (e.g., in mathematical problem-solving) is challenging for existing methods, often leading to imprecise token-level attribution.

## Method

- The paper introduces Trajectory Bellman Residual Minimization (**TBRM**), an algorithm that minimizes a single ==Bellman residual== over entire trajectories instead of per-step errors.
- TBRM directly leverages the LLM's own logits to parameterize Q-values and derived policy probabilities, eliminating the need for a separate critic model.
- The method enables fully off-policy optimization, allowing efficient learning from diverse data sources without the computational overhead of frequent on-policy rollouts or importance sampling ratios.

## Results

- TBRM consistently matched or surpassed the accuracy of PPO and GRPO across multiple mathematical reasoning benchmarks, including AIME24/25, AMC23, MATH500, Minerva-Math, and OlympiadBench.
- Using a single rollout per prompt (`TBRM_n=1`), the method achieved higher accuracies than `PPO_n=1` and performed comparably to `GRPO_n=4` while reducing GPU memory usage by **33.3%** and achieving a **1.3x** speedup.
- Ablation studies confirmed that directly applying classical, token-level Bellman Residual Minimization to LLMs resulted in unstable training and reward collapse, validating TBRM's trajectory-level adaptation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Value-based reinforcement learning can be effectively adapted for LLM reasoning tasks by reformulating Bellman Residual Minimization to operate at the trajectory level.
> - An improved change-of-trajectory-measure analysis demonstrates that trajectory-level objectives in TBRM incur only state-wise distribution shift costs, avoiding the 'curse of horizon' and facilitating efficient off-policy learning.
> - TBRM's objective fosters the emergence of sophisticated, human-like reasoning patterns in LLMs, such as problem decomposition, systematic enumeration, verification, and backtracking.
