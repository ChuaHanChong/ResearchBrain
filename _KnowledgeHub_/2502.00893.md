# ToddlerBot: Open-Source ML-Compatible Humanoid Platform for Loco-Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Stanford University researchers developed ToddlerBot, an open-source, 30-DoF humanoid platform costing under $6,000, explicitly designed for machine learning compatibility in loco-manipulation tasks. This platform facilitates robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for omnidirectional walking and achieves high-success-rate vision-based manipulation with as few as 60 real-world demonstrations.

## Problem

- Conventional robot hardware is often expensive, proprietary, and difficult to modify or repair, impeding rapid iteration and comprehensive system understanding in research.
- Modern learning-based robotics requires platforms designed for scalable, high-quality data collection and seamless sim-to-real transfer, a capability lacking in traditional robot designs.
- Existing miniature research humanoids typically compromise on either degrees of freedom or ML-compatibility, forcing a tradeoff between dexterity and learning-friendly data collection.

## Method

- Designed **ToddlerBot**, an open-source, 3D-printed miniature humanoid platform (0.56m, 3.4kg, 30 DoFs) built with off-the-shelf components for under $6,000.
- Developed a high-fidelity digital twin through precise system identification and a user-friendly teleoperation system for efficient real-world data collection.
- Integrated onboard compute (==Jetson Orin NX==) and optimized mechanical design for robust whole-body loco-manipulation and real-time ML inference.

## Results

- ToddlerBot demonstrated robust physical capabilities, including lifting **40%** of its 3.4kg body weight, performing dynamic push-ups, and walking for 19 minutes on a single charge.
- Reinforcement learning policies for omnidirectional walking showed successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, while imitation learning policies achieved **90%** success in vision-based bimanual manipulation from only 60 real-world demonstrations.
- The design proved highly reproducible, validated by an independent assembly and five global replications, with learned policies transferring seamlessly between different robot instances.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Affordable, open-source hardware, built primarily with 3D-printed parts and off-the-shelf components, can achieve high functional capability and widespread reproducibility in humanoid robotics.
> - A high-fidelity digital twin, achieved through comprehensive system identification including motor dynamics, is critical for enabling effective zero-shot sim-to-real policy transfer.
> - Complex loco-manipulation tasks can be learned and executed by chaining skills derived from both real-world teleoperated imitation learning and simulation-trained reinforcement learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

ToddlerBot emerges at a critical juncture in robotics research, driven by the increasing demand for data-intensive machine learning (ML) paradigms. Traditionally, robot design emphasized robust actuators, precise sensors, and mechanical exactitude, often resulting in prohibitively expensive, proprietary, and complex platforms. While effective for deployment, these characteristics hinder rapid iteration, debugging, and full system understanding crucial for learning-based approaches.

This paper explicitly addresses the shortcomings of conventional robot design for ML research by proposing a new paradigm that prioritizes **affordability, rapid repairability, full-stack ownership, and native ML-compatibility**. This aligns with a growing movement in the robotics community, exemplified by successful open-source quadruped platforms like Mini Cheetah and Stanford Pupper, and low-cost manipulators such as GELLO and LEAP Hand. ToddlerBot extends this philosophy to the more complex domain of humanoid robots.

The paper contextualizes ToddlerBot by comparing it against a comprehensive list of existing humanoid platforms (Table 1), categorizing them into full-size industrial humanoids (e.g., Boston Dynamics Atlas, Tesla Optimus, Unitree H1) and various research-oriented miniature humanoids (e.g., iCub, NAO H25, Berkeley Humanoid Lite). While full-size humanoids offer impressive capabilities, they come with substantial cost (hundreds of thousands of dollars), require extensive engineering teams, and specialized facilities, making them inaccessible to most research groups. Smaller humanoids from research institutions offer more flexibility but often compromise on degrees of freedom (DoFs) or ML-compatibility.

ToddlerBot distinguishes itself by offering an **unprecedented combination of characteristics for its class**:
- **High DoF Count:** With 30 active DoFs (excluding end-effectors) in a miniature package (0.56 m, 3.4 kg), ToddlerBot boasts more upper and lower body DoFs than any existing miniature humanoid. This anthropomorphic design is crucial for whole-body loco-manipulation, which is a major focus of current humanoid research.
- **ML-Compatibility:** It is explicitly designed to be a robust platform for data collection, both in high-fidelity simulation and the real world. This includes a comprehensive system identification (SysID) pipeline for accurate digital twins and an intuitive teleoperation interface for real-world demonstrations. This focus directly supports the development of policies through reinforcement learning (RL) and imitation learning (IL).
- **Reproducibility and Accessibility:** By being entirely 3D-printed with off-the-shelf components (total cost under $6,000 USD) and offering open-source designs and software, ToddlerBot significantly lowers the barrier to entry for humanoid robotics research. This directly addresses the "black box" problem of proprietary systems and promotes democratized research.

In essence, ToddlerBot bridges the gap between highly capable but inaccessible commercial humanoids and rudimentary open-source platforms. It provides a robust, versatile, and affordable research tool that directly supports the data-driven requirements of modern AI and robotics, thereby accelerating progress in areas like whole-body control, sim-to-real transfer, and human-robot interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind the development of ToddlerBot stems from a recognized mismatch between the needs of contemporary learning-based robotics research and the characteristics of conventional robot hardware. The authors articulate this clearly:
- **Limitations of Traditional Hardware:** Conventional robots, while prioritizing strength, sensor accuracy, and mechanical precision for deployment, often fall short in meeting research demands for affordability, rapid repairability, and full-stack ownership. These "black-box" systems impede innovation, debugging, and comprehensive system understanding within a research context.
- **Data-Driven ML Imperative:** Modern machine learning paradigms in robotics are intrinsically driven by embodied data. This necessitates robot platforms capable of seamlessly collecting high-quality observation and action data from both simulation and the real world. Traditional platforms were not designed with this scalable data acquisition in mind.

In response to these challenges, the key objectives for ToddlerBot are multifaceted, encompassing **ML-compatibility, capability, and reproducibility**:

1. **To develop an ML-compatible humanoid platform:** This is central to the project, aiming to create a system that facilitates policy learning for locomotion and manipulation skills. Specific goals include:
 - Enabling seamless acquisition of high-quality simulation data through a high-fidelity digital twin.
 - Supporting zero-shot sim-to-real policy transfer.
 - Facilitating scalable real-world data collection using a user-friendly teleoperation interface for whole-body control.
2. **To design a capable humanoid robot:** ToddlerBot aims for high functionality within a compact size. This involves:
 - An anthropomorphic design with a high number of active degrees of freedom (30 DoFs) to enable human-like motion and versatile loco-manipulation tasks.
 - Sufficient strength and dexterity for a range of open-loop and closed-loop tasks, including manipulation of appropriately scaled objects, push-ups, and pull-ups.
 - Onboard computational power (Jetson Orin NX) for real-time inference of ML models.
3. **To ensure the reproducibility of the platform:** This is treated as a "hard constraint," emphasizing accessibility and ease of replication. Key aspects include:
 - Maintaining a low total cost (under $6,000 USD) by utilizing entirely 3D-printed parts and off-the-shelf components (primarily Dynamixel motors and NVIDIA Jetson).
 - Providing a completely open-source design, including hardware, digital twin, learning algorithms, and comprehensive tutorials, to allow assembly and maintenance with basic technical expertise.
 - Designing for safe operation in real-world environments due to its compact size and weight.

Ultimately, the overarching motivation is to lower the barrier for robotics and AI researchers, democratize access to advanced humanoid platforms, and accelerate the pace of innovation in learning-based robotics by providing a transparent, capable, and scalable tool for both policy learning and execution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The design and development of ToddlerBot were guided by three core principles: reproducibility as a hard constraint, and capability and ML-compatibility as key design objectives.

#### 3.1. Reproducibility (Hardware Design and Open-Source Philosophy)

- **Materials and Cost:** The robot is primarily constructed from 3D-printed plastic parts, a deliberate choice justified by a biomechanical analysis showing comparable strength to full-size aluminum parts for its miniature scale (Appendix 8.1). This enables rapid prototyping and cost-effectiveness. Performance-critical components like motors and bearings are off-the-shelf. The total Bill of Materials (BOM) cost is kept under $6,000, with 90% attributed to the NVIDIA Jetson computer and Robotis Dynamixel motors.
- **Ease of Assembly and Maintenance:** The mechanical design prioritizes modularity, fewer screw types, and unobstructed tool access to simplify assembly and repair by a single person with basic expertise. This was validated by an independent replication.
- **Open-Source Release:** All digital twin models, learning algorithms, hardware designs, and comprehensive tutorials are open-sourced to ensure replicability and foster community contribution.

#### 3.2. Capability (Mechatronic Design)

- **Anthropomorphic Structure:** ToddlerBot replicates human body structure with 30 active DoFs, excluding end-effectors. This includes 7 DoFs per arm, 6 per leg, 2 for the neck, and 2 for the waist, enabling a wide range of human-like motions.
- **Range of Motion:** Joint geometries were optimized to prevent self-collisions and maximize range of motion, often achieving near-human or superhuman mobility (Figure 6).
- **Transmission Mechanisms:** To manage space constraints, reduce inertia, and optimize torque, the design incorporates a combination of three main transmission types (Figure 7, Appendix 8.4):
 - **Spur Gears:** Used for relocating joint axes, modifying torque, and distributing load to protect motor shafts (e.g., in arms and hip yaw).
 - **Coupled Bevel Gears:** Employed in the waist for compact actuation, allowing two motors to drive two perpendicular DoFs while combining their torque output.
 - **Parallel Linkages:** Utilized in the knee and neck pitch to position motors away from the joint axis, reducing rotational inertia and improving structural efficiency.
- **Sensors, Compute, and Power:**
 - **Sensors:** Two fisheye cameras for expanded field of view, an Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) for state feedback, a speaker, and microphones for interaction.
 - **Compute:** An onboard Jetson Orin NX 16GB provides substantial computational power for real-time ML model inference.
 - **Actuators:** Robotis Dynamixel motors (XC330, XC430, XM430, 2XL430, 2XC430) were chosen for their robustness, reliability, accessibility, and varying torque/size profiles for different joints (Table 2).
 - **Power Management:** A custom power distribution board (Figure 9) manages a 14-19V input (from a 3-5 hour battery) and regulates it for motors and the Jetson, featuring an E-stop for safety.

#### 3.3. ML-Compatibility (Digital Twin and Data Collection)

- **High-Fidelity Digital Twin:** This is foundational for effective sim-to-real transfer.
 - **Zero-Point Calibration:** Custom 3D-printed devices allow for quick (within a minute) and precise calibration of motor absolute zero points after reassembly (Figure 10, Appendix 8.8).
 - **Motor System Identification (SysID):** A comprehensive pipeline was developed, including a motor test bed (Figure 11, Appendix 8.9) and automatic test procedures. This measures dynamics parameters (friction loss, damping, armature) and velocity-dependent torque limits (Figure 12, Table 3, Appendix 8.10) for each Dynamixel motor model. An actuation model, inspired by Grandia et al., accounts for active damping and velocity-dependent torque limits. The transferability of these SysID results between robot instances was validated.
- **Data Collection Capabilities:**
 - **Simulation Data:** Leveraging the high-fidelity digital twin with MuJoCo for massive parallel data generation.
 - **Real-World Data (Teleoperation):** An intuitive "pupperteering" system for whole-body loco-manipulation data collection. This involves using a second ToddlerBot upper body as leader arms (with force-sensitive resistors for gripping) and a handheld gaming computer (Steam Deck or ROG Ally X) for joystick/button control of locomotion, neck, and waist movements (Figure 13, Table 4, Appendix 8.11). A two-layer PD controller (CoM and torso pitch) helps maintain balance during manipulation.

#### 3.4. System Control (Policy Learning Approaches)

- **Keyframe Animation:** For open-loop, expressive motions (e.g., push-ups, pull-ups), a GUI-integrated MuJoCo tool allows real-time tuning and dynamic validation of trajectories, enabling zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** For dynamic locomotion tasks like omnidirectional walking, PPO is used. The policy outputs joint position setpoints to PD controllers based on an observable state. The reward function includes imitation of reference motions, regularization (penalizing torque, promoting smooth actions), and survival terms (Table 6, Appendix 8.12).
- **Imitation Learning (IL):** For vision-based manipulation, a diffusion policy is trained on real-world teleoperated data (RGB images and joint angles). The onboard Jetson Orin NX handles inference with low latency (Appendix 8.13).
- **Skill Chaining:** Demonstrated by combining different control strategies (e.g., DP-based grasping with RL-trained walking) to achieve complex, long-horizon tasks (Figure 14).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ToddlerBot's capabilities, ML-compatibility, and reproducibility were rigorously demonstrated through a series of tests and loco-manipulation tasks.

#### 4.1. Capability Validation

- **Arm Span and Payload:** ToddlerBot can grasp objects up to 27 × 24 × 31 cm³, approximately 14 times its torso volume, using its compliant palm gripper. It successfully lifted 1484g, equivalent to 40% of its body weight (3484g), demonstrating significant upper body strength and lower body stability (Figure 3, left).
- **Endurance:** In a continuous walking test, ToddlerBot achieved a 19-minute streak without falling on a single battery charge, outperforming several other miniature humanoids. It withstood up to 7 falls before requiring repairs, which were quick (21 minutes for 3D printing and 14 minutes for assembly and calibration).
- **Expressive Dynamic Motions:** Keyframe-animated push-ups and pull-ups were successfully performed with zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, highlighting the fidelity of the digital twin and the robot's mechanical robustness (Figure 4). These tasks demand strong limbs, balanced upper-lower body strength, and precise coordination.

#### 4.2. ML-Compatibility Validation

- **Omnidirectional Walking (Reinforcement Learning):** An RL walking policy, trained in simulation, achieved successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for omnidirectional walking along a square trajectory. While policy limitations caused some deviation from the command, the sim-to-real gap was notably smaller than the overall tracking error, confirming the effectiveness of the high-fidelity digital twin. Position tracking error variance was reported as 0.018m (Figure 3, right; Table 7, Appendix 8.14).
- **Vision-Based Manipulation (Imitation Learning):** ToddlerBot demonstrated bimanual and full-body manipulation tasks (transferring octopus toys) using an RGB-based diffusion policy trained on only 60 real-world teleoperated demonstrations. It achieved high success rates: 90% for bimanual manipulation and 75% for full-body manipulation across 20 test trials. The onboard Jetson Orin NX successfully ran the 300M parameter diffusion policy with low latency (~100 ms), enabling real-time operation (Figure 4).
- **Skill Chaining:** The robot seamlessly combined a DP-based grasping skill with an RL-trained walking policy to perform wagon pushing, showcasing its ability to chain different learned behaviors for more complex, long-horizon tasks (Figure 14, Appendix 8.15).

#### 4.3. Reproducibility Validation

- **Hardware Reproduction:** The open-source design was validated by a CS-major student with no prior hardware experience independently assembling a second ToddlerBot instance in three days. Furthermore, the authors reported five independent replications by teams worldwide within a week, underscoring the platform's exceptional reproducibility (Figure 15, Appendix 8.16).
- **Policy Transferability:** Manipulation policies trained on data from one ToddlerBot instance successfully transferred to the independently built second instance with an identical 90% success rate. Similarly, the RL walking policy was successfully transferred between both robots.
- **Collaborative Task:** To emphatically demonstrate equivalent performance between two independently built instances, they collaboratively cleaned up a toy session in a long-horizon task (Figure 5), showcasing synchronized loco-manipulation and robust inter-robot performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ToddlerBot represents a significant contribution to the fields of robotics and artificial intelligence, particularly in the realm of embodied AI and learning-based control. Its impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Democratization of Humanoid Robotics Research:** By providing an entirely open-source, low-cost (under $6,000 USD), and highly reproducible humanoid platform, ToddlerBot dramatically lowers the barrier to entry for researchers, educational institutions, and hobbyists worldwide. This accessibility enables a broader community to engage in cutting-edge humanoid robotics research without requiring vast financial resources or specialized manufacturing facilities, fostering diversity and accelerating global innovation.

2. **Acceleration of Machine Learning in Robotics:** ToddlerBot is explicitly designed as an "ML-compatible" platform. Its robust data collection capabilities, both in high-fidelity simulation and through an intuitive real-world teleoperation system, are crucial for training data-hungry machine learning models. The validated zero-shot sim-to-real transfer capability significantly reduces the time and effort traditionally required to bridge the simulation-reality gap, allowing for faster iteration cycles in policy development. This will undoubtedly propel advancements in reinforcement learning, imitation learning, and other learning-based control strategies for complex loco-manipulation tasks.

3. **Versatile Platform for Diverse Research Avenues:** With its anthropomorphic design and high number of degrees of freedom (30 DoFs), ToddlerBot is not limited to just locomotion. It serves as a versatile research platform for a wide array of applications, including full-body manipulation, human-robot interaction, expressive character animation, and multi-robot collaboration. Its compact size further ensures safe operation in diverse real-world environments, making it suitable for studies that might involve interaction with humans or other agents.

4. **Foundation for Future Hardware and Software Development:** The comprehensive system identification (SysID) pipeline developed for ToddlerBot provides a strong methodology for accurately modeling robot dynamics, which is critical for realistic simulations and robust control. The open-source nature encourages continuous improvement and community-driven development of both hardware refinements and advanced software capabilities, promising a self-sustaining ecosystem for future research.

5. **Educational Tool:** Given its affordability and ease of assembly, ToddlerBot is an ideal educational platform. It can be used to teach fundamental concepts in robotics, control theory, mechatronics, and artificial intelligence in university courses, workshops, and even high school settings, inspiring the next generation of roboticists.

In conclusion, ToddlerBot addresses a critical unmet need in robotics research by offering a unique blend of ML-compatibility, advanced capabilities, and unprecedented reproducibility. It promises to catalyze new research directions, foster open collaboration, and significantly accelerate the progress of embodied AI by making sophisticated humanoid robotics accessible to a much wider scientific community.
