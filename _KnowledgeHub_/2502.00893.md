# ToddlerBot: Open-Source ML-Compatible Humanoid Platform for Loco-Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Stanford University researchers developed ToddlerBot, an open-source, 30-DoF humanoid platform costing under $6,000, explicitly designed for machine learning compatibility in loco-manipulation tasks. This platform facilitates robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for omnidirectional walking and achieves high-success-rate vision-based manipulation with as few as 60 real-world demonstrations.

## Problem

- Conventional robot hardware is often expensive, proprietary, and difficult to modify or repair, impeding rapid iteration and comprehensive system understanding in research.
- Modern learning-based robotics requires platforms designed for scalable, high-quality data collection and seamless sim-to-real transfer, a capability lacking in traditional robot designs.

## Method

- Designed **ToddlerBot**, an open-source, 3D-printed miniature humanoid platform (0.56m, 3.4kg, 30 DoFs) built with off-the-shelf components for under $6,000.
- Developed a high-fidelity digital twin through precise system identification and a user-friendly teleoperation system for efficient real-world data collection.
- Integrated onboard compute (==Jetson Orin NX==) and optimized mechanical design for robust whole-body loco-manipulation and real-time ML inference.

## Results

- ToddlerBot demonstrated robust physical capabilities, including lifting **40%** of its 3.4kg body weight, performing dynamic push-ups, and walking for 19 minutes on a single charge.
- Reinforcement learning policies for omnidirectional walking showed successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, while imitation learning policies achieved **90%** success in vision-based bimanual manipulation from only 60 real-world demonstrations.
- The design proved highly reproducible, validated by an independent assembly and five global replications, with learned policies transferring seamlessly between different robot instances.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Affordable, open-source hardware, built primarily with 3D-printed parts and off-the-shelf components, can achieve high functional capability and widespread reproducibility in humanoid robotics.
> - A high-fidelity digital twin, achieved through comprehensive system identification including motor dynamics, is critical for enabling effective zero-shot sim-to-real policy transfer.
> - Complex loco-manipulation tasks can be learned and executed by chaining skills derived from both real-world teleoperated imitation learning and simulation-trained reinforcement learning.
