# How Far Are LLMs from Professional Poker Players? Revisiting Game-Theoretic Reasoning with Agentic Tool Use

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers investigated the strategic reasoning abilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) in poker, identifying systematic flaws like factual misunderstandings and heuristic biases. They introduced ToolPoker, a framework integrating LLMs with external game-theoretic solvers, which enabled LLMs to achieve gameplay performance comparable to traditional solvers and produce professional-level strategic explanations.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) consistently struggle with complex strategic reasoning in imperfect-information games such as poker, failing to compete with traditional game AI.
- Previous evaluations of LLMs in game theory often focus solely on win rates, lacking a deep diagnosis of the underlying reasoning flaws preventing game-theoretic optimal (GTO) play.
- Enhancing LLMs' internal policies through methods like reinforcement learning proves insufficient to overcome fundamental limitations in precise game-theoretic derivation.

## Method

- Conducted a systematic preliminary analysis of vanilla LLMs, identifying persistent ==reasoning flaws== such as heuristic biases, factual misunderstandings, and a 'knowing–doing gap'.
- Developed ToolPoker, a novel framework that augments LLMs with a unified external game-theoretic solver interface, enabling them to generate internal reasoning, invoke the solver for precise calculations, incorporate outputs, and formulate GTO-consistent actions.
- Trained ToolPoker using a two-stage approach: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for tool invocation patterns and Reinforcement Learning (RL) with a composite reward function to align solver execution with GTO actions and precise reasoning.

## Results

- Vanilla LLMs consistently underperformed traditional poker algorithms (e.g., CFR+), exhibiting significant chip losses and low factual alignment (FA) scores (e.g., Qwen2.5-**3B** scored **0.18** FA and **0.53** Heuristic Reasoning score).
- ToolPoker achieved state-of-the-art gameplay, performing comparably to dedicated equilibrium solvers (e.g., -3.0 chips against CFR+ in Leduc Hold'em and -5.0 against DeepCFR in Limit Texas Hold'em).
- The framework produced near-perfect reasoning quality across metrics, closely aligning with professional game-theoretic principles (e.g., HR: 1.96, FA: 1.95, AC: 1.91 in Leduc Hold'em), demonstrating high factual rigor and consistency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vanilla LLMs exhibit systematic flaws in strategic reasoning, often relying on shallow heuristics and misjudging fundamental game aspects like hand strength and pot odds.
> - Internal policy improvements via regret-inspired reinforcement learning, while enhancing reasoning style, are insufficient to fully address factual inaccuracies and achieve robust game-theoretic optimal actions in complex poker variants.
> - Integrating LLMs with specialized, computationally rigorous game-theoretic solvers significantly overcomes their inherent limitations, allowing for both GTO-consistent gameplay and accurate, transparent strategic explanations.
