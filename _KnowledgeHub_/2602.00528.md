# How Far Are LLMs from Professional Poker Players? Revisiting Game-Theoretic Reasoning with Agentic Tool Use

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers investigated the strategic reasoning abilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) in poker, identifying systematic flaws like factual misunderstandings and heuristic biases. They introduced ToolPoker, a framework integrating LLMs with external game-theoretic solvers, which enabled LLMs to achieve gameplay performance comparable to traditional solvers and produce professional-level strategic explanations.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) consistently struggle with complex strategic reasoning in imperfect-information games such as poker, failing to compete with traditional game AI.
- Previous evaluations of LLMs in game theory often focus solely on win rates, lacking a deep diagnosis of the underlying reasoning flaws preventing game-theoretic optimal (GTO) play.
- Enhancing LLMs' internal policies through methods like reinforcement learning proves insufficient to overcome fundamental limitations in precise game-theoretic derivation.

## Method

- Conducted a systematic preliminary analysis of vanilla LLMs, identifying persistent ==reasoning flaws== such as heuristic biases, factual misunderstandings, and a 'knowing–doing gap'.
- Developed ToolPoker, a novel framework that augments LLMs with a unified external game-theoretic solver interface, enabling them to generate internal reasoning, invoke the solver for precise calculations, incorporate outputs, and formulate GTO-consistent actions.
- Trained ToolPoker using a two-stage approach: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for tool invocation patterns and Reinforcement Learning (RL) with a composite reward function to align solver execution with GTO actions and precise reasoning.

## Results

- Vanilla LLMs consistently underperformed traditional poker algorithms (e.g., CFR+), exhibiting significant chip losses and low factual alignment (FA) scores (e.g., Qwen2.5-**3B** scored **0.18** FA and **0.53** Heuristic Reasoning score).
- ToolPoker achieved state-of-the-art gameplay, performing comparably to dedicated equilibrium solvers (e.g., -3.0 chips against CFR+ in Leduc Hold'em and -5.0 against DeepCFR in Limit Texas Hold'em).
- The framework produced near-perfect reasoning quality across metrics, closely aligning with professional game-theoretic principles (e.g., HR: 1.96, FA: 1.95, AC: 1.91 in Leduc Hold'em), demonstrating high factual rigor and consistency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vanilla LLMs exhibit systematic flaws in strategic reasoning, often relying on shallow heuristics and misjudging fundamental game aspects like hand strength and pot odds.
> - Internal policy improvements via regret-inspired reinforcement learning, while enhancing reasoning style, are insufficient to fully address factual inaccuracies and achieve robust game-theoretic optimal actions in complex poker variants.
> - Integrating LLMs with specialized, computationally rigorous game-theoretic solvers significantly overcomes their inherent limitations, allowing for both GTO-consistent gameplay and accurate, transparent strategic explanations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: Large Language Models (LLMs), strategic reasoning, game theory, and agentic AI with tool use.

Firstly, the paper addresses the increasing deployment of LLMs in high-stakes domains (e.g., cybersecurity, strategic decision-making) where strong strategic reasoning under uncertainty is paramount. Game-playing, especially poker, serves as a canonical testbed for these abilities due to its incomplete information, multi-agent dynamics, and requirement for planning and opponent modeling. Previous work has explored LLMs in game-theoretic settings, such as GTBench (Duan et al., 2024) and PokerBench (Zhuang et al., 2025), which primarily evaluate gameplay outcomes, and Suspicion-Agent (Guo et al., 2023), which uses theory-of-mind prompting in simplified poker. While these studies reveal that LLMs often struggle to compete with traditional algorithms, they offer limited insight into *why* LLMs fail, often focusing narrowly on win rates rather than the underlying reasoning process. This paper explicitly aims to fill this gap by systematically analyzing both gameplay *and* reasoning traces.

Secondly, the work critically examines the limitations of improving LLMs' internal policies for complex strategic tasks. Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and related techniques (PPO, DPO, SimPO) have been instrumental in aligning LLMs with human preferences and improving their reasoning in domains like mathematics and code generation. However, this paper demonstrates that even with sophisticated RL fine-tuning using regret-inspired signals, LLMs still exhibit fundamental weaknesses in precise game-theoretic derivation. This finding challenges the notion that internal policy enhancements alone will suffice for mastering highly strategic domains.

Thirdly, the paper connects to the burgeoning field of Tool-Integrated Reasoning (TIR) or agentic tool use, where LLMs are augmented with external tools for tasks requiring precise computation or factual retrieval (e.g., math solvers, web search engines). Existing TIR research, however, predominantly focuses on fully observed, single-agent tasks. Poker, as an imperfect-information, multi-agent game, presents unique challenges for tool integration, such as multi-tool dependency for complex game-theoretic calculations and the need for tools to compute equilibrium-consistent strategies rather than deterministic answers. ToolPoker's contribution lies in extending TIR to this challenging domain, demonstrating a novel approach to integrate specialized game-theoretic solvers with LLM reasoning. This represents a significant step towards creating more reliable and robust AI systems for complex, real-world strategic decision-making where both natural language understanding and precise computation are required.

In essence, this research moves beyond simply benchmarking LLM performance in games to deeply analyzing their cognitive shortcomings and proposing a hybrid solution that combines LLM's natural language generation with the rigorous computational power of specialized solvers.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to thoroughly understand the capabilities and limitations of Large Language Models in strategic reasoning within imperfect-information games, specifically poker, and to develop a framework that mitigates these limitations to enable professional-level play. The paper is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Assessing LLMs' Strategic Reasoning:** As LLMs are deployed in increasingly high-stakes domains, their ability to reason strategically under uncertainty becomes critical. Poker serves as an ideal and rigorous testbed, demanding not just strong actions but also principled, game-theoretic reasoning grounded in equilibrium principles. The primary question posed is: "How far are LLMs from professional poker players?"
2. **Beyond Win Rates – Analyzing Reasoning Traces:** Previous studies on LLMs in game theory often focus solely on gameplay outcomes (e.g., win rate). This paper is motivated to go deeper by evaluating *why* LLMs succeed or fail. It aims to analyze the LLMs' reasoning traces to determine if their strategic thinking reflects principled, game-theoretic understanding, akin to professional players.
3. **Identifying Fundamental Flaws:** A core motivation is to conduct a systematic study to uncover recurring limitations in LLMs' internal reasoning mechanisms when faced with poker's complexity. The authors seek to pinpoint specific flaws that hinder LLMs from achieving game-theoretic optimal (GTO) play.
4. **Developing Mitigating Solutions:** Once fundamental flaws are identified, the research is motivated to explore methods for improvement. This includes investigating whether internal policy enhancements (via behavior cloning and reinforcement learning) can bridge the gap, and if not, pursuing alternative approaches like leveraging LLMs' strength in tool use.
5. **Achieving GTO Actions with Professional Reasoning:** The ultimate objective is to develop an LLM-based agent that can not only execute GTO-consistent actions but also generate precise, professional-style explanations that reflect sound game-theoretic principles. This holistic approach aims to push LLMs closer to truly intelligent strategic agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a multi-stage methodology involving preliminary analysis, an initial attempt at internal policy improvement, and the development of a novel tool-integrated framework.

#### 3.1. Preliminary Analysis of Vanilla LLMs (Section 3)

- **Tasks:** Evaluation was conducted on two widely studied two-player poker variants: Leduc Hold’em and Limit Texas Hold’em, both implemented in the RLCard environment.
- **Comparison Methods:** LLMs were pitted against four traditional baselines: NFSP (Neural Fictitious Self-Play), DQN (Deep Q-Network), DMC (Deep Monte Carlo), and CFR+ (Counterfactual Regret Minimization). For Limit Texas Hold’em, DeepCFR (Deep Counterfactual Regret Minimization) was used instead of CFR+ due to computational demands. These baselines represent diverse strategic paradigms, from self-play RL to equilibrium-based solvers.
- **LLMs Evaluated:** A suite of representative LLMs, including open-source models like Qwen2.5 (3B, 7B, 72B) and Llama3-8B, as well as powerful closed-source models such as GPT-4.1-mini, GPT-4o, and o4-mini, were tested in their instruction-following versions.
- **Evaluation Protocol:** 100 games were played for each comparison, with fixed random seeds and alternated player positions, measuring net chip gains.
- **Reasoning Flaw Identification:** An in-depth analysis involved examining reasoning traces through case studies and a quantitative LLM-as-a-Judge framework (using GPT-4.1-mini as the judge). Three metrics were designed (scored 0-2):
 - **Heuristic Reasoning Score (HR):** Measures reliance on shallow heuristics vs. rigorous game-theoretic principles.
 - **Factual Alignment Score (FA):** Assesses correctness of fundamental game aspects (hand strength, pot odds, range estimation).
 - **Action–Reasoning Consistency Score (AC):** Checks for alignment between articulated reasoning and final actions (the "knowing–doing gap"). Human-in-the-loop evaluation validated the LLM-as-a-Judge reliability.

#### 3.2. Initial Attempt: Improving LLMs via BC-RIRL (Section 4)

- **Motivation:** Address the identified flaws by enhancing LLMs' internal policies.
- **Behavior Cloning (BC):** A small-scale (approx. 5k samples) expert-level reasoning dataset (`D_b`) was curated. Expert actions were derived from a CFR+ solver, and reasoning traces were generated by an LLM guided by domain-specific prompt templates mimicking professional thought processes. This dataset was used for Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) to instill game-theoretic principles.
- **Regret-Inspired Reinforcement Learning (RIRL):** To refine policies beyond imitation and tackle sparse outcome-based rewards, a step-level regret-guided reward was designed. This reward leverages signals from a pre-trained CFR solver to minimize cumulative regret, guiding the LLM towards Nash equilibrium strategies. PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization) was used for fine-tuning with a clipped RL objective, incorporating a KL-divergence penalty.
- **Variants Tested:** Qwen2.5-7B was fine-tuned with BC-RIRL, BC-SPRL (self-play RL with sparse outcome-based rewards), and pure RIRL (without BC).

#### 3.3. Proposed Solution: ToolPoker Framework (Section 5)

- **Core Idea:** Augment LLMs with external poker solvers for GTO-consistent actions and precise game-theoretic reasoning, leveraging LLMs' strength in tool use.
- **Unified Tool Interface:** To address multi-tool dependency and stabilize training, a single API was designed that consolidates various solver functionalities (CFR solver, equity calculator, range analyzer) and returns all necessary quantities (GTO actions, equity, hand ranges) in one query.
- **Rollout Process:** A structured prompt template guides the LLM to:
 1. Generate internal reasoning (`<think>`).
 2. Invoke the unified solver (`<tool>`).
 3. Incorporate solver outputs (`<output>`) into its reasoning.
 4. Produce the final action (`<answer>`).
- **Training Algorithm:** A two-stage strategy:
 1. **BC for TIR:** The `D_b` dataset was programmatically augmented with standardized tool invocation templates and execution outputs (`<tool>` and `<output>` tags) to create `D_c`. SFT on `D_c` teaches the LLM *when* and *how* to call external tools.
 2. **RL Fine-tuning:** PPO with a **composite reward function** `R(a_t^i, â_t^i, ρ_t^i)`:
 - `R_answer`: Rewards alignment between model's predicted action and ground-truth GTO action from CFR solver.
 - `R_format`: Rewards adherence to the structured reasoning schema (correct use of `<think>`, `<tool>`, `<output>`, `<answer>` tags).
 - `R_tool`: Rewards successful tool calls and integration of their outputs.
- **Ablation Studies:** Variants (ToolPoker/BC and ToolPoker/RL) were implemented to assess the impact of each component on gameplay and reasoning quality.
- **Transferability Study:** ToolPoker was also adapted and evaluated in a 3-player Leduc Hold’em environment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study reveals significant insights into LLMs' poker capabilities and the effectiveness of ToolPoker.

#### 4.1. Preliminary Analysis (Vanilla LLMs)

- **Underperformance:** Most vanilla LLMs, especially smaller open-source models (e.g., Qwen2.5-3B, -7B), consistently underperformed traditional algorithms across both Leduc Hold’em and Limit Texas Hold’em. They exhibited severe chip losses (e.g., Qwen2.5-3B: -135.5 in Leduc, -205.8 in Limit Texas).
- **CFR+ Superiority:** CFR+ consistently outperformed all LLMs, even strong closed-source models like GPT-4o, highlighting the critical importance of explicit game-theoretic reasoning.
- **Exploiting Non-Equilibrium Opponents:** Larger and closed-source LLMs (e.g., GPT-4.1-mini, GPT-4o) showed competitive or superior performance against non-equilibrium baselines (NFSP, DQN, DMC), suggesting they can exploit suboptimal play, but still struggle with equilibrium strategies.
- **Systemic Reasoning Flaws:** Quantitative analysis with LLM-as-a-Judge revealed three persistent flaws:
 - **Heuristic Reasoning (HR):** LLMs often relied on shallow heuristics instead of rigorous game theory (e.g., Qwen2.5-3B scored 0.53 HR).
 - **Factual Misunderstanding (FA):** LLMs frequently misjudged hand strength, pot odds, or opponent ranges (e.g., Qwen2.5-3B scored 0.18 FA).
 - **Knowing–Doing Gap (AC):** Actions often deviated from their articulated reasoning (e.g., AC scores for most LLMs were below the professional baseline of 2.0, with o4-mini achieving 1.85). Scaling LLMs improved these metrics but did not eliminate the flaws.

#### 4.2. BC-RIRL Attempt

- **Modest Gameplay Improvement:** All RL-based fine-tuning variants (BC-RIRL, RIRL, BC-SPRL) showed improved performance in Kuhn Poker, a simpler environment. In Leduc Hold’em, BC-RIRL outperformed direct prompting and BC-SPRL (e.g., +17.0 chips vs. GPT-4.1-mini) but still lagged significantly behind CFR+ (-34.0 chips).
- **Reasoning Style Enhancement:** RIRL consistently improved HR and AC scores, reducing heuristic flaws and the knowing–doing gap (e.g., BC-RIRL reached 1.93 HR and 1.86 AC in Leduc, outperforming o4-mini).
- **Persistent Factual Issues:** RIRL yielded only marginal improvements in FA, indicating that factual misunderstandings remained a significant challenge (e.g., BC-RIRL achieved 1.12 FA in Limit Texas Hold’em, still far behind o4-mini's 1.65 FA).
- **Importance of BC:** Pure RIRL without the BC stage failed to yield improvements in Leduc Hold’em, highlighting BC as a necessary foundation for initial policy grounding.

#### 4.3. ToolPoker Performance

- **State-of-the-Art Gameplay:** ToolPoker achieved state-of-the-art gameplay performance, significantly outperforming all vanilla LLMs and the BC-RIRL approach. For instance, in Limit Texas Hold’em, ToolPoker gained +60.5, +63.0, and +61.5 chips against NFSP, DQN, and DMC, respectively, whereas BC-RIRL incurred losses.
- **Comparable to CFR+:** ToolPoker performed remarkably close to traditional equilibrium solvers, with only minor chip losses against CFR+ (-3.0) and DeepCFR (-5.0) in Leduc and Limit Texas Hold’em, respectively. This indicates a high success rate in executing GTO-consistent actions by calling the external solver.
- **Near-Perfect Reasoning Quality:** ToolPoker achieved near-perfect scores across all three reasoning metrics (HR, FA, AC), closely aligning with professional game-theoretic principles (e.g., HR: 1.96, FA: 1.95, AC: 1.91 in Leduc Hold'em).
 - Crucially, ToolPoker yielded substantially higher FA scores compared to BC-RIRL, demonstrating the critical role of solver-derived calculations in ensuring factual rigor and internal consistency in reasoning.
- **Ablation Studies Validation:**
 - ToolPoker/BC (without RL fine-tuning) showed weaker gameplay and lower HR, suggesting it could query the solver but failed to internalize reasoning patterns effectively.
 - ToolPoker/RL (without BC) performed poorly in gameplay and FA/AC, indicating superficial imitation of reasoning without aligning with GTO actions.
 - This confirmed that both BC (for foundational tool invocation) and RL fine-tuning (for aligning solver execution with GTO actions and precise derivation) are complementary and essential for ToolPoker's success.
- **Transferability and Scalability:** ToolPoker successfully adapted to and outperformed vanilla LLMs in a 3-player Leduc Hold’em variant, demonstrating its generalizability beyond two-player poker.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several significant contributions to the field of artificial intelligence, particularly concerning LLMs and strategic decision-making:

1. **Systematic Diagnosis of LLM Limitations:** This is the first systematic study to rigorously diagnose the fundamental reasoning flaws of LLMs in imperfect-information games like poker. Identifying "heuristic biases," "factual misunderstandings," and the "knowing–doing gap" provides a crucial roadmap for future research directions, moving beyond mere performance metrics to deeper cognitive analysis.
2. **Demonstrating the Insufficiency of Internal Policy Enhancement:** The paper empirically shows that while sophisticated RL fine-tuning (BC-RIRL) can improve LLM reasoning style and partially mitigate some flaws, it is ultimately insufficient for achieving precise game-theoretic derivations and competitive gameplay in complex strategic environments. This highlights inherent limitations of LLMs' internal knowledge representation and inference for such tasks.
3. **Introduction of ToolPoker: A Novel Tool-Integrated Reasoning Framework:** The development of ToolPoker is a pioneering step in extending agentic tool use to imperfect-information, multi-agent games. By integrating external, specialized poker solvers with LLMs, ToolPoker leverages the strengths of both: LLMs for natural language reasoning and explanation, and solvers for computationally rigorous, GTO-consistent actions and auxiliary quantities. This hybrid approach significantly advances the state-of-the-art.
4. **Achieving Professional-Level Performance and Reasoning:** ToolPoker's ability to achieve state-of-the-art gameplay performance comparable to dedicated equilibrium solvers, while simultaneously producing reasoning traces that closely reflect professional, game-theoretic principles, marks a critical milestone. It demonstrates that LLMs, when appropriately augmented, can not only act optimally but also *explain* their strategic decisions with high factual accuracy and consistency.
5. **Blueprint for Reliable AI in High-Stakes Domains:** The findings have profound implications for developing more reliable and trustworthy AI systems in other high-stakes domains requiring strategic decision-making under uncertainty, such as cybersecurity, negotiation, financial trading, and military strategy. By providing a mechanism for AI to ground its decisions in verifiable, optimal computations and articulate transparent reasoning, ToolPoker offers a powerful paradigm for accountable and explainable AI.
6. **Shifting Research Focus:** This work encourages a shift in focus from solely striving to make LLMs "smarter" internally to intelligently orchestrating LLMs with external, specialized computational tools. This hybrid approach represents a promising direction for future AI research, especially as task complexity increases beyond the reach of LLMs' inherent capabilities.

Future work, as suggested by the authors, includes exploring richer tool ecosystems, handling more complex multi-agent interactions, and developing principled frameworks for dynamically balancing internal LLM reasoning with external computational calls. The ethical statement emphasizes the research context of simulated environments, mitigating risks associated with real-money gambling, and underlines the goal of advancing AI understanding for safe and beneficial deployment.
