# PhyCheck: Fine-Grained Evidence-Grounded Dataset for Physical Law Understanding in Video-LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Zhejiang University and MBZUAI introduced PhyCheck, a fine-grained, evidence-grounded video question answering dataset to evaluate and enhance Video-LLMs' understanding of physical laws. The dataset, consisting of 6,399 synthetic videos and 69,825 QA pairs across six physical principles, revealed that current Video-LLMs struggle with reliable physical reasoning, especially for counter-physical anomalies, but fine-tuning with PhyCheck significantly improves their comprehension to 81% accuracy.

## Problem

- Current Video-LLMs lack a deep and reliable understanding of physical laws and causal relationships, often relying on statistical correlations rather than underlying principles.
- Existing benchmarks for physical understanding are insufficient, offering coarse-grained evaluations or focusing on video generation quality, rather than systematically assessing a model's comprehension of physical laws or providing structured evidence for violations.
- The absence of robust physical understanding impedes the development of effective World Models and safe, reliable Embodied AI systems that must interact within physical environments.

## Method

- A novel, fine-grained, ==evidence-grounded video question answering (VQA)== dataset called **PhyCheck** was created, featuring 6,399 synthetic videos and 69,825 QA pairs categorized under six major physical principles.
- A ==multi-tier VQA annotation system== was designed, comprising coarse-grained questions for overall physical law adherence and fine-grained, evidence-grounded questions probing specific visual details responsible for compliance or violation.
- A pilot subset for ==context-sensitive physical evaluation== was developed to assess Video-LLMs' ability to dynamically adjust physical judgments based on external causal context, moving beyond static consistency recognition.

## Results

- Most evaluated Video-LLMs achieved around **60%** accuracy on PhyCheck, highlighting significant limitations in reliably discerning physical law compliance and identifying counter-physical anomalies.
- Fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL with PhyCheck training data led to a substantial improvement in accuracy to **81.00%**, demonstrating the dataset's efficacy in enhancing physical understanding.
- Baseline models showed an over-reliance on visual shortcuts in context-sensitive scenarios, failing to update predictions with external context, whereas the fine-tuned model exhibited improved context-sensitive reasoning by integrating hidden physical factors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Existing Video-LLMs exhibit moderate physical reasoning capabilities on PhyCheck, with average accuracies around 60%, and show a conservative bias by being more adept at identifying physical compliance than violations.
> - The PhyCheck dataset is an effective training resource, as fine-tuning a Video-LLM with a sequence of fine-grained followed by coarse-grained VQA training significantly improves its physical comprehension.
> - Models struggle more with implicit physical reasoning related to Conservation laws and Newtonian mechanics compared to visually intuitive phenomena like Inertia, indicating a need for deeper causal understanding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) and World Models necessitates artificial intelligence systems that possess a deep understanding of physical regularities and causal relationships within the real world. Video Large Language Models (Video-LLMs) are central to this pursuit, as video serves as a natural modality for capturing spatiotemporal dynamics governed by physical principles. While contemporary Video-LLMs demonstrate capabilities in general video understanding, commonsense reasoning, and planning, they have exhibited limitations in reliably comprehending and reasoning about the physical world. Critics suggest these models often rely on statistical correlations and superficial patterns, rather than mastering the underlying physical laws, which can lead to "shortcut learning." This deficiency poses a significant impediment to their deployment in safety-critical Embodied AI scenarios and their evolution into robust World Models capable of simulating reality.

Existing research has begun to address the importance of physical world understanding. Benchmarks such as PhysGame evaluate physical commonsense violations in gameplay videos, and PhysBench assesses Vision-Language Models' (VLMs) comprehension of physical phenomena, highlighting their limitations due to a lack of physical knowledge in training data. Other works, like MVP, focus on shortcut-aware evaluation protocols for distinguishing genuine physical reasoning. Concurrently, there is a separate line of research on video generation models (e.g., Sora, Cosmos, Wan) and evaluating their physical consistency. Benchmarks like VideoPhy and VideoPhy2 assess whether generated videos produce physically plausible events, while VBench2 and PhyGenBench incorporate physics-related metrics for generation quality.

This work, PhyCheck, situates itself by addressing a gap in the comprehension side of physical understanding for Video-LLMs. Unlike existing benchmarks that primarily provide coarse-grained evaluations of physical consistency or focus on the physical quality of *generated* videos, PhyCheck specifically aims to evaluate and improve Video-LLMs' *understanding* of physical laws, including their ability to identify and explain explicit violations. It introduces a fine-grained, evidence-grounded approach to systematically decompose complex physical laws into visual evidence, thereby providing structured supervision for model improvement and a more rigorous diagnostic tool than previous, often coarse-grained, evaluations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the recognized gap in current Video-LLMs' ability to understand physical laws, despite their general advancements in video comprehension. The paper highlights several specific motivations:

- **Necessity for World Models and Embodied AI:** Building effective World Models, a prerequisite for AGI, requires AI systems to internalize and simulate the physical working mechanisms of the real world. Similarly, Embodied AI demands safe and reliable interactions within physical environments, which critically depends on mastering physical laws like conservation, optical, and mechanics laws. Current Video-LLMs, described as "sophisticated pattern recognizers" driven by "spurious statistical correlations," lack this fundamental physical understanding, leading to "shortcut learning."
- **Limitations of Existing Benchmarks:** Existing benchmarks for physical understanding in Video-LLMs are considered insufficient. They often focus on evaluating the physical quality of *generated* videos, which is a distinct objective from assessing a model's *comprehension* of physical laws. Benchmarks that do address comprehension typically offer coarse-grained evaluations or focus on specific scenarios (e.g., gameplay videos), lacking systematic decomposition of physical laws into explicit visual evidence or comprehensive assessment of physical violations. This restricts systematic evaluation and improvement of models' ability to differentiate physically consistent from inconsistent dynamics.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of this work are:

- **Introduce PhyCheck Dataset:** To create a novel, fine-grained, evidence-grounded video question answering (VQA) dataset, named PhyCheck, specifically designed for physical law understanding in Video-LLMs. This dataset aims to move beyond coarse-grained descriptions to explicit visual evidence clues related to physical laws.
- **Systematically Evaluate Physical Law Compliance and Violation:** To construct the dataset with a focus on both physically consistent and counter-physical anomaly videos, enabling a rigorous evaluation of Video-LLMs' ability to discern compliance with and violation of physical laws.
- **Develop a Multi-Tier VQA Annotation System:** To design a hierarchical annotation system comprising coarse-grained questions (determining overall physical law adherence) and fine-grained, evidence-grounded questions (probing specific visual details responsible for compliance or violation). This system is intended for both rigorous benchmarking and providing structured reasoning scaffolding for supervised fine-tuning.
- **Incorporate Context-Sensitive Physical Evaluation:** To include a diagnostic subset that assesses whether Video-LLMs can dynamically adjust their physical judgments based on external causal context, moving beyond static consistency recognition.
- **Evaluate and Enhance Video-LLMs:** To conduct extensive evaluations of a diverse range of existing Video-LLMs on PhyCheck to diagnose their current limitations in physical intuition, particularly regarding counter-physical anomalies. Furthermore, to demonstrate the utility of PhyCheck as a training resource by fine-tuning a Video-LLM and observing improvements in its physical comprehension.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology primarily involves the construction of the PhyCheck dataset and its application for evaluating and enhancing Video-LLMs.

**PhyCheck Dataset Overview:**
PhyCheck is a physics-laws-oriented video question answering dataset. It comprises 6,399 synthetic videos, totaling 8.61 hours of content, and contains 69,825 question-answer pairs, averaging approximately 10.9 questions per video. All questions are in a binary yes/no format. The questions are categorized under six major physical principles: Mechanics Laws, Conservation Laws, Material Properties, Fluids and Interface Phenomena, Optical Laws, and Thermal Laws.

**Dataset Construction Framework:**

1. **Data Source:** Synthetic videos were collected from VideoPhy2, a benchmark for evaluating physical consistency in generated videos. These videos were originally generated by seven text-to-video models (Luma Ray2, CogVideo, Cosmos, Hunyuan, VideoCrafter, Sora, and Wan). The raw video files from VideoPhy2 were re-annotated and restructured for physical comprehension assessment.

2. **Rule-Grounded Physical VQA Construction Pipeline (Coarse-grained):**
 - **Label Extraction:** Initial physical rule annotations (followed, violated, or undetermined) from VideoPhy2 were utilized. "Undetermined" cases were discarded to ensure reliable supervision.
 - **Template-Based Generation:** Rule descriptions were converted into VQA pairs using two types of templates: "consistency templates" (e.g., "Does the phenomenon... conform to...?"), and "violation templates" (e.g., "Does the phenomenon... violate...?").
 - **Answer Assignment:** Answers were deterministically assigned based on the original VideoPhy2 annotations, with mappings reversed for violation templates to balance question polarity.
 - **Manual Review:** All generated questions underwent manual review to ensure visual grounding, unambiguous phrasing, and answerability from the video content. This process created instances of video, a rule-oriented question, and a binary answer, with associated physical law metadata.

3. **Evidence-Grounded VQA Annotation Pipeline (Fine-grained):**
 - This pipeline aims to identify specific visual and physical cues that explain physical law violations or compliance.
 - **LLM-Assisted Generation:** Gemini-3 was initially used to analyze each video and generate detailed descriptions of physical phenomena. Given the video description and associated physical law labels, Gemini-3 was instructed to generate "negative prompts" describing specific physical details that should be avoided in physically correct videos (hypotheses of inconsistencies).
 - **Question Refinement:** GPT-5 then transformed these negative prompts into clear, answerable questions, formatted to ask whether a specific physical detail exists in the video.
 - **Human Verification:** All generated questions and candidate physical details were independently reviewed by two human annotators. Disagreements were resolved by a third annotator through majority voting. The inter-annotator agreement (raw agreement rate of 99.74%, Cohen’s κ of 0.9959) indicated high reliability.

4. **Context-Sensitive Physical Evaluation (Pilot Study):**
 - A small pilot subset of 50 hand-crafted video pairs was created to assess context-dependent physical validity.
 - **Construction:** Real-world plausible videos were selected. Causal factors were intentionally cropped out (e.g., a hairdryer beneath a floating ball was removed), making the remaining clip appear physically implausible.
 - **Contextual Cues:** Manually written text descriptions of the hidden causal factors (e.g., "upward airflow") were provided as external context.
 - **Evaluation Focus:** Models were evaluated on their ability to initially recognize the implausibility of the isolated visual effect and subsequently adjust their judgment when provided with the external context.

**Model Evaluation and Enhancement:**

1. **Evaluated Models:** A range of open-source and proprietary Video-LLMs were evaluated, including various Qwen series models, Gemini-3.5-Flash, LLaVA-OneVision2, UniVid, UniVideo, and Skyra (a model specialized for AI-generated video detection).
2. **Evaluation Setup:**
 - **Direct Evaluations:** Models were directly assessed on the PhyCheck test dataset to gauge their intrinsic ability to verify physics-related statements.
 - **Supervised Fine-Tuning Utility:** Qwen2.5-VL was fine-tuned on the PhyCheck training set for 12 epochs to demonstrate the dataset's efficacy as a training resource.
 - **Exploratory Pilot Probe:** Models were tested on the context-sensitive subset in three settings: video-only, context-assisted, and joint accuracy.
3. **Metrics:** Overall accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 scores were reported for both "Conform" and "Violate" examples to provide a comprehensive evaluation and identify potential prediction biases.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Video-LLMs on the PhyCheck dataset yielded several findings regarding their physical understanding capabilities:

1. **Overall Physical Understanding Limitations:**
 - Existing Video-LLMs exhibited moderate performance on PhyCheck, with most achieving accuracies around 60%. This indicates that current models generally struggle with reliable physical-law judgment.
 - Analysis of class-specific metrics revealed a conservative prediction bias: most models showed higher recall on "Conform" examples compared to "Violate" examples. This suggests a tendency to avoid identifying physical violations.
 - Skyra, a model designed for AI-generated video detection based on artifact reasoning, achieved competitive performance but did not consistently surpass general-purpose Video-LLMs, indicating that artifact detection is distinct from a comprehensive understanding of underlying physical laws.

2. **Dataset Utility Validation (Supervised Fine-Tuning):**
 - Experiments with fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL demonstrated the distinct contributions of different supervision granularities within PhyCheck.
 - Training with fine-grained VQA alone provided marginal improvement (accuracy from 50.93% to 51.43%), suggesting it might be insufficient for establishing foundational physical consistency understanding independently.
 - Training with coarse-grained VQA alone resulted in substantial improvement, raising accuracy to 66.83%, indicating its effectiveness in providing foundational signals for physical understanding.
 - The most significant improvement was observed when fine-grained VQA training was followed by coarse-grained VQA training, achieving the best performance of 81.00%. This sequence suggests an effective learning trajectory where fine-grained supervision helps establish learned physical concepts, and coarse-grained supervision then enhances overall physical reasoning.

3. **Category-wise Failure Analysis:**
 - **Physical Law Categories:** Video-LLMs showed uneven capabilities across different physical phenomena. Lower failure rates were observed for visually intuitive phenomena like Inertia, Reflection, and Buoyancy, where physical cues are often directly observable. Conversely, categories requiring more implicit physical reasoning, such as Conservation laws and Newtonian mechanics, exhibited higher failure rates.
 - Fine-tuning with PhyCheck led to substantial reductions in failure rates across all physical categories for Qwen2.5-VL, with particular improvements in Conservation laws and Newtonian mechanics.
 - **Video Generation Sources:** Video-LLMs displayed relatively consistent failure patterns across videos generated by different text-to-video models. This suggests that current failures in physical understanding are primarily due to insufficient physical reasoning capabilities rather than specific visual characteristics or artifacts introduced by different generation models.

4. **Context-Assisted Physical Understanding (Pilot Study):**
 - On the small-scale context-sensitive subset, baseline models exhibited high accuracy in the "video-only" setting (e.g., 0.96 for Qwen3.5-27B) but often failed to update their predictions when additional context was provided. This suggests a potential over-reliance on rigid visual shortcuts, where models prematurely interpret partial visual anomalies as definitive physical violations.
 - In contrast, the fine-tuned Qwen2.5-Finetune showed lower performance in the "video-only" setting (0.18), which may indicate a form of epistemic caution or a learned hesitancy to make definitive physical judgments solely based on partial visual cues. However, when context was provided, its performance significantly increased to 0.98. These preliminary results suggest that alignment with PhyCheck can foster context-sensitive reasoning, encouraging models to integrate hidden physical factors dynamically.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces PhyCheck, a dataset that addresses a critical need in the field of video understanding and AI development. The significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Advancing Physical Understanding in Video-LLMs:** PhyCheck provides a specialized benchmark and training resource specifically engineered to evaluate and improve Video-LLMs' ability to understand physical laws. By explicitly probing both compliance with and violation of physical laws, it encourages models to move beyond superficial visual priors and engage with true physical causality. This is crucial for developing models that can reliably operate in real-world scenarios.

2. **Facilitating World Model Development:** A robust understanding of physical laws is considered indispensable for building World Models capable of internalizing, simulating, and reasoning about reality. PhyCheck's focus on fine-grained, evidence-grounded physical reasoning provides a foundation for developing Video-LLMs that are more physically grounded, thereby contributing to the long-term goal of achieving artificial general intelligence.

3. **Enhancing Embodied AI Safety and Reliability:** For Embodied AI systems to interact safely and reliably within physical environments, they must master underlying physical laws. The limitations in physical understanding revealed by PhyCheck, particularly the struggle with counter-physical anomalies, highlight areas that need improvement for safer deployment of embodied agents. PhyCheck offers a pathway to train models that can better predict and prevent physically implausible or unsafe outcomes.

4. **Novel Diagnostic and Training Methodology:** The dataset's multi-tier VQA annotation system, which includes both coarse-grained and fine-grained evidence-grounded questions, serves a dual purpose: it rigorously diagnoses model capabilities and provides structured reasoning scaffolding for supervised fine-tuning. The finding that fine-grained supervision followed by coarse-grained supervision yields optimal performance suggests an effective learning trajectory for physical concepts, impacting future training strategies for Video-LLMs.

5. **Context-Sensitive Reasoning Evaluation:** The inclusion of a context-sensitive subset provides a unique mechanism to assess whether Video-LLMs can dynamically update their physical law judgments upon integrating external causal context. This moves beyond static consistency recognition to a higher level of contextual physics reasoning, highlighting a critical area for future research and model development, as evidenced by the performance shift in the fine-tuned model.

6. **Guiding Future Research:** The comprehensive evaluation across various Video-LLMs reveals systemic limitations in current models' physical intuition, particularly their acute vulnerability to counter-physical anomalies and their uneven capabilities across different physical law categories. These findings precisely identify weaknesses (e.g., in implicit reasoning, integrating contextual information) and will guide subsequent research toward developing more robust and physically intelligent Video-LLMs.
