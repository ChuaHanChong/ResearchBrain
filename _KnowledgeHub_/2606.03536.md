# Bionic Human-Motion Style Transfer for Physically Executable Whole-Body Control of Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces a bionic generation-to-control pipeline enabling humanoid robots to execute expressive whole-body motions. It generates physically executable references by combining human-motion style transfer with physics-aware latent diffusion and robust whole-body tracking, achieving a 96.0% success rate on a Unitree G1 robot across diverse styles.

## Problem

- Humanoid robot control traditionally prioritizes stability and task completion, often neglecting variations in expressive motion style.
- Motion style transfer methods from computer animation frequently produce physically implausible motions with artifacts (e.g., foot sliding, joint jitter) that are unsuitable for direct physical robot execution.
- The challenge lies in generating expressive robot motions that simultaneously preserve motion content, accurately convey style, and ensure stable physical executability on real hardware.

## Method

- A bionic generation-to-control pipeline integrates human-motion style generation, physics-aware reference conversion, and a robust whole-body tracking policy.
- A multi-condition latent ==diffusion== model, conditioned on content, style, and global trajectory, incorporates contact-consistency and temporal-smoothness regularization during training to generate physically feasible motion references.
- A style-diverse, cluster-and-distill deep ==reinforcement learning== policy is developed for the whole-body tracker, enabling it to robustly track a wide range of stylized motions without requiring retraining for each unique style.

## Results

- The proposed method achieved a **96.02%** simulated closed-loop success rate, outperforming animation-oriented baselines which struggled with robot-oriented feasibility metrics like foot sliding and joint smoothness.
- With both contact-consistency and temporal-smoothness regularizers, the full model generated the most robot-executable references, achieving the lowest foot sliding factor (0.004722 m/frame) and smoother joint trajectories.
- Deployed on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, the framework demonstrated an overall hardware success rate of **96.0%** across 125 trials, successfully executing stylized whole-body movements in real-world conditions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating physics-aware regularization directly into the latent diffusion model significantly reduces physically implausible artifacts like foot sliding and joint jitter, crucial for robot executability.
> - A specialized tracker training strategy (clustering and distillation) allows a single generalist policy to effectively handle diverse stylized motions, improving robustness and simplifying deployment.
> - There is an inherent trade-off between the perceptual intensity of transferred motion style and the physical robustness and executability of the robot, with excessive stylization potentially compromising stability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge at the intersection of humanoid robotics, motion generation, and human-robot interaction. Traditionally, humanoid robot control research has primarily focused on stability, tracking accuracy, disturbance rejection, and task completion. While these aspects are fundamental for reliable hardware operation, they often treat reference motions as fixed inputs, dedicating limited attention to variations in motion style.

Concurrently, the field of computer animation has advanced significantly in arbitrary motion style transfer, enabling the generation of visually coherent and stylized human movements from short exemplars. However, directly applying these animation-oriented outputs to physical robots is problematic. Animated motions frequently lack the physical consistency required for robotic execution, often exhibiting artifacts such as stance-foot sliding, ground penetration, or high-frequency joint oscillations that violate hardware constraints and lead to instability.

This paper draws inspiration from bionic principles, specifically the observation that human motion styles convey affective and intentional cues through gait rhythm, posture, arm swing, and body sway. Prior research on robot emotion expression has indicated that body movement can influence how humans interpret a robot's state and communicative intent. The work positions itself to bridge the gap between expressive motion generation, as seen in animation, and the physical executability demands of humanoid robots. It builds upon established humanoid locomotion control methods (e.g., ZMP preview control, capture point analysis, DCM/MPC formulations) which provide the physical foundation, but extends them by integrating style variations. Furthermore, it leverages advancements in deep reinforcement learning for whole-body tracking, as demonstrated by systems like DeepMimic, AMP, and various robust humanoid trackers, but specifically focuses on generating *suitable* stylized references for these trackers. The core contribution lies in integrating exemplar-driven human motion style transfer with a physics-aware generation process and a robust, style-aware tracking pipeline for real-world humanoid deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable humanoid robots to execute expressive whole-body motions that are both stable and visually interpretable by humans. The authors formulate a specific problem: given a target content motion (e.g., walking direction, turning pattern) and a short human style exemplar (e.g., conveying slower rhythm, asymmetric sway, or a specific arm posture), the goal is to generate a full-body reference motion for a humanoid robot. This generated reference must satisfy three criteria:
1. **Preserve the intended motion content and global trajectory.**
2. **Express the target human motion style.**
3. **Be stably executable by a real humanoid robot.**

The motivation for this objective stems from the increasing expectation for humanoid robots to operate in social, service, and human-coexistence environments. In these settings, merely achieving stable locomotion and task completion is considered insufficient. Robots are expected to exhibit legible, human-like, and adjustable body behaviors that can communicate nonverbal cues. Human motion style is identified as a valuable "bionic source" for this capability, as humans naturally perceive affect and intent from body dynamics.

The authors detail several coupled challenges inherent in achieving this goal:
- **Balance Robustness:** Style amplification can alter contact rhythm and the center-of-mass trajectory, potentially compromising robot balance.
- **Generation Artifacts:** Diffusion-based motion generation methods, if not constrained, may produce physically implausible artifacts such as foot sliding, ground penetration, and high-frequency joint jitter, making hardware execution unreliable.
- **Tracker Generalization:** A single whole-body tracker must be capable of handling a wide range of different style intensities and motion contents without requiring retraining for each unique generated sequence.
- **Style-Executability Trade-off:** There is an inherent trade-off between the strength of stylization (improving perceptual metrics) and physical executability (maintaining support-foot stability and hardware tracking performance).

To address these motivations and challenges, the research aims to develop a unified "generation-to-control" pipeline that explicitly links human-motion-inspired style transfer with robot execution through physics-aware generation and robust tracking.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology involves a bionic generation-to-control pipeline comprising three main stages: human-motion style generation, conversion to a robot-compatible reference, and whole-body tracking on the robot.

#### 3.1. Pipeline Overview

The pipeline takes a short human style exemplar and a target content motion as input. It generates a stylized whole-body reference that preserves the content while transferring the style. This reference is then converted to a G1-compatible format and executed by a preview-based whole-body tracking policy. The design separates bionic style transfer at the generation stage from physical execution at the tracking stage, connected through physics-aware regularization during generation and a style-diverse tracking policy.

#### 3.2. Physics-Aware Multi-Condition Latent Diffusion

The core of the generation stage is a multi-condition latent diffusion model operating in the latent space of a pre-trained Motion VAE.

- **Conditional Inputs:** The diffusion model is conditioned on three streams:
 - **Content Motion:** Encoded by a Transformer encoder, capturing the intended action and global trajectory. Root translation is removed before encoding.
 - **Style Exemplar:** Encoded by a frozen MotionCLIP encoder into a single embedding vector, providing human-derived expressive cues.
 - **Motion Trajectory:** The root translation sequence from the content motion, encoded by a Transformer-based trajectory encoder, maintaining the global path structure.
- **Architecture and Fusion:**
 - A timestep embedding is added to all condition representations.
 - The content, style, and trajectory conditioned token sequences are concatenated to form an explicit condition context `C`.
 - The denoiser is a Diffusion Transformer block with staged conditioning:
 - The style condition modulates the self-attention branch through adaptive normalization.
 - The concatenated context `C` is used in a cross-attention branch, allowing motion latent tokens to retrieve information.
 - The trajectory condition modulates the feed-forward branch.
- **Training Objective and Controllable Inference:**
 - The model is trained using a mean squared error objective (`L_diff`) to predict Gaussian noise.
 - Classifier-free guidance is implemented by randomly dropping the style condition during training. During inference, a guidance scale `λ` allows adjusting style intensity by combining conditional and unconditional noise predictions without retraining.
- **Physics-Aware Regularization:** To enhance hardware executability, two regularization terms are applied to *decoded joint trajectories* (estimated clean latent motion) during training:
 - **Contact-Consistency Loss (`L_contact`):** Penalizes undesired foot sliding (excessive foot speed during stance) and ground penetration (negative foot height) using hinge penalties. Stance phases are identified from reference foot speed.
 - **Temporal-Smoothness Loss (`L_smooth`):** Suppresses high-frequency joint oscillations by penalizing large first and second-order temporal differences (velocities and accelerations) of predicted joint trajectories compared to reference derivatives using L1 penalties. These losses are applied only during training to bias the model towards generating robot-executable motions.

#### 3.3. Humanoid Control and Deployment

- **Reference Conversion:** Generated human motion references are converted offline into G1-compatible format, involving joint ordering, handling joint limits, and kinematic filtering.
- **Reference-Preview Whole-Body Tracking:** A closed-loop control policy, implemented as a deep reinforcement learning agent, tracks the converted reference.
 - **Observations:** Include proprioceptive data (body orientation, angular velocity, joint positions/velocities, previous action) and a short-horizon future reference (1-second preview, 10 future instants for root state and joint references).
 - **Policy Output:** Target joint positions at 50 Hz, which are then fed to a low-level PD controller running at 1000 Hz on the robot.
- **Style-Diverse Cluster-and-Distill Training:** To handle the diversity of generated stylized motions, the tracker is trained with a specialized strategy:
 - **Training Data:** Includes G1-compatible references converted from human motion datasets (LaFAN1, AMASS) and 1000 stylized trajectories generated by the proposed model.
 - **Clustering:** Motions are grouped into four content-level clusters (straight walking, turning, circular walking, upper-body-emphasized locomotion).
 - **Expert Training:** Separate expert policies are trained for each cluster using a DeepMimic-style reference-tracking reward with domain randomization (e.g., link mass, friction, motor response).
 - **Distillation:** Expert behaviors are distilled into a single deployable generalist policy using Dataset Aggregation (DAgger), removing the need for runtime expert selection.
- **Deployment Protocol:** The generation model operates offline (approx. 0.24 s per action for pure generation), producing references. The tracking policy and low-level PD controller run online on the robot, with the policy updated at 50 Hz.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated the proposed framework across three key aspects: style transfer quality, robot-oriented physical feasibility, and real-robot deployment.

#### 4.1. Human Motion Style Generation Results (Table 2)

The method was compared with animation-oriented baselines (MotionDiffuse, Motion Puzzle, MCM-LDM) using perceptual metrics (Fr´echet Motion Distance - FMD, Content Recognition Accuracy - CRA, Style Recognition Accuracy - SRA) and robot-oriented feasibility metrics (Foot Sliding Factor - FSF, Acceleration `A_pos`, Jerk `J_pos` of joint positions, and Simulated Success Rate - Sim. SR).
- The proposed method achieved an FMD of 39.81, CRA of 35.18%, and SRA of 55.55%. While MCM-LDM exhibited slightly better FMD and SRA for perceptual quality, its robot-oriented metrics were less favorable.
- Crucially, the proposed method demonstrated a more favorable trade-off for robot deployment, yielding significantly lower FSF (0.004722 m/frame), smoother joint references (lower `A_pos` and `J_pos`), and a higher simulated closed-loop success rate (96.02%) compared to all baselines. This indicates that the physics-aware generation reduces artifacts detrimental to robot execution.

#### 4.2. Ablation of Tracking Strategy (Table 3)

Four tracker-training strategies were ablated under the same set of converted stylized references:
- **SP-original (Single Policy - original refs):** Achieved 81.25% Sim. SR.
- **SP-stylized (Single Policy - original + stylized refs):** Improved to 88.48% Sim. SR, indicating the benefit of including stylized data in training.
- **SE-only (Clustered Specialized Experts):** Achieved the highest Sim. SR (94.14%) and lowest tracking error, but requires runtime expert selection.
- **CD-generalist (Cluster-and-Distill Generalist):** Achieved 92.97% Sim. SR with slightly higher tracking error than SE-only, but removed the need for runtime expert selection, making it suitable for deployment. This variant was selected for real-robot deployment.

#### 4.3. Robot-Oriented Physical Feasibility and Generation Ablation (Table 4)

The contribution of the physics-aware regularization terms during generation was evaluated:
- **Without both regularizers:** FSF = 0.005005, A_pos = 0.333533, J_pos = 0.308064.
- **With contact regularization only:** FSF = 0.004825, A_pos = 0.320142, J_pos = 0.294568.
- **With smoothness regularization only:** FSF = 0.004770, A_pos = 0.319252, J_pos = 0.294946.
- **With both regularizers (full model):** Achieved the best values for all metrics (FSF = 0.004722, A_pos = 0.307976, J_pos = 0.283434), demonstrating that both contact consistency and temporal smoothness regularization contribute to generating more robot-executable references.

#### 4.4. Style Intensity Analysis (Table 5 & Fig. 4)

The impact of the classifier-free guidance scale `λ` on style expression and robot executability was investigated:
- Increasing `λ` from 0.0 to 1.0 resulted in higher SRA (from 17.22% to 58.30%), indicating stronger style transfer.
- Concurrently, higher `λ` values led to increased FSF, `A_pos`, and `J_pos`, suggesting a trade-off where stronger stylization can introduce more physical inconsistencies and reduce smoothness.
- Hardware Success Rate (Hardware SR) on the Unitree G1 robot was 100% for `λ` values of 0.0, 0.25, and 0.50. It decreased to 96.0% at `λ` = 0.75 and 84.0% at `λ` = 1.00, demonstrating that excessive style amplification can impact real-robot stability.

#### 4.5. Real-Robot Deployment (Section 5.7)

The framework was deployed on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot.
- Across 125 hardware trials (25 trials for each of five `λ` values), the robot successfully completed 120 trials, resulting in an overall hardware success rate of 96.0%.
- Failures were primarily observed at higher guidance scales where strong stylization led to increased upper-body sway, stance-timing distortion, and tracking difficulty.

#### 4.6. Failure Analysis and Limitations (Section 5.8)

The study identified several limitations:
- **Style Amplification Limits:** Excessive guidance scale `λ` can lead to high-frequency jitter, over-extended postures, and reduced support margin due to exaggerated arm motion or center of mass shifts.
- **Asymmetric Styles:** Asymmetric styles can disturb contact rhythm and cause stance-phase inconsistencies.
- **Content-Style Entanglement:** The MotionCLIP style embedding may not perfectly disentangle expressive cues from content-related motion tendencies (e.g., turning bias), potentially leading to residual biases.
- **Environmental Scope:** Real-robot validation was primarily on flat indoor ground, and the framework does not yet model complex terrain, external contacts, or object interaction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of humanoid robotics by bridging a notable gap between advanced motion style transfer techniques from computer animation and the rigorous demands of physical robot execution. Its significance and potential impact are multifold:

- **Enabling Expressive Humanoid Robots:** The framework provides a method for humanoid robots to generate and execute expressive, human-like motions. This is crucial for robots operating in social and service environments, where conveying nonverbal cues, affect, and intent through body language can enhance human-robot interaction, legibility, and acceptance.
- **Reusable Bionic Style Sources:** The work demonstrates the feasibility of using short human motion exemplars as a compact and reusable "bionic source" for diverse robot behaviors. This moves beyond reliance on fixed demonstrations or laborious manual scripting, offering a more flexible and scalable approach to developing a robot's behavioral repertoire.
- **Physically Executable Motion Generation:** By integrating physics-aware regularization directly into the latent diffusion model, the research addresses the critical challenge of ensuring that generated motions are not just visually plausible but also physically executable. This reduces common artifacts like foot sliding and joint jitter that typically hinder the deployment of animation-derived motions on real hardware.
- **Robust and Adaptive Whole-Body Control:** The development of a style-diverse, cluster-and-distill training strategy for the whole-body tracker enhances its robustness. This allows the robot to adapt to a wide range of generated stylized motions without requiring a separate controller for each style, simplifying deployment and increasing versatility.
- **Practical Deployment on Hardware:** The demonstration of a 96.0% success rate across 125 real-robot trials on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot provides strong empirical evidence of the method's practical viability. This validation on physical hardware, rather than solely in simulation, underscores the framework's relevance for real-world robotic applications.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The identified limitations, such as the trade-off between style intensity and physical robustness, content-style entanglement, and environmental constraints, suggest clear avenues for future research. This includes extending the framework to handle more complex terrains, external interactions, and broader style-content combinations, further advancing the capabilities of expressive humanoid control.

In summary, this work represents a step towards making humanoid robots more capable communicators through dynamic and expressive whole-body movements, effectively translating abstract human motion styles into physically grounded and executable robot behaviors.
