# An Open and Comprehensive Pipeline for Unified Object Grounding and Detection

> [!summary] Summary
> MM-Grounding-DINO, an open-source pipeline from Shanghai AI Lab and SenseTime Research, reconstructs and extends Grounding-DINO within the MMDetection framework, providing a unified solution for object grounding and detection tasks. The system leverages extensive public datasets and achieves improved performance, notably a +12.6 AP gain on LVIS MiniVal and strong transferability across diverse downstream applications.

## Problem

- The state-of-the-art Grounding-DINO model lacked publicly available training code and comprehensive technical details, hindering reproducibility and further research.
- The need for a unified and open-ended approach to object understanding to handle open-vocabulary detection, phrase grounding, and referring expression comprehension tasks.
- A systematic, unified evaluation framework was needed to assess model performance across a wide array of benchmarks for OVD, PG, and REC tasks.

## Method

- Developed **MM-Grounding-DINO**, an open-source and comprehensive pipeline, by rebuilding ==Grounding-DINO== within the ==MMDetection== toolbox with detailed settings and released models.
- Utilized an extensive array of 15 public datasets, including GRIT and V3Det as substitutes for non-open-source data, to enhance model performance and broad applicability.
- Introduced a minor architectural modification (adding a bias during initialization of the ==contrastive embedding module==) to improve training stability and accelerate convergence.

## Results

- MM-Grounding-DINO variants consistently surpassed original Grounding-DINO performance, with MM-G-T(c3) achieving **+2.1 mAP** on COCO zero-shot and a significant **+12.6 AP** on LVIS MiniVal.
- Demonstrated robust zero-shot transferability on real-world datasets, scoring **53.3 mAP** on ODinW13 (**+1.9** over G-DINO-T(c)) and **28.4 mAP** on ODinW35 (**+5.7** over G-DINO-T(c)).
- Achieved new benchmarks or substantial improvements in downstream tasks after fine-tuning, such as **69.1 AP** for object detection in haze and **65.5 mAP** for underwater object detection (**+35.7** from zero-shot).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Providing an open-source, comprehensive pipeline significantly democratizes state-of-the-art multi-modal vision technology, fostering reproducibility and extensibility for the research community.
> - Careful selection and integration of large-scale public datasets, particularly those with vast vocabularies like V3Det, are critical for improving zero-shot transferability and performance on open-vocabulary benchmarks.
> - The MM-Grounding-DINO framework exhibits robust generalizability and effectively adapts to diverse real-world downstream tasks through fine-tuning, underscoring its practical utility as a foundation model.

## Detailed Report

### 1. An Open and Comprehensive Pipeline for Unified Object Grounding and Detection

#### 1.1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields in computer vision: **Open-Vocabulary Detection (OVD)**, **Phrase Grounding (PG)**, and **Referring Expression Comprehension (REC)**. Traditionally, object detection models operate in a "closed-set" manner, meaning they can only detect objects belonging to categories seen during training. However, the goal of modern vision systems is to understand and interact with the world in a more human-like, open-ended fashion.

- **Open-Vocabulary Detection (OVD)** aims to detect objects from novel categories not encountered during training, often by leveraging large-scale language models to understand descriptions of new objects.
- **Phrase Grounding (PG)** involves localizing specific phrases or entities described in a text within an image.
- **Referring Expression Comprehension (REC)** is a more precise version of PG, where the goal is to identify a *unique* target object specified by a natural language description, even in the presence of similar distractors.

The emergence of powerful vision-language models, such as CLIP and GLIP, has significantly pushed the boundaries of these tasks by enabling deep integration between visual and textual modalities. **Grounding-DINO** (on which this paper's work is based) represents a state-of-the-art advancement in this domain. It combines the strengths of DINO (a DETR-based end-to-end object detector) with grounded pre-training, achieving superior performance in zero-shot OVD, PG, and REC. Grounding-DINO's success has made it a "mainstream architecture" for various downstream applications due to its strong multi-modal alignment capabilities.

However, a critical challenge in the research community has been the **lack of public training code and comprehensive technical details** for the original Grounding-DINO model. This gap hinders reproducibility, systematic analysis, and further advancements. This paper directly addresses this issue by providing an open-source, comprehensive, and user-friendly pipeline, **MM-Grounding-DINO**, built on the widely adopted MMDetection framework. By doing so, it democratizes access to state-of-the-art vision grounding technology, enabling broader adoption, deeper understanding, and accelerated future research. This contribution aligns with the growing trend of open science and the development of robust, community-driven AI toolkits, similar to the impact of other OpenMMLab projects.

#### 1.2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation for this research stems from a significant limitation in the field: despite Grounding-DINO's status as a state-of-the-art model for unified object grounding and detection tasks (OVD, PG, REC), its **training code and comprehensive technical details were not publicly available**. This "unavailability" created a barrier for researchers wishing to reproduce its results, adapt it for specific applications, or build upon its architecture.

Against this backdrop, the authors establish the following key objectives:

1. **Develop an Open-Source and Comprehensive Pipeline:** The primary goal is to rebuild Grounding-DINO as "MM-Grounding-DINO" within the MMDetection toolbox, making it entirely open-source, user-friendly, and accessible to the research community. This includes providing detailed settings for reproduction and releasing all trained models.
2. **Enhance Model Performance through Extensive Data:** To surpass the original Grounding-DINO, the authors aim to leverage a broader and more diverse set of vision and grounding datasets for pre-training and fine-tuning. This includes substituting non-open-source datasets (like Cap4M) used by Grounding-DINO with publicly available alternatives (GRIT, V3Det) and incorporating additional large-scale datasets (e.g., COCO, Objects365, RefCOCO/+/g, GQA, Flickr30k Entities).
3. **Establish a Unified and Extensive Evaluation Framework:** The paper seeks to provide the first framework that facilitates systematic evaluation across an extensive array of benchmarks for OVD, PG, and REC. This includes integrating evaluation metrics readily available in MMDetection and extending testing to a wide range of datasets like COCO, LVIS, RefCOCO/+/g, Flickr30k Entities, ODinW13/35, gRefCOCO, and D3.
4. **Demonstrate Robust Transferability and Generalizability:** A crucial objective is to extensively evaluate the model's ability to transfer its learned knowledge to novel, unseen categories and diverse downstream tasks through various fine-tuning strategies.

In essence, the project aims not just to replicate Grounding-DINO, but to improve upon it, make it fully transparent and reproducible, and provide a holistic framework for unified object grounding and detection that fosters further research and development.

#### 1.3. Methodology and Approach

The MM-Grounding-DINO project is built upon the foundational architecture of Grounding-DINO but introduces significant enhancements in data utilization and a minor architectural tweak for improved training stability.

##### 1.3.1. Model Architecture

The core architecture of MM-Grounding-DINO largely **remains unchanged** from the original Grounding-DINO, emphasizing its status as an open-source replication and enhancement. The framework is an end-to-end multi-modal understanding architecture designed to align textual descriptions with corresponding bounding boxes. Its main components include:

- **Text Backbone:** A well-pretrained BERT-based-uncased model for extracting textual features.
- **Image Backbone:** A Swin Transformer for extracting multi-scale image features.
- **Feature Enhancer Module:** This crucial module deeply fuses image and text features through a Bi-Attention Block (containing both text-to-image and image-to-text cross-attention layers). Subsequently, self-attention and deformable self-attention layers, followed by FFN layers, further enhance the fused text and image features, respectively.
- **Language-Guided Query Selection Module:** This module selects a fixed number (900, following DINO) of proposals based on cosine similarity with input text features to serve as decoder queries, initialized with dynamic anchor boxes.
- **Cross-modality Decoder:** Extending the DINO decoder, each layer of the cross-modality decoder incorporates an additional text cross-attention layer to inject textual information into the queries, refining object box predictions and associated phrases.
- **Training Loss:** The model uses L1 loss and GIOU loss for box regression. For classification, focal loss is employed as a contrastive loss between predicted boxes and language tokens, consistent with GLIP. Auxiliary loss is also incorporated for each decoder layer and encoder outputs.

##### 1.3.2. Key Difference and Improvement

A notable difference introduced by MM-Grounding-DINO is a minor but effective modification in the **contrastive embedding module**. Inspired by CLIP, the authors **add a bias during the initialization** of this module. This seemingly small change is shown to significantly reduce the initial loss value and accelerate the convergence of the model during training.

##### 1.3.3. Dataset Preparation

The project employs an extensive array of 15 datasets, meticulously categorized into three groups based on the tasks they support:

- **OVD Datasets (Training):** COCO, Objects365V1/V2, V3Det, Open-Images.
- **PG Datasets (Training):** GQA, GRIT, Flickr30K Entities. GRIT and V3Det are explicitly used as substitutes for the non-open-source Cap4M dataset used by original Grounding-DINO.
- **REC Datasets (Training):** RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks (OVD):** COCO, LVIS, ODinW13/35.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks (PG):** Flickr30K Entities.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks (REC):** RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg, gRefCOCO, Description Detection Dataset (D3).

The data format is motivated by Open Grounding-DINO and adapted for MMDetection, ensuring compatibility and ease of use within the framework. Notably, GRIT, while exceeding 13 million instances, is partially utilized per epoch (500,000 segments) due to its scale.

##### 1.3.4. Training Settings

- **Input Rules of Text:** For OVD, categories are concatenated into a single string (e.g., "People. Ball. Racket. Cat."). For PG and REC, following M-DETR, every referred object within the text is annotated during pre-training, enabling the model to predict bounding boxes for all specified entities.
- **Model Variants:** Similar to Grounding-DINO, the paper explores MM-G-Tiny and MM-G-Large variants, differing in the scale of the image backbone and the combination of pre-training datasets (Table 1 lists the specific dataset combinations for various MM-G-Tiny versions).
- **Data Augmentation:** Standard augmentations (random resize, clip, flip) are complemented by **random negative sampling**. This involves concatenating randomly sampled categories or descriptions from other images as negative examples alongside ground-truth positive descriptions. This strategy is crucial for suppressing "hallucination" by teaching the model not to predict objects absent from the image.
- **Computing Resources:** Training for MM-G-Tiny variants was conducted on 32 NVIDIA 3090 GPUs with a batch size of 128 for 30 epochs, highlighting the substantial computational resources required.

#### 1.4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted with MM-Grounding-DINO across various benchmarks demonstrate its superior performance and broad applicability compared to the original Grounding-DINO, particularly in zero-shot transfer and fine-tuning scenarios.

##### 1.4.1. Zero-shot Transfer Performance

- **COCO Benchmark (OVD):** MM-G-Tiny variants consistently outperform Grounding-DINO-Tiny. Notably, MM-G-T(a) (trained only on O365) achieves 48.5 mAP, surpassing G-DINO-T(c) (trained on O365, GoldG, and Cap4M) at 48.4 mAP. MM-G-T(c1) further improves this to 50.5 mAP, an increase of 2.1 mAP over G-DINO-T(c). This is attributed to the improved training strategy (additional bias) and the comprehensive coverage of COCO categories within the O365 dataset. V3Det, however, did not positively contribute to COCO zero-shot performance.
- **LVIS Benchmark (OVD):** LVIS, a long-tail detection dataset with over 1000 categories, shows even more substantial gains. MM-G-T(b) surpasses G-DINO-T(c) by +6.9 AP on LVIS MiniVal and Val. MM-G-T(c3) (incorporating V3Det) achieves a remarkable 41.4 mAP on MiniVal and 31.9 mAP on Val, a significant improvement of +12.6 AP and +11.8 AP, respectively, over G-DINO-T(c). This is attributed to MM-G's more comprehensive training on LVIS vocabulary, especially with V3Det's broad category coverage (13k categories).
- **ODinW Benchmark (OVD):** On this rigorous benchmark assessing real-world transferability, MM-G-T(c3) demonstrates robust performance, scoring 53.3 mAP on ODinW13 (+1.9 over G-DINO-T(c)) and 28.4 mAP on ODinW35 (+5.7 over G-DINO-T(c)). The inclusion of V3Det, with its wide vocabulary, proved particularly beneficial for these diverse datasets.
- **RefCOCO/+/g and gRefCOCO Benchmarks (REC):** MM-G models either surpass or are on par with G-DINO across various REC metrics. MM-G-T(b) and MM-G-T(c1/c3) show improved accuracy on RefCOCO/+/g. For gRefCOCO, MM-G-T(c3) achieves comparable or slightly better performance. V3Det does not provide benefit for REC tasks.
- **Description Detection Dataset (D3) Benchmark (REC/OVD):** MM-G-T(c1) (with GRIT) shows comparable performance to G-DINO-T(c) (with Cap4M), demonstrating advancements in handling long sentences, while G-DINO-T(c) performs better on short sentences. MM-G-T(c3) (with V3Det) improves performance on short sentences but worsens on long sentences, reflecting V3Det's prevalence of short text annotations.

##### 1.4.2. Analysis of Key Datasets (GRIT and V3Det)

- **GRIT:** While intended as a substitute for Cap4M, GRIT's performance did not consistently meet expectations for OVD/REC. The authors identify several reasons: (1) Text annotations from spaCy sometimes contain abstract phrases, names, or events, potentially misdirecting the model. (2) Many GRIT images have single, long caption annotations with noisy bounding boxes spanning the full image, which are less precise for grounding tasks. However, GRIT still proves beneficial for ODinW (due to its large scale and additional concepts) and for enhancing performance on long sentences in D3.
- **V3Det:** The incorporation of V3Det significantly boosts performance on LVIS and ODinW due to its vast vocabulary (13k categories), which likely covers a large portion of these benchmarks' novel categories. However, its annotations' focus on short sentences limits its positive impact on tasks requiring an understanding of longer, more complex descriptions.

##### 1.4.3. Validation through Fine-tuning

MM-Grounding-DINO shows excellent adaptability and further performance gains through fine-tuning:
- **COCO and LVIS Fine-tuning:** Both close-set and open-set fine-tuning strategies yield substantial improvements. MM-G-T(c3) achieves 58.2 mAP (+7.8) on COCO after close-set fine-tuning. For LVIS, open-set fine-tuning leads to significant gains, e.g., +17.3 AP on MiniVal. Open-vocabulary fine-tuning also preserves generalizability while improving performance on novel categories.
- **REC Fine-tuning (RefCOCO/+/g):** Fine-tuning for just 5 epochs drastically improves REC performance, suggesting that the current evaluation metrics for these datasets might be "overly simplistic" and that phrase grounding adaptations still lead to significant enhancements.
- **Fine-tuning on Downstream Tasks:** MM-Grounding-DINO demonstrates remarkable generalizability to diverse real-world applications:
 - **Object Detection in Haze (RTTS):** Achieves 69.1 AP (+a large margin) after 12 epochs of fine-tuning, setting a new benchmark.
 - **Object Detection Underwater (RUOD):** Improves from 29.8 mAP (zero-shot) to 65.5 mAP (+35.7) after 12 epochs of fine-tuning, surpassing previous SOTA by 8.1 mAP.
 - **Object Detection for Cityscapes:** Performs on par with fine-tuned models in zero-shot and reaches 51.5 mAP (+17.3) after 50 epochs of fine-tuning, setting a new SOTA.
 - **Object Detection for People in Painting:** Outperforms fine-tuned models in zero-shot and achieves 38.9 mAP (+15.8) after 50 epochs, establishing a new benchmark.
 - **Brain Tumor Detection:** Underperforms compared to Cascade-DINO, hypothesized to be due to purely numerical labels and lack of descriptive textual context in the dataset, which MM-G relies on.

##### 1.4.4. Observations and Limitations

- **Ground Truth Inaccuracies:** The authors note instances of inaccurate ground-truth annotations in evaluation datasets, where their model's predictions appear more precise.
- **Model Limitations:** The model sometimes struggles with:
 - Interpreting relational terms (e.g., "without" in "horseman without helmet").
 - Complex descriptions (e.g., "railings being crossed by horse").
 - Position-based descriptions (confusing left/right objects).
 - The phrase grounding settings can lead to "over-prediction" of objects if they match a phrase, even if not the primary target.

#### 1.5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MM-Grounding-DINO project carries significant implications and potential impact across the computer vision research community and practical applications:

1. **Democratization of State-of-the-Art (SOTA) Technology:** The most immediate and profound impact is bridging the gap created by the original Grounding-DINO's closed-source training code. By providing a comprehensive, open-source, and user-friendly pipeline within the MMDetection framework, MM-Grounding-DINO makes SOTA object grounding and detection accessible to a much broader research and development community. This transparency fosters greater understanding, faster iteration, and wider adoption.
2. **Enhanced Reproducibility and Extensibility:** The detailed settings, released code, and trained models ensure that researchers can easily reproduce the reported results. Building on MMDetection, a well-established and modular toolbox, also ensures high extensibility, allowing researchers to readily integrate new modules, backbones, datasets, or tasks, thereby accelerating future research directions.
3. **New Baselines and Benchmarks:** MM-Grounding-DINO sets new state-of-the-art or highly competitive baselines across a wide array of open-vocabulary, phrase grounding, and referring expression comprehension benchmarks (COCO, LVIS, ODinW, RefCOCO/+/g, D3). This comprehensive evaluation across an unprecedented number of datasets provides a robust new standard for comparison for future models.
4. **Unified Framework for Multi-modal Vision Tasks:** The project solidifies the concept of a single, unified framework capable of addressing OVD, PG, and REC tasks simultaneously. This unified approach simplifies model development and deployment for diverse vision-language understanding applications.
5. **Robust Transferability to Real-world Scenarios:** The strong performance demonstrated in fine-tuning experiments across various challenging downstream tasks—including object detection in hazy conditions, underwater environments, cityscapes, and even artistic paintings—underscores the model's remarkable generalizability and practical utility. This indicates that MM-Grounding-DINO can serve as a powerful foundation model for specialized industrial and scientific applications.
6. **Catalyst for Future Research:** By openly sharing the pipeline, the authors explicitly aim for it to be a "valuable resource for further investigations." This encourages detailed analysis of model behaviors (e.g., understanding limitations in handling relational terms or complex descriptions), exploration of new datasets, and development of novel architectural improvements for multi-modal grounding tasks. The identified shortcomings of datasets like GRIT and the critique of current REC evaluation metrics also point towards fruitful avenues for future dataset curation and metric development.

In conclusion, MM-Grounding-DINO is more than just an improved model; it is a critical open-science contribution that empowers the community with advanced tools and knowledge, setting a new precedent for research in unified object grounding and detection.
