# PIN-WM: Learning Physics-INformed World Models for Non-Prehensile Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper introduces PIN-WM, a framework that learns a physics-informed world model directly from visual observations to enable robust Sim2Real transfer for non-prehensile manipulation. The approach integrates differentiable physics and rendering to accurately identify physical properties and object appearance, achieving high success rates of 75% in real-world push and 65% in flip tasks with a physical robot.

## Problem

- Non-prehensile robotic manipulation (e.g., pushing, flipping) is challenging due to complex and highly sensitive physical interactions, making it difficult to learn effective policies.
- The 'Sim2Real gap' hinders the deployment of policies trained purely in simulation to the real world, especially for contact-rich tasks where discrepancies in dynamics and visual appearance cause failures.
- Current robot learning methods, including imitation learning and purely data-driven world models, require extensive and costly real-world data for training, limiting their practical applicability.

## Method

- PIN-WM proposes a two-stage Real2Sim2Real pipeline: Real2Sim system identification for a physics-informed world model and Sim2Real policy transfer using physics-aware digital cousins.
- The physics-informed world model learns 3D rigid body dynamics and object appearance end-to-end from few-shot visual observations by optimizing differentiable physics (LCP solver) and differentiable rendering (2D Gaussian Splatting).
- To bridge the residual Sim2Real gap, 'physics-aware digital cousins' are generated by perturbing the identified physics and rendering parameters within a small, targeted range, upon which the manipulation policy is trained via reinforcement learning.

## Results

- PIN-WM achieved 97% success in push and 83% in flip tasks in simulation, significantly outperforming purely data-driven, pre-defined physics, and other learned physics baselines.
- The method demonstrated strong zero-shot Sim2Real transfer, achieving 75% success in real-world push and 65% in real-world flip tasks with a physical robot using only a single, task-agnostic real-world trajectory for system identification.
- PIN-WM showed superior system identification accuracy with the lowest one-step prediction errors for object translation and orientation, and identified physics parameters (friction, mass, restitution) were closer to ground truth compared to other methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating differentiable physics (via LCP solver) and differentiable rendering (2D Gaussian Splatting) allows for end-to-end identification of 3D rigid body dynamics and appearance directly from visual observations, without explicit state estimation.
> - Targeted 'physics-aware' domain randomization, by perturbing identified real-world parameters within a small range, is more effective for robust Sim2Real transfer than broad, untargeted randomization, reducing the policy learning burden.
> - Combining structured physics priors with data-driven visual learning enables highly data-efficient and generalizable world models for complex contact-rich tasks like non-prehensile manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses critical challenges in robotic non-prehensile manipulation, a foundational skill involving pushing or poking objects without grasping them. This domain is inherently difficult due to complex physical interactions (friction, restitution, inertia) and the high sensitivity of object motion to contact. The work positions itself within the broader landscape of robot learning, specifically focusing on Sim2Real transfer for complex physical tasks.

Traditionally, non-prehensile manipulation has been tackled through:
- **Analytical Models:** Relying on simplified physics assumptions (e.g., known parameters, 2D models), which often fail in real-world complexity.
- **Imitation Learning (IL):** Mimicking expert demonstrations. While effective, IL is data-intensive, requiring costly real-world expert trajectories (e.g., 50 demonstrations per environment-object pair) and struggling with generalization to out-of-distribution scenarios or state-action distribution shifts.
- **Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL):** Learning policies through trial-and-error in simulations. This approach is data-efficient in simulation but faces a significant "Sim2Real gap"—the discrepancy between simulated and real-world dynamics and visuals, which hinders direct policy transfer.

To bridge this Sim2Real gap, **world models** have emerged as a promising alternative. World models learn the environment dynamics, allowing for policy training in a learned, data-driven simulation. However, purely data-driven world models suffer from limitations: they are highly data-dependent, struggle to generalize to novel situations, and lack robustness to out-of-distribution inputs.

This paper's core contribution lies in integrating **physics priors** into world models. While previous efforts have attempted this, they often fall short:
- **Gradient-free optimization:** Methods like ASID identify physics parameters but are inefficient and prone to local optima due to the lack of gradient feedback.
- **Simplified physics models:** Approaches using 2D physics simulators cannot capture the full 3D dynamics essential for complex non-prehensile tasks like flipping objects.
- **Reliance on state estimation:** Most existing methods require an explicit state estimation module (e.g., pose estimation) to bridge observations to physics, adding complexity and potential error sources.

PIN-WM differentiates itself by proposing an **end-to-end differentiable framework** that learns 3D rigid body dynamics directly from visual observations, circumventing the need for explicit state estimation by using differentiable rendering. Furthermore, it addresses the residual Sim2Real gap (due to inaccurate/partial observations) through a novel **physics-aware domain randomization** strategy, moving beyond naive randomization that can increase learning burden. By focusing on efficient, accurate system identification and targeted randomization, PIN-WM aims to enable robust Sim2Real transfer with minimal real-world data, pushing the boundaries of deployable robotic manipulation skills.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and efficient method for learning non-prehensile manipulation skills that can transfer seamlessly from simulation to the real world. This goal is driven by several key motivations:

- **Addressing the Sim2Real Gap for Non-Prehensile Manipulation:** Non-prehensile tasks involve highly sensitive and complex physical interactions, making existing Sim2Real transfer methods challenging. Traditional DRL policies learned purely in simulation often fail when deployed in reality due to discrepancies in dynamics and visual appearance.
- **Improving Data Efficiency:** Current robot learning approaches, especially imitation learning and purely data-driven world models, require large quantities of expensive real-world data (either expert demonstrations or diverse interaction trajectories). The authors aim to enable robust policy learning with only "few-shot" and "task-agnostic" real-world interaction data.
- **Enhancing Generalization and Robustness:** Purely data-driven world models struggle with out-of-distribution scenarios. Incorporating structured physics priors is motivated by its known ability to improve generalization with limited data. The authors seek to learn a world model that accurately captures underlying physical laws for better prediction and control.
- **Enabling End-to-End Learning from Visuals:** Many physics-informed methods rely on explicit state estimation (e.g., object pose), introducing potential error sources and complexity. A key motivation is to enable end-to-end identification of physical parameters directly from raw visual observations, leveraging advances in differentiable rendering to minimize reliance on intermediate modules.
- **Developing Effective Domain Randomization:** While domain randomization is common for Sim2Real, naive randomization often covers an excessively large and noisy parameter space, increasing learning difficulty and potentially yielding sub-optimal policies. The motivation is to develop a "physics-aware" randomization strategy that creates meaningful variations around identified real-world parameters, thus reducing the learning burden and improving transfer success.

In essence, the paper aims to create a highly accurate, data-efficient, and generalizable "digital twin" of a rigid body system from visual data, which can then be intelligently randomized into "digital cousins" to train robust manipulation policies with strong zero-shot Sim2Real transfer capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PIN-WM proposes a two-stage Real2Sim2Real pipeline: (1) Real2Sim system identification to construct a physics-informed world model (digital twin), and (2) Sim2Real policy transfer enhanced by physics-aware digital cousins.

**A. Real2Sim System Identification: Physics-INformed World Model (PIN-WM)**

The world model `W` predicts the next observation `o_{t+1}` from current observation `o_t` and action `a_t`. PIN-WM models `W` as a composition of a differentiable physics function `g` and a differentiable rendering function `I`, such that `I_{t+1} = I(g(x_t, a_t, θ), α)`. Here, `g` (parameterized by physics properties `θ`) predicts the next state `x_{t+1}`, and `I` (parameterized by rendering properties `α`) generates the corresponding image `I_{t+1}`. The goal is to optimize both `α` and `θ` to minimize discrepancy with real-world observations.

1. **Differentiable Rendering (Rendering Alignment):**
 - **Goal:** Learn rendering parameters `α` that accurately represent the target object visually from multiple viewpoints.
 - **Approach:** The robot captures multi-view static images of the object in its initial state.
 - **Representation:** 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) is chosen as the differentiable rendering function. 2DGS represents the object surface using a set of 2D Gaussian elliptical disks defined in local tangent planes. This representation is differentiable and effective for capturing surface details.
 - **Optimization:** `α` (including 2DGS parameters like color, opacity, scaling, rotation) is optimized by minimizing a combined rendering loss `L = L_c + ω_d L_d + ω_n L_n`, where `L_c` is an image reconstruction loss (L2 + D-SSIM) against real images, and `L_d`, `L_n` are regularization terms for depth distortion and normal consistency.
 - **Pose Transformation:** Once `α` is optimized for the initial object pose, subsequent changes in the object's 3D pose (predicted by the physics model) can be applied as a transformation to the 2DGS parameters, enabling consistent rendering of the object in motion.

2. **Differentiable Physics (Identification of Physics Parameters):**
 - **Goal:** Estimate `θ` (mass, inertia, friction, restitution) for a 3D rigid body dynamical system, using the optimized `α*` from the rendering alignment stage.
 - **Approach:** The robot performs few-shot, task-agnostic physical interactions (e.g., random pushes) with the object in the real world, collecting a dynamic video.
 - **Dynamics Formulation:** The physics model `g` is formulated as a velocity-based Linear Complementarity Problem (LCP). This LCP describes rigid body dynamics under global constraints (joint, contact, friction) and relates initial state `x_t` and action `a_t` to the next twist velocities `ξ_{t+1}`. The action `a_t` is the translation of the robot end-effector, which is converted to an equivalent velocity.
 - **State Update:** The updated twist velocities `ξ_{t+1}` are then integrated using semi-implicit Euler to derive the new position `p_{t+1}` and orientation `q_{t+1}` of the object.
 - **Optimization:** Physics parameters `θ` are estimated by minimizing the rendering loss between the images generated by the differentiable physics + rendering model (`I(g(x_{t+i-1}, a_{t+i-1}, θ), α*)`) and the real-world observed images (`I_{d_{t+i}}`). Crucially, the LCP solver uses a primal-dual interior point method to allow gradient propagation from the observed image pixels back through the physics simulation to `θ`. The gradients are calculated recursively over simulation steps.

**B. Sim2Real Policy Transfer: Physics-aware Digital Cousins (PADC)**

Even with accurate system identification, minor discrepancies between the learned world model (digital twin) and reality may persist due to unmodeled factors or partial observations. To bridge this "residual" Sim2Real gap, PIN-WM employs PADC:

- **Concept:** Instead of uniformly randomizing parameters over a wide range (standard domain randomization), PADC perturbs the *identified* rendering (`α*`) and physics (`θ*`) parameters within a small, physics-aware range around their learned mean values.
- **Randomization:** Specifically, the spherical harmonics (SH) parameters (a subset of `α*` representing directional rendering components like lighting/materials) and `θ*` are sampled from a uniform distribution `U(ω_r · (1 - δ), ω_r · (1 + δ))`, where `ω_r` are the identified parameters and `δ` (e.g., 0.1) is the perturbation magnitude.
- **Policy Training:** An ensemble of these physics-aware digital cousins is generated. A reinforcement learning algorithm (e.g., PPO) is then used to train the manipulation policy within this diverse yet physically consistent simulated environment.
- **Deployment:** The learned policy is then directly deployed to the real world without any further fine-tuning. This approach ensures that the policy is robust to minor variations that exist in the real world but are not perfectly captured by the single "digital twin".

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents extensive evaluations in both simulation and real-world settings to validate PIN-WM's performance for rigid body non-prehensile manipulation, specifically for "push" (2D translation/1D rotation) and "flip" (3D rotation/translation) tasks.

**A. Evaluations in Simulation:**

- **Experimental Setup:** A single task-agnostic trajectory (robot pushing object in a straight line) is collected in the target domain. Policies are trained using RGB images as input in the learned simulator (Bullet engine + PIN-WM's physics/rendering) with 32 parallel threads, each running an independent physics-aware digital cousin.
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Policy Performance:** Success rate (Succ %) within a fixed number of steps, and average number of steps (#Steps) to complete the task.
 - **System Identification Accuracy:** One-step error (translation and rotation differences) between predicted and ground-truth object states after one action.
- **Baselines:**
 - Purely data-driven: Dreamer V2 (world model), Diffusion Policy (imitation learning).
 - Pre-defined physics-based: Domain Randomization + I (fixed, broad randomization + PIN-WM's renderer), RoboGSim (3DGS rendering optimization, fixed physics).
 - Learned physics-based: ASID (gradient-free physics ID + PIN-WM's renderer), 2D Physics (differentiable 2D physics + PIN-WM's renderer).
- **Key Findings (Policy Performance - Table I):**
 - **Superiority of PIN-WM:** PIN-WM with PADC significantly outperforms all baselines across both tasks, achieving 97% success in push (30.1 steps) and 83% in flip (11.4 steps).
 - **Impact of PADC:** PIN-WM without PADC still performs very well (92% push, 70% flip) but shows a clear performance drop compared to using PADC, highlighting the importance of physics-aware randomization for robust transfer.
 - **Limitations of Data-Driven Methods:** Dreamer V2 and Diffusion Policy perform poorly (≤13% success), indicating that even with more data (100 trajectories), purely data-driven models struggle to capture complex dynamics or generalize effectively for real-world tasks.
 - **Limitations of Pre-defined Physics Methods:** Domain Randomization + I (33% push, 32% flip) struggles due to excessive randomness. RoboGSim (19% push, 21% flip) performs poorly as it only optimizes rendering, not physics.
 - **Limitations of Other Learned Physics Methods:** ASID (58% push, 11% flip) and 2D Physics (55% push, 8% flip) show inferior performance due to inefficient gradient-free optimization or limitations of 2D physics models for 3D tasks.
- **Key Findings (System Identification Accuracy - Table II & Figure 4):**
 - **Highest Accuracy:** PIN-WM achieves the lowest one-step errors in both translation and orientation for both tasks, demonstrating superior accuracy in identifying target domain dynamics.
 - **Learning Efficiency:** The learning curves (Figure 4) show PIN-WM's stability and efficiency in converging to low prediction errors during training.
 - **Identified Parameters (Appendix B, Table V):** PIN-WM's identified friction, mass, and restitution values are notably closer to the ground truth compared to ASID and 2D Physics.
 - **Robustness to Geometry Noise (Appendix B, Table VI):** PIN-WM remains robust even with significant geometry noise, outperforming ASID with perfect geometry, indicating its practical applicability where perfect geometry might not be available.

**B. Evaluations in Real-World:**

- **Experimental Setup:** Uses a physical robot with an eye-in-hand and eye-to-hand camera. Initial object pose estimated with FoundationPose. Single task-agnostic push trajectory for physics identification. Policies trained in simulation (with PIN-WM) and deployed to real robot without fine-tuning. Tasks include pushing a T-shaped object and flipping a mug.
- **Baselines:** Only methods requiring no or few-shot real-world data (excluding data-driven methods that need extensive real-world trajectories).
- **Key Findings (Policy Performance - Table III, Figures 6-11):**
 - **Strong Real-World Transfer:** PIN-WM with PADC achieves 75% success in push and 65% in flip in real-world tests, significantly outperforming all baselines. This demonstrates effective zero-shot Sim2Real transfer.
 - **Visual Validation:** Real-world trajectories (Figures 6, 7, 8) qualitatively show PIN-WM's policies executing tasks efficiently and accurately, unlike baselines that often fail or require longer trajectories.
 - **Challenging Scenarios (Appendix C):** PIN-WM maintains strong performance on slippery surfaces and with more challenging cube objects, reinforcing its robustness and adaptability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PIN-WM framework makes significant contributions to the fields of robotics, machine learning, and computer vision, with substantial potential impact:

- **Breakthrough in Sim2Real Transfer for Non-Prehensile Manipulation:** By enabling accurate, end-to-end identification of 3D rigid body dynamics from visual observations and leveraging physics-aware digital cousins, PIN-WM significantly reduces the Sim2Real gap for notoriously difficult non-prehensile tasks. This is crucial for deploying robotic solutions in unstructured, real-world environments where grasping is impractical or impossible.
- **High Data Efficiency:** The method achieves robust policy learning with "few-shot" and "task-agnostic" real-world interaction data. This addresses a major bottleneck in robotic learning, where collecting large, diverse datasets is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming. This makes robot deployment more feasible for a wider range of applications.
- **Novel Integration of Differentiable Physics and Rendering:** PIN-WM's end-to-end learning from raw pixels to physical parameters, without intermediate state estimation, is a novel and powerful approach. The combination of differentiable 3D rigid body dynamics (via LCP) and differentiable 2D Gaussian Splatting for scene representation provides a robust and learnable pipeline for complex physical interactions.
- **Intelligent Domain Randomization:** The concept of "physics-aware digital cousins" is a significant refinement of traditional domain randomization. By perturbing parameters around identified real-world values, it creates a more meaningful and effective distribution of training environments, leading to more robust and less conservative policies. This reduces the learning burden compared to broad, unconstrained randomization.
- **Broad Applicability:** While demonstrated on non-prehensile manipulation, the underlying principles of differentiable physics-informed world modeling are generalizable. This work could inspire similar approaches for other complex robot-environment interactions, especially those involving contact-rich tasks or unknown physical properties.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** PIN-WM lays a strong foundation for future work, including extending to deformable objects (e.g., using differentiable MPM), incorporating differentiable relighting for improved visual alignment under varying lighting conditions, and applying it to industrial automation settings for tasks like assembly or part feeding.

**Limitations:** The paper acknowledges limitations such as sensitivity to shadows and lighting conditions affecting rendering loss (suggesting differentiable relighting as a solution) and the current focus on rigid-body dynamics, which restricts its application to scenarios involving deformable objects. Additionally, the assumption of known geometry could be relaxed in future work.

Despite these limitations, PIN-WM represents a significant step forward in making robots more capable and adaptable in manipulating objects in real-world scenarios, marking progress towards more intelligent and autonomous robotic systems.
