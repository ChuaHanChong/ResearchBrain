# Qwen-AgentWorld: Language World Models for General Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> Qwen-AgentWorld introduces a family of language world models (LWMs) that simulate diverse agentic environments, surpassing frontier LLMs on the AgentWorldBench and improving agent performance across various downstream tasks. These models enhance agent learning through scalable, controllable simulation and by integrating internal predictive reasoning for action refinement.

## Problem

- Existing research on large language model (LLM) agents predominantly focused on the policy component, leaving the development of general-purpose world models for language-based environments less explored.
- Training agents in real environments faces limitations in scalability, cost, and controllability, making it difficult to generate diverse and challenging scenarios to expose and mitigate agent weaknesses.
- A lack of comprehensive benchmarks and methodologies to systematically develop and evaluate LWMs capable of generalizing across various interactive text-based domains.

## Method

- Developed **Qwen-AgentWorld**, a family of LWMs (e.g., 35B, 397B parameters), capable of simulating agentic environments across seven diverse domains (MCP, Search, Terminal, SWE, Android, Web, OS) using a ==unified textual trajectory schema==.
- Implemented a three-stage training pipeline (Continual Pre-Training, Supervised Fine-Tuning, Reinforcement Learning) to instill world knowledge, activate next-state prediction, and refine simulation fidelity, using hybrid reward signals (LLM judge + rule-based verifier) for RL.
- Created AgentWorldBench, a comprehensive, out-of-distribution benchmark derived from real-world interactions of frontier agents, evaluated by a reference-grounded, domain-aware LLM judge and rule-based verifiers.

## Results

- Qwen-AgentWorld-397B-A17B achieved the highest overall average score of **58.71** on AgentWorldBench, outperforming frontier LLMs like GPT-**5.4** (**58.25**) and Claude Opus **4.8** (**56.59**), particularly in text-based domains like Terminal (+**4.04** vs. GPT-**5.4**) and SWE (+**2.2** vs. GPT-**5.4**).
- LWMs as decoupled simulators enabled substantial gains for agents trained in simulated environments, including +**4.3** on Claw-Eval, +**7.1** on QwenClawBench, and +**12.3** on MCPMark through controllable simulation with targeted perturbations.
- LWM training as an RL warm-up improved agent performance across seven multi-turn benchmarks, including +**12.79** F1 Item on WideSearch and +**11.3** on Claw-Eval (out-of-domain), by enhancing internal prediction accuracy (**+8.4%**) and enabling prediction-driven action refinement.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Language models can be effectively trained as generalist world models to simulate complex, interactive textual environments, offering a scalable and controllable alternative to real-world interaction.
> - A multi-stage training pipeline (CPT→SFT→RL) systematically enhances LWM capabilities, leading to significant performance gains, cross-domain generalization, and improved micro-level simulation fidelity.
> - LWMs serve dual utilities: as decoupled environment simulators for scalable, controllable agentic RL and as unified agent foundation models that integrate prediction-driven action refinement, significantly boosting agent performance across diverse, unseen tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The concept of a "world model," defined as a system that predicts environmental dynamics based on current observations and actions, is recognized as a fundamental component for achieving general intelligence. Prior research has emphasized that learning to predict the world is a prerequisite for effective action within it, with some theoretical work suggesting its necessity for agents capable of generalizing across a broad range of tasks.

Existing research on large language model (LLM) agents has predominantly focused on the policy component, which maps states to actions, while the development of general-purpose world models for language-based environments has received less attention. This paper addresses this gap by investigating the development and application of language world models (LWMs) for agentic environment simulation.

The work builds upon the foundation of world models developed in visual and embodied domains, such as IRIS, DreamerV3, Cosmos, and Genie 3, which employ learned latent-space dynamics models or video-based approaches for prediction and planning. Qwen-AgentWorld extends this paradigm to text-based environments, where observations include structured text like API responses, terminal outputs, accessibility trees, and UI view hierarchies.

Within the landscape of language world models, Qwen-AgentWorld contributes by developing a native LWM as a foundation model, distinguishing itself through its comprehensive coverage of seven diverse domains within a single model and a multi-stage training pipeline (Continual Pre-Training, Supervised Fine-Tuning, Reinforcement Learning) specifically designed to instill and refine environment modeling capabilities from the outset. This contrasts with approaches that fine-tune general-purpose LLMs post hoc or focus on a narrower range of environments. Furthermore, the paper explores two complementary application paradigms for LWMs: as decoupled environment simulators and as unified agent foundation models, thereby addressing both the external utility of simulation for agent training and the internal integration of world modeling for improved agent reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to explore how language models can be developed into robust world models to advance the capabilities of general agents. This encompasses both the technical construction of such models and the investigation of their utility in agent development.

The motivation for developing language world models (LWMs), even when real environments exist, stems from two complementary axes:

**Decoupling: LWM as a Complementary Axis for Environment Simulation:**
- **Scalability:** LWMs facilitate turn-level scaling of diverse environments without requiring dedicated physical or virtual infrastructure (e.g., sandboxes, GUI virtual machines). This enables simulation across various scenarios, including real-world tasks (like those in OpenClaw), extreme conditions, and professional domains where real execution might be infeasible due to irreversible operations, proprietary deployments, or lack of public implementations.
- **Controllability:** LWMs offer precise control over environment conditions, allowing for the generation of more diverse and challenging scenarios. This includes systematically injecting targeted perturbations (e.g., intermittent API errors, paginated responses, incomplete intermediate results, partial failures) that are rare or absent in real environments. Training agents against these controlled conditions can expose and mitigate agent weaknesses, leading to agents that perform more robustly than those trained solely in real environments.

**Unifying: LWM as a Foundation for General Agents:**
- **Enhanced Decision-Making:** A capable general agent should integrate both decision-making (policy) and world-modeling (prediction) abilities. World modeling enables agents to predict future states resulting from candidate actions *before* committing to them. This internal simulation can refine action selection, acting as a meta-level thinking pattern akin to "reflection" but oriented towards future outcomes. Theoretically, an agent with foresight can perform no worse than one without it.
- **Foundation for Agentic Capabilities:** Accurate next-state prediction requires capabilities such as reasoning, factual knowledge, instruction following, and long-context handling. These capabilities are themselves foundational to general agents. By training LWMs, these core capacities are developed, potentially serving as an effective warm-up for broader agentic tasks.

Specific objectives derived from these motivations include:
- Building foundational LWMs, namely Qwen-AgentWorld-35B-A3B and Qwen-AgentWorld-397B-A17B, capable of simulating agentic environments across seven diverse domains (MCP, Search, Terminal, Software Engineering, Android, Web, OS) using long chain-of-thought reasoning.
- Developing and implementing a three-stage training pipeline (Continual Pre-Training, Supervised Fine-Tuning, Reinforcement Learning) to instill and refine world modeling capabilities.
- Creating a comprehensive evaluation benchmark, AgentWorldBench, based on real-world interactions from frontier models across established benchmarks, to assess world modeling quality.
- Demonstrating the utility of LWMs as decoupled environment simulators for scalable and controllable agentic Reinforcement Learning (RL), showing gains over real-environment training alone.
- Investigating the role of LWM training as an effective warm-up or auxiliary stage for unified agent foundation models, demonstrating improvements in downstream performance across various agentic benchmarks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on developing a Language World Model (LWM) as a conditional text generator that predicts the next environment observation given interaction history and an agent's current action. The work describes a three-stage training pipeline and a dedicated evaluation benchmark.

**Unified Environment Trajectory Schema:**
To enable a single world model across seven diverse domains (MCP, Search, Terminal, Software Engineering, Android, Web, OS), a unified textual representation for environment trajectories was adopted. This schema defines a trajectory as a `system_prompt` concatenated with a sequence of `(action_t, observation_t)` pairs. Actions represent agent outputs (e.g., tool calls, shell commands), and observations are environment feedback (e.g., tool responses, command outputs). For GUI domains (Android, Web, OS), observations are represented as accessibility trees and UI view hierarchies, not pixel frames. The `system_prompt` comprises five components: `task_description`, `action_space`, `initial_state`, `demonstrations`, and `simulation_instruction`.

**Three-Stage Training Pipeline:**
Qwen-AgentWorld follows a pipeline of Continual Pre-Training (CPT), Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), and Reinforcement Learning (RL).

1. **Training Data:**
 - **Collection:** Environment trajectories are gathered from three sources: (1) dedicated agent infrastructure (containerized sandboxes, persistent terminal sessions, GUI environments represented textually), which allows for scalable, controlled, and reproducible data; (2) open environment interaction traces (public terminal sessions, tool-call logs, code execution traces), which capture long-tail patterns and are processed via a multi-agent cleaning pipeline; and (3) in-house SFT agentic trajectories, converted to the unified environment trajectory schema.
 - **Processing:** All data is expanded into turn-level prediction samples. Filtering includes dropping short sequences, incomplete traces, and turns with empty actions or no effective state change. Retry cycles are skipped. System prompts are constructed with static components (task description, action space, demonstrations) and dynamic components (initial state, simulation instruction). Prompt templates are optimized using an automated research (AutoResearch) pipeline to maximize prediction accuracy.

2. **Stage 1: Continual Pre-Training (CPT):**
 - **Objective:** To model real-world environment behavior and inject broad world knowledge.
 - **Data:** Environment trajectories are combined with specialized-domain world knowledge corpora (e.g., industrial control, cybersecurity, medicine) to ground the model in factual knowledge not solely derivable from interaction.
 - **Objective:** Standard next-token prediction, where the system prompt, historical observations, and actions condition the prediction of `observation_{t+1}`.
 - **Loss Masking:** Turn-level information-theoretic loss masking is applied based on four statistics (Overlap, Novelty, Jaccard, Length Ratio) to assign turns to seven semantic categories (e.g., retrieval, boilerplate), allowing loss computation only on turns with genuine learning value, while retaining all tokens as context.

3. **Stage 2: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):**
 - **Objective:** Explicitly activate next-state prediction as a reasoning pattern, applying the state-transition knowledge acquired during CPT.
 - **Data:** SFT uses thinking trajectories with explicit reasoning chains. Prompt templates are diversified, and multi-beam rollouts undergo rejection sampling, where an independent judge scores candidates to curate a high-quality training set. A 256k-token context window is used.

4. **Stage 3: Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
 - **Algorithm:** GSPO (Group Sequence Policy Optimization) is employed.
 - **Challenges:** LWM RL faces challenges due to the open-ended nature of environment feedback and extreme prompt-output asymmetry (long context, short output). A 128k-token prompt cap is used.
 - **Reward Design:** A hybrid reward system combines two signals:
 - **Five-Dimensional Rubric (LLM Judge):** Predicted observations are scored by an LLM judge on a 1-5 scale across five dimensions (Format, Factuality, Consistency, Realism, Quality). Tailored judge system prompts and content-type classification mitigate false negatives.
 - **Rule-Based Verifier:** For a subset of data, executable verifier code provides a binary 0/1 correctness signal.
 - The two signals are combined at a 9:1 (rubric:rule) weighting.
 - **Training Stability:** Solutions to identified failure modes include restricting expansion to one turn per trajectory in the RL pool to prevent reward collapse, using the five-dimensional rubric with a rule-based verifier for stable convergence (compared to sparse binary rewards), and implementing tag extraction and content-type classification in judge prompts to mitigate reward hacking from self-praise.

**AgentWorldBench:**
A comprehensive benchmark for LWM evaluation was constructed based on four principles:
- **Widely-Used Queries:** Tasks are from established agentic benchmarks (e.g., Tool Decathlon, Terminal-Bench, OSWorld-Verified).
- **Frontier-Agent Trajectories:** Trajectories are generated by high-performing frontier models (e.g., Claude Opus 4.6) to stress-test LWMs.
- **Real Observations:** Ground-truth observations are obtained from real environment execution.
- **Out-of-Distribution:** Training data and benchmark queries are strictly partitioned by data source to ensure generalization testing.
The benchmark contains 2,170 turn-level evaluation samples across seven domains. Text domains use an asymmetric sampling strategy (first, last, and three intermediate turns), while GUI domains focus on challenging turns.

**Evaluation Protocol:**
- **Open-Ended Rubric:** An LLM judge scores each predicted observation on Format, Factuality, Consistency, Realism, and Quality (1-5 scale, mean scaled to 0-100).
- **Reference-Grounded Judging:** The judge receives the ground-truth observation alongside the prediction, converting evaluation into a factual comparison to reduce judge hallucination.
- **Domain-Aware Rubrics:** Each domain has a tailored judge prompt, applying the five dimensions with domain-specific terms and standards.
- **Differentiated Matching Criteria:** Content is classified into deterministic, pre-existing environment, and runtime metadata, each with different verification standards to avoid false negatives.
- **Judge Selection:** A double-blind Turing test calibrates judge models and refines prompts. GPT-5.2 was selected as the judge for its Turing-test accuracy and high consistency in model rankings.

**Rule-Based Verification:**
An independent set of rule-based verifiers provides deterministic checks on three capabilities: controllability (adherence to simulation instructions), error handling (faithful reproduction of failure modes), and long-context consistency (coherent state tracking). Test cases are synthesized from the evaluation set, and verifiers check against expected outcomes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the performance of Qwen-AgentWorld, the impact of its training methodology, and its utility in agent applications.

**LWM Performance on AgentWorldBench:**
- **Overall Superiority:** Qwen-AgentWorld-397B-A17B achieved the highest overall average score (58.71) on AgentWorldBench, surpassing frontier models like GPT-5.4 (58.25) and Claude Opus 4.8 (56.59).
- **Domain-Specific Performance:** On text-based domains, Qwen-AgentWorld-397B-A17B led with an average of 58.07, outperforming GPT-5.4 (56.84), particularly on Terminal (57.73 vs. 53.69) and SWE (68.49 vs. 66.29). On GUI domains, it ranked fifth (59.69), behind Claude Opus 4.8 and 4.6, GPT-5.4, and Gemini 3.1 Pro, which exhibited advantages potentially due to multimodal pre-training.
- **Challenging Domain:** Search was identified as the most challenging domain for all models, with the highest score being 37.82, approximately half of the top scores in SWE or MCP, indicating difficulty in modeling constantly evolving web content and maintaining factual consistency across retrieval chains.
- **Rule-Based Verification (Appendix C):** Independent rule-based verifiers corroborated the main findings, with GPT-5.4 scoring highest overall (72.93%) and Qwen-AgentWorld-397B-A17B second (67.12%), confirming improved adherence to instructions, error handling, and long-context consistency.

**Effect of World-Model Training:**
- Comparing Qwen-AgentWorld models to their base Qwen checkpoints revealed significant performance gains attributable to the three-stage CPT→SFT→RL pipeline. The 397B model saw its overall average score increase from 54.74 to 58.71 (+3.97 points). The 35B model achieved an even larger gain of 8.66 points (47.73 to 56.39), outperforming Claude Sonnet 4.6. These improvements were consistent across both text and GUI domains, indicating the pipeline's effectiveness beyond general model capabilities.

**Cross-Domain Generalization:**
- RL training on Terminal data alone demonstrated cross-domain generalization. While Terminal performance improved by +14.2 points, held-out domains also showed parallel gains: SWE (+11.5), Search (+11.8), and MCP (+5.0). This suggests that RL reinforces generalizable world knowledge and state transition principles rather than merely domain-specific output formats.

**Application I: LWM as an Environment Simulator (Decoupled):**
- **Generalizable Environment Scaling:** Qwen-AgentWorld-397B-A17B successfully simulated 4,000 OpenClaw environments, entirely out of its training distribution. This scalable simulation for agentic RL led to substantial gains on Claw-Eval (+4.3) and QwenClawBench (+7.1) for agents trained with this simulator, highlighting the importance of LWM quality.
- **Controllable Simulation:**
 - **MCP (Environment Adaptation):** Controllable Sim RL, which injects targeted perturbations into the simulation (e.g., intermittent API errors), resulted in significant improvements on Tool Decathlon (+3.7) and MCPMark (+12.3), in contrast to standard Sim RL which yielded negligible gains. This indicates that controllability is a prerequisite for effective Sim RL in complex domains.
 - **Search (Fictional-World Construction):** Training agents in entirely fictional, self-consistent search worlds (where Qwen-AgentWorld invents all facts and web content) resulted in substantial performance gains on real-world WideSearch tasks (e.g., +16.29 F1 by Item for the 35B model). This approach also shaped agent behavior more effectively than Real RL, forcing agents to issue more `web_extractor` calls for complete information.

**Application II: LWM as an Agent Foundation Model (Unified):**
- **Cross-Task and Cross-Domain Generalization:** LWM RL warm-up, applied to a single-turn, non-agentic next-state prediction task, consistently improved performance across seven multi-turn, tool-calling agentic benchmarks without further fine-tuning.
 - In-domain improvements included WideSearch F1 Item (+12.79), Terminal-Bench 2.0 (+6.30), SWE-Bench Verified (+3.4), and SWE-Bench Pro (+5.2).
 - Significant out-of-domain gains were observed on Claw-Eval (+11.3), QwenClawBench (+9.7), and BFCL v4 (+9.0), even though the LWM training did not include any data from these domains. This suggests that LWM warm-up instills transferable agent capabilities.
- **Prediction-Driven Action Refinement:** Analysis of agent reasoning traces showed that LWM RL-trained models systematically performed mental simulations of environment responses before acting. This improved internal prediction accuracy by +8.4% and enabled agents to refine action plans, anticipate failures, and apply targeted fixes, leading to better task outcomes (e.g., mailman task case study).

**Analysis of LWM Reasoning Patterns:**
- **Deliberative Self-Correction:** The LWM used explicit cognitive interrupts ("Wait!") to re-examine and revise intermediate predictions, especially in state-tracking domains like Terminal and MCP.
- **Information Leakage Prevention:** In the Search domain, the model demonstrated "theory of mind" by preventing leakage of information unrelated to the agent's query, even when the model possessed the full reference answer.
- **Multi-Step Causal Reasoning:** The LWM constructed detailed causal chains across multiple system abstractions, such as predicting complex error outcomes in Terminal tasks involving multiple command interactions.

**RL Enhances Micro-Level Simulation Fidelity:**
- RL training improved fine-grained details in simulation. Examples include increased URL realism and source diversity in Search, character-level byte arithmetic and adherence to cryptographic invariants in Terminal, and perfect API schema consistency across sequential calls in MCP. This indicates that the reward signal propagates to low-salience details.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work introduces Qwen-AgentWorld as the first family of native language world models that comprehensively cover seven diverse agent interaction domains within a single model. This represents an advancement in the capability of LLMs to function as foundational simulators for complex, interactive environments.

The three-stage training pipeline (CPT, SFT, RL) established in this research offers a systematic methodology for developing LWMs, highlighting how different training stages contribute to injecting world knowledge, activating reasoning patterns, and sharpening simulation fidelity. This pipeline could serve as a blueprint for future LWM development.

The creation of AgentWorldBench provides a comprehensive, reference-grounded, and out-of-distribution benchmark. This contributes a standardized tool for evaluating LWMs across a wide range of tasks and domains, fostering comparative research and development in this area.

The demonstration of LWMs as decoupled environment simulators has implications for agent training, enabling:
- **Scalability:** The ability to generate thousands of diverse, realistic environments without extensive infrastructure can significantly reduce the cost and complexity of collecting training data for agents.
- **Robustness:** Controllable simulation allows for the creation of adversarial or "edge case" scenarios that systematically expose and address agent weaknesses. This capacity to train agents in conditions beyond typical real-world interactions could lead to more robust and generalizable agent policies.
- **Safety:** The concept of "fictional worlds" in simulation for domains like Search can prevent agents from confusing simulated facts with real-world knowledge, mitigating risks of factual fabrication or misinformation when agents operate in sensitive contexts.

The validation of LWM training as an effective warm-up for unified agent foundation models suggests a new paradigm for agent development. By internalizing next-state prediction as a meta-reasoning pattern, agents can mentally simulate outcomes before acting, leading to more refined action selection and improved performance across diverse, even out-of-domain, agentic tasks. This capability could be foundational for more intelligent and autonomous agents that can plan and reason internally.

Overall, Qwen-AgentWorld establishes language world modeling as a promising direction for scaling general agents beyond the limitations of real-environment interaction alone. This could impact the development of AI systems capable of operating reliably and effectively in complex, dynamic, and potentially high-stakes digital environments, by enhancing their internal understanding and predictive capabilities.
