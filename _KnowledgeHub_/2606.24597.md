# Qwen-AgentWorld: Language World Models for General Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> Qwen-AgentWorld introduces a family of language world models (LWMs) that simulate diverse agentic environments, surpassing frontier LLMs on the AgentWorldBench and improving agent performance across various downstream tasks. These models enhance agent learning through scalable, controllable simulation and by integrating internal predictive reasoning for action refinement.

## Problem

- Existing research on large language model (LLM) agents predominantly focused on the policy component, leaving the development of general-purpose world models for language-based environments less explored.
- Training agents in real environments faces limitations in scalability, cost, and controllability, making it difficult to generate diverse and challenging scenarios to expose and mitigate agent weaknesses.
- A lack of comprehensive benchmarks and methodologies to systematically develop and evaluate LWMs capable of generalizing across various interactive text-based domains.

## Method

- Developed **Qwen-AgentWorld**, a family of LWMs (e.g., 35B, 397B parameters), capable of simulating agentic environments across seven diverse domains (MCP, Search, Terminal, SWE, Android, Web, OS) using a ==unified textual trajectory schema==.
- Implemented a three-stage training pipeline (Continual Pre-Training, Supervised Fine-Tuning, Reinforcement Learning) to instill world knowledge, activate next-state prediction, and refine simulation fidelity, using hybrid reward signals (LLM judge + rule-based verifier) for RL.
- Created AgentWorldBench, a comprehensive, out-of-distribution benchmark derived from real-world interactions of frontier agents, evaluated by a reference-grounded, domain-aware LLM judge and rule-based verifiers.

## Results

- Qwen-AgentWorld-397B-A17B achieved the highest overall average score of **58.71** on AgentWorldBench, outperforming frontier LLMs like GPT-**5.4** (**58.25**) and Claude Opus **4.8** (**56.59**), particularly in text-based domains like Terminal (+**4.04** vs. GPT-**5.4**) and SWE (+**2.2** vs. GPT-**5.4**).
- LWMs as decoupled simulators enabled substantial gains for agents trained in simulated environments, including +**4.3** on Claw-Eval, +**7.1** on QwenClawBench, and +**12.3** on MCPMark through controllable simulation with targeted perturbations.
- LWM training as an RL warm-up improved agent performance across seven multi-turn benchmarks, including +**12.79** F1 Item on WideSearch and +**11.3** on Claw-Eval (out-of-domain), by enhancing internal prediction accuracy (**+8.4%**) and enabling prediction-driven action refinement.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Language models can be effectively trained as generalist world models to simulate complex, interactive textual environments, offering a scalable and controllable alternative to real-world interaction.
> - A multi-stage training pipeline (CPT→SFT→RL) systematically enhances LWM capabilities, leading to significant performance gains, cross-domain generalization, and improved micro-level simulation fidelity.
> - LWMs serve dual utilities: as decoupled environment simulators for scalable, controllable agentic RL and as unified agent foundation models that integrate prediction-driven action refinement, significantly boosting agent performance across diverse, unseen tasks.
