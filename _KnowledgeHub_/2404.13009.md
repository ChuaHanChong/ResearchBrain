# Online Policy Optimization in Unknown Nonlinear Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at California Institute of Technology developed a meta-framework for online policy optimization in discrete-time, time-varying, nonlinear dynamical systems with unknown models. This work provides the first local regret bound for such challenging control settings, demonstrating that accurate policy performance can be achieved by focusing on local model estimation along the visited trajectory rather than requiring global system identification.

## Problem

- Existing online policy optimization methods struggled with simultaneously handling unknown dynamic models and general nonlinear, time-varying system dynamics.
- Real-world nonlinear systems often have unknown or changing dynamics, making it difficult for controllers to determine optimal policy improvements.
- Prior model learning efforts were largely confined to linear systems, and nonlinear methods often assumed perfectly known dynamics.

## Method

- A ==meta-framework== was designed that modularizes ==online policy optimization== into an ==Online Policy Optimization Algorithm (ALG)== and an ==Online Parameter Estimator (EST)==.
- ALG updates policy parameters using the current state and EST's model estimation, while EST updates its model based on observed system dynamics.
- The analytical strategy compares the actual system dynamics (with EST's inexact parameters) to an ideal scenario (with true parameters), treating ==estimation errors as perturbations== to ALG's policy updates.

## Results

- The meta-framework was proven robust (Theorem 6), bounding policy regret and the cumulative distance between actual and ideal trajectories based on total zeroth-order and first-order model mismatches.
- Online parameter estimation for first-order mismatches (Theorem 9, Corollary 10) showed that under state randomness, a sublinear regret bound for zeroth-order errors implies a bounded sum of squared first-order gradient errors.
- A local regret bound of **O(T)** and an expected total trajectory distance bound of **O(T^3/4)** were established for the instantiated framework, representing the **first** such guarantees for online policy optimization in nonlinear, time-varying systems with unknown dynamics (Theorem 12).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A controller does not need to learn the global true dynamical model; instead, focusing on 'lazy learning' by fitting the system's behavior along the actually traversed trajectory is sufficient.
> - The meta-framework's performance is robust to model estimation errors, provided these errors are bounded locally in both zeroth-order (prediction error) and first-order (gradient error).
> - Sufficient random disturbances in state generation implicitly force an online estimator to minimize not only prediction errors but also the sensitivity of these errors to state perturbations (first-order gradients).

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses the problem of online policy optimization within nonlinear, time-varying dynamical systems where the precise dynamic models are unknown to the controller. This field, broadly known as learning-based control, has garnered significant attention for its potential to adapt to changing system dynamics and leverage data for control decisions.

Previous work in online policy optimization primarily focused on simpler scenarios. Early contributions often considered linear-quadratic regulator (LQR) problems or linear time-invariant dynamics, typically assuming the dynamic models were known. Subsequent research began to tackle challenges arising from either unknown dynamical models or general nonlinear dynamics, but rarely both simultaneously. For instance, some studies explored online policy optimization for general nonlinear and time-varying systems, but under the restrictive assumption that the true dynamical models were perfectly known. Conversely, efforts to learn unknown dynamic models were largely confined to linear systems, often relying on random local exploration. This approach, which attempts to identify the true dynamic model globally through small perturbations, becomes inadequate in complex nonlinear systems where linearization varies significantly across different states.

The adaptive control literature, which focuses on dynamically adjusting parameters to compensate for time-varying disturbances and improve system performance (e.g., stabilization or trajectory tracking), shares a similar spirit. However, these methods typically do not optimize the gains within the control policy itself, distinguishing them from the policy optimization objective of the current work. Similarly, online robust control aims to ensure system stability or safety despite model uncertainties, but often without explicit policy optimization.

A motivating insight from adaptive control for this paper is the concept that a controller may not need to precisely learn the global true dynamical model. Instead, it can focus on "fitting" the system's behavior along the trajectory it actually traverses, a concept sometimes referred to as "lazy learning." This work aims to bridge the gap by proposing a general approach that can handle both unknown dynamic models and general nonlinear dynamics/policy classes concurrently, offering theoretical guarantees in this challenging setting.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and theoretically sound methodology for online policy optimization in discrete-time, time-varying, nonlinear dynamical systems with unknown models. The goal is for an online agent to continuously optimize its control policy to minimize the total incurred cost over a finite horizon, even when it cannot obtain a globally accurate model of the true system dynamics.

The motivation stems from two significant practical challenges in online policy optimization:
1. **Unknown Dynamical Models**: In real-world applications (e.g., drone control, robotic manipulation), the exact mathematical models governing system dynamics are often unknown or subject to change over time. This lack of knowledge makes it difficult for a controller to determine the optimal directions for policy improvement. Unlike linear systems, where local exploration can yield globally accurate dynamic estimates, nonlinear systems exhibit state-dependent behavior, rendering global identification through local perturbations impractical.
2. **Generality of Dynamics and Policy Classes**: Many existing algorithms are tailored for specific, often simplified, dynamics (e.g., linear) or policy classes. There is a need for algorithms that can apply broadly to a wider range of nonlinear and time-varying dynamics, as well as general parameterized policy classes, to capture the complexity of real-world control problems.

The researchers observe that prior works have made progress by addressing these challenges individually: some handle general nonlinear dynamics assuming known models, while others learn unknown models but are restricted to linear systems. However, a comprehensive framework that simultaneously tackles both unknown nonlinear dynamics and general policy classes with provable performance guarantees has been largely absent. This work is motivated by the need to fill this void, drawing inspiration from adaptive control's "lazy learning" principle to propose a method where model estimation is focused on the experienced trajectory rather than global identification.

The chosen performance metric, "local regret," is an online analog of stationary point conditions in nonconvex optimization. This metric is considered more appropriate than static regret for nonconvex cost functions, acknowledging that gradient-based online optimization may get stuck in local minima. The research also aims to address a limitation of local regret in this context, where its definition typically relies on known true dynamics. Thus, a secondary objective is to not only bound the local regret of the policy parameters but also to quantify the distance between the actual system trajectory (under estimated parameters) and an "ideal" trajectory (under true parameters).

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this research is a meta-framework (Algorithm 1) that modularizes the problem into two interacting components: an Online Policy Optimization Algorithm (ALG) and an Online Parameter Estimator (EST). This framework operates in discrete time, where at each step, ALG updates the policy parameters ($\theta_t$) based on the current system state, policy, and EST's model estimation ($\hat{a}_t$), while EST updates its model estimation ($\hat{a}_t$) based on the system's state and observations of the unknown dynamics.

**Meta-Framework Design:**
- **ALG Module**: Receives the current state $x_t$, policy parameter $\theta_t$, known system functions (e.g., $\pi_t, g_t, h_t, f_t$), and EST's current estimate $\hat{a}_t$. It then computes and outputs the next policy parameter $\theta_{t+1}$. ALG is designed to potentially maintain an internal state, $y_t$, to leverage historical information.
- **EST Module**: Receives the current state $x_t$ and a (potentially noisy) observation $\tilde{f}_t$ of the true nonlinear residual $f_t(x_t, a^*_t)$. It then updates and outputs the next model parameter estimation $\hat{a}_{t+1}$. EST is also permitted to maintain internal memory.

**Analytical Approach:**
The central analytical strategy involves comparing the actual dynamics of the meta-framework (using EST's inexact $\hat{a}_t$) with an "ideal" scenario, denoted ALG$^*$, where ALG operates with perfect knowledge of the true model parameters $a^*_t$. This comparison is formalized by expressing the meta-framework's behavior as ALG$^*$ with an added perturbation, $\zeta_t$, on the policy parameter update. The performance of the meta-framework is then shown to be robust to these perturbations, provided certain properties are met.

**Required Properties for the Joint Dynamics Induced by ALG (Section 3.1):**
1. **Lipschitzness (Property 3)**: The dynamics governing the evolution of the state $x_t$, internal state $y_t$, and policy parameter $\theta_t$ (collectively referred to as joint dynamics) must be Lipschitz continuous with respect to zeroth-order ($\epsilon_t = \|f_t(x_t, \hat{a}_t) - f_t(x_t, a^*_t)\|$) and first-order ($\epsilon'_t = \|\nabla_x f_t(x_t, \hat{a}_t) - \nabla_x f_t(x_t, a^*_t)\|_F$) model mismatches. This property quantifies how model estimation errors propagate through the system, ensuring that small errors lead to proportionally small distortions in the joint dynamics.
2. **Contraction Stability (Property 4)**: The partial dynamics governing the system state $x_t$ and internal state $y_t$ (assuming exact $a^*_t$ and a slowly varying policy $\theta_t$) must exhibit contraction stability. This ensures that trajectories remain bounded within certain regions and that differences between nearby trajectories diminish over time, providing robustness against accumulated errors.
3. **Robustness (Property 5)**: ALG$^*$ (the ideal ALG with known parameters) must be robust to additive perturbations $\zeta_t$ on its policy parameter updates. Specifically, its regret guarantees should gracefully degrade with the total magnitude of these perturbations. This property is crucial because model estimation errors effectively act as such perturbations.

**EST's Objective and Online Parameter Estimation (Section 3.2):**
EST's task is formulated as an online regression problem focused on minimizing trajectory-dependent model mismatches, specifically the zeroth-order error $\epsilon_t$ and first-order error $\epsilon'_t$. Unlike traditional system identification, EST is not required to globally identify $a^*_t$. A key challenge is bounding the first-order mismatch, $\epsilon'_t$. The research addresses this by showing a reduction from the regret of minimizing the squared zeroth-order error to bounding the sum of squared first-order gradient errors. This reduction relies on a crucial assumption:
- **State Randomness (Property 8)**: There must be a sufficient level of random disturbances in the generation of system states $x_t$. This randomness "forces" the estimator to implicitly minimize the gradients of the error functions, as the zeroth-order loss cannot converge to zero if the gradient magnitude is persistently large across varied inputs.

**Concrete Instantiation: Matched-Disturbance Dynamics (Section 4):**
To demonstrate the framework, the authors apply it to "matched-disturbance dynamics," where the control input can directly cancel out a nonlinear residual term $f_t(x_t, a^*_t)$ if $a^*_t$ is known. The policy class naturally involves a term to cancel $\hat{f}_t(x_t, \hat{a}_t)$ plus a feedback control term $\psi_t(x_t, \theta_t)$.
- **ALG Instantiation (M-GAPS)**: A new algorithm, Memoryless Gradient-based Adaptive Policy Selection (M-GAPS, Algorithm 2), is developed. M-GAPS extends the prior GAPS algorithm but achieves $O(1)$ computational and memory complexity per time step by efficiently accumulating partial derivatives in its internal state $y_t$. It uses EST's prediction $\hat{a}_t$ to estimate future states and costs for its gradient computations.
- **EST Instantiation (Gradient Estimator)**: A standard online gradient descent algorithm (Algorithm 3) is used for EST. It updates $\hat{a}_t$ by minimizing a prediction loss function based on noisy observations $\tilde{f}_t$.
- **Assumptions**: Specific assumptions (Assumptions 18-22, detailed in Appendix C) are made regarding the Lipschitzness and smoothness of system dynamics, policies, residuals, and cost functions, as well as the contractive stability of the system, the compactness of the parameter space, the affine nature of the residual, and the boundedness and randomness of system disturbances ($w_t$). These assumptions are crucial for deriving the theoretical guarantees for the instantiated framework.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents three main theoretical findings that underpin the meta-framework's effectiveness and provide specific regret guarantees for its instantiation:

1. **Robustness of the Meta-Framework (Theorem 6)**:
 This theorem establishes the fundamental robustness of the proposed meta-framework. It states that if ALG and EST satisfy the specified Lipschitzness, Contraction Stability, and Robustness properties (Properties 3, 4, and 5, respectively), and if the zeroth-order ($\epsilon_t$) and first-order ($\epsilon'_t$) model mismatches generated by EST are uniformly bounded, then:
 - The actual trajectory ($\xi = \{x_t, y_t, \theta_t\}$) produced by the meta-framework remains uniformly bounded.
 - An "ideal" trajectory ($\tilde{\xi} = \{\tilde{x}_t, \tilde{y}_t, \theta_t\}$), obtained by applying the same policy parameters $\theta_{0:T-1}$ from the meta-framework but with *exact* knowledge of the true model parameters $a^*_{0:T-1}$, achieves a regret $R(T, \sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \|\zeta_t\|)$.
 - The total magnitude of the "perturbations" $\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \|\zeta_t\|$ (which represent the effect of EST's inexact parameters on ALG's policy updates) is bounded by a linear combination of the total zeroth-order model mismatches ($\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \epsilon_t$) and total first-order model mismatches ($\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \epsilon'_t$).
 - The cumulative distance between the actual trajectory and the ideal trajectory ($\sum_{t=1}^{T} \|(x_t, y_t) - (\tilde{x}_t, \tilde{y}_t)\|$) is also bounded by a similar linear combination of total $\epsilon_t$ and $\epsilon'_t$.
 - **Implication**: This theorem demonstrates that the meta-framework's performance, as measured by regret and trajectory tracking, is directly tied to the local accuracy of EST's predictions on the states actually visited, rather than requiring global identification of the true dynamic model.

2. **Online Parameter Estimation for First-Order Mismatches (Theorem 9)**:
 This finding addresses the challenge of bounding first-order model mismatches, $\epsilon'_t$. It proves that under specific conditions on the error function's derivatives and the presence of sufficient random disturbances in the system (Property 8, implying a positive covariance of state evolution), a bound on the sum of squared zeroth-order prediction errors can be translated into a bound on the sum of squared first-order gradient errors.
 - Specifically, if the zeroth-order regret $E[\sum_{t=1}^T \ell_t]$ (where $\ell_t = \|e_t(x_t, \hat{a}_t)\|^2$) is sufficiently small (e.g., $R_\ell^0(T) \le \bar{\epsilon}^3 T$), then the expected total squared first-order gradient loss $E[\sum_{t=1}^T \|\nabla_x e_t(x_t, \hat{a}_t)\|_F^2]$ is bounded. The bound is proportional to $k \bar{\epsilon}^3 T + k \gamma^2 \bar{\epsilon}^2 T$, where $k$ is the dimension of the unknown component, $\bar{\epsilon}$ is related to the noise magnitude, and $\gamma$ is a bound on second derivatives.
 - **Corollary 10** further specializes this, showing that if EST achieves a common sublinear regret bound of $O(\sqrt{T})$ for zeroth-order errors, the expected total squared first-order gradient loss is $O(kT^{5/6})$.
 - **Implication**: This result is crucial as it demonstrates that the inherent randomness in state generation (e.g., due to disturbances) implicitly encourages an online estimator to minimize not just the prediction errors themselves, but also the sensitivity of these errors to state perturbations (i.e., the first-order gradients). This reduces the problem of bounding $\epsilon'_t$ to a standard online regression problem.

3. **Local Regret Bound for the Instantiated Framework (Theorem 12)**:
 By combining the theoretical results from Theorem 6 and Theorem 9, the authors derive a concrete local regret bound for the meta-framework when instantiated with M-GAPS as ALG and the Gradient Estimator as EST, operating under matched-disturbance dynamics and specific assumptions (Assumptions 18-22).
 - The expected local regret of the trajectory $\xi = \{x_t, y_t, \theta_t\}$ is bounded by $O(\eta^{-1}(1+V_{sys} + \bar{\epsilon} T) + \eta T + (\sqrt{m\bar{\epsilon}} + m\bar{\epsilon}) T)$, where $\eta$ is the learning rate, $V_{sys}$ is the total system variation, $\bar{\epsilon}$ is the magnitude of random disturbances, and $m$ is the dimension of the control input.
 - The expected total distance between the actual trajectory $\xi$ and the ideal trajectory $\tilde{\xi}$ is bounded by $O(T^{3/4} + \sqrt{m\bar{\epsilon}} T)$.
 - **Significance**: This is presented as the first local regret bound for online policy optimization in nonlinear, time-varying systems with unknown dynamics, providing a quantitative performance guarantee in a challenging control setting.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers a methodical approach to online policy optimization in complex, uncertain environments, addressing a previously open problem in control theory. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Theoretical Framework for Unknown Nonlinear Systems**: The primary contribution is the meta-framework that systematically integrates online policy optimization (ALG) with online parameter estimation (EST) for nonlinear, time-varying systems with unknown dynamics. This provides a novel theoretical foundation for learning-based control in scenarios where traditional methods struggle.

2. **Relaxed Model Estimation Requirements**: A key insight is that EST does not need to identify the global, true dynamical model parameters. Instead, its performance is evaluated based on its ability to accurately predict system behavior and its derivatives along the actual trajectory visited by the controller. This "lazy learning" approach relaxes a significant bottleneck in applying learning-based control to complex nonlinear systems.

3. **First Local Regret Guarantee**: The work culminates in what is reported as the first local regret bound for online policy optimization in nonlinear time-varying systems with unknown dynamics. This quantitative guarantee provides a measure of performance relative to an ideal scenario, advancing the theoretical understanding of such systems.

4. **Computational Efficiency**: The proposed Memoryless Gradient-based Adaptive Policy Selection (M-GAPS) algorithm, developed as an instantiation for ALG, offers a significant practical advantage. By achieving $O(1)$ computational and memory complexity per time step (a notable improvement over prior $O(\log T)$ algorithms like GAPS in similar settings), M-GAPS enhances the feasibility of deploying such controllers in real-time, resource-constrained environments. M-GAPS may also be of independent interest for its efficiency improvements.

5. **Guidance for Future Research**: The specified properties and the modular design of ALG and EST serve as a blueprint for future algorithm development. Researchers can leverage these guidelines to design new online policy optimization methods and dynamics estimation techniques that are robust to model uncertainties and applicable to broader classes of nonlinear systems. This framework encourages interdisciplinary efforts, suggesting that tools from online nonconvex optimization and adaptive control could be combined to tackle even more general and challenging dynamics.

6. **Potential for Real-world Applications**: By providing a provably effective method for controlling systems with unknown nonlinear and time-varying dynamics, this work has implications for various applications, including autonomous robotics, drone control, and other complex cyber-physical systems where precise models are unavailable or change dynamically. The ability to adapt policies online without perfect model knowledge could lead to more robust and high-performing control systems in practice.
