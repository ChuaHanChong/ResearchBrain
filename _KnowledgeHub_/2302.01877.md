# AdaptDiffuser: Diffusion Models as Adaptive Self-evolving Planners

> [!summary] Summary
> AdaptDiffuser introduces a self-evolutionary framework for diffusion-based planners in offline reinforcement learning, addressing limitations stemming from insufficient training data diversity. It leverages reward-guided synthetic data generation and a dynamics-consistent discriminator to iteratively refine planning, achieving up to 27.9% higher performance on unseen robotic tasks and over 20% on complex navigation compared to existing methods.

## Problem

- Existing diffusion-based planners in offline reinforcement learning are constrained by the limited diversity and quality of their initial training data.
- This data insufficiency impedes their ability to accurately capture environmental dynamics and effectively plan.
- The lack of diverse data severely restricts the generalization capabilities of these models to novel or unseen tasks.

## Method

- **AdaptDiffuser** proposes an iterative self-evolutionary framework where a ==diffusion model== continuously refines its planning capabilities.
- It generates diverse, ==goal-conditioned== synthetic data by guiding the diffusion process with task-specific ==reward gradients==.
- A robust discriminator filters this synthetic data based on both dynamics consistency (using an ==inverse dynamics model==) and high reward, ensuring quality and physical plausibility.

## Results

- AdaptDiffuser consistently improved average returns by **20.8%** over Diffuser on Maze2D tasks and **7.5%** on MuJoCo locomotion, especially on datasets with lower initial data quality.
- The framework demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization, achieving **27.9%** higher performance than Diffuser on an entirely unseen KUKA robot pick-and-place task.
- Qualitative analysis showed AdaptDiffuser generated shorter, smoother, and successful paths for complex, sparse-reward navigation, while being resilient to limited expert data and training steps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diffusion models can be transformed into adaptive, self-evolving planners by iteratively generating and learning from their own dynamics-consistent, high-quality synthetic data.
> - Guiding the diffusion process with reward gradients enables the generation of diverse, goal-conditioned trajectories, crucial for adapting to new tasks.
> - A two-pronged data selection process, incorporating both dynamics consistency and reward filtering, is essential for ensuring the realism and efficacy of synthetic training data.
