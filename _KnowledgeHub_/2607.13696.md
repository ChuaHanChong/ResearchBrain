# Anatomy of Uncertainty: Expressive Descriptors of Robotic Manipulator Motion for Non-verbal Communication in Human-Robot Collaboration

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Nanyang Technological University developed a mathematical framework that translates a robot's perceptual uncertainty into observable non-verbal motion using a Commitment-Vigilance state space and eleven computable expressive descriptors. Human-subject studies validated that these motions reliably communicate distinct behavioral states such as confidence, curiosity, hesitance, and fear, with specific descriptor variations significantly modulating perceived intensity.

## Problem

- Existing human-robot collaboration (HRC) methods for uncertainty communication often map motion to discrete emotions or binary states, lacking a general mathematical account for decomposing perceptual uncertainty into behavioral processes.
- Most approaches do not identify a comprehensive set of measurable motion parameters sufficient for expressing different uncertainty-related states and their intensities along a continuum.
- Robots often execute actions with identical motion profiles regardless of internal certainty, potentially misleading human collaborators and hindering effective teamwork.

## Method

- Developed a mathematical framework projecting ==Laban Effort factors== (Weight, Time, Space, Flow) onto a 2D ==Commitment-Vigilance (C-V) state space== to organize five uncertainty-related behavioral states: confidence, curiosity, hesitance, fear, and inactivity.
- Defined five elemental ==motion primitives== (approach, pause, retreat, exploration, oscillation) as observable building blocks for robot end-effector trajectories.
- Derived eleven computable ==expressive descriptors== (e.g., approach acceleration, pause count/duration, retreat characteristics, gaze angles, tilt, shiver amplitude) to parametrically encode the kinematics and geometry of manipulator trajectories.

## Results

- Human observers reliably recognized distinct behavioral states (Confident, Curious, Hesitant, Fearful) from robot trajectories, with chi-squared tests showing significant deviation from a uniform distribution (**p < .001** for all).
- Specific modifications to expressive descriptors significantly modulated the perceived intensity of corresponding behaviors; for instance, shorter pauses and greater approach acceleration increased perceived confidence.
- Each designed expressive trajectory elicited a distinct profile of perceived states, demonstrating the framework's ability to differentiate between subtly distinct internal uncertainty states.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating appraisal theories and active perception concepts with Laban Movement Analysis provides a unified, perceptually grounded framework for expressing a nuanced spectrum of perceptual uncertainty.
> - The Commitment-Vigilance state space offers a structured continuum for categorizing and differentiating subtle uncertainty-related states through distinct combinations of task-directed engagement and information-gathering behavior.
> - Specific kinematic and geometric motion parameters, such as approach acceleration and pause characteristics, can effectively control the perceived intensity of various uncertainty states in robot motion.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in this paper addresses a critical aspect of human-robot collaboration (HRC): the communication of a robot's internal state, specifically perceptual uncertainty, through non-verbal motion. Existing research in HRC has demonstrated the value of nonverbal robot behaviors in improving the understandability, efficiency, and robustness of collaborative interactions. Concepts such as animation principles (e.g., anticipation and reaction), legible motion, and the broader design principle that movement itself is a communicative property have been explored to make robot actions more interpretable.

Laban Movement Analysis (LMA) has emerged as a systematic framework for describing and generating expressive robot motion, with applications ranging from aerial robots communicating affect through flight paths to mobile robots expressing attitudes and robotic manipulators conveying emotional qualities. Independent work has also shown that expressive timing, including speed changes and pauses, can modulate perceived robot confidence. Furthermore, existing approaches to communicating robot uncertainty have explored verbal, visual, and behavioral modalities, with studies demonstrating the effectiveness of nonverbal warning signals for poor performance or explicit expressions of incapability. Hesitation, as a specific uncertainty-related motion, has been particularly studied, with research deriving acceleration-based hesitation profiles from human movements and showing that pauses can make a manipulator appear uncertain.

Despite these advancements, several gaps remain in the broader research landscape that this work aims to address. Most existing methods for expressive motion and uncertainty communication:
- Map selected motion qualities to discrete emotions, personality traits, or intended goals, rather than providing a general mathematical account of how perceptual uncertainty should be decomposed into behavioral processes.
- Do not identify a comprehensive set of measurable motion parameters sufficient for expressing different uncertainty-related states and their intensities.
- Often encode uncertainty as a binary condition, a single probability, an impending failure, or a context-specific gesture, without distinguishing between a continuum of states like confidence, curiosity, hesitance, fear, and inactivity within a common model.
- Provide limited insight into which independent kinematic and geometric variables effectively control the perceived intensity of these uncertainty states.

This paper positions itself as a foundational step towards a unified, mathematical, and perceptually grounded framework for expressing a nuanced spectrum of perceptual uncertainty in robotic manipulator motion, moving beyond discrete labels or binary conditions. It integrates appraisal theories and active perception concepts with LMA to create a model that connects a robot's internal perceptual assessment to observable, interpretable motion.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation for this research stems from the observation that robots operating in human environments often deal with incomplete, noisy, or ambiguous perceptual information. While classical decision-making and active perception methods allow robots to manage internal uncertainty, communicating this internal state to human collaborators is crucial for effective human-robot collaboration. A robot that consistently executes actions with an identical motion profile, regardless of its internal certainty, may mislead human collaborators about its capabilities or current understanding, potentially hindering teamwork and trust. Nonverbal robot motion offers a natural and continuously available channel to convey such internal states.

The primary objectives of this work are articulated through three main contributions:

1. **Development of an Anatomical Behavioral Model for Perceptual Uncertainty:** The researchers aimed to introduce a mathematical framework that projects the four Laban Effort factors (Weight, Time, Space, Flow) onto two orthogonal dimensions: Commitment and Vigilance. This Commitment-Vigilance (C-V) state space is designed to organize and categorize five distinct uncertainty-related behavioral states: confidence, curiosity, hesitance, fear, and inactivity. The model seeks to explain how a robot's task-directed engagement (Commitment) and its uncertainty-management behavior (Vigilance) manifest through different movement qualities, grounded in appraisal theories and approach-avoidance models.

2. **Definition of Motion Primitives and Computable Expressive Descriptors:** A key objective was to translate the abstract behavioral model into a concrete, executable representation for robotic manipulators. This involved identifying five elemental motion primitives—approach, pause, retreat, exploration, and oscillation—each linked to specific Effort factors and a characteristic kinematic profile. Furthermore, the work aimed to derive a set of eleven computable expressive descriptors (including approach acceleration, pause count and duration, retreat characteristics, gaze angles, tilt, and shiver amplitude). These descriptors are intended to parametrically encode the observable kinematics and geometry of manipulator trajectories, enabling the generation of motions corresponding to the defined behavioral states.

3. **Human Perception Validation of the Proposed Representation:** A critical objective was to empirically validate whether the proposed framework effectively communicates the intended uncertainty states to human observers. This involved designing a human-subject study to assess two aspects:
 - Whether descriptor-parameterized trajectories could reliably communicate distinct uncertainty-related behavioral states (confidence, curiosity, hesitance, fear) at a level significantly above chance.
 - Whether systematic variations in individual expressive descriptors could modulate the perceived intensity of these behavioral states. This validation aimed to establish a perceptually grounded basis for the proposed model and its practical application.

In summary, the motivation is to enable robots to transparently communicate their perceptual uncertainty to human collaborators through nuanced non-verbal motion, thereby fostering more intuitive and effective HRC. The objectives are to provide a theoretical model, a practical set of motion parameters, and empirical validation for this communication.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research involved the development of a hierarchical model for expressing perceptual uncertainty through robot motion, followed by a human-subject study for empirical validation.

#### 3.1. Behavioral Modeling of Robot Motion for Perceptual Uncertainty

The approach begins by adopting Laban Movement Analysis (LMA) as the qualitative interpretive framework, specifically focusing on its four Effort factors: Weight (Strong ↔ Light), Time (Sudden ↔ Sustained), Space (Direct ↔ Indirect), and Flow (Bound ↔ Free). These factors are formalized as a normalized 4D Effort vector E = (W, T, S, F) ∈ [−1, +1]^4.

To create a learnable and composable representation, a 2D latent behavioral model is introduced, characterized by two orthogonal dimensions:
- **Commitment (C ∈ [−1, 1]):** Derived from Weight and Time factors (C = −c1W − c2T). It measures the robot’s investment of motion toward or away from a task goal, reflecting its action tendency.
- **Vigilance (V ∈ [−1, 1]):** Derived from Space and Flow factors (V = v1S − v2F). It measures the allocation of motion to information gathering, monitoring, and uncertainty management.

This C-V state space allows for the conceptualization of five canonical behavioral states that express increasing perceptual uncertainty, derived through appraisal-theoretic criteria (certainty and coping potential):
- **Confidence (C=1, V=-1):** Maximal certainty, high coping potential. Characterized by Strong Weight, Sudden Time, Direct Space, Free Flow.
- **Curiosity (C=1, V=1):** Moderate uncertainty, maintained coping potential. Characterized by Strong Weight, Sudden Time, Indirect Space, Bound Flow.
- **Hesitance (C≈0, V≈0):** High uncertainty, low coping potential. Characterized by Light Weight, Sudden Time, Indirect Space, Free Flow.
- **Fear (C=-1, V=1):** High uncertainty, no coping potential. Characterized by Light Weight, Sustained Time, Indirect Space, Bound Flow.
- **Inactivity (C=-1, V=-1):** Maximal uncertainty, no coping potential. Characterized by Light Weight, Sustained Time, Direct Space, Free Flow.

#### 3.2. Robot Motion Primitives and Expressive Descriptors

Five elemental motion primitives are defined as observable building blocks of the robot's end-effector trajectory in a goal-directed task:
- **Approach:** Forward translational movement, governed by Commitment (Time and Weight).
- **Pause:** Cessation of translational motion, primarily a Vigilance manifestation (Bound Flow, Indirect Space).
- **Retreat:** Backward translational movement, inverse of Approach, activated by negative Commitment.
- **Exploration:** Lateral, arc-like, or rotational movement around the target, primarily governed by Indirect Space (Vigilance).
- **Oscillation:** Rapid, low-amplitude alternating motion, expression of extreme Bound Flow (component of Vigilance).

These primitives are composed in varying proportions to realize the behavioral states. For instance, Confidence is primarily Approach, while Curiosity is dominated by Exploration with secondary Approach and tertiary Pause.

To parametrically encode these behaviors, a set of eleven computable expressive descriptors are defined as scalar-valued functions of the end-effector trajectory:
- **Approach Acceleration (α_approach):** Peak translational acceleration during approach episodes.
- **Pause Count (n_pause):** Number of instances where all joints’ velocities are zero.
- **Pause Duration (τ_pause):** Temporal length of a pause.
- **Retreat Count (n_retreat):** Number of distinct episodes of moving away from the goal.
- **Retreat Distance (d_retreat):** Euclidean distance traversed during each retreat.
- **Retreat Acceleration (α_retreat):** Peak translational acceleration during retreat episodes.
- **Horizontal Gaze (θ_H):** Amplitude of the angle traversed in the horizontal plane during gaze episodes.
- **Vertical Gaze (θ_V):** Amplitude of the angle traversed in the vertical plane during gaze episodes.
- **Tilt Angle (χ_tilt):** Amplitude of the end-effector's roll angle around its forward axis.
- **Tilt Velocity (˙χ_tilt):** Average angular velocity during end-effector tilt.
- **Shiver Amplitude (χ_shiver_j):** Peak-to-peak amplitude of high-frequency oscillatory motion of a redundant joint.
Most descriptors are Cartesian-space defined, while Tilt Angle, Tilt Velocity, and Shiver Amplitude are defined in joint space.

#### 3.3. Experimental Design

An IRB-approved, video-based human-subject study was conducted online to validate the methodology. The task involved a robotic manipulator's goal-directed approach.
- **Stimuli:** Four base trajectories (Confident, Curious, Hesitant, Fearful) were manually designed by animators, mapping robot uncertainty to relevant expressive motion descriptors. Each trajectory exhibited distinct kinematic profiles and descriptor values (summarized in Table II).
- **Part A (Recognition):** Participants viewed all four base trajectory videos simultaneously and continuously looping. They were asked to:
 1. Qualitatively describe the robot's behavioral state in their own words.
 2. Select the best descriptor from a fixed set (Confident, Curious, Hesitant, Fearful, None, Other).
 3. Rate the perceived intensity on a five-point Likert scale.
- **Part B (Descriptor Modulation):** For each base trajectory, specific expressive descriptors were systematically varied one at a time to create variant trajectories. Participants viewed <base, variant> trajectory pairs side-by-side and selected which, if any, expressed the base behavioral state with greater intensity.
- **Participants:** 74 initial survey responses were collected, with 55 valid responses used for analysis after filtering incomplete or inconsistent data. Participant demographics and familiarity with robots were also collected.
- **Hypotheses:**
 - **H1:** The four base trajectories would be perceived as expressing distinct behavioral states significantly above chance.
 - **H2:** Specific modifications to motion descriptors would result in statistically significant changes in the perceived intensity of the corresponding expressive behaviors.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study analyzed responses from 55 valid participants to evaluate the communication of uncertainty through robot motion.

#### 4.1. Part A - Recognition of Behavioral States from Expressive Robot Motion

- **Significant Recognition:** Chi-squared goodness-of-fit tests revealed that participants' recognition of behavioral states for all four expressions (Confident, Curious, Hesitant, Fearful) deviated significantly from a uniform distribution (p < .001 for all, with extremely large effect sizes (Cohen’s w = 1.05-1.87)). This indicates that the motions were not perceived randomly.
- **Dominant Behavioral States:** Post-hoc analysis using standardized residuals identified a single dominant behavioral state for each expression, aligning with the intended design:
 - Expression A was predominantly perceived as **Confident** (r = 12.50).
 - Expression B was predominantly perceived as **Curious** (r = 6.55).
 - Expression C was predominantly perceived as **Hesitant** (r = 8.20).
 - Expression D was predominantly perceived as **Fearful** (r = 9.52).
 - No other category for any expression exceeded the significance threshold.
- **Distinct Profiles:** A chi-squared test of independence confirmed a significant association between the expressive trajectory and the perceived behavioral state (χ²(15) = 261.22, p < .001), indicating that each trajectory elicited a distinct profile of perceived states.
- These results collectively support **Hypothesis 1**, demonstrating that the robot motion trajectories reliably communicated distinct uncertainty-related behavioral states.

#### 4.2. Part B - Effect of Expressive Descriptors on Intensity of Expressive Behaviors

Chi-squared goodness-of-fit tests were conducted for each descriptor variation to assess perceived intensity modulation.
- **Confident:**
 - Motions with **shorter pauses** (r = 8.70) and **greater approach acceleration** (r = 9.51) were perceived as significantly more confident.
- **Curious:**
 - Motions with **greater tilt amplitude** (r = 6.00), **greater horizontal gaze angle** (r = 6.00), **greater vertical gaze angle** (r = 6.00), and **faster approach acceleration** (r = 3.03) were perceived as significantly more curious.
 - The effect of pause duration on curiosity was inconclusive, with both variants showing positive residuals.
 - Tilt velocity did not show a significant dominant attribution for curiosity.
- **Hesitant:**
 - Motions with **longer pauses** (r = 6.00), **greater retreat distance** (r = 7.62), **greater retreat count** (r = 10.85), and **slower approach acceleration** (r = 6.27) were perceived as significantly more hesitant.
 - For tilt amplitude, most participants selected "Both appear equally hesitant," suggesting no clear differentiation.
 - Retreat acceleration did not show a significant dominant attribution for hesitation.
- **Fearful:**
 - Motions with **longer pauses** (r = 6.27), **greater number of pauses** (r = 4.92), **greater retreat count** (r = 8.43), and **slower approach acceleration** (r = 6.27) were perceived as significantly more fearful.
 - For shiver amplitude, most participants selected "Both appear equally fearful," suggesting no clear differentiation.
 - Retreat acceleration did not show a significant dominant attribution for fear.
- These findings partially support **Hypothesis 2**, indicating that several expressive descriptors significantly modulated the perceived intensity of the corresponding behaviors, while others did not produce clear perceptual differences in isolation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for expressing perceptual uncertainty in robotic manipulator motion, offering both theoretical advancements and practical implications for human-robot collaboration.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Structured Uncertainty Communication:** The work establishes a perceptually grounded basis for encoding robot uncertainty through motion, moving beyond binary or simplistic representations. By mapping LMA Effort factors onto Commitment-Vigilance dimensions, it provides a structured continuum for various uncertainty states (confidence, curiosity, hesitance, fear, inactivity). This multi-component approach, integrating Body and Space with Effort and Shape, is shown to enable fine-grained differentiation between subtly distinct internal states.
- **Quantifiable Motion Descriptors:** The derivation of eleven computable kinematic and geometric descriptors offers a reusable and measurable set of parameters for generating and analyzing uncertainty-related behaviors. This quantitative approach allows for systematic variation and empirical testing of how specific motion characteristics contribute to perceived robot states.
- **Empirical Validation of Human Perception:** The human-subject study provides strong empirical evidence that observers reliably identify distinct uncertainty states from the proposed expressive trajectories. Furthermore, the study identifies which specific motion parameters are perceptually prominent in modulating the intensity of these expressions, such as approach acceleration, pause duration and frequency, retreat frequency and distance, and gaze amplitude. This indicates that uncertainty is communicated through coordinated variations in task commitment and information-seeking, rather than single, isolated cues.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Enhanced Human-Robot Collaboration (HRC):** By enabling robots to communicate their internal state of uncertainty transparently, this research can significantly improve the quality of HRC. Humans can better infer whether a robot is confident, gathering information, or facing an unresolved situation, leading to more appropriate expectations, smoother coordination, and increased trust. This can reduce miscommunication and enhance the overall efficiency and robustness of collaborative tasks in shared environments.
- **Autonomous Expressive Trajectory Generation:** The parameterized representation lays the groundwork for future autonomous generation of expressive robot trajectories. By linking expressive descriptors to a robot's estimated perceptual uncertainty, task constraints, and safety requirements, robots could dynamically adjust their motion to reflect their internal state in real-time. This transforms the present model from a motion-design methodology into an adaptive computational mechanism.
- **Design Guidelines for Robot Motion:** The identified prominent descriptors and their effects on perceived intensity offer concrete guidelines for robot designers and programmers. This knowledge can inform the development of more intuitive and interpretable robot behaviors, especially for manipulators that lack anthropomorphic features or dedicated display interfaces.
- **Foundation for Adaptive Systems:** Future work focusing on integrating these descriptors with Dynamic Movement Primitives (DMPs) promises a compact and adaptable formulation for trajectory generation. This would allow robots to not only express uncertainty but also to continuously regulate its intensity, spatially adapt, and blend behaviors based on dynamically changing environmental conditions and human feedback.

In conclusion, this research provides a robust framework for communicating robot uncertainty through motion, offering a significant step towards more transparent, intuitive, and effective human-robot collaboration by allowing robots to express their internal state in a perceptually interpretable manner.
