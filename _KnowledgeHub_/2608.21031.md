# PhysCaP: Grounding Code-as-Policy Agent with Physics-Informed Exploration

> [!summary] Summary
> PhysCaP augments Code-as-Policy robot agents with a physics-informed exploration layer: training-free modules infer object mass and stiffness from proprioception alone, and a dual-agent Planner (when to explore/stop) plus Prioritizer (what to explore first) targets interactions efficiently, beating passive VLA and naive interactive baselines on hidden-property manipulation tasks with fewer interactions and less time.

## Problem

- Vision-language-action policies imitate demonstrations via passive observation and cannot infer latent physical properties like mass or stiffness needed for manipulation.
- Code-as-policy agents structure perception, reasoning, and control as modules but remain fundamentally passive, lacking mechanisms to actively acquire missing physical information through interaction.
- Reinforcement learning can in principle discover information-seeking behaviors but is sample-inefficient under sparse, delayed rewards and yields uninterpretable, hard-to-modify policies.

## Method

- ==Physical Property Extraction (PhysX)== modules estimate object mass from differential joint torque during a fixed lift trajectory, and stiffness from gripper jaw displacement versus normalized motor effort during a squeeze routine, both training-free and sensor-free.
- A **Planner agent** decides when exploration is necessary, outputs a JSON list of candidate objects with expected information to gather, and serves as a dynamic stopping criterion once evidence suffices.
- A **Prioritizer agent** filters implausible or redundant candidates from the Planner's list and ranks the rest with a VLM-assigned priority score, enabling targeted rather than exhaustive interaction.

## Results

- On real-world tasks **PhysCaP** reaches **9/10**, **8/10**, and **9/10** success across Find Blue Cube, Identify Empty Can, and Pick Ripe Avocado, using as few as **1.33-2.5** object interactions versus 2.7-4 for CaP+PhysX baselines.
- In simulated LIBERO Identify Empty Cup, PhysCaP achieves **78%** success with **1.44** interactions, outperforming CaP+PhysX (**74%**, 2.16) and CaP+PhysX+Planner (**62%**, 1.45), while OpenVLA and pi-0.5 score **0%** and **4%**.
- A merged Planner-Prioritizer variant (PhysCaP-joint) degrades performance versus the dual-agent design, and an oracle test with ground-truth mass lifts Identify Empty Can success to a perfect **10/10**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Separating exploration decisions (when/what to explore) into distinct Planner and Prioritizer roles outperforms merging them into one agent, which collapses into unstructured exhaustive plans.
> - Latent physical properties can be recovered from ordinary proprioceptive signals (joint torque, gripper displacement) without added sensing hardware, making active perception cheap to retrofit onto existing arms.
> - Passive VLA policies fail near-completely under partial physical observability, showing that scaling imitation data alone does not substitute for explicit interaction-driven information-seeking.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the intersection of embodied AI, robot learning, and large language model (LLM) applications in robotics. Recent advancements in vision-language-action (VLA) policies have enabled robots to perform complex manipulation tasks by leveraging large-scale imitation learning and pretrained vision-language representations. However, these systems primarily operate on passive observation, reproducing demonstrated behaviors, and typically lack the ability to infer or actively acquire latent physical properties of objects. This limitation restricts their performance in partially observable environments where critical task-relevant information (e.g., an object's mass or stiffness) is not visually evident.

Concurrently, agentic robot learning frameworks, particularly "code-as-policy" systems, have emerged as a method to mitigate the reasoning limitations of monolithic VLA models. These frameworks decompose perception, planning, and control into modular, executable components, leveraging foundation models like LLMs and vision-language models (VLMs) for structured, closed-loop decision-making. Such systems offer greater interpretability and sample efficiency. However, existing code-as-policy agents are generally constrained by passive perception, focusing on semantic and procedural reasoning without mechanisms for actively interacting with the environment to acquire missing physical information.

This work addresses a recognized gap in the capabilities of both VLA and current code-as-policy systems: the explicit and efficient acquisition of hidden physical properties through interaction. While reinforcement learning can theoretically discover information-seeking behaviors, it is often sample-inefficient and yields policies that are difficult to interpret. Prior work on active perception and physical measurement has explored reducing uncertainty through interaction, but these approaches often decouple perception from higher-level task decision-making. PhysCaP integrates sensor-free proprioceptive estimation with guided, task-level exploration within a code-as-policy framework, aiming to bridge the gap between semantic reasoning and physical interaction for robust manipulation in uncertain environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for PhysCaP stems from the observation that human intelligence often involves actively acquiring information through physical interaction, rather than solely relying on passive observation. For robots to perform robust manipulation tasks in complex, partially observable real-world environments, they need similar capabilities to infer latent physical properties crucial for decision-making.

The limitations of existing robotic systems highlight several specific challenges that PhysCaP aims to address:

1. **Passive Perception in VLAs**: Current VLA policies, despite their success in imitating demonstrations, are fundamentally limited by passive observation. They excel at reproducing task completion behaviors but generally do not learn information-seeking interactions. This leads to failures when critical physical properties are hidden or ambiguous (e.g., identifying an empty can or a ripe avocado solely by sight).
2. **Lack of Active Physical Grounding in Code-as-Policy Systems**: While code-as-policy frameworks offer structured reasoning and leverage advanced foundation models, they have primarily focused on semantic and procedural understanding. They typically lack explicit mechanisms for actively acquiring missing physical information from the environment through direct interaction. This leaves them unable to address tasks requiring physical property inference.
3. **Inefficiency of Naive Exploration**: Introducing active exploration into robotic tasks presents a trade-off. Without intelligent guidance, a robot might either under-explore (failing due to insufficient information) or over-explore (performing redundant interactions, incurring unnecessary time and energy costs). There is a need for mechanisms that balance the cost of exploration with the value of the information obtained.
4. **Reliance on Specialized Sensors**: Many existing approaches for physical property estimation require dedicated sensing hardware (e.g., tactile sensors, external force-torque sensors). The motivation exists to develop methods that can infer such properties using only standard robot proprioception, enhancing the generalizability and accessibility of such capabilities.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of PhysCaP are:

- To augment code-as-policy frameworks with a "physics-informed exploration layer" that enables explicit information-seeking through physical interaction.
- To develop training-free physical property extraction modules that can estimate latent object properties, such as mass and stiffness, directly from standard robot proprioceptive feedback (e.g., joint torques and gripper data) without requiring additional sensors.
- To design an intelligent exploration framework that can reason about missing information, decide when and how to acquire it, integrate new observations, and determine when sufficient information has been gathered.
- To balance exploration efficiency by employing a dual-agent design: a Planner to determine when to explore and when to stop, and a Prioritizer to filter implausible interactions and rank candidates based on heuristic priority scores.
- To demonstrate the effectiveness and efficiency of this integrated approach on real-world and simulated manipulation tasks that explicitly require inference of hidden physical properties.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PhysCaP (Physics-Informed Code-as-Policy agent) integrates physical property estimation with a structured, agentic exploration framework. The system operates by augmenting existing code-as-policy architectures with a novel physics-informed exploration layer, supported by a dual-agent planning mechanism.

The core components and their functionalities are as follows:

#### 3.1. Physical Property Extraction Modules (PhysX)

These modules are designed to estimate latent physical properties using only the robot's proprioceptive feedback, thus being "training-free" and requiring no additional sensors.

- **Object Mass Measurement (`get_mass`)**:
 - **Principle**: Infers object mass from the robot's joint torques.
 - **Procedure**: The robot executes a fixed lift trajectory, raising its end-effector 15 cm above the grasp point. It first records baseline joint torques (`τ_empty`) while performing this motion with an empty gripper. Subsequently, it grasps the object, repeats the identical motion, and records the loaded joint torques (`τ_loaded`). Short pauses are introduced at the measurement pose to allow vibrations to settle.
 - **Calculation**: The differential torque (`∆τ = τ_loaded - τ_empty`) isolates the gravitational contribution of the object. The object's mass (`ˆm`) is then calculated using the formula `ˆm = (J_z · ∆τ) / (g|J_z|^2)`, where `J_z` is the end-effector Jacobian projected onto the vertical direction, and `g` is the gravitational acceleration. This formulation provides a pose-robust mass estimate.

- **Object Stiffness Measurement (`get_stiffness`)**:
 - **Principle**: Estimates object rigidity from gripper jaw displacement and normalized motor effort.
 - **Procedure (Two Phases)**:
 1. **Contact Detection**: The gripper closes in small increments. Upon detecting a force response, a brief "backoff test" is performed by slightly reopening the jaws. A proportional drop in motor effort (`∆ˆf`) confirms true physical contact and rules out internal friction. The gripper jaw displacement at this verified contact is recorded as `d_0`.
 2. **Deformation Measurement**: The gripper continues closing while recording pairs of jaw displacement (`d_i`) and normalized motor effort (`ˆf_i`). This continues until a target effort threshold (`f* = 0.50`) is reached. Linear interpolation is used to determine the displacement `d(f*)` at this target effort.
 - **Calculation**: The deformation `∆d = |d_0 - d(f*)|` is computed. This value is then mapped to a discrete stiffness level `s ∈ {1, ..., 5}` (1: Ultra-Soft to 5: Rigid) using pre-calibrated thresholds. For robustness, five measurements are taken per object per session, and the final stiffness is determined by majority vote.

#### 3.2. Low-level Robot Control

PhysCaP builds upon the CaP-Agent0 framework, providing modular APIs to ground high-level agentic reasoning in real-world manipulation. These APIs include `get_object_pose()`, `goto_pose()`, `open_gripper()`, and `close_gripper()`. Object localization for `get_object_pose()` is performed using Molmo-based object localization and ZED 2i depth estimation. This abstraction allows the system to map semantic reasoning to embodiment-specific trajectories and controllers.

#### 3.3. Dual-Agent Exploration Framework

To manage the trade-off between exploration cost and information gain, PhysCaP employs a dual-agent architecture:

- **Planner Agent**:
 - **Function**: Determines if current observations are sufficient for task completion. If not, it identifies missing physical information and initiates targeted exploration. It also acts as a dynamic stopping criterion, terminating exploration once enough evidence is gathered.
 - **Input**: Task description and visual scene.
 - **Output**: A JSON-formatted list of potential exploration candidates. Each candidate specifies an object name, a description, the type of information expected from the interaction, and the physical action required.
 - **Backbone**: Gemini 3.1 Pro is used for its VLM capabilities.

- **Prioritizer Agent**:
 - **Function**: Refines the Planner's list of exploration candidates to improve interaction efficiency and prevent over-exploration.
 - **Process**: It filters out implausible or redundant exploration plans based on visual heuristics (e.g., eliminating objects that are visually unsuitable for a given property check). It then assigns a priority score to the remaining candidates, ranking them by their estimated relevance and efficiency for obtaining task-critical information.
 - **Output**: A reordered list of candidates, each with a priority score and a textual reason for that score. The highest-priority candidate is selected for execution.
 - **Backbone**: Gemini 3.1 Pro is used.

#### 3.4. Coding Agent

The Coding Agent, based on the CaP-Agent0 M3 setting with a Visual Differencing Module, receives the output from the Prioritizer (the top-ranked exploration candidate) or the Planner (the final task execution plan if sufficient information is available). It generates executable Python code that invokes the relevant PhysX modules and low-level control APIs to perform the specified interaction or task completion steps. This closed-loop process ensures that robot actions are dynamically informed by acquired physical data.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of PhysCaP was conducted across three real-world tabletop manipulation tasks and one simulated task, comparing its performance against several baselines.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup and Metrics

- **Hardware**: A 7-DoF AgileX PiPER robotic arm with a gripper, a ZED 2i depth camera, and a workstation with an RTX 3090 GPU.
- **Foundation Models**: Gemini 3.1 Pro served as the backbone for the Planner, Prioritizer, and Coding agents, with Molmo2 used for object grounding.
- **Evaluation Protocol**: 10 trials per real-world task. Metrics included Task Success Rate (SR ↑), Objects Interacted (OI ↓, average number of physical exploratory interactions), and Execution Time (Time ↓, total robot execution time). OI and Time were reported only for successful episodes.

#### 4.2. Real-World Tasks

1. **Find Blue Cube**: Locate a blue cube hidden under one of three cups of varying sizes. This task required geometric reasoning to eliminate cups too small to conceal the cube.
2. **Identify Empty Can**: Identify a single empty soda can among four (two sealed, two open with straws; one open is empty, one is half-full). This task required combining semantic priors (straw indicates open) with mass measurement.
3. **Pick Ripe Avocado**: Identify a ripe avocado among four (two green unripe, two dark-skinned; one dark is ripe, one is hard). This required combining visual priors (darker skin implies ripeness) with stiffness measurement.

#### 4.3. Baselines and Variants

- **CaP**: A vision-only baseline (CaP-Agent0) with no physical exploration.
- **CaP+PhysX**: CaP augmented with PhysX modules, but performing exhaustive interaction on all objects.
- **CaP+PhysX+Planner**: CaP+PhysX further enhanced with a VLM-based planner for a stopping criterion, but interactions still selected randomly.
- **PhysCaP-joint**: An ablation where Planner and Prioritizer functions were merged into a single agent.
- **PhysCaP (Ours)**: The full proposed model with a dual-agent Planner and Prioritizer.

#### 4.4. Main Results (Real-World)

Table 1 (in the paper) summarizes the performance:

- **CaP** exhibited low success rates (e.g., 2/10 for "Identify Empty Can", 1/10 for "Pick Ripe Avocado") because it could not infer hidden physical properties. Its execution time, when successful, was low due to random guessing, but this was not indicative of reliable task completion.
- **CaP+PhysX** achieved higher success rates (e.g., 7/10 and 9/10) by leveraging PhysX modules but incurred significantly higher interaction counts and execution times due to exhaustive exploration of all candidates.
- **CaP+PhysX+Planner** showed slight improvements in efficiency (OI and Time) by incorporating a stopping criterion, but its performance was still limited by the random order of exploration.
- **PhysCaP (Ours)** consistently achieved the highest success rates (e.g., 9/10, 8/10, 9/10 across tasks) with substantially fewer objects interacted and reduced execution times. This demonstrates its ability to conduct targeted and efficient exploration. For example, in "Find Blue Cube," PhysCaP reduced interactions to 1.33 on average, compared to 3 for CaP+PhysX+Planner.

#### 4.5. Validation of Physical Property Extraction Modules (PhysX)

- **Mass Measurement**: Evaluations with five reference calibration masses (13g to 963g) over 20 trials showed strong alignment with ground truth and high relative accuracy, reliably distinguishing mass differences. An oracle test (replacing hardware-derived mass with ground truth) for "Identify Empty Can" yielded a 10/10 success rate, indicating the concept's robustness despite potential hardware noise.
- **Stiffness Measurement**: Validated using a custom 3D-printed button with variable rubber bands and real avocados of varying ripeness. The module accurately mapped force-displacement profiles to discrete stiffness levels, correlating with human tactile intuition for object compliance.

#### 4.6. Effect of Merging Planner and Prioritizer

The **PhysCaP-joint** variant, where the Planner and Prioritizer were merged, showed slightly degraded performance compared to the dual-agent PhysCaP (lower SR, higher OI/Time in some tasks). This finding supports the architectural decision to separate planning (when to explore/stop) from prioritization (what to explore next), suggesting that a structured decomposition enhances multi-objective reasoning and efficient sequencing of interactions.

#### 4.7. Comparisons to VLAs in Simulation

The "Identify Empty Can" task was replicated in the LIBERO environment (simulated Franka arm, mass revealed after interaction) to compare PhysCaP against state-of-the-art VLA models (OpenVLA, π₀.₅, MolmoAct2).

- **VLA Baselines**: Achieved near-zero success rates (0% for OpenVLA, 4% for π₀.₅, 23% for MolmoAct2). These models failed because they lack mechanisms for hypothesis-driven exploration and cannot infer hidden physical properties from passive vision alone.
- **CaP-based Architectures**: Succeeded in the simulation. PhysCaP achieved the highest success rate (78%) with minimal interactions (1.44) and efficient execution time (71.24s).

This simulation study corroborated the real-world findings, highlighting a fundamental limitation of current VLAs in partially observable settings and underscoring the effectiveness of PhysCaP's active perception approach.

#### 4.8. VLM/LLM Comparisons

Ablation studies (Appendix G) demonstrated that PhysCaP's architecture is model-agnostic. Replacing Gemini 3.1 Pro with Claude Opus 4.8 or GPT-5.6 Sol Pro as the backbone for the agents yielded comparable or, in the case of Claude Opus 4.8, slightly improved performance (e.g., 10/10 SR, 2.4 OI, 217.43s Time on "Identify Empty Can"). This suggests that PhysCaP's performance can scale with advancements in underlying foundation models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents PhysCaP, a system designed to enable robotic agents to actively acquire physical information through interaction, integrated within a code-as-policy framework. The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

- **Enhanced Robotic Autonomy in Partially Observable Environments**: PhysCaP directly addresses a limitation of current vision-language-action (VLA) and code-as-policy systems, which primarily rely on passive observation. By enabling robots to explicitly infer latent physical properties like mass and stiffness, PhysCaP allows robots to operate more robustly and effectively in real-world scenarios where critical information is often hidden or ambiguous. This capability is fundamental for tasks that require a deeper understanding of object physics beyond visual cues.
- **Improved Efficiency and Reduced Interaction Costs**: The dual-agent Planner and Prioritizer design within PhysCaP allows for targeted and efficient exploration. By intelligently determining when to explore, filtering implausible actions, and prioritizing the most informative interactions, the system minimizes redundant physical contacts. This translates to fewer object interactions and reduced execution times, which are critical for practical robot deployment where interaction costs (wear and tear, time, energy) are significant.
- **Novel Sensor-Free Physical Property Estimation**: The introduction of training-free PhysX modules that infer mass and stiffness solely from standard robot proprioception (joint torques, gripper data) removes the need for additional, specialized sensing hardware. This approach simplifies hardware requirements, reduces system complexity, and improves the generalizability of physical property inference across different robot platforms.
- **Bridging Semantic Reasoning and Physical Grounding**: PhysCaP unifies the high-level semantic and procedural reasoning capabilities of large language models within a code-as-policy framework with low-level physical interaction. This integration allows robots to reason about "what to measure" and "when to stop," leading to more informed and adaptable decision-making in physically complex tasks.
- **Model-Agnostic and Scalable Framework**: The demonstrated ability to integrate different VLM/LLM backbones (Gemini, Claude, GPT) suggests that PhysCaP is not tied to a specific foundation model. This architectural flexibility indicates that the framework can benefit from ongoing advancements in large language and vision models, allowing its performance to scale as these models improve.

Potential impacts include:

- **More Capable Manipulation Robots**: Robots could perform a wider range of manipulation tasks that require subtle physical distinctions, such as quality control, sorting, assembly, and domestic assistance.
- **Advanced Human-Robot Collaboration**: Robots could better understand and respond to the physical properties of objects in shared workspaces, leading to more natural and effective collaboration.
- **Foundation for Future Active Learning in Robotics**: PhysCaP's approach could serve as a foundation for future research into active learning strategies where robots autonomously design experiments to gather necessary information, accelerating their learning in novel environments.

Despite its advancements, the paper acknowledges limitations such as reliance on commercial VLM APIs (suggesting a shift to local, open-source models for consistency), dependence on 2D predictions for object localization, and hardware communication latency. Addressing these areas represents avenues for future research and could further enhance PhysCaP's robustness and real-world applicability.
