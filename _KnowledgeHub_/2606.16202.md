# EgoPhys: Learning Generalizable Physics Models of Deformable Objects from Egocentric Video

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at UC San Diego developed EgoPhys, a framework that constructs deformable physical digital twins from single egocentric RGB-only video by leveraging a codebook-based physics prior for generalizable stiffness prediction. This method achieved lower Chamfer distance (0.015) and track error (0.025) in resimulation, demonstrated zero-shot generalization on unseen objects, and facilitated successful sim-to-real transfer for robotic manipulation tasks with a physical xArm6 robot, reducing object-configuration error by up to 77.6%.

## Problem

- Accurately simulating complex deformable object dynamics from real-world human interactions is challenging for autonomous systems and robotics.
- Existing methods for physical digital twin construction rely on controlled third-person capture, depth sensing, or precise calibration, limiting scalability and applicability to diverse real-world scenarios.
- The lack of generalizable physics priors for deformable objects necessitates expensive per-object or per-scene optimization, hindering rapid adaptation to novel materials.

## Method

- **EgoPhys** utilizes a three-stage framework: Egocentric 4D Reconstruction from single RGB-only video, Per-Object Physics Initialization to fit a coarse spring-mass simulator, and a Codebook-Based Physics Prior for generalizable stiffness.
- It employs a novel ==material codebook== that distills object-specific stiffness properties into a compact, state-conditioned representation, predicting dense spring stiffness fields for unseen objects without per-spring test-time optimization.
- The framework focuses on egocentric (first-person) video captured from wearable cameras, overcoming the need for depth sensors or multi-view setups to reconstruct 4D object geometry and infer physics.

## Results

- EgoPhys achieved superior reconstruction and resimulation, yielding a Chamfer distance of **0.015** and track error of **0.025**, outperforming adapted baselines like PhysTwin (**0.024** CD, **0.035** TE) and Spring-Gaus.
- The framework demonstrated effective zero-shot generalization on 11 held-out sequences, maintaining higher physical and rendering metric performance compared to PhysTwin (e.g., **0.0319** CD and **0.0621** TE for EgoPhys vs. **0.0457** CD and **0.0818** TE for PhysTwin).
- Digital twins derived by EgoPhys enabled successful sim-to-real transfer for deformable manipulation tasks on a physical xArm6 robot, reducing object-configuration error (e.g., **77.6%** reduction in Chamfer distance for a pulling task) without real-world fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Egocentric RGB-only video, despite occlusions and camera motion, can provide sufficient information to reconstruct dynamic 4D point clouds of deformable objects and infer their underlying physics.
> - A compact codebook, consisting of learnable material prototypes that differentiate between tension and compression, effectively captures and generalizes dense spring stiffness fields across diverse deformable materials.
> - Parameterizing spring stiffness as a log-offset relative to a coarse, object-specific global stiffness (obtained via initial optimization) allows the learned codebook to refine and anchor predictions, leading to improved accuracy and stability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The ability to accurately simulate the physical world, particularly the complex dynamics of deformable objects like elastic materials and fabrics, remains a challenge in computer vision and robotics. Human beings naturally infer object physics through everyday interactions, a capability that autonomous systems currently lack. This deficiency limits the development and evaluation of robotic manipulation models, such as those for folding textiles, which could otherwise be accelerated through faithful simulation environments.

Current approaches to modeling object dynamics exhibit limitations. Generative models can produce visually plausible videos of object dynamics but do not explicitly model physics. This restricts their interpretability and their utility for action-conditioned simulations under novel interactions. Conversely, physics-based system identification methods aim to recover simulatable objects by optimizing physical parameters. However, these methods typically require controlled third-person capture, depth sensing, or precise calibration, which limits their scalability. More recent works like PhysTwin and PhysWorld reconstruct appearances and physically simulatable dynamics from interaction videos but still necessitate controlled capture and per-scene optimization. MatPhys predicts spring-mass parameters from single-view videos using learned material priors, but it does not specifically address the challenges inherent in egocentric RGB-only human interaction videos, which include significant camera motion, partial visibility, and hand-object occlusion.

EgoPhys addresses this gap by proposing a framework that constructs deformable physical digital twins from egocentric RGB-only video, a more scalable and natural data source. The work aims to overcome the reliance on controlled capture and depth sensing, which are common in existing methodologies, and to develop generalizable physics priors that can be transferred across unseen objects without requiring extensive per-object optimization. By focusing on egocentric observations, EgoPhys contributes to the development of robust real-to-sim pipelines that leverage human interaction data to build internal world representations for deformable-object planning in robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework, EgoPhys, capable of constructing deformable physical digital twins from egocentric RGB-only video, utilizing generalizable priors. This objective is driven by several motivations:

Firstly, replicating the human capacity for understanding object physics through common sense and interaction is a long-standing goal in artificial intelligence. For autonomous systems, especially in robotics, accurately inferring and simulating object physics is critical for developing robust manipulation policies. For example, tasks like folding laundry require accurate and interactive simulations to train and evaluate robotic models efficiently, circumventing the resource-intensive process of physical deployment.

Secondly, existing methods for physics-based simulation and digital twin generation face significant practical limitations. While generative models can produce visually convincing dynamics, they often lack an explicit physical basis, limiting their applicability for precise action-conditioned control or interpretability. Physics-based system identification methods, while capable of recovering simulatable objects, typically rely on controlled data acquisition environments (e.g., third-person views, depth sensors, or multi-view setups). These requirements make data collection for a diverse range of objects and interactions unscalable for real-world applications.

The motivation for EgoPhys specifically targets these scalability and generalizability challenges. The authors aim to:
- **Overcome data acquisition limitations:** By exclusively using egocentric RGB video—captured from a wearable camera—EgoPhys seeks to bypass the need for depth sensing or calibrated multi-view setups. This approach leverages the abundance of human interaction data available from first-person perspectives, offering a more scalable path to physical model acquisition.
- **Enable generalizable physics:** Current inverse-physics solutions often require expensive per-object or per-scene optimization. EgoPhys aims to distill these object-specific solutions into a compact, state-conditioned material codebook. This codebook is designed to predict dense spring stiffness fields for unseen objects without requiring per-spring test-time optimization, thereby enhancing generalization across diverse deformable materials and interaction styles.
- **Develop reusable priors:** The framework intends to learn transferable stiffness priors from a diverse set of egocentric human-object interactions, enabling the rapid initialization and refinement of physical models for novel objects.
- **Support robotic applications:** A key motivation is to demonstrate the practical utility of these egocentric-video-derived digital twins as internal world representations for deformable-object planning in robotics. This involves validating the framework's output in real-to-sim transfer experiments with a physical robot.
- **Create a benchmark dataset:** To facilitate training and evaluation in this novel setting, the research includes curating a new egocentric interaction dataset covering various deformable objects, scenes, and manipulation styles.

In summary, the core motivation is to move beyond computationally intensive, scene-specific physical parameter estimation towards a more scalable, generalizable, and human-data-driven approach to understanding and simulating deformable object physics for embodied AI and robotics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EgoPhys is structured as a three-stage framework: Egocentric 4D Reconstruction, Per-Object Physics Initialization, and Codebook-Based Physics Prior.

#### 3.1. Egocentric 4D Reconstruction

The first stage focuses on reconstructing temporally coherent 4D point clouds from a single egocentric RGB video. This is a challenging task due to factors such as camera motion, partial object visibility, and hand-object occlusion, coupled with the absence of depth sensing or calibrated multi-view capture.
1. **Frame Processing:** RGB frames are extracted from the video, undistorted to a pinhole model, and cropped.
2. **Segmentation and Tracking:** Object and manipulator masks are obtained using Grounded-SAM2 with text prompts, and then propagated across frames using SAM2's video tracker. Dense 2D trajectories of up to 5000 query pixels are extracted using CoTracker3. Tracks are filtered to remain within propagated semantic masks and to ensure motion consistency. Controller points are downsampled to 30 via farthest-point sampling.
3. **3D Lifting:** VGGT is applied independently to each frame to predict per-pixel 3D world coordinates and camera parameters. Tracked pixels are lifted to 3D world points, retaining only those that pass confidence (ρt ≥ 0.5) and depth range (0.2m < z < 1.5m) checks.
4. **4D Point Cloud Generation:** Mask-aware filtering and motion-consistency checks are applied to the lifted points to produce the final 4D point cloud.
5. **Geometry Completion:** If egocentric occlusion results in significant missing object geometry (e.g., the underside of an object), TRELLIS is used to complete the object's geometry. A mesh is fitted to the first-frame mask, and surface/interior samples are added. The ground plane is inferred from the lowest observed object points in the first frame.

#### 3.2. Per-Object Physics Initialization

In this stage, a coarse spring-mass simulator is fitted to each training sequence.
1. **Spring-Mass Representation:** Each deformable object is represented as a spring-mass graph G=(V, E). The force on each node (mass) is defined by spring forces (stiffness, rest length), damping, and external forces (gravity, collisions, control inputs). The simulator advances using explicit Euler integration.
2. **Parameter Estimation:** Graph topology and coarse physical parameters (α) are estimated by minimizing geometry (Chamfer distance) and motion (track error) discrepancies between the simulated states (ˆXt) and the reconstructed 4D point cloud (Xt) over the training window.
3. **Optimization:** A derivative-free optimizer, Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy (CMA-ES), is employed for this initial parameter estimation. It optimizes parameters such as uniform spring stiffness, node/controller radii, collision terms, damping coefficients, and strain limits, depending on the object type (flat or plush).
4. **Controller Spring Construction:** Controller points (from the human hand) are connected to object points via a radius search, with a nearest-neighbor fallback. Rest lengths for these controller springs are initialized from the observed hand-object distance at frame 0, ensuring forces arise from motion rather than pre-tension.
5. **Object-Specific Dynamics:** Bending springs are enabled for cloth-like objects and disabled for plush toys. Self-collision is activated for cloth-like configurations. Strain limiting is applied to structural springs under tension, and ground-contact force projection is used for particles within a contact band above the ground plane.

#### 3.3. Codebook-Based Physics Prior

This is the core mechanism for achieving generalizable physics. It distills dense per-object stiffness fields into a compact, shared material codebook, eliminating the need for per-spring test-time optimization on unseen objects.
1. **Stiffness Parameterization:** Instead of predicting absolute stiffness, each spring's stiffness (k_e,t) is parameterized as a log-stiffness offset (∆_e,t) relative to the CMA-estimated global stiffness (k_bar): log k_e,t = log k_bar + ∆_e,t. This anchors the learned corrections to the coarse, object-specific fit.
2. **Feature Vector:** For each spring, a feature vector (ϕ_e,t) is constructed by concatenating static graph features (e.g., rest length, endpoint degrees, spring flags, shape cues) and dynamic rollout features (e.g., strain, strain rate, orientation, height, controller flag).
3. **Asymmetric Response Modeling:** To account for different behaviors under tension and compression, two distinct prototype banks (C+ for tension, C- for compression) are used. Each prototype stores a scalar log-stiffness offset.
4. **Prototype Assignment and Prediction:** A lightweight MLP processes the feature vector ϕ_e,t and outputs soft prototype assignments (π_±_e,t) using softmax. The scalar offset ∆_e,t is then computed as a weighted sum of the prototypes from the appropriate bank (tension or compression). The final stiffness k_e,t is obtained by exponentiating the clipped log-stiffness.
5. **Training:** The shared assignment network and prototype banks are trained across a set of training objects. The loss function includes:
 - Chamfer geometry loss and motion/tracking loss between simulated and reconstructed states.
 - A distillation term that encourages the predicted log-stiffnesses to match dense inverse-physics stiffness targets obtained from per-object optimization.
 - Regularization terms for prototype offset magnitude (R_∆), diverse prototype usage (R_use), and decisive assignments (R_ent).
 - Training is sequential across objects, carrying a shared checkpoint.
6. **Test-time Inference:** For an unseen object, after the coarse CMA initialization provides the graph topology and global stiffness (k_bar), the learned codebook directly predicts dense spring stiffnesses using the feature vector ϕ_e,t without any further per-spring gradient-based optimization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of EgoPhys involved a curated egocentric interaction dataset and comparisons against adapted baselines in various scenarios.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Dataset:** A new dataset of 19 egocentric manipulation videos was created using a Meta Project Aria Gen 1 camera. Sequences are 7 seconds long, depicting interactions with diverse deformable objects (plush toys, towels, cloth, bags) and manipulation styles (lifting, pulling, pushing, folding). Each sequence used a 7:3 temporal train/test split. For codebook generalization, 8 sequences were used for training the prior, and 11 distinct sequences were held out for zero-shot evaluation.
- **Baselines:** Since no prior work directly addresses deformable physical twin construction from single egocentric RGB-only video, EgoPhys was compared against adapted versions of PhysTwin (a per-scene physical twin method for sparse RGB-D) and Spring-Gaus (a spring-mass 3DGS baseline). All methods received the same egocentric 4D observations, masks, tracks, and rendering protocol to ensure a fair comparison of physical modeling.
- **Metrics:** Evaluation included visual quality metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) and physical consistency metrics (Chamfer distance (CD), track error (TE), and Intersection over Union (IoU)).

#### 4.2. Reconstruction, Resimulation, and Future Prediction

In a per-sequence refinement setting (where the codebook prior is not used, similar to traditional inverse physics), EgoPhys demonstrated improvements over the adapted baselines.
- **Quantitative Results:** EgoPhys achieved lower Chamfer distance (0.015 vs. 0.024 for PhysTwin, 7.250 for Spring-Gaus) and track error (0.025 vs. 0.035 for PhysTwin, 0.844 for Spring-Gaus) during reconstruction and resimulation, and similar trends for future prediction. IoU and visual metrics also showed gains. These results indicate that EgoPhys provides better geometry, motion, and object-support consistency.
- **Qualitative Results:** Visualizations showed EgoPhys better matching observed deformation, while baselines exhibited divergence and instability under large egocentric deformations, failing to track hand movements and producing static simulations.

#### 4.3. Generalization to Unseen Objects and Interactions (Zero-Shot)

In the zero-shot setting, where EgoPhys utilized its learned physics codebook without per-spring test-time refinement, it demonstrated generalization capabilities.
- **Quantitative Results:** On 11 held-out object-interaction sequences (including unseen object categories and novel interaction patterns), EgoPhys outperformed the adapted PhysTwin across physical and rendering metrics. For example, EgoPhys achieved a Chamfer distance of 0.0319 and track error of 0.0621, compared to PhysTwin's 0.0457 and 0.0818, respectively. This indicated effective transfer of the learned prior.

#### 4.4. Analysis of the Learned Physics Prior (Ablations)

Ablation studies investigated the components of the learned physics prior:
- **Prototype Prior Efficacy:** The learned codebook improved held-out rollout accuracy compared to a CMA-ES-only variant (coarse anchor without a learned dense stiffness prior).
- **Codebook vs. Direct MLP:** Replacing the codebook with a direct MLP (same input features and backbone) led to a substantial worsening of both Chamfer distance and track error. This suggests that the prototype bottleneck functions as a useful regularizer, rather than merely increasing model capacity.
- **Dynamic Conditioning:** Comparing a static codebook (without motion conditioning) to a dynamic one (K=4 prototypes) showed that dynamic conditioning improved temporal consistency, reflected in lower test track error, while maintaining similar Chamfer distance.
- **Compactness of Codebook:** Increasing the number of prototypes from K=4 to K=8 or K=16 yielded only marginal changes in accuracy, suggesting that a compact K=4 dynamic codebook provides a favorable balance between accuracy and representation compactness.
- **Efficiency vs. Refinement:** Appendix A further demonstrated that EgoPhys's codebook inference method achieved better held-out rollout accuracy compared to PhysTwin-style dense per-sequence refinement, while being significantly faster (avoiding optimization loops that were 10.5x to 59.0x slower).

#### 4.5. Sim-to-Real Transfer for Deformable Manipulation

EgoPhys was deployed as a forward model in an MPPI planner to evaluate its utility for downstream manipulation planning on a physical xArm6 robot, without real-world fine-tuning.
- **Task Performance:** For lifting (fox and green monster plush toys) and pulling (Doraemon plush toy) tasks, the MPPI-planned trajectories, based on EgoPhys's digital twin, reduced object-configuration error after execution on the real robot.
- **Consistency:** The deformation patterns observed on the real robot were consistent with EgoPhys's simulated predictions. For instance, the Doraemon pull task showed a 77.6% reduction in Chamfer distance to the target configuration. The reductions were greater for pulling tasks, possibly due to the less ambiguous sliding contact compared to grasp-based lifting from RGB-only egocentric observations. These results suggest that EgoPhys can provide a functional forward model for zero-shot deformable-object planning from human video.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents EgoPhys, a framework that addresses several challenges in the domain of physical simulation and robot learning for deformable objects. Its significance stems from its novel approach and the potential impact it holds for future advancements.

Firstly, EgoPhys introduces a methodology for constructing deformable physical digital twins directly from a single egocentric RGB-only video. This capability is notable because it removes common constraints such as the need for depth sensing, calibrated multi-view capture, or controlled third-person recording environments. By leveraging data from wearable cameras, the framework taps into a scalable and natural source of human interaction data, which is crucial for building comprehensive physical models of the world.

Secondly, the development of a generalizable physics prior through a codebook-based representation is a key contribution. This mechanism allows EgoPhys to distill object-specific inverse-physics solutions into a compact, reusable material codebook. This enables the prediction of dense spring stiffness fields for unseen objects without requiring computationally expensive per-spring test-time optimization. This generalizability is important for reducing the effort and resources typically associated with adapting physical models to new objects or scenarios, thereby improving the efficiency of model deployment.

Thirdly, the demonstration of sim-to-real transfer with a physical xArm6 robot highlights the practical utility of the EgoPhys framework. The digital twins, initialized from a single egocentric human interaction video, serve as an internal world representation for deformable-object planning. The successful transfer of planned trajectories to a real robot, leading to a reduction in object-configuration error, suggests that egocentric RGB observations can indeed form a scalable and effective path toward robust real-to-sim pipelines. This could significantly accelerate the development and evaluation cycles for robotic manipulation policies, particularly for tasks involving complex deformable materials, by reducing the reliance on direct real-world experimentation.

The potential impact of EgoPhys extends to the broader field of embodied AI. By providing a method for robots to acquire and utilize physical understanding from human interaction data, it paves the way for more intelligent and adaptable autonomous systems. Such systems could better reason about and interact with the physical properties of objects in unstructured environments, leading to advancements in areas like domestic robotics, manufacturing, and assistance technologies. The work also encourages further research into the use of human interaction data as a knowledge source for physical intelligence and suggests avenues for improving contact modeling and closed-loop replanning in more complex deformable manipulation tasks.

While the study acknowledges limitations, such as the modest scale of its dataset and the scope of its robot experiments, its methodological innovations and empirical results position EgoPhys as a foundational step toward more scalable and generalizable physical understanding for autonomous agents.
