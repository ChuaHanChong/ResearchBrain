# Mitigating Reward Over-optimization in Direct Alignment Algorithms with Importance Sampling

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed Importance Sampling Direct Alignment Algorithms (IS-DAAs) to mitigate reward over-optimization in Large Language Models by re-weighting offline preference data. This method improved win rates on instruction-following tasks by approximately 3% over standard DPO, maintaining stable performance and better KL efficiency.

## Problem

- Direct Alignment Algorithms (DAAs) like DPO are susceptible to 'over-optimization,' where model performance degrades as training progresses due to excessive policy divergence from the reference.
- The implicit regularization in offline DAAs weakens as the trained policy moves away from the reference policy, failing to adequately constrain responses outside the original data distribution.
- Existing solutions for DAA over-optimization either incur high computational costs (online methods) or offer limited efficacy (explicit KL penalties and other regularization techniques).

## Method

- Proposed ==Importance Sampling== DAAs (**IS-DAAs**) which re-weight the offline DAA objective using importance sampling ratios to effectively approximate an on-policy objective.
- Incorporated a clipping mechanism for the importance ratios to mitigate high variance and ensure stable training.
- The method is theoretically shown to ensure that the gradient of the importance-sampled weighted squared loss aligns with the true KL divergence gradient, providing robust regularization.

## Results

- IS-DAAs demonstrated robustness against over-optimization in a controlled summarization task, preventing the performance degradation observed in standard DPO, RPO, and Length-DPO.
- IS-DPO achieved approximately a **3%** increase in win rate over standard DPO on the Anthropic-HH instruction-following dataset, as judged by both a trained reward model and GPT-4.
- The method consistently outperformed DPO, RPO, and Length-DPO on AlpacaEval 2.0 and MT-Bench benchmarks, showing competitive or superior performance to χ-PO.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Standard offline DAAs suffer from diminishing influence of implicit regularization as the policy drifts, leading to ineffective KL regularization.
> - Importance sampling can effectively enforce KL regularization in offline settings by transforming the objective to estimate on-policy expectations, even when the learned policy deviates significantly from the reference.
> - Importance sampling acts as an implicit support constraint, preventing updates for samples outside the reference policy's support and inducing an early stopping effect that inherently mitigates over-optimization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of aligning Large Language Models (LLMs) with human preferences and values is a central area of research in modern artificial intelligence. Two primary approaches have emerged for preference learning: Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and Direct Alignment Algorithms (DAAs).

**RLHF** is characterized by a multi-stage process involving Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) of a base model, training a separate reward model to capture human preferences, and then fine-tuning the LLM policy using online reinforcement learning algorithms like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). While RLHF has demonstrated empirical success in aligning LLMs, its complexity and computational demands, arising from repeated reward model queries and sampling from the current policy, present significant challenges.

**DAAs**, exemplified by Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and Identity Preference Optimization (IPO), offer a simplified alternative. These methods bypass the need for an explicit reward model and the online RL training phase by directly optimizing the LLM policy on an offline dataset of human preferences. This streamlined approach makes DAAs computationally more efficient and simpler to implement, positioning them as an attractive option for LLM alignment.

However, recent research has identified a limitation of DAAs: their susceptibility to **over-optimization**. This phenomenon describes a degradation in model performance as training progresses, a consequence of the model diverging excessively from its reference policy. Studies have indicated that DAAs tend to experience greater performance decline due to over-optimization compared to online methods, which can explicitly enforce regularization (e.g., reverse KL divergence penalties) and utilize online data sampling to maintain policy stability. The issue in DAAs is attributed to the reliance on offline data, where optimizing the loss function can inadvertently increase the probability of responses outside the original data distribution, for which preference labels are not reliably available. Existing attempts to mitigate over-optimization in DAAs include enforcing explicit KL divergence penalties (which can be costly and sensitive to hyperparameters), online variants of DPO (which lose the computational advantage of offline methods), or other regularization techniques such as adding SFT loss (Regularized Preference Optimization, RPO), χ-squared preference optimization (χ-PO), or length regularization.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by identifying the specific issue of inadequate regularization in offline DAAs as the primary cause of their over-optimization and the performance gap relative to online methods. It proposes a novel, efficient, and offline solution using importance sampling to address this gap. The work aims to enhance the robustness and performance of DAAs, thereby advancing the development of more stable and effective LLMs while retaining the computational benefits of offline training.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the observed performance degradation, termed "over-optimization," prevalent in Direct Alignment Algorithms (DAAs) used for aligning Large Language Models (LLMs) with human preferences. While DAAs offer a simpler and more computationally efficient alternative to Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) by directly optimizing the policy on offline preference data, they have been shown to be more susceptible to performance decline as training progresses and the policy drifts from its initial state. This issue leads DAAs to underperform online alignment methods.

The authors hypothesize that this performance discrepancy and the susceptibility to over-optimization in offline DAAs are primarily due to an inherent limitation in their regularization mechanisms when using only off-policy data. Specifically, when the trained policy ($\pi_\theta$) begins to diverge from the initial reference policy ($\pi_{ref}$) (which was used to collect the preference data), the implicit regularization effects in DAAs—such as those derived from the weighted squared loss term—become insufficient. This implicit regularization provides only a local approximation of KL regularization around the initial offline data distribution. As $\pi_\theta$ moves further away from $\pi_{ref}$, this approximation weakens, failing to adequately prevent the policy from generating responses outside the distribution of the collected offline data. Optimizing these out-of-distribution responses, for which no explicit preference labels exist, can lead to performance degradation.

Motivated by the need for an efficient and effective solution that avoids the high computational costs associated with online sampling or explicit KL divergence penalties, the key objectives of this research are:

1. **To thoroughly investigate the over-optimization phenomenon** in offline alignment methods, specifically identifying the diminishing influence of regularization as the trained policy deviates from the reference policy.
2. **To propose a novel importance-sampling approach, termed Importance Sampling DAAs (IS-DAAs)**, designed to directly mitigate this over-optimization problem in DAAs. This approach aims to provide a more effective approximation of on-policy learning using only offline data, thereby enhancing regularization.
3. **To address the inherent high-variance issue associated with importance sampling** by incorporating a clipping mechanism for the importance ratios, ensuring stable and reliable training.
4. **To empirically demonstrate the efficacy of IS-DAAs** through extensive experimentation, showing that the proposed method can effectively mitigate over-optimization, achieve superior performance compared to existing regularization approaches, and maintain minimal computational overhead.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to provide a robust, scalable, and computationally efficient method for aligning LLMs that overcomes a significant limitation of existing offline DAAs, thereby advancing the field of LLM alignment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology, Importance Sampling DAAs (IS-DAAs), is designed to address the inadequate regularization in offline DAAs by effectively estimating an on-policy objective using only off-policy data. The approach draws inspiration from the observation that the implicit regularization in DAAs weakens as the learned policy deviates from the reference policy, leading to over-optimization.

**1. Importance Sampling Objective:**
The central idea is to re-weight the offline DAA objective using importance sampling ratios. The standard DAA loss function, such as DPO's negative log-likelihood, is modified to an Importance Sampling DPO (IS-DPO) objective:

$L_{IS-DPO}(\pi_\theta, \pi_{ref}) = - E_{x \sim D, y_w, y_l \sim \pi_{ref}(\cdot|x)} \left[ w(x, y_w, y_l) \log \sigma \left( \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_w|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y_w|x)} - \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_l|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y_l|x)} \right) \right]$

Here, $w(x, y_w, y_l)$ represents the importance weight. This weight is calculated as the product of the likelihood ratios for the winning ($y_w$) and losing ($y_l$) responses under the current policy ($\pi_\theta$) relative to the reference policy ($\pi_{ref}$):

$w(x, y_w, y_l) = \frac{\pi_\theta(y_w|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y_w|x)} \frac{\pi_\theta(y_l|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y_l|x)}$

This importance weighting adjusts the contribution of each preference pair (x, $y_w$, $y_l$) to the loss, effectively transforming the expectation over the reference policy's distribution ($\pi_{ref}$) into an unbiased estimate of the expectation over the current policy's distribution ($\pi_\theta$). This formulation theoretically allows IS-DAAs to approximate the behavior of online alignment without requiring actual online sampling.

**2. Theoretical Justification:**
The paper formally demonstrates that, under the assumption that the support of the reference policy is equivalent to the support of the learned policy (Supp($\pi_{ref}$) = Supp($\pi_\theta$)), the IS-DPO objective (Eqn. 11) provides an unbiased estimate of the online DPO objective (Eqn. 10). Crucially, the authors prove that incorporating this importance ratio ensures that the gradient of the weighted squared loss term, when used with importance sampling, becomes equivalent to the gradient of the KL divergence between $\pi_\theta$ and $\pi_{ref}$. This directly addresses the problem of ineffective regularization observed in standard offline DAAs, establishing a theoretically sound mechanism for maintaining effective KL regularization.

**3. Mitigating High Variance with Clipping:**
A known challenge with importance sampling is the potential for high variance, where extreme importance ratio values can lead to unstable gradients and training. To counteract this, the authors propose using a truncated importance weighting estimator. The importance ratio is clipped to a maximum value $\epsilon$:

$w(x, y_w, y_l) = \max \left( \frac{\pi_\theta(y_w|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y_w|x)} \frac{\pi_\theta(y_l|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y_l|x)}, \epsilon \right)$

This clipping mechanism regularizes the importance weights, trading off some bias for a significant reduction in variance, thereby stabilizing the training process. The parameter $\epsilon$ allows for control over this bias-variance trade-off.

**4. Analysis of Regularization Effect:**
The paper provides a detailed analysis, including a multi-arm bandit problem, to illustrate the difference between standard DAA regularization and IS-DAA regularization. It shows that in standard DAAs, the cosine similarity between the gradient of the weighted squared loss and the true KL divergence gradient decreases as $\pi_\theta$ deviates from $\pi_{ref}$ (i.e., at lower $\beta$ values), sometimes even becoming negative. Conversely, the importance-sampled weighted squared loss gradient maintains consistently high cosine similarity with the true KL divergence gradient across a wide range of $\beta$ values, indicating that IS-DAAs enforce a more robust and effective regularization even when the learned policy moves significantly away from the reference policy.

**5. Implementation Details and Baselines:**
- **Base Model:** Llama-3.2-3B was used for experiments, initially fine-tuned via SFT.
- **Baselines:** The performance of IS-DAAs was compared against several existing methods designed to address over-optimization in DAAs:
 - **DPO:** Standard Direct Preference Optimization.
 - **RPO (Regularized Preference Optimization):** DPO augmented with an additional SFT loss.
 - **χ-PO:** Combines $\chi^2$ with KL regularization for stronger constraint.
 - **Length-DPO:** Incorporates length regularization to address length exploitation, a common pattern of reward over-optimization.
- **Computational Overhead:** The authors state that the implementation of IS-DAAs incurs minimal computational overhead, preserving the efficiency benefits of offline DAAs.
- **Datasets:** Experiments were conducted on the Reddit TL;DR summarization dataset (in a controlled environment with a golden reward model) and the Anthropic Helpful and Harmless (HH) dataset for instruction following, along with the UltraFeedback dataset.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluations demonstrate that Importance Sampling DAAs (IS-DAAs) effectively mitigate reward over-optimization and achieve performance improvements compared to existing direct alignment algorithms and regularization methods.

**1. TL;DR Summarization Task (Controlled Environment):**
In a controlled setting where a "golden" reward model provided ground-truth preferences, IS-DAAs exhibited the following:

- **Mitigation of Over-optimization:** While standard DPO, RPO, and Length-DPO exhibited clear signs of over-optimization, with performance degrading as training progressed (especially under low KL constraint strength, $\beta=0.01$), IS-DPO demonstrated robustness. It prevented performance degradation and maintained stability at later epochs.
- **KL Efficiency:** IS-DPO was observed to be "significantly more KL efficient." This implies that for a given "budget" of KL divergence (measuring how far the policy drifts from the reference), IS-DPO achieved a higher win rate, indicating more effective utilization of the optimization budget.
- **Avoidance of Early Convergence:** Unlike other methods that reached peak performance early in training (e.g., after 30-40% of data) and then degraded, IS-DPO continued to improve or stabilized, demonstrating resistance to the early convergence phenomenon while maintaining a lower KL budget.

**2. Clipping Ratio ($\epsilon$) Ablation Study:**
An ablation study on the clipping ratio $\epsilon$ (used to mitigate high variance in importance sampling) revealed that a moderate value of $\log \epsilon = 1.0$ yielded the highest win rate (approximately 92%). This optimal value was associated with a relatively higher KL divergence, suggesting a trade-off between bias and variance. Higher $\epsilon$ values allow for larger policy updates, which can reflect the on-policy objective more accurately, but excessively large values risk instability due to a few dominant samples.

**3. Instruction Following Task:**
Evaluations on instruction-following benchmarks further supported the efficacy of IS-DAAs:

- **Anthropic-HH Dataset:** IS-DPO achieved notable improvements, demonstrating approximately a 3% increase in win rate over standard DPO, as judged by both a trained reward model and GPT-4 evaluations.
- **AlpacaEval 2.0 and MT-Bench:** IS-DPO consistently outperformed DPO, RPO, and Length-DPO on these benchmarks. It also demonstrated competitive performance with $\chi$-PO on AlpacaEval 2.0 and slightly superior performance on MT-Bench.

**4. Importance Sampling as Support Constraint (Implicit Early Stopping):**
The research provides an interpretation of importance sampling's effect as an implicit support constraint, which can function as an early stopping mechanism:

- **Support Violation:** When the learned policy ($\pi_\theta$) generates responses outside the support of the reference policy ($\pi_{ref}$), IS-DAAs effectively cease updating for those specific samples. This prevents the model from extrapolating inappropriately.
- **Empirical Evidence:** The averaged importance ratio ($\pi_\theta / \pi_{ref}$) was observed to decrease throughout training. This indicates that as training progresses, $\pi_\theta$ assigns lower probabilities to samples from $\pi_{ref}$, leading to fewer and fewer samples effectively contributing to updates. This mechanism inherently slows down or halts updates when the policy moves too far from the reference data distribution, thus inducing an early stopping effect and preventing performance degradation due to over-optimization.

In summary, the findings consistently indicate that IS-DAAs provide a robust and efficient solution to the over-optimization problem in offline DAAs, yielding superior performance and better regularization characteristics across different tasks and evaluation metrics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Importance Sampling DAAs (IS-DAAs) represents a significant contribution to the field of Large Language Model (LLM) alignment, with several potential impacts:

**1. Effective Mitigation of Over-optimization:** The primary significance lies in providing a robust and empirically validated solution to the over-optimization problem, a persistent challenge in Direct Alignment Algorithms (DAAs). By addressing the issue of policy drift and insufficient regularization, IS-DAAs enable more stable and reliable training of LLMs, preventing performance degradation that typically occurs with prolonged optimization. This directly contributes to the development of more robust and trustworthy LLMs.

**2. Bridging the Performance Gap between Online and Offline Methods:** The research demonstrates that IS-DAAs can achieve performance comparable to or even surpassing that of online alignment methods like RLHF, while retaining the inherent computational efficiency and simplicity of offline DAAs. This is a crucial advancement, as it suggests that the need for complex and resource-intensive online sampling and explicit reward model training can be reduced or potentially avoided without compromising alignment quality.

**3. Enhanced and Theoretically Sound Regularization:** IS-DAAs introduce a novel mechanism for enforcing KL regularization more effectively in offline settings. The theoretical proof that the importance-sampled weighted squared loss gradient aligns with the true KL divergence gradient provides a strong foundation for why the method maintains regularization even when the learned policy diverges from the reference. This offers a deeper understanding of regularization in preference learning and provides a tool for more precise control over policy deviation.

**4. Computational Efficiency and Scalability:** By utilizing importance sampling rather than requiring explicit online data collection or reward model fitting, IS-DAAs maintain the computational advantages of offline DAAs. This makes the alignment process more accessible, faster, and less resource-intensive, which is critical for training larger and more capable LLMs, particularly in environments with limited computational resources. The minimal computational overhead of IS-DAAs makes them highly scalable.

**5. Inherent Robustness to Distribution Shift:** The interpretation of importance sampling as an implicit support constraint provides an additional layer of robustness. By ceasing updates for samples outside the reference policy's support, IS-DAAs inherently guard against unsupported extrapolations and the negative effects of distribution shift, a common problem in offline reinforcement learning. This "implicit early stopping" mechanism contributes to the model's stability and generalization capabilities.

**6. Practical Implications for LLM Development:** For practitioners and researchers developing LLMs, IS-DAAs offer a more dependable training paradigm. Developers can fine-tune LLMs with greater confidence, knowing that the models are less likely to suffer from performance collapse due to over-optimization. This translates to reduced development cycles, lower computational costs, and ultimately, the deployment of more reliably aligned and performant LLMs in real-world applications.

Overall, this work signifies a step towards more robust, efficient, and theoretically grounded LLM alignment techniques, potentially influencing future research directions in offline reinforcement learning for natural language processing and beyond.
