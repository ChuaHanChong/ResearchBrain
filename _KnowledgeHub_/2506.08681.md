# Mitigating Reward Over-optimization in Direct Alignment Algorithms with Importance Sampling

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed Importance Sampling Direct Alignment Algorithms (IS-DAAs) to mitigate reward over-optimization in Large Language Models by re-weighting offline preference data. This method improved win rates on instruction-following tasks by approximately 3% over standard DPO, maintaining stable performance and better KL efficiency.

## Problem

- Direct Alignment Algorithms (DAAs) like DPO are susceptible to 'over-optimization,' where model performance degrades as training progresses due to excessive policy divergence from the reference.
- The implicit regularization in offline DAAs weakens as the trained policy moves away from the reference policy, failing to adequately constrain responses outside the original data distribution.
- Existing solutions for DAA over-optimization either incur high computational costs (online methods) or offer limited efficacy (explicit KL penalties and other regularization techniques).

## Method

- Proposed ==Importance Sampling== DAAs (**IS-DAAs**) which re-weight the offline DAA objective using importance sampling ratios to effectively approximate an on-policy objective.
- Incorporated a clipping mechanism for the importance ratios to mitigate high variance and ensure stable training.
- The method is theoretically shown to ensure that the gradient of the importance-sampled weighted squared loss aligns with the true KL divergence gradient, providing robust regularization.

## Results

- IS-DAAs demonstrated robustness against over-optimization in a controlled summarization task, preventing the performance degradation observed in standard DPO, RPO, and Length-DPO.
- IS-DPO achieved approximately a **3%** increase in win rate over standard DPO on the Anthropic-HH instruction-following dataset, as judged by both a trained reward model and GPT-4.
- The method consistently outperformed DPO, RPO, and Length-DPO on AlpacaEval 2.0 and MT-Bench benchmarks, showing competitive or superior performance to χ\chiχ-PO.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Standard offline DAAs suffer from diminishing influence of implicit regularization as the policy drifts, leading to ineffective KL regularization.
> - Importance sampling can effectively enforce KL regularization in offline settings by transforming the objective to estimate on-policy expectations, even when the learned policy deviates significantly from the reference.
> - Importance sampling acts as an implicit support constraint, preventing updates for samples outside the reference policy's support and inducing an early stopping effect that inherently mitigates over-optimization.
