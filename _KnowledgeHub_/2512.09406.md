# H2R-Grounder: A Paired-Data-Free Paradigm for Translating Human Interaction Videos into Physically Grounded Robot Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> H2R-Grounder is a video-to-video diffusion framework that translates human interaction videos into physically grounded robot manipulation videos without paired human-robot training data. It uses H2Rep, a shared pose-plus-background representation built by inpainting the manipulator and overlaying a 2D position/orientation marker, to fine-tune Wan 2.2 via LoRA in-context learning, achieving the highest human-preference and VLM-scored realism versus rendering, animation, and commercial editing baselines.

## Problem

- Paired human-robot demonstration collection requires specialized calibrated hardware, which is costly and limits scalability to in-the-wild human videos.
- Prior rendering-based robotization methods overlay simulated robot arms via pose estimation and camera calibration, producing floating, misaligned, physically inconsistent visuals.
- Existing intermediate representations (masks, keypoints, flow, affordances) support feature alignment for policy learning but cannot directly synthesize realistic robot videos from human input.

## Method

- **H2Rep**: a transferable representation combining an inpainted clean background video with a rendered 2D dot-and-arrow overlay marking gripper or hand position and orientation, unifying human and robot domains.
- ==In-context LoRA fine-tuning== of the Wan 2.2 diffusion video generator, jointly encoding condition and target with a shared VAE and ==flow-matching objective== to synthesize physically grounded robot videos.
- ==Training-data construction pipeline== using Grounded-SAM2 segmentation, Minimax-Remover inpainting, and 6-DoF-to-2D gripper pose projection to build unpaired robot H2Rep-video pairs from the Droid dataset.

## Results

- In a **22**-participant human study on DexYCB, H2R-Grounder was ranked first-choice **63.6%** for physical plausibility and **61.4%** for visual quality, far above Kling, Runway Aleph, and RoboMaster baselines.
- Gemini-based VLM scoring rated H2R-Grounder **4.4** on physical plausibility and **4.9** on background consistency versus RoboMaster's **2.8** and **4.5** respectively.
- Ablations on Droid show SSIM **0.82**/LPIPS **0.22** for the full **5B** in-context model, dropping to **0.80**/**0.26** without LoRA and **0.68-0.71**/**0.27-0.30** when swapped for VACE conditioning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing manipulation into a pose trajectory plus a background video gives a domain-agnostic carrier that sidesteps the need for frame-aligned human-robot pairs entirely.
> - Learning physical grounding implicitly from real robot video supervision, rather than explicit rendering or calibration, avoids floating-arm and occlusion artifacts that plague geometry-based pipelines.
> - Lightweight LoRA adapters on a frozen large pretrained video generator preserve out-of-distribution generalization, letting a model trained on one narrow robot dataset transfer to diverse internet videos.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The challenge of enabling robots to acquire a broad range of manipulation skills is central to the field of robotics and embodied AI. A promising avenue is **imitation learning from human demonstrations**. However, this approach faces two major hurdles: the difficulty and cost of collecting large-scale, diverse robot manipulation data, and the significant "visual embodiment gap" between human hands/arms and robotic manipulators. H2R-Grounder directly addresses these fundamental challenges.

Historically, efforts to bridge the human-to-robot (H2R) gap have explored several strategies:

- **Paired Human-Robot Data Collection:** Some works rely on collecting demonstrations from both humans and robots in synchronized settings. While effective for specific tasks, this method is resource-intensive, often requires specialized hardware, and is not scalable to the vast diversity of skills depicted in everyday human videos.
- **Rendering-Based "Robotization":** A popular approach involves taking human videos, segmenting out the human hand, and rendering a 3D robot arm into its place based on estimated human pose. While this attempts to fill the visual gap, these methods frequently suffer from issues of physical inconsistency (e.g., robot arms appearing to "float" above objects, incorrect contacts, unrealistic lighting) and often require precise camera-robot calibration and sensor parameters, which are rarely available for "in-the-wild" human videos. This severely limits their generalization.
- **Intermediate Representations:** Many works propose abstract intermediate representations, such as affordance maps, keypoints, optical flow, or latent features, to facilitate cross-domain learning. While these representations help align human and robot actions, they often do not directly translate into high-fidelity robot videos, potentially losing crucial visual and physical details, or still requiring further steps for policy execution.
- **Generative Robot Video Prediction:** Some contemporary efforts focus on generating robot videos, often conditioned on robot actions or trajectories. RoboMaster, for instance, animates robot-object interactions from a single image given user-defined trajectories, but this requires significant manual input for object masks and trajectories, making it impractical for large-scale application.

H2R-Grounder introduces a novel paradigm that significantly advances the state-of-the-art by combining the strengths of recent advances in generative AI with a clever strategy to overcome the data bottleneck and embodiment gap:

1. **Paired-Data-Free Learning:** This is a major differentiator. Unlike many prior works, H2R-Grounder explicitly avoids the need for paired human-robot videos. It trains exclusively on unpaired robot videos and then generalizes to human videos.
2. **Fully Generative Translation:** Instead of rendering, which struggles with realism and physical consistency, H2R-Grounder *generates* the robot video using a deep generative model. This allows the model to learn complex physical interactions, lighting, and occlusions directly from real robot video data.
3. **Transferable Abstract Representation (H2Rep):** By designing a simple, minimal 2D pose indicator (a colored dot and arrow) overlaid on a clean background video, the method creates a "language" that is common to both human and robot manipulation. This sidesteps the need for precise 3D calibration and makes the system robust to diverse "in-the-wild" human videos.
4. **Leveraging Large Video Diffusion Models:** By fine-tuning a powerful pre-trained video diffusion model (Wan 2.2) with lightweight LoRA adapters, H2R-Grounder benefits from the model's rich prior knowledge, strong generalization capabilities, and inherent temporal coherence, which is crucial for realistic video generation.

In essence, H2R-Grounder pushes the boundary towards truly scalable and physically grounded robot learning from human observation, overcoming key limitations of previous rendering-based, paired-data dependent, or manually-intensive approaches. It represents a significant step towards enabling robots to autonomously acquire complex skills from the vast amount of human video data available online.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the H2R-Grounder framework are deeply rooted in the challenges and aspirations of modern robotics and artificial intelligence:

- **Addressing Robot Data Scarcity:** A core motivation is to overcome the severe limitations in collecting large-scale, diverse, and high-quality robot manipulation datasets. Recording demonstrations with physical robots is inherently slow, expensive, and confined to controlled laboratory environments, leading to datasets that are orders of magnitude smaller and less varied than those available in other AI domains like natural language processing.
- **Harnessing Abundant Human Video Data:** In stark contrast to robot data, human interaction videos are ubiquitous, ranging from casual online clips to egocentric recordings. These videos offer a rich, diverse, and readily available source of information on how to interact with objects. A key objective is to unlock this vast resource for robot learning, thereby dramatically accelerating data collection and skill acquisition.
- **Bridging the Visual Embodiment Gap:** Human hands and arms differ significantly in appearance, kinematics, and interaction dynamics from robot arms and grippers. This visual embodiment gap makes direct imitation learning challenging. The framework aims to effectively translate human visual demonstrations into visually and physically plausible robot actions despite these differences.
- **Ensuring Physical Plausibility and Realism:** A critical objective is to generate robot videos that are not only visually realistic but also *physically grounded*. This means the robot arm should interact with objects plausibly (e.g., correct grasping, realistic contact, proper occlusion behavior), avoiding common artifacts like "floating" arms or geometrically distorted robot structures that plague many rendering-based methods.
- **Eliminating Paired Data Dependency:** A major design goal is to create a system that does *not* require paired human-robot videos for training. This significantly reduces the burden of data collection, making the system easier to scale and deploy in diverse environments.
- **Achieving Robust Generalization to "In-the-Wild" Videos:** Previous rendering methods often require accurate camera calibration and pose estimation, limiting their applicability to controlled lab settings. H2R-Grounder aims for robust generalization to uncalibrated, diverse "in-the-wild" human videos by relying on abstract 2D pose indicators rather than strict 3D alignments.
- **Maintaining Temporal Coherence:** Video generation inherently requires temporal consistency for realism. A key objective is to leverage the strengths of modern video diffusion models and an in-context fine-tuning strategy to produce temporally coherent and smooth robot manipulation videos.

In summary, the H2R-Grounder project is motivated by the vision of a future where robots can learn new skills simply by observing human actions in everyday videos, without requiring tedious data collection, complex calibration, or physically unrealistic translations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

H2R-Grounder introduces a three-stage, paired-data-free video-to-video translation framework. Its core innovation lies in the creation of a simple yet powerful transferable intermediate representation, **H2Rep**, which bridges the visual embodiment gap between humans and robots, and the effective fine-tuning of a state-of-the-art video diffusion model.

### 4. H2Rep: A Shared Abstraction for Human and Robot Videos

The foundational insight is that both human and robot manipulation videos can be abstracted into two key components:
1. **A pose trajectory of the manipulator:** This captures the action semantics (human hand or robot gripper).
2. **A background video:** This preserves the scene layout and the physical state of manipulated objects.
H2Rep combines these by overlaying a minimal visual cue (a colored dot for position and an arrow for orientation) representing the manipulator's 2D pose onto a clean background video of the scene.

### 5. Training Data Construction from Robot Videos (Paired-Data-Free Training)

The model is trained exclusively on unpaired robot manipulation videos (e.g., from the Droid dataset), without any human videos present during this phase. This process involves:
- **Robot-Arm Segmentation:** For each robot video ($V_r$), a pixel-accurate mask sequence ($M_r$) of the robot arm is obtained using a text-prompted video segmentation model (Grounded-SAM2 with the prompt "robotic arm").
- **Gripper Pose Projection:** The 6-DoF end-effector (EEF) trajectory of the robot arm is projected to 2D image space ($P_r$) using calibrated camera parameters. This 2D pose is then rendered as a visual overlay ($R(P_r)$) – a red dot for position and a blue arrow for orientation on each frame.
- **Robot-Arm Removal (Background Video Inpainting):** The robot arm is removed from the original video using a video inpainting model (Minimax-Remover), yielding a clean background video ($V_I^r$). This step is crucial for separating the manipulator from the scene.
- **Composing Robot Video H2Rep:** The generated clean background video ($V_I^r$) is blended with the rendered pose overlay ($R(P_r)$) using alpha blending ($\alpha=0.4$) to form the robot's H2Rep ($H_r$). This $H_r$ now serves as the conditional input, and the original robot video $V_r$ acts as the ground truth.

### 6. In-Context Learning for Physically Grounded Robot Video Generation

A conditional video generator, specifically a fine-tuned Wan 2.2 TI2V-5B model, is trained to synthesize the original robot video ($V_r$) given its corresponding H2Rep ($H_r$) and a fixed text prompt ("A robotic arm is interacting with objects.").
- **Architecture:** The Wan 2.2 backbone is kept frozen, and only lightweight LoRA adapters on the Q/K/V projections within the transformer blocks are trained. This "in-context learning" design allows the model to leverage the powerful prior knowledge of the pre-trained diffusion model while efficiently learning the specific mapping from H2Rep to physically grounded robot videos.
- **Training Objective:** A flow-matching objective is used. The H2Rep ($H_r$) and the target robot video ($V_r$) are encoded by the same VAE. The encoded H2Rep is fused with a text embedding to condition the velocity field prediction, learning to reconstruct the original robot video from a noisy input. This process explicitly supervises the model with *real robot videos*, thereby encouraging physically plausible generations, including correct physics, contact, and occlusions.

### 7. Human Video → Robot Video Translation (H2R Transfer)

At inference time, to translate an arbitrary third-person human-object interaction video ($V_h$) into a robot video, the same H2Rep construction process is applied:
- **Person Segmentation and Hand Pose Estimation:** Grounded-SAM 2.1 segments the human ($M_h$), and a combination of ViT-Pose and HaMeR estimates the human hand pose. A surrogate 2D pose ($P_h$) representing the hand's position (midpoint of index fingertip and thumb tip) and orientation (direction of the thumb) is extracted. This surrogate pose is designed to align with the projected gripper pose in the robot domain.
- **Person Removal (Background Video Inpainting):** Similar to robot arm removal, Minimax-Remover is used to remove the person from the human video, yielding a clean background video ($V_I^h$).
- **Composing Human Video H2Rep:** The clean background video ($V_I^h$) is blended with the rendered human pose overlay ($R(P_h)$) to create the human's H2Rep ($H_h$).
- **H2R Translation:** This $H_h$ is then fed as input to the *trained robot generator* ($G_\theta$). The model, having learned to synthesize robot videos from the robot H2Rep, generalizes to the human H2Rep, producing a physically grounded robot manipulation video that mimics the human's actions in the scene ($bV_r$). The frozen base generator's strong OOD generalization is key here.

This entire methodology intelligently decouples the learning of robot appearance and physics from the human demonstration, enabling robust and scalable translation without the need for expensive paired data.

### 8. Main Findings and Results

The research paper rigorously evaluates H2R-Grounder through both quantitative metrics (VLM-based evaluation, SSIM, LPIPS) and qualitative comparisons, including a human user study. The findings consistently demonstrate the superior performance of H2R-Grounder over baseline methods, especially regarding realism, physical plausibility, and generalization.

### 9. Quantitative Results on DexYCB Dataset

For out-of-distribution (OOD) human videos, the DexYCB dataset was used, covering controlled lab environments with domain shifts from the training data.

- **Human Study:** A user study with 22 computer science participants ranked generated videos across four criteria. H2R-Grounder achieved the highest first-rank preference in all aspects:
 - **Motion Consistency:** 54.5% (vs. Kling 9.1%, Runway Aleph 22.7%, RoboMaster 2.3%) – indicating accurate replication of human motion.
 - **Background Consistency:** 56.8% (vs. Kling 34.1%, Runway Aleph 15.9%, RoboMaster 2.3%) – demonstrating stable and undistorted backgrounds.
 - **Visual Quality:** 61.4% (vs. Kling 40.9%, Runway Aleph 9.1%, RoboMaster 2.3%) – showcasing high fidelity and visual appeal.
 - **Physical Plausibility:** 63.6% (vs. Kling 9.1%, Runway Aleph 6.8%, RoboMaster 18.2%) – confirming realistic robot structure, contact, and interaction with objects.
 Kling performed second in visual quality and background consistency but struggled with motion and physical plausibility, often losing robot structure or having implausible interactions. RoboMaster consistently performed the weakest, suggesting its animation-based approach with manual trajectory input is less effective for natural, consistent output.

- **VLM-Based Evaluation:** Using Gemini, a multimodal visual-language model, to rate videos on a 1-5 scale, H2R-Grounder showed strong alignment with human preferences:
 - **Motion Consistency:** 3.7 (highest, tied with Runway Aleph)
 - **Background Consistency:** 4.9 (highest, tied with Kling)
 - **Visual Quality:** 4.0 (slightly behind Kling's 4.1)
 - **Physical Plausibility:** 4.4 (highest)
 These scores further validate H2R-Grounder's ability to generate videos that are consistent with motion, maintain background integrity, and, critically, exhibit physically plausible interactions.

### 10. Qualitative Results

Figure 5 visually demonstrates H2R-Grounder's superior performance on both internet videos and DexYCB sequences. The model successfully generalizes to diverse in-the-wild videos, maintaining consistent backgrounds, accurate motion alignment, and sharp visual quality. In contrast, commercial tools like Kling and Runway Aleph often produce structurally inconsistent robot arms or distort backgrounds, while RoboMaster fails to precisely follow demonstrated motions. The examples clearly illustrate H2R-Grounder's ability to accurately position the gripper for interaction (e.g., grasping a banana tip), faithfully following human hand trajectories.

### 11. Ablation Study

An ablation study on the Droid dataset quantified the importance of key components:
- **Pose Indicator:** Removing the pose indicator from H2Rep significantly led to motion drift, confirming its essential role in controlling the robot's trajectory (SSIM decreased from 0.82 to 0.80, LPIPS increased from 0.22 to 0.23).
- **LoRA Fine-tuning:** Without LoRA, the model overfit and failed to generate a robot arm, highlighting the necessity of efficient fine-tuning (SSIM 0.80, LPIPS 0.26).
- **In-context Learning (Wan 2.2) vs. ControlNet (VACE):** Replacing the in-context video generator with VACE (a ControlNet-based approach) resulted in lower SSIM (0.68 for 1.3B, 0.71 for 14B) and higher LPIPS (0.30 for 1.3B, 0.27 for 14B), indicating that in-context learning is more effective for maintaining motion-background coherence and overall generation quality.
- **Backbone Size:** Scaling to a larger 14B backbone did not yield clear quality improvements but drastically slowed inference and limited sequence length (49 to 17 frames). The 5B Wan 2.2 model was chosen as the optimal configuration for accuracy and efficiency.

Inference efficiency was also noted, with the 5B model taking approximately 13 seconds per frame (or 648 seconds for a 49-frame video) on a single H200 GPU, consuming 63 GB of memory.

These findings collectively affirm that H2R-Grounder successfully achieves its objectives of generating realistic, physically grounded robot videos from human demonstrations without paired data, demonstrating robust generalization and superior performance over existing methods.

### 12. Significance and Potential Impact

H2R-Grounder represents a significant advancement in the field of robot learning and embodied AI, with several far-reaching implications and potential impacts:

- **Massive Scalability for Robot Learning:** The most profound impact of H2R-Grounder is its ability to bypass the need for paired human-robot demonstration data. This paradigm shift means that the vast, ever-growing reservoir of human interaction videos available on the internet can now be directly leveraged for training robots. This effectively removes one of the most significant bottlenecks in scaling robot capabilities, potentially leading to robots that can acquire a much wider range of skills much faster and at a lower cost than previously imagined.
- **Enhanced Realism and Physical Plausibility:** By training directly on real robot videos and incorporating a physically grounded generation mechanism, H2R-Grounder produces robot videos that are not only visually convincing but also adhere to the laws of physics, including correct contact points, occlusions, and plausible robot kinematics. This is crucial for successful sim-to-real transfer and for developing robust robot policies, as unrealistic generated data can lead to brittle or unsafe real-world robot behaviors.
- **Democratization of Robot Learning:** By eliminating the need for specialized hardware, complex 3D camera-robot calibration, and expensive data collection infrastructure, H2R-Grounder makes robot learning from human demonstrations more accessible. Researchers and developers without access to advanced robotics labs can still contribute to and benefit from this approach, broadening the participation in robotics research and development.
- **Advancement in Generative AI for Embodied Tasks:** The work showcases the immense potential of fine-tuning large, pre-trained video diffusion models with lightweight adapters (LoRA) for highly specific and complex conditional generation tasks, especially those requiring strong temporal coherence and physical consistency. This demonstrates a powerful pathway for leveraging general-purpose generative models for embodied AI challenges.
- **New Avenues for Robot Policy Learning:** H2R-Grounder directly generates high-fidelity robot videos. These generated videos can serve as synthetic training data for various robot learning paradigms, including imitation learning, reinforcement learning, and representation learning. The model effectively acts as a visual "compiler" that translates human intent into robot-executable visual demonstrations, potentially forming a key component in future large-scale robot foundation models.
- **Improved Human-Robot Interaction:** As robots become more capable of understanding and mimicking human actions, the possibilities for intuitive human-robot collaboration and teaching expand. A robot that can interpret "in-the-wild" human demonstrations could be easier to instruct and integrate into human environments.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge current limitations, notably the support for only single-hand to single-arm translation and the generation of only Franka-style robot outputs due to training data constraints. Future work will explore extending the framework to bimanual scenarios and adapting it to other robot embodiments, potentially through modular fine-tuning of lightweight adapters for each robot type. Addressing these limitations would further expand the applicability and impact of H2R-Grounder, solidifying its role as a foundational technology for scalable and grounded robot learning.

In conclusion, H2R-Grounder represents a significant step towards unlocking the vast potential of human video data for robot learning. By offering a robust, scalable, and physically grounded translation framework, it contributes substantially to the broader vision of creating more capable, adaptable, and autonomously learning robots.
