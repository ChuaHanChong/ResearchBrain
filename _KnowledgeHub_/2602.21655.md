# CCCaption: Dual-Reward Reinforcement Learning for Complete and Correct Image Captioning

> [!summary] Summary
> The CCCaption framework employs a dual-reward reinforcement learning strategy to generate complete and correct image captions, moving beyond the limitations of human-annotated ground truths. This approach achieves state-of-the-art performance across multiple captioning benchmarks, notably improving completeness and suppressing hallucinations.

## Problem

- Image captioning models, trained on subjective human annotations, frequently produce incomplete descriptions or contain factual hallucinations.
- Previous reinforcement learning (RL) methods for captioning suffer from single-model bias in query generation for completeness and often exacerbate hallucinations due to a lack of explicit correctness incentives.
- Evaluating caption quality objectively by both completeness (covering all salient facts) and correctness (factual truthfulness) remained a challenge beyond imitation-based metrics.

## Method

- A ==dual-reward== reinforcement learning framework, **CCCaption**, which simultaneously optimizes for completeness and correctness using separate query-based rewards.
- A multi-MLLM query generation pipeline that creates a comprehensive and diverse set of visual queries for each image, forming the CCaption-44k dataset, to provide robust supervision for completeness.
- A dynamic query sampling strategy that focuses RL training on more informative or challenging queries, thereby improving training efficiency and convergence.

## Results

- CCCaption-**2B** achieved an average CapArena score of 46.39, outperforming its Qwen3-VL-**2B** base model by **12.08%** and CapRL-**3B** by **164.24%**.
- The framework improved caption completeness, scoring 52.80 on the Prism evaluation, surpassing CapRL-**3B** by **3.38%** and even the larger Qwen3-VL-**32B** model by **0.54** points.
- CCCaption-**2B** demonstrated higher correctness with an average score of 70.57, showing a modest improvement over its base model, while previous RL methods like CapRL showed increased hallucinations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Objectively quantifying and optimizing for both completeness and correctness in image captions can be achieved by employing a frozen third-party judge model to evaluate generated captions against sets of visual and sub-caption queries.
> - Leveraging multiple diverse MLLMs for query generation, combined with diversity filtering, overcomes the bias and limited coverage of single-MLLM approaches, leading to more comprehensive completeness supervision.
> - Dynamically weighting and sampling queries based on their 'contribution' (how often they are incorrectly answered or their rollout accuracy variance) significantly enhances the efficiency of RL training for captioning tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Image captioning is a foundational task in vision-language understanding, serving as a bridge between visual and textual modalities. Early approaches primarily utilized encoder-decoder architectures, combining convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for image feature extraction and recurrent neural networks (RNNs) or Transformers for text generation. Recent advancements in multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have significantly enhanced captioning capabilities through supervised fine-tuning on vast image-caption datasets. These models are also crucial for pretraining larger MLLMs by re-captioning web image-caption pairs to improve data quality.

However, a significant limitation across these approaches is their reliance on human-annotated ground-truth captions for training. Human annotations are inherently subjective, reflecting individual preferences, expertise, and varying levels of detail. Consequently, captions generated by models trained solely on these references can be incomplete (missing salient visual facts) or incorrect (containing hallucinations not grounded in the image). This imitation-based learning paradigm restricts models from generating objectively comprehensive and factual descriptions.

To move beyond the limitations of human annotation imitation, reinforcement learning (RL) has been adopted in image captioning. RL frameworks optimize models directly against task-specific objectives, rather than merely mimicking reference captions. Previous RL efforts, such as CapRL, have aimed to improve caption completeness by rewarding models for answering more visual queries related to an image. However, these methods often suffer from single-model bias in query generation, leading to incomplete supervision, and can inadvertently exacerbate hallucinations as models strive for more verbose descriptions without explicit factual grounding. The problem of balancing completeness with correctness, particularly controlling hallucination in longer, more detailed captions, remains a significant challenge in the current research landscape.

The reported work, CCCaption, addresses these challenges by introducing a dual-reward reinforcement learning framework. It aims to develop a principled method for training caption models that explicitly optimizes for both completeness and correctness, thereby moving beyond the constraints of human-annotation imitation and the limitations of prior RL approaches in simultaneously managing these two critical aspects of caption quality.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the CCCaption framework stem from the identified limitations of existing image captioning models:

- **Addressing Incompleteness in Existing Captions:** Current captioning models, even powerful MLLMs, frequently produce incomplete descriptions. This is primarily because they are trained to mimic human-annotated reference captions, which often miss salient visual facts due to annotator subjectivity or resource constraints. The authors observe that models may omit crucial details (e.g., "ice cubes" in an image of a drink), preventing the generation of truly comprehensive captions. A key objective is thus to develop a mechanism that encourages models to cover all important visual information present in an image.

- **Mitigating Hallucinations in Detailed Captions:** While some advanced methods attempt to generate more detailed captions, they often introduce hallucinations—statements in the caption that are factually incorrect or not grounded in the image. This issue is particularly pronounced when models are unconstrained or incentivized purely for verbosity. The authors illustrate this by showing that even as a reward function might increase during RL training, caption correctness can decrease due to hallucinated details. Therefore, a critical objective is to explicitly penalize incorrect information to ensure factual accuracy in generated captions, especially as their length and detail increase.

- **Developing an Objective Evaluation of Caption Quality:** The authors argue that caption quality should be assessed by two objective properties: completeness (covering all salient visual facts) and correctness (factual truthfulness with respect to the image). They propose a framework to quantify these properties, moving beyond subjective human preferences embodied in ground-truth annotations.

- **Improving Reinforcement Learning for Captioning:** Previous RL approaches for captioning have introduced challenges such as low training efficiency and a tendency towards overly verbose or hallucinated descriptions. Specific motivations include:
 - **Enhancing Query Coverage for Completeness:** Existing methods (e.g., CapRL) often rely on a single MLLM to generate visual queries, leading to model bias and limited coverage of image content. The authors aim to improve query diversity and comprehensiveness to provide more robust supervision for completeness.
 - **Improving Training Efficiency:** Not all queries contribute equally to gradient updates during RL training. Simple queries, which are quickly mastered, can yield negligible gradients, slowing down learning. The motivation is to design a dynamic strategy to focus training on more informative queries.
 - **Joint Optimization of Completeness and Correctness:** A central motivation is to design a dual-reward mechanism that simultaneously incentivizes models to generate complete captions and penalizes hallucinations, thereby guiding models towards objectively higher quality.

- **Creating a High-Quality Fine-Tuning Corpus:** To support the training of models with an emphasis on completeness, there is a need for a dedicated dataset that provides comprehensive visual queries for each image, overcoming the limitations of human annotations.

In summary, the core motivation is to move image captioning beyond the imitation of potentially flawed human annotations towards the generation of objectively "Complete and Correct Captions" by integrating a novel dual-reward reinforcement learning framework, an enhanced query generation pipeline, and an efficient training strategy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The CCCaption framework addresses the outlined objectives through a dual-reward reinforcement learning mechanism, supported by a specialized data generation pipeline and an efficient training strategy.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Image Captioning and Reinforcement Learning

The work starts by formalizing image captioning as generating a caption `y` for an image `x` from a conditional distribution `pθ(y|x)`. Traditional training uses maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) with cross-entropy loss. To move beyond reference imitation, the authors adopt a reinforcement learning (RL) objective, `JRL(θ)`, which maximizes the expected reward `R(x, ŷ)` for a sampled caption `ŷ` given an image `x`.
The fundamental definitions for completeness `s_comp(x, y)` and correctness `s_corr(x, y)` are based on the overlap between "basic information sets" `B_x` (image facts) and `B_y` (caption facts). However, as these sets are unobservable, the method proposes tractable approximations using query-based rewards.

#### 3.2. Dual-Reward Reinforcement Learning Framework (CCCaption)

The core of CCCaption lies in combining two symmetric rewards—completeness and correctness—into a single objective function.

#### 3.2.1. Completeness Reward

To approximate the basic information set `B_x` of an image, the authors utilize a set of visual queries `Q_x = {qi}m_i=1`. The completeness reward `R_comp(x, ŷ)` is defined as the fraction of these queries that the generated caption `ŷ` can correctly answer. A frozen third-party judge model `MJ` is employed to assess whether `ŷ` answers query `q`.

- **Multi-MLLM Query Generation Pipeline (for CCaption-44k dataset):**
 - **Problem:** Single-MLLM query generation (as in prior work like CapRL) suffers from bias and limited coverage of visual content.
 - **Approach:** A pipeline is designed to generate a diverse and comprehensive set of visual queries.
 1. **Diverse MLLMs:** Multiple diverse MLLMs (e.g., LLaVa-V1.6 34B, Qwen2.5-VL 72B, InternVL3.5 38B, Qwen3-VL 32B) are used to decompose an image into a series of visual queries.
 2. **Query Diversity Measure `V(Q_x)`:** To ensure the generated queries cover a wide range of visual facts, a diversity metric is introduced. This metric calculates the variance of cosine similarities between query embedding vectors `e_i` within a query set `Q_x`. Higher variance indicates greater diversity.
 3. **Filtering and Iteration:** An algorithm (Algorithm 1) iteratively selects MLLMs, generates queries, and filters out low-quality queries based on validity and relevance to the image. If the diversity of the collected queries `Q_x` falls below a threshold `τ`, the least contributing query is removed, and the process continues.
 4. **Dataset Construction (CCaption-44k):** This pipeline was applied to 140k images (after deduplication and cleaning) to produce CCaption-44k, a training dataset comprising 44,077 samples, each with an average of 10 diverse queries. An embedding model (Ops-MM-embedding) is used for query and image embedding.

#### 3.2.2. Correctness Reward

To explicitly penalize hallucinations and promote factual accuracy, the generated caption `ŷ` is decomposed into a set of sub-caption queries `Qŷ = {qi}n_i=1`. These sub-caption queries approximate the basic information set `B_y` of the caption. The correctness reward `R_corr(x, ŷ)` is then computed by feeding each sub-caption query `q` along with the original image `x` into the same frozen judge model `MJ`. This judge model provides a soft assessment `MJ(x, q) ∈` of how well each sub-caption query is grounded in the image. This essentially validates the authenticity of the claims made in the caption.

#### 3.2.3. Dynamic Query Sampling Strategy

- **Problem:** During RL training, simple queries that are easily answered correctly by most rollout captions yield small "advantages" and negligible gradients, reducing training efficiency.
- **Approach:** Inspired by dynamic sample selection, a dynamic query sampling strategy is implemented.
 - **Contribution `c(q)`:** Each candidate query `q` is assigned a normalized contribution `c(q) ∈`. Initially, `c(q)` is set based on how often `q` is incorrectly answered.
 - **Dynamic Update:** `c(q)` is updated at each epoch by adding the variance of the rollout accuracy for that query. This ensures that "controversial" queries (those with varying accuracy across rollouts) maintain higher sampling probabilities.
 - **Sampling:** Queries are sampled from `Q_c` (candidate queries) with a probability proportional to `c(q)`.
- **Benefit:** This strategy focuses the training effort on queries that are more informative or challenging, preventing gradient stagnation and improving convergence speed.

#### 3.2.4. Hybrid Reward Function

The completeness and correctness rewards are combined into a single hybrid reward `R(x, ŷ)` using a weighting factor `α`:
`R(x, ŷ) = α * R_comp(x, ŷ) + (1 - α) * R_corr(x, ŷ)`
The parameter `α` controls the trade-off between prioritizing completeness and correctness. Given the sparsity of hallucinations, `α` is set low (0.05) to give more weight to correctness.

#### 3.3. Training Setting

- **Base Model:** Qwen3-VL-2B.
- **Judge Models:**
 - Completeness reward: Qwen2.5-VL-3B.
 - Correctness reward: Qwen3-VL-2B (for sub-caption query validation).
- **RL Framework:** GRPO (General Reinforcement Learning for Policy Optimization).
- **Hyperparameters:** Rollout set to 5, learning rate 1e-6, 20 epochs.
- **Dynamic Query Sampling:** Applied only for completeness reward, sampling 5 queries per step.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted extensive experiments across various benchmarks to evaluate the CCCaption framework, focusing on its ability to generate complete and correct captions, the effectiveness of its components, and its training efficiency.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance of CCCaption-2B

CCCaption-2B, trained with the proposed framework on the Qwen3-VL-2B base model, demonstrated state-of-the-art performance across multiple evaluation frameworks:

- **CapArena Evaluation:** (Table 1)
 - CCCaption-2B achieved an average score of 46.39, outperforming its base model Qwen3-VL-2B (41.39) by 5.00 points (12.08%).
 - It significantly surpassed CapRL-3B (17.56) by 28.83 points (164.24%) and even larger commercial models like Claude-3.5-Sonnet-2024 (-22.08). This indicates a strong preference from the MLLM judge (Qwen3-VL 32B) for CCCaption-2B's generated captions.

- **Prism Evaluation (Completeness):** (Table 2)
 - CCCaption-2B achieved an average score of 52.80, improving over CapRL-3B (51.07) by 1.72 points (3.38%).
 - Despite being a 2B parameter model, CCCaption-2B outperformed the much larger Qwen3-VL-32B (52.26) by 0.54 points.
 - A notable improvement was observed on the OCRBench dataset, where CCCaption-2B scored 55.34, surpassing the second-place InternVL3.5-38B by 4.53 points (8.91%), suggesting enhanced ability to describe text-rich images.

- **Hallucinations Evaluation (Correctness):** (Table 3)
 - CCCaption-2B achieved an average correctness score of 70.57, showing a modest improvement of 0.38 points (0.54%) over its base model Qwen3-VL-2B (70.19).
 - In contrast, CapRL-3B (66.91), which lacks a dedicated correctness reward, showed a decrease of 0.86 points (1.27%) compared to its base model Qwen2.5-VL-3B (67.77), indicating an increase in hallucination.
 - CCCaption-2B also demonstrated higher correctness than Qwen3-VL-32B (69.87), improving by 0.70 points (1.00%).

#### 4.2. Completeness and Effectiveness of CCaption-44k Queries

- **Completeness:** (Figure 4) t-SNE visualizations of image and query embeddings showed that "Vanilla" queries (generated by a single MLLM) exhibited misalignments and did not fully capture image features. In contrast, queries from CCaption-44k (generated by multi-MLLMs with diversity filtering) effectively covered almost all image features, indicating a more complete query set.
- **Effectiveness:** (Table 4) Training models on CCaption-44k resulted in improved performance. The model trained on CCaption-44k (average Prism score 45.94) outperformed the base model (43.25) by 2.69 points (6.2%) and the model trained on "Vanilla" queries (44.07) by 1.87 points (4.25%). The results also suggested a scaling law, with performance improving as the dataset size increased (e.g., CCaption-44k outperforming 8k and 16k versions).

#### 4.3. Impact of Dynamic Query Sampling Strategy

- **Training Efficiency:** (Figure 5) The dynamic query sampling strategy significantly improved training efficiency. While initial training stages showed little difference, models using the strategy demonstrated sustained performance improvements in later stages, leading to faster convergence and reduced training time. The strategy ensures "controversial" queries, which contribute more meaningfully to gradient updates, are sampled more frequently.

#### 4.4. Suppression of Hallucinations by Correctness Reward

- **Quantitative:** As mentioned in the overall performance, CapRL-3B (without correctness reward) saw an increase in hallucinations, while CCCaption-2B (with correctness reward) showed improved correctness and suppressed hallucinations compared to its base model.
- **Qualitative:** (Figure 3) Case studies demonstrated that CCCaption-2B generated more accurate captions. For example, in a geometric object image, Qwen3-VL-2B hallucinated "the yellow cube behind," and CapRL-3B incorrectly stated "seven objects." In contrast, CCCaption-2B accurately identified eight objects and their relationships, avoiding factual errors. This suppression is attributed to the correctness reward, which evaluates atomic facts within sub-caption queries, providing high-confidence scores that guide the model away from generating incorrect statements.

#### 4.5. Necessity of CCCaption Components (Ablation Study)

- (Table 5) An ablation study confirmed the effectiveness of each component:
 - "w/o Dynamic" (without dynamic query sampling): Led to a 0.91-point (1.32%) decrease in average performance compared to the full CCCaption, confirming its role in training efficiency.
 - "w/o Corr" (only completeness reward): Resulted in a decrease in correctness metrics (e.g., 0.39-point drop in OCR Hallucinations compared to base), showing that completeness alone can't ensure correctness.
 - "w/o Comp" (only correctness reward): Caused a substantial 9.71-point drop in Prism evaluation (completeness) for OCR compared to the base model, indicating that correctness alone does not guarantee completeness.
 - The hybrid reward significantly improved performance by 2.51 points (3.7%) and 4.13 points (6.27%) over using completeness-only and correctness-only rewards, respectively, affirming the necessity of both for comprehensive caption quality.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CCCaption framework represents a notable advancement in image captioning research by explicitly addressing the long-standing challenges of incompleteness and hallucination, which are inherent limitations of models trained on subjective human-annotated datasets.

The significance and potential impact of this work include:

- **Principled Approach to Objective Caption Quality:** By defining and optimizing for "completeness" and "correctness" as objective criteria, the framework moves beyond mere imitation of human references. This offers a more robust and scalable method for evaluating and training captioning models, potentially leading to captions that are consistently more informative and factually grounded regardless of human annotator biases.
- **Enhanced Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) Pretraining:** High-quality image captions are crucial for pretraining MLLMs on web-scale datasets. The ability of CCCaption to generate captions that are both complete and correct provides a method for re-captioning or enhancing existing multimodal datasets, which are often sparse or biased. This can lead to more effective and less hallucination-prone MLLMs in subsequent pretraining stages.
- **Improved Vision-Language Understanding and Applications:** More accurate and comprehensive image captions can significantly enhance downstream vision-language tasks such as visual question answering, image retrieval, and multimodal content generation. Applications requiring precise descriptions, such as medical imaging, scientific data analysis, or autonomous driving, could particularly benefit from the reduced hallucinations and increased detail.
- **Advancements in Reinforcement Learning for Language Generation:** The introduction of a dual-reward mechanism that effectively balances competing objectives (completeness vs. correctness) and the dynamic query sampling strategy for improved training efficiency contribute to the broader field of reinforcement learning applied to natural language generation. These techniques could be adapted for other generative tasks where factual accuracy and comprehensive coverage are paramount.
- **Development of a High-Quality Dataset:** The creation of CCaption-44k, a dataset derived from multi-MLLM query generation, offers a valuable resource for future research. This dataset's comprehensive and diverse queries provide a more objective and thorough supervision signal than traditional human annotations.
- **Feasibility with Smaller Models:** Demonstrating state-of-the-art performance with CCCaption-2B, a relatively smaller model, against much larger models like Qwen3-VL-32B, indicates that the framework is computationally efficient and accessible. This suggests that high-quality captioning can be achieved without relying solely on increasingly larger and more resource-intensive models.

In conclusion, CCCaption provides a foundation for training vision-language models that can produce descriptions which are not only fluent but also objectively complete and factually correct, thus pushing the boundaries of multimodal understanding and generation.
