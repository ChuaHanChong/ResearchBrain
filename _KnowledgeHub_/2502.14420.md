# ChatVLA: Unified Multimodal Understanding and Robot Control with Vision-Language-Action Model

> [!summary] Summary
> ChatVLA presents a Vision-Language-Action model that successfully unifies multimodal understanding and embodied robot control into a single end-to-end neural network. The model achieves state-of-the-art performance in both visual question answering and 25 real-world robot manipulation tasks by mitigating common training challenges.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often lose their multimodal understanding and conversational abilities (spurious forgetting) when fine-tuned for robot control.
- Jointly training for robot control and multimodal understanding leads to task interference, where optimizing one capability degrades the performance of the other.
- The challenge of creating a truly unified AI agent capable of seamless perception, comprehension, and physical interaction with the environment.

## Method

- A ==Phased Alignment Training== strategy, a two-stage curriculum that first focuses on robot control mastery and then incrementally integrates visual-text data to reactivate multimodal understanding.
- A ==Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)== architecture implemented on the MLP layers of the transformer backbone, featuring distinct 'Control-Expert' and 'Understanding-Expert' components with shared self-attention layers.
- The model utilizes Qwen2-VL-2B as its Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone, adapting an action head design from DiVLA for robot control.

## Results

- ChatVLA achieved significant improvements on VQA benchmarks, scoring **71.2%** on TextVQA (a **9.2x** relative improvement over ECoT) and **37.4%** on MMStar (**2.2x** over DiVLA), demonstrating robust multimodal understanding.
- The model exhibited superior performance across **25** real-world robot manipulation tasks, consistently outperforming baselines like Octo and OpenVLA in long-horizon, compositional, and cross-skill multi-tasking.
- These strong results were obtained with greater parameter efficiency, using a **2B** parameter VLM backbone, which is **3.5x** smaller than some comparable VLA models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Spurious forgetting and task interference are critical limitations in developing generalist Vision-Language-Action models, and their existence was empirically validated.
> - A staged training approach (Phased Alignment Training) effectively prevents the degradation of multimodal understanding while acquiring robust robot control skills.
> - The Mixture-of-Experts architecture, with shared self-attention and dedicated MLP experts, allows for simultaneous high performance in both multimodal understanding and robot control tasks by minimizing interference.
