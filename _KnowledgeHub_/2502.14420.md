# ChatVLA: Unified Multimodal Understanding and Robot Control with Vision-Language-Action Model

> [!summary] Summary
> ChatVLA presents a Vision-Language-Action model that successfully unifies multimodal understanding and embodied robot control into a single end-to-end neural network. The model achieves state-of-the-art performance in both visual question answering and 25 real-world robot manipulation tasks by mitigating common training challenges.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often lose their multimodal understanding and conversational abilities (spurious forgetting) when fine-tuned for robot control.
- Jointly training for robot control and multimodal understanding leads to task interference, where optimizing one capability degrades the performance of the other.
- The challenge of creating a truly unified AI agent capable of seamless perception, comprehension, and physical interaction with the environment.

## Method

- A ==Phased Alignment Training== strategy, a two-stage curriculum that first focuses on robot control mastery and then incrementally integrates visual-text data to reactivate multimodal understanding.
- A ==Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)== architecture implemented on the MLP layers of the transformer backbone, featuring distinct 'Control-Expert' and 'Understanding-Expert' components with shared self-attention layers.
- The model utilizes Qwen2-VL-2B as its Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone, adapting an action head design from DiVLA for robot control.

## Results

- ChatVLA achieved significant improvements on VQA benchmarks, scoring **71.2%** on TextVQA (a **9.2x** relative improvement over ECoT) and **37.4%** on MMStar (**2.2x** over DiVLA), demonstrating robust multimodal understanding.
- The model exhibited superior performance across **25** real-world robot manipulation tasks, consistently outperforming baselines like Octo and OpenVLA in long-horizon, compositional, and cross-skill multi-tasking.
- These strong results were obtained with greater parameter efficiency, using a **2B** parameter VLM backbone, which is **3.5x** smaller than some comparable VLA models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Spurious forgetting and task interference are critical limitations in developing generalist Vision-Language-Action models, and their existence was empirically validated.
> - A staged training approach (Phased Alignment Training) effectively prevents the degradation of multimodal understanding while acquiring robust robot control skills.
> - The Mixture-of-Experts architecture, with shared self-attention and dedicated MLP experts, allows for simultaneous high performance in both multimodal understanding and robot control tasks by minimizing interference.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in "ChatVLA" directly addresses a critical and evolving challenge at the intersection of Artificial Intelligence, Robotics, and Multimodal Learning: the unification of comprehensive scene understanding with effective physical interaction. This work is situated within the rapidly expanding landscape of Large Language Models (LLMs), Vision-Language Models (VLMs), and the more recent Vision-Language-Action Models (VLAs).

Historically, these domains have evolved somewhat independently:
- **Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs):** Models like LLaVA, Qwen2-VL, and others have achieved remarkable success in tasks requiring the integration of visual and linguistic information, such as visual question answering (VQA), image captioning, and complex spatial reasoning. They excel at "understanding" the world through perception and language. However, a major limitation is their inherent lack of physical embodiment and ability to interact with the environment, preventing them from translating their understanding into physical actions.
- **Vision-Language-Action Models (VLAs):** This emerging field aims to imbue VLMs with the capacity for physical interaction, primarily by fine-tuning pre-trained VLMs on robot demonstration data to predict actions. Prior works like OpenVLA, RT-2, DiffusionVLA, and ECoT have demonstrated impressive capabilities in robot control and manipulation tasks, both in simulation and real-world settings. They represent a step towards "acting" in the world.

The core problem identified by ChatVLA, and the gap it seeks to fill, is the paradox that current VLA models, despite building upon powerful VLMs, often "struggle to interpret and reason about multimodal data like images and text" once fine-tuned for robotic control. The paper systematically analyzes why existing VLA training paradigms lead to a trade-off: training for robot control degrades multimodal understanding, and vice versa. This "duality" highlights that a truly unified cognitive ability—one that perceives, comprehends, *and* interacts—remains an elusive goal.

ChatVLA positions itself as the first work to successfully unify these two critical capabilities in a single end-to-end neural network. It aims to demonstrate that a robot foundation model can simultaneously "engage in chat" (multimodal understanding) and perform robust robot control. By identifying and tackling the specific issues of "spurious forgetting" and "task interference," ChatVLA pushes the boundaries of what integrated AI systems can achieve, paving the way for more intelligent, versatile, and human-like robotic agents. This moves beyond merely applying LLMs to robotics and instead seeks to fundamentally integrate language-based understanding with embodied action within a coherent framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the ChatVLA research is to develop a unified end-to-end neural network capable of seamlessly performing both comprehensive multimodal understanding (including conversational ability) and effective embodied robot control. The authors are driven by the ambition to replicate the holistic cognitive ability of humans, who effortlessly perceive, comprehend, and interact with the physical world.

The motivation for this work stems from two critical, interconnected challenges identified through a systematic analysis of existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) training paradigms:

1. **Spurious Forgetting:** The authors observe that when pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are fine-tuned for robotic control tasks, they often experience a significant degradation or complete loss of their previously acquired visual-text alignment capabilities. This results in the model "forgetting" its conversational ability and general multimodal understanding. The paper suggests this isn't necessarily a complete loss of knowledge but rather a "shift in how the model aligns its internal representations with different tasks," making the alignment between robot actions and visual-text data fragile.
2. **Task Interference:** When control tasks (generating robot actions) and understanding tasks (interpreting visual-text semantics) are trained jointly, their distinct requirements often lead to conflicting updates within the shared parameter space of the neural network. This "competition" between the parameter spaces causes mutual performance degradation, meaning that optimizing for one task negatively impacts the performance of the other.

To overcome these fundamental limitations, the key objectives for ChatVLA are:
- To propose a novel framework that can effectively integrate multimodal understanding and embodied control within a single neural network without sacrificing performance in either domain.
- To develop a training strategy that prevents spurious forgetting of visual-text alignments during robot training.
- To design a model architecture that minimizes task interference between control and understanding tasks, allowing both to thrive.
- To extensively evaluate the proposed ChatVLA model across diverse benchmarks for visual question answering, general multimodal understanding, and real-world robot manipulation tasks, demonstrating its superior performance and unified capabilities compared to existing state-of-the-art methods.

In essence, the motivation is to create a robot foundation model that is truly "chatty" and "capable," bridging the cognitive gap between perception/reasoning and physical action in artificial agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of ChatVLA is built upon a two-pronged strategy to address the identified challenges of spurious forgetting and task interference: a novel training paradigm called Phased Alignment Training and a specialized Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) neural architecture.

The authors begin by empirically validating the existence of these challenges. They analyze three common VLA training paradigms:
1. **Robot Data Only:** Training solely on expert demonstration data with robot action trajectories.
2. **Robot Data with Reasoning Phrases:** Augmenting robot data with chain-of-thought-like reasoning to guide action.
3. **Co-training with Visual-Text Pairs and Robot Data:** Training with both image-text pairs and robot data.
Their analysis, using DiffusionVLA as a representative model, confirms that robot-only training entirely erodes conversational ability, reasoning data partially preserves it, and co-training with visual-text pairs significantly weakens control capabilities, thus substantiating the problems of spurious forgetting and task interference.

To mitigate these issues, ChatVLA proposes:

#### 3.1. Phased Alignment Training

Inspired by curriculum learning, this is a two-stage training strategy:
- **Stage 1: Control Mastery:** The model is initially trained exclusively on robot data, focusing on mastering embodied control tasks. During this stage, reasoning data is also included to ensure continuous alignment between visual and textual components. Crucially, only the "control expert" part of the model (described below) and its corresponding action head are activated. This prioritizes the acquisition of robust physical manipulation skills.
- **Stage 2: Multimodal Integration:** After achieving proficiency in robot control, the model transitions to co-training with both visual-text data (e.g., LLaVA-1.5 dataset) and robot data. This incremental integration aims to "reactivate" the frozen visual-text alignment links and enable the model to retain and improve its multimodal understanding capabilities without significantly degrading its control performance.

The rationale is that robot control is complex and requires initial focus, while visual-text alignment can be reactivated with smaller, targeted data after control is established.

#### 3.2. Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) Architecture

To address task interference, ChatVLA introduces a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) on the MLP (Multi-Layer Perceptron) layers of the transformer backbone, while sharing the self-attention layers.
- **Dual Routers and Experts:** The architecture employs two distinct types of experts: a "Control-Expert" dedicated to processing robot data and learning action representations, and an "Understanding-Expert" for handling visual-text data and developing representations for multimodal understanding and conversation. A dual router mechanism directs the input to the appropriate expert(s).
- **Shared Self-Attention Layers:** Unlike some MoE designs that might fully separate components, ChatVLA deliberately shares the multi-head self-attention layers between the two expert types. This design choice is motivated by the belief that understanding and robot control tasks share mutually beneficial representations (e.g., scene understanding, object recognition, spatial reasoning are vital for both). Shared attention layers facilitate this cross-task knowledge transfer.
- **Isolated MLP Layers:** The task-specific MLPs within the MoE allow each task to utilize a substantial portion of dedicated neurons. This effectively partitions the shared parameter space, minimizing interference by providing separate processing pathways for action generation and understanding.
- **Inference Efficiency:** During inference, only the relevant expert path is activated (determined by system prompts like "Answer based on question" for understanding or "Predict robot action" for control), preserving the efficiency of the base model.
- **Model Backbone:** ChatVLA utilizes Qwen2-VL-2B as its Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone, with the action head design following DiVLA.

This combined approach, inspired by Dual Coding Theory, allows ChatVLA to learn distinct yet interconnected representations for both physical skills and verbal/visual understanding, leading to simultaneous improvements in both domains.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive evaluations conducted for ChatVLA across multimodal understanding benchmarks and real-world robot tasks demonstrate its effectiveness in achieving unified capabilities, significantly outperforming existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.

#### 4.1. Initial Analysis and Validation of Challenges

The paper's preliminary analysis strongly validates the existence of "spurious forgetting" and "task interference."
- Training solely on robot data (e.g., OpenVLA, DiVLA) resulted in near-zero performance across all VQA and multimodal understanding benchmarks, confirming a complete loss of conversational ability.
- Co-training robot data with visual-text data, while partially preserving understanding, led to a significant performance drop in real-world robot task success rates, highlighting severe task interference.
- Incorporating reasoning into robot data (like ECoT) offered a partial recovery of understanding but still struggled with unified performance.

#### 4.2. Multimodal Understanding and Visual Question Answering

ChatVLA demonstrates highly competitive performance on a broad range of multimodal understanding and VQA benchmarks:
- **VQA Benchmarks (TextVQA, DocVQA, InfoVQA, AI2D, ChartQA, MTVQA, RealWorldQA):** ChatVLA achieves a notable 71.2% on TextVQA, surpassing current state-of-the-art VLAs like ECoT and DiVLA by substantial margins (9.2x and 9.5x relative improvements, respectively). It also shows strong results across other VQA tasks, comparable to or exceeding many dedicated Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs).
- **General Multimodal Understanding Benchmarks (MMMU, MMStar, MME, OCRBench, HallBench, MMBench):** ChatVLA attains a score of 37.4% on MMStar, demonstrating significant performance enhancements (2.2x over DiVLA and 6.9x over ECoT). On MMMU, it achieves 37.4%, a 6x improvement over ECoT. This indicates robust capabilities in complex cross-modal reasoning.
- **Parameter Efficiency:** Remarkably, ChatVLA achieves these strong understanding results with a 2B parameter VLM backbone, which is 3.5x fewer parameters than some comparable VLA models like ECoT (7B parameters).

#### 4.3. Real Robot Control

ChatVLA was extensively evaluated on 25 real-world robot manipulation tasks, categorized by instruction granularity:
- **Long-Horizon Tasks with Direct Prompting (e.g., "Sort toys," "Place the toy in the drawer"):** ChatVLA shows substantial advantages, achieving a 0.54 average success length in "Sort toys" (6.75x higher than Octo) and perfect success rates across the three-step sequence of "Place the toy in the drawer," demonstrating strong high-level instruction following.
- **Long-Horizon Tasks with High-Level Planner (e.g., "Move the orange block to the basket," "Prepare breakfast for me"):** The model consistently outperforms OpenVLA and Octo across all configurations, highlighting its compositional reasoning capabilities in integrating object manipulation, spatial reasoning, and adaptation to diverse sub-tasks and object types.
- **Cross-Skill Multi-Tasking (in bathroom, kitchen, and tabletop environments):** ChatVLA achieves superior average success rates compared to Octo and OpenVLA across all task categories. Its performance is particularly strong in challenging, constrained environments like bathrooms and kitchens, underscoring its operational precision and robustness in real-world scenarios with safety considerations.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Vision-Language Data Preference:** Analysis reveals that performance gaps with pure MLLMs (like Qwen2-VL) on specific MMMU subcategories (e.g., art theory, lab medicine, literature) are linked to the lack of specialized expert knowledge in the LLaVA dataset used for co-training. This suggests significant potential for further improvement with carefully curated, professional visual-text data.
- **Ratio of Visual-Text Data to Robot Data:** A smaller ratio of visual-text data to robot data (1:3) during co-training yielded better overall performance than 1:1 or 3:1 ratios. This supports the hypothesis that a limited amount of visual-text data is sufficient to "reactivate" visual-text alignment and bridge the knowledge gap, while preventing excessive interference with the robot control learning process.

In summary, ChatVLA's main findings are that its Phased Alignment Training and Mixture-of-Experts architecture effectively address spurious forgetting and task interference, leading to a unified model that achieves state-of-the-art or highly competitive performance in both multimodal understanding and real-world robot control tasks, often with greater parameter efficiency than existing methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ChatVLA research marks a significant advancement in the field of artificial intelligence and robotics, with profound potential impact across several domains. Its core contribution is demonstrating the feasibility and efficacy of unifying robust multimodal understanding with dexterous robot control within a single neural network, a challenge that has long plagued the development of intelligent embodied agents.

The significance of ChatVLA stems from several key aspects:

1. **Towards Truly Unified AI Agents:** By successfully overcoming the "spurious forgetting" and "task interference" limitations, ChatVLA paves the way for the creation of truly generalist AI agents. These agents can not only perceive and comprehend the nuances of the physical world through complex visual and linguistic inputs but also translate that understanding into meaningful physical actions. This moves beyond siloed AI capabilities towards a more integrated and human-like intelligence.

2. **Enhanced Robot Capabilities and Autonomy:** The demonstrated superior performance on 25 diverse real-world robot tasks, including long-horizon and cross-skill multi-tasking in challenging environments like kitchens and bathrooms, signifies a leap in robot capabilities. Future robots powered by such unified models could operate with greater autonomy, flexibility, and adaptability in unstructured and dynamic real-world settings. This has direct implications for:
 - **Service Robotics:** More capable home assistants, elder care robots, or hospitality robots that can understand complex instructions, interpret subtle environmental cues, and perform a wide range of tasks.
 - **Industrial Automation:** Flexible manufacturing robots capable of adapting to varying product designs or unexpected changes on the assembly line, requiring less explicit programming.
 - **Exploration and Disaster Response:** Robots that can interpret complex sensory data, engage in natural language reasoning, and perform intricate manipulation in hazardous or unknown environments.

3. **Advancing Multimodal Foundation Models:** ChatVLA shows that foundation models can maintain and even significantly improve their multimodal understanding and conversational abilities *while* learning to control robots. This expands the utility and design space for next-generation Vision-Language-Action models, proving that robotics training doesn't have to come at the cost of cognitive abilities. The insight that visual-text alignment can be "reactivated" with limited data is crucial for continual learning in complex multimodal settings.

4. **Parameter Efficiency and Practical Deployment:** Achieving state-of-the-art or highly competitive results with a more parameter-efficient design (2B parameters compared to 7B in some baselines) is a critical practical advantage. It makes these complex models more amenable to deployment on resource-constrained robotic hardware, reducing computational costs and increasing inference speed, which are essential for real-time robotic operation.

5. **Guiding Future Research:** The ablation studies offer valuable insights for future work, particularly highlighting the importance of carefully curated, specialized visual-text data to further boost multimodal understanding in niche domains (e.g., medical, artistic theory). This opens new research avenues in dataset creation and adaptive training strategies. The success of the shared attention mechanism also suggests a productive direction for designing architectures that foster beneficial knowledge transfer between disparate tasks.

The involvement of Midea Group underscores the strong industrial relevance and demand for such integrated robotic intelligence. ChatVLA represents a tangible step towards a future where robots are not just physical automatons but intelligent collaborators capable of sophisticated perception, reasoning, and interaction, ultimately bridging the gap between digital intelligence and physical embodiment.
