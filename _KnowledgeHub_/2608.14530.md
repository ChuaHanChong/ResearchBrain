# Marionette: Predicting World States, Rendering Geometry, Painting Appearance

> [!summary] Summary
> Marionette is a game world model that predicts an explicit 276-dimensional articulated world state (skeleton, root trajectory, rotation) via autoregressive dynamics, renders it to pose-control video with a zero-parameter geometric bridge, then synthesizes RGB via video diffusion. Forcing wrong actions changes joint error by 31%; state-level rules cut ground penetration 66% with no fidelity loss (FVD 831 vs 799 recorded).

## Problem

- Interactive world models autoregress pixels or latents directly, so pose, geometry, and occlusion are only implicitly maintained by the same generative sequence, not exact.
- Over long rollouts a model re-consumes its own output, so errors in implicit properties like consistency and controllability compound and degrade with horizon.
- Neural generators are weak at exact bookkeeping and discrete logic (e.g. redstone circuit state in Minecraft), yet existing systems force them to carry this burden anyway.

## Method

- Two-stage dynamics: a compact **ActionGPT** autoregresses discrete per-entity action tokens, and a larger **PoseGPT** converts chosen actions into continuous 258D body pose, decoupling decision from animation.
- Zero-parameter ==graphics bridge== reconstructs metric world-space skeletons from state via ==Gram-Schmidt rotation==, cumulative root integration, and joint placement, then rasterizes closed-form geometry and occlusion.
- Control-conditioned ==video-diffusion== observation model (Wan2.2-Fun-5B) renders pose-control video into photorealistic RGB, using ==chunk-relay conditioning== on prior final frames for long-horizon generation.

## Results

- Forcing a mismatched action stream raises root-aligned joint error by **31%** (0.272 to 0.357m) across **48** held-out segments, with forced-correct matching free-run (0.272 vs 0.281).
- Marionette reaches FVD **831** versus the pixel-autoregressive baseline's **975**, and against recorded-pose driving state scores **799**, with bootstrap intervals overlapping across all conditions.
- A terrain-collider rule cuts ground-penetration collision-frame ratio **66%** (0.337 to 0.114) and a separation cap holds character distance at **5.1m** versus **21.2m** uncontrolled, without touching the observation model.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Splitting a generative world model into an explicit metric state plus a fixed deterministic renderer lets exact geometry be guaranteed by construction rather than learned.
> - Long-horizon failure modes are properties of the state layer and can be repaired there by explicit rules, isolating dynamics fixes from appearance quality entirely.
> - Routing appearance synthesis through a predicted rather than recorded state costs no detectable visual fidelity, suggesting decoupling structure from appearance is nearly free.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research by Meng et al. operates within the domain of interactive game world models, a field primarily focused on generating playable video streams from user actions. Prior work in this area has largely relied on autoregressing visual observations directly in pixel or latent space. Systems such as those by Alonso et al., Kim et al., and Bruce et al. have achieved notable visual quality in generating game environments. However, these methods implicitly maintain structured properties like pose, geometry, and occlusion through the same generative sequence. This implicit approach can lead to compounding errors over long rollouts, degrading consistency and controllability as the generative model repeatedly consumes its own outputs.

The paper positions itself as a departure from this prevalent approach by advocating for an explicit representation of the world state. This aligns with a growing body of research that seeks to separate structural and dynamic elements from visual appearance in video generation. Previous efforts in this direction include grounding video generation in explicit 3D or memory, specifying camera trajectories explicitly, or predicting explicit structure before rendering, as seen in driving simulations or human video generation. Some position papers have also argued for the necessity of structured, rule-governed interfaces and deterministic components in generative world models. This work also resonates with mechanistic analyses that interpret video generators through the lens of state and dynamics.

In the context of controllable articulated motion, a sub-problem central to Marionette, advancements have been made in generating motion under text or kinematic constraints using diffusion and sequence models, and through physics simulators. Models like ARDY and Kimodo demonstrate high-quality motion generation by separating explicit root motion from learned body representations or scaling kinematic models with extensive data. Marionette leverages these concepts by isolating the animation task.

Furthermore, the paper differentiates its "world model" from control-oriented latent world models used for planning or occupancy world models for driving perception. Marionette targets an interactive and persistent game world, prioritizing interpretability, render-readiness, consistency, and controllability, which necessitates a state that serves both forward prediction and photorealistic synthesis.

The core contribution of this work lies in formally instantiating a decoupled architecture that explicitly models the low-dimensional world state and delegates exact geometric computations to a fixed, zero-parameter renderer, thus leaving the neural model to focus solely on appearance synthesis. This architecture addresses the limitations of purely autoregressive pixel- or latent-space models by introducing guarantees for consistency and controllability through explicit state manipulation and deterministic rendering.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the Marionette framework are rooted in addressing fundamental limitations of existing interactive game world models.

**Core Problem Statement:**
Interactive game world models often generate future frames by directly autoregressing visual observations in pixel or latent space. This approach implicitly handles all structured properties of the game world—such as character pose, geometry, object identity, occlusion, and the precise effects of control inputs—within the same high-dimensional generative sequence. Over extended time horizons, errors in these implicitly maintained properties accumulate. As the model is continuously fed its own potentially erroneous outputs, the consistency of the generated world and the controllability of its elements degrade significantly. This makes long-term, coherent, and precisely steerable simulations challenging to achieve.

**Underlying Insight and Proposed Solution:**
The authors posit that a video observation is a high-dimensional manifestation of an underlying low-dimensional world state. Learning to predict `p(video)` directly results in an entanglement of physics, entity identity, and control within pixel space. Marionette proposes an explicit decomposition of this problem by positing an explicit world state `s`. This factorization makes dynamics and appearance conditionally independent, effectively splitting the complex task into two distinct and well-defined sub-problems:
1. **Dynamics Model:** `p(s_t+1 | s_<=t, control)` – predicting the evolution of the explicit world state.
2. **Observation Model:** `p(obs | s)` – synthesizing visual observations from the predicted world state.

**Justification for the Decoupling Principle:**
The motivation for drawing the line between these two components stems from the observed strengths and weaknesses of neural generative models. Neural models are highly effective at synthesizing realistic appearance and maintaining perceptual plausibility. However, they are inherently less suited for tasks requiring exact bookkeeping over long durations or precise discrete logic. The paper uses the example of redstone circuits in Minecraft: whether a lamp turns on is a matter of logical circuit state, not visual plausibility. A generator focused on pixels might plausibly render a lamp incorrectly based on its internal state, whereas a deterministic system would compute the correct logical outcome.

Therefore, the core motivation is to assign the parts of the world that demand exactness—specifically geometry, occlusion, and metric motion—to a deterministic renderer. This renderer acts as a "tool" that executes exact computations, analogous to a language model using a calculator. The neural model is then exclusively responsible for adding photorealistic appearance to the geometrically accurate outputs of this deterministic component. This design choice aims to ensure that exact and consistent properties of the world are maintained by construction, rather than being emergent and fragile properties of an unconstrained generative sequence.

**Specific Objectives of Marionette:**
- To instantiate this decoupling principle in a world model tailored for interactive games featuring articulated characters.
- To create an explicit, interpretable 276-dimensional 3D world state that precisely captures multi-entity articulated skeletons, metric root trajectories, and rotations.
- To develop a zero-parameter graphics bridge that deterministically converts this predicted state into pose-control videos, handling all geometric and occlusion computations.
- To leverage a control-conditioned video-diffusion observation model to synthesize photorealistic RGB observations from these structured controls.
- To demonstrate that the predicted world state is directly controllable via explicit action tokens.
- To establish that long-horizon behavior is primarily determined within this explicit state and can be repaired or governed by applying rules directly to the state, without altering the appearance generation model.
- To show that routing appearance through this predicted state does not significantly compromise visual fidelity.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Marionette implements its core idea—separating dynamics and appearance via an explicit world state and a deterministic renderer—through a three-component architecture.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The world is represented by an explicit, interpretable state `s_t`, a 276-dimensional vector for each frame `t`. This state encodes two articulated entities (a monster and a player character, the hunter), detailing their metric root trajectories, root-relative joint positions, and 6D root rotations, along with a small weapon sub-state.
The state `s` is structured as:
`s = [δ_M_0:3 (monster root displacement), p_M_3:162 (monster 53x3 joints), δ_N_162:165 (hunter root displacement), p_N_165:258 (hunter 31x3 joints), w_258:264 (weapon), r_M_264:270 (monster 6D rotation), r_N_270:276 (hunter 6D rotation)]`.
This root-relative and delta-based representation ensures invariance to global position and heading and prepares the state for rendering.
The control signal `c_t` provides, for each entity, a discrete action ID from a predefined vocabulary (e.g., attacking, dodging, walking) and the character's heading. Root displacement and rotation can also be directly overwritten by the control input.

#### 3.2. Three-Component Architecture

The overall framework follows the decomposition: `p(video) = ∫ p(obs | s) p(s_t+1 | s_<=t, control) ds`.

##### 3.2.1. Two-stage Dynamics Model (`p(s_t+1 | s_<=t, c)`)

This model predicts future world states autoregressively, factoring the prediction into discrete decision and continuous animation stages.

- **ActionGPT (Decision Stage):**
 - A compact causal transformer (4 layers, 256 width, ≈2.5M parameters) autoregresses one discrete action token per frame for each entity.
 - It is conditioned on a low-dimensional summary of recent state, including the 18D root/rotation stream, learned action embeddings, animation progress, and the weapon sub-state.
 - It also regresses the next root motion and orientation.
 - Control is applied by directly overwriting the sampled action token at inference, allowing precise command injection without retraining.

- **PoseGPT (Animation Stage):**
 - A larger causal transformer (8 layers, 512 width, ≈25M parameters) maps the chosen action tokens and recent body state to the next-frame 258D body pose (relative joint positions and weapon state for both entities).
 - It functions as an animation player, translating discrete actions into continuous body configurations.
 - This split maintains a compact and interpretable control interface while dedicating sufficient capacity for complex pose synthesis.

- **Autoregressive Rollout:**
 - At each step, ActionGPT proposes actions and root motion.
 - Optional control overrides can replace action tokens or directly manipulate root displacement/rotation.
 - PoseGPT animates the body based on these (potentially overridden) actions.
 - The resulting components are assembled into the next 276D state, which is appended to the context for the next prediction.
 - Optional terrain conditioning (egocentric height patch, per-joint ground clearances encoded by an MLP) is fed to both stages, and the animation stage incorporates ground-contact and non-penetration objectives.

##### 3.2.2. Deterministic Graphics Bridge (`R(s)`)

This component converts a state sequence into a pose-control video with zero learnable parameters, ensuring geometric exactness.

- **Process:**
 1. **Rotation Conversion:** 6D root rotations are converted to 3x3 rotation matrices using Gram–Schmidt orthonormalization.
 2. **Root Integration:** Local root displacements are rotated into the world frame and cumulatively summed to recover absolute root trajectories.
 3. **Joint Placement:** Root-relative joint positions are transformed by adding the root-frame-rotated offsets to the absolute root positions, yielding metric world-space skeletons.
 4. **Terrain Integration:** A scanned height field of the game stage is tessellated into a triangle mesh in the same world frame.
 5. **Camera View:** The scene is viewed either by a deterministic follow-camera (framing both entities) or a supplied ground-truth camera.
 6. **Rasterization:** Skeletons (bones and joints) are projected to image space and rasterized into a three-channel geometry buffer, the pose-control frame.
- **Key Design:** All operations are closed-form geometric computations, guaranteeing world-space consistency, correct occlusion ordering, and metric scale. Drift in rendered geometry can only originate from errors in the predicted state itself.
- **Pose-Control Frame Encoding:** The three channels encode height (R), categorical per-joint identity (G), and inverse depth (B). Terrain and bone height values occupy distinct R-ranges, G-channel provides specific IDs for terrain, weapon, monster, and hunter joints, and occlusion is managed via a depth buffer. Near-camera terrain fragments are discarded to prevent character occlusion. The frame is rendered into a half-float buffer with 10 bits per channel.
- **Terrain Handling:** Beyond rendering, the bridge enables "feasibility enforcement" by projecting an infeasible root back onto the ground surface, preventing compounding errors from impossible configurations.

##### 3.2.3. Observation Model (`p(obs | R(s))`)

This component synthesizes photorealistic RGB observations from the pose-control video.

- **Architecture:** A control-conditioned video-diffusion model (Wan2.2-Fun-5B backbone) based on a modern DiT video backbone.
- **Function:** It maps the pose-control video generated by the graphics bridge, along with a first-frame image (for identity/appearance consistency), to photorealistic RGB frames.
- **Specialization:** Because the geometry, depth, and articulation are precisely defined by the state and rendered by the bridge, the observation model's primary task is to "paint" appearance.
- **Long-Horizon Rollout:** For horizons beyond a single diffusion window, a chunk-relay rollout is employed. The model generates fixed-length chunks, and the final frames of one chunk seed the next, propagating appearance while geometry and dynamics remain governed by the explicit state and deterministic bridge.

#### 3.3. Data and Evaluation Protocol

- **Data Source:** Recordings from a commercial action game, specifically the WildWorld dataset, providing synchronized RGB video streams and 276D articulated world states. Terrain height fields were also scanned and integrated.
- **Training Data:**
 - **Dynamics Model:** 1,395 motion segments (≈227.6 hours) at 20 fps, with 173 monster and 689 hunter action IDs.
 - **Observation Model:** RGB view from 4,008 unique clips (≈673.8 hours) at 704 × 1280 resolution, with 81-frame chunks at 30 fps.
- **Scope:** Dynamics model is trained on single-monster gameplay, while the observation model covers multi-monster identities and various weapon types for broader appearance generalization.
- **Evaluation Protocol:** Combines two layers of metrics (state and observation) with fixed seed contexts, rollout horizons, and control-injection scripts.
 - **State Layer Metrics:** Computed by reconstructing world-space joints from the predicted state and comparing to ground truth or physical plausibility. Examples: ground penetration, foot-skate, entity separation, RA-MPJPE for articulated pose. These are absolute unit measurements.
 - **Observation Layer Metrics:** Computed on rendered RGB, primarily FVD (Fréchet Video Distance) for perceptual and temporal quality. FVD is used for both short clips and across long-horizon chunks.
- **Baseline:** An end-to-end pixel-autoregressive world model trained on the same RGB footage, conditioned on the same first frame, and rolled out with the same chunk relay. This baseline implicitly learns rendering, dynamics, and control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted with Marionette establish properties related to the controllability of its explicit world state and the determinism of its long-horizon behavior.

#### 4.1. Q1: Direct Controllability of the Predicted World State

- **Experiment Design:** To assess if control inputs causally influence the generated world, 48 held-out segments were rolled out from a common seed context under three hunter action stream conditions:
 - **Free:** The model autonomously sampled actions.
 - **Force-GT:** Action IDs were overwritten with recorded ground-truth actions.
 - **Force-Shuf:** Action IDs were overwritten with a temporally shuffled, mismatched version of ground-truth actions.
 - Root-aligned Mean Per-Joint Position Error (RA-MPJPE) against the recorded pose was used as the metric to quantify articulated pose changes, isolating it from global translation. Decoding was deterministic.
- **Results:**
 - A mismatched action stream (Force-Shuf) resulted in a 31% degradation in pose accuracy, with RA-MPJPE increasing from 0.272 meters (Force-GT) to 0.357 meters. Force-GT outperformed Force-Shuf in 33 of 48 segments.
 - Forcing the correct ground-truth stream (Force-GT, 0.272 m) showed only a marginal improvement over the free-run condition (Free, 0.281 m).
 - A breakdown by action class indicated that error inflation from a wrong token was most pronounced for stationary actions (2.3x increase) and in-place attacks (1.8x increase), compared to locomotion actions (1.3x increase), where pose shapes typically vary less across different action IDs.
- **Conclusion:** These results indicate that the explicit action token directly and causally influences the generated articulated pose. The generated body responds to the actions it is commanded to perform, demonstrating that the control interface has authority over the predicted state. The marginal difference between Free and Force-GT suggests that the dynamics model often predicts actions closely aligned with the ground truth on these held-out segments.

#### 4.2. Q2: Long-Horizon Behavior Determined and Repairable in the State

- **Experiment Design:** To investigate the determinants of long-horizon behavior, the observation model was held constant while varying only the state stream driving it. Twelve held-out windows were evaluated using four driving states: recorded ground-truth, generated without rules, generated with a terrain collider, and generated with both a terrain collider and a separation cap. State-layer metrics included ground penetration (collision-frame ratio (CFR), mean maximum penetration depth (MMP)), foot-skate, and entity separation.
- **Baseline Failure (Generated, no rule):**
 - Over the rollout, the generated hunter and monster characters drifted significantly apart, from an initial 4.9 meters to 21.2 meters at the final frame, compared to recorded sessions which stayed near 5 meters.
 - Ground penetration was substantial, with 33.7% of frames showing at least one of 11 key joints below the terrain (CFR = 0.337) and a mean maximum penetration depth (MMP) of 0.444 m.
- **Rule Implementation:**
 - **Terrain Collider:** A rule imposed on the explicit state to prevent the character's root from stepping onto effectively-vertical "walls" (steps >1.5m) and vertically shifting the entire body so its lowest joint rests on the surface, with a rate limit.
 - **Separation Cap:** A rule that cancels the outward component of motion if the two characters would move more than 6 meters apart, splitting the correction evenly between them. Neither rule reads future trajectory information.
- **Results on Rule Effects (Table 2):**
 - **Terrain Collider Alone:** Reduced the ground penetration CFR from 0.337 to 0.114 (a 66% reduction) and MMP to 0.157 m. The entity separation, however, remained at 21.2 meters. Foot-skate was largely unaffected.
 - **Terrain Collider + Separation Cap:** Maintained the reduced ground penetration (CFR = 0.114, MMP = 0.155 m) and effectively constrained the final entity separation to 5.1 meters. This rule introduced a slight increase in foot-skate (from 1.14 m/s to 1.22 m/s), consistent with motion cancellation.
 - For comparison, the recorded state exhibited CFR of 0.082, MMP of 0.023m, foot-skate of 1.13 m/s, and separation of 4.8 m.
- **Conclusion:** These findings support that long-horizon failures (e.g., character drift, ground penetration) are properties of the explicit world state. Furthermore, rules imposed directly on this state can effectively repair these failures without requiring modifications or retraining of the observation model. Each rule exhibited a targeted effect on its corresponding degree of freedom, demonstrating the interpretability and manipulability of the explicit state.

#### 4.3. Observation Layer Fidelity

- **FVD Comparison (Table 1):** Marionette, with its predicted state driving the observation model, achieved an FVD of 831. When the same observation model was driven by recorded (ground-truth) pose, it yielded an FVD of 799. The pixel-autoregressive baseline scored an FVD of 975.
- **Conclusion:** Routing appearance through the explicit predicted state and deterministic renderer incurs no detectable loss in visual fidelity compared to driving with ground-truth pose, as indicated by the overlapping FVD bootstrap intervals. This demonstrates that the decoupling does not compromise visual quality while providing significant advantages in consistency and control. The FVD per chunk for all four state driving conditions remained within a narrow aggregate band (747-831), with no collapse over the horizon.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Marionette framework represents a significant contribution to the field of interactive world modeling by proposing and demonstrating an explicit architectural decoupling of world state dynamics from visual appearance generation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Consistency and Controllability:** By moving exact geometric and physical computations to a fixed, zero-parameter graphics bridge, Marionette guarantees world-space consistency, correct occlusion, and metric scale by construction. This inherently prevents the accumulation of errors in structural properties that plague purely generative, pixel- or latent-space models. The explicit, interpretable state allows for direct control through mechanisms like token overwriting or root manipulation, leading to more precise and reliable interactive experiences. This direct control over the underlying state offers a tangible improvement for long-horizon simulations where consistency is paramount.

- **Interpretability and Debuggability:** The 276-dimensional explicit world state is human-readable and metric. This interpretability allows researchers and developers to directly measure physical plausibility (e.g., ground penetration in meters, character separation) and diagnose failures at the state level. This contrasts with implicit latent states, where errors are harder to pinpoint and debug. The ability to measure and attribute drift directly to the state provides a clear advantage in understanding and improving complex world models.

- **Modular and Efficient Design:** The modular architecture—comprising a two-stage dynamics model, a deterministic graphics bridge, and a control-conditioned observation model—allows each component to specialize. Neural networks are leveraged for their strengths in plausible dynamics and high-fidelity appearance synthesis, while deterministic computation handles exact physics and geometry. This "offloading principle" ensures that tasks are assigned to the most appropriate computational paradigm, potentially leading to more efficient model development and deployment.

- **Direct Repairability of State-Level Failures:** A key impact is the demonstrated ability to repair long-horizon failures by imposing rules directly on the explicit world state. As shown with the terrain collider and separation cap, geometric or physical inconsistencies can be corrected at the state level (e.g., projecting a character onto the ground) without requiring retraining or modification of the neural observation model. This capability is not feasible with implicit, pixel-only models and offers a powerful mechanism for building robust virtual environments.

- **Foundation for Future Interactive Systems:** Marionette's approach offers a blueprint for creating more robust, interpretable, and controllable interactive virtual worlds. This could impact:
 - **Game Development:** Providing tools for generating realistic and controllable game content, characters, and environments, potentially accelerating game design and reducing manual animation efforts.
 - **Simulation Environments:** Offering a framework for high-fidelity simulations where precise control over physical entities and environmental interactions is crucial, such as in robotics training, autonomous driving scenarios, or scientific modeling.
 - **AI Agent Training:** Enabling AI agents to learn and operate within more stable and predictable virtual worlds, where their actions have clear and consistent consequences.
 - **Theoretical Advancement:** The paper's argument that "constraining what recurs is more effective than regularizing an unconstrained generator" challenges the conventional wisdom in some areas of generative modeling and encourages a re-evaluation of intermediate representations.

In summary, Marionette provides a principled and empirically supported approach to world modeling that prioritizes explicit state representation and deterministic rendering. This design choice results in demonstrably improved consistency, controllability, and interpretability, offering a significant step towards more robust and useful interactive generative systems.
