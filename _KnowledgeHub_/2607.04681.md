# Do Vision-Language-Action Models Mean What They Say? On the Role of Faithfulness in Embodied Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces a novel method to explicitly optimize for "faithful reasoning" in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for autonomous driving, employing a learned critic called Pinocchio. The approach enhances the causal consistency between a VLA policy's verbalized reasoning and its actions, demonstrating a 1.6x improvement in overall causal alignment when responding to out-of-distribution hazards.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models utilizing "Embodied Chain-of-Thought" (CoT) lack guarantees that their verbalized reasoning accurately reflects the internal decision process.
- Existing faithfulness concepts primarily focus on "functional reasoning" (task performance improvement) rather than "faithful reasoning" (true internal process reflection), leading to potentially ungrounded or inconsistent explanations.
- The absence of faithful reasoning limits interpretability, generalization, and robustness, particularly in data-sparse "long-tail" scenarios critical for safety-critical applications like autonomous driving.

## Method

- Researchers formalized ==behavioral faithfulness== as a tractable objective by defining a set of semantic consistency checks across reasoning stages, from observation to final action.
- They developed **Pinocchio**, a fine-tuned Vision-Language Model (VLM) critic, trained on a 40,000-record dataset, to reliably identify inconsistencies along the reasoning chain with 0.87 balanced accuracy.
- Pinocchio's log-probabilities were integrated as a dense, granular reward signal within a ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== reinforcement learning (RL) post-training framework to optimize VLA policies for both task performance and explicit embodied faithfulness.

## Results

- The Pinocchio critic achieved a balanced accuracy of **0.87** in detecting faithfulness violations across different reasoning stages on a held-out dataset.
- Policies trained with the Pinocchio critic demonstrated the highest overall faithfulness (**61.4%** on OOD DE data and **64.8%** on in-distribution US data), a significant increase from the SFT baseline (**27.7%**) and ADE-only baseline (**43.4%**).
- In synthetically generated out-of-distribution hazard scenarios, the faithfulness-optimized model showed a **1.6x** improvement in overall causal alignment between its reasoning and actions (**7.6%** vs **4.6%**) compared to a state-of-the-art baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A weak and inconclusive coupling exists between general policy performance improvements (e.g., reduced ADE) and the inherent quality or faithfulness of the generated reasoning.
> - Explicitly optimizing for behavioral faithfulness leads to more transparent and causally aligned decision-making in VLA models, enhancing interpretability and trustworthiness.
> - Faithful reasoning is essential for robust generalization, enabling policies to respond more appropriately and consistently to novel, out-of-distribution hazards in safety-critical situations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

This work operates within the rapidly evolving landscape of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and their application in robotics, particularly autonomous driving. It specifically addresses a critical aspect of model interpretability and reliability known as "faithfulness."

The paper builds upon the recent success of Foundation Models (FMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs), which have demonstrated capabilities in various learning-based decision-making tasks due to their self-supervised training on extensive internet-scale corpora. These models, when post-trained on robot demonstrations, form VLA policies that exhibit generalizable behaviors across diverse robotic environments. A key development in this area is "Embodied Chain-of-Thought" (CoT) reasoning, which augments action generation with intermediate, structured language steps. This approach aims to enhance robot decision-making and provide interpretability for black-box VLA models, paralleling advancements in language-only reasoning models.

However, the paper identifies a gap in existing research: while CoT promises interpretability, the extent to which these verbalized reasoning traces truthfully reflect the policy's internal decision process remains underexplored in embodied contexts. Previous efforts in supervising embodied reasoning have largely focused on "functional reasoning"—where reasoning improves task performance—rather than "faithful reasoning," which concerns whether the generated trace accurately reflects the policy's underlying action selection mechanism. Existing faithfulness metrics, particularly those adapted from Large Language Models (LLMs), are deemed necessary but insufficient for embodied agents, often exhibiting limited causal diagnostic power and allowing for explanations that are ungrounded, inconsistent, or weakly coupled to the actual policy behavior.

This research distinguishes between these two properties—functional and faithful reasoning—and argues that a stronger notion of faithfulness is crucial not only for interpretability but also for the generalization and robustness of VLA policies, especially in "long-tail" scenarios characterized by rare events and sparse data. The paper positions itself as extending the understanding and improvement of faithfulness from language models to embodied intelligence, with autonomous driving serving as a representative testbed due to its safety-critical nature and complex decision-making requirements. It aims to develop a methodology to explicitly optimize for embodied faithfulness during policy training, thereby influencing how robot policies are trained rather than merely how they are evaluated post-hoc.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from a perceived gap in the understanding and optimization of reasoning in embodied Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. While "Embodied Chain-of-Thought" (CoT) has emerged as a method for enhancing robot decision-making and interpretability, the authors contend that it is unclear whether the verbalized CoT accurately reflects the policy’s true internal decision process.

The key objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

1. **Distinguish Functional vs. Faithful Reasoning:** The foundational objective is to formally differentiate between "functional reasoning" and "faithful reasoning." Functional reasoning is defined as the property where the reasoning process contributes to improved task performance (e.g., better driving trajectories). Faithful reasoning, conversely, refers to whether the generated reasoning trace genuinely reflects the policy's internal computation that leads to an action. The paper argues that current VLA models primarily optimize for functional reasoning, potentially leading to plausible but unfaithful explanations.

2. **Address Limitations of Current Faithfulness Notions:** The authors posit that existing approaches to faithfulness, often operationalized as broad reasoning-action alignment, are necessary but insufficient. They argue that these methods can admit reasoning whose intermediate steps mask causal links through confounding factors, leading to explanations that are weakly grounded in the environment, internally inconsistent, or poorly connected across reasoning steps. This restricts policy generalization and hinders true interpretability.

3. **Hypothesize the Value of Faithful Reasoning for Generalization:** A central hypothesis is that faithful reasoning is particularly important for robust performance in "long-tail" scenarios (e.g., rare hazards, unusual interactions, safety-critical edge cases). In such data-sparse situations, a policy relying on faithful reasoning should draw on its internal logical structure to make causally determined decisions, rather than producing rationalizations for precomputed or spuriously correlated actions. This implies that faithfulness is not merely an interpretability feature but a prerequisite for robust and generalizable embodied intelligence.

4. **Develop a Tractable Objective for Embodied Faithfulness:** Given the difficulty of directly optimizing for mechanistic faithfulness (which requires interventional guarantees on internal computations), a major objective is to operationalize a "behavioral surrogate" for embodied faithfulness. This involves deriving a tractable learning objective from a formal definition of faithfulness, which can then be instantiated as a scalable dense reward signal for reinforcement learning (RL) post-training.

5. **Evaluate the Coupling Between Reasoning Quality and Performance:** A preliminary objective is to empirically investigate the relationship between reasoning quality and task performance in a State-of-The-Art (SoTA) VLA model for autonomous driving. This involves a human evaluation to determine if trajectory-level gains from RL post-training consistently translate into improved reasoning quality, or if the policy learns to "route around" its reasoning trace.

6. **Introduce a Learned Critic for Faithfulness Violations:** To implement the behavioral faithfulness objective, a specific goal is to develop "Pinocchio," a learned critic model capable of reliably identifying inconsistencies between observations, intermediate reasoning steps, and actions. This critic is designed to provide dense, granular feedback on various stages of the reasoning process.

7. **Assess Impact on Task Performance and Robustness:** The ultimate objective is to evaluate whether optimizing for this embodied faithfulness objective improves not only reasoning consistency but also policy trustworthiness and robustness, particularly under distribution shifts and in synthetically generated out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios representative of long-tail challenges.

In summary, the research is motivated by the need for more reliable, transparent, and generalizable embodied AI, seeking to achieve this by rigorously defining, measuring, and optimizing for faithfulness in reasoning-based VLA policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employed a multi-stage approach to investigate and improve faithfulness in embodied reasoning policies, using autonomous driving as the primary application domain. The methodology can be broken down into problem formulation, a preliminary investigation, formalization of faithfulness, development of a learned critic, and a comprehensive evaluation of its impact.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The authors define an embodied agent interacting with an environment, receiving an observation ($o_t$) and emitting an action ($a_t$). A key component is the intermediate natural-language reasoning trace ($z_t$), which typically has a structured form, $z_t = (z^{(1)}_t..., z^{(K)}_t)$, corresponding to distinct stages of deliberation. In their instantiation for autonomous driving, $K=3$: $z^{(1)}$ is a scene description, $z^{(2)}$ is a move justification, and $z^{(3)}$ is a discrete meta-action (longitudinal and lateral decisions). Actions ($a_t$) are represented as a sequence of relative waypoints. The primary performance metric for trajectory quality is Average Displacement Error (ADE).

#### 3.2. Preliminary Investigation: Reasoning Quality Coupled with Policy Improvement?

To establish the need for explicit faithfulness optimization, the authors conducted a human evaluation. They compared outputs from a State-of-The-Art (SoTA) driving model (Alpamayo) at its Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) post-training checkpoints. For 200 validation samples, human annotators judged whether the reasoning traces differed substantively, were grounded in the scene, and which model achieved lower ADE. This qualitative assessment aimed to determine if RL-based trajectory improvements inherently led to better reasoning quality or merely bypassed it.

#### 3.3. Formalizing Faithfulness

The core of the methodology involves formalizing faithfulness.
- **Generative Factorization**: A faithful reasoning policy is hypothesized to factorize its joint distribution ($p(a,z|o)$) as a first-order Markov chain along a directed acyclic graph (DAG) $G_{gen} = (V,E)$, where $V=\{o, z_1..., z_K, a\}$. This implies that each reasoning stage $z_k$ summarizes all task-relevant information from its predecessors. In their setup, the path is $o \to z_1 \to z_2 \to z_3 \to a$.
- **Mechanistic Faithfulness**: This is defined interventionally, requiring that the trace variables $z_{1:K}$ are causal mediators of the policy's action computation. Any intervention on $z_k$ must propagate downstream, with no residual causal pathway from the observation ($o$) to the action ($a$) bypassing $z_k$. This strong form is acknowledged as impractical for direct optimization.
- **Behavioral Faithfulness as a Tractable Surrogate**: To enable optimization, a behavioral surrogate is introduced based on a necessary condition for mechanistic faithfulness. This involves a set $C$ of pairwise semantic consistency checks for specific pairs $(u,v)$ where $u$ is upstream of $v$ in $G_{gen}$. In their instantiation, $C$ includes five pairs: $(o,z_1)$ (scene grounding), $(z_1,z_2)$ (scene-to-justification), $(z_2,z_3)$ (justification-to-meta-action), $(z_3,a)$ (meta-action-to-waypoint), and $(z_2,a)$ (justification-to-waypoint). A trace is considered consistent, $F(\tau)=1$, only if all checked pairs are semantically consistent.
- **Constrained RL Objective**: The goal is to maximize task return (minimize ADE) subject to these consistency constraints holding in expectation: $\max_\theta E_{\pi_\theta}[-ADE(a,a^*)] \text{ s.t. } E_{\pi_\theta}[R(u,v)] \geq 1-\epsilon_{u,v}$ for all $(u,v) \in C$.
- **Tractable Surrogate Objective**: The unobserved binary consistency relation $R(u,v)$ is approximated by a learned critic, $c_\phi(u,v) = P_\phi(\text{CONSISTENT}|u,v)$. Applying Lagrangian relaxation yields the unconstrained objective: $L(\theta) = E_{\pi_\theta}[-ADE(a,a^*) + \sum_{(u,v)\in C} \lambda_{u,v} \log c_\phi(u,v)]$. This objective is optimized via policy gradient, with the log-probabilities from the critic acting as a dense reward signal.

#### 3.4. Pinocchio: A Critic of Embodied Faithfulness

The learned critic, named Pinocchio, is implemented as a VLM classifier (Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct). Its development involved three stages:
- **Dataset Construction (D_faith)**: A 40,000-record dataset of $(o_t, z_t, a_t)$ tuples was created, labeled for semantic consistency along each edge in $G_{gen}$. Sources included:
 - Withheld Gemini annotations (expected largely consistent).
 - Free-form SFT policy rollouts (natural inconsistencies).
 - Mechanically perturbed traces (deliberate inconsistencies via field swaps).
 - LLM-corrupted traces (adversarially injected semantic inconsistencies along specific edges).
- **Validation of Gemini as a Judge**: Gemini 3.1 Pro was used to generate consistency labels for D_faith. Its judgment quality was validated against human annotators on 100 samples. Results showed Gemini's agreement with human majority (87-95% accuracy, fair-to-strong Cohen’s κ) was comparable to human-human agreement, supporting its use for large-scale annotation.
- **Training Pinocchio**: The VLM was fine-tuned on D_faith, with each reasoning trace decomposed into five independent training examples (one per edge). The model was trained to predict "CONSISTENT" or "INCONSISTENT" tokens. Pinocchio achieved a balanced accuracy of 0.87 across all edges on a held-out set.

#### 3.5. Reinforcing Embodied Faithfulness with a Critic

The trained Pinocchio critic was integrated into a Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework for post-training the VLM-based planner $\pi_\theta$ (starting from an SFT checkpoint). The composite reward signal consisted of three equally weighted terms:
- $r_{ADE}$: Negative Average Displacement Error.
- $r_{faith}$: The faithfulness term from Pinocchio, which is the sum of log-probabilities of "CONSISTENT" verdicts across all five edges, providing a dense, granular signal.
- $r_{format}$: A reward for adherence to the prescribed reasoning structure, penalizing ill-formed outputs.
Training was conducted on 100,000 driving scenarios for 4 epochs.

#### 3.6. Baselines

Four GRPO variants were used for comparison, all initialized from the same SFT checkpoint and differing only in their reward signal:
- **ADE**: Optimized solely for negative ADE (functionality-oriented).
- **VLM-Judge**: Rewarded based on a frozen VLM (Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct) evaluating causal alignment between generated and expert reasoning on a 0-5 rubric.
- **ADE-Reason**: Rewarded reasoning traces that increased the policy's confidence in its *own* predicted trajectory, encouraging internal self-consistency.
- **ADE-Swap**: Rewarded by contrasting trajectory probability under the policy's own reasoning versus counterfactual reasoning from a replay buffer, incentivizing consistency between reasoning and action.

#### 3.7. Evaluation

The evaluation involved two main settings:
- **Open-loop Driving Benchmarks**: Tested both open-loop trajectory performance (ADE) and reasoning faithfulness (using the Gemini-based protocol) on $\sim$20,000 held-out driving scenarios from Germany (DE, out-of-distribution) and $\sim$15,000 from the US (in-distribution).
- **Counterfactual Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Scenarios**: 66 DE scenes were synthetically augmented by Gemini 3.1 Pro to inject safety-critical hazards (e.g., fires, cyclists). This benchmark assessed policy responsiveness and causal alignment. Gemini 3.1 Pro acted as an automated judge to score "reasoning response," "waypoint response," and "overall causal alignment" (a strict measure of causal consistency between reasoning and action) for models reacting to these novel hazards.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding the role of faithfulness in embodied reasoning, particularly in the context of autonomous driving.

#### 4.1. Preliminary Investigation: Inconclusive Coupling of Reasoning and Performance

The initial human evaluation, comparing SFT and RL post-trained Alpamayo models, revealed a weak and inconclusive coupling between reasoning quality and trajectory improvement. While RL frequently improved trajectory prediction (reduced ADE), these gains did not consistently translate into improved reasoning. Often, trajectory improvements were observed even when the accompanying reasoning remained unchanged, degraded, or was ungrounded in the environment. Conversely, instances where SFT reasoning was preferred still sometimes saw RL achieve comparable trajectory gains. This finding indicated that VLA policies might learn to achieve task objectives without necessarily grounding their decisions in faithfully generated reasoning, thus motivating the explicit optimization for faithfulness.

#### 4.2. Pinocchio Critic Performance

The developed Pinocchio critic, a fine-tuned Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct model, demonstrated strong performance in detecting faithfulness violations. On a held-out validation set of 250 faithfulness annotations, the critic achieved an overall balanced accuracy of 0.87. Its per-edge accuracies ranged from 0.78 (E2: scene-to-justification) to 0.94 (E3: justification-to-meta-action), indicating its reliability as a dense training signal for embodied faithfulness. The validation of Gemini 3.1 Pro as the label source for Pinocchio showed its judgments aligning well with human majority, with Cohen’s κ values generally within the range of human-to-human agreement.

#### 4.3. Reinforcing Embodied Faithfulness with a Critic

Experiments on large-scale driving benchmarks demonstrated that optimizing for embodied faithfulness using Pinocchio as a reward signal improved reasoning consistency while maintaining competitive task performance.

- **On Withheld DE (Out-of-Distribution) Data (Table 3):**
 - **Faithfulness**: The "Ours" model (Pinocchio-trained) achieved the highest overall faithfulness at 61.4%, representing a substantial improvement over the SFT baseline (27.7%) and the ADE-only baseline (43.4%). This model showed particular strength in consistency for E4 (meta-action to waypoints) and E5 (justification to waypoints), crucial for ensuring actions align with explicit reasoning. The VLM-Judge baseline exhibited strong performance on E1 (scene grounding) and E2 (scene-to-justification), areas where Pinocchio itself showed slightly lower individual accuracy.
 - **Trajectory Performance (ADE)**: "Ours" maintained competitive driving performance with an ADE of 4.324, which was slightly higher than the best-in-class ADE baseline (4.169) but still a significant improvement over the SFT baseline (6.117). This indicates that faithfulness gains were not achieved at a substantial cost to nominal driving accuracy.
 - **Tension between Faithfulness and ADE**: The results highlighted a tension where the ADE-only baseline, while achieving the best trajectory performance, was less faithful overall compared to "Ours" and VLM-Judge. The ADE baseline still improved overall faithfulness by about 15% over SFT, but this improvement was not as pronounced as when directly optimizing for consistency.

- **On Withheld US (In-Distribution) Data (Table 4):**
 - Similar trends were observed on in-distribution data. "Ours" again achieved the highest overall faithfulness (64.8%) and competitive ADE (3.858), reinforcing the generalizability of the approach.

#### 4.4. Observing Embodied Faithfulness in the Wild (OOD Hazard Response)

Evaluation on synthetically generated out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios with inpainted hazards further supported the benefits of faithful reasoning, particularly for robustness under extreme domain shift.

- **Hazard Responsiveness**: "Ours" demonstrated superior responsiveness to inpainted hazards. It showed a +7% increase in appropriate waypoint responses compared to a SoTA driving policy (Alpamayo-1.5-10B), with 31.8% of cases leading to physically appropriate reactions.
- **Causal Alignment (Overall)**: On the most stringent metric, "Overall" causal alignment (requiring both correct reasoning and trajectory response attributed to the hazard), "Ours" achieved a 1.6× improvement over Alpamayo (7.6% vs 4.6%). This indicates that explicitly optimizing for faithfulness leads to better causal consistency between a model's stated rationale and its executed action in challenging, rare scenarios.
- **Absolute Scores**: Despite the relative improvements, the absolute scores for hazard response remained low across all methods, suggesting that robust and causally faithful reasoning in such scenarios remains a persistent challenge for current VLA models.
- **Qualitative Examples**: Visualizations (Figures 3, 5, 6, 7) provided qualitative evidence, showing instances where "Ours" successfully updated both its reasoning and its corresponding physical maneuver (e.g., decelerating and adjusting trajectory for a cyclist or construction worker), while baselines might identify the hazard in language but fail to realize the appropriate physical action, demonstrating unfaithful reasoning.

In summary, the research found that explicitly training for faithfulness, guided by the Pinocchio critic, led to policies that generated more consistent reasoning traces and exhibited greater robustness and appropriate responsiveness in novel, hazardous situations, with only a modest trade-off in nominal driving performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of embodied AI by rigorously addressing the concept of faithfulness in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, a property often overlooked or weakly defined. The findings carry several significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Reconceptualization of Embodied Reasoning**: The work redefines the role of reasoning in embodied intelligence, moving beyond its traditional function as merely a performance enhancer or a post-hoc interpretability tool. By distinguishing between "functional" and "faithful" reasoning, the authors argue that true faithfulness, where reasoning genuinely reflects the policy's internal decision process, is a prerequisite for robust and trustworthy behaviors, rather than just an evaluation metric. This framework provides a new lens through which to develop and assess embodied AI systems.

2. **Enhanced Interpretability and Trustworthiness**: By explicitly optimizing for faithful reasoning, VLA policies can generate explanations that more accurately reflect their actual decision-making pathways. This is crucial for interpretability, particularly in safety-critical applications like autonomous driving, where understanding *why* a robot takes a certain action is paramount for human trust and regulatory compliance. Increased faithfulness can reduce "feature entanglement" and "spurious correlations," leading to more transparent and explainable AI behaviors.

3. **Improved Generalization and Robustness in Long-Tail Scenarios**: The most significant practical impact demonstrated is the improvement in policy responsiveness and robustness in challenging "long-tail" and out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios. These rare events, often underrepresented in training data, demand causally consistent reasoning. The study shows that policies trained for faithfulness are better equipped to recognize novel hazards and translate that recognition into appropriate physical actions, suggesting that faithful internal logic can lead to more generalizable and reliable real-world deployment.

4. **Novel Training Paradigm for Embodied AI**: The introduction of Pinocchio, a learned critic that provides dense, granular reward signals for behavioral consistency across different stages of the reasoning trace, offers a novel training paradigm. This approach allows for direct optimization of faithfulness during reinforcement learning, going beyond traditional methods that primarily rely on end-to-end task performance or weak reasoning-action alignment. This methodology could be adapted to train other embodied agents that require structured, interpretable decision processes.

5. **Foundation for Future Research**: The work opens several avenues for future research. It highlights the remaining challenges in achieving perfectly robust, causally faithful reasoning in rare scenarios, as evidenced by the low absolute scores in OOD evaluations. Future work could focus on stronger notions of mechanistic faithfulness, improving the accuracy and calibration of critics (especially for visually demanding aspects like scene grounding), and validating faithful planners in closed-loop simulation environments to assess compounding errors and reactive behaviors.

6. **Broader Applicability**: While demonstrated in autonomous driving, the core concepts and methodologies—distinguishing functional from faithful reasoning, operationalizing behavioral consistency with a learned critic, and using it for RL post-training—could be generalized to other complex embodied tasks such as robot manipulation, human-robot interaction, or other forms of decision-making under uncertainty, where transparency and reliability are essential.

In summary, this research shifts the focus from merely functional reasoning to the more profound concept of faithfulness, proposing a practical method to instill this property into VLA models. This has the potential to yield embodied AI systems that are not only capable but also genuinely interpretable, trustworthy, and robust in complex, real-world environments.
