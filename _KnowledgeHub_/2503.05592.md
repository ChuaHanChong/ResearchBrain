# R1-Searcher: Incentivizing the Search Capability in LLMs via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> R1-Searcher, from Renmin University of China, introduces a two-stage outcome-based reinforcement learning framework that enables Large Language Models to autonomously invoke and leverage external search systems. This approach significantly outperforms strong RAG baselines on multi-hop question answering benchmarks and demonstrates robust generalization to out-of-domain and online search scenarios.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) primarily rely on internal knowledge, leading to hallucinations, outdated information, and inability to access private or real-time data.
- Existing Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) methods face limitations such as reliance on complex prompt engineering, memorization issues with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) distillation, and high inference overhead from test-time scaling methods.
- Current RAG solutions often struggle with generalization to out-of-domain datasets and adapting to different or online retrieval environments.

## Method

- R1-Searcher proposes a novel ==two-stage outcome-based RL framework== that enables LLMs to autonomously invoke and utilize external search systems.
- The approach modifies the ==Reinforce++ algorithm== with ==RAG-based rollouts== for integrating retrieved documents and a retrieval mask for loss calculation, ensuring the model focuses on generating queries and answers.
- It uses purely ==outcome-based rewards==, with Stage-1 incentivizing retrieval invocation and format adherence, and Stage-2 focusing on answer accuracy via F1-score and penalizing format errors, all without needing supervised fine-tuning for a cold start.

## Results

- R1-Searcher significantly outperforms strong RAG baselines, including GPT-4o-mini based methods, achieving up to **48.22%** higher performance on HotpotQA and **21.72%** on 2WikiMultiHopQA (LLM-as-Judge metric).
- The model demonstrates robust generalization, performing well on out-of-domain datasets like Musique and Bamboogle despite being trained on a limited dataset.
- R1-Searcher effectively generalizes to online search scenarios, showing an **18.2%** improvement when switching from local retrieval to Google Web Search API on the Bamboogle dataset.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement Learning (RL) consistently outperforms Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) in teaching LLMs genuine search capabilities, leading to more accurate query timing and relevance.
> - A two-stage reward design, using F1-score for answer quality and strict format rewards, is crucial for stable RL training and preventing 'reward hacking'.
> - Training with a curriculum that includes 'difficult' questions and diverse datasets significantly enhances the model's overall performance and generalization abilities for retrieval and reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Executive Summary

This report analyzes "R1-Searcher: Incentivizing the Search Capability in LLMs via Reinforcement Learning," a research paper proposing a novel two-stage outcome-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) approach to enhance Large Language Models' (LLMs) ability to autonomously invoke and leverage external search systems. Authored primarily by researchers from the Gaoling School of Artificial Intelligence at Renmin University of China, this work addresses critical limitations of current LLMs, such as reliance on internal knowledge leading to hallucinations and the inefficiencies of existing Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) methods. R1-Searcher distinctively relies solely on RL, bypassing the need for supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for a cold start or process rewards. Experimental results demonstrate significant performance improvements over strong RAG baselines, including closed-source models like GPT-4o-mini, across various multi-hop question answering benchmarks. The study also highlights R1-Searcher's robust generalization to out-of-domain datasets and online search scenarios, marking a substantial step towards more reliable and adaptable LLM-based reasoning systems.

The research group involved appears to be deeply engaged in developing advanced LLM capabilities, particularly focusing on how reinforcement learning can enhance reasoning and knowledge utilization.

The title "R1-Searcher" and the repeated citation of "OpenAI-o1," "Deepseek-R1," and "Kimi-k1.5" (references, respectively) in the introduction strongly suggest that this work is part of a broader research initiative, possibly at RUC or in collaboration with other leading AI labs, focused on "R1-like" reasoning models. The mention of "Deepseek-R1: Incentivizing reasoning capability in LLMs via reinforcement learning" (Ref) by DeepSeek-AI further solidifies this connection, indicating that the RUC group is contributing to or building upon a recognized paradigm of using RL to imbue LLMs with enhanced reasoning capabilities. #### 1.3. Limitations of Existing LLMs

Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated impressive complex reasoning capabilities, often referred to as Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) when enhanced by techniques like Reinforcement Learning (RL). However, these models primarily rely on their internal, pre-trained knowledge. This reliance presents significant limitations, especially for:
- **Knowledge-intensive questions:** Where internal knowledge may be insufficient or outdated.
- **Time-sensitive issues:** Requiring access to very recent information not captured during training.
- **Private information:** Stored in local databases, inaccessible to general LLMs.
- **Inaccuracies and Hallucinations:** When models attempt to answer beyond their knowledge base, leading to fabricated or incorrect information.

To overcome these challenges, enabling LLMs to access and integrate external information during their reasoning process is crucial for achieving more "deliberative reasoning."

#### 1.4. Challenges in Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)

**Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)** has emerged as a prominent paradigm to address the aforementioned limitations by augmenting LLMs with external information sources. However, existing RAG approaches face their own set of challenges:
- **Prompting Strategies:** Early approaches relied on intricate prompt engineering (e.g., iterative question decomposition, query generation) to guide LLMs. These often perform optimally only with closed-source, highly capable LLMs (like GPT-4o-mini), limiting their accessibility and transferability.
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) Distillation:** Subsequent efforts attempted to distill RAG capabilities into smaller LLMs via SFT. While seemingly effective, recent research suggests that SFT can lead models to "memorize solution paths" rather than truly generalize, hindering performance on novel scenarios.
- **Test-Time Scaling Methods:** Approaches like Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) enhance solution-finding by expanding the search space during inference. While promising, these methods incur significant inference overhead due to increased retrieval invocations, making them impractical for widespread use.

#### 1.5. R1-Searcher's Position

R1-Searcher is positioned as a novel solution that addresses the shortcomings of existing RAG methods by integrating an **external retrieval environment directly into the training process via Reinforcement Learning**. This approach aims to "incentivize the search capability in LLMs," allowing models to proactively learn *when* and *how* to effectively utilize external retrieval for problem-solving. By focusing on learning through exploration with RL, R1-Searcher seeks to enhance LLM generalization and improve inference efficiency, moving beyond mere memorization or computationally expensive test-time interventions. The core distinction lies in its complete reliance on outcome-based RL, obviating the need for process rewards, distillation, or SFT for a cold start.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of "R1-Searcher" is to **significantly enhance the retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) through Reinforcement Learning (RL).**

The core motivation stems from the recognition that current powerful Large Reasoning Models (LRMs), while proficient in complex reasoning, are fundamentally limited by their reliance on internal knowledge. This leads to inaccuracies, hallucinations, and an inability to handle dynamic, time-sensitive, or private knowledge. While RAG methods offer a partial solution by connecting LLMs to external knowledge, existing techniques (complex prompting, SFT distillation, test-time scaling) suffer from issues such as dependence on closed-source models, poor generalization due to memorization, or high computational overhead.

R1-Searcher aims to overcome these limitations by:
- **Incentivizing Autonomous Search:** Enabling LLMs to self-learn how to autonomously invoke external search systems during their reasoning process, rather than being explicitly prompted or fine-tuned.
- **Leveraging Outcome-Based RL:** Relying exclusively on final outcome rewards (e.g., correctness of the answer) rather than detailed process rewards or pre-training via supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for a "cold start." This allows for more flexible and generalizable learning.
- **Improving Generalization and Efficiency:** Developing a framework that not only achieves higher accuracy on knowledge-intensive tasks but also generalizes well to out-of-domain datasets and online search scenarios, all while improving inference efficiency compared to computationally intensive methods like MCTS.
- **Applicability to Diverse LLMs:** Designing a method effective for both base LLMs and instruction-tuned models, expanding its practical utility.

In essence, the paper seeks to train LLMs to be "smart searchers" – models that intelligently decide when to retrieve information, formulate effective queries, and integrate the retrieved knowledge seamlessly into their reasoning, all learned through an exploration-based RL paradigm.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

R1-Searcher employs a novel two-stage outcome-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) approach to enable LLMs to autonomously utilize external retrieval systems during reasoning. The method modifies the Reinforce++ algorithm to suit the RAG scenario.

#### 3.1. Data Selection Strategy

To enhance training efficiency and focus the model on relevant problem-solving, the authors perform a crucial data selection step. They use the Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct model with a local retrieval system to categorize questions from the training sets of HotpotQA and 2WikiMultiHopQA based on difficulty. Difficulty is defined by the number of "rollouts" (retrieval steps) required for the model to answer correctly:
- **Easy:** Fewer than 10 rollouts.
- **Medium:** 10 to 20 rollouts.
- **Difficult:** More than 20 rollouts.

These categorized data are then used to construct the training datasets for each RL stage, as detailed in Table 1 of the paper. This curriculum-like approach ensures the model is exposed to varying complexities, aiding in progressive skill acquisition.

#### 3.2. Two-Stage Outcome-based Reinforcement Learning

The training process is divided into two distinct stages, each with tailored reward designs to progressively build search capabilities.

#### 3.3. Reward Design

The RL process is driven purely by outcome rewards, as no intermediate annotations are available.

- **Stage-1 Reward:**
 The primary goal of Stage-1 is to teach the model to correctly *invoke* the external retrieval system and understand its format, without yet prioritizing answer accuracy.
 - **Retrieval Reward ($R_{retrieval}$):** A fixed positive reward (0.5) if the model performs at least one retrieval invocation (n ≥ 1), and 0 otherwise. This incentivizes the act of searching.
 - **Format Reward ($R_{format}$):** A positive reward (0.5) if the model adheres to strict output format rules (e.g., correct <think> and <answer> tags, query within <begin_of_query>...<end_of_query> tags, no hallucinated documents). 0 otherwise. This ensures the model learns the correct interaction protocol.
 - **Total Stage-1 Reward:** Sum of $R_{retrieval}$ and $R_{format}$.

- **Stage-2 Reward:**
 Once the model can reliably invoke retrieval, Stage-2 focuses on using retrieval effectively to solve questions accurately. The retrieval reward is removed, and an answer reward is introduced.
 - **Format Reward ($R'_{format}$):** Similar format judgment criteria as Stage-1, but with a *penalty* (-2) for incorrect formats. This strongly discourages malformed outputs.
 - **Answer Reward ($R_{answer}$):** Calculated using the F1 score between the ground-truth answer and the predicted answer. F1 is chosen for its balance, as demonstrated in later ablation studies. This directly incentivizes accurate problem-solving leveraging retrieved information.
 - **Total Stage-2 Reward:** Sum of $R'_{format}$ and $R_{answer}$.

#### 3.4. Training Algorithm Modifications

The R1-Searcher training algorithm is based on **Reinforce++**, with two key modifications designed for RAG scenarios:

- **RAG-based Rollout:**
 During the model's generation process, if it produces the `<begin_of_query>...<end_of_query>` tags, the generation pauses. The query is extracted and used to perform retrieval from the external system (e.g., Wikipedia). The retrieved documents are then seamlessly integrated back into the model's input stream, encapsulated within `<begin_of_documents>...<end_of_documents>` tags. The model then resumes its reasoning based on this augmented context. This ensures that retrieval is an integral part of the generation process, allowing the model to adapt its reasoning dynamically.

- **Retrieval Mask-based Loss Calculation:**
 When retrieved documents are inserted into the model's context, they are treated as "environment observations" rather than text the model should generate. To prevent these external tokens from influencing the model's intrinsic generation loss and ensuring it doesn't try to "generate" the retrieved documents, the tokens within `<begin_of_documents>...<end_of_documents>` tags are masked during the RL loss calculation. This modification helps the model focus on generating *queries* and *answers*, not memorizing or replicating retrieval results.

The training uses a local dense embedding-based retrieval system (BGE-large-en-v1.5) with English Wikipedia as the corpus. For generalization testing, it also employs the Google Web Search API.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

R1-Searcher demonstrates significant advancements in RAG capabilities for LLMs, validated through extensive experiments on four multi-hop QA datasets: HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA (in-domain), Musique, and Bamboogle (out-of-domain). Evaluation metrics include Cover Exact Match (ACC_R) and LLM-as-Judge (ACC_L) using GPT-4o-mini.

#### 4.1. Performance on Multi-Hop QA Benchmarks

The core finding is that R1-Searcher consistently and significantly outperforms existing strong RAG baselines, including those utilizing powerful closed-source models like GPT-4o-mini (e.g., ReARTeR, CR-Planner).

- **Superior Performance:** R1-Searcher, using Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Qwen-2.5-7B-Base backbones, achieves substantial performance improvements. For instance, Qwen-2.5-7B-Base-RL surpasses ReARTeR (GPT-4o-mini) by up to 48.22% on HotpotQA and 21.72% on 2WikiMultiHopQA (LLM-as-Judge metric). Llama-3.1-8B-RL also shows significant gains over its ReARTeR counterpart (48.2% on HotpotQA, 21.7% on 2WikiMultiHopQA, and 4.0% on Bamboogle).
- **Efficiency:** The paper notes that while ReARTeR (a test-time scaling method) performs well among baselines, it incurs significant overhead. R1-Searcher achieves superior results without such high inference costs, suggesting more efficient learning and utilization of retrieval.

#### 4.2. Generalization Capabilities

A key strength of R1-Searcher is its strong generalization ability:

- **Out-of-Domain Datasets:** Trained on a limited set of 8148 samples from HotpotQA and 2WikiMultiHopQA, the model not only excels on these in-domain datasets but also performs robustly on out-of-domain benchmarks like Musique and Bamboogle. This indicates that the RL training effectively teaches the model *how* to retrieve and integrate information, rather than just memorizing paths.
- **Online Search:** The model seamlessly generalizes to online search scenarios (using Google Web Search API for Bamboogle, which requires up-to-date information). R1-Searcher (Qwen-2.5-7B-RL-Online) shows an 18.2% improvement over its local retrieval counterpart and an 11.4% increase compared to Search-o1 (a 32B model using online search), demonstrating its adaptability to different retrieval environments and its ability to access timely information.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Training Insights

The paper provides valuable ablation studies that offer insights into the design choices:

#### 4.4. RL Algorithms: GRPO vs. Reinforce++

- **Reinforce++** (the chosen algorithm for R1-Searcher) shows superior performance on in-domain test sets (HotpotQA and 2Wiki), suggesting higher learning efficiency for data similar to training.
- **GRPO** (another RL algorithm without a critic) demonstrates advantages in generating longer text and more frequent retrievals, potentially leading to better generalization on out-of-domain data like Bamboogle. This suggests a trade-off between in-domain optimization and broader generalization depending on the RL algorithm.

#### 4.5. RL vs. SFT for Search Capability

- **RL consistently outperforms SFT** across both in-domain and out-of-domain test sets.
- The analysis reveals that while SFT can help models generate retrieval queries, the *timing* and *relevance* of these queries are inferior to those produced by RL. SFT models tend to rely on potentially erroneous internal knowledge, whereas RL models learn to invoke retrieval precisely when uncertain, leading to more accurate responses. This highlights that RL is more effective in enhancing genuine retrieval *skills* rather than just memorizing query patterns.

#### 4.6. Impact of Reward Design

- **F1-score as Answer Reward:** Using F1-score as the answer reward yields the best overall performance and encourages longer, more comprehensive responses compared to Exact Match (EM) or Cover Exact Match (CEM). EM's strictness makes it less suitable for open-ended generation, leading to shorter responses and poorer results. F1 provides a balanced measure for outcome-based rewards.
- **Strict Format Rewards:** The meticulously designed format rewards are crucial for stable RL training, preventing issues like "reward hacking" (e.g., generating `<begin_of_documents>` without queries), nonsensical outputs, or bypassing retrieval entirely.

#### 4.7. Influence of Training Data Characteristics

- **Difficulty Distribution:** Training with a dataset including "difficult" questions (requiring more than 20 rollouts) leads to better overall performance and encourages more retrievals compared to datasets without difficult questions. This suggests that exposing the model to challenging problems during training significantly enhances its reasoning and retrieval capabilities.
- **Data Diversity:** Training on a mixture of datasets (HotpotQA and 2Wiki) results in a higher number of retrievals, longer generated responses, and significantly better average performance compared to training on each dataset individually. Overfitting was observed with less diverse datasets (e.g., 2Wiki alone), underscoring the importance of data diversity for generalizability.

#### 4.8. Generalization to Online Search

- The ability to switch from a locally indexed Wikipedia corpus (used for training) to Google Web Search API for inference on Bamboogle with strong performance gain validates R1-Searcher's capacity to generalize beyond the specific training environment. This proves that the model learns the *skill* of querying and integrating external information, rather than just being optimized for a particular data source.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

R1-Searcher represents a notable advancement in the field of Large Language Models and Retrieval-Augmented Generation, offering several significant contributions and potential impacts:

#### 5.1. Key Contributions

- **Pioneering Outcome-Based RL for RAG:** The work demonstrates a novel and effective two-stage outcome-supervised RL framework that empowers LLMs to autonomously invoke external search engines. This is a significant departure from methods relying on extensive process rewards or SFT.
- **Elimination of Cold Start/Distillation Dependency:** R1-Searcher’s ability to learn autonomously solely through RL, without requiring an SFT cold start or distillation from larger models, simplifies the training pipeline and makes the approach more accessible and scalable.
- **Superior Performance and Generalization:** The consistent and substantial outperformance over strong RAG baselines, including closed-source GPT-4o-mini models, on both in-domain and out-of-domain multi-hop QA datasets, highlights its effectiveness. Its robust generalization to different knowledge domains and, crucially, to online search scenarios, is a major step towards real-world applicability.
- **Comprehensive Ablation Studies:** The detailed analysis of various training aspects—RL algorithms, reward design, and data characteristics—provides invaluable insights for future research in training robust RAG-LLMs. This empirical understanding can guide subsequent developments in this rapidly evolving field.
- **Applicability to Diverse Models:** The method's effectiveness for both base and instruction-tuned models makes it broadly applicable across different LLM architectures.

#### 5.2. Practical Implications

- **Reduced Hallucinations and Increased Accuracy:** By enabling LLMs to autonomously fetch and integrate external, up-to-date information, R1-Searcher can significantly mitigate the problem of hallucinations and improve the factual accuracy of LLM responses, particularly for knowledge-intensive or time-sensitive queries.
- **Enhanced Deliberative Reasoning:** The learned ability to strategically retrieve information during the reasoning process allows LLMs to engage in more "deliberative" or "iterative" reasoning, akin to human problem-solving, leading to more robust and verifiable answers.
- **Improved Efficiency:** By learning *when* to search effectively during training, R1-Searcher avoids the high inference overhead associated with test-time scaling methods, making RAG more practical for deployment in real-world applications.
- **Foundation for Adaptive Agents:** This work lays a strong foundation for developing more adaptive and agentic LLMs that can interact with external tools (like search engines) in a highly intelligent and self-directed manner, opening avenues for more sophisticated AI agents.

#### 5.3. Future Directions

The authors outline clear future work, acknowledging areas for further refinement:
- **Sophisticated Data Curricula:** Exploring more structured and advanced data curricula beyond simple data mixing, given the observed impact of data difficulty and distribution on learning.
- **Scaling to Larger Models:** Investigating the effectiveness of R1-Searcher with larger LLM backbones (e.g., 32B parameters) to further assess its scalability and potential for even higher performance.

Overall, R1-Searcher marks a significant stride in training LLMs to be more knowledgeable, reliable, and autonomous agents, bridging the gap between internal reasoning and external information access through a powerful RL paradigm.
