# Sample Efficient Reinforcement Learning via Large Vision Language Model Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called LVLM2P distills knowledge from large vision-language models (LVLMs) into compact reinforcement learning (RL) agents, enhancing sample efficiency by an average of 2.56 times for PPO and 2.86 times for A2C. This approach enables faster learning for RL agents in decision-making tasks without the computational overhead of deploying large foundation models directly.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) suffers from high sample inefficiency, requiring vast numbers of interactions with an environment, which is costly and impractical for real-world applications.
- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) possess powerful reasoning capabilities but are computationally expensive and too large for efficient real-time deployment on resource-constrained systems.
- Prior approaches leveraging LLMs/LVLMs in RL often require labor-intensive, hand-engineered textual descriptions of environmental states.

## Method

- LVLM2P employs a ==knowledge distillation framework== where a pre-trained LVLM acts as a "teacher" for a smaller "student" RL agent during training.
- The LVLM teacher uses a ==two-stage prompting mechanism== (Analysis and Action Inference) to generate soft probability distributions over actions directly from raw visual observations, eliminating manual textual descriptions.
- The student RL agent is trained with a combined objective function that balances traditional RL rewards with a ==Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence loss== to imitate the teacher's soft action probabilities.

## Results

- LVLM2P consistently enhanced the sample efficiency of both PPO and A2C algorithms across four MiniGrid environments, achieving an average improvement of **2.56x** for PPO and **2.86x** for A2C.
- The LVLM teacher policy, Gemini-1.5-Flash, demonstrated strong decision-making, achieving an average success rate of approximately **0.96** across tasks, validating its effectiveness as a guide.
- Ablation studies confirmed that using soft probability distributions for distillation yielded better performance than hard targets, and an optimal **lambda=0.01** was identified for balancing RL and distillation objectives.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The broad, generalized knowledge within LVLMs can be effectively transferred to compact RL agents to accelerate learning, even for tasks the LVLM hasn't been explicitly trained on as a policy.
> - Distilling soft probability distributions from the teacher LVLM provides richer information to the student, leading to more robust policies and improved sample efficiency compared to using hard (one-hot) action targets.
> - A balanced objective, combining environmental feedback with teacher guidance (controlled by hyperparameter "lambda"), is crucial for the student to leverage teacher knowledge for efficient exploration while adapting to the specific task.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of two rapidly evolving and impactful fields: Reinforcement Learning (RL) and large-scale multimodal foundation models, particularly Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs). The paper addresses critical limitations within both domains while simultaneously exploring their synergistic potential.

Reinforcement Learning has demonstrated remarkable success in complex decision-making scenarios, from mastering games to enabling autonomous driving and robotic control. However, a perennial challenge for RL is its **sample inefficiency**, meaning it often requires an enormous number of interactions with an environment to learn an effective policy. This is particularly problematic in real-world applications where data collection is expensive, time-consuming, or even unsafe. The extensive data demands lead to high costs in human supervision, safety checks, and environment resets, making real-world deployment often unfeasible.

Concurrently, multimodal foundation models, trained on vast internet-scale datasets, have emerged as powerful general-purpose agents. LVLMs, such as Gemini and GPT-4V, exhibit exceptional capabilities in understanding and reasoning across diverse modalities, particularly vision and language. These models can perform complex tasks, drawing on a broad internalized knowledge base. Recent research has explored leveraging these models in RL in several ways:
- **LVLMs as planners:** Guiding an agent's sequence of actions or high-level strategy.
- **Fine-tuning LVLMs as direct policies:** Adapting the large model to directly output actions for specific tasks.
- **Prompting techniques to assist RL:** Using LLMs/LVLMs to suggest plans, generate reward signals, or discover meaningful skills.

Despite their intelligence, foundation models come with significant drawbacks: their immense size (hundreds of billions of parameters) makes them computationally expensive, resource-intensive, and impractical for real-time deployment on constrained systems. Relying on commercial APIs for such models also incurs substantial usage costs, limiting long-term viability.

This paper's work, LVLM to Policy (LVLM2P), specifically targets the **resource-constrained deployment gap**. It recognizes the valuable general knowledge within LVLMs but aims to transfer this intelligence to compact, efficient RL agents rather than directly deploying or fine-tuning the large models. This differentiates it from approaches that use LVLMs directly as policies or heavily rely on them for online planning, which still face the computational overhead issue.

Furthermore, LVLM2P connects to the established field of **Knowledge Distillation (KD) in RL**. Previous KD work in RL has focused on:
- Distilling multiple pre-trained policies into a single network for multi-task learning.
- Using model-based controllers or existing teacher agents to guide exploration.
- Combining on-policy distillation with an RL objective to imitate a pre-trained teacher.

LVLM2P extends this by using a *multimodal foundation model* as the teacher, rather than a task-specific pre-trained RL policy. This is a crucial distinction: instead of relying on a teacher that has already mastered the target task, LVLM2P taps into the broad, generalized knowledge of an LVLM (like Gemini-1.5-Flash) through sophisticated prompting. This allows the student RL agent to leverage a rich, pre-existing understanding of the world to accelerate learning, even for tasks the LVLM hasn't been explicitly trained on as a policy. The innovation lies in effectively extracting actionable "instructional actions" from an LVLM and distilling them into a compact RL agent, thereby addressing both sample inefficiency and deployment practicality simultaneously.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind the LVLM2P framework stems from the inherent limitations of both traditional Reinforcement Learning (RL) and contemporary large multimodal foundation models (FMs), particularly Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs), when considered for real-world deployment in decision-making tasks.

The **key objectives** of this research are multi-faceted:

1. **Overcome Sample Inefficiency in RL:** A central goal is to significantly reduce the high sample complexity that plagues many RL algorithms. In real-world environments, requiring millions of interactions to learn a task is often unfeasible due to time, safety, and resource constraints. The aim is to accelerate the learning progress of RL agents, making them more practical for real-world applications.

2. **Bridge the Gap between Foundation Models and Efficient Deployment:** While LVLMs demonstrate exceptional reasoning and general-purpose capabilities, their massive parameter counts (hundreds of billions) render them computationally prohibitive for real-time inference on resource-constrained systems, such as robots or embedded devices. The objective is to harness the intelligence of these powerful models without incurring their massive computational overhead at inference time. The paper explicitly asks: "can we design a compact agent that quickly acquires the capabilities of multimodal FMs while maintaining efficiency and adaptability for specific tasks in resource-constrained systems?" LVLM2P is designed as an affirmative answer to this question.

3. **Eliminate the Need for Manual Textual Descriptors:** Many prior approaches leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs) or LVLMs in RL environments require labor-intensive, hand-engineered textual descriptions of environmental states. This process is not only time-consuming but also prone to errors and may not be universally applicable across diverse tasks. A key objective of LVLM2P is to bypass this requirement by allowing the LVLM teacher to directly infer instructional actions from raw visual observations, thereby enhancing the framework's applicability and ease of use.

4. **Enable Robust Learning through Combined Objectives:** The research aims to develop a training paradigm where a student RL agent benefits from both the high-level guidance of an LVLM teacher and its own online interactions with the environment. This dual learning objective is intended to allow the student to leverage the teacher's knowledge for efficient exploration while simultaneously correcting any potential inaccuracies or limitations of the teacher, leading to superior task performance.

5. **Ensure Versatility and Broad Applicability:** The framework is designed to be algorithm-agnostic, meaning it can be seamlessly integrated with various existing RL algorithms (demonstrated with PPO and A2C) to improve their sample efficiency across a diverse range of decision-making tasks.

In essence, the motivation is to create a new paradigm for RL agent training that intelligently leverages the "wisdom" of large, pre-trained multimodal foundation models to make RL significantly more sample-efficient and practical for deployment in real-world, resource-limited scenarios, all while streamlining the development process by reducing the need for manual environment annotation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the LVLM2P framework is a novel knowledge distillation approach that transfers the reasoning capabilities of a Large Vision-Language Model (LVLM) into a compact, task-specific Reinforcement Learning (RL) agent. The methodology can be broken down into the overall framework, the design of the LVLM teacher agent, and the student policy training process.

**A. LVLM2P Framework Overview (Fig. 1)**
The LVLM2P framework operates by using a pre-trained LVLM as a "teacher" to guide a smaller, "student" RL agent during its training phase. The student agent learns from its interactions with the environment through a conventional RL objective, but simultaneously imitates the teacher's suggested actions via a knowledge distillation objective. This dual learning mechanism allows the student to benefit from the LVLM's broad understanding, reducing inefficient exploration, while still adapting to the specific task and correcting any teacher biases. Crucially, the LVLM teacher is only involved during training; at inference time, the compact student agent operates independently without any further need for the large, computationally expensive LVLM.

**B. LVLM as Teacher Agent (Fig. 2)**
A critical component is how the LVLM acts as a teacher. The paper leverages advanced prompting techniques with an LVLM (specifically Gemini-1.5-Flash, chosen for its balance of inference time and accuracy) to generate "instructional actions" from visual observations. This process consists of two stages:

1. **Analysis Stage:** Given a raw visual observation (image) from the environment, the LVLM is prompted to analyze the scene and summarize key information. This includes identifying the agent's coordinates and orientation, as well as the target object's location. This stage essentially transforms the visual input into a structured understanding of the environment, similar to how a human might describe the scene.

2. **Action Inference Stage:** The extracted analysis from the first stage is then fed back to the LVLM along with few-shot examples. These examples are crucial for ensuring consistency in the LVLM's response format and guiding its decision-making. In this stage, the LVLM is prompted to provide a *numerical probability distribution (soft probabilities)* over a predefined set of possible actions (e.g., 'Go Forward', 'Turn left', 'Turn right'). Using soft probabilities rather than a single "hard" action (one-hot encoding) is a deliberate choice, as it has been shown to provide richer information for the student, improving sample efficiency and preventing overfitting during distillation. This two-stage prompting mechanism allows the LVLM to first comprehend the scene contextually and then infer appropriate actions probabilistically, all based on visual input, thus eliminating the need for manual textual state descriptions.

**C. Training Process of Student Policy (Algorithm 1)**
The student policy, parameterized by $\theta$ (denoted as $\pi_\theta(a|s)$), is trained using a combined objective function:

$L(\pi_\theta) = L_{RL}(\pi_\theta) + \lambda D_{KL}(\pi_T(\cdot|o, \ell_{in}) \parallel \pi_\theta(\cdot|s))$

Where:
- $L_{RL}(\pi_\theta)$ is the conventional Reinforcement Learning objective (e.g., from PPO or A2C), which aims to maximize the agent's expected long-term rewards.
- $D_{KL}(\pi_T(\cdot|o, \ell_{in}) \parallel \pi_\theta(\cdot|s))$ is the Knowledge Distillation objective, specifically the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the teacher's policy $\pi_T$ (which outputs soft probabilities $p_t$ based on visual observation $o$ and linguistic prompt $\ell_{in}$) and the student's policy $\pi_\theta$ (which takes the environment state $s$ as input). This term encourages the student policy to imitate the teacher's behavior.
- $\lambda$ is a hyperparameter that balances the influence of the RL objective and the knowledge distillation objective.

The training procedure unfolds as follows:
1. The student policy $\pi_\theta$ interacts with the environment, collecting a trajectory of states, actions, rewards, and visual observations.
2. For each observation ($o_t$) in the collected batch, the LVLM teacher is queried using the two-stage prompting mechanism to obtain its soft probability distribution over actions ($p_t$).
3. The student policy is then updated by optimizing the combined loss function, using an algorithm like PPO or A2C. This iterative process ensures that the student learns from both environmental feedback and the teacher's guidance.

This approach effectively harnesses the LVLM's general reasoning abilities to guide the student's early exploration, preventing less meaningful actions and quickly directing the agent towards high-reward regions, thereby significantly enhancing sample efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to validate whether the LVLM2P method significantly enhances the sample efficiency of baseline RL algorithms in decision-making tasks within sparse reward environments.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Environments:** Four challenging grid-based tasks from MiniGrid were used:
 - **LavaGap:** Navigating a narrow, lava-surrounded gap, emphasizing safe exploration.
 - **Dynamic Obstacles:** Avoiding moving obstacles under partial observability.
 - **Fetch:** Retrieving specific objects based on textual instructions, testing object recognition and manipulation.
 - **GoToDoor:** Locating and approaching the correct door based on textual cues, assessing navigation skills.
- **LVLM Teacher:** Gemini-1.5-Flash was employed as the teacher policy, leveraging its multimodal capabilities with few-shot prompts. The teacher received a fully observable view of the environment.
- **Student Baselines:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Advantage Actor-Critic (A2C) were used as the underlying RL algorithms for the student policies, representing standard on-policy approaches.
- **Evaluation:** Mean success rate and the number of training samples required to achieve specific mean returns were tracked, with results averaged over three random seeds.

**Key Findings and Results:**

1. **LVLM Teacher Policy Performance:** The LVLM policy itself demonstrated strong decision-making capabilities, achieving an average success rate of approximately 0.96 across the evaluated environments. This high performance is crucial, as it validates the teacher's ability to provide effective guidance to the student models.

2. **Significant Enhancement in Sample Efficiency:**
 - In all four tasks, both A2C and PPO, when integrated with LVLM2P, converged significantly faster than their vanilla counterparts. This indicates that the knowledge transferred from the LVLM teacher leads to substantially improved learning efficacy.
 - **Quantitative Improvements:**
 - **LavaGap:** To reach a mean return of 0.91, A2C+LVLM2P required 9.18k samples (2.18 times more sample-efficient than vanilla A2C's 20k samples). PPO+LVLM2P required 5.26k samples (2.11 times more sample-efficient than vanilla PPO's 11.05k samples).
 - **Across all environments:** LVLM2P improved sample efficiency by an average of **2.56 times for PPO** and **2.86 times for A2C**. This consistent and substantial improvement is a central finding, demonstrating LVLM2P's effectiveness in reducing the interaction requirements for RL agents.

3. **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Soft vs. Hard Probabilities:** The ablation study confirmed the benefit of using soft probability distributions from the LVLM teacher. LVLM2P trained with soft probabilities consistently outperformed versions using hard (one-hot) distributions. This supports the hypothesis that soft targets provide richer, more nuanced information, allowing the student to learn a more robust policy and preventing issues like overfitting that can arise from extreme error signals associated with hard targets.
 - **Impact of $\lambda$ Coefficient:** The hyperparameter $\lambda$, which balances the RL objective and the knowledge distillation objective, was found to be crucial. Optimal success rates were observed with $\lambda = 0.01$. Performance declined when $\lambda$ was too low (e.g., $\lambda=0$, meaning no teacher influence, reverting to baseline RL) or too high (e.g., $\lambda \ge 1$), suggesting that an over-reliance on the teacher's immediate action probabilities can hinder the student's ability to learn long-term optimal policies through its own environmental interactions. This highlights the importance of carefully balancing the teacher's guidance with the student's self-directed learning.

In summary, the results unequivocally demonstrate that LVLM2P is a highly effective framework for enhancing the sample efficiency of RL agents. By leveraging the comprehensive understanding of LVLMs through knowledge distillation, the method enables faster learning and more robust policy acquisition across a variety of challenging tasks, while also confirming the benefits of soft probability distillation and appropriate objective balancing.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The LVLM2P framework represents a significant step forward in making Reinforcement Learning more practical and deployable, particularly in resource-constrained real-world scenarios. Its significance and potential impact can be elaborated as follows:

1. **Addressing the RL Sample Inefficiency Bottleneck:** The primary contribution of LVLM2P is its ability to significantly enhance the sample efficiency of RL algorithms. By accelerating the learning process by 2-3 times on average, the framework drastically reduces the amount of real-world interaction data, time, and computational resources required for training. This is critical for applications like robotics, autonomous systems, and industrial automation where data collection is expensive, time-consuming, or risky. Faster learning translates directly into quicker development cycles and reduced operational costs.

2. **Bridging the Gap between Foundational Models and Edge Deployment:** LVLM2P provides an elegant solution to the dilemma of leveraging the immense intelligence of large multimodal foundation models (LVLMs) without succumbing to their computational burden. Instead of deploying or fine-tuning colossal models on edge devices, LVLM2P distills their general knowledge into compact, efficient RL agents. This means advanced AI capabilities, previously restricted to powerful cloud-based systems, can now be practically implemented on resource-constrained platforms, broadening the scope of intelligent agents in diverse physical environments. This could pave the way for more sophisticated on-device AI in robotics, smart sensors, and IoT devices.

3. **Simplified Development and Broader Applicability:** The framework's ability to leverage LVLMs to directly infer instructional actions from visual observations eliminates the need for labor-intensive, handcrafted textual descriptions of environment states. This greatly simplifies the development process for new RL tasks, making the approach more flexible and applicable across a wider range of environments without requiring extensive domain-specific engineering. This automation of state interpretation can unlock deployment in previously intractable scenarios.

4. **Versatility and Compatibility with Existing RL Algorithms:** The demonstrated seamless integration of LVLM2P with standard on-policy RL algorithms like PPO and A2C highlights its versatility. This adaptability means that researchers and practitioners can readily incorporate this knowledge distillation strategy into their existing RL pipelines, potentially boosting the performance of a broad spectrum of current and future RL applications without requiring a complete overhaul of their core algorithms.

5. **Foundation for Future Complex Control Tasks:** The paper explicitly states that this work "lays the foundation for extending LVLM-based frameworks to future continuous control tasks." MiniGrid environments are grid-based and typically involve discrete actions. Extending this success to continuous action spaces, which are prevalent in complex robotic manipulation, locomotion, and fine motor control, would be a transformative development. It suggests a future where robots can quickly learn sophisticated behaviors from visual demonstrations or natural language instructions interpreted by an LVLM, significantly reducing programming effort and enabling more adaptable, intelligent robotic systems.

In essence, LVLM2P addresses fundamental challenges in current AI paradigms by offering a practical pathway to create intelligent, sample-efficient agents that benefit from advanced multimodal reasoning without the associated computational overhead. Its impact could be felt across various domains, accelerating the development and deployment of intelligent agents that can learn faster and operate more efficiently in the real world.
