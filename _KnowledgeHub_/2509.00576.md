# Galaxea Open-World Dataset and G0 Dual-System VLA Model

> [!summary] Summary
> The Galaxea Team introduces a 500-hour Open-World Dataset collected in diverse real-world environments with a consistent robotic embodiment, alongside G0, a dual-system Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model. This work demonstrates that single-embodiment pre-training on high-quality, in-domain data is critical for achieving robust action execution and few-shot learning for complex mobile manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- A severe scarcity of large-scale, high-quality, and diverse robot behavior data collected in authentic, unstructured human environments.
- Existing robot datasets often feature controlled settings, insufficient environmental richness, or inconsistencies across heterogeneous embodiments, creating a "domain gap" for real-world deployment.
- Unclear understanding of optimal pre-training strategies for VLA models, particularly the effectiveness and interplay of cross-embodiment versus single-embodiment data for specific robot platforms.

## Method

- Introduction of the ==Galaxea Open-World Dataset==, comprising 500 hours of high-fidelity data from a single robot embodiment (Galaxea R1 Lite) across 50 diverse real-world scenes with fine-grained ==subtask language annotations==.
- Development of **G0**, a ==dual-system framework== where ==G0-VLM== (System 2) handles high-level task planning and ==G0-VLA== (System 1) performs real-time, fine-grained action execution asynchronously.
- A robust ==3-stage training curriculum== for G0-VLA: cross-embodiment pre-training, single-embodiment pre-training on the Galaxea dataset, and task-specific post-training.

## Results

- The full **G0** model, utilizing both cross-embodiment and single-embodiment pre-training, achieved the highest average progress scores across diverse tabletop and long-horizon mobile manipulation tasks.
- Models with Stage-2 (single-embodiment) pre-training demonstrated superior language following, action consistency, and whole-body control, and significantly outperformed other models in few-shot transfer scenarios.
- The fine-tuned G0-VLM significantly surpassed baseline VLM models by over **50%** in instruction accuracy for generating subtask sequences, validating its role as an effective task planner for robotic applications.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-quality, single-embodiment pre-training on in-domain data is indispensable for strong VLA performance, significantly enhancing language following, action consistency, whole-body control, and few-shot transfer capabilities.
> - Cross-embodiment pre-training alone provides limited benefits and can even degrade performance for embodiment-specific skills when a large embodiment gap exists between source and target robots.
> - Domain-specific fine-tuning of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) is crucial for robotic task planning, enabling significantly higher instruction accuracy and better alignment with executable action primitives.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses several critical bottlenecks and ongoing debates within the field of embodied AI, particularly in the domain of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic control.

The broader research landscape for VLA models has seen rapid advancements, with these models emerging as a pivotal paradigm for enabling robots to perceive, reason, and perform complex tasks autonomously. However, a significant limitation hindering their generalization to diverse real-world contexts has been the scarcity of large-scale, high-quality, and truly "open-world" robot data. Existing datasets, such as Open-X Embodiment, BridgeData V2, and DROID, have made crucial contributions in scaling data collection or increasing embodiment diversity. Yet, they often fall short in terms of task realism, environmental richness, or consistency across heterogeneous data sources. Many are collected in controlled or artificial settings, leading to a "domain gap" when models trained on this data are deployed in unstructured real-world environments. Galaxea's work directly aims to bridge this gap by providing a dataset specifically designed to capture the diversity and complexity of human living and working environments.

The paper also engages with the architectural choices for VLA systems, specifically the "dual-system design" inspired by Kahneman's System 1 (fast, reactive) and System 2 (deliberative, planning) theory. This hierarchical approach, where a VLM (System 2) handles high-level planning and a VLA (System 1) executes fine-grained actions, has gained traction with works like SayCan. Galaxea contributes to this by proposing a robust dual-system framework, G0, and investigating effective methods for training and fine-tuning both components.

Furthermore, the research delves into a critical methodological debate concerning pre-training strategies: the optimal use of cross-embodiment versus single-embodiment data. While pre-training on diverse, multi-embodiment datasets is common for broad generalization, its efficacy for embodiment-specific specialization, especially when a significant "embodiment gap" exists, has yielded conflicting findings in prior work (e.g.,). By creating a high-fidelity, single-embodiment, real-world dataset, Galaxea provides a unique testbed to systematically analyze and disentangle the actual contribution of different pre-training paradigms to a model's real-world generalization performance. This systematic analysis helps to clarify when and how cross-embodiment data can positively transfer knowledge and when it might even hinder performance for specific robot platforms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the acknowledged "substantial bottleneck" in the development of generalist robot policies: the severe scarcity of large-scale, high-quality, and diverse robot behavior data collected in authentic, unstructured human environments. Existing datasets, despite their scale, are often limited by controlled settings, insufficient environmental richness, or inconsistencies arising from heterogeneous data collection across multiple robot embodiments. These limitations severely impair the generalization capabilities of trained VLA models when confronted with the inherent diversity and unpredictability of the real world.

Against this backdrop, the Galaxea Team set forth several key objectives:

1. **To introduce the Galaxea Open-World Dataset:** This involves creating an extensive, meticulously curated, and high-fidelity dataset of robot behaviors. The dataset is specifically designed to overcome the limitations of prior work by featuring data collected in diverse, authentic human living and working environments, using a consistent robotic embodiment, and enriched with precise subtask-level language annotations.
2. **To propose G0, a novel dual-system framework:** This framework aims to capitalize on the strengths of a Vision-Language Model (VLM) for generalized, deliberative planning (System 2, G0-VLM) and a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model for fine-grained, fast execution (System 1, G0-VLA). The objective is to enable asynchronous operation, allowing for both efficient training and robust real-world deployment.
3. **To develop and evaluate a robust 3-stage training curriculum for G0-VLA:** This curriculum is designed to progressively refine the model's capabilities, moving from acquiring general world knowledge through cross-embodiment pre-training, to specializing in the target platform's perceptual-action pairs via single-embodiment pre-training on the Galaxea dataset, and finally mastering specific complex skills through task-specific post-training. A key objective here is to systematically investigate the effectiveness and interplay of these stages, particularly the role of single-embodiment data.
4. **To establish a rigorous benchmark for evaluating mobile manipulation methodologies:** This includes diverse tasks spanning tabletop manipulation, few-shot learning, and long-horizon mobile manipulation, assessed in both normal and few-shot learning settings, to thoroughly demonstrate the effectiveness and generalization capabilities of their approach.
5. **To open-source the Galaxea Open-World Dataset and G0 models:** The ultimate objective is to contribute a valuable, accessible resource to the broader embodied AI community, fostering collaborative research and accelerating the development of more capable and general-purpose robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed by the Galaxea Team is two-fold, encompassing both the creation of a high-quality dataset and the development of a sophisticated dual-system VLA model with a structured training pipeline.

#### 3.1. Galaxea Open-World Dataset Creation

The **Galaxea Open-World Dataset** is the foundation of this work. It comprises 500 hours of high-fidelity data, totaling 100K demonstration trajectories, spanning 150 task categories performed in 50 distinct real-world scenes. This includes interaction with over 1,600 unique objects and demonstrating 58 operational skills.

- **Data Collection Platform:** All demonstrations were recorded using the **Galaxea R1 Lite platform**, a mobile, bimanual robot with 23 Degrees of Freedom (DoF). It features two 6-DoF arms, a 3-DoF torso for workspace extension, and a 6-DoF omnidirectional base. Perception is provided by a head stereo RGB camera for context and dual Intel RealSense D405 RGB-D wrist cameras for close-range manipulation. Its compact size allows navigation in human-centric environments.
- **Teleoperation Scheme:** An **isomorphic teleoperation scheme** was used, directly mapping human operator movements to the robot's kinematics. This approach was chosen to ensure natural, feasible motions, avoid inverse kinematics failures, and eliminate morphology re-targeting.
- **Collection Guidelines:** Data collection adhered to three principles: (1) **Observability:** All task-relevant visual cues were kept in view. (2) **Data Quantity and Quality:** A quality-first approach was adopted, with sufficient demonstrations for simple tasks and pilot phases for complex ones. (3) **Linguistic Grounding:** Each demonstration was meticulously annotated at the subtask level with structured language descriptions for multi-modal alignment.
- **Environmental and Object Diversity:** Data was collected across 11 physical sites (residential, catering, retail, office), yielding 50 unique scenes. Objects were sourced from real-world retail suppliers, and high-fidelity replicas were used for impractical items to maintain realism and efficiency.
- **Annotation Process:** Episodes were segmented into atomic subtasks, with annotators using a fixed schema for consistency and speed. Rigorous quality checks were performed at both episode and clip levels to exclude unqualified data.
- **Key Differentiators:** The dataset stands out for its (i) single-embodiment consistency across diverse skills and environments, (ii) fine-grained subtask-level annotations for precise multi-modal alignment, and (iii) significantly higher scene diversity in real-world settings compared to existing datasets.

#### 3.2. G0 Dual System Framework

The **G0 dual system** is composed of two asynchronously operating models:

- **G0-VLA (System 1 - Fast Execution):** This is an end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model responsible for real-time perception, subtask interpretation, and action execution (200 Hz control, 15 Hz planning). It generates continuous action chunks (A_t) conditioned on language instruction (l), visual observations (o), and proprioceptive state (s). The VLA embeds visual observations and language instructions using a pre-trained VLM, then generates continuous actions via a flow matching action expert conditioned on the VLM's KV cache.

 - **G0-VLA 3-Stage Training Strategy:**
 1. **Stage-1 (Cross-embodiment Pre-training):** Only the VLM component of G0-VLA is trained. It is initialized from PaLiGemma and consists of a SigLIP vision encoder, MLP projector, and a Transformer. This VLM is trained autoregressively to predict discrete action tokens (using FAST tokenizer) based on image, language, and proprioceptive inputs. Data for this stage (~1700 hours) includes diverse robot embodiment recordings like OXE, Galaxea's proprietary unlabeled dataset, and other in-house data, where only high-level task descriptions are used. The rationale is to acquire general world knowledge while avoiding inconsistent action expert learning from diverse, variably annotated cross-embodiment data or potential harm from diffusion loss early on.
 2. **Stage-2 (Single-embodiment Pre-training):** The full G0-VLA (VLM + action expert) is trained on the *labeled Galaxea Open-World Dataset*. A flow-matching loss is used to maximize the conditional distribution over action chunks. This stage leverages the dataset's single-embodiment consistency and fine-grained language-action alignment to improve action precision and language grounding specifically for the target robot platform.
 3. **Post-training (Task-oriented Training):** To assess generalization, the VLA is fine-tuned on downstream tasks using a limited number of high-quality demonstration trajectories (max 100 per task), applying the same training objective as Stage-2.

- **G0-VLM (System 2 - Slow Thinking):** This VLM acts as the high-level planner, processing complex natural language instructions from users, understanding the scene, and decomposing tasks into a sequence of simpler subtask instructions for G0-VLA execution (subtask instruction generation at < 2 Hz).

 - **G0-VLM Training:** G0-VLM is based on an instruction-tuned Qwen2.5-VL model. It is trained using image and subtask annotation pairs from the Galaxea Open-World Dataset. To enhance its planning capabilities for long temporal contexts, it receives historic image observations and robot actions. Crucially, human-style high-level instructions and robot verbal responses are synthesized using a reasoning LLM (DeepSeek-R1) based on the episode's task name and action sequences. This scalable approach allows the VLM to learn task planning and human interaction without directly feeding images to the reasoning LLM.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides a comprehensive evaluation of the G0 dual-system framework, particularly focusing on the G0-VLA training strategy and the impact of the Galaxea Open-World Dataset.

#### 4.1. Evaluating G0-VLA

The G0-VLA was benchmarked across tabletop manipulation (Table Bussing, Blocks Stacking), device operation (Microwave Operation), and long-horizon mobile manipulation (Bed Making).

- **Impact of Pre-trained Weights:**
 - **G0 (Full)**, which incorporates both Stage-1 (cross-embodiment) and Stage-2 (single-embodiment) pre-training, achieved the **highest average progress score**. It demonstrated superior object-picking abilities in tasks like Table Bussing, Microwave Operation, and Bed Making.
 - **G0 (Stage-2 400h)** and **G0 (Stage-2 200h)**, which involved only single-embodiment pre-training on the Galaxea dataset, showed the **best performance in language following, action consistency, and whole-body control** capabilities.
 - In contrast, **G0 (Stage-1)**, with only cross-embodiment pre-training, performed the **worst among all pre-trained models**, often comparable to a model trained from scratch (**G0 (Scratch)**). This finding strongly underscores the critical importance of single-embodiment pre-training for strong performance.
 - The authors concluded that Stage-1 pre-training primarily enhances the VLA's ability to perform simple, universal action patterns (e.g., pick-and-place), while Stage-2 pre-training specifically grounds the model to the target robot platform, leading to improved action stability and instruction following.

- **Few-shot Transfer Capability:**
 - Models with **Stage-2 pre-training significantly outperformed those without** in few-shot settings (using only 20 trajectories per task for Table Bussing and Microwave Operation). These models not only showed quantitative improvement in success rates but also produced noticeably smoother and more stable actions.
 - Models pre-trained solely with Stage-1 showed **no clear advantage over models trained from scratch** in few-shot scenarios. This suggests that cross-embodiment pre-training alone is insufficient for rapid adaptation to a new embodiment in few-shot settings.

- **Embodiment-specific Actions:**
 - Analysis of the long-horizon Bed Making task, which requires extensive whole-body control (chassis, torso, arms), revealed that **Stage-2 pre-training on single-embodiment data substantially improved the model’s performance on these embodiment-specific skills**.
 - Conversely, models pre-trained with cross-embodiment data (G0 (Stage-1) and baseline π0) demonstrated **substantially weaker instruction following for chassis-related actions and less accurate torso control**, sometimes even underperforming models trained from scratch. The authors hypothesize that a large embodiment gap between the Galaxea R1 Lite and robots in cross-embodiment datasets like OXE hinders the acquisition of such specific skills.

#### 4.2. Evaluating G0-VLM

The G0-VLM's performance as a task planner was evaluated based on instruction accuracy and action-grounding.

- **Necessity of Fine-tuning:** Benchmarking against established models (Gemini-2.5-pro, Qwen2.5-VL variants) with standardized prompts, the **fine-tuned G0-VLM significantly surpassed baseline accuracy by over 50%** across all benchmark tasks (Table Bussing, Microwave Operation, Make the Bed, Build Blocks).
- This result validates the hypothesis that for robotic applications, general-purpose vision-language understanding needs to be augmented with **precisely aligned action primitives through domain-specific adaptation (fine-tuning)** to enable language instructions directly executable by VLAs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several significant contributions with far-reaching potential impacts on the field of embodied AI and robotics:

1. **Addressing the Data Scarcity Problem with Real-World Diversity:** The introduction of the **Galaxea Open-World Dataset** is a monumental step towards bridging the "domain gap" between robot learning in controlled lab settings and deployment in unstructured, authentic human environments. Its large scale (500 hours, 100K trajectories), diverse scenes (50 unique, real-world locations), consistent single-embodiment collection, and fine-grained subtask annotations provide an unprecedented resource. This dataset will enable the training of more generalizable and robust VLA models capable of operating reliably in complex, dynamic real-world scenarios.

2. **Advancing Dual-System Architectures for Generalist Robots:** The proposed **G0 dual-system framework** offers a principled and effective architecture for tackling complex, long-horizon robotic tasks. By decoupling deliberative planning (G0-VLM) from fast, reactive execution (G0-VLA), the system benefits from the high-level reasoning capabilities of large language models while maintaining efficient, real-time control. This approach represents a significant step towards creating generalist robots that can understand and execute open-ended human commands.

3. **Clarifying Pre-training Strategies for Embodied AI:** The systematic evaluation of the 3-stage training curriculum, particularly the comparative analysis of cross-embodiment versus single-embodiment pre-training, yields a critical insight. The work definitively highlights the **indispensable role of high-quality, single-embodiment pre-training** on in-domain data (like Galaxea Open-World Dataset) for achieving strong performance, especially for few-shot learning and embodiment-specific skills (e.g., whole-body control). It also provides a crucial caution: naive application of cross-embodiment data with large embodiment gaps can even degrade performance for specific, intricate robot behaviors. This finding will guide future research and development in designing more effective and targeted pre-training strategies for robotic systems.

4. **Enabling Robust Language Grounding and Task Planning:** The demonstrated success of the fine-tuned G0-VLM in achieving significantly higher instruction accuracy underscores the necessity of domain-specific adaptation for VLM planners in robotics. This ensures that language instructions are precisely aligned with action primitives, leading to more reliable task planning and execution.

5. **Accelerating Community Progress through Open-Sourcing:** The commitment to open-sourcing the Galaxea Open-World Dataset and the G0 models will democratize access to cutting-edge resources. This will likely spark further research, benchmarking, and innovation by a wider community of developers and researchers, fostering collaborative efforts to push the boundaries of what is possible in embodied AI.

In summary, this research not only presents state-of-the-art performance in mobile manipulation but also provides foundational data and methodological insights that are crucial for the development of truly intelligent, versatile, and general-purpose robots capable of operating safely and effectively in human environments.
