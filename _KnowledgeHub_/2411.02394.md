# AutoVFX: Physically Realistic Video Editing from Natural Language Instructions

> [!summary] Summary
> AutoVFX, developed by researchers at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, is a framework that enables physically realistic video editing from natural language instructions. It integrates 3D scene modeling, an LLM-driven program generator, and a physics-based VFX module to allow users to create diverse, dynamic, and physically plausible visual effects like object insertion with real-world interactions, particle effects, and structural fracturing.

## Problem

- Traditional visual effects (VFX) creation is highly laborious, complex, and requires expert skills and specialized software, making it largely inaccessible to everyday users.
- Existing generative AI models for video editing, while accessible via natural language, often struggle with physical plausibility, precise 3D-aware control, and the ability to produce a wide array of dynamic special effects.
- Prior 3D-aware video editing methods, while working with 3D structures, often remain limited in their editing capabilities and still necessitate manual interaction through cumbersome graphical user interfaces (GUIs).

## Method

- **AutoVFX** introduces a three-module pipeline that integrates 3D scene modeling, LLM-based program generation, and physically-based VFX modules within a standard rendering engine like ==Blender==.
- It establishes a comprehensive 3D representation of the input video, capturing geometry (using **BakedSDF**/**StreetSurf**), appearance (**SuGaR**/textured mesh), semantics (Grounding ==SAM==/**DEVA** lifted to 3D), and environmental lighting (**DiffusionLight**).
- The system utilizes GPT-4 within an LLM-agent framework to interpret natural language instructions and compose executable Python programs that call modular VFX functions for operations like 3D asset insertion, material editing, and physical simulations.

## Results

- AutoVFX produces more realistic and accurate object insertions and manipulations, robustly preserving original scene structure, and uniquely supports diverse, physically plausible dynamic simulations (rigid body, particle effects, fracturing) not possible with generative baselines.
- Quantitative evaluations show AutoVFX's superior performance in object detection (Grounded-SAM: 0.537), temporal consistency (CLIP Directional Similarity: 0.419), and multimodal LLM evaluation for text alignment (GPT-4o: 0.791) and overall quality (GPT-4o: 0.647).
- User studies confirm strong preference for AutoVFX, with 83% for text alignment and 90% for overall realism, validating its practical effectiveness and user satisfaction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Seamlessly integrating large language models with a physically-based graphics pipeline allows for intuitive, natural language control over complex, realistic dynamic visual effects.
> - A comprehensive 3D scene understanding, including geometry, appearance, semantics, and lighting, is crucial for accurate and photorealistic integration of new elements and physically plausible interactions.
> - Modularizing complex VFX operations into callable functions enables LLMs to compose sophisticated editing workflows, democratizing access to professional-grade tools.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of visual effects (VFX) has long been dominated by labor-intensive, expert-driven processes requiring specialized software and skills. While capable of producing highly realistic imagery, this traditional pipeline remains largely inaccessible to everyday users due to its complexity and cost. Simultaneously, recent advancements in generative AI, particularly large-scale diffusion models, have opened new avenues for video editing, allowing users to modify content through natural language prompts. However, purely data-driven generative approaches often struggle with fundamental limitations: they lack guaranteed physical plausibility, precise 3D-aware control, and the ability to produce a wide array of dynamic special effects. Edits frequently appear inconsistent with real-world physics or lack fine-grained control over object interactions and properties.

Another line of research attempts to bridge this gap by building 3D representations from video inputs, enabling object insertion, texture changes, and then rendering the modified scene. While this approach aligns more closely with traditional VFX pipelines by working with 3D structures, it frequently remains limited in its editing capabilities and still necessitates manual interaction through cumbersome graphical user interfaces (GUIs). This limits its reach to a broader audience.

AutoVFX strategically positions itself at the intersection of these paradigms, aiming to overcome their respective shortcomings. It addresses the core challenge of democratizing VFX by integrating the accessibility of natural language interfaces with the rigor of physically-based simulation and the photorealism of advanced neural scene modeling. Unlike purely generative models (e.g., FRESCO, Instruct-N2N, DGE) that often fail to produce physically plausible or precisely controllable results, AutoVFX leverages explicit 3D scene understanding and physics engines. Conversely, it distinguishes itself from existing physics-based 3D editing methods (e.g., PhysGaussian, Gaussian Splashing, ChatSim) by replacing their typically script-driven or GUI-centric interfaces with intuitive natural language instructions, enabled by large language models (LLMs).

The work builds upon the emerging trend of using LLMs for code generation in various domains, including robotics (Code-as-Policies) and visual content creation (Visual Programming, Chat-Edit-3D, GPT4Motion). AutoVFX extends this concept to the highly complex domain of realistic video effects, enabling a depth of physical interaction and dynamic effects not previously achievable through natural language. By meticulously integrating scene reconstruction, semantic understanding, 3D asset management, and a robust rendering pipeline with an LLM-driven program generator, AutoVFX offers a comprehensive framework that combines the "open-world query" capability of generative methods with the "physical simulation" and "free-viewpoint rendering" of traditional VFX, all accessible through a user-friendly natural language interface. This unique synthesis marks a significant advancement toward making professional-grade visual effects production broadly accessible and efficient.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind AutoVFX is to **democratize the creation of visual effects (VFX)**. The authors identify a significant barrier: while modern VFX software allows for the creation of virtually any imagery, the process remains prohibitively laborious, complex, and expensive, requiring expert skills and professional tools. This renders VFX largely inaccessible to everyday users and even poses challenges for skilled artists seeking efficient workflows.

To address this, AutoVFX sets forth several key objectives:

- **Automate VFX Creation:** The core objective is to develop a framework that can automatically generate realistic and dynamic VFX videos. This automation aims to drastically reduce the manual effort and expertise currently required.
- **Leverage Natural Language Instructions:** To achieve widespread accessibility, the system must allow users to control VFX creation using natural language prompts, rather than complex GUIs or programming scripts. This objective aims to make video editing as intuitive as describing desired changes.
- **Ensure Physical Grounding and Photorealism:** A critical objective is to produce VFX that are not only visually appealing but also physically plausible and photorealistic. This means inserted objects should interact realistically with the scene, shadows and lighting should be consistent, and dynamics should obey physical laws. This addresses a major limitation of purely data-driven generative approaches, which often lack physical consistency.
- **Achieve Versatility in Editing Capabilities:** The framework aims to support a diverse range of editing effects. This includes modifying appearance and geometry, enabling dynamic interactions, applying particle effects (like smoke and fire), and even inserting animated characters, all within the same unified system.
- **Integrate Key Technologies:** To meet these ambitious goals, a foundational objective is the seamless integration of state-of-the-art neural scene modeling, advanced large language models (LLMs) for code generation, and robust physical simulation engines. This multi-modal integration is central to the system's functionality.
- **Outperform Existing Methods:** A final objective is to quantitatively and qualitatively demonstrate that AutoVFX surpasses competing methods in generative quality, alignment with instructions, editing versatility, and physical plausibility. This validates the effectiveness and convenience of the proposed hybrid approach.

In essence, AutoVFX seeks to bridge the gap between complex professional VFX pipelines and accessible generative video editing, offering a powerful yet user-friendly tool that can transform how visual content is created.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AutoVFX operates on a three-module pipeline that uniquely integrates 3D scene modeling, LLM-based program generation, and physically-based VFX modules within a standard rendering engine (Blender). The entire process begins with a single input video and natural language instructions, culminating in an edited, free-viewpoint video.

#### 3.1. 3D Scene Modeling

The foundational step involves creating a comprehensive 3D representation of the input video, capturing critical scene properties:

- **Geometry:** Camera poses are first inferred using COLMAP. For high-fidelity 3D geometry, BakedSDF is employed. This method optimizes a hybrid implicit neural representation into a triangulated mesh, chosen for its compatibility with standard VFX pipelines, rendering efficiency, and accuracy in physical simulations. For challenging long, narrow camera trajectories common in autonomous driving scenes, StreetSurf is used.
- **Appearance:** The scene's appearance is captured in two ways: (1) SuGaR (a Gaussian Splatting-based method) provides photorealistic free-view rendering, and (2) a textured BakedSDF mesh is used for physically-based rendering effects like accurate shadow casting and multi-bounce light interactions, which are crucial for integrating new elements seamlessly.
- **Semantics:** To enable localized edits, Grounding SAM performs open-vocabulary instance segmentation, and DEVA tracks these instances across video frames. This 2D segmentation is then lifted to 3D by un-projecting pixels onto the scene geometry and using a visibility-based voting mechanism to identify corresponding mesh faces and 3D Gaussians. An mIoU optimization step ensures accurate 3D segmentation.
- **Lighting:** Environmental lighting is estimated using DiffusionLight to generate High Dynamic Range (HDR) maps from a single input image by in-painting and unwrapping chrome balls. For fully-captured indoor scenes, emitter meshes are extracted from over-saturated pixels to serve as light sources. For outdoor scenes, a directional sunlight source is added based on the brightest area of the HDR map to enhance shadow realism.

#### 3.2. Scene Editing and Simulation

A modular suite of executable functions, termed "VFX Modules," is designed to perform diverse editing, simulation, and rendering operations. This modularity allows for easy expansion of capabilities.

- **3D Asset Creation and Insertion:** The system can retrieve 3D assets from datasets like Objaverse 1.0 and Richdreamer, using Sentence-BERT and CLIP for textual and visual alignment. For novel queries, Meshy AI generates new high-quality 3D assets. For realistic insertion, positions are sampled on sufficiently flat surfaces of the supporting mesh, and GPT-4V estimates real-world dimensions for accurate scaling.
- **Removal:** Target objects are extracted using semantic modules, and their associated Gaussians and mesh faces are removed. Geometric restoration is achieved by covering exposed areas with a planar mesh, while appearance restoration involves LaMa for inpainting missing regions across frames, followed by fine-tuning the 3D Gaussian Splatting model for 3D consistency.
- **Material Editing:** Users can modify material properties (e.g., metallic, specular, roughness, color) of 3D assets by adjusting material nodes in the renderer or by applying materials from databases like PolyHaven.
- **Physical Simulation:** Leveraging Blender's robust capabilities, AutoVFX performs rigid-body physics simulation (based on the Bullet physics engine), pre-computing centers of mass and convex hulls for accurate interactions. Particle effects like smoke and fire are simulated using Mantaflow, with modified default settings for convincing visual results. Object fracturing is enabled via Blender's Cell Fracture algorithm.

#### 3.3. Scene Rendering and Video Compositing

A meticulous rendering and compositing scheme ensures photorealistic output:

- **Rendering:** Blender's Cycles renderer is used. Inserted objects are rendered while the background mesh is set to be invisible for first-bounces but visible for higher-order bounces, ensuring proper global illumination effects. The background mesh is then rendered twice (with and without inserted objects) to approximate their effect on background surfaces.
- **Compositing:** The ratio of pixel values between the combined and background renders approximates object effects. This ratio is applied to either a SuGaR rendering or the original video frames, depending on whether novel views are desired. Finally, inserted objects are alpha-blended into the video using depth maps for accurate occlusion reasoning. Premultiplied alpha is used for emissive elements (like fire), while straight alpha is used otherwise.

#### 3.4. LLM Integration

The accessibility of AutoVFX stems from its natural language interface, powered by GPT-4 within an LLM-agent framework.

- **Modular Function Encapsulation:** All scene editing, simulation, and rendering operations are encapsulated into callable Python functions. Scene objects are represented as Python dictionaries, allowing transparent manipulation.
- **LLM-driven Program Composition and Execution:** GPT-4 is prompted with in-context examples that demonstrate how natural language instructions map to sequences of calls to these predefined functions. Given a user instruction, the LLM generates an executable Python-like program, which is then executed by a Python interpreter to produce the final video. This approach simplifies complex VFX creation for a broad audience.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

AutoVFX's efficacy is thoroughly validated through extensive experiments, encompassing qualitative and quantitative comparisons against leading text-based visual editing methods, as well as user studies.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Evaluation

The qualitative results (Figures 4, 5, 6 in the paper and Figures 7, 8, 16 in the supplementary material) clearly demonstrate AutoVFX's superior performance:

- **Static Scene Editing:** Compared to baselines like Instruct-N2N, DGE, and FRESCO, AutoVFX consistently produces more realistic and accurate object insertions and manipulations while robustly preserving the original scene structure. Baselines often show limitations such as struggling with localized editing (Instruct-N2N), failing at structural preservation (FRESCO), or lacking precise instruction alignment despite realistic output (DGE). AutoVFX's ability to handle complex instructions like "make it mirror-like" for material editing, "drop five basketballs" for accurate object counting, and "make it on fire" for advanced particle effects highlights its versatility.
- **Dynamic Video Simulation:** A significant advantage of AutoVFX is its support for dynamic VFX. It can generate a wide range of realistic, physically plausible simulations, including rigid body dynamics, object animation along custom paths, complex smoke and fire effects, and realistic object fracturing (e.g., breaking a sculpture, books falling). These capabilities are explicitly not supported by the purely generative editing baselines, which primarily focus on static appearance modifications.
- **Autonomous Driving Scenes:** AutoVFX demonstrates its utility in complex real-world scenarios, such as autonomous driving simulations using the Waymo dataset. It enables both photorealistic rendering and physically accurate interactions between vehicles in collision scenarios or when new objects are introduced (e.g., "Insert a physics-enabled Benz... Add a Ferrari moving forward"). This highlights its potential for applications in simulation and content generation for challenging dynamic environments.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Evaluation

A comprehensive quantitative assessment (Table 2) based on semantic consistency measures, multimodal LLM quality evaluation, and user studies further corroborates AutoVFX's strong performance:

- **Semantic Consistency Measures:**
 - **Object Detection (Grounded-SAM):** AutoVFX achieved a score of 0.537, significantly outperforming baselines (e.g., Instruct-N2N: 0.343, FRESCO: 0.373). This indicates AutoVFX's superior ability to correctly compose and maintain the presence of desired objects in the edited video, reflecting successful object-level changes.
 - **CLIP Similarity:** While comparable to baselines (around 0.206), the authors note that global CLIP similarity might be less sensitive to subtle local changes or dynamic effects.
 - **CLIP Directional Similarity:** AutoVFX scored 0.419, substantially higher than baselines (e.g., Instruct-N2N: 0.019, DGE: 0.278). This metric, which evaluates temporal consistency across frames in CLIP space, confirms that AutoVFX maintains better consistency in its edits over time, crucial for dynamic VFX.

- **Multimodal LLM Quality Evaluation (GPT-4o):** Utilizing GPT-4o to assess edited videos across "Photorealism," "Text Alignment," "Structure Preservation," and "Overall Quality" (on a 0-1 scale), AutoVFX demonstrated significant leads:
 - **Text Alignment:** AutoVFX scored 0.791, far exceeding baselines (e.g., FRESCO: 0.427). This highlights its exceptional ability to accurately interpret and execute natural language instructions.
 - **Photorealism:** AutoVFX achieved 0.735 (vs. FRESCO: 0.622), indicating highly realistic video output.
 - **Structure Preservation:** AutoVFX scored 0.749 (vs. FRESCO: 0.632), demonstrating its capacity to make edits without distorting the underlying scene.
 - **Overall Quality:** AutoVFX's score of 0.647 was markedly higher than any baseline (e.g., FRESCO: 0.204), validating its comprehensive quality advantage.

- **User Study:** A study with 36 participants reinforced the quantitative findings. AutoVFX received substantially higher preference from users for both "Text Alignment" (0.83) and "Overall Realism" (0.90) compared to all competing methods. This strong user preference confirms that the edits are not only accurate by objective metrics but also visually appealing and satisfying to human judgment.

#### 4.3. Failure Case Analysis

The authors also provide a transparent failure analysis from 55 predefined instructions, identifying 19 failure cases (supplementary Figure 15). The majority of failures stem from:
- **Scene Modeling (largest category):** Errors in geometry and appearance, such as imperfect mesh reconstruction, inaccurate semantic predictions, or blurry inpainting after object removal.
- **Editing Modules:** Issues like inaccurate object placement, incorrect asset retrieval, or faulty scale estimation.
- **Unsupported Functions:** Limitations in handling certain physical effects (e.g., fluid or snow simulation) or global style changes.
- **Code Generation:** Misinterpretations by GPT-4 leading to syntax errors or incorrect function calls.
This analysis points to areas for future improvement, particularly in making the underlying scene modeling and editing modules more robust, and refining LLM prompting for better accuracy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AutoVFX represents a significant leap forward in the field of visual content creation, offering a compelling blend of accessibility, realism, and versatility. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Democratization of VFX:** The most profound impact of AutoVFX is its potential to make sophisticated visual effects creation accessible to a dramatically wider audience. By enabling control through intuitive natural language instructions, it removes the formidable barriers of specialized software, complex interfaces, and extensive training typically associated with VFX. This empowers not only professional artists to accelerate their workflows but also everyday users, content creators, and hobbyists to realize their creative visions without prior technical expertise.
- **Acceleration of Content Production:** For professionals, AutoVFX could serve as a powerful tool to rapidly prototype ideas, generate initial effects, or automate repetitive editing tasks. This acceleration could significantly reduce production times and costs in filmmaking, advertising, and game development. The ability to quickly iterate on complex, physically-grounded effects by simply describing them opens new avenues for creativity and efficiency.
- **Bridging the AI-Graphics Gap:** AutoVFX successfully unifies two distinct paradigms: the intuitive, open-ended generative capabilities of large language models and the precise, physically-grounded control offered by traditional computer graphics and simulation engines. This integration addresses critical limitations of purely data-driven generative models (lack of physical plausibility, precise control) and traditional GUI-based tools (lack of accessibility). It demonstrates a viable path towards AI systems that can reason about and manipulate the physical world in a photorealistic manner.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The modular architecture of AutoVFX is a key strength. It allows for continuous improvement by integrating new advancements in neural scene modeling (e.g., more robust 3D reconstruction, better instance segmentation), more sophisticated physical simulation techniques (e.g., fluid dynamics, soft body physics), and increasingly capable LLMs. This framework can serve as a robust platform for future research into AI-driven content generation, embodied AI, and interactive simulation.
- **Applications Beyond Entertainment:** While immediately impactful for film and video production, AutoVFX's capabilities extend to other domains. It could be transformative for creating realistic training simulations (e.g., for robotics, autonomous driving), developing interactive augmented and virtual reality (AR/VR) experiences, and even for scientific visualization where dynamic, physically accurate scenarios are needed. The ability to generate realistic car collisions in autonomous driving simulations, for instance, highlights its practical utility beyond traditional VFX.

In conclusion, AutoVFX pushes the boundaries of AI-driven visual content creation by offering an unprecedented level of control, realism, and accessibility. It represents a significant step towards a future where high-quality visual effects are not a luxury reserved for experts but a powerful tool available to anyone with an idea.
