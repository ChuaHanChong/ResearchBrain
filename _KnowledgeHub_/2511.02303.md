# Unlocking the Power of Multi-Agent LLM for Reasoning: From Lazy Agents to Deliberation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Penn State, Harvard, Michigan State, University of Utah, and Microsoft developed Dr. MAMR, a framework that addresses the "lazy agent" problem in multi-agent Large Language Model (LLM) reasoning by correcting biases in reinforcement learning objectives and enabling agent deliberation. Dr. MAMR consistently outperforms baselines, achieving an average pass@1 accuracy of 58.43% on mathematical reasoning benchmarks for the 7B Qwen2.5 model, improving upon ReMA's 51.97% and GRPO's 55.08%.

## Problem

- Existing multi-agent LLM reasoning frameworks (e.g., ReMA) suffer from a "lazy agent" problem, where one agent contributes trivially while another dominates, diminishing collaborative benefits.
- The multi-turn Group Relative Preference Optimization (GRPO) objective contains a normalization bias (`1/T_i`) that implicitly incentivizes shorter, less collaborative reasoning trajectories.
- In prolonged multi-turn interactions, reasoning agents can get lost or propagate errors, lacking a mechanism to adaptively discard noisy past outputs and restart their reasoning.

## Method

- **Dr. MAMR** (Multi-Agent ==Meta-Reasoning== Done Right) addresses the normalization bias by removing the `1/T_i` term from the multi-turn GRPO objective, preventing implicit penalties for longer, more deliberative trajectories.
- A Shapley-inspired causal influence (CI) mechanism measures individual agent step contributions more stably by averaging one-step causal influences across semantically similar steps, guiding balanced credit assignment.
- A verifiable reward for "restart behavior" (RB) allows the reasoning agent to adaptively discard noisy outputs and restart its process, with rewards tied to the restart's causal impact on final answer correctness.

## Results

- Dr. MAMR significantly outperforms all baselines (single-agent GRPO, VRP, ReMA) across seven mathematical reasoning benchmarks and various model sizes, showing average pass@1 accuracy gains (e.g., **58.43%** for **7B** Dr. MAMR vs. **51.97%** for ReMA).
- Training with Dr. MAMR fosters balanced agent contributions, with both meta-thinking and reasoning agents exhibiting consistently increasing causal influence during training, unlike ReMA where the reasoning agent's influence diminishes.
- Dr. MAMR exhibits significantly more stable training curves, maintaining high mean rewards, while baselines like ReMA suffer rapid collapse of mean reward after a small number of training steps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The `1/T_i` normalization term in multi-turn GRPO is a root cause for the "lazy agent" phenomenon, biasing the model towards shorter, less collaborative reasoning paths.
> - Stabilizing causal influence measurement through semantic grouping and averaging provides a more robust signal for credit assignment in online multi-agent reinforcement learning.
> - Enabling agents to "deliberate" by restarting their reasoning process with verified rewards is crucial for maintaining effective collaboration and preventing error propagation in complex, multi-turn tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields within artificial intelligence: Large Language Models (LLMs), multi-agent systems, and reinforcement learning.

- **Advancements in LLM Reasoning:** The broader landscape has seen significant progress in LLM reasoning, driven by techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting (Wei et al., 2022) and more structured methods such as Tree-of-Thoughts (Yao et al., 2023). This has led to the emergence of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) that combine supervised and reinforcement learning to excel in tasks like math, code, and planning (Guo et al., 2025a). This paper contributes directly to this trajectory by enhancing the reasoning capabilities of LLMs, specifically in complex, multi-step scenarios.
- **Rise of Multi-Agent LLM Systems:** A key trend is the move beyond single-LLM reasoning to multi-agent frameworks, where specialized LLMs collaborate through planning, delegation, and debate (e.g., Li et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2024a). This approach mirrors human team dynamics, aiming to leverage diverse perspectives and capabilities. This paper operates squarely within this paradigm, seeking to optimize the collaborative potential of such systems.
- **Reinforcement Learning for LLMs (RLHF/RLAIF):** The use of reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards has become crucial for training LLMs on complex tasks. Techniques like Group Relative Preference Optimization (GRPO) and its multi-turn variants (Wan et al., 2025) are central to this. This paper critically analyzes and improves upon the multi-turn GRPO framework, particularly as implemented in ReMA (Wan et al., 2025), which serves as a direct predecessor and baseline.
- **Addressing Challenges in Multi-Agent RL (MARL):** The "lazy agent" problem is a well-known challenge in traditional MARL, especially in sparse-reward settings (Sunehag et al., 2018). This work extends this problem to the sequential, multi-turn LLM context, which is a novel and important contribution. By tackling credit assignment and incentivizing balanced participation, the paper advances the robustness and effectiveness of MARL in the LLM domain.
- **Enhancing Multi-Turn Interaction:** The paper also addresses the general challenge of LLMs getting "lost" or overcommitting in prolonged multi-turn interactions (Laban et al., 2025). The introduction of a "restart" mechanism for deliberation directly contributes to making multi-turn LLM interactions more robust and effective, whether in single-agent or multi-agent settings.

In summary, this work pushes the boundaries of multi-agent LLM reasoning by deeply analyzing and remedying a fundamental flaw in existing RL-based multi-agent frameworks, thereby making multi-agent LLM collaboration more stable, balanced, and powerful.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research stem from identifying and addressing a critical limitation in current multi-agent LLM reasoning frameworks, specifically the "lazy agent" phenomenon.

- **Identify and Characterize Lazy Agent Behavior:** The foremost objective is to empirically demonstrate and systematically characterize the "lazy agent" problem within existing multi-agent LLM reasoning setups (like ReMA). This involves showing how one agent (typically the reasoning agent) contributes trivially while the other (meta-thinking agent) dominates, effectively collapsing the collaborative system into a less effective single-agent model.
- **Provide Theoretical Explanation:** To move beyond empirical observation, a key motivation is to theoretically analyze the root cause of lazy agent behavior. The authors aim to pinpoint specific biases in the underlying reinforcement learning objectives, particularly the multi-turn Group Relative Preference Optimization (GRPO) framework, that inadvertently incentivize shorter, less collaborative reasoning trajectories.
- **Develop a Stable Causal Influence Measurement:** Given that lazy agents contribute little, a core objective is to reliably measure the contribution of each agent's reasoning steps. The motivation here is to overcome the limitations of single-trajectory observations and phrasing bias in online RL by introducing a stable, Shapley-inspired method for quantifying causal influence, thereby allowing for more accurate credit assignment.
- **Enable Agent Deliberation and Recovery:** As agents become more collaborative and engage in more turns, a secondary but crucial problem arises: agents can get lost or trapped by their own noisy prior outputs. The objective is to equip the reasoning agent with the ability to "deliberate"—to adaptively discard past noisy outputs, consolidate instructions, and restart its reasoning process when necessary, thereby preventing error propagation and sustaining effective collaboration over longer interactions.
- **Design Verifiable Reward Mechanisms:** To support the newly introduced deliberation capability, a key objective is to design a novel verifiable reward mechanism that accurately credits or penalizes the "restart" behavior based on its impact on the final correctness of the answer.
- **Unlock Full Potential of Multi-Agent LLMs:** The overarching motivation is to transform multi-agent LLM systems from a promising but often underperforming paradigm into a robust and superior approach for complex reasoning tasks. By mitigating lazy behavior, ensuring balanced contributions, and enabling adaptive deliberation, the authors aim to truly "unlock the power" of multi-agent LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose **Dr. MAMR (Multi-Agent Meta-Reasoning Done Right)**, a framework designed to address the identified limitations. Their methodology involves a multi-pronged approach combining theoretical analysis, modifications to the RL objective, and novel reward mechanisms.

- **Theoretical Analysis of Lazy Agent Emergence:**
 - The paper begins by analyzing the multi-turn GRPO objective, specifically focusing on the `1/T_i` normalization term (where `T_i` is the number of turns in trajectory `i`).
 - **Core Insight:** They theoretically demonstrate that this normalization term creates a bias that incentivizes the model to prefer trajectories with fewer turns, even if longer, more deliberative trajectories yield the same final reward. This implicit incentive for brevity is identified as the root cause of lazy agent behavior.
- **Loss Formulation Correction:**
 - **Normalization Debias:** The first step in addressing the theoretical bias is to remove the `1/T_i` normalization term from the multi-turn GRPO objective (Eq. 2), ensuring that longer, more collaborative trajectories are not implicitly penalized.
- **Shapley-Inspired Causal Influence (CI):**
 - **Problem:** Measuring causal influence of individual steps in online RL is challenging due to single-trajectory observations and sensitivity to phrasing.
 - **Approach:** They flatten trajectories into sequences of actions. For an "anchor" step, they identify a group of *semantically similar steps* across different rollouts using cosine similarity of their embeddings.
 - **Measurement:** For each step in this group, they measure its one-step causal influence by comparing the model's likelihood of generating the subsequent token with and without suppressing attention to the current step (using KL divergence).
 - **Aggregation:** The causal influence of the anchor step is then defined as the *average* of these one-step influences across its group of semantically similar steps. This averaging process makes the influence estimation more stable and less biased by specific phrasings.
- **Verifiable Reward for Restart Behavior (RB):**
 - **Motivation:** To counter the problem of reasoning agents getting "lost" or propagating errors in extended multi-turn interactions.
 - **Mechanism:** A special control token, `<restart>`, is introduced. When the reasoning agent generates this token, it signals its decision to discard previous reasoning outputs, re-aggregate instructions from the meta-thinking agent, and start anew.
 - **Reward Design:** A novel verifiable reward `r_restart` is designed for the `<restart>` action. This reward is binary (+1 or -1) and depends on two factors: (1) whether the final answer for the trajectory is correct (`z_i`), and (2) how the restart action *causally influences* the probability of generating that final answer (`∆ℓ_i,t`). Specifically, if the final answer is correct, a restart is rewarded if masking prior reasoning (before the restart) increases the model's confidence in that correct answer. Conversely, if the final answer is incorrect, a restart is rewarded if masking prior reasoning decreases confidence in that incorrect answer. This ensures that restarts are reinforced only when they genuinely help correct the reasoning trajectory.
 - **Cold Start:** To enable the model to learn restart behavior, expert data (simulated noisy reasoning followed by a GPT-4o-generated restart) is used for supervised fine-tuning (SFT) before RL.
- **Aggregated Step-Level Advantage Function:**
 - All signals—outcome-based advantage (`˜A_i,t`), Shapley-inspired causal influence (`˜C_i,t`), and restart reward (`˜R_i,t`)—are normalized and combined linearly to form a comprehensive `A_step_i,t`.
 - **Formula:** `A_step_i,t = ˜A_i,t + α ˜C_i,t + β ˜R_i,t`, where `α` and `β` are tunable hyperparameters balancing the contributions of causal influence and restart rewards.
- **Dr. MAMR Training Objective:** The final training objective replaces the advantage function in the original multi-turn GRPO (Eq. 2) with the newly defined `A_step`, and removes the `1/T_i` normalization term.
- **Experimental Setup:**
 - **Model:** Qwen2.5-Instruct family (3B, 7B, 14B models) are used.
 - **Dataset:** DeepScaleR for training, and evaluated on seven diverse mathematical reasoning benchmarks (MATH500, GSM8K, AIME24, AMC23, Gaokao2023en, Minerva Math, OlympiadBench).
 - **Framework:** Verl RL framework. Agents share model weights but use role-specific system prompts.

This systematic approach, combining theoretical diagnosis with practical, verifiable solutions, forms the robust methodology of Dr. MAMR.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted provide strong evidence for the effectiveness of Dr. MAMR in addressing the lazy agent issue and enhancing multi-agent LLM reasoning.

- **Empirical Confirmation of Lazy Agent Problem:**
 - Initial experiments (Fig. 1a, Fig. 2a-c) clearly demonstrate that ReMA, despite training, leads to a significant imbalance in agent contributions. The reasoning agent’s causal influence diminishes considerably compared to the meta-thinking agent, often resulting in trivial outputs and a performance drop (e.g., from 75.0% to 74.4% on MATH500 with a 7B model).
 - While prompting can slightly mitigate this (ReMA w/ prompt, 75.6%), it doesn't fully resolve the underlying issue.
- **Theoretical Analysis Validated:**
 - The empirical observation that reasoning processes exhibiting lazy behavior consistently involve fewer turns than non-lazy ones (Appendix F) supports the theoretical claim that the `1/T_i` normalization in multi-turn GRPO implicitly incentivizes shorter trajectories, thus fostering lazy agent behavior.
- **Superior Performance of Dr. MAMR:**
 - Dr. MAMR consistently and significantly outperforms all baselines—single-agent GRPO, VRP (CoT), and ReMA—across all seven mathematical reasoning benchmarks and all model sizes (3B, 7B, 14B) (Table 1).
 - For the 7B model, Dr. MAMR achieves an average pass@1 accuracy of 58.43%, substantially higher than ReMA's 51.97% and GRPO's 55.08%.
 - The performance gains are even more pronounced with larger base models (e.g., 14B model: Dr. MAMR 62.49% vs. ReMA 57.24% vs. GRPO 58.05%), indicating that Dr. MAMR effectively leverages stronger instruction-following capabilities.
 - Crucially, Dr. MAMR elevates the multi-agent system from underperforming single-agent GRPO to clearly surpassing it, demonstrating the successful unlocking of multi-agent potential.
- **Balanced Agent Contribution and Stable Training:**
 - **Causal Influence Evolution:** Unlike ReMA, where the reasoning agent's causal influence steadily decreases during training, Dr. MAMR fosters a balanced evolution where both meta-thinking and reasoning agents exhibit consistently increasing causal influence (Fig. 4a). This confirms that Dr. MAMR ensures active and meaningful participation from both agents.
 - **Training Stability:** Dr. MAMR shows significantly more stable training curves, maintaining high mean rewards throughout the process. In stark contrast, ReMA suffers from rapid collapse, with its mean reward dropping to zero after a relatively small number of training steps (e.g., 150 steps for 7B, 20 steps for 3B) (Fig. 4b, H.2). This highlights the robustness of Dr. MAMR in multi-agent RL settings.
- **Enhanced Scaling with Pass@K:**
 - When evaluated with Pass@K (measuring success within K independent attempts), Dr. MAMR consistently outperforms ReMA, and the performance gap *widens* as K increases, especially on more challenging benchmarks like AIME25 (Fig. 4c, H.4). This indicates that Dr. MAMR's enhanced collaboration and deliberation lead to more reliable and robust problem-solving, which is crucial for tackling difficult tasks.
- **Ablation Study Validates Component Contributions:**
 - The ablation study (Table 2) confirms the importance of each proposed component:
 - Removing **Normalization Debias (w/o ND)** or **Shapley-inspired Causal Influence (w/o CI)** leads to noticeable performance drops, demonstrating their complementary roles in preventing shortcuts and promoting balanced contributions.
 - Removing **Restart Behavior (w/o RB)** also degrades performance, although less severely, underscoring its value in allowing the agent to recover from errors and maintain a coherent reasoning trajectory.
- **Process Reward Limitation:**
 - Supplementary experiments (Appendix G) show that directly incorporating process reward models (PRMs) for turn-level credit assignment in ReMA leads to rapid model collapse due to reward hacking. This finding emphasizes the difficulty of designing effective intermediate rewards and highlights the advantage of Dr. MAMR's intrinsic, verifiable signals.
- **Deliberation Benefits Confirmed:**
 - Preliminary experiments (Fig. 3) using a modified ReMA+ with a deliberation-enabling prompt at inference time show consistent performance improvements, particularly on harder benchmarks and in scenarios requiring more turns. This result validates the motivation behind the formal "restart" mechanism introduced in Dr. MAMR.

These findings collectively affirm that Dr. MAMR successfully diagnoses and mitigates the lazy agent problem, leading to a more effective, stable, and powerful multi-agent LLM reasoning system.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "UNLOCKING THE POWER OF MULTI-AGENT LLM FOR REASONING: FROM LAZY AGENTS TO DELIBERATION" carries significant implications for the development and application of advanced LLM systems.

- **Foundational Advancement in Multi-Agent LLMs:** This work represents a crucial step forward for multi-agent LLM systems. By effectively addressing the pervasive "lazy agent" problem, Dr. MAMR transforms these systems from a conceptually appealing but often underperforming paradigm into a demonstrably superior one. This shift validates the potential of collaborative AI and paves the way for more complex, human-like reasoning architectures.
- **Improved Robustness and Reliability:** The enhanced training stability, balanced agent contributions, and the ability for agents to "deliberate" (restart reasoning) make LLM-based multi-agent systems significantly more robust and reliable. This is critical for deploying LLMs in high-stakes applications where error propagation and unpredictable behavior must be minimized.
- **Deeper Understanding of LLM Behavior in Multi-Turn Settings:** The theoretical analysis of the `1/T_i` normalization bias provides a fundamental insight into how subtle aspects of RL loss functions can profoundly influence LLM behavior in multi-turn interactions. This understanding is valuable not only for multi-agent systems but also for single-agent multi-turn contexts, guiding future design of RL objectives.
- **Enhanced Credit Assignment in Complex Systems:** The Shapley-inspired causal influence method offers a more stable and less biased way to attribute contributions in sequential, multi-agent interactions. This methodology could be generalized to other complex RL systems where credit assignment is challenging, leading to more interpretable and fairer reinforcement signals.
- **Unlocking New Applications:** With improved reliability and performance, multi-agent LLMs powered by Dr. MAMR could tackle a broader range of complex reasoning tasks that require sustained collaboration, planning, and adaptive problem-solving. Potential applications include:
 - **Advanced Scientific Discovery:** Simulating scientific teams to formulate hypotheses, design experiments, and analyze results.
 - **Complex Engineering Design:** Collaboratively tackling multi-faceted design challenges in areas like robotics or software architecture.
 - **Automated Planning and Logistics:** Developing more intelligent and adaptive planning agents for dynamic environments.
 - **Education and Tutoring:** Creating AI tutors that can guide students through complex problem-solving with dynamic adjustments.
 - **Creative Problem Solving:** Agents collaborating on creative tasks, potentially leading to novel solutions.
- **Catalyst for Future Research:** The paper opens several avenues for future research, including exploring more sophisticated semantic grouping for causal influence, integrating different forms of deliberation, adapting the framework to other multi-agent architectures (e.g., hierarchical, debate-based), and further investigating biases in RL objectives for long-horizon tasks. The failure of process reward models also reinforces the need for carefully designed intrinsic or verifiable rewards.

In conclusion, this research not only solves a pressing technical problem in multi-agent LLM reasoning but also provides a more profound understanding of the underlying dynamics of large language models in collaborative, sequential environments, laying a robust foundation for the next generation of intelligent agents.
