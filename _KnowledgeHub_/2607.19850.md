# SOPD-SocialNav: Selective On-Policy Distillation for Vision-Language Social Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Hokkaido University developed SOPD-SocialNav, a selective on-policy distillation method that transfers social reasoning capabilities from large Vision-Language Models to lightweight models for robot navigation. This approach selectively focuses on socially informative decision tokens, leading to improved action accuracy and reasoning consistency on benchmarks like SNEI and MUSON, alongside demonstrated socially appropriate navigation in real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs), while capable of rich social reasoning, are computationally intensive and challenging to deploy on resource-constrained robotic platforms.
- Current on-policy distillation methods often suffer from mode-seeking behavior, which limits action diversity and can lead to mode collapse in student models.
- Standard distillation techniques apply loss uniformly across all tokens, diluting the learning focus on critical social interactions by giving equal weight to routine, low-entropy navigation actions.

## Method

- **SOPD-SocialNav** employs a ==selective on-policy distillation== framework to transfer social reasoning from a large teacher VLM (Qwen3-VL-8B) to a lightweight student VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-3B).
- It uses ==Jensen-Shannon Divergence (JSD)== as the distillation objective to encourage broader distribution alignment, avoiding the mode-seeking issues of Reverse KL divergence.
- An ==entropy-based token filter== selectively applies the distillation loss to "socially informative decision tokens," focusing learning on complex social situations rather than routine movements.

## Results

- SOPD consistently achieved superior performance on social navigation benchmarks, recording an Action Accuracy of **0.889** on SNEI and **0.953** on MUSON, along with higher Perception and Reasoning Cosine Similarities compared to all baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the Jensen-Shannon Divergence objective and the entropy-based token filter individually contribute to performance gains, with their combined application yielding the highest metrics.
- The distilled model successfully demonstrated socially appropriate navigation in real-world scenarios on a Scout Mini robot, such as navigating around conversational groups and queues without intrusion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Advanced social reasoning capabilities from large Vision-Language Models can be effectively transferred to lightweight models through targeted knowledge distillation.
> - Employing a symmetric divergence like Jensen-Shannon Divergence helps mitigate mode-seeking behavior, promoting more robust and diverse action policies in student models.
> - Selectively focusing distillation on tokens where the teacher exhibits higher uncertainty (indicating socially informative decision points) enhances the efficiency and relevance of knowledge transfer, preventing dilution from routine actions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot navigation increasingly emphasizes human-centered decision-making, particularly in environments with high pedestrian traffic. Traditional social navigation methods, such as the social force model, proxemics-based approaches, velocity obstacles, DWA with social costs, imitation learning, and reinforcement learning, typically rely on direct observation-to-action mappings. While these methods can produce socially compliant behaviors, they often capture semantic and social reasoning implicitly, limiting their ability to interpret human intentions, group dynamics, or social norms explicitly.

Recent advancements in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have introduced a new avenue for social robot navigation. Large-scale VLMs demonstrate strong semantic understanding and commonsense reasoning capabilities, which can be leveraged to assess complex social costs and inform navigation decisions that align with human expectations. For instance, VLM-SocialNav uses a large, closed-source VLM as a social reasoning module to provide social costs to a downstream planner. However, these large models pose significant challenges for deployment on resource-constrained robotic platforms due to their computational demands and potential for high inference latency.

To address deployment constraints, several strategies have been explored. One approach involves fine-tuning VLMs on task-specific social navigation datasets, as seen with Social-LLaVA and AutoSpatial. These methods aim to adapt VLMs to domain-specific social behaviors but often require substantial training resources. Another direction focuses on developing lightweight VLMs for social navigation, such as MAction-SocialNav, SocialNav-MoE, and E-SocialNav. These methods typically improve performance through task-specific architectural designs or direct policy optimization, but may not fully exploit the sophisticated social reasoning knowledge embedded in larger VLM teachers.

Knowledge distillation emerges as a method to transfer knowledge from large, capable teacher models to more lightweight student models, allowing the student to retain performance at a lower computational cost. Early distillation methods focused on supervising student models with soft labels or aligning intermediate features. With the advent of large language models (LLMs) and VLMs, distillation has been extended to compress perception, reasoning, and instruction-following capabilities. Many traditional VLM distillation methods operate under an off-policy paradigm, where the student learns from static, offline datasets or fixed teacher-generated outputs. This can lead to a distribution mismatch between training and deployment, potentially causing performance degradation as the student encounters novel states during real-time interaction.

On-Policy Distillation (OPD) attempts to mitigate this mismatch by allowing the student model to generate its own response sequences, with the teacher providing token-level supervision based on the student's generated context. While OPD is suitable for sequence generation and decision-making tasks where models continuously rely on previous outputs, existing OPD methods often face two limitations:
1. They typically employ optimization objectives like Reverse Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence, which can exhibit mode-seeking properties. This encourages the student to fit only the highest-probability action in the teacher distribution, potentially reducing action diversity and leading to mode collapse, especially when teacher distributions are high-entropy or multimodal.
2. They often apply the distillation loss uniformly across all tokens in a sequence. Navigation trajectories frequently contain routine motion states (e.g., moving in an open corridor) where both teacher and student models are highly confident about the appropriate action. The corresponding low-entropy tokens represent deterministic geometric motion rather than complex social interactions. Treating these tokens equally can cause gradient updates to be dominated by abundant routine-motion examples, thereby diluting the emphasis on socially critical behaviors (e.g., yielding, avoiding pedestrian flows).

This work addresses these identified limitations by proposing a selective on-policy distillation method designed to efficiently transfer social reasoning capabilities from large VLMs to lightweight models, specifically by focusing on socially informative decision tokens.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable the deployment of Vision-Language Models for social robot navigation on resource-constrained platforms, while preserving the advanced social reasoning capabilities typically found in larger models. This involves transferring social navigation knowledge from a large teacher VLM to a lightweight student VLM.

The motivation for this work stems from two core challenges:

1. **Deployment Challenge of Large VLMs**: Large-scale VLMs, despite their capacity for rich semantic understanding and complex social reasoning, are computationally intensive. This makes their direct deployment on edge devices common in robotics impractical due to limited processing power, memory, and energy. A lightweight VLM is necessary for efficient real-time operation.
2. **Limitations of Existing Knowledge Distillation Approaches**:
 - **Mode-Seeking in Traditional On-Policy Distillation (OPD)**: Current OPD methods often utilize Reverse Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence as the optimization objective. This divergence metric is known for its mode-seeking property, which can compel the student model to replicate only the most probable action from the teacher's distribution. In the context of social navigation, where situations can be ambiguous and multiple socially acceptable actions might exist, reducing action diversity can hinder the student's ability to adapt to varied social contexts or lead to mode collapse, especially when the teacher's output distribution is high-entropy or multimodal.
 - **Uniform Loss Application in Distillation**: Standard distillation approaches, including traditional OPD, apply the distillation loss uniformly across all tokens generated in a sequence. Many navigation scenarios involve routine or "trivial" actions, such as moving straight through an empty space, where the appropriate action is clear and offers limited social reasoning insight. The corresponding tokens are typically low-entropy for both teacher and student. Focusing equal attention on these low-entropy tokens can dilute the learning signal from truly "socially informative" or "decision-critical" tokens, which relate to complex social interactions like yielding to pedestrians, navigating crowded spaces, or interpreting group dynamics. This uniform treatment can lead to gradient updates being dominated by abundant routine-motion examples, thereby diminishing the relative importance given to crucial social behaviors during knowledge transfer.

To overcome these issues, the authors propose a novel approach that aims to selectively transfer social reasoning knowledge. The specific motivations are to:
- Develop a method that allows a lightweight VLM to learn sophisticated social navigation behaviors from a large VLM.
- Mitigate the mode-seeking problem in on-policy distillation by using a divergence metric that encourages broader distribution alignment rather than just peak fitting.
- Enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of knowledge transfer by selectively focusing the distillation process on socially informative decision tokens, identified by the teacher's uncertainty, thus suppressing gradients from less relevant, low-entropy tokens.
- Encourage the student model to concentrate its learning on socially relevant cues and compliant navigation behaviors, leading to more robust and contextually appropriate decision-making.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, SOPD-SocialNav (Selective On-Policy Distillation for Vision-Language Social Navigation), aims to transfer social reasoning capabilities from a large teacher VLM to a lightweight student VLM. The approach consists of four sequential stages, as illustrated in Fig. 1.

**A. Overview of SOPD Framework**
1. **Teacher Model Preparation**: A large teacher VLM (Qwen3-VL-8B) is initially fine-tuned using Supervised Instruction Tuning (SFT) on social navigation datasets (SNEI and MUSON). After fine-tuning, the teacher model is frozen and remains static throughout the distillation process.
2. **Student Response Generation**: Conditioned on the current visual observation and a task prompt, the lightweight student VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) generates a response sequence autoregressively. Each token in this sequence is produced based on the multimodal input and all previously generated tokens. This process allows the student to operate under its own policy, generating states it is likely to encounter during deployment.
3. **Teacher Evaluation**: The student-generated response sequences are then evaluated by the frozen teacher VLM. The teacher model provides token-level probability distributions for each token in the sequence.
4. **Selective Knowledge Transfer**: A selective distillation mechanism is applied. This mechanism identifies "socially informative decision tokens" based on the teacher's uncertainty and performs knowledge transfer exclusively on these selected tokens. This selective focus is intended to improve the efficiency and relevance of the distillation process.

**B. On-Policy Distillation (OPD)**
The core of SOPD utilizes an on-policy distillation paradigm to address the distribution mismatch between training and inference stages.
- **Student Policy Sampling**: The student model generates a response `y` from its current policy `π_S`: `y ∼ π_S(x)`, where `x` represents the user prompt and visual observation.
- **Temperature-Scaled Distributions**: For each token in the generated response, both the teacher and student models produce logits (`z_t` and `z_s`). To highlight fine-grained relative probabilities, these logits are scaled using a temperature parameter `λ` (set to 2.0) and then passed through a softmax function to obtain softened probability distributions `~P_t` and `~P_s`:
 `~P_t = softmax(z_t / λ)`
 `~P_s = softmax(z_s / λ)`
- **Jensen-Shannon Divergence (JSD) Objective**: Knowledge transfer is achieved by minimizing the Jensen-Shannon Divergence (JSD) between the softened teacher and student distributions. JSD is preferred over Reverse KL divergence due to its symmetry and property of not suffering from the mode-seeking behavior that can reduce action diversity. The JSD is defined as:
 `L_JSD(~P_t || ~P_s) = (1/2) D_KL(~P_t || M) + (1/2) D_KL(~P_s || M)`, where `M = (1/2)(~P_t + ~P_s)` and `D_KL` is the Kullback–Leibler divergence.

**C. Selective Token Distillation**
To focus the distillation on crucial social reasoning, a selective mechanism is introduced:
- **Entropy-based Uncertainty Estimation**: The uncertainty of the teacher's prediction at each decoding step `i` is estimated using Shannon entropy:
 `H_i = Σ_k ~P_t(i) log ~P_t(i)`, where `~P_t(i)` is the teacher's probability distribution for token `i`. High entropy indicates higher uncertainty or multiple plausible options, suggesting a more complex decision-making point relevant to social interaction.
- **Binary Selection Mask**: A binary mask `m_i` is applied to select tokens for distillation. This mask is defined by an indicator function based on an entropy threshold `τ` (set to 1.5):
 `m_i = I(H_i > τ)`
 Only tokens where the teacher's entropy `H_i` exceeds the threshold `τ` are selected for distillation. This filters out low-entropy tokens corresponding to straightforward, routine navigation states, concentrating the learning signal on socially ambiguous or critical situations.
- **Final SOPD Loss**: The selective distillation loss, `L_SOPD`, is computed only for the selected tokens:
 `L_SOPD = Σ_i m_i · L_JSD(~P_i_t || ~P_i_s)`
 This objective guides the student to focus on socially informative navigation decisions, while the teacher provides all token-level supervisory signals.

**D. Experiment Settings**
- **Datasets**: Experiments were conducted on two social navigation benchmarks: SNEI and MUSON. For both, an 8:2 training/test split was used.
- **Models**: Qwen3-VL-8B was used as the large teacher VLM, and Qwen2.5-VL-3B as the lightweight student VLM.
- **Training Parameters**: The student model was trained for 5 epochs with a learning rate of 1 × 10⁻⁵, employing a cosine learning rate scheduler with a warmup ratio of 0.1.
- **Baselines**: The SOPD method was compared against several knowledge distillation baselines:
 - **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)** (student model trained directly on the dataset).
 - **Off-policy Knowledge Distillation**: SKD and VL-KD.
 - **On-policy Knowledge Distillation**: OPD and OPSD.
- **Metrics**: Model performance was evaluated using:
 - **Action Accuracy (Acc)**: Measures the exact match between the predicted action and the ground truth action.
 - **Perception Cosine Similarity (Per-cos)**: Uses a pre-trained Sentence BERT model to compute cosine similarity between the embeddings of generated and reference text for perception.
 - **Reasoning Cosine Similarity (Rea-cos)**: Similar to Per-cos, but for reasoning text.
- **Hardware**: All experiments were conducted using eight NVIDIA A100 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of SOPD-SocialNav, including comparisons with baselines, ablation studies, and real-world deployment, yielded several findings.

**A. Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods (Table I)**
SOPD consistently achieved the highest performance across all evaluation metrics on both the SNEI and MUSON datasets.
- **SNEI Dataset**: SOPD recorded an Action Accuracy (Acc) of 0.889, Perception Cosine Similarity (Per-cos) of 0.901, and Reasoning Cosine Similarity (Rea-cos) of 0.894. These results represent improvements over the strongest baseline, OPSD, by 0.026 (Acc), 0.050 (Per-cos), and 0.022 (Rea-cos), respectively. Compared to the SFT baseline, SOPD showed more substantial gains, particularly increasing Action-Acc from 0.692 to 0.889.
- **MUSON Dataset**: SOPD attained an Action-Acc of 0.953, Per-cos of 0.938, and Rea-cos of 0.955. On this dataset, SOPD improved Action-Acc over SKD by 0.021, Per-cos over SKD by 0.002, and Rea-cos over SKD by 0.021.

These results indicate that transferring knowledge from a stronger teacher model is beneficial, as off-policy distillation methods generally outperformed SFT. Furthermore, on-policy methods (OPD, OPSD) showed advantages over off-policy methods by considering student-generated sequences. SOPD, however, further improved upon these by explicitly distinguishing and focusing on high-uncertainty, decision-critical tokens, leading to more stable and effective knowledge transfer.

**B. Ablation Studies (Table II)**
Ablation studies were conducted to assess the individual contributions of the Jensen–Shannon Divergence (JSD) objective and the entropy-based token filter.
- **Impact of JSD**: When using JSD alone (without the entropy filter), Action-Acc improved from 0.811 (SFT baseline) to 0.893, Per-cos from 0.810 to 0.892, and Rea-cos from 0.803 to 0.884. This indicates that distribution-level alignment provides effective supervision for both action prediction and reasoning consistency.
- **Impact of Entropy Filter**: Using only the entropy filter (without JSD) also improved performance, yielding 0.836 Action-Acc, 0.834 Per-cos, and 0.829 Rea-cos. Although these gains were smaller than with JSD alone, they suggest that prioritizing high-entropy tokens helps reduce the influence of routine, low-entropy motion tokens and places greater emphasis on socially informative decision states.
- **Combined Effect**: The full SOPD model, combining JSD with the entropy filter, achieved the best overall performance, with 0.953 Action-Acc, 0.938 Per-cos, and 0.955 Rea-cos. This combination further improved Action-Acc, Per-cos, and Rea-cos by 0.060, 0.046, and 0.071, respectively, compared to using JSD alone. This demonstrates the complementary nature of the two components: JSD aligns distributions, while entropy-based selection concentrates supervision on informative tokens.

**C. Ablation Studies on Divergence Functions (Table III)**
The study investigated the effect of different divergence functions (Reverse KL, Forward KL, Weighted JSD, and Temperature JSD) in the distillation objective.
- **Performance of Temperature JSD**: Temperature-controlled JSD achieved the highest performance across all metrics (Acc 0.953, Per-cos 0.938, Rea-cos 0.955).
- **Comparison to KL Divergences**: Reverse KL (Acc 0.885) showed lower performance than Temperature JSD, suggesting its mode-seeking property might limit action diversity in social navigation. Forward KL achieved slightly better Per-cos and Rea-cos than Reverse KL but lower Action-Acc (0.853), implying that directly encouraging mode-covering behavior could introduce uncertainty in action prediction.
- **Comparison to Weighted JSD**: Weighted JSD (Acc 0.861) offered a more balanced result than KL-based objectives but still performed below Temperature JSD. This indicates that the temperature control mechanism in JSD plays a role in stabilizing the distillation process and improving knowledge transfer beyond simple JS-based distribution matching.

**D. Real-world Experiments (Fig. 3 & 4)**
The SOPD-distilled model was deployed on a Scout Mini robot to evaluate its real-world performance.
- **Throughput**: The model achieved an inference throughput of 0.984 FPS, with an average end-to-end system latency of 2.74 seconds per sample (0.365 FPS effective throughput), including network communication.
- **Conversational Scenario (Fig. 3)**: In a scenario where two pedestrians were conversing, SOPD generated "move forward left," allowing the robot to bypass the interaction from the side and maintain social distance. In contrast, SKD and OPSD suggested "move forward," which was geometrically feasible but socially less appropriate, potentially interrupting the conversation.
- **Queuing Scenario (Fig. 4)**: When faced with pedestrians queuing, SOPD predicted "move forward left," enabling the robot to bypass the queue from the side. SKD and OPSD both predicted "move forward," which could lead to the robot approaching too closely and violating social norms by intruding on queue space.

These real-world results suggest that the SOPD-distilled model can recognize socially sensitive situations and generate navigation actions that align with human-aware navigation conventions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of social robotics and VLM deployment by addressing the practical challenges of integrating advanced AI capabilities into resource-constrained robotic platforms. The significance and potential impact of SOPD-SocialNav can be summarized as follows:

- **Enabling Lightweight Social Navigation**: The core impact is the provision of a methodology for deploying Vision-Language Models with sophisticated social reasoning capabilities on computationally limited robotic systems. This bridges the gap between the advanced understanding of large VLMs and the practical operational requirements of mobile robots, facilitating their integration into human-centric environments.
- **Enhancing Socially Compliant Behavior**: By effectively transferring social navigation knowledge, SOPD allows lightweight robots to generate more human-aware and socially appropriate navigation behaviors. The demonstrated improvements in conversational and queuing scenarios illustrate the potential for robots to navigate alongside humans in a manner that reduces discomfort and respects social norms, which is crucial for public acceptance and successful deployment of robots.
- **Advancing Knowledge Distillation Techniques**: The proposed selective on-policy distillation method introduces methodological innovations. The use of teacher uncertainty (entropy) for selective token distillation is a novel approach to focus learning on "socially informative decision tokens," rather than diluting gradients with routine motion commands. This targeted approach improves the efficiency and effectiveness of knowledge transfer, particularly for complex, context-dependent tasks like social navigation.
- **Mitigating Distillation Limitations**: The research effectively addresses two known limitations in current distillation paradigms. First, by adopting a temperature-controlled Jensen-Shannon Divergence objective, it counteracts the mode-seeking property of Reverse KL divergence, promoting greater action diversity and robustness in the student's policy. Second, the entropy-based token filtering mechanism prevents gradient contributions from trivial states, thereby concentrating the learning process on critical social interactions.
- **Systematic Evaluation and Benchmarking**: The systematic comparison of SOPD against various off-policy and on-policy distillation baselines on established benchmarks (SNEI and MUSON) provides a comprehensive understanding of its performance advantages. The ablation studies further elucidate the individual contributions and synergy of its components (JSD and entropy filter), offering insights for future research in distillation.
- **Practical Applicability**: The successful real-world deployment on a Scout Mini robot, demonstrating socially appropriate behaviors in complex scenarios, confirms the practical feasibility and readiness of the distilled model. This validates the approach beyond theoretical benchmarks, indicating its potential for broader application in areas such as service robotics, logistics, and assistive robotics where human-robot interaction is paramount.

In future work, the authors plan to explore adaptive entropy threshold selection, extend the method to longer-horizon navigation tasks, and evaluate it in more diverse human-robot interaction scenarios. This indicates a foundation for continued research and development toward more sophisticated and contextually aware robotic systems.
