# Learning Without Losing Identity: Capability Evolution for Embodied Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces a capability-centric framework for embodied agents, enabling continuous skill acquisition through modular, versioned Embodied Capability Modules (ECMs) while maintaining a stable core agent identity. The approach achieved a task success rate of 91.3% with zero policy drift, outperforming agent-modification baselines in stability and performance on simulated manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Embodied agents require continuous capability acquisition in dynamic environments, but direct modification of the agent's core for improvement often leads to instability and loss of identity.
- The tension between the need for continuous performance improvement and the imperative for system stability and reproducibility creates challenges for long-lived embodied systems.
- Existing methods for modular skill learning, continual learning, or safe reinforcement learning do not fully integrate continuous capability evolution with agent identity preservation under explicit runtime safety constraints.

## Method

- Proposes a ==capability-centric evolution paradigm== where a persistent agent maintains a stable identity while its functional capabilities evolve continuously through **Embodied Capability Modules (ECMs)**.
- Introduces ==ECMs== as modular, versioned, self-contained units of embodied functionality, managed through a ==four-stage lifecycle==: creation, deployment, evolution, and deprecation.
- Integrates a ==Runtime Governance layer== that mediates all agent-environment interactions, enforcing predefined safety and policy constraints, managing execution, and performing ==gated deployment== for new ECM versions.

## Results

- Achieved a sustained task success rate increase from an initial **32.4%** to **91.3%** over 20 evolution iterations in simulated manipulation tasks.
- Maintained **zero policy drift** (∆π=0.00) and the lowest performance variance (σ²=2.0), outperforming agent-modification baselines by **6.6** percentage points in success rate with significantly greater stability.
- The runtime governance layer successfully blocked **100%** of attempted unsafe actions with negligible computational overhead (**2.3 ms** per decision) in experiments designed to incentivize unsafe behaviors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the agent's persistent identity from its evolving capabilities allows for continuous performance improvement without sacrificing system stability or predictability.
> - Modular, versioned capability management through ECMs offers a robust and auditable framework for skill acquisition, akin to software engineering principles for learned behaviors.
> - A dedicated runtime governance layer can effectively enforce hard safety and policy constraints, enabling aggressive capability optimization without compromising system integrity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied agents are designed to operate persistently in physical environments, necessitating continuous learning and adaptation to dynamic conditions. A central challenge in this domain is enabling agents to acquire new capabilities over time without compromising their stable behavior, consistent control, or fundamental identity.

Current approaches to improving agent performance often involve direct modification of the agent itself, such as through prompt engineering, policy updates, or structural redesign. While these methods can achieve short-term performance gains, they frequently lead to system instability, reduced reproducibility, and a loss of the agent's distinct identity across iterations. This issue is particularly pronounced in embodied systems, where inconsistencies in decision-making can result in unsafe or irreversible physical outcomes in the real world. Consequently, there is an inherent tension between the desire for continuous improvement and the requirement for system stability.

The existing research landscape addresses related aspects across several subfields:
- **Modular Skill Learning:** Approaches like hierarchical reinforcement learning, option-critic, and learned skill libraries (e.g., SPiRL, SkiMo, Voyager) focus on decomposing complex tasks into reusable sub-policies. However, many skill libraries remain static or append-only, and their evolution is typically not explicitly decoupled from the core agent's identity. LLM-based systems often compose or invoke modular capabilities at inference time without a formalized evolution framework for the capabilities themselves.
- **Continual Learning:** This area largely focuses on mitigating catastrophic forgetting at the parameter level in neural networks, primarily within supervised learning contexts. While some methods, like PathNet, explore evolutionary path selection in modular networks, most research does not specifically address forgetting in the context of embodied control policies or the architectural preservation of agent identity.
- **Safe Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Methods in safe RL incorporate constraints into the learning process or use shielding mechanisms to ensure safety during deployment. However, these often assume a fixed policy that is being protected, rather than addressing safety within a framework of continuously evolving capabilities.
- **LLM-based Autonomous Agents:** Systems such as ReAct, Reflexion, and Toolformer integrate large language models to enable agents to reason and act, often improving performance through modifications to prompts, memory, or tool invocation. These modifications, however, can alter the agent's effective policy across iterations, similar to the agent-modification problem observed in other approaches.

The current work distinguishes itself by integrating these disparate concerns. It proposes a novel paradigm that formally separates agent identity from capability evolution, introduces explicit lifecycle management and versioning for capabilities, and incorporates a runtime governance layer to ensure safety under continuous learning. This integration aims to resolve the tension between continuous improvement and system stability that current agent-centric or skill-learning approaches have not fully addressed.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the challenges faced by embodied agents operating in dynamic physical environments over extended periods. Such agents are expected to continuously acquire new capabilities to adapt and improve, yet simultaneously maintain stable behavior, consistent control, and a persistent cognitive identity. The paper identifies a fundamental tension: current methods that directly modify the agent (e.g., through prompt engineering, policy updates, or structural redesign) achieve improvement at the cost of altering the agent's core identity, leading to instability, reduced reproducibility, and potential physical hazards in real-world scenarios.

To address this, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **Propose a Capability-Centric Evolution Paradigm:** Shift the focus of intelligence growth from the agent itself to its capabilities. This involves maintaining a persistent agent as a stable cognitive identity while allowing its capabilities to evolve independently.
2. **Introduce Embodied Capability Modules (ECMs):** Define ECMs as modular, versioned, and self-contained units of embodied functionality. These modules are designed to be learned, refined, and composed over time, embodying the evolutionary aspect of the system.
3. **Design a Closed-Loop Capability Evolution Framework:** Develop a system that iteratively executes tasks, collects experience, updates the parameters of capability modules, and reintegrates these updated modules for future execution. This closed-loop process facilitates continuous learning and improvement.
4. **Incorporate a Runtime Governance Layer:** Establish a runtime layer that mediates all interactions between the agent and the environment. This layer is responsible for enforcing safety and policy constraints, ensuring that capability evolution and deployment occur within defined boundaries and without compromising system stability or control.
5. **Empirically Demonstrate Performance Improvement and Stability:** Validate the proposed framework through experimental evaluation on embodied manipulation tasks, demonstrating that it can achieve sustained performance improvement, outperform direct agent modification baselines on complex tasks, preserve agent identity, and ensure safe execution.

The overarching goal is to provide a scalable and safe architectural foundation for the long-term deployment of intelligent embodied agents in dynamic environments, ensuring continuous adaptation without sacrificing the integrity or safety of the system.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The methodology centers on a capability-centric evolution paradigm designed to enable continuous improvement in embodied agents while preserving a stable cognitive identity and ensuring runtime safety. This is achieved through the definition of a persistent agent, Embodied Capability Modules (ECMs), a closed-loop evolution process, and a runtime governance layer.

**A. Formal Framework**

1. **Persistent Agent (A):**
 - Defined as a tuple A = (π_A, M_id_A, M_ep_A).
 - **π_A (Decision Policy):** Responsible for planning and task decomposition; remains fixed.
 - **M_id_A (Identity Memory):** Stores long-term beliefs, goals, and identity parameters; remains fixed throughout the evolution process, ensuring identity invariance.
 - **M_ep_A (Episodic Memory):** Accumulates episodic records, such as execution traces and past interactions; this is the only part of the agent that may change over time, analogous to acquiring new experiences without altering core personality.

2. **Embodied Capability Modules (ECMs):**
 - These are the units of embodied functionality that evolve. An ECM at version *k* is defined as c^(k) = (θ^(k), I_c, O_c, ϕ_c).
 - **θ^(k) (Learnable Parameters):** These can include neural network weights, executable code, or LLM prompts, representing the specific learned behaviors of the module.
 - **I_c, O_c (Input/Output Interfaces):** Define the data contract for the module.
 - **ϕ_c (Capability Descriptor):** A natural-language specification of what the module does and its applicability conditions.
 - **Capability Set C(t):** At any time *t*, the system maintains a set of all active ECMs, each at its latest version.

3. **State, Decision, and Execution:**
 - The agent's decision policy π_A selects an action (or sub-plan) a_t based on the current world state s_t, the current capability set C(t), and its memory M_A.
 - Crucially, a_t is not executed directly. Instead, it is dispatched through a **Runtime Layer (R)**, which can modify or reject the action based on a set of safety and policy constraints Φ, yielding the executed action ˆa_t.

4. **Capability Evolution:**
 - The capability set evolves through an `UPDATE` function: C(t+1) = `UPDATE`(C(t), D_t), where D_t is the collected experience at time *t*.
 - The `UPDATE` function *only* operates on the learnable parameters θ^(k) of ECMs, leaving the persistent agent A untouched.
 - **Identity Invariance Property:** Formally states that (π_A, M_id_A) remains constant for all *t*.
 - **Capability-Driven Improvement Property:** Implies that any improvement in system performance must be attributed to the evolution of C(t) if the agent identity is fixed.

5. **Capability Composition:**
 - Complex tasks are achieved by composing multiple ECMs (c_comp = c_i ◦ c_j). The agent's planner π_A selects and orders these compositions, while the runtime layer verifies interface compatibility and enforces constraints.

**B. Core Mechanisms**

1. **ECM Lifecycle:**
 - **Creation:** New ECMs can be manually specified, generated via LLM-based code synthesis from natural language, or cloned from existing modules. Each receives a unique ID and initial version (k=0).
 - **Deployment:** Created ECMs are added to the active capability set. The runtime validates interfaces before execution. All invocations are logged.
 - **Evolution:** As an ECM gathers experience (D_t), a learning subsystem produces an updated version c^(k+1). The previous version c^(k) is retained in a version registry for potential rollback.
 - **Deprecation:** An ECM is deprecated if a strictly superior version exists or its capability is no longer required. Deprecated ECMs remain in the registry for auditing.
 - **Distinction from Microservices:** ECMs encapsulate learned stochastic policies, requiring statistical evaluation for version gating, accounting for distributional shift, and operating in continuous action spaces with safety constraints, unlike deterministic microservices.

2. **Runtime Governance (R):**
 - **Policy Enforcement:** Before any ECM execution, R checks the proposed action a_t against fixed safety and policy constraints Φ. It allows a_t if safe, modifies it if a safe alternative exists (e.g., clipping values), or rejects it entirely. Constraints include workspace boundaries, force limits, and velocity caps.
 - **Execution Management:** R orchestrates ECM invocation, including input handling, timeout enforcement, and exception handling. It can trigger recovery strategies upon failure.
 - **Trace and Logging:** Every execution generates a structured trace record, which forms the raw data for the learning subsystem.
 - **Version Gating:** When a new ECM version is generated, R evaluates it on a held-out set of task instances. The new version replaces the current one only if it meets a minimum performance threshold, preventing regressions.

3. **Capability Learning (UPDATE Function):**
 - The learning subsystem updates ECM parameters θ^(k) using three modalities, chosen based on the ECM's representation:
 - **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** For differentiable parameters (e.g., neural networks), policy gradient methods (e.g., SAC) are used with a reward function that balances success, execution time, and retries.
 - **Imitation Learning (IL):** Updates parameters via behavioral cloning when expert demonstrations are available.
 - **LLM-based Code Synthesis:** For code-based ECMs, a language model analyzes failure traces and proposes code modifications.
 - **Hybrid Updates:** Combinations of these modalities are supported, for instance, an LLM proposing structural changes followed by RL fine-tuning.

**C. Evaluation Hypotheses**
The evaluation is structured around three hypotheses:
- **H1:** Capability evolution enables sustained performance improvement over time.
- **H2:** Separating identity from capability evolution outperforms direct agent modification in both mean and variance.
- **H3:** Runtime governance ensures safe execution even when evolving capabilities attempt unsafe actions.

### 4. Main findings and results

The framework was evaluated on six manipulation tasks of increasing complexity within the MuJoCo/robosuite simulation environment, using Franka Panda 7-DoF arm(s). The tasks ranged from a 2-step single-arm pick (T1) to an 8-step bimanual peg-in-hole assembly (T6). Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) was used for RL, with 20 evolution iterations per task. Stochastic perturbations were applied to object positions, observations, and actuation. Baselines included Agent Modification (AM), Static ECM (S-ECM), SPiRL, and SkiMo.

**A. Capability Evolution Over Time (H1: Sustained Performance Improvement)**

- **Reward Trajectories:** Across all six tasks, the proposed capability evolution method demonstrated monotonic improvement in average reward over 20 iterations (Figure 2, Table III).
- **Reward Ratios:** Peak-over-first reward ratios ranged from 3.1× to 8.4× for most tasks, with T3 (Stack) showing a significant increase from 3.13 to 21.74. T6 (TwoArmPegInHole), while having a lower ratio (1.1×) due to a high initial reward baseline, still exhibited an absolute reward gain of 30 points.
- **Success Rate:** Binary success (based on robosuite's strict `_check_success` criterion) was achieved only on T1 (100%) and T6 (60% peak). For other tasks (T2-T5), success rates remained at 0% due to the strictness of the success criteria, though reward signals still indicated learning progress.
- **Conclusion for H1:** The results confirm that the capability-centric evolution paradigm enables sustained performance improvement through the continuous refinement of ECMs.

**B. Method Comparison on Composite Tasks (H2: Superiority over Agent Modification)**

- **T6 (TwoArmPegInHole):** This 8-step bimanual task served as a flagship evaluation.
 - **Ours (Capability Evolution):** Achieved a mean peak success rate of **63.3 ± 27.6%** across four random seeds (individual seed results: 86.7%, 73.3%, 23.3%, 70.0%).
 - **Agent Modification (AM):** Achieved a mean peak success rate of only **10.8 ± 8.8%** across the same four seeds (individual seed results: 3.3%, 20.0%, 16.7%, 3.3%).
 - **Statistical Significance:** A Welch’s t-test comparing Ours and AM on T6 peak success yielded t=3.62, p=0.027, indicating a statistically significant difference. The mean gap was approximately sixfold.
 - **Other Baselines:** Static ECM, SPiRL, and SkiMo performed at or below 6.7% success on this task (seed 42 reference run).
- **Shorter Tasks (T1-T5):**
 - T1 (Pick) saturated at 100% success for all methods, showing no discriminative power.
 - On T2-T5, all methods yielded 0% binary success due to the strictness of robosuite's `_check_success` function. While AM and SkiMo sometimes achieved higher peak rewards on these tasks (Table V), this often came at the cost of significant agent-policy drift.
- **Conclusion for H2:** The proposed method demonstrated a substantial performance advantage over baselines, particularly on complex, long-horizon, composite tasks, where the stability provided by a fixed agent identity appears to be critical.

**C. Identity Preservation**

- **Agent-Policy Drift (∆π):** This metric quantified the normalized ℓ2 drift of the agent’s decision-policy parameters between the first and last iteration, with ∆π=0 indicating perfect identity preservation.
 - **Ours, SPiRL, Static ECM:** Exhibited ∆π = 0 across all six tasks, as expected due to their architectural design (fixed agent decision policy).
 - **Agent Modification:** Showed significant drift with a mean ∆π = 1.28 across the six tasks.
 - **SkiMo:** Also exhibited substantial drift with a mean ∆π = 1.38.
- **Conclusion:** The framework successfully maintains a fixed agent identity, contrasting with agent-mutating baselines which undergo considerable policy drift. This supports the architectural design choice and highlights a key benefit.

**D. Runtime-Constrained Safety (H3: Effective Governance)**

- **Blocking Rate:** The runtime governance layer actively intervened, blocking 100% of actions that would have violated safety constraints on tasks T1-T5, and 61% on T6 (Table VII).
- **Unsafe Execution Rate:** With governance enabled, the unsafe execution rate was **0.0%** across all six tasks. Without governance, 80% of T1 episodes contained at least one safety violation, indicating aggressive exploration in early learning stages.
- **Overhead:** The per-action overhead for the governance layer was measured between 0.02-0.03 ms, confirming its efficiency.
- **Conclusion for H3:** The runtime governance layer effectively eliminates unsafe executions with minimal computational overhead, providing a robust safety envelope independent of capability evolution.

**E. Learning Modality Ablation**

- An ablation study on T3 (Stack) evaluated the contribution of Reinforcement Learning (RL), Imitation Learning (IL), and LLM-based code synthesis.
- **RL Necessity:** RL was found to be necessary for performance, with RL-only achieving R_peak=16.55 and 3.3% success. IL-only and LLM-only configurations were largely ineffective, remaining at 0% success.
- **Complementarity:** Combining modalities showed complementary benefits. RL+IL achieved the highest peak reward (R_peak=22.80), and the full three-modality configuration (Ours) yielded strong final reward (R_final=9.16) with reduced variance compared to RL-only.
- **Conclusion:** While RL is foundational, IL and LLM-based synthesis contribute to boosting peak reward and stabilizing performance, demonstrating the value of a multi-modal learning subsystem.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The research presented in this paper offers a significant contribution to the field of embodied artificial intelligence by proposing a robust framework that addresses critical challenges in developing long-lived, adaptable, and safe robotic systems.

**Primary Significance:**

- **Resolution of Fundamental Tension:** The work successfully resolves the inherent tension between continuous performance improvement and system stability in embodied agents. By architecturally decoupling the persistent cognitive identity of an agent from the evolution of its capabilities, the framework enables ongoing learning without compromising the agent's core stability, control, or reproducibility.
- **Enhanced Performance on Complex Tasks:** The empirical results demonstrate that this separation yields substantial performance advantages, particularly for long-horizon, composite tasks. The significant outperformance over agent-modification baselines on the bimanual peg-in-hole assembly (T6) highlights the framework's scalability to complex real-world challenges where monolithic policies often struggle due to identity drift.
- **Guaranteed Safety:** The integration of a runtime governance layer provides a critical safety mechanism. This layer, operating independently of the evolving capabilities, enforces pre-defined constraints at every action execution, eliminating unsafe behaviors with minimal computational overhead. This is paramount for the deployment of robots in physical environments where safety violations can have severe consequences.
- **Modular and Versioned Capability Management:** The introduction of Embodied Capability Modules (ECMs) with a formalized lifecycle (creation, deployment, evolution, deprecation, rollback support) introduces software engineering best practices into embodied AI. This modularity facilitates easier development, maintenance, auditing, and debugging of individual skills, contributing to more reliable and manageable systems.
- **Identity Preservation:** The explicit commitment to a fixed agent identity, verified by zero agent-policy drift, ensures that the agent's core decision-making structure remains consistent. This contributes to predictability and trust in autonomous systems, which is crucial for long-term human-robot interaction.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Long-term Robotic Autonomy:** The framework provides a foundational architecture for developing truly persistent and adaptable robotic systems that can operate continuously over extended periods in dynamic environments, learning and improving their skills incrementally without requiring frequent re-initialization or extensive retraining of the entire system.
- **Safer AI Deployment:** By integrating hard safety constraints at the execution layer, the work paves the way for safer deployment of learning-based embodied AI in sensitive applications, such as industrial automation, healthcare, or personal assistance robotics, where safety is non-negotiable.
- **New Design Principles for Embodied AI:** The concept of decoupling identity from capability evolution offers a new paradigm for designing intelligent agents. It suggests that complex AI systems can benefit from stable, invariant core components that orchestrate and manage a dynamic, evolving set of functional modules.
- **Advanced Human-Robot Collaboration:** Stable and predictable agent behavior, enabled by identity preservation, can foster greater trust and more effective collaboration between humans and robots.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper identifies several promising future directions, including comparisons with parameter-level continual learning, standardized benchmarks for capability evolution, formal interface contracts for robust composition across skill versions, and extending the framework to multi-agent systems with shared, asynchronously evolving skill registries. These areas build directly on the established principles of this work.

In conclusion, this research presents a principled and empirically validated approach to building resilient and continuously improving embodied agents. By formalizing the separation of agent identity and capability evolution under robust runtime governance, it lays a significant groundwork for the development of the next generation of intelligent robotic systems.
