# Representation World Model: Learning States, Transition and Executable Plans in Representation

> [!summary] Summary
> Representation World Model (RWM) learns states, transitions, and executable plans inside one latent space. Instead of an explicit forward dynamics model plus inference-time search, it linearly interpolates between encoded start and goal representations and trains a shared inverse dynamics model locally along that constructed path. Planning becomes encode-construct-decode. RWM reaches 98.25% macro success on four continuous-control tasks and 93.0% on LIBERO-Goal.

## Problem

- Latent world models avoid prediction collapse with fixed representation priors such as SIGReg, imposing assumptions on the latent space that may restrict scaling with data and tasks.
- Proxy forward-prediction objectives preserve whatever helps predict observations, including state factors irrelevant to action, and never require the latent geometry to organize around task-relevant transitions.
- Search-based planners evaluate hundreds of candidate rollouts at inference, while goal-conditioned policies skip search but leave the division between representation and planning underdetermined under end-to-end training.

## Method

- ==Latent path construction==: intermediate representations are linear interpolations of encoded segment endpoints, weighted by relative temporal position, exposing previously unconstrained regions between observed states to supervision.
- ==Interpolation inverse-dynamics loss==: a shared three-layer-MLP ==inverse dynamics model== regresses ground-truth actions from neighboring constructed representations, forcing both endpoints and the path between them into an actionable geometry.
- Auxiliary terms and rollout-free inference: encoded-pair inverse dynamics plus a soft ==interpolation-consistency loss== supplement training, no forward dynamics model is learned, and a delta predictor substitutes for the unavailable goal latent on LIBERO.

## Results

- Macro success **98.25%** across PushT, Cube, Reacher and TwoRoom, above the strongest INTACT variant at **96.86%** that scores 384 candidate trajectories, and goal-conditioned IDM at **95.33%**.
- LIBERO-Goal average **93.0%** with a 0.03B model versus **71.2%** for LeWM and **81.2%** for RC-aux, with a 7B OpenVLA-OFT external reference at **97.0%**.
- Ablation: removing the interpolation inverse-dynamics loss drops PushT from **96.00%** to **39.37%**, and replanning every 5 steps gives **98.33%** macro versus **69.17%** at 25 steps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Action-level supervision can replace explicit latent regularizers: the inverse-dynamics task itself rules out collapsed features, so no distributional prior on the representation is needed.
> - Fixing the planner to a simple geometric rule pushes the learning signal into the encoder, resolving the usual representation-versus-planner ambiguity in end-to-end training.
> - Search-free plans still need feedback: probed information degrades off the observed manifold and open-loop execution decays sharply, so frequent re-encoding stays load-bearing.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

An agent in goal-conditioned control sees where it is and where it should go, and it must choose actions to get there. Conventional latent world models can predict what actions might do, but they typically plan by generating and searching through candidate action sequences. The Representation World Model (RWM) makes the path between the current and goal representations itself actionable. It constructs intermediate latent states and learns to decode each local transition into an action. The authors frame the practical bottleneck as the online search needed to turn a learned model into a goal-directed action sequence. The reported continuous-control results are strong.

### 2. A path that can act

**Training.** RWM encodes two observations from the same action-labeled trajectory. It then constructs evenly spaced intermediate representations between their endpoint codes.

Interpolation alone does not establish a valid physical transition. To supply the missing link, RWM trains a shared inverse-dynamics model (IDM), which predicts the action from a pair of neighboring representations. The IDM learns to recover the demonstrated actions along those constructed intermediate pairs. The intermediate points depend on the encoded endpoints, so this action supervision also shapes the endpoint representations and the geometry between them.

**Inference.** In the standard setting, the goal observation is available. RWM encodes the current and goal observations, constructs intermediate codes directly between them, and applies the IDM to each neighboring pair to obtain actions. Those actions can be decoded in parallel for open-loop execution. For closed-loop control, RWM re-encodes after an action chunk and reconstructs the path toward the goal. This path-construction procedure requires no recursive forward-dynamics rollout and no action-space search. This makes it "search-free," but the paper does not provide a comparable wall-clock measurement for RWM, so it does not establish faster elapsed inference or lower total computation.

### 3. Does path supervision matter?

**The objective ablation.** The central test comes from removing parts of the training objective on PushT. The full objective reaches 96.00% success. Success drops to 39.37% when interpolation IDM is removed and only the auxiliary encoded-state IDM and consistency terms remain. All tested variants that retain the interpolation IDM term score between 95.23% and 96.17%. These ablations use training seed 3072 and are specific to PushT. They support the authors' interpretation that action grounding along constructed paths is the primary learning signal for executable paths. They do not show that this term is the only cause of performance.

**The cross-task comparison.** [[2603.19312|LeWorldModel]] (LeWM) learns action-conditioned latent forward dynamics and uses CEM search over rollouts to choose actions. [[2607.26056|INTACT]]'s goal-conditioned IDM takes a more direct approach: it maps current and goal representations to actions. RWM instead constructs intermediate representations and decodes neighboring transitions, which makes the INTACT comparison especially relevant.

Across PushT, Cube, Reacher, and TwoRoom, RWM reports a **98.25% macro success rate**, compared with **95.33%** for INTACT's goal-conditioned IDM row. Macro success rate is the unweighted mean of the four task success rates. RWM's task rates are 95.44% on PushT, 100.00% on Cube, 97.89% on Reacher, and 99.67% on TwoRoom.

The protocol reports three training seeds and averages the mean and standard deviation across those seeds. The table labels its published rows as external references, not paired controls. This is a reported comparison, not a paired causal test. It supports strong search-free performance but does not attribute that performance to the mechanism alone.

### 4. Plans, not rollouts

The ablation connects the mechanism to these results. The path is not executable merely because it is geometrically interpolated. It becomes useful when action supervision trains the representation and its intermediate regions to support local action recovery. The authors' conclusion emphasizes learning planning structure within the representation and decoding the constructed path at inference.

The main boundary is the goal observation. Endpoint interpolation is the standard setting when the goal observation is available. LIBERO-Goal is a separate, goal-unobserved setting that adds a latent-delta predictor. Its results are evidence of an adaptation to manipulation, not proof that the standard endpoint-path procedure works unchanged without a goal.
