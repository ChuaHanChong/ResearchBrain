# When to Trust Imagination: Adaptive Action Execution for World Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A new framework, FFDC-WAM, enables World Action Models to adaptively execute actions by continuously verifying their predicted future observations against real-world sensory input. This approach improves robotic task success rates on challenging manipulation tasks while reducing computational overhead for simpler, predictable actions, mimicking human-like adaptive planning.

## Problem

- World Action Models (WAMs) traditionally execute fixed-size action chunks, leading to computational inefficiencies in predictable scenarios and accumulating errors in uncertain environments.
- This fixed-chunk approach does not dynamically account for the varying reliability of WAM predictions across different task phases.
- Prior adaptive execution methods for other policies lack an explicit mechanism to compare the policy's imagined future observations with real-world sensor data.

## Method

- A framework, **FFDC-WAM**, is proposed, combining a **WAM** backbone for ==macro planning== with a lightweight, high-frequency ==verifier== for online decision-making.
- The ==Future Forward Dynamics Causal Attention== (**FFDC**) verifier assesses the trustworthiness of the **WAM**'s predicted action segment by comparing predicted future visual tokens with real-world observations.
- The system adaptively adjusts the effective action chunk size, continuing execution when **FFDC** confidence is high and triggering replanning when significant deviations are detected.

## Results

- In simulated RoboTwin environments, FFDC-WAM improved success rates on 'hard' tasks (e.g., from **54.20%** to **76.40%** on Rand.hard) while reducing WAM inference calls by **69.10%** on average.
- Real-world experiments with an Astribot S1 robot showed FFDC-WAM increased average success rates from **45%** (baseline) to **80%** on pick-and-place tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that predicted visual tokens were the most critical input for the FFDC verifier, as their removal led to the largest drop in success rate (from **76.4%** to **71.6%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human-like adaptive execution, involving continuous verification of internal predictions against external reality, is crucial for robust physical interaction.
> - A WAM's unique ability to predict future visual observations can be explicitly leveraged to dynamically assess the trustworthiness of its imagined future during physical execution.
> - Adaptive action chunking, based on this future-reality verification, achieves a better trade-off between computational efficiency and robustness in robotic control.
