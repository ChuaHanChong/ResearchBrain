# Beyond Data Scaling: Representation-Centric Continued Pre-training for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> VLAct is a representation-centric continued pre-training recipe for vision-language-action backbones: starting from Qwen3-VL, it freezes the vision encoder and shallow LLM layers, mixes caption data, co-supervises one shared backbone with three continuous action heads, and shares physically aligned action dimensions across embodiments. Downstream users attach fresh heads. It reaches 82.6% on LIBERO-Plus and 92.5% on RoboTwin 2.0 using open data and 16 GPUs.

## Problem

- Robot trajectories cannot be web-scraped and sparsely cover a combinatorial physical interaction space, so pure data scaling cannot deliver the coverage that language and vision pre-training enjoy.
- Naive end-to-end action fitting on narrow robot data erodes the broad vision-language prior inherited from the pretrained VLM, degrading exactly the features that make backbones transferable.
- Single-head pre-training collapses backbone geometry toward one decoder, and embodiment-specific output heads hide shared action semantics such as gripper open/close behind isolated projections.

## Method

- ==Shallow-layer protection== plus ==caption-mixed pre-training==: freeze the vision encoder and lower half of LLM layers, mix ==dense captioning supervision==, then unfreeze fully at downstream fine-tuning.
- ==Multi-head continuous co-supervision==: ==OFT==, ==PI==, and ==GR00T== heads attached to one shared latent, summed losses on identical ==action chunks==, forcing head-agnostic action features at negligible extra backbone compute.
- ==Partially unified cross-embodiment action layout==: gripper dimensions shared across robots, incompatible arm dimensions ==masked per embodiment==, plus a ==wrap-aware modulo-360 loss== on periodic absolute joint angles.

## Results

- LIBERO-Plus total **82.6%**, beating the identical-backbone Qwen3VL-OFT baseline by **7.6 points** and the industrial Abot-M0 system by **2.1 points**, with largest gains on camera and layout perturbations.
- RoboTwin 2.0 data-scaling Clean **92.5%** (OFT head), **93.0%** with PI head; all three heads fall within **3.4 points**, and the base 50-trajectory setting reaches **80.5%** Clean.
- On held-out humanoid RoboCasa-GR1, **20%** of downstream data gives **49.5%**, exceeding full-data GR00T-N1.6 at **47.6%**; RoboDojo score **10.66** / **7.60%** success beats every designated world-action-model entry.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The pretrained VLM backbone is a first-order design variable, not a fixed inherited component: with heads, data, and optimizer held constant, backbone choice alone moved results 7.6-21.4 points.
> - Strong same-head fine-tuning overstates backbone reusability; head diversity used purely as supervision is a cheap regularizer that also improves matched-head performance, not just transfer.
> - Share supervision only where action semantics are physically comparable; naive full padding-based unification and fully separate embodiment heads are both worse than partial alignment with masking.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models is advancing towards the development of generalist robot policies capable of performing a wide range of tasks across diverse environments and embodiments. This progression is largely influenced by the success of large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs), which benefit from vast web-scale data. In robotics, a similar data scaling paradigm has been pursued, with researchers aiming to collect increasingly large datasets of robot trajectories.

However, robot learning faces a unique challenge in data scaling. Unlike web-scale image-text data, robot trajectories require embodied collection in the physical world, often involving human teleoperation or specialized protocols. The space of physical interactions is combinatorial and continuous, leading to robot datasets that are inherently sparse and provide uneven coverage of possible situations. This sparsity implies that simply accumulating more data may not be sufficient to achieve broad generalization.

Current VLA systems, such as the $\pi$ series, GR00T, and ABot-M0, often involve continued pre-training of foundation VLMs on robot data before task-specific fine-tuning. This work positions itself within this landscape by focusing on the "representation quality" aspect of VLA model development. It argues that under a fixed robot-data budget, the efficacy of VLA continued pre-training depends not just on the quantity of trajectories but on how effectively these trajectories are converted into transferable visual-action knowledge within the model's backbone. The paper aims to address this bottleneck by proposing a representation-centric approach to continued pre-training, rather than solely relying on data scaling or mere action fitting. It also contributes to the open-source community by releasing models and training pipelines, which is a stated goal for improving reproducibility and accessibility in robot learning, an area where many frontier models often rely on closed-source data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a VLA-oriented VLM backbone that can learn strong, transferable visual-action representations from limited and heterogeneous robot data. This goal is driven by the recognition that robot data scaling faces fundamental limitations compared to web-scale data. The work aims to move "beyond data scaling" by emphasizing the quality of the learned representations.

The specific motivations for VLAct's design stem from identified failure modes in conventional VLA continued pre-training:

1. **Erosion of Broad VLM Priors:** Robot trajectories are often visually narrower than the web-scale corpora used to train initial VLMs. Naive end-to-end continued pre-training on this narrower data can degrade the general vision-language features that make the VLM backbone broadly useful, leading to over-specialization to the pre-training scenarios.
2. **Over-specialization to a Single Action Head:** Different robot tasks and embodiments may necessitate different action heads (e.g., discrete token-based, continuous regression, flow-matching, diffusion-style). If continued pre-training uses only a single action head, the backbone may encode action-relevant information in a form specialized to that head's decoding geometry, limiting its transferability to alternative downstream heads.
3. **Weak Cross-Embodiment Sharing:** Embodiment-specific output spaces can hinder the generalization of action semantics across different robot platforms. Physically comparable actions (e.g., gripper open/close) may be represented through isolated, robot-specific heads, preventing the model from learning shared underlying action structures.

To counteract these issues, the paper's objectives include:
- Preserving the broad VLM prior knowledge while injecting action-specific knowledge.
- Enabling the VLA backbone to support diverse downstream action heads without suffering from "decoder lock-in."
- Encouraging shared action semantics across heterogeneous robot embodiments.

Ultimately, the goal is to enhance the transferability and data efficiency of VLA models, making them more adaptable to new tasks, scenes, and unseen robot platforms under modest computational budgets.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of VLAct is a representation-centric continued pre-training recipe applied to a pre-trained VLM backbone, specifically Qwen3-VL-4B in this study. The methodology consists of three main components: VLM prior preservation, action representation alignment and diversification, and cross-embodiment action representation unification. These components are applied during continued pre-training, with downstream task-specific fine-tuning using a freshly initialized action head.

#### 3.1. VLM Prior Preservation

To prevent the narrow robot data from eroding the general vision-language capabilities of the initial VLM, VLAct employs two mechanisms:

- **Shallow-Layer Protection:** The entire vision encoder and the lower half of the LLM layers are frozen during VLA continued pre-training. This strategy aims to protect the fundamental low-level visual processing and early vision-language alignment features. The upper LLM layers and action heads remain trainable, allowing for adaptation to action-conditioned reasoning. During downstream fine-tuning, the entire model is unfrozen. Ablation studies indicate that this freezing strategy improves downstream performance by 3.7% on LIBERO-Plus and 3.4% on Agilex.
- **Caption-Mixed Pre-training:** Auxiliary non-action data, specifically image captioning data, is mixed with robot trajectories during continued pre-training. This provides dense supervision over objects, attributes, spatial relations, and scene context, anchoring the trainable layers to the VLM's original vision-language representation. Ablation studies show that captioning data yields the strongest single-source gain (from 75.0% to 82.6% on LIBERO-Plus) compared to other auxiliary data types like BBox-QA, Point-QA, Spatial-QA, and pure language instruction data. The total objective combines action loss and a VLM cross-entropy loss with a weight of 0.5 for the latter.

#### 3.2. Aligning and Diversifying the Action Representation

To prevent over-specialization to a single action head and ensure broad transferability, VLAct uses a co-supervised multi-head pre-training approach:

- **Multi-head Continuous Action Co-supervision:** The same VLA backbone is pre-trained with multiple continuous action heads in parallel. Specifically, three representative continuous heads—OFT (parallel continuous regression), PI (flow-matching action expert), and GR00T (dual-system flow-matching motor module)—are attached to the shared backbone. All heads receive the same shared latent representation *z* from the backbone and predict the same ground-truth action chunk *a*. The training objective is a sum of the individual losses from each head: L_action = L_OFT + L_PI + L_GR00T. This forces the backbone to learn a representation that is broadly accessible and useful across different action parameterizations, thus mitigating "decoder lock-in" and potentially yielding stronger action features. Ablation studies indicate that head-diverse pre-training improves compatibility across action heads and also enhances same-head adaptation (e.g., +1.7 points for OFT, +1.6 for PI, +4.3 for GR00T compared to single-head pre-training).

#### 3.3. Unifying the Action Representation across Embodiments

To encourage shared action semantics across different robot platforms, VLAct adopts a partially unified cross-embodiment action space:

- **Partially Unified Cross-Embodiment Action Layout:** Instead of using separate heads for each embodiment or a naively fully unified space, VLAct employs one shared action head where dimensions are unified only along physically comparable actions. For instance, gripper dimensions are shared across embodiments due to comparable semantics, while arm dimensions remain embodiment-specific if robots have different kinematics. During training, losses are computed only on the active dimensions for a given embodiment, with inactive dimensions masked out. The action space is a fixed 20-dimensional vector, where dimensions 1-6 are for a left 6-DoF arm (absolute joint angles), 7-12 for a right 6-DoF arm (absolute joint angles), 13-18 for a single 6-DoF arm (delta end-effector pose), 19 for a shared gripper, and 20 for a right gripper. This design aims to share supervision where physically aligned action semantics exist, avoiding forced alignment of incompatible degrees of freedom. Ablation shows this improves performance over separate and naively unified heads.
- **Wrap-aware Loss for Periodic Joint Angles:** For absolute joint angles, which are periodic (e.g., 179° and -179° are physically close but numerically far), a standard regression loss can be misleading. VLAct applies a wrap-aware loss that measures angular residuals modulo 360° (or 2π radians). This correction is applied to absolute joint-angle dimensions (e.g., for AgileX's joint control) and improves performance (e.g., from 78.6% to 80.5% on RoboTwin).

#### 3.4. Training Data and Resources

VLAct is pre-trained on a mixture of fully open-source robot datasets, including DROID, InternA1, RoboCoin, and MolmoAct, alongside captioning data. A unified data cleaning pipeline is applied to address inconsistencies and noise across heterogeneous datasets. The base VLM is Qwen3-VL-4B. All training is conducted using a 16-GPU setup.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

VLAct demonstrates consistent improvements across various benchmarks, simulation, and real-world experiments, often surpassing established baselines and large-scale industrial VLA systems, particularly under fixed fine-tuning protocols and modest compute budgets.

#### 4.1. Effectiveness on Single-arm Franka Manipulation

- **LIBERO-Plus:** VLAct achieved an overall score of 82.6%, outperforming the strongest in-house baseline (Qwen3VL-OFT) by 7.6 percentage points (82.6% vs. 75.0%). It also surpassed the large-scale industrial system Abot-M0 (80.5%) by 2.1 percentage points. The largest gains were observed on Camera, Robot, Noise, and Layout perturbations, indicating improved robustness in visual-spatial representations.
- **VLA-Arena:** VLAct achieved an overall average success rate of 54.8%, a 21.4 percentage point improvement over Qwen3-VL-OFT (33.4%) and 10.5 points over the strongest public baseline ($\pi^{0.5}$, 44.3%). Notable gains were seen in Long-Horizon (21.0 points) and Safety (11.7 points) tasks.

#### 4.2. Effectiveness on Bimanual AgileX Manipulation

- **RoboTwin 2.0:**
 - **Base Setting:** VLAct-OFT reached 80.5% on Clean and 41.5% on Random, showing strong performance even with limited downstream data.
 - **Data Scaling Setting:** VLAct-OFT achieved 92.5% on Clean and 90.8% on Random, outperforming InternVLA-A1 (89.4%), Being-H0.7 (90.2%), Motus (88.7%), LingBot-VLA (88.6%), and ABot-M0 (86.1%). It also remained competitive with HoloBrain-0 (92.3%) and Fast-WAM (91.8%). The performance stability across different VLAct head choices (OFT, GR00T, PI) supports the head-transfer claim.
- **DOMINO:** VLAct-OFT achieved the best performance on this dynamic manipulation benchmark with an 18.50 Success Rate (SR) and 34.20 Manipulation Score (MS). This represents a 7.64 SR and 3.71 MS point improvement over the Qwen3VL-OFT baseline.

#### 4.3. Real-world Experiments (Franka Research 3)

VLAct consistently improved real-world performance over the non-pre-trained Qwen3VL-4B-OFT baseline across various task regimes:
- **Single-arm Short-horizon ID Tasks:** 92.5% average success for VLAct vs. 77.5% for baseline, with significant gains in spatially sensitive tasks (e.g., cube stacking, pen-in-cup).
- **Single-arm Short-horizon OOD Tasks:** 90.0% success on novel object tasks (from-pot, in-cup) for VLAct vs. 73.3% and 65.0% for baseline, respectively, indicating better generalization to unseen objects.
- **Single-arm Long-horizon Tasks:** Weighted scores of 86.6% on table cleaning and 80.0% on scooping beans for VLAct vs. 73.3% and 33.3% for baseline.
- **Single-arm Long-horizon OOD Tasks:** VLAct achieved 82.5% for extended-sequence table cleaning and 83.3% for full object substitution, significantly outperforming baseline scores of 47.5% and 46.6%, demonstrating robustness under object replacement and sequence extension.
- **Dual-arm Coordination:** Despite pre-training only on single-arm data, VLAct achieved 72.0% average success vs. 44.0% for baseline, with largest gains in deformable-object and synchronization-heavy tasks (e.g., pants folding, towel folding).

#### 4.4. Unseen Embodiment Transfer

- **RoboCasa-GR1 (Humanoid):** VLAct, with only 20% of downstream trajectories, achieved 49.5% success, matching or exceeding the full-data GR00T-N1.6 (47.6%) and Qwen3VL-OFT (48.8%) baselines. With full data, VLAct reached 54.0%, demonstrating strong initialization and data-efficient adaptation to an entirely unseen humanoid embodiment.
- **RoboDojo (ARX X5):** VLAct ranked sixth by success rate (7.60%) and eighth by average score (10.66) among 35 policies on the official leaderboard (snapshot Aug 24, 2026). It outperformed all explicitly designated world-action-model (WAM) entries, including X-WAM, and several industry-developed systems. This was achieved using open-source data and a 16-GPU setup, indicating competitive performance against predominantly industry-backed systems.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance of VLAct lies in its demonstration that representation-centric continued pre-training is a substantial and independent axis of progress in VLA model development, extending beyond mere data scaling. The consistent improvements observed across diverse benchmarks, real-world scenarios, and unseen embodiments, achieved with fully open-source data and a modest 16-GPU training setup, challenge the notion that VLA progress is solely contingent on massive, proprietary robot datasets or extensive computational resources.

The findings suggest that treating the VLM backbone not as a fixed component, but as a primary design variable for VLA, can lead to more transferable and robust robot policies. By actively shaping the backbone's representation to preserve general VLM priors, diversify action decoding capabilities, and unify cross-embodiment semantics, VLAct fosters more generalizable visual-action knowledge. This approach makes robot learning more efficient in terms of data, as models can adapt rapidly and effectively even to novel embodiments with limited downstream data, as shown by the RoboCasa-GR1 results.

The open-sourcing of all models and training pipelines associated with VLAct has the potential to broaden access to advanced VLA capabilities, enabling more researchers to build and experiment with generalizable VLA models. This could reduce barriers to entry in a field often dominated by large-scale industry efforts, fostering further innovation in how foundation VLMs can be optimized to serve as stronger backbones for physical tasks and embodied AI. The work implies that strategic architectural and training recipe design can yield highly competitive performance, highlighting the importance of thoughtful pre-training beyond simply scaling data volume.
