# WorldSimBench: Towards Video Generation Models as World Simulators

> [!summary] Summary
> WorldSimBench introduces a hierarchical categorization for predictive models and a dual evaluation framework to assess video generation models as 'World Simulators' in embodied AI scenarios. This framework, which includes an automated human preference evaluator surpassing GPT-4o's alignment, reveals that current models largely fail to effectively capture physical rules for actionable video generation.

## Problem

- The broad family of predictive models lacks a structured categorization based on their inherent characteristics and degree of embodiment, hindering targeted research.
- Existing evaluation benchmarks for generative models primarily focus on aesthetic visual quality or high-level text-based planning, failing to assess physical consistency and actionability for embodied agents.
- Difficulty in reliably assessing whether generated videos adhere to physical laws and can drive precise control signals in dynamic, interactive environments.

## Method

- Proposes a four-stage hierarchical categorization (S0-S3) for predictive models, defining S3 models as 'World Simulators' capable of generating physically consistent, actionable videos.
- Introduces **WorldSimBench**, a novel dual evaluation framework comprising Explicit Perceptual Evaluation (human-aligned visual quality, condition, and embodiment assessment) and Implicit Manipulative Evaluation (closed-loop task performance in simulation).
- Develops the HF-Embodied Dataset, a large-scale video assessment dataset with fine-grained human feedback, used to train a Human Preference Evaluator (HPE) for automated perceptual scoring.

## Results

- The Human Preference Evaluator (HPE) consistently outperformed GPT-4o in aligning with human preferences across all scenarios (OE, AD, RM), showing higher accuracy and Pearson Linear Correlation Coefficient.
- Video generation models demonstrate weaknesses in 'Embodied Interaction' (e.g., plausible object deformation), 'Instruction Alignment' for task-oriented movements, and consistent 'Perspectivity' and 'Key Elements' in complex dynamic scenes.
- In implicit manipulative evaluations, video generation models conditioned on both text and image inputs sometimes yielded lower success rates compared to text-only conditioned models, particularly in open-ended embodied environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The developed Human Preference Evaluator (HPE) significantly outperforms GPT-4o in aligning with human preferences for embodied video quality, including in zero-shot settings.
> - Current state-of-the-art video generation models largely struggle to effectively capture complex physical rules and consistently generate actionable videos for embodied agents.
> - Effective integration of multimodal inputs (e.g., image conditioning) can sometimes interfere with generating accurate physical laws and 3D scene representations for actionable guidance, leading to reduced performance in interactive tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the crucial intersection of generative AI, predictive modeling, and embodied artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on the nascent field of "World Simulators."

Historically, AI agents have relied on explicit programming or reinforcement learning within predefined environments. However, a significant paradigm shift is occurring towards models that can *predict* future states of the world, much like humans imagine outcomes before acting. This concept of "predictive models" or "world models" (pioneered by works like Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018) is seen as a foundational step towards Artificial General Intelligence (AGI), enabling agents to perform high-level planning, image-based guidance, and future video prediction to drive actions.

The paper argues that despite recent advancements in generative models, the evaluation of these predictive models remains fragmented and insufficient, particularly for highly embodied scenarios. Existing benchmarks typically fall into two categories:
- **Text-based evaluations (S0):** These focus on high-level task planning through textual outputs, primarily for Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). Examples include AgentBench and EgoPlan-Bench.
- **Image/Video aesthetic evaluations (S1, S2):** These assess the visual quality or aesthetic appeal of generated images or videos, often using feature similarity metrics with ground truth. Examples include LEGO, VBench, and EvalCrafter.

The critical gap identified by the authors is the lack of a comprehensive evaluation framework for models that generate *actionable videos*—i.e., videos that not only look realistic but also adhere to physical laws and can be translated into precise control signals for real-world (or simulated) embodied agents. These advanced models, termed "World Simulators," are capable of integrating 3D scene understanding and physical rule priors, representing the most sophisticated level of predictive capability (S3 in their proposed hierarchy).

"WORLDSIMBENCH" directly addresses this void by moving beyond superficial aesthetic evaluation to encompass the crucial aspects of physical consistency, dynamic interaction, and actionability. It pushes the research landscape from static content generation towards dynamic, interactive, and embodied simulation. By proposing a new hierarchical categorization and a dual evaluation framework, this work provides a much-needed standardized approach to benchmark progress in a field critical for the development of truly intelligent, adaptive agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind WorldSimBench stems from two core challenges in the development and evaluation of predictive models for embodied AI:

1. **Lack of Categorization:** The broad family of predictive models, ranging from text-based planners to video generators, lacks a structured categorization based on their inherent characteristics and degree of embodiment. Without such a hierarchy, comparing and assessing the true capabilities of different models becomes challenging, hindering targeted research and development. This leads to their first guiding question: *Can we establish a reasonable hierarchical system for Predictive Models based on their degree of embodiment?*

2. **Inadequate Embodied Evaluation:** Existing benchmarks primarily focus on task planning (text outputs) or aesthetic visual quality (image/video outputs). These methods fail to adequately assess highly embodied predictive models, which require evaluation concerning physical properties (e.g., perspective consistency, object breakability, realistic motion) and, crucially, their ability to drive actions in dynamic environments. This forms their second guiding question: *Can we conduct a more detailed evaluation of highly embodied Predictive Models from an embodied perspective?*

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of the paper are:
- To **categorize the functionalities of Predictive Models into a hierarchy** (S0 to S3), with S3 models defined as "World Simulators" capable of generating actionable videos that adhere to physical rules and align with executable actions.
- To **propose WorldSimBench**, a novel dual evaluation framework specifically designed to evaluate these World Simulators from an embodied perspective.
- To implement WorldSimBench with two complementary evaluation approaches: **Explicit Perceptual Evaluation** (assessing visual fidelity, condition consistency, and embodiment via human preference) and **Implicit Manipulative Evaluation** (assessing action-level performance by converting generated videos into control signals in embodied tasks).
- To **conduct comprehensive testing** across multiple state-of-the-art video generation models within three representative embodied scenarios: Open-Ended Embodied Environment (Minecraft), Autonomous Driving (CARLA), and Robot Manipulation (CALVIN).
- To **develop the HF-Embodied Dataset**, a large-scale video assessment dataset with fine-grained human feedback, to train a Human Preference Evaluator that aligns with human perception.
- To **provide key insights** into the strengths and limitations of current video generation models, guiding future innovation towards more capable World Simulators and ultimately advancing embodied artificial intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WorldSimBench employs a dual evaluation framework to provide a comprehensive assessment of World Simulators, focusing on both human-perceived quality and objective task performance in embodied scenarios.

#### 3.1. Hierarchical Categorization of Predictive Models

The paper first establishes a four-stage hierarchy for Predictive Models based on their output modality and level of embodiment:
- **S0 (Predictive Text Model):** Generates textual predictions (e.g., high-level plans). Evaluated by text-level and task completion assessments.
- **S1 (Predictive Image Model):** Generates visual predictions without temporal information (e.g., goal images). Evaluated for aesthetic quality.
- **S2 (Predictive Video Model):** Generates video predictions but primarily evaluated for aesthetic quality due to limited physical consistency.
- **S3 (Predictive Actionable Video Model / World Simulators):** Generates video predictions that adhere to physical rules and can be translated into executable actions. This is the focus of WorldSimBench.

#### 3.2. Dual Evaluation Framework (WorldSimBench): Explicit Perceptual Evaluation

This component assesses the human-perceived quality of generated videos, focusing on visual fidelity, consistency with instructions, and adherence to physical properties.
- **Hierarchical Evaluation Dimensions:** A detailed checklist of criteria is defined for each of the three embodied scenarios (OE, AD, RM). These dimensions are categorized into:
 - **Visual Quality:** Aesthetics, Background Consistency, Foreground Consistency.
 - **Condition Consistency:** Instruction Alignment, Scenario Alignment (for OE).
 - **Embodiment:** Velocity, Trajectory, Embodied Interaction, Perspectivity, Key Element (for AD), Safety (for AD).
- **Instruction Prompt Generation:** A comprehensive list of prompts is created by expanding meta-prompts with LLMs (ChatGPT) and manual verification, ensuring diversity and relevance to evaluation dimensions.
- **HF-Embodied Dataset Generation:**
 - Popular video generation models are fine-tuned on scenario-specific large datasets (Minecraft gameplay, nuScenes driving data, RH20T-P robot manipulation videos).
 - These models generate a large number of instruction-following embodied video clips.
 - Human annotators then provide multi-dimensional scores (e.g., 1-2 for OE, 1-5 for AD/RM) and fine-grained feedback/reasons for deficiencies across all defined dimensions. This results in the HF-Embodied Dataset, comprising 35,701 tuples.
- **Human Preference Evaluator (HPE):** A VideoLLM (Flash-VStream) is fine-tuned on the HF-Embodied Dataset to automate the perceptual scoring process, aiming to align with human judgments.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** The final metric for a model is the average score assigned by the HPE across selected instructions strongly aligned with specific evaluation criteria.

#### 3.2.2. Implicit Manipulative Evaluation

This component implicitly assesses the World Simulator's capability by evaluating its performance in closed-loop embodied tasks, where generated videos are converted into control signals.
- **Simulation Construction:** Three simulation environments are used:
 - **Open-Ended Embodied Environment (OE):** MineRL (Minecraft simulator) with adapted Steve-1 benchmarks (e.g., "chop a tree").
 - **Autonomous Driving (AD):** CARLA simulator with LangAuto Benchmark (e.g., "do not deviate from this route").
 - **Robot Manipulation (RM):** CALVIN simulator (robot arm control, e.g., "pull the handle to open the drawer").
- **Embodied Task Evaluation Pipeline:**
 - Pre-trained video-to-action models (e.g., Steve-1, Susie, LMdrive) serve as intermediaries, mapping predicted future videos from the World Simulator to executable actions.
 - The World Simulator generates situation-aware videos in real-time based on current observations and text instructions.
 - These generated videos are fed to the video-to-action model, and the resulting actions are executed in the simulation environment.
 - The video predictions are periodically refreshed.
 - The World Simulator's effectiveness is measured by the agent's performance in completing tasks within the simulation.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Task-specific metrics are used:
 - **OE:** Travel distance, dig depth, item collection (logs, seeds, dirt).
 - **AD:** Route Completion (RC), Infraction Score (IS), Driving Score (DS), various collision types (VC, PC, LC), Red Light Violations (RV), Offroad Infractions (OI).
 - **RM:** Task success rates and average task length completed for multi-task sequences.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

Eight popular video generation models (e.g., Open-Sora-Plan, Lavie, ModelScope, OpenSora) are fine-tuned on scenario-specific datasets and evaluated using both explicit and implicit methods.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The comprehensive evaluation by WorldSimBench reveals significant insights into the current capabilities and limitations of video generation models as World Simulators.

#### 4.1. Validation of Human Preference Evaluator (HPE)

The HPE, fine-tuned on the HF-Embodied Dataset, consistently outperforms GPT-4o in aligning with human preferences across all scenarios (OE, AD, RM), showing higher accuracy (Acc) and Pearson Linear Correlation Coefficient (PLCC). Crucially, HPE demonstrates robust generalization capabilities even in zero-shot settings for unseen models (e.g., OpenSora, Lavie), where GPT-4o sometimes shows negative correlation with human preferences. This validates HPE as a reliable tool for explicit perceptual evaluation.

#### 4.2. Explicit Perceptual Evaluation Insights

- **Open-Ended Embodied Environment (OE):**
 - Most models achieve high scores in "Velocity" due to the infrequency of dynamic object movement generation.
 - Models struggle significantly with "Embodied Interaction" (e.g., plausible object deformations like block shattering) due to the complexity of physical rules.
 - "Scenario Alignment" is generally better than "Instruction Alignment," indicating models can generate relevant scenes but struggle with temporal actions required for task completion.
- **Autonomous Driving (AD):**
 - The complexity of outdoor dynamic scenarios leads to a significant decline in generated video quality.
 - Models face substantial challenges in understanding and generating diverse traffic elements (roads, vehicles, pedestrians) and their realistic motion.
 - While top models (e.g., OpenSora) achieve high scores, many struggle with "Perspectivity" (3D depth and realism) and consistent rendering of "Key Elements."
- **Robot Manipulation (RM):**
 - Models perform relatively well in static scene depiction, excelling in "Perspectivity" and "Background/Foreground Consistency."
 - A major weakness is "Instruction Alignment," with models often generating aimless actions rather than task-oriented movements.
 - Despite this, "Trajectory" scores are often high, but this is sometimes artificially inflated because the lack of targeted movement reduces instances of unreasonable trajectories or collisions. True robotic manipulation remains a significant hurdle.

#### 4.3. Implicit Manipulative Evaluation Insights

- **Overall Variation:** There is significant variation in the performance of video generation models across different tasks and scenarios.
- **Open-Ended Embodied Environment (OE):**
 - Video generation models conditioned on the first frame (Text & Image) show significantly *lower* success rates compared to text-only conditioned models. This suggests current models struggle with effectively integrating multimodal inputs, and image conditioning can interfere with generating accurate physical laws and 3D scene representations for actionable guidance.
 - Tasks requiring high-quality trajectories and 3D representation (e.g., "travel") show the greatest variation, while simpler tasks (e.g., "collect wood" with minimal background variation) show similar performance across models.
- **Autonomous Driving (AD):**
 - Models demonstrating better trajectory generation in explicit perceptual evaluations (e.g., Open-Sora-Plan) generally perform better in AD tasks (e.g., higher Driving Score).
- **Robot Manipulation (RM):**
 - In settings with minimal background variation, models perform similarly on simple tasks. However, as task complexity increases, only more robust models achieve higher success rates.

#### 4.4. Alignment of Physical Rules and Actions

- A general consistency is observed between the Explicit Perceptual Evaluation and Implicit Manipulative Evaluation results. For example, models excelling in trajectory generation perceptually also perform better in trajectory-focused implicit tasks.
- However, discrepancies exist. For example, in OE's long-sequence tasks, Open-Sora-Plan outperforms Dynamicrafter despite the latter's higher perceptual scores, suggesting that *robustness in generating stable, high-quality video sequences* for continuous guidance is crucial, beyond just instantaneous visual quality.
- **Overall Conclusion:** The empirical evidence from both evaluation frameworks strongly suggests that current video generation models largely *fail to effectively capture many physical rules*. Significant improvements are still required before they can reliably function as true World Simulators for embodied agents.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

WorldSimBench represents a pivotal step forward in the evaluation of generative AI for embodied intelligence. Its significance and potential impact are multi-faceted:

- **Pioneering a New Evaluation Paradigm:** By being the "first step in evaluating World Simulators" from a comprehensive embodied perspective, WorldSimBench establishes a much-needed benchmark for a critical and rapidly advancing field. It shifts the focus from merely aesthetically pleasing video generation to *actionable* and *physically consistent* video generation.

- **Structured Research Advancement:** The proposed hierarchical categorization (S0-S3) of predictive models provides a clear framework and common vocabulary for researchers. This systematic classification can help in better understanding the capabilities and limitations of different models, guiding targeted research efforts to advance models through these stages towards S3 capabilities.

- **Comprehensive and Robust Evaluation Standard:** The dual evaluation framework, combining explicit human preference assessments with implicit manipulative evaluations in diverse embodied scenarios, offers a more holistic and reliable measure of a World Simulator's performance. It goes beyond simplistic metrics, incorporating human perception, physical plausibility, and real-world (simulated) task success. The HF-Embodied Dataset, with its fine-grained human feedback, is a valuable resource for training and validating future evaluation models and for preference alignment research.

- **Guiding Future Model Development:** The detailed experimental findings provide actionable insights for researchers and developers. By highlighting specific weaknesses—such as the struggle with complex physical interactions (e.g., object deformation in Minecraft), inconsistent 3D depth and key elements in autonomous driving, and poor instruction alignment in robot manipulation—the benchmark clearly points to areas where significant algorithmic and architectural improvements are needed. The observation that image conditioning can sometimes *degrade* performance in interactive settings is a crucial finding, indicating challenges in multimodal fusion for embodied control.

- **Catalyst for Embodied Artificial General Intelligence (AGI):** World Simulators are considered a cornerstone for achieving embodied AGI, as they enable agents to learn, plan, and adapt by simulating future outcomes. By providing a rigorous evaluation framework, WorldSimBench directly contributes to accelerating progress in this domain. More capable World Simulators could lead to:
 - **Advanced Robotics:** More intelligent and adaptive robots capable of complex manipulation, navigation, and human interaction in unstructured environments.
 - **Safer Autonomous Systems:** Better predictive capabilities for self-driving cars, leading to more reliable and safer navigation in unpredictable real-world scenarios.
 - **Enhanced Game AI:** More realistic and responsive AI agents in virtual environments.
 - **Improved Scientific Simulation:** Generating accurate physical predictions for complex systems.

In conclusion, WorldSimBench is a significant contribution to the field of embodied AI and generative modeling. It not only fills a critical gap in evaluation methodologies but also provides a structured roadmap and valuable dataset that will undoubtedly drive innovation towards the creation of truly intelligent, physically-aware, and actionable World Simulators.
