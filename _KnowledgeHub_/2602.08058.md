# Picasso: Holistic Scene Reconstruction with Physics-Constrained Sampling

> [!summary] Summary
> Picasso introduces a pipeline for constructing multi-object 3D scene reconstructions that are both geometrically accurate and physically plausible. This method utilizes a physics-constrained sampling approach guided by an inferred contact scene graph, leading to improved object pose accuracy and significantly higher physical stability on established and newly introduced datasets.

## Problem

- Current 3D scene reconstruction methods often generate geometrically accurate but physically impossible configurations, such as interpenetrating or unstable objects, limiting their use in digital twins for robotics.
- Object pose and shape estimators often process objects in isolation, failing to account for critical inter-object interactions and physical constraints.
- Traditional optimization-based approaches for incorporating physics tend to converge to local minima, potentially yielding solutions that are physically plausible but geometrically inaccurate.

## Method

- **Picasso** proposes a physics-constrained reconstruction pipeline that retrofits existing object pose and shape estimators, refining their outputs to satisfy ==non-penetration== and static stability.
- A fast, ==coarse-to-fine rejection sampling== method, guided by an inferred ==Contact Scene Graph (CSG)==, is employed to solve the high-dimensional, non-convex optimization problem by decoupling it into sequential subproblems.
- The research introduces the Picasso dataset, comprising real-world scenes with rich contact, and a novel ==Scene Plausibility Score (SPS)== to quantitatively evaluate the physical coherence of reconstructions.

## Results

- Picasso improved CRISP's mean ADD-S to **0.008m** and reduced SPS to **8.71** on the YCB-V dataset, outperforming CRISP-Real and other baselines, while operating under **1 second**.
- When applied to SAM3D, Picasso reduced the mean ADD-S by up to **51%** (e.g., from **21.68mm** to **10.56mm** on YCB-V) and significantly lowered SPS and NPS on both YCB-V and the new Picasso dataset.
- A user study confirmed that human perception of physical plausibility aligns with Picasso's reconstructions and the proposed Scene Plausibility Score, with participants consistently rating Picasso's outputs as more stable.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Treating physics as hard constraints within a sampling framework, rather than soft penalty terms, can achieve more globally plausible and accurate scene reconstructions, avoiding common local minima issues.
> - Decoupling the multi-object pose estimation problem into sequential, lower-dimensional subproblems using an inferred Contact Scene Graph significantly improves the tractability and efficiency of physics-constrained sampling.
> - Physics-constrained refinement can substantially bridge the sim-to-real gap, enabling models trained purely on synthetic data to outperform or match models trained on real data in terms of geometric and physical plausibility.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "Picasso" addresses a challenge in 3D scene reconstruction for robotics: generating digital twins that are not only geometrically accurate but also physically plausible. Current approaches in computer vision and robotics often struggle with inconsistencies when estimating object poses and shapes, particularly in scenes with occlusions and measurement noise. While many methods can reconstruct object geometry by fitting sensor data, they frequently produce physically impossible configurations, such as interpenetrating objects or objects in unstable equilibrium. Such implausible reconstructions hinder the reliable prediction of dynamic scene behavior, which is crucial for simulation-based planning and control in contact-rich robotic manipulation tasks.

Existing approaches to 3D reconstruction and object understanding typically fall into several categories. Foundation models for 3D reconstruction often create dense 3D maps without explicitly reasoning about inter-object interactions. Object understanding models, including recent 3D vision models, tend to estimate individual object poses and shapes in isolation, which can lead to the aforementioned physical inconsistencies. To incorporate physics constraints, some methods employ physics-guided losses (e.g., discouraging co-penetration, encouraging stable contact) optimized via gradient descent. However, these can converge to local minima that are physically plausible but geometrically inaccurate. Another strategy involves differentiable physics simulators to generate corrective signals, but these can be brittle due to modeling inaccuracies and numerical noise. Sampling-based approaches, while less common, offer an alternative by formulating physical plausibility probabilistically and performing inference through sampling, often utilizing Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC).

"Picasso" positions itself as a model-based approach that retrofits existing object pose and shape estimators to enhance physical plausibility. It differs from optimization-based methods by treating physics as hard constraints rather than soft penalty terms, thereby avoiding the introduction of competing losses and local minima. It distinguishes itself from prior sampling methods by offering a fast rejection sampling scheme that is guided by an inferred object contact graph, enabling it to handle arbitrary contact types and co-penetration in a computationally tractable manner. The paper aims to bridge the gap between geometrically faithful and physically plausible scene reconstructions, a critical step toward enabling robust robotic interaction in complex, cluttered, and contact-rich environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that geometrically accurate scene reconstructions, even when closely fitting sensor data, are often physically incorrect. This discrepancy is particularly problematic for creating digital twins, which are essential for predicting dynamic behaviors in simulation-based planning and control for contact-rich robotic tasks. When estimated object poses and shapes are imported into a simulator, minor errors can lead to major implausibilities like object interpenetration or unstable configurations, rendering the digital twin unsuitable for predicting real-world outcomes.

The paper posits that accurate object pose and shape estimation necessitates a holistic scene understanding, moving beyond individual object processing to account for inter-object interactions and physical plausibility. To address this, the authors establish several key objectives:

1. **Develop a Physics-Constrained Reconstruction Pipeline:** The core objective is to propose "Picasso," a pipeline capable of building multi-object scene reconstructions that explicitly consider object geometry (fitting sensor data), non-penetration (objects should not occupy the same space), and physical stability (objects should be in a stable equilibrium if the scene is static). This pipeline is designed to be model-based and to retrofit existing object pose and shape estimators.

2. **Implement a Fast and Robust Sampling Method:** To achieve physics-constrained reconstruction, Picasso aims to leverage a fast rejection sampling method. This method is intended to reason over multi-object interactions by guiding samples based on an inferred object contact graph, thereby reducing the dimensionality of the sampling space and promoting global exploration over local optimization.

3. **Introduce a New Dataset and Plausibility Metric:** A significant objective is the creation and release of the "Picasso dataset." This dataset comprises a collection of real-world scenes characterized by rich contact interactions, accompanied by ground truth annotations. Alongside the dataset, the authors aim to propose and open-source a novel metric to quantitatively assess the physical plausibility of scene reconstructions, facilitating standardized evaluation in this domain.

4. **Extensively Evaluate the Proposed Approach:** The final objective involves a comprehensive evaluation of Picasso. This evaluation aims to demonstrate that Picasso can effectively integrate with modern object pose and shape estimators, outperform state-of-the-art methods in terms of both pose accuracy and physical plausibility, and generate reconstructions that align more closely with human intuition regarding physical reality.

In essence, the motivation is to enable robots to form a coherent "mental picture" of their environment, akin to human scene understanding, which can then serve as a reliable digital twin for interaction and prediction in complex physical settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Picasso is designed as a physics-constrained reconstruction pipeline that integrates geometric measurements with physical principles to produce plausible multi-object scene reconstructions. The approach builds upon existing object pose and shape estimators and refines their outputs.

**Problem Formulation:** The core problem is formulated as a maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) task (Eq. 1). Given an RGB image, depth map, and object masks, the goal is to estimate the scale, translation, and rotation (SIM(3) transformation) $T_i$ and shape $S_i$ for each object $i$, such that the resulting configuration belongs to the set $F$ of physically plausible pose and shape estimates.

**Measurement Likelihood:**
- **Shape Likelihood:** The paper assumes the availability of a pretrained network (e.g., SAM3D, CRISP) that provides object shape predictions given sensor data. For simplicity, a single shape estimate is assumed, though the framework can accommodate multiple candidates.
- **Pose Likelihood:** The pose likelihood is analytically expanded, assuming independent measurement noise for each object. This translates to a point cloud registration problem, where the objective is to minimize the sum of squared distances (Chamfer distance) between measured 3D points within an object's mask and the surface of its estimated shape (Eqs. 5, 6). The formulation is robustified with a Geman-McClure loss to mitigate sensor noise and segmentation artifacts.

**Physics Constraints:** The methodology enforces physical plausibility using four types of analytical configuration-space constraints, focusing on static scenes:
1. **Inter-object non-penetration:** Ensures that no two objects intersect, using Signed Distance Fields (SDFs) to check that all surface points of one object lie outside another (Eq. 7).
2. **Object-environment non-penetration:** Prevents objects from penetrating the static environment (e.g., table), which is identified by a planar surface estimated via RANSAC from depth measurements (Eq. 8).
3. **Object-free-space non-penetration:** Guarantees consistency with observed free space by ensuring object points do not enter regions known to be empty. This is checked by rendering the object as a depth map and comparing it with the observed depth map (Eq. 9).
4. **Contact (no floating objects):** Discourages floating objects by requiring each object to be in contact (within a small tolerance $\delta$) with at least one other object or the environment (Eq. 10). The libigl library is used for inter-object and contact checking, while a voxel-based depth renderer is used for object-free-space checks.

**Physics-Constrained Pose Estimator (Eq. 12):** The measurement likelihood objective (minimizing Chamfer distance) is combined with the derived physics constraints. This results in a highly nonconvex optimization problem involving multiple similarity transformations. Picasso solves this problem using rejection sampling: object configurations are sampled, and those violating any constraint are rejected. Among the valid samples, the one with the smallest objective value is selected.

**Regaining Tractability via Contact Scene Graph (CSG):** To manage the high dimensionality of the sampling space in multi-object scenes, the concept of a Contact Scene Graph (CSG) is introduced.
- **CSG Inference:** Instead of relying on noisy 3D point cloud distances, the topology of the CSG (which objects are in contact) is inferred using a Vision-Language Model (VLM) queried with the RGB image, depth map, and object masks.
- **DAG Approximation:** The inferred CSG, which can be cyclic, is approximated as a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) using a breadth-first search, with the environment as the root. Edges are oriented in the order of traversal.
- **Decoupled Subproblems:** This DAG structure enables a greedy, sequential estimation approach. The problem (Eq. 12) decouples into N subproblems (Eq. 13), where each object's pose is estimated given the fixed, already estimated poses of its parent nodes in the DAG. This significantly reduces the dimensionality of each sampling step.

**Implementation of Coarse-to-Fine Rejection Sampling:**
- **Initialization:** Object translation is initialized from the mean of masked depth point clouds, and scale is estimated by comparing mean depths of masked and rendered point clouds from the initial network estimate.
- **Hierarchical Sampler:** Each decoupled subproblem is solved using a hierarchical, coarse-to-fine sampler over SIM(3), separating scale from SE(3) pose. Scale, translation, and rotation are sampled in a progressively refined manner. For rotation, global sampling on SO(3) is followed by local refinements in tangent space (Table V). For translation, a uniform grid search is refined around promising candidates.
- **Physics Constraints Integration:** Rejection sampling is applied to a buffer of top-B candidates (B=16) ranked by Chamfer loss. If all candidates violate constraints, the best-scoring pose is returned without physics satisfaction.
- **System Overview:** Picasso functions as a pose corrector, leveraging rough initial poses. It performs GPU-optimized parallel sampling to achieve fast inference (fractions of a second per object).

**Picasso Dataset and Physics Metric:**
- **Picasso Dataset:** A new dataset of 10 contact-rich real-world static scenes with 10 everyday objects. RGB-D data is captured with an iPad LiDAR. Object masks are generated using SAM2, and 3D poses are obtained by solving PnP from 2D/3D keypoint correspondences. Raw depth maps are refined by rendering object models. Scenes feature diverse contact types (point, line, flush).
- **Scene Plausibility Score (SPS):** A metric to quantify scene-level physical plausibility. For a given scene reconstruction, a digital twin is created in a physics simulator (PyBullet), and a short rollout (T=20 steps) is performed. The SPS is defined as the kinetic energy ($1/2 mv^T v + 1/2 \omega^T J \omega$) of the system, with canonical mass and inertia. Lower SPS indicates higher stability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Picasso was conducted on both the newly introduced Picasso dataset and the YCB-Video (YCB-V) dataset, comparing its performance against various state-of-the-art methods and ablation studies.

**A. Boosting CRISP’s Performance Using Picasso (YCB-V Dataset):**
- **Sim-to-Real Gap Mitigation:** Picasso demonstrated the ability to bridge the sim-to-real gap for the CRISP object pose and shape estimator. CRISP-Syn (trained on synthetic data) combined with Picasso (CRISP-Syn+Picasso) outperformed CRISP-Real (trained on real data), as well as other baselines like CosyPose, BundleSDF, GDRNet++, and CRISP-Syn with gradient descent or GICP-based refinement.
- **Geometric Accuracy:** In terms of the ADD-S metric (a measure of 3D object pose accuracy), CRISP-Syn+Picasso achieved a mean ADD-S of 0.008m and an AUC (Area Under Curve) of 0.77 at 3cm threshold, matching or surpassing CRISP-Real+Picasso and outperforming all other variants.
- **Physical Plausibility:** Picasso significantly improved physical plausibility. CRISP-Syn+Picasso yielded the lowest Scene Plausibility Score (SPS) of 8.71 and Non-Penetration Score (NPS) of 3.99, indicating more stable and less penetrating reconstructions compared to CRISP-Real (SPS 9.05, NPS 4.57) and other baselines.
- **Observable Correctness:** Observable correctness (OC) also improved, with CRISP-Syn+Picasso achieving 55%, compared to CRISP-Real's 36%.
- **Runtime:** Picasso achieved competitive runtime, operating under 1 second (0.35s for CRISP-Syn+Picasso, 0.33s for CRISP-Real+Picasso), while offering significant improvements.

**B. Boosting SAM3D’s Performance Using Picasso:**
- **YCB-V Dataset:** When applied to SAM3D, Picasso substantially improved performance across 12 YCB-Video trajectories. SAM3D+Picasso (S+P) achieved a mean ADD-S of 10.56mm, a significant reduction from SAM3D's 21.68mm. Physics metrics also saw notable improvements, with S+P reducing SPS from 5.84 to 1.85 and NPS from 9.29 to 2.79. The benefits were observed even when comparing against SAM3D+Picasso without physics constraints (S+P-w/o PC), although the difference was modest due to the physics checks being performed on a limited set of candidates.
- **Picasso Dataset:** On the new Picasso dataset, Picasso consistently improved SAM3D and SAM3D-PM (SAM3D with pointmap input) across all metrics. For SAM3D-PM, Picasso reduced mean ADD-S from 11.45mm to 7.84mm, SPS from 8.91 to 6.59, and NPS from 5.67 to 3.50. It also outperformed GICP-based registration on most metrics. Qualitative results (Fig. 7) visually demonstrated that Picasso corrected severe inter-object penetrations and floating objects present in SAM3D reconstructions, yielding physically plausible configurations.

**C. User Study: Alignment with Physics Metrics:**
- A user study involving both public participants and expert researchers confirmed that Picasso’s reconstructions are perceived as more physically plausible than those from SAM3D alone.
- Human evaluations consistently rated SAM3D+Picasso as more stable. The study also indicated a good alignment between human intuition and the proposed Scene Plausibility Score (SPS), with SPS showing a stronger distinguishing capability between good and poor reconstructions.

**D. Ablation Studies:**
- Ablation studies confirmed that enabling physics constraints in Picasso improved both geometric and physical plausibility metrics compared to a constraint-free version. The satisfaction rate for physics constraints increased from 52.25% to 72.34% with the physics corrector enabled.
- Individual constraint terms contributed to performance gains, with inter-object and contact constraints showing a larger role. The object-environment constraint yielded the largest gain on YCB-V, while object-free-space constraints were more influential on datasets like IC-BIN, particularly in rejecting ambiguous solutions (Fig. 10).

**E. Shape Correction:**
- Picasso also demonstrated the capability to improve shape prediction when integrated with CRISP by selecting the best shape from a library of latent codes based on Chamfer loss, leading to modest improvements in shape error metrics (e.g., e_shape).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Picasso" represents an advancement in the field of 3D scene reconstruction, particularly in its focus on integrating physical plausibility with geometric accuracy. The significance and potential impact of this research can be delineated as follows:

- **Enabling Robust Digital Twins for Robotics:** A primary impact is the creation of more reliable digital twins. By generating scene reconstructions that are both geometrically consistent with sensor data and physically plausible, Picasso directly addresses a critical limitation in current robotics applications. These improved digital twins can facilitate more accurate simulation-based planning, control, and reasoning for contact-rich manipulation tasks, moving beyond scenarios where physically impossible configurations undermine predictive models.

- **Enhancing Scene Understanding:** Picasso contributes to a holistic understanding of scenes by explicitly modeling inter-object relationships and physical constraints. This moves beyond isolated object detection and pose estimation, which often overlooks critical interactions that govern real-world stability and dynamics. Such a comprehensive understanding is essential for robots to operate effectively and safely in cluttered and interactive environments.

- **Generalizable Pose Correction Framework:** The methodology positions Picasso as a pose corrector that can be retrofitted to existing object pose and shape estimators (demonstrated with CRISP and SAM3D). This characteristic suggests broad applicability, allowing current and future vision models to enhance their outputs without requiring extensive re-training or modification of their core architecture. This modularity makes Picasso a valuable tool for improving the practical utility of various 3D perception systems.

- **Novel Dataset and Evaluation Metric:** The introduction of the Picasso dataset provides a valuable resource for the research community. As a collection of contact-rich, real-world scenes with ground truth annotations, it fills a gap in existing datasets that often lack explicit physical interaction models. Complementing this, the Scene Plausibility Score (SPS) offers a quantitative and simulator-agnostic metric for evaluating physical plausibility, fostering standardized benchmarking and driving future research in physics-aware scene reconstruction. The alignment of SPS with human intuition further validates its utility.

- **Advancement in Sampling-Based Optimization:** The adoption of fast, GPU-parallelized coarse-to-fine rejection sampling, guided by an inferred Contact Scene Graph, presents an effective strategy for navigating highly nonconvex optimization landscapes. This approach demonstrates that treating physics as hard constraints within a sampling framework can overcome limitations of gradient-based methods prone to local minima, particularly for high-dimensional, multi-object problems.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper identifies several promising avenues for future work, including applying Picasso to other highly nonconvex objectives (e.g., semantic, rendering-based losses), accelerating the solver, improving Contact Scene Graph inference to handle mutual interactions, and integrating physics constraints directly into foundation model training or data generation. These directions suggest that Picasso could serve as a foundational step towards more sophisticated, physics-informed AI systems.

In summary, Picasso's contribution to creating physically plausible digital twins, its generalizability, and its provision of a new dataset and metric position it as a significant work with the potential to advance robotic manipulation, autonomous navigation, and general AI capabilities in understanding and interacting with the physical world.
