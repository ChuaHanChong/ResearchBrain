# Causal Object-Centric Models for Planning with Monte Carlo Tree Search

> [!summary] Summary
> COMET is an MCTS-based object-centric model-based reinforcement learning algorithm that integrates frozen unsupervised object-centric representations with a transformer-based world model and object-causal attention for enhanced planning. It achieved higher mean normalized scores during early training stages across eight diverse object-oriented visual control tasks, demonstrating improved sample efficiency compared to monolithic and other object-centric baselines.

## Problem

- Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) struggles with learning accurate world models in complex visual environments using monolithic state representations.
- Holistic representations fail to capture critical object-level structure and interactions, leading to inefficiency and information overload from irrelevant entities.
- Existing object-centric MBRL approaches often lack explicit object interaction modeling or rely on expensive, supervised instance segmentation masks.

## Method

- Developed **COMET**, an MCTS-based algorithm for planning in a slot-structured latent space, integrating a frozen unsupervised object-centric encoder.
- Employed a transformer-based world model with a novel ==action-object binding mechanism== that fuses action embeddings with individual object slots for next-state prediction.
- Implemented object-causal attention in policy and value networks, using learned per-slot relevance scores to focus on task-relevant objects and modulate attention.

## Results

- COMET achieved a higher mean normalized score in early training phases across eight diverse object-oriented visual control tasks, indicating improved sample efficiency.
- The method demonstrated faster convergence and superior final performance in visually simpler tasks, such as Object Goal and Object Comparison.
- Visualizations confirmed that the object-causal attention mechanism successfully assigned higher relevance scores to task-relevant objects for policy and value prediction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structured object-centric representations, when integrated into an MCTS-based planning framework, can improve sample efficiency compared to monolithic approaches.
> - Explicitly binding actions to individual object slots within a transformer architecture allows for more granular and accurate prediction of object-level dynamics.
> - Learning object relevance through a "causal" attention mechanism enables policy and value networks to focus computational resources on task-pertinent entities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work operates within the field of Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL), specifically focusing on enhancing planning capabilities in complex visual environments. MBRL algorithms aim to improve learning efficiency by learning a model of the environment's dynamics, which is then used to generate imagined experiences, reducing the reliance on real-world interactions. Notable MBRL approaches include the Dreamer family of algorithms, Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control, and methods integrating Monte-Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) with learned models, such as MuZero.

A persistent challenge in MBRL, particularly in visually rich environments, is learning accurate world models. Traditional methods often rely on convolutional neural network (CNN) encoders that produce a single, holistic representation of the input image. These monolithic representations can struggle to capture object-level structure and interactions, which are often critical for effective decision-making. In scenes with many entities, small but relevant objects, or dynamic backgrounds, such representations may lead to degraded performance.

Inspired by human perception, which decomposes the world into discrete entities (objects), object-centric reinforcement learning has emerged as an approach to represent environments as sets of object-level components. This paradigm facilitates more focused and interpretable decision-making by explicitly modeling object dynamics and interactions. While some object-centric methods require instance segmentation masks (either supervised or self-supervised), a significant body of research focuses on unsupervised object-centric representation learning, which discovers structured representations directly from raw images, making it suitable for reinforcement learning without external annotations. Examples include Slot Attention and its extensions like SAVi and SAVi++, as well as models like SLATE, STEVE, DINOSAUR, and Slot Contrast, which focus on temporal consistency, reconstruction quality, or feature-level objectives.

Object-centric MBRL methods, like COBRA, FOCUS, OC-STORM, STICA, SOLD, and OCDreamer, incorporate these structured representations to better capture the compositional nature of environments. However, previous object-centric MBRL methods have encountered limitations. For instance, COBRA may lack explicit modeling of object interactions, while FOCUS and OC-STORM often depend on annotated segmentation masks. Other approaches, such as OCDreamer, extend existing frameworks like Dreamer with object-centric components.

This paper introduces COMET, an algorithm that builds upon the MCTS-based MuZero-style latent planning framework, specifically the UniZero pipeline, and integrates unsupervised object-centric representations. It aims to address the limitations of monolithic state embeddings by introducing an explicit object-level inductive bias. The work distinguishes itself by proposing a novel action-object binding mechanism and incorporating object-causal attention, which are designed to improve interaction modeling and focus decision-making on task-relevant objects within a transformer-based world model. By using frozen, pre-trained object-centric encoders, COMET leverages advances in unsupervised representation learning while concentrating its learning efforts on dynamics and control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation for this research stems from the observation that humans can reason about the consequences of their actions by mentally simulating potential future outcomes. In reinforcement learning, world models are designed to replicate this ability to enhance learning efficiency and reduce the need for extensive real-world interactions. However, a significant challenge arises in complex, high-dimensional visual environments where traditional holistic representations struggle to effectively capture the underlying object-level structure and dynamics. Specifically, in many environments, decision-making often depends on a small subset of interacting objects, making a holistic representation inefficient and prone to information overload from irrelevant entities.

The key objectives of this work are articulated as follows:

- **Introduce COMET, an MCTS-based object-centric MBRL algorithm**: The primary objective is to develop a novel algorithm that performs Monte Carlo Tree Search within a slot-structured latent space, moving beyond monolithic state representations common in MuZero-style planning.
- **Integrate a frozen unsupervised object-centric encoder with a transformer-based world model**: The work aims to combine the benefits of advanced unsupervised object-centric representation learning (which decomposes scenes into disentangled object representations) with a transformer-based world model capable of processing these structured latents. The use of a "frozen" encoder is intended to leverage pre-existing perceptual capabilities without requiring additional end-to-end training.
- **Propose a novel action-object binding mechanism**: A specific objective is to implement an action-slot fusion mechanism. This mechanism binds actions to individual object-centric slots, allowing the world model to predict how actions affect specific objects, thereby enabling more granular and accurate object-centric dynamics prediction within a unified transformer backbone.
- **Implement object-causal attention in policy and value heads**: The algorithm aims to incorporate an object-causal attention mechanism. This mechanism modulates token interactions by learned per-slot relevance scores, enabling the policy and value networks to concentrate decision-making and value estimation on objects that are pertinent to the current task.
- **Introduce an explicit object-level inductive bias into MuZero-style latent planning**: By adopting object-centric representations and interaction mechanisms, the research seeks to embed a structured understanding of the environment, mirroring human perception of discrete entities, directly into the planning process of MuZero-like algorithms.
- **Evaluate performance across diverse object-oriented visual control tasks**: A practical objective is to empirically demonstrate that COMET achieves consistent performance and higher sample efficiency during early training stages compared to existing object-centric and monolithic baselines across a varied set of environments, including object-centric benchmarks, robotic manipulation tasks, and game environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

COMET (Causal Object-centric Model for Efficient Tree search) is presented as an MCTS-based object-centric model-based reinforcement learning algorithm designed for planning in a slot-structured latent space. It integrates three main components: a frozen object-centric encoder, a transformer-based world model, and policy/value heads featuring object-causal attention. The implementation builds on the LightZero framework and the UniZero training pipeline, adapting them for object-centric representations.

#### 3.1. Slots Extractor

The slots extractor component is responsible for transforming a raw image observation $o_t$ into a set of object-centric latent representations, denoted as $\bar{s}_t = \{s_1^t, \dots, s_n^t\}$. Here, $n$ is a fixed hyperparameter corresponding to the maximum number of objects in the scene plus one slot for the background. To address the issue of inconsistent slot ordering over time, which arises from the permutation-invariant nature of slot attention in static image-trained architectures, the slots at time $t+1$ are initialized using the slots obtained at time $t$. This promotes temporal consistency in object assignments. The slot extractors (e.g., SLATE, DINOSAUR, Slot Contrast) are pretrained on observations collected via a random policy and subsequently remain frozen during the reinforcement learning phase.

#### 3.2. Object-Centric Token Processing (World Model)

The world model in COMET is built upon a transformer backbone, adapting the UniZero architecture. In contrast to UniZero, which processes a single monolithic state embedding, COMET processes the $n$ object slots, $\bar{s}_t$, producing a set of latent representations $\{h_1^t, \dots, h_n^t\}$. These latents are then used by the policy and value networks.

A key innovation is the **action-object binding mechanism** for predicting the next state. Directly generating $n$ future slots from a single action embedding would create a representational bottleneck. To overcome this, COMET concatenates the action embedding $a_t$ with each individual slot $s_i^t$ (i.e., $a_t \oplus s_i^t$). These concatenated vectors are then passed through a shared MLP projector, resulting in slot-conditioned action embeddings $\bar{a}_t = \{a_1^t, \dots, a_n^t\}$. These $\bar{a}_t$ embeddings are fed into the transformer backbone, yielding latents $\bar{z}_t = \{z_1^t, \dots, z_n^t\}$. These latents are then used to predict the next-step object slots, $\bar{\hat{s}}_{t+1}$, via a shared observation MLP head. For reward prediction, the latents $\bar{z}_t$ are summed into a single vector and processed by an MLP-based reward head.

The input sequence to the transformer consists of blocks corresponding to time steps, each containing $\bar{s}_t$ and $\bar{a}_t$. The transformer uses learnable absolute positional encodings and a custom attention mask. This mask ensures that each slot embedding attends to all slots within its own time block, all slots from previous blocks, and the action embeddings associated with its position. Each action embedding attends to itself and all slots in the current and preceding blocks. This mechanism is conceptualized as a soft action-object binding, where each slot's dynamics are influenced by an action embedding conditioned on its current state.

#### 3.3. Object-Causal Attention (Policy and Value Networks)

The policy and value networks are implemented as separate transformer modules that incorporate a modified attention mechanism, adapted from STICA. In addition to the object latent representations $\{h_1^t, \dots, h_n^t\}$, a learnable target token (specific to either the policy or value head) is provided as input. The transformer's output for this target token is then decoded by an MLP to generate the respective prediction.

To integrate a form of causal structure into attention, a causal graph $G$ is defined. This graph categorizes objects into three groups: target, causal, and non-causal. $G_{i,j}=1$ indicates that group $j$ influences group $i$. In this formulation, causal objects influence the target and each other, while non-causal objects only influence themselves.

Since true object causality is not known a priori, a causality score $\alpha_k^t \in$ is estimated for each latent $h_k^t$. This score represents the learned probability that the corresponding object is relevant for the policy or value prediction. A matrix $W_t$ is constructed using these $\alpha_k^t$ scores, with its first row corresponding to the target token and subsequent rows to the object latents. The product $W_t G W_t^T$ then lifts the group-level causal graph $G$ to token-level interactions, encoding the "strength of causal influence" between token pairs. This transformed matrix modulates the scaled dot-product attention mechanism, concentrating attention on objects that are deemed relevant to the target and suppressing irrelevant ones. The authors clarify that these "causality scores" are learned object-relevance weights rather than independently identified causal effects.

#### 3.4. Policy and World Model Learning

COMET utilizes a MuZero-like learning framework. The model comprises a representation function (encoding observation history into a root latent state), a dynamics function (predicting imagined transitions and rewards), and a prediction function (outputting policy prior and value estimate). MCTS is performed entirely in this learned latent space, using the dynamics function to expand trajectories and the prediction function to evaluate states and provide action priors. After MCTS simulations, the visit counts at the root state are normalized to produce an improved policy target $p_t$.

The model is trained end-to-end by unrolling the dynamics for $K$ steps. The joint optimization objective $L_{\text{COMET}}$ is a sum of four loss terms:
- **Next-state prediction loss**: Mean Squared Error (MSE) between predicted slots $\hat{s}_i^{t+1}$ and ground-truth slots $s_i^{t+1}$ (obtained from the frozen encoder).
- **Reward prediction loss**: Cross-entropy between predicted reward $\hat{r}_t$ and target reward $r_t$.
- **Policy prediction loss**: Cross-entropy between predicted policy $\pi_t$ and the MCTS-improved policy target $p_t$.
- **Value prediction loss**: Cross-entropy between predicted value $\hat{v}_t$ and the bootstrapped n-step TD target $v_t$.
Reward and value predictions are formulated as discrete regression problems in a log-transformed space. The training context length $H$ (transformer context window) matches the rollout length used during training.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

COMET is evaluated across eight visually and dynamically diverse tasks from the Object-Centric Visual RL benchmark (Object Goal, Object Interaction, Object Comparison, Property Comparison, Object Reaching), ManiSkill (Cube Pushing), Robosuite (Block Lifting), and VizDoom (Defend The Line). These environments feature both discrete and continuous action spaces and vary in visual complexity.

For evaluation, COMET is compared against:
- **OCRL**: A model-free, object-centric PPO baseline.
- **OCDreamer**: An object-centric MBRL baseline.
- **SOLD**: An object-centric MBRL baseline (applicable only to continuous action spaces).
- **UniZero**: A monolithic MBRL baseline.

Different pretrained slot extractors (SLATE, DINOSAUR, Slot Contrast) are chosen based on the task, with SLATE models often sourced from the OCRL repository and others trained on collected data. Performance is quantified using a "Mean Normalized Score" which aggregates an agent's performance across tasks by normalizing scores relative to the best-performing method in each environment, addressing differences in evaluation metrics and scales.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of COMET was conducted across eight diverse object-oriented visual control tasks, encompassing various visual complexities and action spaces (discrete and continuous). The performance of COMET was compared against several baselines: OCRL (model-free, object-centric), OCDreamer (model-based, object-centric), SOLD (model-based, object-centric, continuous-only), and UniZero (model-based, monolithic).

The primary finding is that **COMET achieves a higher mean normalized score during the early stages of training** when averaged across all considered tasks, compared to both object-centric and monolithic baselines. This indicates improved sample efficiency in the initial learning phases.

Specific observations regarding performance across different task categories include:

- **Visually Simple Tasks (Object Goal, Object Comparison, Property Comparison)**: In these environments, which often feature a single target object and multiple distractors, COMET demonstrated faster convergence and achieved higher final performance than the baselines. This performance is attributed to COMET's ability to leverage strong object-centric representations and the object-causal attention mechanism. The causality scores, as illustrated in the appendices, generally highlight task-relevant objects by assigning them higher scores, enabling more focused decision-making.
- **Dynamically Complex Tasks (Defend the Line, Object Interaction)**: For tasks with more intricate dynamics, COMET achieved performance comparable to the baselines. The paper suggests this might be due to task-specific factors; for instance, in "Defend the Line," many objects are relevant for prediction, potentially diluting the benefit of selective attention. In "Object Interaction," the goal may involve deeper causal chains of object-pushing mechanics, which might not be fully captured by the current causal object attention mechanism.
- **Challenging Visual Complexity and Control Tasks (Block Lifting, Cube Pushing)**: In these manipulation tasks, COMET exhibited moderate performance and did not consistently outperform all competing methods. The authors attribute this to potential limitations of the underlying object-centric representation models, which sometimes struggled to reliably segment specific objects (e.g., merging the cube with the background into a single slot). In such cases, OCDreamer demonstrated better performance, suggesting its robustness to imperfect representations in these specific settings.

Further supporting observations from the appendices include:

- **Trajectory Rollouts (Appendix H)**: Visualizations of predicted trajectories by COMET's dynamics model generally show accurate prediction of object representations across several tasks. However, in certain scenarios (e.g., Object Interaction when objects are spatially close, or Defend the Line near the trajectory end), the model occasionally produced inaccurate predictions, sometimes correlating with inconsistencies in ground-truth slots inferred by the encoder.
- **Causality Scores (Appendix I)**: Visualizations of per-slot causality scores for the policy and value transformers indicated that COMET typically assigns higher relevance scores to agent-related and target objects. This suggests that the object-causal attention mechanism functions as intended by focusing on pertinent entities. However, instances of incorrect high causality scores for background objects or lower scores for relevant objects were also noted in certain tasks (e.g., Object Interaction, Property Comparison, Defend The Line), suggesting areas for potential improvement in robustly identifying task-relevant entities.

In summary, COMET demonstrates a benefit in sample efficiency during early training stages across a broad spectrum of tasks by integrating object-centric representations and causal attention into an MCTS-based planning framework. Its performance is particularly pronounced in tasks where object identification and selective attention to key entities are crucial.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of COMET contributes to the field of reinforcement learning by integrating object-centric perception with advanced planning algorithms, specifically Monte Carlo Tree Search within a transformer-based world model. The significance and potential impact of this work can be understood through several points:

- **Enhanced Sample Efficiency in Model-Based RL**: By incorporating an explicit object-level inductive bias into MuZero-style latent planning, COMET demonstrates a higher mean normalized score during the early stages of training. This improvement in sample efficiency is critical for applying RL to real-world problems where data collection is expensive or time-consuming. The ability to learn more effectively with less data can accelerate the development and deployment of intelligent agents.
- **Improved Reasoning in Complex Visual Environments**: Traditional monolithic representations often struggle with high-dimensional, cluttered, and dynamic visual scenes. COMET's use of disentangled object-centric representations allows the model to reason about individual entities and their interactions, mirroring human cognitive processes. This structured approach can lead to more robust and generalizable policies, especially in environments inherently composed of multiple interacting objects.
- **Novel Action-Object Binding Mechanism**: The proposed action-slot fusion mechanism is a novel approach to binding actions to specific objects within a transformer backbone. This mechanism allows for a more granular understanding of how actions affect the environment at an object level, moving beyond global state transitions to specific object-state changes. This fine-grained control could be particularly beneficial for robotic manipulation and other tasks requiring precise interaction with individual objects.
- **Interpretable Decision-Making**: The object-causal attention mechanism, by modulating token interactions based on learned per-slot relevance scores, provides a degree of interpretability. It allows researchers to observe which objects the policy and value networks consider important for decision-making. This transparency can be valuable for debugging agents, understanding their reasoning, and potentially ensuring their safety and reliability in critical applications.
- **Advancement in Object-Centric RL**: COMET pushes the boundaries of object-centric reinforcement learning by effectively combining frozen unsupervised object-centric encoders with a sophisticated planning mechanism. This reduces the need for pixel-level reconstruction losses during RL, allowing the focus to shift towards dynamics modeling and control over abstract object representations.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The work identifies limitations, such as the struggle of current unsupervised object-centric models with complex, cluttered scenes and the quadratic scaling of transformer attention. These insights pave the way for future research into more robust object segmentation, efficient transformer architectures, and advanced causal attention mechanisms that dynamically prioritize relevant objects not just for policy/value but also for transition dynamics, potentially leading to better relational reasoning and scalability in highly complex multi-object settings like household tasks.
- **Broad Applicability**: Evaluation across a diverse set of tasks, including object-centric benchmarks, robotic manipulation, and game environments, indicates the potential generalizability of the COMET framework to various control problems with both discrete and continuous action spaces.
