# Embodied.cpp: A Portable Inference Runtime of Embodied AI Models on Heterogeneous Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Embodied.cpp introduces a portable C++ inference runtime designed for efficient deployment of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) and World-Action Models (WAMs) on diverse robotic platforms. This framework enables high task success rates for VLA models and reduces WAM component memory by 71.8% while preserving numerical accuracy.

## Problem

- Deployment of embodied AI models on heterogeneous, resource-constrained robotic hardware is fragmented, often requiring model-specific Python stacks and extensive 'glue code'.
- Existing inference runtimes are not optimized for the unique demands of embodied AI deployment, which include closed-loop control, stringent latency, and efficient batch-1 inference.
- Embodied AI models often require diverse, extensible multimodal input/output interfaces, which are not well-supported by traditional token-based runtimes.

## Method

- Developed **`Embodied.cpp`**, a unified and portable ==C++ inference runtime== specifically engineered for embodied AI models (==VLA== and ==WAM==) on edge devices.
- Designed a ==five-layer architecture== comprising input/deployment adapters, backbone execution, and head plugins, supported by subsystems for ==modular multi-rate execution==, ==latency-first batch-1 inference==, and an ==AI kernel warehouse==.
- Implemented C++ with a ==backend abstraction layer== and ==Q4_K quantization== to ensure high performance and portability across various heterogeneous hardware platforms.

## Results

- Successfully deployed VLA models (HY-VLA and pi0.5) with task success rates of **100.0%** and **91.0%** respectively in closed-loop execution, demonstrating functional accuracy preservation.
- A WAM microbenchmark (LingBot-VA Transformer block) showed a **71.8%** reduction in resident weight memory (from **312.2 MiB** to **88.1 MiB**) using Q4_K quantization.

## Takeaways

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Embodied AI has witnessed significant advancements, leading to the development of a diverse array of models, including Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and World-Action Models (WAMs). These models demonstrate capabilities in perception, language understanding, and action generation for robotic systems. Concurrently, the supporting ecosystem for embodied AI, encompassing data, training frameworks, and simulation environments like LeRobot, Open X-Embodiment, ManiSkill, LIBERO, and Isaac Sim, has also progressed.

Despite these advancements in model architecture and training infrastructure, the practical deployment of embodied AI models onto heterogeneous and resource-constrained robotic hardware remains a challenge. Current deployment practices are characterized by fragmentation, often involving model-specific Python stacks, varied backend assumptions, and extensive robot-side "glue code." This situation necessitates rebuilding significant portions of the software stack for each new model family or hardware platform, hindering widespread adoption and robust robotic autonomy.

Existing inference runtimes, such as llama.cpp, ONNX Runtime, SGLang, and vLLM-Omni, are primarily designed for request-response serving paradigms. These systems often optimize for throughput, assume relatively uniform token interfaces, and are typically deployed in cloud or workstation environments with ample resources. Their design priorities do not align with the specific "runtime contract" required for embodied AI deployment, which involves closed-loop control, stringent latency requirements, batch-1 inference efficiency, and diverse, extensible input/output interfaces.

A recent development, `vla.cpp`, has addressed some of these issues by providing a portable C++ inference runtime for a range of VLA architectures. While an important step, `vla.cpp` remains specialized to VLA models and does not offer first-class support for the expanding family of WAMs or explicit optimization for modular, multi-component embodied models.

`Embodied.cpp` is positioned to bridge this gap by offering a unified, portable C++ inference runtime that supports both VLA models and WAMs. It aims to address the specific demands of real-world robotic deployment on heterogeneous edge devices, thereby contributing to the maturation of embodied AI from research prototypes to deployable robot-side systems. This work seeks to standardize the inference pathway for an evolving set of embodied architectures, reducing the engineering overhead associated with deploying advanced AI models on physical robots and simulators.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a portable C++ inference runtime, named `Embodied.cpp`, capable of efficiently deploying a broad spectrum of embodied AI models, including both Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and World-Action Models (WAMs), onto heterogeneous and resource-constrained robotic platforms. This objective is motivated by the identified limitations of existing inference runtimes, which do not adequately address the unique requirements of embodied AI deployment.

The motivation for `Embodied.cpp` stems from three key challenges that define the runtime contract for embodied inference, distinguishing it from conventional AI model serving:

1. **Multi-rate Execution:** Embodied inference within a closed-loop control system is not always a single, synchronous forward pass. Modern embodied architectures, particularly hierarchical and asynchronous VLA systems, as well as many WAMs, are composed of multiple modules (e.g., perception encoders, transformer backbones, predictive branches, action heads) that may need to operate at different frequencies. For example, a perception module might refresh at a lower rate, while an action head requires a higher control rate. Existing runtimes typically assume uniform execution rates, making it difficult to efficiently manage these decoupled, multi-rate components. The runtime needs to support explicit execution units, pluggable modules, shared state management, and configurable refresh policies to orchestrate these varying rates without imposing a monolithic synchronous path.

2. **Latency-first Closed-Loop Control:** Unlike cloud-based serving, which often optimizes for throughput with large batches or numerous concurrent users, embodied deployment prioritizes stable closed-loop control. This requires minimal latency, low jitter, and efficient batch-1 inference performance. Robotic platforms are diverse, ranging from embedded systems like Jetson and RK-based boards to x86 edge boxes and workstation-class robots. Achieving low-latency, predictable behavior across such heterogeneous hardware in a batch-1 setting presents a performance challenge. The runtime must support techniques like graph replay, buffer reuse, operator fusion, backend-specific dispatch, and optimized host-device data movement to ensure efficient small-batch execution while maintaining stable control performance.

3. **Extensible Embodied Interfaces:** Embodied AI models require input/output capabilities beyond the fixed token interfaces common in traditional LLM or VLM runtimes. Inputs can be multimodal and varied, including images, natural language instructions, proprioceptive data, historical context, force or tactile signals, and simulator-provided states. Outputs can also be heterogeneous, ranging from discrete action tokens or continuous action vectors to action chunks, world predictions, or intermediate control representations (e.g., subgoals). Furthermore, new model families frequently introduce custom operators and specific dependency stacks. A practical embodied inference runtime must therefore provide typed embodied interfaces, support pluggable heads, offer first-class deployment adapters, and maintain broad backend and operator coverage to accommodate evolving embodied paradigms without requiring a complete rebuild of the runtime for each new model variant.

By addressing these challenges, `Embodied.cpp` aims to provide a stable, reusable core for embodied inference, while offering sufficient extensibility to integrate future embodied model variants and optimizations. The project makes three specific contributions: an architectural analysis of representative VLA and WAM models, the design of `Embodied.cpp` as a portable C++ runtime based on a five-layer architecture with backend abstraction, and an evaluation demonstrating improved efficiency and accuracy preservation on VLA models and a WAM benchmark.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The development of `Embodied.cpp` is predicated on an architectural analysis of existing embodied AI models and a set of design principles derived from the unique challenges of embodied deployment.

**Architectural Analysis of Embodied Models:**
The authors performed an architectural analysis of representative embodied models, categorizing them into two primary families for online inference: Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and World-Action Models (WAMs). This classification is based on whether the runtime primarily executes an action policy (VLA) or must jointly maintain predictive state and action generation (WAMs).

- **VLA Models:** This family progresses from monolithic action generation to structured modular execution.
 - **AR-Token VLA:** Models like RT-2 and OpenVLA use a single shared backbone for autoregressive action token generation.
 - **VLM-Backboned VLA:** Models such as Octo, pi0, pi0.5, and MuseVLA utilize a shared visual-language backbone coupled with explicit continuous-action heads.
 - **Hierarchical VLA:** Examples include Hi Robot and RT-H, which separate semantic planning (producing subgoals) from low-level control.
 - **Asynchronous VLA:** Models like GR00T N1 and DAM-VLA decouple modules that operate at different rates, coordinating through buffered state.
- **WAMs:** These models integrate future prediction with action generation.
 - **Predict-then-Act WAM:** UniPi keeps world model prediction and action expert execution as explicit, sequential stages.
 - **Unified AR-Modeling WAM:** WorldVLA and LingBot-VA merge future modeling and action generation into a single autoregressive token space.
 - **Shared-Backbone WAM:** Models such as DreamZero and UWM reuse a backbone for both world modeling and action generation, while potentially allowing separate scheduling or extension of auxiliary blocks.
 - **Latent-space WAM:** LaWAM and Being-H0.7 compress the predictive path by generating a compact latent future or subgoal for an action expert.

This analysis revealed that many recent embodied systems are structured as interacting modules (e.g., planner, backbone, world-model, action modules). Intermediate states (subgoals, buffered context, predicted futures) are explicit runtime objects, and timing structures are often model-defined, requiring stateful multi-component orchestration rather than uniform synchronous request-response.

**Design Principles:**
Based on the challenges and architectural analysis, three design principles guided the development of `Embodied.cpp`:
1. **Modular Multi-rate Execution:** To support decoupled execution, the runtime exposes explicit execution units, pluggable modules, shared state/feature pools, and configurable refresh policies.
2. **Latency-first Fused Execution:** To prioritize stable control, the runtime integrates graph replay, buffer reuse, operator fusion, backend-specific dispatch, and optimized host-device data movement for efficient small-batch inference on heterogeneous devices.
3. **Extensible Operator and I/O Support:** The runtime provides typed embodied interfaces, pluggable heads, first-class deployment adapters, and broad backend/operator coverage to accommodate diverse input/output types and custom operations.

**Runtime Architecture:**
`Embodied.cpp` implements these principles through a unified five-layer architecture:
1. **Input Adapters:** These modules absorb diverse sensor streams (e.g., cameras, force/tactile sensors, IMU data) and offline dataset samples (e.g., LIBERO, DROID, BridgeData, RT-X) through a typed embodied interface.
2. **Sequence Builders:** (Implicitly part of the execution path, assembling inputs for backbone).
3. **Backbone Execution:** This layer provides a reusable core for transformer-style execution, forming the central computational path for various models.
4. **Head Plugins:** These are extensible modules designed for model-specific heads, predictive modules, and custom outputs, allowing divergence from the shared backbone execution.
5. **Deployment Adapters:** These components bridge the runtime's outputs to external systems, including simulators (e.g., Isaac Sim) and real-world robot software stacks.

Beneath this five-layer structure, `Embodied.cpp` integrates three supporting subsystems that directly address the core challenges:
- **Modular Multi-rate Execution:** Manages decoupled scheduling and persistent runtime state for different components.
- **Latency-first Batch-1 Execution:** Facilitates efficient inference on heterogeneous hardware (CPUs, GPUs, NPUs, other accelerators) by prioritizing low latency and predictable timing.
- **Embodied AI Kernel Warehouse:** A repository for reusable operators and model-specific kernels required by evolving embodied architectures.

The entire system is implemented in C++ to leverage its performance characteristics and portability. A backend abstraction layer allows `Embodied.cpp` to operate across heterogeneous edge devices, robots, and simulators using a unified interface. This design aims to maintain a stable deployment boundary while allowing for the evolution of model structures, hardware targets, and embodied interfaces.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of `Embodied.cpp` included deployments of two VLA models and a preliminary microbenchmark for a WAM component, demonstrating the runtime's capabilities in terms of task success, latency, memory efficiency, and accuracy preservation.

**VLA Model Evaluation:**
The runtime was evaluated with two VLA models: HY-VLA and pi0.5, deployed through the `Embodied.cpp` C++ inference path.

- **HY-VLA:**
 - **Task:** RoboTwin `place_empty_cup`.
 - **Success Rate:** Achieved 100.0%.
 - **Amortized Environment-Step Latency:** 735.9 ms.
 - **Server-Side Inference Latency:** 1340.3 ms.
 - **Peak GPU Memory (VRAM):** 6850 MiB.
 - **Characteristics:** This model uses a larger Hunyuan-VL backbone, three-view inputs, and a video-history/MEM vision path, contributing to its higher latency compared to pi0.5.

- **pi0.5:**
 - **Task:** Tested with its corresponding C++ deployment configuration.
 - **Success Rate:** Achieved 91.0%.
 - **Amortized Environment-Step Latency:** 56.85 ms.
 - **Server-Side Inference Latency:** 266.6 ms.
 - **Peak GPU Memory (VRAM):** 6546 MiB.
 - **Characteristics:** This model utilizes a lighter PaliGemma backbone and a longer action chunk, which contributes to its lower amortized step cost.

The results indicate that `Embodied.cpp` successfully supports the deployment of different VLA architectures, enabling correct closed-loop execution while preserving the intended task behavior and accuracy. The observed differences in latency and memory consumption are attributed to variations in model backbone size, visual input complexity, and action chunking strategies employed by the respective VLA models.

**WAM Evaluation (Preliminary Microbenchmark):**
For World-Action Models, a preliminary microbenchmark was conducted using a single Transformer block from LingBot-VA. The full LingBot-VA model was not included in closed-loop evaluation due to stability concerns on the constrained local edge setup used for this draft.

- **Model Component:** First Transformer block of LingBot-VA.
- **Comparison:** Original PyTorch implementation with BF16 weights versus `Embodied.cpp` implementation using GGUF Q4_K quantization.
- **Metrics:** Latency per block, memory per block, Mean Absolute Error (MAE), and Cosine Similarity.
- **Results:**
 - **Python (Original BF16):**
 - Latency/block: 3.236 ms.
 - Memory/block: 312.2 MiB.
 - MAE: 0.
 - Cosine Similarity: 1.
 - **Embodied.cpp (Q4_K Quantized):**
 - Latency/block: 3.171 ms.
 - Memory/block: 88.1 MiB.
 - MAE: < 3.3 × 10⁻².
 - Cosine Similarity: > 9.997 × 10⁻¹.

The evaluation demonstrated that the `Embodied.cpp` implementation with Q4_K quantization reduced the resident weight memory of the Transformer block from 312.2 MiB to 88.1 MiB, a reduction of approximately 71.8%. This memory reduction was achieved with a minimal impact on numerical accuracy, evidenced by a mean absolute error below 3.3 × 10⁻² and a cosine similarity exceeding 9.997 × 10⁻¹. This microbenchmark provides initial evidence that `Embodied.cpp` can host and validate WAM-side Transformer components with substantial memory savings while maintaining output fidelity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of `Embodied.cpp` addresses a critical need within the embodied AI research and deployment landscape. Its significance and potential impact are multifold:

Firstly, `Embodied.cpp` offers a **unified and portable inference runtime** for a broad spectrum of embodied AI models, encompassing both Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and World-Action Models (WAMs). This directly tackles the existing fragmentation in deployment practices, where model-specific software stacks and extensive custom integration are often required. By providing a stable C++ core and extensible plugin architecture, `Embodied.cpp` reduces the engineering overhead associated with transitioning embodied AI research from laboratories to real-world and simulated robotic systems.

Secondly, the runtime's design principles directly address the **unique challenges of embodied deployment on heterogeneous and resource-constrained edge devices**. By supporting modular multi-rate execution, `Embodied.cpp` enables efficient orchestration of complex, multi-component models where different parts operate at varying frequencies, which is crucial for hierarchical and asynchronous control systems. Its emphasis on latency-first fused execution ensures low-latency, low-jitter, and efficient batch-1 inference, which is fundamental for stable closed-loop robot control. This capability is particularly important for deploying advanced models on hardware with limited computational and memory resources, ranging from embedded boards to edge servers.

Thirdly, `Embodied.cpp` facilitates **extensible embodied interfaces**, moving beyond the fixed token I/O common in general LLM runtimes. By supporting diverse multimodal inputs (e.g., images, language, proprioception, tactile feedback) and heterogeneous outputs (e.g., action chunks, world predictions), the runtime can accommodate the evolving data types and action representations used in embodied AI. This extensibility, combined with support for custom operators and backend abstraction, positions `Embodied.cpp` to remain relevant as new embodied model paradigms emerge, reducing the need for complete runtime redesigns.

The empirical results demonstrate the practical benefits of `Embodied.cpp`. The successful closed-loop deployment of HY-VLA and pi0.5, with task success rates of 100.0% and 91.0% respectively, indicates that the runtime can effectively execute complex VLA models while preserving their functional accuracy. Furthermore, the preliminary WAM microbenchmark on LingBot-VA showcased a significant memory reduction (from 312.2 MiB to 88.1 MiB) through quantization, with minimal impact on numerical output fidelity. This finding suggests the potential for deploying larger and more complex WAMs on memory-constrained edge hardware, which was previously challenging.

The availability of `Embodied.cpp` as an open-source project (via the provided GitHub link) further amplifies its potential impact. It can serve as a common infrastructure for the embodied AI community, fostering collaboration, accelerating research translation, and enabling broader access to efficient deployment solutions for robotics. This work represents a step towards making advanced embodied AI models more accessible and deployable in diverse robotic applications, moving beyond theoretical benchmarks to practical, reliable, and resource-efficient robot-side systems.
