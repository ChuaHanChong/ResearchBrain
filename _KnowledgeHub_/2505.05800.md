# 3D CAVLA: Leveraging Depth and 3D Context to Generalize Vision Language Action Models for Unseen Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> 3D-CAVLA, developed by NYU researchers, enhances Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by integrating depth, chain-of-thought narratives, and task-aware region of interest detection, achieving an absolute 8.8% improvement in success rate on unseen robotic manipulation tasks compared to OpenVLA-OFT.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models exhibit limited generalization capabilities, performing poorly on unseen tasks or environments.
- Many VLAs primarily rely on 2D visual data, lacking sufficient spatial or semantic context for complex robotic manipulation.
- Current VLAs often learn direct input-output mappings, bypassing explicit intermediate reasoning steps that could improve task decomposition and transfer.

## Method

- The **3D-CAVLA** model enhances ==OpenVLA-OFT== by integrating ==Chain-of-Thought Narrative Instructions==, using GPT-4 to decompose tasks into step-by-step prompts.
- It incorporates ==depth features== by converting RGB-D camera data into ==3D point clouds==, processed by a lightweight, trainable ==depth encoder==.
- It implements ==Task Aware Region of Interest (TA-ROI) Detection== to focus visual attention on relevant objects and regions through an offline pipeline of ==named entity recognition==, object detection, and tracking.

## Results

- 3D-CAVLA achieved an average success rate of **98.1%** on in-distribution LIBERO benchmarks with a dual camera setup, outperforming OpenVLA-OFT (**97.1%**).
- On the new LIBERO-Unseen benchmark of novel tasks, 3D-CAVLA demonstrated an absolute improvement of **8.8%** over OpenVLA-OFT, achieving a **45.2%** success rate.
- Ablation studies indicated that the integration of depth maps caused the highest performance improvement, underscoring their critical importance for robust policy learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Incorporating 3D spatial information (depth maps) is the most impactful addition for robust VLA policy learning, significantly improving precision.
> - Explicit intermediate reasoning via chain-of-thought prompts aids VLA generalization, especially for complex, long-horizon tasks.
> - Task-aware region of interest detection helps focus attention for generalization, though it can sometimes hinder performance on seen tasks by excluding useful context.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is actively pursuing the development of systems capable of perceiving, reasoning about, and interacting with their environments. A significant challenge lies in enabling robots to perform complex manipulation tasks with adaptability similar to humans, especially when encountering novel scenarios. Recent advancements in foundation models, particularly Vision-Language Models (VLMs), have shown promise in bridging this gap by integrating visual perception and natural language understanding. VLMs, trained on extensive internet data, can interpret visual inputs, comprehend language instructions, and generate contextually relevant outputs, which are valuable capabilities for tasks such as visual question answering, visual grounding, and task planning in robotics.

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models extend VLMs by translating vision and language inputs directly into robot actions. These models are typically pre-trained on large-scale real-world datasets and then fine-tuned on high-quality teleoperated demonstrations to predict robot joint movements or end-effector velocities. While current VLA models, such as OpenVLA and its variants, exhibit high success rates (often ≥95%) on tasks within their training distribution (in-domain tasks), their ability to generalize to unseen or out-of-distribution tasks remains a significant limitation. This generalization gap restricts their deployment in varied, unstructured environments.

Existing research has explored several avenues to address this generalization challenge. One direction involves incorporating explicit reasoning mechanisms within VLA training objectives, often inspired by Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting from large language models (LLMs). Approaches like ECoT and CoT-VLA aim to enforce step-by-step reasoning or predict intermediate visual goals. Another area focuses on enhancing spatial grounding through improved geometric representations or multimodal inputs, recognizing that robust manipulation requires understanding 3D object geometry and spatial relationships, which RGB-only inputs may not fully capture. Recent works such as GeoVLA and QDepth-VLA have begun to integrate 3D geometric priors and depth-aware representations.

This work, 3D-CAVLA, positions itself within this landscape by proposing a finetuning framework that combines advancements in both reasoning and spatial perception. It specifically augments existing VLA architectures with three components: chain-of-thought narrative instructions, depth-aware perception, and task-oriented region-of-interest detection. The aim is to leverage a structured reasoning approach and richer 3D environmental context to improve generalization, particularly for zero-shot performance on unseen tasks, while maintaining high inference speed. By integrating these elements, 3D-CAVLA seeks to create more robust and adaptable robotic manipulation policies that can bridge the simulation-to-real gap more effectively.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the task generalization capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, enabling them to perform effectively on tasks that were not explicitly included in their training datasets. This involves addressing the limitations of current VLA models, which typically achieve high success rates on in-domain tasks but demonstrate reduced performance when faced with novel task variations or unseen object configurations.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges in robotic manipulation:
- **Limited Generalization:** Existing VLA models, despite leveraging large-scale pre-training data, often struggle to generalize beyond the specific distributions encountered during fine-tuning. This "in-distribution overfitting" prevents their widespread applicability in dynamic real-world environments where task variations are common. The authors observe that even state-of-the-art VLAs exhibit significant performance drops on unseen tasks.
- **Lack of Structured Reasoning:** Many VLAs operate on direct input-output mappings (RGB images + language to actions) without explicitly incorporating intermediate reasoning steps. Human task execution often involves breaking down complex instructions into sequential sub-goals and leveraging contextual understanding. Without such structured reasoning, VLA policies may fail to adapt to tasks that require re-composition of learned skills or understanding of implicit steps.
- **Insufficient 3D Spatial Understanding:** Traditional VLA models predominantly rely on 2D RGB image inputs. While effective for visual feature extraction, these models may lack explicit 3D geometric and spatial understanding, which is crucial for precise object localization, manipulation in cluttered environments, and robust interaction with objects of varying shapes and sizes. The absence of depth information can lead to ambiguities in spatial reasoning and hinder accurate action generation.
- **Inefficient Perception:** Visual inputs often contain a substantial amount of information irrelevant to the immediate task. Processing the entire image frame can dilute the focus on critical task-relevant objects or regions, potentially increasing noise and reducing efficiency in policy learning and inference.
- **Training Efficiency:** Current VLA models, especially those involving complex reasoning, can require extensive training data and computational resources, leading to long convergence times. Improving training efficiency while enhancing performance is a practical consideration for developing scalable robotic systems.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of 3D-CAVLA are:
1. To integrate Chain-of-Thought (CoT) style narrative prompts into VLA input instructions, providing enriched contextual reasoning and promoting structured decision-making for long-horizon and novel tasks.
2. To incorporate depth-aware features derived from workspace point clouds, thereby equipping the VLA policies with robust 3D spatial understanding for more precise object interaction and scene interpretation.
3. To implement task-oriented Region-Of-Interest (ROI) detection, focusing the VLA's visual attention on task-relevant regions to enhance manipulation accuracy and reduce distractions from irrelevant visual information.
4. To demonstrate that these integrated components lead to improved performance on both in-domain tasks and, critically, significant gains in zero-shot generalization capabilities on unseen tasks in simulation.
5. To validate the effectiveness and transferability of the proposed framework to real-world robotic systems, demonstrating performance gains and faster training convergence on physical robot manipulation tasks.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to contribute towards developing more adaptable, robust, and efficient VLA policies for robotic manipulation, narrowing the gap between simulated performance and practical deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The 3D-CAVLA framework is presented as a finetuning paradigm designed to enhance task generalization of existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, rather than a novel architectural design. It builds upon OpenVLA-OFT, an open-source autoregressive VLA known for its computational efficiency. The core approach involves augmenting the standard VLA policy with three key components: chain-of-thought (CoT) narrative instructions, depth-based 3D features, and task-aware region-of-interest (TA-ROI) pooling.

**Standard VLA Policy Overview:**
A VLA policy, denoted as $\pi_{\theta}$, maps multimodal observations (visual inputs $x_{vis_t}$, language instructions $x_{text}$, and robot proprioceptive states $x_{prop_t}$) to low-level control actions $a_t$. The visual inputs are processed by a vision encoder $f_{vis}$ to extract patch-level embeddings. Language instructions are tokenized and processed by a language encoder $f_{text}$. Proprioceptive states are encoded by a multilayer perceptron (MLP) $f_{prop}$. These embeddings are then projected into a common latent space, concatenated to form a fused representation $z_t$, and fed into an LLM backbone $f_{LLM}$. Finally, a linear head maps the LLM output to the desired N-dimensional action vector, typically representing end-effector 6D pose and gripper state. The model is trained using an $\ell_1$ regression loss on teleoperated demonstrations.

**Proposed 3D-CAVLA Components:**

1. **Chain-of-Thought Narrative Instructions:**
 - **Motivation:** Standard single-sentence language instructions may lack the structured reasoning necessary for complex, generalizable tasks.
 - **Approach:** Instead of plain natural language, task instructions $x_{text}$ are augmented with CoT decompositions, denoted as $\tilde{x}_{text}$. Each task instruction is rephrased into a sequence of intermediate steps (e.g., "Grasp ball → move over basket → release").
 - **Implementation:** These narrative instructions are generated automatically offline using a frozen GPT-5.2 model. A specific prompt is designed to guide GPT-5.2 in breaking down task instructions into executable steps based on real-world intuition and camera view (Figure 2 in the paper). The generated $\tilde{x}_{text}$ are then tokenized and processed by the VLA's standard language encoder $f_{text}$ to produce enriched text embeddings $\tilde{l}$.
 - **Rationale:** Using a frozen LLM for CoT reasoning prevents overfitting to training distributions and allows generalization to unseen tasks where only objects change, but the reasoning structure remains similar. This avoids the need for the VLA itself to learn to predict reasoning tokens, which could degrade performance on out-of-distribution data.

2. **Integrating Depth Features:**
 - **Motivation:** RGB-only inputs lack explicit 3D geometric information, limiting spatial reasoning crucial for robust manipulation, especially in crowded environments.
 - **Approach:** The framework incorporates depth information from RGB-D sensors. For each RGB-D input, a metric 3D point cloud $P$ is recovered using camera intrinsics.
 - **Implementation:** A lightweight, PointNet-inspired encoder $f_{depth}$ is employed to transform the point cloud $P$ into depth embeddings $d_t$. This encoder is designed to be compact (approximately 1 million parameters) to allow for multiple camera views without significant computational overhead. These depth embeddings $d_t$ are then concatenated with the vision, language, and proprioception embeddings ($\tilde{v}_t$, $\tilde{l}$, $p_t$) before the fusion step (Eq. 6).
 - **Rationale:** Provides fine-grained spatial cues, enhancing geometric understanding and object localization, particularly beneficial for manipulation of novel objects and in complex scenes.

3. **Task-Aware Region of Interest Detection (TA-ROI):**
 - **Motivation:** Visual embeddings from an entire image may contain irrelevant patches, diluting the focus on task-critical regions and objects.
 - **Approach:** During training, binary ROI masks $M$ are estimated to highlight task-relevant visual regions.
 - **Implementation:** A pipeline combines named-entity recognition (using GLiNER) to extract objects and locations from $x_{text}$, object detection (using Molmo) to localize these entities, and object tracking (using SAMURAI) to follow their motion across frames. The union of these detections forms a binary mask $M$. This mask is then used to pool visual features: $\tilde{v}_{ROI_t} = \text{Pool}(\tilde{v}_t, M)$, concentrating the visual representation on areas critical for action generation.
 - **Training Strategy:** To prevent over-dependence on masks and preserve broader contextual cues, ROI pooling is stochastically disabled for 30% of training samples.
 - **Inference Strategy:** During inference, when teleoperated object tracking data is unavailable, Molmo and SAM 2 are used to generate relevant object and gripper masks, whose union defines the task-relevant image region.
 - **Rationale:** Focuses the VLA's attention, improving efficiency and robustness by reducing distractions from background elements. This is particularly beneficial for zero-shot generalization as it helps the policy concentrate on the novel target object.

**Modularity and Efficiency:**
The 3D-CAVLA framework emphasizes modularity. The CoT reasoning and TA-ROI mask generation are primarily performed offline or with frozen external models, ensuring they do not impact the VLA's real-time control frequency during policy rollout. The depth encoder is lightweight and runs in parallel with existing vision and language encoders. This design ensures that 3D-CAVLA operates at a similar inference frequency to standard VLAs (4.3 Hz with depth vs. 4.4 Hz without depth), while avoiding the computational burden of retraining large reasoning models or requiring explicit reasoning token prediction during inference.

**Experimental Setup:**
Experiments are conducted on a single NVIDIA A100 GPU. LoRA fine-tuning and action chunking are adopted, consistent with other VLA baselines. CoT decompositions and ROI masks are pre-computed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of 3D-CAVLA was conducted across three stages: in-domain performance on the LIBERO benchmark, zero-shot generalization on unseen tasks, and real-world validation using a Franka robotic arm.

**A. Comparisons on LIBERO Benchmarks (In-domain Performance)**

- **Benchmark Suites:** 3D-CAVLA was evaluated on the four LIBERO task suites: LIBERO-Spatial (same object, different locations), LIBERO-Object (fixed location, different object), LIBERO-Goal (higher-level goals), and LIBERO-Long (long-horizon, multi-object tasks).
- **Performance with RGB + Language:** With only third-person RGB input and text instructions, 3D-CAVLA showed improvements in success rates on the Spatial (86.1% vs 85.3% for CoA-VLA) and Long (66.8% vs 55.0% for CoA-VLA) suites, and slightly outperformed the Diffusion Transformer on average (82.6% vs 82.4%). This suggests that chain-of-thought instructions provide reusable semantic structure for more precise action generation.
- **Performance with Dual Camera + Proprioception + Depth:** When augmented with robot proprioception, an additional wrist camera, and depth maps, 3D-CAVLA consistently surpassed all baselines across all four suites. It achieved an average success rate of 98.1%, compared to 97.1% for OpenVLA-OFT. This outcome indicates the value of augmenting 2D inputs with 3D spatial cues, particularly in complex or crowded environments where accurate object localization is critical.

**B. Zero-Shot Evaluation (Unseen Tasks)**

- **LIBERO-Unseen Benchmark:** A new benchmark of 10 unseen tasks was created by modifying BDDL specifications to feature novel combinations of familiar objects and goal locations.
- **Performance:** 3D-CAVLA achieved an average success rate of 45.2% on these unseen tasks. This represents an absolute improvement of 8.8% over OpenVLA-OFT (36.4%) and 4.6% over ECoT* (40.6%). This finding underscores the framework's ability to generalize beyond its training distribution.
- **Contributing Factors:** The improved zero-shot performance was attributed to:
 - **Chain-of-Thought (CoT):** Decomposing unseen tasks into sub-steps that were partially observed during training.
 - **Task-Aware Region-of-Interest (TA-ROI):** Focusing attention on relevant regions, which reduced distractions and aided in applying learned skills to novel contexts.
 - **Depth Features:** Providing crucial 3D cues for transferring skills to new situations and enhancing precision.
- **Comparison with ECoT\*:** While ECoT\* uses reasoning, its performance degrades on unseen tasks due to overfitting its reasoning token predictions to the training distribution. 3D-CAVLA's use of a frozen LLM for CoT input prevents this. Furthermore, 3D-CAVLA maintains a higher control frequency (4.3 Hz) compared to ECoT\* (approximately 1.5 Hz).
- **Qualitative Observations (Table III):** 3D-CAVLA consistently localized target objects and generated motions aligned with task structure on unseen tasks, whereas OpenVLA-OFT often defaulted to similar training trajectories, leading to errors. The integration of depth was noted to provide significant gains in cluttered scenes by enabling better spatial disambiguation.
- **Failure Modes:** Two primary failure modes were observed:
 - **Object Misidentification:** Errors sometimes arose from inaccurate object masks generated by Molmo, leading the policy to target incorrect objects.
 - **Unfavorable Grasp Angles:** The policy occasionally struggled to complete grasps due to limited variations in training data for contact points and approach angles, resulting in oscillatory behavior near target objects.

**C. Ablation Studies**

- **Impact of Components:**
 - **Without Depth:** Removing depth features resulted in the most substantial decline in performance, dropping from 98.1% to 97.0% on seen tasks and from 45.2% to 41.0% on unseen tasks. This highlights the criticality of 3D perception for robust manipulation and generalization.
 - **Without CoT:** Performance decreased from 98.1% to 97.4% on seen tasks and from 45.2% to 42.4% on unseen tasks.
 - **Without TA-ROI:** While marginally improving in-distribution performance (98.2% vs 98.1%), its absence led to a notable drop on unseen tasks (from 45.2% to 41.4%).
- **Conclusion:** The ablation studies confirmed that each component of 3D-CAVLA contributes significantly to generalization performance, with depth features being particularly impactful.

**D. Real-World Robot Experiments**

- **Setup:** Experiments were conducted on a Franka robotic arm for ten tabletop pick-and-place tasks, using 50 teleoperated demonstrations per task. Policies were evaluated on "seen," "similar," and "unseen" tasks (compounded tasks with seen motions but unseen instructions).
- **Performance:**
 - **Seen Tasks:** 3D-CAVLA (90.0%) showed comparable performance to OpenVLA-OFT (88.6%).
 - **Similar Tasks:** All models struggled, but 3D-CAVLA (60.0%) still outperformed OpenVLA-OFT (54.4%), suggesting CoT prompts aid in breaking down instructions for novel combinations of seen subtasks.
 - **Unseen Tasks:** 3D-CAVLA achieved a 38.0% success rate, outperforming OpenVLA-OFT (30.2%) by 25%. This demonstrates that the benefits of structured reasoning, 3D spatial awareness, and ROI-guided perception translate effectively from simulation to physical robots.
- **Training Efficiency:** 3D-CAVLA converged in 3,000 epochs, which is over 3 times faster than OpenVLA-OFT, requiring 10,000 epochs.
- **Inference Frequency:** Both OpenVLA-OFT and 3D-CAVLA maintained similar test-time frequencies (4.4 Hz and 4.3 Hz respectively), as the CoT and ROI detection components are computed offline.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of 3D-CAVLA represents a methodical advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly in addressing the critical challenge of task generalization for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The significance and potential impact of this work can be understood through several dimensions:

- **Enhanced Generalization to Unseen Tasks:** The most notable contribution is the significant improvement in zero-shot generalization capabilities. By achieving an 8.8% absolute improvement in success rate on unseen tasks in simulation and a 25% gain on unseen real-world tasks compared to leading baselines, 3D-CAVLA demonstrates a more robust ability to adapt to novel scenarios. This advancement is crucial for deploying robots in dynamic, unstructured environments where it is impractical to train for every conceivable task variation.

- **Improved Spatial Reasoning and Precision:** The integration of depth-based 3D features provides VLAs with explicit geometric understanding, moving beyond purely 2D perception. This enhanced spatial awareness leads to more precise object localization and manipulation, especially in cluttered scenes. Such precision is vital for complex manipulation tasks requiring fine motor control and collision avoidance, thus increasing the reliability of robotic systems.

- **Structured Reasoning for Complex Tasks:** Incorporating Chain-of-Thought (CoT) narrative instructions allows for the decomposition of complex, long-horizon tasks into manageable sub-steps. This structured reasoning approach, derived from frozen foundational models, provides a robust mechanism for task planning and execution that is less prone to overfitting to training data distributions, thereby fostering better transferability of skills.

- **Efficient and Focused Perception:** Task-Aware Region-of-Interest (TA-ROI) pooling directs the VLA's visual attention to the most relevant parts of the scene. This focused perception reduces distractions and can lead to more efficient policy learning and more accurate action generation. The ability to concentrate on critical visual cues is particularly beneficial when faced with novel objects or environmental layouts.

- **Practical Applicability and Efficiency:** 3D-CAVLA is presented as a finetuning framework, meaning it can be applied to existing VLA architectures, facilitating its adoption and integration into current robotic systems. The demonstrated over 3x faster training convergence and maintenance of high inference frequency (4.3 Hz) make the approach computationally efficient and practical for real-time robotic control, addressing key bottlenecks in deploying complex AI models on physical hardware.

- **Bridging the Simulation-to-Real Gap:** The successful validation of 3D-CAVLA on a physical Franka robotic arm, with consistent outperformance on "similar" and "unseen" real-world tasks, provides strong evidence for the framework's ability to translate from simulation to real-world deployment. This is a crucial step towards building truly general-purpose robotic manipulators.

- **Direction for Future Research:** The identified failure modes, such as reliance on object masks for accurate identification and limitations in generalizing grasp approaches, offer clear avenues for future research. This includes exploring stronger grounding mechanisms, incorporating geometric priors, and diversifying training data with negative examples or varied grasp poses to further enhance robustness and adaptability.

- **Community Contribution:** The commitment to releasing code and datasets is significant, as it promotes reproducibility, facilitates comparative studies, and encourages further community-driven research in VLA for robotic manipulation.

In conclusion, 3D-CAVLA contributes to making robotic manipulation systems more intelligent, adaptable, and deployable in a broader range of real-world scenarios. By systematically enhancing spatial perception, structured reasoning, and focused attention within existing VLA frameworks, this work moves towards building generalizable robotic policies capable of handling the inherent variability and uncertainty of physical environments.
