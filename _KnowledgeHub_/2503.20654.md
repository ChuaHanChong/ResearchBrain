# AccidentSim: Generating Vehicle Collision Videos with Physically Realistic Collision Trajectories from Real-World Accident Reports

> [!summary] Summary
> AccidentSim, developed by researchers from Beijing Technology and Business University and The University of Sydney, generates physically realistic vehicle collision videos by integrating real-world accident reports with a physics simulator and a fine-tuned large language model. This framework produces accurate pre- and post-collision trajectories, addressing the scarcity of safety-critical data for autonomous driving systems.

## Problem

- Autonomous driving systems suffer from the "Curse of Rarity," lacking sufficient real-world data for rare, safety-critical scenarios like vehicle collisions.
- Current video generation models fail to produce physically realistic vehicle collision dynamics, particularly post-collision trajectories, as they do not account for physical constraints.
- Physical simulators for collision scenarios are computationally intensive, require precise parameter specification, and cannot easily interpret natural language descriptions for diverse scenario generation.

## Method

- A multi-stage framework that first plans pre-collision trajectories by extracting parameters from accident descriptions and identifying valid lane paths.
- Predicts physically realistic post-collision trajectories using AccidentLLM, a large language model fine-tuned on data generated from real-world accident reports within a ==physics simulator==.
- Synthesizes complete collision videos by integrating the generated pre- and post-collision trajectories with rendered vehicle models and dynamic backgrounds.

## Results

- Generated trajectories demonstrated lower L2 errors and significantly smaller MAE/RMSE for impulse and momentum changes, indicating higher physical consistency compared to existing methods.
- Videos achieved the highest VBench Motion Smoothness (**0.997**) and superior scores in human and GPT-4o evaluations for Physical Realism and Semantic Consistency.
- Training autonomous driving systems with AccidentSim-generated videos reduced the average collision rate to **0.073**, a substantial improvement over baselines (e.g., **0.552** without this data).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physically consistent collision dynamics, especially post-collision trajectories, can be effectively learned and generated by an LLM (AccidentLLM) when trained on data distilled from a high-fidelity physics simulator.
> - Real-world accident reports provide rich, underutilized physical cues and contextual information that can be extracted to ground and diversify synthetic collision scenarios.
> - The fusion of natural language understanding, robust physical simulation, and generative AI enables the creation of diverse and controllable physically realistic collision videos.
