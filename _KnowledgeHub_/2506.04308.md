# RoboRefer: Towards Spatial Referring with Reasoning in Vision-Language Models for Robotics

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboRefer introduces a 3D-aware Vision-Language Model that enhances spatial referring with reasoning for embodied robots. It achieves precise spatial understanding and multi-step reasoning by integrating a dedicated depth encoder, a two-stage supervised and reinforcement fine-tuning strategy, and a new large-scale dataset, outperforming existing models on complex spatial tasks.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle with accurate understanding of complex 3D scenes and dynamic reasoning for instruction-indicated locations, often suffering from 'modality gaps' or 'modality interference'.
- Most research focuses on single-step spatial understanding, with multi-step reasoning (breaking down intricate instructions into sequential references) remaining largely underexplored, and supervised fine-tuning (SFT) often leading to memorization rather than generalized explicit reasoning.
- There is a critical scarcity of large-scale, high-quality datasets and benchmarks explicitly annotated for multi-step spatial reasoning, hindering the development and rigorous evaluation of models in this area.

## Method

- **RoboRefer** employs a disentangled VLM architecture with separate RGB and dedicated depth encoders to prevent modality interference, preserving pre-trained RGB encoder integrity while enhancing precise 3D perception.
- A two-stage fine-tuning approach combines ==Supervised Fine==-tuning (SFT) for foundational spatial understanding with Reinforcement Fine-tuning (RFT), guided by metric-sensitive process reward functions, to foster explicit, generalized multi-step spatial reasoning.
- The research introduces `RefSpatial`, a large-scale dataset (20M QA pairs, up to 5 reasoning steps) supporting both SFT and RFT, and `RefSpatial-Bench`, a challenging benchmark for multi-step reasoning in real-world cluttered scenes, addressing data and evaluation gaps.

## Results

- RoboRefer-8B-SFT achieved state-of-the-art performance on single-step spatial understanding benchmarks, with an average success rate of **89.6%**, surpassing Gemini-**2.5**-Pro by **5%** and baseline NVILA-2B by **21.7%**.
- On `RefSpatial-Bench`, RFT-trained RoboRefer-2B demonstrated superior multi-step reasoning, outperforming Gemini-**2.5**-Pro by **17.4%** average accuracy and showing a **9.1%** improvement in generalization to unseen spatial combinations over SFT-only models.
- RoboRefer enabled robust real-world robotic applications, achieving a **6.8%** absolute improvement in success rate over VLA baselines in simulation and demonstrating the ability to execute long-horizon, dynamic manipulation and navigation tasks on various real robots while adapting to real-time disturbances.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A dedicated depth encoder, separate from the RGB encoder, effectively prevents modality interference and enhances precise 3D spatial understanding without compromising the VLM's general visual knowledge.
> - Reinforcement Fine-tuning (RFT) with metric-sensitive process reward functions is crucial for teaching explicit, generalized multi-step spatial reasoning, overcoming the memorization tendencies of SFT for complex, sequential tasks.
> - A comprehensive data recipe, integrating 2D web images, 3D embodied videos, and simulated scenes with ground-truth reasoning processes, is essential for training models capable of both precise spatial understanding and robust multi-step reasoning across diverse environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied AI, where robots interact intelligently with the physical 3D world, is rapidly advancing, with spatial intelligence being a cornerstone capability. Within this, "spatial referring" – the ability of an agent to interpret and act upon spatially constrained instructions – is a critical yet underexplored challenge. Traditional Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have shown remarkable capabilities in general visual understanding, but their application to complex 3D spatial reasoning for robotics often falls short.

This work addresses two distinct levels of complexity in spatial referring:
1. **Single-step spatial understanding:** This involves recognizing objects' spatial properties (position, orientation) and relations (distance, direction). While much current VLM research focuses here, existing methods struggle. Some approaches require costly 3D reconstruction from multi-view images, creating "modality gaps," while others treat depth as an RGB-like input, leading to "modality interference" and degrading pre-trained image encoders.
2. **Multi-step spatial reasoning:** This transcends basic understanding by requiring compositional reasoning to resolve intricate spatial references sequentially. This capability is crucial for sophisticated embodied intelligence but remains significantly underexplored due to a scarcity of suitable datasets and benchmarks. Current supervised fine-tuning (SFT) approaches for VLMs often lead to memorization rather than generalized explicit reasoning.

RoboRefer significantly advances the state-of-the-art by specifically tackling these limitations. It bridges the gap between powerful VLMs and robust 3D spatial reasoning for robotics by:
- **Overcoming Modality Interference:** Instead of treating depth as an RGB-like input through a shared encoder, RoboRefer employs a dedicated depth encoder. This disentangled approach preserves the integrity of pre-trained RGB encoders while enhancing precise 3D perception, mitigating issues like modality interference and the need for extensive co-training data.
- **Enabling Multi-Step Reasoning:** It explicitly introduces a Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) stage after SFT, guided by metric-sensitive process reward functions, to teach the VLM how to break down complex instructions into sequential analytical steps. This is a crucial departure from implicit reasoning via SFT, fostering better generalization.
- **Addressing Data Scarcity:** The introduction of `RefSpatial`, a large-scale, high-quality dataset, and `RefSpatial-Bench`, a challenging benchmark with multi-step reasoning tasks and real-world scenes, directly fills the critical data and evaluation gaps that have hindered progress in this area. `RefSpatial` significantly expands on prior datasets by covering more spatial relations and supporting deeper reasoning processes.

Compared to related work like tool-based VLM approaches that rely on external vision foundation models for spatial cues, or data-driven methods that fine-tune VLMs on pseudo-3D or real-world 3D datasets, RoboRefer offers a more integrated and explicit reasoning framework. Its sequential SFT-RFT training strategy, combined with a bespoke dataset designed for multi-step reasoning, positions it as a pioneering effort towards truly intelligent embodied robots capable of understanding and executing complex spatial commands in dynamic 3D environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a Vision-Language Model (VLM) capable of robust and precise **spatial referring with reasoning** for embodied robots operating in complex 3D physical environments. The authors identify several key motivations for pursuing this objective:

1. **Fundamental Capability for Embodied AI:** Spatial referring is highlighted as a fundamental requirement for robots to interact effectively with the 3D world. Without it, robots cannot accurately interpret instructions like "pick the apple in front of the logo side of the leftmost cup" or "place it between the plate nearest to me and the soy sauce dish."
2. **Limitations of Existing VLMs in 3D Scenes:** While pre-trained VLMs are powerful, they are not yet "qualified to accurately understand the complex 3D scenes and dynamically reason about the instruction-indicated locations for interaction." Current methods often suffer from:
 - **Modality Gaps:** Requiring costly 3D reconstruction from multi-view images.
 - **Modality Interference:** Treating depth as an RGB-like input through a shared encoder, which degrades pre-trained image encoders and demands additional co-training data.
3. **Lack of Multi-Step Spatial Reasoning:** Most existing research focuses on single-step spatial understanding, which forms a foundation but is insufficient for complex tasks. Multi-step reasoning—breaking down intricate instructions into sequential, simpler spatial references—remains largely underexplored.
4. **Dataset and Benchmark Scarcity:** The absence of large-scale, high-quality datasets explicitly annotated for multi-step spatial reasoning, along with corresponding benchmarks, has limited model development and evaluation in this crucial area. Existing datasets either have a limited number of spatial relations or lack explicit reasoning process annotations.
5. **Over-reliance on Implicit Reasoning (SFT):** Current VLMs predominantly use supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for reasoning. While effective for implicit learning, SFT often leads to memorization of answers rather than generalized explicit reasoning, which hinders robustness and accuracy in open-world scenarios.

To address these motivations, the authors propose RoboRefer with the following key objectives:
- To achieve **precise spatial understanding** by integrating a disentangled, dedicated depth encoder via supervised fine-tuning (SFT), thereby avoiding modality interference.
- To enable **generalized multi-step spatial reasoning** through reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT), utilizing metric-sensitive process reward functions tailored for spatial referring tasks.
- To create and release **RefSpatial**, a large-scale dataset (20M QA pairs, 31 spatial relations, up to 5 reasoning steps) that supports both SFT and RFT, specifically designed for spatial referring with reasoning.
- To introduce **RefSpatial-Bench**, a challenging benchmark for evaluating spatial referring with multi-step reasoning in real-world cluttered scenes, addressing the current evaluation gap.
- To demonstrate RoboRefer's capability for **real-world robotic applications**, including long-horizon, dynamic manipulation and navigation tasks across diverse robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoboRefer's methodology is built upon a novel 3D-aware Vision-Language Model (VLM) architecture and a two-stage training strategy (SFT-RFT), supported by a custom-designed, large-scale dataset and benchmark.

#### 3.1. VLM Architecture

RoboRefer employs a disentangled architecture designed to process both RGB and depth information effectively:
- **Separate RGB and Depth Encoders:** Unlike prior methods that share an encoder or treat depth as an RGB-like input, RoboRefer uses distinct encoders for RGB images and depth maps. The RGB encoder is initialized from a powerful pre-trained VLM (NVILA with siglip-so400m-patch14-448), while the dedicated depth encoder structurally mirrors it and is initialized with its weights. This design prevents modality interference, preserves the RGB encoder's general VQA performance, and enhances spatial understanding through depth cues (distance, near–far relations).
- **Multi-Modal Projectors:** Linear connectors are used to align the features extracted by both RGB and depth encoders with the Large Language Model (LLM). Separate projectors ensure modality-specific processing, allowing the LLM to integrate visual and spatial information for VQA and point prediction.
- **LLM Backbone:** The Qwen2 LLM backbone from NVILA is used, pre-trained for rich visual knowledge, which provides a strong foundation for spatial reasoning.

#### 3.2. Training Strategies

RoboRefer employs a sequential two-stage fine-tuning approach:

**Stage 1: Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT)**
The SFT stage aims to build foundational spatial understanding and implicit reasoning capabilities:
1. **Depth Alignment:** Initially, only the newly introduced depth projector is trained to align depth features with the LLM's textual space. This step uses RGB-D annotations from the `RefSpatial` dataset, ensuring that depth information is effectively integrated without perturbing the pre-trained RGB path.
2. **Spatial Understanding Enhancement:** All model parameters (including both encoders, projectors, and the LLM) are then fine-tuned on a diverse set of datasets:
 - **RefSpatial:** Used with both RGB and RGB-D inputs to enhance single-step fine-grained spatial understanding and bolster implicit multi-step reasoning through data annotated with explicit reasoning processes. This ensures the image encoder learns spatial cues beyond just depth.
 - **Auxiliary Datasets:** Instruction-following datasets (e.g., LLaVA-1.5, LRV) and referring expression comprehension (REC) datasets (e.g., RefCOCO/+/g) are included to improve general instruction-following and referring capabilities, bridging distribution gaps.

**Stage 2: Reinforcement Fine-tuning (RFT)**
Following SFT, an RFT stage is introduced to foster generalized multi-step spatial reasoning, explicitly breaking down complex tasks:
- **GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization):** RoboRefer utilizes GRPO, a computationally efficient reinforcement learning method, which estimates relative advantages by comparing intra-group rewards.
- **Metric-Sensitive Process Reward Functions:** To guide RFT towards accurate point predictions and refine intermediate reasoning, novel reward functions are designed:
 - **Outcome Reward Functions:**
 - `R_OF` (Outcome Format Reward): Rewards adherence to a structured output format (reasoning in `<think>...</think>`, final answer in `<answer>...</answer>`).
 - `R_P` (Point L1 Reward): Rewards the final predicted point if it falls within a 50-pixel L1 distance from the ground-truth point.
 - **Process Reward Functions (Order-Invariant & Rule-Based):** These leverage ground-truth step-wise annotations from `RefSpatial` to assess intermediate perceptual results directly, circumventing the need for a costly Process Reward Model.
 - `R_PF` (Process Format Reward): Rewards adherence to a structured format for each reasoning step (`[Perception Type] [Target Object]: [Value]`). Perception types include "Position" (2D coordinates), "Orientation" (3D unit vector), and "Size" (scalar in meters).
 - `R_Acc` (Accuracy Reward): Applies specific metrics for each perception type (L1 distance for Position, cosine similarity for Orientation, percentage deviation for Size) to reward accurate intermediate predictions. This reward is only given if the "Target Object" is correctly identified in the key-step annotations.
- The final reward combines these components, and relative advantages are used to update the policy, reinforcing high-quality responses.

#### 3.3. RefSpatial Dataset and RefSpatial-Bench Benchmark

- **RefSpatial Dataset:** A large-scale (2.5M samples, 20M QA pairs) and diverse dataset curated for spatial referring, integrating data from three sources:
 1. **2D Web Images (OpenImages):** Provides fundamental spatial concepts and broad depth perception (indoor/outdoor). A multi-stage filtering pipeline (SigLIP2, Qwen2.5-VL) curates high-quality images. Pseudo-3D scene graphs are constructed (RAM, GroundingDINO, UniDepth V2, WildeCamera, SAM 2.1) to extract 3D-aware information. Hierarchical object descriptions are generated (Qwen2.5-VL, heuristic spatial ordering) to enable unambiguous referring. QA pairs cover qualitative VQA, quantitative queries, and point prediction.
 2. **3D Embodied Videos (CA-1M):** Focuses on fine-grained spatial understanding in indoor scenes. A robust annotation and filtering pipeline (GroundingDINO, RAM, Florence-2) addresses ambiguities and lack of semantic labels. It supports comprehensive 3D scene graphs and generates QA for object location and free space placement, including metric distances and oriented 3D bounding boxes.
 3. **Simulated Scenes (Infinigen with Objaverse assets):** Crucial for multi-step reasoning, as it provides ground-truth reasoning processes. A rigorous asset selection, annotation (GPT-4o), scene population, and rendering process ensures high-quality, diverse scenes. QA templates guide the generation of unique referring expressions and complex multi-step reasoning chains.
- **RefSpatial-Bench Benchmark:** A challenging benchmark with 200 real-world images (100 for Location, 100 for Placement). Over 70% of samples require multi-step reasoning (up to 5 steps) and include precise ground-truth masks. It features an "Unseen" subset (77 samples) with novel spatial relation combinations to evaluate generalization.

This comprehensive methodology, from architecture design to data creation and training strategies, ensures RoboRefer's ability to precisely understand 3D scenes and dynamically reason about instructions for robotic interaction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate RoboRefer's effectiveness across various spatial understanding and referring tasks, both in benchmarks and real-world robotic applications.

#### 4.1. Single-Step Spatial Understanding

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** SFT-trained RoboRefer (both 2B and 8B variants) significantly outperforms all existing spatial specialist models and even proprietary VLMs like Gemini-2.5-Pro on public single-step spatial understanding benchmarks (CV-Bench, BLINK, RoboSpatial, SAT, EmbSpatial). The 8B variant achieves an average success rate of 89.6%, surpassing Gemini-2.5-Pro by an absolute 5% and the baseline NVILA-2B by 21.7%.
- **Impact of Depth Input:** Incorporating depth information during inference consistently improves performance on 3D-centric benchmarks by about 1.5% compared to RGB-only input, although the model already shows strong RGB-only spatial understanding due to mixed RGB and RGB-D training in SFT.

#### 4.2. Multi-Step Spatial Referring

- **Superior Reasoning Ability:** On the `RefSpatial-Bench` (including Location and Placement tasks), the RFT-trained RoboRefer-2B variant demonstrates superior performance, outperforming all baselines, including Gemini-2.5-Pro, by a large margin (17.4% absolute average accuracy). This highlights the RFT stage's effectiveness in enhancing complex reasoning, especially for 3D spatial relations where proprietary models often struggle.
- **Generalization to Unseen Cases:** In the `RefSpatial-Bench -Unseen` subset (novel spatial relation combinations), the RFT-trained model surpasses its SFT-only counterpart by an absolute 9.1%. This indicates that RFT-trained RoboRefer generalizes better to novel spatial constraints, mitigating SFT's tendency to memorize training distributions.
- **Performance on Existing Robotic Referring Benchmarks:** RoboRefer also performs exceptionally well on current robotic referring benchmarks (RoboRefIt, Where2Place, RoboSpatial), which are limited to 2 reasoning steps. For instance, on Where2Place, RoboRefer-8B-SFT achieves 70.0% accuracy, significantly higher than Gemini-2.5-Pro's 61.9%.

#### 4.3. Public Vision-Language Benchmarks

- **Enhanced 2D General Referring:** RoboRefer improves performance on general 2D referring tasks (RefCOCO/+/g datasets). By predicting single points within ground-truth bounding boxes, RoboRefer-8B-SFT achieves 94.3% on RefCOCOg (val/test), outperforming Qwen-2.5-VL-72B's point-based predictions (91.5%/92.0%). This suggests that the spatial referring training also benefits general 2D grounding.
- **Preservation of Commonsense Knowledge:** Training with combined RGB and RGB-D data from `RefSpatial` and general visual instruction datasets allows RoboRefer to maintain or slightly improve performance on standard VLM benchmarks (MME, MMBench, OK-VQA, POPE) compared to the NVILA-2B baseline. This indicates that enhancing spatial understanding does not compromise general VQA capabilities.

#### 4.4. Simulator and Real-World Evaluation for Robotics

- **Tool Integration and Efficiency:** In the Open6DOR V2 simulation benchmark, RoboRefer, when integrated as a lightweight tool for object localization and placement, achieves a 6.8% absolute improvement in success rate over VLA-based baselines (Octo, OpenVLA) and tool-based models (SoFar). Its compact size also reduces execution time by 27.5% compared to GPT-4o-based approaches.
- **Crucial for Real-World Robots:** RoboRefer enables various control policies to execute long-horizon, dynamic tasks in cluttered real-world scenes with different robots (UR5, G1 humanoid). It demonstrates robustness to real-time disturbances (e.g., objects being moved) by rapidly updating target predictions (2.5 Hz) and re-planning. This highlights its capability to unify manipulation and navigation under a single spatial referring formulation.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Data Recipe Importance:** The combination of 2D, 3D, and simulated data in `RefSpatial` is critical for optimal SFT performance, with each data source contributing uniquely to different aspects of spatial understanding and generalization (e.g., 2D for diverse depth perception, 3D for indoor scene detail, simulated for multi-step reasoning).
- **Dedicated Depth Encoder:** A dedicated depth encoder maintains image understanding better than a shared image-depth encoder, especially with limited RGB-only data, confirming its role in preventing modality interference.
- **Depth Encoder for Reasoning:** The depth encoder significantly improves both single-step spatial understanding and, more importantly, multi-step spatial referring accuracy, attributing this to the precision required for coordinate prediction and the cumulative utility of depth cues across reasoning steps.
- **Process Reward Functions:** The metric-sensitive process reward functions in RFT lead to a 5-point improvement, validating their effectiveness in refining step-wise perception and improving accuracy for complex spatial relations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboRefer represents a significant stride forward in the field of embodied AI and robotics, particularly in empowering robots with advanced spatial intelligence. Its contributions have several key implications and potential impacts:

#### 5.1. Advancement in Embodied AI and Robotics

- **Bridging Perception-Action Gap:** RoboRefer directly addresses a critical challenge in robotics: enabling robots to seamlessly translate complex natural language instructions into precise actions in the 3D physical world. By accurately predicting 2D points for both object location and placement, it provides a crucial "spatial anchor" that can drive diverse robotic functions, including grasping, navigation, and object rearrangement.
- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy:** The ability to perform multi-step spatial reasoning in dynamic and cluttered real-world environments allows robots to handle more intricate, long-horizon tasks, moving beyond simple pick-and-place operations. This significantly enhances robot autonomy and adaptability in unstructured settings.
- **Robustness to Real-World Variability:** RoboRefer's demonstrated ability to adapt to changes in real-time (e.g., moved objects, new commands) showcases its potential for robust operation in human-centric environments, where dynamic interactions are common.
- **Unified Spatial Understanding:** By formulating manipulation and navigation tasks under a single spatial referring framework, RoboRefer offers a unified approach to robot control, simplifying task planning and execution for complex scenarios.

#### 5.2. Methodological Contributions

- **Novel Architecture for 3D-Aware VLMs:** The dedicated depth encoder design effectively overcomes limitations of prior VLM approaches, such as modality interference, ensuring that 3D perception is enhanced without compromising general visual understanding. This sets a new standard for integrating depth in VLMs.
- **Effective Multi-Stage Training Paradigm:** The sequential SFT-RFT strategy, particularly the use of metric-sensitive process reward functions in RFT, provides a powerful recipe for teaching explicit, generalized reasoning. This paradigm can inspire future work in training AI models for complex, sequential tasks beyond just spatial referring.
- **Foundational Datasets and Benchmarks:** `RefSpatial` and `RefSpatial-Bench` fill critical gaps in the research landscape. These resources will enable future generations of models to be trained and rigorously evaluated on multi-step spatial reasoning tasks, accelerating progress in embodied AI. Their scale, diversity (2D/3D/simulated), and fine-grained annotations are invaluable contributions.

#### 5.3. Potential Real-World Applications

- **Industrial Robotics:** Streamlining complex assembly, logistics, and quality control tasks with natural language instructions.
- **Service Robotics:** Enabling domestic robots to understand and execute nuanced commands in homes, or assistive robots to aid individuals with daily tasks more intelligently.
- **Exploration and Rescue:** Guiding autonomous robots to locate specific items or navigate to precise locations in complex, unknown environments.
- **Human-Robot Collaboration:** Facilitating more natural and intuitive communication between humans and robots, as robots can better interpret ambiguous or high-level spatial commands.

#### 5.4. Future Research Directions

The authors themselves identify crucial areas for future work, highlighting the ongoing challenges:
- **Understanding Human Priors and Intent:** Addressing ambiguous human instructions that imply unique locations based on common sense or context, which current models struggle with due to their reliance on precise textual descriptions. This could involve exploring procedural synthesis of intent-aware data or co-training with intent-rich datasets.
- **Improving 3D Perception Capabilities:** Moving beyond qualitative spatial relations and 2D image-plane predictions (which necessitate depth-based 3D conversion) to directly model quantitative geometry for precise 3D reasoning or direct prediction of 3D points/visual traces.

#### 5.5. Broader Impacts

As an advanced VLM, RoboRefer inherits the broader ethical considerations of large AI models, including potential biases from training data, hallucination risks, and the computational energy demands of large-scale models. However, its release to the research community is a net positive, fostering open science and collaborative efforts to mitigate these risks while maximizing the benefits of enhanced robot capabilities. The ability of robots to operate more intelligently and autonomously in human environments also necessitates careful consideration of safety protocols and human-robot interaction design.

In conclusion, RoboRefer marks a significant step towards creating truly intelligent embodied agents. By tackling both precise spatial understanding and generalized multi-step spatial reasoning through an innovative architectural design, a robust training pipeline, and comprehensive data, it paves the way for a future where robots can interpret and act upon complex spatial language with unprecedented accuracy and flexibility.
