# Multi-Loco: Unifying Multi-Embodiment Legged Locomotion via Reinforcement Learning Augmented Diffusion

> [!summary] Summary
> Multi-Loco unifies legged robot locomotion by training a single policy capable of controlling diverse robot embodiments like bipeds, quadrupeds, and humanoids. This framework, developed by researchers from SUSTech, LimX Dynamics, and the University of Notre Dame, achieves an average 10.35% improvement in locomotion task performance over baselines and demonstrates zero-shot transfer to unseen physical robots, including the Unitree Go2.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning policies for legged robots are morphology-specific, requiring extensive, separate training for each robot type.
- This platform-specific engineering bottleneck limits scalability, knowledge reuse, and the development of versatile, general-purpose robot controllers.
- Generalizing control across diverse robot embodiments with varying observation and action dimensions remains a significant challenge.

## Method

- A ==dimension alignment strategy== (zero-padding, binary masking, normalization) unifies observation and action spaces across diverse robot morphologies.
- A ==generative diffusion model== learns morphology-invariant locomotion priors offline from aggregated multi-robot datasets, using ==masked denoising== to handle padded dimensions.
- A shared, lightweight ==residual reinforcement learning== policy, trained with a ==multi-critic PPO== and a ==residual penalty==, refines the diffusion model's output for task-specific adaptation and robust real-world deployment.

## Results

- Multi-Loco achieved a **10.35%** average return improvement over standard RL baselines across four robot morphologies in simulation, with up to **13.57%** gains for wheeled-bipeds.
- The cross-robot diffusion policy improved average performance by **17.96%** compared to single-robot diffusion, demonstrating enhanced adaptability to challenging terrains like discrete obstacles.
- Policies were successfully deployed in real-time on four diverse physical robots (biped, wheeled biped, humanoid, quadruped) and demonstrated zero-shot transfer to an entirely unseen Unitree Go2 quadruped without fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A hybrid architecture, combining a generative diffusion model with residual reinforcement learning, effectively captures broad locomotion patterns and provides fine-grained, adaptive control.
> - Training a diffusion model on aggregated multi-robot datasets facilitates the emergence of shared, morphology-invariant locomotion skills, outperforming single-robot diffusion training.
> - Unified policies can achieve robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and morphology-agnostic generalization to unseen robot platforms without requiring explicit morphology inputs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of legged robot locomotion has seen remarkable progress with reinforcement learning (RL), enabling robots to achieve agile and dynamic behaviors on complex terrains. However, a fundamental challenge persists: these RL policies are almost exclusively *morphology-specific*. Each robot, even with minor design variations, typically requires a separate, extensive training process, leading to siloed knowledge and limited scalability. This "platform-specific engineering" bottleneck hinders the development of versatile, general-purpose robot controllers.

**Multi-Loco directly addresses this critical generalization challenge.** It positions itself at the intersection of several burgeoning research areas:

- **Reinforcement Learning for Locomotion:** Building upon seminal works that demonstrated RL's power in robot control (e.g.,), Multi-Loco seeks to transcend the single-robot paradigm.
- **Diffusion Models in Robotics:** Diffusion models, known for their ability to learn complex, multi-modal distributions, have recently gained traction in robotics for tasks like contact-rich manipulation and even single-embodiment legged locomotion (DiffuseLoco). Multi-Loco extends this by leveraging diffusion models for *cross-embodiment* pattern learning.
- **Cross-Embodiment Learning:** This nascent field aims to create policies that generalize across robots with differing kinematics, sensing, and actuation. Prior work in this domain has largely focused on manipulation tasks, with locomotion presenting unique challenges due to its deep reliance on robot dynamics and environmental interaction. For legged robots, early efforts like GenLoco used procedural morphology randomization, but were limited to fixed degrees of freedom (DoFs). Others, such as ManyQuadrupeds and MorAL, relied on specific morphology embeddings or predefined task-space control. More recently, URMA and CrossFormer employed transformer-based architectures to unify control, but often still depended on structured embodiment descriptors or per-robot modules.

Multi-Loco differentiates itself by proposing a **morphology-agnostic generative diffusion model** augmented with a **lightweight, shared residual RL policy**. Crucially, it aims to unify control *without relying on explicit morphology information* (such as structural descriptors or per-robot observation encoders) during inference, a significant departure from many existing cross-embodiment approaches (as highlighted in Table 1). This focus on implicit learning of shared patterns, combined with a robust sim2real transfer mechanism, positions Multi-Loco as a significant step towards truly generalizable and scalable locomotion policies for diverse legged robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the Multi-Loco framework can be summarized as follows:

- **Overcome Morphology-Specific Training:** The core motivation is to move beyond the current paradigm where each legged robot morphology requires independent and extensive RL training. This significantly limits scalability, knowledge reuse, and development efficiency for multi-robot systems.
- **Enable Generalization Across Diverse Embodiments:** The central objective is to develop a unified locomotion policy capable of controlling a wide array of legged robots (e.g., bipeds, quadrupeds, humanoids, wheeled-bipeds) despite their inherent differences in observation/action dimensions and system dynamics.
- **Facilitate Knowledge Reuse:** By learning a generalizable policy, the framework aims to enable the transfer of learned locomotion skills from one robot to others, reducing the need for "training from scratch" for new or modified platforms.
- **Improve Robustness and Adaptability:** The goal is to develop policies that are not only generalized but also robust enough to handle complex, uneven, and dynamic real-world environments across different robot types.
- **Bridge the Sim2Real Gap Effectively:** A crucial objective is to ensure that the unified policies trained in simulation can be directly deployed on physical hardware (zero-shot transfer) with high performance and stability.
- **Develop a Morphology-Agnostic Approach:** The authors explicitly aim to achieve generalization without relying on explicit, hand-engineered morphology inputs or robot-specific architectural components, thereby enhancing scalability to potentially unseen morphologies.
- **Leverage Generative Models for Multi-Modality:** The motivation includes exploring the capabilities of diffusion models to capture the inherent multi-modal nature of diverse locomotion patterns across different embodiments, providing strong, morphology-invariant action priors.

In essence, Multi-Loco seeks to create a **scalable and efficient learning paradigm** for legged robots by unifying their control policies, thereby advancing the field towards truly versatile and autonomous robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Multi-Loco proposes a novel unified framework integrating a generative diffusion model with a lightweight residual reinforcement learning (RL) policy. The methodology consists of three key components:

#### 3.1. Dimension Alignment for Multi-Embodiment Observations and Actions

The foundational challenge of multi-embodiment learning is the variation in observation and action spaces. Multi-Loco addresses this by:
- **Zero-Padding:** All robot-specific proprioceptive observations ($O_m$) and actions ($A_m$) are expanded to common, maximum dimensions ($\bar{O}$, $\bar{A}$) by padding unused dimensions with zeros. For instance, if the largest action space is 20D, a 12D quadruped action will be padded with 8 zeros.
- **Binary Masking:** A binary mask ($b$) is created for each robot, indicating which action dimensions are valid (1) and which are padded (0). This mask is critical for guiding the learning process.
- **MinMax Normalization:** To ensure training stability and consistent numerical ranges across heterogeneous robots, all observation and action dimensions are normalized to a common scale (e.g., [-1, 1]).

This preprocessing step creates a unified data format, allowing a single model to process data from diverse robots.

#### 3.2. Multi-Embodiment Locomotion Unification via Diffusion Model

The core of the unified policy is a generative diffusion model, specifically the Elucidated Diffusion Model (EDM) with a lightweight Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbone.
- **Generative Prior:** The diffusion model directly parameterizes the robot's action distribution $\mu(\bar{a}|\bar{o})$, learning morphology-invariant locomotion patterns from diverse cross-embodiment datasets. It leverages the multi-modal capabilities of diffusion models to generate high-quality, robust action priors.
- **Real-time Inference:** EDM is chosen for its ability to approximate the Stein score function and reconstruct samples by solving ODEs, enabling faster inference crucial for real-time robotic control (e.g., 50Hz).
- **Masked Denoising Score Matching:** To effectively handle the zero-padded dimensions, the standard denoising objective is augmented with a mask. The loss function ensures that the model only considers the valid action dimensions (using element-wise multiplication with the binary mask $b$), preventing the model from learning to denoise the irrelevant padded values.
- **Offline Training:** The diffusion model is pre-trained offline on a rich library of multi-robot datasets, distilling generalizable locomotion behaviors.

#### 3.3. Enhancing Diffusion with Residual Policy and Multi-Critic RL

While the diffusion model provides strong action priors, it might not fully capture fine-grained dynamics or task-specific variations. To address this, Multi-Loco introduces a residual policy:
- **Residual Action:** The final action $a$ is computed as the sum of the action prior sampled from the diffusion model ($\bar{a}_{prior}$) and a residual action ($\Delta a$) predicted by a separate policy ($a = \bar{a}_{prior} + \Delta a$).
- **Shared Residual Policy:** A *single* residual policy ($\pi_\theta(\Delta a | \bar{o}, \bar{a}_{prior})$) is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and is shared across all robot embodiments. This policy refines the diffusion model's output, adapting it to specific task requirements and bridging the sim2real gap.
- **Multi-Critic PPO:** To manage the varied reward landscapes and optimization conflicts across different robot types while sharing a single actor (the residual policy), a multi-critic architecture is employed. A separate critic $V_k$ is instantiated for *each* robot type $k$, each receiving its corresponding privileged state. The shared actor is then optimized using a loss function that aggregates advantages from all critics.
- **Task-Aware Reward Design:** Each robot's reward function includes task-specific components (e.g., velocity tracking, base height, orientation), regularization terms (e.g., torque, joint acceleration penalties), and a novel **residual penalty** ($r_d(\Delta a_t) = \alpha\|\Delta a_t\|_1$). This residual penalty encourages the residual action to remain small, ensuring that the residual policy primarily refines rather than overrides the generative prior, thereby leveraging the learned multi-modal behaviors.
- **Online Adaptation:** Only the residual policy is updated online through reinforcement learning, while the pre-trained diffusion model remains frozen. This design allows the residual term to quickly specialize to real-world dynamics and task-level feedback.

In summary, Multi-Loco leverages the power of generative models to learn morphology-agnostic locomotion priors and combines it with the adaptive capabilities of RL for task-specific refinement and robust real-world deployment, all within a unified and scalable framework.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Multi-Loco demonstrates its effectiveness across diverse legged robots, both in simulation and real-world experiments. The key findings are categorized as follows:

#### 4.1. Performance Superiority and Cross-Embodiment Generalization

- **Average Return Improvement:** Multi-Loco (specifically the CR-DP+RA variant) consistently outperforms standard RL baselines across all four tested robot morphologies (biped, wheeled biped, humanoid, quadruped). It achieves a **10.35% average return improvement** over the RL baseline, with gains up to **13.57% in wheeled-biped locomotion tasks** and 12.49% for biped, 10.97% for humanoid, and 4.38% for quadruped locomotion (Figure 3a, Table 2).
- **Mechanism of Improvement:** The results indicate that the hybrid architecture (diffusion model + residual RL) is critical. The diffusion model (CR-DP) alone reaches 87.78% of the baseline RL's potential, consistent with behavioral cloning limitations. However, with the addition of Residual Adaptation (CR-DP+RA) using identical RL hyperparameters, performance significantly jumps to 113.57% of baseline effectiveness. This validates that the diffusion model distills transferable skills, and the residual RL module dynamically adapts these skills to embodiment-specific dynamics.

#### 4.2. Emergence of Shared Locomotion Skills via Cross-Embodiment Learning

- **Cross-Robot Diffusion Policy (CR-DP) vs. Single-Robot Diffusion Policy (SR-DP):** Training the diffusion model on aggregated multi-robot datasets (CR-DP) leads to significantly better performance than training on individual robot datasets (SR-DP). CR-DP achieves an average 17.96% improvement over SR-DP (Figure 3a). This indicates that the cross-embodiment training facilitates the emergence of shared, morphology-invariant locomotion patterns.
- **Enhanced Adaptability to Challenging Terrains:** For wheeled-bipeds, CR-DP shows a 2.67% overall terrain traversal improvement over SR-DP, with notable gains on discrete obstacles (6.10%) and rough slopes (3.87%) (Figure 4a). Furthermore, CR-DP+RA (diffusion + residual RL) achieves a 15.13% improvement on discrete terrain and an 11.57% enhancement on rough slopes compared to the PPO-wheeled baseline (Figure 4b). This is particularly impressive as the wheeled-biped's original dataset and RL training struggled with leg-lifting locomotion, a skill effectively transferred from other embodiments by the cross-robot diffusion component.
- **Asymmetric Knowledge Transfer:** An interesting observation is that while cross-robot training benefits most morphologies, the quadruped scenarios show CR-DP+RA matching, but not significantly exceeding, the RL baseline. The authors hypothesize this is due to asymmetric knowledge transfer: quadruped locomotion expertise provides valuable guidance for other morphologies, but motion patterns from simpler or different embodiments offer limited additional benefit to complex quadruped agents.

#### 4.3. Zero-Shot Sim2Real Transfer

- **Robust Real-World Deployment:** Multi-Loco policies were successfully deployed on four diverse physical robots (biped, wheeled biped, humanoid, quadruped) in real-time at 50Hz. The framework enabled robust locomotion across various challenging real-world terrains, including grass, slopes, stairs, and gravel paths (Figure 1, Figure 3b,c). This demonstrates the policy's capacity to maintain dynamic stability and adapt motor patterns in unstructured environments.
- **Zero-Shot Transfer to an Unseen Platform:** Crucially, the unified policy demonstrated successful zero-shot transfer to an entirely *unseen* platform, the Unitree Go2 quadruped robot, without any fine-tuning (Figure 9, Table 5). This confirms the morphology-agnostic generalization capability of the model, showcasing its potential for rapid deployment on new hardware. The observed average return difference was largely attributed to base height parameter discrepancies, which when normalized, showed comparable performance.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies and Hyperparameter Analysis

- **Diffusion Sampling Steps:** Experiments showed that 3 to 5 denoising steps offer a good balance between control accuracy and sampling efficiency, with diminishing returns beyond 20 steps (Figure 6). 5 steps were chosen for deployment.
- **Dataset Size:** Performance scales sublinearly with dataset size, implying good generalization even with partial data. However, very small datasets (<25%) led to unstable behaviors (Figure 7).
- **Dataset Composition:** Analysis of varying data ratios revealed that reducing data for more complex or dynamically rich robots (like humanoids and wheeled bipeds) had a more significant negative impact on overall performance, and could even lead to cross-domain performance degradation, highlighting unexpected synergies between morphologically distinct systems (Figure 8).

Overall, the findings strongly support the Multi-Loco framework's ability to unify locomotion policies, achieve robust generalization across diverse embodiments, and effectively transfer learned skills to physical robots without specific tuning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Multi-Loco represents a significant advancement in robot locomotion, particularly for the development of generalist controllers. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

- **Pioneering Unified Control for Multi-Embodiment Legged Robots:** The framework successfully addresses the long-standing challenge of generalizing locomotion policies across fundamentally different legged robot morphologies. By demonstrating a single policy controlling bipeds, wheeled bipeds, humanoids, and quadrupeds, Multi-Loco offers a foundational step towards truly universal robot controllers.
- **Scalability and Efficiency in Robot Development:** By enabling knowledge reuse and eliminating the need for morphology-specific training, Multi-Loco drastically reduces the development time and computational resources required for new robot platforms or variations. This is a critical enabler for rapidly deploying diverse robotic fleets or adapting existing robots to new tasks.
- **Robustness through Generative Priors and RL Refinement:** The hybrid approach of combining a generative diffusion model with a residual RL policy offers a potent combination. The diffusion model provides a robust, multi-modal prior that captures essential locomotion dynamics across embodiments, while the lightweight RL refines these actions for task-specific performance and real-world robustness. This synergistic approach ensures both broad generalization and fine-grained adaptation.
- **Morphology-Agnostic Design:** A key strength is the framework's ability to achieve generalization without explicit morphology inputs. This design choice simplifies the policy architecture, enhances its scalability to unseen robots, and reduces the engineering effort typically associated with incorporating robot-specific descriptors.
- **Enhanced Zero-Shot Sim2Real Transfer:** The empirical validation of zero-shot transfer on four distinct physical robots, including an *unseen* Unitree Go2 platform, is a major breakthrough. This demonstrates the framework's practical viability and its potential to accelerate real-world deployment of sophisticated legged locomotion skills, significantly reducing the cost and complexity of robot deployment.
- **Foundation for Future Generalist Robots:** Multi-Loco lays crucial groundwork for future generalist robotic systems capable of performing a wide range of tasks across various physical forms. It highlights the potential of combining powerful generative models with adaptive control strategies to unlock unprecedented versatility in robotics.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors candidly discuss limitations, which also point to avenues for future research:
- **Reliance on Observation-Action Paired Datasets:** The current framework requires paired observation-action data for training. Future work aims to leverage action-free motion capture data (e.g., human or animal locomotion) to exploit larger and more diverse datasets, which would require advancements in inverse dynamics or behavior inference.
- **Direct Synthesis for Entirely New Morphologies:** While it generalizes well to *diverse* known morphologies, direct synthesis of policies for *entirely new, unforeseen* morphologies remains an open challenge. Future efforts will focus on extending shared locomotion principles to novel designs through zero-shot adaptation or few-shot fine-tuning, further pushing the boundaries of generalization.

In conclusion, Multi-Loco significantly advances the field of legged robot locomotion by providing a scalable, robust, and morphology-agnostic framework for unified control. Its successful demonstration across a wide range of real-world platforms, coupled with its innovative hybrid architecture, positions it as a pivotal development towards the realization of truly versatile and autonomous robotic systems.
