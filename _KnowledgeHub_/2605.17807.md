# Curriculum Group Policy Optimization: Adaptive Sampling for Unleashing the Potential of Text-to-Image Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> A Curriculum Group Policy Optimization (CGPO) framework improves Text-to-Image (T2I) model training efficiency and compositional accuracy by adaptively sampling prompts based on reward variance and calibrating learning across diverse reward categories. This approach allows models to reach peak performance twice as fast as Flow-GRPO while achieving superior results on benchmarks like GenEval.

## Problem

- Existing RL-based Text-to-Image (T2I) training uses uniform sampling, leading to inefficient utilization of prompts that are either too easy or too difficult.
- Static curriculum learning struggles to adapt to the dynamic evolution of a model's learning capabilities.
- Multi-category datasets often present data imbalance, with certain reward criteria being underrepresented or harder to optimize simultaneously.

## Method

- A Curriculum Group Policy Optimization (**CGPO**) framework dynamically identifies "==actively learnable==" prompts by measuring reward variance within generated image groups.
- The framework uses an online adaptive sampling strategy, akin to the Zone of Proximal Development, to prioritize prompts where the model shows inconsistent performance.
- A category calibration mechanism is introduced, employing proportional fairness optimization, to dynamically balance learning across diverse reward criteria in multi-category datasets.

## Results

- CGPO achieved peak performance approximately twice as fast as Flow-GRPO (160 GPU hours), demonstrating substantial training efficiency gains.
- The framework surpassed Flow-GRPO and other baselines on GenEval (**0.96** overall) and T2I-CompBench++, showing improved accuracy in compositional tasks like attribute binding, position, and counting.
- Ablation studies confirmed that adaptive sampling and category calibration cumulatively enhanced performance by **1.68%** over the Flow-GRPO baseline, validating the contribution of each component.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Group reward variance effectively identifies prompts within a model's "Zone of Proximal Development," signaling actively learnable examples that offer high marginal learning utility.
> - Adaptive, online curriculum learning, driven by prompt inconsistency and calibrated for multi-category rewards, significantly enhances T2I model training efficiency and compositional accuracy.
> - Balancing exploration for less-selected prompts and smoothing historical probabilities prevents catastrophic forgetting and ensures continuous learning across the entire dataset.
