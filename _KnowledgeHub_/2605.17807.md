# Curriculum Group Policy Optimization: Adaptive Sampling for Unleashing the Potential of Text-to-Image Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> A Curriculum Group Policy Optimization (CGPO) framework improves Text-to-Image (T2I) model training efficiency and compositional accuracy by adaptively sampling prompts based on reward variance and calibrating learning across diverse reward categories. This approach allows models to reach peak performance twice as fast as Flow-GRPO while achieving superior results on benchmarks like GenEval.

## Problem

- Existing RL-based Text-to-Image (T2I) training uses uniform sampling, leading to inefficient utilization of prompts that are either too easy or too difficult.
- Static curriculum learning struggles to adapt to the dynamic evolution of a model's learning capabilities.
- Multi-category datasets often present data imbalance, with certain reward criteria being underrepresented or harder to optimize simultaneously.

## Method

- A Curriculum Group Policy Optimization (**CGPO**) framework dynamically identifies "==actively learnable==" prompts by measuring reward variance within generated image groups.
- The framework uses an online adaptive sampling strategy, akin to the Zone of Proximal Development, to prioritize prompts where the model shows inconsistent performance.
- A category calibration mechanism is introduced, employing proportional fairness optimization, to dynamically balance learning across diverse reward criteria in multi-category datasets.

## Results

- CGPO achieved peak performance approximately twice as fast as Flow-GRPO (160 GPU hours), demonstrating substantial training efficiency gains.
- The framework surpassed Flow-GRPO and other baselines on GenEval (**0.96** overall) and T2I-CompBench++, showing improved accuracy in compositional tasks like attribute binding, position, and counting.
- Ablation studies confirmed that adaptive sampling and category calibration cumulatively enhanced performance by **1.68%** over the Flow-GRPO baseline, validating the contribution of each component.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Group reward variance effectively identifies prompts within a model's "Zone of Proximal Development," signaling actively learnable examples that offer high marginal learning utility.
> - Adaptive, online curriculum learning, driven by prompt inconsistency and calibrated for multi-category rewards, significantly enhances T2I model training efficiency and compositional accuracy.
> - Balancing exploration for less-selected prompts and smoothing historical probabilities prevents catastrophic forgetting and ensures continuous learning across the entire dataset.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Text-to-Image (T2I) generation has experienced significant advancements, enabling the creation of realistic and artistic images from natural language descriptions. These advancements are largely driven by large-scale multimodal data and developments in generative models, including diffusion models and latent diffusion techniques like Stable Diffusion. Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a key approach for fine-tuning T2I models, particularly to align generated images with human preferences and complex semantic compositions. Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) are prominent RL methods applied in this domain, with GRPO enhancing efficiency through group-wise advantage estimation.

However, a prevailing limitation in many existing RL-based T2I training methodologies is the reliance on uniform sampling strategies. This approach often overlooks the varied learning utility of different prompts at different stages of model training. Prompts that are either too easy (already mastered) or too difficult (beyond the model's current capacity) provide limited marginal learning signals, leading to inefficient sample utilization and slower convergence.

Curriculum learning offers a paradigm to address this inefficiency by structuring the training process to progressively expose the model to samples of increasing difficulty. While classical curriculum learning often relies on static, predefined difficulty orderings, these methods are challenging to define consistently across large datasets and fail to adapt to the dynamic evolution of a model's capabilities during training. More recent efforts in self-paced and automatic curriculum learning aim to adapt sampling to the model's evolving competence, drawing parallels to educational concepts such as the Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD), which identifies an optimal learning zone where tasks are neither too simple nor too complex.

This work positions itself within this landscape by addressing the sample efficiency bottleneck in GRPO-based T2I training through an adaptive, online curriculum learning framework. It seeks to overcome the limitations of uniform sampling and static curricula by dynamically identifying and prioritizing "actively learnable" prompts. Furthermore, it tackles data imbalance issues in multi-category datasets, which arise from inherent difficulty disparities across different reward criteria, thereby aiming for a more balanced and effective optimization of T2I models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the training efficiency and generation performance of reinforcement learning-based Text-to-Image (T2I) models. The authors identify a core inefficiency in existing methods, particularly those based on Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), which commonly employ uniform sampling strategies.

The key motivations driving this work are as follows:

1. **Addressing Low Training Efficiency from Uniform Sampling:** The authors argue that uniform sampling, while simple, is inefficient because it does not account for the varying learning utility of different prompts. Some prompts may be too simple, offering minimal new information to an already proficient model, while others may be excessively complex, exceeding the model's current learning capacity. This results in training batches containing many samples with low marginal utility, thereby slowing down convergence and increasing computational costs.

2. **Aligning Prompt Difficulty with Model Capability:** A central hypothesis is that the most informative prompts for training are those that align with the model's current learning capability. This concept is likened to the Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD), where optimal learning occurs when a learner can partially handle a task but has not yet achieved stable mastery. For a T2I model, such prompts are those it can partially fulfill but on which its performance is inconsistent.

3. **Developing an Adaptive and Online Curriculum:** To operationalize the ZPD principle, the authors aim to create a dynamic curriculum that does not rely on predefined difficulty labels. Instead, it should adapt continuously to the model's evolving proficiency. The motivation is to automatically identify and prioritize prompts that are "actively learnable" at any given stage of training, offering the most significant learning signals. The authors propose using the *variance of rewards within a group of generated images* for a single prompt as an online proxy for prompt inconsistency. A high reward variance indicates that the model has partially captured the prompt's requirements but has not yet achieved stable mastery, suggesting these prompts are ripe for further learning.

4. **Managing Data Imbalance in Multi-Category Datasets:** T2I generation tasks often involve optimizing for multiple human preference categories (e.g., compositional accuracy, aesthetic appeal). These categories can have inherent difficulty disparities and different reward computation mechanisms. When trained jointly, some categories might be underrepresented or overlooked, hindering balanced learning. The motivation is to introduce a mechanism that can dynamically calibrate sampling probabilities to address these inter-category imbalances, ensuring that underperforming or more challenging categories receive appropriate attention without disrupting the adaptive prompt-level sampling strategy. This aims to foster a more robust and comprehensive improvement across all evaluation criteria.

In summary, the detailed objectives are to:
- Propose Curriculum Group Policy Optimization (CGPO), an adaptive curriculum framework that leverages group reward variance to prioritize prompts with high marginal learning utility.
- Introduce a category calibration method based on proportional fairness optimization to dynamically adjust sampling probabilities, balancing training difficulty across different reward categories.
- Validate that this framework improves T2I model performance and training efficiency, allowing models to achieve superior results with fewer training steps.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed **Curriculum Group Policy Optimization (CGPO)** framework builds upon the principles of Flow-GRPO, integrating a self-adaptive curriculum learning mechanism and a category calibration strategy. The method operates through a cyclical four-stage process designed to dynamically identify and prioritize actively learnable prompts.

**Overall Framework: Four Sequential Stages**

1. **Probability Sampling:** A batch of prompts is sampled from the dataset based on a continuously updated prompt-probability list, which reflects the estimated learning utility of each prompt.
2. **Reward Calculation (Policy Update):** For each sampled prompt, the T2I model generates a group of images. A reward model scores these images, and group relative advantages are computed to update the T2I model's parameters using a GRPO-like loss.
3. **Probability Computation:** Based on the rewards obtained in the previous stage, group-level reward statistics (specifically variance) are used to compute new prompt-level "proposal probabilities," and category-level calibration weights are updated.
4. **Probability Update:** The computed probabilities are refined through exploration balancing and historical smoothing, and the prompt-probability list and category weight list are updated for the next training iteration.

**Detailed Mechanisms:**

#### 3.1. Adaptive Sampling (Core Curriculum Mechanism)

The adaptive sampling component aims to identify prompts that fall within the model's "Zone of Proximal Development" (ZPD), where the model exhibits inconsistent performance, indicating partial mastery and potential for significant learning. Reward variance is used as an online proxy for this inconsistency.

- **Reward Variance as Inconsistency Proxy:** For each prompt `p` in a training batch, the T2I model generates a group of `G` images (`x_1..., x_G`). A reward model assigns a score `R(x_i, p)` to each image. The variance (`V_p`) of these `G` rewards is calculated (Eq. 3). A high `V_p` suggests that the model's performance on that prompt is inconsistent, meaning it has partially understood the prompt but not stably mastered it, thus offering a strong learning signal.
- **Prompt-level Proposal Probability (`P_var(p)`):** The calculated variance `V_p` for each prompt is linearly rescaled within the current batch to produce a "proposal probability" `P_var(p)` (Eq. 4). This step normalizes the variance values to a range, where higher variance leads to a higher `P_var`.
- **Probability List (`L_probability`):** All prompts in the dataset are maintained in a list, each associated with a current sampling probability (`P_list`). Initially, all prompts have a probability of 1.
- **Poisson-based Sampling with Rejection:** During batch sampling, a Poisson-based paradigm is used, where each prompt's selection is modeled as an independent Bernoulli trial based on its calibrated probability (`w_i * P_list_i`). A rejection sampling mechanism is employed to fill the batch: candidate samples are randomly selected and accepted probabilistically based on their assigned probabilities, with rejected samples replaced until the batch is complete.
- **Policy Update with Group Relative Advantage:** For selected prompts, the group relative advantage (`A_i`) is calculated (Eq. 2) by comparing individual image rewards to the mean and standard deviation of the group's rewards. This advantage is then used in a standard GRPO loss for gradient descent, updating the T2I model parameters.
- **Probability Update for `L_probability` (Exploration and Smoothing):**
 - **Exploration Balancing:** To prevent certain challenging prompts from being permanently neglected if they are rarely selected, a self-increment mechanism is applied. Non-selected prompts (`p /∈ S_b`) have their `P_list(p)` slightly increased by `1/N` (where `N` is the total number of prompts). This encourages continued exploration.
 - **Historical Smoothing:** For selected prompts (`p ∈ S_b`), their `P_list(p)` is updated by applying smoothing to `P_var`. The current `P_var` is averaged with the values from the two preceding iterations (a `1/3` weighted average over `P_var(t), P_var(t-1), P_var(t-2)` is implied by the text `1/3 P_t t-2 P_var(t)` and typical practice), which prevents abrupt drops in probability and mitigates catastrophic forgetting (Eq. 5).

#### 3.2. Category Calibration

This component addresses data imbalance arising from diverse reward categories, which may have different intrinsic difficulties or reward scales.

- **Problem Formulation:** The calibration aims to find optimal category coefficients (`q_i`) by solving an optimization problem (Eq. 6) that balances proportional fairness (`Σ log(q_i)`) with a penalty for deviating from a reference distribution (`KL(v || q)`).
- **Reference Coefficient (`v`):** A reference `v_i` is constructed for each category `i` (Eq. 7). It is inversely proportional to the average reward (`r_i`) obtained for that category within the current batch (`v_i = (1/r_i) / Σ (1/r_j)`). This design prioritizes categories with lower average rewards, directing more learning focus to underperforming areas.
- **Analytical Solution:** Using the Lagrange multiplier method, the authors derive a closed-form solution for `q_i` (Eq. 8): `q_i = (1 + λv_i) / (c + λ)`. Here, `λ` is a trade-off parameter.
 - When `λ = 0`, `q_i = 1/c`, resulting in uniform sampling across categories.
 - As `λ → ∞`, `q_i → v_i`, fully prioritizing categories based on their inverse average rewards.
 - Intermediate `λ` values allow a smooth transition, balancing these two extremes.
- **Implementation:** The analytical solution is converted into practical calibration coefficients `w_i = 1 + λv_i`. These `w_i` factors are periodically computed and updated. During sampling, `P_list_i` for a prompt is multiplied by its category's `w_i` to obtain the final sampling probability `P_sampling_i = w_i * P_list_i`.

In essence, CGPO integrates an online, dynamic curriculum at the prompt level, driven by reward inconsistency, with a macro-level category balancing mechanism. This multi-level adaptive sampling strategy is designed to optimize both the efficiency and effectiveness of RL-based T2I model training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate CGPO focused on performance, training efficiency, ablation studies, and qualitative/distributional visualizations across multiple benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Comparative Performance

- **GenEval Benchmark (Table 1):** CGPO demonstrated superior performance in complex compositional evaluation. It achieved an overall metric of 0.96, surpassing Flow-GRPO (0.94) and significantly outperforming SD3.5-M (0.63, although the table shows 0.55 for SDXL which might be a typo for SD3.5-M in the text, but the paper states SD3.5-M overall as 0.63 in Table 4, implying a different baseline for this specific table entry). CGPO showed notable improvements in tasks like Attribute Binding (exceeding Flow-GRPO by 0.07), Position, and Counting, areas where SD3.5-M was particularly weak. It achieved the best performance across all six specific GenEval categories.
- **T2I-CompBench++ (Table 2):** CGPO maintained top performance in most tasks on this comprehensive compositional benchmark. It registered an improvement in the Texture task (0.7521 vs. Flow-GRPO's 0.7298), an area where Flow-GRPO had shown regression compared to SD3.5-M.
- **DPG Bench (Table 3):** On this benchmark for complex semantic alignment and instruction-following, CGPO slightly outperformed Flow-GRPO (85.5 vs. 85.4 overall) and showed significant improvement over SD3.5-M, indicating its effectiveness even with longer textual descriptions.
- **Multiple Rewards Experiment (Table 5):** CGPO consistently outperformed both the reproduced 8-GPU Flow-GRPO and the 24-GPU results reported in the original Flow-GRPO paper across GenEval, OCR, and PickScore metrics when trained with respective reward models, showcasing its robustness and efficiency across different reward functions.

#### 4.2. Training Efficiency

- **Performance-Training Time Curves (Figure 3):** The comparison illustrated that CGPO achieved the peak performance of Flow-GRPO (0.944) in approximately 160 GPU hours, which is roughly twice the training speed of Flow-GRPO. This finding directly supports the claim that the adaptive sampling strategy significantly improves training efficiency and offers a more favorable performance-efficiency trade-off.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (Table 4)

The ablation studies demonstrated the cumulative effectiveness of CGPO's components:
- **Baseline (Flow-GRPO):** Overall performance of 94.42%.
- **+Probability Sampling:** Introducing the variance-based adaptive sampling strategy alone yielded a significant performance gain of +0.73%, reaching 95.15%. This indicates that prioritizing actively learnable prompts is the most impactful component.
- **+Exploring Balance:** The addition of the exploration balancing mechanism (preventing neglect of certain prompts) further improved performance by +0.59%, leading to a total improvement of +1.32% (95.74%).
- **+Category Calibration:** The final integration of the category calibration method contributed an additional +0.36%, resulting in a total improvement of +1.68% (96.10%) over the baseline. These results confirm the individual and synergistic contributions of each component to CGPO's overall performance.

#### 4.4. Visualization of Adaptive Sampling

- **Sampling Probability Difficulty Distribution (Figure 5):** This visualization tracked the number of high-probability prompts (P > 0.7) across different difficulty levels (stratified by object count) over training steps. It clearly showed a progression:
 - Early Training (before step 1100): High-probability samples concentrated in Level 1 (3–4 objects, easier).
 - Mid-Training (steps 1800–2000): Focus shifted to Level 2 (6–7 objects, medium difficulty).
 - Late Training (after step 2300): High-probability samples predominantly drawn from Level 3 (9–10 objects, hardest).
 This dynamic shift demonstrates that CGPO's variance-based adaptive sampling strategy effectively aligns the curriculum with the model's evolving capabilities, progressively focusing on more challenging yet learnable prompts.
- **Qualitative Comparison (Figure 4, 7, 8):** Visual examples illustrated CGPO's superior accuracy in compositional tasks, including attribute binding, counting, color, and spatial relationships, compared to SD3.5-M and Flow-GRPO. The generated images from CGPO demonstrated more coherent and accurate adherence to complex textual instructions.

#### 4.5. Hyperparameter Study (Table 7)

A study on the `λ` hyperparameter for category calibration revealed an optimal setting at `λ = 10`, which achieved the best overall performance (0.96). Both lower (`λ < 10`) and higher (`λ > 10`) values resulted in performance degradation. This indicates that `λ = 10` strikes an effective balance between addressing inter-category imbalances and preserving the prompt-level probabilistic discrimination.

#### 4.6. Reward During Training (Figure 6)

The training curves showed a continuous increase in the model's average reward (`reward avg`) and a concurrent decrease in the standard deviation of rewards (`reward std mean`). This pattern of "increasing reward with decreasing variance" indicates that the model gradually transitioned from inconsistent partial mastery to stable and reliable performance on training prompts, confirming the method's effectiveness in achieving both strong performance and training stability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Curriculum Group Policy Optimization (CGPO) framework represents a notable advancement in the training of Text-to-Image (T2I) models, particularly within the reinforcement learning paradigm. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

1. **Enhanced Training Efficiency:** By introducing an adaptive, online curriculum, CGPO addresses the long-standing problem of inefficient sample utilization in RL-based T2I training. The demonstrated ability to achieve comparable or superior performance in significantly fewer GPU hours (e.g., doubling training speed compared to Flow-GRPO) has substantial implications for resource consumption, development cycles, and the accessibility of high-quality T2I model training. This efficiency gain can accelerate research and practical applications in the field.

2. **Improved Generative Performance:** CGPO consistently achieves superior performance across multiple benchmarks (GenEval, T2I-CompBench++, DPG Bench), particularly in complex compositional tasks such as attribute binding, counting, and spatial relations. This indicates that the adaptive sampling strategy enables models to develop more robust and precise understandings of complex textual instructions, leading to higher quality, more coherent, and semantically accurate image generations.

3. **Adaptive and Dynamic Curriculum Learning:** The core innovation of using group reward variance as an online proxy for prompt inconsistency offers a generalizable mechanism for dynamic curriculum generation. This approach eliminates the need for predefined, static difficulty labels, allowing the training curriculum to evolve synchronously with the model's capabilities. This self-adaptive learning paradigm could inspire similar approaches in other deep learning and reinforcement learning domains where identifying optimal learning signals is critical.

4. **Robustness to Data Imbalance:** The category calibration mechanism, based on proportional fairness optimization, provides a method to mitigate disparities in difficulty or representation across multiple reward categories. This contributes to more balanced learning, ensuring that underperforming or inherently more challenging aspects of T2I generation receive adequate attention. Such a mechanism enhances the overall robustness and comprehensiveness of the model's capabilities when trained on diverse objectives.

5. **Generalizability of Principles:** The fundamental principles of CGPO—using an online measure of performance inconsistency to guide adaptive sampling and employing fairness-based calibration for multi-objective optimization—are not strictly limited to T2I generation. They could potentially be applied to other complex generative models or reinforcement learning tasks where sample efficiency, dynamic curriculum adaptation, and balanced learning across diverse criteria are important considerations.

In conclusion, CGPO contributes to the field by offering a more intelligent and efficient method for training T2I models. Its adaptive sampling and category calibration mechanisms enable models to learn more effectively from complex data, ultimately leading to higher quality outputs and reduced computational overhead, thereby having a tangible impact on the future development and application of generative AI.
