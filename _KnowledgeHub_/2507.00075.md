# Theoretical Modeling of Large Language Model Self-Improvement Training Dynamics Through Solver-Verifier Gap

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper models large language model self-improvement as a physics-inspired dynamical system driven by the solver-verifier gap, the uncertainty difference between a directly sampled response and a Best-of-N response the model itself scores. Coupled differential equations yield exponential convergence of both capabilities toward a fittable limit, and extend to cross-improvement, where limited external data raises the ceiling regardless of when in training it is spent.

## Problem

- Self-improvement works empirically across reasoning, alignment and planning, yet prior work offers no model of how performance actually evolves epoch by epoch during the procedure.
- Earlier theory covers self-distillation convergence for linear models, networks and general models, but never describes the training trajectory of a language model fine-tuned on its own outputs.
- Prior generation-verification-gap and sharpening accounts explain why self-improvement helps, without quantifying its capability ceiling or telling practitioners when scarce external data should be spent.

## Method

- Defines ==solver capability== and ==verifier capability== as average ==negative log-likelihood== of a sampled response versus a ==Best-of-N== response, selected by a self-assigned score threshold with length normalization.
- Models both capabilities with ==coupled differential equations== driven by a ==gap potential energy==, linearized by ==first-order Taylor expansion==, yielding closed-form ==exponential decay== plus explicit convergence limits and required-epoch bounds.
- Extends the framework to ==cross-improvement==, where external data from a stronger model rescales verifier capability each epoch, giving an ultimate-uncertainty solution that depends only on the total external budget.

## Results

- Exponential fits over ten self-improvement epochs of Phi-4-mini reach R-squared above **0.94** for solver uncertainty, verifier uncertainty and their gap on both Math and GSM8k.
- The linear gap-potential approximation holds with R-squared **0.966** and **0.899** on Math train and test splits and **0.938** on both GSM8k splits.
- Cross-improvement with DeepSeek-V3 data lifts Llama-3.2-3B Math solver accuracy **3.35** points over self-improvement, while early, uniform and late allocation differ by at most **2.17** points.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A larger initial verifier-minus-solver gap predicts a lower final solver uncertainty, so heavily post-trained models self-improve slowly because that driving gap is already small.
> - Under a limited external-data budget, scheduling barely matters: only the total amount spent moves final solver capability, so use it whenever convenient.
> - External data helps only when its verifier genuinely beats the model's own; otherwise cross-improvement can underperform plain self-improvement, as one GSM8k case shows.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) have become a prominent area in artificial intelligence, driving various applications from chatbots to mathematical reasoning and robotics. However, the training of these models typically requires extensive datasets. Concerns regarding the future depletion of available data sources and challenges in data collection have spurred research into alternative training methodologies that reduce reliance on external data.

One such approach is self-improvement, where LLMs are trained or fine-tuned using data they generate themselves. Empirical studies have indicated the effectiveness of self-improvement across different domains, including reasoning, alignment, and planning. Despite its observed utility, the theoretical principles governing self-improvement remain largely unexamined. Specifically, there is an absence of theoretical models that elucidate the underlying mechanisms and training dynamics of this process. Prior theoretical work on self-improvement has largely focused on self-distillation techniques, in-context alignment, reinforcement learning, meta-learning, and diffusion models.

This paper builds upon the foundational contributions of works such as Song et al. (2025) and Huang et al. (2025). Song et al. posited the significance of a "generation-verification gap" as key to self-improvement, while Huang et al. proposed a "sharpening mechanism" driven by the verification step. This current research draws inspiration from these concepts by employing a "solver-verifier gap" to develop a theoretical model for the *training dynamics* of self-improvement. Furthermore, it extends this theoretical framework to analyze "cross-improvement," which involves the strategic integration of limited external data to enhance model capabilities. Cross-improvement is a related area exploring the use of external data, such as human-annotated data or data from stronger models, to augment LLM performance. The present work contributes to a more fundamental understanding of how these self-driven and externally-aided improvement processes unfold over time.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that while self-improvement techniques for LLMs are practically significant, the underlying theoretical mechanisms governing their performance evolution are not well understood. Existing research lacks comprehensive theoretical models to explain how LLM capabilities improve during self-improvement training.

The central objective of this paper is to address this gap by theoretically modeling the training dynamics of LLM self-improvement. This modeling is primarily inspired by the conjecture that performance gains in self-improvement originate from a "solver-verifier gap" within the LLM itself. The authors define these capabilities as:
- **Solver capability (U_s(t))**: The quality of responses generated directly by the LLM.
- **Verifier capability (U_v(t))**: The quality of responses generated by the LLM and subsequently evaluated by the same LLM.

Specific objectives derived from this motivation include:
- To develop a theoretical framework that can quantitatively describe the entire training trajectory of LLM self-improvement.
- To utilize this framework to quantify the ultimate capability limit achievable through self-improvement by fitting the theoretical model to empirical data.
- To extend the analytical framework to cross-improvement scenarios, investigating how the integration of external data influences these training dynamics.
- To provide insights, based on theoretical analysis, into optimal strategies for allocating limited external data during cross-improvement training, particularly regarding the timing of such data integration.

By establishing a theoretical foundation for these observed phenomena, the research aims to contribute to the development of more predictable and reliable LLM training methodologies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors employ a phenomenological modeling approach to describe the training dynamics, acknowledging the inherent complexity of deriving these dynamics from the first principles of neural network processes. This approach is inspired by the concept of potential energy in physics.

#### 3.1. Definitions and Self-Improvement Algorithm

- **Uncertainty Metrics**: Model capability is quantified inversely by uncertainty. For a response $\hat{y}$ to a prompt $x$ by model $f$, its uncertainty $U_f(\hat{y})$ is defined as its negative log-likelihood: $-\log \pi_f(\hat{y}|x)$. Lower uncertainty indicates higher capability.
- **Solver Capability**: $U_s(t)$ is the average uncertainty of single responses $\hat{y}_i(t)$ directly sampled from the LLM for each prompt $x_i$ at epoch $t$.
- **Verifier Capability**: The LLM itself acts as a verifier. For each prompt $x_i$, $N$ responses are sampled. These responses are scored $s(\hat{y}_{i,j}) \in$ by the LLM. The Best-of-N (BoN) response, $\hat{y}_{BoN_i}$, is chosen as the one minimizing the length-regularized uncertainty $U_f(\hat{y}_{i,j}|x_i)/L(\hat{y}_{i,j})$ among responses with scores $s(\hat{y}_{i,j}) \ge \sigma$ (a threshold). $U_v(t)$ is the average uncertainty of these BoN responses.
- **Capability Gap**: $G(t) \triangleq U_s(t) - U_v(t)$, representing the difference between the solver's direct generation quality and the verifier's selected quality.
- **Self-Improvement Algorithm (Algorithm 1)**: In each step, the model generates $N$ responses per prompt. The BoN response is selected based on a score threshold and minimal uncertainty. The model is then fine-tuned (Supervised Fine-Tuning, SFT) to minimize the verifier uncertainty $U_v(t)$, effectively making the model generate responses similar to its internally-verified best outputs.

#### 3.2. Theoretical Framework for Self-Improvement Dynamics

The framework assumes that changes in solver and verifier capabilities are driven by a "gap potential energy" $E(t)$, which is a function of $G(t)$.
- **Initial Conditions**: $U_s(0) = U_{s,0}$ and $U_v(0) = U_{v,0}$. It is generally assumed that $U_{s,0} > U_{v,0}$ (verifier outperforms solver initially).
- **Iterative Conditions**: $\frac{dU_s(t)}{dt} = -\alpha E(t)$ and $\frac{dU_v(t)}{dt} = -\beta E(t)$, where $\alpha, \beta \ge 0$ are coefficients, and $\alpha > \beta$ implies the solver capability improves faster.
- **Linear Approximation of Potential Energy**: For tractability, $E(t)$ is approximated linearly as $E(t) \approx kG(t) - b$ (a first-order Taylor expansion). Empirical data (Figure 2 in the paper) shows a linear relationship between $G(t)$ and $dG/dt$, supporting this approximation with high $R^2$ values.
- **Resulting Dynamics (Proposition 3.1)**: Under these assumptions, the uncertainties $U_s(t)$, $U_v(t)$, and the gap $G(t)$ are shown to follow exponential decay functions, converging towards asymptotic limits $U_{s,\infty}$, $U_{v,\infty}$, and $G_\infty$, respectively.
- **Corollaries**:
 - **Corollary 3.1**: The partial derivative $\partial U_{s,\infty} / \partial G_0 = -\beta / (\alpha-\beta)$, indicating that a larger initial solver-verifier gap leads to a lower (better) final solver uncertainty.
 - **Corollary 3.2**: Provides a theoretical lower bound for the training time required for the capability gap to be within a specified tolerance $\epsilon$ of its convergence limit: $t > \frac{\ln(\delta/\epsilon)}{k(\alpha-\beta)}$.

#### 3.3. Theoretical Framework for Cross-Improvement Dynamics

This framework extends the analysis to scenarios where limited external data is introduced.
- **External Data Integration**: With a total of $M$ external data points, $\eta_t M$ prompts utilize external data in epoch $t$ (where $\sum \eta_t = 1$). For these prompts, external data serves as the BoN response; for others, internal BoN is used.
- **Cross-Data Effects**: The verifier capability is affected by external data, modeled as $U^c_v(t) = (1 + \gamma\eta_t)^{-1} U_v(t-1)$, where $\gamma$ is a coefficient representing the impact of cross-improvement (positive if external data enhances capability).
- **Modified Dynamics (Proposition 5.1)**: The ultimate uncertainty $U(T)$ after $T$ epochs is approximated as $e^{-\Delta'} U(0)$, where $\Delta'$ is a matrix that depends on the *summation* $\sum_{t=1}^T \eta_t$, rather than the individual $\eta_t$ values. This implies that the total amount of external data matters more than its specific allocation timing. It also predicts that cross-improvement can outperform self-improvement if $\gamma > 0$.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Models**: Phi-4-mini, Phi-3.5-mini, Phi-3-mini, Llama-3.2-3B, Llama-3.1-8B were used. For studying the impact of training stages, EvoLM models (Llama-2 1B at Base-SFT, Mid-train Enhanced, and Fully Post-trained stages) were employed.
- **Datasets**: Primarily mathematical reasoning datasets (GSM8k, Math). Additionally, ProntoQA (QA) and MBPP (code generation) were used with the TrueFalse method.
- **Verification Methods**:
 - **TrueFalse (TF)**: Verifier assigns a binary (0 or 1) score indicating correctness.
 - **Quality Evaluation (QE)**: Verifier assigns a continuous score for response quality.
- **Training Details**: Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) was applied for SFT, with a learning rate of 1e-5, weight decay of 0.01, solver temperature of 1, and verifier temperature of 0.1. Sample size $N$ for BoN was typically 16.
- **Evaluation Settings**:
 - **Self-Evaluation**: Explored solver and verifier performance across varying $N$ (2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64) with the same model.
 - **Cross-Evaluation**: Used one model as a solver and a different model as a verifier ($N=16$).
 - **Cross-Improvement Strategies**: Investigated "Early" (all external data in epoch 1), "Uniform" (equal external data per epoch), and "Late" (all external data in epoch 8) allocation strategies. External data was generated by DeepSeek-V3.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Dynamics of Self-Improvement

- **Empirical Trajectories**: Experiments on models like Phi-4-mini (Figure 3) demonstrated that during self-improvement, solver and verifier accuracy consistently increased, while their respective uncertainties decreased. A concurrent narrowing of the solver-verifier gap $G(t)$ was also observed. Similar trends were found for other models and methods (Figures 10-13 in Appendix).
- **Validation of Exponential Law**: Fitting the empirical uncertainty data (solver, verifier, and their gap) to the proposed exponential model revealed strong fits, with coefficients of determination ($R^2$) consistently exceeding 0.9 for Phi-4-mini across different datasets and verification methods (Figure 4). This empirically validated the exponential convergence law derived from the theoretical framework. Similar high $R^2$ values were observed for other models (Figures 14-17 in Appendix).

#### 4.2. Verifier Outperforms Solver

- **Existence of the Gap**: Experiments confirmed that the verifier capability generally outperforms the solver capability.
 - **Self-Evaluation**: Across various models (Phi, Llama families), datasets (Math, GSM8k, ProntoQA, MBPP), and verification methods (TF, QE), the verifier consistently exhibited higher accuracy and lower uncertainty than the solver, particularly as the number of sampled responses $N$ increased (Figures 18-20 in Appendix). This performance difference supports the existence and utility of the solver-verifier gap.
 - **Cross-Evaluation**: Even when different LLMs were used as solver and verifier, the verifier typically outperformed the solver (Figures 21-22 in Appendix). This further suggests that the gap is a general phenomenon.

#### 4.3. Impact of Training Stages on Self-Improvement

- Analysis using EvoLM checkpoints (Base-SFT, Mid-train Enhanced, Fully Post-trained) demonstrated how upstream training stages influence self-improvement dynamics (Figure 5 and 9).
 - **Base-SFT**: Exhibited the highest plasticity, characterized by the largest initial solver-verifier gap and decay rate.
 - **Mid-train Enhanced**: Showed a reduction in initial uncertainty and potential energy, indicating that mid-training optimizes the model's initialization state.
 - **Fully Post-trained**: Demonstrated dynamic saturation, with the lowest decay rates and minimized capability gap, confirming the theoretical prediction of asymptotic convergence as the model approaches its capability limit.

#### 4.4. Discussions on Cross-Improvement

- **Theoretical Prediction**: Proposition 5.1 predicted that for cross-improvement with limited external data, the final solver uncertainty would primarily depend on the total quantity of external data used ($\sum \eta_t$), rather than the specific timing of its allocation. It also suggested that cross-improvement would enhance solver capability if the external data improved the verifier's capability (i.e., $\gamma > 0$).
- **Experimental Validation**: Experiments involving different external data allocation strategies (Early, Uniform, Late) validated the theoretical findings (Table 1).
 - The average solver accuracy across all three strategies was generally higher than the baseline self-improvement on the Math dataset. For instance, Phi-4-mini showed a +1.16% improvement, and Llama-3.2-3B showed a +3.35% improvement compared to baseline on Math.
 - The observed differences in accuracy between the Early, Uniform, and Late strategies were marginal (Max-Min differences ranging from 0.46% to 2.17%), supporting the theoretical conclusion that the timing of external data allocation has a limited effect on final performance.
 - The effectiveness of cross-improvement was observed to be contingent on whether the external model's verifier capability significantly surpassed that of the original model. For example, Llama-3.2-3B showed a slight performance drop on GSM8k, indicating that in this specific scenario, the external data might not have sufficiently improved the verifier capability.

#### 4.5. Pass@K Analysis

- An investigation using Pass@K metrics (Figure 23 in Appendix) revealed that while self-improvement increases Pass@K for small values of K (indicating improved quality of top responses), it resulted in a slight decrease for large K. This suggests that self-improvement, by converging to certain optimal responses, might reduce the overall diversity of the generated outputs, potentially contributing to the observed performance plateaus.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the theoretical understanding of large language model self-improvement processes by introducing and validating a novel, physics-inspired theoretical framework.

The proposed framework, based on the solver-verifier gap, provides a quantitative model for the training dynamics, demonstrating that LLM capabilities (solver uncertainty, verifier uncertainty, and their gap) evolve following exponential decay laws. This offers a predictive tool for estimating training trajectories and quantifying the capability limits of self-improvement for a given model and task. Such insights can inform decisions regarding optimal training duration and resource allocation, potentially leading to more efficient and predictable LLM development.

The empirical validation of the solver-verifier gap across various models, datasets, and evaluation settings confirms its role as a fundamental driver of self-improvement. This provides a mechanistic explanation for how LLMs enhance their performance without external supervision.

Furthermore, the extension of this framework to cross-improvement offers practical guidance for scenarios involving limited external data. The finding that the timing of external data allocation has minimal impact on final performance, while the total quantity and quality are crucial, can simplify strategies for integrating external knowledge. This is particularly relevant in data-scarce environments or when leveraging proprietary strong models via API calls.

The research also highlights a potential limitation of self-improvement: a decrease in generation diversity at later stages, which may contribute to performance plateaus. This observation opens avenues for future work on maintaining diversity during self-improvement or combining it with other techniques.

Overall, this work enhances the theoretical foundation of LLM training, providing tools for analysis, prediction, and optimization of self-improvement and cross-improvement strategies. Future directions include refining the time-invariant assumptions, exploring external data for post-finetuning, and delving deeper into the underlying mechanisms that explain the observed phenomenological dynamics.
