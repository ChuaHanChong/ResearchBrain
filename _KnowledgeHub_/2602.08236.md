# When and How Much to Imagine: Adaptive Test-Time Scaling with World Models for Visual Spatial Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UNC Chapel Hill and Nanyang Technological University developed Adaptive Visual Imagination Control (AVIC), a test-time framework that enables multimodal large language models to selectively and efficiently use visual world models for spatial reasoning. AVIC improves visual spatial reasoning accuracy (e.g., GPT-4.1 from 74.0% to 79.3% on SAT-Real) while significantly reducing computational costs, such as decreasing world-model calls by approximately 17 times and language tokens by about 9 times compared to always-on imagination methods.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with complex visual spatial reasoning tasks that require understanding beyond a single static observation.
- Existing visual world models, designed to augment MLLMs with 'imagination,' employ fixed and exhaustive strategies, indiscriminately generating views regardless of necessity.
- This 'always-on' imagination leads to high computational overhead and can degrade MLLM performance by introducing misleading or redundant visual evidence.

## Method

- The ==Adaptive Visual Imagination Control (AVIC)== framework is introduced, which adaptively decides 'when' and 'how much' to invoke a visual world model during test-time inference.
- AVIC incorporates a ==policy model as a gatekeeper==, deciding whether to skip imagination or call the world model, and proposing a short, discrete action plan if imagination is needed.
- A ==trajectory-level verification mechanism== evaluates entire imagined trajectories to select the most useful one, ensuring temporal and geometric consistency for downstream reasoning.

## Results

- AVIC consistently improved average accuracy on visual spatial reasoning benchmarks like SAT-Real (e.g., GPT-4.1 from **74.0%** to **79.3%**) and MMSI-Bench compared to baseline and fixed imagination methods.
- AVIC achieved these performance gains with significantly reduced computational resources, using approximately **9 times** fewer language tokens and **17 times** fewer world-model calls than 'always-on' strategies.
- The framework successfully integrated into embodied navigation tasks (R2R), improving key metrics (e.g., Oracle Success Rate, Success Rate) and reducing Navigation Error in MapGPT with GPT-4o.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Indiscriminate, 'always-on' visual imagination is computationally inefficient (e.g., nearly two orders of magnitude more tokens, ~30x higher inference time) and can lead to performance degradation (9% of cases were 'misleading').
> - Visual imagination is most beneficial for action-conditioned spatial reasoning, yielding the largest accuracy gains in scenarios requiring predictions of future states under hypothetical actions.
> - Optimal benefits from imagination are achieved with a small, targeted number of imagined views (1-2), beyond which additional imagination provides diminishing returns or can harm performance.
