# Annotations as Rollouts: Efficient and Scalable Reinforcement Learning for Video MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> OraRL is a reinforcement learning recipe for video MLLMs that appends each ground-truth annotation as an extra oracle rollout in the on-policy group, then decouples the advantage baseline from that oracle to avoid destabilizing on-policy gradients. Sign-balanced pruning retains a small, sign-diverse rollout subset for efficiency. Video-ORA-9B beats GRPO and prior specialists across seven task families at 2.2x SFT training cost, with 130 ms answer-only decoding.

## Problem

- GRPO-style video RL only uses annotations as reward references, so groups rarely contain a rollout that hits the exact interval, box, mask, or trajectory the task needs.
- Chain-of-thought reasoning lengthens every rollout and raises training and inference cost without producing reliable accuracy gains for structured grounding tasks.
- Naively injecting the annotation as an extra rollout raises the group-mean baseline, flipping positive advantages negative for above-average on-policy samples, unexplained by prior work.

## Method

- ==Annotation-as-rollout== serializes each ground-truth annotation into the model's response format and appends it as an oracle rollout to the on-policy group without discarding sampled rollouts.
- **Decoupled advantage estimator** centers on-policy advantages on the on-policy-only mean, then adds an oracle-gap directional gain and a separately scaled, detached oracle advantage term.
- ==Sign-balanced pruning== keeps the oracle plus top-magnitude positive and negative on-policy rollouts, then re-centers and rescales via post-selection moment correction before backpropagation.

## Results

- Video-ORA-9B raises temporal grounding mIoU from **62.5** to **66.0**, tracking AO from **73.0** to **78.2**, and segmentation from **64.3** to **70.4** versus prior best models.
- Naive oracle mixing inverts **22.4%** of GRPO-positive rollouts and cuts training average from **60.3** to **55.4**; OraRL cuts the flip rate to **0.3%** after pruning and reaches **62.7**.
- OraRL needs only **2.2x** the step time of SFT versus **4.9x** for GRPO with chain-of-thought, decoding in **130 ms** instead of 4,780 ms while scoring **73.1** on VSI-Bench versus **55.0** for GPT-5.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Mixing a high-reward oracle into standard group-normalized advantage estimation silently inverts gradient signs for good rollouts, a failure mode invisible without explicit gap-based diagnosis.
> - Excluding the oracle from the baseline while keeping it as a detached target lets a single update rule handle any annotation type without task-specific reward shaping.
> - Magnitude-only rollout pruning can retain a single advantage sign and bias updates; explicitly balancing positive and negative signs preserves learning signal cheaply.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are an emerging paradigm for unified video perception, integrating capabilities such as temporal localization, spatial grounding, segmentation, object tracking, and spatial understanding within a single model. Despite their potential, current MLLMs, including large proprietary models, often lag behind task-specific models in fine-grained perception. This performance gap is attributed to limitations in task-aligned post-training rather than solely model scale.

Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) methods, a common approach, use annotations as maximum-likelihood targets, which helps enforce output formats but does not provide task-level supervision to differentiate between near-correct and clearly incorrect predictions. Reinforcement Learning (RL) methods, particularly those based on Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), attempt to address this by comparing task rewards across multiple on-policy rollouts. However, these methods typically use annotations only as scoring references for sampled outputs, and on-policy rollouts rarely precisely match the detailed specifications of annotations (e.g., exact intervals, boxes, masks, trajectories). This leads to a scarcity of high-quality positive signals for learning. The use of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, while intended to improve reasoning, exacerbates this issue by lengthening rollouts and increasing training and inference costs without consistently yielding substantial performance gains in fine-grained tasks.

This work, OraRL, directly addresses the sample efficiency and scalability challenges of RL post-training for video MLLMs. It introduces a mechanism to leverage annotations more effectively by integrating them directly into the RL optimization process as "oracle rollouts." This approach aims to provide a reliable positive optimization target for every query, thereby enhancing the learning signal and improving performance in fine-grained video perception tasks while also aiming for greater efficiency and scalability compared to existing RL and CoT-based methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the sample efficiency and scalability of reinforcement learning post-training for video MLLMs in unified video perception tasks. This involves improving the quality of the learning signal and reducing computational overhead.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in current MLLM training paradigms:

- **Lagging Fine-grained Perception:** Existing generalist MLLMs, even large proprietary ones, often underperform specialized models in precise temporal and spatial perception. This suggests that the bottleneck is not merely model scale but the effectiveness of task-aligned post-training.
- **Limitations of Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** SFT treats each annotation as a maximum-likelihood target. While this helps with output format compliance, it lacks task-level supervision, meaning it struggles to differentiate between nearly correct and fundamentally incorrect predictions at a functional level.
- **Sample Inefficiency in Existing Reinforcement Learning (RL) Methods:** Current RL methods for video MLLMs, such as those based on GRPO, primarily use annotations only as scoring references for on-policy rollouts. On-policy rollouts, particularly for fine-grained tasks (e.g., precise localization, segmentation), rarely perfectly align with the annotations. This leads to many "on-policy groups" lacking a strong positive anchor for optimization.
- **Ineffectiveness and Cost of Chain-of-Thought (CoT):** While CoT reasoning aims to improve model reasoning, applying it in the context of RL for fine-grained video tasks often increases training and inference costs (due to longer rollouts) without providing clear, consistent performance gains for precise perception.
- **Need for Reliable Positive Supervision:** A central challenge is providing a consistent and high-quality positive learning signal for RL that directly guides the model towards the precise outputs specified by annotations, without solely relying on potentially suboptimal on-policy generations.

To address these motivations, the authors propose "annotation-as-rollout," which posits that annotations themselves can serve as direct positive optimization targets. This principle aims to supply a reliable anchor for policy learning, which is currently lacking, and to do so in a task-independent manner. However, the direct integration of high-reward oracle rollouts into standard group normalization mechanisms can paradoxically lead to "advantage inversion," where otherwise good on-policy rollouts receive negative advantages. This problem motivated the development of a decoupled advantage estimator and efficient pruning strategies to ensure that the oracle provides effective and balanced supervision.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OraRL introduces a novel reinforcement learning paradigm designed to improve sample efficiency and scalability for video MLLMs by rethinking the role of annotations.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)

GRPO samples `n` on-policy rollouts `O_op = {o_i}` for a given multimodal query `q = (v, x)`. Each rollout `o_i` receives a task reward `r_i = R(o_i, q)`. GRPO estimates an advantage `A_GRPO_i` by normalizing `r_i` against the group mean `μ_grp` and standard deviation `σ_grp`. The policy objective `J_GRPO(θ)` then uses clipped importance ratios `ρ_i,t(θ)` and `A_GRPO_i` to update the policy. OraRL builds upon this objective but modifies the rollout group composition, advantage estimation, and rollout selection.

#### 3.2. OraRL Framework

OraRL's core idea is "annotation-as-rollout," treating each annotation as a direct positive optimization target.

- **Annotation-as-Rollout Construction:** For an annotation `y` associated with query `q`, a task-specific transform `T_task` serializes `y` into the model's response format to create an "oracle rollout" `o_gt`. This `o_gt` is appended to the `n` on-policy rollouts, forming an augmented group `O_aug = O_op ∪ {o_gt}`. This ensures the oracle provides supervision without reducing on-policy exploration.

- **Problem: Advantage Inversion under Mixed-Group Normalization:** Naively normalizing the augmented group `O_aug` (which includes the high-reward `o_gt`) can lead to "advantage inversion." If `r_gt` (oracle reward) is significantly higher than `μ_op` (on-policy mean reward), the mixed group mean `μ_aug` increases. Consequently, on-policy rollouts `o_i` that perform better than `μ_op` but worse than `μ_aug` receive negative advantages, even though they are positive relative to the current policy's average performance. This phenomenon suppresses potentially good on-policy rollouts.

- **Solution: Decoupled Advantage Estimator:** OraRL addresses advantage inversion by excluding the oracle from the advantage baseline while retaining it as a detached optimization target.
 - **On-policy Advantage Estimation:** Advantages `A^(0)_i = r_i - μ_op` are calculated for on-policy rollouts, centered only on the on-policy mean `μ_op` (without variance normalization). This ensures that any rollout outperforming the on-policy mean receives a non-negative advantage, eliminating advantage inversion within the on-policy group.
 - **Oracle-Gap Directional Gain (`g_q`):** To re-incorporate the oracle-policy gap, a bounded directional gain `g_q` is computed based on the change in reward dispersion when the oracle is included (`σ_aug / σ_op`). This gain is applied *only* to above-mean on-policy rollouts, amplifying their advantages without penalizing below-mean ones. A subsequent re-centering ensures the sum of on-policy advantages remains zero.
 - **Detached Oracle Advantage (`A_gt`):** The oracle's contribution is encoded separately as `A_gt`. This is derived using a normalized reward-gap weight `w_q` (which decays as the policy approaches the oracle) and capped relative to the strongest positive on-policy advantage `A^+_max`. This prevents the oracle from overly dominating the update and provides a small bootstrap signal when `w_q > 0`.

- **Sign-Balanced Advantage Pruning:** To improve training efficiency, OraRL prunes the augmented group. It retains a subset of `K` rollouts (where `K = ⌊n(1 − κ)⌋`), which always includes the oracle `o_gt`. The remaining `K-1` slots are allocated as evenly as possible between positive and negative on-policy rollouts, ranked by absolute advantage magnitude. This "sign-balanced" approach ensures that both reinforcing and suppressive signals are preserved, unlike magnitude-only pruning which might retain only one sign.

- **Post-Selection Moment Correction:** Pruning can alter the mean and scale of retained advantages. OraRL applies corrections:
 - It re-centers the selected advantages `a_i` to a zero mean (`z_i = a_i - K^(-1) Σ a_l`).
 - If centering makes the oracle advantage `z_gt` negative, it's projected to zero, and its offset is uniformly distributed across other retained rollouts (`ez_gt = 0`).
 - The retained scale is then controlled by matching the Root Mean Square (RMS) of the retained advantages (`RMS_S`) to the pre-pruning on-policy RMS (`RMS_op`), scaled by `λ_q`. This prevents selection from biasing or amplifying the policy update.

- **Unified Policy Update:** The final clipped policy objective `J_ours(θ)` is applied to the corrected advantages `b_A_i` of the retained `K` rollouts, including the oracle. This objective increases the likelihood of the oracle and positive on-policy rollouts while reducing that of negative ones.

#### 3.3. Oracle Serialization and Task Rewards

OraRL utilizes task adapters to serialize annotations `y` into `o_gt` and to evaluate rollouts `o` with scalar scores `R_k(o, y)`. A format indicator `F_k(o)` ensures only valid outputs are rewarded. Adapters are instantiated for a wide range of tasks, including temporal grounding, spatial grounding, visual tracking, segmentation, video question answering, and spatial intelligence, demonstrating the task-independent nature of the "annotation-as-rollout" principle.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

OraRL, implemented as Video-ORA, demonstrates significant performance improvements and efficiency gains across various video perception and reasoning tasks:

- **Overall Performance (Video-ORA-9B):** Without Chain-of-Thought (CoT) decoding, Video-ORA-9B achieves leading performance across seven task families. It raises temporal mIoU from 62.5 to 66.0 (compared to prior best models), tracking AO from 73.0 to 78.2, segmentation from 64.3 to 70.4, and the three-benchmark spatial-intelligence macro average from 51.0 to 56.1. On VSI-Bench, it scores 73.1, outperforming GPT-5 (55.0) and Gemini-3-Pro (55.1).

- **Temporal Grounding:** Video-ORA-9B leads every metric on three TimeLens benchmarks, improving mIoU by 2.3 to 5.0 points over temporal specialist TimeLens2-8B and exceeding Gemini-2.5-Pro. The margin widens with stricter overlap requirements, which aligns with the predicted benefit of annotation-as-rollout for precise boundary learning.

- **Referring Grounding:** Video-ORA-9B achieves first place in R@0.5 across all eight validation and test splits of RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and RefCOCOg, with gains of 0.5 to 2.7 points over the strongest baseline, particularly strong where reliance on visual appearance and object relations is critical.

- **Video Question Answering:** Video-ORA-9B ranks first on five of seven benchmarks, improving the macro average over its Qwen3.5-9B backbone from 61.9 to 66.8.

- **Visual Tracking:** On GOT-10k, Video-ORA-9B leads in Average Overlap (AO) and recall at all thresholds, surpassing OneThinker-8B by 5.2 AO points. The recall margin significantly increases at higher IoU thresholds.

- **Spatial-Temporal Grounding:** Video-ORA-9B ranks first on all four metrics for STVG, with substantial gains (15.5 and 11.9 points) on sIoU@0.5 and mean sIoU, indicating improved frame-level spatial localization.

- **Segmentation:** Video-ORA-9B achieves leading results on RefCOCO-family cIoU and MeViS J&F. It shows large improvements in video segmentation, gaining 29.2 J&F on MeViS and 42.2 on ReasonVOS over the Qwen3.5-9B backbone.

- **Spatial Intelligence:** Video-ORA-9B achieves the highest reported VSI-Bench average of 73.1 among both open-source and proprietary models, leading in object count, object size, relative distance, and appearance order. Its performance on MMSI-Bench and MindCube-Tiny is also strong, though slightly below some proprietary models.

- **Model Scaling:** Video-ORA consistently improves performance across all seven task families and exceeds its Qwen3.5 backbone at all evaluated scales (0.8B, 2B, 4B, and 9B parameters), demonstrating robust scalability.

- **Data Scaling and Reward Dynamics:** OraRL shows a consistent advantage over GRPO as the number of training prompts increases from 6.4k to 100k, with larger gains in perception and spatial intelligence aggregates, supporting the benefit of reliable positive targets for every prompt.

- **Backbone Generalization:** OraRL consistently outperforms GRPO and SFT across different backbone families (Qwen3-VL-8B and Qwen3.5-9B), indicating its general applicability.

- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Ablation:** OraRL with answer-only generation achieves better average temporal grounding scores (61.4 mIoU) and significantly faster training step times (62.4 s/step) compared to GRPO with CoT (58.5 mIoU, 135.6 s/step). This indicates CoT is not necessary and can be detrimental for fine-grained video tasks with OraRL.

- **Training Paradigm Comparison:** OraRL (62.7 average score) substantially outperforms other on-policy group RL methods (e.g., GRPO 60.3, Dr. GRPO 60.7, CPPO 60.5) and continued SFT (58.7), highlighting the benefit of direct oracle supervision.

- **Advantage Inversion Analysis:** Naive oracle mixing inverted 22.4% of GRPO-positive rollouts, with 42.5% of groups experiencing at least one inversion. OraRL effectively reduces this to 1.9% before pruning and 0.3% after pruning, demonstrating the success of its decoupled advantage estimation.

- **Component Ablations:** Each component of OraRL (directional gain, detached oracle advantage, reward-gap weight, sign-balanced pruning, moment correction) contributes to the overall performance, validating the design choices.

- **Efficiency:** Sign-balanced pruning at `κ=0.5` reduces training step time by 1.48x (from 92.5 s/step to 62.4 s/step) with a minimal performance loss of 0.4 average points. End-to-end inference latency on ten-minute videos is reduced from a median of 29.03 seconds (Qwen3.5-9B with CoT) to 24.30 seconds (Video-ORA-9B without CoT), with a substantial drop in post-TTFT latency from 4,780 ms to 130 ms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces OraRL, a novel and efficient reinforcement learning framework that significantly advances the capabilities of unified video MLLMs. By effectively integrating annotations as "oracle rollouts," OraRL addresses fundamental limitations in sample efficiency and scalability that hinder existing RL and supervised fine-tuning approaches.

The primary significance lies in its ability to consistently achieve leading performance across a broad spectrum of challenging video perception and reasoning tasks, from precise temporal grounding to complex spatial intelligence, without relying on computationally expensive Chain-of-Thought reasoning. This demonstrates a more effective and efficient pathway for MLLM post-training, particularly for tasks requiring fine-grained understanding and precise output.

The potential impact of OraRL is multi-faceted:

- **Advancement of Unified Video Perception:** By enabling a single model to excel across diverse video tasks, OraRL contributes to the development of more generalist and capable MLLMs, moving closer to foundational models for "physical intelligence."
- **Improved Efficiency:** The framework's demonstrated reductions in both training step time and inference latency (by avoiding CoT) make advanced MLLM capabilities more accessible and practical for real-world applications. This could lead to faster development cycles and more responsive AI systems.
- **Novel RL Paradigm:** The "annotation-as-rollout" principle and the proposed solution to "advantage inversion" offer a new perspective on leveraging existing data more effectively in RL contexts, potentially inspiring further research into data-efficient RL for multimodal models.
- **Scalability:** The consistent performance gains across various model sizes and data budgets suggest that OraRL provides a robust foundation for scaling MLLMs to even greater capabilities.

While the current formulation assumes well-defined, scalar-rewardable annotations, its robust performance across seven task families and two backbone architectures underscores its broad applicability. Future work exploring its behavior under ambiguous or partial supervision, and its application to even broader model families and reasoning paradigms, represents important next steps. The insights from OraRL could accelerate the development of the next generation of intelligent systems that can perceive and reason about the complex dynamics of the physical world with greater precision and efficiency.
