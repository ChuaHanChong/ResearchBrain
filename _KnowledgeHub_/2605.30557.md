# Seeing Isn't Knowing: Do VLMs Know When Not to Answer Spatial Questions (and Why)?

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced SPATIALUNCERTAIN, a diagnostic framework evaluating vision-language models' ability to recognize unreliable visual information for spatial reasoning and identify necessary additional observations. The study found that current models are overconfident when faced with missing or misleading visual evidence and struggle to proactively seek informative views, though fine-tuning with diverse uncertainty conditions can improve this observational awareness.

## Problem

- Existing spatial reasoning benchmarks for vision-language models (VLMs) implicitly assume visual observations are always sufficient and reliable, neglecting real-world complexities like occlusion and perspective distortion.
- Current VLM evaluations primarily focus on the correctness of answers rather than the model's capacity to express uncertainty, abstain from answering, or identify when visual evidence is insufficient or misleading.
- For embodied AI agents, making decisions based on unreliable visual information can lead to errors or safety hazards, highlighting the critical need for observational awareness in real-world applications.

## Method

- A controlled evaluation framework, SPATIALUNCERTAIN, was developed using 3D indoor scenes from Holodeck and AI2-THOR to systematically introduce two types of observational challenges: occlusion (missing information) and perspective ambiguity (misleading information).
- Spatial questions were generated with answerability varying based on the observation condition, including a 'Cannot determine' option for unanswerable cases, and a two-stage 'Abstain-then-ViewSelect' task to assess both abstention and informative viewpoint identification.
- Eight state-of-the-art VLMs were evaluated in a zero-shot setting, and mitigation strategies like structured prompting and fine-tuning on a small model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) were explored to improve observational uncertainty.

## Results

- Models achieved an average accuracy of only **30%** on unanswerable questions under occlusion and less than **10%** under perspective ambiguity, demonstrating a consistent failure to abstain from overconfident answering.
- Performance on the combined 'Abstain-then-ViewSelect' task dropped sharply compared to pure view selection (e.g., GPT-5.4 from **70.9%** to **22.6%**), indicating difficulty in initiating evidence-seeking behavior.
- Fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL-7B on diverse ambiguity conditions (LoRA-Mixed) substantially improved both answerable and unanswerable performance across occlusion and perspective scenarios, demonstrating the learnability and generalizability of observational uncertainty.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current VLMs exhibit systematic overconfidence, performing poorly on unanswerable spatial questions, particularly under perspective ambiguity where misleading visual cues actively suppress appropriate abstention.
> - Models struggle significantly with the proactive step of recognizing unreliable observations and subsequently identifying informative alternative viewpoints, even if they can select informative views when explicitly prompted.
> - Observational awareness, including the ability to abstain, is a learnable capability for VLMs but requires fine-tuning on diverse types of visual ambiguity to generalize effectively and resolve the answer-abstention trade-off.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has significantly advanced the capabilities of intelligent agents, particularly in perceiving and interacting with their environments, which is fundamental for embodied artificial intelligence systems. A core ability for such systems is spatial reasoning, and a substantial body of research has focused on developing benchmarks to evaluate this capability. These existing benchmarks typically assess a model's capacity to provide correct answers to spatial questions (e.g., object relations, distance, size comparison) based on visual inputs such as images or videos. A common underlying assumption in these evaluations is that the visual input provided to the model is both sufficient and reliable for the task at hand.

However, in real-world scenarios, visual observations are inherently two-dimensional projections of a three-dimensional environment, which can introduce limitations. Occlusion may render critical objects invisible, and perspective can distort geometric properties, leading to visual evidence that is either incomplete or misleading. This challenge is particularly pertinent for embodied agents, where actions based on unreliable visual information can result in errors or safety hazards. In such situations, the appropriate response for an intelligent system is not to attempt an uncertain answer but to abstain, defer judgment, or actively seek further observations.

A similar paradigm shift has emerged in the field of language modeling, where models are increasingly evaluated on their ability to express uncertainty or abstain when presented with insufficient evidence. In contrast, the exploration of uncertainty awareness within visual spatial reasoning has remained less explored, with current evaluation metrics predominantly focused on the correctness of answers rather than the recognition of observational reliability. This paper addresses this gap by constructing a framework designed to assess whether vision-language models can discern when visual observations are unreliable and identify the necessity for additional, more informative evidence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to challenge the prevalent assumption in existing spatial reasoning benchmarks that visual observations are always sufficient and reliable. The authors aim to evaluate whether vision-language models (VLMs) possess the capacity to recognize when a spatial question cannot be reliably answered from the available visual input and, furthermore, whether they can identify what additional observations would be necessary to resolve such ambiguity.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical considerations:
- **Inherent Limitations of Visual Observations:** Visual input is fundamentally a 2D projection of a 3D world. This inherent characteristic means that scenes can contain occluded objects, rendering them invisible, and perspective can lead to misleading visual cues that misrepresent actual geometric properties (e.g., an object appearing larger or smaller than its true size due to viewpoint).
- **Deficiencies in Current Benchmarking Practices:** Existing spatial reasoning benchmarks primarily focus on whether models produce correct answers. They largely overlook the critical aspect of whether models understand the limitations of their visual input, often assuming perfect or near-perfect observational conditions. This leads to an incomplete assessment of model intelligence, especially for real-world deployment.
- **Necessity for Robust Embodied AI Systems:** For embodied agents operating in dynamic and complex environments, acting on incomplete or misleading visual evidence can have severe consequences, ranging from incorrect decision-making to unsafe behaviors. An ideal intelligent agent should not guess when evidence is insufficient but should recognize its limitations and either abstain from answering or actively seek more reliable information.
- **Analogy to Language Model Uncertainty:** Recent advancements in language modeling have emphasized the importance of truthfulness, self-knowledge, and calibrated uncertainty expressions, encouraging models to abstain when evidence is insufficient. The authors argue that a similar focus on uncertainty awareness is essential for visual spatial reasoning to build more trustworthy and reliable multimodal AI systems.
- **Diagnostic Understanding of Model Failures:** By constructing a controlled evaluation framework that systematically introduces observational challenges, the research aims to uncover specific failure modes in current VLMs. This includes understanding whether models are prone to overconfident answering and if they struggle to acquire informative observations proactively.

In summary, the core motivation is to move beyond evaluating mere answer correctness to assessing a model's capacity for observational awareness—its ability to understand "when not to answer" and "what additional information is needed" for reliable spatial reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The researchers developed a controlled evaluation framework named SPATIALUNCERTAIN to assess the observational uncertainty of vision-language models in spatial reasoning tasks.

#### 3.1. 3D Scene Collection

- **Scene Generation:** 3D indoor scenes were generated using Holodeck, an LLM-based automated layout generation system that uses GPT-4o to plan object selection and placement, ensuring diverse and realistic room configurations.
- **Ground Truth:** Holodeck provides full 3D asset placement, including object positions, orientations, and bounding box information, enabling automatic ground truth annotation.
- **Rendering:** Scenes were rendered using AI2-THOR, which supports controllable camera placement and produces photo-realistic RGB images. This controlled environment allows isolating the effects of varying camera viewpoints or object configurations.

#### 3.2. Observation Challenges

Two types of observation challenges were systematically introduced:

- **Occlusion Configurations (Missing Information):**
 - **Target-Occluder Selection:** For each clean scene, target objects were selected based on clear visibility and uniqueness. Occluders were chosen based on proximity to the target and sufficient physical size to plausibly block it.
 - **Camera Placement:** The occluder was placed along the line of sight between the camera and the target, closer to the camera, subject to geometric constraints (no penetration, minimum depth separation, within room boundaries).
 - **Occlusion Types:** Due to variations in object geometry, both partial and full occlusion cases were generated. Human annotation was used to accurately categorize each configuration.

- **Perspective Ambiguity Configurations (Unreliable Information):**
 - **Object Pair Selection:** Pairs of same-category objects with similar physical sizes were selected to appear comparable under neutral viewpoints but visually distorted under perspective. This included "floor pairs" (two floor-standing objects) and "wall pairs" (two wall-mounted objects).
 - **Camera Placement:**
 - **Reference View:** Camera placed on the perpendicular bisector, equidistant from both objects, ensuring full visibility and equal distance.
 - **Perspective View:** Camera translated laterally, closer to one object, inducing systematic appearance differences (e.g., nearer object appearing larger, object viewed obliquely appearing foreshortened).

#### 3.3. Task Design

- **Spatial Question Types:** A unified set of spatial questions was constructed, with ground truth automatically derived from 3D scene geometry. Four types were considered: Visibility, Relative position, Depth ordering, and Size/Shape.
- **Systematic Answerability Variation:**
 - **Clean Observations:** All questions are answerable.
 - **Partial Occlusion:** Questions remain answerable.
 - **Full Occlusion:** Visibility questions are answerable; questions requiring access to the hidden target (Relative position, Depth, Size/Shape) become unanswerable, requiring "Cannot determine." This constitutes *missing information*.
 - **Perspective Ambiguity (Reference View):** All questions are answerable.
 - **Perspective Ambiguity (Perspective View):** Visibility and Relative position questions remain answerable; Size and Shape questions become unanswerable due to misleading visual appearance, requiring "Cannot determine." This constitutes *unreliable information*.
- **View Selection Tasks (under perspective ambiguity):**
 - **ViewSelect (ViewSel):** Models are presented with five candidate views (one informative reference view, four ambiguous alternatives) and asked to identify the best view to answer a physical size question. This evaluates pure viewpoint selection.
 - **Abstain-then-ViewSelect (AbstainViewSel):** A two-stage task. Stage 1: Model sees a biased view and answers the question or abstains. Stage 2 (if abstained): Model selects the informative view from candidates. Correctness requires both correct abstention in Stage 1 and correct view selection in Stage 2.

#### 3.4. Human Validation and Statistics

- **Scene Corpus:** 240 unique scenes (43 room types), yielding 1,222 occlusion configurations and 701 perspective object pairs.
- **QA Pairs:** 10,322 QA pairs were generated.
- **Annotation:** All scenes underwent human validation to ensure accurate categorization of occlusion and effective induction of perspective ambiguity. Configurations not meeting criteria were discarded.

#### 3.5. Evaluation Models and Protocol

- **Models:** Eight vision-language models were evaluated: three open-source (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-32B, InternVL3-8B) and five closed-source (GPT-4o, GPT-5-mini, GPT-5.4, Gemini-2.5-Flash, Gemini-3.0-Flash).
- **Setting:** Zero-shot evaluation using standardized multiple-choice prompts. Models chose one option, including "Cannot determine."
- **Metrics:**
 - **Ans. (Answerable Accuracy):** Accuracy on questions that are reliably answerable from the given view.
 - **Unans. (Unanswerable Accuracy):** Accuracy on questions where "Cannot determine" is the correct response.
 - **All (Overall Accuracy):** Micro-averaged accuracy over all questions.
 - **ViewSel Accuracy:** Accuracy on the pure view selection task.
 - **AbstainViewSel Accuracy:** Accuracy on the two-stage abstention and view selection task.

#### 3.6. Mitigation Strategies

- **Prompting:** Investigated standard prompts vs. structured reasoning prompts that explicitly guide the model to assess visibility and viewpoint reliability.
- **Fine-tuning:** Qwen2.5-VL-7B was fine-tuned using LoRA on: occlusion data only (LoRA-Occ), perspective data only (LoRA-Pers), and a mix of both (LoRA-Mixed) to assess learnability and generalization of abstention.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of vision-language models (VLMs) within the SPATIALUNCERTAIN framework revealed consistent limitations in their ability to handle observational uncertainty in spatial reasoning.

#### 4.1. Failure to Abstain Under Unreliable Observations

- **Overconfident Answering:** Models consistently performed well on answerable questions (indicating competence in spatial reasoning with sufficient visual evidence) but struggled significantly with unanswerable cases. For questions that required abstention due to missing or misleading visual evidence, average accuracy was around 30% under occlusion and below 10% under perspective ambiguity across tested models. This indicates a tendency for overconfident answering even when visual evidence is inadequate.
- **Answer–Abstention Trade-off:** There was an observed trade-off between performance on answerable questions and the ability to correctly identify unanswerable cases. Models that showed higher accuracy in abstaining often exhibited lower accuracy on answerable questions, and vice-versa (e.g., Gemini-2.5-Flash showing high Occ-Unans. but lower Occ-Ans. compared to GPT-5-mini with high Perspective Ans. but low Unans.). This suggests a lack of a unified uncertainty awareness mechanism.
- **Inconsistent Uncertainty Behavior:** No consistent pattern of abstention was observed across different model families or even within the same model across different types of observational challenges. For instance, Gemini-2.5-Flash showed relatively strong abstention under occlusion (Occ-Unans. 45.0%) but collapsed under perspective ambiguity (Pers-Unans. 2.4%). This inconsistency implies that current VLMs do not possess a generalizable understanding of when visual evidence is unreliable.
- **Sensitivity to Observation Uncertainty:** Model performance systematically degraded as the reliability of visual observations decreased. Accuracy dropped from clean views to partial, and further to full occlusion. Even partial occlusion caused performance drops, indicating a reliance on near-complete visual evidence. Under perspective ambiguity, performance particularly collapsed for questions dependent on appearance-based cues (e.g., size and shape comparison), which are prone to visual distortion, while tasks like visibility and relative position (viewpoint-invariant) remained more stable.

#### 4.2. Failure to Identify Informative Viewpoints

- **Gap Between Pure View Selection and Combined Task:** Models showed some capability in the pure ViewSelect (ViewSel) task, where they were explicitly asked to identify an informative view (e.g., GPT-5.4 achieved 70.9% and GPT-5-mini 53.7%). However, performance dropped sharply in the Abstain-then-ViewSelect (AbstainViewSel) task, which requires both correct abstention from an unreliable view and subsequent selection of an informative view. For example, GPT-5.4’s accuracy decreased from 70.9% to 22.6%, and Gemini-3.0-Flash dropped from 50.3% to 2.4%. This indicates that while some models can identify informative views when prompted, they struggle with the crucial first step of recognizing their current observation as unreliable and initiating the search for better evidence.

#### 4.3. Effect of Visual Input on Observational Uncertainty

- **Asymmetry of Visual Input:** Visual input was found to be consistently beneficial for improving performance on answerable questions across all models.
- **Missing vs. Misleading Information:**
 - Under occlusion (missing information), adding visual input generally improved unanswerable performance for some models (e.g., GPT-5.4: +6.4% on Occ-Unans., Gemini-3.0-Flash: +29.8%). This suggests visual signals can help detect the absence of evidence.
 - Under perspective ambiguity (misleading information), the effect reversed. Both GPT-5.4 (-21.7% on Pers-Unans.) and Gemini-3.0-Flash (-35.8% on Pers-Unans.) showed substantial drops in unanswerable performance with visual input. This indicates that misleading visual cues actively suppress appropriate abstention, leading models astray. The findings highlight that current models struggle to accurately assess the reliability of visual evidence, especially when it is deceptive.

#### 4.4. Toward Improving Observational Uncertainty

- **Prompting Limitations:** Structured prompting, designed to guide models to assess object visibility and viewpoint reliability, partially improved unanswerable performance for some models (e.g., GPT-5-mini’s Occ-Unans. improved from 7.8% to 30.4%). However, this often came at the cost of answerable accuracy, introducing an answer-abstention trade-off (e.g., GPT-5-mini’s Occ-Ans. dropped from 64.7% to 54.7%). Prompting alone was insufficient to reliably enhance observational uncertainty.
- **Fine-tuning Effectiveness:** Fine-tuning experiments on Qwen2.5-VL-7B showed that abstention is a learnable capability but requires diverse training exposure:
 - **Diversity is Key:** Fine-tuning on diverse ambiguity conditions (LoRA-Mixed) substantially improved both answerable and unanswerable performance across both occlusion and perspective conditions, effectively resolving the abstention–accuracy trade-off.
 - **Lack of Generalization:** Fine-tuning on single conditions (LoRA-Occ or LoRA-Pers) failed to generalize, and in some cases, exhibited negative transfer (e.g., LoRA-Pers caused a dramatic drop in occlusion unanswerable performance to 7.7%). This suggests that models require exposure to various forms of observational uncertainty during training to acquire a generalizable understanding of evidence reliability.

In conclusion, the results indicate that current VLMs are overconfident when visual evidence is unreliable and struggle to identify informative alternative viewpoints. While visual input can aid in detecting missing information, it can actively mislead models when cues are deceptive. Fine-tuning on diverse uncertainty conditions shows promise in developing more robust observational awareness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several significant contributions and highlights critical areas for future development in the field of multimodal artificial intelligence.

#### 5.1. Introduction of a Novel Diagnostic Framework

The primary significance lies in the introduction of SPATIALUNCERTAIN, a controlled diagnostic framework. This framework moves beyond conventional evaluations that primarily focus on answer correctness by systematically introducing two fundamental real-world visual challenges: occlusion (leading to missing information) and perspective ambiguity (leading to misleading information). This structured approach enables a precise diagnosis of VLM capabilities concerning observational uncertainty.

#### 5.2. Identification of Critical VLM Limitations

The study reveals two consistent and impactful failure modes in current state-of-the-art VLMs:
- **Overconfidence:** Models exhibit a systematic tendency to provide confident answers even when visual evidence is incomplete or actively misleading. This overconfidence poses a substantial risk for real-world deployment where reliability is paramount.
- **Ineffective Evidence Seeking:** Models demonstrate limited ability to identify when their current observations are unreliable and consequently struggle to select additional, more informative viewpoints to resolve ambiguity. This indicates a passive rather than proactive approach to evidence acquisition.

#### 5.3. Redefinition of Evaluation Paradigms

The findings advocate for a shift in how VLMs are evaluated. Beyond merely assessing answer correctness, future benchmarks should explicitly incorporate metrics for observational awareness, including the ability to:
- **Abstain:** Know when to refrain from answering due to insufficient or unreliable evidence.
- **Seek Evidence:** Understand what additional information is needed and actively identify means to obtain it (e.g., selecting an informative viewpoint).

#### 5.4. Implications for Embodied AI and Safety

The identified limitations have profound implications for the development of robust and safe embodied AI systems and robotic applications. In dynamic real-world environments, these agents constantly encounter imperfect visual observations. A system that cannot recognize unreliable evidence or proactively seek clarification is prone to making erroneous decisions or executing unsafe actions. The work underscores that observational awareness is a foundational capability for trustworthy embodied intelligence.

#### 5.5. Insights into Learning Mechanisms

The research provides valuable insights into the learning mechanisms of VLMs regarding uncertainty:
- **Asymmetry of Visual Input:** The finding that visual input can be beneficial for missing information (occlusion) but detrimental for misleading information (perspective ambiguity) highlights a fundamental challenge in how models process and assess the reliability of visual cues. Models currently struggle to differentiate between genuine information and deceptive appearances.
- **Learnability of Abstention:** The fine-tuning experiments demonstrate that observational awareness, specifically the ability to abstain, is a learnable capability. However, it critically requires exposure to diverse forms of visual ambiguity during training. Single-condition training proved insufficient, indicating the need for comprehensive datasets that encompass various uncertainty types.

#### 5.6. Future Research Directions

SPATIALUNCERTAIN serves as a call to action for future research. It motivates the development of VLMs that are not only proficient in spatial reasoning but are also "observational-aware"—capable of self-assessing the reliability of their inputs and proactively engaging in strategies to acquire more robust evidence. This could involve exploring novel model architectures, training objectives, and data augmentation techniques specifically designed to foster uncertainty quantification and active perception in multimodal systems.
