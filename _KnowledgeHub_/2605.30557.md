# Seeing Isn't Knowing: Do VLMs Know When Not to Answer Spatial Questions (and Why)?

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced SPATIALUNCERTAIN, a diagnostic framework evaluating vision-language models' ability to recognize unreliable visual information for spatial reasoning and identify necessary additional observations. The study found that current models are overconfident when faced with missing or misleading visual evidence and struggle to proactively seek informative views, though fine-tuning with diverse uncertainty conditions can improve this observational awareness.

## Problem

- Existing spatial reasoning benchmarks for vision-language models (VLMs) implicitly assume visual observations are always sufficient and reliable, neglecting real-world complexities like occlusion and perspective distortion.
- Current VLM evaluations primarily focus on the correctness of answers rather than the model's capacity to express uncertainty, abstain from answering, or identify when visual evidence is insufficient or misleading.
- For embodied AI agents, making decisions based on unreliable visual information can lead to errors or safety hazards, highlighting the critical need for observational awareness in real-world applications.

## Method

- A controlled evaluation framework, SPATIALUNCERTAIN, was developed using 3D indoor scenes from Holodeck and AI2-THOR to systematically introduce two types of observational challenges: occlusion (missing information) and perspective ambiguity (misleading information).
- Spatial questions were generated with answerability varying based on the observation condition, including a 'Cannot determine' option for unanswerable cases, and a two-stage 'Abstain-then-ViewSelect' task to assess both abstention and informative viewpoint identification.
- Eight state-of-the-art VLMs were evaluated in a zero-shot setting, and mitigation strategies like structured prompting and fine-tuning on a small model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) were explored to improve observational uncertainty.

## Results

- Models achieved an average accuracy of only **30%** on unanswerable questions under occlusion and less than **10%** under perspective ambiguity, demonstrating a consistent failure to abstain from overconfident answering.
- Performance on the combined 'Abstain-then-ViewSelect' task dropped sharply compared to pure view selection (e.g., GPT-5.4 from **70.9%** to **22.6%**), indicating difficulty in initiating evidence-seeking behavior.
- Fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL-7B on diverse ambiguity conditions (LoRA-Mixed) substantially improved both answerable and unanswerable performance across occlusion and perspective scenarios, demonstrating the learnability and generalizability of observational uncertainty.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current VLMs exhibit systematic overconfidence, performing poorly on unanswerable spatial questions, particularly under perspective ambiguity where misleading visual cues actively suppress appropriate abstention.
> - Models struggle significantly with the proactive step of recognizing unreliable observations and subsequently identifying informative alternative viewpoints, even if they can select informative views when explicitly prompted.
> - Observational awareness, including the ability to abstain, is a learnable capability for VLMs but requires fine-tuning on diverse types of visual ambiguity to generalize effectively and resolve the answer-abstention trade-off.
