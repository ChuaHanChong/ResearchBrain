# Towards Safe Robot Foundation Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This research develops a modular safety layer for pre-trained robot foundation models (RFMs), enabling them to operate safely by enforcing explicit, domain-specific constraints without requiring retraining or fine-tuning of the base policy. The integrated safety module demonstrates complete adherence to safety constraints while maintaining and improving task performance in a robotic air hockey task.

## Problem

- Robot Foundation Models (RFMs) offer broad generalization but lack formal safety guarantees, relying implicitly on expert demonstration data.
- Current RFMs can lead to catastrophic failures and physical damage when encountering observations outside the training data distribution.
- The absence of explicit safety assurances limits the reliable deployment of RFMs in real-world, safety-critical applications.

## Method

- A modular safety layer is introduced as a final processing step for actions generated by pre-trained generalist policies (e.g., ==Vision-Language-Action== models).
- The module extends the ATACOM algorithm to project proposed actions onto a constraint manifold, ensuring compliance with predefined, continuously differentiable safety constraints.
- Implementation requires access to the robot's system state and a control-affine dynamics model, common in robotic manipulators.

## Results

- The safety-augmented policy consistently operated without any violations of defined safety constraints throughout all tests on the physical robot.
- The unsafe baseline (unmodified policy) exhibited frequent and increasing safety constraint violations with prolonged training.
- The safety module maintained and improved task success rates as fine-tuning progressed, while the unsafe baseline's performance degraded due to safety breaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating domain-specific knowledge through a model-based safety layer effectively addresses safety shortcomings of purely data-driven RFMs.
> - A post-hoc, modular safety mechanism allows pre-trained generalist policies to operate safely without requiring extensive retraining or safety fine-tuning.
> - Safety can be rigorously guaranteed for RFMs without compromising their ability to learn and achieve complex task objectives.
