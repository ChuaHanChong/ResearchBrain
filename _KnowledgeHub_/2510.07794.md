# HiPRAG: Hierarchical Process Rewards for Efficient Agentic Retrieval Augmented Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> HiPRAG presents a framework that uses hierarchical process rewards to train agentic RAG models, enabling fine-grained optimization of search decisions. This approach significantly reduces suboptimal search behaviors like over-search and under-search, leading to improved answer accuracy and efficiency.

## Problem

- Agentic RAG systems often exhibit suboptimal search behaviors such as "over-search" (redundant retrieval) and "under-search" (missed necessary retrieval).
- These inefficiencies result in increased computational overhead and can compromise the reliability of generated outputs.
- Existing RL-based training methods primarily rely on outcome-based rewards, which lack the granularity to provide specific feedback on individual search actions during multi-step reasoning.

## Method

- A structured, ==machine-parsable== output format is introduced, decomposing the reasoning trajectory into discrete search and non-search steps.
- On-the-fly detection mechanisms for over-search (using an external LLM judge for semantic equivalence) and under-search (using a verifier for factual accuracy) are implemented.
- A hierarchical reward function combines conventional outcome and format rewards with a novel process-based bonus, applied only when the final answer and output format are correct.

## Results

- HiPRAG-**7B** achieved an average Cover Exact Match (CEM) of **67.2%**, surpassing the leading agentic RAG baseline, R1-Searcher++ (**62.1%**).
- The over-search rate (OSR) was significantly reduced from over **27%** in baselines to **2.3%** (HiPRAG-**7B** GRPO), and the under-search rate (USR) lowered to **29.0%** (HiPRAG-**7B** PPO).
- Average search calls per question were reduced from 2.45 (Search-R1) to 1.75 with HiPRAG, representing a **29%** reduction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimizing the intermediate reasoning and retrieval process at a fine-grained level, rather than solely on final outcomes, is effective for improving agent reliability and efficiency.
> - A hierarchical reward design can effectively balance multiple objectives (format adherence, final correctness, process efficiency) by gating process-level bonuses.
> - Direct, on-the-fly detection of suboptimal search actions using external LLM judges provides robust and practical feedback signals for RL training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Models (LLMs) has led to advanced capabilities in problem-solving and question-answering. However, LLMs often lack up-to-date or specific external knowledge. To address this, Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems have emerged, allowing LLMs to query external knowledge sources. The evolution of RAG systems into "agentic RAG" further enhances this by empowering LLMs to autonomously issue search queries and integrate retrieved information over multiple reasoning steps. Recent advancements in this field integrate Reinforcement Learning (RL) to train LLMs to make dynamic decisions regarding when and what to retrieve during their reasoning processes.

Despite the potential of agentic RAG, a prevalent challenge is the exhibition of suboptimal search behaviors. These include "over-search," where agents retrieve information they already possess or which is unnecessary, and "under-search," where agents fail to retrieve essential external knowledge when it is required. Such inefficiencies lead to increased computational overhead and can compromise the reliability of generated outputs.

Current RL-based training methods for agentic RAG primarily rely on outcome-based rewards. These rewards evaluate the final answer but lack the granularity to provide specific feedback on the quality or necessity of individual search actions taken during the multi-step reasoning process. Prior attempts to improve search efficiency have included length-based penalties, which risk over-suppressing search, or model confidence/knowledge awareness metrics, which serve as imperfect proxies for true step quality. This research aims to address the limitations of these existing approaches by introducing a more fine-grained, process-oriented reward mechanism that directly evaluates and optimizes each search decision.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this work stems from the observed inefficiencies in existing agentic RAG systems, specifically the widespread occurrence of over-search and under-search behaviors. These suboptimal search patterns result in:
- **Unnecessary computational overhead:** Redundant searches consume resources without contributing meaningfully to the solution.
- **Unreliable outputs:** Failure to retrieve critical information (under-search) can lead to factual errors or hallucinations, while excessive, unfocused retrieval (over-search) can obscure relevant information or complicate reasoning.

Existing training paradigms, predominantly relying on outcome-based rewards within an RL framework, are considered insufficient for addressing these fine-grained issues. Such methods provide feedback only on the final answer, neglecting the quality and efficiency of the intermediate reasoning and retrieval steps. This absence of step-specific supervision means agents do not receive explicit guidance on whether a particular search was necessary or if a search should have been performed but was omitted.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the HiPRAG framework are:
1. **Introduce fine-grained, knowledge-grounded process rewards:** Develop a mechanism to evaluate the necessity and optimality of each search decision made by the agent.
2. **Enable on-the-fly evaluation of search decisions:** Design a system that can assess search behaviors dynamically during the RL training process, rather than relying on post-hoc or aggregate metrics.
3. **Decompose reasoning trajectories into discrete, parsable steps:** Create a structured output format that facilitates clear identification and evaluation of individual reasoning and retrieval actions.
4. **Implement a hierarchical reward function:** Combine conventional outcome and format rewards with a novel process-based bonus, ensuring that agents are first incentivized for basic correctness and adherence to structure, then for the efficiency and optimality of their reasoning path.
5. **Improve both accuracy and efficiency:** Develop a methodology that simultaneously enhances the correctness of answers and reduces the rates of over-search and under-search in agentic RAG systems.

By achieving these objectives, the authors aim to overcome the limitations of prior methods and foster a more nuanced understanding of knowledge utilization in LLM agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HiPRAG introduces a novel training methodology centered on a hierarchical process reward framework. This approach is structured around three key components: a redesigned output format, efficient on-the-fly detection of suboptimal searches, and a hierarchical reward function.

#### 3.1. Decomposing Reasoning Trajectory into Parsable Steps

A fundamental challenge for process-based rewards is the ambiguity of reasoning step boundaries in existing agentic RAG outputs. Formats like Search-R1 interleave `<think>` blocks with search operations, making it difficult to isolate distinct reasoning steps or explicitly identify non-search (internal reasoning) steps.
To address this, HiPRAG enforces a structured, machine-parsable output format. The agent's system prompt is modified to generate its entire reasoning trajectory within a single `<think>` block, which then contains a sequence of discrete `<step>` blocks.
- Each `<step>` can be either a **search step** or a **non-search step**.
- **Search steps** are identified by the presence of a `<search>` block (containing the query) and a `<context>` block (containing retrieved information), alongside `<reasoning>` and `<conclusion>` tags.
- **Non-search steps** contain only `<reasoning>` and `<conclusion>` tags, indicating reliance on the model's parametric knowledge.
Adherence to this format is ensured through explicit instructions and few-shot examples in the agent's system prompt, and by incorporating a positive reward for correct format adherence within the RL framework.

#### 3.2. On-the-Fly Detection of Suboptimal Searches

With the reasoning trajectories segmented into discrete steps, HiPRAG implements efficient mechanisms for detecting over-search and under-search during the RL training phase.
- **Over-search Detection:** For each search step, the search query (`q_i`) is extracted and used to prompt the policy model as a standalone question, yielding a regenerated answer (`o'_i`). An external LLM judge (GPT-4.1 mini) then assesses the semantic equivalence between the original step's conclusion (`o_i`) and the regenerated answer (`o'_i`). If they are semantically equivalent, the search is deemed redundant and flagged as an over-search. This method is designed to be more direct and computationally efficient than prior regeneration-based approaches.
- **Under-search Detection:** For each non-search step, an external verifier model (GPT-5 mini) assesses the factual and logical accuracy of the step's reasoning (`r_i`) and conclusion (`o_i`). If the content is found to be factually or logically incorrect, the step is flagged as an under-search, indicating a missed opportunity to retrieve necessary external information. The methodology avoids penalizing locally correct but incomplete intermediate steps to prevent over-suppressing parametric knowledge use. Both detection methods can operate concurrently, and the re-generation phase for over-search can be batched to improve training speed.

#### 3.3. Hierarchical Process Reward Calculation

The core of HiPRAG's training methodology is its hierarchical reward function, designed to incentivize both correctness and efficiency without unduly penalizing necessary search actions. The reward `R(T)` for a trajectory `T` is defined as:
`R(T) = A(T) * (1 - λ_f) + λ_f * F(T) + λ_p * A(T) * F(T) * (N_corr(T) / N(T))`
Where:
- `A(T)` is 1 if the final answer `a` is correct (Cover Exact Match), 0 otherwise.
- `F(T)` is 1 if the trajectory `T` follows the required parsable format, 0 otherwise.
- `N(T)` is the total number of steps in the trajectory.
- `N_corr(T)` is the number of optimal steps (neither over-search nor under-search).
- `λ_f` is the format weight (set to 0.2 in main experiments).
- `λ_p` is the process bonus coefficient (set to 0.4 in main experiments).

This hierarchical structure ensures that the process bonus (related to `N_corr(T) / N(T)`) is only applied when both the final answer `A(T)` and the output format `F(T)` are correct. This approach prioritizes the agent's ability to produce correct, well-structured reasoning before refining its search efficiency, thereby avoiding scenarios where efficiency is gained at the expense of accuracy.

#### 3.4. Training Details

Experiments utilize a diverse set of seven QA benchmarks (NQ, PopQA, TriviaQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, Bamboogle, HotpotQA, MuSiQue) for evaluation, with NQ and HotpotQA serving as training data. Cover Exact Match (CEM) is the primary accuracy metric, complemented by Over-search Rate (OSR) and Under-search Rate (USR) for efficiency. The Qwen2.5 (3B/7B)-Instruct models are primarily used, with Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-3B models for generalization studies. Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is the main RL algorithm, with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for comparative analysis. A 2018 Wikipedia dump with E5-base retriever provides the knowledge source.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of HiPRAG demonstrates enhancements in both accuracy and retrieval efficiency across various configurations and benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Efficiency

- **Accuracy:** HiPRAG-7B achieved an average Cover Exact Match (CEM) of 67.2%, surpassing the leading agentic RAG baseline, R1-Searcher++, which recorded 62.1%. The HiPRAG-3B model also performed strongly with an average CEM of 65.4%.
- **Search Efficiency:** HiPRAG significantly reduced suboptimal search behaviors. The over-search rate (OSR) decreased from over 27% in prior baselines to 2.3% (HiPRAG-7B GRPO) and 4.9% (HiPRAG-3B PPO). Concurrently, the under-search rate (USR) was lowered to 29.0% (HiPRAG-7B PPO) and 38.1% (HiPRAG-3B PPO).
- **Average Searches:** Under a controlled Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct + PPO setup, HiPRAG reduced the average number of search calls per question from 2.45 (Search-R1) and 2.15 (β-GRPO) to 1.75, representing a 29% and 19% reduction, respectively.

#### 4.2. Analysis on Individual Parameters

- **Influence of Model Size:** Larger models (7B) generally showed higher CEM scores than 3B models. However, HiPRAG enabled smaller 3B models (e.g., Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct + GRPO at 64.4% CEM) to outperform some 7B baselines (e.g., R1-Searcher++ at 62.1% CEM) and 7B models trained with baseline rewards (61.2% CEM). Larger models also tended to make more efficient search decisions.
- **Influence of Model Family:** HiPRAG showed generalizability across Qwen2.5 and Llama-3.2 models. Qwen-3B models achieved lower OSR and USR compared to Llama-3B models with comparable accuracy, indicating that inherent model tendencies can influence final search efficiency.
- **Influence of RL Algorithm:** GRPO, while potentially less stable than PPO, yielded higher average CEM scores and more efficient search behaviors (lower OSR and USR) for both 3B and 7B models. For instance, the Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct with GRPO achieved 67.2% CEM and 2.3% OSR, compared to 64.5% CEM and 6.2% OSR with PPO.
- **Influence of Format Reward Weight (λ_f):** A format reward weight of λ_f = 0.2 provided the optimal balance between accuracy and efficiency. Higher values (e.g., 0.6) over-emphasized format correctness, leading to a degradation in overall performance.
- **Influence of Instruction Tuning:** HiPRAG was effective on both base models (Qwen2.5-3B) and their instruction-tuned counterparts (Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct). Interestingly, base models, once fully trained with HiPRAG, occasionally achieved slightly higher CEM scores and lower OSR, possibly due to learning reasoning and search behaviors more purely from the RL objective.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Output Format:** Adopting the structured, parsable output format (e.g., `Search-R1-step*` and `β-GRPO-step*` variants) maintained or slightly improved performance compared to original formats, confirming its robustness as a foundation for process-based rewards.
- **Process Bonus Coefficient (λ_p):** An optimal λ_p of 0.4 was identified. Lower values (0.2) resulted in performance similar to outcome-only rewards, failing to sufficiently incentivize efficiency (higher OSR and USR). Higher values (0.6) over-prioritized step purity, leading to a slight degradation in overall accuracy.
- **Training with Over-search or Under-search Only:** Training to reduce only over-search led to a low average CEM and a very high USR (52.7%), as the agent became too hesitant to search. Training to reduce only under-search was more effective (63.3% Avg. CEM), dramatically lowering USR but slightly increasing OSR. The best performance was achieved only when addressing both suboptimal behaviors simultaneously, confirming the necessity of a holistic optimization approach.

#### 4.4. Efficacy and Robustness (Appendix Findings)

- **Format Correctness:** 96.3% of generated trajectories adhered to the required output format.
- **Detection Efficacy:** Manual inspection revealed 98.3% accuracy for over-search detection and 95.6% accuracy for under-search detection, validating the reliability of the LLM-based judges.
- **CEM Metric Efficacy:** Cover Exact Match (CEM) judgments aligned with human assessments in 98% of cases.
- **Robustness to LLM Judges:** Experiments with open-source LLMs (Qwen3-30B, Qwen3-4B) replacing proprietary judges (GPT-4.1 mini, GPT-5 mini) showed minimal performance degradation. Over-search detection proved highly robust to judge choice, while under-search detection exhibited slightly more sensitivity, with weaker judges leading to a minor increase in USR and slight CEM decrease.
- **Computational Overhead:** HiPRAG's reward assignment accounts for approximately 12.95% of the total RL training time, an overhead mitigated by batching and asynchronous processing.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of agentic Retrieval-Augmented Generation by addressing fundamental inefficiencies that impede the reliability and cost-effectiveness of LLM-based search agents.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Process-level Optimization:** HiPRAG demonstrates the efficacy of optimizing the intermediate reasoning and retrieval process, rather than solely relying on final outcome metrics. This shift provides more granular control over agent behavior, allowing for targeted correction of suboptimal actions.
- **Hierarchical Reward Design:** The proposed hierarchical reward function is a novel approach to balancing multiple objectives (format adherence, final answer correctness, and process efficiency). By gating the process bonus with correct outcome and format, the framework ensures that efficiency gains do not compromise fundamental capabilities.
- **Efficient Suboptimal Search Detection:** The introduction of direct, on-the-fly mechanisms for detecting over-search and under-search, leveraging external LLM judges, offers a robust and practical solution for generating fine-grained training signals.
- **Improved Agent Reliability and Efficiency:** The substantial reductions in over-search and under-search rates, alongside state-of-the-art accuracy, indicate a notable improvement in the ability of LLMs to utilize external tools judiciously and effectively.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Enhanced LLM Applications:** More efficient and accurate agentic RAG systems can lead to more reliable applications in knowledge-intensive domains such as complex question answering, factual verification, and research assistance.
- **Sustainable AI:** By significantly reducing unnecessary searches, HiPRAG contributes to the development of more computationally efficient AI models, potentially lowering the energy consumption associated with deployed LLMs and large-scale AI operations.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a precedent for process-aware training in LLM agents, potentially inspiring further research into fine-grained control mechanisms for various aspects of LLM reasoning, tool use, and interactive agent development.
- **Broader Generalizability:** The demonstrated effectiveness across different model families, sizes, types, and RL algorithms suggests that the HiPRAG methodology is broadly applicable and can be integrated into diverse agentic architectures.

In conclusion, HiPRAG presents a methodological advancement in training LLM search agents, validating the importance and potential of fine-grained, knowledge-aware feedback for optimizing the reasoning process itself, ultimately leading to more capable and resource-efficient AI systems.
