# HiPRAG: Hierarchical Process Rewards for Efficient Agentic Retrieval Augmented Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> HiPRAG presents a framework that uses hierarchical process rewards to train agentic RAG models, enabling fine-grained optimization of search decisions. This approach significantly reduces suboptimal search behaviors like over-search and under-search, leading to improved answer accuracy and efficiency.

## Problem

- Agentic RAG systems often exhibit suboptimal search behaviors such as "over-search" (redundant retrieval) and "under-search" (missed necessary retrieval).
- These inefficiencies result in increased computational overhead and can compromise the reliability of generated outputs.
- Existing RL-based training methods primarily rely on outcome-based rewards, which lack the granularity to provide specific feedback on individual search actions during multi-step reasoning.

## Method

- A structured, ==machine-parsable== output format is introduced, decomposing the reasoning trajectory into discrete search and non-search steps.
- On-the-fly detection mechanisms for over-search (using an external LLM judge for semantic equivalence) and under-search (using a verifier for factual accuracy) are implemented.
- A hierarchical reward function combines conventional outcome and format rewards with a novel process-based bonus, applied only when the final answer and output format are correct.

## Results

- HiPRAG-**7B** achieved an average Cover Exact Match (CEM) of **67.2%**, surpassing the leading agentic RAG baseline, R1-Searcher++ (**62.1%**).
- The over-search rate (OSR) was significantly reduced from over **27%** in baselines to **2.3%** (HiPRAG-**7B** GRPO), and the under-search rate (USR) lowered to **29.0%** (HiPRAG-**7B** PPO).
- Average search calls per question were reduced from 2.45 (Search-R1) to 1.75 with HiPRAG, representing a **29%** reduction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimizing the intermediate reasoning and retrieval process at a fine-grained level, rather than solely on final outcomes, is effective for improving agent reliability and efficiency.
> - A hierarchical reward design can effectively balance multiple objectives (format adherence, final correctness, process efficiency) by gating process-level bonuses.
> - Direct, on-the-fly detection of suboptimal search actions using external LLM judges provides robust and practical feedback signals for RL training.
