# TASTE-Rob: Advancing Video Generation of Task-Oriented Hand-Object Interaction for Generalizable Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> TASTE-Rob is a 100,856-video dataset of task-aligned ego-centric hand-object interaction clips with fixed camera viewpoint and precise language grounding, paired with a three-stage pose-refinement video diffusion pipeline (coarse I2V generation, motion-diffusion pose refinement, pose-conditioned regeneration) that fixes grasp inconsistency and sets new SOTA on HOI video generation and downstream robot manipulation success rate.

## Problem

- Existing HOI datasets like Ego4D use varying head-mounted camera perspectives, causing viewpoint inconsistency unsuited to fixed-view imitation-learning demonstrations.
- Ego4D clips are segmented from long multi-task recordings, so language instructions often misalign with the actual action shown in the clip.
- General-purpose video diffusion models generate realistic human video but fail at accurate task understanding and stable grasp poses for manipulation-specific HOI clips.

## Method

- **TASTE-Rob** dataset: 100,856 fixed-viewpoint ego-centric clips each under 8 seconds, one action per clip, with determiner-rich language instructions precisely aligned to the depicted action.
- Stage I coarse action planner: fine-tunes ==DynamiCrafter== image-to-video latent diffusion (image context projector + spatial layers) on TASTE-Rob to map task text and environment image to a coarse HOI video.
- Stage II/III pose refinement: an ==Image-to-Hand-Params Motion Diffusion Model== refines extracted hand-pose sequences via ==partial denoising==, then a frame-wise ==pose encoder== (==ControlNet==-style) reconditions the I2V model to regenerate a fine-grained video.

## Results

- On TASTE-Rob-Test, the method scores KVD **0.03**, FVD **9.43**, PIC **0.90**, beating CogVideoX (**0.16**/**48.72**/**0.85**) and DynamiCrafter (**0.21**/**62.36**/**0.79**).
- Ablating the dataset alone: fine-tuning on TASTE-Rob (KVD **0.04**, FVD **10.85**, PIC **0.88**) far outperforms an Ego4D-trained baseline (KVD **0.18**, FVD **52.17**, PIC **0.77**).
- Adding pose refinement over the coarse planner cuts grasp classification error from **67.8%** to **9.7%** and raises downstream robot manipulation success rate from **84%** to **96%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Camera-viewpoint consistency and precise action-language alignment in demonstration data matter as much as dataset scale for imitation-learning-oriented video generation.
> - Decomposing generation into coarse semantic planning then explicit pose-space refinement resolves a fidelity gap (grasp consistency) that raw scaling of a single video diffusion model does not fix.
> - There is a spatial-awareness versus motion-consistency trade-off in partial denoising strength, so refinement should start from the coarse pose estimate rather than pure noise.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the rapidly evolving landscape of **generalizable robotic manipulation**, a critical area for deploying robots in diverse, unstructured real-world environments. Traditional robotic imitation learning (IL) methods, which enable robots to learn from expert demonstrations, are often limited by their inability to generalize to novel scenes or tasks that differ from the recorded demonstrations. This limitation arises because these methods typically require video demonstrations from environments nearly identical to the robot's execution setup.

To overcome this constraint, recent research has begun exploring the combination of IL with **generative models**, particularly video diffusion models (VDMs). The idea is to generate adaptable demonstrations for IL, thereby enhancing the robot's generalization capabilities. However, a significant bottleneck in this approach for robot-object interactions is the scarcity of large-scale, high-quality robot-specific datasets.

A promising alternative involves leveraging **human hand-object interaction (HOI) videos**, as human manipulation datasets are far more abundant. While human hand motion differs from robotic motion, advanced policy models can effectively bridge this gap. Yet, even with powerful general VDMs, generating high-quality *task-oriented* HOI videos with accurate task understanding and realistic hand postures remains a significant challenge.

Existing large-scale ego-centric HOI datasets, such as Ego4D, while impressive in scale and diversity, suffer from key limitations when applied to precise imitation learning: inconsistent camera perspectives (IL demonstrations typically require fixed viewpoints) and misaligned interactions (clips extracted from longer videos may contain partial or overlapping actions, reducing instructional clarity). These issues negatively impact the quality of generated videos and their utility for reliable robot imitation.

This paper directly addresses these critical gaps. It introduces **TASTE-Rob**, a novel, large-scale dataset specifically designed to circumvent the shortcomings of existing HOI datasets by ensuring consistent camera perspectives and meticulously aligned language-action pairs. Furthermore, it proposes a **three-stage pose-refinement pipeline** to tackle the crucial issue of inconsistent hand grasping postures often observed in VDM-generated HOI videos. By focusing on both data quality and model consistency, this work aims to create a robust foundation for advancing HOI video generation, making it a more effective tool for developing generalizable robotic manipulation policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching goal of this research is to **advance generalizable robotic manipulation** by improving the quality and consistency of video demonstrations used in imitation learning. The authors identify several specific objectives and motivations underpinning their work:

1. **Address Limitations in Existing HOI Datasets:** A primary motivation stems from the observed deficiencies in current large-scale ego-centric hand-object interaction (HOI) video datasets, particularly Ego4D. These datasets often feature inconsistent camera perspectives and poor alignment between video segments and language instructions, making them suboptimal for generating precise demonstrations required by robotic imitation learning. The objective is to create a dataset that rectifies these issues.
2. **Develop a High-Quality, Task-Oriented HOI Video Dataset (TASTE-Rob):** Motivated by the need for better data, the authors aim to introduce TASTE-Rob, a pioneering large-scale dataset of over 100,000 ego-centric HOI videos. This dataset is designed with specific characteristics to enhance clarity and consistency, including fixed camera viewpoints and meticulous alignment of single, complete interactions with language instructions.
3. **Enhance Task Understanding in Generated Videos:** While existing video diffusion models (VDMs) can generate visually impressive human videos, they often struggle with *task-oriented* HOI videos, particularly in accurately understanding *what* object to manipulate and *how* to manipulate it according to a given instruction. An objective is to train a VDM that exhibits superior task understanding.
4. **Resolve Hand Grasping Posture Inconsistencies:** Even when VDMs show proficiency in general task understanding, they frequently produce inconsistencies in hand grasping postures throughout a manipulation sequence. These unnatural changes in grip are critical drawbacks for robotic imitation learning, which demands precise and stable hand-object interactions. The authors are motivated to develop a method to achieve realistic and consistent hand poses.
5. **Achieve Superior Generalizable Robotic Manipulation:** Ultimately, the motivation is to leverage these improved HOI video generation capabilities to enable robots to perform novel tasks with greater accuracy and adaptability in unseen scenarios, thereby pushing the boundaries of generalizable robotic manipulation through more effective imitation learning.

In essence, the paper is motivated by the gap between the potential of generative models to create diverse demonstrations for IL and the practical limitations imposed by suboptimal datasets and inconsistencies in generated motions, particularly hand grasping.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research comprises two main pillars: the creation of a novel, specialized dataset called TASTE-Rob, and the development of a three-stage pose-refinement pipeline for video generation.

#### 3.1. Building TASTE-Rob Dataset

TASTE-Rob is a large-scale dataset of 100,856 ego-centric hand-object interaction videos, each paired with corresponding language task instructions. The design addresses key limitations found in existing datasets like Ego4D.
- **Collection Strategy and Camera Setting:**
 - **Consistent Camera Perspective:** Unlike datasets with dynamic head-mounted cameras, TASTE-Rob ensures all videos are recorded from a static camera viewpoint, mirroring the conditions preferred for effective robot imitation learning. The perspective is aligned with head-mounted camera setups (ego-centric).
 - **Precise Language-Action Alignment:** Each video is short (under eight seconds) and captures a single, complete action precisely corresponding to its language instruction. This is achieved through a controlled recording protocol where collectors execute a specified task from start to finish within a single recording, avoiding the segmentation issues of longer, unscripted recordings.
- **Data Diversity:**
 - **Environment and Task Diversity:** Videos are collected across various environments (kitchens, bedrooms, offices, etc.) and cover a wide range of HOI tasks (picking, placing, pushing, pouring, opening/closing, etc.). It includes both single-hand (75,389 videos) and double-hand (25,467 videos) tasks.
 - **Grasping Hand Diversity:** To ensure varied hand poses, the dataset captures diverse palm orientations (global pose) and varied grasping postures (detailed pose). Analysis using HaMeR confirms a broad distribution of palm orientations, inter-finger angles, and finger curvatures, reflecting a rich set of manipulation patterns.
- **Enriched Language Instructions:** To improve comprehension of target objects and actions, TASTE-Rob incorporates distinctive determiners for objects (e.g., "red bowl," "bowl next to the kettle") and detailed spatial information for actions (e.g., "put it in the pen holder," "put it on the pencil case"), addressing the ambiguity of instructions in other datasets.

#### 3.2. Three-Stage Pose-Refinement Pipeline

The proposed pipeline aims to generate high-fidelity, task-oriented HOI videos that exhibit both accurate task understanding and consistent hand grasping poses.

- **Stage I: A Coarse Action Planner:**
 - A learnable Image-to-Video (I2V) latent diffusion model (specifically, DynamiCrafter) is fine-tuned on the TASTE-Rob dataset.
 - This model, conditioned on a task description and an environment image, generates a "coarse" HOI video (ˆvc).
 - This stage successfully achieves *accurate task understanding* (identifying objects, general manipulation), but the generated videos suffer from *inconsistent hand grasping postures* (e.g., unnatural changes in grip over time).
 - **Training Details:** Fine-tuned DynamiCrafter for 30K steps, batch size 16, learning rate 5 × 10⁻⁵. Only image context projector and spatial layers of the U-Net are fine-tuned.

- **Stage II: Revising Hand Pose Sequences:**
 - To address the pose inconsistency, a dedicated Image-to-Hand-Params Motion Diffusion Model (MDM), an extension of MDM, is trained.
 - First, a coarse hand pose sequence (pc) is extracted from the coarse video (ˆvc) generated in Stage I using tools like MediaPipe.
 - This learnable MDM (M) is conditioned on the task description (text embedding from CLIP) and the environment image (image embedding from CLIP).
 - Instead of generating poses from scratch (Gaussian noise), the MDM *refines* the extracted coarse pose sequence (pc). This refinement process (Nrv-step denoising initialized with pc) ensures the resulting hand pose sequence (ˆp) is both *physically plausible* (from MDM's generative capacity) and *spatially accurate* (preserving information from the coarse video).
 - **Training Details:** MDM trained for 100K steps, batch size 64, learning rate 1 × 10⁻⁴.

- **Stage III: Regenerating with Refined Pose:**
 - The refined hand pose sequence (ˆp) from Stage II is then used as an *additional conditioning factor* to regenerate a high-fidelity HOI video.
 - A frame-independent pose encoder (S), inspired by ToonCrafter, is introduced. This encoder acts as a frame-wise adapter, adjusting the intermediate features of each video frame based on visualized images of the refined hand pose sequence (s). The output (Finject) is integrated into the VDM similar to ControlNet.
 - This stage generates the final "fine" HOI video (ˆv), which satisfies both accurate task understanding and consistent, realistic hand grasping poses.
 - **Training Details:** Pose encoder fine-tuned from Stable Diffusion 2 for 30K steps, batch size 32, learning rate 5 × 10⁻⁵. All parameters of the VDM are frozen during this stage.

Finally, the generated fine HOI videos (ˆv) serve as demonstrations for imitation learning, integrated with policy models like Im2Flow2Act, to enable generalizable robotic manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the significant advantages of the TASTE-Rob dataset and the proposed three-stage pose-refinement pipeline across video generation quality, grasping pose consistency, and robot manipulation performance.

1. **Superior Video Generation Quality with TASTE-Rob Dataset:**
 - **Quantitative Comparison (Table 4):** A coarse action planner fine-tuned on TASTE-Rob ("Coarse-TASTE-Rob") vastly outperforms a baseline fine-tuned on Ego4D ("Ego4D-Gen"). Coarse-TASTE-Rob achieved FVD of 10.85, KVD of 0.04, and PIC of 0.88, significantly better than Ego4D-Gen's FVD of 52.17, KVD of 0.18, and PIC of 0.77. This highlights the superior quality and suitability of the TASTE-Rob dataset for task-oriented HOI video generation.
 - **Qualitative Comparison (Figure 8):** Visual examples confirm that videos generated using TASTE-Rob exhibit more accurate actions and better generalization.

2. **State-of-the-Art Video Generation Performance:**
 - **Quantitative Comparison (Table 3):** The complete TASTE-Rob pipeline achieves state-of-the-art results compared to other powerful Image-to-Video diffusion models (DynamiCrafter, consistI2V, Open-Sora Plan, CogVideoX). TASTE-Rob recorded an FVD of 9.43, KVD of 0.03, and PIC of 0.90, which are substantially better than the next best baseline (e.g., CogVideoX with FVD 48.72, KVD 0.16, PIC 0.85). This demonstrates the overall high quality and relevance of the generated videos.
 - **Qualitative Comparison (Figure 7, 14):** Visual results clearly show that TASTE-Rob generates high-quality videos with accurate task-oriented actions, whereas general VDMs often fail to produce coherent manipulation sequences.

3. **Enhanced Grasping Pose Consistency via Pose-Refinement Pipeline:**
 - **Quantitative Comparison (Table 5):** The pose-refinement pipeline ("TASTE-Rob") significantly improves upon the coarse generation ("Coarse-TASTE-Rob") in terms of grasping pose consistency.
 - **Grasping Pose Variance (GPV):** Reduced from 0.28 to 0.24 (lower is better).
 - **Grasp Type Classification Error (GTCE):** Drastically cut from 67.8% to 9.7% (lower is better), indicating a much higher fidelity in grasping types.
 - **Hand Movement Direction Accuracy (HMDA):** Improved from 26.4° to 11.3° (lower is better), demonstrating better alignment of hand movements with the intended task trajectory.
 - **Qualitative Comparison (Figure 9):** Visual examples illustrate that the pipeline produces HOI videos with more accurate and feasible grasping poses, addressing the temporal inconsistency issue.

4. **Improved Generalizable Robotic Manipulation:**
 - **Quantitative Comparison (Table 5):** The generated videos from the full TASTE-Rob pipeline lead to a higher success rate in robot manipulation tasks. The success rate increased from 84% (Coarse-TASTE-Rob) to 96% (TASTE-Rob).
 - **Qualitative Comparison (Figure 10, 17):** Demonstrations on a Mujoco simulation dataset confirm that the higher-quality HOI videos generated by TASTE-Rob enable more precise robot manipulation, such as accurate target placement.

5. **Optimal Denoising Steps for Pose Refinement:**
 - **Ablation Study (Table 6):** An ablation study on the number of denoising steps (Nrv) for pose refinement revealed that Nrv=10 yielded the best overall video generation quality (lowest FVD/KVD, highest PIC), striking an optimal balance between pose consistency and spatial awareness. Too few steps (Nrv=0, no refinement) resulted in inconsistencies, while too many (e.g., Nrv=50, generating from Gaussian noise) degraded spatial awareness.

In summary, the authors successfully demonstrate that their meticulously curated TASTE-Rob dataset, combined with the innovative three-stage pose-refinement pipeline, significantly advances the state-of-the-art in task-oriented HOI video generation, leading to substantial improvements in the generalization capabilities of robotic manipulation systems.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "TASTE-Rob: Advancing Video Generation of Task-Oriented Hand-Object Interaction for Generalizable Robotic Manipulation" carries significant implications and potential impacts across several research domains:

1. **Catalyst for Generalizable Robotic Manipulation:** The most direct and profound impact is on the field of robotic manipulation. By providing a method to generate high-quality, task-oriented hand-object interaction videos that are suitable for imitation learning, TASTE-Rob directly addresses a major bottleneck: the limited generalization of robots to novel scenes and tasks. This work offers a scalable alternative to costly and scarce robot-specific datasets, potentially accelerating the development of more versatile and robust robotic systems capable of operating in diverse, unstructured real-world environments.

2. **Advancement in Video Generation Models:** The research significantly pushes the boundaries of video diffusion models, particularly in the context of controlled and task-oriented generation. While general VDMs excel at visual realism and temporal coherence, they often falter on fine-grained control and interaction consistency. The proposed three-stage pose-refinement pipeline introduces a novel and effective mechanism to impose precise control over hand postures, enhancing both realism and utility for specific applications. This methodological innovation could inspire future work in controllable video generation across other domains requiring high-fidelity interaction.

3. **Contribution of a High-Quality Dataset (TASTE-Rob):** The TASTE-Rob dataset itself is a substantial contribution to the research community. By meticulously curating over 100,000 ego-centric HOI videos with consistent camera perspectives and precise language-action alignment, the authors fill a critical gap identified in existing datasets like Ego4D. The public release of TASTE-Rob will provide a valuable, standardized resource for researchers working on video generation, imitation learning, human-robot interaction, and embodied AI, fostering new avenues of research and enabling fair comparisons of future methods.

4. **Bridging Computer Vision and Robotics:** This work exemplifies a successful interdisciplinary effort at the intersection of computer vision (video generation, pose estimation, action recognition) and robotics (imitation learning, manipulation). It demonstrates how advancements in generative AI can be directly translated into tangible improvements in robotic capabilities, highlighting the synergistic relationship between these fields.

5. **Potential for Broader Applications:** Beyond robotic manipulation, the ability to generate highly realistic and controllable human hand-object interaction videos could have a broader impact on various applications. These might include:
 - **Virtual Reality (VR) and Augmented Reality (AR):** For creating more immersive and interactive virtual environments.
 - **Human-Computer Interaction (HCI):** For synthesizing realistic interaction sequences in user interface design or training.
 - **Animation and Content Creation:** For generating complex human actions and interactions more efficiently.
 - **Assistive Technologies:** For training AI systems that can understand and predict human intentions in physical tasks.

6. **Future Research Directions:** The paper also openly acknowledges its limitations, particularly concerning objects undergoing significant transformations (rotation, opening). This not only provides a realistic assessment of the current state but also clearly outlines avenues for future research, indicating that this work serves as a foundational step rather than an exhaustive solution.

In conclusion, TASTE-Rob represents a significant step forward in the quest for intelligent, adaptable robotic systems. By tackling fundamental challenges in data quality and model control for task-oriented video generation, this research paves the way for more robust and generalizable robotic manipulation, while also enriching the broader fields of computer vision and artificial intelligence.
