# Hold My Beer: Learning Gentle Humanoid Locomotion and End-Effector Stabilization Control

> [!summary] Summary
> The SoFTA framework enables humanoid robots to achieve significant end-effector stabilization during dynamic locomotion by using a decoupled slow-fast control architecture. This approach reduces end-effector acceleration by 50-80% compared to baselines, allowing humanoids like Unitree G1 and Booster T1 to perform delicate tasks such as carrying liquids without spilling.

## Problem

- Integrating robust locomotion with high-precision end-effector (EE) stabilization in humanoids remains challenging due to the conflicting objectives and dynamics of these tasks.
- Existing humanoid controllers produce high end-effector accelerations during movement, making delicate tasks like carrying liquids or capturing steady video infeasible.
- Traditional single-policy control suffers from policy interference and suboptimal learning when trying to optimize for both dynamic locomotion and static EE precision simultaneously.

## Method

- ==Introduces SoFTA== (Slow-Fast Two-Agent framework), which decouples whole-body control into a "slow" lower-body agent (50 Hz for legs/waist) and a "fast" upper-body agent (100 Hz for arms).
- Each agent is trained independently with separate actor/critic networks and distinct, semantically aligned reward groups to mitigate interference and improve credit assignment.
- Employs extensive domain randomization and trains with random end-effector position commands to enhance sim-to-real transfer and promote adaptable compensation patterns.

## Results

- Achieves a 50-**80%** reduction in end-effector acceleration compared to baseline controllers, bringing stability closer to human levels (often below 2m/s²).
- Enables humanoids to successfully perform delicate tasks such as carrying nearly full cups of liquid without spilling during fast walking and strong pushes, and recording stable video footage while moving.
- Demonstrates robust sim-to-real transferability and cross-embodiment generalization, outperforming existing methods on both Unitree G1 and Booster T1 humanoid robots.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling control based on the temporal dynamics and task objectives (slow-fast frequencies) effectively resolves policy interference between locomotion and end-effector stabilization.
> - Higher control frequencies for the upper body (100 Hz) enable rapid, precise compensation for base disturbances, while lower frequencies for the lower body (50 Hz) are crucial for robust real-world locomotion.
> - Training with randomized end-effector position commands significantly improves the generalization and transferability of compensation behaviors to real-world scenarios and diverse tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robotics is a rapidly evolving field, with significant progress in both robust locomotion and intricate manipulation. Recent advances, particularly those powered by reinforcement learning (RL), have enabled humanoids to achieve impressive feats such as traversing challenging terrains, dancing, and basic object handling. However, a critical capability remains largely underexplored: the ability to perform fine-grained end-effector (EE) stabilization *during* dynamic locomotion. This work directly addresses this crucial gap.

The authors contextualize the current state of humanoid control by highlighting several limitations in existing approaches:
- **Decoupled Capabilities:** While locomotion policies (e.g., for robust walking) and manipulation policies (e.g., for precise grasping) have seen individual breakthroughs, their seamless and stable integration for tasks requiring high precision *while moving* is still a major challenge.
- **Performance Gap:** Even advanced default controllers (like Unitree G1's) produce high end-effector accelerations during basic movements (e.g., 5m/s² when tapping in place, 10x higher than human levels), making delicate tasks like carrying liquids or capturing steady video infeasible.
- **Limitations of Prior Work:**
 - **Mobile Manipulators (Non-Humanoid):** Previous efforts in stabilizing end-effectors on mobile manipulators (wheeled or aerial robots) often rely on model-based optimization. These methods, while effective, struggle with the complexity and hybrid dynamics of humanoids, requiring accurate models and predefined contact schedules.
 - **Hybrid Approaches:** Some approaches combine learned locomotion with planned arm movements, but often involve "freezing" the base during manipulation, which sacrifices whole-body coordination and dynamic interaction.
 - **Joint Learning Limitations:** While joint learning of locomotion and manipulation has been explored, it's primarily applied to non-humanoid platforms or struggles with the inherent disparities between the two types of tasks.
- **Humanoid Policy Architectures:** Single-agent whole-body policies face challenges in high-dimensional state-action spaces and struggle with conflicting reward signals when trying to optimize for both robust locomotion and precise end-effector control simultaneously. Multi-critic or decentralized control methods exist but often do not explicitly account for the fundamental *dynamic* differences between upper and lower body control.

SoFTA directly tackles this performance gap and the limitations of existing architectures by proposing a novel framework that explicitly decouples control based on temporal and objective characteristics. This represents a significant contribution to the field by enabling capabilities that were previously considered beyond the reach of dynamic humanoids, pushing them closer to operating effectively in human-centric environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable humanoid robots to execute delicate tasks that demand high-precision end-effector (EE) stabilization *concurrently* with dynamic, robust locomotion. The authors articulate this through a compelling example: "Can your humanoid walk up and hand you a full cup of beer—without spilling a drop?" This seemingly simple human ability highlights a profound challenge in robotics.

The core motivation stems from identifying a "fundamental performance gap" rooted in the inherent disparities between end-effector stabilization and locomotion tasks:
- **Task Objective Mismatch:**
 - **EE Stabilization:** Requires extreme precision and minimal motion of the end-effector, ideally with a base that remains as static as possible. It benefits from sharp, continuous, fine-grained rewards.
 - **Locomotion:** Demands traversability, robustness to disturbances, and dynamic, non-quasi-static movements, often involving momentum changes and varying gaits. It favors long-horizon, robustness-focused rewards.
 - Attempting to optimize these conflicting objectives with a single policy leads to "policy interference and suboptimal learning."
- **Dynamics Level Mismatch:**
 - **Lower Body (Locomotion):** Operates with "slow" dynamics, controlled by discrete ground contacts over relatively large timescales. It is more susceptible to the sim-to-real gap, requiring robustness.
 - **Upper Body (EE Control):** Involves "fast" dynamics, with fully actuated arms capable of continuous torques for rapid and precise corrections. It can leverage aggressive control strategies.
 - A uniform control frequency for the entire robot is suboptimal; higher frequencies can exacerbate sim-to-real issues for the lower body, while lower frequencies sacrifice precision for the upper body.

Therefore, the key objectives are to:
1. **Develop a framework** that can bridge this performance gap by explicitly addressing the temporal and objective disparities between locomotion and EE stabilization.
2. **Decouple control** of the upper and lower body to mitigate policy interference and enable specialized learning for each task.
3. **Achieve rapid, high-precision EE control** while maintaining robust, dynamic locomotion.
4. **Demonstrate these capabilities** on real-world humanoid robots in tasks requiring delicate handling and stable interaction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

To address the identified challenges, the authors propose **SoFTA (Slow-Fast Two-Agent framework)**, a novel reinforcement learning (RL) approach characterized by its decoupled architecture and frequency-aware control strategy.

#### 3.1. Framework Design: Slow-Fast Two-Agent Architecture

The central tenet of SoFTA is to decompose the whole-body control problem into two distinct, independently operating agents:
- **Upper-Body Agent:** Controls the robot's 14 arm joints. It operates at a **high frequency (100 Hz)** to enable precise, rapid adjustments for end-effector stability and compensation behaviors.
- **Lower-Body Agent:** Manages the robot's legs and waist (13 degrees of freedom). It operates at a **lower frequency (50 Hz)**, prioritizing robust locomotion and overall balance.

Both agents share the *full-body observation* to facilitate coordinated behavior, but they maintain separate actor and critic networks and do not share parameters. This allows each agent to specialize while remaining aware of the robot's overall state.

#### 3.2. Training SoFTA with Separate Reward Groups

Given the differing control dynamics and timescales, SoFTA employs **separate, semantically aligned reward groups** for each agent. This decomposition is crucial for:
- **Mitigating Reward Interference:** Prevents conflicting signals from hindering learning for either task.
- **Improving Credit Assignment:** Provides targeted feedback, allowing each agent to optimize its specific objectives more effectively.
- **Promoting Collaboration:** While rewards are distinct, both agents receive the `termination reward`, encouraging them to work together to sustain task execution.

**Reward Formulation for Stable EE Control:** The policy is trained using PPO to maximize cumulative discounted rewards. Specific rewards for EE stabilization include:
- Penalties for high linear and angular acceleration (`r_acc`, `r_ang-acc`).
- Rewards for near-zero linear and angular acceleration (`r_zero-acc`, `r_zero-ang-acc`).
- Penalty for gravity tilt in the end-effector frame (`r_grav-xy`).

**Reward Formulation for Robust Locomotion:** While not explicitly detailed in the main text abstract/intro, the methodology section mentions typical locomotion rewards:
- Tracking desired linear and angular base velocities.
- Tracking desired base height.
- Encouraging desired gait contact sequences based on phase.

#### 3.3. Problem Statement and Observations

The problem is framed as a goal-conditioned RL task.
- **Action Space:** The policy outputs target joint positions for all 27 DOFs.
- **Proprioceptive Input (`s_prop_t`):** Includes a 5-step history of joint positions, joint velocities, root angular velocities, projected gravity vectors, and past actions. The actor relies only on onboard-accessible inputs, enhancing sim-to-real robustness.
- **Goal State (`s_goal_t`):** Contains target root linear and yaw angular velocities, desired base heading, gait commands (binary stand/walk, frequency), and an EE command (`c_EE_t`). The `c_EE_t` is 5-dimensional, specifying EE activation, local x/y coordinates, global z-coordinate, and tracking tolerance (`σ`). This allows for flexible EE goal definitions.

#### 3.4. Sim-to-Real Transfer and Robustness

- **Training Environment:** Policies are trained in Isaac Gym at a simulation frequency of 200 Hz.
- **Domain Randomization:** Extensive domain randomization is applied to parameters like P/D gains, friction coefficient, base mass, control delay, and external push perturbations/forces on the EE. This is crucial for bridging the sim-to-real gap.
- **Random EE Position Commands:** A key insight for generalization is training with random EE position commands, which promotes reactive and adaptable compensation patterns, preventing overfitting to specific poses and dynamics.
- **Real-world Deployment:** Policies are deployed on Unitree G1 and Booster T1 humanoids, utilizing the HumanoidVerse sim-to-real pipeline.
- **Metrics:** EE stability is evaluated using linear acceleration norm (Acc), angular acceleration norm (AngAcc), projected gravity in the XY plane of the EE frame (Grav-XY), and z-acceleration (Acc-Z) during locomotion, reported as mean and max absolute values. Real-world data is collected using a mocap system, interpolated, and double-differentiated for acceleration calculation.

#### 3.5. Ablation Studies

The authors conducted ablations on control frequencies (33.3 Hz, 50 Hz, 100 Hz for both upper and lower body) to validate the "slow-fast" design choice. Baselines for comparison included:
1. **Robot Default Controller:** The built-in Unitree locomotion controller (real-world only).
2. **Lower-body RL + IK:** Learned lower-body policy for locomotion, with Inverse Kinematics (IK) for EE stabilization.
3. **Whole-body RL:** A single RL agent jointly controlling the entire body.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments, conducted in both simulation (Isaac Gym) and real-world environments (Unitree G1 and Booster T1 humanoids), clearly demonstrate the superior performance and practical utility of the SoFTA framework.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance Improvements

- **Significant EE Acceleration Reduction:** SoFTA achieves a **50-80% reduction in end-effector acceleration** compared to baselines, and a **2-5x reduction** specifically in mean EE acceleration. This brings the EE stability "much closer to human-level stability," with acceleration often less than 2m/s².
- **Outperformance Across Baselines:**
 - **Simulation (Table 1):** SoFTA consistently outperforms "Lower-body RL + IK" (which performs worst due to lack of dynamics awareness) and "Whole-body RL" across tapping, random command, and push tasks in metrics like linear acceleration (Acc), angular acceleration (AngAcc), vertical acceleration (Acc-Z), and gravity tilt (Grav-XY). Notably, it handles external disturbances (Push task) much better than Whole-body RL.
 - **Real-World (Table 2):** SoFTA again shows superior performance over "Robot Default Controller" (which has the highest accelerations) and "Whole-body RL" in tapping, trajectory tracking (TrajTrack), and turning tasks. It particularly excels in reducing Acc-Z, a crucial metric for stability during dynamic locomotion.
- **Strong Sim-to-Real Transfer:** SoFTA exhibits robust sim-to-real transferability, outperforming Whole-body RL, which showed "sluggish and hesitant steps" and stability issues in real-world deployments despite similar training conditions.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Capabilities and Emergent Behaviors

- **Delicate Task Execution (Figure 1, 5):** SoFTA enables the humanoid to:
 - Carry nearly full cups of liquid (e.g., beer, water bottles) without spilling, even during fast walking or fierce pushes.
 - Demonstrate strong disturbance rejection, actively compensating to maintain EE stability when the robot is pushed.
- **Humanoid as Camera Stabilizer (Figure 6):** The robot can record smooth, stable video footage during continuous turning, effectively acting as an off-gantry-level camera stabilizer, avoiding visible jitter.
- **Emergent Compensation (Figure 4):** The policy learns to actively compensate for sharp base accelerations (caused by ground contacts or external pushes) by making precise, rapid adjustments with the arm joints. This is not merely due to reduced base motion but active stabilization, with arm DOF activations aligning with locomotion rhythm and contact events.

#### 4.3. Validation of the Slow-Fast Frequency Design (Figure 7, 9)

- **Optimality of SoFTA's Frequencies:** The chosen slow-fast design (50 Hz for lower body, 100 Hz for upper body) consistently yields the lowest end-effector accelerations across various tasks and domains (tapping, sudden stops).
- **Benefits of High-Frequency Upper Body:** Higher upper-body control frequencies (e.g., 100 Hz) enable faster reaction times, quicker recovery from disturbances, and reduced peak acceleration/velocity errors, leading to enhanced disturbance compensation.
- **Challenges of High-Frequency Lower Body:** Deploying a 100 Hz lower-body policy in the real world led to degraded performance, increased oscillations, and even failures, indicating its higher sensitivity to observation noise and control delays. This validates the "slow" aspect for robust locomotion.

#### 4.4. Benefits of Random EE Position Commands (Table 3)

- Training with random EE position commands (instead of fixed ones) significantly improved generalization and transferability to the real world, fostering more reactive and adaptable compensation patterns.

#### 4.5. Cross-Embodiment Validation (Figure 8)

- The framework successfully generalized to the Booster T1 robot, a platform with different joint configurations and body proportions. The T1 policy trained with SoFTA exhibited better stability and coordination than its default controller, especially during sudden stops and precise EE control tasks, underscoring the transferability of the design principles.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SoFTA framework represents a significant advancement in humanoid robotics, offering both practical capabilities and methodological contributions with substantial potential impact.

#### 5.1. Advancement in Humanoid Robot Capabilities

- **Enabling Delicate Human-Centric Tasks:** By achieving unprecedented levels of end-effector stability during dynamic locomotion, SoFTA allows humanoids to perform tasks previously considered the exclusive domain of humans or stationary manipulators. This includes carrying liquids without spilling, precise object handling, operating sensitive tools, and stable video recording while moving. These capabilities are crucial for humanoids to genuinely operate and be useful in human-centric environments (e.g., homes, hospitals, offices).
- **Increased Reliability and Safety:** The enhanced stability and disturbance rejection capabilities contribute to safer and more reliable human-robot interaction, reducing the risk of accidents (e.g., spilling, dropping objects) during complex movements.

#### 5.2. Methodological Contribution to Robot Control

- **Principled Decoupling Strategy:** SoFTA introduces a principled approach to decomposing complex, multi-objective control problems in high-DOF robots. The temporal and objective separation into "slow-fast" agents provides a robust architectural blueprint for managing the inherent disparities between different robotic tasks (e.g., gross locomotion vs. fine manipulation). This methodology could inspire similar multi-agent or hierarchical control designs for other complex robotic systems.
- **Mitigating Reward Interference:** The effective use of separate reward groups to prevent interference is a valuable contribution to the field of reinforcement learning for complex robotic tasks, particularly where sub-tasks have conflicting optimization landscapes.
- **Insights into Control Frequencies:** The detailed ablation study on control frequencies provides critical empirical evidence on the optimal assignment of control rates for different body parts, demonstrating that a "one-size-fits-all" approach is suboptimal and can even degrade real-world performance. This insight is highly valuable for designing future high-performance robot controllers.

#### 5.3. Robustness and Generalization

- **Strong Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The demonstration of robust sim-to-real transfer is a key indicator of practical utility. SoFTA's careful use of domain randomization and random EE commands provides a valuable recipe for developing deployable RL policies for humanoids.
- **Cross-Embodiment Generalizability:** The successful deployment on different humanoid platforms (Unitree G1 and Booster T1) without embodiment-specific tuning highlights the framework's inherent generalizability, suggesting that the learned structural priors are transferable across varied robot morphologies. This is crucial for wider adoption and impact.

#### 5.4. Potential for Future Research

- SoFTA opens several avenues for future work, including exploring more dynamic coordination between the two agents (e.g., for dynamic reaching tasks), bridging the remaining gap to true human-level stability, and incorporating more advanced learning strategies (like attention mechanisms) for better generalization across diverse tasks and platforms.
- The publicly available code and project website will likely accelerate research in stable humanoid loco-manipulation by providing a strong baseline and a framework for further experimentation.

In conclusion, "Hold My Beer" is a landmark paper that addresses a fundamental challenge in humanoid robotics. By elegantly decoupling control and leveraging the distinct dynamics of the upper and lower body, SoFTA enables humanoids to perform delicate tasks that dramatically enhance their utility and integration into human environments, marking a significant step towards truly versatile and gentle general-purpose robots.
