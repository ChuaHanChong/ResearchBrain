# Hold My Beer: Learning Gentle Humanoid Locomotion and End-Effector Stabilization Control

> [!summary] Summary
> The SoFTA framework enables humanoid robots to achieve significant end-effector stabilization during dynamic locomotion by using a decoupled slow-fast control architecture. This approach reduces end-effector acceleration by 50-80% compared to baselines, allowing humanoids like Unitree G1 and Booster T1 to perform delicate tasks such as carrying liquids without spilling.

## Problem

- Integrating robust locomotion with high-precision end-effector (EE) stabilization in humanoids remains challenging due to the conflicting objectives and dynamics of these tasks.
- Existing humanoid controllers produce high end-effector accelerations during movement, making delicate tasks like carrying liquids or capturing steady video infeasible.
- Traditional single-policy control suffers from policy interference and suboptimal learning when trying to optimize for both dynamic locomotion and static EE precision simultaneously.

## Method

- ==Introduces SoFTA== (Slow-Fast Two-Agent framework), which decouples whole-body control into a "slow" lower-body agent (50 Hz for legs/waist) and a "fast" upper-body agent (100 Hz for arms).
- Each agent is trained independently with separate actor/critic networks and distinct, semantically aligned reward groups to mitigate interference and improve credit assignment.
- Employs extensive domain randomization and trains with random end-effector position commands to enhance sim-to-real transfer and promote adaptable compensation patterns.

## Results

- Achieves a 50-**80%** reduction in end-effector acceleration compared to baseline controllers, bringing stability closer to human levels (often below 2m/s²).
- Enables humanoids to successfully perform delicate tasks such as carrying nearly full cups of liquid without spilling during fast walking and strong pushes, and recording stable video footage while moving.
- Demonstrates robust sim-to-real transferability and cross-embodiment generalization, outperforming existing methods on both Unitree G1 and Booster T1 humanoid robots.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling control based on the temporal dynamics and task objectives (slow-fast frequencies) effectively resolves policy interference between locomotion and end-effector stabilization.
> - Higher control frequencies for the upper body (100 Hz) enable rapid, precise compensation for base disturbances, while lower frequencies for the lower body (50 Hz) are crucial for robust real-world locomotion.
> - Training with randomized end-effector position commands significantly improves the generalization and transferability of compensation behaviors to real-world scenarios and diverse tasks.
