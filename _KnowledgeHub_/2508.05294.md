# Towards Embodied Agentic AI: Review and Classification of LLM- and VLM-Driven Robot Autonomy and Interaction

> [!summary] Summary
> A survey proposing a taxonomy for how LLMs, VLMs, and VLA/LBM models get integrated into robotic autonomy stacks: protocol-focused, interface/agentic, orchestration-oriented, and direct/embedded integration, plus a second taxonomy of agent roles (planner, orchestration, task-specific, model-centric, generalist, generalist-systemic). Classifies academic and community/industrial systems and outlines open challenges.

## Problem

- Existing surveys on foundation models in robotics emphasize multimodal architectures and end-to-end low-level control, without examining how agents interface with existing control software, libraries, or middleware.
- No prior survey has captured emerging design patterns where LLM/VLM agents act as intermediaries to ROS, APIs, or middleware rather than as direct policy generators.
- Practical systems such as GitHub-hosted projects, ROS packages, and startup prototypes are underrepresented in academic literature despite growing real-world relevance and impact.

## Method

- A two-dimensional ==integration taxonomy== classifying systems into ==Protocol-Focused==, ==Interface/Agentic==, ==Orchestration-Oriented==, and ==Direct/Embedded== categories based on how foundation models connect to robotic control stacks.
- A complementary ==agent-role taxonomy== distinguishing ==Planner==, ==Orchestration==, ==Task-Specific==, ==Model-Centric==, ==Generalist==, and ==Generalist-Systemic== agents by their functional design and decision-making structure.
- A comparative classification table (Table II) covering representative academic and community works, scored on toolset, memory, multimodality, world model, open-source status, and adaptation mechanism.

## Results

- Table II classifies over **15** representative systems (CaP, ROSA, RAI, BUMBLE, AutoRT, Gemini Robotics, OpenMind OM1, and others) across integration approach, year, and **six** comparative dimensions.
- Analysis shows most current systems occupy a single integration category, with only a handful of frameworks (RAI, OpenMind) spanning multiple categories as multi-agent frameworks.
- Table I contrasts Protocol, Interface, and Orchestration integration along **six** qualitative axes (tooling focus, user interaction, physical interaction, agent focus), showing progressively higher autonomy and lower direct user dialogue.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Categorizing by integration pattern rather than task or model type reveals that agentic robotics is converging toward a common architectural layer regardless of application domain.
> - Memory, world models, and long-horizon temporal reasoning remain the least mature components across surveyed systems, limiting persistent situational awareness.
> - Higher agent autonomy makes evaluation harder: low-autonomy tool-based systems can be checked via tool-call correctness, while high-autonomy agents need environment-impact-based metrics that current benchmarks lack.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This review paper strategically positions itself within the burgeoning field of robotics and artificial intelligence, specifically at the intersection of large language models (LLMs), vision-language models (VLMs), and robotic autonomy. The authors acknowledge the existence of several prior surveys that address the general integration of foundation models in robotics, often focusing on multimodal architectures, general decision-making, high-level planning, or end-to-end learning frameworks for low-level control.

However, this work identifies a critical gap in the existing literature: the lack of a comprehensive examination of "the emerging design patterns in which AI agents interface with existing control software, libraries, or middleware." While previous surveys might have touched upon how foundation models are used *in* robots, they have largely overlooked *how* these powerful new AI agents are being structurally integrated into the broader robotic system architecture, particularly with an emphasis on "intelligent intermediaries" rather than direct, end-to-end policy generators. Furthermore, the paper explicitly highlights that many practical systems, including community-driven projects, ROS packages, and startup prototypes, remain underrepresented in academic literature despite their increasing real-world impact.

By focusing on "Embodied Agentic AI" and proposing taxonomies for both model integration approaches and agent roles, this review provides a much-needed framework for understanding and classifying the rapid proliferation of LLM/VLM-driven robotic systems since the advent of models like ChatGPT. It serves as a timely synthesis that not only aggregates current academic research but also critically incorporates insights from the open-source community and industry, offering a more technical and application-oriented perspective. The paper effectively bridges the gap between theoretical advancements in foundation models and their practical deployment in robotic systems, thereby carving out a unique and valuable niche in the contemporary AI and robotics research landscape.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind this review paper stems from the transformative impact of foundation models, particularly large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs), on robot autonomy and human-robot interaction. The authors observe a rapid shift from traditional end-to-end robot control to more modular systems where foundation models act as high-level intelligent agents. These agents are capable of interpreting user intent, generating plans, invoking robot APIs, and interfacing with existing middleware like the Robot Operating System (ROS), without necessarily replacing the underlying, well-tested robot software stack. This "agentic approach" offers significant advantages in flexibility, usability, and extensibility.

Given this dynamic landscape, the paper identifies a crucial need for a structured understanding of how these AI agents are being integrated and how they function within broader robotic systems. Despite the emergence of "embodied and generalist AI agents," the authors point out the absence of a survey specifically detailing the *design patterns* of AI agent interfaces with existing control software and the underrepresentation of practical, community-driven projects.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of this paper are:
- **To discuss the concept of Agentic AI in robotics**, clearly distinguishing it from purely end-to-end learning or classical symbolic planning methodologies. This aims to define the unique characteristics and advantages of the agentic paradigm.
- **To propose a comprehensive taxonomy**, structured along two critical dimensions: the *integration approach* of foundation models into robotic systems and the *functional role* that these AI agents play within the robotic architecture. This classification provides a systematic framework for analyzing and comparing diverse implementations.
- **To present a practical design toolkit**, offering guidance for the implementation and prototyping of agentic physical AI systems. This objective underscores the paper's application-oriented focus, aiding practitioners in the field.
- **To highlight key open questions and outline promising future research directions** in agentic embodied AI. This forward-looking objective aims to stimulate further innovation and address current limitations.

In essence, the paper's overarching goal is to provide a "technical and application-oriented" alternative view on the use of foundation models in robot systems, moving beyond a general overview to offer specific insights into architectural patterns, practical tools, and the path forward for truly intelligent, embodied robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This paper employs a comprehensive review and classification methodology to analyze the rapidly evolving field of embodied agentic AI in robotics. Unlike an experimental research paper, its "methodology" is the systematic approach taken to survey, categorize, and synthesize existing knowledge.

The authors' approach is characterized by several key aspects:
- **Broad Scope of Literature**: The review extends beyond traditional peer-reviewed academic papers to include "community-driven projects, ROS packages, and industrial frameworks." This inclusive scope is crucial for capturing the cutting-edge, fast-evolving nature of the field and addressing the identified gap where practical implementations often precede formal academic publications.
- **Taxonomic Development**: The central methodological contribution is the proposal of two distinct taxonomies for classifying current work:
 - **Integration Approaches (Section III)**: This taxonomy focuses on *how* foundation models are integrated into robotic systems from a conceptual and interaction perspective. It delineates four primary categories:
 1. **Protocol-Focused Integration**: Where LLMs act primarily as translators of user input into predefined tool or API calls, often through single-shot invocations (e.g., `ros2ai`, MCP servers).
 2. **Interface or Agentic Integration**: Characterized by interactive, conversational approaches where the model engages in iterative reasoning, multi-step tool use, and environmental feedback loops (e.g., ROSA, RAI, BUMBLE, OpenMind).
 3. **Orchestration-Oriented Integration**: Here, the model assumes a supervisory role, managing resources, coordinating multiple agents, skills, or subsystems (e.g., AutoRT).
 4. **Direct or Embedded Integration**: This category covers end-to-end or model-centric policies (e.g., VLAs, LBMs) that directly map perception to action in continuous control loops, often as learned submodules (e.g., RT-2, Gemini Robotics).
 - **Agent Roles and Architectures (Section IV)**: This taxonomy classifies agents based on their *functional design* and how they structure decision-making, control, and modularity, rather than their application domain. Five main roles are identified:
 1. **Planner Agents**: LLMs generate action sequences or select discrete skills, with execution handled by lower-level controllers (e.g., SayCan, SELP).
 2. **Orchestration Agents**: LLMs manage interactions between multiple skills, components, or agents, performing decision-level control (e.g., AutoRT, LABOR Agent).
 3. **Task-Specific Agents**: LLMs enhance performance for narrowly defined problems, enabling zero-shot reasoning or dynamic planning for particular objectives (e.g., BUMBLE, NavGPT).
 4. **Model-Centric Agents**: A single, unified model is responsible for grounding multimodal inputs and directly producing action outputs (e.g., LEO, RoboCat).
 5. **Generalist Agents**: Centralized reasoning models (LLMs/multimodal transformers) interface flexibly with grounded, executable components for multi-task, multi-domain operations (e.g., Voyager, ROSA, RAI). This category is further refined into Scalable Agentic Learning, LLM-Centered Skill Selectors, and Generalist Systemic Agents.
- **Practical Toolkit Compilation**: A dedicated section (Section V) is included to guide implementation, detailing essential components such as state-of-the-art LLM providers, tool-calling mechanisms (with emphasis on the Model Context Protocol, MCP), and prominent agentic frameworks like LangChain/LangGraph, LlamaIndex, CrewAI, and AutoGen. It also addresses domain-specific considerations for robotics, such as multimodal data streams and embodiment variability.
- **Identification of Open Questions and Future Directions**: The discussion section (Section VI) extends the analysis by outlining critical challenges and promising avenues for future research, including distinctions between AI Workflows, AI Agents, and Agentic AI Systems, nuances of embodiment, multi-robot coordination, ethical considerations, edge-cloud continuum integration, memory architectures, continuous data stream handling, world models, and evaluation metrics.

By systematically reviewing a broad range of sources and organizing them through novel, dual taxonomies, the authors provide a structured and actionable understanding of the current state-of-the-art and future trajectory of embodied agentic AI in robotics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The primary findings and results of this review are encapsulated in its comprehensive analysis and structured classification of LLM- and VLM-driven robot autonomy and interaction. Instead of presenting experimental results, the paper's findings are insights derived from synthesizing and categorizing a diverse body of work.

Key findings include:

- **Emergence of "AI Agent" Layer**: The review confirms a clear and rapid trend towards integrating a higher-level "AI agent" layer into robotic systems. This layer empowers robots to understand human intent more flexibly and manage their own software toolkits, moving beyond simple instruction following or end-to-end control.
- **Dual Taxonomies for Classification**: The paper successfully proposes and applies two novel taxonomies that provide a structured understanding of the field:
 - **Four Integration Approaches**: The classification into Protocol-Focused, Interface/Agentic, Orchestration-Oriented, and Direct/Embedded Integration offers a clear framework for distinguishing how foundation models are architecturally woven into robotic systems. This reveals that while direct action generation exists, many effective solutions leverage LLMs as intelligent intermediaries or translators.
 - **Five Agent Roles**: The delineation of Planner, Orchestration, Task-Specific, Model-Centric, and Generalist Agents (with further sub-categories) highlights the diverse functional capacities and architectural designs of LLM-integrated robots. This shows a progression from specialized planning to more comprehensive, adaptable generalist systems.
- **Rapid Evolution and Maturation**: The timeline analysis (Figure 2) illustrates the exponential growth and significant advancements in the field, especially since the release of ChatGPT. Early efforts focused on simple protocol integration, evolving into sophisticated agentic frameworks like ROSA, RAI, and OpenMind, which enable complex conversational interactions and multi-step tool use.
- **Prevalence of Middleware-Agnostic Approaches**: Many current systems, particularly in the Protocol-Focused and Interface/Agentic categories, demonstrate effective integration with existing robotic middleware like ROS, often abstracting robot operations into tool-enabled functions. Projects like ROS-MCP-Server exemplify efforts to create unified interfaces without requiring changes to core robot code.
- **Shift Towards Orchestration**: There is a growing trend towards using LLMs not just as direct controllers or planners, but as orchestrators that manage and coordinate multiple agents, skills, or even entire fleets of robots (e.g., AutoRT). This indicates a move towards more complex, distributed robotic intelligence.
- **Practical Toolkit Identification**: The review identifies and describes essential components for building agentic physical AI systems, including major LLM/VLM providers, standardized tool-calling protocols (like MCP), and agentic frameworks (LangChain/LangGraph, LlamaIndex, CrewAI, AutoGen). This underscores the practical, engineering-focused aspect of the field.
- **Interplay of Categories**: The authors note that more complex and advanced systems often span multiple categories of both integration approaches and agent roles, suggesting that future intelligent robotic systems will likely be hybrids, combining different strengths for higher degrees of intelligence.
- **Identification of Critical Open Challenges**: A significant result is the comprehensive outlining of numerous open questions and future research directions. These include refining the definitions of AI agents and workflows, achieving true embodiment, scaling to multi-robot systems, addressing ethical concerns, navigating the edge-cloud continuum, developing robust long-term memory, managing continuous data streams, implementing sophisticated world models, and establishing appropriate evaluation metrics. These challenges collectively define the current research agenda for the field.

In summary, the paper's main findings are a structured understanding of the current state of embodied agentic AI, a clear mapping of existing solutions, and a roadmap for future research, all informed by a broad consideration of both academic and practical developments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This review paper carries substantial significance and potential impact for the fields of artificial intelligence, robotics, and human-robot interaction. Its contributions are multifaceted, ranging from clarifying theoretical concepts to guiding practical implementation and setting future research agendas.

Firstly, its most immediate impact is **clarifying and structuring a rapidly evolving and often ambiguous research area**. The introduction of dual taxonomies for model integration approaches and agent roles provides a much-needed common language and framework for researchers and practitioners. This helps to categorize diverse projects, understand their underlying architectural patterns, and facilitate comparisons, thereby reducing fragmentation and fostering more systematic progress in the field of embodied agentic AI.

Secondly, the paper serves as a **practical guide and toolkit for developers and engineers**. By detailing state-of-the-art LLMs, tool-calling protocols like MCP, and agentic frameworks such as LangChain/LangGraph, the authors offer concrete resources for prototyping and implementing intelligent robotic systems. This practical orientation, combined with discussions on domain-specific considerations for robotics, directly empowers the community to build more capable and adaptable robots.

Thirdly, the work **legitimizes and integrates community-driven and industrial innovations** into academic discourse. By including ROS packages, GitHub projects, and startup prototypes alongside peer-reviewed literature, the review acknowledges the rapid pace of development outside traditional academic publishing cycles. This inclusive approach ensures that the survey reflects the true state of the art, bridging the gap between theoretical research and real-world application.

Fourthly, the paper's identification of **key open questions and future research directions** is crucial for setting the agenda for the next generation of embodied AI research. By highlighting challenges related to robust memory, handling continuous data streams, developing sophisticated world models, scaling to multi-robot systems, and addressing critical ethical considerations, the paper guides researchers towards impactful problems that require innovative solutions. This foresight is vital for directing resources and intellectual effort effectively.

Finally, the review reinforces a **paradigm shift towards LLMs and VLMs as intelligent intermediaries** rather than solely end-to-end policy generators. This perspective emphasizes building on existing, robust robot control stacks through high-level AI agents, which promises greater flexibility, interpretability, and safety. This approach can lead to robots that are more easily commanded by humans, adapt to novel situations with minimal retraining, and integrate seamlessly into complex environments, ultimately making intelligent interaction with robotic systems more accessible and effective across industrial, service, and even domestic applications. The potential for "higher degrees of intelligence" through combining various integration approaches and subsystems suggests a future where robots possess genuinely adaptive and collaborative autonomy.
