# D2E: Scaling Vision-Action Pretraining on Desktop Data for Transfer to Embodied AI

> [!summary] Summary
> The D2E framework introduces a scalable approach for vision-action pretraining by leveraging diverse desktop interactions, particularly from gaming, and demonstrates the transferability of these learned representations to embodied AI tasks. A 1B-parameter model trained with this framework achieves comparable or superior performance to significantly larger models on robotics benchmarks and real-world robot tasks.

## Problem

- High cost and inherent difficulties in collecting large-scale, diverse, and standardized physical robot interaction data for embodied AI.
- Lack of unified, internet-scale datasets and fragmented tooling, which impedes the development of generalist embodied AI agents.
- Inefficient methods for leveraging the vast amounts of unlabeled online video data, such as gameplay videos, for embodied learning.

## Method

- Developed the ==OWA Toolkit==, an open and scalable infrastructure for synchronously capturing diverse desktop interactions (screen, keyboard, mouse, audio) into a standardized, compressed format.
- Created a Generalist Inverse Dynamics Model (Generalist-IDM) that uses timestamp-based event prediction to pseudo-label large-scale unlabeled YouTube gameplay videos.
- Pretrained a Vision-Action PreTraining (VAPT) foundation model (InternVL3-1B) on human-collected and pseudo-labeled desktop data for transfer to various embodied AI tasks.

## Results

- The Generalist-IDM exhibited robust in-distribution performance and generalized to unseen games, achieving, for example, **63%** keyboard accuracy on Battlefield 6.
- VAPT models (1B parameters) achieved **96.6%** total success on LIBERO long-horizon manipulation tasks, matching or surpassing models up to **7x** larger, and an **80%** success rate on a real-world pick-and-place task.
- Inclusion of pseudo-labeled data in VAPT led to an 8-point performance increase (to **83.3%** success) for robot navigation on the CANVAS benchmark, particularly improving handling of misleading instructions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Desktop environments, especially gaming, provide a scalable and cost-effective source of rich sensorimotor data that can effectively pretrain embodied AI models, bypassing the high costs of physical robot data collection.
> - A generalist inverse dynamics model can effectively pseudo-label internet-scale unlabeled gameplay videos, demonstrating zero-shot generalization across diverse game domains.
> - Pretraining on desktop vision-action trajectories provides better-aligned and more efficient representations for embodied control, leading to improved performance and data efficiency in robot manipulation and navigation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have been significantly driven by the availability of internet-scale text data for pretraining. In contrast, the field of embodied AI has encountered challenges in achieving similar scaling breakthroughs due to the inherent difficulties and prohibitive costs associated with collecting physical robot trajectories. Existing embodied AI datasets are typically smaller, often domain-specific, and fragmented across various incompatible formats, which collectively impede the development of generalist agents and a self-reinforcing "data flywheel" for learning.

Prior work has explored the use of virtual environments and desktop interactions for vision-action learning. Examples include Video PreTraining (VPT) for Minecraft, which focused on a single domain, and SIMA, which demonstrated cross-game generalization but maintained proprietary data. In robotics, efforts like RT-1, RT-2, Open X-Embodiment, and LeRobot have worked to scale vision-language-action learning to real robots and aggregate heterogeneous datasets. However, these initiatives still face challenges such as fragmented tooling, hardware overhead, and safety constraints that make real-robot data collection at scale complex and expensive. For instance, DROID, a large-scale robot manipulation dataset, required significant multi-institutional effort over 12 months for a dataset of comparable scale to the human demonstrations collected in this work.

This research positions itself within this landscape by proposing desktop environments, particularly gaming, as a scalable and cost-effective alternative for pretraining embodied AI models. Desktop interactions offer rich sensorimotor feedback and a structured observation-action coupling, which are considered essential for embodied learning. The work differentiates itself by establishing a comprehensive and open pipeline from data collection to validated transfer in embodied domains, aiming to overcome the limitations of domain-specific or proprietary approaches. It also introduces a "Generalist Inverse Dynamics Model" (Generalist-IDM) that moves beyond single-domain inverse dynamics models used in prior work (e.g., VPT), and employs a timestamp-based event prediction method to address the inefficiencies of fixed-interval, tick-based action prediction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate whether interactions within desktop environments can serve as an effective and scalable substrate for pretraining vision-action models, with subsequent transferability to embodied AI tasks. The authors aim to demonstrate that sensorimotor primitives acquired from digital interactions can enhance performance in real-world physical tasks, thereby establishing desktop pretraining as a viable paradigm for embodied AI.

The motivation for this work arises from several challenges currently facing the field of embodied AI:
1. **High Cost of Physical Data Collection:** Unlike text data, which can be acquired from the internet at minimal cost, collecting embodied trajectories necessitates specialized hardware, expensive human operation, and complex annotation pipelines. This significantly limits the scale and diversity of available datasets, preventing embodied AI from leveraging the "internet-scale" data advantage seen in LLMs.
2. **Lack of Standardized and Unified Datasets:** Existing embodied AI datasets are often fragmented, domain-specific, and employ incompatible formats. This fragmentation hinders the development of generalist agents capable of performing across a wide array of tasks and environments. A unified data collection and storage framework is needed to consolidate diverse interaction patterns.
3. **Untapped Potential of Desktop Interactions:** Millions of users generate abundant and rich interaction trajectories through everyday digital activities, especially gaming. These environments naturally preserve the observation-action coupling crucial for embodied learning and abstract away hardware-specific constraints. Gaming, in particular, involves complex sensorimotor patterns such as navigation, object manipulation, and strategic planning that mirror many challenges in embodied AI.
4. **Inefficiency in Leveraging Unlabeled Online Videos:** While vast amounts of gameplay videos are available online, effectively leveraging this unlabeled data beyond manual annotation remains a challenge. A robust method for automatically pseudo-labeling actions from these videos is necessary to achieve true internet-scale data utilization.

To address these motivations, the D2E framework was developed with the specific goals of: (1) establishing a unified, open, and scalable pipeline for high-quality desktop data capture, (2) developing a generalist model capable of robust action inference for pseudo-labeling internet-scale unlabeled videos, and (3) empirically validating the transferability of learned representations from the desktop domain to physical robotics tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The D2E framework is composed of three interconnected components designed to facilitate scalable vision-action pretraining from desktop data and its transfer to embodied AI:

**1. OWA Toolkit (Open-World Agents Toolkit):**
This toolkit provides the infrastructure for capturing and storing diverse desktop interactions.
- **ocap (Omnimodal CAPture):** A specialized desktop recorder developed using Windows APIs (DXGI for screen capture, WASAPI for audio) and GStreamer for media processing. It captures multimodal streams (screen, keyboard, mouse, audio) synchronously with high temporal precision (FHD/QHD at 60 Hz) and low overhead.
- **OWAMcap (OWA MCAP):** A standardized data format that extends the robotics-standard MCAP format. It defines standardized message schemas for desktop events (screen, keyboard, mouse) to enable unified processing. The format incorporates MediaRef, which allows for efficient video storage using modern codecs like H.265/HEVC, achieving significant compression ratios (e.g., 152x for the VPT dataset, 34.45x for a CS:GO dataset).
- **Optimized Data Pipeline:** To maximize training throughput for foundation models, a four-stage pipeline was developed: (1) Media transcoding with optimized x264 parameters for consistent random access, (2) Event dataset conversion to HuggingFace datasets format, (3) Fixed Sequence Length Dataset (FSLDataset) generation that packs events into uniform lengths while preserving episode structure, and (4) On-the-fly media loading with an adaptive batch decoding strategy to defer expensive media operations and improve I/O efficiency. This pipeline achieved 10.2x higher throughput and 41x lower average disk read per image compared to a baseline.
- **Data Collection:** Using the ocap recorder, 14 human annotators collected 335 hours of demonstrations across 31 diverse games and applications (e.g., GTA V, Cyberpunk 2077, Apex Legends, Minecraft, Stardew Valley). Additionally, 2.3K hours from existing public datasets (VPT Minecraft, CS:GO) were converted to the OWAMcap format.

**2. Generalist Inverse Dynamics Model (Generalist-IDM):**
This component aims to pseudo-label large-scale unlabeled internet videos.
- **Timestamp-Based Event Tokenization:** Desktop interactions are represented as discrete events, each serialized into a token sequence. Events include Screen, Keyboard (press/release), and Mouse (clicks, movements, scrolls). Unlike tick-based IDMs, this approach directly predicts event timestamps, preserving asynchronous timing and avoiding "no-op" tokens, which enhances efficiency and temporal resolution. Numeric encoding tokens are used for mouse deltas, scroll values, and timestamps (10ms resolution, 10-second cyclic window), along with specific tokens for mouse buttons and virtual key codes.
- **NEP-τ (Next-Event Prediction with Temporal Offset):** The Generalist-IDM is trained with a next-event-prediction objective that incorporates a temporal offset (τ). The model predicts action *a_t* conditioned on observations up to *o_min(t+τ, T)* and preceding actions *a_1:t-1*. An ablation study identified τ = 100 ms as an effective offset, showing that future context is crucial for resolving the current action and that performance stabilizes with sufficient context.
- **Pseudo-labeling with YouTube Gameplay Videos:** The pipeline curates long-form YouTube gameplay videos (e.g., "no commentary" videos) with permissive licenses. These are downloaded (at 20 Hz, 480p max resolution), and frames are converted into Screen events. The Generalist-IDM, based on the InternVL3-1B architecture, then autoregressively produces Keyboard and Mouse events between consecutive screen frames using a temporal stopping criterion and sliding context window. This process generated over 1,000 hours of pseudo-labeled trajectories across twenty publicly shared titles.

**3. Vision-Action PreTraining (VAPT) Foundation Model:**
This component focuses on transferring the learned desktop representations to embodied AI tasks.
- **Training Configuration:** The InternVL3-1B model architecture serves as the backbone. Two pretraining settings were explored:
 - **VAPT without pseudo-labels:** Trained solely on the 259 hours of human-collected desktop demonstrations.
 - **VAPT with pseudo-labels:** Trained on the combined dataset of human demonstrations and the 1K+ hours of pseudo-labeled YouTube gameplay (totaling 1.3K+ hours).
- **Transfer Hypotheses:** The authors hypothesize that desktop pretraining facilitates transfer due to: (1) explicit vision-action trajectory training that aligns action modalities, (2) learning goal-directed sequential decision-making patterns from gameplay, and (3) high diversity of the multi-game corpus encouraging general-purpose control priors.
- **Downstream Evaluation:** VAPT models were evaluated on simulated robot manipulation tasks (LIBERO, Meta-World), a real-world pick-and-place manipulation task (SO101 robot arm), and a simulated robot navigation task (CANVAS).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

**Performance of the Generalist-IDM:**
- **In-Distribution Performance:** The Generalist-IDM demonstrated robust performance across six in-distribution 2D and 3D games (Brotato, Stardew Valley, Core Keeper, Apex Legends, GTA V, Minecraft). It showed improvements over game-specific Specialist-IDMs, with notable gains in Pearson correlation for mouse movement (e.g., +39.5 points on Stardew Valley X-axis) and Keyboard accuracy (e.g., +57.6 points on Brotato), indicating its ability to generalize across diverse control dynamics within seen domains.
- **Out-of-Distribution Generalization:** On unseen games, the Generalist-IDM exhibited generalization capabilities. For Battlefield 6 (3D FPS), it achieved 63% keyboard accuracy, comparable to or slightly exceeding a Specialist-IDM. Providing a few-shot prefix improved the predicted mouse scale ratio, suggesting an in-context adaptation capability. For Ogu and the Secret Forest (2D adventure), the Generalist-IDM more than doubled the Specialist-IDM's performance (from approximately 12% to nearly 28% keyboard accuracy), indicating effective transfer even with significant domain gaps.

**Transferability to Downstream Tasks:**
- **Robot Manipulation (LIBERO Benchmark):** VAPT without pseudo-labels achieved a total success rate of 96.6% and 93.6% on long-horizon tasks. This performance was comparable to or surpassed much larger models, such as π0 (3.3B parameters) and OpenVLA (7B parameters). Interestingly, incorporating pseudo-labeled data (VAPT with pseudo-labels) did not yield additional gains on manipulation tasks, achieving 92.2% total success. The authors attribute this to the potential requirement for precise human supervision in manipulation tasks.
- **Robot Manipulation (Meta-World Benchmark):** VAPT consistently outperformed the InternVL3-1B baseline across tasks of varying difficulty, showing an average success rate improvement of approximately 5%, representing a relative gain of about 25%. The performance advantage was most pronounced in the Hard and Very Hard categories (e.g., 8.0% vs. 20.0–24.0% on Very Hard), suggesting robustness in complex manipulation scenarios.
- **Robot Manipulation (Real-World SO101 Pick-and-Place):** In a real-world pick-and-place task, the baseline InternVL3-1B achieved a 70% success rate. Both VAPT variants (with and without pseudo-labels) achieved an 80% success rate, providing empirical evidence for effective transfer to real-world robot hardware.
- **Robot Navigation (CANVAS Benchmark):** For robot navigation, VAPT without pseudo-labels performed comparably to the baseline (75.3%). However, the inclusion of pseudo-labeled demonstrations (VAPT with pseudo-labels) led to an 8-point increase in performance, reaching 83.3% success. This benefit was particularly significant under misleading instructions (e.g., 86.7% vs. 53.3% in `sim_orchard`), suggesting that pseudo-labeling contributes to improved high-level planning for navigation tasks.
- **Training Loss Curves:** Analysis of training loss curves for downstream tasks (LIBERO and CANVAS) indicated that VAPT-initialized models converged immediately and maintained lower loss values throughout training compared to baseline models, which often exhibited an initial plateau. This suggests that desktop pretraining provides better-aligned representations for embodied control.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several contributions to the field of embodied AI and large-scale vision-action learning.
The work provides evidence that desktop interactions, particularly from gaming, can serve as a practical and scalable substrate for pretraining embodied AI models. This addresses a critical bottleneck in embodied AI research—the high cost and difficulty of collecting large-scale physical robot interaction data. By demonstrating effective transfer of sensorimotor primitives from the digital to the physical domain, the D2E framework offers a pathway to leverage abundant, low-cost data for tasks such as robot manipulation and navigation.

The introduction of the **OWA Toolkit** provides a significant practical contribution. Its ability to unify diverse desktop interactions into a standardized, compressed format (achieving up to 152x compression) and its optimized data pipeline for efficient ML training reduce the technical and computational barriers to large-scale data collection. This enables broader participation in collecting and sharing desktop datasets, which could accelerate research in this area.

The **Generalist-IDM** marks a step towards creating more adaptable agents. Its capacity for zero-shot generalization across unseen games and in-context adaptation (e.g., mouse sensitivity) is a key advancement, enabling efficient pseudo-labeling of internet-scale videos. This capability broadens the potential data sources for embodied AI beyond meticulously collected human demonstrations.

The **VAPT foundation model** demonstrates that a 1B-parameter model pretrained on 1.3K+ hours of desktop data can match or exceed the performance of significantly larger models (up to 7x larger) on established robotics benchmarks like LIBERO manipulation and CANVAS navigation. This suggests that the learned representations are efficient and effective, potentially democratizing access to powerful embodied AI models for researchers with limited computational resources (evidenced by the IDM training cost of approximately $800 for 192 H100-hours).

The differential impact of pseudo-labels, enhancing navigation tasks but not manipulation, suggests avenues for future research into task-specific transfer mechanisms and the quality requirements of pretraining data for different embodied skills. Overall, this work contributes to establishing desktop data as a viable and scalable resource for advancing embodied intelligence, moving towards more general-purpose agents without relying exclusively on prohibitively expensive physical data collection. The public release of all tools, datasets, and models further supports reproducibility and community building.
