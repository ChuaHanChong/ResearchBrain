# Safe Exploration via Policy Priors

> [!summary] Summary
> SOOPER is a model-based reinforcement learning algorithm developed at ETH Zurich that ensures high-probability safety throughout online learning and achieves sublinear cumulative regret for optimal policy convergence. This method leverages conservative policy priors for safe exploration and has been validated on benchmarks and real-world robotic hardware, achieving approximately twice the reward of an initial prior while maintaining strict safety.

## Problem

- Deploying reinforcement learning agents in physical, real-world environments necessitates ensuring safety during online exploration, where catastrophic outcomes are unacceptable.
- Existing safe RL methods either offer strong theoretical guarantees but lack scalability to complex, high-dimensional problems, or they are scalable but fail to consistently ensure safety throughout the entire learning process.
- Ineffective utilization of prior knowledge (e.g., from offline data or simulators) to simultaneously ensure safety and guide efficient exploration.

## Method

- **SOOPER** combines pessimistic invocation of a ==conservative policy prior== during online data collection with optimistic planning in a learned world model.
- The algorithm continuously tracks predicted accumulated costs, falling back to a safe policy prior if an action from the current policy risks exceeding a predefined safety budget.
- The constrained optimization problem is reformulated into an unconstrained MDP for planning, which includes intrinsic rewards that encourage optimistic exploration and active expansion of the known safe policy set.

## Results

- SOOPER provides theoretical guarantees for high-probability safety throughout online learning (Theorem 1) and achieves sublinear cumulative regret for convergence to an optimal policy (Theorem 2).
- Empirically, the algorithm maintained safety (no constraint violations) and matched or outperformed state-of-the-art baselines in performance across safe transfer, vision-based control, and offline-to-online learning tasks.
- SOOPER successfully fine-tuned policies on real-world robotic hardware (a race car), achieving approximately twice the reward of an initial prior policy while strictly adhering to safety constraints during real-time online learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Conservative, suboptimal policy priors can be effectively integrated to provide high-probability safety guarantees during online learning, even under initial model uncertainty or distribution shifts.
> - An online cost-tracking mechanism, which switches to a safe prior when an uncertainty-penalized accumulated cost exceeds a budget, ensures safety throughout all learning episodes.
> - Reformulating the constrained problem as an unconstrained MDP with intrinsic exploration and expansion bonuses enables both scalability with deep RL and provable sublinear cumulative regret, ensuring good performance during and after learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The ability of intelligent agents to learn and adapt online in dynamic environments is a recognized characteristic of intelligence. Reinforcement learning (RL) provides a framework for online learning without direct supervision, enabling applications in various real-world scenarios. However, the deployment of RL agents in physical environments necessitates stringent safety guarantees to prevent catastrophic outcomes during the learning process, especially when agents operate with incomplete environmental knowledge. This requirement defines the challenge of safe exploration in RL.

Current approaches to safe exploration generally fall into two categories:
- **Theoretically Principled Methods**: These methods often provide guarantees on safety and, in some cases, optimality. Examples include safe Bayesian optimization (Sui et al., 2015; Berkenkamp et al., 2016) and Lyapunov stability-based control (Berkenkamp et al., 2017). While effective in specific scenarios, they frequently struggle with scalability to complex, high-dimensional, or general-purpose tasks. Limitations include reliance on manual system resets, state-space discretization, or absence of optimality guarantees for certain Model Predictive Control (MPC)-based methods. Many of these approaches are also designed for discrete constrained Markov Decision Processes (CMDPs), making scaling to continuous domains computationally intensive.
- **Scalable Methods**: These approaches prioritize practicality by integrating scalable tools from deep RL (e.g., Dalal et al., 2018; Achiam et al., 2017). While effective for high-dimensional problems, they often lack robust theoretical guarantees for maintaining safety throughout the entire learning process. Furthermore, they sometimes overlook the active exploration aspect of learning that maximizes information about the environment, potentially hindering optimality.

A gap exists between these two categories, where methods struggle to simultaneously provide strong theoretical safety guarantees during online learning while also scaling to complex, continuous-action, and high-dimensional environments. Prior work aiming for optimality guarantees often focuses on "simple regret" (optimality only at the end of training) or relies on restrictive assumptions, such as an "emergency stop" action or unproven vanishing probabilities of policy resets (Wagener et al., 2021; As et al., 2025b; Wachi et al., 2023).

This work addresses these limitations by introducing an approach that aims to bridge the gap between theoretical guarantees and practical scalability. It proposes a model-based RL algorithm that utilizes prior knowledge, in the form of conservative policies, to guide safe exploration and facilitate convergence to optimal behavior, while providing guarantees on cumulative regret throughout the learning process.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the critical need for safe online learning and adaptation of RL agents in physical, real-world environments. When deployed beyond controlled simulations, RL agents cannot afford actions that lead to detrimental or catastrophic outcomes, even during the exploratory phases of learning when their knowledge about the environment is incomplete. The paper identifies a persistent challenge in current safe RL research: the trade-off between achieving strong theoretical safety guarantees and ensuring practical scalability to complex tasks. Methods with provable safety often do not scale, while scalable deep RL approaches frequently lack guarantees on safety during learning.

The authors propose that prior knowledge about the environment, particularly instantiated as suboptimal yet conservative policies, can be instrumental in addressing this challenge. Beyond merely ensuring safety, these prior policies can actively guide exploration towards promising regions of the environment, thereby enabling agents to learn near-optimal policies in a provably safe manner.

Based on this motivation, the key objectives of the research are:

- **Develop a Scalable Safe Exploration Algorithm**: To propose SOOPER (Safe Online Optimism for Pessimistic Expansion in RL), a model-based RL algorithm designed for safe exploration in continuous state-action spaces within the Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP) framework.
- **Leverage Policy Priors for Safety and Guidance**: To utilize existing suboptimal but conservative policies—derived from sources like scarce offline data or simulations potentially affected by distribution shifts—as reliable priors. These priors are to be invoked pessimistically during online rollouts to guarantee safety.
- **Guarantee Constraint Satisfaction Throughout Learning**: To mathematically prove that SOOPER ensures constraint satisfaction with high probability across all stages of learning, from initial exploration to convergence.
- **Establish Convergence to an Optimal Policy with Bounded Cumulative Regret**: To demonstrate that SOOPER gradually expands an implicit set of safe policies until it converges to the feasible set of the true CMDP. This includes establishing a novel upper bound on the cumulative regret, which signifies that performance remains robust throughout learning, as opposed to only guaranteeing optimality at the conclusion of training.
- **Reformulate CMDPs for Scalability**: To reformulate the constrained problem as an unconstrained MDP, facilitating natural compatibility with standard deep RL methods and avoiding the complexities typically associated with direct min-max formulations of CMDPs.
- **Empirically Validate Performance and Safety**: To conduct extensive empirical evaluations of SOOPER against state-of-the-art safe exploration algorithms on recognized safe RL benchmarks (RWRL, SafetyGym) and real-world hardware, demonstrating its scalability, safety, and performance advantages.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology revolves around SOOPER, a model-based RL algorithm designed for safe online learning in continuous state-action spaces, specifically within the Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP) framework.

**Problem Setting:**
The work defines safety within the context of a discounted infinite-horizon CMDP, specified by `M_c := (S, A, p, r, c, γ, ρ_0)`. `S` and `A` are continuous state and compact action spaces, respectively. The transition probability `p` is governed by unknown stochastic dynamics `s_t+1 = f(s_t, a_t) + ω_t`, where `ω_t` is assumed to be zero-mean Gaussian noise (Assumption 1). Reward `r` and cost `c` functions are Lipschitz continuous (Assumption 2), and the unknown dynamics `f` reside in a Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS) (Assumption 3). The objective is to find a policy `π*c` that maximizes the reward value `V_r^π(s)` while ensuring the cost value `V_c^π(s)` remains below a safety budget `d`. Learning occurs in an episodic setting, where online interaction updates knowledge of `f`, and cumulative regret `R(N)` (performance difference from `π*c`) is the optimality metric, with safety maintained across all `N` episodes.

**Probabilistic World Models:**
The agent manages epistemic uncertainty by maintaining a confidence set of plausible dynamics models `F_n` for each episode `n`. This set is defined by a nominal model `μ_n` and an uncertainty `σ_n`, ensuring `f` is contained within `F_n` with high probability (Definition 1). `F_0` represents initial prior knowledge from offline data or simulators.

**Pessimistic Policy Priors:**
A key assumption (Assumption 4) is access to a pessimistic policy prior `ˆπ` that satisfies constraints under all dynamics `f ∈ F_0` and yields a cost `V_c^ˆπ(s)` less than or equal to `V_c^π*c(s)`. To quantify pessimism, the algorithm defines a pessimistic cost value function `¯V_c^ˆπ(s)` which is the maximum expected cost under the worst-case dynamics `˜f ∈ F_n`. This intractable maximization is upper-bounded by augmenting the cost function with an uncertainty penalty `λ_pessimism ||σ_n(s_t, a_t)||`, resulting in `Q_c,n^ˆπ(s, a)` (Equation 5), which is amenable to temporal difference learning.

**SOOPER Algorithm (Algorithm 2):**
SOOPER operates in two primary modes:

1. **Safe Online Data Collection (Algorithm 1):**
 - During real-world rollouts, a "cost tracking" mechanism monitors the cumulative discounted cost `c_<t` and the pessimistic cost `γ^t Q_c,n^ˆπ(s_t, a_t)` for the current policy's action `a_t ∼ π_n(·|s_t)`.
 - If `Φ(s_t, a_t, c_<t, Q_c,n^ˆπ) := c_<t + γ^t Q_c,n^ˆπ(s_t, a_t)` exceeds the safety budget `d`, the agent *switches* to executing the safe prior `ˆπ(s_t)`.
 - **Theorem 1** formally guarantees that this mechanism maintains safety throughout all episodes with high probability. This allows the agent to explore states that `ˆπ` might deem unsafe initially.

2. **Simulated Exploration and Planning:**
 - A policy `π_n` is trained on a simulated environment, termed the "planning MDP" `M_f`. This `M_f` is constructed from the learned world model (`μ_n`) and differs from the real CMDP `M_c`.
 - **Modified Transitions**: In `M_f`, trajectories terminate and transition to an absorbing state `s†` if the safety condition `Φ(.) ≥ d` is met (i.e., if `ˆπ` would have been invoked in the real environment).
 - **Modified Rewards**: Upon termination, the reward `˜r(s_t, a_t)` in `M_f` is set to the pessimistic value `V_r^ˆπ(s_t)` of the prior policy (Equation 7). This incentivizes `π_n` to outperform `ˆπ` by avoiding early terminations.
 - **Unified Objective for Exploration-Exploitation-Expansion**: `π_n` is learned by optimizing a single objective that augments the nominal reward `˜r` with intrinsic reward bonuses. These bonuses, `(γ^t λ_explore + √(γ^t λ_expand)) ||σ_n(s_t, a_t)||`, encourage both exploration (of high-reward regions) and expansion (of the known safe set) by seeking out states with high epistemic uncertainty (Equation 9). `λ_explore` and `λ_expand` are weighting factors derived to enable this.

**Theoretical Guarantees:**
**Theorem 2** establishes that SOOPER achieves sublinear cumulative regret `R(N) ≤ O(Γ^(7/2) √(N) log(N))` while guaranteeing safety. The proof decomposes the per-episode regret into two terms: one related to the invocation of `ˆπ` (`∆1n`) and another related to the `πn`'s performance on `M_f` (`∆2n`). Both terms are bounded by model uncertainty, which shrinks as `n` increases.

**Practical Implementation:**
SOOPER is implemented as a "deep" model-based actor-critic algorithm, similar to MBPO. A probabilistic ensemble of neural networks learns the dynamics, reward, and cost functions. Epistemic uncertainty `σ_n` is estimated from the standard deviation of ensemble predictions. Policy `π_n` and cost value `Q_c,n^ˆπ` are computed via model-generated rollouts and actor-critic updates, demonstrating scalability to continuous control tasks. The theoretical analysis extends to unknown reward and cost functions (Section C).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of SOOPER was conducted across a range of benchmark tasks and real-world hardware, comparing its performance against state-of-the-art safe exploration algorithms.

- **Safe Transfer under Dynamics Mismatch:**
 - SOOPER was evaluated on five tasks from RWRL (Dulac-Arnold et al., 2021), SafetyGym (Ray et al., 2019), and RaceCar (Kabzan et al., 2020), where prior policies were trained under simulated dynamics shifts.
 - The algorithm consistently maintained safety constraints throughout learning in all tasks, a significant differentiator from many baselines.
 - In terms of performance improvement (relative to the initial prior policy), SOOPER consistently outperformed or matched existing methods such as SAILR, CRPO, and Primal-Dual, when those baselines managed to satisfy the safety constraints.

- **Scaling to Vision Control:**
 - SOOPER demonstrated scalability to tasks with image-based observations, specifically on the CartpoleSwingup task.
 - By learning a dynamics model on embeddings from a pretrained DrQ vision encoder, SOOPER maintained safety constraints and achieved near-optimal performance, indicating its applicability beyond state-based environments.

- **Safe Offline-to-Online Learning:**
 - When initialized with a conservative policy derived from a fixed offline dataset (using MOPO with a Primal-Dual optimizer on PointGoal1), SOOPER outperformed all baselines while upholding safety during online learning. This highlights its capability to leverage offline data effectively for online adaptation.

- **Safe Online Learning on Real Hardware:**
 - A critical validation involved deploying SOOPER on a real-world robotic race car, a highly dynamic system with stochasticity and delays.
 - Starting from a prior policy trained in a first-principles simulator, SOOPER successfully fine-tuned on the physical system.
 - Results showed that SOOPER improved the reward by approximately twofold compared to the prior policy while strictly satisfying safety constraints throughout the real-world training process.

- **Additional Ablations (from Appendix):**
 - **Role of Prior Policies**: Experiments showed that policy priors not only facilitate safety but also significantly accelerate learning by guiding exploration, outperforming scenarios where `π_n` is learned from scratch or uses a generic "braking" backup policy.
 - **Learning from Terminations**: The termination mechanism in the planning MDP (`M_f`) was shown to be crucial. Without it, agents struggle to reduce dependency on the prior and may incur higher costs, demonstrating the importance of this feedback signal for optimal safe learning.
 - **Robustness to Policy Priors**: SOOPER proved robust to varying levels of conservatism in initial policy priors, successfully converging to optimal performance while maintaining safety, even with different initial performance levels.
 - **Robustness to Noise**: The algorithm maintained safety and performance under significant non-Gaussian, non-additive noise in the action space, suggesting its practical resilience beyond its theoretical assumptions.
 - **Pessimism for Safe Exploration**: Higher values of the pessimism factor `λ_pessimism` were shown to increase the robustness of constraint satisfaction, albeit with an expected trade-off in exploration efficiency.
 - **Optimistic Planning for Sublinear Regret**: The intrinsic reward components (`λ_explore`, `λ_expand`) were identified as crucial for optimizing cumulative regret, demonstrating a balance between under- and over-exploration.
 - **Runtime**: Despite incorporating additional algorithmic components, SOOPER demonstrated no significant runtime overhead compared to other model-based baselines.
 - **Offline-to-Online on Real Hardware**: An additional experiment with an offline-trained prior on real-world data further confirmed SOOPER's ability to improve performance safely.
 - **Learning Directly from Costs**: A variant (SafeCRPO) using a standard CMDP solver with Algorithm 1 also ensured safety but showed slower learning and lower asymptotic performance than SOOPER, validating the design choice of using terminations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of reinforcement learning by addressing the critical challenge of ensuring safety during online learning in autonomous systems. The proposed algorithm, SOOPER, offers a method for provably improving agent performance over existing suboptimal policies (e.g., those from simulation or offline training) while simultaneously guaranteeing the satisfaction of safety constraints throughout the entire learning process.

The significance and potential impact of this work include:

- **Advancing Deployable RL**: By combining strong theoretical safety guarantees with practical scalability to continuous state-action spaces and compatibility with deep RL methods, SOOPER moves towards enabling the deployment of RL agents in sensitive, real-world physical systems where safety is paramount.
- **Novel Theoretical Foundations**: The establishment of a novel upper bound on the cumulative regret is a key theoretical contribution. This guarantee means that agents are not only optimal at the end of training but also perform well and safely throughout the learning curve, a critical aspect for online systems. This differentiates it from prior works that often rely on simpler regret metrics or less robust conditions.
- **Effective Use of Prior Knowledge**: The method demonstrates a principled way to leverage existing, potentially suboptimal, but conservative policies as priors. This is crucial for bootstrapping learning in complex environments where starting from scratch is infeasible or unsafe.
- **Robustness and Scalability**: Empirical validation on diverse benchmarks, including tasks with dynamics mismatch, vision-based control, offline-to-online transfer, and direct application on real robotic hardware, indicates that SOOPER is both scalable and robust under various challenging conditions. This practical evidence supports the translation of the theoretical guarantees to real-world scenarios.
- **Unified Approach to Exploration and Safety**: SOOPER's unified objective for planning, exploration, and safe set expansion, by reformulating the CMDP into an unconstrained MDP with termination signals and intrinsic rewards, offers a potentially more efficient and less complex approach compared to traditional min-max CMDP solvers or sequential expand-then-explore strategies.

While the paper highlights several remaining challenges, such as extending to high-probability constraints over specific states, developing methods for non-episodic settings, and scaling to more complex tasks with advanced models, the contributions of SOOPER represent a step towards developing more reliable and trustworthy RL systems that can learn and adapt safely in autonomous applications.
