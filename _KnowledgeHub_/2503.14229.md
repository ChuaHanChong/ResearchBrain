# HA-VLN 2.0: An Open Benchmark and Leaderboard for Human-Aware Navigation in Discrete and Continuous Environments with Dynamic Multi-Human Interactions

> [!summary] Summary
> HA-VLN 2.0 introduces a unified benchmark and dataset for Vision-and-Language Navigation, integrating discrete and continuous environments with dynamic multi-human interactions and socially grounded instructions. The benchmark includes refined human motion data and evaluation metrics, demonstrating that human-aware training improves robot navigation success rates in both simulations and real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) agents often operate in simplified static environments, neglecting dynamic human presence and complex social interactions.
- Existing VLN benchmarks predominantly use object-centric instructions, failing to capture the nuanced, human-referential language needed for socially intelligent navigation.
- Prior human-aware navigation datasets lacked sufficient diversity in human activities, robust physics-consistent control, and accurate multi-human interaction models.

## Method

- A unified benchmark, **HA-VLN 2.0**, was developed, integrating both discrete and continuous navigation paradigms with explicit social-awareness constraints.
- A refined HAPS 2.0 dataset provides diversified, SMPL-based human motions and complex multi-human interactions, generated via LLM-guided human-in-the-loop annotation.
- ==The HA-R2R== dataset offers 16,844 socially grounded, human-centric instructions, requiring agents to interpret nuanced language and adapt to dynamic social contexts.

## Results

- Agents trained on HA-VLN **2.0** achieved up to a **28.6%** increase in Success Rate on human-aware validation sets while maintaining robustness in cross-domain evaluations.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that the dynamic presence and specific interactions of humans significantly impact navigation complexity, reducing Total Collision Rate by up to **36%** and increasing Success Rate by up to **25%** when human dynamics are simplified.
- Real-world robot experiments confirmed successful sim-to-real transfer, with HA-VLN trained agents achieving a **0.18** Navigation Success Rate, outperforming VLN-CE trained agents (**0.12**) in dynamic human environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating dynamic human presence and interactions into navigation tasks substantially increases complexity, underscoring the need for continuous replanning under partial observability.
> - Socially grounded, human-centric instructions enable agents to better interpret contextual cues and improve in-domain navigation performance.
> - Sim-to-real transfer experiments validate that policies trained on HA-VLN 2.0 data enhance navigation success and social compliance for physical robots in dynamic, real-world settings.
