# HA-VLN 2.0: An Open Benchmark and Leaderboard for Human-Aware Navigation in Discrete and Continuous Environments with Dynamic Multi-Human Interactions

> [!summary] Summary
> HA-VLN 2.0 introduces a unified benchmark and dataset for Vision-and-Language Navigation, integrating discrete and continuous environments with dynamic multi-human interactions and socially grounded instructions. The benchmark includes refined human motion data and evaluation metrics, demonstrating that human-aware training improves robot navigation success rates in both simulations and real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) agents often operate in simplified static environments, neglecting dynamic human presence and complex social interactions.
- Existing VLN benchmarks predominantly use object-centric instructions, failing to capture the nuanced, human-referential language needed for socially intelligent navigation.
- Prior human-aware navigation datasets lacked sufficient diversity in human activities, robust physics-consistent control, and accurate multi-human interaction models.

## Method

- A unified benchmark, **HA-VLN 2.0**, was developed, integrating both discrete and continuous navigation paradigms with explicit social-awareness constraints.
- A refined HAPS 2.0 dataset provides diversified, SMPL-based human motions and complex multi-human interactions, generated via LLM-guided human-in-the-loop annotation.
- ==The HA-R2R== dataset offers 16,844 socially grounded, human-centric instructions, requiring agents to interpret nuanced language and adapt to dynamic social contexts.

## Results

- Agents trained on HA-VLN **2.0** achieved up to a **28.6%** increase in Success Rate on human-aware validation sets while maintaining robustness in cross-domain evaluations.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that the dynamic presence and specific interactions of humans significantly impact navigation complexity, reducing Total Collision Rate by up to **36%** and increasing Success Rate by up to **25%** when human dynamics are simplified.
- Real-world robot experiments confirmed successful sim-to-real transfer, with HA-VLN trained agents achieving a **0.18** Navigation Success Rate, outperforming VLN-CE trained agents (**0.12**) in dynamic human environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating dynamic human presence and interactions into navigation tasks substantially increases complexity, underscoring the need for continuous replanning under partial observability.
> - Socially grounded, human-centric instructions enable agents to better interpret contextual cues and improve in-domain navigation performance.
> - Sim-to-real transfer experiments validate that policies trained on HA-VLN 2.0 data enhance navigation success and social compliance for physical robots in dynamic, real-world settings.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) is an area of artificial intelligence that challenges embodied agents to interpret natural language instructions and navigate to specified goals within visual environments. Prior research in VLN has predominantly focused on either discrete (DE) or continuous (CE) action spaces. However, these environments often simplify real-world conditions by neglecting dynamic elements, particularly human presence and interactions. Most existing VLN benchmarks either treat humans as static obstacles or omit them entirely, failing to account for their unpredictable behaviors, the need to respect personal space, or the complexities of navigation in crowded, partially observable spaces.

Previous attempts to introduce dynamic humans, such as HA-VLN 1.0, provided an initial framework but exhibited several limitations. These included issues with motion data alignment, restricted diversity in human activities, a bias towards discrete navigation without robust physics-consistent control, underdeveloped multi-human interaction models, and coarse, object-centric instructions rather than human-centric ones. These limitations constrained the realism and reproducibility of earlier human-aware navigation research.

HA-VLN 2.0 addresses these identified gaps by presenting a unified benchmark that integrates discrete and continuous navigation paradigms with explicit social-awareness constraints. It aims to elevate the realism and applicability of VLN research by focusing on dynamic, multi-human interactions, finer-grained socially grounded instructions, and the necessity of real-time replanning under partial observability. This work moves VLN beyond static, simplified settings towards scenarios that more accurately reflect the complexities of human-populated real-world environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the field of Vision-and-Language Navigation by developing a comprehensive, standardized, and open benchmark for human-aware navigation in dynamic, multi-human environments. This overarching goal is motivated by several specific limitations in existing VLN research that restrict its applicability to real-world scenarios:

1. **Underexplored Social Awareness:** Current VLN agents often disregard human presence or perceive humans merely as inert obstacles. This prevents agents from learning to respect personal space, avoid collisions in a socially appropriate manner, or react to the diverse activities of bystanders. The motivation is to integrate explicit social-awareness constraints into navigation, enabling agents to understand and adhere to human personal space and behavioral norms.

2. **Lack of Finer-Grained Human-Centric Instructions:** Existing VLN corpora primarily contain object-centric or general navigation instructions. These typically do not reflect the detailed, human-referential language commonly used in real-world directives, such as instructions that describe ongoing human activities or specify social cues (e.g., "avoid the couple chatting near the bar"). A key motivation is to provide socially grounded, finer-grained instructions that require agents to align language with dynamic human behaviors and visual context.

3. **Static Environment Assumptions:** Most VLN benchmarks assume static environments, which simplifies planning by neglecting the need for real-time adaptation to moving entities. In human-populated spaces, agents must contend with dynamic and unpredictable human behaviors, requiring continuous replanning under partial observability. The motivation is to introduce dynamic multi-human interactions and partial observability into the navigation task, compelling agents to adjust their paths online.

4. **Limitations of Previous Human-Aware Benchmarks (HA-VLN 1.0):** The previous iteration, HA-VLN 1.0, introduced dynamic humans but suffered from several shortcomings. These included motion data with alignment errors and limited diversity, a bias towards discrete navigation, and rudimentary multi-human interaction modeling. The current work aims to overcome these by standardizing regime disclosure, expanding motion fidelity and diversity, thoroughly incorporating multi-human interactions, and supporting detailed, socially grounded instructions across both discrete and continuous settings.

In response to these motivations, HA-VLN 2.0 aims to provide:
- A standardized task and metrics that capture both goal accuracy and personal-space adherence.
- A robust dataset (HAPS 2.0) and simulators (HA-VLN-DE, HA-VLN-CE) that accurately model multi-human interactions, diverse outdoor contexts, and finer language–motion alignment.
- Comprehensive benchmarks that reveal the performance of leading agents under human dynamics and partial observability.
- Real-world robot experiments to validate sim-to-real transfer and an open leaderboard for transparent comparison and advancement of research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of HA-VLN 2.0 involves the creation of a unified benchmark, a refined dataset, dual simulators, specialized instructions, and baseline agents, all designed to facilitate human-aware navigation research.

**Unified Human-Aware VLN Task:**
The framework formalizes a unified task for both discrete and continuous environments under human-centric constraints. Agents receive natural language instructions referencing human activities and must navigate photorealistic 3D scenes, anticipate human trajectories, maintain socially compliant distances, and adapt plans online. The state is defined by agent position, orientation, and egocentric visual observation, with a shared action space including forward movement, turns, and stopping. Constraints include dynamic human models from HAPS 2.0, personal-space enforcement (3m in DE, overlapping radii in CE), and human-focused instructions. The task is framed as a partially observable Markov decision process (POMDP), accounting for stochastic human dynamics.

**HA-VLN Simulator:**
A simulator was developed to embed dynamically moving humans in both discrete and continuous 3D environments, supporting high-fidelity motions and multi-human interactions.
- **HAPS 2.0 Dataset:** This dataset enhances HAPS 1.0 by offering refined and diversified human motions across 26 region types in 90 architectural scenes. It includes 486 SMPL-based motion sequences (120 frames each) that convert textual activity descriptions into 3D human motion models.
 - **Human Activity Annotation (Coarse-Level):** A coarse-to-fine strategy is used. Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) determines initial placements of human motions relative to objects within defined regions, enforcing a minimum safe distance (ϵ = 1m) from other objects and region boundaries.
 - **Human Activity Annotation (Fine-Level):** Multi-view cameras (nine RGB cameras around each human model) are used to refine placements, ensuring precise alignment with scene geometry and resolving inconsistencies such as mesh–object clipping. This stage involved over 430 hours of annotation.
 - **Multi-Human Enrichment:** A human-in-the-loop approach, guided by LLMs (ChatGPT-4o, LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct) and refined through manual review, adds new characters and complex motion paths (e.g., group activities, stair-walking). The dataset includes 910 human models across 428 regions in 90 scans, with diverse interactions.
- **Real-Time Rendering & Agent Interaction:** A dual-thread producer–consumer architecture manages continuous human motion updates. Thread 1 enqueues refresh signals, and Thread 2 processes them, loading new meshes before the agent’s perception step. Agents perceive these dynamics via a navigation mesh, and collisions (bounding volumes overlap) trigger automatic reverts.
- **Discrete vs. Continuous Settings:** HA-VLN-CE supports continuous, real-valued actions (e.g., 0.25m forward, 15° turns) with real-time physics. HA-VLN-DE extends HA3D with HAPS 2.0 data, where agents hop between panoramic viewpoints while humans move continuously.
- **Unified API:** A single API provides core functions for human state queries, dynamic scene updates, and collision checks, enabling consistent comparisons across both modes.

**HA-R2R Dataset:**
To ground the task, the existing R2R-CE dataset was extended into HA-R2R. This dataset includes 16,844 socially grounded instructions that emphasize social nuance and human interactions. Instructions were generated using LLM-guided generation with iterative human refinement, ensuring a neutral tone and contextual alignment with human-related scenarios. HA-R2R instructions feature increased length (average 112 words vs. 27 in R2R-CE) and vocabulary, with 20%–60% human-related content.

**HA-VLN Agents (Baselines):**
Two baseline agents were introduced:
- **HA-VLN-VL Agent:** Adapted from Recurrent VLN-BERT, this agent focuses on visual-language alignment. It uses a Transformer-based architecture with a state token that jointly attends to visual and linguistic tokens, and an imitation learning objective.
- **HA-VLN-CMA Agent:** Built upon VLN-CE, this agent emphasizes collision avoidance and real-time adaptation. It fuses textual (BERT) and visual (ResNet) features via multi-head attention and uses an MLP to predict action probabilities. Strategies like Envdrop (for occlusions) and DAgger (for error correction) are employed to enhance replanning in dynamic environments.

**Evaluation Metrics:**
Performance is assessed using two categories of metrics:
- **Social Compliance:** Total Collision Rate (TCR) measures overall collision frequency, while Collision Rate (CR) indicates the proportion of navigation instances with at least one socially inappropriate interaction.
- **Navigation Accuracy:** Navigation Error (NE) quantifies the distance to the goal, and Success Rate (SR) measures episodes where the agent stops sufficiently close to the goal and demonstrates effective obstacle avoidance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted using HA-VLN 2.0 yielded several findings regarding agent performance, social compliance, and the impact of environmental dynamics.

**HA-VLN-CE Benchmarking:**
- Evaluations on the HA-VLN 2.0 benchmark with continuous environments (HA-VLN-CE) demonstrated performance gains for models that incorporate human-aware training. For instance, BEVBert's Success Rate (SR) increased from 0.19 to 0.27 in the seen validation split and from 0.15 to 0.21 in the unseen validation split when retrained on HA-VLN-CE compared to its zero-shot performance.
- Agents trained on HA-R2R achieved comparable SR to R2R-CE-trained agents (e.g., 0.35 vs. 0.37 for BEVBert) when evaluated on the R2R-CE validation set, but showed a +28.6% increase in SR on the HA-R2R validation set. This suggests that explicit references to dynamic crowd behavior enhance navigation readiness and that HA-VLN-CE contributes to robust generalization.
- Visualizations of agent trajectories indicated that dynamic human activities increase the difficulty of navigation, emphasizing the need for robust human-aware approaches.

**HA-VLN-DE Benchmarking:**
- In discrete environments (HA-VLN-DE), agents achieved moderate Success Rates but often incurred high Collision Rates. For example, Airbert, while achieving an SR of 0.36, had a CR of up to 0.83, highlighting persistent collision risks. This indicates that adaptive collision-avoidance strategies are crucial even in discrete settings, especially when multiple bystanders are present in confined areas.

**Analysis and Ablation Studies:**
- **Cross-domain Generalization:** HA-R2R-trained agents demonstrated improved in-domain performance (+28.6% SR on HA-R2R val_unseen) while maintaining robustness in cross-domain scenarios (comparable SR on R2R-CE).
- **Human Presence and Interaction Enrichment:** Ablation studies showed that removing human presence (replacing humans with cylinders) reduced Total Collision Rate (TCR) by approximately 36% and increased SR by around 10%. Similarly, removing human interaction enrichment (skipping complex human dynamics) reduced TCR by up to 22% and increased SR by up to 25%. These results suggest that humans are not simply generic moving obstacles but dynamic entities whose specific interactions significantly impact navigation complexity.
- **Step Size:** Increasing the navigation step size (from 0.1m to 1.0m, approximating discrete-style navigation) could improve performance under certain conditions. However, if collisions were checked at every sub-step within a larger step, SR dropped close to zero, underscoring the "teleport-like" nature of discrete actions and the necessity for fine-grained collision checks in continuous settings.
- **Sensor Modalities:** The inclusion of either depth or RGB observations consistently reduced collisions and improved SR, indicating the importance of 3D cues for effective navigation around moving humans.

**Leaderboard and Real-World Validation:**
- The HA-R2R test dataset, comprising 3,408 instructions across 18 withheld buildings, emphasizes multi-human routes, posing a significant challenge for agents.
- Real-world robot experiments using a Unitree Go2-EDU quadruped in various indoor environments (office, living room, hallway, lobby) with 2–4 free-moving human volunteers demonstrated successful sim-to-real transfer.
- Agents trained on HA-VLN achieved a higher Navigation Success Rate (NSR) (0.18) compared to those trained solely on VLN-CE (0.12), confirming the benefits of HA-VLN training in realistic dynamic conditions.
- The HA-VLN-VL agent consistently outperformed HA-VLN-CMA-Base in real-world NSR.
- The robot successfully navigated in moderately congested conditions, frequently pausing or deviating to avoid pedestrians. However, challenges persisted in tight corridors or during sudden crowd convergence, highlighting the need for more robust replanning capabilities under partial observability and unpredictable human behavior.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

HA-VLN 2.0 represents a notable contribution to the Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) research area, primarily due to its comprehensive and standardized approach to human-aware navigation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Standardization of Human-Aware VLN:** By unifying discrete and continuous navigation paradigms under explicit social-awareness constraints, HA-VLN 2.0 provides a standardized framework that allows for direct and consistent comparisons of agent performance across different action spaces and environmental dynamics. This reduces fragmentation in the research community and offers a clear path for progress.

2. **Enhanced Realism in Simulation:** The introduction of the HAPS 2.0 dataset, coupled with dual simulators (HA-VLN-DE and HA-VLN-CE), significantly elevates the realism of VLN benchmarks. The dataset's detailed SMPL-based motion sequences, multi-human interaction enrichment via LLM-guided human-in-the-loop annotation, and real-time rendering of dynamic human behaviors move simulations closer to the complexities of real-world crowded environments. This allows researchers to train and evaluate agents in scenarios that closely mirror practical deployment conditions.

3. **Comprehensive Benchmarking and Identification of Challenges:** The HA-R2R dataset, with its 16,844 socially grounded instructions, compels agents to interpret nuanced, human-centric language and adapt to dynamic social contexts. The benchmarking results, which reveal sharp performance drops for leading agents under human dynamics and partial observability, underscore the inherent difficulties of human-aware navigation and highlight specific areas where current agents fall short. This clarity can guide future research efforts toward more robust solutions.

4. **Foundation for Future Research:** By publicly releasing the datasets, simulators, baseline agents, and evaluation protocols, HA-VLN 2.0 establishes an open and accessible foundation. This resource can accelerate research into various aspects of human-aware navigation, including advanced collision forecasting, more sophisticated reactive control, improved natural language understanding of social cues, and ethical considerations for robot behavior in human spaces.

5. **Validation of Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The successful demonstration of sim-to-real transfer through real-world robot experiments validates the practical relevance of the benchmark. This finding indicates that policies learned in the HA-VLN 2.0 simulation environment can be effectively deployed on physical robots, a crucial step toward widespread adoption of embodied AI. The results confirm that explicit social modeling enhances navigation robustness and helps reduce collisions in real-world settings.

6. **Promotion of Transparent Progress:** The establishment of an open leaderboard facilitates transparent comparison of different agent architectures and training methodologies. This competitive environment can foster innovation and rapid advancement within the human-aware navigation domain.

In summary, HA-VLN 2.0 is expected to serve as a pivotal benchmark, steering VLN research towards developing agents that are not only efficient in reaching goals but also socially compliant and safe in dynamic, human-populated environments.
