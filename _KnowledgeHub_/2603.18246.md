# Rapid Adaptation of Particle Dynamics for Generalized Deformable Object Mobile Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Stanford University and the University of Texas at Austin developed RAPID, a framework enabling mobile manipulators to adapt to unknown deformable object dynamics and shape changes in real-time using visual inputs. The system achieved 85% success in 1D Inserting and 80% in 2D Covering tasks on unseen real-world objects, outperforming baselines.

## Problem

- Manipulating deformable objects is challenging due to their infinite-dimensional state space and unknown, non-linear dynamics.
- Existing methods struggle with generalization to unseen object dynamics or categories, particularly under occlusions, and lack real-time adaptation capabilities.
- Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA) frameworks, successful for rigid objects, do not directly account for the continuous shape changes inherent to deformable objects.

## Method

- RAPID employs a two-phase simulation training method: Phase I trains a visuomotor policy using privileged ground-truth dynamics and shape information.
- Phase II replaces privileged encoders with 'Shape Adaptation' and 'Dynamics Adaptation' modules that infer embeddings from non-privileged real-world observations (depth images, robot actions) using L1 losses.
- The trained policy and adaptation modules are directly transferred to a real robot, inferring dynamics and shape changes from a buffer of recent observations to enable real-time adaptive manipulation.

## Results

- RAPID achieved **85%** success on 1D Inserting and **80%** on 2D Covering tasks with unseen real-world deformable objects, significantly outperforming state-of-the-art baselines by a large margin (e.g., **82.5%** total success rate vs. **10%** and **17.5%**).
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of both the dynamics adaptation module (**52.5%** success rate drop) and the shape adaptation module (**42.5%** success rate drop).
- Training the entire system end-to-end with reinforcement learning (without L1 losses for adaptation modules) led to a **60%** reduction in success rate, validating the two-phase training approach.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Ground-truth particle positions in simulation effectively capture deformable object shape changes, allowing for the extension of Rapid Motor Adaptation to non-rigid objects.
> - A two-phase training process with L1 losses for the adaptation modules, separate from the visuomotor policy, is crucial for stable convergence and effective learning of dynamics and shape inference.
> - Real-time inference of both object dynamics and shape changes from raw visual inputs provides robustness against occlusions and allows for diverse mobile manipulation strategies across various deformable materials.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The manipulation of deformable objects presents a complex challenge in robotics due to their infinite-dimensional state space and non-linear dynamics, which are often unknown a priori. Existing approaches to deformable object manipulation can be broadly categorized into model-based methods, rapid motor adaptation (RMA), learning from real-world data, and learning from simulation.

**Model-Based Deformable Object Manipulation** often relies on state estimation using representations such as particle systems, points, graphs, or differentiable simulators. While these methods can learn or plan manipulation strategies, they typically require full object observability and can struggle with occlusions or real-time adaptation to unseen object dynamics, categories, and instances in unstructured real-world environments. Failures in state estimation under hand-eye coordination or occlusions limit their generalization capabilities.

**Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA)** has demonstrated effectiveness in other robotic domains, including legged locomotion and in-hand rigid object manipulation. These approaches learn a policy conditioned on a latent dynamics parameter vector, which is inferred from robot state-action sequences. However, directly applying RMA to deformable objects is complicated because their dynamics extend beyond mass and position to include continuous changes in shape, a dimension not typically captured in rigid object RMA frameworks.

**Learning Deformable Object Manipulation from Real-World Data** involves collecting large-scale datasets from random trajectories, human demonstrations, annotations, or teleoperation. While these methods can achieve complex tasks and leverage vision-language pre-training, they often face challenges in zero-shot generalization to new robot embodiments (e.g., different manipulator configurations) and require extensive, labor-intensive data collection, making them less practical for broad deployment.

**Learning Deformable Object Manipulation from Simulation** leverages the benefits of controlled environments for training policies. Strategies include domain randomization, domain adaptation, and real-to-sim transfer. However, many of these methods do not incorporate multiple visual observations and robot actions into their manipulation policies, limiting their ability to infer and adapt to deformable object dynamics online. Some methods attempt to adjust simulation parameters based on real-world experience, but these often require multiple real-world trajectories for adaptation, precluding real-time adaptation to unknown dynamics.

This work, RAPID (Rapid Adaptation of Particle Dynamics), aims to bridge these gaps. It addresses the limitation of existing RMA by incorporating deformable object shape changes into the dynamics embedding. Furthermore, it overcomes the generalization and real-time adaptation challenges of model-based and simulation-to-real methods by inferring dynamics from non-privileged visual observations, enabling zero-shot transfer from simulation to real-world deformable mobile manipulation tasks without real-robot demonstrations. The approach combines the advantages of learning in simulation with a mechanism for real-time adaptation to unknown dynamics, offering a model-free solution that operates on raw visual inputs and robustly handles occlusions and shape deformations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable robots to manipulate deformable objects with unknown dynamics in real-world mobile manipulation scenarios. The authors identify the critical challenge in deformable object manipulation as the objects' dynamics parameters (e.g., how they stretch, bend, or compress) being largely unknown a priori and continuously changing with interaction. This contrasts with rigid objects, where dynamics are primarily defined by mass and position.

The core motivation stems from the observation that humans exhibit rapid adaptation to unknown material properties of deformable objects. For example, a human would instinctively use different manipulation strategies for covering a bowl with a rigid hat versus a soft piece of cloth, swiftly inferring material properties and adjusting actions. The research aims to endow robots with a similar capability for real-time adaptation to unknown dynamics, enabling efficient manipulation of diverse deformable objects across various dynamics, categories, and instances.

Specifically, the paper highlights that while Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA) has been successful in adapting to unknown dynamics for rigid-body tasks, extending it to deformable objects introduces the complexity of accounting for object shape changes. The key insight proposed is that the recent ground-truth particle positions of a deformable object in simulation can effectively capture these shape changes. This insight forms the foundation for extending the RMA framework to deformable object manipulation.

Therefore, the detailed objectives are:
1. **Develop a two-phase method (RAPID)**: This method should learn to perform real-robot deformable object mobile manipulation by:
 - **Phase 1:** Training a visuomotor policy conditioned on a dynamics embedding derived from privileged simulator information, including ground-truth particle positions, mass, and object position.
 - **Phase 2:** Learning to infer this dynamics embedding using only non-privileged information available in the real world, such as robot visual observations (depth images) and actions, thereby enabling direct transfer to the real robot.
2. **Achieve high success rates**: Demonstrate robust performance in real-world vision-based deformable object mobile manipulation tasks (e.g., 1D Inserting, 2D Covering) across unseen object dynamics, categories, and instances.
3. **Outperform existing sim2real and deformable manipulation baselines**: Establish RAPID's effectiveness in adapting to unknown deformable object dynamics compared to methods that lack real-time adaptation or struggle with occlusions and generalization.
4. **Validate the importance of explicit shape and dynamics adaptation**: Through ablation studies, ascertain the necessity of both the shape and dynamics adaptation modules for task success and the benefit of the two-phase training process with L1 losses over end-to-end reinforcement learning.

Ultimately, the motivation is to create a versatile and generalizable robotic solution that can handle the inherent variability and unknown properties of deformable objects in real-world, in-the-wild settings, moving beyond static, fully observable, or rigid-object manipulation paradigms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RAPID (Rapid Adaptation of Particle Dynamics) is a two-phase training method developed in simulation for learning to manipulate deformable objects in the real world by adapting to their unknown dynamics using raw visual inputs. The trained policy and adaptation modules are then directly deployed to a real robot. The method frames both training phases as reinforcement learning (RL) problems within a Markov decision process.

**A. Train Phase I: Learning a Visuomotor Policy with Ground-Truth Encoders in Simulation**

In the initial phase, RAPID trains a visuomotor policy (π) that generates full-DOF robot actions to achieve a deformable object mobile manipulation task in a simulated environment.
1. **Policy Input**: The policy receives a single depth image ($o_t$) and a "Dynamics Embedding" ($z^d_t$) as input.
2. **Dynamics Embedding ($z^d_t$)**: This embedding is crucial for task achievement and is encoded by a "Dynamics Encoder" ($\mu_d$). $\mu_d$ takes as input the masses and positions of objects in the simulation, along with a "Shape Embedding" ($z^s_t$).
3. **Shape Embedding ($z^s_t$)**: This embedding captures information about how the object's shape changes. It is encoded by a "Shape Encoder" ($\mu_s$), which accepts privileged ground-truth object shape information, specifically recent positions of the deformable object's particles and corresponding robot actions.
4. **Training**: Both the visuomotor policy and the shape and dynamics encoders are trained end-to-end using reinforcement learning. This phase relies on privileged, low-dimensional information available only in the simulator.

**B. Train Phase II: Learning Shape and Dynamics Adaptation Modules in Simulation**

The objective of the second phase is to transition from privileged simulator information to non-privileged, real-world observable inputs. The previously trained shape and dynamics encoders are replaced with new adaptation modules.
1. **Shape Adaptation Module ($\phi_s$)**: This module replaces the Shape Encoder ($\mu_s$). Instead of ground-truth particle positions, $\phi_s$ takes recent depth images and robot joint angles and actions as input. It is trained to infer an "Inferred Shape Embedding" ($\hat{z}^s_t$). An L1-loss is used to regress $\hat{z}^s_t$ towards the ground-truth $z^s_t$ generated by $\mu_s$ from Phase I.
2. **Dynamics Adaptation Module ($\phi_d$)**: Similarly, this module replaces the Dynamics Encoder ($\mu_d$). It takes recent depth images and robot joint angles and actions as input. It infers an "Inferred Dynamics Embedding" ($\hat{z}^d_t$). An L1-loss is applied to regress $\hat{z}^d_t$ towards the ground-truth $z^d_t$ from Phase I.
3. **Gradient Control**: To ensure the Shape Adaptation module does not encode redundant dynamics information, upstream gradients from training $\phi_d$ are prevented from flowing into $\phi_s$. Additionally, upstream RL gradients are stopped from flowing into both adaptation modules during the fine-tuning of the visuomotor policy. This separation helps ensure the modules distinctly encode object dynamics and shape information that are not directly observable from a single depth image.
4. **Policy Fine-tuning**: The visuomotor policy obtained in Phase I is then fine-tuned using RL with the new inferred embeddings ($\hat{z}^s_t$ and $\hat{z}^d_t$) generated by the adaptation modules.
5. **Encoder Discard**: The ground-truth dynamics and shape encoders from Phase I are discarded before deployment.

**C. Test Time Deployment**

At test time, the trained visuomotor policy and the two adaptation modules ($\phi_s$ and $\phi_d$) are directly transferred to a real robot without any further fine-tuning or real-world demonstrations.
1. **Robot Platform**: A TIAGo 22-DOF bimanual mobile manipulator with an RGB-D camera is used. Input consists of depth images (224 × 224 resolution at 3 Hz) and joint angles.
2. **Interaction Loop**: For each timestep, the robot's policy takes the current depth image and joint angles, along with the inferred Dynamics Embedding ($\hat{z}^d_t$), to output full-DOF robot actions.
3. **Embedding Generation**: The robot maintains a running buffer of the 10 most recent depth-image-observation-action pairs. This buffer is fed into both the shape and dynamics adaptation modules to generate the current $\hat{z}^s_t$ and $\hat{z}^d_t$.
4. **Update Frequency**: To maintain short-term temporal consistency, the shape and dynamics embeddings are updated periodically every 5 timesteps. This cycle continues until the task is completed.
5. **Environment**: Tasks are performed on unseen real-world objects (20 categories each for 1D and 2D deformable objects), environments, and lighting conditions. No QR codes or external markers are used, relying solely on onboard visual inputs.

**Tasks and Baselines**: RAPID was evaluated on two challenging deformable object mobile manipulation tasks: "1D Inserting" (e.g., rope into a container) and "2D Covering" (e.g., cloth over a bowl). Baselines included DMfD (Learning Deformable Object Manipulation from Expert Demonstrations) and DDOD (Dense Object Descriptors). Ablation studies were also conducted on RAPID-No-Adapt, aRAPID-No-Shape, and RAPID-E2E to analyze the contribution of different components.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of RAPID involved testing its performance on two real-world deformable object mobile manipulation tasks: 1D Inserting and 2D Covering. The method was compared against two state-of-the-art baselines and subjected to three ablation studies.

**A. Performance Against Baselines (Q1)**

RAPID demonstrated superior performance compared to the baselines:
- **1D Inserting**: RAPID achieved a success rate of 85% (17 out of 20 trials). DMfD achieved 15% (3 out of 20), and DDOD achieved 10% (2 out of 20).
- **2D Covering**: RAPID achieved a success rate of 80% (16 out of 20 trials). DMfD achieved 5% (1 out of 20), and DDOD achieved 25% (5 out of 20).
- **Overall**: RAPID's total success rate was 82.5% across 40 trials, significantly higher than DMfD's 10% and DDOD's 17.5%.

Qualitatively, DMfD frequently failed because severe object deformation and occlusions by the robot gripper during manipulation hindered accurate segmentation, which is critical for its operation. Even when segmentation was sufficient, DMfD struggled to adapt to the unknown dynamics of the deformable objects. DDOD encountered similar issues, failing to predict accurate dense object descriptors under non-top-down camera viewpoints, severe deformation, and robot gripper occlusions. Like DMfD, DDOD also lacked the capacity for online adaptation to unknown dynamics. In contrast, RAPID performed closed-loop 6-DOF control and hand-eye coordination directly from non-top-down depth images, exhibiting robustness to these challenges.

**B. Ablation Studies**

The ablation studies provided insights into the necessity of RAPID's components:

- **Q2: Importance of Shape and Dynamics Adaptation Modules (RAPID-No-Adapt)**
 - **Result**: RAPID-No-Adapt, which removed both adaptation modules, showed a 52.5% drop in overall success rate compared to full RAPID (30% vs. 82.5%). It achieved 35% in 1D Inserting and 25% in 2D Covering.
 - **Finding**: Qualitatively, RAPID-No-Adapt treated deformable objects as rigid, leading to failures when manipulating soft materials. This indicates the crucial role of the adaptation modules in inferring and adapting to object dynamics.

- **Q3: Importance of Inferring Object's Shape Changes (aRAPID-No-Shape)**
 - **Result**: aRAPID-No-Shape, which removed only the Shape Adaptation Module, experienced a 42.5% success rate degradation compared to full RAPID (40% vs. 82.5%). It achieved 35% in 1D Inserting and 45% in 2D Covering.
 - **Finding**: This variant could grasp and move objects closer to the target but failed to execute the precise dynamic motions required for insertion or covering. This suggests that the ability to infer how an object's shape changes is critical for successful deformable object manipulation, especially for fine, adaptive movements.

- **Q4: Necessity of Two-Phase Training with L1 Losses (RAPID-E2E)**
 - **Result**: RAPID-E2E, which trained the entire architecture end-to-end with RL without the L1 losses for the adaptation modules, suffered a 60% success rate reduction (22.5% vs. 82.5%). It achieved 25% in 1D Inserting and 20% in 2D Covering.
 - **Finding**: RAPID-E2E failed to converge optimally during training. The high-dimensionality of the recent depth image observations combined with unstable RL gradient signals prevented it from learning a useful dynamics embedding, effectively ignoring crucial information and relying solely on current depth observations. This highlights the necessity of RAPID's two-phase training process with L1 losses for effectively optimizing the adaptation modules.

**C. Dynamics Adaptive Behavior and Embedding Visualization**

Visualizations (Figures 5 and 6) further supported the findings:
- **Adaptive Behavior**: RAPID exhibited diverse and appropriate manipulation strategies based on the inferred object dynamics. For example, it would hang a soft rope before lowering it for insertion but flip the gripper for a stiff adapter. For covering, it would sweep a towel sideways but place a hat directly. This demonstrated RAPID's ability to infer different object dynamics and adjust actions accordingly.
- **Embedding Correlation**: Analysis of the dynamics embedding showed that certain dimensions were strongly correlated with the softness of the objects during robot execution, indicating that the adaptation module successfully infers material properties.

In summary, RAPID demonstrated significant advancements in real-world deformable object mobile manipulation by effectively learning to infer and adapt to unknown object dynamics and shape changes, outperforming existing methods and validating the contribution of its novel architectural components and training methodology.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of RAPID represents a notable advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for deformable objects, which have historically posed significant challenges for autonomous systems. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling Robust Deformable Object Mobile Manipulation**: RAPID addresses a critical gap by allowing a mobile manipulator to robustly interact with and manipulate deformable objects in unstructured, real-world environments. The achieved success rates of 80%+ in complex tasks like 1D Inserting and 2D Covering, under conditions of unseen object dynamics, categories, and instances, signify a substantial step towards more versatile and capable robotic systems.

2. **Extending Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA) to Deformable Objects**: A core contribution of this work is the successful extension of the RMA framework to deformable objects. By recognizing that ground-truth particle positions in simulation capture shape changes, RAPID effectively integrates this crucial aspect of deformable object dynamics into its dynamics embedding. This addresses a fundamental limitation of prior RMA applications that primarily focused on rigid body properties.

3. **Effective Sim-to-Real Transfer with Online Adaptation**: RAPID demonstrates a potent strategy for zero-shot transfer from simulation to the real world using only onboard visual sensors. The two-phase training procedure, which transitions from privileged simulator information to non-privileged robot observations via L1-loss-trained adaptation modules, provides a generalizable mechanism. This approach minimizes the need for extensive, costly, and often impractical real-world data collection and fine-tuning, accelerating the deployment of robotic solutions.

4. **Real-Time Adaptation to Unknown Dynamics and Shape Changes**: The ability of RAPID to infer and adapt to unknown deformable object dynamics and shape changes in real-time is a key differentiator. This enables the robot to modify its manipulation strategy on the fly, as evidenced by its varied approaches to handling objects of different rigidities (e.g., rope vs. adapter, towel vs. hat). This adaptability is crucial for robustness in diverse real-world scenarios where object properties are unknown or variable.

5. **Robustness to Occlusions and Deformation**: Unlike model-based methods that often fail under hand-eye coordination or object occlusions, RAPID's model-free approach, which learns to infer dynamics embeddings directly from visual observations and actions, exhibits robustness to severe deformation and occlusions by the robot gripper. This makes it suitable for tasks requiring close-range interaction.

6. **Insights from Ablation Studies**: The comprehensive ablation studies validate the design choices of RAPID. They rigorously demonstrate the necessity of both the shape and dynamics adaptation modules and the benefits of the two-phase training process over end-to-end reinforcement learning. This contributes to the understanding of how to effectively train adaptive policies for complex manipulation tasks.

The potential impact of RAPID extends to various domains requiring interaction with non-rigid items. In **household robotics**, it could enable robots to perform tasks such as folding laundry, making beds, or tidying up by handling soft fabrics and flexible objects. In **logistics and manufacturing**, it could improve automated handling of irregularly shaped soft goods, packaging, or assembly tasks involving cables and flexible components. Furthermore, this research paves the way for future work in more generalized robotic intelligence, where robots can interact with a wider range of objects with varying physical properties, reducing the need for pre-programming or extensive domain-specific data. By providing a scalable and adaptable framework, RAPID contributes to the development of more autonomous and versatile robots for complex physical interactions.
