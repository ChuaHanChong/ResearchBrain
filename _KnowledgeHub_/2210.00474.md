# Saving the Limping: Fault-tolerant Quadruped Locomotion via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Nanyang Technological University and NVIDIA AI Technology Centre developed a reinforcement learning framework enabling quadruped robots to maintain stable locomotion despite critical hardware failures like joint locking, achieving zero-shot transfer from simulation to a physical Unitree A1 robot. The approach improved post-failure survival rates to 100% on the physical robot for tested joint failures, compared to baselines.

## Problem

- Quadruped robots face significant challenges maintaining operation and stability when encountering hardware failures like joint locking in uncontrolled environments.
- Traditional control methods require extensive manual tuning and specific modeling for each failure scenario, limiting generalization across different failure types.
- Existing reinforcement learning solutions for fault tolerance primarily validate in simulation, struggling with the sim-to-real gap for hardware failures due to complex modeling challenges.

## Method

- A realistic joint locking simulation strategy was designed with randomized parameters (failure time, joint, tolerance) to train a generalized agent, complemented by physical locking mechanisms for real-world testing.
- A joint teacher-student ==reinforcement learning== framework was introduced, where the policy and student network are optimized simultaneously, adaptively fusing teacher's privileged information and student's inferred dynamics.
- Extensive domain randomization, including ground friction, PD controller settings, and motor strength, was applied to enhance the robustness and sim-to-real transferability of the control policies.

## Results

- On a physical Unitree A1 robot, the fault-tolerant agent achieved **100%** survival across all tested single joint locking scenarios (hip, thigh, calf, using softlock and hardlock), whereas a baseline agent achieved only 5-**20%** survival.
- In simulation, the fault-tolerant agent maintained a higher post-failure forward velocity (**0.47** m/s) and significantly longer survival times (averaging **56.5%** of the post-failure period) compared to a baseline agent (**0.42** m/s and **44.7%**).
- The built-in Unitree A1 controller consistently failed immediately (**0%** survival) when subjected to a hardlock, highlighting the improved resilience of the developed RL-based approach.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit privilege information regarding failure states is crucial for teacher policies to effectively learn and minimize the negative impact of hardware failures.
> - The joint teacher-student training approach, with adaptive fusion of latent representations, provides more robust and efficient knowledge transfer for fault-tolerant policies compared to traditional supervised transfer methods.
> - Randomizing failure scenarios and jointly optimizing the teacher-student network enables a generalized policy that can adapt even to untrained multi-joint failure scenarios on a physical robot.
