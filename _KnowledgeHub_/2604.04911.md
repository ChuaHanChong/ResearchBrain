# SpatialEdit: Benchmarking Fine-Grained Image Spatial Editing

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced SpatialEdit-Bench, a benchmark for evaluating fine-grained image spatial editing, and SpatialEdit-500k, a large synthetic dataset for training. Their SpatialEdit-16B baseline model achieved superior performance in object-level and camera-level spatial manipulation, demonstrating up to a 0.358 point reduction in overall camera error compared to previous state-of-the-art methods.

## Problem

- Existing image editing models often struggle with precise, geometry-driven edits, failing to achieve geometric compliance despite appearing semantically plausible.
- Current methods for spatially-conditioned image generation typically require complex expert inputs like full 6-DoF camera trajectories, creating a high barrier to usability.
- Evaluation for spatial editing is underdeveloped, relying on coarse metrics or Vision-Language Model (VLM) based checks that do not reliably distinguish between plausible and geometrically accurate outputs.

## Method

- The team developed **SpatialEdit-Bench**, a benchmark featuring ==geometry-aware metrics== (==Viewpoint Error== and ==Framing Error==) to jointly assess perceptual plausibility and geometric fidelity.
- Constructed **SpatialEdit-500k**, a large-scale ==synthetic dataset== of 500,000 samples, generated using a controllable Blender-based pipeline with precise ground-truth geometric transformations.
- A baseline model, **SpatialEdit-16B**, was introduced, utilizing a ==cascaded diffusion architecture== pre-trained on general image editing and specialized for spatial manipulation via ==LoRA fine-tuning== on SpatialEdit-500k.

## Results

- **SpatialEdit-16B** achieved the highest overall performance on the SpatialEdit-Bench, securing a Moving Score of **0.673** and a Rotation Score of **0.632** for object-level tasks.
- For camera-level tasks, SpatialEdit-16B recorded the lowest Viewpoint Error (**0.243**) and Framing Error (**0.527**), yielding an overall camera error of **0.385**, a **0.358** point reduction from the previous state-of-the-art.
- Ablation studies confirmed that multi-task mixed training improved performance, and geometry-aware metrics showed significantly higher Spearman correlations with true rankings (**0.932** for VE) compared to VLM-based evaluation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - There is a fundamental gap between semantic alignment and geometric compliance in current image editing, which necessitates specialized benchmarks and data for true fine-grained spatial control.
> - Scalable synthetic data generation with ground-truth geometric transformations is effective for training models to accurately perform complex spatial manipulation tasks.
> - Geometry-aware evaluation metrics provide a more reliable assessment of a model's actual spatial editing capabilities than traditional VLM-based semantic plausibility checks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of image editing and generative artificial intelligence has experienced rapid advancements, particularly with the advent of diffusion-based generative models. These models have demonstrated increased fidelity and controllability in modifying image content. A significant trend in this domain involves moving beyond semantic "what to change" edits (e.g., adding, removing, replacing, or restyling elements) towards "where and how to change" edits, which involve geometry-driven transformations in a 3D spatial context. This capability, termed "image spatial editing" by the authors, is becoming increasingly relevant for applications such as world-modeling, embodied perception, interactive content creation, simulation, and 3D reasoning, where controllable viewpoint changes and object reconfigurations are foundational.

Despite progress in instruction-based image generation, precise spatial control remains a challenge for existing systems. Current limitations identified by the authors include:
1. **Requirement for expert inputs:** Many spatially-conditioned pipelines, such as those for world models or video generation, necessitate detailed inputs like full 6-Degrees-of-Freedom (6-DoF) camera trajectories, which can impose a high barrier to usability for typical image editing scenarios.
2. **Discrepancy between semantic alignment and geometric compliance:** General-purpose instruction-based editors often excel at semantic edits but may fail to accurately fulfill metric or viewpoint instructions (e.g., "rotate the camera 90 degrees to the right" or "rotate the object to show its front-right side"). The resulting images might appear plausible but are geometrically inaccurate.
3. **Narrow scope of existing spatial methods:** Some methods incorporate spatial reasoning but are typically specialized for a single operation or specific setting, limiting their generalization across the diverse range of operations required by users.

These limitations collectively indicate a gap between merely "semantic alignment" and faithful "geometric compliance" in current image editing paradigms. The authors argue that this gap is exacerbated by the underdeveloped state of evaluation for spatial editing. Existing benchmarks tend to rely on coarse metrics or vision-language model (VLM) based semantic alignment checks, which may not reliably distinguish between an output that "looks right" and one that "is right" geometrically. This deficiency in evaluation impedes the reliable iteration and progress of models designed for precise spatial manipulation. This work aims to address these identified gaps by introducing a specialized benchmark, a synthetic dataset, and a baseline model specifically tailored for fine-grained image spatial editing.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the field of image spatial editing by providing the necessary tools and methodologies for developing and rigorously evaluating models capable of fine-grained geometry-driven transformations. The motivation stems from the observed shortcomings of current image editing models and evaluation practices regarding precise spatial control.

The specific objectives and contributions of this work are threefold:

1. **To introduce SpatialEdit-Bench, a comprehensive benchmark for fine-grained spatial editing:** The motivation behind this is the current lack of adequate evaluation metrics that can reliably assess geometric fidelity in spatial edits. Existing VLM-based judging often prioritizes perceptual plausibility over geometric correctness. SpatialEdit-Bench is designed to jointly measure perceptual plausibility and geometric fidelity through viewpoint reconstruction and framing analysis, providing a more robust and geometry-aware assessment.
2. **To construct SpatialEdit-500k, a large-scale synthetic dataset:** Training models for fine-grained spatial editing is hampered by a data bottleneck. Such data is difficult to acquire at scale because it requires precise ground-truth geometric transformations, consistent object identity across edits, and unambiguous textual instructions, all while encompassing a wide variety of scenes, object categories, and camera configurations. SpatialEdit-500k addresses this by providing a scalable and controllable data engine built on Blender, capable of synthesizing paired images with corresponding textual instructions and precise ground-truth transformations for both object- and camera-centric operations.
3. **To develop SpatialEdit-16B, a baseline model for fine-grained spatial editing:** Leveraging the newly created SpatialEdit-500k dataset, the authors aim to develop a model that can achieve competitive performance on general image editing tasks while substantially improving its capabilities for fine-grained spatial manipulation, a regime where previous methods frequently underperform. This model is intended to serve as a strong foundation and demonstrate the utility of the benchmark and dataset.

In essence, the motivation is to bridge the gap between semantic understanding and geometric precision in image editing, enabling models to accurately interpret and execute instructions involving spatial transformations. This capability is considered crucial for the progression of interactive content creation, simulation, and advanced 3D reasoning systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research addresses the identified challenges by developing a unified task definition, a scalable data generation pipeline, geometry-aware evaluation metrics, and a specialized model architecture.

#### 3.1. Revisiting Image Spatial Manipulation and Task Definition

The authors redefine image spatial manipulation by categorizing it into two primary axes, aiming to bridge the gap between semantic intent and geometric precision:

- **Object-Level Spatial Manipulation:** This involves editing individual objects within an image.
 - **Translation and Scaling:** Users specify target regions for movement and resizing via red bounding boxes or textual instructions.
 - **Rotation:** Object orientation is discretized into eight canonical viewpoints (e.g., front, front-right, right).
- **Camera-Level View Control:** This focuses on manipulating the global imaging perspective to synthesize novel viewpoints without altering scene content.
 - **Pitch & Yaw:** Vertical tilt (pitch) is discretized at 15° intervals, and horizontal panning (yaw) at 45° increments.
 - **Zoom:** Focal length variations simulate movement toward or away from a focal point.

#### 3.2. SpatialEdit-500k Dataset Generation Pipeline

The dataset, SpatialEdit-500k, is generated using a controllable Blender-based pipeline to produce paired supervision with ground-truth geometric transformations and corresponding textual instructions.

- **Object-Centric Data Generation Pipeline (Left of Fig. 3):**
 1. **Asset Curation and Rendering:** Utilizes GLB assets from TexVerse, rendered in Blender with a canonical front-facing camera.
 2. **Viewpoint Verification:** An advanced Vision-Language Model (VLM) verifies that rendered images represent valid front views with minimal side-view characteristics.
 3. **Multi-View Rendering and Segmentation:** For each verified GLB model, eight uniformly distributed viewpoints are rendered. The Segment Anything Model (SAM3) is applied to obtain object masks, using TexVerse captions as prompts for verification.
 4. **Spatial Diversity Augmentation:** Eight additional renderings per valid view are generated with randomized translations and scaling factors in Blender. SAM3 is re-applied to confirm object visibility and containment.
 5. **Background Integration and Annotation:** A text-to-image model (Nano-Pro) synthesizes semantically compatible backgrounds for canonical front views. These backgrounds are composited with the multi-view and spatially perturbed images. Ground-truth 3D bounding boxes are projected to 2D for precise annotations.

- **Camera-Centric Data Generation Pipeline (Right of Fig. 3):**
 1. **Scene and Target Selection:** A large pool of high-quality 3D indoor and outdoor scenes is established. Salient objects are manually selected as camera focus targets.
 2. **Systematic Camera Pose Sampling:** Camera motion is parameterized relative to the focus object using yaw, pitch, and distance. Blender is used to systematically sample camera poses across predefined ranges of these parameters.
 3. **Quality Filtering:** A dual-branch pipeline ensures reliability:
 - A YOLO-based detector verifies the visibility of the focus object.
 - QwenVL-30B (VLM) assesses semantic and geometric plausibility, filtering out undesirable renderings.
 4. **Instruction Generation:** Raw camera parameters are recorded, and viewpoint pairs are formed by sampling two poses for the same object. Relative transformations are converted into templated camera-editing instructions, augmented with human-like instructions and concise scene descriptions.

#### 3.3. SpatialEdit-Benchmark Evaluation

The benchmark rigorously evaluates spatial transformation tasks, focusing on geometric accuracy, semantic consistency, and structural preservation.

- **Object-Level Task Metrics:**
 - **Moving Score (MS):** Combines geometric correctness and semantic fidelity. It is calculated as the geometric mean of Intersection over Union (IoU) between ground-truth and predicted bounding boxes (from a detector) and an object consistency score ($S_{oc}$) from a VLM (evaluating subject integrity and environmental coherence).
 - **Rotation Score (RS):** Assesses viewpoint correctness and appearance consistency. It is the geometric mean of a viewpoint correctness score ($S_{view}$) from a closed-source VLM (measuring angular configuration match) and a consistency score ($S_{cons}$) from the same VLM (preventing appearance drift).

- **Camera-Level Task Metrics:** A dual-metric protocol is adopted, using a detector-based metric (YOLO) and a geometry-aware metric (VGGT).
 - **Viewpoint Error (VE):** Measures camera viewpoint correctness in a geometry-aware manner. VGGT, a feed-forward transformer, infers 3D attributes (camera parameters, depth maps) from source, ground-truth, and predicted images ($I_{src}$, $I_{gt}$, $I_{pred}$). It estimates world-to-camera extrinsics, from which camera centers are computed. VE aggregates a baseline-normalized translation error ($\epsilon_{xyz}$) and a rotation error ($\epsilon_{rot}$, based on geodesic distance on SO(3)). Lower VE indicates higher accuracy.
 - **Framing Error (FE):** Addresses whether the edited output preserves meaningful spatial layout. It comprises:
 - **Ray Angle Difference ($\epsilon_{rag}$):** Calculates the average ray angle difference between detected objects in $I_{gt}$ and $I_{pred}$ (using YOLO and Hungarian Matching), ensuring spatial alignment.
 - **Zoom Direction Error ($\epsilon_{zde}$):** A binary metric that verifies if zoom commands result in the correct scaling direction of objects based on bounding box areas.
 - FE is the average of $\epsilon_{rag}$ and $\epsilon_{zde}$.

#### 3.4. SpatialEdit-16B Model Architecture and Training

The SpatialEdit-16B model utilizes a cascaded editing pipeline (Fig. 4). A Vision-Language Model (VLM) provides semantic embeddings for global conditioning. The input image is encoded into a VAE latent representation. An MM-DiT (Multimodal Diffusion Transformer) decoder then denoises this latent representation under multimodal guidance to produce the edited latent, which is subsequently decoded by the VAE into the final output image.

Training proceeds in two stages:
1. **Pre-training:** The model is initially adapted for general image editing by fine-tuning on open-source editing datasets and proprietary internal data, explicitly excluding spatial editing samples.
2. **Specialization:** The model is then specialized for image spatial editing using Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (LoRA) on the curated SpatialEdit-500k dataset. This approach aims to improve transformation control while preserving the general priors learned during pre-training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating the effectiveness of SpatialEdit-Bench, SpatialEdit-500k, and the SpatialEdit-16B model.

#### 4.1. Image Spatial Editing Performance (Quantitative)

- **Overall Performance (Table 2):** SpatialEdit-16B achieved the highest overall performance on the proposed SpatialEdit-Bench across both object-level and camera-level metrics, outperforming various closed-source and open-source models.
- **Object-Level Tasks:**
 - **Moving Score:** SpatialEdit significantly surpassed all baselines with a score of 0.673. For context, the next best open-source model, LongCatImage-Edit, scored 0.373.
 - **Rotation Score:** SpatialEdit achieved a score of 0.632, which was competitive and superior to most baselines.
- **Camera-Level Tasks:**
 - **Viewpoint Error:** SpatialEdit recorded the lowest viewpoint error at 0.243, indicating high accuracy in camera pose reconstruction.
 - **Framing Error:** SpatialEdit also yielded the lowest framing error at 0.527.
 - **Overall Camera Error:** SpatialEdit showed an overall camera error of 0.385, representing a significant improvement (0.358 points reduction) over the previous state-of-the-art model, LongCatImage-Edit (0.743).
- **Comparison to World Models:** When evaluating video-based world models (ViduQ2-Turbo, Kling-V2.5, Veo3.1, ReCamMaster, LingBot-World) on precise camera viewpoint control, SpatialEdit (an image-based model) consistently demonstrated weaker camera-level errors compared to most image editing models, with overall camera errors for world models ranging from 0.699 to 1.050 (Table 6). This suggests that maintaining consistent camera motion during video generation for fine-grained control remains a challenge for these models.

#### 4.2. General Editing Performance

- **GEdit-Bench Evaluation (Table 5):** SpatialEdit demonstrated competitive performance on the GEdit-Bench for general image editing tasks, achieving an overall score of 7.52. This indicates that the pre-training strategy provided a strong foundation, allowing the model to retain general editing capabilities alongside its specialized spatial editing proficiency.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Training Data Combinations (Table 3):** Experiments confirmed that multi-task mixed training (combining object moving, object rotation, and camera operation tasks) led to more reliable convergence and better overall performance compared to single-task training. Training on all three tasks yielded the best overall trade-off (Mov: 0.673, Rot: 0.632, Cam: 0.385), suggesting positive transfer of learning from shared spatial supervision.
- **Camera Evaluation Metrics (Table 4):** A controlled validation using fine-grained views showed that the proposed geometry-aware metrics (VE and FE) exhibit substantially higher Spearman correlations with true rankings (0.932 for VE, 0.659 for FE) compared to VLM-based GPT (0.445). This supports the reliability of VE and FE for diagnosing true spatial capabilities in camera evaluation.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results

- **Camera View Manipulation (Fig. 5 & 9):** SpatialEdit demonstrated a greater fidelity in following requested viewpoint shifts (e.g., zoom-out, yaw/pitch changes) compared to baseline methods. It preserved scene geometry more effectively and reduced artifacts such as texture stretching, boundary drift, and hallucinations.
- **Object-Level Manipulation (Fig. 6 & 8):** For tasks like moving objects into specified regions or rotating them to target views, SpatialEdit produced cleaner edits with higher object fidelity and stronger background preservation. Competing methods often introduced artifacts or inadvertently altered the background, while SpatialEdit maintained a better accuracy-preservation balance.

#### 4.5. Enhancement Tool for Single-view Reconstruction

The authors illustrate an application of SpatialEdit as an enhancement tool for 3D reconstruction from single-view inputs (Fig. 7). By synthesizing novel viewpoints through camera editing, SpatialEdit can add geometric constraints, which can lead to more accurate, structurally consistent, and detailed 3D reconstructions compared to those derived solely from a single perspective, which often suffer from depth-scale ambiguity and missing geometric cues.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a framework that addresses a critical need in the field of image editing: the precise and fine-grained control of spatial transformations. The significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Advancement in Image Spatial Editing:** The work shifts the focus of image editing beyond appearance and semantic changes to robust geometry-driven manipulations. This is crucial for enabling more sophisticated interactions with visual content, where precise control over object placement, scaling, rotation, and camera viewpoints is required.
2. **Establishment of Robust Evaluation Standards:** `SpatialEdit-Bench` provides a rigorous and geometry-aware evaluation suite. Its novel metrics, Viewpoint Error (VE) and Framing Error (FE), are demonstrated to be more reliable in assessing true geometric compliance than traditional VLM-based semantic assessments. This benchmark is expected to foster more accurate model development and progress by allowing researchers to differentiate between edits that merely "look right" and those that "are right" from a geometric perspective.
3. **Resolution of the Data Bottleneck:** The creation of `SpatialEdit-500k`—a large-scale, synthetic dataset generated through a controllable Blender pipeline with precise ground-truth transformations—is a substantial contribution. This dataset mitigates a significant challenge in training models for fine-grained spatial editing, enabling scalable and systematic development without the prohibitive costs and difficulties associated with real-world data collection for such complex annotations.
4. **Demonstration of Model Capabilities:** The `SpatialEdit-16B` baseline model, developed using the new dataset and benchmark, demonstrates superior performance in challenging spatial manipulation tasks while retaining competitive general editing capabilities. This validates the utility of the proposed dataset and benchmark and establishes a new performance standard for future research.
5. **Broadening of Application Scope:** The fine-grained spatial editing capabilities have implications beyond traditional image editing. They are directly relevant to advancing various domains such as:
 - **World-Modeling and Embodied AI:** Providing more controllable environments for training and simulating intelligent agents.
 - **Interactive Content Creation:** Enabling users to precisely arrange elements and perspectives in generated or edited images.
 - **Simulation:** Generating diverse scenarios with accurate spatial configurations.
 - **3D Reasoning:** Facilitating applications like single-view 3D reconstruction by synthesizing additional geometrically consistent views, thereby enhancing the accuracy and detail of 3D outputs.
6. **Promotion of Reproducible Research:** The commitment to making all resources—the benchmark, dataset, and model—publicly available is a key aspect of its impact. This will enable other researchers to reproduce results, build upon this work, and accelerate progress in the field of geometry-aware image synthesis and spatial AI.
