# SketchAgent: Language-Driven Sequential Sketch Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> SketchAgent introduces a method for language-driven, sequential sketch generation that leverages off-the-shelf multimodal large language models without additional training. The system generates sketches stroke-by-stroke, enabling real-time interactive collaboration and achieving human-like drawing aesthetics with high recognition rates.

## Problem

- Traditional sketch generation methods are limited by training data, lack generalizability, and often produce static or non-sequential output.
- Existing vision-language model (VLM)-based approaches are slow, generate sketches in a single step (lacking interpretable intermediates), and often produce mechanical-looking vector graphics.
- Multimodal Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle with fine-grained spatial reasoning, which is crucial for creating and editing visual content like sketches.

## Method

- Leverages off-the-shelf, frozen multimodal LLMs (e.g., Claude3.5-Sonnet) to generate sequential sketches without additional training, using a comprehensive prompting strategy (system prompt, ==Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning==, ==in-context learning (ICL) examples==) to guide generation and support interactive editing.
- Introduces a ==structured 50x50 grid-based canvas== and a precise =="sketching language"== (specifying points and t-values) to enhance the LLM's spatial reasoning abilities.
- Employs a ==cubic Bezier curve fitting process== to convert LLM-specified points into smooth, freehand-like strokes, ensuring a natural, human-like appearance.

## Results

- Achieved a Top-1 CLIP accuracy of **0.23** and Top-5 accuracy of **0.44** on 50 QuickDraw categories, approaching human-level recognition rates (human sketches **0.27** Top-1, **0.49** Top-5).
- User studies showed SketchAgent's drawings were perceived as more human-like (**74.90%** preference over direct prompting) and achieved **45.32%** human-likeness when compared to actual human drawings.
- Generated sketches in approximately **20 seconds**, enabling real-time interactive editing with **92%** accuracy for instructions and effective human-agent collaborative sketching with high recognizability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - It is possible to achieve language-driven, sequential sketch generation using off-the-shelf multimodal LLMs without any specific training or fine-tuning, by carefully structuring prompts and outputs.
> - Augmenting LLMs with a structured "sketching language" and a grid-based canvas can significantly improve their spatial reasoning for visual creation tasks.
> - The stroke-by-stroke generation process of SketchAgent allows for meaningful intermediate steps, real-time interaction, and effective human-AI collaborative sketching, mimicking human creative workflows.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

SketchAgent operates at the nexus of several rapidly evolving fields: sketch generation, multimodal large language models (LLMs), and human-computer interaction (HCI) in creative tasks.

Traditionally, sketch generation methods have largely relied on data-driven approaches, where generative models (e.g., GANs, VAEs, RNNs like SketchRNN) are trained on extensive datasets of human-drawn sketches (e.g., QuickDraw). While successful in producing diverse sketches within predefined categories, these methods are limited by the scale and diversity of their training data and often struggle with concepts outside their learned distribution. Moreover, they often generate sketches in a single step or lack a semantically meaningful sequential process, which deviates from how humans typically draw.

More recently, advancements in large vision-language models (VLMs) like CLIP and text-to-image diffusion models (e.g., DALL-E, Stable Diffusion) have enabled novel approaches to sketch generation. These methods leverage the rich visual priors embedded in massive pre-trained models, often guiding the optimization of parametric curves in vector space to generate sketches from text prompts. While these approaches can produce high-quality, diverse sketches beyond dataset limitations, they still often optimize all strokes simultaneously, making the intermediate drawing steps uninterpretable and the process slow (minutes to hours per sketch), thus hindering real-time interactivity. Furthermore, these models primarily operate in pixel space, or if they generate vector graphics (e.g., SVG), they often produce "mechanical" outputs lacking the spontaneous irregularities of human freehand drawing.

Concurrent to these developments, multimodal LLMs have emerged, capable of processing and generating both text and images. While powerful, their visual output capabilities are often mediated through external "tools" (e.g., calling a text-to-image model) or by generating code (e.g., Python, SVG) that is then rendered. The latter often results in precise, geometric shapes rather than expressive, freehand sketches. A key challenge for these LLMs has been their struggle with fine-grained spatial reasoning, which is critical for visual content creation and editing.

SketchAgent distinguishes itself by directly addressing the limitations of existing methods. It leverages the *sequential nature* and *rich prior knowledge* of *off-the-shelf multimodal LLMs* (without training or fine-tuning) to enable *language-driven, sequential sketch generation*. By introducing an intuitive "sketching language" and a grid-based canvas, it enhances the LLM's spatial reasoning. Its stroke-by-stroke generation process inherently emulates human drawing, providing semantic meaning to intermediate steps and enabling real-time, dynamic, conversational interaction and iterative refinement. This positions SketchAgent as a significant step towards creating general-purpose artificial sketching systems that genuinely collaborate with humans, fostering creativity and problem-solving in a more intuitive and flexible manner than prior art.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind SketchAgent are multifaceted, stemming from the desire to bridge the gap between AI's generative capabilities and the nuanced, dynamic nature of human sketching:

- **Capture the Dynamic and Abstract Nature of Human Sketching:** Human sketching is a fluid, iterative process involving continuous adaptation, visual feedback, and the gradual externalization of ideas. Existing AI systems often fall short in replicating this dynamic, stroke-by-stroke progression, either generating static images or optimizing all strokes simultaneously without semantic temporal structure. SketchAgent aims to create an artificial system that inherently supports this sequential, evolving quality.
- **Enable Language-Driven, Sequential Sketch Generation:** The goal is to allow users to create and modify sketches using natural language instructions, much like conversing with another human. This necessitates an AI that can translate high-level linguistic concepts into a series of drawing actions, rather than just producing a final image.
- **Facilitate Interactive and Collaborative Sketching:** True human-computer collaboration requires real-time interaction and shared understanding. Previous methods are often too slow or lack the interpretability of intermediate steps to support meaningful, turn-taking collaboration. SketchAgent aims to enable dialogue-driven drawing and iterative refinement where both human and agent can contribute and edit.
- **Leverage Off-the-Shelf Multimodal LLMs Without Training:** A significant motivation is to utilize the vast pre-trained knowledge and inherent sequential text generation capabilities of existing multimodal LLMs. This approach eliminates the need for extensive, domain-specific training or fine-tuning on human-drawn datasets, which are often limited in scale, diversity, and style.
- **Overcome LLM Limitations in Spatial Reasoning and Sketch Appearance:** While LLMs excel at textual reasoning, they often struggle with precise spatial understanding and translating high-level concepts into visually natural, freehand strokes. Code-generated vector graphics tend to look mechanical. SketchAgent seeks to address this by introducing a structured "sketching language" and a grid canvas to enhance spatial reasoning, and by employing a Bezier curve fitting process to ensure a smoother, more human-like stroke appearance.
- **Pave the Way for General-Purpose Artificial Sketching Systems:** Ultimately, the researchers are motivated to develop a versatile AI sketching system that can support diverse creative, problem-solving, and communication tasks across various disciplines, moving beyond specialized applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SketchAgent’s methodology centers on leveraging an off-the-shelf, frozen multimodal Large Language Model (LLM)—primarily Claude3.5-Sonnet—to generate sequential sketches based on natural language inputs, without requiring additional training or fine-tuning. The approach involves a novel sketching language, a grid-based canvas, and specific prompting strategies.

#### 3.1. The Canvas and Spatial Reasoning

Recognizing that multimodal LLMs often struggle with fine-grained spatial reasoning, SketchAgent introduces a structured "canvas" as a numbered grid (50x50 cells). Each cell is uniquely identified by x and y coordinates (e.g., `x1y1`). The agent interacts with this canvas by specifying desired (x,y) coordinates, which provides a discrete, explicit spatial framework that is easier for the LLM to interpret and manipulate than raw pixel coordinates.

#### 3.2. Sketch Representation and Processing

A sketch is defined as a sequence of ordered strokes, `S = {S_1, S_2..., S_n}`. Each stroke `S_i` is defined by a sequence of `m` cell coordinates `{(x_j, y_j)}` and a corresponding set of `t` values `(T_i = {t_j})` ranging from 0 to 1, indicating progression along the curve. The LLM outputs these as string-based actions within `<points>` and `<t_values>` tags.

Crucially, instead of simply connecting points with polylines (which would appear non-smooth due to the sparse grid) or directly using coordinates as Bezier control points (which do not lie on the curve), SketchAgent treats the specified `(x,y)` coordinates as *desired points sampled along the curve*. It then fits a smooth cubic Bezier curve to these sampled points by solving a system of linear equations using least squares. This approach allows the agent to specify points it intends the stroke to pass through, while ensuring the resulting visual output is smooth and more naturally resembles freehand drawing. Long or complex curves can be recursively split for better fitting.

#### 3.3. Drawing Instructions and Prompting Strategy

The LLM receives two main inputs:
- **System Prompt:** This fixed prompt introduces the agent as an "expert artist" and explains the grid canvas and the rules of the new sketching language, including examples of how to draw single-stroke primitives (e.g., smooth curves, ellipses, non-smooth shapes like triangles/rectangles by repeating corner points, straight lines, dots). It also encourages a step-by-step thinking process.
- **User Prompt:** This prompt contains the specific sketching task (e.g., "Draw a shark") and critically includes an in-context learning (ICL) example of a complete sketch (a house) drawn using the sketching language format. This ICL example is found to be crucial for the model to preserve the correct output format.

The agent's response follows a structured format, including:
- `<thinking>` tags: The agent outlines its overall sketching strategy, describing sketch components, intended order, and placement. This leverages Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting to enhance planning capabilities.
- `<strokes>` tags: Contains the sequential definition of each stroke with its `<points>`, `<t_values>`, and a semantic `<id>` tag (e.g., "dorsal fin").

#### 3.4. In-Chat Editing and Collaborative Sketching

The system supports iterative interaction:
- **In-Chat Editing:** The rendered canvas (as an image) is fed back to the agent along with an updated user prompt requesting edits. The agent then generates new strokes to modify the existing sketch.
- **Collaborative Sketching:** The canvas is shared. An adjustable "stopping token" (`</s{j}>`) allows the agent to pause generation after a certain number of strokes. The human user can then add their own strokes directly to the canvas. These human-drawn strokes are converted into the agent's string-based format by reversing the fitting process (sampling at `t` values and mapping to grid cells). The converted user strokes are integrated into the agent's sequence, allowing the agent to resume drawing based on the updated canvas state.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SketchAgent demonstrates significant capabilities across several key areas of sketch generation and human-AI collaboration.

#### 4.1. Text-Conditioned Sketch Generation

- **Diversity and Generalization:** SketchAgent successfully generates sketches for a wide range of diverse concepts, including animals, diagrams (e.g., circuit diagrams, flowcharts), scientific concepts (e.g., double-slit experiment), and notable landmarks (e.g., Taj Mahal). This ability to go beyond predefined categories, without specific training, is a major advantage over traditional data-driven methods.
- **Quantitative Evaluation (CLIP Score):** Using a CLIP zero-shot classifier on 500 sketches across 50 QuickDraw categories, SketchAgent (with Claude3.5-Sonnet) achieved a Top-1 accuracy of 0.23 and Top-5 accuracy of 0.44. This performance was the best among all tested multimodal LLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, Claude3-Opus) and approached human-level recognition rates (human sketches from QuickDraw had 0.27 Top-1, 0.49 Top-5).
- **Comparison to Direct SVG Generation:** When prompted to directly generate SVG code, Claude3.5-Sonnet produced comparable CLIP scores but resulted in sketches with a uniform, mechanical appearance lacking the fluidity and natural irregularities of freehand human sketches.
- **Human-Likeness User Study (2AFC):** A 2AFC user study with 150 participants showed that SketchAgent's drawings were perceived as more human-like (chosen in 74.90% of cases) compared to direct prompting. When compared to actual human drawings, users slightly preferred human sketches (54.68%), but SketchAgent's outputs were still considered human-like significantly more often (45.32%) than direct prompting's (38.9%).

#### 4.2. Sequential Sketching

- **Stroke-by-Stroke Process:** SketchAgent generates sketches gradually, stroke by stroke, with each stroke semantically annotated by the agent (e.g., "dorsal fin," "house base"). This mirrors the human drawing process, providing interpretable intermediate steps.
- **Process Comparison:** Unlike optimization-based methods like SVGDreamer (which can take over an hour and have meaningless intermediate steps), SketchAgent produces sketches in real-time (approx. 20 seconds for a complete sketch). Compared to SketchRNN, which is also sequential but limited to its training categories, SketchAgent leverages the broad prior knowledge of its backbone LLM for general concepts.
- **CLIP Score Evolution:** Both SketchAgent's and human-drawn sketches (from QuickDraw) show a similar pattern of increasing CLIPScore as more strokes are added, indicating that sketches become progressively more recognizable as they evolve.

#### 4.3. Human-Agent Collaborative Sketching

- **Effective Collaboration:** In a web-based environment where humans and SketchAgent took turns drawing, collaboratively produced sketches achieved recognition levels close to those made solely by users and higher than those produced by the agent alone.
- **Meaningful Contributions:** Analyzing partial sketches (agent-only or user-only strokes) in collaborative mode showed a significant reduction in recognizability, indicating that both human and agent contributions were meaningful and complementary to the final sketch's recognizability.
- **Consistent Complexity:** The average number of strokes per completed sketch was consistent across solo and collaborative modes, suggesting comparable levels of detail.

#### 4.4. Chat-Based Sketch Editing

- **Interactive Refinement:** SketchAgent effectively performs interactive text-based sketch editing within a chat dialogue, combining both text and image inputs.
- **Spatial and Semantic Reasoning:** The agent successfully followed instructions to add objects, both with specified relative locations (e.g., "tree to the left") and by inferring placement based on semantic understanding (e.g., adding a hat to an animal's head). It correctly followed instructions 92% of the time, with 94% accuracy for specified relations and 88% accuracy for inferred semantic relations.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study

- The ablation study confirmed the importance of each component: the system prompt, Chain-of-Thought reasoning (CoT), and especially the in-context learning (ICL) example of a complete sketch. Removing or modifying these components significantly reduced sketch recognition accuracy, highlighting their synergistic contribution to the agent's overall performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SketchAgent represents a significant advancement in the field of AI-powered creative tools, with several profound implications and potential impacts:

- **Pioneering Unsupervised Sequential Sketch Generation:** The most significant contribution is demonstrating, for the first time, that off-the-shelf, pre-trained multimodal LLMs can be effectively leveraged for *sequential* sketch generation *without any additional training or fine-tuning*. This "zero-shot" capability for a dynamic, stroke-based process is a critical breakthrough, reducing development costs and dependency on large, specialized datasets.
- **Emulating Human Drawing Process:** By focusing on stroke-by-stroke generation with semantic annotations, SketchAgent moves beyond static image generation or simultaneous stroke optimization. This inherent sequentiality more closely emulates the human creative process, making the AI's output more interpretable and aligned with human intuition. This could lead to more natural and effective human-AI co-creation.
- **Enhancing Human-Computer Communication and Interaction:** The language-driven, chat-based interface for sketch generation and editing significantly lowers the barrier to entry for interacting with drawing AI. Users can simply describe their ideas in natural language, fostering a more intuitive and collaborative design process. This could revolutionize applications in design, education, and visual communication.
- **Towards General-Purpose Sketching Systems:** By relying on the broad prior knowledge of powerful LLMs, SketchAgent can generate sketches for a vast range of concepts, including abstract and scientific ideas, overcoming the category limitations of data-driven models. This pushes the field closer to truly general-purpose artificial sketching systems capable of adapting to diverse user needs and domains.
- **Improved Spatial Reasoning in LLMs:** The introduction of a grid-based canvas and a structured sketching language provides a valuable blueprint for enhancing the spatial reasoning capabilities of LLMs for visual tasks. This is a crucial step towards more intelligent and visually aware multimodal AI.
- **Catalyst for AI in Creative Industries:** The ability to generate and collaboratively refine sketches in real-time has immense potential for creative industries (e.g., product design, architecture, animation storyboard creation), educational tools (e.g., for visual learners, concept explanation), and even scientific illustration, accelerating ideation and visualization workflows.
- **New Avenues for Cognitive Science Research:** The semantically annotated, sequential nature of SketchAgent's output provides a unique dataset and tool for cognitive scientists to study human-AI collaboration, the emergence of shared representations, and the mechanisms of visual thinking, offering insights into both human and artificial intelligence.

While the paper acknowledges limitations, such as the current amateur aesthetic for complex figures and difficulties with letters/numbers (inherent to the LLM's visual prior), the foundational methodology of SketchAgent lays robust groundwork for future research. As multimodal LLMs continue to improve their visual understanding and generation capabilities, SketchAgent's approach is poised to yield increasingly sophisticated and versatile creative AI companions.
