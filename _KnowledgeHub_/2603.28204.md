# ERPO: Token-Level Entropy-Regulated Policy Optimization for Large Reasoning Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Entropy-Regulated Policy Optimization (ERPO) introduces a token-level credit assignment mechanism for large reasoning models, utilizing policy entropy and implicit progress signals. This approach enhances reasoning accuracy and generates more concise solution paths, allowing smaller LLMs to achieve performance competitive with or superior to much larger models on complex mathematical benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning methods for LLMs, such as GRPO, assign uniform sequence-level rewards, failing to account for the varying informational value of individual tokens in a reasoning chain.
- This coarse-grained credit assignment can lead to premature entropy collapse, suppressing necessary exploration at critical decision points, and inefficiency from redundant derivations in deterministic steps.
- There was a lack of mechanisms to explicitly identify "Critical Decision Pivots" (CDPs) in reasoning and adaptively adjust exploration efforts at these crucial junctures.

## Method

- **ERPO** integrates ==token-level policy entropy== to identify ==critical decision pivots== and an ==implicit progress signal==, providing fine-grained feedback without extra reward training.
- An ==adaptive gating function== dynamically adjusts reward density based on local predictive uncertainty, concentrating gradient signals on high-entropy logical junctions.
- The framework uses ==relative position bucketing== and ==outcome-driven anchors== to normalize implicit advantages and prevent ==reward hacking==, ensuring process-level feedback aligns with final correct outcomes.

## Results

- **ERPO** consistently outperformed GRPO and SFT baselines in reasoning accuracy across various Qwen2.5 model scales and mathematical benchmarks, with Qwen2.5-7B ERPO achieving **20.70%** average accuracy versus GRPO's **19.43%**.
- The Qwen2.5-7B **ERPO** model surpassed the performance of significantly larger commercial models like DeepSeek-R1-671B (**17.48%** accuracy) on average, demonstrating improved reasoning capability with fewer parameters.
- **ERPO** models generated significantly shorter reasoning sequences while achieving higher accuracy, indicating improved inference efficiency and conciseness by effectively suppressing redundancy in derivations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reasoning processes in LLMs are characterized by heterogeneous token-level uncertainty, with "Critical Decision Pivots" exhibiting high entropy requiring adaptive exploration.
> - Preventing premature entropy collapse is vital for robust learning and sustained exploration, allowing models to develop more diverse and generalizable reasoning strategies.
> - Fine-grained, token-level credit assignment mechanisms can significantly boost reasoning accuracy and efficiency, enabling smaller language models to surpass the performance of much larger models on complex tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the field of enhancing Large Language Models (LLMs) for complex reasoning tasks, particularly through Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). LLMs have demonstrated capabilities in solving complex logical problems via Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. RLVR, in particular, has emerged as a significant paradigm for post-training these models, with approaches like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) showing effectiveness in improving reasoning abilities by reducing computational overhead and enhancing training efficiency through intra-group relative advantages without a critic network.

However, existing RLVR methods, including GRPO, typically assign a uniform, sequence-level advantage to all tokens within a reasoning chain. This approach overlooks the intrinsic heterogeneity in the informational value and decision-making criticality of different tokens. Prior research in RLVR has focused on various aspects:
- **Stability and Exploration**: Methods like Geometric-Mean Policy Optimization (GMPO) and BAPO aim to mitigate gradient variance and stabilize training through global objectives or adaptive clipping. Exploration strategies often rely on predefined static scheduling (e.g., fixed entropy regularization) or attempt to focus on specific logical tokens (SIREN). These approaches are generally passive and global, applying uniform averaging that can suppress critical logical hubs alongside redundant tokens.
- **Structural Constraints**: Approaches such as TreePO and Seer focus on efficiency through prefix sharing, tree-structured rollouts, or online context learning to manage computational overhead. While effective for throughput, these methods may introduce structural rigidity that inadvertently prunes high-value inference paths.
- **Preference Balancing**: Methods like S-GRPO, AW-GRPO, and Dynamic Reward Weighting typically operate on a sequence-level reward interface, addressing problems like "overthinking" or balancing multiple objectives. Iterative frameworks like CoRLHF optimize policy and reward models cooperatively. However, by treating the entire reasoning chain as a single scalar advantage, these methods often fail to provide the asymmetric compensation required for critical logical jumps.
- **Exploration Efficiency**: Techniques such as Knapsack RL and EEPO (Exploration-Enhanced Policy Optimization) address the difficulty of exploration by dynamic resource allocation or mechanisms to prevent entropy collapse and encourage novel trajectories.

The authors identify a fundamental tension in existing methodologies between global stability and localized exploration, often relying on passive, static scheduling or coarse-grained sequence-level feedback. This hinders the capture of dynamic uncertainty inherent in reasoning processes. ERPO positions itself as a refinement to this landscape by introducing a token-level, entropy-regulated approach to credit assignment, aiming to address the limitations of uniform advantage signals and enhance exploration precisely at critical decision points within the reasoning chain. This allows for a more granular and adaptive optimization that is hypothesized to improve both reasoning accuracy and efficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the reasoning capabilities and efficiency of Large Language Models (LLMs) by introducing a more fine-grained and adaptive policy optimization framework called Entropy-Regulated Policy Optimization (ERPO). The motivation stems from observed limitations in current reinforcement learning methodologies, particularly Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), when applied to complex reasoning tasks.

The key motivations and problems identified are:
- **Information Heterogeneity in Reasoning Chains**: GRPO, a prominent RLVR algorithm, assigns a uniform, sequence-level advantage to all tokens within a generated reasoning path. This implicitly assumes that every token contributes equally to the final outcome. However, the authors argue that the intrinsic informational value of tokens along a reasoning chain is heterogeneous.
- **Critical Decision Pivots (CDPs)**: Through empirical analysis, the authors demonstrate that LLMs exhibit transient high-entropy states at specific points during reasoning. These "Critical Decision Pivots" (CDPs) represent logical branches or critical decision points where the model's policy is uncertain and highly sensitive to perturbations. Conversely, during deterministic logical expansion phases, token distributions tend to converge with near-zero entropy.
- **Suppressed Exploration and Redundancy**: Current token-agnostic reward designs (e.g., length penalties, KL divergence) are insufficient. They tend to suppress necessary exploration at CDPs, where substantial randomness and diversity are required to identify correct reasoning paths. Simultaneously, these designs fail to effectively compress redundancy during deterministic computations, leading to inefficient generation and potentially extraneous computational overhead.
- **Diluted Reward Signals**: Standard reinforcement learning methods like GRPO, by applying a uniform advantage, dilute the crucial reward signal from high-stakes CDPs with gradient noise originating from redundant or less critical tokens. This prevents the model from concentrating its learning effort on the most causally significant steps.
- **Empirical Validation**: The authors conducted a multi-stage empirical analysis:
 - **Lexical Entropy Visualization**: Visualizing lexical entropy in inference trajectories (Figure 1a) confirmed high entropy at CDPs and low entropy during deterministic steps.
 - **Causal Perturbation Study**: A large-scale perturbation study on the MATH dataset revealed that perturbing high-entropy tokens led to a significant 35.2% drop in inference accuracy, while low-entropy steps showed robustness (Figure 1b). This established the "irreplaceable structural transitions" nature of CDPs.

Based on these insights, the core objective of ERPO is to transform sparse, sequence-level outcomes into dense, token-level signals. This refined credit assignment aims to prioritize exploration at CDPs to fortify critical links in the reasoning chain, while simultaneously dampening advantage signals during deterministic computations to encourage conciseness and robustness. By focusing gradient updates on the most causal steps, ERPO seeks to prevent premature entropy collapse and discourage the generation of redundant reasoning paths.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ERPO (Entropy-Regulated Policy Optimization) is a novel reinforcement learning framework designed to refine credit assignment in complex reasoning tasks by focusing on fine-grained token dynamics rather than coarse sequence-level advantages. The method integrates token-level uncertainty diagnostics with implicit progress signals and temporal bucketing.

The approach starts by formalizing LLM generation and RLVR, then outlines GRPO as the baseline. ERPO is built upon three synergistic components:

#### 3.1. Token-level Diagnostic Metrics

The framework first defines two diagnostic metrics for each token $o_{i,t}$ in a generated response $o_i$ for prompt $x$:
- **Uncertainty Estimation**: Policy entropy $H_{i,t}$ quantifies the model’s instantaneous uncertainty at token $t$. High entropy signals Critical Decision Pivots (CDPs) where distinct reasoning paths might diverge.
- **Implicit Progress Signal**: $s_{i,t}$ measures the confidence gain relative to a frozen reference policy $\pi_{ref}$. It is defined as $\beta_{progress}(\log \pi_{\theta}(o_{i,t} | x, o_{i,<t}) - \log \pi_{ref}(o_{i,t} | x, o_{i,<t}))$. This acts as an intrinsic motivator, rewarding tokens that show refined understanding over the base model and mitigating sparse reward challenges.

#### 3.2. Entropy-aware Gating

To leverage the uncertainty signal for optimization, an adaptive gating function $W_{i,t}$ is introduced. This function translates local predictive uncertainty into a relative importance weight. For each token $o_{i,t}$ in a prompt group $\mathcal{G}$, $W_{i,t}$ is computed as a sigmoid-calibrated, intra-group normalized entropy:
$W_{i,t} = \sigma(\gamma \cdot \frac{H_{i,t} - \mu_{H,\mathcal{G}}}{\sigma_{H,\mathcal{G}} + \delta})$.
Here, $\mu_{H,\mathcal{G}}$ and $\sigma_{H,\mathcal{G}}$ are the moving mean and standard deviation of entropy within the current group, and $\gamma$ is a scaling factor. This gating mechanism ensures that the gradient signal is concentrated on high-stakes decision points, where multiple reasoning paths diverge.

#### 3.3. Bucketing Implicit Advantage

Reasoning processes involve different temporal stages (e.g., planning, branching, derivation). To ensure fair comparison of implicit progress values, ERPO employs relative position binning.
- The relative progress of a token $o_{i,t}$ is defined as $\tau_{i,t} = t/|o_i|$.
- The reasoning process is partitioned into $K$ discrete temporal buckets $\mathcal{B}_1, \ldots, \mathcal{B}_K$.
- Within each bucket $k$ and prompt group $\mathcal{G}$, intra-group bucket normalization is performed: $\tilde{s}_{i,t} = \frac{s_{i,t} - \mu_{k,\mathcal{G}}}{\sigma_{k,\mathcal{G}} + \delta}$. This aligns the optimization signal with the model’s multi-stage reasoning trajectory by judging a token's advantage against peers at the same reasoning stage.

#### 3.4. Final Advantages Synthesis

The refined process signals are then synthesized with the original sequence-level advantages, while incorporating an Outcome Anchoring mechanism to prevent reward hacking. The sign of the sequence-level advantage ($\text{sgn}(\hat{A}_{group_i})$) acts as a directional guardrail, ensuring that even confident pivotal tokens are penalized if they lead to an incorrect final conclusion.
- The calibrated process reward $\Psi_{i,t}$ is computed as $\sigma_{target} \cdot W_{i,t} \cdot \text{sgn}(\hat{A}_{group_i}) \cdot \frac{\tilde{s}_{i,t}}{\text{std}(\Psi_{active}) + \delta}$.
- The final token-level advantage $\hat{A}_{final_{i,t}}$ is obtained by combining the group-level advantage with the calibrated process reward, followed by intra-group Z-score normalization: $\hat{A}_{final_{i,t}} = \text{Norm}_{\mathcal{G}}(\hat{A}_{group_i} + \eta \cdot \Psi_{i,t})$.

#### 3.5. Theoretical Analysis

The paper provides a theoretical analysis of ERPO, demonstrating its properties:
- **Equivalence to Entropy-Weighted Regularization**: ERPO's gradient is shown to correspond to optimizing the GRPO objective augmented with a dynamic, entropy-weighted relative entropy regularization term. This regularization is precision-guided and most active at critical decision pivots.
- **Stability via Zero-Sum Normalization**: The intra-group normalization of $\hat{A}_{final_{i,t}}$ ensures that the total advantage across a prompt group remains conserved (summing to zero with unit variance). This prevents reward hacking by forcing credit reallocation, making optimization prioritize causally significant steps.
- **Causality and Consistency**: The advantage function $\hat{A}_{final_{i,t}}$ relies only on the current token and its preceding context, as well as statistics computed from the old policy, thereby preserving the causality of the trajectory and satisfying the Policy Gradient Theorem requirements.

The overall algorithm (Algorithm 1) iteratively samples prompts, generates responses, computes token-level diagnostics (entropy, implicit progress), applies hierarchical normalization and gating, synthesizes final outcome-anchored advantages, and updates the policy using a GRPO-like loss function.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of ERPO was conducted across various scales of the Qwen2.5 model series (1.5B, 3B, and 7B parameters) and benchmarked against several baselines.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Models:** Qwen2.5-1.5B, Qwen2.5-3B, Qwen2.5-7B.
- **Fine-tuning:** Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) with rank $r=32$ and $\alpha_{lora}=64$.
- **Hyperparameters:** $\beta_{progress}=0.1$, $\gamma=5$, $\eta=0.2$.
- **Training:** Single epoch, maximum sequence length 2048, global batch size 16, 8 rollouts per prompt, learning rate $5 \times 10^{-6}$ with cosine decay, AdamW optimizer.
- **Dataset:** MATH dataset (difficulty levels 3-5) for reinforcement learning training.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:** AMC23, AIME24, AIME25, and Minerva.
- **Metrics:** Reasoning Accuracy (Acc %), Format Consistency (Fmt %), `pass@k` (for $k \in \{2, 4, 8, 16\}$), Average Generation Tokens, and Total Training Time.
- **Baselines:** Base model (no fine-tuning), Supervised Fine-Tuned (SFT) model, commercial instruct models (DeepSeek-R1-671B-0528, Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct), and GRPO (the direct baseline for comparison).

**Overall Performance (Table 1):**
- ERPO consistently outperformed GRPO across all parameter scales (1.5B, 3B, 7B) and most evaluation benchmarks in terms of reasoning accuracy and format consistency.
- For the 3B model, ERPO achieved a nearly 5% absolute performance improvement over GRPO on the AMC23 dataset.
- Notably, ERPO-7B demonstrated competitive performance, achieving 49.53% accuracy on AMC23, surpassing much larger commercial models like DeepSeek-R1-671B-0528 (33.91%) and Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct (47.81%) on this benchmark. This suggests that token-level credit assignment can compensate for smaller parameter counts.
- SFT models occasionally performed worse than their base counterparts on harder benchmarks, indicating that mimicking specific solution paths from a simpler training set might limit generalization, whereas ERPO promotes robust internal logic through exploration.

**Multi Sample Scaling Analysis (Table 2):**
- The `pass@k` metric, which evaluates the number of problems solved with multiple samples, showed that ERPO consistently outperformed GRPO for most `k` values at the same parameter scale.
- ERPO-3B partially surpassed the performance of commercial baselines (DeepSeek-R1, Qwen3) on AMC23 for `pass@k`, and ERPO-7B completely surpassed them. This suggests that ERPO expands the effective search space of the policy, allowing smaller models to achieve performance comparable to much larger models under high-throughput sampling.

**Scale-Dependent Training Dynamics (Figure 3):**
- For 3B and 7B models, ERPO exhibited higher final accuracy and faster convergence compared to GRPO. This indicates that the entropy-regulated mechanism provides beneficial guidance to models with more stable latent representations.
- For the 1.5B model, ERPO initially underperformed GRPO but then rose sharply in later training stages. The authors attribute this to the entropy-regulation mechanism introducing significant perturbations to smaller, less stable models, leading to a longer initial exploration phase before rapid performance gains.

**Policy Stability (Figure 4):**
- ERPO's reward signal eventually exceeded GRPO's, despite being lower initially due to its emphasis on exploration.
- Crucially, ERPO maintained a healthy entropy level (between 0.2 and 0.4) in the late stages of training, effectively preventing the entropy collapse observed in GRPO. This preservation of diversity is presented as key to avoiding mode collapse.
- Gradient norms for ERPO remained stable throughout training, and KL divergence, though slightly higher than GRPO's (reflecting a more aggressive departure from the base model), remained well controlled.

**Efficiency and Computational Overhead (Figure 5):**
- A key finding is that ERPO achieved higher accuracy with significantly shorter reasoning sequences (fewer generation tokens). For example, on AMC23, ERPO-7B generated paths that were approximately 100 tokens shorter than GRPO-7B's. This indicates that ERPO encourages conciseness by suppressing redundancy at low-entropy steps.
- While ERPO's generation length was initially slightly higher, it dropped significantly below GRPO's in later training stages while maintaining superior accuracy.
- The total training time for ERPO was comparable to GRPO (e.g., 14.46 hours for ERPO vs. 14.02 hours for GRPO for 7B models). This is attributed to the computational efficiency of the additional diagnostic signals used by ERPO. The authors anticipate further efficiency gains as reduced rollout lengths directly translate to higher training throughput.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ERPO framework represents a contribution to the field of reinforcement learning for large reasoning models, particularly in the context of mathematical problem-solving. Its significance stems from addressing the limitations of coarse-grained credit assignment in existing RLVR approaches, such as GRPO.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Enhanced Credit Assignment**: ERPO introduces a method to transform sparse, sequence-level rewards into dense, token-level guidance. This fine-grained credit assignment mechanism, driven by token-level uncertainty and implicit progress signals, allows for a more precise and effective policy optimization.
- **Targeted Exploration**: By identifying Critical Decision Pivots (CDPs) through entropy-aware gating, ERPO adaptively amplifies exploration precisely where the model's policy is most uncertain and sensitive. This promotes the discovery of diverse and correct reasoning paths, which is crucial for complex problem-solving.
- **Reduced Redundancy**: The framework effectively suppresses redundant computations during deterministic reasoning steps, leading to more concise and robust derivation paths. This not only improves the quality of reasoning chains but also contributes to efficiency.
- **Improved Policy Stability**: ERPO demonstrates the ability to maintain healthy entropy levels in the policy during late-stage training, thereby preventing the "entropy collapse" observed in baseline methods like GRPO. This stability is critical for sustaining exploration capacity and avoiding mode collapse.
- **Performance-to-Efficiency Trade-off**: The research shows that ERPO achieves a superior balance between reasoning accuracy and inference efficiency. Models trained with ERPO not only yield higher accuracy but also generate significantly shorter reasoning sequences, which reduces the computational footprint and inference latency of LLMs.
- **Scalability and Competitiveness**: ERPO's effectiveness is demonstrated across various model scales, with smaller ERPO-trained models (e.g., 7B parameters) rivaling or surpassing the performance of much larger commercial models (hundreds of billions of parameters) on competitive mathematical benchmarks. This indicates that optimized learning mechanisms can partially compensate for parameter count.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **More Efficient LLMs for Reasoning**: ERPO has the potential to make large language models more efficient and practical for deployment in applications requiring complex reasoning, such as scientific discovery, engineering problem-solving, and automated theorem proving, by reducing the computational resources needed for both training and inference.
- **Democratization of High-Performance LLMs**: By enabling smaller models to achieve high reasoning capabilities, ERPO could contribute to making advanced LLMs more accessible to researchers and developers with limited computational resources.
- **Advancement in RL for LLMs**: The framework offers a template for integrating rich internal diagnostics of LLMs (like token-level entropy) into reinforcement learning objectives, potentially inspiring new directions for fine-grained policy optimization and agent learning.
- **Enhanced Interpretability and Control**: The ability to identify and specifically influence CDPs could open avenues for better understanding and control over the reasoning process of LLMs, moving beyond black-box optimization.

#### 5.3. Future Work

The authors propose further research directions, including addressing the gradient vanishing problem when intra-group advantages exhibit near-zero variance and developing smoother, more fine-grained normalization approaches that adapt to the dynamic evolution of reasoning chains. These efforts could further enhance the robustness and adaptability of entropy-regulated policy optimization.
