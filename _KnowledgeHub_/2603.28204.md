# ERPO: Token-Level Entropy-Regulated Policy Optimization for Large Reasoning Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Entropy-Regulated Policy Optimization (ERPO) introduces a token-level credit assignment mechanism for large reasoning models, utilizing policy entropy and implicit progress signals. This approach enhances reasoning accuracy and generates more concise solution paths, allowing smaller LLMs to achieve performance competitive with or superior to much larger models on complex mathematical benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning methods for LLMs, such as GRPO, assign uniform sequence-level rewards, failing to account for the varying informational value of individual tokens in a reasoning chain.
- This coarse-grained credit assignment can lead to premature entropy collapse, suppressing necessary exploration at critical decision points, and inefficiency from redundant derivations in deterministic steps.
- There was a lack of mechanisms to explicitly identify "Critical Decision Pivots" (CDPs) in reasoning and adaptively adjust exploration efforts at these crucial junctures.

## Method

- **ERPO** integrates ==token-level policy entropy== to identify ==critical decision pivots== and an ==implicit progress signal==, providing fine-grained feedback without extra reward training.
- An ==adaptive gating function== dynamically adjusts reward density based on local predictive uncertainty, concentrating gradient signals on high-entropy logical junctions.
- The framework uses ==relative position bucketing== and ==outcome-driven anchors== to normalize implicit advantages and prevent ==reward hacking==, ensuring process-level feedback aligns with final correct outcomes.

## Results

- **ERPO** consistently outperformed GRPO and SFT baselines in reasoning accuracy across various Qwen2.5 model scales and mathematical benchmarks, with Qwen2.5-7B ERPO achieving **20.70%** average accuracy versus GRPO's **19.43%**.
- The Qwen2.5-7B **ERPO** model surpassed the performance of significantly larger commercial models like DeepSeek-R1-671B (**17.48%** accuracy) on average, demonstrating improved reasoning capability with fewer parameters.
- **ERPO** models generated significantly shorter reasoning sequences while achieving higher accuracy, indicating improved inference efficiency and conciseness by effectively suppressing redundancy in derivations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reasoning processes in LLMs are characterized by heterogeneous token-level uncertainty, with "Critical Decision Pivots" exhibiting high entropy requiring adaptive exploration.
> - Preventing premature entropy collapse is vital for robust learning and sustained exploration, allowing models to develop more diverse and generalizable reasoning strategies.
> - Fine-grained, token-level credit assignment mechanisms can significantly boost reasoning accuracy and efficiency, enabling smaller language models to surpass the performance of much larger models on complex tasks.
