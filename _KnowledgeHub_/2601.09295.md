# MACRO-LLM: LLM-Empowered Multi-Agent Collaborative Reasoning under Spatiotemporal Partial Observability

> [!summary] Summary
> The University of Hong Kong researchers developed MACRO-LLM, an LLM-empowered multi-agent framework designed for collaborative reasoning in environments with spatiotemporal partial observability. This system leverages decentralized negotiation, combining semantic reasoning with statistical spatial features, to achieve superior coordination, scalability, and robustness in tasks like cooperative adaptive cruise control and pandemic containment.

## Problem

- Deploying LLM agents in complex real-world scenarios inherently leads to spatiotemporal partial observability due to physical dispersion.
- Spatial partial observability limits agents' local perception, hindering their ability to form a full global understanding.
- Temporal partial observability arises from LLMs' fixed context windows, causing uncertainty about future dynamics and limited historical context for aligning long-term objectives.

## Method

- MACRO-LLM employs a ==decentralized negotiation framework==, where each agent uses three modules: ==CoProposer==, Negotiator, and Introspector.
- The CoProposer generates and verifies action proposals by integrating long-term objectives with immediate spatial constraints and mitigating future uncertainty via ==k-step rollout simulations==.
- The Negotiator resolves conflicting proposals and updates spatial strategies by exchanging aggregated ==mean-field approximation== features with neighbors, followed by proposal regeneration and multi-round negotiation.

## Results

- MACRO-LLM achieved superior performance in Cooperative Adaptive Cruise Control (CACC), reducing the Standard Deviation of Headway by **86.3%** and RMSE of Headway by **24.4%** compared to existing baselines.
- In Pandemic Control tasks, the framework consistently attained lower normalized infection rates, fewer peak infections, and significantly shorter pandemic durations, reducing cumulative infections by over **99.2%** in complex scenarios.
- The system demonstrated robust scalability with increasing agent numbers (up to **32**) and effective generalization across different LLM backbones, with smaller models performing comparably to larger ones in strategic tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly decomposing coordination challenges into spatial and temporal dimensions, managed by specialized modules, enhances multi-agent performance under partial observability.
> - Combining LLM's semantic reasoning with statistical mean-field approximations enables scalable, decentralized neighborhood negotiation without relying on central coordination.
> - An adaptive learning mechanism, inspired by semantic gradient descent, allows agents to continuously refine their temporal strategies based on historical performance for long-term consistency.
