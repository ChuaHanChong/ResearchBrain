# MACRO-LLM: LLM-Empowered Multi-Agent Collaborative Reasoning under Spatiotemporal Partial Observability

> [!summary] Summary
> The University of Hong Kong researchers developed MACRO-LLM, an LLM-empowered multi-agent framework designed for collaborative reasoning in environments with spatiotemporal partial observability. This system leverages decentralized negotiation, combining semantic reasoning with statistical spatial features, to achieve superior coordination, scalability, and robustness in tasks like cooperative adaptive cruise control and pandemic containment.

## Problem

- Deploying LLM agents in complex real-world scenarios inherently leads to spatiotemporal partial observability due to physical dispersion.
- Spatial partial observability limits agents' local perception, hindering their ability to form a full global understanding.
- Temporal partial observability arises from LLMs' fixed context windows, causing uncertainty about future dynamics and limited historical context for aligning long-term objectives.

## Method

- MACRO-LLM employs a ==decentralized negotiation framework==, where each agent uses three modules: ==CoProposer==, Negotiator, and Introspector.
- The CoProposer generates and verifies action proposals by integrating long-term objectives with immediate spatial constraints and mitigating future uncertainty via ==k-step rollout simulations==.
- The Negotiator resolves conflicting proposals and updates spatial strategies by exchanging aggregated ==mean-field approximation== features with neighbors, followed by proposal regeneration and multi-round negotiation.

## Results

- MACRO-LLM achieved superior performance in Cooperative Adaptive Cruise Control (CACC), reducing the Standard Deviation of Headway by **86.3%** and RMSE of Headway by **24.4%** compared to existing baselines.
- In Pandemic Control tasks, the framework consistently attained lower normalized infection rates, fewer peak infections, and significantly shorter pandemic durations, reducing cumulative infections by over **99.2%** in complex scenarios.
- The system demonstrated robust scalability with increasing agent numbers (up to **32**) and effective generalization across different LLM backbones, with smaller models performing comparably to larger ones in strategic tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly decomposing coordination challenges into spatial and temporal dimensions, managed by specialized modules, enhances multi-agent performance under partial observability.
> - Combining LLM's semantic reasoning with statistical mean-field approximations enables scalable, decentralized neighborhood negotiation without relying on central coordination.
> - An adaptive learning mechanism, inspired by semantic gradient descent, allows agents to continuously refine their temporal strategies based on historical performance for long-term consistency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses challenges within the rapidly developing field of Large Language Model (LLM)-based Multi-Agent Systems (MAS). Existing LLM agents have demonstrated capabilities in problem-solving and task automation, but single agents often encounter scalability and complexity issues in long-horizon scenarios. The research landscape has thus shifted towards exploring multi-agent LLM systems that aim to leverage collective intelligence through coordination and negotiation.

However, a significant limitation in most current LLM-based MAS frameworks is the assumption of shared global context or unrestricted communication among agents. As LLM agents are increasingly deployed in real-world, physical scenarios, they operate as distributed entities. This physical dispersion inherently introduces spatiotemporal partial observability, where agents have limited local perception and finite temporal horizons due to fixed context windows and future uncertainty.

This paper positions itself to bridge the gap between advanced LLM agent capabilities and the constraints of real-world distributed environments. It contrasts with:
- **Existing LLM-based MAS:** These often rely on hierarchical structures (aggregating information to a central node) or fully-connected graphs (constructing a pseudo-global view), which introduce communication bottlenecks and limit scalability in dynamic, large-scale environments. Some approaches enhance individual reasoning (Theory-of-Mind, structured reasoning chains) but may struggle with the coupled spatiotemporal uncertainty. Swarm-based optimization methods typically operate offline and lack runtime coordination mechanisms.
- **Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL):** Traditional MARL frameworks, such as Dec-POMDP, address partial observability but are computationally intensive, suffer from poor generalization, and require extensive retraining for new scenarios or agent numbers. MACRO-LLM aims to offer zero-shot generalization by leveraging LLMs for inference-time reasoning without parameter updates.

By explicitly characterizing and jointly addressing the coupled nature of spatial and temporal partial observability, MACRO-LLM seeks to enable robust, decentralized coordination in complex, distributed LLM agent environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework, MACRO-LLM, that enables LLM-empowered multi-agent collaborative reasoning under conditions of spatiotemporal partial observability. This objective is motivated by several key challenges inherent in deploying LLM agents in real-world, physically distributed scenarios:

1. **Addressing Spatiotemporal Partial Observability:** Agents operating in physical environments are constrained by limited local perception (spatial partial observability) and finite temporal horizons (temporal partial observability). These limitations prevent agents from accessing the full context of a collective task or predicting long-term consequences of actions.
2. **Recognizing the Coupling of Spatial and Temporal Constraints:** The paper highlights a core insight: spatial and temporal partial observability are fundamentally intertwined. Resolving spatial conflicts often necessitates reasoning about neighbors' future actions, which is a temporal challenge. Conversely, effective temporal planning requires spatial context that extends beyond an agent's immediate local perception. Existing approaches are noted to address these dimensions in isolation.
3. **Enabling Decentralized Coordination:** The motivation includes moving beyond centralized or globally-informed multi-agent systems. Many current LLM-MAS assume either a central coordinator or unrestricted communication, which become bottlenecks in scalable, dynamically evolving, and bandwidth-constrained real-world environments. The goal is to achieve robust coordination without reliance on central nodes or global views.
4. **Enhancing Generalizability and Reducing Training Costs:** Unlike MARL approaches that require extensive retraining for different environments or configurations, the objective is to leverage LLMs to achieve zero-shot generalization across diverse domains and scenarios, thereby reducing computational costs and increasing applicability in dynamic settings.
5. **Improving Collaborative Efficiency and Effectiveness:** The overarching motivation is to develop a framework that allows agents to align immediate local actions with long-term collective goals, leading to more efficient and effective collaborative outcomes in complex, long-horizon tasks. This includes mitigating suboptimal or conflicting actions that arise from partial observability.

To achieve these objectives, MACRO-LLM proposes a negotiation-centric architecture where spatial and temporal reasoning are interleaved within each decision cycle, employing three interdependent modules to overcome the identified limitations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MACRO-LLM is structured as a decentralized negotiation framework designed for multi-agent systems (MAS) operating under spatiotemporal partial observability. The system models MAS as a peer-to-peer graph without a central coordinator, where each agent interacts with its defined neighborhood. The architecture comprises three interdependent modules: the CoProposer, the Negotiator, and the Introspector, which interleave spatial and temporal reasoning within each decision cycle.

**A. CoProposer: Collaborative Proposal Generation**
The CoProposer's function is to generate collaborative action proposals for an agent and its observable neighbors, integrating long-term objectives with immediate spatial constraints.

- **Temporal Strategy Formulation:** Each agent develops a high-level temporal strategy based on its current observation, action space, and global objectives. This strategy, derived via LLM inference, guides the agent's step-by-step progression towards its goal.
- **Spatial Strategy Formulation:** The agent infers potential cascading effects from unobservable agents by analyzing its local observation and the network topology. This informs a spatial strategy.
- **Proposal Generation via Rollout-Simulated Verification:**
 - An initial proposal is generated, consisting of an action for the agent itself and inferred cooperative actions for its observable neighbors.
 - This proposal undergoes validation through *rollout-simulated verification*, an LLM-based multi-step projection from time `t` to `t+k`. The LLM predicts future states and actions, forming a trajectory.
 - To evaluate the rollout, a progressive constraint relaxation mechanism is used: immediate actions (`t`) must strictly satisfy all constraints, while subsequent steps (`t+1` to `t+k`) apply relaxed viability constraints due to increasing uncertainty.
 - The cumulative reward `R(τ_n)` for the trajectory is estimated. If a proposal violates constraints, the agent revises its action and re-attempts verification (up to `Att_max` times) until a valid proposal with the highest `R(τ_n)` is found.

**B. Negotiator: Conflict Resolution and Spatial Strategy Updates**
The Negotiator handles conflicts arising from concurrently generated proposals and updates spatial strategies, extending agents' perception beyond observable limits.

- **Neighborhood State Aggregation via Mean-Field Approximation:** To address bandwidth constraints and limited perception, agents transmit aggregated statistical features (weighted mean `μ_n` and weighted variance `σ^2_n`) instead of raw observations. These statistics approximate the influence of the neighborhood. The variance `σ^2_n` serves as an error indicator, with a proven bound (`R_n(s^t_Nn) - R_n(μ_n) ≤ L^2 / (2w_min) * σ^2_n`), where `L^2` is a smoothness constant. A topology-aware partitioning method is introduced to compute separate statistics for disjoint subgroups, yielding a tighter error bound.
- **Proposal Confidence Assessment and Regeneration:** An agent evaluates incoming neighbor proposals by calculating pairwise differences. If local consensus (`|P^(q)_n - P^(q)_m| < δ`) is not met, the agent analyzes discrepancies, updates its spatial strategy using received mean-field states, and integrates weighted neighbor proposals to generate a refined candidate. This new candidate then undergoes rollout verification by the CoProposer.
- **Multi-Round Negotiation:** The CoProposer and Negotiator form a negotiation loop that can iterate for `r` rounds. Each round extends effective perception by one communication hop. The process terminates early if local consensus is reached or after `r` rounds. A key property is that proposal quality is monotonically non-decreasing across rounds (`R(τ^(ℓ+1)_n) ≥ R(τ^(ℓ)_n)`).

**C. Introspector: Temporal Strategy Refinement**
The Introspector facilitates continuous adaptation by refining strategies based on environmental drift and performance changes.

- **Revision Intensity Estimation:** If an agent observes a reward decline, it quantifies environmental shifts by computing the cosine similarity between consecutive transition vectors (`Γ^t_n = (Δõ^t_n, ã^t_n)`). Higher drift intensity (`λ_drift = 1 - (Γ^(t-1)_n ⋅ Γ^t_n) / (||Γ^(t-1)_n|| ⋅ ||Γ^t_n||)`) prompts more aggressive strategy revisions.
- **Revision Direction Identification:** The LLM analyzes observation logs and negotiation records to identify deviations from the temporal strategy and assess neighbor impacts. It generates a `semantic revision signal`—natural language rules characterizing action-state-reward associations for diagnostic guidance.
- **Strategy Update:** The temporal strategy is updated using the revision signal and modulated by the drift intensity. A high `λ_drift` leads to aggressive revisions, while a low `λ_drift` confines adjustments to fine-grained refinements, preserving the core strategy.

The three modules are designed to form a closed reasoning loop: the CoProposer generates and verifies proposals, which are then evaluated and refined by the Negotiator based on mean-field insights. The Introspector monitors performance, attributes changes, and updates strategies, feeding back into the CoProposer for subsequent decision cycles.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of MACRO-LLM was conducted on two distinct, complex long-horizon tasks: Cooperative Platoon Planning (CPP) and Pandemic Control (PC). Experiments utilized six different LLM backbones (GPT-4o, DeepSeek-V3.1, Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct, etc.) and benchmarked against both LLM-based MAS and MARL baselines.

**A. Performance on Cooperative Platoon Planning (CPP) Task:**
- **Superior Coordination:** MACRO-LLM consistently outperformed both MARL (DPPO, DMPO, IC3Net) and LLM-based MAS (ToM-Belief, ChatEval, LAMEN) baselines across "Catch-up" and "Slow-down" scenarios (Table 1).
- **Stability Metrics:** In the Catch-up scenario, MACRO-LLM variants achieved lower RMSE and Standard Deviation (SD) for both Headway (H) and Velocity (V). For instance, MACRO-GPT's SD-H (0.409 ± 0.034) was 87.7% lower than LAMEN and 58.9% lower than DPPO.
- **Headway Safety:** In the Slow-down scenario, MACRO-LLM prioritized headway safety, with MACRO-GPT achieving the lowest SD-H (0.128 ± 0.054), a 43.1% improvement over ToM-Belief, while maintaining competitive velocity errors.
- **Backbone Independence:** The consistent advantage across multiple LLM backbones indicated that performance gains originated from the framework's negotiation mechanism rather than specific model capabilities.

**B. Performance on Pandemic Control (PC) Task:**
- **Effective Containment:** MACRO-LLM consistently achieved lower normalized infection rates (I_n, PI_n, D_n) and shorter pandemic durations (PD) across Helsinki, Hong Kong, and New York urban topologies (Table 2).
- **Significant Reductions:** In the New York topology, MACRO-LLM variants reduced cumulative infections by over 99% compared to DPPO and shortened pandemic duration from 95 days to as few as 11 days (MACRO-DS).
- **Simultaneous Optimization:** The framework demonstrated the ability to achieve both low infections and short durations concurrently, indicating effective strategic planning and coordination.

**C. Scalability Analysis:**
- **Robustness with Increased Agents:** On the CPP Catch-up scenario, MACRO-LLM maintained consistent robustness as the fleet size increased from N=8 to N=32 (Fig. 3, Table 6). Oscillation amplitudes remained bounded, and agents converged to target equilibrium within 40 seconds regardless of fleet size.
- **Efficiency in Large Fleets:** RMSE-H decreased with increasing N (from 1.237 for N=8 to 0.728 for N=32), suggesting that stabilized agents diluted transient deviations. SD-H increased moderately but remained significantly lower than baselines (e.g., 56.4% lower than DPPO at N=32), confirming MACRO-LLM's ability to manage large-scale interactions effectively.

**D. Ablation Study:**
- **Module Interdependence:** Ablating individual modules drastically degraded performance (Figs. 4, 5).
 - **CoProposer:** Its removal led to the largest degradation (9.1x higher RMSE-H, 18.3x higher SD-H in CPP; 2.5x longer pandemic duration in PC), indicating the necessity of verified initial proposals.
 - **Negotiator:** Its absence resulted in a 6.1x higher RMSE-H in CPP and a 32-day pandemic duration in PC, demonstrating its role in resolving conflicts and reaching consensus.
 - **Introspector:** Without it, agents sacrificed headway stability for velocity optimization (1.9x degradation in RMSE-H in CPP) and experienced 44.4% longer pandemic durations in PC, highlighting its importance for suppressing oscillations and adapting strategies.
- **Cross-Module Dependencies:** Ablations revealed that removing the CoProposer also impacted the Negotiator's confidence assessment, underscoring the architectural interdependence.

**E. Model Generalizability (Appendix K):**
MACRO-LLM exhibited generalizability across various LLM backbones. Performance variations were observed, particularly in CPP where fine-grained continuous control demands highlighted differences in LLM numerical precision. However, even lightweight models like Qwen3-flash and GPT-4o-mini achieved competitive results in PC, suggesting the structured negotiation mechanism enables complex coordination regardless of LLM scale.

**F. Robustness (Appendix N):**
Analysis over 5 independent runs showed low variance across all metrics for the CPP task, confirming consistent superior performance. For the PC task, infection-related metrics were tightly concentrated, while pandemic duration showed slightly higher variance in complex topologies, attributed to larger state spaces and stochastic dynamics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MACRO-LLM framework presents several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of multi-agent systems, particularly those powered by large language models:

1. **Enabling Robust Coordination under Spatiotemporal Partial Observability:** This work provides a method for LLM agents to operate effectively in distributed environments where information is inherently fragmented. By explicitly addressing the coupled challenges of limited local perception (spatial) and future uncertainty (temporal), MACRO-LLM facilitates coordination in complex, real-world scenarios such as autonomous platooning and pandemic management. This capability expands the practical applicability of LLM agents beyond idealized, globally observable settings.
2. **Novel Integration of Semantic Reasoning and Statistical Features:** The framework innovatively combines LLM-based semantic reasoning (for proposal generation, strategy formulation, and introspective diagnosis) with mean-field statistical approximations (for aggregating unobservable neighbor states). This hybrid approach allows agents to overcome spatial myopia efficiently by compressing large-scale interactions into manageable statistical features, without incurring prohibitive communication overheads or requiring raw global data.
3. **Decentralized and Scalable Architecture:** MACRO-LLM offers a fully decentralized negotiation framework, eliminating the need for central coordinators or global views that often become bottlenecks in scalable MAS. The communication complexity per agent remains largely invariant to the total number of agents, demonstrating potential for scalability in large and dynamic networks. This decentralized design is crucial for applications where central control is impractical or undesirable due to privacy, security, or latency constraints.
4. **Enhanced Generalization and Reduced Training Costs:** Unlike traditional MARL methods that require extensive, task-specific training, MACRO-LLM leverages the zero-shot generalization capabilities of LLMs. This allows for rapid adaptation to new scenarios and topologies without costly retraining, offering a practical advantage for dynamic, open-world deployments.
5. **Interdependent Reasoning Loop for Continuous Adaptation:** The architecture's closed reasoning loop, involving the CoProposer, Negotiator, and Introspector, facilitates continuous learning and adaptation. The Introspector's scenario-aware adaptive reflection allows agents to diagnose performance changes, attribute causes, and refine strategies based on environmental drift, ensuring long-term robustness and performance stability.
6. **Potential for Hierarchical Deployment:** The strategic nature of MACRO-LLM's outputs lends itself to hierarchical deployment, where it can provide high-level directives to lightweight, domain-specific execution layers. This approach can mitigate concerns about inference latency in safety-critical domains by reducing the frequency of LLM invocations while maintaining superior coordination quality compared to baselines.

In summary, MACRO-LLM represents a step towards scaling LLM-based multi-agent systems into complex, real-world applications by providing a principled framework for decentralized collaborative reasoning under inherent spatiotemporal partial observability. The reported performance gains across diverse tasks and LLM backbones indicate its potential to advance the field of collective AI.
