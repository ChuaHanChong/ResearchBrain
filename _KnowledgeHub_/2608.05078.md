# SpikingNav: Robust Embodied Navigation with Spiking Neural Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> The SpikingNav framework integrates Spiking Neural Networks into both sensing and policy modules for embodied navigation, achieving competitive performance on PointNav and outperforming ANN-based navigators on ObjectNav, while demonstrating a 40.18% robustness retention ratio under visual corruptions with significantly fewer FLOPs.

## Problem

- Existing Artificial Neural Network (ANN)-based embodied navigation systems incur high computational costs, limiting deployment on resource-constrained platforms.
- ANN navigators show significant performance degradation when exposed to common visual corruptions, such as blur or noise, compromising reliability in real-world scenarios.
- The effectiveness and robustness of Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) in complex, visually rich embodied navigation tasks within standard reinforcement learning frameworks remained largely unexplored.

## Method

- Developed **SpikingNav**, an SNN-based framework for embodied navigation, integrating spike-based sensing and policy networks into an actor-critic Reinforcement Learning pipeline.
- The framework includes a ==Spiking Sensing Encoder (SSE)== for visual feature and goal information extraction and a ==Spiking Policy Network (SPN)== with Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) neurons for recurrent policy state maintenance.
- Employed ==surrogate gradient== methods for end-to-end training of SNN components within a ==Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)==-style objective, allowing optimization despite non-differentiable spike generation.

## Results

- SpikingNav achieved competitive performance, outperforming the ANN baseline on ObjectNav (**34.12%** SR vs. **31.05%** SR) while using significantly fewer parameters (**12.1M** vs. 14M) and FLOPs (**0.97G** vs. 4.21G).
- The framework demonstrated superior robustness against visual corruptions, with SpikingNav's average ObjectNav success rate under corruption increasing to **13.71%**, compared to ANNNav's **8.45%**.
- The Spiking Sensing Encoder (SSE) was successfully deployed on a Thruster–V2 neuromorphic chip, validating hardware feasibility and consuming **3.92 ± 0.14 J** of dynamic energy per sample.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robustness in dynamic embodied navigation for SNNs arises from the integrated, closed-loop interaction of spike-based perception and policy dynamics, not solely superior static feature extraction.
> - The Spiking Policy Network's membrane-based temporal regulation effectively absorbs transient sensory perturbations, maintaining a stable policy state for sequential decision-making.
> - SNNs provide a balance between competitive navigation performance and reduced computational overhead, offering efficiency suitable for deployment on compact, resource-constrained platforms.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied navigation, a critical capability for autonomous agents in cyber-physical systems, requires agents to perceive, reason, and act continuously in dynamic environments. Recent advancements in this field have largely been driven by Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs), particularly through strong visual backbones and reinforcement learning (RL) frameworks. These ANN-based models have demonstrated high performance on tasks like PointNav and ObjectNav, but often at the cost of substantial computational resources and a susceptibility to visual corruptions such as blur or noise. These limitations impede their deployment on resource-constrained platforms or in real-world scenarios prone to sensory disturbances.

Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) represent a third generation of neural models, characterized by event-driven computation and intrinsic temporal dynamics. These properties suggest SNNs could offer advantages in efficiency and robustness for tasks requiring continuous decision-making, such as embodied navigation. Prior research into SNNs has shown promise in various areas, including architectural innovations and training methods that aim to close the performance gap with ANNs in frame-level perception tasks. SNNs have also been explored for robustness and energy efficiency in static vision tasks, and in simpler navigation settings, such as mapless control or using low-dimensional sensory inputs like lidar. However, their effectiveness in complex, visually rich embodied navigation, particularly concerning their ability to maintain performance and robustness against visual corruptions while operating within standard reinforcement learning frameworks, has remained an open question.

This work contributes to the landscape by investigating the direct application of SNNs to the core components of a visual navigation policy—from sensory encoding to recurrent policy dynamics—within challenging indoor environments. It aims to bridge the gap between the theoretical advantages of SNNs and their practical utility in complex sequential decision-making tasks relevant to cyber-physical systems. By demonstrating competitive performance, enhanced robustness, and hardware deployability for compact models, this research positions SNNs as a viable and advantageous alternative to ANNs for energy-efficient and disturbance-tolerant embodied agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to propose and evaluate SpikingNav, a spiking framework designed for robust indoor embodied navigation. This framework aims to leverage the inherent properties of Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) to overcome critical limitations observed in conventional Artificial Neural Network (ANN)-based navigation systems.

The key motivations driving this work stem from two main areas: the practical challenges of existing ANN-based solutions and the unexplored potential of SNNs in complex embodied AI tasks.

**Challenges with Existing ANN-based Navigation Models:**
1. **High Computational Cost:** While ANNs have achieved strong performance in embodied navigation, they typically rely on dense feature extraction and computationally intensive policy networks. This computational overhead makes them unsuitable for deployment on compact, resource-constrained embodied platforms, which are often a requirement for real-world cyber-physical systems.
2. **Lack of Robustness to Visual Corruptions:** Embodied agents operating in physical environments are frequently exposed to sensor noise and visual disturbances, such as blur, noise, or lighting variations. ANN-based models often exhibit significant performance degradation under these visual corruptions, limiting their reliability and safety in realistic deployment scenarios. Improving robustness under such perturbations is therefore a critical practical concern.
3. **Limitations in Temporal Information Integration:** Navigation is a sequential decision-making process that requires effective integration of continuous sensory streams and maintenance of a consistent internal state. While ANNs use recurrent mechanisms, the potential for SNNs to offer intrinsically robust temporal dynamics, particularly against transient fluctuations, remains to be fully explored in this context.

**Potential of Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs):**
1. **Energy Efficiency and Compactness:** SNNs inherently operate with event-driven, sparse computations, leading to potential for higher energy efficiency and compact model sizes compared to dense ANN computations. This aligns with the requirements of resource-constrained edge devices for embodied agents.
2. **Intrinsic Temporal Dynamics:** The membrane potential integration, thresholding, and spike-triggered reset mechanisms of spiking neurons naturally support temporal information processing. This dynamic behavior could be advantageous for sequential decision-making tasks like navigation, where consistent state estimation and action generation are paramount. The hypothesis is that these dynamics can help suppress transient visual fluctuations, preventing them from propagating into the policy state and thereby enhancing robustness.
3. **Unclear Robustness for Complex Navigation:** Despite recent progress in SNNs, most empirical evidence for their robustness is confined to static, frame-level perception tasks. It is unclear whether these advantages translate consistently to complex embodied navigation, which involves semantic discrimination, stable temporal information integration, and action generation in closed-loop systems.

**Specific Objectives:**
- To propose a spiking navigation framework (SpikingNav) that integrates SNNs not only into perceptual encoding but also into the policy core, making it suitable for compact physical navigation systems.
- To evaluate whether SpikingNav can achieve competitive navigation performance compared to ANN-based navigators of similar model scale under clean observations.
- To determine if SpikingNav exhibits stronger robustness and degrades more gracefully under various visual corruptions than its ANN counterparts.
- To investigate the hardware deployability of the spike-based sensing method on a real neuromorphic processor, providing a physical validation for cyber-physical system integration.

By addressing these motivations and objectives, the research aims to demonstrate the practical viability and advantages of SNNs for building compact, robust, and efficient embodied navigation policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SpikingNav is designed as a minimally disruptive framework integrated into a standard actor-critic Reinforcement Learning (RL) pipeline. It comprises two main spike-based components: the Spiking Sensing Encoder (SSE) and the Spiking Policy Network (SPN).

**A. Spiking Sensing Encoder (SSE)**
The SSE is responsible for extracting task-conditioned visual features from sensor inputs and goal information. It consists of three sub-components:
1. **Visual Backbone:** A spike-based ResNet18-style network processes the current RGB or RGB-D observation (x_t). This backbone operates over T=4 internal spiking steps to produce a visual representation (z_t). Following the visual backbone, a feature compressor with two convolutional layers reduces the feature dimensions.
2. **Target Backbone:** This component encodes the task-specific goal input (g_t) into a target representation (e_t). For ObjectNav, g_t is an object token, mapped to a learnable feature. For PointNav, g_t is a continuous goal descriptor transformed by a goal encoder. The target vector is then spatially expanded to match the dimensions of the visual feature map (q_t).
3. **Fusion Backbone:** A lightweight module consisting of two convolutional layers processes the concatenation of the visual feature (z_t) and the spatially expanded target feature (q_t) along the channel dimension. A subsequent flatten operation converts this fused representation into a compact sensing feature (r_t), which serves as the input to the SPN.

**B. Spiking Policy Network (SPN)**
The SPN forms the core of the navigation policy, maintaining a recurrent policy state through spike-driven temporal dynamics and generating actor-critic outputs.
1. **Spiking Recurrent Core:** The SPN uses Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) neuron dynamics. At each time step t, the membrane potential (v_t) integrates the current sensing feature (r_t) (transformed into a sensory current c_sens_t), the previous membrane state (u_{t-1}) (transformed into a recurrent current c_mem_t), and a leak factor (λ) that dictates memory decay.
2. **Spike Generation and Reset:** If the accumulated membrane potential (v_t) crosses a predefined firing threshold (ϑ), the neuron emits a binary spike (s_t = 1) via a Heaviside step function. Following a spike, the membrane potential is reset, typically to zero or a resting potential (u_t = v_t ⊙ (1 - s_t)). This spike-triggered reset mechanism enables event-driven memory regulation: subthreshold information is retained, while threshold-crossing activations are selectively cleared.
3. **Policy State and Outputs:** The post-reset membrane state (u_t) is used as the recurrent policy state (h_t). This state is then fed into two separate fully connected layers to produce the action distribution (π) for the actor and the scalar value estimate (V_t) for the critic, preserving the standard actor-critic RL interface.

**C. Training Method**
SpikingNav is trained within the AllenAct framework, employing a Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)-style actor-critic objective. This objective optimizes a combined loss function that includes a clipped surrogate policy loss, a mean-squared error value loss, and an entropy regularizer to encourage exploration. Due to the non-differentiable nature of spike generation, surrogate gradient methods are utilized during backpropagation to enable end-to-end training of the SNN components.

**D. Temporal Robustness Perspective**
The paper provides a theoretical analysis of the local temporal stability of the SPN dynamics. It introduces concepts like the "accumulated perturbation budget" (B_τ,j) and the "spike margin" (¯m_t). The analysis posits that if the accumulated perturbation in sensory features remains below the spike margin (i.e., the minimum distance to the firing threshold), the spike pattern of the recurrent policy core is preserved. This "spike-pattern preservation" directly implies a bounded variation in the recurrent policy state and consequently in the actor logits, thus offering a theoretical basis for the observed robustness.

**E. Experimental Setup**
- **Environment and Tasks:** Experiments are conducted in RoboTHOR, a high-fidelity simulation environment, on both PointNav (goal-directed navigation to a point) and ObjectNav (navigation to an instance of a target object category) tasks.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Success Rate (SR) and Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) are used to assess performance, both under clean and perturbed conditions.
- **Robustness Benchmarking:** The robustness is specifically evaluated against various visual corruptions from the RobustNav benchmark, including low lighting, motion blur, camera crack, defocus blur, speckle noise, reduced field of view (lower FOV), and spatter.
- **Baselines:** A re-implemented ANN baseline, termed ANNNav, is used for direct comparison, ensuring matched data processing, training protocols, and evaluation settings. Comparisons are also made with other published SNN and ANN navigation methods.
- **Ablation Study:** Controlled experiments are performed by replacing individual ANN modules with their spiking counterparts (SSE-only, SPN-only) to isolate the contributions of each spiking component to overall performance and robustness.

**F. Neuromorphic Hardware Deployment**
To validate practical deployability, the SSE component of SpikingNav is mapped and executed on the Thruster–V2 neuromorphic chip. This step involves measuring on-chip execution characteristics such as cycle counts, dynamic current, and dynamic energy consumption, providing a hardware-level assessment of spike-based visual perception and target fusion.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on SpikingNav demonstrate its performance, efficiency, robustness, and hardware deployability, particularly in comparison to ANN-based counterparts.

**A. Navigation Performance, Model Scale, and Efficiency**
- **Model Size and Computational Cost:** SpikingNav operates with fewer parameters (12.1M) and significantly lower per-step FLOPs (0.97G) compared to the matched ANNNav baseline (14M parameters, 4.21G FLOPs). This indicates a substantial reduction in computational overhead.
- **Clean Performance on PointNav:** SpikingNav achieves competitive performance on PointNav with a Success Rate (SR) of 96.54% and Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) of 72.93%, nearing the ANNNav's performance of 98.21% SR and 82.13% SPL.
- **Clean Performance on ObjectNav:** For the more challenging ObjectNav task, SpikingNav outperforms ANNNav, achieving 34.12% SR and 16.20% SPL, compared to ANNNav's 31.05% SR and 14.26% SPL. This suggests that SpikingNav offers a more favorable solution for achieving strong embodied navigation performance with compact models.

**B. Navigation Robustness**
- **PointNav Robustness:** Under various visual corruptions, SpikingNav demonstrates stronger robustness than ANNNav. While ANNNav might achieve higher clean performance, its performance degrades more severely under perturbations. SpikingNav generally maintains a higher SR and SPL across several corruption types.
- **ObjectNav Robustness:** SpikingNav maintains its performance advantage over ANNNav in ObjectNav under most visual corruptions. Notably, it shows clear gains under motion blur, camera crack, and defocus blur. For instance, the average corrupted ObjectNav success rate for SpikingNav rises to 13.71%, a substantial improvement over ANNNav's 8.45%. Only under lower FOV does ANNNav perform slightly better. This indicates that the spike-based policy degrades more gracefully under visual disturbances.

**C. Ablation Study**
The ablation experiments on ObjectNav clarify the individual contributions of the Spiking Sensing Encoder (SSE) and Spiking Policy Network (SPN):
- **SSE-only Variant:** Replacing only the ANN perception module with SSE (while keeping an ANN policy) leads to improved clean SPL (15.30% vs. 14.26%) and average robustness (9.58% SR, 4.74% SPL) under visual corruptions compared to ANNNav. This suggests SSE enhances the stability of visual feature extraction.
- **SPN-only Variant:** Replacing only the ANN policy module with SPN (while keeping an ANN perception) maintains similar clean SR (31.00% vs. 31.05%) but significantly improves average robustness (10.20% SR, 4.25% SPL) under corruptions. This indicates that the SPN's recurrent dynamics contribute to policy robustness even with an ANN visual front-end, though a slight decrease in clean SPL is observed.
- **Full SpikingNav:** The combination of both SSE and SPN yields the best overall performance, achieving the highest clean SR (34.12%) and significantly superior average robustness (13.71% SR, 5.67% SPL) under corruptions. This suggests a complementary interaction between the two spiking components.

**D. Neuromorphic Hardware Deployment**
- The Spiking Sensing Encoder (SSE) was successfully mapped and executed on the Thruster–V2 neuromorphic chip.
- On-chip execution statistics revealed that the deployed SSE required (2.133 ± 0.048) × 10^9 cycles per sample and consumed 3.92 ± 0.14 J of dynamic energy per sample.
- This hardware validation confirms the feasibility of instantiating SpikingNav's sensing component on a physical neuromorphic substrate, demonstrating a crucial step towards deployment in real cyber-physical systems.

**E. Analysis and Discussion (Discrepancy with Static Recognition)**
A key finding is the distinct behavior of SNNs in embodied navigation compared to static image recognition. While the SNN visual encoder alone showed slightly lower robustness on ImageNet-C (44.82% retention ratio) than its ANN counterpart (49.33% retention ratio), the full SpikingNav system exhibited a much higher robustness retention ratio for ObjectNav SR (40.18%) compared to ANNNav (27.21%). This indicates that the robustness gain in SpikingNav is not merely from superior static feature extraction but emerges from the integrated, closed-loop interaction of spike-based perception and policy dynamics. The SPN's membrane-based temporal regulation (accumulation, gating, reset) helps in absorbing transient sensory perturbations, maintaining a stable policy state for sequential decision-making.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SpikingNav framework offers several contributions and potential impacts on embodied AI and cyber-physical systems:

**A. Advancing Embodied AI with SNNs:** This research demonstrates the viability and advantages of integrating Spiking Neural Networks into complex embodied navigation tasks. By achieving competitive clean-task performance and superior robustness with reduced model parameters and computational costs, SpikingNav presents a promising alternative to conventional ANN-based approaches, particularly for resource-constrained agents. This represents a significant step beyond previous SNN studies, which primarily focused on simpler perception or control tasks.

**B. Enhanced Robustness for Real-World Deployment:** The demonstrated robustness of SpikingNav under various visual corruptions is highly significant for the practical deployment of embodied agents in uncontrolled, dynamic, and potentially degraded physical environments. Agents must operate reliably despite sensor noise, adverse lighting, or visual occlusions. SpikingNav's ability to maintain a higher success rate under such disturbances directly contributes to the safety, reliability, and utility of autonomous robots and cyber-physical systems.

**C. Efficiency for Resource-Constrained Platforms:** The reduced parameter count and per-step FLOPs of SpikingNav address a critical limitation of modern ANN-based systems: their computational intensity. This efficiency makes SpikingNav more suitable for deployment on edge devices, such as small mobile robots or drones, where computational power and energy budgets are strictly limited. This could enable more widespread adoption of intelligent agents in diverse industrial and consumer applications.

**D. Bridging Algorithm and Neuromorphic Hardware:** The successful deployment and characterization of the Spiking Sensing Encoder (SSE) on the Thruster–V2 neuromorphic chip validate the hardware-level feasibility of SpikingNav's components. This demonstrates a crucial link between abstract algorithms and specialized energy-efficient hardware, paving the way for full closed-loop neuromorphic embodied systems. This has implications for developing ultra-low-power, real-time autonomous systems.

**E. Deeper Understanding of SNN Benefits:** The detailed analysis distinguishing robustness in static image recognition versus dynamic embodied navigation provides new insights into where SNNs offer distinct advantages. It suggests that the benefits of SNNs extend beyond mere feature extraction to the temporal regulation of internal states within a closed-loop decision-making process. This understanding can guide future research in designing SNNs for sequential and real-time AI tasks.

**F. Foundation for Future Research:** This work opens several avenues for future research, including:
- Full neuromorphic deployment of the entire SpikingNav framework, including the SPN, for comprehensive evaluation of latency and energy efficiency.
- Exploring hardware-aware sparse execution and event-driven optimization techniques to further reduce deployment costs.
- Scaling SNNs to larger, more complex Vision-Language-Action models and multimodal tasks, to assess whether the benefits persist in increasingly sophisticated embodied intelligence systems.

In conclusion, SpikingNav represents a substantial advancement in developing compact and disturbance-tolerant sensing and policy modules for embodied navigation, particularly for cyber-physical systems that require robust performance under real-world constraints.
