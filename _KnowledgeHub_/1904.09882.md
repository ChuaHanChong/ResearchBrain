# You2Me: Inferring Body Pose in Egocentric Video via First and Second Person Interactions

> [!summary] Summary
> You2Me estimates the 3D body pose of a chest-mounted camera wearer from monocular egocentric video by exploiting dyadic interaction: an LSTM fuses inter-frame homography motion features, ResNet-152 scene features, and the visible interactee's OpenPose 2D keypoints, predicting finely quantized upper/lower body pose clusters per frame. It cuts average joint error to 14.3 cm (Kinect) and 8.6 cm (Panoptic), beating prior ego-pose methods.

## Problem

- Prior ego-pose work assumes static environments without inter-person interaction, forcing reliance on low-level cues like apparent camera motion or coarse scene layout alone.
- Earlier geometry-based inside-out mocap needs 16 body-mounted cameras; a control-based imitation method needs a humanoid simulator and is evaluated only on synthetic sequences.
- Existing methods target sweeping motions that move the camera, and no dataset pairs egocentric video with 3D ground-truth poses for both interacting people.

## Method

- Three input streams per frame: stacked normalized homographies over the previous 15 frames for camera motion, ==ResNet-152== ImageNet features for static scene context, and ==OpenPose== 25-keypoint second-person pose.
- A two-layer ==LSTM== consumes concatenated features plus the previous predicted pose embedding, trained with cross-entropy over mixed-granularity K-means-quantized classes (upper K=700 / lower K=100 codebooks, two parallel LSTMs recombined per frame).
- A new **You2Me** dataset of 42 two-minute dyadic sequences from 10 subjects, captured with Panoptic Studio (14 seqs, 19 joints) and Kinect V2 (28 seqs, 25 joints) ground truth.

## Results

- Average joint error **14.3 cm** on Kinect and **8.6 cm** on Panoptic, versus **21.2**/**15.2 cm** for the motion-graph ego-pose method and **23.3**/**19.4 cm** for an adapted deconvolution pose network.
- Removing the second-person pose stream degrades error to **18.0**/**12.8 cm** (upper body worst hit, **15.3**-**19.4 cm**); dropping scene features alone costs less (**15.3**/**9.4 cm**); corrupted poses raise error to **18.7**-**20.4 cm**, ground-truth 3D poses improve it to **13.2**/**6.9 cm**.
- Per-joint gains concentrate on interaction-relevant joints: hands **15.9 cm** versus **28.5 cm** for the prior method, elbows **8.5** versus **15.2 cm**; gains are largest in conversation, smallest in sports.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An observable partner's pose is a strong prior for an unobservable one: action-reaction coupling in dyadic interaction carries recoverable information about the out-of-view body.
> - Explicitly extracted keypoints beat implicit encoding: generic scene CNN features contain the partner but learn the pose link far less data-efficiently than explicit pose input.
> - Fine-grained classification over quantized poses trains more robustly than direct coordinate regression, and mixing 2D partner input with 3D output is fine for learned models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of computer vision has an extensive history in human body pose estimation, primarily from a "third-person" viewpoint where individuals are fully visible within the camera's frame. Recent advancements, particularly with deep learning architectures, have led to substantial improvements in detecting and tracking visible body poses in images and video. However, a significant challenge arises when the camera's perspective is "first-person" or "egocentric," such as from a wearable camera. In such scenarios, the camera wearer's own body is often largely outside the field of view, rendering traditional third-person pose estimation methods ineffective.

Prior research in egocentric vision has largely focused on tasks like object recognition, activity recognition, visible hand and arm pose estimation, eye gaze tracking, or anticipating camera trajectories. Within the more specific domain of first-person full body pose estimation, efforts have been limited. Early attempts included geometry-based "inside-out mocap" systems that utilized multiple body-mounted cameras. More recent learning-based approaches have sought to infer ego-pose from monocular first-person video. These methods typically rely on subtle cues from apparent camera motion or coarse scene layout. For example, one approach optimized an implicit motion graph with hand-crafted cost functions, while another used a humanoid simulator for recovery of action sequences. These prior learning-based methods have often been restricted to static environments or sequences involving sweeping motions that induce noticeable camera movements, such as bending or walking. They have also tended to overlook the contextual information present in human-human interactions.

This work addresses a recognized gap in the existing literature by proposing a method that explicitly leverages inter-person interactions to infer the camera wearer's 3D body pose. While some research in egocentric video has explored social signals, such as detecting social groups or mutual gaze, and other work has attempted to learn gestures from paired egocentric videos, these typically require multiple cameras or focus on action classification rather than full 3D body pose inference from a single egocentric stream. "You2Me" distinguishes itself by modeling the action-reaction dynamics between the camera wearer and a visible interactee, thereby extending the capabilities of egocentric pose estimation beyond reliance solely on self-motion or static scene cues.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to estimate the full 3D body pose sequence of a person wearing a camera, given only a monocular egocentric video stream captured by that camera. The output is intended to be a stick figure skeleton comprising 3D joint positions for the camera wearer at each frame, expressed in a person-centric coordinate system and normalized for individual body size.

The motivation for this objective stems from the growing prevalence of wearable cameras and their potential applications across various domains:
- **Augmented Reality (AR):** Understanding the user's body pose is crucial for seamlessly blending virtual information with the real world, enabling more natural interaction with virtual objects or games.
- **Healthcare:** Wearables could facilitate remote therapy and rehabilitation by providing insights into a patient's physical function and movement patterns in their home environment.
- **Robotics:** Accurate egocentric pose estimation could simplify video-based learning from human demonstrations, allowing robots to better interpret and replicate human actions.

A core challenge in egocentric pose estimation is that a significant portion of the camera wearer's body is typically outside the camera's field of view. While previous methods have attempted to infer ego-pose from implicit cues like camera motion or scene appearance, these cues can be ambiguous, particularly during subtle upper-body movements or gestures where global camera motion is minimal.

The key insight motivating this work is to exploit the dynamics of human-human interactions. The authors propose that by observing the body pose of a "second person" (an interactee) who is visible in the egocentric video, valuable information about the largely unobserved "first person" (the camera wearer) can be inferred. This motivation is supported by principles from cognitive science, which indicate an inherent synchronization and interplay between interacting individuals' body poses. For instance, a person's reaching gesture may elicit a reciprocal reaching response, or animated storytelling may be met with nodding. By learning to model these temporal action-reaction dynamics, even when one party is largely out of view, the researchers aim to overcome the limitations of relying solely on self-centric visual cues. The "You2Me" approach is designed to explicitly capture this interplay, offering a novel method for ego-pose estimation from a single wearable camera.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "You2Me" approach is a learning-based method that utilizes a recurrent neural network to infer the camera wearer's 3D body pose from egocentric video. The methodology integrates three distinct types of features extracted from the monocular egocentric video, along with a quantized representation of the wearer's previous pose, as input to an LSTM network.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation and Pose Encoding

The task is defined as estimating a sequence of N 3D human poses (p_t ∈ R^(3J)) for the camera wearer from N video frames. Each pose consists of J 3D joint positions, defined in a person-centric coordinate system with the origin at the chest-mounted camera. Skeletons are normalized for scale based on shoulder width. The objective is to infer articulated pose rather than classify actions.

#### 3.2. Feature Extraction

Three types of features are extracted from the egocentric video:

1. **Dynamic First-Person Motion Features (m_t):**
 - These features aim to capture the camera wearer's global movements, even when their body is out of view.
 - A sequence of homographies is estimated between successive video frames within a short temporal window (e.g., 15 frames for a half-second interval at 30 fps). This follows previous work and provides a scene-invariant cue for camera motion.
 - Homographies are calculated from optical flow correspondences and normalized.
 - The resulting 3x3 homography matrices are vectorized and concatenated, forming a vector m_t ∈ R^135. This representation is intended to capture sweeping actions that induce noticeable camera movements, while generalizing to varying scene appearances.

2. **Static First-Person Scene Features (s_t):**
 - These features provide contextual cues from the visible environment.
 - A ResNet-152 model, pre-trained on ImageNet, is used as a fixed feature extractor. The last fully connected layer is removed, and the output for each frame f_t is a feature vector s_t ∈ R^2048.
 - The rationale is that static scene elements (e.g., floor, furniture) can imply certain body postures, providing information even when camera motion is subtle. This visual encoding is considered more comprehensive than simpler binary classifiers (e.g., sit/stand).

3. **Second-Person Body Pose Interaction Features (o_t):**
 - This is the central novel feature of the You2Me approach, designed to leverage interaction dynamics.
 - Since the interacting "second person" is visible in the egocentric video (effectively a third-person view of them), their pose can be estimated using existing methods.
 - OpenPose, a real-time multi-person 2D pose estimation method, is employed to infer the interactee's 2D keypoint positions for each frame.
 - The normalized 25 2D keypoint estimates are flattened into a vector o_t ∈ R^50. Missing or occluded joints are set to zero, which can implicitly signal the camera wearer's proximity to the interactee.
 - This explicit pose representation of the interactee is intended to capture the symbiotic action-reaction patterns in human interactions, which are critical for inferring the camera wearer's pose, especially for gestures and micro-actions. The use of 2D OpenPose is justified by the difficulty of state-of-the-art 3D methods on this data due to occlusions.

#### 3.3. Recurrent Neural Network for Pose Inference

The three extracted features, along with an embedding of the wearer's pose from the previous frame, are fed into a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network.

- **Input Embedding:**
 - The static scene feature s_t is projected into a lower-dimensional embedding x_t ∈ R^E (where E=256) via a linear layer (φ_x) and batch normalization.
 - The camera wearer's pose from the previous frame (p_t-1) is represented as a K-dimensional one-hot vector (representing a quantized pose ID). This is mapped to an embedding z_t ∈ R^E via a linear layer (φ_z).
 - The features m_t, o_t, x_t, and z_t are concatenated into a single vector b_t, which serves as the input to the LSTM cell at time t.
- **LSTM Processing:** The LSTM processes the sequence, with its hidden state h_t capturing the cumulative latent representation of the wearer's pose, scene, and interactee information over time. The hidden state dimension is D=512. The network uses two recurrent layers.
- **Training Objective:** Instead of direct regression of 3D joint coordinates, the task is formulated as a fine-grained classification problem.
 - The space of ground-truth body poses is quantized into a large number of clusters using K-means. To account for varying levels of detail, a mixed granularity clustering is applied: K_upp = 700 clusters for upper body joints and K_bot = 100 for lower body joints.
 - This necessitates training two separate LSTMs: LSTM_upp for upper body pose prediction and LSTM_bot for lower body. The predicted cluster IDs from both LSTMs are combined to reconstruct the full 3D skeleton.
 - The network is trained using a cross-entropy loss across an entire sequence, aiming to predict the correct quantized pose ID for each frame. The predicted pose ID at time t is used as the input for z_(t+1) for the subsequent frame.
- **Implementation Details:** Training is performed with a batch size of 32 for 20 epochs (18 hours on a single GPU), with a learning rate schedule. Testing is conducted at 36 fps.

#### 3.4. You2Me Video Datasets

A novel dataset was collected for this research, consisting of 42 two-minute sequences of one-on-one interactions between 10 individuals. All data is publicly available.
- **Capture Methods:**
 - **Panoptic Studio:** 14 sequences captured in a studio dome environment (GoPro Hero3, 1920x1080, 30fps) with highly accurate 19-joint 3D ground truth skeletons for both individuals using the Panoptic Studio system.
 - **Kinect Capture:** 28 sequences captured in unconstrained indoor environments (GoPro Hero4, 1920x1080, 30fps) with 25-joint 3D ground truth skeletons obtained from Kinect V2 sensors. This method offers greater mobility but potentially noisier ground truth.
- **Activity Classes:** The dataset covers four types of activities: hand games, tossing and catching, sports, and conversation, ensuring variety in interaction types.
- **Dataset Split:** Training and test sets are activity-agnostic and person-agnostic (no individuals appearing in the test set are present in the training set) to ensure generalization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the You2Me approach was conducted using the Euclidean distance (in cm) between predicted 3D joint positions and continuous ground truth, averaged over sequences, with a reference shoulder distance of 30 cm for scaling. Separate error metrics were provided for overall joints ("All"), upper body joints ("Upp"), and lower body joints ("Bot").

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Comparison to Baselines (Table 1)

The You2Me approach consistently demonstrated superior performance compared to all competing methods on both the Kinect and Panoptic Studio datasets.
- On the Kinect dataset, You2Me achieved an average joint error of 14.3 cm, notably lower than the state-of-the-art ego-pose method MotionGraph (21.2 cm) and a adapted third-person pose method, DeconvNet (23.3 cm).
- Similar performance gains were observed on the Panoptic Studio dataset, with You2Me at 8.6 cm, MotionGraph at 15.2 cm, and DeconvNet at 19.4 cm.
- Simple baselines like "Always standing" (Stand) and "Always sitting" (Sit) were also outperformed, indicating that You2Me makes informed limb estimates beyond exploiting common priors.
- An ablation study comparing You2Me with a regression objective ("Ours as Regression") confirmed that the fine-grained classification approach was significantly more robust and accurate.

#### 4.2. Impact of Second-Person Pose (o_t) (Table 1 and 2)

The inclusion of second-person pose information (o_t) was identified as a critical component of the You2Me approach:
- The "Ours w/o o_t" baseline, which excludes second-person 2D joints, showed a substantial increase in error (e.g., 18.0 cm on Kinect vs. 14.3 cm for full You2Me).
- This improvement was particularly pronounced for upper body joints, which are heavily involved in gestural interactions (e.g., 19.4 cm for "Ours w/o o_t" vs. 15.3 cm for "Ours" on Kinect upper body). This supports the hypothesis that interaction dynamics provide essential cues for subtle upper-body movements.
- Further ablation in Table 2 confirmed that both second-person pose (o_t) and scene features (x_t) contribute to performance, with o_t providing the most significant impact, particularly for upper body predictions. The explicit pose estimate of the interactee was more effective than relying on implicit features captured by ResNet alone.

#### 4.3. Analysis of Second-Person Pose Source (Table 3)

- Substituting the ground truth 3D skeleton of the interactee for the 2D OpenPose estimates (o_t) further improved results, indicating potential for even higher accuracy with more precise interactee pose information (e.g., 13.2 cm for GT 3D vs. 14.3 cm for OpenPose on Kinect).
- However, using 2D OpenPose (o_t) was found to be more effective than integrating predicted 3D poses from existing state-of-the-art 3D monocular pose methods, which struggled with occlusions in the dataset. This justified the design choice of using 2D pose for the interactee.
- Feeding incorrect second-person pose information (e.g., average standing pose, empty poses, or random poses) led to significantly degraded performance, confirming that the network actively learns and leverages the true correlative structure in human interactions.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results (Figures 5, 6, 7)

- **Success Cases (Figure 6):** You2Me demonstrated an ability to accurately predict upper body movements, such as arm swings before catching a ball or reaching gestures during conversations, outperforming MotionGraph. It also showed robustness even when the interactee was partially or fully occluded in the ego-view.
- **Interaction Links (Figure 5):** The model effectively learned to associate certain second-person poses with specific ego-poses, like mutual hand extensions or tied conversational gestures.
- **Failure Cases (Figure 7):** Primary failure instances occurred when the camera wearer was severely crouched (camera pointing towards the floor) or when the view of the interactee was obstructed for extended durations. This highlights the ongoing reliance on interactee visibility for optimal performance.

#### 4.5. Impact of Activity Type and Quantization (Appendix Tables 4, 5, 6)

- The impact of You2Me was most pronounced in conversation sequences (where subtle upper body gestures are key) and lowest in sports sequences (where the interactee may frequently move out of view).
- The controlled quantization (separating upper and lower body clustering) generally improved performance over a single, full-body quantization scheme, though the gains from the second-person pose were significant regardless of the quantization method.
- Per-joint analysis (Table 5) showed substantial error reductions for joints critical to interaction, such as hands and elbows, further supporting the method's effectiveness in capturing interaction-driven movements.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "You2Me" approach represents a notable advancement in egocentric 3D body pose estimation. By introducing and effectively leveraging the dynamics of first- and second-person interactions, the research addresses a critical limitation of previous methods that primarily relied on implicit cues from self-motion or static scene context. The ability to infer detailed 3D body pose for a camera wearer, even when their body is largely out of view, marks a significant step towards a more comprehensive understanding of human behavior from a first-person perspective.

The significance and potential impact of this work extend across several domains:

- **Enhanced Augmented Reality (AR) Experiences:** A more accurate understanding of the user's physical state enables AR systems to integrate virtual content more contextually and allow for more intuitive interactions. This could lead to more immersive games, useful informational overlays, and seamless integration of digital and physical worlds.
- **Advancements in Healthcare and Rehabilitation:** For remote monitoring and therapy, knowing a patient's precise body movements is invaluable. This technology could aid in tracking rehabilitation progress, providing real-time feedback, and supporting remote assistance for individuals seeking to improve physical function.
- **Improved Robotics and Learning from Demonstration:** Robots learning from human demonstrations could gain a deeper understanding of human intentions and actions by observing not just the environment, but also the demonstrator's internal physical state, even when parts of the body are obscured. This could facilitate more natural and effective human-robot collaboration.
- **Pioneering Socially-Aware AI Systems:** By demonstrating a method to infer subtle human poses based on social interaction cues, this research contributes to the development of AI systems that are more aware of human social dynamics. This could eventually lead to more empathetic and responsive AI agents.
- **Contribution to Egocentric Vision Research:** The novel dataset collected and made publicly available by the authors fills a gap in resources for first-person interaction and ego-pose estimation. This will likely serve as a valuable benchmark for future research in this specialized area.

Looking forward, the authors have identified several avenues for future work, including reasoning about the absence of interactions, extending the model to scenarios with multiple interactees, and exploring reciprocal mechanisms where ego-pose estimates could, in turn, enhance second-person pose predictions. These directions suggest a foundation for more sophisticated and generalized understanding of human interaction from a first-person view.
