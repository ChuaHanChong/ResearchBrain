# GELLO: A General, Low-Cost, and Intuitive Teleoperation Framework for Robot Manipulators

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Berkeley developed GELLO, a low-cost, intuitive teleoperation framework for robot manipulators that uses a scaled, 3D-printed kinematically equivalent controller. This system enables efficient collection of high-quality human demonstration data, achieving superior task success rates and faster completion times compared to other low-cost teleoperation methods.

## Problem

- Collecting large, high-quality human demonstration data is a bottleneck for training robust, data-driven robot learning policies, especially in imitation learning.
- Existing high-fidelity teleoperation systems are prohibitively expensive, limiting accessibility and scalability for widespread data collection.
- Low-cost teleoperation devices often abstract robot kinematics, leading to unintuitive control, increased operator errors (e.g., self-collisions), and lower quality demonstration data.

## Method

- **GELLO** introduces a low-cost teleoperation framework utilizing a scaled, 3D-printed, ==kinematically isomorphic== replica of the target robot arm as a controller.
- It employs inexpensive ==backdrivable servo motors== primarily for their integrated ==high-resolution encoder== functionality, alongside ==passive joint regularizers== to manage arm posture and provide subtle tactile feedback.
- The entire system, including hardware designs and software stack, is open-sourced with a bill of materials under $300 per device, ensuring ease of build and replication for various robot manipulators.

## Results

- GELLO achieved an average **92%** task success rate across **five** bi-manual manipulation tasks, substantially outperforming 3D mouses (**63%**) and VR controllers (**72%**).
- Human operators using GELLO completed tasks faster and experienced significantly fewer failures, including a notable reduction in self-collisions, compared to other low-cost teleoperation devices.
- The framework was successfully implemented and demonstrated on **three** different robot arms (UR5, xArm7, Franka Panda), enabling complex, contact-rich, and bi-manual manipulation tasks with a total bill of materials under **$300** per device.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A scaled, kinematically isomorphic controller built from economical components can provide highly intuitive and precise robot teleoperation, significantly reducing operator errors like self-collisions.
> - Inexpensive servo motors can effectively function as high-resolution encoders, offering a cost-effective alternative to expensive haptic devices or full robot-to-robot teleoperation.
> - Simple passive joint regularization, using components like springs, can enhance user experience by providing natural damping and maintaining arm posture without requiring complex active force feedback systems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "GELLO" positions itself at the intersection of several critical areas within modern robotics: **imitation learning**, **teleoperation systems**, and **data-driven robot control**. The broader research landscape it addresses is characterized by the increasing reliance on large datasets for training robust and generalizable robot skills, particularly through imitation learning (IL).

Historically, teleoperation has been crucial for controlling robots in complex or hazardous environments, with applications ranging from robot surgery to disaster relief. However, the performance of these systems is often bottlenecked by the quality, scale, and variety of human demonstration data. High-fidelity, bilateral teleoperation systems, which provide force feedback to the operator, are exceptionally performant but come with prohibitively high costs due to their strict sensor and hardware requirements (often tens of thousands of dollars). This cost severely limits their accessibility and scalability for large-scale data collection efforts.

In response to this, researchers have explored various low-cost teleoperation alternatives, such as 3D mouses, virtual reality (VR) controllers, or camera-based systems. While affordable, these solutions often abstract away the robot's inherent kinematic constraints. This abstraction can lead to unintuitive control, increased cognitive load for the operator, higher rates of errors (e.g., self-collisions, hitting kinematic singularities), and ultimately lower quality or less efficient data collection.

A notable recent development in this space is the "ALOHA" system, which demonstrated impressive fine-grained bi-manual manipulation using an off-the-shelf servo-based arm as a controller for another manipulator of similar form factor. ALOHA represented a significant step towards more affordable kinematically-matched teleoperation. GELLO builds upon the core idea of a kinematically isomorphic controller but pushes the boundaries further by aiming for even greater affordability, generality across different robot types, and simplicity of replication. Unlike ALOHA, which still uses a relatively expensive "additional robot arm" as a controller, GELLO proposes building a *scaled replica* using inexpensive 3D-printed parts and commodity motors primarily for their encoder functionality.

Therefore, GELLO's contribution fits into the landscape by directly addressing the crucial need for a **general, low-cost, and intuitive teleoperation framework** that can facilitate the collection of the massive, high-quality demonstration datasets required for the next generation of data-driven robot learning algorithms, including the increasingly complex foundation models and generalist agents (e.g., RT-1, RT-2 mentioned in the references). By open-sourcing its hardware and software designs, GELLO also contributes to the broader movement of democratizing access to advanced robotics research tools, fostering greater collaboration and accelerating progress across the community.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the GELLO project is to **lower the barrier to collecting large and high-quality human demonstration data** for robot manipulators. This objective is driven by a clear motivation stemming from the current state of robot learning, particularly imitation learning.

The authors explicitly state that the performance of learned policies in imitation learning is fundamentally **bottlenecked by the quality, scale, and variety of demonstration data**. While data-driven methods have shown remarkable success in robotics, acquiring sufficient, diverse, and high-fidelity human demonstrations remains a significant challenge. Existing approaches for collecting this data face a dilemma:

1. **High-Fidelity Teleoperation Systems:** These systems (e.g., advanced haptic devices, robot-to-robot teleoperation) offer excellent control and often provide force feedback, enabling precise and reliable demonstrations. However, their **prohibitive cost** (tens of thousands of dollars) limits their accessibility to only a few well-funded labs and makes large-scale deployment for data collection impractical.
2. **Low-Cost Commodity Devices:** Systems leveraging common devices like 3D mouses or VR controllers are affordable but suffer from significant drawbacks. They often **abstract away the robot's kinematic constraints**, making them unintuitive for operators. This leads to increased cognitive load, slower operation, higher rates of errors like self-collisions or hitting joint limits, and ultimately, less efficient collection of lower-quality data.

To overcome this bottleneck, GELLO aims to develop a teleoperation system that strikes a balance between affordability and intuitive, high-quality control. The key objectives for GELLO are:

- **Low-Cost:** To build a system that is economically accessible, enabling broad adoption and scalable data collection, with a target cost of less than $300 per device.
- **Intuitive to Use:** To provide a control interface that aligns with human intuition, allowing operators to easily understand and control the robot's kinematics, thereby reducing errors and improving efficiency.
- **Generalizable:** To offer a framework that can be adapted and instantiated for various types of commonly used robot manipulators.
- **Easy to Build and Replicate:** To simplify the construction process, requiring minimal technical expertise and relying on readily available, economical components (3D-printed parts, off-the-shelf motors).
- **Capable:** To enable human operators to perform complex manipulation tasks, including bi-manual and contact-rich operations.

The authors explicitly summarize their contributions as:
1. Presenting **practical implementations** of GELLO for three commonly used robot arms (Franka, UR5, xArm) with simple and low-cost designs.
2. Conducting a **comprehensive user study** to demonstrate GELLO's effectiveness compared to other prevalent low-cost teleoperation systems.
3. **Fully open-sourcing** all hardware and software components, including a bill of materials and assembly instructions, to ensure accessibility and foster community adoption.

In essence, GELLO's motivation is to accelerate progress in robot learning by making the essential task of human demonstration collection significantly more accessible, efficient, and reliable.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The GELLO framework's methodology revolves around a core principle: creating a **miniature, kinematically equivalent controller device** for a target robot arm, built with readily available, low-cost components. This design philosophy is guided by four main principles: low-cost, capability, portability, and simplicity of replication.

**A. Core Design Principles and Components:**

1. **Low-Cost Construction:**
 - **Economical Servos:** The system leverages inexpensive backdrivable servo motors, specifically the DYNAMIXEL XL330 series. These servos are chosen not primarily for their actuation capabilities but for their integrated high-resolution 12-bit encoders (providing 0.088 mechanical degree accuracy) and easy-to-use communication protocols. This "servo-as-encoder" approach simplifies construction compared to using separate encoders and control electronics. The backdrivable nature of the servos also provides natural damping and resistance, enhancing user stability.
 - **3D Printed Parts:** A significant portion of the GELLO device is fabricated using 3D printing. This allows for high customization to match specific robot kinematics, significantly reduces manufacturing costs, and enables rapid prototyping for different robot types. The authors instantiate GELLO for three distinct robot arms: Universal Robot UR5, uFactory xArm7, and Franka Panda.
 - **Minimalist Design:** The overall design emphasizes simplicity, aiming for a total bill of materials under $300 per device.

2. **Scaled Kinematically Equivalent Structure:**
 - **Morphological Similarity:** GELLO is designed as a scaled-down physical replica of the target robot arm. This means its joints and links directly correspond to those of the robot. This **kinematically isomorphic structure** is crucial for intuitiveness, as the human operator manipulates the GELLO device as if directly controlling the robot.
 - **Direct Joint Control:** Joint angles are read directly from GELLO's servo encoders and then commanded as joint positions to the follower robot. This eliminates the need for complex inverse kinematics computations in the control loop, further simplifying the software stack and reducing latency.
 - **Kinematic Awareness:** By mirroring the robot's kinematics, the operator naturally feels resistance or understands when the controller (and thus the robot) approaches kinematic singularities or joint limits, making them more aware of potential failure modes and leading to more reliable teleoperation. The authors found a scaling factor of α = 0.5 to be effective.

3. **Joint Regularization:**
 - To counteract gravity and prevent the passively controlled arm from collapsing into undesirable configurations, simple, passive **joint regularizers** are incorporated. These involve using mechanical components like springs or rubber bands.
 - **Functionality:** They help the device maintain a "natural" posture, avoid collisions with the workspace (e.g., the table), and provide a form of passive force feedback to the user. This feedback can subtly inform the operator about the arm's current configuration, particularly near joint extremities, without requiring complex active haptic systems. The authors quantitatively show that regularization reduces the force required to hold the arm stationary and provides varying restorative forces.

4. **Software Stack:**
 - The control software is designed for simplicity. It utilizes the DYNAMIXEL Python API to read joint angles from the GELLO device. These angles are then directly transmitted as joint commands to the target robot using its respective Python API.
 - **Extensibility:** Message passing between processes is handled using ZMQ, providing a robust and extensible protocol for integrating new robot types.

**B. Experimental Evaluation:**

The authors conducted a two-pronged evaluation: a quantitative user study and qualitative demonstrations of system capabilities.

1. **Quantitative User Study:**
 - **Participants & Setup:** 12 inexperienced university volunteers participated in a study involving bi-manual teleoperation of two Universal Robot UR5 arms.
 - **Comparison Devices:** GELLO was compared against two common low-cost teleoperation devices: 3D mouses (SpaceNavigator) and commercial VR controllers (Meta Quest 2).
 - **Tasks:** Five diverse bi-manual manipulation tasks were designed to probe different aspects of teleoperation performance:
 1. Place a hat on a rack (single-arm, large workspace).
 2. Open a case and fetch a sleeping mask (articulated object interaction).
 3. Hand over a banana to the kitchen area (bi-manual, large workspace traversal).
 4. Fold a towel (bi-manual, deformable object manipulation).
 5. Plug in a USB cable (fine-grain insertion, high precision).
 - **Procedure:** Participants received a brief orientation, followed by a 5-minute practice phase for each device. The order of device use was randomized to mitigate bias. Tasks had time limits, and failures (self-collision, environment collision, timeout) were recorded.
 - **Metrics:** Task success rate, task completion time (for successful trials), and a breakdown of failure modes.

2. **Qualitative System Capabilities Demonstration:**
 - To showcase GELLO's versatility and robustness, the authors performed various challenging manipulation tasks, including contact-rich and long-horizon operations, across all three target robot platforms (UR5, xArm7, Franka Panda). These demonstrations were conducted in diverse settings (lab and "in-the-wild") and supplementary videos were provided on a project website.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of GELLO yielded compelling results, strongly supporting its effectiveness as a low-cost, intuitive, and generalizable teleoperation framework.

**A. User Study Results (Quantitative Comparison):**

The user study provided a direct comparison of GELLO against 3D mouses and VR controllers for bi-manual robot teleoperation.

- **Superior Success Rate:** GELLO consistently achieved the highest task success rates across all five evaluated manipulation tasks. On average, GELLO achieved a 92% success rate, significantly outperforming 3D Mouses (63% average) and VR controllers (72% average).
 - For instance, in complex tasks like towel folding, GELLO's success rate was 92% compared to 58% for both 3D Mouses and VR. Similarly, for the precise USB cable insertion task, GELLO achieved 83% success, while 3D Mouses and VR managed only 58% and 50%, respectively. This indicates GELLO's superior intuitiveness and precision, particularly for demanding bi-manual and fine-grain tasks.

- **Faster Completion Times:** For successful task executions, GELLO consistently enabled faster completion times across all tasks. This demonstrates increased efficiency, allowing operators to achieve more successful demonstrations within a given timeframe. The paper notes that human operators (without a robot) provide a lower bound, and GELLO approaches this efficiency more closely than other devices.

- **Reduced Failures and Improved Reliability:** An analysis of failure modes highlighted GELLO's robustness:
 - GELLO exhibited the lowest number of failures across all categories (timeout, self-collision, environment collision, and other).
 - Crucially, GELLO significantly reduced instances of **self-collisions**. The authors observed that self-collisions were a prevalent issue for 3D Mouses and VR controllers, as these devices operate in abstract Cartesian space, making operators less aware of the robot's kinematic limits and potential self-intersections. GELLO's kinematically isomorphic design inherently provides this awareness, leading to a much lower collision risk.
 - The lower "timeout" count for GELLO suggests that operators found it easier and faster to learn and perform tasks, even with limited practice, indicating a higher degree of intuitiveness.

**B. Teleoperation System Capabilities (Qualitative Demonstrations):**

Beyond the controlled user study, GELLO's capabilities were qualitatively demonstrated on more challenging manipulation tasks across three different robot platforms (UR5, xArm7, Franka Panda).

- **Versatility Across Robot Types:** GELLO was shown to be effective for various robot kinematics, including 6-DOF (UR5) and 7-DOF arms (xArm, Franka Panda). The kinematically equivalent structure allowed users to manage the extra degree of freedom (null space) in 7-DOF arms effectively, which is advantageous in cluttered environments.
- **Complex Task Performance:** GELLO enabled the performance of complex tasks, including contact-rich operations (e.g., filling water bottles, requiring payload management) and long-horizon bi-manual coordination tasks.
- **Portability and Real-World Application:** The portable and self-sufficient design of GELLO facilitated data collection in diverse environments, both in the lab and in "in-the-wild" settings, demonstrating its practical applicability beyond controlled laboratory conditions.

**C. Cost-Effectiveness:**

A key finding is GELLO's affordability. With a bill of materials under $300, it stands as a significantly more economical solution compared to traditional high-fidelity teleoperation systems (e.g., robot-to-robot teleoperation at $30,000 or haptic devices at $40,000). Its cost is comparable to commodity VR headsets but offers superior control characteristics for robotics.

In summary, GELLO proved to be a superior low-cost teleoperation solution, enabling more reliable, efficient, and intuitive control for human operators, ultimately leading to higher quality and faster collection of robot demonstration data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GELLO represents a significant contribution to the field of robotics, particularly in the domain of robot learning and manipulation, with the potential for broad impact.

**A. Democratization and Acceleration of Robot Learning:**
The most profound impact of GELLO lies in its ability to **democratize access to high-quality robot demonstration data collection**. By offering a low-cost, easy-to-build, and intuitive teleoperation system, GELLO effectively lowers the financial and technical barriers that have historically bottlenecked progress in imitation learning and data-driven robotics. This means that more researchers, academic institutions, and even smaller labs or individual practitioners, regardless of their budget, can now collect the extensive and diverse datasets necessary to train modern, generalizable robot skills. This widespread accessibility could accelerate the development of more capable and autonomous robots, as the availability of rich data is a critical ingredient for current state-of-the-art learning algorithms.

**B. Improved Data Quality and Collection Efficiency:**
GELLO directly addresses the crucial problem of data quality and collection efficiency. The user study conclusively demonstrates that GELLO enables significantly higher task success rates and faster completion times compared to other low-cost alternatives. Its kinematically isomorphic design minimizes operator errors such as self-collisions and improves intuitive control, leading to more reliable and higher-fidelity demonstrations. This efficiency is critical for scaling up data collection efforts, allowing researchers to gather larger and more varied datasets in less time and with fewer retries.

**C. Fostering Reproducibility and Collaborative Research:**
The decision to fully open-source both the hardware designs (3D-printable parts, bill of materials) and the software stack is highly impactful. This commitment to open science ensures that the GELLO framework is not only accessible but also fully reproducible. Researchers worldwide can replicate, modify, and build upon the GELLO system for their specific robot platforms and research needs. This open-source approach fosters a collaborative environment, encourages community engagement, and can lead to rapid iteration and improvement of the system by a wider audience. It sets a valuable precedent for how foundational tools in robotics can be shared.

**D. Foundation for Advanced Robot Learning:**
As robot learning progresses towards more generalist agents and foundation models that require vast amounts of varied interaction data, GELLO provides a vital tool for generating this essential training material. Its ability to handle complex bi-manual and contact-rich tasks across diverse robot platforms makes it suitable for collecting data for a wide range of manipulation challenges. This foundational capability could enable breakthroughs in areas like learning from human videos, task generalization, and adaptive control.

**E. Practical Applications Beyond Research:**
Beyond academic research, the principles and implementation of GELLO could have practical implications in several areas:
- **Rapid Skill Prototyping:** Industries could use GELLO for quickly demonstrating and teaching new skills to robots in flexible manufacturing or logistics.
- **Educational Robotics:** Its low cost and intuitive nature make GELLO an excellent platform for teaching robot control and programming in educational settings.
- **Assistive Robotics:** In scenarios where direct human control is beneficial for assistive tasks, GELLO could offer an affordable and user-friendly interface.

While the authors acknowledge the current lack of force feedback due to cost considerations, the system's demonstrated capabilities and the clear path for future optional enhancements indicate a well-reasoned trade-off. The clever use of passive joint regularization to provide a degree of tactile feedback further underscores the practical ingenuity of the GELLO design. Overall, GELLO is poised to make a substantial impact by enabling more widespread and efficient data collection, thereby accelerating the development of more intelligent and capable robotic systems.
