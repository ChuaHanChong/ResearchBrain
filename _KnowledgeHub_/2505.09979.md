# Learning Diverse Natural Behaviors for Enhancing the Agility of Quadrupedal Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Nanjing University and UTS developed an integrated control architecture that enables quadrupedal robots to autonomously perform diverse, natural behaviors and complete complex agility challenges. The method uses semi-supervised imitation learning and efficient sim-to-real transfer to achieve high-speed obstacle navigation with robust depth perception, operating on a Go2 robot.

## Problem

- Quadrupedal robots lack the agility of biological animals, struggling with diverse natural behaviors and complex, dynamic tasks in real-world environments.
- Existing Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) and Imitation Learning (IL) methods often lead to unnatural behaviors, require extensive data, or suffer from mode collapse when trying to learn and disentangle multimodal behaviors from unstructured data.
- The 'sim-to-real' gap makes it difficult to transfer policies trained in simulation to physical robots efficiently, often requiring extensive manual tuning or broad domain randomization.

## Method

- Developed an integrated controller comprising a Basic Behavior Controller (BBC) for diverse gaits and a Task-Specific Controller (TSC) for high-level command generation, drawing inspiration from biological motor control.
- Trained the BBC using a novel semi-supervised InfoGAIL framework with dynamic latent skill distribution and Regularized Information Maximization (RIM) to learn diverse and controllable behaviors from imbalanced motion capture data while preventing mode collapse.
- Utilized Evolutionary Adversarial Simulator Identification (EASI) to efficiently optimize simulator physical parameters using minimal real-world data, thereby closing the sim-to-real gap for robust policy transfer.
- Trained the TSC using a privileged learning (teacher-student) architecture with depth images as exteroceptive input, integrating Bootstrap Your Own Latent (BYOL) self-supervised learning into the depth encoder for robustness against real-world perception noise.

## Results

- The quadrupedal robot autonomously completed a novel 7x10m agility challenge with six diverse obstacles in 26 seconds, achieving an average speed of **1.1** m/s and over **78%** success rate in randomized configurations using only depth perception.
- Demonstrated high-speed hurdling capabilities, achieving a peak speed of **3.2** m/s and a maximum jump height of 0.54m, by autonomously switching between canter and jump behaviors based on depth perception.
- The Basic Behavior Controller (BBC) successfully learned diverse, controllable behaviors (walk, pace, trot, canter, jump) with superior disentanglement (lower ENT and higher NMI) compared to unsupervised and other partially supervised GAIL baselines.
- Evolutionary Adversarial Simulator Identification (EASI) converged simulator parameters in less than 10 minutes using only 80 seconds of real-world data, enabling direct sim-to-real transfer with minimal performance degradation for learned policies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Semi-supervised imitation learning, combined with dynamic latent skill distribution and RIM, effectively disentangles multimodal behaviors and prevents mode collapse when learning from imbalanced, unstructured datasets.
> - Evolutionary Adversarial Simulator Identification (EASI) offers a highly efficient and data-sparing approach to align simulator dynamics with real-world physics, enabling rapid and robust sim-to-real transfer.
> - A privileged learning architecture combined with self-supervised perception pre-training (BYOL) significantly enhances robustness to real-world sensor noise, allowing robots to autonomously complete complex tasks with limited sensory input.
