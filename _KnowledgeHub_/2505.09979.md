# Learning Diverse Natural Behaviors for Enhancing the Agility of Quadrupedal Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Nanjing University and UTS developed an integrated control architecture that enables quadrupedal robots to autonomously perform diverse, natural behaviors and complete complex agility challenges. The method uses semi-supervised imitation learning and efficient sim-to-real transfer to achieve high-speed obstacle navigation with robust depth perception, operating on a Go2 robot.

## Problem

- Quadrupedal robots lack the agility of biological animals, struggling with diverse natural behaviors and complex, dynamic tasks in real-world environments.
- Existing Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) and Imitation Learning (IL) methods often lead to unnatural behaviors, require extensive data, or suffer from mode collapse when trying to learn and disentangle multimodal behaviors from unstructured data.
- The 'sim-to-real' gap makes it difficult to transfer policies trained in simulation to physical robots efficiently, often requiring extensive manual tuning or broad domain randomization.

## Method

- Developed an integrated controller comprising a Basic Behavior Controller (BBC) for diverse gaits and a Task-Specific Controller (TSC) for high-level command generation, drawing inspiration from biological motor control.
- Trained the BBC using a novel semi-supervised InfoGAIL framework with dynamic latent skill distribution and Regularized Information Maximization (RIM) to learn diverse and controllable behaviors from imbalanced motion capture data while preventing mode collapse.
- Utilized Evolutionary Adversarial Simulator Identification (EASI) to efficiently optimize simulator physical parameters using minimal real-world data, thereby closing the sim-to-real gap for robust policy transfer.
- Trained the TSC using a privileged learning (teacher-student) architecture with depth images as exteroceptive input, integrating Bootstrap Your Own Latent (BYOL) self-supervised learning into the depth encoder for robustness against real-world perception noise.

## Results

- The quadrupedal robot autonomously completed a novel 7x10m agility challenge with six diverse obstacles in 26 seconds, achieving an average speed of **1.1** m/s and over **78%** success rate in randomized configurations using only depth perception.
- Demonstrated high-speed hurdling capabilities, achieving a peak speed of **3.2** m/s and a maximum jump height of 0.54m, by autonomously switching between canter and jump behaviors based on depth perception.
- The Basic Behavior Controller (BBC) successfully learned diverse, controllable behaviors (walk, pace, trot, canter, jump) with superior disentanglement (lower ENT and higher NMI) compared to unsupervised and other partially supervised GAIL baselines.
- Evolutionary Adversarial Simulator Identification (EASI) converged simulator parameters in less than 10 minutes using only 80 seconds of real-world data, enabling direct sim-to-real transfer with minimal performance degradation for learned policies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Semi-supervised imitation learning, combined with dynamic latent skill distribution and RIM, effectively disentangles multimodal behaviors and prevents mode collapse when learning from imbalanced, unstructured datasets.
> - Evolutionary Adversarial Simulator Identification (EASI) offers a highly efficient and data-sparing approach to align simulator dynamics with real-world physics, enabling rapid and robust sim-to-real transfer.
> - A privileged learning architecture combined with self-supervised perception pre-training (BYOL) significantly enhances robustness to real-world sensor noise, allowing robots to autonomously complete complex tasks with limited sensory input.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of agile quadrupedal robots that can perform complex maneuvers akin to biological animals is a significant and ongoing challenge in robotics. Historically, control methods for quadrupedal robots, such as model predictive control (MPC) and whole-body control, have relied on simplified models, which often limit their performance in highly dynamic and intricate tasks. The introduction of Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) and Imitation Learning (IL) has offered avenues to overcome these limitations, enabling robots to learn robust behaviors without explicit models and to process high-dimensional sensory inputs.

However, both DRL and IL present their own challenges. DRL often requires complex reward function design, which can lead to unnatural behaviors. Imitation Learning, particularly Behavior Cloning (BC), necessitates extensive data to mitigate compounding errors. Adversarial Imitation Learning (AIL) has shown promise by leveraging both offline data and online interaction, reducing data requirements and enabling the imitation of dynamic, natural behaviors. Despite these advancements, a persistent issue in AIL, especially when combined with latent variable models for diverse behaviors, is mode collapse. This phenomenon makes it difficult to disentangle multimodal behavior features from unstructured data, resulting in policies that are difficult to control and exhibit limited diversity.

Another fundamental challenge in robotic control, particularly for agile locomotion, is the "sim-to-real" gap. Policies trained in simulation often perform suboptimally when deployed in the physical world due to discrepancies between the simulated and real environments. Domain randomization (DR) is a common approach to mitigate this gap, but it requires careful tuning of randomization levels. Recent efforts have explored automatic optimization of simulator parameters using real-world data, providing alternative solutions.

This research addresses these identified challenges by proposing an integrated control architecture that combines advancements in semi-supervised imitation learning, simulator identification, and robust perception. It positions itself as a step towards bridging the gap between current robotic capabilities and the agility observed in natural animals, specifically tackling the issues of learning diverse, controllable behaviors from imbalanced data and efficiently transferring these learned skills from simulation to real-world deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to significantly enhance the agility of quadrupedal robots, enabling them to execute a wide range of natural behaviors in real-world, complex, and obstacle-filled environments. This overarching goal is motivated by the observation that despite recent progress, current quadrupedal robots still fall short of the agility demonstrated by animals, limiting their practical applicability in scenarios requiring intricate navigation and dynamic responsiveness.

The specific motivations and objectives driving this work are as follows:

- **Achieving Animal-like Agility:** Inspired by the sophisticated behavioral patterns of quadrupedal animals, the research aims to develop control systems that allow robots to exhibit comparable agility, capable of traversing diverse and challenging terrains.
- **Developing a Biologically-Inspired Control Architecture:** The authors draw inspiration from neuroscience, recognizing that animal locomotion involves both high-level neural regions for sensory integration and motion planning, and low-level regions for basic gait patterns. This motivates the design of an integrated controller comprising a Basic Behavior Controller (BBC) and a Task-Specific Controller (TSC), mirroring these biological functions.
- **Learning Diverse and Controllable Natural Behaviors:** A key objective is to enable robots to learn and execute a broad spectrum of natural quadrupedal behaviors (e.g., walk, trot, canter, jump) from raw motion capture data. This directly addresses the challenge of mode collapse in existing AIL methods and the difficulty of disentangling multimodal behavior features. The authors aim to achieve this through a novel semi-supervised generative adversarial imitation learning algorithm, ensuring both diversity and controllability of the learned behaviors, even from imbalanced datasets.
- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap Efficiently:** Recognizing the inherent discrepancies between simulation and reality, a crucial motivation is to develop a method for efficient and accurate transfer of learned behaviors from simulation to the real robot. This involves optimizing simulator parameters to better align with real-world dynamics, using minimal real-world data and computational resources. The goal is to avoid extensive manual tuning or overly broad domain randomization.
- **Enabling Complex Task Completion with Limited Sensory Input:** The research aims to demonstrate that the integrated controller can allow the robot to autonomously navigate complex scenarios and complete multi-stage agility tasks using only simple depth perception, without requiring precise global localization. This emphasizes robustness to environmental changes and disturbances in long-horizon tasks.
- **Establishing a Benchmark for Quadrupedal Agility:** A further motivation is to establish a standardized "quadrupedal agility challenge" as a benchmark platform. This aims to inspire further research and accelerate progress towards robots with agility comparable to or surpassing that of real dogs.

In summary, the research is motivated by the desire to push the boundaries of quadrupedal robot agility, drawing inspiration from biology, and addressing critical technical challenges in imitation learning and sim-to-real transfer through a comprehensive, integrated control solution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology involves a three-stage training framework, executed in the IsaacGym physics simulation environment, designed to develop an integrated legged locomotion controller for diverse natural behaviors and efficient real-world transfer.

#### 3.1. Overall Framework

The integrated controller consists of a **Basic Behavior Controller (BBC)** and a **Task-Specific Controller (TSC)**. The training proceeds as follows:
1. **BBC Pretraining:** A general-purpose BBC is trained to replicate common dog behaviors using a semi-supervised imitation learning approach.
2. **Simulator Enhancement:** The simulator's physical parameters are optimized using a limited amount of real-world data via Evolutionary Adversarial Simulator Identification (EASI) to reduce the sim-to-real gap. The BBC is then fine-tuned in this enhanced simulator.
3. **TSC Training:** The TSC is trained using a privileged learning architecture to generate commands for the BBC, enabling the robot to perform complex downstream tasks.

#### 3.2. Basic Behavior Controller (BBC) Training

The BBC is responsible for generating diverse, natural quadrupedal behaviors based on proprioceptive perception.
- **Semi-supervised InfoGAIL:** The BBC is trained using a novel semi-supervised variation of Information Maximizing Generative Adversarial Imitation Learning (InfoGAIL). This method extracts diverse behavioral styles from raw motion capture data of real dogs.
- **Latent Variables:**
 - **Latent Skill Variable (c):** Represents five discrete behavior modes (walk, pace, trot, canter, jump). It is learned in a semi-supervised manner, using a small amount of labeled data (less than 5% of the total motion capture data) to guide the disentanglement of multimodal behaviors and prevent mode collapse. The objective `L_SS` maximizes the variational lower bound of mutual information `I(y; o_I)` and `I(c; o_I)`.
 - **Latent Shifting Variable (ϵ):** Captures continuous behavior styles (e.g., speed variations within a gait). It is learned in an unsupervised manner from a continuous uniform distribution, with its objective `L_US` maximizing mutual information `I(ϵ; o_I)`.
- **Handling Imbalanced Data:** Motion capture data often has imbalanced behavior modes (e.g., more walking than jumping). Two improvements are introduced:
 - **Dynamic Latent Skill Distribution:** The sampling distribution for `c` (`p(c)`) is dynamically adjusted during training, estimated by the predictor `Q_c` from unlabeled data, to align with the empirical label distribution.
 - **Regularized Information Maximization (RIM):** `L_RIM` is incorporated to utilize intrinsic information within imbalanced unlabeled demonstrations. RIM is a discriminative clustering technique that helps identify boundaries between categories.
- **Discriminator and Predictor Objectives:** The overall objective for training the discriminator `D` and predictors `Q_c`, `Q_ϵ` is `min_(D,Qc,Qϵ) L_GAIL - L_SS - L_US - L_RIM`. The discriminator `D` aims to distinguish policy-generated state transitions from expert demonstrations, providing an imitation reward `r_D`.
- **Observations and Actions:**
 - **Observation (o):** Includes command inputs (behavior mode, linear/angular velocities, jump/locomotion height), measured proprioception (roll/pitch, angular velocity, joint rotations/velocities, foot contacts), estimated proprioception (root height, linear velocities inferred by an MLP), and latent physical parameters (inferred from proprioception history).
 - **Imitation Observation (o_I):** Includes ground-truth proprioception and local foot positions for the discriminator/predictors.
 - **Action:** Target joint rotations for a PD controller.
- **Reward Function:** The BBC's policy (`π`) is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) to maximize a total reward `r = w_I * r_I + w_T * r_T`. `r_I` is the imitation reward (from `D`, `Q_c`, `Q_ϵ`), and `r_T` is a task-related reward for command following (linear/angular velocity tracking, jump/locomotion height) and additional regularization terms (e.g., joint torque, torque change, joint acceleration, joint position/velocity/torque limits, collision, action smoothness).

#### 3.3. Evolutionary Adversarial Simulator Identification (EASI)

EASI is employed to minimize the sim-to-real gap by optimizing simulator parameters using limited real-world data.
- **Mechanism:** Simulator parameter identification is framed as a search problem. An Evolutionary Strategy (ES) acts as a generator, proposing parameter distributions. A neural discriminator (`~D`) acts as an adversary, distinguishing real-world state-action transitions from simulated ones.
- **Objective:** The ES maximizes the scores assigned by `~D` to simulated transitions, guiding the evolution of physical parameters (e.g., joint PD parameters).
- **Data Collection:** An arbitrary controller (e.g., the pre-trained BBC) collects motion trajectory data (80 seconds) in the real world.
- **Efficiency:** The entire parameter search process is parallelized on a GPU-based physics simulator, completing in less than 10 minutes.
- **Fine-tuning:** After EASI, the BBC is fine-tuned in the enhanced simulator for approximately 4000 steps (around 2 hours) to ensure robustness and agility in the real world.

#### 3.4. Task-Specific Controller (TSC) Training

The TSC generates commands for the BBC to perform complex downstream tasks. It uses a privileged learning architecture (teacher-student).
- **TSC-Teacher Policy Training:**
 - **Observation (o_T):** Accesses privileged environmental information including scandots (elevation map), relative yaw angles to current and next waypoints, obstacle type (one-hot vector), and proprioception.
 - **Action (a_T):** Outputs a mixture of discrete (behavior mode) and continuous (behavior style, speed, root height) commands.
 - **Training:** Trained using Hybrid-PPO to maximize `r_TSC = w_I * r_I + w_TSC * r_TSC`. `r_I` is from the fixed BBC discriminator. `r_TSC` includes linear/yaw velocity tracking, waypoint reaching, and termination penalties, along with regularization terms.
- **TSC-Student Policy Training:**
 - **Observation:** Uses depth images as exteroceptive perception, combined with proprioceptive inputs. These are processed by a Gate Recurrent Unit (GRU) encoder to predict privileged information and generate an environment embedding.
 - **Action (ˆa_T):** An MLP processes the GRU output to generate hybrid student actions, mimicking the teacher's behavior.
 - **Objective:** Minimizes cross-entropy loss for discrete actions and mean squared error for continuous actions.
 - **Robustness to Noise:** To address real-world depth image noise, a self-supervised learning method called Bootstrap Your Own Latent (BYOL) is integrated into the depth image encoder. This maximizes similarity between augmented views of the same depth image, enhancing robustness against noise (e.g., white noise, background noise, random cropping, edge noise, Gaussian blur).

Neural network architectures are detailed, primarily using MLPs for policies and value functions, convolutional layers for proprioception history and depth images, and a GRU for the student policy's temporal integration.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings based on the deployment and evaluation of the integrated controller on a Go2 quadrupedal robot equipped with an Intel RealSense D435i depth camera.

#### 4.1. Quadrupedal Agility Challenge Performance

- The robot successfully completed a newly constructed quadrupedal agility challenge, designed in a 7 × 10 m space with six randomly placed obstacles (A-frame, bar-jump, poles, seesaw, tire-jump, tunnel). This marks the first time such a challenge was completed autonomously by a quadrupedal robot.
- The robot achieved an average speed of 1.1 m/s, completing the entire challenge in 26 seconds. It operated solely on depth perception without precise global localization, demonstrating robustness to random environmental changes.
- The Task-Specific Controller (TSC) autonomously switched between natural behaviors (trot, canter, jump) based on depth images and commands. Trot was used for lower speeds and sharp turns, canter for higher speeds, and jump for clearing obstacles.
- **Comparison with Baseline:** The proposed controller significantly outperformed a state-of-the-art baseline. In isolated obstacle environments, the proposed method achieved nearly 100% success rates across all obstacle types while maintaining higher speeds. The baseline struggled with obstacles requiring jumping (e.g., bar-jump) without contact. Even with a support box for the baseline to step onto, the proposed method achieved higher success rates and speeds.
- **Obstacle Height Robustness:** The controller maintained high success rates across varying bar-jump heights (0.05 to 0.35 m) and tire-jump heights (0.45 to 0.7 m), outperforming the baseline. Human operators, while able to control the robot, demonstrated lower overall success rates in the full challenge due to reaction time limitations. The proposed controller achieved over 78% success in the full, randomized challenge.
- The robot's historical depth vision encoding enabled anticipation of upcoming obstacle information, even when not immediately visible.

#### 4.2. Natural Jump for High-Speed Hurdling

- In a high-speed hurdling task with four bar-jump obstacles (randomly spaced 2.5 m to 3.5 m apart), the robot demonstrated a peak speed of 3.2 m/s.
- It achieved a maximum body height of 0.54 m during jumps.
- The robot autonomously transitioned from canter to jump at appropriate moments using depth perception and adjusted its stride before and after jumps by modifying velocity commands and the latent shifting variable, which is crucial for maintaining speed and avoiding collisions.

#### 4.3. Evaluation of Multimodal Behaviors of BBC

- The Basic Behavior Controller (BBC) successfully learned diverse, dog-like behaviors (walk, pace, trot, canter, jump) and continuous style variations (via the latent shifting variable `ϵ`) from raw motion capture data.
- The robot exhibited rapid response to speed commands, with an average linear speed tracking error of approximately 0.04 m/s for canter behavior within a range of 1 to 3.5 m/s.
- **Quantitative Comparison:** The proposed method achieved a lower Average Entropy (ENT) and higher Normalized Mutual Information (NMI) compared to unsupervised GAIL, InfoGAIL, and supervised ACGAIL (trained with classifier-predicted labels). This indicates better disentanglement and control over multimodal behaviors, and improved utilization of imbalanced data.
- **Ablation Studies:** Removing labeled data or the Regularized Information Maximization (RIM) technique degraded performance, confirming their effectiveness in guiding feature disentanglement and handling imbalanced data.
- **Visualization:** t-distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding (t-SNE) visualization confirmed that the behavior features learned by the proposed method were more distinctly separated by category compared to baseline methods.

#### 4.4. Evaluation of TSC in Quadrupedal Agility Challenge

- **Privileged Learning:** The privileged learning architecture was crucial for task completion. A baseline without privileged learning achieved a 0% success rate in the quadrupedal agility challenge.
- **BYOL for Robustness:** Incorporating Bootstrap Your Own Latent (BYOL) self-supervised learning into the depth image encoder significantly improved the student policy's robustness to real-world perception noise. The method with BYOL achieved a higher success rate (~78%) and obstacle recognition accuracy (~92%), along with higher task rewards, compared to a baseline without BYOL under noise interference.
- BYOL also resulted in more accurate predictions of the relative yaw angle compared to the baseline without it.
- Grad-CAM visualizations demonstrated that the BYOL-enhanced encoder consistently focused on important areas in depth images even under various noise disturbances (random rectangles, white noise, Gaussian blur), whereas the encoder without BYOL was less consistent.

#### 4.5. Evaluation of EASI in Sim2Real

- **Simulator-Reality Alignment:** Evolutionary Adversarial Simulator Identification (EASI) effectively optimized simulator parameters, reducing the sim-to-real gap.
- **Parameter Convergence:** In sim-to-sim tasks, EASI caused parameters to converge close to their true values within about 50 generations, whether the true parameters were initially within or outside the initial distribution range. In sim-to-real tasks, EASI adjusted the simulator's parameter range effectively.
- **Performance Transfer:** Post-EASI adjustment, the joint frequency spectra and kinematic trajectories in the simulator more closely matched those from real-world data, enabling direct transfer of policies to the real world with minimal performance loss.
- **Efficiency:** EASI required less than 10 minutes for the entire parameter search process, utilizing only 80 seconds of real-world data. Subsequent fine-tuning of the BBC in the enhanced simulator took approximately 2 hours.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work represents a substantial advancement in the field of quadrupedal robotics, addressing several critical challenges in achieving animal-like agility and real-world deployment. The significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Quadrupedal Agility:** The integrated controller enables quadrupedal robots to exhibit a diverse repertoire of natural, dynamic behaviors, bridging a notable gap between current robotic capabilities and the agility observed in biological counterparts. The successful autonomous completion of a complex agility challenge, featuring multiple obstacles and requiring diverse locomotion modes, demonstrates a higher level of dexterity and adaptive control than previously reported.
- **Advancements in Imitation Learning:** The development of a semi-supervised InfoGAIL framework, coupled with techniques like dynamic latent skill distribution and Regularized Information Maximization (RIM), offers a robust solution for learning multimodal and controllable behaviors from imbalanced, unstructured motion capture data. This mitigates issues like mode collapse and reduces the need for extensive manual data labeling, thereby enhancing the practical applicability of imitation learning for complex robot behaviors.
- **Efficient Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The Evolutionary Adversarial Simulator Identification (EASI) technique provides an efficient and data-sparing method for aligning simulation environments with real-world dynamics. By requiring only limited real-world data and computational time, EASI facilitates direct transfer of simulation-trained policies to physical robots with minimal performance degradation, a long-standing challenge in robot learning.
- **Robust Perception and Control:** The integration of a privileged learning architecture with self-supervised techniques (BYOL) for depth perception significantly improves the robot's ability to navigate complex environments robustly against real-world sensor noise. This allows the robot to make autonomous decisions and adapt its behavior using simple, onboard depth sensors.
- **Establishment of a Benchmark:** The introduction of the quadrupedal agility challenge serves as a novel benchmark platform, akin to dog agility competitions. This can stimulate further research and development in robot agility, providing a standardized measure for comparing different control approaches and accelerating progress in the field.
- **Broader Applicability for Quadrupedal Robots:** The enhanced agility and robustness demonstrated by this work open avenues for deploying quadrupedal robots in increasingly complex and unstructured real-world environments. This could include applications in inspection, search and rescue, logistics, or even entertainment, where traversing varied terrain and interacting with dynamic obstacles is crucial.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** The work highlights several potential extensions, such as leveraging larger-scale motion capture data, integrating more diverse visual information, simulating dynamic environmental parameters with EASI, and exploring multimodal human-robot collaboration (e.g., voice, gestures). These directions suggest a clear path for continued progress towards more intelligent and adaptive robotic systems.

In conclusion, this research not only presents a functional and high-performing integrated control system but also contributes methodological advancements in imitation learning and sim-to-real transfer, potentially shaping future directions in agile robotics.
