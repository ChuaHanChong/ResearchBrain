# Generalized Locomotion in Out-of-distribution Conditions with Robust Transformer

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University developed the Robust Locomotion Transformer (ROLT), a neural network architecture designed for generalized robot locomotion in out-of-distribution dynamic and perceptual conditions. ROLT demonstrates superior performance against baselines in handling unseen limb damage and diverse sensor noise in simulation, along with successful zero-shot transfer to a physical robot across various novel failure scenarios.

## Problem

- Robots struggle with 'distribution shift' in real-world environments, encountering dynamic gaps (unforeseen physical changes like limb damage) and perceptual gaps (novel or corrupted sensor data).
- Existing generalization methods like domain randomization are limited by the inability to simulate all possible real-world scenarios and cannot guarantee robustness to truly novel out-of-distribution (OOD) conditions.
- Adaptive control mechanisms add system complexity and may still fail with completely novel scenarios if their underlying models were not exposed to sufficiently diverse data during training.

## Method

- Proposed the **ROLT** (==Robust Locomotion Transformer==), a ==multi-modal transformer== architecture focused on network design to enhance generalization and robustness to OOD conditions.
- Introduced ==Body Tokenization== with an ==action token== to provide data-dependent position information, enabling knowledge transfer across different robot limbs for fault tolerance.
- Implemented a ==Consistent Dropout== strategy, applying the same dropout mask during rollout and update phases for both features and patch position embeddings (PEs) to improve resilience to unseen perceptual noise.

## Results

- ROLT consistently outperformed baseline methods in simulated OOD dynamic conditions, demonstrating strong generalization to unseen limb damage patterns on both quadrupedal and hexapodal robots.
- The proposed ROLT architecture exhibited superior robustness to unseen low-frequency and high-frequency perceptual noise in elevation maps, maintaining stable performance as noise levels increased.
- Achieved successful **zero-shot transfer** to a physical Unitree Go1 robot, significantly outperforming a strong baseline (**RMA**) in challenging real-world OOD scenarios such as weakened limbs, dragging a box, and navigating on rolling skates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit network architecture design can inherently enhance generalization and robustness to out-of-distribution (OOD) conditions, moving beyond reliance on extensive domain randomization or explicit adaptation mechanisms.
> - Utilizing data-dependent position information in transformer architectures, rather than fixed position embeddings, is crucial for facilitating knowledge transfer across similar robot modules (e.g., legs) and preventing overfitting to specific spatial positions.
> - A consistent dropout strategy, particularly for patch position embeddings, encourages the policy to infer reliable spatial information from the interaction of proprioception and exteroception, leading to robustness against diverse unseen perceptual noise patterns.
