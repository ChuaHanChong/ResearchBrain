# VB-Com: Learning Vision-Blind Composite Humanoid Locomotion Against Deficient Perception

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai AI Laboratory and The University of Hong Kong developed VB-Com, a composite control framework enabling humanoid robots to achieve robust locomotion in challenging environments despite significant perception deficiencies. The system maintains 82-84% goal-reaching success across varying noise levels by dynamically switching between vision-based and blind policies, demonstrating superior adaptability and real-world recovery capabilities on Unitree G1 and H1 robots.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle with robust locomotion in dynamic and unstructured environments due to their inherent instability and high susceptibility to falls.
- Existing locomotion policies face a fundamental trade-off: vision policies are proactive but brittle to real-world perception errors, while blind policies are robust but reactive and slow.
- The 'sim-to-real' gap for perceptive policies arises from noisy, incomplete, or failing sensors and difficulties in accurately modeling dynamic terrains in simulation.

## Method

- **VB-Com** employs a composite framework integrating a vision-based locomotion policy and a proprioception-only blind policy, both trained via PPO on challenging dynamic terrains.
- A compositor dynamically switches between these policies based on predictions from two concurrently trained, hardware-deployable return estimators.
- The return estimators predict future cumulative returns using only reliable historical proprioceptive observations and incorporate a dynamic threshold for robust policy selection during motion abnormalities.

## Results

- VB-Com achieved 82-**84%** goal-reaching success across all tested perception deficiency levels (0-**100%** noise types), significantly outperforming standalone vision policies which saw success drop from **73%** to **48%**.
- Real-world experiments on Unitree G1 and H1 robots demonstrated proactive obstacle avoidance with vision and rapid recovery from collisions with dynamic or unobserved obstacles by switching to the blind policy.
- The TD-based return estimator and dynamic switching threshold proved critical for robust performance, with the latter preventing severe degradation (e.g., from **48.48%** success without it).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically switching between specialized vision-based and blind policies, guided by proprioception-only return estimators, effectively combines proactive agility with reactive robustness for humanoid robots.
> - Leveraging return estimators that rely solely on reliable proprioceptive data enables intelligent policy selection, allowing the robot to trust or disregard external perception based on its estimated reliability.
> - The composite framework implicitly mitigates the sim-to-real gap for perception-dependent tasks, providing a robust fallback mechanism for real-world uncertainties and enhancing safety.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of legged locomotion control has seen significant advancements, particularly with the rise of reinforcement learning (RL). However, a persistent challenge remains in bridging the gap between simulated performance and real-world deployment, especially when robots encounter unpredictable or partially observable environments. This paper directly addresses this critical challenge, specifically for humanoid robots, which present unique difficulties.

Traditionally, locomotion policies can be broadly categorized:
- **Blind Policies (Proprioception-only)**: These policies rely solely on internal robot states (joint angles, velocities, IMU data). They are generally robust to sensor noise and environmental perception errors because they do not depend on external sensing. However, they are inherently reactive, often requiring physical contact or collisions with the terrain to adapt, limiting their speed and ability to proactively plan for obstacles. This "collision-to-adapt" mechanism is slow and can be risky.
- **Vision Policies (Perceptive)**: These policies integrate external sensor data (e.g., lidar elevation maps, depth images) to perceive the environment, allowing the robot to anticipate terrain changes, plan motions in advance, and move more quickly. While impressive, their performance is highly contingent on the accuracy and consistency of perceived external states, both in simulation and reality. Real-world perception is often noisy, incomplete, or can suffer from sensor failures, leading to misguidance and potential locomotion failures. Moreover, current simulators struggle to accurately model dynamic or deformable terrains, creating a "sim-to-real" gap for perceptive policies.

Previous research has explored combining proprioception and exteroception, primarily for quadruped robots. These methods often involve belief encoders or address deficient perception at the path planning level. However, these solutions typically focus on low-risk scenarios where a delayed response does not immediately lead to catastrophic failure. Humanoid robots, with their high degrees of freedom, bipedal instability, and higher center of gravity, are far more susceptible to unrecoverable falls when perception is deficient or when unexpected physical interactions occur. Existing perceptive humanoid locomotion studies are largely confined to static environments, heavily relying on high-quality perception modules.

VB-Com distinguishes itself by specifically targeting **humanoid robots** in **high-dynamic tasks** and providing a mechanism for **rapid recovery** against **deficient perception**. Instead of trying to make a single policy robust to *all* possible noise and deficiencies (which often leads to a "jack of all trades, master of none" scenario), it proposes a composite framework that strategically leverages the strengths of two specialized policies. This approach is novel in its explicit focus on dynamic switching for humanoids to overcome perception limitations, moving beyond static terrain traversal and low-risk scenarios. It also draws inspiration from hierarchical reinforcement learning and Q-informed policy composition, adapting these concepts to the critical and safety-sensitive domain of humanoid locomotion.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and adaptive control framework, named **VB-Com (Vision-Blind Composite Humanoid Control)**, that enables humanoid robots to navigate challenging, dynamic, and unstructured environments despite significant perception deficiencies.

The motivation behind VB-Com stems from several critical observations and limitations in current legged locomotion research:

- **Bridging the gap between reactive robustness and proactive agility**: Current approaches often force a trade-off. Blind policies are robust but slow and reactive, while vision policies are fast and proactive but brittle to perception errors. The authors aim to combine the best of both worlds.
- **Addressing the vulnerability of humanoid robots**: Humanoids are inherently less stable than quadrupeds and thus more prone to unrecoverable falls when faced with unexpected environmental changes or misleading perceptual information. Existing solutions for quadrupeds do not directly translate to the higher stakes involved with bipedal robots.
- **Handling realistic perception challenges**: In real-world environments, sensors can be noisy, suffer from occlusions, or fail entirely. Furthermore, dynamic terrains (e.g., moving obstacles, deformable surfaces) are difficult to model accurately in simulation, leading to perception discrepancies. VB-Com is motivated by the need for a system that can gracefully handle these real-world uncertainties.
- **Enabling intelligent decision-making about perception trust**: A key motivation is to empower the robot to *decide* when its visual perception is reliable enough to trust and when it should be disregarded in favor of more reliable proprioceptive data to avoid potential failures. This meta-learning capability for policy selection is crucial for robust autonomy.
- **Achieving rapid recovery**: When perception fails and the robot is in a precarious situation (e.g., stepping into an unobserved gap), the system must react quickly to prevent a fall. The goal is to provide a mechanism for rapid, safe recovery.

In essence, VB-Com is designed to endow humanoid robots with a form of "perceptual intelligence," allowing them to be agile and anticipatory when conditions permit, and resilient and reactive when perception is compromised, thereby enhancing their safety and operational capabilities in complex, real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VB-Com employs a composite framework centered around two distinct locomotion policies and a sophisticated switching mechanism driven by return estimators.

**A. System Overview**
The core idea is to train a **vision policy ($\pi_v$)** that leverages external visual observations and a **blind policy ($\pi_b$)** that relies solely on proprioceptive observations. These two policies are then integrated by a **compositor** that uses two concurrently trained **return estimators ($\pi_{e_v}, \pi_{e_b}$)**. These estimators predict the expected future cumulative return for each policy, guiding the system to choose the appropriate policy action at any given moment.

**B. Locomotion Policies ($\pi_v$ and $\pi_b$)**
Both $\pi_v$ and $\pi_b$ are trained using **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)**, a common and effective algorithm for continuous control tasks. The training focuses on challenging, highly dynamic terrains (gaps, hurdles, high walls for obstacle avoidance) rather than static stairs or discrete steps, requiring high joint velocities. A **goal-reaching formulation** is used instead of velocity tracking to suit these dynamic tasks.

1. **Observation Space**:
 - **Proprioceptive Observations ($o_p$)**: Common to both policies. Includes commands (target directions $d_1, d_2$ from waypoints, linear velocity $v_c$), joint position ($\theta$), joint velocity ($\dot{\theta}$), base angular velocity ($\omega$), and gravity direction ($g$).
 - **Visual Observations ($o_v$)**: Only for $\pi_v$. A robot-centric height map around the robot (1.2m x 0.7m), acquired from onboard lidar sensors.
 - **Domain Randomization**: Both $o_p$ and $o_v$ are subjected to noise (e.g., 10% Gaussian noise, random perception delays up to 0.5s) during training to simulate real-world sensor imperfections.
 - **Critic Network**: For stable training, the critic network (used for state evaluation) has access to *privileged information*: accurate linear velocity and a larger, noise-free height map (1.6m x 1.0m).

2. **Rewards**: Adapted from prior work, these include:
 - **Task Rewards**: Track target direction and linear velocity, penalize collisions. Obstacle avoidance is handled at the locomotion level by balancing goal-reaching and collision penalties.
 - **Regularization Rewards**: Encourage natural locomotion (e.g., penalize high linear/angular velocities, joint accelerations/velocities, torques, action rates, joint limit violations, base height deviation).
 - **Motion Style Rewards**: Promote human-like whole-body motion (e.g., feet air time, feet stumble, arm/hip/waist joint deviation, feet distance, feet lateral distance, knee lateral distance, feet ground parallel).
 - The reward scales are consistent for both policies, aiming for a unified capability to traverse various challenges.

3. **State Estimation**: An efficient, hardware-deployable state estimator is proposed to predict the next linear velocity ($v_{t+1}$) from historical proprioceptive observations ($o_{p_{t-H:t}}$). It is trained online with a regression loss, avoiding complex multi-stage training or network architectures common in other POMDP solutions.

**C. Vision-Blind Composition**
The composition allows the robot to select an action from either $\pi_v$ or $\pi_b$. The selection is framed as a Q-informed policy composition, where the probability of selecting an action from a policy is proportional to its expected cumulative return.

1. **Policy Return Estimation**: Two separate **return estimators ($\pi_{e_v}$ and $\pi_{e_b}$)** are trained concurrently with their respective locomotion policies. These estimators predict the *expected cumulative return over a switch period T* (e.g., 50 steps) for each policy.
 - **$\lambda$-return**: Used to weigh time-sequenced returns within the switch period, reducing bias and variance.
 - **TD-based Update**: The estimators are updated using a Temporal Difference (TD) approach, leveraging the advantage function ($\hat{A}(s_t)$) and value function ($V(s_t)$) from PPO.
 - **Proprioception-only Input**: Crucially, the return estimators take *only historical proprioceptive observations* ($o_{p_{t-H:t}}$) as input. This design choice makes them hardware-deployable and robust to vision misleading, as they do not rely on potentially deficient external perception.

2. **Policy Switch Mechanism**:
 - **Primary Rule**: The system ideally chooses the policy with the higher estimated return ($\hat{G}_{e_{\pi}}(s_t)$). $\pi_v$ is typically preferred due to its access to more information.
 - **Threshold Trigger ($G_{th}$)**: To handle situations where *both* policies' estimated returns decline due to perception deficiency (making $\hat{G}_{e_{\pi_b}} > \hat{G}_{e_{\pi_v}}$ hard to strictly maintain due to approximation errors and noisy $\hat{G}_{e_{\pi_v}}$), a threshold is introduced. The robot switches to $\pi_b$ if $\hat{G}_{e_{\pi_v}}(s_t) > \hat{G}_{e_{\pi_b}}(s_t) > G_{th}$ is *false*. Otherwise, it uses $\pi_v$.
 - **Dynamic Threshold**: $G_{th}$ is defined as a smoothed average of recent blind policy returns minus a hyperparameter $\alpha$. This makes the threshold adaptive.
 - **Safety Lock**: Switches are avoided at high joint velocities to prevent dangerous abrupt changes.
 - **Switch Period (T)**: Controls the duration for which a policy remains active once selected, decoupling switching actions and allowing a sequence of actions from the chosen policy.

**D. Implementation Details**
- **Humanoid Robots**: Implemented and tested on Unitree G1 (20 DoF) and H1 (19 DoF) robots.
- **Collision Links**: A subset of collision links ($C_e^l$) is enabled for simulation efficiency and to allow the blind policy to learn contact-based reactions (e.g., using hands for obstacle contact).
- **Perception & Noise**: Lidar-based elevation maps for visual input. Training noise (Gaussian noise, random delays) is introduced for $\pi_v$ to improve robustness, but the system is designed to handle *more severe* deficiencies that these training noises alone cannot cover.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of VB-Com demonstrates its superior robustness and adaptability for humanoid locomotion in challenging environments with deficient perception, both in simulation and on real hardware.

**A. Example Case and Operational Mechanics (Fig. 5)**
The paper illustrates VB-Com's operation during a 100% noise level scenario. On flat ground, the vision policy ($\pi_v$) is active, reflected by $\hat{G}_{e_{\pi_v}}(s_t)$ being consistently higher than $\hat{G}_{e_{\pi_b}}(s_t)$. When the robot encounters an unobserved obstacle or gap (due to deficient perception), both estimated returns drop sharply. This decline triggers a rapid switch to the blind policy ($\pi_b$), allowing the robot to react to the physical collision and recover. Once stable motion is regained, the vision policy often takes over again. This showcases VB-Com's ability to respond quickly to failures caused by perception deficiency while avoiding unnecessary switches.

**B. Evaluations on Different Noise Levels (Table II, Fig. 6)**
VB-Com consistently achieves high goal-reaching success (82-84%) across all tested perception deficiency levels (0%, 30%, 70%, 100% noise types, including Gaussian, shifting, and floating noise). In stark contrast, the standalone vision policy's performance degrades significantly with increasing noise, plummeting from 73% goal completion at 0% noise to 48% at 100% noise. The vision policy even struggles at 0% noise (73% success, 40% termination rate) on maximal curriculum terrains, highlighting the severity of the task.

VB-Com also demonstrates a lower collision rate when perception is accurate, and its collision rate gracefully increases, approaching that of the blind policy as noise levels rise. This indicates that VB-Com effectively leverages vision when available to minimize collisions, while seamlessly relying on blind robustness when perception is compromised. The "Reach Steps" metric, reflecting motion smoothness, shows VB-Com sometimes takes slightly more steps at higher noise levels due to the inherent delay of the switching mechanism, but still performs far better than the vision-only policy.

**C. Comparisons with Blind Policy (Table II, Fig. 6)**
The blind policy achieves a high goal completion rate (83.76%) regardless of perception, as expected. However, VB-Com achieves *fewer collision steps* when perception is accurate or partially available, demonstrating the benefits of integrating vision for proactive avoidance. As perception becomes increasingly deficient, VB-Com's collision performance converges towards that of the blind policy. This highlights that VB-Com combines the goal-reaching reliability of the blind policy with the collision-avoidance capability of the vision policy when conditions allow.

**D. Comparisons with Noisy Perceptive Training (Table II, Fig. 7, Fig. 8)**
A "Noisy Perceptive" baseline, trained with curriculum noise, achieved 80.52% goal completion but suffered from a higher collision rate (3.49%) compared to VB-Com. This suggests that simply training a single policy with various noises doesn't fully overcome the fundamental trade-off.
Specifically, VB-Com outperforms the Noisy Perceptive policy on "gap" terrains, which demand swift recovery from missed steps. The Noisy Perceptive policy, however, performs slightly better on "hurdle" situations. This differential performance underscores VB-Com's advantage in handling highly dynamic and fall-critical scenarios.
Furthermore, the Noisy Perceptive policy's training curriculum progressed slowly and failed to reach the maximum terrain levels achieved by VB-Com's sub-policies (Fig. 8-a). This indicates the inherent difficulty for a single policy to balance trusting noisy external perception versus relying on proprioception, requiring additional complexity that VB-Com's composite approach handles more efficiently.

**E. Return Estimator Evaluations (Table III, Table IV, Fig. 8)**
The proposed TD-based return estimator proved crucial. It converges stably during training (Fig. 8-b), unlike a Monte Carlo-based estimator which struggles with noise and variance, leading to poor policy composition performance (Table III). The "switch period (T)" (e.g., 50 steps) was found to be important, with excessively short periods causing policy conflicts and failures.
The dynamic threshold ($G_{th}$) for switching was also evaluated as critical (Table IV). Without $G_{th}$, the system's performance severely degrades (48.48% success, 6.24% collisions), indicating its necessity for robust switching during motion abnormalities. An $\alpha < 1.0$ (e.g., 0.5 or 0.1) provides sensitive and effective responses.

**F. Real-World Experiments (Fig. 9, Fig. 10, Fig. 11)**
Deployment on Unitree G1 and H1 robots confirmed VB-Com's practical viability:
- **Hardware Return Estimations**: For static obstacles, $\hat{G}_{e_{\pi_v}}$ remained high, and the robot used the vision policy to avoid collisions proactively. With dynamic obstacles (a person moving rapidly towards the robot), the perception module failed, leading to an initial collision. Upon collision, both $\hat{G}_{e_{\pi}}$ dropped sharply, triggering a rapid switch to $\pi_b$, allowing the robot to quickly avoid the person, demonstrating successful rapid recovery.
- **Obstacle Avoidance**: VB-Com successfully navigates two consecutive obstacles, avoiding a static one with vision and then recovering from a collision with a dynamically appearing second obstacle by switching to $\pi_b$. The blind policy makes unnecessary contact with static obstacles, while the vision policy struggles with dynamic, unperceived obstacles.
- **Deficient Perception**: With zero visual input (simulating complete perception failure), VB-Com-enabled robots successfully recover from collisions with hurdles and execute large forward steps to traverse unobserved gaps, showcasing the robust recovery capabilities learned by the blind policy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VB-Com represents a significant advancement in humanoid locomotion control, addressing the long-standing challenge of achieving both agility and robustness in complex, unpredictable real-world environments. Its potential impact is multi-faceted:

- **Enhanced Robustness for Humanoid Robots**: By dynamically switching between perceptive and proprioceptive control strategies, VB-Com provides humanoids with an unprecedented level of resilience against sensor noise, failures, and dynamic environmental changes. This is critical for practical deployment of humanoids outside of controlled lab settings.
- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap**: The framework implicitly mitigates the discrepancies between simulated and real-world perception, as the proprioception-only return estimators and blind policy offer a reliable fallback when visual data is misleading or absent in the real world.
- **Increased Safety and Reliability**: The ability to rapidly recover from situations caused by deficient perception (e.g., unobserved gaps, suddenly appearing obstacles) directly translates to safer robot operation, reducing the likelihood of falls and potential damage to the robot or its surroundings.
- **Intelligent Policy Adaptation**: The concept of using learned return estimators (driven solely by reliable proprioceptive states) to inform policy switching is a powerful paradigm for intelligent control, allowing the robot to "know" when to trust its high-level perception and when to revert to more fundamental, robust behaviors.
- **Foundational for Future Humanoid Applications**: Robust locomotion is a prerequisite for a wide array of humanoid applications, from disaster response and elder care to logistics and exploration. VB-Com moves the field closer to deploying humanoids in these challenging and dynamic scenarios.
- **Inspiration for Other Robotic Systems**: The composite control framework, where specialized sub-policies are intelligently combined based on confidence or expected returns derived from reliable state observations, could be generalized to other complex robotic systems facing similar perception and control challenges (e.g., aerial robots in GPS-denied environments, manipulators with occluded objects).
- **Efficient Training Paradigm**: The single-stage training for locomotion policies combined with concurrently trained, hardware-deployable return estimators presents an efficient approach, avoiding cumbersome multi-stage training pipelines often seen in hierarchical or belief-based RL.

**Limitations and Future Work (as noted by authors)**:
The primary limitation identified by the authors is that the performance upper bound is constrained by the simulated terrains encountered during training. Specifically, if the blind policy has not learned a suitable recovery action for a completely novel and unobserved terrain (e.g., a gap too wide for a learned "larger step"), the system may still fail. This points to the inherent challenge of generalization beyond training data. Future work aims to address this by incorporating additional sub-policies, potentially expanding the repertoire of recovery behaviors and further improving the system's adaptability to extremely novel challenges.

In conclusion, VB-Com offers a robust, efficient, and intelligent solution to a critical problem in humanoid robotics, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable in autonomous navigation within complex and unpredictable real-world environments.
