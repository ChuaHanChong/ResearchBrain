# SPARQ: Synthetic Problem Generation for Reasoning via Quality-Diversity Algorithms

> [!summary] Summary
> SPARQ introduces an algorithm for generating high-quality and diverse synthetic math problems by leveraging Quality-Diversity methods and a model-centric "solve-rate" to filter problem quality. The approach improved a Gemma-2-9b model's math reasoning accuracy by 9% and enabled successful recursive self-improvement where the model generated its own training data.

## Problem

- A scarcity of high-quality and diverse training data for large language models (LLMs) performing complex reasoning tasks, particularly in domains like mathematics.
- Existing synthetic data generation methods often rely on large "teacher" models, generate only solutions for fixed problems, or lack robust mechanisms to filter out illogical or invalid generated problems.
- The absence of a reliable ground truth signal makes it challenging to assess the quality and correctness of newly generated problem statements for LLM training.

## Method

- Introduces ==SPARQ==, an iterative pipeline that generates and filters synthetic math problem-solution pairs using a single student model for quality assessment.
- Defines a novel =="solve-rate" metric==, representing the student model's average success at solving a problem, which serves as an internal proxy for problem difficulty and quality to filter out trivial, impossible, or potentially invalid problems.
- Integrates ==Quality-Diversity (QD) algorithms== to explicitly promote the generation of problems diverse in their required skill-sets, alongside quality optimization, within the iterative generation process.

## Results

- SPARQ improved the Gemma-2-9b model's pass@1 accuracy on the MATH test set by **9%** (from **38%** to **47%**) when using the static uniform generation method.
- Training on the synthetic data also enhanced out-of-distribution generalization, boosting AIME pass@n accuracy from **20%** to **25%** (with K=96 inference samples).
- Successfully demonstrated recursive self-improvement, with a Gemma-2-9b model showing a **20%** relative performance increase over its baseline when generating and learning from its own problems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The "solve-rate" metric, while effective for filtering, is a noisy proxy for true logical validity, especially for harder problems, yet LLM fine-tuning proved robust to this moderate level of noise in training data.
> - Post-generation filtering strategies, specifically those that jointly optimize for quality and diversity, can achieve strong performance comparable to larger datasets with fewer samples.
> - Data quality primarily correlates with improvements in in-distribution performance, while data diversity is crucial for enhancing out-of-distribution generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of increasingly powerful Large Language Models (LLMs) has highlighted a critical bottleneck: the availability of vast quantities of high-quality, diverse training data. While internet-scale text corpora are abundant, data specifically tailored for complex reasoning tasks, such as mathematics, remains scarce and expensive to produce. This paper addresses this challenge by proposing a novel synthetic data generation approach.

The existing landscape of synthetic data generation for LLMs generally falls into two categories, each with limitations:
1. **Distillation-based methods:** These often involve a large, state-of-the-art "teacher" model generating solutions for a fixed set of problems, which are then used to train a smaller "student" model (e.g., Yu et al., 2024; Yue et al., 2023). A key limitation here is the reliance on a powerful oracle, which might not always be available or scalable for increasingly difficult problems. Additionally, these methods often focus on generating new *solutions* to existing problems, rather than entirely new problem statements.
2. **Solution Generation for Fixed Problems:** Other approaches (often involving Reinforcement Learning for LLMs) generate novel solutions to a pre-defined, fixed problem set (e.g., Havrilla et al., 2024b; Singh et al., 2024). While effective for improving solution quality, they do not address the fundamental scarcity of diverse problem statements themselves.
3. **Problem Generation without Quality Control:** Some methods generate new problems but lack robust mechanisms for filtering out illogical or invalid problem-solution pairs (e.g., Yue et al., 2023; Yu et al., 2024). This severely limits the scalability and utility of the generated data, as noisy or incorrect data can harm model performance.

SPARQ enters this landscape by specifically tackling the "key difficulty in problem generation: the lack of a ground truth signal which can be used to filter out illogical/invalid problems." Unlike distillation, SPARQ utilizes *only a single student model* (Gemma-2-9b) for both problem generation and quality evaluation, eliminating the need for a larger oracle. Furthermore, it goes beyond merely generating solutions by focusing on creating *novel problem-solution pairs*. Its unique contribution lies in defining a novel quality metric, the "solve-rate," which acts as a proxy for problem difficulty and correctness, enabling effective filtering.

Moreover, SPARQ significantly integrates concepts from **Quality-Diversity (QD) algorithms** into synthetic data generation. QD, traditionally used in evolutionary computation and reinforcement learning, aims to discover a diverse collection of high-performing solutions rather than a single optimal one. By applying QD principles, SPARQ explicitly seeks to generate not just high-quality problems, but also problems exhibiting high diversity in terms of underlying skills and concepts. This holistic approach distinguishes SPARQ from prior work, aiming for broader generalization and robustness in LLMs. The paper also explores the critical concept of **recursive self-improvement**, demonstrating that a model can enhance its own reasoning capabilities by generating and learning from its own problems and solutions, a crucial step toward more autonomous AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce **SPARQ (Synthetic Problem Generation for Reasoning via Quality-Diversity Algorithms)**, a novel algorithm designed to generate high-quality and diverse synthetic math problem-solution pairs.

The motivation behind SPARQ stems from several critical observations and unmet needs in the field of LLM development for reasoning tasks:

- **Data Scarcity for Reasoning:** High-quality problem statements are a paramount factor influencing the downstream performance of fine-tuned LLMs. However, acquiring such data, especially for complex reasoning domains like mathematics, is labor-intensive, expensive, and often leads to a limited diversity of problems.
- **Limitations of Existing Methods:** Current synthetic data generation methods for reasoning fall short in scalability and robustness. They either rely on distilling from large, state-of-the-art models (which limits problem complexity to the oracle's capabilities and is not truly self-improving) or generate only new solutions to existing problems (limiting problem diversity). Critically, many methods lack a robust mechanism to filter out invalid or low-quality *generated problems*, leading to noisy datasets.
- **Need for Ground Truth Proxy:** A central challenge in generating novel problems is the absence of a readily available ground truth signal to assess problem quality and correctness. This paper aims to overcome this by introducing an internal, model-based proxy for problem difficulty and quality.
- **Importance of Diversity:** Beyond quality, the diversity of training data is crucial for fostering robust generalization, particularly to out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks. Existing methods often neglect explicit diversity optimization.
- **Potential for Self-Improvement:** The ultimate goal for intelligent systems is recursive self-improvement. The authors are motivated to explore whether a single, relatively smaller model can improve its reasoning abilities by generating its own training data, minimizing reliance on external data sources or larger, more capable models.

In summary, the key objectives are:
1. To introduce the `solve-rate` of a problem-solution pair with respect to a student model as an effective measure and filtering mechanism for synthetic problem quality.
2. To present SPARQ as a new synthetic problem generation approach that directly optimizes for data quality and diversity, leading to improved downstream model performance.
3. To meticulously ablate and understand the individual and combined impacts of synthetic data quantity, quality, and diversity on both in-distribution and out-of-distribution model generalization.
4. To demonstrate the feasibility of recursive self-improvement, where an LLM can generate its own useful training problems and solutions without relying on larger teacher models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SPARQ's methodology revolves around an iterative synthetic data generation pipeline, distinct from traditional teacher-student distillation, focusing on internal quality assessment and diversity optimization.

**Core Idea: Solve-Rate as a Quality Proxy**
The central innovation is the concept of `solve-rate`. For a given problem-solution pair (Q, A) and a student model $S_{\theta_2}$, the solve-rate is calculated as the average score $S_{\theta_2}$ achieves when attempting to solve Q, based on K Monte-Carlo rollouts (i.e., multiple attempts to solve Q and checking if the output matches A).
`SOLVERATE((Q,A), S_θ) := (1/K) * Σ (is_correct(Q, A, S_k))` where $S_k \sim S_{\theta_2}(\cdot|Q)$.
This intuitively measures the difficulty of Q for $S_{\theta_2}$. A problem that $S_{\theta_2}$ consistently solves (high solve-rate) is easy, while one rarely solved (low solve-rate) is difficult or impossible.

**Defining Problem Quality:**
The `QUALITY` score is derived from the solve-rate:
`QUALITY((Q,A), S_θ) = 1 - SOLVERATE((Q,A), S_θ)` if $T_l \leq \text{SOLVERATE} \leq T_u$, else 0.
Thresholds $T_l$ (lower) and $T_u$ (upper) are introduced to filter out:
a) Impossible problems (solve-rate near 0, meaning $S_{\theta_2}$ can never solve them).
b) Invalid problems (solve-rate sometimes > 0, but the intended solution A might be incorrect).
c) Trivial problems (solve-rate near 1, too easy for the student).
This mechanism aims to select problems that are challenging but solvable for the student model. A key added benefit is that successful verifications $S_k$ generated by $S_{\theta_2}$ are directly used as training solutions, making all training solutions self-generated.

**SPARQ Pipeline (Two Phases):**
1. **Data Generation Phase:**
 - **Initialization:** A `working set` ($W^{(0)}$) of mutation candidates (initially from a seed dataset $D$) and an `archive` ($A^{(0)}$) for all generated data are initialized.
 - **Iteration:** In each round, a batch of parent pairs ($Q, A$) is sampled from $W^{(t)}$ using a mutation selection distribution $C^{(t)}$.
 - **Mutation:** A `generator model` ($G_{\theta_1}$) produces "child" mutations ($Q', A'$) from the parents.
 - **Quality Assessment:** The `QUALITY` of the new children is measured using the solve-rate with $S_{\theta_2}$.
 - **Working Set Update:** An update function $U^{(t)}$ incorporates high-quality new mutations into $W^{(t)}$ and removes low-quality data, producing $W^{(t+1)}$.
 - **Archive Accumulation:** All new mutations are added to $A^{(t)}$.
2. **Data Filtration Phase:** After all generation rounds, the `archive` $A$ is filtered to retain only problem-solution pairs with `QUALITY > 0`. For each remaining high-quality problem Q, all its successful verifications $S_k$ by $S_{\theta_2}$ are paired with Q to form the final synthetic training dataset ($Q, S_k$).

**Measuring Data Diversity:**
Diversity is measured using `skill-sets`. Each problem-solution pair (Q, A) is associated with an alphabetically ordered k-tuple of semantic skills (e.g., algebra, pigeonhole principle) required to solve Q, denoted $\Phi((Q,A))$. The diversity measure `DIV` for a set of pairs is simply the number of unique skills present in their skill-sets.

**Variations on Data Generation (affecting `C` and `U`):**
- **Static Uniform:** Samples uniformly from the seed dataset ($D$); working set $W$ remains fixed to $D$.
- **Static Diverse:** Samples uniformly over skill-sets within the seed dataset; $W$ remains fixed to $D$.
- **Dynamic Uniform:** Samples uniformly from $W$; $W$ is dynamically updated to contain the highest-quality samples from the archive.
- **Dynamic Diverse (QD):** Combines dynamic updates with skill-set-based partitioning of $W$. Samples uniformly over skill-sets, and new high-quality samples are inserted into their corresponding skill-set classes, while enforcing a uniform population limit per class. This aims to maximize diverse, high-quality problems.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Domain:** Math reasoning problems.
- **Seed Dataset:** 7.5K training samples from the MATH dataset.
- **Student Model ($S_{\theta_2}$):** Gemma-2-9b (fine-tuned on the seed dataset).
- **Generator Model ($G_{\theta_1}$):** Gemma-2-27b-it (default, for general experiments) or Gemma-2-9b/2B (for recursive self-improvement).
- **Solve-Rate Rollouts:** K=16 verifications.
- **Quality Thresholds:** $T_l=0.1$, $T_u=0.9$.
- **Diversity Skills:** Top 100 most common skills, max 3 skills per set, identified by Gemma-2-9b-it.
- **Generation Scale:** 5K steps, 64 samples/batch -> 320K synthetic problems generated. This involves ~5 million verification attempts by $S_{\theta_2}$. Final filtered training data averages ~80K unique problems, leading to ~500K (Q,S) problem-verification pairs.
- **Evaluation:** In-distribution performance on the MATH test set (pass@1 accuracy) and out-of-distribution (OOD) performance on the AIME benchmark.
- **Ablations:** Conducted by taking a large archive generated by Static Uniform method and then *filtering* it to create datasets with varying degrees of quality and diversity to isolate their impacts on performance.
- **Recursive Self-Improvement Experiments:** $S_{\theta_2}$ (Gemma-2-9b or Gemma-2-2B) also acts as $G_{\theta_1}$.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding the efficacy of SPARQ and the impact of data quality and diversity on LLM reasoning capabilities:

1. **Significant Performance Improvement via Synthetic Data:** Training LLMs on synthetically generated data from SPARQ consistently improved performance over a supervised fine-tuning (SFT) baseline. The **static uniform generation method** showed the most substantial improvement, boosting the Gemma-2-9b student model's pass@1 accuracy on the MATH test set by an absolute **9% (from 38% to 47%)**. This demonstrates the overall effectiveness of the `solve-rate` based filtering and synthetic data approach.

2. **Data Quantity Scaling Laws:** The study confirmed the existence of data scaling laws for synthetically generated problems. Tripling the amount of generated problems led to a notable 1.5% performance increase, indicating that more high-quality synthetic data continues to benefit downstream model generalization.

3. **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Generalization Benefits:** The synthetic data also improved OOD generalization. For example, the static uniform method improved AIME pass@n accuracy from 20% to 25% (with K=96 inference samples). This suggests that the generated problems do not merely overfit to the initial seed dataset but enable broader reasoning capabilities.

4. **Diversity Generation vs. Downstream Performance:**
 - **Dynamic Diverse (QD) methods** were demonstrably most effective at generating truly diverse data, discovering significantly more unique skill combinations (e.g., 5000 new combinations in the archive compared to 4250 for static uniform).
 - However, despite generating more diverse data, **static uniform models consistently outperformed dynamic diverse models** in both in-distribution (47% vs. 44%) and OOD performance. The authors hypothesize this might be due to the "hackability" of the quality measure in dynamic methods, where the algorithm might reward and propagate high-quality but logically invalid problems.

5. **Quality of Generated Problems and Noise:**
 - A surprising finding was that **problems with higher "quality" (lower solve-rate, meaning harder for the student model) were more likely to be logically invalid** when cross-verified by a stronger SOTA model (Gemini-2.5-flash). This suggests the `solve-rate` metric, while effective for filtering, is a noisy proxy for true logical validity.
 - Despite this noise, training on higher-quality (harder) samples consistently led to better downstream performance, indicating that **fine-tuning is robust to a moderate level of invalid reasoning/noise** in the training data.

6. **Impact of Post-Generation Filtering:** When conducting ablations by *filtering* a large, fixed archive (generated by static uniform) for specific quality and diversity levels:
 - **Joint Quality-Diversity (QD) Filtering performs best:** A dataset specifically filtered to optimize both quality and diversity (post-generation) achieved comparable performance (45.5% on MATH with 32K samples) to the best static uniform model trained on a much larger (500K) dataset. This indicates that the *filtering strategy* is crucial, even if the *generation strategy* isn't explicitly optimizing for QD throughout.
 - **Quality for In-Distribution, Diversity for OOD:** Filtering for higher quality data strongly correlated with better in-distribution performance. Conversely, filtering for more diverse data (even without explicit quality optimization) led to superior OOD generalization, especially as inference compute budgets increased. This clearly separates the roles of these data attributes.

7. **Recursive Self-Improvement Demonstrated:** The paper successfully showed that LLMs can self-improve by generating their own problems. A Gemma-2-9b model, acting as both generator and student, achieved a **20% relative improvement** over its SFT baseline, while a 2B model saw a 15% relative improvement. Furthermore, scaling up the generator model size (e.g., from 2B to 27B) led to additional performance gains, implying that a more capable generator produces more useful self-generated problems.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SPARQ presents a significant advancement in the field of synthetic data generation for large language models, with profound implications for improving LLM reasoning capabilities and fostering AI autonomy.

1. **Scalable and Autonomous Data Generation:** The most immediate impact of SPARQ is its ability to generate high-quality and diverse problem-solution pairs for complex reasoning tasks without relying on expensive human annotation or larger, more powerful "teacher" models. By using a single model for both generation and quality evaluation, SPARQ offers a truly scalable and potentially autonomous pipeline for creating training data, critical for domains like mathematics where ground truth data is scarce.

2. **Novel Quality Assessment Mechanism:** The introduction and validation of `solve-rate` as a proxy for problem difficulty and quality is a key methodological contribution. This internal, model-centric evaluation mechanism bypasses the need for external ground truth, offering a practical way to filter and curate synthetic data. While the paper acknowledges its "noisy" nature, demonstrating its effectiveness despite logical inaccuracies in harder problems is a crucial insight into LLM training robustness.

3. **Disentangling Data Attributes:** The comprehensive ablations on data quantity, quality, and diversity provide invaluable insights for future research and practical application. Clearly demonstrating that higher quality data primarily benefits in-distribution performance, while greater diversity is crucial for robust out-of-distribution generalization, offers a roadmap for designing targeted synthetic datasets to achieve specific generalization goals. This understanding can guide fine-tuning strategies and dataset curation efforts across various LLM applications.

4. **Advancement in Recursive Self-Improvement:** The successful demonstration of an LLM self-improving by generating its own problems and solutions marks a significant step towards more autonomous and continually learning AI systems. This reduces reliance on curated human data or external computational resources, potentially leading to a virtuous cycle where models iteratively improve their own capabilities. This aligns with broader research ambitions of creating general-purpose AI that can learn and evolve independently.

5. **Broad Applicability and Future Directions:** While demonstrated in the math domain, the core principles of SPARQ – using model-centric quality proxies, diversity-driven generation, and iterative refinement – are broadly applicable to other complex reasoning tasks. This framework could pave the way for generating synthetic data for scientific discovery, code generation, logical puzzles, and more, thus accelerating progress in various specialized AI applications. Future work on refining QD-inspired generation algorithms to improve the yield rate of high-quality samples and mitigating the "hackability" of the quality measure promises to further enhance SPARQ's effectiveness.

In essence, SPARQ offers a robust and adaptable framework for synthetic data generation that could democratize access to high-quality training data for reasoning tasks, propel LLMs towards greater self-sufficiency, and deepen our understanding of the fundamental properties of training data for robust AI generalization.
