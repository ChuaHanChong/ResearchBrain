# The Lie We Tell: Correcting the Euclidean Fallacy in Vision Language Action Policies via Score Matching on Tangent Space

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from National Taiwan University and collaborating institutions developed Lie Diffuser Actor (LDA), a diffusion framework that generates robotic manipulation trajectories by operating directly on the SE(3) Lie group. This method intrinsically corrects the "Euclidean Fallacy" in existing policies, ensuring geometrically valid, coordinate-frame equivariant, and kinematically optimal robot poses, leading to improved manipulation performance in both simulated and real-world tasks.

## Problem

- Many state-of-the-art diffusion-based Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies represent SE(3) poses (rigid-body transformations) as flat Euclidean vectors, ignoring the intrinsic curved Riemannian manifold geometry.
- This "Euclidean Fallacy" leads to "manifold drift," where intermediate diffusion states violate geometric constraints (e.g., non-orthogonal rotation matrices), requiring ad-hoc post-hoc corrections.
- The Euclidean formulation results in "broken equivariance" to coordinate frame changes and generates non-geodesic trajectories, leading to suboptimal kinematic costs and unnatural robot motions.

## Method

- Introduced Lie Diffuser Actor (LDA), a diffusion framework that defines the generative process directly on the SE(3) Lie group using left-invariant Stochastic Differential Equations (SDEs).
- The forward noising process is formulated multiplicatively as `g_t = g_0 * exp(σ_t * ξ)`, where `exp` is the exponential map from the Lie algebra se(3) to SE(3), ensuring all intermediate poses `g_t` remain valid SE(3) elements by construction.
- The neural network predicts a 6-dimensional twist in the flat Lie algebra se(3), which is then mapped via the exponential map for denoising updates, intrinsically maintaining geometric validity without post-hoc projections.

## Results

- LDA improved the average task length on the CALVIN benchmark by **7.3%** (from **3.27** to **3.51**) in zero-shot transfer scenarios and demonstrated enhanced scalability and robustness in multi-environment training.
- In real-world robotic tasks, LDA achieved higher success rates on tasks requiring precise geometric control, reaching **100%** on "Move Doll Platform" (vs. **90%** for baseline) and **75%** on "Sort Blocks" (vs. **55%**).
- Quantitative analysis showed that LDA maintained rotation matrix orthogonality (error ~10^-7) and unit quaternion norm at machine precision throughout the diffusion process, while Euclidean baselines exhibited significant geometric violations (up to O(10^0)).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Approximating the curved SE(3) manifold with Euclidean vector space operations fundamentally misrepresents rigid-body transformations, causing geometric inconsistencies and reduced performance in VLA policies.
> - By formulating diffusion directly on the Lie group using left-invariant SDEs, the framework intrinsically guarantees geometric constraint satisfaction and ensures coordinate-frame equivariance throughout the generative process.
> - Operating in the tangent space (Lie algebra) for score prediction allows the model to generate kinematically optimal trajectories corresponding to screw motions, improving the smoothness and efficiency of robot actions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies for robotic manipulation, specifically focusing on pose trajectory generation. Recent advancements in VLA policies, particularly those based on diffusion models, have demonstrated success in enabling robots to execute complex tasks. These diffusion-based policies often treat entire action sequences as samples from a generative model, offering advantages in capturing multimodal behaviors and maintaining long-horizon consistency.

However, a recurring issue in these state-of-the-art methods is the representation of Special Euclidean group (SE(3)) poses. SE(3), which describes rigid-body transformations (rotations and translations), is a curved Riemannian manifold. Many existing diffusion policies, including trajectory-level approaches, approximate SE(3) poses as flat Euclidean vectors (e.g., R^12) and apply additive Gaussian noise. This approximation, termed the "Euclidean Fallacy" by the authors, neglects the intrinsic geometric structure of SE(3).

Prior work in VLA has explored various approaches, from token generation methods that introduce quantization errors, to hierarchical methods that separate semantic reasoning from low-level control but still suffer from geometric inconsistencies. While equivariant policy learning has sought to embed geometric structure into neural architectures (e.g., using SIM(3) or SE(3) equivariance in encoders), the generative process itself often remains Euclidean, leading to a fundamental asymmetry. Separately, Riemannian generative models have laid theoretical foundations for diffusion on general manifolds, but their application to robotics, particularly for Lie groups, has been less explored in the context of stochastic dynamics for multimodal action generation.

This research aims to bridge the gap by integrating intrinsic Lie group operations into the diffusion process itself, providing a geometrically principled foundation for VLA policies. It seeks to correct the "Euclidean Fallacy" by operating directly on the SE(3) manifold, thereby ensuring geometric validity throughout the generative process and enhancing the reliability and generalizability of robotic manipulation policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this work is to address and correct a fundamental geometric inconsistency, termed the "Euclidean Fallacy," prevalent in diffusion-based Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies for robotic manipulation. The authors identify that current state-of-the-art diffusion policies represent SE(3) poses (rigid body configurations) as flat Euclidean vectors (e.g., R^12) and apply standard additive Gaussian noise. This approximation is deemed structurally incompatible with the curved Riemannian manifold nature of SE(3).

The specific problems and motivations arising from this "Euclidean Fallacy" are detailed as follows:

1. **Manifold Drift:** When SE(3) poses, particularly rotation matrices from SO(3), are treated as Euclidean vectors and subjected to additive Gaussian noise, the intermediate states generated during the diffusion process often violate the underlying geometric constraints. For instance, adding Gaussian noise to a rotation matrix results in a non-orthogonal matrix with high probability. This "manifold drift" forces the neural network to implicitly learn and apply correction mechanisms (e.g., SVD orthogonalization or quaternion normalization) rather than focusing solely on the semantics of manipulation. This introduces an unnecessary computational burden and potential inaccuracies.

2. **Broken Equivariance:** The Euclidean formulation implies that the noise distribution used in the diffusion process does not transform covariantly under rigid transformations of the workspace. Consequently, the learned score function, which guides the denoising process, becomes dependent on the chosen coordinate frame. This means that if the robot's environment or its own base frame changes, the policy might perform differently or require re-training, hindering generalization and robustness in varied deployment scenarios. An ideal robotic policy should be intrinsic to the task, independent of arbitrary coordinate system choices.

3. **Non-Geodesic Trajectories and Suboptimal Kinematic Cost:** Euclidean interpolation methods, which underlie additive Gaussian noise in a flat space, fail to capture the natural, kinematically optimal motions on SE(3). The shortest path between two rigid body poses on SE(3) is a "screw motion," which combines rotation about an axis with translation along that same axis (Chasles' theorem). Euclidean interpolation, by contrast, can produce paths that are not geodesics, leading to trajectories with excessive angular jerk, unnatural movements, and higher kinematic costs. This can result in less efficient, less smooth, or even physically unrealizable robot motions.

To address these issues, the authors propose **Lie Diffuser Actor (LDA)**, a diffusion framework designed to operate intrinsically on SE(3). The core objectives of LDA are to:
- Eliminate manifold drift by construction, ensuring all generated poses remain valid elements of SE(3).
- Guarantee coordinate-frame equivariance, making the learned policy robust to changes in the reference frame.
- Generate kinematically optimal (geodesic) trajectories that correspond to natural screw motions.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to enhance the reliability, generalizability, and kinematic efficiency of VLA policies for robotic manipulation, moving beyond heuristic post-hoc corrections to a geometrically principled foundation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of Lie Diffuser Actor (LDA) fundamentally redefines the diffusion process to operate directly on the Special Euclidean group SE(3), a Lie group. This involves a shift from Euclidean vector space operations to intrinsic manifold operations, grounded in Lie group theory.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Geometry of SE(3) and Lie Algebra

The paper first reviews the mathematical framework. SE(3) is the group of rigid-body transformations, combining rotation (R ∈ SO(3)) and translation (t ∈ R^3) into 4x4 homogeneous matrices. It is a 6-dimensional Lie group and a curved Riemannian manifold. Standard Euclidean operations (e.g., vector addition) are not geometrically defined on SE(3).
The **Lie algebra se(3)** is the tangent space to SE(3) at its identity element. Elements of se(3) are 6-dimensional "twists" ξ = (ω, v), representing infinitesimal rigid motions (angular velocity ω, linear velocity v). Unlike SE(3), se(3) is a flat vector space where linear operations are well-defined.
The **exponential map exp: se(3) → SE(3)** bridges these spaces, mapping a twist to a finite rigid transformation. A critical property is that exp(ξ) is guaranteed to be a valid element of SE(3), satisfying all manifold constraints.

#### 3.2. Problem Formulation: Euclidean vs. Intrinsic Perturbation Models

- **Euclidean Formulation (Baseline):** Standard diffusion policies represent poses as flattened R^12 vectors and use additive Gaussian noise: `x_t = x_0 + σ_t * ϵ`, where `ϵ ~ N(0, I)`. This approach leads to:
 1. Violation of SO(3) constraints (e.g., non-orthogonal rotation matrices).
 2. Non-covariant noise distributions under coordinate changes.
- **Intrinsic Formulation (LDA):** LDA defines diffusion directly on the SE(3) manifold using left-invariant Stochastic Differential Equations (SDEs). The forward process for a pose `g_t ∈ SE(3)` is: `g_t = g_0 * exp(σ_t * ξ)`, where `ξ ~ N(0, I_6)`. This formulation ensures `g_t ∈ SE(3)` by construction due to the exponential map and the closure property of SE(3) under multiplication. This multiplicative perturbation model ensures outputs remain on the manifold and induces coordinate-frame equivariance.

#### 3.3. Geometric Cost of Euclidean Embeddings (Motivational Experiments)

Before detailing LDA, the paper presents experiments demonstrating the limitations of Euclidean embeddings. They compare a baseline (3D Diffuser Actor) that predicts 9D rotation matrices with post-hoc SVD orthogonalization against their method.
- **Rotation Constraint Violation:** Quantification using orthogonality error `∥R^T R - I∥_F`. Results show that the baseline exhibits significantly higher orthogonality violations (e.g., 5.7% to 11.8% higher across percentiles) and greater temporal variance throughout trajectories. LDA maintains stable, low violations near floating-point precision, as the exponential map inherently guarantees valid rotations. This empirical evidence motivates the core design choice of operating on the Lie algebra.

#### 3.4. Lie Diffuser Actor (LDA) Framework: Diffusion via Left-Invariant SDEs

The forward noising process is defined by a left-invariant SDE on the Lie group: `dg_t = g_t * (Σ_{i=1 to 6} σ_t * E_i ◦ dW_i_t)`, where `{E_i}` is an orthonormal basis for se(3) and `{W_i_t}` are independent Wiener processes. In discrete form, this is `g_t = g_0 * exp(σ_t * ξ)` with `ξ ~ N(0, I_6)`.
- **Manifold Drift Elimination (Proposition 4.1):** This construction ensures `g_t ∈ SE(3)` almost surely for all `t`. The exponential map always produces valid SE(3) elements, and SE(3) is closed under multiplication.

#### 3.4.2. Reverse Process and Equivariance

The reverse-time dynamics are derived to reconstruct the original `g_0` from `g_t`. It involves a learned score function `s_θ: SE(3) × [0, T] → se(3)`: `dg_t = g_t * (σ_t^2 * s_θ(g_t, t) dt + σ_t * d¯B_t)`. For sampling, a discrete update `g_{t-Δt} = g_t * exp(σ_t^2 * s_θ(g_t, t)Δt + σ_t * √Δt * ζ)` is used.
- **Left-Invariant Equivariance (Theorem 4.2):** The optimal score function `s_θ` satisfies `s_θ(h * g, t) = Ad_h(s_θ(g, t))` for any rigid transformation `h ∈ SE(3)`. `Ad_h` is the adjoint representation, which describes how twists transform under changes of reference frame. This ensures the learned policy is intrinsic to the task and robust to coordinate frame variations.

#### 3.4.3. Kinematic Optimality

The deterministic probability flow ODE (`dg_t/dt = g_t * σ_t^2 * s_θ(g_t, t)`) connects to Riemannian geometry.
- **Geodesic Trajectories (Proposition 4.3):** When the score function is constant (`s_θ(g_t, t) = ξ*`), the probability flow generates geodesics on SE(3) under the bi-invariant metric. These geodesics correspond to screw motions, which are kinematically optimal paths (constant angular and linear velocities). While the learned score varies, the intrinsic formulation biases towards geodesic-like behavior, leading to smoother motions.

#### 3.5. Neural Architecture

LDA extends the 3D Diffuser Actor framework (Ke et al., 2024) with specific modifications.
- **Geometric Context Encoding:** RGB-D camera observations are back-projected into a point cloud, processed by a Graph Attention Transformer (GAT) for spatially-grounded geometric features. Language instructions are encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder. Cross-attention fuses these modalities into a unified context representation `C`.
- **Iterative Denoising Transformer:** A Transformer backbone refines noisy pose trajectories `g_t` over a horizon `H`. Poses are tokenized (positional embeddings for translation, axis-angle for rotation via MLPs, gripper state). Self-attention models temporal dependencies, and cross-attention incorporates the `C` context.
- **Tangent Space Prediction Head:** The key modification. The output head predicts a 6-dimensional twist `ξ_h = (ω_h, v_h) ∈ R^6` for each waypoint `h` using separate MLPs for angular and linear velocity. The denoising update is `g_h_{t-1} = g_h_t * exp(-β_t * ξ_h)`. The exponential map ensures `g_h_{t-1}` is a valid SE(3) element, eliminating post-hoc projections. Gripper actions are predicted by a separate sigmoid classifier.
- **Training Objective:** Denoising score matching in se(3). For a ground-truth trajectory `g_0`, noisy poses `g_h_t = g_h_0 * exp(σ_t * ξ_h)` are generated. The loss function is: `L = λ_s * E_{t,ξ}[Σ_h ∥s_θ(g_h_t, t) - ξ_h∥^2] + λ_p * L_pos + λ_g * L_grip`. This directly trains the network to predict the noise twist `ξ_h`, combined with auxiliary losses for translation accuracy (`L_pos`) and gripper state (`L_grip`).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluations consistently demonstrate the benefits of the Lie Diffuser Actor (LDA) framework, both in simulation and on real-world robotic systems.

#### 4.1. Long-Horizon Manipulation Performance on CALVIN

- **ABC→D Protocol:** LDA was evaluated on the CALVIN benchmark for zero-shot transfer to an unseen environment (D). The method improved the average task length from 3.27 (3D Diffuser Actor baseline) to 3.51 (+7.3%). Success rates (SR1-SR5) also saw consistent improvements across tasks. For example, SR3 increased from 63.9% to 70.3%, and SR5 from 41.2% to 46.2%.
- **Ablation Studies:** Controlled ablations indicated that both intrinsic Lie group diffusion and the geometry-aware GAT encoder contributed independently to the performance gains. Replacing only Euclidean diffusion with Lie group diffusion improved performance, as did augmenting the baseline's 3D relative attention with the GAT encoder. The full LDA, combining both, achieved the highest success rates and chain lengths.
- **ABCD→D Setting:** When trained on all four environments, the Euclidean baseline exhibited instability and limited generalization. LDA demonstrated superior scalability and robustness, with even ablated variants outperforming the baseline. This is attributed to the baseline's sensitivity to coordinate-frame variations, which are exacerbated by environment diversity, while LDA’s geometric consistency prevents compounding errors.

#### 4.2. Real Robot Validation

LDA was deployed on a real robot arm across four manipulation tasks involving varying degrees of rotation-translation coupling: Move Doll Platform, Put Block in Box, Sort Blocks, and Stack Cups.
- **Performance:** LDA achieved superior success rates on tasks demanding high geometric fidelity and precise orientation control. It attained 100% success on "Move Doll Platform" (vs. 90% for baseline), 75% on "Sort Blocks" (vs. 55%), and 60% on "Stack Cups" (vs. 55%). For "Put Block in Box," performance was comparable (75% for LDA vs. 80% for baseline), where unconstrained Euclidean exploration might offer a slight advantage in simpler insertion.
- **Interpretation:** These gains confirm that Lie group diffusion effectively mitigates geometric drift and provides more reliable pose control in real-world settings, where sensor noise and actuation errors can accumulate.

#### 4.3. Diffusion Manifold Comparison

An analysis of intermediate rotation predictions during the reverse diffusion process (from noisy state to clean target) empirically demonstrated the manifold-preserving capabilities of LDA.
- **Qualitative Analysis (Quaternion Trajectories):** When projected onto the unit sphere S^3, Euclidean diffusion trajectories exhibited erratic behavior and frequently departed from the unit sphere surface, particularly in early, noisy stages. LDA trajectories, however, remained precisely on the manifold, following geodesic paths that respect the curved geometry of rotation space.
- **Quantitative Analysis (Constraint Violations):**
 - **Orthogonality Error (`∥R^T R - I∥_F`):** The Euclidean baseline showed substantial violations (up to O(10^0)) throughout diffusion. LDA maintained errors at floating-point precision (∼10^-7).
 - **Determinant Error (`|det(R) - 1|`):** Similar to orthogonality, Euclidean methods showed significant determinant errors, while LDA kept them at machine precision.
 - **Quaternion Norm (`∥q∥`):** Euclidean outputs deviated significantly from the unit norm (0.5 to 2.0). LDA maintained a unit norm throughout, eliminating the need for post-hoc normalization.
- **Implications:** The results highlight that geometric constraints are not just theoretical but manifest as measurable violations during inference in Euclidean methods. Post-hoc projections, while common, introduce training-inference mismatch, amplify errors (especially for near-degenerate matrices), and break generative consistency by altering the reverse SDE in an un-accounted manner. LDA avoids these issues by natively operating on the tangent space, where every step is geometrically valid.

#### 4.4. Denoising Stability and Look-ahead Consistency

The stability of the model's estimate of the final clean action (`x_0^(t)`) during reverse diffusion was analyzed using "Geodesic Jitter" (angular distance between consecutive look-ahead predictions).
- **Stability:** The Euclidean baseline exhibited nearly an order of magnitude higher jitter than LDA throughout the reverse diffusion process, particularly in early noisy steps. This instability is attributed to manifold drift, where unconstrained updates in R^12 and subsequent orthogonalization amplify small network errors.
- **LDA Performance:** LDA, by diffusing natively on the manifold, achieved smooth, monotonic refinement of the target action. This stability is crucial for consistent control signals and preventing jerky robot behavior.

#### 4.5. Cross-Architecture Validation

To isolate the benefit of SE(3) score matching from architectural specifics, the Lie formulation was transplanted onto OpenVLA-OFT (a 7B-parameter VLA model with an MLP action head, without 3D scene representation).
- **Results:** On the LIBERO-Long protocol, OpenVLA-OFT with Lie Score Matching achieved a success rate of 94.13%, surpassing OpenVLA-OFT with Euclidean Score Matching (93.87%) and the original OpenVLA-OFT MLP baseline (92.20%). This confirms that the benefit of SE(3) score matching is intrinsic to the Lie formulation itself, independent of the perceptual backbone.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a geometrically principled advancement in the field of robotic manipulation policies, particularly for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The primary significance of the Lie Diffuser Actor (LDA) lies in its direct and intrinsic approach to handling rigid-body transformations (SE(3) poses), which are fundamental to robot control.

The introduction and correction of the "Euclidean Fallacy" address a foundational flaw in many existing diffusion-based VLA policies that approximate the curved SE(3) manifold as a flat Euclidean space. By formulating diffusion directly on the Lie group through left-invariant Stochastic Differential Equations (SDEs), LDA provides several theoretical and practical advantages:

1. **Enhanced Reliability:** The elimination of "manifold drift" ensures that all generated poses are geometrically valid (e.g., rotation matrices remain orthogonal). This removes the need for heuristic, computationally expensive, and potentially error-amplifying post-hoc corrections (like SVD orthogonalization or quaternion normalization). This inherent geometric consistency is expected to lead to more stable and predictable robot behavior, especially in long-horizon or complex tasks where small errors can compound.

2. **Improved Generalization:** The guarantee of coordinate-frame equivariance ensures that the learned policy is intrinsic to the manipulation task rather than dependent on arbitrary coordinate system choices. This property facilitates zero-shot generalization to novel workspace configurations, varying object placements, or different robot base positions without requiring re-training or extensive data augmentation. This can significantly reduce the data requirements and deployment costs for robotic systems.

3. **Kinematically Optimal Trajectories:** The generation of Riemannian geodesics, corresponding to natural screw motions, implies that LDA produces kinematically optimal and smoother robot trajectories. This can reduce angular jerk and lead to more energy-efficient and safer movements, resembling human-like dexterity more closely. This has implications for reducing wear and tear on robotic hardware and improving the efficiency of operations.

4. **Broader Applicability:** The method's benefits are demonstrated across both simulation and real-robot experiments, and its efficacy is validated even when integrated into different architectural backbones (e.g., OpenVLA-OFT). This suggests that the Lie group diffusion principle is broadly applicable to various VLA architectures, offering a general framework for geometrically consistent action generation.

**Potential Impact:**
The enhanced reliability, generalization, and kinematic efficiency offered by LDA could accelerate the responsible deployment of robotic systems in various domains, including:
- **Manufacturing and Logistics:** More robust and adaptable robots can perform complex assembly, sorting, and handling tasks in dynamic environments.
- **Service Robotics:** Improved precision and safety for robots operating in human environments (e.g., assistive robotics, healthcare).
- **Exploration and Inspection:** More reliable control for robots navigating and manipulating in unstructured or hazardous environments.

While increased automation may raise concerns about workforce displacement, the authors suggest that safer, more predictable, and reliable robot behavior—enabled by methods that inherently respect physical constraints—is a prerequisite for responsible and beneficial integration of robotics into society. The work contributes to the foundational understanding of how to build more capable and robust intelligent robotic systems.
