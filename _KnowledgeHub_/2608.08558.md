# Vid2WAM: Distilling Video Diffusion Priors into World Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Vid2WAM distills a large video diffusion teacher into a compact World Action Model. Teacher-generated task-conditioned rollouts are re-encoded into student latents to supervise future prediction, while an inverse dynamics model recovers pseudo-actions, and source-aware residual adapters isolate noisy synthetic labels from real ones. Teacher and IDM are discarded at deployment, lifting novel-task success on RoboTwin 2.0 by 9.7 points at unchanged latency.

## Problem

- Existing world action models draw both future-video and action supervision from recorded target-task expert trajectories, so generalization stays bounded by costly teleoperated demonstration coverage.
- Video-generation pipelines that label rollouts only with inverse dynamics bottleneck the teacher's predictive knowledge through an imperfect action-recovery model, discarding the generated futures themselves as supervision.
- Deploying a video generator online adds heavy generation latency and exposes control to rollout errors; naive mixing of pseudo and expert actions causes interference.

## Method

- Video-teacher adaptation: a ==Wan2.1-14B== LVP checkpoint is lightly fine-tuned on in-domain demonstration videos, then frozen to generate task-conditioned future rollouts offline from an observation plus instruction.
- ==Dual-channel distillation==: generated frames are re-encoded by the student ==VAE== into future latents supervising the video branch, while a ==ResNet-50== ==inverse dynamics model== yields pseudo-actions and states.
- ==Source-aware residual action adapters==: zero-initialized bottleneck adapters wrap a shared action backbone with separate real and pseudo parameters, trained by weighted ==flow-matching== losses; only the real adapter survives inference.

## Results

- On the RoboTwin 2.0 novel subset Vid2WAM reaches **54.7%** clean and **55.3%** randomized success, beating Fast-WAM by **9.7** and **12.5** points without target-task demonstrations.
- LIBERO low-data average rises to **89.7%** versus **87.3%** for Fast-WAM, and LIBERO-Plus overall robustness reaches **39.0%** low-data and **48.1%** novel, above all baselines.
- Ablations: future-latent-only distillation beats pseudo-action-only by **1.8** points, while an online teacher controller scores just **71.3%** at **4894ms** latency versus **212.5ms** for the student.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Future supervision need not come from target-task demonstrations; a generative video prior can supply it offline, decoupling policy generalization from teleoperation budget.
> - Route generated rollouts through two channels: latent futures preserve predictive structure that inverse-dynamics pseudo-labels compress away and partly corrupt.
> - Isolating noisy synthetic supervision in source-specific residual adapters beats naive data mixing; a stronger online teacher is not automatically a better controller.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of robot policies capable of generalizing across diverse tasks, objects, and environments represents a central objective in embodied artificial intelligence. Current research in this area broadly follows two complementary trajectories.

One direction involves Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which leverage extensive pretraining on internet-scale vision-language datasets. These models aim to transfer broad semantic and linguistic understanding to robotic control, enabling robots to interpret natural language instructions and adapt to varied visual contexts (e.g., Zitkovich et al. 2023; Kim et al. 2025; Black et al. 2024).

The second direction focuses on video-based policies and World Action Models (WAMs). These models integrate future prediction capabilities into the policy learning process. By anticipating future visual dynamics, WAMs provide dense spatiotemporal supervision over motion, interaction, and task progression, which extends beyond the static semantic supervision or instantaneous action labels typically used in VLA models (e.g., Du et al. 2023; Wu et al. 2024; Hu et al. 2025). A significant advantage of WAMs lies in this future-predictive supervision, which generates rich temporal learning signals that can enhance action representations, even when explicit future rollouts are not required during deployment (Li et al. 2025; Xu et al. 2025).

However, a prevailing assumption in many existing WAMs is that future supervision must originate from task-specific expert demonstrations. Both the future visual targets and the corresponding action labels are typically derived from paired robot trajectories, often collected through costly simulated data generation or teleoperation. This reliance on expert demonstrations intrinsically links the predictive and generalization capabilities of WAMs to the scale, diversity, and acquisition cost of these robot-specific datasets. Consequently, it limits their ability to generalize effectively to scenarios or tasks not extensively covered by the recorded trajectories.

In parallel, recent advancements in video foundation models (VFMs) have demonstrated a robust capacity to model object motion, human-object interactions, and long-horizon visual dynamics. These models, when minimally adapted with embodiment-specific data, can synthesize task-conditioned visual rollouts for novel tasks and environments (Jang et al. 2025; Wang et al. 2026). Furthermore, Inverse Dynamics Models (IDMs) can be employed to recover embodiment-specific pseudo-actions from these generated visual rollouts (Jang et al. 2025; Ye et al. 2025). Existing pipelines that integrate VFMs into robot learning often treat these generated rollouts primarily as sources of pseudo-labeled action data. This approach, however, discards the rich predictive visual knowledge embedded in the generated futures themselves, creating an information bottleneck. Additionally, potential errors arising from video generation artifacts or imperfect action recovery by the IDM can introduce noise into the pseudo-actions.

Vid2WAM addresses this identified gap by proposing a method to decouple future supervision for WAMs from target-task expert demonstrations. It leverages the predictive capabilities of large video foundation models as an alternative source of future supervision. The work distinguishes itself by using a dual-channel distillation approach that directly transfers both the future visual dynamics and IDM-inferred pseudo-actions from the VFM teacher to a compact WAM student, while incorporating mechanisms to mitigate the impact of noisy synthetic data. This approach aims to enhance the data efficiency and generalization of WAMs without incurring the computational overhead of online video generation at deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for Vid2WAM stems from the limitations observed in current World Action Models (WAMs), particularly their dependence on expensive and task-specific expert demonstrations for acquiring future-predictive supervision. This dependency restricts WAMs' scalability and generalization capabilities, making it challenging for them to adapt to novel tasks or operate efficiently with limited real-world data.

The authors challenge the assumption that future supervision for WAMs must exclusively originate from expert trajectories relevant to the target task. Instead, they propose leveraging the knowledge embedded in large video foundation models (VFMs). These models possess a broad understanding of visual dynamics, object interactions, and long-horizon temporal progressions, acquired through pretraining on vast datasets. With minimal adaptation to a specific robotic embodiment, VFMs can synthesize realistic, task-conditioned future rollouts given an initial observation and a language instruction. Furthermore, Inverse Dynamics Models (IDMs) can derive pseudo-actions from these generated visual sequences, linking the predicted visual future to executable robot movements.

However, the existing paradigm for integrating VFM-generated data into robot learning often treats these synthetic rollouts merely as a source of pseudo-labeled action data. This approach presents several issues:
1. **Information Bottleneck**: The rich predictive visual knowledge captured by the VFM is compressed and potentially degraded when translated solely into action labels via an IDM. The direct visual supervision signal from the generated future frames is discarded.
2. **Noise and Inconsistencies**: The video generation process can introduce artifacts, ambiguous transitions, or physically inconsistent motions. Similarly, the IDM, being an imperfect recovery model, can introduce errors into the pseudo-actions. Directly combining these noisy pseudo-actions with accurate expert actions can interfere with policy learning.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the Vid2WAM research are:

1. **Develop an Offline Distillation Framework**: To transfer the robust visual diffusion priors from a large video foundation model (acting as a teacher) into a compact World Action Model (WAM) student. This aims to allow the WAM to learn from the teacher's broad understanding of dynamics without requiring online inference from the large VFM.
2. **Implement a Dual-Channel, Source-Aware Transfer Strategy**: This strategy involves two complementary distillation channels:
 - **Task-conditioned future rollouts**: Directly supervise the student's future prediction branch, providing explicit visual foresight.
 - **IDM-recovered pseudo-actions**: Ground the generated visual dynamics in the robot's action space, providing embodiment-specific action learning signals.
3. **Integrate Robust Supervision with Residual Adaptation**: To address the challenges of noise and potential inconsistencies in pseudo-actions, introduce source-aware residual action adapters. These adapters are designed to learn source-specific corrections around a shared action backbone, thereby mitigating interference from noisy synthetic data while allowing for joint training with real expert demonstrations.
4. **Ensure Efficient Deployment**: The framework is designed such that during inference, both the large video teacher and the Inverse Dynamics Model are entirely discarded. Only the compact, distilled WAM student remains for efficient, low-latency action generation.
5. **Enhance Generalization and Data Efficiency**: To improve the WAM's ability to generalize to novel tasks and perform effectively with a limited budget of real expert demonstrations.

By achieving these objectives, Vid2WAM seeks to improve the scalability and adaptability of robot policies by leveraging the strengths of large generative models without compromising inference efficiency, thus advancing robot learning beyond the constraints of costly expert data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Vid2WAM is an offline distillation framework designed to transfer the predictive capabilities of a large video foundation model (referred to as the "teacher") into a compact World Action Model (WAM) student. The framework operates by generating synthetic future visual dynamics and corresponding pseudo-actions offline, which then serve as supervision signals for the WAM student alongside real expert demonstrations.

**Overall Framework Design:**
The core idea involves a training phase where a pre-adapted video teacher and an Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) generate distillation targets. The WAM student then learns from a combination of these teacher-derived targets and real robot demonstrations. Crucially, during inference, the video teacher, IDM, and auxiliary components specific to the pseudo-data are removed, leaving only the compact WAM student for efficient action generation.

**Teacher-Derived Distillation Targets:**
The framework converts the video teacher's generative prior into two types of student-compatible supervision: future-video latent targets and IDM-derived pseudo-action targets.

1. **Video Teacher Adaptation:**
 - A large, pre-trained video diffusion model (e.g., LVP checkpoint, based on Wan2.1-14B) is instantiated as the video teacher (Tψ).
 - This teacher is fine-tuned using a limited set of robot demonstration videos. This adaptation specializes the teacher to the robot's visual appearance and embodiment-specific physical constraints, while retaining its general motion-prediction and instruction-following abilities from large-scale pretraining.
 - Once fine-tuned, the teacher is frozen. It then generates future rollouts, ˆv1:T, conditioned on an initial observation (o0) and a language instruction (ℓ). This generation occurs entirely offline.

2. **Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) Training:**
 - An IDM is trained to recover robot actions and proprioceptive states from visual trajectories. The IDM maps a short video window (e.g., three observations separated by a stride) to the action at its center frame.
 - The IDM is supervised using action-labeled robot trajectories, but specifically *does not* require language instructions or task-specific semantic annotations. This allows it to be trained on general, task-agnostic interaction data from the same robot embodiment.
 - After training, the IDM is frozen and functions solely as a labeler for teacher-generated visual rollouts.

3. **Pseudo-Data Generation and Labeling:**
 - An offline distillation buffer is constructed. This process starts by collecting a pool of initial states, which can be simulator snapshots, inexpensive real-world observations, or controllable image syntheses.
 - For each initial state and a language instruction, the frozen video teacher generates a future visual rollout (ˆv1:T).
 - These generated visual rollouts are then decoded to RGB images and subsequently re-encoded by the student's variational autoencoder (VAE). This step is critical to transform the teacher's raw video output into representation-compatible future latents (ˆz1:T) that can directly supervise the student's latent space.
 - Concurrently, the frozen IDM processes these generated rollouts to recover corresponding embodiment-specific pseudo-action chunks (ˆa1:H) and robot state sequences (ˆe1:H).
 - The resulting tuples (o0, ℓ, ˆz1:T, ˆa1:H, ˆe1:H) constitute the pseudo-data, which are stored in the distillation buffer.

**Source-Aware Student Training:**
The WAM student is trained jointly using both the real expert demonstrations and the teacher-generated pseudo rollouts from the distillation buffer. Both types of data leverage the student's shared video and action backbones.

1. **Source-Specific Residual Adaptation:**
 - To address the potential for noise and errors in the IDM-recovered pseudo-actions, a mechanism called "source-aware residual action adapters" is introduced.
 - These are lightweight residual modules strategically placed before and after the shared action backbone of the WAM student.
 - Crucially, separate sets of parameters are maintained for the adapters corresponding to the "real" and "pseudo" data domains.
 - Each adapter computes a residual correction: `Ada_d(x) = x + αW^d_up σ(W^d_down LN(x))`, where `W^d_up` and `W^d_down` form a bottleneck projection, `σ` is a nonlinear activation, and `α` controls the residual contribution.
 - The `W^d_up` matrices are initialized to zero, ensuring that the shared representation remains the default processing path, and the residual branches only learn domain-specific corrections as needed.
 - During inference, only the adapter parameters associated with the *real* domain are retained and used, allowing for clean, efficient execution.

2. **Training Objective:**
 - The WAM student is trained using a flow-matching objective (Lipman et al. 2022) for both action chunks and future-video latents.
 - The overall training objective combines terms from both real and pseudo domains:
 - `L_real = L_FM(a, real) + βL_FM(z, real)`: Supervises with expert actions (`a`) and observed future-video latents (`z`) from real demonstrations.
 - `L_pseudo = L_FM(ˆa, pseudo) + βL_FM(ˆz, pseudo)`: Supervises with IDM-derived pseudo-actions (`ˆa`) and teacher-generated future-video latents (`ˆz`).
 - The complete objective is `L = λ_real L_real + λ_pseudo L_pseudo`, where `β` balances the contribution of action and video supervision, and `λ_real`, `λ_pseudo` balance the two training domains.

**Inference:**
At deployment time, the entire offline teacher system—including the video teacher, the IDM, the auxiliary future-video prediction head, and the pseudo-source adapters—is discarded. The system operates solely with the compact WAM student, which uses a single current-frame pass through its shared video backbone to produce latent world features for direct action prediction, maintaining low latency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of Vid2WAM was evaluated across diverse simulation benchmarks and a real-world bimanual manipulation platform. The evaluation focused on two key regimes: a low-data regime (10 expert trajectories per task) and a novel-task regime (no expert trajectories for target tasks). Baselines included π₀.₅, Motus, and Fast-WAM, with Fast-WAM serving as a direct architectural comparison.

**Simulation Results:**

1. **RoboTwin 2.0 (Bimanual Manipulation):**
 - **Novel-Task Generalization:** Baselines, including pretrained VLA policies and WAMs, exhibited substantial performance degradation when transferring to novel tasks. Fast-WAM's success rate dropped from 75.7% to 45.0% under clean evaluation and from 74.7% to 42.8% under randomized conditions on the novel subset of tasks. Vid2WAM improved novel-subset success rates to 54.7% (clean) and 55.3% (randomized), demonstrating an increase of 9.7 and 12.5 percentage points over Fast-WAM, respectively.
 - **Low-Data Regime:** Vid2WAM also achieved the highest performance in the low-data regime, indicating benefits for learning with limited real demonstrations.
 - **Qualitative Observations:** Visual comparisons of predicted future videos showed that Vid2WAM consistently generated more coherent and task-progressive motions towards the instructed outcome, particularly for longer-horizon tasks, whereas Fast-WAM frequently stopped prematurely or lost visual coherence.

2. **LIBERO and LIBERO-Plus (Robustness Benchmarks):**
 - **LIBERO (Low-Data & Novel Regimes):** Vid2WAM achieved an average success rate of 89.7% in the low-data regime, outperforming or matching all other baselines across various suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long). In the novel regime, Vid2WAM also secured the highest average success rate.
 - **LIBERO-Plus (Perturbation Robustness):** Vid2WAM achieved the highest overall performance in both low-data and novel regimes when evaluated under seven types of perturbations (camera, robot initial state, language, lighting, background, sensor noise, layout). Notable gains were observed under robot-state, language, and lighting perturbations in the low-data regime.
 - **Robustness to Task Selection:** Additional experiments detailed in the appendix confirmed that Vid2WAM consistently outperformed Fast-WAM across different splits of novel tasks for both LIBERO and LIBERO-Plus, suggesting that the observed improvements are not contingent on specific task selections.

**Real-World Experiments:**

- **Task Performance:** On a real-world bimanual platform, involving nine contact-rich manipulation tasks (six seen, three novel), Vid2WAM achieved competitive or superior success rates across all tasks.
- **Novel-Task Execution:** For novel tasks, where no real action-labeled trajectories were provided, Vid2WAM enabled the robot to successfully perform actions such as picking a test tube from a rack, extracting a tissue from a pack, and placing a tomato in a basket. This demonstrated its ability to generalize behaviors to new objects or manipulation patterns solely from initial observations, language instructions, and offline teacher supervision.

**Ablation Studies:**

- **Importance of Dual Channels and Adapters:** The full Vid2WAM model, incorporating both future latent and pseudo-action supervision with source-aware residual adapters, achieved the best average performance. The "Future Latent Only" variant outperformed "Pseudo Action Only" and Fast-WAM, indicating the value of direct future visual guidance. The performance of "Pseudo Action Only" still contributed to the full model's superiority, suggesting complementary roles for both types of supervision. The "No Adapter" variant, which mixed both data sources without source-specific corrections, performed lower than the full model and "Dual Action DiT," supporting the efficacy of residual adaptation in mitigating noise from pseudo-actions.
- **Offline vs. Online Teacher:** An "Online Teacher Policy" (composing the video teacher and IDM at inference time) significantly underperformed Fast-WAM and all distilled Vid2WAM variants. This result implies that the benefits derive from offline distillation and ground-truth action anchoring rather than a stronger online controller, highlighting the issues of accumulating generation and IDM errors during online execution.

**Inference Latency:**

- Vid2WAM demonstrated low-latency inference, approximately matching Fast-WAM (around 210-215 ms per action chunk on an RTX 4090 GPU). This is substantially faster than Motus and the online Teacher Policy (which incurred thousands of milliseconds), although slower than π₀.₅. The efficiency is attributed to the removal of the large video teacher and IDM during deployment.

In summary, the findings consistently indicate that Vid2WAM improves data efficiency and novel-task generalization in robotic manipulation by effectively distilling visual diffusion priors from a large video foundation model into a compact WAM student. This approach preserves low-latency inference and demonstrates robustness across various conditions and tasks, including real-world scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Vid2WAM framework represents a notable advancement in robot policy learning by re-evaluating and expanding the sources of supervision for World Action Models (WAMs). Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Decoupling Supervision from Expert Demonstrations**: The most significant contribution is challenging the conventional reliance of WAMs on costly and task-specific expert demonstrations for future supervision. By leveraging pre-existing knowledge in large video foundation models (VFMs), Vid2WAM offers an alternative, scalable source of visual dynamics and action priors. This approach has the potential to dramatically reduce the need for extensive, curated robot datasets, which are often expensive and time-consuming to collect.

2. **Enhanced Data Efficiency and Novel-Task Generalization**: The experiments demonstrate that Vid2WAM consistently improves data efficiency, enabling robust policy learning with limited real expert demonstrations. Crucially, it enhances generalization to novel tasks where no target-specific expert trajectories are available. This capacity for "zero-shot" or "few-shot" generalization to unseen tasks or objects is critical for developing truly versatile and adaptable robotic systems.

3. **Robust Integration of Synthetic Data**: The introduction of source-aware residual action adaptation is a key methodological contribution. This mechanism effectively mitigates the interference from noisy pseudo-actions generated by the VFM and Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM), allowing for the robust integration of synthetic supervision with real data. This capability is essential for practical applications of generative models in robot learning, where synthetic data imperfections are common.

4. **Efficient Deployment with Compact Policies**: A core strength of Vid2WAM is its ability to discard the large video teacher and IDM during inference. This distillation process results in a compact WAM student that maintains low-latency action generation, making it suitable for real-time robotic control and deployment in resource-constrained environments. This circumvents the computational overhead typically associated with directly using large generative models for online planning or control.

5. **Broader Paradigm for Foundation Models in Robotics**: Vid2WAM supports a broader paradigm where large, pre-trained multimodal foundation models (e.g., video generators, Vision-Language Models) serve as versatile *offline knowledge sources* for more efficient and task-specific robot policies. This opens avenues for transferring diverse capabilities—such as predicted subgoals, future representations, or action-conditioned outcomes—from powerful generative models to practical robotic applications without requiring these large models to operate in real-time.

6. **Advancement in Multi-Modal Learning for Robotics**: By demonstrating the effective distillation of complex visual dynamics and action information from a video model, Vid2WAM contributes to the field of multi-modal learning in robotics. It illustrates how rich visual foresight, even when synthetically generated, can provide valuable temporal learning signals that complement action supervision.

While the paper acknowledges that adapting to some novel tasks without any target-task action trajectories remains challenging, Vid2WAM's improvements across diverse simulation and real-world settings underscore its potential. This work lays groundwork for future research exploring how to further refine synthetic data generation, improve the fidelity of pseudo-action recovery, and enhance the distillation process to enable even broader and more robust robot generalization.
