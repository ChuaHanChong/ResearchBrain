# 4DAnyone: Create Anyone in 4D from a Casual Monocular Video

> [!summary] Summary
> 4DAnyone reconstructs 4D humans from a single uncalibrated monocular video by generating reconstruction-grade multiview-consistent videos via a skeleton-conditioned diffusion transformer, then lifting them into 4D Gaussian Splatting. Two mechanisms, Reference Context Packing and Target Context Routing, fix cross-group drift, beating prior camera-controlled and geometry-conditioned baselines on DNA-Rendering and DyMVHumans.

## Problem

- Camera-controlled video diffusion models stay plausible for few novel views but lose cross-view consistency once scaled to the tens of views 4DGS reconstruction needs.
- Splitting target views into groups for tractable denoising leaves reference context growing linearly and disjoint target groups unable to exchange structural information.
- Dense-depth or camera-parameter geometric conditioning is unreliable on in-the-wild monocular video, while 2D skeleton conditioning suffers unresolved front-back occlusion ambiguity.

## Method

- **Reference Context Packing** compresses a growing set of generated reference views into a fixed-length mixed-resolution token context via multi-scale patchify, cutting reference-context cost from linear to constant.
- **Target Context Routing** cyclically rotates four-view target groupings at high-noise denoising steps to propagate global structure, then fixes adjacent groups at low-noise steps to stabilize local detail.
- ==3D-aware skeleton conditioning== rasterizes a compact 40-keypoint 3D skeleton with pixelwise z-buffering into depth-buffered renderings, injected as a zero-initialized residual into noisy DiT latent tokens.

## Results

- On DNA-Rendering, the full method reaches **24.33** PSNR for generated-video consistency versus **21.47** for the strongest fine-tuned baseline and only **13.56** for the depth-warping baseline.
- 4DGS reconstruction PSNR on DNA-Rendering hits **24.15** versus **20.55** and **20.38** for the two next-best camera-conditioned and geometry-conditioned baselines respectively.
- Ablations show removing both routing and packing drops consistency PSNR from **22.63** to **21.09**, and only a sliding routing strategy improves all three metrics over fixed grouping.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - When per-forward-pass attention context is bounded, splitting a generation problem into groups creates two distinct bottlenecks, growing conditioning cost and lost cross-group communication, that need separate fixes.
> - Accuracy of a sparse geometric signal can matter more than density, a reliable sparse skeleton beats a noisy dense depth map for consistency-critical conditioning.
> - Diffusion's high-noise steps carry global structure while low-noise steps carry local detail, so scheduling different information-sharing strategies per noise level generalizes beyond human video generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The reconstruction of four-dimensional (4D) human avatars, renderable from arbitrary viewpoints, holds relevance for applications in embodied artificial intelligence, immersive content creation, and virtual reality. While recent 4D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS) methods have demonstrated real-time, photorealistic rendering of dynamic scenes, their primary limitation is the requirement for dense multi-view video captured from calibrated, static camera arrays. This dependency restricts their accessibility and broader application.

Previous attempts to reconstruct 4D humans directly from monocular video typically necessitate known camera parameters and often exhibit limitations in inferring occluded body regions and achieving high-fidelity appearance. An alternative approach involves a two-stage pipeline: first generating novel-view videos using camera-controlled video diffusion models, and subsequently performing 4DGS reconstruction. However, existing camera-controlled video generation methods, including those with implicit camera conditioning (e.g., CameraCtrl, ReCamMaster) and explicit geometry conditioning (e.g., Gen3C, TrajectoryCrafter), encounter challenges when scaled to generate the "tens" of target-view videos required for robust 4DGS reconstruction. These methods tend to suffer from cross-view inconsistency, leading to visual artifacts such as appearance disparities and structural drift, which degrade the quality of the downstream 4D reconstruction. For human-specific video generation, methods like ControlNet and MagicAnimate often focus on motion transfer using 2D pose skeletons rather than achieving reconstruction-grade novel view synthesis.

The paper identifies a core architectural limitation in Diffusion Transformers (DiTs), termed the "bounded-attention-context problem." When the number of required target views (N) exceeds the computational and memory capacity of a single DiT forward pass, these views must be processed in groups. This grouping introduces two coupled bottlenecks:
1. **Reference-context bottleneck**: The need to condition each group on all previously generated views results in a reference context length that grows linearly with N (O(N)). This growth quickly exhausts computational resources and weakens cross-view appearance guidance.
2. **Target-context bottleneck**: Disjoint processing of target view groups prevents direct information exchange between views in different groups, leading to a lack of global structural coherence and observable drift across groups.

This work positions itself as an advancement in overcoming these specific consistency limitations in camera-controlled video diffusion models, thereby enabling the generation of multi-view-consistent videos from monocular input at a scale suitable for high-fidelity 4D human avatar reconstruction. It differentiates from prior multi-view diffusion models (e.g., Zero-1-to-3, MVDream, SV4D, Diffuman4D) by specifically tackling the challenges of scaling to *many* reconstruction views from *monocular* input, without requiring synchronized sparse-view videos or known camera parameters.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable the reconstruction of high-fidelity, free-viewpoint 4D human avatars from a single, uncalibrated monocular video. This objective is motivated by several limitations of existing methodologies:

- **Accessibility of 4DGS**: Current state-of-the-art 4DGS techniques for dynamic scenes necessitate expensive and complex multi-camera capture setups. This requirement creates a barrier for broader adoption in various applications such as embodied AI, immersive content creation, and virtual reality. The research aims to democratize 4D human content creation by reducing the hardware requirements to a casual monocular video.

- **Limitations of Monocular 4D Reconstruction**: Direct 4D human reconstruction from monocular video suffers from inherent challenges. These methods typically assume known camera parameters and struggle to accurately infer appearance and geometry in occluded body regions, limiting the fidelity and completeness of the reconstructed avatars. The motivation is to overcome these limitations by introducing a generative step that can synthesize novel views.

- **Consistency Challenges in Video Diffusion Models**: While camera-controlled video diffusion models can generate visually plausible novel-view videos for a limited number of viewpoints, they fail to maintain the rigorous cross-view consistency required for downstream 4DGS reconstruction when a larger number of views (e.g., tens) are needed. This inconsistency manifests as appearance shifts and structural drift, rendering the generated videos unsuitable for high-quality 4D reconstruction. The research specifically aims to address this consistency gap at a "reconstruction scale."

- **Addressing Architectural Bottlenecks**: The work identifies two specific architectural constraints in Diffusion Transformer (DiT) models that contribute to the consistency failure when scaling to many views:
 1. **Inefficient Reference Context**: The conventional approach of conditioning each target view group on all previously generated views leads to a linearly growing (O(N)) reference context length. This becomes computationally prohibitive and dilutes the effectiveness of appearance guidance. A key motivation is to develop a method for scalable reference context management.
 2. **Cross-Group Structural Drift**: When multiple target view groups are denoised independently due to memory constraints, they cannot exchange information, leading to structural inconsistencies and drift across groups. The research aims to devise a mechanism for propagating global structural information across these disjoint groups.

- **Robust Geometric Guidance**: Existing explicit geometry-conditioned methods often rely on dense metric depth or 3D coordinates, which are difficult to estimate reliably from unconstrained "in-the-wild" monocular videos. Errors in these estimates can introduce conflicting geometric constraints. A motivation is to utilize a sparse but more robust geometric signal that can be reliably recovered from monocular input, such as 3D skeletons, to guide generation.

- **Generalization to Diverse Videos**: To ensure the practical applicability of the framework, there is a motivation to train the model on a diverse range of multi-view and monocular video datasets, including synthetic and in-the-wild captures, to achieve robust generalization.

In summary, the objective is to create a robust, scalable framework (4DAnyone) that can generate multiview-consistent videos from a single monocular input, enabling high-fidelity 4D human avatar reconstruction by overcoming specific limitations in video diffusion model scaling and geometric conditioning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

4DAnyone operates as a multi-stage framework to reconstruct 4D humans from a casual monocular video.

#### 3.1. Overview of the Pipeline

The system takes an uncalibrated monocular source video (V_src) as input. Initially, a Human Mesh Recovery (HMR) model estimates a 3D skeleton sequence of the subject. These 3D skeletons are then rendered into depth-buffered skeleton videos for a set of prescribed static target viewpoints. A 3D-aware skeleton encoder integrates these geometric cues into the noisy latent tokens. A Diffusion Transformer (DiT) model subsequently denoises these latents, conditioned on the source video, the skeleton information, and a progressively assembled Reference Context Packing (RCP). To handle the generation of numerous views, Target Context Routing (TCR) manages information exchange among target view groups. The output comprises a set of multi-view consistent human videos, which are then used to reconstruct a 4D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS) model using a method like FreeTimeGS.

#### 3.2. 3D-Aware Skeleton Conditioning

The methodology emphasizes "accuracy over density" for geometric guidance. Instead of relying on potentially noisy dense depth maps from unconstrained videos, the system utilizes sparse but reliably estimated 3D skeletons.
- **Depth-buffered Skeleton Rendering**: To address the inherent 2D pose ambiguity (e.g., front vs. back arm positions) that can lead to inconsistencies, 3D skeletons are rasterized with a pixel-wise z-buffer. This ensures that closer body parts correctly occlude farther ones, providing an occlusion-aware 2D representation.
- **Keypoint Selection**: A compact subset of 40 keypoints from the Goliath vocabulary is used, focusing on body, foot, and palm-level hand keypoints. This selection avoids noise from fine-grained facial or finger keypoint detections, allowing the video model to infer these details from the source appearance.
- **Skeleton Encoder (g_phi)**: This component, comprising 10 Conv3d layers followed by a 1x1x1 projection, processes the depth-buffered RGB skeleton video. It generates a DiT-resolution residual that is added to the noisy latent tokens, effectively injecting the geometric condition. The final projection layer is zero-initialized to stabilize training with pretrained DiT weights.

#### 3.3. Scalable Multiview Consistency

The framework introduces two complementary designs to manage consistency when generating tens of views, addressing the identified bottlenecks.
- **Reference Context Packing (RCP)**: This design addresses the O(N) growth of reference context by compressing previously generated views. It leverages multi-scale patchify layers (Conv3d with varying kernel/stride) to pack a growing set of generated reference views into a fixed-length set of reference token slots, thereby reducing the reference-context complexity to O(1). The fixed RCP context (C_R) is composed of the source video (P1(V_src)) and multi-resolution versions of selected generated reference videos ({P2(V_a_j)}, {P4(V_b_j)}). During inference, reference views are generated in two rounds using farthest-point sampling to ensure diverse viewpoint coverage before constructing the fixed C_R.
- **Target Context Routing (TCR)**: This mechanism mitigates cross-group structural drift during denoising. It exploits the diffusion process's temporal structure: global structure is established at high noise levels, and local details are refined at low noise levels.
 - **High-noise phase (t > t_s)**: Target views, partitioned into four-view groups, are cyclically shifted and regrouped at each denoising step. This dynamic regrouping allows views to exchange context across groups over time, propagating global structure.
 - **Low-noise phase (t ≤ t_s)**: The groupings are fixed to adjacent four-view sets. This stabilization promotes joint refinement of fine details and consistent transitions between neighboring views. Groups can be processed sequentially or in parallel.

#### 3.4. Training Protocol

A three-stage curriculum progressively builds the model's capabilities:
- **Stage 1**: Training on foreground-only DNA-Rendering videos focuses on learning skeleton-conditioned camera control. To decouple pose from appearance, there is a 20% probability of sampling source and target clips from different temporal windows.
- **Stage 2**: Training is extended to all multi-view datasets (MVGameHuman, SynCamVideo, DNA-Rendering) without foreground masking. This addresses mask boundary artifacts and enables the model to learn lighting and shadow cues.
- **Stage 3**: Monocular datasets (Pexels, TedTalk) are added to enhance in-the-wild generalization. Finger keypoints are removed from the skeleton input, encouraging the model to infer hand details from the source video, addressing potential noise in fine-grained monocular keypoint detections.
The model is trained with a latent flow-matching loss (MSE) and a perceptual reconstruction loss (LPIPS) applied to body-part-aware semantic crops to manage memory.

#### 3.5. Dataset and Implementation

The training dataset includes MVGameHuman (a proprietary dataset generated with an in-house game engine), SynCamVideo, DNA-Rendering, TedTalk, and Pexels. 2D keypoints are detected using Sapiens2-1B, and 3D skeletons are obtained via cross-view triangulation for multi-view data or estimated from Sapiens2 pointmap predictions for monocular data. The framework is built upon Wan2.2-TI2V-5B, a 5B-parameter DiT-based video diffusion model, trained at 704x1280 resolution with 20 denoising steps during inference, and `t_s/T` for TCR set to 0.2.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents both quantitative and qualitative evaluations to support the efficacy of the 4DAnyone framework.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Comparison with State-of-the-Art

Evaluations were conducted on two unseen test sets: DNA-Rendering and DyMVHumans, using 16 approximately uniformly distributed cameras and 98 frames per scene. The metrics utilized were PSNR↑, SSIM↑ (higher is better), and LPIPS↓ (lower is better). Three evaluation dimensions were assessed:
- **4DGS Reconstruction**: All 16 generated views were used to reconstruct a 4DGS model (via FreeTimeGS), and the rendered views from this model were compared against ground-truth videos.
- **Generated Video Consistency**: Four evenly spaced views were held out as a test set, while the remaining 12 views were used to train 4DGS. The 4DGS renderings at the held-out views were then compared against the corresponding generated videos to quantify 3D consistency among the generated views.
- **Generated Video Reconstruction**: All 16 generated videos were directly compared against ground-truth videos.

**Findings:**
- **Superior Performance**: 4DAnyone consistently outperformed all baseline methods—MV-Performer, TrajectoryCrafter, and a fine-tuned ReCamMaster† (equipped with RCP and TCR for fair comparison)—across all three evaluation dimensions and on both DNA-Rendering and DyMVHumans datasets.
 - For example, on DNA-Rendering, 4DAnyone achieved 24.33 PSNR, 0.862 SSIM, and 0.163 LPIPS for generated video consistency, compared to ReCamMaster†'s 21.47 PSNR, 0.806 SSIM, and 0.210 LPIPS. Similar improvements were observed for 4DGS reconstruction and generated video reconstruction.
- **Consistency Leads to Better Reconstruction**: The enhanced consistency observed in the generated videos by 4DAnyone directly translated to stronger performance in the downstream 4DGS reconstruction task, indicating that the generated inputs were more coherent for 4D lifting.
- **Baseline Limitations**:
 - **MV-Performer**: Showed reasonable frontal views but degraded performance and distortion in side and back views, attributed to limitations in training diversity.
 - **TrajectoryCrafter**: Experienced accumulation of depth errors, leading to failures, particularly in scenarios involving significant viewpoint changes like front-to-back transitions.
 - **ReCamMaster†**: Despite producing visually plausible videos, its implicit camera control resulted in imprecise cross-view alignment, which negatively impacted the fidelity of 4DGS reconstruction.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

Visual comparisons (Figure 4) corroborate the quantitative findings.
- 4DAnyone produced geometrically accurate and visually detailed results across various viewpoints, demonstrating coherent hallucination of unseen back-view content consistent with the front-view input.
- Baselines exhibited visible deficiencies: MV-Performer distorted body shapes and poses, TrajectoryCrafter suffered from severe geometric distortion and front-to-back failures, and ReCamMaster† showed misalignments that led to noisy 4DGS renderings.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study on DNA-Rendering investigated the contributions of RCP and TCR.
- **Effect of Reference Context Packing (RCP)**: Removing RCP led to a degradation across all consistency metrics (e.g., PSNR dropped from 24.33 to 22.03), indicating that relying solely on monocular context without appearance guidance from previously generated views resulted in inconsistent hallucinations.
- **Effect of Target Context Routing (TCR)**: Eliminating TCR (fixed view groups denoised independently) also degraded consistency metrics (e.g., PSNR dropped from 24.33 to 22.21), highlighting its role in sharing context and mitigating structural and appearance inconsistencies across groups. When both RCP and TCR were removed, performance deteriorated substantially, confirming their complementary importance.
- **Effect of Routing Strategy**: Among different TCR routing strategies, "Sliding" (cumulative shift of view indices) demonstrated the best performance, improving all consistency metrics compared to fixed grouping, "Random," or "Strided" strategies. This suggests that preserving local view adjacency during regrouping is beneficial.
- **3D-Aware Skeleton Conditioning**: Qualitative analysis demonstrated that depth buffering in skeleton rendering resolved front-back ambiguity for overlapping body parts, leading to improved geometric consistency in the generated content.

#### 4.4. Generalization to In-the-Wild Videos

The framework exhibited robust generalization capabilities when tested on diverse in-the-wild human-centric videos. It produced consistent target-view videos and high-quality 4DGS renderings for challenging inputs, including back-view source videos, complex subject appearance, and intricate human motions (Figures 6 and 7).

#### 4.5. Inference Efficiency

The complete 4DAnyone pipeline for 4D human reconstruction demonstrated practical efficiency: preprocessing (HMR inference, skeleton rendering) took approximately 2 minutes, multi-view video generation (4 videos, 121 frames, 20 denoising steps) about 7 minutes, and 4DGS training (FreeTimeGS) around 30 minutes on a single GPU (RTX 4090 or H20).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The 4DAnyone framework presents several significant contributions and potential impacts within the fields of computer graphics, computer vision, and artificial intelligence.

- **Democratization of 4D Human Reconstruction**: The primary significance lies in enabling high-fidelity 4D human avatar reconstruction from widely accessible uncalibrated monocular videos, thereby removing the dependency on expensive, specialized multi-camera capture setups. This advancement has the potential to democratize the creation of dynamic 3D human content.

- **Advancement in Generative Models**: The work introduces novel architectural and algorithmic solutions, Reference Context Packing (RCP) and Target Context Routing (TCR), that specifically address fundamental limitations of scaling camera-controlled video diffusion models. These designs overcome the "bounded-attention-context problem" by enabling scalable reference context management (O(1) complexity) and mitigating cross-group structural drift during multi-view generation. This represents a methodological step forward in achieving reconstruction-grade consistency from diffusion models for numerous views.

- **Robust Geometric Guidance for In-the-Wild Data**: By prioritizing "accuracy over density" and using sparse, depth-buffered 3D skeletons as robust geometric conditioning, the framework demonstrates an effective strategy for handling unconstrained, in-the-wild monocular videos where dense depth estimation is often unreliable. This approach allows the generative model to infer appearance details while maintaining structural coherence.

- **Improved 4DGS Inputs**: The demonstrated superior consistency and quality of generated multi-view videos directly contribute to higher-fidelity 4DGS reconstructions compared to prior methods. This indicates that 4DAnyone produces inputs that are more suitable for 4D scene representation and rendering techniques.

- **Broadening Application Areas**: The ability to create realistic, dynamic 4D human avatars from casual videos expands possibilities across various domains:
 - **Embodied AI**: More realistic and dynamically controllable human representations for AI agents.
 - **Immersive Content Creation**: Facilitating the rapid generation of 3D human content for augmented reality (AR) and virtual reality (VR) applications, character animation, and virtual try-on.
 - **Virtual Reality**: Populating virtual environments with dynamic, photorealistic humans that can be viewed from any angle.

- **Ethical Considerations**: The paper acknowledges the potential for misuse, such as the creation of deepfakes, violations of identity privacy, and copyright infringement, which is inherent to technologies that synthesize realistic human videos. The authors advocate for clear disclosure of synthetic content and require consent from depicted subjects, underscoring the broader ethical responsibilities associated with such powerful generative capabilities.

In summary, 4DAnyone addresses a critical gap in 4D human reconstruction accessibility and generative model scalability, offering a robust solution with broad potential for advancing immersive technologies while also highlighting the importance of ethical deployment.
