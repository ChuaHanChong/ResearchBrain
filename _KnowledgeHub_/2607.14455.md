# Motion Planning with Model-Based Diffusion via Constraint Optimization and Adaptive Scheduling

> [!summary] Summary
> MD-COAS, developed by researchers from Carnegie Mellon University and the University of Pennsylvania, integrates an inexact Augmented Lagrangian Method with a Convex Feasible Set-based projection and an adaptive scheduling mechanism for single-robot motion planning. This method robustly generates collision-free and dynamically feasible trajectories, achieving higher safety and success rates with faster convergence and lower planning costs in complex environments.

## Problem

- Generating robust, collision-free, and dynamically feasible trajectories in highly non-convex environments remains computationally challenging for single-robot motion planning.
- Existing safety-enhanced model-based diffusion methods typically rely on either soft priors or hard projections, lacking a unified framework for synergistic constraint enforcement.
- These methods often use static safety enforcement parameters, leading to inefficiency and suboptimal performance throughout the dynamic trajectory denoising process.

## Method

- Introduces a unified constraint optimization framework integrating an ==inexact Augmented Lagrangian Method (iALM)== as a soft feasibility prior with a ==Convex Feasible Set (CFS)==-based hard projection operator.
- Develops a ==primal-dual adaptive scheduling== strategy to dynamically adjust iALM dual/penalty parameters and CFS projection parameters (probability, active-set size, iterations).
- Co-optimizes the adaptive scheduling with the ==reverse diffusion== process, regulating constraint enforcement intensity based on trajectory refinement state and computational budget.

## Results

- Achieved **100% success** with **zero violations** in a **7-DoF** robotic arm avoidance task across various difficulty levels.
- Demonstrated superior Safe & Success Rates (SSR), lower planning costs, and faster convergence in highly non-convex 2D obstacle avoidance maps compared to baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the unified constraint optimization and the adaptive scheduling mechanisms are critical for achieving high safety and efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining soft, continuous guidance (iALM) with strict, local correction (CFS) within a unified framework enhances both robustness and statistical efficiency in handling complex constraints.
> - Dynamically adapting constraint enforcement parameters throughout the diffusion denoising process is crucial for balancing safety, computational efficiency, and multimodal exploration.
> - A primal-dual view allows for co-optimizing constraint satisfaction and computational resource allocation, enabling more effective and targeted safety enforcement.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Single-Robot Motion Planning (SRMP) is a fundamental challenge in robotics, particularly in highly non-convex environments where trajectories must satisfy collision-free guarantees, dynamic feasibility, and various task-related constraints. The difficulty is compounded by the need for computationally efficient solutions.

Traditionally, SRMP has been tackled by sampling-based planners like Probabilistic Roadmaps (PRM) and Rapidly-exploring Random Trees (RRT). However, these methods often exhibit performance degradation as environments become larger or constraints become tighter. More recently, diffusion models have emerged as a new paradigm, recasting SRMP as a probabilistic inference problem. These models learn a score function from demonstration data to guide a denoising process, generating feasible trajectories. A significant limitation of early diffusion-based methods is their reliance on extensive demonstration datasets for training and their infrequent rigorous enforcement of system dynamics within the generative model.

Model-Based Diffusion (MBD) approaches addressed these limitations by explicitly incorporating known dynamics and formulating SRMP as a trajectory optimization problem. MBD analytically estimates the score function from rollout samples, guiding the denoising process towards low-cost trajectories without requiring demonstration learning. Unlike conventional deterministic trajectory optimization methods that might converge to suboptimal single solutions, MBD retains multimodality through its stochastic sampling nature.

Building upon MBD, subsequent research has focused on integrating safety enforcement mechanisms to ensure constraint satisfaction. These efforts generally fall into two categories:
1. Encoding constraints as *soft feasibility priors* within the diffusion process.
2. Implementing *hard post-projection operators* to correct constraint violations.

The existing landscape, prior to this work, faced two primary limitations:
- **Lack of Unified Framework**: Safety-enhanced MBD methods typically employ either soft priors or hard projections, but rarely a unified framework that synergistically combines both. This often leads to struggles in complex, highly non-convex environments where soft priors might not guarantee strict safety and hard projections can be overly restrictive or computationally expensive.
- **Static Safety Enforcement**: These methods often maintain fixed safety enforcement parameters throughout the diffusion denoising process. This approach neglects the evolving nature of the trajectory, which transitions from a highly noisy state in early diffusion steps to a refined, clean state in later steps. Consequently, safety enforcement may be either too aggressive when not needed or insufficient when critical.

This paper introduces Model-Based Diffusion via Constraint Optimization and Adaptive Scheduling (MD-COAS) to address these identified gaps. It positions itself as the first approach to unify an inexact Augmented Lagrangian Method (iALM) as a soft diffusion prior with a Convex Feasible Set (CFS)-based hard projection operator, while also adaptively scheduling and co-optimizing these safety enforcement mechanisms alongside the diffusion schedule.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a robust and efficient single-robot motion planning (SRMP) method that can reliably generate feasible, collision-free, and dynamically consistent trajectories in highly non-convex and constrained environments. This is formulated as optimizing a trajectory to minimize a task objective subject to dynamic feasibility and a set of inequality constraints, particularly obstacle avoidance.

The motivation for MD-COAS stems from several critical limitations observed in the current state of model-based diffusion (MBD) and other diffusion-based motion planning techniques:

1. **Challenges with Complex Constraints**: Existing MBD approaches, even those incorporating safety, often struggle in environments characterized by highly non-convex obstacles and tight passages. These complexities demand sophisticated constraint handling beyond simple penalty terms or basic post-hoc corrections.
2. **Inadequate Constraint Enforcement Strategies**: Current safety-enhanced MBD methods typically enforce constraints either through soft feasibility priors or hard projection operators, but not a unified framework combining both.
 - **Soft Feasibility Priors**: While valuable for guiding the diffusion process without discarding infeasible samples (which leads to statistical inefficiency), purely soft guidance may not guarantee strict collision avoidance, especially in tight or highly constrained scenarios. It might "stall" near feasible regions without strictly entering them.
 - **Hard Projection Operators**: These can ensure strict constraint satisfaction by directly projecting trajectories onto a feasible set. However, if applied blindly or restrictively, they can limit multimodal exploration, induce overly conservative paths, or be computationally expensive if applied at every step without discrimination.
3. **Lack of Adaptive Scheduling**: A significant limitation is that existing methods fix their safety enforcement parameters throughout the entire diffusion denoising process. The required level and type of constraint enforcement can change dramatically as a noisy, ill-defined trajectory progressively refines into a clean, coherent path. Maintaining fixed parameters means enforcement might be overly aggressive on noisy, early-stage trajectories (potentially hindering exploration) or too lenient on late-stage trajectories (failing to ensure final safety).

To address these motivations, MD-COAS sets out to achieve two primary objectives:

1. **Unified Constraint Optimization Framework**: Develop a framework for MBD that seamlessly integrates a soft feasibility prior, specifically the inexact Augmented Lagrangian Method (iALM), with a hard projection operator, specifically a Convex Feasible Set (CFS)-based method. This unification aims to leverage the benefits of both: iALM provides continuous, informative guidance to mildly infeasible trajectories, while CFS offers rigorous local feasibility correction when strict safety is required.
2. **Adaptive and Co-Optimized Scheduling**: Introduce a primal–dual adaptive scheduling strategy that dynamically adjusts the parameters of both the iALM soft prior and the CFS hard projection operator. This scheduling is designed to be co-optimized with the reverse diffusion process, allowing the method to adaptively regulate constraint enforcement based on the current state of trajectory refinement, the observed constraint violations, and a computational budget. This dynamic adaptation aims to improve efficiency and effectiveness, concentrating computational effort where and when it is most needed.

Ultimately, the motivation is to create a more robust, efficient, and reliable motion planner for complex robotic tasks, capable of high safety and success rates with reduced final costs and faster convergence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Model-Based Diffusion via Constraint Optimization and Adaptive Scheduling (MD-COAS), builds upon Model-Based Diffusion (MBD) by introducing a novel constraint optimization framework and an adaptive scheduling mechanism.

**Core MBD Framework (Preliminaries):**
MD-COAS treats trajectory generation as a process of denoising from Gaussian noise, similar to Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs). It leverages the MBD approach, which analytically estimates the score function from sampled control sequences propagated through known dynamics. The process involves:
1. Sampling denoised candidate trajectories from a Gaussian proposal (Ω_k).
2. Evaluating these candidates under a target density p_1(τ).
3. Computing an importance-weighted average to form a Monte Carlo Score Ascent (MCSA) estimator (Ŝ_k).
4. Using Ŝ_k to update the noisy trajectory via a reverse diffusion step.

The critical aspect for MD-COAS is the construction of p_1(τ) to incorporate both low-cost and feasible trajectories.

**Constraint Optimization (CO) Framework:**

1. **Inexact Augmented Lagrangian Method (iALM) as Soft Feasibility Prior:**
 - **Motivation**: Standard hard indicator priors for dynamics and safety lead to sparse weighting landscapes where infeasible trajectories contribute no gradient information, making the MCSA statistically inefficient.
 - **Approach**: MD-COAS replaces the hard feasibility prior with a soft kernel p_S(τ) that assigns continuous, non-zero weights to mildly infeasible trajectories. This is achieved using an inexact Augmented Lagrangian Method (iALM).
 - The softened objective is defined as J̃(τ) = J(τ) + λᵀḡ(τ) + (ρ/2)||ḡ(τ)||², where J(τ) is the task objective, ḡ(τ) is the horizon-normalized sum of positive constraint violations (g_t ≥ 0), λ ≥ 0 is a dual parameter, and ρ > 0 is a penalty parameter.
 - The target distribution p(τ) becomes proportional to exp(-J̃(τ)/β), where the iALM terms form the explicit soft feasibility kernel p_S(τ).
 - **Benefit**: This retains informative signals for MCSA, preventing "dead samples."
 - **Limitations (addressed by AS)**: Static λ and ρ values do not adapt to the diffusion process, and purely soft guidance may not ensure strict feasibility in highly constrained, non-convex environments.

2. **Convex Feasible Set (CFS)-based Hard Projection Operator:**
 - **Motivation**: To provide strict feasibility guarantees and handle highly non-convex constraints, a hard correction mechanism is necessary to complement the soft prior.
 - **Approach**: MD-COAS introduces a hard feasibility operator, P_I,H(·), as a proximal correction step. Given an MBD nominal proposal τ̃, this operator projects it onto a local convex inner approximation of the safe set.
 - The projection is implemented using the Convex Feasible Set (CFS) method, which iteratively convexifies collision-avoidance constraints and solves a structured Quadratic Program (QP) over the full control sequence.
 - **QP Formulation**: The QP minimizes the deviation from the nominal control sequence (u) to a corrected one (u*), subject to linear constraints. These constraints are derived from first-order linearizations of the signed-distance functions around the most violated obstacle–time pairs (the active set A_k,m).
 - **Iterative Refinement**: CFS runs an inner loop for I_k iterations, updating the control sequence and re-rolling out, until convergence or the iteration budget is reached.
 - **Benefit**: Provides explicit, local feasibility correction, pulling proposals back into a safe region without breaking dynamics.

**Adaptive Scheduling (AS) in Reverse Diffusion Process:**

**Motivation**: The required correction effort and type of constraint enforcement (soft vs. hard) should change as the trajectory transitions from a noisy state to a clean, refined state during reverse diffusion. Fixed parameters are inefficient and suboptimal.

**Approach**: MD-COAS integrates adaptive scheduling mechanisms for both the soft iALM prior and the hard CFS projection, operating in a primal–dual view.

1. **Adaptive iALM Soft Prior Scheduling**:
 - A robust batch statistic, r̃_k (90th percentile of constraint violation norms exceeding a tolerance ξ), is used as a step-wise feasibility signal.
 - The dual parameter (λ_k) and penalty parameter (ρ_k) of the iALM prior are updated adaptively using damped projected ascent and stall-driven penalty adjustment (Equations 20, 21).
 - **Mechanism**: If violations persist (r̃_k > r̄), λ_k increases (boosting feasibility pressure) and ρ_k might increase (tightening the penalty). If violations decrease (r̃_k < r), ρ_k might decrease (relaxing the penalty). This non-monotonic adaptation allows the soft prior to respond to the current violation level.

2. **Adaptive CFS-based Hard Projection Scheduling**:
 - The hard projection operator is invoked selectively based on a Bernoulli probability p_k.
 - The projection probability (p_k), active-set size (H_k), and number of CFS inner iterations (I_k) are dynamically adapted. These parameters collectively determine the computational effort (c_k ≈ p_k * I_k * H_k).
 - A compute dual (ν_k) regulates the average computational budget (B) for projection. ν_k is updated using damped projected ascent (Equation 23).
 - **Mechanism**: If the realized effort c_k exceeds the budget B, ν_k increases, discouraging aggressive projection by potentially reducing p_k, I_k, or H_k. If effort is below budget, ν_k decreases, allowing more frequent or stronger corrections.

**Unified Primal-Dual View**:
The iALM soft prior and the hard operator schedule are integrated into a single augmented Lagrangian surrogate (Equation 24). This formalizes how the residual sequence (r̃_k) adjusts (λ_k, ρ_k) to modulate feasibility pressure, while the computed residual (c_k - B) adjusts ν_k to regulate projection effort. This allows MD-COAS to co-optimize safety enforcement and diffusion scheduling, applying aggressive constraint enforcement only when violations are significant and computational resources allow.

**Overall Algorithm (Algorithm 1)**:
At each reverse diffusion step, MD-COAS samples nominal candidates, evaluates their feasibility, adaptively updates iALM parameters, schedules the hard projection effort, selectively applies CFS projection, rolls out candidates through dynamics, reweights them by the adaptive iALM target, computes the Monte Carlo score, and finally updates the trajectory. This integrated process ensures that trajectories are progressively refined toward dynamically feasible, low-cost, and collision-free solutions while adapting its enforcement strategy throughout the denoising process.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental analysis evaluated MD-COAS against several baseline motion planners on two distinct benchmarks: a 2D constrained obstacle avoidance task and a higher-dimensional 7-DoF robotic arm avoidance task.

**A. 2D Constrained Obstacle Avoidance Benchmark**

- **Setup**: The benchmark comprised 10 levels of increasing difficulty, ranging from easy convex obstacles (L1-L3) to dense convex obstacles (L4-L6) and highly non-convex "Union Maps" (L7-L10). Each level was tested with 10 random seeds, generating 20 trajectory multimodals per seed.
- **Baselines**: MBD, EB-MBD, MDOC, SafeDiffuser, DPCC-C. Ablation studies included MD-COAS-A (adaptive iALM soft prior only, no hard projection) and MD-COAS-F (fixed constraint optimization parameters with hard projection, no adaptive scheduling).
- **Key Metrics**: Safe & Success Rate (SSR), Mean Cost, Planning Time, and qualitative visualization of denoising dynamics.

- **Results Overview (Fig. 3, Fig. 2)**:
 - **Safe & Success Rate (SSR)**: MD-COAS and MD-COAS-F consistently achieved an SSR of 1.00 in Easy and Constrained Maps. In the highly non-convex Union Maps, they maintained an approximate 0.5 SSR, outperforming all other baselines. EB-MBD dropped to ~0.7 and MDOC to ~0.5 in Constrained Maps. MD-COAS-A, without hard projection, exhibited lower SSR and higher terminal costs in tighter maps, indicating that soft priors alone were insufficient for strict safety.
 - **Planning Cost**: MD-COAS demonstrated the lowest planning cost across all difficulty levels and the fastest convergence during the diffusion denoising process.
 - **Qualitative Denoising Dynamics (Fig. 2)**:
 - MBD frequently generated trajectories that penetrated obstacles, even in late denoising stages, due to the absence of explicit safety enforcement.
 - EB-MBD, with its fixed soft barrier, failed to adapt to varying violation patterns, resulting in many final samples still violating constraints.
 - MDOC's CBF-based projections led to an overly restrictive feasible set, slowing convergence, failing to reach targets, and collapsing trajectory multimodality.
 - MD-COAS-F and MD-COAS maintained strong safety with faster convergence while preserving a larger effective feasible region, enabling diverse multimodal trajectory generation.

- **Insights from Ablations**:
 - **Importance of Constraint Optimization (CO)**: Model-based baselines (MBD, EB-MBD) showed degraded SSR and higher costs as constraint non-convexity increased. MD-COAS-A (soft prior only) showed improvement over MBD but had a higher terminal cost and reduced SSR in tight maps, suggesting soft weighting alone may not guarantee strict safety. The addition of CFS-based hard projection (MD-COAS-F and MD-COAS) provided explicit feasibility correction, leading to lower costs and higher SSR.
 - **Importance of Adaptive Scheduling (AS)**: While hard projection improved robustness (MD-COAS-F), applying it uniformly led to higher runtime costs, particularly in more constrained environments. MD-COAS, with its adaptive scheduling (Fig. 4), achieved comparable terminal performance and safety with improved efficiency. The adaptive mechanism dynamically modulated iALM dual variables (λ_k, ρ_k) and CFS projection parameters (p_k, I_k, H_k), concentrating projection effort only when needed, thus enhancing runtime without compromising solution quality.

**B. D3IL 7-DoF Arm Avoidance Task**

- **Setup**: This benchmark involved a 7-DoF robot arm avoiding cylindrical obstacles while reaching a target line. Planning occurred in the 7-dimensional joint space, with end-effector motion projected to a 2D plane. Three difficulty levels were defined by increasing obstacle radius (0.03, 0.04, 0.05). 5 random seeds were used, with 20 trajectory multimodals per seed.
- **Key Metrics**: SSR, Violation Rate (V), Planning Time (T), and Path Length (P).

- **Results (Table I)**:
 - **Robustness**: MD-COAS consistently achieved 100% success with zero violations across all obstacle radii and difficulty levels. It maintained stable performance as the feasible region progressively shrank.
 - **Comparison with Baselines**:
 - SafeDiffuser and DPCC-C (model-free) showed reduced robustness and lower SSR as task difficulty increased.
 - EB-MBD maintained zero violations but experienced a gradual decline in SSR under tighter constraints.
 - MDOC performed competitively at lower difficulty levels but exhibited minor violations and a noticeable drop in SSR under tighter constraints. Its shorter paths indicated a more restrictive feasible set, potentially limiting exploration and diversity.
 - In contrast, MD-COAS preserved greater stochasticity and trajectory diversity, occasionally leading to slightly longer paths but consistently ensuring safety and success.
 - **Computational Performance**: MD-COAS generally had higher planning times than some model-based baselines (EB-MBD, MDOC) due to its CFS projection process, which is applied to all unweighted trajectory samples. However, it achieved a substantial planning-time advantage over DPCC-C, which also performs batch sample projection but does not fully leverage GPU parallelization.

In summary, the experimental results demonstrate that MD-COAS consistently outperforms baseline planners in terms of safety, success rate, convergence speed, and final trajectory cost across various levels of complexity and dimensionality. The ablation studies confirm the individual importance of both the unified constraint optimization (iALM + CFS) and the adaptive scheduling mechanisms for achieving this performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper, with the introduction of MD-COAS, offers several significant contributions to the field of single-robot motion planning and has the potential for considerable impact:

1. **Advancement in Constraint Handling for Model-Based Diffusion**: MD-COAS is the first method to unify the benefits of both soft and hard constraint enforcement within a model-based diffusion framework. By integrating an inexact Augmented Lagrangian Method (iALM) as a soft diffusion prior with a Convex Feasible Set (CFS)-based hard projection operator, it addresses a critical limitation of previous approaches that typically relied on either one or the other. This unified strategy allows for robust, continuous guidance towards feasibility while providing rigorous, local correction for strict safety guarantees in highly non-convex environments.

2. **Novel Adaptive Scheduling Paradigm**: The introduction of adaptive and co-optimized scheduling for safety enforcement parameters is a key innovation. By dynamically adjusting the iALM dual and penalty parameters and controlling the projection probability, active-set size, and iteration budget of the CFS operator based on real-time violation statistics and a computational budget, MD-COAS efficiently allocates computational resources. This primal-dual adaptive approach optimizes enforcement intensity throughout the denoising process, preventing over-correction on noisy trajectories and ensuring sufficient rigor on refined ones. This dynamic adaptability contrasts sharply with static enforcement strategies, leading to improved efficiency and performance.

3. **Enhanced Performance in Challenging Environments**: The experimental results demonstrate a notable improvement in key performance metrics. MD-COAS achieves higher safety and success rates, faster convergence, and lower final costs compared to state-of-the-art model-based and model-free diffusion planners. Its robust performance in highly non-convex 2D maps and, crucially, in a higher-dimensional 7-DoF robotic arm avoidance task, highlights its scalability and reliability in complex, real-world scenarios. The ability to preserve multimodality while ensuring safety is particularly valuable for exploring diverse solutions.

4. **Training-Free and Dynamics-Aware Planning**: As a model-based and training-free method, MD-COAS bypasses the need for large-scale demonstration datasets that are typically required by many diffusion-based planners. It rigorously enforces known system dynamics throughout the trajectory generation process, which is fundamental for real-world robot deployment where dynamic feasibility is paramount. This makes the method appealing for applications where data collection is expensive or impractical.

5. **Broad Applicability and Future Research Directions**: The principles of integrating soft and hard constraint enforcement with adaptive scheduling could be extended to other control and planning paradigms beyond single-robot motion planning. The paper points towards future work, including improving sampling projection efficiency, adapting to higher-dimensional tasks with richer dynamics models (e.g., underactuated systems), and exploring applications in multi-robot coordination settings. This suggests that the proposed framework serves as a foundational step for addressing more complex robotic challenges.

In conclusion, MD-COAS represents a substantial step forward in developing safe, efficient, and robust motion planning solutions. Its methodological contributions concerning unified and adaptively scheduled constraint optimization provide a powerful tool for autonomous systems operating in increasingly complex and safety-critical environments.
