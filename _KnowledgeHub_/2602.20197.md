# Controllable Exploration in Hybrid-Policy RLVR for Multi-Modal Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Aberystwyth University and China Telecom's Institute of Artificial Intelligence developed CalibRL, a hybrid-policy RLVR framework that redefines expert supervision as a calibration baseline for multi-modal large language models. This approach achieved an average performance gain of 5.45 percentage points on in-domain geometry reasoning tasks and demonstrated improved generalizability across model scales and out-of-domain benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) in Multi-Modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for reasoning tasks often leads to entropy collapse, policy degradation, and over-exploitation of suboptimal behaviors due to vast state spaces and sparse rewards.
- Sequential Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)-then-RL paradigms anchor the policy to a static demonstration distribution, limiting the policy's exploratory capacity in subsequent RL stages.
- Current hybrid-policy frameworks that directly embed SFT supervision often suffer from distributional mismatch, leading to high bias, variance, unstable learning, and accelerated entropy collapse by suppressing alternative responses.

## Method

- **CalibRL** reinterprets expert supervision not as a direct imitation target, but as a ==distributional== calibration baseline for evaluating on-policy behaviors.
- It introduces a controllable exploration objective based on a log-probability gap between model and expert responses, weighted by a distribution-aware advantage, and processed through an asymmetric LeakyReLU activation function.
- This objective is integrated into a modified Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework to selectively reinforce underrepresented correct reasoning paths and moderate overconfident incorrect predictions, maintaining calibrated policy entropy.

## Results

- CalibRL achieved an average performance gain of 5.45 percentage points over the GRPO baseline on in-domain geometry reasoning tasks and a 2.61 percentage point improvement on out-of-domain benchmarks.
- The framework demonstrated robust generalizability, consistently outperforming baselines across different model scales (**3B**, **7B**, **32B**) and architectures (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3).
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical roles of the advantage weighting and the asymmetric LeakyReLU activation, with an optimal α=**0.5**α=0.5α=**0.5** for balanced exploration regulation, showing superior performance compared to conventional entropy control methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Treating expert supervision as a relative calibration baseline allows for guided and stable exploration, preventing the policy from prematurely converging to suboptimal or overly deterministic behaviors.
> - The combination of distribution-aware advantage weighting and asymmetric activation (LeakyReLU) provides explicit, controllable regulation of policy updates, ensuring both targeted reinforcement and moderation of predictions.
> - Maintaining sufficient and guided policy entropy is crucial for discovering novel, higher-reward reasoning strategies and enhancing the generalizability of MLLMs across diverse tasks and data distributions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research operates within the domain of enhancing the reasoning capabilities of Multi-Modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) through Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). Recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated increased proficiency in complex, multi-step logical inference via techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. MLLMs have further extended these capabilities to multi-modal contexts, encompassing visual reasoning and cross-modal inference.

A significant enabler of these advancements has been RLVR frameworks. However, the application of RL to MLLMs presents challenges. The vast state space inherent to MLLMs, coupled with sparse reward signals, often leads to issues such as entropy collapse, where the policy becomes overly deterministic; policy degradation, where performance plateaus or declines; and over-exploitation of suboptimal behaviors, where the model converges on locally optimal but globally inferior solutions. These issues underscore the need for effective exploration strategies during RL training.

Conventional RL methods often employ entropy regularization to encourage stochasticity. However, these methods frequently rely on unguided random sampling, which can result in inefficient exploration, particularly in complex MLLM environments. The integration of Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with RL, either through sequential "SFT-then-RL" pipelines or direct hybrid-policy frameworks, has been explored to combine expert knowledge with self-improvement mechanisms. Nevertheless, existing hybrid-policy approaches have shown limitations in providing stable and controllable exploration. The "SFT-then-RL" paradigm can anchor the policy to a static demonstration distribution, inhibiting its exploratory capacity in subsequent RL stages. Hybrid-policy methods that directly embed SFT supervision often face distributional mismatch between the current policy and expert trajectories, leading to high bias, variance, and unstable learning that can accelerate entropy collapse.

This paper positions itself by addressing these specific limitations, aiming to develop a framework that can maintain productive stochasticity and guide exploration effectively within the RLVR paradigm for MLLMs, thereby mitigating entropy collapse and improving reasoning performance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed limitations of current Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods when applied to Multi-Modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for reasoning tasks. While RLVR has been instrumental in advancing MLLM capabilities, a fundamental tension exists between policy performance improvement and the maintenance of policy entropy. Previous studies have indicated that performance gains often correlate with a reduction in policy entropy, creating a bottleneck that restricts further progress.

Specifically, the authors identify several issues:
1. **Entropy Depletion:** Conventional RL methods, even those incorporating entropy regularization, often lead to premature entropy collapse. This occurs because on-policy updates tend to concentrate probability mass on a narrow set of high-reward trajectories, limiting the exploration necessary for discovering novel reasoning strategies. Unguided random sampling, while increasing stochasticity, is deemed inefficient in the vast state space of MLLMs.
2. **Limitations of SFT-then-RL:** In this sequential paradigm, the initial SFT phase can overly anchor the policy to a static demonstration distribution. This diminishes its inherent exploratory tendency in subsequent RL training, preventing the policy from effectively adapting to reward signals and failing to concentrate probability mass on potentially novel, high-reward behaviors. The policy struggles to move beyond the supervised baseline.
3. **Challenges in Hybrid-Policy Frameworks:** While hybrid-policy methods directly embed SFT supervision into the RL training objective, they typically rely on direct log-likelihood maximization of expert data. This imposes a unidirectional optimization pressure toward expert distributions, which can also accelerate entropy collapse by suppressing alternative responses. The resulting distributional mismatch between the current policy and expert trajectories introduces high bias and variance, leading to unstable policy learning and further interfering with exploration, driving the policy toward either overly deterministic or excessively random behaviors.

Given these challenges, the key objectives of this work are:
- To propose a hybrid-policy RLVR framework, named CalibRL, that supports **controllable exploration** within reasoning-oriented MLLMs.
- To redefine the role of expert supervision from a rigid imitation signal to a **distributional calibration baseline**. This means using expert data as a reference against which the model’s on-policy behaviors are evaluated, rather than an absolute target for imitation.
- To design mechanisms that can **maintain sufficient policy entropy** while **guiding effective exploration** toward meaningful reasoning behaviors, thereby preventing entropy collapse and over-exploitation of suboptimal patterns.
- To achieve this by **selectively reinforcing underrepresented yet correct reasoning paths** and **moderating overconfident but erroneous predictions**, ensuring that policy entropy is retained and exploration is directed.
- To validate the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed framework across a diverse set of benchmarks, including both in-domain and out-of-domain reasoning tasks, and across different model scales and architectures.

Ultimately, the motivation is to overcome the fundamental limitations of existing RLVR approaches by providing a stable and controllable balance between exploration and exploitation, which is critical for enhancing the generalization and reasoning capabilities of MLLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, CalibRL, addresses the limitations of existing RLVR and hybrid-policy methods by reinterpreting expert supervision as a calibration baseline rather than a direct imitation target. This approach aims to facilitate controllable exploration and stabilize policy updates in MLLMs.

The methodology is structured around two primary mechanisms integrated into a modified Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) objective:

#### 3.1. Limitations of Direct Expert Optimization

The paper first analyzes the drawbacks of conventional expert optimization methods. In both sequential Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)-then-RL and current hybrid-policy frameworks, expert knowledge is typically incorporated by minimizing the negative log-likelihood of expert demonstrations ($L_{expert} = -E_{(q_i, \tau^{expert}_i) \sim D} [\log \pi_{\theta}(\tau^{expert}_i | q_i)]$). This objective monotonically increases the probability of expert trajectories, leading to a unidirectional shift of probability mass toward expert responses.

This unidirectional optimization has two main consequences:
1. **SFT-then-RL:** After the SFT phase, the policy becomes anchored to the expert distribution, and subsequent RL updates are constrained by importance sampling and ratio clipping (as in PPO/GRPO), which penalize deviations from this initialization. This restricts exploration to the immediate vicinity of expert behaviors.
2. **Hybrid-Policy Frameworks:** While continuously aligning with experts, increasing $\pi_{\theta}(\tau^{expert}|q)$ necessarily decreases $\pi_{\theta}(\tau \neq \tau^{expert}|q)$ due to normalization. This reduces the overall policy entropy ($H(\pi_{\theta}(\cdot|q))$), accelerating entropy collapse and pushing the model toward overly deterministic, expert-like behaviors.

To counteract these issues, CalibRL adopts a relative calibration perspective.

#### 3.2. Controllable Exploration Under Expert Guidance

CalibRL redefines expert supervision as a distributional baseline for evaluating on-policy behaviors. It introduces a controllable exploration term based on a **log-probability gap** and a **correctness signal**, weighted by an **advantage weighting** and processed through an **asymmetric activation function**.

1. **Log-Probability Gap ($\Delta\ell_i$):** For an input $q_i$, a model-generated response $\tau^{policy}_i$, and the corresponding expert response $\tau^{expert}_i$, the log-probability gap is defined as:
 $\Delta\ell_i = \log \pi_{\theta}(\tau^{policy}_i | q_i) - \log \pi_{\theta}(\tau^{expert}_i | q_i)$.
 This term quantifies the model's relative preference between its own response and the expert's, indicating overconfidence (positive value) or underconfidence (negative value) relative to the expert.

2. **Correctness Signal ($s_i$):** A binary signal $s_i$ is used: $s_i = +1$ for correct responses and $s_i = -1$ for incorrect responses. This is a explicit signal decoupled from the raw reward, ensuring robust definition across varied reward ranges.

3. **Exploration Objective ($L_{exploration}$):** The core of the controllable exploration is defined by:
 $L_{exploration} = |\hat{A}_i| \cdot \text{LeakyReLU}(-\text{s}_i \cdot \Delta\ell_i, \alpha)$.
 This objective is designed to modulate probability updates and guide exploration:
 - **Asymmetric Activation (LeakyReLU):** The LeakyReLU function introduces asymmetric gradient gating. When $s_i \cdot \Delta\ell_i$ is negative (e.g., correct response but underrepresented, or incorrect response but overrepresented), the gradient is scaled differently compared to when it's positive. The slope parameter $\alpha \in (0, 1)$ controls the degree of reinforcement or suppression once a response's probability crosses the expert baseline. This allows the model to amplify underrepresented correct reasoning patterns while reducing overconfident incorrect predictions. This enables exploration that is both directed and regulated, preventing excessive promotion or suppression.
 - **Distribution-Aware Advantage Weighting ($|\hat{A}_i|$):** This term is the absolute value of the group-wise advantage ($\hat{A}_i$), derived from GRPO, which captures group-wise rarity. It scales the update magnitude based on how rare or common a response is within its group. For example, a large $|\hat{A}_i|$ value for a rare correct response among mostly incorrect ones amplifies its reinforcement, treating it as a strong exploration signal. Conversely, it increases suppression for a rare incorrect response among mostly correct ones. This modulation helps maintain distributional diversity by emphasizing informative deviations while damping misleading outliers.

4. **Integration into GRPO:** The controllable exploration term is integrated into the standard GRPO objective, which is based on PPO-style policy optimization with group-normalized advantage estimation:
 $J(\theta) = E_{q \sim D, \tau \sim \pi_{\theta}(\cdot|q)} \left[ \sum_{t=1}^{|\tau|} \min \left( r_{i,t}(\theta) \hat{A}_{i,t}, \text{clip}(r_{i,t}(\theta), 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon) \hat{A}_{i,t} \right) - \lambda L_{exploration_i} \right]$.
 Here, $\lambda$ is a hyperparameter that balances the standard PPO-style policy optimization term and the expert-guided controllable exploration term. The GRPO advantage $\hat{A}_{i,t}$ is calculated as:
 $\hat{A}_{i,t} = \frac{R(\tau_i) - \text{mean}(R(\{\tau_i\}^G_{i=1}))}{\text{std}(R(\{\tau_i\}^G_{i=1}))}$.
 The KL-divergence penalty term, typically present in GRPO, is omitted following recent MLLM training practices where the model's distribution is expected to diverge from the initial policy.

Through these mechanisms, CalibRL aims to achieve a stable balance between exploration and exploitation by treating expert supervision as a relative reference, guiding probability redistribution, and maintaining calibrated stochasticity. The framework estimates the on-policy distribution through online sampling, driving updates from informative behaviors and preventing convergence to erroneous patterns.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate CalibRL covered a range of benchmarks, model scales, and ablation studies. The Qwen2.5-VL-7B model served as the primary base model.

#### 4.1. Training Dynamics and General Performance

- **Training Dynamics (Figure 1):**
 - **SFT-then-GRPO** initiated with high reward values but exhibited consistently high entropy, indicating a lack of meaningful exploration. This led to a quick solidification of the policy, stagnant reward learning, and low performance.
 - **RL-PLUS** showed an opposite tendency, with rapid entropy collapse in the early phase, leading to premature convergence to specific patterns. This suppressed exploration, making later reward learning ineffective and resulting in suboptimal reward curves and declining accuracy.
 - **CalibRL** demonstrated a guided increase in policy entropy and maintained stability throughout training, suggesting effective exploration and consistent performance improvement.

- **Quantitative Performance (Tables 1, 2, Figure 2):**
 - **In-domain geometry reasoning tasks (GeoEval, Geo3K, GeoQA):** CalibRL achieved an average performance gain of **5.45 percentage points** over the GRPO baseline. This significantly outperformed existing hybrid-policy methods (LUFFY and RL-PLUS), which showed performance degradation (-0.84 and -4.8 percentage points, respectively) compared to GRPO.
 - **Out-of-domain benchmarks (Sci, Sp, MathVerse, MathVision, MathVista, MMMU, ScienceQA):** CalibRL showed a consistent improvement of **2.61 percentage points** over GRPO, maintaining competitive advantages over LUFFY and RL-PLUS. DAPO, another baseline, did not outperform GRPO in this setting, which the authors attribute to uncontrolled exploration from a higher clipping threshold.
 - **Challenging GeoEval dataset:** On GeoEval, which comprises samples where even GPT-4o struggled, CalibRL achieved **33.44% accuracy**, a substantial improvement compared to SFT+GRPO (6.00%) and other hybrid-policy methods that largely struggled to match GRPO. This indicates superior capability in handling complex reasoning scenarios.

#### 4.2. Generalizability Across Models

- **Different model scales and architectures (Table 3):**
 - On the smaller **Qwen2.5VL-3B model**, CalibRL outperformed GRPO by **2.65 points**, while LUFFY and RL-PLUS showed performance degradation.
 - On the **InternVL3-8B architecture**, CalibRL maintained its advantage with a **2.05-point improvement** over GRPO, whereas competing methods experienced substantial drops. These results suggest that CalibRL's controllable exploration mechanism generalizes effectively across varying models.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Controllable Exploration Objective (Table 4, Figure 3):**
 - **Removal of Advantage Weighting ($|\hat{A}_i|$):** Removing this component led to substantial performance degradation across all benchmarks, highlighting its essential role in targeted distributional calibration and entropy preservation.
 - **Hyperparameter $\alpha$ in LeakyReLU:**
 - Small $\alpha$ (e.g., 0.3) resulted in aggressive but unstable exploration, characterized by entropy oscillations and premature decay.
 - Large $\alpha$ (e.g., 0.8) over-constrained the exploration signal, leading to rapid entropy decay after an initial peak.
 - An intermediate setting of $\alpha = 0.5$ achieved optimal exploration regulation, maintaining smooth entropy growth and leading to the highest overall performance.

- **Balance Weight $\lambda$ (Table 5):**
 - Small $\lambda$ values favored in-domain improvements but limited generalization.
 - Very large $\lambda$ values suppressed policy learning, harming both in-domain and out-of-domain performance.
 - A moderate $\lambda=0.1$ achieved the best overall performance, balancing in-domain results with out-of-domain generalization.

- **Different Entropy Control Methods (Table 6):**
 - Fixed-coefficient entropy regularization (coefficient=0.01) resulted in performance degradation.
 - KL-Cov provided slight improvements on some tasks but was weaker than CalibRL.
 - Clip-Cov led to a performance drop.
 - CalibRL consistently delivered improvements across benchmarks, indicating its superior guided exploration approach over conventional entropy regularization.

- **Different Activation Functions (Table 7):**
 - ReLU, Sigmoid, and Huber functions either failed to improve upon GRPO or provided only slight gains, indicating their inability to provide controllable scaling or modulate magnitude.
 - Tanh showed a relatively strong improvement, underscoring the importance of handling negative values in entropy shaping.
 - LeakyReLU with $\alpha=0.5$ consistently achieved the highest performance.

- **Different Reference Policies for $\Delta\ell_i$ (Table 8):**
 - Using the expert baseline for computing $\Delta\ell_i$ significantly suppressed the performance compared to a reference policy baseline, demonstrating the importance of expert guidance in the controllable exploration design.

#### 4.4. Additional Experimental Results (Appendix)

- **General applicability beyond visual reasoning (Table 10):** CalibRL showed stronger gains than GRPO on pure text math reasoning tasks (MATH-500 and AMC), demonstrating effectiveness beyond MLLMs.
- **Scalability to larger model sizes (Table 11):** CalibRL exhibited consistent improvements when scaled to the Qwen2.5-VL-32B model, outperforming GRPO.
- **Analysis of length bias in $\Delta\ell_i$ (Table 12):** The model without length normalization in $\Delta\ell_i$ performed better, supporting the original design where a small length preference aligned with desired expert response style was deemed acceptable.
- **Effect of weaker expert baselines (Table 13):** CalibRL consistently delivered substantial improvements over GRPO even when trained on data generated by a weaker expert (Qwen2.5-VL-72B), although the magnitude of improvement was influenced by expert quality.

#### 4.5. Effectiveness Analysis (Table 15)

Statistical data points indicated that CalibRL maintained the broadest policy entropy, suggesting continued exploration rather than collapse into a single mode. It also showed a negative $\Delta\ell_i$, implying the model consistently assigned higher likelihood to expert answers, using this signal for guided exploration. This calibrated behavior led to higher rewards and more precise, shorter outputs compared to baselines.

#### 4.6. Case Studies (Figures 5, 6)

Case studies from the MathVision benchmark illustrated that CalibRL avoided erroneous reasoning and hallucinatory requirements observed in GRPO, and bypassed the overly constrained or inefficient exploration issues seen in SFT+GRPO, LUFFY, and RL-PLUS, leading to direct and precise problem resolution.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for Multi-Modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) by addressing the fundamental challenge of balancing exploration and exploitation while mitigating entropy collapse. The paper proposes a novel hybrid-policy framework, CalibRL, which reinterprets expert supervision as a relative calibration baseline rather than a direct imitation target.

The significance of this work lies in several aspects:
1. **Novel Exploration Control Mechanisms:** CalibRL introduces two complementary mechanisms: a distribution-aware advantage weighting and an asymmetric activation function (LeakyReLU). These designs provide explicit, controllable regulation of policy updates. The advantage weighting scales updates by group rarity, allowing the model to emphasize rare but informative behaviors for distribution calibration. The LeakyReLU function, with its adjustable slope, leverages expert knowledge to moderate overconfident updates while preserving corrective directions, thereby fostering stable and guided exploration.
2. **Mitigation of Entropy Collapse:** By preserving policy entropy in a guided manner, CalibRL aims to overcome a common limitation in RL-trained MLLMs where performance improvements often lead to reduced policy diversity. This allows the model to continue exploring novel and higher-reward reasoning paths beyond initial estimations.
3. **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** The consistent performance improvements demonstrated across a diverse set of eight benchmarks, including both in-domain and out-of-domain tasks, validate the effectiveness of CalibRL. Its robustness is further supported by consistent gains across different model scales (3B, 7B, 32B) and architectures (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3), and even with varied expert data quality. This suggests enhanced generalizability of reasoning capabilities in complex scenarios, including challenging edge cases where other models struggle.
4. **Redefinition of Expert Guidance:** The reinterpretation of expert data from an absolute imitation target to a distributional calibration baseline offers a new perspective on integrating human knowledge into RL training. This approach moves beyond the limitations of direct log-likelihood maximization, which often restricts exploration.
5. **Addressing Limitations of Existing Hybrid-Policy Methods:** The paper highlights that existing hybrid-policy frameworks often suffer from distributional mismatch or accelerate entropy collapse. CalibRL directly addresses these by providing a mechanism to alleviate the mismatch and achieve a more stable balance between exploration and exploitation.

The potential impact of this work is in advancing the development of more generalizable and robust reasoning capabilities in MLLMs. By enabling controllable exploration, CalibRL provides a foundation for future post-training strategies that can more effectively discover novel reasoning patterns, rather than merely amplifying pre-existing behaviors. This could lead to MLLMs that are better equipped to handle diverse, complex, and unseen reasoning tasks, potentially accelerating progress in areas like multi-modal scientific problem-solving and logical inference.
