# QPILOTS: Efficient Test-Time Q-Steering for Flow Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> The QPILOTS framework enhances flow-matching and diffusion policies by introducing a Q-function-guided drift correction during action generation at inference time, without modifying the base policy. This method achieved the highest aggregate online success rates on the OGBench benchmark and improved the performance of a 3-billion parameter frozen VLA foundation model by up to 333.8% on robot manipulation tasks with minimal latency increases.

## Problem

- Integrating expressive multi-step generative policies (flow/diffusion models) with temporal-difference (TD) reinforcement learning (RL) is challenging due to the computational cost and instability of backpropagating critic gradients through the denoising process.
- Existing policy extraction methods often involve trade-offs, such as sacrificing the multi-modal expressivity of generative models (distillation), requiring expensive retraining (fine-tuning), or providing unreliable guidance in noisy latent spaces (some inference-time steering methods).
- Adapting large, pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) foundation models to specific downstream tasks via RL is difficult without modifying their immense parameter sets, which is often computationally unfeasible.

## Method

- **QPILOTS** introduces a Q-function-guided drift correction term to the base flow policy's Euler integrator during inference, effectively steering action generation towards higher Q-values without altering the base policy's parameters.
- Two variants for estimating the critic's gradient are proposed: QPILOTS-U uses a computationally lightweight Tweedie-denoised point estimate of the clean action Q-gradient, while QPILOTS-M employs Meta Flow Maps (MFM) for a more accurate, asymptotically unbiased estimate from the conditional posterior.
- The method enables the use of frozen, pre-trained base generative policies, where only a task-specific critic is learned online, thus making it applicable to large foundation models without expensive fine-tuning.

## Results

- QPILOTS-M (**90%**) and QPILOTS-U (**89%**) achieved the highest aggregate online success rates on the OGBench benchmark, outperforming various training-time and inference-time policy extraction baselines.
- QPILOTS-U improved the success rate over a frozen 3-billion parameter VLA foundation model (π0.5) on all six LIBERO tasks, becoming the top performer on five, and notably achieved a **333.8%** success rate improvement on one task with only a **1.04x** increase in inference time.
- The method demonstrated substantial improvements on challenging tasks, such as improving by 25 absolute percentage points over the nearest baseline in humanoid-large domains on OGBench.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reliably estimating Q-value gradients on clean action estimates, rather than noisy intermediate latents, provides effective guidance for steering multi-step flow policies at inference time.
> - Both a lightweight approximation (QPILOTS-U) and a more sophisticated, theoretically grounded method (QPILOTS-M using MFMs) for posterior-tilt gradient estimation significantly improve policy performance.
> - Decoupling the base generative policy from the critic allows for efficient adaptation of large, pre-trained foundation models to new tasks with online rewards, minimizing computational overhead.
