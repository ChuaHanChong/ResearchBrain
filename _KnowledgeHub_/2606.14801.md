# QPILOTS: Efficient Test-Time Q-Steering for Flow Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> The QPILOTS framework enhances flow-matching and diffusion policies by introducing a Q-function-guided drift correction during action generation at inference time, without modifying the base policy. This method achieved the highest aggregate online success rates on the OGBench benchmark and improved the performance of a 3-billion parameter frozen VLA foundation model by up to 333.8% on robot manipulation tasks with minimal latency increases.

## Problem

- Integrating expressive multi-step generative policies (flow/diffusion models) with temporal-difference (TD) reinforcement learning (RL) is challenging due to the computational cost and instability of backpropagating critic gradients through the denoising process.
- Existing policy extraction methods often involve trade-offs, such as sacrificing the multi-modal expressivity of generative models (distillation), requiring expensive retraining (fine-tuning), or providing unreliable guidance in noisy latent spaces (some inference-time steering methods).
- Adapting large, pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) foundation models to specific downstream tasks via RL is difficult without modifying their immense parameter sets, which is often computationally unfeasible.

## Method

- **QPILOTS** introduces a Q-function-guided drift correction term to the base flow policy's Euler integrator during inference, effectively steering action generation towards higher Q-values without altering the base policy's parameters.
- Two variants for estimating the critic's gradient are proposed: QPILOTS-U uses a computationally lightweight Tweedie-denoised point estimate of the clean action Q-gradient, while QPILOTS-M employs Meta Flow Maps (MFM) for a more accurate, asymptotically unbiased estimate from the conditional posterior.
- The method enables the use of frozen, pre-trained base generative policies, where only a task-specific critic is learned online, thus making it applicable to large foundation models without expensive fine-tuning.

## Results

- QPILOTS-M (**90%**) and QPILOTS-U (**89%**) achieved the highest aggregate online success rates on the OGBench benchmark, outperforming various training-time and inference-time policy extraction baselines.
- QPILOTS-U improved the success rate over a frozen 3-billion parameter VLA foundation model (π0.5) on all six LIBERO tasks, becoming the top performer on five, and notably achieved a **333.8%** success rate improvement on one task with only a **1.04x** increase in inference time.
- The method demonstrated substantial improvements on challenging tasks, such as improving by 25 absolute percentage points over the nearest baseline in humanoid-large domains on OGBench.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reliably estimating Q-value gradients on clean action estimates, rather than noisy intermediate latents, provides effective guidance for steering multi-step flow policies at inference time.
> - Both a lightweight approximation (QPILOTS-U) and a more sophisticated, theoretically grounded method (QPILOTS-M using MFMs) for posterior-tilt gradient estimation significantly improve policy performance.
> - Decoupling the base generative policy from the critic allows for efficient adaptation of large, pre-trained foundation models to new tasks with online rewards, minimizing computational overhead.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the field of reinforcement learning (RL), specifically addressing the challenges of integrating complex action generation models, such as flow-matching and diffusion policies, with temporal-difference (TD) RL. Flow-matching and diffusion policies have become established methods for representing multi-modal action distributions in continuous control and vision-language-action (VLA) models. This is due to their expressivity in generating diverse and complex action sequences.

However, the effective optimization of these generative policies with TD-RL, which relies on a critic (Q-function) to evaluate actions, presents difficulties. A primary challenge is the extraction of an optimal policy from a base generative policy and a learned critic. Directly backpropagating the critic's action gradient through the multi-step denoising process inherent in flow and diffusion models is often numerically unstable or computationally prohibitive.

Prior research has approached this problem through several strategies:
- **Discarding gradient information:** Some methods simplify the process by ignoring direct gradient signals from the critic, potentially limiting optimization effectiveness.
- **Policy distillation:** This involves distilling the multi-step generative policy into a simpler, often one-step, actor. While this can improve stability, it may sacrifice the expressivity inherent in the multi-step generation process.
- **Repeated fine-tuning:** This approach involves iteratively updating the denoising policy as the critic improves. While effective, it can be computationally expensive, particularly for large-capacity generative models, and requires retraining the policy when the critic changes.
- **Inference-time steering:** This category of methods modifies the action generation process at test time without altering the base policy's training. Existing inference-time methods often involve training separate auxiliary policies (e.g., DSRL, FEdit), sampling multiple actions and selecting the best (Best-of-N), or applying guidance at noisy intermediate states where critic predictions may be unreliable (e.g., some classifier guidance variants). These methods have historically been outperformed by fine-tuning approaches in offline-to-online RL benchmarks and can suffer from limited expressivity or high computational costs.

QPILOTS positions itself as an advancement in inference-time steering. It aims to leverage the critic's action gradient more effectively and directly within the denoising process, without modifying the base policy or relying on auxiliary policies that operate in noise space. This aligns it with approaches like classifier guidance but extends them by specifically addressing the challenges of reliable Q-value gradient estimation on clean actions within a multi-step flow. The method builds on concepts from Meta Flow Maps (MFM), which provide a mechanism for differentiable posterior sampling, thereby offering a more principled way to estimate clean-action gradients.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop an efficient and stable method for steering flow-matching and diffusion policies at inference time using a learned Q-function, without requiring modifications to the original base generative policy. This is framed as a policy extraction problem where the goal is to derive an optimal action distribution from a given base policy and a critic.

The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from several limitations observed in existing approaches to combining expressive flow/diffusion policies with TD-RL:

1. **Instability and Expense of Backpropagation Through Denoising Chains:** Flow policies generate actions through a multi-step denoising process. Directly backpropagating the critic's action gradient through this entire sequence to update the policy parameters is frequently unstable or computationally intensive. This hinders the direct application of gradient-based policy optimization techniques.

2. **Trade-offs in Existing Policy Extraction Methods:**
 - **Policy Distillation:** Methods that distill a multi-step flow into a simpler, one-step actor sacrifice the iterative expressivity and potentially the multi-modal capabilities of the original generative model.
 - **Policy Fine-tuning:** Approaches that repeatedly fine-tune the generative policy with critic-aware objectives are computationally demanding. This is particularly problematic for large-capacity models, where retraining or fine-tuning billions of parameters is often impractical. Moreover, such methods couple the policy and critic, necessitating policy re-training whenever the critic is updated.
 - **Inference-time Steering Limitations:** While offering the advantage of leaving the base policy frozen, existing inference-time steering methods have their own drawbacks:
 - Some are limited in expressivity or suffer from high computational costs (e.g., Best-of-N sampling).
 - Others, like DSRL, train auxiliary policies in noise space, which may not directly align with clean-action Q-values and can be less effective than direct policy modification.
 - A common issue is the evaluation of the critic on noisy intermediate actions, where Q-value predictions are often unreliable, leading to biased or ineffective guidance.

3. **Scalability to Pre-trained Foundation Models:** The emergence of large, pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) foundation models presents a compelling use case. These models are typically frozen after extensive pre-training. An effective policy extraction method should be able to improve their behavior with downstream rewards from online interaction without modifying their large underlying parameter sets. Existing fine-tuning methods are often unfeasible in this context.

Therefore, QPILOTS is motivated by the need for a method that:
- Can leverage the full expressivity of multi-step flow policies.
- Is computationally efficient, especially at inference time.
- Does not require modifying or re-training the base generative policy, thus enabling its application to large, frozen pre-trained models.
- Provides reliable guidance by estimating critic gradients on clean action estimates, rather than noisy intermediate latents, addressing the calibration issues of prior guidance approaches.

By fulfilling these requirements, QPILOTS aims to bridge the gap between expressive generative action models and robust reinforcement learning, facilitating their deployment in complex real-world control tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

QPILOTS (Q-guided Posterior Inference for Learning Off-policy and Test-time Steering) is designed as an inference-time steering mechanism for flow policies. The core idea is to introduce a drift correction term to the base flow policy's Euler integrator during action generation, guided by the Q-function, without altering the base policy's parameters.

**Theoretical Foundation:**
The method frames policy improvement as a KL-regularized optimization problem, aiming to maximize expected Q-value while staying close to a base behavior policy $\pi_\beta$. This yields an exponentially tilted target policy $\pi^\star(a|s) \propto \pi_\beta(a|s) \exp(\tau Q(s,a))$. QPILOTS aims to realize this tilted policy by adding a steering term to the probability-flow ODE, proportional to the gradient of a state-conditioned log-tilt potential $V_t(x_t;s) := \log E_{x_1 \sim p_{1|t}(x_1|x_t,s)}[\exp(\psi_s(x_1))]$, where $\psi_s(a) := \tau \bar{Q}(s,a)$ is the critic-induced utility. A pessimistic ensemble critic $\bar{Q}(s,a)$ is used to mitigate overestimation bias.

**Gradient Estimation:**
The main challenge lies in estimating $\nabla V_t$, as the conditional distribution $p_{1|t}(x_1|x_t,s)$ is generally intractable. QPILOTS proposes two variants to address this:

1. **QPILOTS-U (Universal Guidance with Tweedie Estimate):**
 - This variant provides a computationally lightweight approximation. It replaces the intractable expectation in $V_t$ with a point estimate at the posterior mean of the clean action.
 - Using Tweedie's identity for linear-interpolant flows, the minimum mean squared error (MMSE) denoiser $\hat{x}_1 \approx x_t + (1-t)v_\theta(x_t, t, s)$ is employed as an estimate of the final clean action.
 - The log-tilt potential is approximated as $V_t(x_t;s) \approx \psi_s(\hat{x}_1) = \tau \bar{Q}(s, \text{clip}[\hat{x}_1, -1, 1])$.
 - The gradient $\nabla V_t$ is then computed by backpropagating through this approximate Q-value, which involves a single forward-backward pass through the base velocity field and the critic.
 - This approach is simple and adds minimal overhead but introduces bias due to the point estimate and the approximation of the true conditional mean velocity.

2. **QPILOTS-M (Meta Flow Maps for $\nabla V_t$ Estimation):**
 - This variant aims for a more accurate and asymptotically unbiased estimate of $\nabla V_t$.
 - It leverages Meta Flow Maps (MFM), an auxiliary learned network that amortizes the problem of sampling from the conditional posterior $p_{1|t}(x_1|x_t,s)$. The MFM is trained to produce differentiable samples $\hat{X}_{0,1}(\epsilon; t, x_t, s)$ from this posterior.
 - With these samples, the log-tilt-potential gradient is estimated using a Monte Carlo log-sum-exp estimator: $\nabla_{x_t} \log \frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=1}^N \exp(\tau \bar{Q}(s, \hat{X}_{0,1}(\epsilon_n; t, x_t, s)))$.
 - This requires $N$ forward passes through the MFM and critic, plus one backward pass, per Euler step, making it more computationally intensive than QPILOTS-U, but potentially more accurate. Under idealized conditions (exact MFM sampler, large $N$), this estimator is consistent for the true posterior-tilt gradient.

**Inference-Time Steering Mechanism:**
Algorithm 1 outlines the shared inference-time procedure.
1. An initial noise vector $x_0$ is sampled.
2. The base velocity field $v_\theta(x_i, t, s)$ is computed at each Euler step $i$.
3. For $t > 0$, the gradient of the log-tilt potential (using either QPILOTS-U or QPILOTS-M's estimator) is computed.
4. This gradient is then rescaled to match the magnitude of the base velocity field and added as a drift correction: $v^\text{steered}_\theta = v_\theta + \alpha \cdot g_t$, where $g_t$ is the normalized gradient. Steering is explicitly skipped at $t=0$.
5. The intermediate action $x_{i+1}$ is updated using this steered velocity.
6. The final action $x_K$ is clipped to the valid action range.

**Training Regimes:**
QPILOTS is evaluated in two distinct training setups:
1. **From Scratch (OGBench):** The base flow $v_\theta$, critic ensemble $\{\bar{Q}_\phi\}$, and MFM $\hat{v}_\xi$ (for QPILOTS-M) are jointly trained. The base flow uses the standard conditional flow-matching loss. The critic is trained with TD learning against a pessimistic target ensemble, with next actions drawn from the steered policy. MFM training involves diagonal and consistency losses.
2. **Frozen Base Policy (LIBERO):** A large, pre-trained VLA foundation model (π$_{0.5}$) is used as a *frozen* base flow. Only a task-specific critic is learned online. For QPILOTS-M, an MFM is distilled offline on demonstration data from the frozen base flow. The critic is trained using SARSA targets, a pragmatic choice given the high inference cost of large VLAs for Bellman-style target computation.

**Theoretical Guarantees:**
QPILOTS-M, under an idealized setting with exact posterior samples and the theoretical drift term, inherits convergence guarantees. The Wasserstein-2 and KL divergence between the produced action distribution and the target tilted policy converge at rates of $O(1/\sqrt{K} + 1/\sqrt{N})$ and $O(1/K + 1/N)$ respectively, where $K$ is the number of Euler steps and $N$ is the number of MFM posterior samples. These error sources (time discretization and Monte Carlo estimation) are controlled independently. QPILOTS-U, being a point estimate approximation, does not carry similar formal guarantees beyond specific Gaussian assumptions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to evaluate QPILOTS across two primary scenarios: jointly training all components from scratch on a diverse RL benchmark and applying it to steer a large, frozen pre-trained foundation model.

#### 4.1. OGBench: Offline and Offline-to-Online Reinforcement Learning (All Components Trained from Scratch)

- **Performance Leadership:** Both QPILOTS-M and QPILOTS-U achieved the highest aggregate online success rates among all evaluated methods on the OGBench benchmark, which comprises 50 continuous-control tasks. QPILOTS-M obtained an average success rate of 90%, and QPILOTS-U achieved 89%.
- **Outperformance of Baselines:** QPILOTS variants surpassed both training-time policy extractors (e.g., FQL, QAM, QAM-E) and other inference-time steering methods (e.g., DSRL, IFQL, FEdit, CGQL variants). This indicates that the proposed clean-action Q-gradient steering mechanism is more effective than approaches that distill policies, fine-tune them with specialized objectives, or apply guidance in noise space or after action completion.
- **QPILOTS-U Effectiveness:** QPILOTS-U, despite its computational simplicity and reliance on a Tweedie-denoised point estimate, demonstrated strong performance, often outperforming more complex baselines like QAM-E. This suggests that even an approximate clean-action gradient can provide substantial benefits.
- **QPILOTS-M Advantage Offline:** QPILOTS-M showed a slight improvement over QPILOTS-U in offline performance. This was hypothesized to be due to its more accurate posterior estimates, which could be particularly beneficial when the replay buffer's action coverage is limited.
- **Specific Task Improvements:** QPILOTS methods exhibited substantial improvements on challenging tasks, such as the humanoid-large domains, improving by 25 absolute percentage points over the nearest baseline.

#### 4.2. LIBERO: Online Reinforcement Learning (Frozen Base Policy, Learned Critic Ensemble)

- **Steering Large Frozen Models:** QPILOTS was successfully applied to steer a frozen, state-of-the-art 3-billion parameter Vision-Language-Action (VLA) foundation model (π$_{0.5}$-LIBERO), demonstrating its scalability and applicability in a regime where full fine-tuning is impractical.
- **Improved Zero-Shot Performance:** QPILOTS-U improved the success rate over the frozen π$_{0.5}$ base policy on all six evaluated tasks from the LIBERO-90 suite. It was the top-performing method on five out of these six tasks.
- **Outperformance of Inference-Time Baselines:** On average, QPILOTS-U surpassed other inference-time approaches, including DSRL, Best-of-N (BoN), EXPO, and CGQL-LinEx. For instance, on task 64, QPILOTS-U showed a 55% improvement over DSRL.
- **QPILOTS-M in LIBERO:** QPILOTS-M performed comparably to QPILOTS-U on most LIBERO tasks and surpassed it on one task (task 59). However, it did not consistently outperform QPILOTS-U in aggregate on this benchmark. This was attributed to a potential "distillation gap" for the MFM, as it was trained on a subset of data (LIBERO-90 demonstrations) that only partially overlapped with the proprietary pretraining mixture of π$_{0.5}$, implying that the MFM's posterior samples might not perfectly match the true posterior of the large frozen VLA.
- **SARSA Target Effectiveness:** An ablation study demonstrated that SARSA targets for critic training were more effective and stable in the LIBERO online RL setting compared to Monte Carlo returns, Implicit Q-Learning (IQL), and Flow Q-Learning (FQL) when working with large VLAs and non-stationary replay buffers.

#### 4.3. Latency Analysis (Appendix D.1 - LIBERO Context)

- An inference-time latency analysis on task 59 (LIBERO) with the π$_{0.5}$ model showed that QPILOTS-U achieved a substantial increase in success rate (333.8%) with only a minor increase in inference time (1.04x slower than the base π$_{0.5}$), making it more efficient than Best-of-N methods (e.g., Best-of-20 yielded 136.9% improvement at 1.44x slower inference).
- QPILOTS-M, while achieving the highest success rate on this task, incurred higher latency (2.14x slower) due to the multiple posterior estimations per Euler step.

In summary, QPILOTS demonstrated high effectiveness and efficiency across diverse RL benchmarks and scenarios, particularly excelling in improving policies based on large, frozen generative models by providing reliable Q-value-guided steering at inference time.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The QPILOTS framework presents several areas of significance and potential impact within reinforcement learning and the broader field of generative modeling:

1. **Efficient Policy Extraction for Expressive Models:** The work provides an efficient and principled approach for extracting improved policies from highly expressive flow-matching and diffusion policies. These generative models are capable of representing complex, multi-modal action distributions, but their integration with TD-RL has been challenging due to the instability and cost of backpropagation through multi-step denoising. QPILOTS addresses this by offering a stable, inference-time steering mechanism.

2. **Scalability to Large Foundation Models:** A notable impact of QPILOTS is its applicability to large, pre-trained foundation models, such as Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, without requiring their fine-tuning. The ability to steer a frozen, multi-billion-parameter model using a lightweight, task-specific critic represents a significant step towards adapting general-purpose models to specific downstream tasks with minimal computational overhead. This is crucial for real-world deployment of such powerful but expensive models.

3. **Decoupling Policy and Critic Training:** QPILOTS decouples the training of the base generative policy from the learning of the critic. This modularity allows for the use of off-the-shelf, independently trained components. Base policies can be pre-trained on vast datasets (e.g., imitation learning) and then improved with online rewards without modification, enabling greater flexibility and potentially faster iteration cycles in RL development.

4. **Principled Q-Value Guidance:** By estimating critic gradients on clean action estimates (especially with QPILOTS-M's MFM approach), the method provides a more reliable guidance signal compared to prior techniques that apply guidance in noisy latent spaces or after a full action is generated. This reduces bias and improves the effectiveness of Q-steering.

5. **State-of-the-Art Performance:** The achievement of leading aggregate performance on the OGBench offline-to-online RL benchmark, alongside substantial improvements on the LIBERO robot manipulation tasks, validates the effectiveness of the QPILOTS approach. This establishes it as a competitive method for policy extraction in continuous control.

6. **Advancement in Generative Model Steering:** The work contributes to the broader field of guided generative modeling by demonstrating how Meta Flow Maps can be adapted to efficiently estimate posterior-tilt gradients for Q-learning. This conceptually links RL to reward-guided generation and classifier guidance, extending these ideas to a more robust and effective framework for sequential decision-making.

7. **Future Research Directions:** The paper also opens avenues for future research, such as developing adaptive steering coefficients that automatically adjust to local critic curvature, exploring residual edit-flow analogues for further performance gains, and integrating offline-trained value functions with large VLAs to fully leverage the critic-agnostic nature of the method. These potential extensions could further enhance the capabilities and robustness of Q-steered generative policies.
