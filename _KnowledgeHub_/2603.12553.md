# Beyond Dense Futures: World Models as Structured Planners for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> StructVLA reformulates a generative world model to provide sparse, physically meaningful visual foresight for robotic manipulation. It introduces an explicit structured planner grounded in intrinsic kinematic cues, leading to improved reliability, robustness, and generalization across simulated and real-world long-horizon tasks, with success rates up to 94.8% in simulations and 87.5% in real-world pick-and-place.

## Problem

- Existing VLA methods relying on dense future predictions suffer from visual redundancy and error accumulation, leading to plan drift over long horizons.
- Current sparse visual foresight approaches lack explicit geometric grounding, using semantic subgoals or implicit latent states that do not provide verifiable targets for precise low-level kinematic execution.
- A weak alignment exists between high-level planning and low-level control due to the absence of clear physical grounding in sparse visual representations.

## Method

- Developed a physics-grounded data curation pipeline to automatically extract sparse, physically meaningful 'structured frames' from raw robotic trajectories using kinematic cues like gripper state transitions and turning points.
- Implemented a two-stage training paradigm within a unified discrete token vocabulary for vision, language, and action, enabling efficient learning and transfer.
- Reformulated the generative world model into an explicit structured planner that autoregressively predicts these sparse, kinematically-grounded visual subgoals, directly translating into an executable action policy.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **75.0%** on SimplerEnv-WidowX and **94.8%** across LIBERO task suites, outperforming prior methods like UniVLA and DreamVLA.
- Demonstrated enhanced real-world performance with an **87.5%** average success rate on basic pick-and-place tasks and 8 out of 10 completions on a long-horizon tidy-up task.
- Showcased robust generalization to unseen objects (**80.0%** success) and resilience to human interventions (**80.0%** success) in real-world settings, indicating stability amidst dynamic changes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Deriving visual subgoals from intrinsic robot-centric kinematic cues provides explicit physical grounding, bridging the gap between abstract planning and precise low-level control.
> - Sparse, structured visual foresight inherently mitigates error accumulation and plan drift, offering stable progress anchors for long-horizon manipulation tasks.
> - A shared discrete token space for all modalities facilitates seamless integration and transfer, allowing a visual foresight model to be directly adapted into an actionable policy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation relies on the effective integration of task planning and physical control. Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have emerged as a significant paradigm in this field, aiming to translate natural language instructions and visual observations directly into low-level robot actions. A recent development within VLA architectures is the incorporation of generative world models. These models internalize environmental dynamics to provide predictive visual foresight, offering spatiotemporal context that enables policies to anticipate future states beyond immediate perception.

However, existing approaches to visual foresight present distinct challenges. Many VLA methods, such as those relying on dense or step-wise future predictions (e.g., pixel-level or latent representations), face issues of visual redundancy and error accumulation, particularly over long horizons. The continuous generation of temporal rollouts can compound local visual artifacts or physical inconsistencies, leading to plan drift.

To mitigate these issues, a separate line of research has explored sparse visual foresight. These methods aim to compress the prediction horizon by focusing on key events or subgoals. However, current sparse frameworks often define subgoals semantically or rely on implicit latent representations. Semantic subgoals, while high-level, may lack the explicit geometric grounding necessary for precise manipulation. Implicit latent states, while useful for policy learning, do not offer verifiable geometric targets for closed-loop control. Consequently, these sparse representations can struggle to provide actionable guidance for low-level kinematic execution due to a lack of clear physical grounding.

This paper addresses these limitations by proposing StructVLA, which reformulates a generative world model to provide sparse, physically meaningful visual foresight. It seeks to bridge the gap between abstract planning and precise low-level control by introducing an explicit structured planner that is grounded in intrinsic kinematic cues, thereby aiming to improve the reliability and robustness of long-horizon robotic manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to address fundamental limitations in current world-model-based VLA architectures for robotic manipulation and to develop a system that offers improved reliability and control.

The motivation stems from two main challenges observed in the existing landscape:

1. **Limitations of Dense Future Prediction:**
 - **Visual Redundancy:** Approaches that predict dense or step-wise future observations (e.g., pixel or latent rollouts) generate extensive visual information, much of which may be irrelevant to the essential task events. This redundancy can obscure critical progress markers within high-frequency noise.
 - **Error Accumulation and Plan Drift:** Generating continuous temporal rollouts in a predictive manner tends to accumulate errors. Over extended planning horizons, minor prediction inaccuracies or physical inconsistencies can compound, leading to significant deviations from the intended plan, a phenomenon referred to as plan drift. This makes stable long-horizon manipulation challenging.

2. **Limitations of Existing Sparse Visual Foresight Methods:**
 - **Lack of Explicit Kinematic Grounding:** While sparse methods aim to reduce redundancy and error accumulation, they often represent visual foresight using high-level semantic subtasks or implicit latent states. Semantic subgoals, such as "move object A to location B," may lack the precise geometric and kinematic details required for successful low-level execution. Implicit latent representations, while facilitating policy learning, do not provide explicit, verifiable geometric targets for closed-loop control.
 - **Weak Alignment Between Planning and Execution:** Without explicit physical grounding, these sparse representations can fail to provide actionable guidance for low-level kinematic execution. This disconnect weakens the alignment between the high-level planning stage and the fine-grained motion control needed for manipulation tasks.

To address these issues, the key objective of StructVLA is to reformulate the generative world model into an explicit, physically grounded structured planner. This involves predicting sparse, physically meaningful "structured frames" that serve as mid-level visual subgoals. By deriving these frames from intrinsic kinematic cues, such as gripper state transitions and kinematic turning points, the aim is to capture spatiotemporal milestones that are closely aligned with task progress and are directly actionable for low-level execution, thereby providing stable long-horizon manipulation and robust generalization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

StructVLA operates as a unified framework that reformulates a generative world model into an explicit structured planner for robotic control. The methodology involves a physics-grounded data curation pipeline and a two-stage training paradigm within a shared discrete token vocabulary.

#### 3.1. Physics-Grounded Data Curation

At the core of StructVLA is the concept of "structured frames," which are sparse, physically grounded visual subgoals. These frames are extracted from raw robotic trajectories through an automated offline pipeline, designed to identify essential manipulation milestones without relying on manual semantic subtask labeling.

The extraction process involves:
- **Kinematic Structured Frame Extraction:** This step leverages robot-centric kinematic cues to identify significant points in a trajectory.
 - **Gripper State Transitions:** Frames are selected around changes in gripper state (open/close), which often correspond to contact state changes. To account for signal noise and physical actuation lag, a stabilization window (δ_settle) is applied after each gripper state flip. The last quasi-static observation before the robot resumes major motion (exceeding an adaptive threshold τ_high) is selected.
 - **Kinematic Turning Points:** These points represent brief slowdowns or shifts in motion regimes, such as when an end-effector aligns above a target before descending. They are identified by applying a sliding window (length W) and selecting frames where the smoothed end-effector speed remains below an adaptive threshold (τ_low) throughout the window.
 - **Temporal Coverage:** To prevent redundancy and ensure adequate subgoal coverage, a minimum temporal gap is enforced between selected frames. For long segments with sparse kinematic events, additional frames are sampled by selecting observations with the smallest L2 change in consecutive action commands, serving as a proxy for low-motion, stable states.
- **Offline Trajectory Filtering (VLM-based):** The initial set of candidate structured frames is further refined using a Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically GPT-5.2. The VLM, provided with the global instruction and candidate observations, is used strictly offline to remove visually redundant or task-irrelevant frames based on a fixed set of filtering rules. This step ensures that the final sequence of structured frames reflects coherent and executable milestones. Crucially, this VLM assistance is only for dataset construction and is not used during online inference.

#### 3.2. Two-Stage Training Paradigm

StructVLA employs a two-stage training approach with a unified discrete token vocabulary for vision, language, and action. The Emu3 tokenizer with Vector Quantization (VQ) discretizes language instructions and image frames. Actions are discretized using the FAST tokenizer, mapped to the final 1024 token IDs of the language vocabulary, allowing all modalities to share a single discrete token space.

- **Stage 1: Structured Planner (Visual Foresight)**
 - **Objective:** To train the generative world model to autoregressively predict the physics-grounded structured frames (s) identified during data curation.
 - **Input:** A language instruction (l) and a visual context window (O_t) comprising recent observations.
 - **Process:** The model learns the conditional distribution P_θ(s | l, O_t). Language, visual context, and target structured frames are converted into discrete token sequences (T_l, T_O_t, T_s, respectively).
 - **Loss Function:** A cross-entropy loss (L_planner) is minimized over the predicted visual tokens (T_s), conditioned on the tokenized instruction and historical observations. This process enables the model to forecast kinematically grounded structured frames, developing an implicit awareness of task progress.
 - **Efficiency:** LoRA (rank=32, scaling factor α=32) is employed for parameter-efficient fine-tuning, preserving foundational visual prediction capabilities while adapting to structured-frame supervision.

- **Stage 2: Action Policy (Plan-to-Act Transfer)**
 - **Objective:** To formulate the pretrained structured planner from Stage 1 into an executable action policy by shifting the optimization objective to predicting low-level action tokens.
 - **Input:** An explicitly constructed conditioning context, concatenating the tokenized global language instruction (T_l) with interleaved visual and action tokens (H_t = {T_o_t-H+1, T_a_t-H+1..., T_o_t}).
 - **Process:** The model autoregressively predicts the sequence of discretized action tokens (T_a_t) required for the current subtask.
 - **Loss Function:** A cross-entropy loss (L_act) is computed exclusively over these sequentially generated action tokens, restricting supervision to the action domain. This enables the generative structured planner to translate its internalized visual foresight directly into reliable, low-level action control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

StructVLA was evaluated across various simulation benchmarks and real-world deployments, demonstrating competitive performance, enhanced generalization, and improved robustness compared to baseline methods.

#### 4.1. Simulation Benchmarks

- **SimplerEnv-WidowX:** StructVLA achieved an average success rate of 75.0%, outperforming all reported baselines, including UniVLA (69.8%), mimic-video (56.3%), VideoVLA (53.1%), and Octo-Small (29.5%). Notably, on the "Stack Green on Yellow Block" task, StructVLA achieved 45.8% success, matching VideoVLA and exceeding other baselines, indicating its capability for tasks requiring precise spatial execution and sustained progress tracking.
- **LIBERO:** StructVLA demonstrated an average success rate of 94.8% across four task suites (LIBERO-Spatial, LIBERO-Object, LIBERO-Goal, LIBERO-Long). It outperformed prior methods like UniVLA (93.7%) and DreamVLA (92.6%). StructVLA showed particularly strong performance on the challenging LIBERO-Long suite with a 92.2% success rate, indicating more reliable execution over extended horizons. This is attributed to the structured planner providing sparse mid-level visual subgoals, which act as progress anchors to guide the policy and reduce drift.

#### 4.2. Real-World Deployments

- **Basic Pick-and-Place and Long-Horizon Tidy-Up:** On four pick-and-place tasks, StructVLA achieved an 87.5% average success rate, surpassing UniVLA by 20.0%. It achieved 10/10 successes on the "eggplant" task. For the long-horizon "tidy-up" task, StructVLA completed 8 out of 10 trials, compared to 4/10 for UniVLA and 2/10 for SpatialVLA, suggesting enhanced stability over prolonged execution in physical environments.

#### 4.3. Real-World Generalization and Robustness

- **Zero-Shot Generalization (Unseen Objects):** For out-of-distribution pick-and-place tasks involving novel object colors (e.g., pink bowl) and geometries (e.g., blue cup), StructVLA achieved 80.0% success, compared to 30.0% for UniVLA. On the long-horizon tidy-up task with unseen objects, StructVLA maintained 70.0% success.
- **Robustness to Human Interventions:** Under dynamic perturbations, such as human-introduced objects during execution, StructVLA retained 80.0% success, while UniVLA achieved 40.0%. These results suggest that kinematically grounded milestones from structured planning provide stable progress anchors, enabling the policy to remain consistent amidst appearance shifts and dynamic disturbances.

#### 4.4. Extended Analysis

- **Structured Planner Performance:** Qualitative comparisons showed that StructVLA's structured planner generates coherent, long-horizon visual foresight with less drift than a conventional world model, which often produces near-static or distorted predictions over longer ranges.
- **Mechanistic Insights:** Attention map visualizations indicated that StructVLA's planner focuses attention on motion-related regions, particularly around the gripper during kinematic transitions, unlike the more diffuse attention of baseline models. The planner also demonstrated visually coherent predictions on out-of-distribution scenes and tasks, suggesting preservation of useful pretrained priors and generalization to novel environments.
- **Training Efficiency:** StructVLA exhibited strong sample efficiency, reaching high success rates with fewer training steps than prior methods under identical batch sizes. This is attributed to structured planning providing task progress cues, reducing the policy’s effective search space.
- **Grasp Quality vs. Task Success:** StructVLA achieved the highest mean grasp success rate (89.6%) and a higher grasp-to-task conversion rate compared to UniVLA, indicating improved multi-step, long-horizon execution stability beyond grasping itself, potentially by reducing plan drift.
- **History Context Length:** Unlike UniVLA, StructVLA's performance improved with longer historical context windows (from 70.8% with 2 frames to 75.0% with 4 frames), suggesting that the structured planner effectively leverages extended temporal information for long-range task progress and planning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The StructVLA framework presents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation by addressing critical limitations of existing VLA models, particularly in the domain of long-horizon tasks and real-world deployment.

The key significance lies in its reformulation of generative world models into an *explicit, physically grounded structured planner*. By predicting sparse, kinematically meaningful structured frames, StructVLA offers several advantages:

- **Enhanced Reliability and Reduced Plan Drift:** Unlike dense future prediction methods that suffer from error accumulation and visual redundancy, StructVLA's sparse, structured foresight provides compact, key progress anchors. This design inherently mitigates plan drift, leading to more stable and reliable execution over extended horizons.
- **Improved Alignment Between Planning and Execution:** Current sparse methods often lack explicit kinematic grounding, creating a disconnect between high-level planning and low-level control. StructVLA bridges this gap by deriving its structured frames from intrinsic robot-centric cues (gripper transitions, kinematic turning points). This ensures that visual foresight is directly actionable and tightly coupled to executable interaction phases, providing precise physical guidance.
- **Robust Generalization and Real-World Performance:** The empirical results demonstrate StructVLA's strong generalization capabilities to unseen objects and robustness against dynamic human interventions in real-world settings. This suggests that the physically grounded milestones enable policies to maintain consistency despite appearance shifts and environmental disturbances, which is crucial for practical robot deployment.
- **Efficiency in Learning:** The structured planning approach appears to encourage representations that efficiently capture task progress, potentially reducing the effective search space for the action policy and leading to faster learning with less training data.

The potential impact of StructVLA is substantial for developing more capable and autonomous robotic systems. By providing a method for reliable, long-horizon manipulation grounded in physical reality, it moves beyond solely semantic or implicit planning. This can facilitate the deployment of robots in complex, unstructured environments where tasks require sequential, precise interactions and adaptability. Future work building on this foundation could lead to more robust and general-purpose robots capable of performing intricate manipulation tasks with minimal human supervision.
