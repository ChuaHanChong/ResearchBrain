# VLM Q-Learning: Aligning Vision-Language Models for Interactive Decision-Making

> [!summary] Summary
> A reinforcement learning framework called AFSFT (Advantage Filtered SFT) enables vision-language models to learn interactive decision-making from both successful and unsuccessful demonstrations through off-policy learning, achieving improved performance across Gym Cards, BabyAI, and MiniWoB benchmarks while maintaining the stability of supervised fine-tuning approaches.

## Problem

- Vision-language models (VLMs) struggle with strict output syntax requirements and long-context prompting in interactive tasks
- Current approaches rely heavily on high-quality supervised demonstrations, limiting performance to the best strategy in training data
- Pure reinforcement-learning methods for VLM agent alignment lack the stability and simplicity of the widely used supervised fine-tuning workflow

## Method

- Combines ==supervised fine-tuning== with ==off-policy reinforcement learning== through ==token-level advantage filtering==
- Uses ==actor-critic architecture== where VLM acts as policy and separate ==critic head== estimates token values
- Enables ==offline-to-online learning== where agent initially learns from fixed dataset before collecting new interactions

## Results

- Matches or exceeds performance of previous RL methods on Gym Cards tasks
- Successfully enables online learning and policy refinement in BabyAI, while also recovering effective policies from noisy datasets in the MiniWoB benchmark
- Retains performance while requiring minimal parameter overhead through LoRA adaptation

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Token-based action space allows treating each word in agent's reply as separate action for standard RL formulation
> - Filtering out suboptimal tokens from training data enables self-improvement beyond demonstration quality
> - Combining SFT stability with RL exploration capabilities produces more robust agents

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper contributes to the growing field of language model agents and their extension to vision-language models (VLMs). Here's how it fits into the broader research landscape:

- **LLM Agents:** The work builds upon recent advancements in using large language models (LLMs) as agents that can accomplish user-specified goals in interactive environments. This is a rapidly expanding area, with research focusing on techniques like prompting, in-context learning, and reinforcement learning to align LLMs with specific decision-making objectives.
- **Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** The paper extends the concept of LLM agents to VLMs, enabling agents to process both visual and textual information. This is crucial for applications in computer automation, robotics, and other areas where agents need to understand visual inputs. However, the paper acknowledges that VLMs currently lag behind LLMs in critical areas like action syntax adherence and long-context prompting.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) for Agent Alignment:** The paper addresses the challenge of aligning VLMs with decision-making objectives by adopting a reinforcement learning (RL) approach. This is a departure from the more common supervised fine-tuning (SFT) approach, which relies on collecting successful demonstrations. The paper argues that RL, especially offline RL, can enable agents to learn from both successful and unsuccessful decisions, allowing them to self-improve and overcome limitations in training data.
- **Offline-to-Online RL:** The paper focuses on an offline-to-online RL approach, where the agent learns from a fixed dataset before interacting with the environment to collect new data. This is a practical approach for VLM agent training, as it allows the agent to initially learn from existing datasets or demonstrations before fine-tuning its behavior in a more interactive setting.
- **Tool Use and Function Calling:** The paper also touches on the important concept of "tool use" or "function calling," where LLMs/VLMs can interact with external APIs or environments by generating structured outputs that can be interpreted and executed. This is essential for building agents that can perform complex tasks by leveraging external tools and services.
- **Relationship to Prior Work:** The paper cites and discusses relevant prior work in areas such as LLM agents, VLMs, reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), and tool use. It highlights the limitations of existing approaches, such as the reliance on supervised data and the challenges of training VLMs to adhere to strict output syntax requirements.
- **Novelty:** The paper introduces a novel approach called "Advantage Filtered SFT (AFSFT)," which combines the stability and simplicity of SFT with the self-improvement capabilities of off-policy RL. This approach allows VLM agents to learn from low-quality datasets and outperform their demonstrations, while also retaining the ability to collect new data for further improvement.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The paper's key objectives and motivations are:

- **Address Limitations of VLMs for Agent Tasks:** To address the limitations of current VLMs in areas such as following environment's strict output syntax requirements and long-context prompting, which hinder their performance in agent tasks.
- **Overcome Reliance on High-Quality Supervised Data:** To overcome the reliance on supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on task-specific expert demonstrations, which cannot outperform the best strategy in the training data.
- **Develop a Self-Improving VLM Agent:** To develop a VLM agent that can self-improve and learn from both successful and unsuccessful decisions, using a reinforcement learning (RL) approach.
- **Retain Stability and Simplicity of SFT:** To develop an RL solution that retains the stability and simplicity of the widely used SFT workflow, while allowing the agent to self-improve.
- **Demonstrate Effectiveness of Off-Policy RL:** To demonstrate the effectiveness of off-policy RL for fine-tuning VLMs to agent tasks, using open-weight models and multi-modal agent domains.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper proposes an off-policy RL solution called "Advantage Filtered SFT (AFSFT)" to fine-tune VLMs for agent tasks. The methodology and approach can be summarized as follows:

- **Problem Formulation:** The paper formalizes the problem of training VLM agents as a reinforcement learning problem, where the VLM acts as a policy that maps text and image observations to text actions.
- **Offline-to-Online RL:** The approach is based on offline-to-online RL, where the agent initially learns from a fixed dataset before interacting with the environment to collect new data.
- **Advantage Filtered SFT (AFSFT):** AFSFT combines the stability and simplicity of SFT with the self-improvement capabilities of off-policy RL. It involves filtering out tokens from the SFT dataset that are estimated to degrade the agent's performance.
- **Actor-Critic Architecture:** AFSFT utilizes an actor-critic architecture, where the VLM acts as the actor (policy) and a separate output head (critic) is trained to estimate the value (Q-value) of each token in the vocabulary. The critic is used to filter the dataset for the language modeling head (actor).
- **Token-Based Action Space:** The paper converts turn-based interactions to a token-based action space, where each token of the agent's reply is treated as a separate action. This allows for a more standard RL problem formulation.
- **Loss Function:** The VLM is trained using a joint loss function that combines a supervised fine-tuning (SFT) loss with a temporal difference (TD) loss for training the critic.
- **Training Techniques:** The paper utilizes various training techniques to improve stability and efficiency, including mixed precision training, 8-bit optimizers, and LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) for adapting the VLM's behavior with a small number of trainable parameters.
- **Experimental Setup:** The approach is evaluated on two open-weight VLMs (PaliGemma, xGen-MM, and MoonDream2) across three multi-modal agent domains: Gym Cards (Blackjack and NumberLine), BabyAI, and MiniWoB.
- **Prompting and Action Parsing:** The method is designed to be compatible with arbitrary prompting schemes, including chain-of-thought reasoning. It also incorporates action parsing to handle the challenges of generating structured outputs that can be interpreted and executed by the environment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper's main findings and results are:

- **VLMs Struggle with Action Syntax:** Base VLMs struggle to cooperate with the expected action syntax, even in simple agent-like tasks.
- **AFSFT Enables Self-Improvement:** AFSFT enables VLM agents to self-improve and outperform their demonstrations, by filtering out suboptimal tokens and learning from both successful and unsuccessful decisions.
- **Improved Performance in Gym Cards:** AFSFT significantly improves the performance of VLM agents in Gym Cards tasks, allowing them to match or exceed the performance of previous RL methods.
- **Successful Online Learning in BabyAI:** AFSFT enables successful online learning in BabyAI, where the agent can refine its behavior through interaction with the environment.
- **Robustness to Noisy Datasets in MiniWoB:** AFSFT demonstrates robustness to noisy datasets in MiniWoB, allowing the agent to recover a policy that more closely resembles a larger, more capable model.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The paper's findings have several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Improved VLM Agents:** The AFSFT approach provides a promising solution for training more capable and robust VLM agents that can effectively interact with complex environments.
- **Reduced Reliance on Supervised Data:** The approach reduces the reliance on high-quality supervised data, making it easier to train VLM agents in scenarios where expert demonstrations are scarce or unavailable.
- **Enhanced Self-Improvement Capabilities:** The self-improvement capabilities of AFSFT allow VLM agents to adapt to new tasks and environments, without requiring extensive retraining.
- **Wider Applicability of VLMs:** The paper's findings could lead to wider applicability of VLMs in various domains, including computer automation, robotics, and other areas where agents need to understand visual inputs and interact with complex environments.
- **Practical Approach for VLM Fine-Tuning:** AFSFT offers a practical approach for fine-tuning VLMs, as it retains the stability and simplicity of SFT while also incorporating the benefits of RL.
- **Contribution to Offline-to-Online RL:** The paper contributes to the growing body of research on offline-to-online RL, demonstrating the effectiveness of this approach for training VLM agents.

In conclusion, this research paper presents a valuable contribution to the field of VLM agents, addressing key limitations and proposing a novel approach that enables self-improvement and robustness to noisy data. The AFSFT method offers a promising pathway for developing more capable and adaptable VLM agents that can effectively interact with complex environments.
