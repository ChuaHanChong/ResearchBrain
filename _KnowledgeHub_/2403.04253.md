# Mastering Memory Tasks with World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Mila introduce Recall to Imagine (R2I), a model-based reinforcement learning agent that integrates State Space Models (SSMs) into its world model. R2I establishes new state-of-the-art performance in memory-intensive RL tasks like Memory Maze and POPGym, achieving superhuman scores in Memory Maze, while also demonstrating up to 9 times faster training compared to DreamerV3.

## Problem

- Existing Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) agents, like Dreamer, rely on RNNs or Transformers whose limitations (vanishing gradients, quadratic complexity) hinder long-term memory and credit assignment.
- Current MBRL agents struggle with tasks requiring recall of distant observations or evaluating actions based on long-horizon future rewards.
- Achieving superhuman performance in complex, partially observable 3D memory environments, such as Memory Maze, has been a significant challenge for existing RL algorithms.

## Method

- Integrates State Space Models (SSMs), specifically a variant of S4, into the DreamerV3 ==world model== to create the Structured State-Space Model (S3M).
- Modifies DreamerV3's representation model to a non-recurrent form (q_θ(z_t | o_t)) to enable parallel computation of posterior samples, essential for SSM efficiency.
- Utilizes parallel scan for SSM computation and adapts policy inputs to optionally leverage the internal SSM hidden states (x_t) in addition to (z_t, h_t) for memory-intensive tasks.

## Results

- Achieves up to 9 times faster training time compared to DreamerV3 in vector environments and up to 6 times faster in image environments.
- Establishes new state-of-the-art performance in memory-intensive tabular benchmarks (BSuite, POPGym), solving tasks like Autoencode and RepeatPrevious at 'Medium' and 'Hard' difficulties where prior methods struggled.
- Attains superhuman performance in the 9x9, 11x11, and 13x13 Memory Maze environments, demonstrating robust long-term information retention over thousands of environment steps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - State Space Models (SSMs) are effective backbones for world models in reinforcement learning, efficiently capturing very long-range dependencies.
> - The internal hidden states (x_t) of SSMs contain critical long-term information distinct from the output (h_t) and are crucial for policy learning in memory-intensive tasks.
> - Including entire episodes within the training batch sequence is vital for leveraging SSMs' capability to model very long sequences and handle memory effectively.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of **Reinforcement Learning (RL)**, specifically **Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL)**, and **Deep Sequence Modeling**. The broader landscape of RL has seen significant advancements, with MBRL emerging as a powerful paradigm due to its potential for sample efficiency, transferability, and safer planning by explicitly learning a "world model" of the environment's dynamics. The Dreamer family of agents (Hafner et al., 2019a, 2020, 2023) represents the state-of-the-art in this domain, integrating perception, simulation, and planning within learned latent dynamics.

A persistent challenge in complex RL tasks is **long-term temporal reasoning**, encompassing both **long-term memory** (recalling distant observations) and **long-horizon credit assignment** (evaluating an action's impact on distant future rewards). Current MBRL agents, including Dreamer, typically rely on **Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs)**, such as Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs), as their world model backbone. However, RNNs are notoriously hampered by the **vanishing gradient problem**, which limits their ability to capture dependencies over extended time steps.

More recently, **Transformers** have been adopted for world modeling due to their success in language modeling. While powerful, Transformers suffer from **quadratic computational complexity** with respect to sequence length, making them computationally expensive and often unstable on very long sequences required for complex memory tasks.

This paper addresses a crucial gap by introducing **State Space Models (SSMs)**, particularly the S4 family (Gu et al., 2021a), into the MBRL framework. SSMs have revolutionized sequence modeling in supervised and self-supervised learning by demonstrating remarkable capabilities in capturing dependencies in extremely long sequences (up to 16K length) with sub-quadratic (linear) complexity and parallelizability. The work pioneers the application of these powerful sequence models within the dynamic and online learning setting of MBRL, aiming to transfer their long-range dependency modeling strengths to the challenges of memory and credit assignment in RL.

The paper differentiates itself from concurrent work like S4WM (Deng et al., 2023), which also explores SSMs for world modeling but primarily focuses on improving the world model's likelihood/accuracy (a supervised learning objective) rather than directly translating these improvements into enhanced end-to-end RL agent performance (i.e., higher rewards). As the authors note, higher world model likelihood does not automatically guarantee better policy performance, making R2I's focus on RL task outcomes a significant distinction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind "MASTERING MEMORY TASKS WITH WORLD MODELS" stems from the inherent limitations of current model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) agents, particularly the prominent DreamerV3 framework, when confronted with tasks demanding extensive temporal reasoning.

The **key objectives** of this research are:

1. **Enhance Long-Term Memory and Credit Assignment:** To overcome the inability of existing MBRL agents to effectively handle long-term dependencies, extended time gaps between actions and outcomes, and tasks requiring the recall of distant observations. This directly addresses the shortcomings of RNNs and Transformers in modeling very long sequences within the dynamic RL environment.
2. **Integrate State Space Models (SSMs) into World Models:** To leverage the demonstrated strengths of SSMs (specifically a variant of S4) in efficiently capturing long-range dependencies in supervised and self-supervised learning, and apply this capability to the world model component of MBRL agents. This represents the core architectural innovation, leading to the proposed "Recall to Imagine (R2I)" method.
3. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance on Memory-Intensive RL Tasks:** To empirically demonstrate that R2I can surpass existing methods in challenging benchmarks explicitly designed to test long-term memory and credit assignment, such as BSuite, POPGym, and Memory Maze.
4. **Demonstrate Superhuman Performance in Complex Memory Domains:** A specific ambition is to achieve or exceed human-level performance in highly complex 3D memory tasks like Memory Maze, which has historically posed a significant challenge for existing memory-augmented RL algorithms.
5. **Maintain Generality Across Diverse RL Tasks:** To ensure that the improvements in memory capabilities do not compromise performance on classic, less memory-intensive RL benchmarks, such as Atari and DeepMind Control Suite (DMC), thereby showcasing the broad applicability of the R2I method.
6. **Improve Computational Efficiency:** To develop a method that is not only more effective in handling memory but also faster than the current state-of-the-art MBRL methods (e.g., DreamerV3), leading to faster wall-time convergence and more accessible research.

In essence, the authors are motivated to push the boundaries of what MBRL agents can achieve in complex, temporally extended environments, by strategically integrating a novel, efficient, and powerful sequence modeling architecture into the world model.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of R2I (Recall to Imagine) is the integration of State Space Models (SSMs) into the world model of DreamerV3, creating what the authors term the **Structured State-Space Model (S3M)**. This integration is designed to achieve both superior long-range dependency modeling and computational efficiency.

#### 3.1. World Model Details (S3M)

- **Foundation:** R2I builds directly upon the successful DreamerV3 framework, inheriting its general structure for learning latent dynamics and policy optimization.
- **Non-Recurrent Representation Model:** A key modification from DreamerV3's Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM) is transforming the representation model from `q_θ(z_t | h_t, o_t)` to `q_θ(z_t | o_t)`. This crucial change removes the sequential dependency on the previous deterministic state `h_t`, enabling the computation of posterior samples `z_t` for all time steps in parallel. This parallelizability is essential for leveraging the efficiency of SSMs. Ablation studies confirm this modification does not degrade performance and can even enhance it in memory-intensive tasks.
- **SSM Architecture:** The S3M's sequence model `f_θ` is composed of multiple layers of SSMs. Each SSM layer processes its input, followed by a GeLU activation, a fully-connected GLU transformation, and LayerNormalization, similar to the architecture used by Smith et al. (2023). The final output of the last SSM layer forms the deterministic state `h_t`, while the internal SSM hidden states are denoted `x_t`.
- **SSM Design Choices (from Appendix B.1):**
 - **Parameterization:** R2I employs a **diagonal parameterization** for its state matrices, choosing simplicity and computational speed over the potentially higher expressiveness of Diagonal-Plus-Low-Rank (DPLR) matrices, as empirical results showed comparable agent performance but significant speedups with diagonal.
 - **Dimensionality:** R2I utilizes **Multi-Input Multi-Output (MIMO) SSMs**. This choice allows for a reduction in latent size, facilitates efficient parallelization, eliminates the need for separate mixing layers, and offers flexibility for diverse input feature dynamics compared to single-input single-output (SISO) SSMs.
 - **Computational Modeling:** The paper opts for **parallel scan** over convolution mode for SSM computation. Parallel scan is critical because it efficiently computes `y_1:T` and, crucially, `x_1:T` (all intermediate hidden states) given `u_1:T` and `x_0`. Convolution mode does not yield `x_1:T`, which R2I finds necessary for policy learning. Parallel scan also allows for efficient scaling across distributed devices and supports hidden state resetting.
 - **Hidden State Resetting:** R2I modifies the parallel scan operator to incorporate a "done" flag from the environment, enabling the resetting of hidden states (`x_t`) at episode boundaries. This is vital for handling sampled sequences from the replay buffer that may span multiple episodes, improving early training performance.
- **Prediction Heads:** The world model includes prediction heads for reconstructing observations `p_θ(o_t | z_t, h_t)`, rewards `p_θ(r_t | z_t, h_t)`, and episode continuation flags `p_θ(c_t | z_t, h_t)`.
- **Training Objective:** R2I optimizes a loss function similar to DreamerV3's Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO), incorporating KL-divergence clipping, KL-balancing to prioritize S3M training, and scaling coefficients (β_pred, β_rep, β_dyn). The learning rate for SSM matrices is kept consistent with the rest of the world model.

#### 3.2. Actor-Critic Details

- **Latent Imagination:** The policy and value functions are trained entirely within the learned latent space, using trajectories imagined by the world model. The parallel scan ensures that `z_1:T`, `h_1:T`, and `x_1:T` are readily available for imagination without needing burn-in steps.
- **Policy Input Adaptation:** A significant empirical finding is the choice of inputs for the actor and critic networks. Unlike DreamerV3 which relies solely on `h_t` and `z_t`, R2I experiments reveal that:
 - For **memory-intensive environments**, conditioning the policy on `x_t` (either `π(hat_a_t | z_t, x_t)` or `π(hat_a_t | z_t, h_t, x_t)`) is crucial. This suggests that `x_t` (the internal SSM hidden state) retains critical long-term information not fully encapsulated by `h_t` (the output of the last SSM layer). The paper notes that using `h_t` alone in memory tasks can lead to policy learning breakdown due to non-stationarity of features.
 - For **non-memory environments**, `π(hat_a_t | z_t, h_t)` (the "output state policy") generally performs well.
- **Training Protocol:** R2I adheres to DreamerV3's actor-critic training methodology, including Reinforce for discrete action spaces, backpropagation through latent dynamics, fixed entropy bonus, two-hot regression in the critic, and percentile-based normalization for returns.
- **Replay Buffer:** A large FIFO replay buffer (10M steps) is used to stabilize world model training and prevent overfitting, particularly beneficial in lifelong learning settings.
- **Fixed Hyperparameters:** A key design principle is the use of fixed world model hyperparameters across all domains, demonstrating the generality of R2I. The only variations are network sizes (Small, Medium, Memory configurations) and the policy input choice.
- **Full Episode Inclusion in Batches:** Ablation studies reveal that including the entire episode within a single sequence in the training batch is critical for effective memory handling, leveraging the SSM's capability to model very long sequences.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

R2I demonstrates compelling performance improvements across diverse RL benchmarks, particularly excelling in memory-intensive tasks while maintaining competitive performance in standard domains and offering significant computational advantages.

1. **Computational Efficiency:**
 - R2I shows **up to 9 times faster training time** compared to DreamerV3 in vector environments (e.g., POPGym) and up to 6 times faster in image environments (e.g., Memory Maze). This translates directly to **faster wall-time convergence**, making research more efficient and accessible.

2. **State-of-the-Art in Memory-Intensive Tabular Tasks:**
 - **BSuite (Memory Length & Discounting Chain):** R2I **significantly outperforms DreamerV3**, which was the previous SOTA. In "Discounting Chain," R2I learns dependencies beyond DreamerV3's limit of ~30 steps. In "Memory Length," it maintains learning ability across a much wider range of increasing episode complexities (up to ~100 steps of memorization), demonstrating superior long-term memory.
 - **POPGym (RepeatPrevious, Autoencode, Concentration):** R2I establishes a **new state-of-the-art**. It vastly outperforms DreamerV3 and various model-free baselines (PPO with MLP, GRU, LSTM, S4D) across "Easy," "Medium," and "Hard" difficulty levels. Notably, R2I solves tasks like "Autoencode: Easy" and "Medium" and "RepeatPrevious: Medium" and "Hard" where other baselines struggle, pushing the memory limits up to approximately 100 steps.

3. **Superhuman Performance in Complex 3D Memory Tasks:**
 - **Memory Maze:** R2I consistently **outperforms Dreamer (with truncated backpropagation through time) and IMPALA** across all tested maze sizes (9x9, 11x11, 13x13, 15x15). Remarkably, R2I achieves **superhuman performance** in the 9x9, 11x11, and 13x13 mazes, demonstrating its ability to retain information about object locations, maze layouts, and its own position over thousands of environment steps. This is a significant breakthrough in a domain previously challenging for RL agents.

4. **Generality in Non-Memory Domains:**
 - **Atari 100K & DeepMind Control Suite (DMC):** R2I maintains **comparable performance to DreamerV3**. This is crucial as it demonstrates that the enhanced memory capabilities do not come at the expense of generality, allowing R2I to perform well across diverse tasks requiring different control types (continuous vs. discrete), dynamics (stochastic vs. deterministic), and reward structures (sparse vs. dense).

5. **Ablation Study Insights:**
 - **Non-recurrent Representation Model:** The ablation confirmed that removing the recurrent connection to `h_t` in the representation model (`q_θ(z_t | o_t)`) does not hurt performance and can even enhance it, validating a key architectural decision for parallelization.
 - **Policy Input:** The choice of policy input (`z_t, h_t` vs. `z_t, x_t` vs. `z_t, h_t, x_t`) significantly impacts performance in memory-intensive environments, with policies leveraging `x_t` often being superior. This highlights the distinct information content of `h_t` (output) and `x_t` (hidden states) within the S3M.
 - **Full Episode Inclusion:** Including entire episodes within the training batch sequence is critical for success in memory-intensive tasks, as performance scales positively with longer sequence lengths, underscoring the importance of unbroken temporal context for SSMs.

In summary, R2I sets new benchmarks for memory and credit assignment in RL while providing substantial computational efficiency and maintaining its general applicability across a broad range of tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Recall to Imagine (R2I) marks a significant advance in model-based reinforcement learning, with several profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Breakthrough in Long-Term Memory and Credit Assignment:** R2I represents the first successful integration of State Space Models (SSMs) into the world model of an MBRL agent. This directly addresses one of the most long-standing and challenging problems in RL: effectively handling long-term dependencies, extended credit assignment, and complex memory tasks. By setting new state-of-the-art performance across diverse memory benchmarks (BSuite, POPGym, Memory Maze), R2I expands the frontier of tasks that RL agents can reliably solve.

2. **Unlocking Superhuman Performance in Complex Environments:** Achieving superhuman performance in challenging 3D memory environments like Memory Maze is a testament to R2I's enhanced capabilities. This demonstrates the potential for RL agents to operate effectively in highly complex, partially observable environments where human-like long-term recall is essential, opening doors for applications in advanced robotics, navigation, and large-scale simulated environments.

3. **Increased Computational Efficiency:** The impressive speedup of up to 9 times faster training compared to DreamerV3 is a crucial practical contribution. Faster training translates into reduced computational costs, accelerated research cycles, and greater accessibility for researchers who might not have access to vast computing resources. This efficiency can democratize complex RL research and foster faster innovation.

4. **Generality and Robustness:** The demonstration that R2I maintains comparable performance in classic RL benchmarks (Atari, DMC) while excelling in memory tasks highlights its generality. This suggests that the architectural improvements are not specialized but offer a robust enhancement applicable across a broad spectrum of continuous and discrete control, and stochastic and deterministic environments. This enhances the utility and applicability of MBRL agents in real-world scenarios.

5. **Advancing the MBRL Paradigm:** R2I strengthens the overall MBRL paradigm by improving the fidelity and temporal reasoning capabilities of world models. A more accurate and temporally coherent world model allows for more effective planning and policy optimization, potentially leading to further breakthroughs in sample efficiency and transfer learning.

6. **Paving the Way for Future Research:**
 - **SSMs in RL:** This work validates SSMs as a powerful backbone for sequential processing in RL, inspiring further exploration of their variants and optimizations within various RL architectures.
 - **Hybrid Architectures:** The paper explicitly suggests investigating hybrid architectures combining SSMs with attention mechanisms (Transformers). This could leverage the complementary strengths of both: SSMs for global long-range dependencies and Transformers for local, context-aware interactions, potentially leading to even more powerful generalist agents.
 - **Scaling Sequence Lengths and Imagination Horizons:** The success of R2I underscores the importance of sequence length in training batches and the imagination horizon during planning. Future work can further optimize these aspects to push the memory boundaries even further.
 - **Rethinking Model Learning for Control:** The observation that improved world model likelihood does not always translate to better agent performance (as seen in comparisons with S4WM) prompts a re-evaluation of how world models are optimized for control objectives, moving towards more policy-centric model learning.

In conclusion, R2I offers a fundamental improvement to the temporal reasoning capabilities of model-based RL agents, making them more effective, efficient, and versatile. Its implications extend beyond academic benchmarks, potentially influencing the development of more intelligent and adaptable AI systems for complex real-world challenges.
