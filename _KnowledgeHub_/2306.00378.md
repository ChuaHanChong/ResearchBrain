# Example-based Motion Synthesis via Generative Motion Matching

> [!summary] Summary
> A generative motion matching framework, GenMM, synthesizes diverse and high-quality character motions from minimal example sequences by adapting classical motion matching with generative capabilities. The system produces stable, natural animations quickly, scales effectively to complex skeletal structures, and supports various animation applications such as motion completion and reassembly.

## Problem

- Traditional motion acquisition (mocap, manual authoring) is costly and time-intensive, leading to limited diversity in available motion datasets.
- Deep learning-based motion synthesis methods require substantial training time, are prone to visual artifacts (jittering, over-smoothing), and struggle to scale efficiently to complex skeletal structures.
- Classical Motion Matching, while producing high-quality motion, is dependent on extensive datasets and cannot generate novel, diverse motions from a small number of examples.

## Method

- GenMM, a multi-stage, coarse-to-fine progressive synthesis pipeline, adapts the ==Motion Matching== paradigm by incorporating generative capabilities.
- The framework uses skeleton-aware motion patches, extracted from overlapping sub-trees of the skeletal structure, to enhance spatial diversity.
- Generative matching employs a bidirectional similarity cost function for patch comparison, ensuring both completeness (exemplar content preservation) and coherence (synthesized content fidelity) without explicit training.

## Results

- GenMM synthesizes diverse, high-quality motion sequences within a fraction of a second (e.g., 0.08s for 100,000 frames) and requires no training time, outperforming deep learning baselines in efficiency and stability.
- The system demonstrates robust performance across varying skeletal complexities, maintaining high coverage (e.g., **86.90%** for 433 joints) and quality, an area where deep learning methods typically degrade.
- GenMM is versatile, successfully applying to various animation tasks including motion completion, key frame-guided generation, infinite looping, and motion reassembly for heterogeneous skeletons.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating generative principles like bidirectional similarity and multi-stage refinement into classical Motion Matching enables high-quality, diverse motion synthesis from limited examples without deep learning's training overhead or artifacts.
> - Decomposing full-body motion into skeleton-aware partial-body patches allows for more granular control over movement, increasing spatial diversity and enabling novel combinations of movements across body parts.
> - Utilizing bidirectional similarity as a generative cost function within an iterative refinement process ensures that synthesized motion patches closely match examples while comprehensively covering the input's motion distribution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of natural, varied, and detailed character motion is a fundamental challenge in computer animation. Traditional methods for acquiring motion data, such as motion capture (mocap) systems, or manually authoring animations are costly and time-intensive. Consequently, motion datasets are often limited in terms of diversity in style, skeletal structures, or creature types, which affects the performance of many data-driven motion synthesis techniques. The advancement of generative models that can synthesize diverse and extensive motions from limited example sequences is an important area of research.

Recent developments in deep learning have significantly impacted computer animation, enabling the synthesis of diverse and natural motions when trained on large datasets. Some advanced methods have even demonstrated the ability to learn internal distributions and synthesize variants from a single example sequence. However, deep learning-based motion synthesis methods typically entail several limitations: they require substantial training time, are prone to visual artifacts such as jittering or over-smoothing, and may not scale efficiently to large and complex skeletal structures.

This work explores an alternative approach by revisiting classical computer animation concepts, specifically "motion nearest neighbors," which predates the deep learning era and forms the basis of industrial solutions like Motion Matching. Motion Matching is known for producing high-quality, natural, and responsive character animations by searching a large mocap database for patches that fit a given local context. Its primary limitation, however, is its dependency on extensive datasets, which conflicts with the goal of generating diverse motions from a small number of examples.

The presented research, GenMM (Generative Motion Matching), aims to integrate generative capabilities into the Motion Matching paradigm. It draws inspiration from patch-based image synthesis, particularly the use of bidirectional similarity for measuring patch distribution discrepancy and multi-stage refinement. By doing so, GenMM seeks to overcome the drawbacks of deep learning methods (training time, artifacts, scalability) while retaining the quality and efficiency of Motion Matching, even when starting from a single or few example sequences. This positions GenMM as an effort to bridge established, high-quality, training-free methods with modern generative principles to enhance the diversity and applicability of motion synthesis from limited data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a generative model, named GenMM, capable of synthesizing a wide range of diverse, high-quality motions from a minimal input—either a single example sequence or a small set of examples. The motivation for this objective stems from several identified limitations in existing motion synthesis approaches:

1. **Addressing Limitations of Data-Driven Deep Learning Methods:** Current deep learning-based motion synthesis techniques, while capable of generating diverse motions from large datasets, face significant practical challenges. These include:
 - **Long Training Times:** Neural networks often require hours or days of offline training, making them less suitable for rapid prototyping or iterative design.
 - **Visual Artifacts:** Synthesized motions can exhibit undesirable visual artifacts like jittering, instability, or over-smoothing, reducing their fidelity.
 - **Scalability Issues:** Deep learning models can struggle to scale effectively to characters with complex or very large skeletal structures, limiting their applicability in diverse animation scenarios.
 The researchers are motivated to find an alternative that bypasses these issues.

2. **Leveraging Strengths of Motion Matching:** Motion Matching, a classical and widely adopted industrial solution for character animation, is recognized for its ability to produce exceptionally high-quality and natural motions without extensive training. However, its core mechanism relies on searching a large pre-existing database, making it ill-suited for generating *novel* and *diverse* motions solely from a limited set of examples. The motivation here is to infuse the training-free nature and superior quality of Motion Matching with generative capabilities.

3. **Introducing Generative Capabilities to Patch-Based Synthesis:** Inspired by recent advancements in image synthesis, particularly patch-GAN based methods and techniques utilizing bidirectional visual similarity, the researchers aim to adapt these concepts to motion synthesis. Specifically, they seek to use bidirectional similarity as a generative cost function within a motion matching framework to ensure both completeness (all example patches are represented in synthesis) and coherence (synthesized patches closely match examples), thereby preventing artifacts while generating new content.

4. **Enabling Versatile Motion Synthesis Applications:** Beyond simply generating diverse variants, the research is motivated to extend the framework's utility to a range of related applications that are not readily achievable with traditional Motion Matching alone. These include:
 - **Motion Completion:** Filling in missing parts of a motion sequence.
 - **Key Frame-Guided Generation:** Synthesizing motion coherent with a sparse set of user-defined keyframes.
 - **Infinite Looping:** Creating seamlessly repeatable animations.
 - **Motion Reassembly:** Combining motion attributes from different example creatures or skeletal parts.

In summary, the key objective is to create an efficient, high-quality, training-free generative motion synthesis framework that addresses the shortcomings of current deep learning methods, builds upon the strengths of Motion Matching, and provides a versatile tool for various animation tasks, particularly when only limited example data is available.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GenMM, the proposed framework, is a multi-stage generative model designed to synthesize diverse and high-quality motions from limited examples by adapting and extending the classical Motion Matching paradigm. The methodology is structured around a coarse-to-fine progressive synthesis, utilizing skeleton-aware motion patch extraction and a generative matching process based on bidirectional similarity.

#### 3.1. Motion Representation

A motion sequence is represented as a temporal series of poses. Each pose comprises root joint displacements, which are converted to local root velocities (V), and joint rotations (R) defined in their parent's coordinate frame using a 6D rotation representation. Foot contact labels (L), derivable from input motion by velocity thresholds, can be optionally incorporated to manage end-effector interaction with the ground, though the method's inherent quality preservation often makes this less critical. The combined features (R, V, and optionally L) form the motion feature space for `H` frames, denoted `M_H`.

#### 3.2. Multi-stage Motion Synthesis

The core of GenMM is a multi-stage, progressive synthesis pipeline (S stages).
- **Exemplar Pyramid:** From the input example motion (T), an exemplar pyramid `{T_1..., T_S}` is constructed. `T_S` is the original sequence, and `T_s` is a downsampled version of `T_{s+1}` by a factor `r > 1`.
- **Synthesis Pyramid:** A corresponding synthesis pyramid `{F_1..., F_S}` is generated, where `F_S` is the final output motion.
- **Coarse-to-Fine Generation:** Synthesis proceeds from the coarsest stage (`s=1`) to the finest (`s=S`). At each stage `s`, the generative motion matching module refines an initial guess. For stages `s > 1`, this guess `˜F_s` is an upsampled version of the output from the preceding stage `F_{s-1}` (`F_{s-1}↑r`). For the coarsest stage (`s=1`), the input `˜F_1` is purely generative, initialized with Gaussian noise, which contributes to the diversity of the synthesized motion. Each stage generates a motion sequence `F_s` with a progressively finer temporal resolution.

#### 3.3. Generative Motion Matching Module

At each stage `s`, the generative motion matching module iteratively refines the motion sequence over `E` iterations.
- **Skeleton-aware Motion Patch Extraction:**
 - A motion patch is generally a sub-sequence of `p` consecutive frames.
 - To enhance diversity, especially spatially, the method proposes **skeleton-aware motion patches**. The skeletal tree (B) is partitioned into multiple overlapping sub-trees or "skeletal parts" (`{¯B_1..., ¯B_B}`).
 - The full-body motion is decomposed into corresponding partial-body motions (`{¯T_1^s..., ¯T_B^s}`), and motion patches are extracted from these partial-body motions. This allows for more granular control and combination of movements across different body parts.
- **Generative Matching:**
 - Let `X` be the set of motion patches from the current initial guess (`˜F_s`) and `Y` be the set of motion patches from the exemplar motion (`T_s`).
 - Pairwise patch distance matrices (`D_b`) are calculated for each skeletal part `b` using the squared-L2 distance: `D_{b_i,j} = ||X_b_i - Y_b_j||^2_2`.
 - **Bidirectional Similarity:** To achieve both high completeness (all exemplar patches appear in synthesis) and coherence (synthesis patches don't deviate from examples), the distance matrices are normalized using a per-example-patch factor: `ˆD_{b_i,j} = D_{b_i,j} / (α + min_ℓ(D_{b_ℓ,j}))`. The parameter `α` controls the degree of completeness; a smaller `α` encourages more exemplar content preservation. This normalization guides the matching process to seek patches in the synthesis that are close to exemplar patches and vice versa.
- **Blending:**
 - For each patch in `X_b`, its nearest neighbor in `Y_b` (based on `ˆD_b`) is identified.
 - These collected matched patches are blended using average voting to form a synthesized partial-body motion `¯F_b`.
 - Finally, values are averaged over overlapping joints between different skeletal parts to assemble all synthesized partial-body motions into the full-body result `F_s`.

#### 3.4. Extension to More Settings

The framework is designed for versatility:
- **Multiple Examples:** When multiple sequences are input, motion patches are simply extracted from all of them to form the exemplar set. The `α` parameter becomes critical for ensuring coverage across all examples.
- **Heterogeneous Skeletons:** For inputs with different skeletal structures (e.g., a monster and a zombie), users can manually define overlapping skeletal parts for each input. The method then combines partial-body motions from these diverse examples to synthesize a novel motion for a new composite creature (e.g., a Frankenstein).
- **Custom Synthesis Length:** The synthesized motion can be of arbitrary length, not restricted to the length of the example.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates GenMM across several dimensions, including novel motion synthesis, the impact of specific methodological components, hyper-parameter effects, performance metrics, and a variety of applications.

#### 4.1. Novel Motion Synthesis

- **Qualitative Comparison:** GenMM demonstrated the ability to synthesize diverse, high-quality motion sequences within a fraction of a second, even for characters with complex skeletons (e.g., 433 joints), while preserving highly dynamic and agile movements.
 - Compared to **MotionTexture [Li et al. 2002]**, GenMM produced motions without unnatural transitions, which MotionTexture often generated when given a single example.
 - Compared to **acRNN [Zhou et al. 2018]**, GenMM generated stable and continuous motions, whereas acRNN tended to produce noisy or quickly converging to static poses due to data scarcity and error accumulation.
 - Compared to **GANimator [Li et al. 2022]**, a neural patch-GAN method, GenMM avoided the jittery or over-smoothed results often seen from GANimator, especially with complex skeletons. GenMM also required no training time, in contrast to GANimator's hours to days of training.
- **Quantitative Comparison (Table 1):** Using metrics of Coverage, Set Diversity, Global Patch Distance, and Local Patch Distance, GenMM achieved significantly high coverage (99.89%) and sufficient set diversity (0.28), with low patch distances (Global: 0.22, Local: 0.18). Crucially, GenMM required no training time (N/A) and had a very fast inference time (0.08s), outperforming all baselines in efficiency while maintaining competitive or superior quality metrics.

#### 4.2. Specific Generative Settings

- **Skeleton-aware Motion Patches (Figure 7):** This component was shown to be effective in generating spatial diversity. For an example of a character waving two hands simultaneously, GenMM with skeleton-aware patches could synthesize sequences where only one hand was waving, which was not possible without this component.
- **Multiple Examples (Table 2, Figure 8):** GenMM demonstrated robust performance with multiple input examples, maintaining high coverage (e.g., 99.64% for five examples). This contrasted with GANimator, whose coverage significantly dropped as the number of examples increased, indicating difficulty in incorporating all exemplar content.
- **Complex Skeletons (Table 3, Figure 5):** GenMM maintained consistent high coverage across skeletons of varying complexities (24, 65, and 433 joints), with 86.90% coverage for the 433-joint skeleton. GANimator's performance drastically degraded with increasing skeletal complexity, failing to produce reasonable results for highly complex structures.

#### 4.3. Effects of Hyper-parameters (Table 4)

- **`α` (Completeness Control Knob):** Decreasing `α` generally increased coverage, as it encourages preserving more exemplar content. However, setting `α` too low could lead to unstable generation, resulting in decreased coverage and increased patch distances.
- **`K` (Receptive Field at Coarsest Stage):** A larger `K` (smaller receptive field) increased diversity but could lead to less natural transitions and higher patch distances as the generated motion deviated more from the input.
- **`p` (Patch Size):** A smaller patch size `p` (smaller temporal receptive field) also led to less coherent results, indicated by increased global and local patch distances.
- **`r` (Upsampling Factor):** A large upsampling factor `r` could result in unstable generation due to significant gaps between consecutive scales in the pyramid.

#### 4.4. Time and Memory Consumption (Figure 9)

Both time and memory consumption of GenMM exhibited a linear growth pattern with respect to the number of generated frames. On an NVIDIA V100 GPU, synthesizing 100,000 frames took approximately 3 seconds, demonstrating the method's efficiency for generating very long sequences.

#### 4.5. Applications (Figure 10, 11)

GenMM's versatility was demonstrated across several applications:
- **Motion Completion:** Successfully completed upper-body motion when provided with only lower-body motion, generating coherent and natural movements.
- **Key Frame-Guided Generation:** Generated smooth and detailed motion sequences guided by a sparse set of user-specified keyframes.
- **Infinite Looping:** Created seamless looping animations by setting identical start and end poses.
- **Motion Reassembly:** Combined motion elements from heterogeneous skeletons (e.g., stitching a monster's arm to a zombie) to synthesize natural motions for a new composite creature.
- **Random Locomotion Generation:** Generated novel locomotion paths from an example, illustrating flexibility in trajectory generation.

#### 4.6. User Interface (Figure 12)

A user-friendly web-based interface and a Blender add-on were developed, making the framework accessible for both novice users and professional artists, demonstrating practical integration.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GenMM framework presents a notable contribution to the field of computer animation, particularly in the domain of example-based motion synthesis. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **High-Quality and Efficient Motion Synthesis:** By injecting generative capabilities into the Motion Matching paradigm, GenMM offers a method that retains the high fidelity and naturalness characteristic of Motion Matching. The ability to synthesize high-quality motion within a fraction of a second, without any pre-training, represents a substantial improvement in efficiency compared to deep learning methods that require extensive training. This speed and quality are critical for real-time applications, interactive content, and rapid prototyping in animation production.

2. **Scalability to Complex Skeletons:** GenMM demonstrates robust performance with highly complex and large skeletal structures (e.g., 433 joints), an area where deep neural networks often face challenges, leading to visual artifacts or failure. This scalability broadens the applicability of example-based synthesis to a wider range of characters and creatures beyond standard humanoids.

3. **Versatility in Applications:** The framework's demonstrated adaptability to various animation tasks—including motion completion, key frame-guided generation, infinite looping, and motion reassembly with heterogeneous skeletons—highlights its versatility. This makes GenMM a comprehensive tool that can address multiple common challenges in animation workflows, potentially streamlining production processes.

4. **Accessibility and User Empowerment:** The development of a web-based interface and a Blender add-on enhances the accessibility of this advanced motion synthesis technique. This allows both casual users and professional artists to leverage the framework without requiring deep technical expertise, fostering creativity and iteration in animation design.

5. **Alternative to Deep Learning Limitations:** GenMM provides a strong alternative to deep learning-based approaches, especially when faced with limited datasets or when the computational overhead and artifact generation of neural networks are prohibitive. It shows that classical methods, when intelligently augmented with modern generative principles, can still yield competitive or superior results in specific contexts.

6. **Bridging Classical and Modern Techniques:** The research effectively bridges classical, established methods (Motion Matching) with contemporary concepts from generative models (bidirectional similarity, multi-stage pyramids from image synthesis). This cross-pollination of ideas can inspire further research into combining the strengths of different algorithmic paradigms.

Despite its current advantages, the paper acknowledges that GenMM's discrete patch distribution results in lower generative diversity compared to methods that learn continuous distributions, like GANs, which can generate entirely novel poses. Future work could aim to integrate the quality of motion matching with neural models capable of continuous representation learning. Furthermore, limitations concerning overly long example sequences and the generation of temporal variations from very short, non-periodic inputs are noted, suggesting avenues for further refinement through techniques like approximate nearest neighbor search.

Overall, GenMM represents a significant step forward in example-based motion synthesis, offering a practical, high-performance, and versatile solution that is particularly beneficial in scenarios with limited data and a need for rapid, high-quality animation generation.
