# ActProbe: Action-Space Probe for Early Failure Detection of Generative Robot Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> ActProbe is a pure action-space early failure detector for generative robot policies. It extracts two scalars from each emitted action chunk, Temporal Consistency Error (overlap MSE between consecutive chunks) and Action Chunk Magnitude (L2 norm), then maps them via a task-embedding-conditioned bridged LSTM-MLP of roughly 24K parameters to per-step failure probability, improving the F1-timeliness Pareto frontier by 12.7% average hypervolume.

## Problem

- Internal-feature detectors probe policy hidden states, needing white-box access unavailable for closed-source models, and their scene-entangled features overfit training tasks and degrade on unseen ones.
- External-feature detectors pair action scores with observation-side or resampling components, adding runtime cost, though prior work found visual features contribute marginally beyond low-dimensional signals.
- Existing monitors alert only once failure is already visually recognizable, leaving no margin to trigger fallback behavior, request human intervention, or terminate an unrecoverable rollout.

## Method

- **Temporal Consistency Error**: ==mean squared error== over the overlapping horizon of two consecutive action chunks, a single-pass surrogate for the trajectory jitter that resampling-based detectors elicit explicitly.
- **Action Chunk Magnitude**: ==L2 norm== of the flattened current chunk, separating small smooth motions of normal progress from the oversized corrections that typically precede failure.
- **Bridged LSTM-MLP** probe with a ==skip connection== feeding raw features to the MLP, recurrent state initialized by a frozen Qwen3-Embedding task-label projection, thresholded by ==split conformal calibration==.

## Results

- Averages **75.8%** early-detection ROC-AUC at q=**0.25** across nine splits, **4.6%** above the strongest baseline SAFE-MLP overall and **9.0%** ahead on unseen tasks.
- Ablations show native chunk overlap beats K-resampling and MSE beats MMD and ACE; the bridged LSTM-MLP adds **5.4%** over an overlap-plus-MLP mapping.
- Transfers zero-shot from GR00T simulation to a Unitree G1-D humanoid (**6/12** and **5/12** correct trials, beating both baselines) and cuts PPO fine-tuning interactions **2.9** times.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - What a policy commands can be more diagnostic than what it perceives or what its hidden states encode; low-dimensional behavioral signals generalize where deeply-fused representations overfit.
> - Native overlap between consecutive replans is free uncertainty: it matches or beats explicit resampling while costing zero extra forward passes at runtime.
> - Conditioning only the recurrent initial state, not every timestep, pushes a probe toward task-conditional thresholds instead of memorized per-task patterns, aiding held-out-task transfer.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

A robot can be moving toward an unrecoverable mistake before the mistake is visually obvious. A failure detector can give a robot time to stop, fall back, or ask for human help. In one illustrated task, a robot is instructed to pick up a bell pepper. ActProbe watches the robot's own emitted action chunks for signs that plans are changing or commands are unusually large. It does not look inside the policy or generate extra samples. In the paper's figure, ActProbe detects jerky motion at step 9. The pepper is dropped and the task is marked failed at step 61, and SAFE detects at step 66, after the failure. This is one illustrative rollout, not proof that every failure can be detected before it happens.

### 2. Read the action stream

An **action chunk** is the policy's prediction of actions for several upcoming control steps. After some actions have been executed, the policy replans and emits another chunk. ActProbe extracts two signals from this stream.

**Temporal Consistency Error (TCE)** compares the overlapping future steps in two consecutive chunks and measures their mean-squared difference. Large or persistent differences mean the policy is revising its short-horizon plan. **Action Chunk Magnitude (ACM)** is the L2 norm of the current chunk, a coarse measure of command size. Large revisions and oversized corrections often precede failure. These signals are proposed as useful cues. Neither is itself a diagnosis or a proven unique cause.

A small task-conditioned bridged LSTM–MLP turns the signals into an alert score. The LSTM integrates the history of these signals. A skip connection also gives the MLP direct access to the current input, so the probe can respond to gradual drift and abrupt anomalies. A task-description embedding initializes the recurrence, which lets the probe condition its interpretation on the task. The monitor generates a probability for each new chunk and tracks the running maximum. It alerts when that maximum passes a scalar threshold calibrated on held-out successful rollouts. The probe has about 24K trainable parameters and adds approximately 3 ms per probe call in the reported implementation.

### 3. Test the signal, then the detector

The main metric is **q = 0.25 early-detection ROC-AUC**. For each rollout, it takes the maximum detector score in the rollout's first quarter. It then measures how well those scores separate failed from successful rollouts, independent of any threshold. On OpenVLA + LIBERO unseen tasks, ActProbe scores 73.8% versus 67.9% for SAFE-MLP, averaged over three seeds.

All methods were trained on the same 50 episodes per task. Across nine splits, ActProbe averages 75.8%. That is 4.6 percentage points above the strongest baseline overall and 9.0 points ahead on unseen tasks. Hidden-state methods remain competitive on seen tasks.

An ablation on π⁰·⁵ + RoboCasa tests whether the gain is tied to the proposed choices, using q = 0.25 ROC-AUC. The closest prior alternative, STAC, compares action distributions using repeated samples. With MSE, native overlap + MLP reaches 64.3% seen / 70.5% unseen. Replacing native overlap with K-resampling + MLP gives 62.5% / 68.2%. Adding the recurrent component raises the reported scores to 69.6% unseen for overlap + LSTM and 69.7% seen / 72.1% unseen for full ActProbe. The paper reports the full bridged architecture as +5.4 percentage points over overlap + MLP on MSE-seen. On this benchmark, the results suggest that both the signal source and temporal modeling help. They do not show that the LSTM–MLP is the sole cause of gains everywhere.

### 4. A monitor in the action stream

In the illustrated rollout, signals in successive action chunks flag trouble before an obvious failure. The unseen-task comparison indicates that this is more than a visual anecdote. The gains are scoped, though: hidden-state probes remain competitive on seen tasks. TCE and ACM are predictive cues, not diagnoses of a failure's cause.
