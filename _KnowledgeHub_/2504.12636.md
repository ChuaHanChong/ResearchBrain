# A0: An Affordance-Aware Hierarchical Model for General Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The A0 model introduces an affordance-aware hierarchical framework that learns embodiment-agnostic spatial affordances for general robotic manipulation. This system demonstrates superior performance and generalization across diverse robotic platforms and complex tasks, achieving high success rates while significantly improving execution efficiency.

## Problem

- Robotic systems often lack robust spatial reasoning, struggling to understand where and how to interact with objects for complex manipulation tasks like wiping or stacking.
- Existing modular-based methods frequently exhibit shallow physical understanding, while end-to-end vision-language-action (VLA) models often fail in precise spatial interpretation.
- Current affordance-based approaches tend to be computationally intensive and embodiment-specific, limiting their generalization across different robotic platforms.

## Method

- **A0** employs a hierarchical affordance-aware diffusion model that first predicts object-centric 2D contact points and post-contact trajectories (high-level understanding) and then translates these into robot actions (low-level execution).
- It utilizes an embodiment-agnostic representation of 2D waypoints, integrating actionable knowledge from real-world, synthetic, and human-object interaction data.
- The model is pre-trained on 1 million contact points for foundational localization, followed by supervised fine-tuning on diverse trajectory datasets, and incorporates novel ==Position Offset Attention== and Spatial Information Aggregation Layer for motion awareness and precise coordinate mapping.

## Results

- A0 achieved average success rates of **62.50%** on Franka and **53.75%** on Kinova platforms, outperforming state-of-the-art 2D affordance methods like Molmo by **18.75** percentage points and end-to-end VLA models (RDT-1B, π0) by over 33 percentage points.
- The model demonstrated strong performance in trajectory-intensive tasks such as 'Wipe Board', achieving a **50%** success rate on Kinova, a **40%** improvement over VLA baselines.
- A0 significantly improved execution efficiency by predicting key waypoints in a single-shot inference, requiring only 4-5 execution steps compared to 25-50 steps for VLA methods, leading to much faster task rollout.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale pre-training on generic contact point data is crucial for building robust foundational object localization capabilities, significantly improving generalization to unseen objects and environments.
> - An embodiment-agnostic representation focusing on object-centric 2D waypoints enables seamless transfer of learned manipulation skills across diverse robotic platforms (e.g., Franka, Kinova) without extensive re-training.
> - Novel architectural components like Position Offset Attention enhance the model's ability to reason about motion, while the Spatial Information Aggregation Layer ensures precise mapping to physical coordinates, critical for dynamic and complex tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation stands as a fundamental yet profoundly challenging domain within robotics and embodied AI, demanding that robots precisely interact with objects in dynamic and complex environments. The core challenge addressed by this paper lies in the robot's ability to understand **spatial affordances**—the intrinsic properties of objects that dictate "where" and "how" an interaction should occur. This understanding is paramount for executing tasks ranging from simple object placement to complex sequences like wiping a surface or assembling components.

The existing research landscape in robotic manipulation can be broadly categorized into several approaches, each with its strengths and limitations:

1. **Modular-based Methods:** These approaches, exemplified by works like ReKep and MOKA, typically leverage large vision foundation models (e.g., those based on large language models, LLMs, or vision-language models, VLMs) to interpret visual scenes and extract high-level semantic information. While powerful for general scene understanding, a common limitation is their shallow grasp of the physical world and the operability of objects, often failing to capture nuanced spatial and physical interactions necessary for precise manipulation.

2. **End-to-End Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Methods:** Models like RDT and π0 aim to directly map visual and linguistic inputs to robot actions. These methods simplify the control pipeline but frequently struggle with adequately interpreting spatial positions, leading to suboptimal performance in tasks demanding fine-grained manipulation or complex spatial reasoning, such as wiping or stacking objects accurately.

3. **Affordance-based Methods (Point-based and Flow-based):** Recognizing the importance of spatial interactions, recent advancements have focused on explicitly modeling affordances. Point-based methods (e.g., SpatialVLA, RoboPoint, Track2Act) and flow-based methods (e.g., General Flow, Im2Flow2Act) aim to create dense spatial representations or model interaction trajectories. While effective in certain scenarios, these approaches can be computationally intensive and often yield "embodiment-specific" solutions, meaning they are tightly coupled to a particular robot's kinematics or sensor configuration, limiting their generalization across different robotic platforms.

**A0's contribution to this landscape is distinct and addresses several critical gaps:**

- **Hierarchical Approach:** Unlike many end-to-end VLA models, A0 adopts a hierarchical structure, explicitly decomposing tasks into a high-level "spatial affordance understanding" phase and a low-level "action execution" phase. This modularity allows for more robust reasoning and easier integration of specialized components.
- **Embodiment-Agnostic Affordance Representation:** A0 introduces a novel representation that captures object-centric spatial affordances by predicting abstract contact points and post-contact trajectories. This is a significant departure from dense, computationally expensive representations, allowing for generalization across different robotic platforms (e.g., Franka, Kinova) without extensive re-training or adaptation. This focus on "where" and "how" an object should be interacted with, rather than direct robot joint commands, makes the learned knowledge highly transferable.
- **Leveraging Diffusion Models:** A0 utilizes a diffusion model architecture, which is known for its ability to generate high-quality outputs and handle uncertainty, making it suitable for predicting complex action trajectories. This provides a robust framework for probabilistic action generation, offering more flexibility than deterministic methods.
- **Large-scale Pre-training:** The model is pre-trained on a massive dataset of 1 million contact points, followed by fine-tuning on annotated trajectories. This strategy allows A0 to acquire foundational localization capabilities from diverse internet-scale data before specializing in robotic manipulation, a key enabler for its generalization power.
- **Object-centric Focus:** By concentrating on object-centric contact points and trajectories, A0 avoids the computational overhead associated with dense spatial representations, making it more efficient and adaptable for real-world deployment.

In essence, A0 positions itself as a robust, generalizable, and efficient solution that bridges the gap between high-level semantic understanding and low-level physical interaction. It stands apart from existing modular and end-to-end methods by providing a deeper, transferable understanding of spatial affordances, making significant strides towards truly general robotic manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the "A0" research is to **address the critical challenges in robotic manipulation related to understanding spatial affordances**, which are essential for enabling robots to perform complex interaction tasks effectively. Existing robotic systems, whether modular or end-to-end, often fall short in robust spatial reasoning—the ability to discern "where" and "how" to interact with objects in a given environment.

The key motivations driving the development of A0 can be summarized as follows:

1. **Lack of Robust Spatial Reasoning:** Current robotic manipulation methods frequently exhibit insufficient understanding of spatial affordances. For instance, in tasks like wiping a whiteboard or stacking objects, robots may perform incomplete or inefficient actions due to a shallow grasp of interaction points and trajectories. This indicates a fundamental deficiency in spatial intelligence that limits the complexity and reliability of tasks robots can perform.

2. **Limitations of Existing Paradigms:**
 - **Modular-based methods** (e.g., ReKep, MOKA) often rely on Large Vision Models (LVMs) that excel at visual recognition but lack deep physical and spatial understanding, particularly regarding object operability. They don't inherently encode the "actionable knowledge" of how to manipulate an object.
 - **End-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) methods** (e.g., RDT, π0) aim for direct action generation but often bypass explicit spatial understanding, leading to suboptimal performance in tasks requiring precise positional or trajectory control.
 - **Recent affordance methods** (point-based, flow-based) focus on dense spatial representations or trajectory modeling, which can be computationally expensive and often "embodiment-specific," meaning they don't easily transfer between different robot platforms.

3. **Need for Unified and Generalizable Representation:** Actionable knowledge about object interactions, spatial properties, and physical attributes is abundantly available in diverse datasets (real-world, synthetic, hand-object interaction, internet data). The motivation is to devise a unified, efficient, and **embodiment-agnostic** representation that can capture this actionable knowledge and generalize across these varied data sources and, crucially, across different robot platforms. This would drastically reduce the need for platform-specific training and enable wider deployment.

4. **Enabling Complex Manipulation Tasks:** The ultimate goal is to empower robots to tackle more complex, real-world manipulation tasks with higher success rates and greater efficiency. This requires a model that can perform sophisticated spatial affordance reasoning and physical interaction, moving beyond simple pick-and-place to dynamic, multi-step actions.

To achieve this, the specific objectives of the A0 model are:

- To propose a **hierarchical affordance-aware diffusion model** that logically decomposes manipulation tasks into high-level spatial understanding and low-level action execution.
- To introduce an **Embodiment-Agnostic Affordance Representation** that predicts object-centric contact points and post-contact trajectories, abstracting away robot-specific kinematics.
- To leverage **large-scale pre-training** on millions of contact points to instill foundational localization capabilities, followed by supervised fine-tuning on annotated trajectories for task-specific adaptation.
- To incorporate **novel architectural components** like Position Offset Attention for motion-aware feature extraction and a Spatial Information Aggregation Layer for precise coordinate mapping.
- To **validate the model's effectiveness and generalization capabilities** across multiple, diverse robotic platforms (Franka, Kinova, Realman, Dobot) and a range of complex real-world tasks.

By addressing these motivations and pursuing these objectives, A0 aims to significantly advance the field of robotic manipulation by enabling robots to acquire a deeper, more generalizable spatial intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The A0 model proposes a novel, hierarchical, affordance-aware diffusion model for general robotic manipulation. Its methodology is structured around two core levels: high-level spatial affordance understanding, handled by the A0 model itself, and low-level action execution, performed by a dedicated module. This design emphasizes an *Embodiment-Agnostic Affordance Representation* to ensure broad generalization across robotic platforms.

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture Overview

A0 decomposes robotic manipulation tasks into:
1. **High-level Spatial Affordance Understanding:** The A0 model, based on a diffusion transformer architecture, learns to predict object-centric contact points and post-contact trajectories.
2. **Low-level Action Execution:** A separate module translates the A0 model's 2D predictions into robot-executable 3D poses and trajectories.

#### 3.2. Embodiment-Agnostic Affordance Representation

To achieve platform-agnosticism and efficient spatial understanding, A0 introduces a unified representation `R`. This representation integrates actionable knowledge from diverse data sources:
- `R_R`: Real-world or synthetic robotic data.
- `R_H`: Hand-object interaction (HOI) data.
- `R_C`: Custom data.

Each entry in `R` consists of:
- An object-centric RGB image `I` (current frame).
- A natural language manipulation task instruction `L`.
- A set of 2D waypoints, including a contact point `c_2D_0` and post-contact trajectories `T = (t_2D_0, t_2D_1, t_2D_2...)`. These 2D coordinates are normalized according to the image size, making them independent of camera resolution.

This representation is crucial as it focuses on *what* needs to be done to an object in image space, rather than *how* a specific robot should move, making the learned affordances transferable.

#### 3.3. Dataset

The effectiveness of A0 heavily relies on a large, multi-source dataset, carefully curated for both localization and trajectory learning:
- **PixMo-One-Point (1 million samples):** Sourced from PixMo-Points, this dataset provides single contact point annotations from internet images, specifically for pre-training foundational object localization.
- **HOI4D-22k (22,000 trajectories):** Extracted and verified from the HOI4D human-object interaction dataset. It converts 3D human-centric data into 2D object mask center points as waypoints, with action-object names as instructions.
- **DROID-3k (3,056 trajectories):** Verified manipulation trajectories from the DROID dataset, annotated using a semi-automated pipeline.
- **Maniskill-5k (4,965 trajectories):** Trajectories from the ManiSkill Scene dataset, converted from 3D to 2D for compatibility.

A multi-stage, semi-automated annotation pipeline was developed to efficiently generate high-quality 2D waypoint trajectories from these diverse sources, often employing models like CoTracker or SAM2 for tracking.

#### 3.4. A0 Model Structure

The A0 model is built upon the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture, designed to predict sequences of 2D waypoints `x_t:t+T` where `T` is the future step chunk size.

- **Inputs:** Diffusion timestep `k`, noisy 2D waypoints, current observation image `I_t`, previous observation image `I_t-1` (for motion), and natural language instruction `ℓ`.
- **Encoders:** Pre-trained **SigLiP (400M)** for vision and **Qwen2.5-7B** for language, extracting robust features from input images and text.
- **Position Offset Attention:** A novel component designed to enhance motion awareness. It calculates motion tokens (`I^m_i`) by subtracting tokens from `I_t` and `I_t-1`, then concatenates these with `I_t` tokens. This directs the model's attention to changes between frames, critical for dynamic tasks.
- **Spatial Information Aggregation Layer (SIAL):** A final non-linear MLP decoder that projects the latent features from the DiT blocks back into precise 2D physical coordinates. This layer is crucial for mapping abstract representations to actionable spatial outputs.
- **Cross-Attention:** Image and text tokens are alternately injected into successive layers' cross-attention blocks to condition the diffusion process, enabling multimodal understanding.

The model learns a conditional distribution `p(x_t|ℓ, I_t-1:t)` and uses a fast ordinary differential equation (ODE) solver for inference, performing de-noising in a small number of steps (`K_D` ≪ `K_F`).

#### 3.5. Training Affordance Prediction Model A0

The training strategy is divided into two phases:

1. **Pre-training:** The A0 model is initially trained on the 1 million contact-point localization samples from PixMo-One-Point. This phase focuses on developing a general object localization capability, predicting only the first waypoint (`x_t^0` / `c_2D_0`). The loss function is a Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the predicted and ground truth normalized 2D coordinates. This stage builds a strong foundation for identifying relevant interaction points based on textual descriptions.

2. **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** After pre-training, the model is fine-tuned on the trajectory datasets (HOI4D-22k, DROID-3k, Maniskill-5k). In this stage, the text condition is expanded to full language instructions, and the model predicts multiple `T` waypoints (contact point + post-contact trajectories). It learns dynamic robotic manipulation, leveraging the motion information from `I_t-1:t`. The loss function remains an MSE between the predicted and ground truth sequences of 2D waypoints.

#### 3.6. Action Execution

Once A0 predicts the 2D waypoints (`x_t:t+T`), an action execution module translates these into robot commands:

1. **2D-to-3D Projection:** The predicted 2D waypoints are projected into 3D space using depth information from an RGB-D camera and the camera's intrinsic matrix. The first point `x_t` becomes the 3D contact point.

2. **Grasp Pose Estimation:** For grasping tasks, an external grasp sampler (e.g., GraspNet or other methods) is queried. It generates a set of grasp candidates based on local geometric features, and the candidate closest to the projected 3D contact point is selected.

3. **Waypoint Selection and Execution:** The projected 3D post-contact waypoints guide the robot's motion. A discrete selection strategy, often assisted by a VLM, is used to determine appropriate heights in free space. Finally, the selected grasp pose and 3D waypoints are used to generate smooth, feasible motion trajectories in SE(3) space for the robotic arm.

This comprehensive methodology, combining large-scale pre-training, an embodiment-agnostic representation, a specialized diffusion transformer, and a modular action execution pipeline, allows A0 to achieve robust and generalizable robotic manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to validate A0 focus on demonstrating its architectural effectiveness, the impact of pre-training, its real-world performance, and its generalization capabilities across diverse robotic platforms, comparing it against state-of-the-art methods.

#### 4.1. Offline Validation

Offline evaluation used the Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of predicted 2D waypoints against ground truth.

- **Effectiveness of Pre-training:** Pre-training on 1 million contact-point localization samples (PixMo-One-Point) proved crucial. Ablation studies showed that pre-training significantly reduced MAE, enhancing A0's ability to generalize to unseen objects and environments. For instance, in a Real-to-Sim setting (training on HOI4D/DROID, testing on Maniskill), MAE on Maniskill-5k decreased from 50.4 to 43.9. In a Sim-to-Real setting (training on Maniskill, testing on HOI4D/DROID), MAE on HOI4D-22k dropped dramatically from 172.2 to 35.1, and on DROID-3k from 125.2 to 29.1. This highlights the indispensable role of large-scale pre-training in building robust foundational localization capabilities.

- **Effectiveness of Network Structure:**
 - **Position Offset Attention (POA):** Removing POA increased MAE (e.g., by 0.8 on Maniskill-5k). This confirms that POA is vital for motion-aware reasoning, especially in tasks requiring dynamic interactions like wiping or stacking.
 - **Spatial Information Aggregation Layer (SIAL):** The SIAL was also found to be critical. Without it, MAE increased substantially (e.g., by 13.2 pixels on HOI4D-22k), indicating its importance for precise waypoint prediction, particularly in complex or occluded environments.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

A0's real-world performance was evaluated on multiple robotic platforms (Franka Emika, Kinova Gen3, Realman, Dobot X-Trainer) across four common household tasks: Place Object, Open Drawer, Press Button, and Wipe Board.

- **Comparison with 2D Affordance Methods:** A0 demonstrated superior performance against state-of-the-art 2D affordance-based methods (MOKA, ReKep) and VLM-based point predictors (Molmo, Magma), whose outputs were fed into A0's action execution module.
 - On the **Franka** platform, A0 achieved an average success rate of **62.50%**, significantly outperforming the next best method, Molmo (43.75%), by 18.75 percentage points. It excelled in Open Drawer (75%) and Press Button (70%).
 - On the **Kinova** platform, A0 achieved an average success rate of **53.75%**, surpassing MOKA (45.00%) and ReKep (33.75%). It particularly showed strength in Open Drawer (65%) and Wipe Board (50%).
 - Notably, A0 demonstrated robust performance in **trajectory-based tasks** like "Wipe Board," achieving 45% on Franka and 50% on Kinova, where precise motion is critical. While slightly weaker on the "Place Object" task on Kinova compared to MOKA and ReKep (possibly due to their training on vast real-world object datasets), its overall performance across complex tasks was superior.

- **Comparison with Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Methods:** A0 was compared against prominent end-to-end VLA models like RDT-1B and π0 on the Kinova platform, particularly focusing on trajectory-intensive tasks.
 - A0 significantly outperformed these methods, achieving an average success rate of **53.75%** on Kinova, compared to RDT-1B's 11.25% and π0's 20.00% (or 18.75% with FAST). This represents a substantial improvement of over 33 percentage points.
 - Crucially, for the "Wipe Board" task, A0 achieved a **50% success rate**, a 40% improvement over the VLA baselines, underscoring its superiority in tasks requiring precise trajectory following.
 - **Execution Efficiency:** A0 offers a significant advantage in efficiency. VLA methods typically require 25-50 step-by-step inferences to complete a task. In contrast, A0 predicts 4-5 key waypoints in a **single-shot inference** at the beginning, leading to significantly fewer execution steps and much faster task rollout.

- **Qualitative Evaluation:** Visual demonstrations (Figures 5, 8, and 12) showcase A0's successful execution of various tasks (opening drawers, placing objects, pressing buttons, wiping boards, inserting flowers, putting fruits) on both Franka and Kinova robots. These examples effectively illustrate A0's platform-agnostic capabilities and the speed of single-shot inference. Figure 7 further confirms accurate predictions on diverse datasets, including Droid, real Franka robot data, and ManiSkill simulation environments.

In summary, A0's empirical results strongly validate its core design principles. The hierarchical model, coupled with embodiment-agnostic representation and large-scale pre-training, enables superior performance in complex manipulation tasks, strong generalization across various robotic platforms, and remarkable efficiency compared to existing paradigms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of A0 marks a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, offering a robust, generalizable, and efficient solution to a long-standing challenge: understanding spatial affordances for intelligent object interaction. Its impact can be multifaceted, spanning research, development, and real-world applications.

**1. Advancing Spatial Intelligence in Robotics:** A0 directly addresses the critical limitation of current robotic systems in robust spatial reasoning. By decomposing complex manipulation tasks into high-level spatial affordance understanding and low-level action execution, A0 provides a deeper, more actionable intelligence. This is crucial for tasks beyond simple pick-and-place, enabling robots to perform intricate operations like wiping, stacking, and opening drawers with high precision and reliability. This fundamental shift towards explicit spatial affordance reasoning paves the way for truly intelligent manipulation.

**2. Unprecedented Generalization Across Robotic Platforms:** The introduction of the Embodiment-Agnostic Affordance Representation is a game-changer. By focusing on object-centric contact points and trajectories in 2D image space, A0 abstracts away the specific kinematics and physical characteristics of individual robots. This allows the learned knowledge to be seamlessly transferred and deployed across diverse robotic systems (Franka, Kinova, Realman, Dobot) with minimal, if any, platform-specific fine-tuning. This level of generalizability significantly reduces the development and deployment costs associated with new robotic applications, making advanced manipulation more accessible.

**3. Enhanced Robustness and Efficiency:** A0's diffusion model architecture, combined with key components like Position Offset Attention for motion awareness and Spatial Information Aggregation Layer for precise mapping, contributes to its superior performance and robustness in complex, dynamic environments. Furthermore, its single-shot inference for waypoint prediction drastically reduces execution steps (4-5 waypoints vs. 25-50 steps for VLA models). This efficiency is vital for real-time robotic operations, enabling faster task completion and increased throughput in industrial and service robotics settings.

**4. Bridging Large-Scale Data with Robotic Tasks:** The strategy of pre-training on a massive dataset of 1 million internet-sourced contact points for foundational localization, followed by fine-tuning on diverse robotic and human-object interaction trajectories, demonstrates an effective paradigm for leveraging vast, readily available data for robotic learning. This approach allows A0 to inherit general visual knowledge and then specialize it for robotic manipulation, mitigating the common data scarcity problem in robotics.

**5. Foundational for Future Robotics Research:** A0's modular and hierarchical design offers a strong foundation for future research. The paper explicitly identifies avenues for improvement, such as enhancing grasp pose estimation by leveraging VLMs, improving height estimation with more contextual information, and extending to long-horizon planning by using VLMs for task decomposition. These outlined future directions show that A0 is not just a solution but a stepping stone towards more capable and autonomous robotic systems. The plan to release collected datasets will further stimulate community-wide research.

**6. Potential Real-World Applications:** The capabilities demonstrated by A0 have profound implications for various industries and domains:
- **Manufacturing and Logistics:** Enhanced precision and adaptability for assembly, packaging, and handling diverse objects in dynamic factory and warehouse environments.
- **Service Robotics:** More reliable and versatile robots for tasks in healthcare (e.g., assisting patients, handling instruments), hospitality, and domestic settings (e.g., cleaning, organizing).
- **Hazardous Environments:** Robots that can perform complex manipulation in areas unsafe for humans, such as nuclear facilities, disaster zones, or space exploration.
- **Skill Transfer:** The embodiment-agnostic nature of A0 could enable easier transfer of learned skills from simulation to real robots, or between different real robot models.

In conclusion, A0 represents a significant leap forward in robotic manipulation by providing a truly generalizable and efficient framework for spatial affordance understanding. Its impact will be felt in accelerating research, lowering the barriers to robotic deployment, and ultimately paving the way for more intelligent, adaptable, and autonomous robots in our daily lives and industries.
