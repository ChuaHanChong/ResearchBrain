# Stabilizing Humanoid Robot Trajectory Generation via Physics-Informed Learning and Control-Informed Steering

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Italian Institute of Technology developed a framework to stabilize humanoid robot trajectory generation, combining a physics-informed loss for training with a control-informed steering block during inference. This approach significantly reduced support foot sliding and trajectory drift, allowing for robust real-world deployment on the ergoCub robot for various walking behaviors.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots require smooth, safe, and human-like motion for collaborative environments, which is challenging due to their high degrees of freedom and nonlinear dynamics.
- Imitation Learning (IL) methods generate human-like trajectories but often produce physically unfeasible motions (e.g., foot sliding, trajectory drift) due to limitations in demonstration data.
- Existing Physics-Informed Machine Learning (PIML) applications in humanoid control either use non-differentiable rewards, limiting their influence, or do not leverage IL, potentially resulting in less human-like policies.

## Method

- An end-to-end ==Mode-Adaptive Neural Network (MANN)== learns the full kinematic state of a floating-base robot from human locomotion data.
- A physics-informed loss component is incorporated during training to enforce kinematically feasible contacts, specifically encouraging the support foot to remain stationary.
- A control-informed steering block is applied at inference time to correct trajectory drift by blending user input with network predictions using proportional-integral control concepts.

## Results

- The proposed method, with the control-informed correction block, notably mitigated lateral and yaw drift in forward walking trajectories compared to a baseline MANN model.
- Models trained with the physics-informed loss exhibited reduced support foot sliding, yielding significantly lower RMSE values for both linear and angular velocities (e.g., down from **22.88** m/s and **17.74** rad/s respectively).
- The framework was successfully deployed on the ergoCub humanoid robot using both offline and online tracking controllers for mixed walking behaviors, demonstrating its modularity and real-world applicability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating a differentiable physics-informed loss term directly into the supervised learning process significantly enhances the physical feasibility of generated trajectories, reducing support foot sliding.
> - A modular, control-informed correction mechanism at inference time effectively mitigates trajectory drift and steers the robot towards desired user inputs without requiring retraining of the learned model.
> - There is an optimal weighting for the physics-informed loss; excessive weighting can overpower data-fitting, leading to undesirable motion artifacts while satisfying physical constraints (e.g., tip-toe walking).
