# DPL: Depth-only Perceptive Humanoid Locomotion via Realistic Depth Synthesis and Cross-Attention Terrain Reconstruction

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed a system for depth-only perceptive locomotion in humanoid robots across challenging terrains, integrating realistic depth synthesis and a cross-attention transformer for terrain reconstruction. The approach enables accurate inference of occluded regions and robust, adaptive locomotion with a perception latency of approximately 20 ms in real-world deployment.

## Problem

- The significant sim-to-real gap hinders the deployment of depth image-based end-to-end learned locomotion policies for humanoids in real-world scenarios.
- Traditional elevation map methods for terrain awareness suffer from high latency and hardware complexity due to multi-sensor fusion and reliance on external localization.
- Existing terrain perception systems struggle to reconstruct occluded or 'blind' regions (e.g., the bottom of gaps), which are critical for safe and effective locomotion planning.

## Method

- A Realistic Depth Images Synthetic Method employs GPU ray casting with a comprehensive noise model and self-occlusion awareness to generate highly realistic simulated depth data, bridging the sim-to-real gap.
- A Multi-Modality Cross-Attention Transformer fuses temporal depth observations with proprioceptive history to reconstruct detailed terrain heightmaps, capable of inferring hidden geometry.
- A Terrain-Aware Locomotion Policy utilizes a teacher-student reinforcement learning architecture with a blind backbone for baseline stability and a vision-based modulator for adaptive gait, fine-tuned end-to-end with real sensory inputs.

## Results

- The system achieved an average Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 3.25 cm for terrain reconstruction in simulation and accurately inferred the geometry of occluded regions like gap bottoms, outperforming baseline methods.
- Real-world perception update delay was consistently stable at approximately 20 ms, a reduction compared to typical elevation map-based approaches.
- A full-sized humanoid robot successfully performed agile and adaptive locomotion across diverse challenging terrains, including stairs, gaps, and sloped platforms, demonstrating zero-shot generalization to unseen dynamics and improved robustness after end-to-end fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Synthesizing depth images with physically accurate noise, self-occlusion, and dropout patterns is critical for effective sim-to-real transfer, reducing reconstruction error by over 30%.
> - Fusing proprioceptive history with depth via a cross-attention transformer allows the system to intelligently reconstruct occluded terrain features, enabling a more complete understanding of the environment.
> - Combining a stable, pre-trained 'blind' locomotion policy with a vision-based modulator provides a robust foundation, allowing for efficient learning of terrain-adaptive behaviors and better generalization without sacrificing stability.
