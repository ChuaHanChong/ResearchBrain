# DPL: Depth-only Perceptive Humanoid Locomotion via Realistic Depth Synthesis and Cross-Attention Terrain Reconstruction

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed a system for depth-only perceptive locomotion in humanoid robots across challenging terrains, integrating realistic depth synthesis and a cross-attention transformer for terrain reconstruction. The approach enables accurate inference of occluded regions and robust, adaptive locomotion with a perception latency of approximately 20 ms in real-world deployment.

## Problem

- The significant sim-to-real gap hinders the deployment of depth image-based end-to-end learned locomotion policies for humanoids in real-world scenarios.
- Traditional elevation map methods for terrain awareness suffer from high latency and hardware complexity due to multi-sensor fusion and reliance on external localization.
- Existing terrain perception systems struggle to reconstruct occluded or 'blind' regions (e.g., the bottom of gaps), which are critical for safe and effective locomotion planning.

## Method

- A Realistic Depth Images Synthetic Method employs GPU ray casting with a comprehensive noise model and self-occlusion awareness to generate highly realistic simulated depth data, bridging the sim-to-real gap.
- A Multi-Modality Cross-Attention Transformer fuses temporal depth observations with proprioceptive history to reconstruct detailed terrain heightmaps, capable of inferring hidden geometry.
- A Terrain-Aware Locomotion Policy utilizes a teacher-student reinforcement learning architecture with a blind backbone for baseline stability and a vision-based modulator for adaptive gait, fine-tuned end-to-end with real sensory inputs.

## Results

- The system achieved an average Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 3.25 cm for terrain reconstruction in simulation and accurately inferred the geometry of occluded regions like gap bottoms, outperforming baseline methods.
- Real-world perception update delay was consistently stable at approximately 20 ms, a reduction compared to typical elevation map-based approaches.
- A full-sized humanoid robot successfully performed agile and adaptive locomotion across diverse challenging terrains, including stairs, gaps, and sloped platforms, demonstrating zero-shot generalization to unseen dynamics and improved robustness after end-to-end fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Synthesizing depth images with physically accurate noise, self-occlusion, and dropout patterns is critical for effective sim-to-real transfer, reducing reconstruction error by over 30%.
> - Fusing proprioceptive history with depth via a cross-attention transformer allows the system to intelligently reconstruct occluded terrain features, enabling a more complete understanding of the environment.
> - Combining a stable, pre-trained 'blind' locomotion policy with a vision-based modulator provides a robust foundation, allowing for efficient learning of terrain-adaptive behaviors and better generalization without sacrificing stability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of legged robot locomotion has seen rapid advancements, with quadrupedal robots now demonstrating remarkable agility and robustness in navigating challenging terrains. However, transferring these capabilities to humanoid platforms introduces unique complexities due to their bipedal nature, higher center of mass, and inherent instability. Perceptive locomotion, where robots use sensors to understand their environment and adapt their movements, is critical for achieving autonomous mobility in unstructured, human-centric spaces.

Current approaches to terrain-aware humanoid locomotion generally fall into two categories:
1. **Depth image-based end-to-end learning:** These methods directly map raw depth images to control actions. While conceptually elegant, they often suffer from a significant "sim-to-real" gap (discrepancy between simulated and real-world sensor data), limited training efficiency, and poor generalization to unseen scenarios or sensor noise/occlusion. Examples include "Robot Parkour Learning" and similar works (e.g.,–).
2. **Elevation map-based methods:** These approaches reconstruct structured terrain representations (e.g., heightmaps or elevation maps) from multiple vision sensors and localization systems, then use these maps for planning and control (e.g.,–,–). While effective in simulation, they typically incur high latency due to multi-sensor fusion and reliance on accurate global localization, compromising robustness in dynamic real-world deployments. Furthermore, these methods struggle with occluded or "blind" regions (e.g., gaps or overhangs) where traditional sensing cannot provide direct measurements, often necessitating complex hand-crafted rules.

This paper positions itself as a novel solution that addresses the limitations of both paradigms. It aims to achieve **depth-only perceptive humanoid locomotion**, simplifying the sensor suite while enhancing robustness. By integrating realistic depth synthesis and a cross-attention transformer for terrain reconstruction with a reinforcement learning policy, the authors seek to bridge the sim-to-real gap, reduce latency and hardware complexity, and improve generalization, especially in scenarios with partial or noisy depth information and self-occlusions. The focus on a full-sized humanoid robot in challenging real-world scenarios underscores its ambition to push beyond simulation toward practical, deployable solutions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind this research are:

- **Overcoming the Sim-to-Real Gap in Depth Perception:** A major hurdle in applying reinforcement learning policies trained in simulation to real robots is the difference between simulated and actual sensor data. The authors aim to synthesize highly realistic depth images in simulation that closely mimic real-world sensor outputs, including noise, self-occlusion, and other artifacts, thereby facilitating smoother sim-to-real transfer.
- **Improving Training Efficiency and Generalization:** End-to-end learning methods can be data-hungry and inefficient to train. This work seeks to enable efficient policy training with limited data and hardware resources while ensuring that the learned policy can generalize to diverse and challenging terrains beyond its training distribution.
- **Reducing Latency and Enhancing Robustness:** Elevation map-based methods often rely on complex multi-sensor systems and external localization, leading to perception delays and reduced robustness in dynamic or sensor-degraded environments. The objective here is to develop a perception system that is low-latency, robust to noisy and partial depth inputs, and requires only a single depth camera.
- **Addressing Occluded and Blind Regions:** Traditional terrain reconstruction methods struggle with unobserved areas (e.g., the bottom of a gap or a steep drop-off). A key motivation is to enable the system to reconstruct structured terrain representations even from noisy, partial, and self-occluded depth images, allowing the robot to infer unseen geometry critical for safe locomotion.
- **Enabling Agile and Adaptive Humanoid Locomotion:** Ultimately, the goal is to develop a unified framework that allows a full-sized humanoid robot to perform agile and adaptive locomotion across a wide variety of challenging and unstructured terrains in real-time, relying solely on depth perception.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework tightly integrates three key components: (1) a Terrain-Aware Locomotion Policy with a Blind Backbone, (2) a Multi-Modality Cross-Attention Transformer for Terrain Reconstruction, and (3) a Realistic Depth Images Synthetic Method. This multi-stage training framework allows for end-to-end fine-tuning based on pre-trained policies without relying on external localization systems.

#### 3.1. Terrain-Aware Locomotion Policy with Blind Backbone

- **Policy Structure:** A teacher-student architecture is employed. The "teacher" comprises terrain-specific expert policies trained with privileged information (full-state observability) for various environments (gaps, stairs, flat). The "student" policy, which is the deployed policy, learns from these experts via L2 loss distillation and also optimizes through Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).
- **Blind Backbone:** This core component provides a stable, pre-trained locomotion controller using only proprioceptive states and user commands (excluding terrain heightmap). It ensures basic robust motion.
- **Vision-Based Modulator:** This part augments the blind backbone by incorporating the terrain heightmap and the blind action. It outputs modulating joint actions and residual adjustments for gait phase and velocity commands, allowing the robot to adapt to complex environments. The final joint action is a convex combination of blind and modulated actions.
- **Reward Functions:** The training uses a comprehensive reward system encouraging commanded velocity following, stable orientation, and smooth, efficient locomotion. Crucially, specific reward terms like "stumble" and "stumble during swing" penalize undesired foot impacts, tailored for humanoid gaits.
- **Training Paradigm:** The policy is trained end-to-end, directly mapping partial and noisy observations to actions. Fine-tuning on real sensory inputs further closes the sim-to-real gap.

#### 3.2. Multi-Modality Cross-Attention Transformer for Terrain Reconstruction

- **Input:** The module takes proprioceptive history (past 50 states) and temporal depth observations (5 frames) as input.
- **Feature Extraction:** Depth images are processed by a convolutional encoder, and proprioceptive history is embedded into latent vectors.
- **Cross-Attention Fusion:** A transformer module fuses these modalities. Proprioceptive embeddings act as the query, and depth embeddings provide keys and values. This allows the reconstructor to selectively attend to relevant terrain features based on the robot's current motion state. Recurrent memory units (GRU) maintain temporal consistency.
- **Heightmap Reconstruction:** A decoder first produces a "rough" heightmap. To improve fidelity, a conditional U-Net then refines this rough prediction using the encoded depth latent as an additional input, sharpening edges and improving flatness.
- **Loss Function:** The reconstruction is supervised by a combination of L2 loss for the rough heightmap and L1 loss for the refined heightmap against the ground-truth local heightmap.

#### 3.3. Realistic Depth Images Synthetic Method

This method is crucial for bridging the sim-to-real gap by generating synthetic depth images that realistically reflect real-world sensor characteristics.
- **GPU Ray Casting:** Depth images are synthesized by tracing virtual rays from a pinhole camera model into a dynamic 3D triangle mesh (static terrain + articulated robot geometry). This accounts for self-occlusion from the robot's body. The process computes accurate 3D hit locations and corresponding depth values for each pixel.
- **Depth Domain Randomization with Noise Model:** A stochastic corruption model is applied to the geometrically accurate depth images to simulate real-world sensor artifacts:
 - **Cropping and Resampling:** A fixed margin of pixels is cropped, and the central region is resampled to the target resolution to mitigate border distortions.
 - **Axial Noise:** Range-dependent Gaussian noise is added, with variance increasing quadratically with depth, consistent with empirical studies of real depth sensors (e.g., Kinect).
 - **Lateral Uncertainty:** A small, range-proportional perturbation is introduced to simulate mixed-pixel effects and spatial smearing.
 - **Depth Holes/Missingness:** A per-pixel uncertainty map (combining axial and lateral components) is used to generate a pixel-wise dropout probability. This simulates realistic uncertainty-induced holes.
 - **Edge-Conditioned Dropout:** Additional dropout is applied along geometric discontinuities (edges detected by Sobel gradients), reproducing observed hole patterns at depth discontinuities and grazing angles.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the DPL framework was validated through extensive simulation and real-world experiments on a full-sized humanoid robot, TienKung Ultral.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results

- **Terrain Reconstruction Accuracy:** The proposed full model consistently achieved the lowest Mean Absolute Error (MAE) across seven diverse terrain types (e.g., rough slopes, stairs, gaps, high planes), with an average MAE of 3.25 cm. Ablation studies confirmed the critical contributions of the GRU module (for temporal features) and the conditional U-Net (for refinement), demonstrating their roles in reducing error and improving generalization. Compared to CNN-based and ResNet-based baselines, the full model showed superior performance.
- **Locomotion Policy Robustness:** Ablation studies on locomotion success rate and traversing rate revealed the importance of key policy components:
 - **Multi-teacher distillation** and **gait-command adaptation** significantly improved performance in high-difficulty terrains like gaps and hurdles by leveraging expert knowledge and dynamically adjusting gait parameters.
 - The **blind backbone** played a crucial role in preventing conservative behaviors (e.g., halting at challenging terrains), encouraging the robot to attempt traversal and learn effective crossing strategies.

#### 4.2. Real-World Reconstruction Results

- **Depth Preprocessing Impact:** Ablations on the depth preprocessing pipeline (geometric-aware cropping, self-occlusion, noise modeling) demonstrated its significant impact. The "Origin" (raw depth) variant yielded the highest error (16.07 cm MAE). The full pipeline, including self-occlusion, cropping, resizing, and the noise model, achieved the lowest MAE (3.25 cm), indicating that physically informed noise synthesis and structured processing are crucial for real-world accuracy.
- **Reconstruction of Occluded Regions:** Visual comparisons showed that the proposed method could successfully reconstruct the full geometry of complex terrains, such as gaps, including the otherwise occluded bottom. This is a critical advantage over traditional elevation map methods, which fail to capture such regions due to missing depth information, preventing reliable locomotion planning.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiments

- **Perception Delay:** The DPL framework achieved a consistently lower and more stable perception update delay (approximately 20 ms) compared to elevation map-based approaches (average 30 ms with larger fluctuations). This reduction in latency is vital for real-time control and narrows the sim-to-real performance gap.
- **Generalization to Unseen Dynamics:** The humanoid robot successfully performed stable stepping motions on a movable platform, a scenario not encountered during training. This demonstrated significant zero-shot generalization capabilities, highlighting the policy's robustness and adaptability to unexpected dynamic environments.
- **Adaptive Locomotion on Diverse Terrains:** The robot demonstrated reliable adaptability across various real-world terrains, including climbing and descending stairs, crossing consecutive gaps, and stepping down from sloped elevated platforms. This showcased the framework's ability to enable robust and reactive gait adaptation.
- **Impact of End-to-End Fine-tuning:** A crucial finding was the impact of end-to-end fine-tuning on real sensory inputs. Without it, the policy experienced 8 stumbles out of 10 trials on stair traversal due to sim-to-real mismatches. With end-to-end fine-tuning, stumbles were significantly reduced to 4 out of 10 trials, illustrating its effectiveness in mitigating perception-induced delays and improving robustness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes significant contributions to the field of humanoid perceptive locomotion, with substantial potential impact:

- **Pioneering Depth-Only Perception:** By achieving robust perceptive locomotion using only a single depth camera, the research simplifies the hardware requirements, potentially reducing cost, power consumption, and complexity for humanoid robot deployment. This makes advanced locomotion capabilities more accessible.
- **Effective Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The proposed Realistic Depth Images Synthetic Method, with its self-occlusion-aware ray casting and comprehensive noise modeling, provides a powerful tool for bridging the sim-to-real gap. This is a perpetual challenge in robot learning, and the demonstrated over 30% reduction in terrain reconstruction error is a notable improvement.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** The combination of a blind backbone for baseline stability, a multi-modality cross-attention transformer for intelligent terrain reconstruction, and end-to-end fine-tuning ensures that the humanoid robot can handle diverse, challenging, and even dynamic terrains with high robustness and adaptability. The ability to infer occluded regions like gap bottoms is a critical advancement over traditional methods.
- **Efficient Training Paradigm:** The multi-stage training framework, leveraging teacher-student distillation and pre-trained policies, addresses the computational demands of humanoid robot training, making the process more efficient and feasible with limited resources.
- **Reduced Perception Latency:** Achieving low and stable perception update times (20 ms) is crucial for real-time, responsive control, especially for highly dynamic and unstable platforms like humanoids. This allows for more synchronized perception-action loops and better real-world performance.
- **Foundation for Autonomous Humanoids:** The demonstrated capabilities, particularly in zero-shot generalization to unseen scenarios (like movable platforms) and robust traversal of complex outdoor-like terrains, represent a significant step towards truly autonomous humanoids capable of operating reliably in unstructured, human-centric environments such as homes, workplaces, or disaster zones.

In essence, this research provides a comprehensive and practical framework that pushes the boundaries of humanoid robot autonomy, addressing several long-standing challenges in perception, control, and sim-to-real transfer. Its impact could accelerate the development and deployment of next-generation humanoid robots in a wide range of applications.
