# Video2Layout: Recall and Reconstruct Metric-Grounded Cognitive Map for Spatial Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from several Chinese institutions developed a framework, Video2Layout, that equips multimodal large language models with a metric-grounded cognitive map, enabling precise spatial reasoning by converting video inputs into continuous Bird's-Eye View object coordinates. This system demonstrates enhanced accuracy on spatial benchmarks and improves generalization to real-world environments through a two-stage training approach.

## Problem

- Existing multimodal large language models (MLLMs) struggle with precise spatial perception and reasoning, particularly in forming coherent global spatial representations from multi-frame visual inputs.
- Previous attempts to integrate cognitive maps into MLLMs relied on discrete, grid-based representations, which led to coarse positional descriptions, inaccuracies in real-world metrics, and object overlaps.
- The inherent ambiguity of natural language descriptions for spatial relationships hinders fine-grained, quantitative spatial computation required for advanced embodied AI tasks.

## Method

- The Video2Layout framework constructs a ==metric-grounded cognitive map== from video inputs, representing spatial layouts using continuous ==Bird's-Eye View (BEV) bounding box coordinates== for precise object localization and size.
- A structured Chain-of-Thought (COT) process explicitly decouples ==spatial perception (Map Module)== from mathematical and logical deduction (Think Module) to perform rigorous numerical spatial computation.
- A two-stage training paradigm utilizes Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on simulated data to instill foundational spatial perception and a structured output format, followed by ==Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT)== on real-world data to enhance generalization and bridge the sim-to-real gap.

## Results

- V2LO-7B achieved an average accuracy of **47.46%** across mainstream spatial reasoning benchmarks, demonstrating an absolute improvement of **3.29%** over its base model and **4.92%** over grid-map-trained counterparts.
- The framework outperformed leading closed-source models like GPT-4o (**46.25%** overall) and Gemini 2.0 Flash (**32.56%** on ViewSpatial-Bench), with V2LO-7B achieving **40.18%** on ViewSpatial-Bench and slightly surpassing GPT-4o's overall average.
- Analysis on the QVS-Bench diagnostic benchmark revealed that cognitive map accuracy and overall spatial task performance peak with **4-8** input images (e.g., **61.32%** map accuracy with 4 images), indicating an optimal range for visual information aggregation, and V2LO-7B consistently outperformed baselines across varying image quantities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Metric-grounded cognitive maps, using continuous object coordinates, offer a significantly more powerful foundation for fine-grained spatial knowledge learning compared to discrete grid-based representations.
> - Transforming abstract spatial problems into explicit mathematical deductions via a structured numerical Chain-of-Thought enhances reasoning accuracy by overcoming the imprecision of purely linguistic spatial descriptions.
> - The synergistic combination of SFT with precisely annotated synthetic data and RFT with real-world data effectively generalizes MLLM spatial reasoning capabilities to diverse real-world scenarios without requiring explicit ground-truth coordinates during reinforcement learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "Video2Layout" addresses a key challenge in the field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs): the development of robust spatial intelligence. Spatial understanding is considered a critical frontier for MLLMs, enabling them to comprehend and interact with the physical world, which is a core component for achieving embodied intelligence.

Prior research in spatial reasoning within MLLMs has often drawn inspiration from human cognitive map mechanisms, attempting to construct spatial representations for models. Many existing approaches have relied on grid-based cognitive maps, which discretize continuous space. While these grid maps provide a foundation for spatial understanding, they are noted to have limitations in fine-grained spatial reasoning due to their coarse-grained representation of inter-object relationships, approximations of real-world spatial metrics, and susceptibility to object overlap within a single grid cell. This discretization can lead to metric and semantic inaccuracies regarding real distances, object sizes, and precise directions.

The current research paradigm for MLLMs and spatial reasoning is advancing across several dimensions, including dataset construction, model architecture innovation (e.g., specialized encoders), task-specific training methodologies (SFT, RL), and customized reasoning strategies like Chain-of-Thought (CoT). While general free-text CoT has shown success in general reasoning, its ambiguity limits its utility for tasks requiring accurate geometric localization and perspective transformation. This has led to a shift towards structured CoT, which incorporates explicit geometric and relational constraints, often leveraging coordinate information through "cognitive map" approaches.

This paper positions itself within the "cognitive map" branch of customized spatial reasoning, specifically targeting the limitations of discrete grid maps. It highlights that current implementations of cognitive maps face challenges such as limited capacity to accurately capture actual distances and true sizes, reliance on external depth estimation modules increasing complexity, and restriction to single-frame inputs rather than dynamic video sequences.

"Video2Layout" aims to advance this landscape by proposing a framework that moves beyond discretized grid representations to reconstruct metric-grounded spatial layouts from video. By utilizing continuous object boundary coordinates, it seeks to establish a quantitative foundation for fine-grained spatial computation, thereby reducing ambiguity in natural language spatial descriptions and improving the model's capacity for rigorous spatial reasoning in dynamic, real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the spatial reasoning capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) by enabling them to construct and utilize a metric-grounded cognitive map from video inputs. The authors propose the "Video2Layout" framework to achieve this, aiming to overcome limitations inherent in existing grid-based spatial representation methods.

The detailed motivations for this work are as follows:

- **Addressing Limitations of Discretized Spatial Representations:** Prior studies on spatial understanding in MLLMs have largely adopted grid-based cognitive maps. These representations discretize continuous space into a grid (e.g., M×M cells). While foundational, this discretization intrinsically limits the model's capacity for fine-grained spatial reasoning. Such grid maps can only describe inter-object spatial relationships in a coarse manner, provide only approximate real-world spatial metrics, and are prone to object overlap within a single grid cell, which is suboptimal for tasks requiring high precision. For instance, they may inaccurately represent real distances, precise object sizes, or exact directions.

- **Enabling Quantitative Spatial Computation:** Natural language descriptions of spatial relationships often contain inherent ambiguity. The authors argue that a precise, quantitative spatial representation is necessary for rigorous spatial computation. By shifting from discrete grid cells to continuous object boundary coordinates (Bird’s-Eye View bounding boxes), the model can operate with real-world metrics. This continuous representation is intended to provide a solid foundation for mathematical and logical operations, thereby mitigating ambiguity and enhancing the accuracy of spatial localization and calculation.

- **Improving Real-World Generalization:** A significant challenge in developing spatial intelligence for MLLMs is the gap between training data (often synthetic or simulated) and real-world scenarios. The high cost, noise, and limited accuracy of target localization in real-world spatial data complicate direct training. The authors are motivated to develop a training paradigm that effectively bridges this "sim-to-real" gap, ensuring that models trained on precise simulated data can generalize robustly to complex real-world environments.

- **Supporting Dynamic Spatial Reasoning from Video:** Many existing spatial understanding methods are limited to single-frame image inputs, which are insufficient for dynamic scenarios requiring continuous spatial reasoning. The research aims to process sequential video inputs to aggregate multi-frame local spatial features into a coherent and unified global spatial representation over time, thereby catering to the demands of dynamic spatial reasoning.

- **Investigating Factors Affecting Cognitive Map Accuracy:** Beyond developing the framework, a secondary objective is to conduct a comprehensive study on cognitive maps themselves. This includes investigating how various factors (e.g., number of input frames, object-to-camera distance, camera rotation) influence map accuracy and quantifying the relationship between the precision of the cognitive map and downstream task performance. This analysis aims to provide insights into the functional utility and sensitivities of metric-grounded spatial representations.

In summary, the key objective is to empower MLLMs with a metric-grounded spatial understanding by reconstructing precise layouts from video, enabling quantitative reasoning, and ensuring robust generalization to real-world applications, ultimately advancing MLLMs towards more sophisticated physical world comprehension and embodied intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Video2Layout framework is designed to reconstruct metric-grounded spatial layouts from video and improve spatial reasoning in MLLMs. The methodology comprises three sequential components: Data Preparation, Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), and Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT).

#### 3.1. Data Preparation

The initial stage focuses on curating a dataset, V2LO-28K, specifically designed to enhance metric-grounded map perception. This dataset is partitioned into three subsets:

- **SFT Training Set (16K samples):** This set combines 12K samples of simulated spatial data with 4K samples of general Visual Question Answering (VQA) data. The simulated data, sourced from the AI2THOR simulator, provides precise coordinate information as a supervisory signal, teaching the model the mapping from visual inputs to accurate bounding box coordinates. The general VQA data (randomly sampled from Video-R1-260k) helps retain the model’s general domain capabilities.
- **RL Training Set (8K samples):** Derived from the ScanNet dataset, this set uses real-world spatial data to enhance the model's adaptability and generalization to real-world scenarios during the RFT stage.
- **QVS-Bench (4K samples):** Also derived from ScanNet, this benchmark is strictly isolated from the RL training set by scene ID to prevent data leakage. It serves as a dedicated testbed to evaluate the effectiveness of the RL approach in achieving sim-to-real generalization.

**Data Collection:** A unified pipeline processes both simulated and real scenes.
- **Simulated Data:** A path planning algorithm generates motion trajectories, along which cameras capture videos. These videos are segmented and filtered using object-centric metadata (e.g., bounding box size, pixel coverage) to extract clips containing salient targets. Image sequences of varying lengths (1, 4, 8, 12, 16 frames) are systematically extracted.
- **Real Data:** For the ScanNet dataset, scene metadata are parsed, RGB frames are resampled to 20 FPS for temporal consistency, and the same multi-scale sampling strategy extracts image sequences of identical lengths.

**QA Generation:** An automatic QA pair generation framework is tailored for spatial reasoning tasks, leveraging parsed meta-information.
- **Multiple-choice QA:** Focuses on spatial relationship judgment, including relative distance, vertical directional relationships, and horizontal directional relationships.
- **Numerical QA:** Focuses on spatial quantity calculation, covering minimum distance and object count.
For the SFT stage, structured Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning processes are generated via predefined templates and refined by Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct. These CoT processes serve as supervision for coordinate representation and numerical calculation learning. The coordinate system is based on the first frame's starting position and initial camera orientation. For the RFT stage, only QA pairs are used without CoT processes, relying on result-level supervision for real-world data without fine-grained annotations.

#### 3.2. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

This phase aims to train the MLLM (Qwen-2.5-VL-7B-Instruct as the base model) to generate a metric-grounded map and adhere to a structured reasoning output format. A structured Chain-of-Thought (CoT) process with three modules is employed:

- **Map Module:** This module performs spatial perception and formal scene representation. It constructs a structured Bird’s-Eye View within a Cartesian coordinate system, defined by the first frame's starting position and initial camera orientation. Relevant objects are projected to precise bounding box coordinates, intended to alleviate ambiguities in natural language descriptions and improve spatial localization accuracy.
- **Think Module:** This module handles deductive reasoning. Based on the coordinate-based representation from the Map Module, it performs explicit, step-by-step mathematical and logical operations. For example, it computes Euclidean distances for minimum distance queries or dynamically establishes local coordinate systems and uses vector operations for complex relational reasoning (e.g., determining an object's position from a novel viewpoint). This numerical computation-based approach aims to address the inaccuracy of purely natural language-based spatial reasoning.
- **Answer Module:** This module generates the final output directly from the explicit reasoning process of the Think Module. It transforms structured reasoning results (e.g., computed distances, orientation judgments) into task-aligned formats like natural language answers or standardized numerical outputs.
The Map and Think modules are optional; they are omitted for non-spatial queries or simple reasoning tasks, ensuring efficiency and flexibility. LoRA (rank 64, alpha 128, learning rate 1e-4) is used for parameter-efficient fine-tuning over 3 epochs.

#### 3.3. Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT)

The RFT stage addresses the distributional discrepancies between simulated and real-world data. It leverages the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm to train the model on real-world spatial data, transferring and extending spatial representation capabilities to real scenarios. Direct result-level supervision is adopted, given that most spatial tasks concern relative positional relationships and continuous camera motion can induce unstable reward calculations.

Two verifiable reward functions guide policy optimization:

- **Format Reward ($R_{format}$):** Assigns a reward of 1 if the model's response adheres to the predefined output structure, 0 otherwise.
- **Task Answer Reward ($R_{task}$):**
 - **Multi-choice Reward ($R_{mc}$):** Binary reward (1 for exact match, 0 otherwise) for multiple-choice QA.
 - **Numerical Reward ($R_{num}$):** Computes relative accuracy across varying confidence thresholds (0.5 to 0.95). The reward is the average of indicators checking if the relative error is within each threshold.
The overall reward ($R_i$) is a weighted sum of the format reward and task reward ($R_i = \alpha * R_{format} + (1 - \alpha) * R_{task}$, with $\alpha = 0.1$). An advantage ($A_i$) is calculated for each response within a group of G responses, normalized by the mean and standard deviation of rewards within that group. The policy update follows the clipped surrogate objective of GRPO to limit the update degree.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the Video2Layout framework and its V2LO-7B model on a suite of spatial reasoning benchmarks, along with conducting detailed analysis studies and ablations.

#### 4.1. Overall Analysis

- **Benchmark Performance:** V2LO-7B achieves an average accuracy of 47.46% across four open-source benchmarks (EmbSpatial-Bench, ViewSpatial-Bench, OmniSpatial-Bench, SPAR-Bench), representing a 3.29% absolute improvement over the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B model (44.17%). It also surpasses the closed-source GPT-4o (46.25%) and other open-source models like SpaceR-7B (46.52%).
- **Critical Spatial Sub-tasks:** V2LO-7B demonstrates robust performance on Perspective Taking (46.70% on OmniSpatial) and Camera-based reasoning (41.66% on ViewSpatial), outperforming SpaceR-7B in these areas. This suggests that fine-grained coordinate modeling and symbolic mathematical reasoning can augment spatial reasoning capabilities.
- **QVS-Bench Evaluation:** On the QVS-Bench benchmark, V2LO-7B scores 56.56%, outperforming GPT-5 (43.57%) and SpaceR-7B (40.09%). However, it still lags behind the human baseline (62.96%). The performance gap is mainly concentrated in numerical estimation tasks, where the model's accuracy in predicting precise metrics does not match human intuition.
- **Directional Judgment:** V2LO-7B shows a competitive advantage in directional judgment tasks, achieving 73.0% accuracy for vertical orientation and 72.0% for horizontal orientation on QVS-Bench. This performance exceeds both existing models and the human baseline for this specific task. The authors attribute this to the framework's ability to translate abstract qualitative spatial cues into deterministic geometric calculations by constructing new local coordinate systems.

#### 4.2. Analysis Study: Factors Influencing Cognitive Map Accuracy

The study quantifies the divergence between predicted and ground-truth layouts using three relative metrics: Bounding box area score, Center-point distance score, and Angular alignment score.

- **Input Video Frames:** The number of input frames has a modest influence on accuracy. Performance is relatively stable in shorter sequences (60.05% at 1 frame to 61.32% at 4 frames). However, accuracy declines to 56.28% at 16 frames, suggesting potential accumulation of estimation noise or redundant information with longer sequences.
- **Object-to-Camera Distance:** Average object-to-camera distance significantly impacts map precision. Accuracy is highest at close proximity (64.25% at 3m) but drops to 50.03% at 15m. This degradation indicates the difficulty of metric estimation for distant objects due to reduced parallax and sparse visual features.
- **Cumulative Camera Rotation:** Accuracy shows an inverse correlation with cumulative camera rotation. Performance declines from 62.35% at 0° to 51.52% at 240°. Large angular changes are challenging for maintaining spatial consistency and can introduce cumulative estimation drift.

In summary, cognitive map precision is robust to frame count in short sequences but sensitive to increased distances and large rotations.

#### 4.3. Analysis Study: Impact of Cognitive Map Accuracy on Task Performance

The study examines how map precision influences downstream task performance by sampling across map accuracy levels (56% to 64%).

- **Minimum Distance Estimation:** This task is strongly sensitive to cognitive map accuracy. Task scores consistently rise from 25.10% to 38.30% with improved map precision, highlighting the necessity of precise coordinates for accurate distance calculations.
- **Object Counting:** This task is relatively robust to variations in map precision, with performance marginally varying from 50.80% to 52.40%. This suggests that for discrete tasks, object presence is more critical than absolute metric precision.
- **Direction Reasoning Task:** This task achieves high baseline performance and improves moderately with map accuracy before plateauing. Accuracy increases from 70.80% to a peak of 76.50%. This robustness is attributed to directional reasoning primarily relying on relative spatial orientation, which tolerates minor metric inaccuracies.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study: Reasoning Forms

Comparison of reasoning forms on the QVS-Bench and four other benchmarks:

- **Free-style CoT:** Introduction of Free-style CoT (43.20%) offers a small gain over the vanilla Qwen2.5VL-7B (41.96%), but it is surpassed by methods incorporating explicit spatial structures. This indicates that while CoT is beneficial, spatial reasoning requires structured representation to bridge visual tokens and geometric relations.
- **Grid Maps:** Performance peaks at a 20x20 resolution (46.04%), but plateaus or declines at higher granularities (45.62% at 40x40). This suggests a trade-off where higher resolutions, while theoretically reducing quantization errors, can create an overly fragmented state space that complicates learnability.
- **Metric-Grounded Map (V2LO-7B):** V2LO-7B achieves 49.28% accuracy, outperforming all other reasoning forms. The metric-based approach facilitates fine-grained spatial reasoning and yields consistent improvements across benchmarks.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study: Training Phase

The contributions of each training stage are quantified:

- **SFT Impact:** Standalone SFT training improves performance from 41.96% (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) to 44.30%, demonstrating its role in providing reliable coordinate perception and numerical reasoning.
- **RL Impact and Sim-to-Real Generalization:** The full V2LO-7B model (SFT + RL on real data) achieves the best overall score of 49.28%. Direct application of RL on real data without SFT (44.00%) is significantly lower, highlighting SFT's role in establishing a high-quality optimization foundation. Comparing RL using simulated data after SFT (43.32%) with RL using real data after SFT (49.28%) demonstrates that real-world RL training substantially elevates performance, confirming its efficacy in endowing the model with strong real-scenario generalization ability and bridging the sim-to-real gap.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Video2Layout" framework introduces a metric-grounded cognitive map, representing a shift from discretized grid-based spatial representations to continuous object boundary coordinates for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). This methodological advancement has several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Enhanced Fine-Grained Spatial Reasoning:** By enabling quantitative spatial computation, the framework addresses the inherent limitations of coarse-grained grid maps and the ambiguity of natural language descriptions in complex spatial relationships. This allows MLLMs to perform more precise calculations of distances, sizes, and directions, which is crucial for tasks requiring high spatial accuracy.
- **Improved Robustness and Generalization to Real-World Scenarios:** The two-stage training paradigm, combining supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on high-quality simulated data with reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) on real-world data, is designed to effectively bridge the "sim-to-real" gap. This is critical for deploying MLLMs in practical applications where they must operate reliably in diverse and noisy physical environments. The demonstrated improvements in generalization capabilities suggest a path towards more dependable AI systems for real-world tasks.
- **Foundation for Embodied AI:** Spatial intelligence is a core component of embodied AI, where agents interact with and navigate the physical world. A metric-grounded cognitive map provides a more accurate internal representation of the environment, which can support more sophisticated planning, navigation, and manipulation tasks for embodied agents. The ability to recall and reconstruct precise layouts from video is a foundational step towards enabling MLLMs to truly comprehend and interact with their physical surroundings.
- **Deeper Understanding of Cognitive Map Utility:** The detailed analysis of factors influencing cognitive map accuracy and its correlation with task performance provides valuable insights into the sensitivities and strengths of metric-grounded representations. This includes understanding the trade-offs involved with varying input frame counts, object distances, and camera movements, as well as task-specific dependencies on map precision. Such insights can guide future research in designing more robust spatial perception and reasoning systems.
- **Advancement in Multimodal Language Models:** The work contributes to the broader field of MLLMs by demonstrating how specialized spatial processing can significantly augment their overall capabilities. The integration of structured Chain-of-Thought reasoning, grounded in precise geometric calculations, offers a model for enhancing MLLMs in other domains that require rigorous, structured inference beyond free-form text.
- **New Benchmarking and Evaluation Tools:** The introduction of the V2LO-28K dataset and the QVS-Bench benchmark provides valuable resources for the research community. These datasets, with their focus on metric-grounded spatial reasoning and sim-to-real generalization, can facilitate further development and standardized evaluation of advanced spatial intelligence in MLLMs.

Overall, this research represents a significant step towards equipping MLLMs with a more sophisticated and reliable understanding of space, paving the way for advancements in embodied artificial intelligence and more capable general-purpose AI systems operating in the physical world.
