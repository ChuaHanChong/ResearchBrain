# EgoDemoGen: Egocentric Demonstration Generation for Viewpoint Generalization in Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> EgoDemoGen introduces a framework for generating paired observation-action demonstrations from novel egocentric viewpoints, enhancing the generalization of visuomotor policies in robotic manipulation by synthetically expanding training data. The method achieved average success rate gains of +16.9% for novel viewpoints in simulation and +23.0% in real-world settings, significantly improving policy robustness against viewpoint shifts.

## Problem

- Imitation learning policies in robotic manipulation often exhibit sensitivity and fragility to egocentric viewpoint shifts during deployment, leading to task failures.
- Collecting comprehensive datasets covering every possible egocentric viewpoint for robust policy training is prohibitively resource-intensive and impractical.
- Existing novel-view synthesis and data generation methods for robots often fail to account for simultaneous changes in camera pose and robot action coordinate frames, leading to visual-action misalignment.

## Method

- **EgoDemoGen** employs a two-component framework: EgoTrajTransfer for adapting robot action trajectories to novel egocentric viewpoints, ensuring kinematic feasibility and task semantics.
- EgoViewTransfer synthesizes photorealistic and temporally consistent RGB observation videos corresponding to these transferred actions using a conditional video diffusion model.
- A self-supervised double reprojection strategy is utilized to train EgoViewTransfer, enabling it to learn to repair reprojection artifacts and seamlessly composite robot motion without requiring multi-viewpoint demonstration data.

## Results

- Policies trained with EgoDemoGen achieved average success rates of **27.9%** on novel viewpoints in simulation, a **+16.9%** absolute gain over the standard-view baseline.
- In real-world settings, EgoDemoGen improved average success rates by **+23.0%** on novel viewpoints and **+16.0%** on standard viewpoints, demonstrating practical transferability.
- EgoViewTransfer consistently outperformed baseline video generation methods across PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and FVD metrics, producing visually faithful and temporally coherent videos with repaired artifacts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Egocentric viewpoint shifts necessitate simultaneous adaptation of both visual observations and robot action trajectories to maintain consistency and feasibility.
> - Action trajectories can be effectively transferred by segmenting them into 'motion' and 'skill' phases, applying geometry-aware transformations, and filtering for kinematic feasibility via inverse kinematics.
> - Decoupled conditioning on a reprojected, inpainted background scene and a rendered robot motion video allows for high-quality, aligned video synthesis under novel viewpoints.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Imitation learning has emerged as a prominent paradigm for robotic manipulation, particularly with the development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models trained on large datasets. While these policies demonstrate strong performance in various tasks, they often exhibit sensitivity to viewpoint shifts during deployment. This sensitivity is a practical concern in real-world scenarios, where factors such as imprecise robot base positioning, platform reconfiguration, or changes in the environment layout can naturally lead to viewpoint variations. The prohibitive cost of collecting demonstrations under every possible viewpoint necessitates methods for generating synthetic data under diverse conditions.

The existing research landscape addresses viewpoint generalization through several avenues, but with limitations regarding egocentric shifts:
- **Novel-view synthesis (NVS):** Many NVS methods focus on generating observations from novel *third-person* viewpoints. In these scenarios, only the camera's position changes, while the robot's base coordinate frame and action trajectories remain fixed.
- **Egocentric viewpoint shifts:** In contrast, an egocentric viewpoint shift involves a simultaneous change in both the camera's pose and the robot's action coordinate frame. This means that a source action trajectory becomes invalid under the new base frame and cannot be directly replayed without modification. Existing NVS methods do not account for this action frame change, leading to visual-action misalignment.
- **Action-conditioned video generation and world models:** These models are primarily designed for prediction or planning, or for generating future frames conditioned on actions. They typically do not explicitly model egocentric viewpoint changes or aim to produce paired observation-action demonstrations for policy training.
- **Data generation for policy learning:** Current approaches include geometry-based methods (which generate novel views but do not transfer actions), visual synthesis methods (which may generate views but lack corresponding actions or misalign action frames), and motion retargeting methods (which adapt actions but often from a third-person perspective).

This work addresses a recognized gap in the current research: the lack of a framework capable of generating paired observation–action demonstrations that maintain consistency and feasibility under egocentric viewpoint changes. EgoDemoGen aims to bridge this gap by jointly transferring robot actions to a novel egocentric frame and synthesizing corresponding, photorealistic observation videos that are aligned with these transferred actions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed fragility of imitation learning policies in robotic manipulation when faced with egocentric viewpoint shifts. Policies trained on demonstrations from a single egocentric perspective frequently fail if the robot's relative position or orientation to the task changes. Such shifts are not uncommon in practical applications due to factors like robot repositioning, varying environmental setups, or even slight inaccuracies in deployment.

Collecting a comprehensive dataset that covers every potential egocentric viewpoint is resource-intensive and often impractical. Therefore, the ability to synthetically generate demonstrations from novel egocentric viewpoints is critical for improving the robustness and generalization of robotic policies.

The authors identify a fundamental distinction between third-person and egocentric viewpoint changes. A third-person change solely involves camera movement, leaving robot actions unchanged. However, an egocentric shift implies a change in the robot's base coordinate frame, which invalidates the original action trajectory relative to the new frame. This necessitates a dual approach:
1. **Action Trajectory Transfer:** The robot's actions must be adapted to be kinematically feasible and task-completing within the new egocentric coordinate frame.
2. **Observation Synthesis:** Photorealistic and temporally consistent observation videos must be generated from the new viewpoint, accurately depicting the robot executing the transferred actions. Crucially, these synthetic observations and transferred actions must be precisely aligned.

The key objectives of this research are to develop a comprehensive framework, EgoDemoGen, that can:
- Generate kinematically feasible robot actions adapted to novel egocentric viewpoints.
- Synthesize realistic and temporally consistent observation videos that correspond to these transferred actions from the novel viewpoint.
- Ensure precise visual-action alignment within the generated demonstrations.
- Improve the generalization capabilities and success rates of visuomotor policies when deployed under various egocentric viewpoint shifts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EgoDemoGen operates by generating novel egocentric demonstrations from a single source demonstration through a multi-step process involving trajectory transfer and conditional video generation. The framework is composed of two main technical components: EgoTrajTransfer for action trajectory manipulation and EgoViewTransfer for observation video synthesis.

The overall methodology involves four main steps:
1. **Random Egocentric Viewpoint Sampling:** Novel egocentric viewpoints are generated by sampling planar robot base motions (∆x, ∆y, ∆θ). This base motion simultaneously defines the new camera pose and the robot's action coordinate frame.
2. **EgoTrajTransfer (Action Trajectory Transfer):** This component adapts the source robot trajectory to the novel egocentric viewpoint while maintaining task semantics and kinematic feasibility. The process includes:
 - **Trajectory Segmentation:** Each arm's trajectory is segmented based on gripper states. A closed gripper typically indicates a "skill segment" (contact-rich manipulation), while an open gripper denotes a "motion segment" (free-space movement). Segment boundaries between dual arms are synchronized to handle sensor noise.
 - **Geometry-aware Transformation:**
 - For **motion segments**, the end-effector path structure is preserved but adapted to new endpoints. The new end pose is calculated by applying the base transformation (∆T) to the old end pose. Position is scaled and aligned based on new start and end points. Orientation is interpolated using a progress-based spherical linear interpolation (Slerp).
 - For **skill segments**, a rigid body transformation is applied to the end-effector poses directly using ∆T, preserving the object-relative motion. For dual-arm setups, the transformation for one arm is converted to the other arm's local coordinate frame via camera-to-base extrinsics.
 - **Inverse Kinematics (IK) and Feasibility Filtering:** Batch inverse kinematics (IK) is solved for each arm, using the original joint angles as seeds to maintain arm configuration continuity. Frames where IK fails are interpolated from successful neighbors. Crucially, viewpoints are filtered as "infeasible" if the IK success rate falls below a threshold (τ_IK) or if maximum joint angle changes exceed a limit (τ_jump), ensuring that only kinematically viable demonstrations are generated.
3. **EgoViewTransfer (Novel Viewpoint Video Generation):** This component synthesizes photorealistic RGB observation videos (`˜V`) corresponding to the transferred trajectory (`˜Q`) from the novel viewpoint. It utilizes a conditional video diffusion model, building upon a DiT-based image-to-video architecture (CogVideoX-5B-I2V). The process consists of three stages:
 - **Scene Video Preparation:** The original video (`V`) is reprojected to the novel viewpoint, resulting in a scene with potential holes and artifacts. A robot mask (`M_t`) is rendered using the original trajectory, URDF, and novel viewpoint parameters. This mask is applied to remove the robot from the reprojected scene. The masked regions are then inpainted using an algorithm like INPAINT_TELEA to produce a clean background scene video (`V_nov_S`).
 - **Robot Motion Rendering:** A robot-only video (`V_nov_R`) is rendered depicting the robot's motion from the novel viewpoint, specifically using the *transferred trajectory* (`˜Q`), URDF model, and novel viewpoint camera parameters. This explicitly conditions the video generator on the robot's actions.
 - **Conditional Video Generation:** The diffusion model generates the final video (`˜V`) by fusing both the `V_nov_S` (reprojected scene) and `V_nov_R` (rendered robot motion) as decoupled conditions. These conditions are encoded and concatenated with the noisy latent input before being processed by the DiT blocks, which are finetuned for this task while the VAE remains frozen.
4. **Self-Supervised Training Strategy:** To train EgoViewTransfer without requiring multi-viewpoint demonstration data, a double reprojection strategy is employed. For each source demonstration:
 - A random *training viewpoint* is sampled.
 - The source video is reprojected to this training viewpoint and then immediately *reprojected back to the original source viewpoint*. This "double reprojection" introduces realistic artifacts and missing regions that simulate viewpoint transformation challenges.
 - The robot is masked and inpainted from this double-reprojected scene to form the training `V_train_S`.
 - A robot motion video (`V_train_R`) is rendered using the original trajectory from the source viewpoint.
 - The original source video (`V`) serves as the ground truth target. This setup allows the model to learn to repair reprojection artifacts and seamlessly composite robot motion, generalizing to arbitrary novel viewpoints at inference time.

Finally, the generated paired demonstrations `(˜V, ˜Q)` are combined with the original demonstrations to train downstream visuomotor policies (e.g., ACT, π0) via standard behavior cloning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of EgoDemoGen was conducted in both simulation (RoboTwin 2.0 with dual ARX-X5 manipulators) and real-world settings (Mobile ALOHA platform with dual arms), across a range of manipulation tasks. The key findings are as follows:

- **Policy Generalization under Egocentric Viewpoint Shifts (Q1):**
 - **Simulation:** Policies trained only on standard-viewpoint demonstrations experienced a significant performance drop under novel egocentric viewpoints (average 11.0% success rate). EgoDemoGen consistently improved policy success rates, achieving an average of 27.9% on novel viewpoints and maintaining 53.6% on standard viewpoints. This represents absolute gains of +16.9% for novel viewpoints and +24.6% for standard viewpoints compared to the standard-view baseline, demonstrating its effectiveness in improving robustness. Baselines like VISTA, while performing well on standard views, showed limited novel-view gains due to the lack of egocentric action trajectory transfer.
 - **Real World:** EgoDemoGen demonstrated substantial improvements on the real robot, with average success rates of 76.0% on standard viewpoints and 60.0% on novel viewpoints. This corresponded to absolute gains of +16.0% and +23.0% respectively over the standard-view baseline. These gains were consistent across all five real-world tasks, indicating the practical transferability of the generated demonstrations.
- **Video Generation Quality:** EgoViewTransfer consistently outperformed all tested baselines (Direct Reprojection, TrajectoryCrafter, VISTA, Phantom) across quantitative metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FVD) in both simulation and real-world settings. Qualitatively, EgoViewTransfer generated visually faithful and temporally coherent videos, effectively repairing reprojection artifacts and producing robot motion consistent with the transferred trajectories. In contrast, baselines often showed artifacts, distortions, or inconsistencies.
- **Ablation Study (Q2):**
 - **Trajectory Transfer:** Removing EgoTrajTransfer resulted in the largest drop in novel-view performance, highlighting its critical role in ensuring kinematically feasible actions under new egocentric frames. Open-loop replay experiments validated EgoTrajTransfer's high success rate (99.3%) for transferred actions compared to original actions (22.7%) under novel viewpoints.
 - **Double Reprojection:** Training without the self-supervised double reprojection strategy degraded both policy success rates and video generation quality (FVD), confirming its necessity for learning to handle reprojection artifacts effectively.
 - **Mask & Inpaint:** Omitting the mask-and-inpaint step in scene video preparation led to reduced novel-view performance, indicating that residual robot artifacts in the conditioning video can mislead the generation process.
- **Data Scaling Analysis (Q3):**
 - When the total demonstration budget was fixed, novel-view performance peaked when generated data comprised 40-50% of the training set, suggesting that a balanced mixture is more effective than fully replacing original demonstrations.
 - Adding generated demonstrations to a fixed set of original demonstrations consistently improved both standard-view and novel-view success rates in both simulation and real-world settings, with diminishing returns for larger amounts of added data. Even with 1000 initial demonstrations, EgoDemoGen provided substantial novel-view gains (from 31.0% to 58.0%), indicating complementary viewpoint coverage.
- **Viewpoint Generalization Range (Q4):** Policies trained with EgoDemoGen using wider viewpoint generation ranges showed improved generalization to larger unseen viewpoint shifts. The average success rate increased monotonically with the generation range. While larger shifts inherently posed greater challenges (due to robot kinematic limits), EgoDemoGen consistently outperformed the standard-view baseline without compromising in-distribution performance.
- **Base-Camera Decoupled Generalization (Q5):** EgoDemoGen maintained its effectiveness even when the robot base motion and egocentric camera motion were decoupled (e.g., with additional random camera perturbations). This confirmed the robustness and flexibility of its decoupled conditioning design, which independently processes scene geometry and robot motion.
- **Generation Efficiency:** The EgoDemoGen pipeline, while dominated by EgoViewTransfer inference (34.0 seconds for 100 frames on a single H20 GPU), is practical due to its offline and parallelizable nature, enabling large-scale data generation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EgoDemoGen addresses a significant limitation in current robotic imitation learning, which is the vulnerability of visuomotor policies to egocentric viewpoint shifts. By providing a comprehensive framework for generating paired observation-action demonstrations under novel egocentric viewpoints, this work offers several important implications and potential impacts:

- **Enhanced Policy Robustness and Generalization:** The core contribution lies in enabling visuomotor policies to generalize more effectively across diverse egocentric viewpoints. This directly translates to more robust and reliable robotic systems that can operate in dynamic or imprecisely configured environments without requiring extensive manual recalibration or retraining.
- **Reduced Data Collection Costs:** The ability to generate high-quality synthetic demonstrations from a single source viewpoint offers a scalable and cost-effective alternative to collecting real-world demonstrations from every possible perspective. This significantly lowers the barrier to entry for developing and deploying advanced robotic manipulation skills.
- **Novel Technical Contributions to Robotic Data Generation:**
 - **EgoTrajTransfer** introduces a principled method for action trajectory adaptation under egocentric shifts, accounting for varying motion phases and ensuring kinematic feasibility. This contributes to the understanding and implementation of action transfer in complex robotic tasks.
 - **EgoViewTransfer** presents an innovative conditional video generation model that leverages decoupled scene and robot motion conditioning. Its self-supervised double reprojection training strategy is a notable advance for training such models without requiring large, expensive multi-viewpoint datasets.
- **Broad Applicability:** The framework's demonstrated effectiveness across multiple simulation and real-world manipulation tasks, including bimanual operations, and its adaptability to decoupled base and camera motions, suggest its broad applicability to various robotic platforms and scenarios.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** EgoDemoGen lays a foundation for future advancements in robot learning. The authors suggest future integration with world models for closed-loop demonstration generation, extension to cross-embodiment transfer, and exploration of end-to-end training that jointly optimizes demonstration generation and downstream policy learning. These directions could lead to more autonomous and versatile robot learning systems.

In summary, EgoDemoGen offers a practical and effective solution to a fundamental challenge in robot learning, with the potential to significantly improve the generalization, robustness, and scalability of robotic manipulation policies by providing a novel means of data augmentation.
