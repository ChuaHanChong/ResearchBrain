# EgoDemoGen: Egocentric Demonstration Generation for Viewpoint Generalization in Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> EgoDemoGen introduces a framework for generating paired observation-action demonstrations from novel egocentric viewpoints, enhancing the generalization of visuomotor policies in robotic manipulation by synthetically expanding training data. The method achieved average success rate gains of +16.9% for novel viewpoints in simulation and +23.0% in real-world settings, significantly improving policy robustness against viewpoint shifts.

## Problem

- Imitation learning policies in robotic manipulation often exhibit sensitivity and fragility to egocentric viewpoint shifts during deployment, leading to task failures.
- Collecting comprehensive datasets covering every possible egocentric viewpoint for robust policy training is prohibitively resource-intensive and impractical.
- Existing novel-view synthesis and data generation methods for robots often fail to account for simultaneous changes in camera pose and robot action coordinate frames, leading to visual-action misalignment.

## Method

- **EgoDemoGen** employs a two-component framework: EgoTrajTransfer for adapting robot action trajectories to novel egocentric viewpoints, ensuring kinematic feasibility and task semantics.
- EgoViewTransfer synthesizes photorealistic and temporally consistent RGB observation videos corresponding to these transferred actions using a conditional video diffusion model.
- A self-supervised double reprojection strategy is utilized to train EgoViewTransfer, enabling it to learn to repair reprojection artifacts and seamlessly composite robot motion without requiring multi-viewpoint demonstration data.

## Results

- Policies trained with EgoDemoGen achieved average success rates of **27.9%** on novel viewpoints in simulation, a **+16.9%** absolute gain over the standard-view baseline.
- In real-world settings, EgoDemoGen improved average success rates by **+23.0%** on novel viewpoints and **+16.0%** on standard viewpoints, demonstrating practical transferability.
- EgoViewTransfer consistently outperformed baseline video generation methods across PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and FVD metrics, producing visually faithful and temporally coherent videos with repaired artifacts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Egocentric viewpoint shifts necessitate simultaneous adaptation of both visual observations and robot action trajectories to maintain consistency and feasibility.
> - Action trajectories can be effectively transferred by segmenting them into 'motion' and 'skill' phases, applying geometry-aware transformations, and filtering for kinematic feasibility via inverse kinematics.
> - Decoupled conditioning on a reprojected, inpainted background scene and a rendered robot motion video allows for high-quality, aligned video synthesis under novel viewpoints.
