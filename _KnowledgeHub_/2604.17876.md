# OFlow: Injecting Object-Aware Temporal Flow Matching for Robust Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> OFlow injects object-aware temporal flow matching into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, unifying foresight and object reasoning within a shared semantic latent space for robotic manipulation. The framework achieves 96.6% success on the LIBERO benchmark and an average of 69% on real-world tasks, demonstrating enhanced robustness to dynamic environments and distribution shifts.

## Problem

- Existing VLA models primarily operate reactively, lacking foresight for dynamic object interactions, complex temporal dependencies, or long-horizon planning.
- Integrations of future prediction and object-aware reasoning often occur in disjoint latent spaces, leading to computational inefficiencies and representational inconsistencies requiring costly decoding/re-encoding.
- Current methods struggle with robustness under various environmental changes and often rely on pixel-level predictions that can be brittle to appearance noise.

## Method

- **OFlow** unifies ==temporal foresight== and ==object-aware reasoning== within a ==shared semantic latent space==, using ==DINOv2 features== as stable latent embeddings.
- It employs a ==causally-constrained Diffusion Transformer== with ==flow matching== to ==autoregressively predict future semantic states==, enabling predictive reasoning beyond immediate observations.
- ==Object-aware scene factorization== is achieved by ==learning-free K-means clustering== on multi-granularity DINOv2 features, which are then integrated into the VLM using a ==ControlNet-style conditioning mechanism== for action generation.

## Results

- OFlow achieved the highest overall success rates across multiple simulation benchmarks, including **96.6%** on LIBERO, **72.3%** on LIBERO-Plus (demonstrating robustness to perturbations), and **85.6%** on MetaWorld's MT50 suite.
- On seven diverse real-world manipulation tasks, OFlow averaged a **69%** success rate, representing an **18%** improvement over GR00T-N1.5 and **28%** over π0, especially for tasks involving dynamic objects or precise contact.
- Ablation studies confirmed that latent future prediction and object-aware decomposition are critical for performance gains and robustness, maintaining an effective control frequency of approximately **30 Hz** with minimal overhead.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unifying object-aware perception and temporal foresight within a single semantic latent space (DINOv2 features) significantly enhances VLA model robustness and computational efficiency.
> - Predicting abstract semantic states rather than pixel-level future frames provides a more stable and effective form of foresight, reducing brittleness to visual noise and improving generalization.
> - Self-supervised object decomposition using learning-free clustering on latent features allows for autonomous extraction of task-relevant information without explicit annotations, making the system more adaptable.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen advancements with the development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models leverage large-scale pretrained vision-language models to enable robots to follow instructions and ground language in visual observations, performing actions in various environments. Examples include RT-2, Octo, OpenVLA, and π0, which employ transformer architectures and diverse action parameterizations for continuous control. A common limitation of many existing VLA approaches is their predominantly reactive nature, where actions are generated solely based on the current observed frame.

Beyond reactive control, two capabilities are considered important for robust real-world manipulation: foresight over scene dynamics and accurate object-aware perception. Foresight models, as seen in reinforcement learning (e.g., IRIS, Dreamer V3) and large-scale video modeling (e.g., Sora, CogVideoX), aim to predict future observations or states, enabling longer-horizon planning and mitigating myopic decision-making. In robotic learning, some approaches (e.g., CoT-VLA, F1-VLA, DreamVLA, VPP, TriVLA) integrate visual foresight by generating future visual states, often at the pixel level, to condition action generation.

Object-aware features focus on organizing image features into semantically coherent regions, rather than processing the entire image as a monolithic representation. Prior work in this area includes methods leveraging explicit segmentation models like Segment Anything Model (SAM) (e.g., ControlVLA) or learning object-centric representations through slot-attention mechanisms (e.g., SlotVLA). These approaches often either rely on human intervention to define target regions or require explicit object-level supervision.

A challenge in combining foresight and object-awareness within a single manipulation system arises from representational misalignment, as existing models typically learn these capabilities in separate latent spaces. Directly integrating them can lead to redundant processing steps, such as decoding and re-encoding, resulting in computational overhead. This paper aims to address this gap by unifying these capabilities within a shared semantic latent space.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The paper identifies two primary limitations of existing VLA models that motivate the development of OFlow:
1. **Reactive Control and Lack of Foresight:** Current VLA models predominantly operate reactively, generating actions based solely on the immediate observation. This approach can be insufficient for tasks involving dynamic object interactions, complex temporal dependencies, or long-horizon planning, as it hinders the robot's ability to anticipate future scene evolutions.
2. **Disjoint Latent Spaces for Future Prediction and Object Awareness:** When future prediction and object-aware reasoning are integrated, they are often learned in separate latent spaces. This separation can lead to computational inefficiencies and representational inconsistencies, requiring complex and indirect pipelines for combining these functionalities. Specifically, extracting object-level information from predicted future frames might involve costly decoding (e.g., from VAE latents to pixels) and subsequent re-encoding (e.g., by object detectors), introducing significant overhead.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the OFlow framework are:
- **Unify Temporal Foresight and Object-Aware Reasoning:** Integrate the prediction of future semantic states and the recognition of task-relevant objects into a single, shared semantic latent space. This aims to create a more direct and computationally efficient pipeline.
- **Enable Predictive Reasoning for Robust Manipulation:** Develop a system that can forecast future semantic states, allowing the robot to reason beyond immediate observations. This predictive capability is intended to facilitate more effective long-horizon planning, reduce myopic decision-making, and improve overall task performance, particularly in dynamic environments.
- **Enhance Robustness to Distribution Shifts:** By integrating object-aware temporal foresight, the aim is to develop a manipulation policy that exhibits improved robustness under various environmental changes, including visual perturbations, physical initial state shifts, and dynamic object behaviors.
- **Achieve Autonomous Object-Level Perception:** Leverage self-supervised visual representations and learning-free clustering to autonomously extract object-aware information, reducing reliance on explicit annotations or human assistance for target region specification.
- **Simplify Model Architecture:** Inspired by human manipulation's reliance on abstract semantics rather than pixel-level future imagination, the objective is to create a simplified model architecture that predicts future semantic states directly, emphasizing object-aware cues to produce stable temporal representations.

These objectives collectively aim to develop a more reliable and autonomous robotic manipulation system capable of handling complex, dynamic real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OFlow integrates semantic foresight with continuous action generation through a structured framework. The methodology involves multimodal perception, object-aware temporal flow matching for future prediction, and a controlled visuomotor policy for action generation.

#### 3.1. Multimodal Perception

The policy utilizes Eagle-2.5 as a Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone for understanding and reasoning with image and language instructions. Eagle-2.5 employs SigLIP to extract visual features, which are then projected via an MLP into the language space and aligned with Qwen2.5-VL for cross-modal reasoning. This VLM, noted for its tiling-based processing scheme and grounding ability, produces a fused feature ($\psi_t$) from the current image observation ($I_t$) and language instruction (p). This feature encodes environmental understanding and task goals, serving as a conditional input for action generation.
For temporal foresight, images are further processed by DINOv2, a self-supervised visual backbone. DINOv2 maps video sequences into a semantic latent space ($Z \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times C \times H \times W}$), which is chosen for its rich, high-level representations suitable for semantic understanding rather than mere compression.

#### 3.2. Object-Aware Temporal Flow Matching

This core module is responsible for predicting future semantic states and factorizing them into object-aware representations.

- **Semantic Latent Embeddings:** DINOv2 features are used as the semantic latent embeddings. These features inherently encode semantic organization, which is leveraged for object-aware reasoning.
- **Causally-Constrained Framewise Autoregression:** The temporal structure of videos is captured using an autoregressive decomposition of frame-wise latents ($Z = [z_1, z_2..., z_T]$). The conditional distribution $p(Z) = \prod_{i=1}^T p(z_i | z_{<i})$ ensures that predictions at time $t$ depend only on past frames. The backbone for this is a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) with causal attention over time and dense (full) attention within each frame. A specific mask enforces this causal constraint, allowing spatial tokens within a frame to be fully connected while preventing future-to-past information flow across frames.
- **Flow Matching for Temporal Extrapolation:** Future latents are predicted in the DINOv2 space using a temporal autoregressive flow matching model. For each frame, the conditional distribution $p(z_i | z_{<i})$ is modeled with a flow-matching objective, learning a neural network $v_\theta(x_t, t)$ to approximate the vector field from a noise distribution to the target distribution. To enable parallel training, independent noise levels are added to history frames. Future latents ($z_{i+1...i+M}$) are generated autoregressively by running a multi-step diffusion process conditioned on previously generated frames, with the final diffusion state sampled from the conditional distribution and appended to the history.
- **Object-Aware Scene Factorization:** To focus on physically relevant dynamics and reduce pixel-level noise, the predicted semantic latents are structured into object-aware representations. This is achieved by clustering patch-wise DINOv2 features into a small set of semantically coherent prototypes. Given feature embeddings $\{z_l\}_{1 \leq l \leq L}$ of a frame, K-means clustering is applied to obtain $K$ prototype tokens $C_K = \{c_1..., c_K\}$ that minimize intra-cluster variance. Each centroid $c_k$ represents an object-aware prototype. To capture multi-granularity semantics, a hierarchical representation $S = C_{K_1} \cup C_{K_2} \cup ... \cup C_{K_N}$ is constructed by varying the number of clusters, providing coarse-to-fine semantic abstractions.

#### 3.3. Controlled Visuomotor Policy Generation

- **Flow-Based Visuomotor Policy:** The action sequence is modeled using flow matching over action chunks ($A \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times D}$), where $L$ is the chunk length and $D$ is the degrees of freedom. The flow-matching loss (L$_{fm}(\theta)$) trains a network to generate actions conditioned on the visual-language feature $\psi$, the current robot state $q$, and the predicted object-aware features $S$. The action generation backbone is a DiT architecture with Adaptive LayerNorm for denoising.
- **ControlNet-style Conditioning:** Object features $S$ are integrated into the action generation process using a ControlNet-style mechanism. This involves adding an additional cross-attention layer initialized to zero, allowing the model to leverage pretrained VLA representations while incorporating the object-aware foresight. The query for this attention comes from the action token, while key/value pairs are derived from both the original visual-language features and the object features.

The training pipeline consists of two stages: Stage I pretrains the foresight model, and Stage II finetunes the VLA policy with the VLM and foresight model frozen.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of OFlow was conducted across four simulation benchmarks (LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, SimplerEnv, MetaWorld) and seven real-world manipulation tasks, comparing its performance against various VLA baselines.

#### 4.1. Simulation Benchmarks

- **LIBERO Benchmark:** OFlow achieved an overall success rate of 96.6%, the highest among the compared VLA models. It demonstrated strong performance across all four task suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), including a 94.5% success rate on the challenging LIBERO-Long suite, which requires long-horizon reasoning. This outcome suggests that the method effectively captures both temporal and object-aware cues.
- **LIBERO-Plus Benchmark:** On this benchmark designed to assess robustness and generalization under diverse perturbations, OFlow achieved the best overall success rate of 72.3%. It consistently outperformed baselines, including GR00T-N1.5, with an approximate 3% improvement in overall success rate and larger gains on specific perturbation types (e.g., +5.1% on Plus-Object, +5.9% on Robot Initial States, +4.8% on Sensor Noise). This robustness is attributed to semantic and object-aware foresight, which operates on stable DINOv2 semantic latents rather than brittle pixel-level predictions.
- **SimplerEnv Benchmark:** OFlow achieved an overall success rate of 67.1% on this benchmark for visual generalization with the WidowX robot. This result surpassed prior baselines, indicating improved robustness under real-to-sim domain shifts and visually diverse scenes, which is attributed to the object-aware scene decomposition.
- **MetaWorld Benchmark:** OFlow demonstrated the highest overall success rate of 85.6% on the MT50 suite of Sawyer-robot tasks, outperforming GR00T-N1.5 by 8.8% and TriVLA by 14.2%. Notable improvements were observed on tasks requiring temporal reasoning and precise contact, such as the "Kick a soccer into the goal" task, where OFlow achieved 60% success compared to 46% for GR00T-N1.5.

#### 4.2. Real-World Results

OFlow was evaluated on seven diverse real-world manipulation tasks, including dynamic object interaction, precise grasping and placement, handling bulky objects in clutter, stacking, and deformable object manipulation.
- OFlow achieved an average success rate of 69% across these tasks, which represented an 18% improvement over GR00T-N1.5 and a 28% improvement over π0.
- Significant gains were observed in tasks involving dynamic motion, such as "Pick the toy panda from the moving car and place it," where OFlow's success rate increased from approximately 20-25% (baselines) to 70%.
- Consistent improvements were also noted in tasks requiring precise placement ("Cup-Place") and cluttered grasping ("Cabbage-MW," "Apple-Pot"), multi-object stacking ("Cup-Stack"), and deformable materials ("Towel-Fold").
- On the "Receive an object handed over by a human" task, OFlow showed a 75% success rate compared to 45-50% for baselines, indicating improved temporal coordination.
- **Generalization and Robustness Tests:** Under real-world distribution shifts (background changes, unseen object instances, human perturbations), OFlow maintained a success rate of approximately 60% on the "Apple-Pot" task without fine-tuning, while baselines dropped below 40%. This resilience is attributed to its object-aware perception.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

Qualitative visualizations of future prediction in DINOv2 feature space, projected via PCA, showed temporally consistent changes. Reconstructions using RAE from these predicted features confirmed their decodability and semantic-level temporal consistency, supporting that the model forecasts reasonable trajectories.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Contribution of Components:** Ablation studies on the LIBERO-Plus benchmark indicated that while current-frame DINO features provided marginal gains, enabling foresight modeling with latent future prediction delivered the main performance improvements, particularly on Plus-Goal tasks. Combining object-aware decomposition with future prediction further enhanced robustness, suggesting complementary benefits.
- **Prediction Horizon:** Predicting a short future horizon improved performance, with success peaking at a prediction horizon of $M=4$ frames (69.2% on Plus-Goal) and slightly decreasing with longer horizons ($M=8$).
- **K-Means Clusters:** A hierarchical multi-scale clustering approach (using $K=\{1,2,4,8\}$ clusters) for object-aware grouping performed better than single-scale clustering, indicating the benefit of multi-granularity decomposition.
- **Denoising Steps:** The model, trained with 4 denoising steps, showed robustness under reduced denoising steps, with the matched setting ($T=4$) yielding the highest performance.

#### 4.5. Module Efficiency

The foresight model is comparatively lightweight, adding 0.2 billion parameters and 120 milliseconds for four-step future prediction, compared to the VLM backbone (1.6 billion parameters, 49 milliseconds) and action head (1.2 billion parameters, 207 milliseconds). The latency introduced by the foresight model is amortized over action chunks (16 actions per pass), maintaining an effective control frequency of approximately 30 Hz.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

OFlow represents a contribution to the field of robotic manipulation by addressing the limitations of reactive control and disjoint reasoning spaces in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Its significance lies in the successful integration of object-aware perception and temporal foresight within a unified semantic latent space.

The primary impact of this work is the **demonstrated enhancement in robustness and generalization** for robotic manipulation tasks. By enabling robots to predict future semantic states and reason about object interactions over time, OFlow allows for more proactive and less myopic decision-making. The extensive experimental validation across diverse simulation benchmarks (LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, SimplerEnv, MetaWorld) and challenging real-world tasks indicates consistent performance gains over established baselines, particularly in scenarios involving dynamic objects, visual perturbations, and varied task semantics. This improved robustness is a critical factor for the deployment of robotic systems in uncontrolled, real-world environments.

Furthermore, the methodology of using **semantic-level foresight with self-supervised visual features (DINOv2)** offers a more stable and potentially scalable approach compared to pixel-level prediction, which can be brittle to appearance noise. The **unsupervised object-aware scene factorization** via learning-free clustering provides a means to extract task-relevant information without requiring explicit object annotations or human intervention, thereby increasing autonomy.

The integration of these capabilities into a VLA framework, while maintaining compatibility with pretrained backbones through a ControlNet-style conditioning mechanism, contributes to the development of **more reliable and adaptable embodied intelligent agents**. This research positions semantic foresight and object-centric temporal reasoning as a promising direction for achieving higher levels of autonomy and success in complex robotic manipulation, potentially influencing future designs of generalist robot policies capable of advanced environmental reasoning.
