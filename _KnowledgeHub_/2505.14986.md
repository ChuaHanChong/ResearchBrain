# AnyBody: A Benchmark Suite for Cross-Embodiment Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Princeton University researchers developed "AnyBody," a benchmark suite for cross-embodiment manipulation, revealing that while multi-embodiment policies can achieve competitive performance on seen robots and interpolation tasks, they largely fail at zero-shot generalization to entirely novel or compositionally different robot structures. This highlights the limitations of current methods in achieving broad morphological adaptability for manipulation.

## Problem

- A lack of standardized, comprehensive benchmarks for systematically evaluating cross-embodiment learning in robotic manipulation tasks.
- The inability of current robotic control policies to generalize effectively to novel physical embodiments, requiring extensive retraining for each new robot.
- Existing large-scale datasets often confound morphological generalization with complex perception challenges, while simulation benchmarks typically feature limited robot morphological variation and focus on locomotion over manipulation.

## Method

- Introduced **AnyBody**, a benchmark suite within ==NVIDIA Isaac-Sim== featuring **18 diverse robot variations** (procedural and real-world) and two fundamental manipulation tasks (==reach== and ==push==).
- Defined clear generalization axes: ==interpolation== (geometric variations within a category), ==extrapolation== (fundamentally different link structures), and ==composition== (novel component combinations).
- Evaluated ==multi-embodiment (ME)== policies, including Transformer-based architectures, against single-embodiment (SE) baselines, incorporating stable RL techniques like ==symlog returns== and discrete action spaces.

## Results

- ME-Transformer agents achieved comparable or superior multi-task scores on training morphologies and demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization for interpolation tasks, often matching or surpassing SE baselines.
- For extrapolation and composition, ME agents showed low success rates (e.g., **0%** for `Arms-push` in extrapolation), lagging far behind SE baselines — a critical failure to generalize to truly novel morphologies.
- Adoption of `symlog` returns and discrete action spaces boosted ME-Transformer performance by **4×** multi-task and **2×** zero-shot for `Arm3 reach`, proving crucial for stable multi-embodiment learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-embodiment (ME) training can achieve comparable or even superior multi-task performance on *seen* robots compared to single-embodiment policies, benefiting from a regularizing effect leading to smoother learning.
> - Zero-shot generalization is feasible and strong for interpolation tasks, where agents adapt to geometric variations within known morphological categories.
> - However, zero-shot generalization remains a significant challenge for extrapolation and composition tasks, indicating that current methods struggle to reason about entirely novel link structures or novel combinations of components.
