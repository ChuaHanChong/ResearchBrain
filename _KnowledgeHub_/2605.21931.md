# EvoVid: Temporal-Centric Self-Evolution for Video Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> EvoVid introduces a self-evolving framework for Video Large Language Models, enabling them to improve video understanding and reasoning directly from raw, unannotated videos. The approach uses temporal-centric reward signals for question generation and answer grounding, yielding consistent performance gains across benchmarks, such as a 19.3% improvement on TempCompass for the Qwen2.5-VL-3B model, and outperforming supervised fine-tuning in some comparisons.

## Problem

- Video Large Language Models (Video-LLMs) heavily depend on costly, scalable, and human-expert-limited human-annotated data for training, particularly challenging for complex video content.
- Existing self-evolving frameworks, primarily for static modalities, often generate temporally insensitive questions due to generic reward signals that do not explicitly emphasize temporal dependencies.
- The absence of verifiable temporal-centric learning signals prevents Video-LLMs from autonomously learning sophisticated temporal order, causality, and state transitions from raw video data.

## Method

- A temporal-centric self-evolving framework that employs an iterative Questioner–Solver self-play mechanism, where a single base model learns directly from raw, unannotated videos.
- A temporal-aware Questioner reward (`r_Q_temp`) is introduced, which positively reinforces questions that require an understanding of temporal order by penalizing queries answerable from temporally perturbed video frames.
- A temporal-grounded Solver reward (`r_S_temp`) provides explicit temporal supervision, compelling the Solver to not only answer correctly but also localize the relevant temporal segments using automatically sampled K-frame windows as pseudo ground-truth.

## Results

- EvoVid demonstrated consistent performance gains across all four Qwen-VL base models and six video understanding benchmarks, achieving up to a **19.3%** improvement on TempCompass for the Qwen2.5-VL-3B model.
- The framework, using Qwen2.5-VL-7B, outperformed supervised fine-tuning (SFT) by an average of **1.0%** and achieved the best performance among all baselines on benchmarks like VideoMMMU, MMVU, and TempCompass.
- Ablation studies confirmed the positive contributions of both temporal-aware Questioner reward (+**0.7%** average gain) and temporal-grounded Solver reward (+**0.6%** average gain), with their combined effect leading to a **1.4%** increase over the VANILLA self-evolving baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly integrating temporal sensitivity into the reward functions of a self-evolving framework effectively guides Video-LLMs to acquire complex temporal reasoning skills without human annotation.
> - The co-evolution of a temporal-aware Questioner (generating more challenging and temporally dependent questions) and a temporal-grounded Solver (improving its ability to answer and temporally localize) leads to sustained performance enhancements.
> - The dual approach of using temporal perturbation for question generation and temporal segment grounding for answer verification are complementary strategies that foster autonomous temporal understanding in video models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Video Large Language Models (Video-LLMs) have demonstrated capabilities in video understanding and reasoning. These models often leverage reinforcement learning (RL) to improve their performance. However, a limitation of current RL-based Video-LLMs is their reliance on extensive human-annotated tasks and solutions. This dependency results in high annotation costs, limited scalability, and constraints imposed by human expertise. The video domain presents a particular challenge for annotation due to the complex interplay of spatial variance and temporal causality.

To address the limitations of human supervision, self-evolving frameworks have emerged as an alternative paradigm. These frameworks, primarily developed for Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), operate through iterative Questioner–Solver self-play. In this setup, training tasks and learning signals are generated intrinsically during the RL process, reducing or eliminating the need for external annotations. While effective for static modalities such as text and images, existing self-evolving methods typically generate generic questions that can often be answered from a single frame or static visual cues. This is largely due to generic reward signals (e.g., difficulty and diversity) that do not explicitly emphasize temporal dependencies, leading to temporally insensitive self-evolution.

EvoVid positions itself within this landscape by addressing the absence of temporal dynamics in existing self-evolving frameworks for multimodal models. The work proposes a method for Video-LLMs to learn directly from raw, unannotated videos by incorporating temporal-centric verifiable learning signals. This approach aims to bridge the gap between the success of self-evolving paradigms in static modalities and the unique requirements for understanding dynamic video content, thereby offering a scalable and autonomous learning solution for video reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable Video-LLMs to autonomously improve their video understanding and reasoning capabilities directly from raw, unannotated videos, without relying on human-annotated tasks or solutions. This overarching goal is driven by two main motivations and addressed through specific technical objectives:

**Motivation 1: The Scalability and Expertise Bottleneck of Human Annotation in Video-LLMs.**
- Current Video-LLMs, despite their advancements in video reasoning via reinforcement learning, fundamentally depend on human-annotated data to construct verifiable reward signals. This reliance incurs significant costs, limits the scalability of training datasets, and constrains the models' abilities to generalize beyond the scope of human expertise.
- The challenge is particularly pronounced in the video domain, where the intricate nature of spatial variance and temporal causality makes exhaustive and high-quality annotation exceptionally difficult and resource-intensive. This bottleneck hinders the development of more robust and broadly capable Video-LLMs.

**Motivation 2: The Temporal Insensitivity of Existing Self-Evolving Frameworks.**
- Self-evolving models, which use Questioner–Solver self-play to generate their own training tasks and learning signals, offer a promising alternative to human supervision. However, current implementations of these frameworks are predominantly designed for static modalities like text and images.
- When directly applied to video, these "VANILLA" self-evolving methods often produce questions that can be answered based on single frames or static visual attributes. The generic reward structures (e.g., for difficulty or diversity) in these frameworks provide insufficient incentive for the Questioner to generate queries that require understanding temporal dependencies. Consequently, the Solver also remains temporally insensitive, failing to learn reasoning over temporal order, causality, or state transitions inherent in video. This indicates a fundamental limitation: the lack of temporal-centric and verifiable learning signals explicitly exploiting the dynamic nature of video.

**Key Objectives to Address These Motivations:**
To overcome these limitations, EvoVid aims to introduce temporal-centric mechanisms into the self-evolving paradigm:

1. **Develop a Temporal-Aware Questioner:** Design a reward signal that encourages the Questioner to generate questions requiring an understanding of temporal dependencies. The specific objective is to make question generation sensitive to the correct temporal ordering of frames, thereby moving beyond static or single-frame-answerable queries.
2. **Develop a Temporal-Grounded Solver:** Introduce a mechanism to provide explicit temporal supervision to the Solver without human annotations. The objective is for the Solver to not only answer questions correctly but also to localize the relevant temporal segments within the video, fostering grounded reasoning.
3. **Formulate a Co-Evolutionary Process:** Integrate these temporal-centric rewards into an iterative Questioner–Solver self-play loop, allowing a single base model to progressively acquire stronger temporal reasoning capabilities directly from raw, unannotated videos.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EvoVid proposes a temporal-centric self-evolving framework for Video-LLMs that operates through an iterative Questioner–Solver self-play mechanism. A single pretrained base model alternates between the Questioner and Solver roles, optimized by novel temporal-centric rewards. The framework aims to eliminate reliance on human-annotated data for video reasoning improvement.

#### 3.1. Self-evolving Framework Preliminaries

The foundation is a Questioner (πQ) that generates questions (q) given an unlabeled video (v), and a Solver (πS) that produces multiple candidate answers (ˆam) for a given video and question. A pseudo target answer (a\*) is derived by majority voting among the Solver's candidates.

- **Solver Confidence (s(v,q))**: Measures the empirical agreement rate of the Solver's M candidates with the majority-voted answer.
- **Preliminary Questioner Reward (rQ)**: Composed of a difficulty term (rQdiff), which favors questions of moderate difficulty (s(v,q) neither too high nor too low), and a diversity penalty (rQdiv), which discourages semantically similar questions within a batch. The final preliminary reward is `rQ = max(0, rQ diff - rQ div)`.
- **Preliminary Solver Reward (rS)**: Consists of an answer correctness term (acc ∈ {0,1}) and a format term (fmt ∈ {0,1}), weighted by `w`. `rS = (1 - w) acc + w fmt`.
- **Optimization**: Both Questioner and Solver are optimized using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), which normalizes rewards within each batch and incorporates a KL regularization term.

#### 3.2. Temporal-aware Questioner

This component addresses the temporal insensitivity of preliminary Questioner rewards.

- **Motivation**: Meaningful video questions should inherently depend on the correct temporal ordering of frames.
- **Mechanism**: For a generated question (q), the Solver's confidence is evaluated on two versions of the video: the original video (v) and a temporally perturbed version (˜v). The perturbed video is created by applying a permutation (σ) to the frame sequence, disrupting the temporal order.
- **Temporal-aware Questioner Reward (rQtemp)**: Defined as `max(0, s(v,q) - s(˜v,q))`. This reward provides a positive signal to the Questioner when the Solver's confidence significantly degrades on the temporally perturbed video compared to the original. This encourages the Questioner to generate questions that require temporal understanding.
- **Final Questioner Reward**: The `rQ temp` is added to the preliminary `rQ ` with a weighting factor `λq `: `rQ total = rQ
- λqrQ temp`. This guides the Questioner to generate queries sensitive to temporal structure, such as action sequences and event relations.

#### 3.3. Temporal-grounded Solver

This component provides explicit temporal supervision to the Solver.

- **Motivation**: Preliminary Solver rewards only focus on answer correctness, not on localizing supporting evidence within the video's temporal structure.
- **Mechanism**: During the phase where questions are generated for Solver training, the Questioner is provided with a *contiguous K-frame window* sampled from the full video (v). This sampled window, denoted as `W = [ts, te]`, automatically serves as a pseudo ground-truth temporal segment for the question generated within that context. The Solver is then required to predict both the answer and a temporal segment (`W_hat = [ˆts, ˆte]`) from the *full video*.
- **Temporal-grounded Solver Reward (rStemp)**: Calculated as the Intersection-over-Union (IoU) between the Solver's predicted segment (`W_hat`) and the sampled window (`W`): `rS temp = IoU(W_hat, W)`. This reward is applied only if the Solver's predicted answer is correct.
- **Final Solver Reward**: The `rS temp` is added to the preliminary `rS ` with a weighting factor `λs `: `rS total = rS
- λsrS temp`. This incentivizes the Solver to learn temporally localized reasoning without requiring human-annotated timestamps.

#### 3.4. Training and Implementation Details

- **Training Data**: A diverse collection of 5,778 raw, unannotated videos from training splits of datasets like LLaVA-Video-178K, STAR, CLEVRER, PerceptionTest, NeXT-QA, and Video-Holmes. No annotations from these datasets are used.
- **Base Models**: Four Qwen-VL models are used as backbones: Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B-Instruct and Qwen3-VL-4B/8B-Instruct.
- **Fine-tuning**: LoRA fine-tuning is applied to both Questioner and Solver.
- **Input**: Limited to 16 frames with a resolution of 128x14x14.
- **Co-evolution Process**: Consists of three iterative rounds, with each phase (Questioner or Solver training) running for 20 steps.
- **Hyperparameters**: `λq = 0.1` for temporal-aware Questioner reward, `λs = 0.3` for temporal-grounded Solver reward, and a grounding window of `K = 8` frames.
- **Solver Dataset Construction**: Only samples with Solver confidence in a specific range ([0.3, 0.8]) are retained to form a balanced training curriculum, filtering out questions that are too difficult or too trivial.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

EvoVid's effectiveness was evaluated through extensive experiments across multiple base models and video benchmarks, with consistent improvements reported.

#### 4.1. Consistent Performance Improvements

- **Across Models and Benchmarks:** EvoVid demonstrated consistent performance gains across all four base MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B-Instruct, Qwen3-VL-4B/8B-Instruct) and six video understanding benchmarks (Video-Holmes, VSI-Bench, VideoMMMU, MMVU, TempCompass, VideoMME). These improvements occurred despite the absence of human-curated questions or annotations.
- **Significant Gains on Challenging Benchmarks:** The Qwen2.5-VL-3B model, a relatively weaker initialization, showed the most substantial improvements on temporally challenging benchmarks, including TempCompass (+19.3%), VideoMMMU (+6.1%), and MMVU (+6.1%).
- **Alignment with Design Motivation:** Across all model scales, larger improvements were consistently observed on video reasoning benchmarks that demand temporal understanding, aligning with the framework's design to optimize for temporally grounded reward signals.

#### 4.2. Comparison with Supervised Baselines and VANILLA Self-evolving Baseline

- **Outperforming SFT:** EvoVid, based on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, surpassed supervised fine-tuning (SFT) by an average of +1.0%, where SFT was trained on a human-curated dataset (Video-R1-CoT-165k).
- **Competitive with Supervised RL:** While SFT + VANILLA GRPO achieved a 2.3% improvement over SFT, EvoVid achieved a 2.1% improvement over the base model *without* any human annotations.
- **Superior to VANILLA Self-evolving:** EvoVid improved the average performance from 48.6% (VANILLA self-evolving baseline) to 50.0%, achieving the best results across all evaluated datasets in this comparison.
- **Leading on Specific Benchmarks:** EvoVid achieved the best performance among all baselines (including supervised methods) on VideoMMMU, MMVU, and TempCompass, highlighting the efficacy of its temporal-centric rewards.
- **Enhancing Pretrained Models:** When initialized with an SFT checkpoint, EvoVid achieved performance competitive with the SFT + VANILLA GRPO baseline, but with significantly fewer RL training steps. This indicates the framework's potential to further enhance pretrained Video-LLMs efficiently.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Reward Contributions:** Both the temporal-aware Questioner reward (`rQtemp `) and the temporal-grounded Solver reward (`rStemp `) contributed positively to performance. `rQtemp ` alone yielded a +0.7% average gain over the VANILLA Self-evolving baseline, while `rStemp ` alone resulted in a +0.6% gain. Combining both rewards led to a +1.4% increase over the baseline, suggesting complementary benefits from their distinct roles in optimizing different temporal aspects.
- **Shuffle Strategy:** Temporal perturbation strategies (random shuffle, reverse-order frames, block-wise random shuffle) consistently outperformed the no-shuffle baseline. Random shuffle and block shuffle yielded stronger gains, supporting the effectiveness of thorough temporal disruption for encouraging sensitivity.
- **Window Size for Solver Reward:** Performance remained stable for moderate window sizes (K={4, 8, 12}), with K=8 (default) showing robust performance. A drop in performance at K=16 (full video) indicated that the benefit comes from informative temporal segment supervision rather than merely introducing a segment output.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Analysis of Generated Questions

- Word cloud visualizations revealed a lexical shift in the questions generated by EvoVid's temporal-aware Questioner compared to the VANILLA baseline. EvoVid's questions showed a richer vocabulary related to temporal and reasoning concepts (e.g., "primary," "frame," "consider," "change," "reason," "interaction"). This indicates successful guidance toward generating more temporally dependent and reasoning-intensive queries. Detailed keyword frequency analysis in the appendix further supports this, showing increased usage of terms like "sequence," "primary," "change," "after," and "before."

#### 4.5. Iteration Scaling and Question Evolution

- **Iteration Scaling:** Most larger backbones (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Qwen3-VL-4B, Qwen3-VL-8B) demonstrated a generally stable increasing performance trend across extended training iterations (up to 6), indicating sustained benefits from self-evolution. The Qwen2.5-VL-3B model exhibited some instability in later iterations, potentially due to its limited capacity.
- **Question Evolution:** Solver accuracy decreased substantially when evaluated on questions generated by the Questioner from later iterations (e.g., from 33.0% on D
Iter 1
to 13.5% on D
Iter 3
for the base model). This suggests that the Questioner progressively constructs a more challenging curriculum. Concurrently, each successive Solver consistently outperformed its predecessor on these evolving questions, demonstrating joint co-evolution and improved Solver capability alongside increased question difficulty.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work on EvoVid presents a significant development in the field of Video Large Language Models, particularly in addressing the challenges of scalability and temporal understanding.

**New Paradigm for Video-LLMs:** EvoVid introduces a temporal-centric self-evolving framework, which represents a new paradigm for Video-LLMs. This approach allows models to improve their video understanding and reasoning capabilities directly from raw, unannotated video data, without requiring extensive human supervision. This offers a scalable and autonomous learning method, potentially reducing the prohibitive costs and labor associated with manual video annotation.

#### 5.1. Addressing Key Limitations

- **Annotation Bottleneck:** The framework directly addresses the scalability and resource bottleneck posed by the reliance on human-annotated tasks and solutions for training Video-LLMs. By generating learning signals intrinsically, EvoVid enables continuous improvement on diverse video data without external labels.
- **Temporal Insensitivity:** It overcomes the limitation of existing self-evolving frameworks, which are often designed for static modalities and fail to adequately capture the temporal dynamics crucial for video reasoning. The introduction of temporal-aware Questioner and temporal-grounded Solver rewards ensures that the self-play process explicitly promotes temporal sensitivity and causality in generated questions and learned reasoning.

**Enhanced Temporal Reasoning:** By explicitly integrating temporal perturbation sensitivity and temporal grounding supervision, EvoVid enables Video-LLMs to progressively acquire stronger temporal reasoning abilities. This leads to models that can better understand action sequences, event relations, and state transitions, moving beyond merely recognizing static visual attributes.

**Efficiency and Competitiveness:** The experimental results indicate that EvoVid achieves competitive performance with supervised methods, and in some cases surpasses them, while relying on significantly less human effort and data. When initialized with supervised checkpoints, EvoVid can further enhance performance with fewer additional training steps, indicating an efficient pathway for model improvement.

#### 5.2. Broader Implications and Future Directions

- This research suggests a pathway for developing more robust and generalizable Video-LLMs that can adapt to a wider range of real-world video content.
- The methodology could potentially be extended to other dynamic or time-series data modalities where human annotation is challenging.
- Future work could explore extending the framework to handle longer videos, model more complex fine-grained spatial understanding, and incorporate multi-event dependency modeling.

**Potential Broader Impacts:** As a methodological research paper, the immediate social impacts are indirect. However, like other large language models trained on vast datasets, EvoVid may inherit or amplify biases present in the raw internet video data or the pretrained base models. This could potentially lead to unfair or unreliable behavior when applied across different environments, populations, or specific use cases. Researchers should consider these potential biases in future applications and deployments.
