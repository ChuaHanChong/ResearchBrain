# WOMBET: World Model-based Experience Transfer for Robust and Sample-efficient Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> WOMBET introduces a reinforcement learning framework that combines uncertainty-aware world model-based experience generation from a source task with adaptive online fine-tuning for a target task. This method enhances sample efficiency and robustness, achieving comparable or superior performance on continuous control benchmarks using less than half the interaction budget of online baselines.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) in real-world robotics is limited by the expense and safety risks of extensive data collection.
- Existing offline-to-online RL methods typically assume the prior availability of a high-quality, fixed offline dataset, which is often unrealistic.
- Model-Based RL (MBRL) data generation can suffer from model bias and over-optimism, leading to unreliable or non-diverse synthetic trajectories for transfer.

## Method

- **WOMBET** actively constructs a reliable offline dataset from a source task using ==uncertainty-aware world models== and ==planning with an uncertainty penalty==.
- A ==dual-criterion filtering== process selects high-return and low-uncertainty trajectories, which are then relabeled with the target ==reward function==.
- Policy optimization for the target task utilizes an ==adaptive sampling strategy== that dynamically mixes the generated offline data with online interaction data, coupled with ==implicit regularization== techniques for stable learning.

## Results

- WOMBET consistently achieved faster learning and higher asymptotic returns, using less than **half** the interaction budget compared to online RL baselines (SAC, PPO, TD3) on MuJoCo benchmarks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both adaptive sampling and dual-criterion filtering are necessary components, with their absence leading to reduced performance.
- The framework outperformed state-of-the-art offline-to-online RL methods in sample efficiency and asymptotic returns when all were fine-tuned on the same WOMBET-generated offline dataset.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generating a reliable offline dataset from a source task using uncertainty-aware planning, rather than relying on pre-existing data, is critical for effective experience transfer.
> - A dual-criterion filtering process, incorporating both high reward and low model uncertainty, is essential to curate offline trajectories that are both relevant and robust for subsequent learning.
> - Dynamically balancing the use of generated offline data and new online experience through adaptive sampling is crucial for mitigating distributional shifts and ensuring stable, sample-efficient fine-tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has demonstrated capabilities in various domains, but its application to real-world robotics often encounters limitations related to the expense and safety risks associated with data collection. This constraint motivates research into experience transfer, where knowledge gained from a source task is leveraged to improve learning efficiency and robustness in a distinct but related target task.

Existing paradigms partially address this challenge, each with its own limitations. **Online RL** requires extensive interaction with the environment and is susceptible to distributional shifts between source and target experiences. **Offline RL**, while mitigating unsafe exploration by learning from pre-collected data, can experience performance degradation when the available data is limited or mismatched with the target task, often necessitating high-quality expert demonstrations. **Offline-to-online RL** attempts to combine the strengths of both by pretraining policies offline and then fine-tuning them online. However, a common assumption in this paradigm is the prior availability of a high-quality, fixed offline dataset, without addressing the critical step of how to reliably generate or safely reuse such data.

**Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL)** offers a method for data generation by learning a predictive world model. Approaches within MBRL include planning-based methods, which use the world model for control through Model Predictive Control (MPC), and Dyna-style methods, which generate synthetic rollouts for policy learning. Despite their potential, these MBRL approaches also face limitations in the context of experience transfer. MPC-based methods may generate trajectories that lack diversity due to their exploitative nature, while model-generated data, if not carefully managed, can introduce bias and lead to unreliable policies. Consequently, previous methods frequently struggle to provide data that is both reliable and adaptable for effective transfer.

This work, WOMBET, addresses the gap by proposing a unified framework that *jointly* generates and utilizes prior data. It focuses on developing a method for constructing reliable offline data from a source task using uncertainty-aware world models and then facilitating its adaptive use for online fine-tuning in a target task. This places WOMBET in the intersection of MBRL, offline RL, and offline-to-online RL, specifically targeting the challenge of principled data generation and robust transfer in scenarios where real-world interaction is costly.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the sample efficiency and robustness of Reinforcement Learning in robotic applications by enabling effective experience transfer. The authors identify a significant limitation in existing offline-to-online RL methodologies: the assumption of a readily available, high-quality, and fixed offline dataset. This assumption is often unrealistic in real-world robotics, where generating such reliable data can be as challenging as learning the target task itself.

The core motivation behind WOMBET stems from the need to overcome this limitation by developing a framework that not only *utilizes* prior data but also *generates* it in a reliable and transferable manner. The authors are motivated by observations in biological systems, which continually reuse past experiences via internal world models, suggesting a path for artificial agents.

Specifically, the key objectives driving the WOMBET framework are:

- **To address the problem of generating transferable experience:** Instead of relying on a pre-existing dataset, WOMBET aims to actively construct a high-quality offline dataset from a source task. This involves learning a world model and employing uncertainty-aware planning to create synthetic trajectories.
- **To ensure the reliability of generated data:** The world model, while powerful, can be prone to bias and over-optimism. A critical objective is to control this inherent unreliability by introducing mechanisms like uncertainty-penalized planning and a dual-criterion filtering process to select only the most reliable and high-return trajectories. This ensures that the generated offline data provides a robust foundation for subsequent learning.
- **To enable a stable and efficient transition from offline pre-training to online fine-tuning:** The framework seeks to integrate the strengths of model-based offline data generation with model-free online adaptation. This involves developing an adaptive sampling strategy that dynamically balances the use of offline and online data, thereby mitigating distributional shifts and maintaining stable learning as the agent transitions to interacting with the target environment.
- **To provide theoretical understanding and guarantees:** The research aims to mathematically establish that the proposed uncertainty-penalized planning objective maximizes a lower bound on the true return. Furthermore, it seeks to derive a finite-sample error decomposition that accounts for both distribution mismatch between source and target data and approximation errors in the learned value functions.
- **To empirically demonstrate improved performance:** A practical objective is to validate the effectiveness of WOMBET through empirical evaluations on continuous control benchmarks, demonstrating superior sample efficiency and final performance compared to existing online, offline, and offline-to-online RL baselines.

By pursuing these objectives, the authors aim to provide a more comprehensive solution for real-world robotic RL, reducing the dependence on extensive real-world interaction and facilitating robust learning across related tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WOMBET operates as a unified framework for World Model-based Experience Transfer, coupling uncertainty-aware model-based offline data generation with online fine-tuning through an iterative process of model and policy co-evolution. The methodology can be broken down into several interconnected components:

#### 3.1. World Model-based Data Generation in the Source Task

The initial phase involves constructing an offline dataset, $D_S$, from a source task. Unlike methods that assume a given dataset, WOMBET actively generates $D_S$.
- **Probabilistic Ensemble World Model:** A probabilistic ensemble of models, $\hat{P}_S = \{ \hat{P}^{(i)}_S \}_{i=1}^E$, is trained to approximate the true transition dynamics $P(s'|s,a)$ of the source task.
- **Model Predictive Control (MPC):** This ensemble model is used within an MPC framework to optimize short-horizon action sequences. The goal is to maximize the expected sum of rewards under the learned model over a planning horizon $H$.

#### 3.2. Robust Planning with an Uncertainty Penalty

To ensure the reliability of trajectories generated by the world model, which can be prone to bias, WOMBET introduces an uncertainty penalty during MPC.
- **Uncertainty-Penalized Reward:** The planning objective is modified to maximize a penalized reward function, $\tilde{r}(s,a) = r_S(s,a) - \lambda u(s,a)$, where $u(s,a)$ represents the epistemic uncertainty of the model (e.g., ensemble predictive variance), and $\lambda > 0$ is a scaling factor.
- **Provable Lower Bound:** The paper demonstrates that maximizing this penalized return under the learned model $\hat{P}$ corresponds to maximizing a certified lower bound on the true return under the real dynamics $P$. This derivation relies on the Lipschitz continuity of the value function and bounding the one-step model bias using the Wasserstein distance between the true and learned transition kernels, which is, in turn, bounded by the ensemble uncertainty $u(s,a)$. This mechanism intrinsically balances reward exploitation under the learned model with avoidance of high-uncertainty regions.

#### 3.3. Dual-Criterion Filtering for Reliable Offline Data

After generating trajectories using the uncertainty-penalized MPC, a filtering step is applied to curate a high-quality $D_S$.
- **Dual Criteria:** A trajectory $\tau$ is accepted into $D_S$ only if it satisfies two conditions:
 - Its mean epistemic uncertainty over the trajectory, $\bar{u}(\tau)$, is below a threshold $u_{th}$.
 - Its total return, $J(\tau)$, is above a threshold $J_{th}$.
- **Relabeling:** Accepted rollouts are then relabeled with the target reward function $r_T(s,a)$ to align them with the target task objective.
- **Iterative Refinement:** This entire process is iterative; as online data $D_T$ is collected in the target task, the world model $\hat{P}$ is continuously refined using the aggregated dataset $D = D_S \cup D_T$. This refinement progressively improves predictive accuracy and expands the region where reliable planning and data generation can occur.

#### 3.4. Mixed Data Training in the Target Task

With the curated $D_S$ and online data $D_T$, policy optimization for the target task proceeds using a mixture of both.
- **Adaptive Sampling:** Batches for training are sampled from a mixture distribution $D_{mix} = \alpha D_S + (1-\alpha)D_T$. The mixing coefficient $\alpha \in$ is adaptive, balancing the influence of offline and online data.
- **Ensemble Critic Training:** An ensemble of Q-critics, $\{Q_\phi^i\}_{i=1}^N$, is trained using a robust Bellman target: $T_\pi Q_{\bar{\phi}}(s,a) = r_T(s,a) - I_{s \in D_S} \lambda u(s,a) + \gamma E_{a' \sim \pi(\cdot|s')}[\min_i Q_{\bar{\phi}}^i(s',a')]$. This target incorporates the uncertainty penalty for offline samples and uses the ensemble minimum for pessimism, reducing overestimation. Layer Normalization (LayerNorm) is applied within the critics to stabilize training.
- **Actor Optimization:** The actor policy $\pi_\phi$ is trained to maximize the conservative value estimated by the ensemble minimum: $L_{actor} = -E_{s \sim D_{mix}}[\min_i Q_\phi^i(s, \pi_\phi(s))]$.

#### 3.5. Bounding Extrapolation Error via Implicit Regularization

WOMBET mitigates extrapolation error on out-of-distribution (OOD) states and actions, which typically causes Q-value overestimation, through implicit regularization.
- **Architectural Bounding (LayerNorm):** LayerNorm layers within the critic network bound the activations, which in turn constrains the magnitude of Q-values. This prevents value explosion even for OOD inputs.
- **Algorithmic Robustness (Ensemble Minimum):** Using the minimum Q-value from an ensemble of critics for the Bellman target introduces pessimism. This implicitly regularizes extrapolated values, keeping them conservative, especially in regions of high critic disagreement.

#### 3.6. Mitigating Distributional Shift with Adaptive Sampling

To address the global shift in state-action distribution as the policy evolves online, WOMBET employs an adaptive sampling mechanism.
- **Dynamic Mixing Coefficient:** The mixing ratio $\alpha_k$ is dynamically adjusted at each iteration $k$.
- **TD Error-based Adaptation:** The coefficient $\alpha_k$ is determined by the mean absolute Temporal Difference (TD) error $\bar{\delta}_T^{(k)}$ on recent online data. When TD error is high (indicating underfitting or instability), $\alpha_k$ increases, leveraging more stable offline samples. As TD error decreases (indicating better adaptation), $\alpha_k$ reduces, emphasizing online experience to reduce bias. This approximation aims to minimize an upper bound on the critic's generalization error.

#### 3.7. Robust Surrogate Optimization and Performance Guarantee

The framework provides a theoretical performance guarantee by decomposing the total estimation error into two components:
- **Distribution Mismatch:** The difference between the true target value function $Q_{\pi_T}$ and the ideal value function under the mixed distribution $Q_{\pi_{mix}}$. This term is reduced by the dual-criterion filtering of $D_S$ and by decreasing the mixing weight $\alpha_k$ as online data accumulates, diminishing the influence of source-specific samples.
- **Finite-Sample Approximation Error:** The difference between $Q_{\pi_{mix}}$ and the learned critic $\hat{Q}$. This is mitigated by adaptive sampling, which balances bias and variance, and by implicit regularization (LayerNorm and ensemble-min targets), which improves generalization.
Combining these, the paper presents a bound on the suboptimality of the WOMBET policy, demonstrating its ability to achieve conservative yet asymptotically consistent transfer.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of WOMBET was conducted on various MuJoCo continuous control benchmarks, focusing on sample efficiency, the necessity of fine-tuning, the effectiveness of experience transfer, and the contribution of core components.

#### 4.1. Sample Efficiency: WOMBET vs. Online RL

The experiments compared WOMBET against standard online RL algorithms, including SAC, PPO, and TD3, all trained from scratch on the target task.
- **Finding:** WOMBET consistently demonstrated faster learning and achieved higher asymptotic returns across multiple environments. It attained comparable or superior final returns using less than half the interaction budget of the online baselines.
- **Interpretation:** This improvement is attributed to the strong prior provided by the filtered offline dataset ($D_S$), generated through uncertainty-aware planning and filtering. This initialization allowed WOMBET to focus on refinement rather than extensive initial exploration, which is characteristic of uninformed online exploration.

#### 4.2. Necessity of Online Fine-tuning: WOMBET vs. Offline RL

This set of experiments assessed the contribution of online fine-tuning by comparing WOMBET with several offline-only methods (BC, IQL, CQL, MOPO, COMBO). These baselines were trained solely on the model-generated dataset $D_S$ and evaluated in a zero-shot transfer setting without further interaction in the target environment.
- **Finding:** Offline-only methods performed well when the source and target tasks were closely aligned but showed significant degradation under even moderate shifts between tasks.
- **Interpretation:** While $D_S$ is reliable, it inherently reflects source dynamics and rewards. Without online updates, policies could not adapt to new reward functions or unseen state-action regions of the target task. WOMBET's continuous refinement of both policy and model through online interaction enabled it to adapt beyond the support of the initial offline data, highlighting that the benefits arise from both effective pretraining and subsequent adaptation.

#### 4.3. Effectiveness of Experience Transfer: WOMBET vs. Offline-to-Online Baselines

WOMBET was compared against state-of-the-art offline-to-online RL methods. All baselines were pretrained offline on the *same* WOMBET-generated dataset $D_S$ and then fine-tuned online in the target environment, thereby isolating the effect of the transfer mechanism.
- **Finding:** WOMBET consistently achieved higher sample efficiency and asymptotic returns than all other offline-to-online baselines.
- **Interpretation:** This superior performance was attributed to WOMBET's integrated transfer design. The dual-criterion filtering provided a reliable initialization, and the adaptive sampling strategy facilitated a smooth transition from offline to online learning by maintaining a balanced bias-variance trade-off. Conventional pretrain-finetune pipelines, in contrast, often experienced abrupt distributional shifts, leading to unstable or inefficient online exploration.

#### 4.4. Importance of WOMBET’s Core Components for Offline Data Generation

Ablation studies were conducted to evaluate the individual contributions of adaptive sampling and dual-criterion filtering.
- **Adaptive Sampling (Figure 5):** The full WOMBET method with its adaptive mixing rule ($\alpha_k$ adjusted by TD error) was compared to a fixed-ratio variant ($\alpha_k = 0.5$).
 - **Finding:** The adaptive rule learned faster and achieved higher final returns, while the fixed schedule was stable but less efficient.
 - **Interpretation:** The adaptive rule dynamically shifted weight between offline and online data, maintaining an optimal bias-variance balance crucial for continuous improvement.
- **Dual-Criterion Filtering (Figure 6):** The full filtering rule (high reward and low uncertainty) was compared to variants with only reward-based filtering, only uncertainty-based filtering, or no filtering.
 - **Finding:** All degraded variants resulted in reduced performance. Reward-only filtering included unreliable samples, uncertainty-only filtering was overly conservative, and no filtering amplified model bias.
 - **Interpretation:** The dual-criterion filter was found to be essential for producing trajectories that are simultaneously reliable and relevant to the task, supporting stable transfer and data-efficient fine-tuning. These components were complementary and necessary for the observed performance gains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The WOMBET framework presents a methodical approach to experience transfer in reinforcement learning, particularly addressing challenges in robotic applications where data acquisition is a significant constraint. Its significance lies in several aspects:

Firstly, WOMBET shifts the paradigm for offline-to-online RL by actively **generating transferable experience** from a source task, rather than assuming the existence of a pre-collected, high-quality offline dataset. This contribution directly addresses a fundamental practical limitation in real-world scenarios. By providing a principled method for data creation, it expands the applicability of offline learning techniques to contexts where expert demonstrations or extensive prior data are unavailable.

Secondly, the framework introduces a **unified and iterative methodology** that couples conservative, uncertainty-aware model-based data generation with robust, model-free online fine-tuning. This integration mitigates the inherent model bias typically associated with MBRL by employing an uncertainty penalty and dual-criterion filtering during data generation. This ensures that the generated offline data ($D_S$) is not only high-return but also reliable, forming a solid foundation for subsequent learning.

Thirdly, the **adaptive sampling strategy** and **implicit regularization techniques** (LayerNorm and ensemble minimum) employed during online fine-tuning contribute to the stability and efficiency of knowledge transfer. These mechanisms effectively manage distributional shifts and prevent extrapolation errors, facilitating a smooth and robust transition from prior-driven initialization to task-specific adaptation using real-world interactions. This balance between leveraging prior knowledge and adapting to new environmental specifics is crucial for practical robotic deployment.

Fourthly, the **theoretical contributions**—including the derivation of a provable lower bound on the true return through uncertainty-penalized planning and a finite-sample error decomposition—provide a deeper understanding of the framework's guarantees and limitations. These theoretical underpinnings strengthen the claims of robustness and reliability.

The **potential impact** of WOMBET is substantial for various domains, especially robotics. By significantly improving sample efficiency, it can reduce the substantial cost, time, and safety risks associated with training robotic agents. This could enable faster deployment of RL solutions in complex, safety-critical environments where extensive real-world interaction is impractical, such as autonomous navigation, manipulation, and human-robot collaboration. Furthermore, the iterative refinement of the world model and policy, capable of expanding its reliable planning region, suggests a pathway toward more autonomous and continuously learning systems that can adapt to evolving environments or task requirements with reduced human intervention. The ability to systematically generate and transfer reliable experience positions WOMBET as a foundational step toward more practical and widely applicable reinforcement learning systems.
