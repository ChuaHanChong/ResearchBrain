# CordViP: Correspondence-based Visuomotor Policy for Dexterous Manipulation in Real-World

> [!summary] Summary
> Peking University researchers developed CordViP, a visuomotor policy for dexterous robotic manipulation that addresses challenges in 3D perception by generating robust, 'interaction-aware' point clouds using 6D pose estimation and digital twins. The policy explicitly learns fine-grained contact and coordination correspondences between the hand and objects, achieving over 85% success rates on various real-world manipulation tasks while demonstrating superior data efficiency and generalization to unseen objects and varying conditions.

## Problem

- Single-view RGB-D cameras provide noisy, incomplete, and occlusion-prone 3D representations, especially for multi-fingered dexterous hands that frequently occlude manipulated objects.
- Existing 3D-based imitation learning methods often lack fine-grained contact information and explicit spatial correspondences between the object and the dexterous hand, critical for precise manipulation.
- Achieving robust, generalizable, and data-efficient dexterous manipulation in real-world scenarios remains challenging due to the variability and complexity of physical interactions.

## Method

- A framework called **CordViP** is proposed that generates 'interaction-aware' 3D point clouds by leveraging robust 6D object pose estimation (using FoundationPose with SAM), digital twin generation (TripoSR), and robot forward kinematics to overcome single-view camera limitations.
- An encoder network is pre-trained to learn fine-grained interaction dynamics by synthesizing object-centric contact maps between the hand and object, and modeling hand-arm coordination using cross-attention transformers.
- The pre-trained encoder is integrated into a conditional denoising diffusion policy that takes these enhanced features as conditions to predict robot actions, learning a visuomotor policy through imitation.

## Results

- CordViP achieved over **85%** completion rate across four base tasks (PickPlace, FlipCup, Assembly, ArtiManip) and strong performance on advanced contact-rich tasks (e.g., **80%** on FlipCap) using a Leap Hand on a UR5 arm, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines.
- The method demonstrated superior sample efficiency, achieving higher accuracy with fewer demonstrations, and strong generalization across different lighting conditions, visual scenarios, unseen objects, and camera viewpoints.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical importance of contact pre-training for contact-rich tasks and coordination pre-training for tasks requiring continuous hand-arm motion, with the full CordViP model yielding the highest success rates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning explicit spatial and temporal correspondences, such as contact maps between the hand and object and coordination between the hand and arm, is crucial for robust dexterous manipulation.
> - Robust 3D object pose estimation from a single view, combined with digital twin technology and robot kinematics, can generate high-quality, 'interaction-aware' point clouds that are complete and resilient to occlusions.
> - Pre-training an encoder with tasks focused on contact prediction and hand-arm coordination enhances the learned features, making the subsequent diffusion policy more effective and sample-efficient for complex manipulation.
