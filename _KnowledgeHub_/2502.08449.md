# CordViP: Correspondence-based Visuomotor Policy for Dexterous Manipulation in Real-World

> [!summary] Summary
> Peking University researchers developed CordViP, a visuomotor policy for dexterous robotic manipulation that addresses challenges in 3D perception by generating robust, 'interaction-aware' point clouds using 6D pose estimation and digital twins. The policy explicitly learns fine-grained contact and coordination correspondences between the hand and objects, achieving over 85% success rates on various real-world manipulation tasks while demonstrating superior data efficiency and generalization to unseen objects and varying conditions.

## Problem

- Single-view RGB-D cameras provide noisy, incomplete, and occlusion-prone 3D representations, especially for multi-fingered dexterous hands that frequently occlude manipulated objects.
- Existing 3D-based imitation learning methods often lack fine-grained contact information and explicit spatial correspondences between the object and the dexterous hand, critical for precise manipulation.
- Achieving robust, generalizable, and data-efficient dexterous manipulation in real-world scenarios remains challenging due to the variability and complexity of physical interactions.

## Method

- A framework called **CordViP** is proposed that generates 'interaction-aware' 3D point clouds by leveraging robust 6D object pose estimation (using FoundationPose with SAM), digital twin generation (TripoSR), and robot forward kinematics to overcome single-view camera limitations.
- An encoder network is pre-trained to learn fine-grained interaction dynamics by synthesizing object-centric contact maps between the hand and object, and modeling hand-arm coordination using cross-attention transformers.
- The pre-trained encoder is integrated into a conditional denoising diffusion policy that takes these enhanced features as conditions to predict robot actions, learning a visuomotor policy through imitation.

## Results

- CordViP achieved over **85%** completion rate across four base tasks (PickPlace, FlipCup, Assembly, ArtiManip) and strong performance on advanced contact-rich tasks (e.g., **80%** on FlipCap) using a Leap Hand on a UR5 arm, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines.
- The method demonstrated superior sample efficiency, achieving higher accuracy with fewer demonstrations, and strong generalization across different lighting conditions, visual scenarios, unseen objects, and camera viewpoints.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical importance of contact pre-training for contact-rich tasks and coordination pre-training for tasks requiring continuous hand-arm motion, with the full CordViP model yielding the highest success rates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning explicit spatial and temporal correspondences, such as contact maps between the hand and object and coordination between the hand and arm, is crucial for robust dexterous manipulation.
> - Robust 3D object pose estimation from a single view, combined with digital twin technology and robot kinematics, can generate high-quality, 'interaction-aware' point clouds that are complete and resilient to occlusions.
> - Pre-training an encoder with tasks focused on contact prediction and hand-arm coordination enhances the learned features, making the subsequent diffusion policy more effective and sample-efficient for complex manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Achieving human-level dexterity in robotic manipulation, particularly with multi-fingered hands, is a long-standing and challenging goal in robotics. This paper positions itself within the field of **imitation learning (IL)**, where robots learn complex behaviors by observing human demonstrations.

Traditionally, IL approaches have relied on 2D image-based representations. While effective for some tasks, these methods often struggle with fine-grained dexterous manipulation dueasing to their inability to fully capture intricate 3D spatial relationships and being susceptible to occlusions inherent in manipulation tasks. This limitation has led to a growing interest in **3D-based imitation learning**, which utilizes representations like point clouds or voxels to provide a more holistic understanding of the environment.

However, existing 3D IL methods face their own set of challenges in real-world scenarios. A primary issue is the **quality and completeness of 3D representations** obtained from single-view RGB-D cameras. Factors such as camera resolution, positioning, lighting, and especially occlusions caused by the robot's own body (e.g., a dexterous hand) can lead to noisy, incomplete, or geometrically inaccurate point clouds. Furthermore, global 3D point clouds often lack crucial information about direct physical contact and fine-grained spatial correspondences between the manipulated object and the robot's hand, which are vital for precise dexterous tasks. Some prior works have explored tactile sensors, but these often come with high costs and stability issues.

CordViP directly addresses these critical limitations. Instead of solely relying on raw, potentially noisy, or incomplete single-view point clouds, it leverages **robust 6D pose estimation and digital twin technologies** to generate more complete and "interaction-aware" 3D observations. More importantly, it introduces **correspondence learning** by explicitly modeling contact information between the hand and object, and coordination between the hand and arm. This focus on "spatial and temporal consistency" and "interaction-aware point clouds" differentiates CordViP from prior 3D IL works that might treat point clouds as general geometric features without explicitly encoding the crucial contact and coordination dynamics. By doing so, CordViP aims to push the boundaries of real-world dexterous manipulation towards greater robustness, generalization, and data efficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and generalizable visuomotor policy that enables dexterous manipulation in real-world scenarios, approaching human-level dexterity. The authors identify two key problems motivating their work:

1. **Quality of Single-View 3D Representations:** Point clouds captured by a single-view RGB-D camera are significantly affected by practical real-world factors. These include limitations in camera resolution, sub-optimal camera positioning, and, critically, **occlusions caused by the multi-fingered dexterous hand** during manipulation. Such occlusions lead to a loss of spatial information about the object and obscure crucial contact details necessary for precise actions.
2. **Lack of Crucial Contact and Spatial Correspondence Information:** Traditional global point cloud representations often fail to capture fine-grained contact information and explicit spatial correspondences between the object and the dexterous hand. This granular information is essential for complex, fine-motor tasks that require precise interaction.

To eliminate these limitations, the paper proposes **CordViP**, a novel framework focused on constructing and learning correspondences by leveraging robust 6D pose estimation of objects and robot proprioception. The ultimate motivation is to bridge the gap between impressive simulation results and the challenges of deploying dexterous manipulation policies in the unpredictable real world, especially with minimal expert demonstrations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CordViP operates through a meticulously designed three-phase framework:

#### 3.1. Interaction-aware Generation of 3D Point Clouds

This phase aims to overcome the limitations of single-view RGB-D cameras by creating high-quality, complete, and robust 3D observations:

- **Real-to-Sim for Digital Twin Generation:** To obtain a complete static point cloud of the manipulated object, the authors employ a 3D generation technique based on TripoSR. This method leverages strong priors and a broad understanding of 3D visual concepts to generate accurate 3D digital assets (digital twins) from a single-view image. These digital twins ensure geometric and material consistency with real-world objects, providing a robust initial 3D observation.
- **Pose-Driven Point Cloud Tracking:** To track the object's point cloud in dynamic, occluded real-world environments, CordViP uses foundation models for precise 6D pose estimation. Specifically, the Segment Anything Model (SAM) extracts the object mask, which, combined with the digital twin, enables FoundationPose to estimate the object's 6D pose accurately. This pose is then used to transform the initial 3D point cloud, aligning it to the desired coordinate frame, thus maintaining an accurate representation even under severe occlusions.
- **Robot Point Cloud Forward Kinematics Model:** For the dexterous hand's 3D representation, the authors develop a robot point cloud forward kinematics model ($F_{pc}$). This model parses the robot's URDF file, samples points on each link's surface, and maps any joint configuration to the corresponding 3D pose of the hand's point cloud. By focusing on the hand and ignoring the arm for this specific representation, the system emphasizes crucial interaction areas. This process operates at a sufficient frequency (8Hz) for real-time inference (5Hz).

The result of this phase is "interaction-aware point clouds" – a combination of the object's tracked point cloud and the hand's point cloud, which are complete, robust to viewpoint changes, and less affected by occlusions.

#### 3.2. Contact and Coordination-Enhanced Feature Extraction

This phase focuses on pre-training an encoder network to learn fine-grained interaction dynamics from large-scale play data:

- **Contact Map Synthesis:** A critical component is the object-centric contact map ($C$), which quantifies the interaction between the hand and the object. This is computed as a normalized distance from each object surface point to the hand surface, incorporating the object's surface normal. A sigmoid function transforms this distance into a contact value between 0 and 1.
- **Point Cloud Feature Extraction:** Two identical PointNet encoders are used to extract embeddings for the object ($f_{\theta_O}(P_{obj})$) and hand ($f_{\theta_H}(P_{hand})$) point clouds. To establish correspondences, two multi-head cross-attention transformers ($g_{\theta_O}$, $g_{\theta_H}$) fuse these embeddings, producing aligned representations ($\phi_R$, $\phi_O$). A three-layer MLP is trained to predict the object-centric contact map ($C_{pred}$) from these fused point cloud observations, minimizing the MSE loss between $C_{pred}$ and $C$. This forces the encoder to learn implicit contact information.
- **Hand-Arm Coordination Enhancement:** To capture cooperative motion, the arm and hand states are projected into uniform-dimensional vectors. The same cross-attention transformers are then used to establish correspondences between the hand and arm states. The pre-training task here involves predicting the robot arm's action sequence based on the point clouds and hand state, and vice versa for the hand's action sequence using arm state. MSE loss is applied to this reconstruction task, enabling the encoder to learn the collaborative dynamics.

The overall pre-training objective minimizes a combined loss of contact map prediction and action sequence reconstruction, effectively teaching the encoder spatial interaction and temporal coordination.

#### 3.3. Correspondence-based Diffusion Policy

In the final phase, the pre-trained encoder is integrated into an imitation learning framework:

- **Conditional Denoising Diffusion Model:** The core policy is a conditional denoising diffusion model (similar to DDPM). The pre-trained encoder extracts semantic features ( $\phi_{H,O}$ and $\psi_{A,H}$) from the 3D visual input (interaction-aware point clouds) and robot joint states. These features, which encapsulate spatial consistency, contact dynamics, and collaborative information, serve as conditions for the diffusion model.
- **Action Prediction:** The denoising network ($\epsilon_{\theta}$) iteratively denoises a Gaussian noise sample ($A_K$) into a noise-free action ($A_0$), conditioned on the extracted features. The DDIM scheduler is used for faster inference. The training objective is to predict the noise added to the original action data (MSE loss). The key innovation here is that the encoder, implicitly containing contact and coordination information learned during pre-training, guides the policy's understanding of spatial relationships, and is fine-tuned during downstream task training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

CordViP demonstrates significant performance improvements across six real-world dexterous manipulation tasks using a Leap Hand on a UR5 arm, evaluated against state-of-the-art baselines.

#### 4.1. Effectiveness

- **Superior Performance:** CordViP achieves over 85% completion rate across four base tasks (PickPlace, FlipCup, Assembly, ArtiManip), significantly outperforming all other baselines (Table I). It also shows strong performance on advanced contact-rich and fine-grained tasks like FlipCap and LongHoriManip (Table II), achieving 80% success on FlipCap and 100%/100%/70%/60% on the LongHoriManip subtasks (Pull, Pick, Place, Push) respectively.
- **Baseline Comparison:** Image-based policies (BCRNN, ACT, DP) struggle with complex tasks and occlusions. 3D-based variants (ACT+3D) show improvement, highlighting the importance of geometric information. State-based policies perform poorly due to lack of geometric detail. G3Flow, another 3D-based method leveraging semantic flow, is the strongest baseline but is still surpassed by CordViP, especially in tasks requiring fine correspondence. The authors also note that DP3, despite being a 3D diffusion policy, underperforms due to sensitivity to point cloud quality, unlike CordViP's robustness.
- **Inference Efficiency:** CordViP achieves an efficient inference speed of 12.84 FPS, slightly higher than DP3's 11.79 FPS, indicating low computational overhead despite enhanced performance.

#### 4.2. Efficiency

- **Sample Efficiency:** CordViP demonstrates superior sample efficiency (Figure 7). It achieves higher accuracy with fewer demonstrations compared to ACT, DP, and DP3. Notably, with just 10 demonstrations, CordViP maintains a high success rate, showcasing its ability to learn effectively from limited data by leveraging correspondences and geometric features.

#### 4.3. Generalization

CordViP exhibits exceptional generalization capabilities across various real-world perturbations:
- **Different Lighting Conditions:** CordViP shows robustness to dim, white, and colored lighting conditions (Table III), maintaining high success rates. This is attributed to its reliance on FoundationPose for 6D tracking, which integrates multi-modal information and is inherently robust to lighting changes, unlike image-based policies.
- **Different Scenarios:** The method generalizes well to diverse scenarios, including varying visual appearances (Scene-1, Scene-2) and challenging cluttered environments (Table IV). This is because its interaction-aware point clouds, based on robust 6D pose estimation, allow it to focus effectively on the manipulated subjects and objects.
- **Unseen Objects:** CordViP demonstrates strong generalization to unseen objects (varying in color, shape, and dynamics) in PickPlace and FlipCup tasks (Table V), making fine-grained adjustments based on established spatial and temporal correspondences.
- **Different Viewpoints:** Despite being trained with a fixed camera, CordViP exhibits robust performance under significant variations in camera perspectives (Table VI). This is a strong point, as 3D-based methods often struggle when synthesized point clouds capture only partial information from a single viewpoint. CordViP's use of comprehensive 3D priors for view-agnostic interaction-aware point cloud generation is key here.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Effectiveness of Components (Table VII):**
 - Merging interaction-aware point clouds into DP3 significantly improves its performance, indicating DP3's sensitivity to point cloud quality and CordViP's superior 3D representation.
 - Removing contact pre-training significantly reduces success rates, especially for contact-rich tasks like FlipCup, highlighting its critical role.
 - Removing coordination pre-training also degrades performance, particularly for tasks requiring continuous hand-arm motion like Assembly and ArtiManip, emphasizing the importance of collaborative dynamics.
 - The full CordViP model with both contact and coordination pre-training achieves the highest average success rate.
- **Choice of Point Cloud Encoders (Table VIII):** PointNet (the chosen encoder) outperforms PointNet++ and PointNeXt. Fine-tuning the PointNet encoder during downstream task training further enhances performance compared to freezing it, indicating that learned features are beneficial.

#### 4.5. Transferability

- **Backbone Agnostic:** CordViP's approach (interaction-aware point clouds and correspondence learning) is transferable. It achieves excellent performance on both diffusion-based and Transformer-based (CVAE) backbones (Table IX), showcasing its versatility.

#### 4.6. Failure Case Analysis

While highly effective, CordViP has limitations. In some FlipCup failures, the hand hovers due to suboptimal solutions learned from slight jitters in teleoperated demonstrations. However, the model can also auto-correct, as seen in the Assembly task where it recovered from initial insertion failures by adjusting the cup's orientation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

CordViP represents a significant advancement in real-world dexterous robotic manipulation, particularly for fine-grained and contact-rich tasks. Its core contributions and potential impact are:

1. **Robust 3D Perception in Real-World Scenarios:** By introducing a pipeline that combines digital twin generation (via TripoSR), robust 6D object pose estimation (FoundationPose with SAM), and robot proprioception, CordViP effectively generates "interaction-aware point clouds." This addresses a critical long-standing challenge: obtaining complete and accurate 3D observations in the presence of severe occlusions (e.g., from the multi-fingered hand) and varying environmental conditions. This ability to consistently perceive the manipulation scene is foundational for reliable robotic operation.
2. **Correspondence-Based Learning for Dexterity:** The proposal of CordViP, a visuomotor policy that explicitly leverages contact maps and hand-arm coordination information during pre-training, is a key innovation. This allows the policy to learn fine-grained spatial and temporal correspondences between the robot's end-effector and the object. This deep understanding of interaction dynamics is crucial for achieving high levels of dexterity that go beyond simple pick-and-place tasks, enabling complex operations like flipping, assembling, and articulated object manipulation.
3. **High Effectiveness, Efficiency, and Generalization:** The experimental results unequivocally demonstrate CordViP's superior performance compared to state-of-the-art 2D and 3D baselines across diverse real-world tasks. Its remarkable data efficiency (learning from few demonstrations) and strong generalization capabilities to different lighting, scenarios, unseen objects, and camera viewpoints significantly reduce the practical barriers to deploying dexterous robots. This implies less manual engineering, faster skill acquisition, and broader applicability.
4. **Bridging Sim-to-Real Challenges:** By using robust pose estimation to "correct" real-world point cloud imperfections, CordViP indirectly mitigates some sim-to-real transfer challenges. The digital twin generation also facilitates a tighter coupling between real and virtual environments.

**Potential Impact:**
This work has the potential to significantly impact the development of truly agile and adaptable robotic systems. Robots equipped with CordViP-like policies could perform a wider array of tasks in unstructured environments, from industrial assembly of complex parts to domestic tasks requiring delicate object handling. It advances the state-of-the-art in visuomotor control by providing a robust framework for learning dexterous skills that are otherwise difficult or impossible with current methods.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
Despite its strengths, the authors acknowledge several limitations:
- **Deformable Objects:** The current method struggles with accurately estimating the 6D pose of deformable objects, as FoundationPose's expressive capacity is limited for non-rigid geometry.
- **Digital Twin Accuracy:** The accuracy of the initial digital twin modeling significantly impacts pose tracking and the quality of the initial object point clouds.
- **Complete Occlusion:** If the dexterous hand completely occludes the object, FoundationPose may fail to track it.
Future work could explore visuotactile 6D pose tracking to overcome the challenge of complete visual occlusion and extend the framework to handle deformable objects. The robust and generalizable nature of CordViP, even with these limitations, marks a substantial step forward in robotic dexterity.
