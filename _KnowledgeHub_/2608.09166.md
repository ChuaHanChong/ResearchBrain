# Particle-Based Conformal Prediction for Contact-Aware Uncertainty Calibration in Stratified Configuration Spaces

> [!summary] Summary
> CaPTURe builds conformal prediction regions over a robot's next configuration in contact-rich tasks. It samples particles from an approximate dynamics model, scores them by k-nearest-neighbor distance, and calibrates separate thresholds per Mondrian group defined by state, action, and the future configuration's stratum via a regression decision tree. Regions stay inside feasible strata, hold 90% coverage in every contact mode, and lift task success up to 30 points.

## Problem

- Conformal prediction in robotics usually supplies one global uncertainty bound, ignoring how prediction error varies with state, action, and nearby contact geometry.
- Gaussian or hyperellipsoidal belief representations cannot express post-contact distributions that are multimodal, disjoint, or collapsed onto lower-dimensional contact manifolds.
- Prediction regions measured with ambient configuration-space distances leak into obstacle-occupied infeasible regions, wasting volume and making uncertainty-aware planners needlessly conservative.

## Method

- ==Particle-based nonconformity score==: sample L particles from an approximate dynamics model, then score the true next configuration by its distance to the ==k-th nearest particle==, a local density proxy.
- ==Stratum-aware Mondrian grouping==: a ==LOCART== regression decision tree partitions state, action, and future stratum index into leaves of near-constant error, each receiving its own ==split-conformal threshold==.
- Region construction unions per-stratum sets: for each candidate stratum keep only configurations inside it scoring below that threshold, yielding trans-dimensional obstacle-free regions consumed by an ==MPPI planner==.

## Results

- Marble labyrinth over **427,680** test transitions: CaPTURe holds the **90%** target in every stratum (**90.0** free space, **90.2** edge, **90.6** corner) while PCP falls to **76.4%** in free space.
- Tight-tolerance peg insertion succeeds **78%** versus **48%** for PCP, LUCCa, and the no-stratum ablation and **14%** uncalibrated, finishing in **28.7** steps versus **38** to **43**.
- Ablations confirm both design choices: dropping the stratum label undercovers free space at **84.9%**, and k equal to **1** scoring keeps coverage but collapses planning success to **20%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Marginal coverage hides regime-specific failure: one global bound can undercover free space while over-covering contact manifolds, so condition calibration on the interaction regime.
> - Valid coverage does not imply useful planning; smoother regions from mid-rank particle scoring optimize better than tighter, particle-sensitive ones despite identical guarantees.
> - Encoding known geometric structure into the prediction set, not the model, keeps uncertainty off infeasible states without weakening distribution-free finite-sample guarantees.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The research addresses the critical challenge of representing and managing uncertainty in autonomous systems, particularly robots operating in dynamic, contact-rich environments. Such environments—ranging from navigating cluttered spaces to precise assembly tasks—require robots to accurately reason about how uncertainty, arising from both inherent stochasticity (aleatoric) and model inaccuracies (epistemic), changes during physical interactions.

Existing approaches to planning under uncertainty in robotics often rely on parametric distributions, such as Gaussians, to model predictive uncertainty. However, in contact-rich scenarios, the distribution of future robot configurations can become multimodal, discontinuous, or lie on lower-dimensional manifolds, which these parametric models may not accurately represent. Particle-based representations offer a more flexible approach to capturing such complex, trans-dimensional uncertainty.

Conformal Prediction (CP) is a distribution-free framework that provides finite-sample probabilistic coverage guarantees for prediction regions, without strong assumptions on the underlying model or data distribution. While CP has been applied in various robotic contexts—including safety filters, warning systems, and state estimation—prior applications in robotics have frequently provided a single global uncertainty bound. This global bound often fails to account for the heterogeneous nature of uncertainty, which can vary significantly across a robot's configuration, action space, and local environmental geometry. Specifically, existing local CP methods that adapt uncertainty bounds based on state-action pairs often rely on parametric models and do not explicitly consider the feasibility of prediction regions within the configuration space (C-space) or how uncertainty changes across different contact regimes.

This work integrates and extends several established concepts to address these limitations. It builds upon Mondrian Conformal Prediction (MondrianCP), which partitions data to provide group-conditional coverage guarantees, thereby enabling adaptive prediction regions. It also leverages Probabilistic Conformal Prediction (PCP), which extends CP to handle particle-based predictive models. Furthermore, it incorporates the concept of stratified configuration spaces, which explicitly models the geometric structure induced by contacts, representing C-space as a collection of manifolds of varying intrinsic dimensions. While planning in stratified C-spaces has been explored to improve planning robustness, these methods typically assume sufficiently accurate dynamics models.

By combining particle-based predictive models, MondrianCP, and the geometric representation of stratified C-spaces, this research proposes a novel method for generating *contact-aware, probabilistically valid, trans-dimensional uncertainty estimates*. This advancement allows for calibrated uncertainty regions that are guaranteed to contain the true future configuration at a specified probability, adapt to local dynamics and contact conditions, and intrinsically respect the physical constraints of the robot's environment. This positions the work as a contribution to robust and safe robotic autonomy in complex interaction scenarios, bridging the gap between rigorous uncertainty quantification and practical application in contact-rich robotics.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable the safe and efficient deployment of autonomous systems that interact with their environment by providing reliable and calibrated representations of uncertainty. This is motivated by several challenges inherent in robotic operations within contact-rich settings.

Firstly, autonomous systems require robust uncertainty representation because robots must reason about how uncertainty—stemming from both stochastic elements (aleatoric uncertainty) and model inaccuracies (epistemic uncertainty)—is influenced by interactions with obstacles. For example, during navigation in cluttered environments or assembly tasks like part insertion, contact events can drastically alter the distribution of a robot's future configurations.

Secondly, current analytical or learned motion models often exhibit inaccuracies. These inaccuracies can arise from limited training data, simplifying assumptions, unmodeled physical effects (e.g., stiction, friction coefficients), or the inherent unreliability of learned models when operating outside their training distribution. Planning with such approximate models can lead to unsafe executions or task failures, particularly as uncertainties compound over time in dynamic settings. Therefore, there is a clear need for calibrated uncertainty estimates that can support reliable planning and control.

Thirdly, the nature of uncertainty itself changes significantly during contact. When a robot contacts an obstacle, the distribution of its future configurations can become multimodal (having multiple probable outcomes), disjoint (separated into distinct regions), or lie along manifolds of lower intrinsic dimension than the full configuration space. Standard uncertainty representations, such as symmetric parametric distributions (e.g., Gaussians), are generally insufficient to capture these complex, trans-dimensional characteristics. Prediction regions that span infeasible C-space regions (e.g., occupied by obstacles) are not only volume-inefficient but also lead to overly conservative or uninformative plans, hindering a planner's ability to reason about safety effectively.

To address these motivations, the research establishes several key objectives:
- To propose an algorithm, Calibrated Particle-sets for Trans-dimensional Uncertainty Representation (CaPTURe), which is geometry-aware and conformal prediction-based. This algorithm aims to generate probabilistically valid prediction regions for unknown future system configurations using particle-based models of arbitrary fidelity.
- To develop a calibration procedure that captures how motion uncertainty varies between contact-rich and contactless motions, ensuring sufficient coverage in both scenarios.
- To construct one-step prediction regions that are guaranteed to contain the true unknown future robot configuration with at least a user-specified probability (1-α), despite both epistemic and aleatoric uncertainties.
- To provide finite-sample, stratum-aware, group-conditional guarantees for these prediction regions, validated using an approximate dynamics model and a finite calibration dataset.
- To ensure that the constructed prediction regions are feasible, meaning they do not assign probability mass to physically impossible configurations (i.e., they are contained within the robot's feasible C-space, C_feas).
- To demonstrate the practical utility of this approach for safe and efficient motion planning in simulated contact-rich tasks under significant uncertainty and model mismatch. This includes ensuring the prediction regions are as tight (volume-efficient) and adaptive as possible while maintaining coverage guarantees.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The proposed method, Calibrated Particle-sets for Trans-dimensional Uncertainty Representation (CaPTURe), integrates concepts from conformal prediction, particle-based dynamics modeling, and stratified configuration spaces to construct probabilistically valid, contact-aware prediction regions.

**Configuration Space Formulation:**
The planning problem is formulated within the robot's Configuration Space (C-space), where a robot's configuration is a point and motion is a path. Crucially, the feasible C-space ($C_{feas}$) is treated as a finite *stratified space*, which is a disjoint union of smooth submanifolds (strata $S_m$) of differing intrinsic dimensions. This formalization allows the method to inherently respect environmental constraints (e.g., obstacles) and represent trans-dimensional uncertainty (e.g., motion in free space vs. sliding along a surface). A known *stratum indexer* $T: C_{feas} \to M$ maps any configuration to its corresponding stratum index.

**Uncertainty Quantification with Conformal Prediction:**
CaPTURe builds on three variants of conformal prediction:
1. **Split Conformal Prediction (SplitCP)**: This foundational method uses a calibration dataset ($D_{cal}$) to compute a single nonconformity score threshold ($\hat{q}$). This threshold guarantees that a prediction region $\hat{C}(X_{n+1})$ (defined as all outputs $y$ whose nonconformity score $r(\hat{f}(X_{n+1}), y)$ is below $\hat{q}$) will contain the true unknown output $Y_{n+1}$ with a probability of at least $1-\alpha$.
2. **Probabilistic Conformal Prediction (PCP)**: This extends SplitCP to scenarios where the predictive model ($\hat{f}$) returns samples (particles) from an approximate conditional distribution $\hat{p}(Y|X)$, rather than a single point prediction. The nonconformity score $r$ is defined as the distance between the true output $Y_i$ and its $k_{NN}$-th nearest neighbor among the $L$ particles sampled from $\hat{f}(X_i)$. This allows for calibrated prediction sets using non-parametric distributional representations, suitable for multimodal or discontinuous dynamics.
3. **Mondrian Conformal Prediction (MondrianCP)**: This method enhances SplitCP by partitioning examples into disjoint groups ($J$) using a grouping function $g$. A separate conformal threshold ($\hat{q}_j$) is computed for each group, providing *group-conditional coverage guarantees* ($P\{Y_{n+1} \in \hat{C}(X_{n+1}) | g(X_{n+1}, Y_{n+1}) = j\} \ge 1-\alpha$). This enables the prediction regions to be adaptive, varying in size and shape based on the local uncertainty characteristics of each group.

**CaPTURe's Specific Implementation (Algorithm 1 & 2):**

1. **Nonconformity Score**: For each calibration transition $(X_i, Y_i)$, $L$ particles ($\hat{Y}_i$) are sampled from the approximate dynamics model $\hat{f}(X_i)$. The nonconformity score $R_i$ is then computed as the distance from $Y_i$ to its $k_{NN}$-th closest particle in $\hat{Y}_i$. The paper uses $k_{NN} = L/2$ for empirical reasons, aiming for smoother prediction regions.

2. **Grouping Function ($g$)**: To achieve adaptive and contact-aware prediction regions, the grouping function $g$ is designed to partition examples based on the current state-action pair ($X$) and the *predicted future C-space stratum* ($T(Y)$). This choice allows the calibration to differentiate between distinct contact modes and their associated uncertainty profiles.

3. **Offline Calibration (Algorithm 1, Figure 1)**:
 - The raw calibration dataset $D_{cal}$ is augmented with the computed nonconformity scores $R_i$ and the stratum index $T(Y_i)$ for each transition, forming $\bar{D}_{cal} = \{(X_i, T(Y_i), R_i)\}$.
 - $\bar{D}_{cal}$ is split into two disjoint sets: $\bar{D}_{part\_cal}$ (for partitioning) and $\bar{D}_{cp\_cal}$ (for conformal threshold calculation).
 - A regression decision tree (DTree) is trained on $\bar{D}_{part\_cal}$. The DTree takes $(X_i, T(Y_i))$ as input and $R_i$ as the target, partitioning the joint $X \times M$ space into leaf nodes (groups) where prediction scores are approximately constant. This DTree *defines* the grouping function $g$.
 - For each group $j$ (leaf node) defined by the DTree, a separate conformal threshold $\hat{q}_j$ is computed using the $R_i$ scores from $\bar{D}_{cp\_cal}$ examples that fall into that group, following the SplitCP procedure. This ensures exchangeability and preserves coverage guarantees.

4. **Online Prediction Region Construction (Algorithm 2, Figure 2)**:
 - At inference time, given a current state-action pair $X_\tau$ and particles $\hat{Y}_{\tau+1}$ from $\hat{f}(X_\tau)$, CaPTURe constructs the prediction region $\hat{C}_{\tau+1}$.
 - Since the future stratum $T(Y_{\tau+1})$ is unknown, the method considers *all candidate future strata* $m \in M$.
 - For each $m$, the DTree is queried with $(X_\tau, m)$ to determine the appropriate group $j_m$ and its corresponding threshold $\hat{q}_{j_m}$.
 - A stratum-specific prediction region $\hat{C}^{(m)}_{\tau+1} = \{y \in S_m : r(y, \hat{Y}_{\tau+1}) \le \hat{q}_{j_m}\}$ is then constructed within that stratum $S_m$.
 - The final prediction region $\hat{C}_{\tau+1}$ is the union of all such stratum-specific regions: $\hat{C}_{\tau+1} = \bigcup_{m \in M} \hat{C}^{(m)}_{\tau+1}$.

**Theoretical Guarantees:**
- **Theorem 5**: Proves that CaPTURe provides valid *group-conditional coverage* for each group $j$, meaning $P\{Y_{n+1} \in \hat{C}(X_{n+1}) | g(X_{n+1}, T(Y_{n+1})) = j\} \ge 1-\alpha$. This relies on the exchangeability property (Lemma 4) and the fixed, data-independent nature of the grouping function.
- **Corollary 6**: Demonstrates that the union of group-specific regions over all candidate output strata yields the desired *marginal coverage guarantee* $P\{Y_{n+1} \in \hat{C}(X_{n+1})\} \ge 1-\alpha$. Furthermore, by design, since each stratum $S_m$ is within the feasible C-space, the resulting $\hat{C}$ is guaranteed to be physically feasible.

**Integration with Motion Planning**:
CaPTURe's prediction regions are incorporated into a Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework, specifically using Model Predictive Path Integral Control (MPPI). The prediction regions are used to inform the objective function, penalizing unsafe configurations within the predicted set and guiding the planner to prioritize safe and efficient trajectories that account for the quantified uncertainty. The paper notes that while single-step guarantees are formal, the multi-step application in MPC is heuristic, as exchangeability does not hold recursively across rollout steps.

### 4. Main findings and results

The research evaluates CaPTURe's performance on two simulated contact-rich robotic tasks: controlling a marble in a labyrinth and performing tight-tolerance peg-in-hole insertion with a manipulator. The evaluation focuses on numerical coverage validation and downstream probabilistic motion planning performance, comparing CaPTURe against several baselines.

**Numerical Coverage Validation:**
For both tasks, CaPTURe was validated against an uncalibrated particle-based planner (ParticleNoCP), a local CP method for Gaussian models (LUCCa), Probabilistic CP (PCP), and an ablation of CaPTURe without stratum-aware partitioning (Ablation w/o stratum label). The user-specified coverage requirement ($\alpha = 0.1$, so $90\%$ coverage) was used for all methods.

- **Marble Labyrinth Control:**
 - **Coverage**: CaPTURe (with $k_{NN}=L/2=8$) achieved the target 90% coverage *per stratum* (Free Space, Edge, and Corner), demonstrating its ability to adapt and provide sufficient coverage across distinct contact modes. In contrast, baselines (LUCCa, PCP, Ablation w/o stratum label) achieved marginal coverage but often significantly *undercovered in free space* (e.g., LUCCa at 83.4%, PCP at 76.4%) while being *overconservative in lower-dimensional strata* (e.g., PCP at 96.4% on Edges, 99.0% on Corners).
 - **Volume Efficiency**: CaPTURe's prediction regions were competitive in terms of C-space volume, with the $k_{NN}=8$ variant being slightly more volume-efficient than its $k_{NN}=1$ ablation. Particle-based methods generally produced smaller prediction regions than LUCCa, which outputs hyperellipsoids.
- **Tight-Tolerance Peg Insertion:**
 - **Coverage**: CaPTURe (with $k_{NN}=L/2=4$) was the only method that achieved both marginal coverage and sufficient coverage *within each reported contact category* (Free Space, Point Contact, Edge Contact). Other methods, including LUCCa, PCP, and the stratum-agnostic ablation, exhibited undercoverage in Free Space (e.g., 83.8% for LUCCa, 83.1% for PCP).
 - **Volume Efficiency**: CaPTURe maintained competitive C-space volume ratios, similar to the marble task.

These results indicate that CaPTURe's stratum-aware approach effectively quantifies and adapts to heterogeneous uncertainty across different interaction regimes, overcoming the limitations of methods that do not explicitly consider the C-space's stratified structure.

**Probabilistic Motion Planning Performance:**
The validated prediction regions were used in an MPPI-based planner, where the objective function balanced task progress and safety by penalizing unsafe regions within the predicted set.

- **Marble Labyrinth Control:**
 - CaPTURe ($k_{NN}=8$) consistently achieved the highest task success rates across the six tested maze sections, demonstrating an *absolute improvement of up to 30%* over the best baseline (e.g., 90% success rate for the "Center" section compared to 57% for the Ablation w/o stratum label).
 - Baselines often struggled with navigation, leading to failures (marble falling into pits or timing out), particularly in sections requiring precise maneuvers or exhibiting significant model mismatch. ParticleNoCP could accumulate too much momentum, while LUCCa was often overconservative due to its hyperellipsoidal, geometry-ignorant prediction regions.
 - The $k_{NN}=1$ ablation, despite achieving adequate coverage, exhibited degraded planning performance compared to $k_{NN}=8$, suggesting that smoother prediction regions (produced by $k_{NN}=8$) are more amenable to optimization by the MPPI controller.
- **Tight-Tolerance Peg Insertion:**
 - CaPTURe ($k_{NN}=4$) achieved the highest success rate (78%) for peg insertion, significantly outperforming all baselines (e.g., ParticleNoCP at 14%, PCP and LUCCa at 48%).
 - CaPTURe also resulted in the lowest average number of steps for successful insertions (28.7 $\pm$ 8.9 steps), indicating improved efficiency.
 - Conformal prediction methods generally outperformed the uncalibrated ParticleNoCP baseline. The $k_{NN}=1$ ablation again showed reduced success compared to $k_{NN}=4$, reinforcing the finding that smoother prediction regions can enhance planning performance.

Overall, the main findings demonstrate that CaPTURe successfully constructs probabilistically valid, geometry-aware, and trans-dimensional uncertainty representations. This capability, stemming from its stratum-aware adaptive calibration, translates into substantial improvements in downstream motion planning performance, enabling higher task success rates and efficiency in complex, contact-rich robotic tasks characterized by significant model mismatch and aleatoric disturbances.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

This research presents a significant advancement in uncertainty quantification for autonomous systems, particularly those operating in environments demanding physical interaction. Its potential impact spans several areas:

1. **Enhanced Safety and Robustness in Robotic Deployment**: By providing probabilistically valid prediction regions with finite-sample guarantees, CaPTURe directly contributes to the safety and robustness of robots. The guarantee that the true future configuration will be contained within the predicted region with a specified probability, combined with the assurance that these regions remain within feasible C-space, can reduce the risk of collisions, damage to the robot or environment, and task failures. This is crucial for deploying robots in real-world applications such as manufacturing, healthcare, and exploration, where unexpected interactions can have severe consequences.

2. **Improved Motion Planning under Uncertainty**: The adaptive and geometry-aware nature of CaPTURe's uncertainty estimates allows motion planners to make more informed decisions. By distinguishing between different contact regimes and adjusting uncertainty bounds accordingly, planners can avoid overly conservative actions in low-uncertainty areas and allocate sufficient caution where uncertainty is high or trans-dimensional. This leads to more efficient and successful task execution, as demonstrated by the improved success rates and reduced steps to goal in the experimental tasks.

3. **Advancement in Conformal Prediction Applications**: The work extends the utility of conformal prediction by integrating it with particle-based models and the geometric complexities of stratified configuration spaces. This provides a novel framework for applying CP to high-dimensional, non-Euclidean, and discontinuous state spaces common in robotics, pushing the boundaries of what CP can achieve in practical, safety-critical systems.

4. **Generality to Diverse Robotic Systems**: CaPTURe is designed to work with particle-based dynamics models of arbitrary fidelity. This makes the approach broadly applicable to various robotic platforms and tasks, whether they rely on simplified analytical models or more complex learned models (e.g., from deep learning). This flexibility means the method can be adopted even when exact dynamics are unknown or highly complex, common challenges in modern robotics.

5. **Addressing Model Mismatch and Aleatoric Uncertainty**: The ability of CaPTURe to calibrate uncertainty estimates even in the presence of significant model mismatch and aleatoric disturbances is highly impactful. Real-world systems are rarely perfectly modeled, and sensors/actuators introduce noise. CaPTURe provides a data-driven way to account for these discrepancies, making the deployment of robots more reliable under realistic conditions.

6. **Foundation for Future Research**: The identified limitations, such as the single-step nature of the formal guarantees and the need for a known stratum indexer, open clear avenues for future research. Extending formal coverage guarantees to multi-step predictions and developing methods for learning or adapting stratum definitions would further enhance the method's capabilities and applicability. A rigorous analysis of data efficiency and hyperparameter sensitivity could also optimize practical implementations.

In summary, CaPTURe offers a principled and practically effective solution for robust uncertainty quantification in contact-rich robotic tasks. Its contributions are significant for enabling the next generation of autonomous systems to operate more safely, reliably, and efficiently in complex, unstructured, and interactive environments.
