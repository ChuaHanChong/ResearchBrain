# PIER-Flow: Physics-Informed Efficient Rectified Flow for Real-Time Mobile Robot Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Northwestern Polytechnical University and Mohamed bin Zayed University of Artificial Intelligence developed PIER-Flow, a generative navigation policy for mobile robots that leverages physics-informed rectified flow for real-time, safe, and multimodal navigation. The system achieved a 98.85% success rate with zero collisions in dynamic simulated environments while maintaining an average inference latency of 1.29 ms, and demonstrated stable 5.3 ms latency in real-world edge hardware deployments.

## Problem

- Existing optimization-based navigation methods like MPC suffer from high computational latency, making them unsuitable for real-time, high-frequency control in dynamic environments.
- Reactive methods such as Control Barrier Functions are computationally efficient but exhibit conservativeness and local myopia, leading to "freezing" behaviors or deadlocks in cluttered scenarios.
- Generative policies, especially diffusion models, offer multimodal capabilities but are computationally intensive, while deterministic policies struggle to represent diverse behaviors, both posing challenges for real-time deployment on edge hardware.

## Method

- In **PIER-Flow**, a lightweight ==Rectified Flow (RF)== network is trained to conditionally generate finite-horizon velocity chunks in a single step, learning an approximately straight path between noise and expert actions.
- A ==physics-informed loss==, based on ==differentiable kinematic unrolling==, is integrated into the training objective to enforce physical consistency and implicitly promote safety.
- A feasibility-aware ==action chunking== strategy selects the most executable chunk from parallel candidates, addressing the frequency mismatch between planning and chassis control for real-time execution on edge hardware.

## Results

- PIER-Flow achieved a **98.85%** success rate with **zero collisions** in simulated dynamic environments, surpassing diffusion models and mitigating the conservative "freezing" behavior of CBF.
- The system demonstrated a mean inference latency of **1.29 ms** in simulation, approximately **37.2 times** faster than MPC, and maintained stable **5.3 ms** latency on resource-constrained edge hardware.
- It successfully navigated all **four** real-world dynamic scenarios, providing smooth, evasive maneuvers, while MPC and diffusion models failed due to high latency or inability to react, and CBF exhibited excessive conservatism.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The Rectified Flow objective effectively learns a straight-line probability path, enabling accurate and efficient single-step generation, with additional integration steps not improving performance.
> - Integrating a differentiable kinematic unrolling loss during training serves as an effective inductive bias, significantly improving kinematic consistency, safety, and reducing the sim-to-real gap.
> - A combination of parallel multimodal action candidate generation and a lightweight feasibility selector allows for flexible and safe navigation in dynamic environments without requiring complex online optimization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous navigation in environments characterized by high density and dynamism presents a fundamental challenge in robotics. The existing research landscape offers several approaches, each with distinct advantages and limitations that PIER-Flow aims to address.

**Optimization-based methods** such as Model Predictive Control (MPC) are recognized for their ability to explicitly handle robot kinematics and safety constraints, offering valuable look-ahead capabilities in complex interactions. However, their primary drawback lies in computational expense, as repeatedly solving nonlinear programs can lead to significant latency, variable convergence times, and sensitivity to initialization, particularly in scenarios with increasing prediction horizons and obstacle counts. Control Barrier Function (CBF) based quadratic programs offer a computationally lighter alternative by minimally modifying nominal commands to enforce safety. Nevertheless, conventional CBF filters can exhibit local myopia and conservativeness, potentially causing excessive deceleration, deadlocks, or "freezing" behaviors in cluttered environments due to their lack of long-horizon foresight.

**Behavior Cloning (BC)** policies represent an alternative that prioritizes efficiency, learning direct observation-to-action mappings from expert demonstrations. While efficient in inference, deterministic BC policies often struggle to represent multimodal avoidance behaviors in ambiguous situations, potentially averaging incompatible action modes. Moreover, closed-loop execution can expose these policies to observations outside the expert's training distribution, leading to covariate shift and compounding errors, thereby limiting generalization.

**Generative policies**, notably those based on diffusion models, have emerged to address the multimodal limitations of BC. These models formulate trajectory planning as iterative denoising, capable of generating observation-conditioned action sequences and capturing diverse robot behaviors. However, their inference process typically requires multiple sequential neural network evaluations, making them time-consuming. Physics-aware diffusion variants, which incorporate safety or executability constraints, further increase this computational burden, creating a conflict with the high-frequency, low-jitter control requirements of mobile robot deployment on edge hardware.

**Flow Matching (FM) and Rectified Flow (RF)** methods offer a promising direction for reducing generative inference costs. RF, in particular, learns an approximately straight transport path from noise to data, enabling accurate generation with fewer integration steps. While these methods demonstrate efficiency potential, standard flow objectives primarily match demonstration distributions and do not inherently guarantee consistency with robot kinematics or safety constraints. Existing constraint-aware flow approaches often rely on test-time barrier correction, numerical integration, or sampling-based MPC refinement, which can negate the efficiency benefits for resource-constrained hardware.

PIER-Flow is positioned to bridge these gaps by developing a lightweight generative navigation policy that integrates finite-horizon decision-making, multimodal behavior generation, real-time inference, and safety-aware execution. It aims to combine the efficiency of flow-based generation with explicit kinematic and safety guarantees, without relying on computationally intensive online procedures, thereby making it suitable for real-time deployment on edge computing platforms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching motivation for this research stems from the critical need for robust, real-time, and safe autonomous navigation capabilities for mobile robots operating in dense and dynamic environments. Current state-of-the-art approaches face inherent trade-offs that limit their practical deployability, particularly on resource-constrained hardware.

The specific motivations and objectives are detailed as follows:

- **Address Computational Latency of Optimization-Based Methods:** Optimization-based planners like MPC, while providing robust, optimal, and safe trajectories, suffer from high computational costs due to repeated nonlinear optimization. This leads to variable convergence times and delayed control, manifesting as "latency spikes" that are detrimental to real-time responsiveness. A key objective is to develop a planning method that can emulate MPC's performance while significantly reducing inference latency to meet real-time requirements.

- **Overcome Conservativeness and Myopia of Reactive Methods:** Reactive approaches like CBF-based methods are computationally efficient but can be locally myopic and overly conservative. This often results in undesirable behaviors such as aggressive deceleration, deadlocks, and "freezing" in cluttered environments when facing obstacles, hindering efficient task completion. PIER-Flow aims to provide safer, smoother, and more goal-oriented navigation without exhibiting these conservative behaviors.

- **Enable Multimodal Behavior Representation beyond Deterministic Policies:** Deterministic behavior cloning (BC) policies are efficient for inference but often fail to capture the multimodal nature of valid avoidance strategies (e.g., passing an obstacle on either side). This can lead to averaging incompatible action modes or making suboptimal decisions in ambiguous situations. An objective is to develop a policy that can represent and generate diverse, context-appropriate multimodal behaviors without the complexities of iterative denoising.

- **Improve Inference Efficiency of Generative Policies:** While diffusion models offer multimodal capabilities, their iterative denoising process is computationally intensive, making them unsuitable for high-frequency control loops on edge devices. Flow-matching techniques, like Rectified Flow, offer a path to more efficient generation. The motivation is to leverage these efficient generative models to achieve single-step action generation while retaining high-quality, multimodal outputs.

- **Integrate Physical Consistency and Safety into Generative Models:** Standard generative models primarily match data distributions and do not inherently guarantee physical consistency (e.g., kinematic limits) or collision safety. Existing solutions typically involve expensive online correction or refinement procedures at test time. A core objective is to embed physics-informed constraints directly into the training objective to ensure kinematic consistency and implicitly promote safety without incurring online computational overhead.

- **Facilitate Robust Sim-to-Real Deployment on Resource-Constrained Hardware:** Deploying complex navigation policies on physical robots with limited computational resources (e.g., edge GPUs) requires consistent, low-latency performance and robustness to real-world sensory data. The objective is to design an architecture that accounts for the frequency mismatch between perception and control, and minimizes latency variations, ensuring stable and reliable operation in real-world environments.

In summary, the key objective is to propose PIER-Flow as a lightweight generative navigation framework that unifies finite-horizon action generation, multimodal decision-making, real-time inference, and safety-aware execution for mobile robots, effectively addressing the limitations of existing methods across computational cost, behavioral flexibility, and physical consistency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PIER-Flow operates in two distinct stages: an offline physics-informed training phase and an online inference and control phase.

**A. State Representation and Expert Data Generation**

1. **System State and Control Input:** The robot's state at time $t$ is defined as $x_t = [p_x, p_y, \theta, v_x, v_y, \omega]^T \in \mathbb{R}^6$, encompassing Cartesian position, orientation, and body-frame velocities. The control input $u_t = [v_{cx}, v_{cy}, \omega_c]^T \in \mathbb{R}^3$ represents commanded body-frame velocities.
2. **Observation Formulation:** The egocentric observation $S_t \in \mathbb{R}^{24}$ comprises:
 - Goal information: $s_{goal,t} = [d_g, \cos \alpha_g, \sin \alpha_g]^T \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (polar coordinates of the relative goal).
 - Ego-velocity: $s_{ego,t} = [v_x, v_y, \omega]^T \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
 - Obstacle features: For $K=3$ most threatening obstacles, each $o^{(j)}_t = [d^{(j)}_s, \cos(\alpha^{(j)}), \sin(\alpha^{(j)}), v^{(j)}_{x,rel}, v^{(j)}_{y,rel}]^T \in \mathbb{R}^5$ (distance, angle, relative velocities). Dummy values are used for fewer than three obstacles.
 - Short-term Intention Memory: The previously executed command $u_{t-1} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is included to mitigate mode-switching in symmetric scenarios.
3. **Action Chunk Generation:** The policy generates a horizon-$H$ (set to $H=20$, corresponding to a 1.0 s predictive horizon with $\Delta t = 0.05$ s) body-frame velocity chunk $\hat{A}_t = [\hat{u}_{t,0}, \dots, \hat{u}_{t,H-1}] \in \mathbb{R}^{3H}$.
4. **Expert Data Collection via MPC:** A finite-horizon nonlinear programming (NLP) problem (Eq. 1) is solved using an MPC controller to generate high-quality expert demonstrations. This MPC problem minimizes a cost function incorporating state, control input, terminal costs, and collision penalties, subject to discrete-time kinematics, control input bounds, and collision avoidance constraints (using slack variables for recursive feasibility). The MPC operates in randomized dynamic environments, and only successful, collision-free trajectories are recorded. Velocity actions are scaled by $\gamma_{scale}$ to enhance gradient flow during neural network training. The collected dataset comprises 2000 expert rollouts, yielding 166,599 supervised samples.

**B. Lightweight Physics-Informed Rectified Flow Policy**

1. **Rectified Flow Formulation:** The problem of local navigation is cast as the conditional generation of expert velocity chunks. Let $z_1 \sim \gamma_{scale} \cdot p_{expert}(A_{expert}|S_t)$ be the scaled expert chunk and $z_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ be base Gaussian noise. Rectified Flow constructs an approximately straight transport path between $z_0$ and $z_1$: $z_\tau = (1-\tau)z_0 + \tau z_1$, where $\tau \sim \mathcal{U}(0,1)$. The target velocity for the flow is $v^* = z_1 - z_0$.
2. **Velocity Network:** A conditional velocity field $v_\theta(z_\tau, \tau, S_t)$ is trained. This network is a compact multi-layer perceptron (MLP) with three 1024-unit hidden layers and SiLU activations. The flow time $\tau$ is embedded using a 64-dimensional Gaussian Fourier projection. The network's input is $[z_\tau, \phi(\tau), S_t] \in \mathbb{R}^{60+64+24}$, and its 60-dimensional output predicts the full $H=20$ action chunk. The network comprises approximately 2.32M parameters.
3. **Flow-Matching Objective:** The primary training objective is the flow-matching loss:
 $L_{flow} = \mathbb{E}[\|v_\theta(z_\tau, \tau, S_t) - (z_1 - z_0)\|^2_2]$ (Eq. 5).
4. **Physics-Informed Loss:** To enhance physical consistency, the standard flow objective is augmented with a differentiable kinematic unrolling loss:
 - For each sampled point on the PIER-Flow transport path, the model implies a predicted endpoint $\hat{z}_1 = z_\tau + (1-\tau)v_\theta(z_\tau, \tau, S_t)$, yielding the predicted action chunk $\hat{A}_\theta = \hat{z}_1 / \gamma_{scale}$.
 - Both $\hat{A}_\theta$ and $A_{expert}$ are reshaped into horizon-$H$ velocity commands and unrolled using omnidirectional kinematics (Eq. 7a-c) from an initial pose of $(0,0,0)$. This produces predicted trajectories $\hat{X}_{1:H}$ and expert trajectories $X^{expert}_{1:H}$.
 - The physics-informed state loss is calculated as: $L_{state} = \mathbb{E}[\frac{1}{H}\sum_{h=1}^H \|\hat{x}_h - x^{expert}_h\|^2_2]$ (Eq. 8). This loss penalizes accumulated trajectory-level deviation, acting as an inductive bias to enforce kinematic consistency.
 - The total training objective is $L_{total} = L_{flow} + \lambda_{phys}L_{state}$ (Eq. 9), where $\lambda_{phys}$ is an empirically chosen weighting coefficient (set to 10 in experiments).

**C. Feasibility-aware Action Chunking (Online Inference & Control)**

This mechanism addresses the frequency mismatch between the planning loop ($f_{plan}$) and the high-frequency chassis control ($f_{ctrl}$).

1. **Parallel Latent Candidate Generation:**
 - At each replanning cycle, a batch of $M$ latent candidates $Z_0 = [z^{(1)}_0, \dots, z^{(M)}_0]$ is constructed. For evaluation, $M=2$ is used: one candidate anchored at the noise mean ($z^{(1)}_0=0$) and another generated from a fixed-seed Gaussian noise ($z^{(m)}_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ for $m>1$). This balances deterministic exploitation with topological diversity.
 - A single-step Euler integration is applied to solve the PIER-Flow ODE $\frac{dz_\tau}{d\tau} = v_\theta(z_\tau, \tau, S_t)$ simultaneously for all candidates in $Z_0$. This parallel computation yields $M$ candidate trajectory chunks, $\{\hat{A}^{(1)}_t, \dots, \hat{A}^{(M)}_t\}$, each spanning $H$ steps.
2. **Lightweight Feasibility Selector:**
 - To select an executable chunk without online optimization, a lightweight feasibility selector is employed. It evaluates only the imminent $E = f_{ctrl} / f_{plan}$ execution steps of each candidate chunk, as these are the commands that will be executed before the next replanning cycle.
 - The feasibility cost $C_{feas}(\hat{A})$ (Eq. 10) for a chunk $\hat{A}$ sums penalties for:
 - Velocity and angular limit violations ($c_{vel}$, $c_{yaw}$).
 - Intrusion into inflated safety margins of perceived dynamic obstacles ($c_{obs}$).
 - The selected action chunk is then $\hat{A}^*_t = \arg\min_{m \in \{1,\dots,M\}} C_{feas}(\hat{A}^{(m)}_t)$.
3. **Asynchronous Action Chunking:** The low-level chassis controller sequentially executes the first $E$ velocity commands from $\hat{A}^*_t$ at the high frequency $f_{ctrl}$. A new action chunk is generated and selected based on the updated observation in the subsequent planning cycle.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive evaluations in both simulation and real-world hardware, comparing PIER-Flow against various baselines.

**A. Simulation Experiments**

Simulations were conducted in a custom mecanum-wheel robot simulator with randomized dynamic environments featuring three circular dynamic obstacles. The goal was fixed, and initial robot states were randomized. The MPC expert generated 2000 successful, collision-free rollouts for training.

1. **Comparative Analysis (Table I, Fig. 3):**
 - **Success Rate and Safety:** PIER-Flow achieved the highest success rate of 98.85% with zero collisions, effectively distilling the safety guarantees of the expert MPC. In contrast, BC-MLP, DDPM, and DDIM exhibited collision rates (0.30%, 1.35%, and 1.40% respectively), indicating limitations in safety for multimodal scenarios. The CBF baseline, while collision-free, suffered from a high timeout rate (12.85%) due to conservative "freezing" behavior.
 - **Inference Latency:** PIER-Flow demonstrated a mean inference latency of approximately 1.29 ms and a P95 latency of 1.40 ms. This is approximately 37.2 times faster than the MPC solver (48.01 ms), 65.7 times faster than DDIM (84.73 ms), and over 800 times faster than standard DDPM (1077.24 ms). While BC-MLP and CBF were faster (0.54 ms and 0.18 ms respectively), PIER-Flow provided superior success and safety. The results indicated that PIER-Flow effectively resolved the computational bottleneck of robust generative navigation models.

2. **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Effect of Integration Sampling Steps (Fig. 4):** Counter-intuitively, the single-step Euler solver (1 step) achieved the fastest inference (approx. 1.29 ms) and the highest success rate (98.85%). Increasing the number of integration steps did not improve performance, suggesting that the Rectified Flow objective effectively learned a straight-line probability path, enabling accurate single-step generation.
 - **Impact of the Physics-Informed Loss (Table II):** The inclusion of the differentiable kinematic unrolling loss ($L_{state}$) improved the success rate from 98.10% (action-only RF) to 98.85% and eliminated collisions (from 0.10% to 0.00%). This finding highlights the physics-informed loss's role as an inductive bias, enforcing dynamically feasible trajectory predictions during training and reducing the sim-to-real gap.

**B. Hardware Validation and Real-World Experiments**

Physical experiments were conducted on a ROSMASTER X3 omnidirectional mobile robot equipped with a RPLIDAR-S2 and an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano edge computer running ROS2. A lightweight onboard perception pipeline was implemented for obstacle detection and velocity estimation. Four distinct scenarios were designed: Single Static, Multi Static, Single Dynamic, and Multi Dynamic. Baselines included MPC, CBF, and DDIM (DDPM and BC-MLP were excluded due to their poor simulation performance or high latency).

1. **Real-World Performance (Table III, Figs. 5-7):**
 - **Overall Outcome:** PIER-Flow successfully navigated all four real-world scenarios, maintaining a stable mean onboard inference latency of approximately 5.3 ms, with a maximum latency of 6.65 ms.
 - **Latency Stability and Real-world Failures:**
 - **DDIM:** Consistently required approximately 700 ms per inference step on the edge computer, making it unable to react to dynamic changes and failing in all scenarios.
 - **MPC:** Exhibited a long-tail latency distribution on hardware, with maximum latencies reaching 454.53 ms in the Multi Dynamic scenario. This led to the execution of outdated commands and caused collisions in three out of four scenarios.
 - **CBF:** Achieved the lowest latency (approx. 3.3 ms) but suffered from overly conservative behavior. In the Single Static scenario, CBF took 12.27 s to complete, nearly double PIER-Flow's 6.57 s, demonstrating the "freezing robot" problem.
 - **PIER-Flow:** Showed a near-vertical latency CDF (Fig. 5), indicating highly predictable and low-jitter onboard computation, crucial for continuous high-frequency control.
 - **Trajectory Visualization and Analysis (Fig. 6, 7):** PIER-Flow generated smooth, evasive maneuvers and maintained a non-zero velocity profile until reaching the goal, even with suddenly appearing dynamic obstacles. This behavior was attributed to its finite-horizon action-chunk generation, multimodal candidate sampling, and lightweight feasibility selection. In contrast, MPC failed due to excessive latency, CBF exhibited stopping behavior, and DDIM was unable to produce timely commands.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research on PIER-Flow presents several significant contributions and has substantial potential impact on the field of mobile robotics and autonomous navigation:

1. **Resolution of Key Trade-offs in Navigation Policies:** PIER-Flow effectively addresses the long-standing trade-offs between physical feasibility, multimodal behavior generation, and real-time inference in dense and dynamic environments. It offers a solution that combines the safety and look-ahead capabilities typically associated with optimization-based methods with the efficiency and behavioral diversity of generative models, without succumbing to their respective drawbacks.

2. **Enabling Real-Time Performance on Edge Hardware:** A primary impact of PIER-Flow is its demonstrated ability to achieve near-millisecond inference speeds in simulation and maintain stable, low-millisecond latency (approximately 5.3 ms) on resource-constrained edge computing platforms (e.g., NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano). This capability is critical for deploying advanced navigation algorithms in real-world mobile robots, which require high-frequency and low-jitter control loops to react promptly to dynamic environments. The elimination of computational latency spikes, a common issue with MPC, and the avoidance of "freezing" behaviors seen with CBF, represents a significant step towards reliable autonomous operation.

3. **Enhanced Safety and Robustness through Physics-Informed Design:** By incorporating a differentiable kinematic rollout into its training objective, PIER-Flow is trained to generate physically consistent actions. This inductive bias not only improves the success rate and eliminates collisions in simulation but also drastically minimizes the sim-to-real gap, leading to safer and more robust real-world deployments. The framework's ability to maintain zero collisions in complex dynamic scenarios, even on physical hardware, is a crucial achievement for safety-critical applications.

4. **Effective Multimodal Behavior Generation:** Unlike deterministic behavior cloning policies, PIER-Flow's generative nature, particularly its ability to generate parallel action-chunk candidates, allows it to capture and represent multimodal avoidance behaviors. This is important in ambiguous situations where multiple valid maneuvers exist, leading to more flexible and intelligent navigation strategies.

5. **Practical Deployment Architecture (Feasibility-aware Action Chunking):** The proposed asynchronous action chunking strategy, coupled with lightweight feasibility selection, offers a practical and efficient solution for bridging the frequency mismatch between sensory perception/neural inference and low-level motor control. This design enables continuous, high-frequency physical motion from a lower-frequency planning output without needing expensive online trajectory optimization, making it highly suitable for current mobile robot architectures.

6. **Broad Applicability:** The advancements presented by PIER-Flow have potential impact across various domains requiring autonomous navigation, including industrial mobile robots, logistics automation, autonomous ground vehicles, and robots for search and rescue or hazardous environment exploration. Its efficiency and robustness could facilitate wider adoption of advanced AI-based control in these fields.

7. **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper lays a foundation for future work, explicitly pointing towards extensions in vision-based end-to-end perception and scaling the policy for multi-agent decentralized navigation in unstructured environments. This suggests PIER-Flow is not just a standalone solution but a building block for more complex and capable autonomous systems.

In summary, PIER-Flow represents a significant advancement in autonomous navigation, offering a computationally efficient, safe, and robust policy that addresses the critical requirements for real-time deployment on mobile robots in dynamic and dense environments.
