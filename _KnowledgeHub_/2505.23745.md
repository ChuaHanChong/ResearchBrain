# To Trust Or Not To Trust Your Vision-Language Model's Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework named TrustVLM was developed to estimate the trustworthiness of Vision-Language Model predictions by combining image-to-text and image-to-image similarity scores. This training-free approach enhances misclassification detection, showing an average 20.69% reduction in AURC on fine-grained datasets and improving classification accuracy by up to 12.7% in its variants.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) frequently produce confident yet incorrect predictions, which poses risks in safety-critical applications.
- Existing misclassification detection (MisD) methods for unimodal vision models do not adequately address the multimodal complexities or the inherent 'modality gap' in VLMs.
- Despite significant research on VLM accuracy, the crucial task of reliably identifying when a prediction is untrustworthy (MisD) remains relatively underexplored in multimodal contexts.

## Method

- TrustVLM is introduced as a ==training-free framework== that assesses VLM prediction trustworthiness by combining the standard ==image-to-text similarity== with an additional ==image-to-image similarity score==.
- ==Visual prototypes== for each class are pre-computed by averaging N-shot image embeddings using an auxiliary vision encoder.
- The overall confidence score for an input is derived from the sum of the VLM's image-to-text prediction similarity and the image-to-image similarity between the input image and the visual prototype of the predicted class.

## Results

- TrustVLM-D (using DINOv2) achieved state-of-the-art misclassification detection, yielding average improvements of **20.69%** lower AURC, **4.78%** higher AUROC, and **15.63%** lower FPR95 on fine-grained datasets.
- TrustVLM* variants, which leverage visual prototypes for zero-shot classification, boosted accuracy by an average of **12.7%** on fine-grained datasets, reducing the overall error rate.
- The framework demonstrated robustness to distribution shifts, proved effective with few-shot samples (N=**1**) for prototypes, and consistently outperformed existing concept-based MisD methods like ORCA across various VLM architectures.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The inherent 'modality gap' in VLMs, where image and text embeddings occupy distinct regions, can be exploited to enhance trustworthiness assessment by leveraging the image embedding space's discriminative capacity.
> - Specialized misclassification detection methods are necessary for VLMs, as general out-of-distribution detection techniques often perform suboptimally for in-distribution misclassifications.
> - Combining confidence signals from both image-to-text and image-to-image similarity leads to a more robust and accurate assessment of VLM prediction trustworthiness.
