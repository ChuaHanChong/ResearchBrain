# To Trust Or Not To Trust Your Vision-Language Model's Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework named TrustVLM was developed to estimate the trustworthiness of Vision-Language Model predictions by combining image-to-text and image-to-image similarity scores. This training-free approach enhances misclassification detection, showing an average 20.69% reduction in AURC on fine-grained datasets and improving classification accuracy by up to 12.7% in its variants.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) frequently produce confident yet incorrect predictions, which poses risks in safety-critical applications.
- Existing misclassification detection (MisD) methods for unimodal vision models do not adequately address the multimodal complexities or the inherent 'modality gap' in VLMs.
- Despite significant research on VLM accuracy, the crucial task of reliably identifying when a prediction is untrustworthy (MisD) remains relatively underexplored in multimodal contexts.

## Method

- TrustVLM is introduced as a ==training-free framework== that assesses VLM prediction trustworthiness by combining the standard ==image-to-text similarity== with an additional ==image-to-image similarity score==.
- ==Visual prototypes== for each class are pre-computed by averaging N-shot image embeddings using an auxiliary vision encoder.
- The overall confidence score for an input is derived from the sum of the VLM's image-to-text prediction similarity and the image-to-image similarity between the input image and the visual prototype of the predicted class.

## Results

- TrustVLM-D (using DINOv2) achieved state-of-the-art misclassification detection, yielding average improvements of **20.69%** lower AURC, **4.78%** higher AUROC, and **15.63%** lower FPR95 on fine-grained datasets.
- TrustVLM* variants, which leverage visual prototypes for zero-shot classification, boosted accuracy by an average of **12.7%** on fine-grained datasets, reducing the overall error rate.
- The framework demonstrated robustness to distribution shifts, proved effective with few-shot samples (N=**1**) for prototypes, and consistently outperformed existing concept-based MisD methods like ORCA across various VLM architectures.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The inherent 'modality gap' in VLMs, where image and text embeddings occupy distinct regions, can be exploited to enhance trustworthiness assessment by leveraging the image embedding space's discriminative capacity.
> - Specialized misclassification detection methods are necessary for VLMs, as general out-of-distribution detection techniques often perform suboptimally for in-distribution misclassifications.
> - Combining confidence signals from both image-to-text and image-to-image similarity leads to a more robust and accurate assessment of VLM prediction trustworthiness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Vision-Language Models (VLMs), exemplified by models like CLIP and SigLIP, have revolutionized multimodal learning by integrating visual and textual information into a unified framework. These models excel in zero-shot and transfer learning scenarios, enabling a wide array of applications from image classification and cross-modal retrieval to image captioning. Trained on vast datasets of image-text pairs, VLMs learn rich, transferable representations that have significantly pushed the boundaries of AI capabilities.

Despite their impressive performance, a critical challenge persists: VLMs are prone to misclassification, often producing confident yet incorrect predictions. This issue is particularly problematic in safety-critical domains such as autonomous driving, medical diagnostics, and surveillance, where erroneous decisions can have severe consequences. While extensive research has focused on enhancing the *accuracy* of VLM outputs, the equally important aspect of *trustworthiness*—that is, reliably identifying when a prediction should be accepted or flagged for human review—remains comparatively underexplored.

This paper addresses a crucial gap in the existing literature by specifically focusing on **misclassification detection (MisD)** within VLMs, distinguishing it from related but distinct fields such as **out-of-distribution (OOD) detection**. While both aim to identify problematic inputs, MisD targets in-distribution samples that are incorrectly classified (e.g., an image of a cat mislabeled as a dog), often due to subtle features or proximity to decision boundaries. In contrast, OOD detection focuses on identifying inputs from entirely unseen or novel distributions (e.g., an image of an alien if the model was trained only on animals). The authors highlight that methods optimized for OOD detection frequently perform poorly on MisD tasks, underscoring the need for specialized approaches.

Existing MisD methods, primarily developed for unimodal vision models, often fail to account for the unique complexities arising from the interaction between visual inputs and textual semantics in VLMs. Recent attempts, like ORCA, have leveraged human-level concepts for VLM misclassification detection but often require labor-intensive concept construction. This paper proposes a novel, training-free solution, TrustVLM, that directly addresses these limitations by exploiting an inherent characteristic of VLMs: the "modality gap" between image and text embeddings and the improved discriminative capacity within the image embedding space itself. By doing so, this work pushes the frontier of VLM reliability, enabling safer and more dependable real-world deployments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce a robust, training-free framework, named TrustVLM, for accurately estimating the trustworthiness of Vision-Language Model predictions. The core motivation stems from several critical observations and challenges:

1. **Risk of Confident Misclassifications:** VLMs, despite their advanced capabilities, frequently make confident but incorrect predictions. This "overconfidence" poses a significant risk, particularly in safety-critical applications where an undetected error can lead to severe outcomes.
2. **Under-exploration of VLM Trustworthiness:** While much research has concentrated on improving VLM accuracy, the equally vital challenge of knowing *when to trust* a VLM's prediction (i.e., misclassification detection) has been relatively overlooked, especially within the multimodal context.
3. **Limitations of Existing MisD and OOD Methods for VLMs:** Traditional misclassification detection methods, often designed for unimodal vision models, do not adequately capture the complexities of multimodal interactions in VLMs. Furthermore, methods for out-of-distribution detection, while related, are fundamentally distinct and often perform suboptimally for identifying in-distribution misclassifications.
4. **Leveraging the Modality Gap:** The authors are motivated by the observed "modality gap" in VLMs like CLIP, where image and text embeddings occupy distinct regions of the shared representation space. Crucially, they identify that certain concepts are *more distinctly represented* in the image embedding space than in the text embedding space. This insight forms the foundation for developing a novel confidence-scoring mechanism.
5. **Need for Training-Free Solutions:** To facilitate rapid and broad deployment without incurring additional computational costs or requiring extensive retraining, the framework aims to be training-free.

In essence, the paper seeks to enhance the practical utility and safety of VLMs by providing a reliable mechanism to assess prediction confidence, leveraging intrinsic properties of multimodal embedding spaces that have been largely overlooked by prior work.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The TrustVLM framework is a training-free approach designed to estimate the reliability of VLM predictions by leveraging information from the image embedding space, complementing the traditional image-to-text similarity used in zero-shot classification.

The methodology unfolds in three main steps:

1. **Step 1: Save Visual Prototypes:**
 - For each class `c` in the target dataset, `N`-shot samples from the training data are used.
 - A pre-trained auxiliary vision encoder `E` (e.g., the original CLIP image encoder, MoCo v2, or DINOv2) extracts embeddings from these `N` samples.
 - The visual prototype `P_c` for class `c` is computed by averaging these `N` embeddings. These prototypes are then stored. The choice of auxiliary vision encoder is flexible, with more powerful encoders potentially leading to better performance.

2. **Step 2: Zero-shot Prediction with VLMs:**
 - For a given input image `x`, the VLM performs its standard zero-shot classification.
 - This involves embedding the image into `f_x` and class names (e.g., "a photo of a [CLASS]") into text embeddings `t_c`.
 - The prediction probability `p(y|x)` for each class `y` is calculated based on the cosine similarity between `f_x` and `t_c`, followed by a softmax normalization.
 - The predicted class is `ˆy = argmax_y p(y|x)`.
 - An initial confidence score, `S_i-t = max_y p(y|x)`, derived from image-to-text similarity, is obtained.

3. **Step 3: Verify with Image-to-Image (I2I) Similarity:**
 - To complement `S_i-t`, an additional confidence score from the image embedding space is generated.
 - The same auxiliary vision encoder `E` used in Step 1 extracts an embedding `E_x` of the input image `x`.
 - An image-to-image similarity score, `S_i-i = E_x · P_ˆy`, is computed as the cosine similarity between `E_x` and the visual prototype `P_ˆy` of the predicted class `ˆy`.
 - The rationale here is that if `ˆy` is incorrect, `E_x` should have low similarity with `P_ˆy`, thereby mitigating overconfidence. Conversely, a correct `ˆy` should yield high `S_i-i`, reinforcing reliability.
 - Finally, the overall confidence score for input `x` is a combination of these two scores: `κ(x) = S_i-t + S_i-i`.

**Variants of TrustVLM:**

- **TrustVLM\*:** This variant extends the use of visual prototypes to also enhance fine-grained classification accuracy. Instead of solely relying on image-to-text similarity for prediction, it combines the probabilities from both image-to-text (Eq. 1) and image-to-image similarity (Eq. 3), where the latter uses `E_x` and `P_c`. The confidence score for TrustVLM\* is `κ(x) = max_y p(y|x) + S_i-i`, where `p(y|x)` is the combined probability. This approach aims to leverage the discriminative power of the image embedding space for both prediction and confidence estimation.
- **TrustVLM\*(F):** Building on TrustVLM\*, this variant introduces a lightweight fine-tuning step. The pre-computed visual prototypes `P_c` are treated as learnable parameters and optimized using stochastic gradient descent. The VLMs and the vision encoder are frozen, and only the prototypes are fine-tuned via a cross-entropy loss on the `N`-shot labeled samples used to create the prototypes. This aims to refine the prototypes for improved affinity estimation and downstream performance.

The chosen evaluation metrics are Area Under the Risk-Coverage Curve (AURC), Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic curve (AUROC), and False Positive Rate at 95% True Positive Rate (FPR95), along with standard classification Accuracy (ACC). The framework is extensively tested across 17 diverse datasets, 4 architectures (e.g., ViT-B/16, ResNet-101, ViT-B/32, ResNet-50), and 2 VLMs (CLIP, SigLIP), using various auxiliary vision encoders (CLIP-I, MoCo v2, DINOv2).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of TrustVLM across a diverse range of 17 datasets, 4 VLM architectures, and 2 distinct VLMs yielded significant improvements in misclassification detection (MisD) performance and, in some variants, classification accuracy.

#### 4.1. Superior Misclassification Detection Performance

- **Outperforming Baselines:** TrustVLM consistently demonstrated state-of-the-art performance in MisD. Even simple baselines like Maximum Softmax Probability (MSP) often outperformed more sophisticated Out-of-Distribution (OOD) detection methods (e.g., MaxLogit, Energy, MCM) for MisD tasks, validating the authors' premise that specialized MisD approaches are needed for VLMs.
- **Impact of Image-to-Image Similarity:** The integration of an image-to-image similarity confidence score in TrustVLM significantly enhanced MisD performance. This was true regardless of the auxiliary vision encoder used (CLIP-I, MoCo v2, or DINOv2), highlighting the framework's versatility.
- **Best Performance with DINOv2 (TrustVLM-D):** TrustVLM-D, utilizing the powerful DINOv2 encoder as the auxiliary vision model, achieved the best overall MisD performance. On fine-grained classification datasets, it yielded average improvements of 20.69% in AURC (lower is better), 4.78% in AUROC (higher is better), and 15.63% in FPR95 (lower is better) compared to the strongest baseline. Similar improvements were observed on ImageNet and its variants, with 9.5% in AURC, 2.11% in AUROC, and 6.96% in FPR95 over the strongest baseline.
- **Robustness to Distribution Shifts:** When prototypes were computed solely from the ImageNet training split and then applied to ImageNet variants (introducing distribution shifts), TrustVLM-D maintained leading performance across all metrics, showcasing its robustness.

#### 4.2. Improved Classification Accuracy and Confidence Trade-offs

- **TrustVLM\* for Fine-grained Classification:** When visual prototypes were leveraged for zero-shot classification (TrustVLM\* variants), classification accuracy (ACC) improved substantially. TrustVLM\*-D achieved an average ACC improvement of 12.7% on fine-grained datasets. This enhanced accuracy led to a marked reduction in AURC (average 56.06% decrease compared to TrustVLM-D), indicating a lower overall error rate across all confidence coverage levels.
- **AUROC/FPR95 Nuance with Accuracy Gains:** While TrustVLM\* variants significantly boosted ACC and reduced AURC, they sometimes showed marginally lower AUROC and FPR95 compared to TrustVLM-D (which focuses purely on MisD). This suggests a complex interplay: as overall accuracy improves, differentiating between *remaining* misclassifications and *difficult but correct* classifications becomes harder for the confidence detector.
- **Fine-tuning Visual Prototypes (TrustVLM\*(F)):** A lightweight fine-tuning of the visual prototypes (TrustVLM\*(F)-D) further improved performance across all metrics, including ACC by an additional 1.29% compared to TrustVLM\*-D, demonstrating the benefit of even minimal training on the prototypes.

#### 4.3. Comparison with Concept-based Methods and Ablation Studies

- **Superior to ORCA:** TrustVLM significantly outperformed ORCA, a recent concept-based MisD method for VLMs. With CLIP ResNet-101, TrustVLM achieved improvements up to 9.57% in AUROC, 35.86% in FPR95, and 47.02% in ACC over ORCA.
- **Versatility Across Architectures and VLMs:** Ablation studies confirmed TrustVLM's compatibility and superior performance across different VLM architectures (e.g., CLIP ResNet-50, SigLIP ViT-B/16) and VLMs, consistently outperforming baselines.
- **Complementary Components:** Ablation studies demonstrated that both image-to-text and image-to-image similarity components are crucial and complementary, with their combination yielding the best results.
- **Robustness to N-shot Samples and Prompts:** The method showed robust performance even with a small number of N-shot samples (N=1), with performance saturating beyond N=4. It also proved robust to variations in text prompts.
- **Visualization:** Visualizations of confidence score distributions clearly showed that TrustVLM achieves better separation between correctly classified and misclassified samples, leading to improved detection.
- **Generated Data for Prototypes:** The feasibility of using generated images (e.g., from Stable Diffusion 3) for prototypes was demonstrated, offering an alternative in privacy-sensitive or low-data scenarios, although with a slight performance gap compared to real data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of TrustVLM marks a significant step forward in enhancing the reliability and trustworthiness of Vision-Language Models, with profound implications for their real-world deployment.

#### 5.1. Enhanced Safety in Critical Applications

The most immediate and impactful contribution is to safety-critical domains such as autonomous driving, medical diagnostics, and surveillance. By providing a robust, training-free mechanism to detect when a VLM's prediction is likely incorrect (even if highly confident), TrustVLM acts as a crucial safeguard. It allows for flagging ambiguous or erroneous predictions for human review, preventing potentially severe consequences that could arise from overconfident misclassifications. This directly addresses one of the major hurdles to the widespread adoption of VLMs in high-stakes environments.

#### 5.2. Bridging the Trust Gap in AI Deployment

In an era where AI models are increasingly powerful but also opaque, building trust is paramount. TrustVLM directly contributes to this by offering a transparent and effective way to quantify prediction reliability. This capability can foster greater confidence among users, developers, and regulators in deploying VLM-powered systems, knowing that mechanisms are in place to identify and mitigate risks associated with erroneous outputs.

#### 5.3. Novel Exploitation of VLM Intrinsic Properties

The framework's innovative use of the "modality gap" and the enhanced discriminative power within the image embedding space is a significant conceptual advancement. It highlights that overlooked intrinsic properties of VLMs can be leveraged not just for prediction but also for crucial meta-tasks like uncertainty quantification, without needing to modify or retrain the core VLM. This insight could inspire new research directions into exploiting other underutilized aspects of multimodal representation spaces.

#### 5.4. Practicality and Efficiency (Training-Free)

A key strength of TrustVLM is its training-free nature. This eliminates the need for expensive, time-consuming retraining of VLMs or the acquisition of large, labeled datasets specifically for confidence estimation. This practicality makes TrustVLM readily applicable to existing VLM deployments, reducing computational overhead and accelerating the integration of reliability features into real-world systems. Its data-efficient approach, even performing well with few-shot (N=1) samples for prototypes, further enhances its practical utility. The ability to use generated data for prototypes also offers flexibility in privacy-sensitive contexts.

#### 5.5. Foundations for Future Research

TrustVLM opens several promising avenues for future research. This includes:
- **Generalization to Broader Multimodal Tasks:** Extending the framework beyond zero-shot classification to tasks like visual question answering, image retrieval, and image captioning.
- **Adaptive Confidence Scoring:** Exploring dynamic weighting strategies for image-to-text and image-to-image similarities based on dataset characteristics or real-time confidence needs.
- **Human-in-the-Loop Systems:** Integrating TrustVLM with human feedback loops to continuously refine confidence estimation and model reliability.
- **Robustness to Evolving Data:** Adapting the framework for continual learning or streaming environments where data distributions shift over time.
- **Integration with Explainable AI (XAI):** Combining reliability estimation with interpretability to not only detect misclassifications but also explain *why* the model might be untrustworthy.

In conclusion, TrustVLM provides a robust, efficient, and fundamentally important solution for VLM trustworthiness. By empowering systems to intelligently "know what they don't know," it paves the way for the safer, more ethical, and ultimately more effective deployment of these powerful AI models across a wide spectrum of applications.
