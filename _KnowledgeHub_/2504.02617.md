# PicoPose: Progressive Pixel-to-Pixel Correspondence Learning for Novel Object Pose Estimation

> [!summary] Summary
> PicoPose introduces a three-stage progressive pixel-to-pixel correspondence learning framework for precise 6D pose estimation of novel objects using only RGB images. The system achieves a mean Average Recall (AR) of 47.5% without iterative refinement on the BOP benchmark, outperforming prior RGB-only methods.

## Problem

- Estimating the 6D pose of novel objects in zero-shot settings, where CAD models are only available at inference, is challenging for robotic applications.
- Existing RGB-only novel object pose estimation methods often suffer from noisy and outlier-prone correspondences, leading to imprecise pose predictions.
- Reliance on depth sensors (RGB-D methods) can be costly and impractical in many real-world robotic deployment scenarios.

## Method

- **PicoPose** employs a three-stage ==progressive pixel-to-pixel correspondence learning== framework, starting with feature matching for coarse correspondences and template selection.
- A ==global 2D affine transformation== is estimated to smooth coarse correspondences, filter outliers, and align the template's feature map with the observation.
- A final local refinement stage uses stacked ==offset regression blocks== and ==correlation lookup== to generate fine-grained, locally adjusted pixel correspondences.

## Results

- **PicoPose** achieved a mean Average Recall (AR) of **47.5%** without iterative refinement on the BOP benchmark, surpassing FoundPose (**42.6%**) and GigaPose (**25.6%**).
- When combined with iterative refinement, the system reached **58.8%** mean AR (1 hypothesis), outperforming FoundPose (**55.0%**) and GigaPose (**54.7%**).
- Ablation studies confirmed that each progressive stage contributed to improved correspondence quality (decreasing End-Point Error) and higher 6D pose estimation accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Progressive refinement of pixel-to-pixel correspondences, from coarse feature matching to global transformation and local adjustments, significantly improves both correspondence quality and 6D pose accuracy.
> - Learning affine transformations directly from a correspondence map more effectively captures variations compared to using concatenated features, providing a robust basis for smoothing.
> - Iterative local refinement using stacked offset regression blocks, aided by an explicit correlation lookup module, is crucial for achieving high-fidelity pixel correspondences and higher accuracy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of object pose estimation, which involves determining the 6D (3D rotation and 3D translation) pose of objects relative to a camera, is a critical component for various robotic applications such as manipulation, grasping, and assembly. Early research in this area primarily focused on scenarios where 3D CAD models of objects were available during both training and testing. These methods often achieved high accuracy for known objects but exhibited limited generalization to objects not encountered during training.

A significant challenge in this domain is "novel object pose estimation" (NOPE), particularly in "zero-shot" settings where objects are entirely new and their CAD models are only available at inference time, not during training. This capability is crucial for rapid deployment in dynamic robotic environments. Previous work has approached NOPE using either RGB-D (color and depth) or RGB (color-only) images. RGB-D methods, such as MegaPose and FoundationPose, have shown strong performance by leveraging the explicit geometric information provided by depth maps. However, depth sensors can be costly and impractical in certain real-world applications, making RGB-only approaches more desirable.

RGB-only NOPE methods, like GigaPose and FoundPose, typically rely on establishing correspondences between observed scenes and rendered object templates through feature matching. A common limitation of these approaches is the generation of noisy correspondences prone to outliers, which can lead to imprecise pose predictions. The current work, PicoPose, positions itself within this context by addressing the challenge of robust and accurate RGB-based novel object pose estimation. It aims to improve upon existing RGB-only methods by introducing a progressive correspondence learning framework that refines pixel-to-pixel matches, thereby enhancing the accuracy and reliability of pose computation in zero-shot scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust framework for precise 6D pose estimation of novel objects solely from RGB images, enabling zero-shot generalization for rapid deployment in robotic applications. This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Bridging the Generalization Gap for Novel Objects**: Traditional object pose estimation methods often require extensive training data for each specific object, limiting their applicability to new, unseen objects. The authors aim to overcome this limitation by developing a system that can accurately estimate the pose of any novel object, given its CAD model at inference time, without prior training on that specific object. This "zero-shot" capability is crucial for flexible and adaptable robotic systems.

2. **Addressing Limitations of RGB-D vs. RGB-only Systems**: While RGB-D methods have demonstrated strong performance in novel object pose estimation due to the geometric information from depth, their reliance on expensive and sometimes cumbersome depth sensors can hinder practical deployment. The research is motivated by the need for high-performing RGB-only solutions, which are more cost-effective and versatile for real-world scenarios.

3. **Improving Correspondence Quality in RGB-only Approaches**: Existing RGB-only methods often struggle with establishing accurate and robust pixel-to-pixel correspondences between an observed object and its templates. Simple feature matching can yield noisy and outlier-prone correspondences, leading to inaccurate pose calculations. The authors specifically aim to develop a method that systematically refines these correspondences to enhance their quality and subsequently, the accuracy of the estimated 6D pose.

4. **Enabling Precise Robotic Manipulation**: Accurate 6D object pose estimation is a foundational requirement for numerous robotic tasks, including precise manipulation, grasping, and interaction. By improving the accuracy of pose estimation for novel objects, the proposed framework seeks to directly contribute to the reliability and effectiveness of robotic systems operating in unstructured or dynamic environments.

In summary, the core motivation is to achieve high-accuracy, zero-shot novel object pose estimation using only RGB input, by focusing on a progressive refinement of pixel-to-pixel correspondences, thereby enhancing the practicality and deployment potential of robotic applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PicoPose proposes a novel framework for generalizable pixel-to-pixel correspondence learning, structured into three progressive stages, to achieve precise 6D pose estimation of novel objects from RGB images.

**Overall Architecture:**
Given an RGB image of a cluttered scene and an object's CAD model (unseen during training), PicoPose first renders object templates from various viewpoints of the CAD model. A zero-shot segmentation technique (e.g., CNOS) is then used to detect and crop the target object from the RGB scene. Both the cropped observation and the rendered templates are resized to a fixed resolution (H x W). PicoPose then utilizes these inputs to identify the best-matched template and progressively learns pixel-to-pixel correspondences, which are subsequently used with PnP/RANSAC to compute the 6D object pose.

**Feature Extraction:**
PicoPose employs a ViT-L backbone, pre-trained by DINOv2, to extract patch features (F_I for the observation, {F_T_i} for templates).

**Progressive Pixel-to-Pixel Correspondence Learning:**

1. **Stage 1: Feature Matching for Coarse Correspondences**
 - **Objective**: To identify the best-matched template and establish an initial set of coarse correspondences.
 - **Approach**: The system calculates a template matching score (c_i) for each template (T_i) by averaging the maximum cosine similarities between foreground patches in the RGB observation (I) and all patches in T_i. The template with the highest score is selected as the best match (T). Coarse correspondences are then derived by finding the most similar patch in T for each patch in I.
 - **Loss Function**: InfoNCE loss is used to train feature matching, similar to prior work.

2. **Stage 2: Global Transformation Estimation for Smooth Correspondences**
 - **Objective**: To smooth the coarse correspondences, filter outliers, and align the template's feature map with the observation using a global 2D affine transformation.
 - **Approach**: The normalized feature vectors from Stage 1 (F_I and F_T) are used to compute a correspondence map A = F_I (F_T)^T. This N x N map (where N is the number of patches) is reshaped and passed through stacked convolutions to reduce spatial dimensions, yielding a global pose vector. Three parallel Multi-layer Perceptrons (MLPs) then regress the parameters of a 2D affine transformation (M): in-plane rotation angle (α), relative scale (s), and 2D translation (t_u, t_v). Applying M to transform the template facilitates smoother pixel alignments.
 - **Loss Function**: L_smooth combines a geodesic distance for the rotation angle and L1 distances for the logarithmic scale and 2D translations.

3. **Stage 3: Local Refinement for Fine Correspondences**
 - **Objective**: To further refine correspondences with fine-grained, local adjustments, building upon the smooth correspondences from Stage 2.
 - **Approach**: The 2D affine transformation M from Stage 2 is used to calculate initial smooth correspondences (P) for all pixels in I to T. Dense Prediction Transformer (DPT) generates L hierarchical feature maps for both I and T. L offset regression blocks iteratively update P. In each block (l):
 - The current P is resized and scaled (P_l).
 - P_l is used as indices to gather features from F_T_l, creating a transformed feature map F'_T_l aligned with F_I_l.
 - A Correlation Lookup module (inspired by RAFT) generates a correlation feature map F_C_l.
 - F_I_l, F'_T_l, and F_C_l are concatenated and fed into stacked convolutions to regress local coordinate offsets (∆P_l) and a certainty map (S_l).
 - ∆P_l is interpolated, scaled, and added to P for updating. The certainty map S_l (upsampled to C'_l) indicates confidence.
 - **Loss Function**: L_fine uses an L1 distance for the coordinate offsets, masked by the ground truth certainty map, and binary cross-entropy for the certainty map itself.

**Pose Computation:**
For each foreground pixel in I whose correspondence certainty exceeds a threshold (0.5), the refined 2D position in P is mapped to its corresponding 3D surface point on the CAD model. These 2D-3D pairs are then used by PnP/RANSAC (specifically, the EPnP algorithm) to compute the final 6D object pose.

**Training:**
PicoPose is trained end-to-end on synthetic datasets (ShapeNet-Objects and Google-Scanned-Objects) by optimizing a combined loss function: L = L_coarse + L_smooth + L_fine. Data augmentation, including random 2D translation, in-plane rotation, and scale noise, is applied to ground truth affine transformations during Stage 3 training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

PicoPose was evaluated on the seven core datasets of the BOP benchmark (LM-O, T-LESS, TUD-L, IC-BIN, ITODD, HB, and YCB-V) and compared against existing methods. The mean Average Recall (AR) across Visible Surface Discrepancy (VSD), Maximum Symmetry-Aware Surface Distance (MSSD), and Maximum Symmetry-Aware Projection Distance (MSPD) metrics was reported.

#### 4.1. Comparisons with Existing Methods

- **Performance without Iterative Refinement**: PicoPose demonstrated performance metrics surpassing previously reported methods. When using a single pose hypothesis, PicoPose achieved a mean AR of 47.5%, exceeding FoundPose (42.6%), GigaPose (25.6%), MegaPose (20.8%), and GenFlow (23.5%). With five pose hypotheses, PicoPose attained a mean AR of 49.2%, outperforming FoundPose (44.1%) and GigaPose (26.8%).
- **Performance with Iterative Refinement**: When integrated with the iterative refinement approach from MegaPose, PicoPose continued to exhibit strong performance. With one hypothesis, PicoPose achieved a mean AR of 58.8%, compared to FoundPose (55.0%) and GigaPose (54.7%). Using five hypotheses, PicoPose reached a mean AR of 59.9%, again exceeding FoundPose (59.6%) and GigaPose (57.8%). This indicates that PicoPose's initial pose estimates are robust, providing a strong foundation for further refinement.
- **Qualitative Results**: Visualizations indicated that PicoPose rendered 3D models with superior overlap rates compared to MegaPose, GigaPose, and FoundPose on various BOP datasets, suggesting more accurate 6D pose estimations.
- **Single Object Reference Images**: In a challenging scenario using only one reference image per object (reconstructing the CAD model with Wonder3D), PicoPose achieved a mean AR of 20.4% (1 hypothesis) and 22.0% (5 hypotheses) on LM-O, demonstrating its capability to handle such extreme settings, comparable to GigaPose.
- **Runtime Analysis**: PicoPose demonstrated comparable per-image processing times to GigaPose (0.659s vs. 0.631s for 1 hypothesis on an RTX 3090, including segmentation and pose estimation), despite its higher accuracy. This suggests an efficient design, particularly when contrasted with FoundPose, which recorded significantly higher computational costs (3.360s on a Tesla P100) due to its larger template library.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies and Analyses

- **Efficacy of Progressive Correspondence Learning**:
 - **Correspondence Quality and Pose Accuracy**: Evaluation across the three stages (Stage 1, Stage 2, Stage 3) demonstrated a progressive improvement in correspondence quality (decreasing End-Point Error, EPE) and consequently, in 6D pose estimation accuracy (increasing AR). Stage 3, with its local refinement, yielded the highest AR (48.2%) and lowest EPE (2.4) across LM-O, T-LESS, and YCB-V, compared to Stage 1 (AR 32.4%, EPE 4.2) and Stage 2 (AR 34.1%, EPE 3.1).
 - **Impact of Stage 2**: Stage 2 significantly improved translation accuracy by smoothing coarse correspondences and filtering outliers from Stage 1. While its 6D pose enhancement over Stage 1 was noted as marginal by the authors (due to the lack of viewpoint rotation updates), it provided crucial smooth correspondences for the subsequent refinement stage.
- **Efficacy of Stage 2 Design**:
 - **Input Representation**: Learning the affine transformation from a correspondence map (as in PicoPose) proved more effective (AR 34.1%) than using concatenated features (AR 27.9%), suggesting that explicitly modeling coarse correspondences better captures affine transformation variations.
 - **Necessity of Stage 2**: Directly inputting noisy coarse correspondences from Stage 1 into Stage 3 resulted in lower AR (34.5%) compared to using Stage 2's smooth correspondences (46.3%). Using predicted poses from Stage 1 for initialization was more accurate (45.7%) but less efficient.
- **Efficacy of Stacked Offset Regression Blocks in Stage 3**: The performance of Stage 3 progressively improved with the use of more offset regression blocks. Using all three blocks (corresponding to feature spatial sizes 16×16, 32×32, 64×64) achieved the highest AR (48.2%), indicating that iterative local refinement contributes to finer correspondences.
- **Effects of Correlation Lookup in Stage 3**: Including the correlation lookup operation in Stage 3 led to a significant improvement in mean AR (48.2%) compared to its absence (35.9%), confirming its role in providing explicit correlation degrees that facilitate offset learning.
- **Influence of Number of Templates**: Performance for both PicoPose and GigaPose generally improved with an increased number of templates. Notably, PicoPose showed greater effectiveness with fewer templates, achieving higher AR scores than GigaPose across comparable template counts (e.g., PicoPose with 2 templates: 12.5% AR vs. GigaPose with 2 templates: 3.7% AR).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PicoPose's introduction of a progressive pixel-to-pixel correspondence learning framework for RGB-based novel object pose estimation presents several significant contributions and potential impacts:

- **Enhanced Zero-Shot Generalization**: The research demonstrates a notable improvement in zero-shot generalization capabilities for novel object pose estimation using only RGB images. This advancement reduces the need for object-specific training data, enabling robotic systems to operate effectively with previously unseen objects without extensive recalibration or retraining.
- **Improved Accuracy in RGB-Only Settings**: By addressing the challenge of noisy correspondences through its three-stage progressive refinement process, PicoPose achieves performance metrics that surpass existing RGB-only methods on standard benchmarks. This increased accuracy is crucial for tasks requiring precise interaction, such as grasping and manipulation, where even small pose errors can lead to failure.
- **Practicality and Cost-Effectiveness**: The reliance solely on RGB images, rather than depth information, makes PicoPose a more practical and cost-effective solution for various real-world robotic applications. This can facilitate broader adoption in industries where depth sensors might be too expensive, complex to integrate, or unsuitable due to environmental conditions.
- **Efficiency and Scalability**: PicoPose maintains competitive inference speeds while delivering higher accuracy, making it suitable for deployment in scenarios that require real-time or near real-time performance. Its ability to achieve strong results with fewer templates than some existing methods also suggests potential for scalability and reduced computational overhead in template generation and matching.
- **Foundational for Robotic Manipulation**: The robust and accurate 6D pose estimation provided by PicoPose directly supports critical robotic tasks. As demonstrated in simulated robotic grasping experiments, the precise pose estimates enable effective interaction with novel objects, paving the way for more autonomous and versatile robotic systems in areas like industrial assembly, bin picking, and auto depalletizing.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
Despite its advancements, the paper acknowledges several limitations:
1. **Template Reliance**: PicoPose's performance is still tied to the quantity and quality of object templates used for 3D representation. Future work could explore more efficient template utilization or alternative 3D representations.
2. **Refinement Gains**: While PicoPose benefits from external iterative refinement methods (like MegaPose's refiner), the performance gains are noted as modest, suggesting that PicoPose's initial estimates are already quite strong. This indicates a potential for specialized refinement approaches tailored to high-quality initial predictions.
3. **Single Reference Image Quality**: When only a single reference image is available, the quality of the reconstructed 3D CAD model (e.g., via Wonder3D) acts as a limiting factor. Integrating advanced neural reconstruction techniques could mitigate this issue.

Overall, PicoPose represents a notable step forward in RGB-based novel object pose estimation, offering a robust and practical solution for zero-shot generalization in robotic manipulation.
