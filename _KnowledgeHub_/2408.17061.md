# Robotic Object Insertion with a Soft Wrist through Sim-to-Real Privileged Training

> [!summary] Summary
> OMRON SINIC X Corporation researchers introduced a sim-to-real privileged training framework for compliant robotic object insertion using a soft wrist. The method achieved high success rates, including 100% for circular peg insertion with zero misalignment, and demonstrated generalization to unseen square peg shapes on a physical robot without real-world training, reducing engineering efforts for complex assembly.

## Problem

- Achieving robust, precise object insertion, like peg-in-hole, in unstructured environments with significant uncertainties using traditional rigid robots.
- Controlling soft robots effectively for contact-rich tasks due to their complex, partially observable dynamics and the need for expensive external sensing systems.
- The high cost, time, and safety risks associated with collecting sufficient real-world data for training reinforcement learning policies in complex robotic manipulation tasks.

## Method

- A two-phase sim-to-real privileged training framework: a "teacher" policy learns the insertion task in simulation with access to full state information.
- A "student" Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) is subsequently trained to infer the teacher's privileged information solely from observable robot sensor history.
- Extensive domain randomization during training, along with a custom MuJoCo simulation environment modeling the soft wrist's compliance, to enable robust zero-shot transfer to a physical robot without real-world fine-tuning.

## Results

- The method achieved high success rates on a physical robot for circular peg insertion, demonstrating **100%** success for 0° misalignment and **95%** for +5° misalignment across 20 trials.
- Policies generalized to unseen square pegs with **70%** success at 0° misalignment on the physical robot, showcasing robustness to novel object geometries not present during training.
- Comparative simulation experiments confirmed the superior performance of the privileged training approach over unprivileged baselines and highlighted the critical role of the "alignment" state for effective student learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The sim-to-real privileged training framework effectively addresses the dual challenges of soft robot partial observability and the real-world data burden for RL, enabling autonomous control from proprioceptive sensors.
> - Decomposing the learning problem into teacher-student stages (privileged training) significantly boosts learning efficiency and success rates compared to direct unprivileged RL for complex manipulation.
> - Domain randomization across key parameters like grasp angle, hole position, and controller gains is essential for generating policies robust enough to achieve zero-shot transfer to a physical robot and handle real-world uncertainties.
