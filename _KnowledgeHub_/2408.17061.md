# Robotic Object Insertion with a Soft Wrist through Sim-to-Real Privileged Training

> [!summary] Summary
> OMRON SINIC X Corporation researchers introduced a sim-to-real privileged training framework for compliant robotic object insertion using a soft wrist. The method achieved high success rates, including 100% for circular peg insertion with zero misalignment, and demonstrated generalization to unseen square peg shapes on a physical robot without real-world training, reducing engineering efforts for complex assembly.

## Problem

- Achieving robust, precise object insertion, like peg-in-hole, in unstructured environments with significant uncertainties using traditional rigid robots.
- Controlling soft robots effectively for contact-rich tasks due to their complex, partially observable dynamics and the need for expensive external sensing systems.
- The high cost, time, and safety risks associated with collecting sufficient real-world data for training reinforcement learning policies in complex robotic manipulation tasks.

## Method

- A two-phase sim-to-real privileged training framework: a "teacher" policy learns the insertion task in simulation with access to full state information.
- A "student" Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) is subsequently trained to infer the teacher's privileged information solely from observable robot sensor history.
- Extensive domain randomization during training, along with a custom MuJoCo simulation environment modeling the soft wrist's compliance, to enable robust zero-shot transfer to a physical robot without real-world fine-tuning.

## Results

- The method achieved high success rates on a physical robot for circular peg insertion, demonstrating **100%** success for 0° misalignment and **95%** for +5° misalignment across 20 trials.
- Policies generalized to unseen square pegs with **70%** success at 0° misalignment on the physical robot, showcasing robustness to novel object geometries not present during training.
- Comparative simulation experiments confirmed the superior performance of the privileged training approach over unprivileged baselines and highlighted the critical role of the "alignment" state for effective student learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The sim-to-real privileged training framework effectively addresses the dual challenges of soft robot partial observability and the real-world data burden for RL, enabling autonomous control from proprioceptive sensors.
> - Decomposing the learning problem into teacher-student stages (privileged training) significantly boosts learning efficiency and success rates compared to direct unprivileged RL for complex manipulation.
> - Domain randomization across key parameters like grasp angle, hole position, and controller gains is essential for generating policies robust enough to achieve zero-shot transfer to a physical robot and handle real-world uncertainties.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: **soft robotics, contact-rich manipulation, reinforcement learning (RL), and sim-to-real transfer learning**. Its primary contribution is addressing the long-standing challenge of performing precise, compliant object insertion tasks in unstructured environments without extensive human intervention or specialized sensing hardware.

Traditionally, contact-rich tasks like peg-in-hole insertion have been addressed by rigid robots employing force control. However, these systems often struggle with high compliance requirements due to limited servo controller bandwidth, making them less robust to significant uncertainties. **Soft robots**, with their inherent passive compliance, offer a promising alternative by enabling safer and more robust contact interactions. Despite these advantages, soft robots introduce their own set of challenges, particularly in control due to their complex nonlinear dynamics and the difficulty in directly observing their deformable states (partial observability). Previous attempts to control soft robots often relied on external sensing systems like motion capture or complex Finite Element Method (FEM) models for pose estimation, which are impractical for industrial deployment due to calibration demands and occlusion issues.

In parallel, **reinforcement learning (RL)** has emerged as a powerful paradigm for learning complex robot control policies. Recent RL approaches for insertion tasks have utilized force/torque (FT) sensors for contact localization. However, these methods typically demand extensive and often unsafe real-world data collection, or rely on heuristics and human demonstrations, limiting their scalability and practicality.

The concept of **sim-to-real transfer** aims to bridge the gap between simulation and the real world, allowing policies learned in simulation to be deployed on physical robots. This is particularly attractive for RL, as it avoids the pitfalls of real-world data collection. For soft robots, sim-to-real transfer has been demonstrated, but often with caveats such as relying on accurate FEM models or external vision systems for state estimation.

This paper distinguishes itself by employing a **privileged training strategy** (also known as teacher-student learning) for sim-to-real transfer in the context of soft robot object insertion. Privileged training has previously been successfully applied to other robotic tasks, such as quadrupedal locomotion, aerial robotics, and general manipulation, primarily for predicting external interaction states or object properties. However, this study marks its *first application* to a soft robot's object insertion task, specifically interpreting it as a method to estimate internal soft robot states (like peg pose relative to the wrist) that are not directly observable. Unlike some prior sim-to-real works with soft grippers that didn't model compliant dynamics, this research develops a simulation environment that explicitly accounts for the soft wrist's compliance using MuJoCo's spring-damper systems, thereby improving the fidelity of the simulation for contact-rich interactions. By doing so, the authors aim to overcome the dual challenges of soft robot control (partial observability) and RL deployment (real-world data burden) simultaneously, pushing the boundaries of autonomous and adaptable robotic assembly.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this study is to enable **contact-rich object insertion tasks, specifically peg-in-hole, using a robot equipped with a soft wrist in unstructured environments**. This goal is driven by a desire to significantly reduce the engineering overhead currently associated with industrial assembly tasks.

The key motivations and specific objectives can be broken down as follows:

- **Alleviate Engineering Efforts:** Current industrial assembly often necessitates elaborate calibration procedures and the development of specialized jigs to precisely define object and hole poses. This study aims to eliminate or substantially reduce these requirements, making robotic assembly more flexible and less resource-intensive.
- **Handle Environmental Uncertainties:** Real-world environments are inherently uncertain. The paper specifically addresses **uncertainties in object grasp pose** (the exact position and orientation of the peg within the gripper) and **hole pose** (the exact location of the target hole). The ability to cope with such variations without prior calibration is a critical objective for practical robotic deployment.
- **Leverage Soft Robotics for Safety and Compliance:** The use of a soft wrist is motivated by its inherent passive compliance, which facilitates safe contact interactions and enables the robot to deform and adapt to uncertainties during insertion. This compliance is essential for robust operation where rigid robots would struggle or potentially damage components.
- **Overcome Soft Robot Observability Challenges:** A significant challenge with soft robots is the difficulty in directly measuring their deformed state or the exact pose of the grasped object relative to the end-effector, especially without external sensing. A core objective is to achieve control without relying on expensive and often problematic external sensors like motion capture systems.
- **Reduce Real-World Data Collection Burden:** Traditional reinforcement learning methods require vast amounts of data, which is time-consuming, costly, and potentially unsafe to collect in the real world. A key objective is to propose a learning approach that minimizes or eliminates the need for real-world data collection for policy training.
- **Achieve Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer:** To realize the above objective, the study aims for zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, meaning a policy trained entirely in simulation can be directly deployed on a physical robot without further real-world fine-tuning. This requires robust simulation, effective domain randomization, and a learning framework capable of generalizing to real-world complexities.
- **Develop a Proprioceptive State Estimation and Control Strategy:** The method aims to simultaneously derive a control policy and an internal state estimation module (for privileged information) based solely on readily available on-robot sensors (arm pose, FT sensor readings), effectively enabling the robot to "sense" its internal state relative to the task.

In essence, the paper seeks to bridge the gap between the theoretical advantages of soft robotics and advanced learning algorithms with the practical demands of industrial automation, providing a robust, autonomous, and cost-effective solution for complex contact-rich manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology is a **sim-to-real privileged training strategy**, a two-phase learning process designed to enable robust contact-rich object insertion with a soft wrist while minimizing real-world data collection.

**A. Problem Setting and Simulation Environment**
The system comprises a Universal Robotics UR5e arm, a Robotiq Hand-E gripper, and a custom soft wrist connection modeled after three springs. The objective is to insert a grasped peg into a hole. Crucially, the system operates under uncertainties: unknown in-grasp peg pose and unknown hole location. The controller only has access to on-robot sensor data: wrist pose (from the UR5e) and a 6-axis Force/Torque (FT) sensor mounted above the soft wrist. The soft wrist's passive DOFs and the peg's true pose are not directly observable on the real robot.

To enable sim-to-real transfer, the authors developed a custom **MuJoCo simulator** within the Robosuite framework. The soft wrist mechanism is modeled as a series of four springs with specified stiffness and damping constants, providing one linear and three rotational passive DOFs. While a more accurate system identification could be performed, parameters were set to visually approximate the physical robot's behavior, emphasizing robustness to approximate modeling rather than perfect fidelity. Hole geometries are decomposed into convex meshes for precise contact simulation.

**B. Phase 1: Privileged Teacher Training**
In this initial phase, a "teacher" policy learns to solve the peg-in-hole task within the simulator, having access to "privileged information" that is unavailable to the real robot.

1. **Markov Decision Process (MDP) Formulation:**
 - **Algorithm:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with simple 2-layer 256x256 Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) actor and critic networks.
 - **State:** The teacher policy receives:
 - **Sensor Observation ($o_t$):** An R^6 vector comprising wrist position ($p_{wrist}$) and force ($F_{wrist}$) from the simulated FT sensor. Rotational components were omitted from the command space, as translational motions were found sufficient given the soft wrist's passive rotational DOFs.
 - **Privileged Information ($x_t$):** An R^10 vector including the peg's true position ($p_{peg}$), orientation ($\theta_{peg}$ represented by the first two columns of the SO(3) rotation matrix for regression stability), and a binary alignment state ($b_{align}$).
 - **Action ($a_t$):** End-effector position changes ($\Delta p_{wrist}$) along the x, y, and z axes, with a maximum magnitude of 3mm over a 50ms interval (20Hz policy frequency). These commands are converted into smooth linear trajectories using Robosuite's interpolator and an Operational Space Control (OSC) to compute joint torques. OSC parameters were increased and randomized to account for sim-real differences.
 - **Reward Function:** A composite reward $r = r_p - r_i - r_a + r_s$ is used:
 - **Progress Reward ($r_p$):** Encourages reducing the weighted distance between the peg tip and the hole.
 - **Insertion Reward ($r_i$):** Penalizes vertical movement unless an "alignment condition" is met, encouraging horizontal search until the hole is found.
 - **Action Smoothness Reward ($r_a$):** Prevents jerky motions, important for sim-to-real transfer.
 - **Sparse Success Reward ($r_s$):** A bonus for successful insertion (peg tip within 5mm of the hole).
 - **Alignment Condition ($b_{align}$):** A binary state that is true when the horizontal positional error of the peg is below 7mm. This condition is critical for preventing the policy from getting stuck in local minima by applying excessive vertical force before proper alignment.
 - **Termination:** Episodes terminate upon successful insertion, if the peg moves significantly away from the hole (indicating a local minimum), or after a maximum of 200 timesteps (10 seconds).

**C. Phase 2: Student Training**
Once the teacher policy has converged, the second phase involves training a "student" encoder network.

1. **Goal:** The student network learns to estimate the privileged information ($x_t$) directly from the robot's sensor history ($o_t$).
2. **Architecture:** A Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) is used, taking a sequence of 20 past sensor observations (1 second of data) as input.
3. **Training:** The student encoder is trained using supervised learning. Data is collected by running the learned teacher policy in the simulator. The loss function combines Mean Squared Error (MSE) for peg pose estimation and Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) for the alignment state prediction, guiding the student to accurately infer the privileged information from limited sensor data. Unlike some prior work, this study directly regresses on physical quantities rather than latent embeddings, which aids interpretability.

**D. Sim-to-Real Transfer Mechanisms**
To ensure the learned policies transfer effectively from simulation to the real robot, several techniques are employed:

- **Domain Randomization:** Applied during both teacher and student training, this technique systematically varies simulation parameters to improve policy robustness:
 - In-grasp peg angle (±5°).
 - Hole position (±10mm).
 - OSC controller gains (uniformly on a logarithmic scale between $10^3$ and $10^4$) to account for the discrepancy between the simulated torque-controlled robot and the physically stiff, position-controlled UR5e.
 - Initial robot pose (Gaussian noise to all six joints).
- **Real Robot Controller:** The physical robot uses Python interfaces to Universal Robot's ROS drivers, ensuring that position commands are executed and tracked at the consistent 20Hz update frequency matching the simulation, which was found crucial for successful transfer despite control differences.

This comprehensive approach, blending a compliant physical design with sophisticated learning and robust simulation techniques, underpins the study's success in tackling complex manipulation challenges.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling results from both real robot and simulation experiments, demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed sim-to-real privileged training approach for contact-rich object insertion with a soft wrist.

**A. Sim-to-Real Peg-in-Hole Insertion (Real Robot Experiments)**
The real robot experiments validated the zero-shot transfer capability of the trained policies under various uncertainties (grasp and hole pose, randomized by ±10mm shift from a default position). Policies were trained exclusively with a circular peg.

- **Circular Peg Insertion:**
 - For 0° peg misalignment: 100% success rate (20/20 trials).
 - For +5° peg misalignment: 95% success rate (19/20 trials).
 - For -5° peg misalignment: 80% success rate (16/20 trials).
 These high success rates, achieved with the best-performing network selected from simulation evaluation, underscore the robustness of the method to typical manufacturing uncertainties and the effectiveness of the soft wrist's compliance.
- **Square Peg Insertion (Unseen Shape):** The policies demonstrated a remarkable ability to generalize to a square peg, which was never encountered during training.
 - For 0° peg misalignment: 70% success rate (14/20, best network) and 45% (9/20, median network).
 - For +5° peg misalignment: 45% success rate (9/20, best network) and 10% (2/20, median network).
 - For -5° peg misalignment: 55% success rate (11/20, best network) and 85% (17/20, median network).
 While performance decreased for the unseen square peg, particularly with misalignment, the ability to generalize at all is a significant finding. Interestingly, the median-performing network sometimes outperformed the best-performing one in specific misalignment cases (-5° square peg), indicating variability in learned policies and their suitability for different conditions. Snapshots and an accompanying video visually confirm the successful zero-shot transfer and the real-time visualization of the student encoder's estimated alignment state.

**B. Effectiveness of Privileged Training (Simulation Experiments)**
A comparative study in simulation highlighted the benefits of the two-stage privileged training setup.

- **Privileged vs. Baseline:** The proposed "student" method (teacher-student) consistently achieved significantly higher success rates across all tested peg shapes (circular, square, triangular, hexagonal) compared to a "TCN" baseline, which used a single RL training stage on simulated sensor data without privileged information. This demonstrates the clear advantage of decomposing the learning problem.
- **Importance of Alignment State:** Removing the "alignment" state from the privileged information ("no alignment" case) led to a noticeable degradation in student performance, emphasizing the crucial role of this specific signal in guiding the learning process.
- **Learning Curve:** The learning curves showed that the privileged teacher training converged faster and achieved higher rewards than the unprivileged TCN policy, even with the TCN's potentially more complex architecture. This confirms that privileged information helps accelerate and stabilize RL training.
- **Sim-to-Real Gap Discussion:** The simulation success rates were generally lower than the real robot results. This is attributed to the purposefully harsher training environment in simulation, where dynamics parameters (like joint controller gains) were randomized more extensively to encourage robustness, while the physical robot's parameters are likely more fixed.

**C. Importance of Domain Randomization (Sim-to-Sim Evaluation)**
Further simulation evaluations underscored the critical role of domain randomization.

- Policies trained with specific randomizations turned off (e.g., "fixed angle," "fixed hole," "fixed stiffness") showed significantly degraded performance when subsequently evaluated in a fully randomized environment.
- This directly confirms that domain randomization, particularly for grasp and hole pose uncertainties, is essential for policies to adapt to variations encountered during real-world deployment.
- An interesting observation was that the teacher policy's performance in simulation was not significantly degraded when the alignment state was removed from its privileged information ("no alignment" vs. "all" in Fig. 8), unlike the student's performance (Fig. 6). This suggests that the alignment state's primary benefit is in aiding the *student encoder* to accurately infer critical task states from imperfect sensor data, which then feeds into the teacher policy's successful execution when operating in the real world.

**D. Student Network Estimation Quality**
The analysis of the student encoder's predictions against ground truth values in simulation confirmed its effectiveness.

- The student encoder produced generally high-quality estimates of the peg's position, orientation, and especially the binary alignment state.
- The ability to directly regress on physical quantities (as opposed to latent embeddings) proved beneficial for interpretability and debugging, allowing direct visualization of what the student network "believes" to be the peg's state.

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate that sim-to-real privileged training, augmented by careful simulation design and domain randomization, offers a powerful and practical solution for robust, contact-rich manipulation tasks with soft robots, achieving high success rates and generalization capabilities on physical hardware.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for tasks involving contact and compliance, with broad implications for industrial automation, soft robotics, and reinforcement learning.

**A. Core Contribution and Technical Significance:**
The primary significance lies in being the **first study to successfully apply a sim-to-real privileged training framework for contact-rich object insertion tasks using a robot with a soft wrist**. This addresses a critical gap by combining the advantages of compliant hardware with a data-efficient and robust learning paradigm.

- **Bridging Soft Robotics and Learning:** The work effectively overcomes key challenges inherent to soft robots—their complex nonlinear dynamics and partial observability of their deformed state. By leveraging privileged information in simulation, the learning algorithm can access "ground truth" states (like the true peg pose relative to the wrist) that are impossible to measure directly on a real soft robot without expensive external sensors. The student network then learns to infer these critical states from available on-robot sensors (arm pose, FT readings), making the system truly autonomous.
- **Robustness to Uncertainty:** The approach demonstrates high success rates for object insertion despite significant uncertainties in grasp pose and hole location. This robustness is achieved through the synergistic combination of passive compliance from the soft wrist, sophisticated learning from contact interactions, and extensive domain randomization during training, which prepares the policy for real-world variability.
- **Reduced Engineering Overhead:** By achieving zero-shot sim-to-real transfer without requiring specialized external sensing hardware (like motion capture) or laborious, task-specific calibration procedures, the method dramatically reduces the engineering effort typically associated with deploying robots for precision assembly. This is a crucial step towards more adaptable and less costly robotic systems.
- **Novel Application of Privileged Learning:** The study extends the application of privileged learning beyond external state estimation (e.g., terrain properties) to internal state estimation within a highly deformable robotic body, offering a new interpretation and demonstrating its versatility. The finding that including an explicit "alignment state" in privileged information significantly boosts student performance is a valuable insight for future applications of this framework.

**B. Potential Impact:**

- **Industrial Automation and Assembly:** The most direct impact is on industrial assembly. The ability to perform delicate peg-in-hole tasks with high success rates under uncertainty and without extensive calibration can revolutionize manufacturing processes. This could enable more flexible production lines, rapid deployment of robots for new tasks, and reduced downtime, ultimately leading to greater efficiency and cost savings. It allows robots to operate in less structured environments, akin to human workers who adapt to slight variations.
- **Advancement in Soft Robotics:** This work provides a robust control and state estimation methodology for soft robots, addressing a long-standing barrier to their wider adoption in precise manipulation tasks. By demonstrating how to effectively control and interpret the state of a compliant system using standard sensors, it opens doors for more complex applications of soft robots in fields ranging from manufacturing to healthcare.
- **Reinforcement Learning for Real-World Tasks:** The success of the sim-to-real privileged training paradigm reinforces its potential as a powerful tool for deploying RL policies in real-world scenarios. It offers a practical blueprint for tackling complex robotic problems where real-world data collection is prohibitive, showing how to leverage the richness of simulation without demanding perfect fidelity.
- **Enhanced Safety and Adaptability:** The inherent compliance of the soft wrist, combined with a policy learned to navigate contact, means the robot is more tolerant to unforeseen impacts and can interact more safely with its environment, including potentially with humans. This increases the robot's adaptability to dynamic and unpredictable situations.

**C. Future Directions:**
The paper also outlines promising avenues for future research. Further reducing the sim-to-real gap through more sophisticated randomization of physical parameters (e.g., spring stiffness, damping, surface friction) or even including randomization parameters in the privileged information could lead to even more robust policies. Encouraging faster task completion through adjusted reward functions could address failure modes like the peg "missing" the hole. Extending the methodology to handle tighter tolerance parts or complex electrical connectors would further demonstrate its applicability to advanced industrial challenges.

In conclusion, this research is a pivotal step towards realizing truly autonomous and adaptable robotic manipulation in complex, contact-rich scenarios. By cleverly integrating soft robotics with advanced learning, it promises to significantly impact industrial automation and push the boundaries of what robots can achieve in unstructured environments.
