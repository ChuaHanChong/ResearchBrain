# Vision-Language Models for Deployable Social Robot Navigation: Bridging Semantic Reasoning and Low-Level Control

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey paper provides a unified system-level formulation for Vision-Language Model (VLM)-based social robot navigation (SRN), categorizing existing integration approaches into five distinct paradigms. It highlights current research gaps and outlines key challenges for deploying socially compliant robots in human environments, emphasizing the need for robust bridging between high-level reasoning and low-level control.

## Problem

- Robots operating in human-centered environments require sophisticated social reasoning and adherence to social norms, which extends beyond traditional geometric path planning.
- A significant challenge involves effectively bridging the semantic understanding and reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models with the real-time, safety-critical demands of low-level robot control.
- A lack of a unified framework and standardized evaluation protocols hinders the systematic development and deployment of VLM-enabled social robot navigation systems.

## Method

- The paper proposes a ==hierarchical system-level formulation== for VLM-based SRN, explicitly defining ==high-level VLM reasoning==, a ==bridging module== for translation, and low-level planning and control.
- It presents a structured roadmap by categorizing current VLM-SRN approaches into ==five distinct paradigms== based on how VLMs interface with navigation systems (e.g., as reasoners, evaluators, for semantic mapping, intermediate representations, or action interfaces).
- A comprehensive analysis of existing datasets and evaluation platforms is provided, consolidating resources and identifying gaps in the context of VLM-based social robot navigation.

## Results

- The survey established a unified system formulation that details the inputs, outputs, and interactions between VLM reasoning, the bridging module, and low-level control in a closed-loop execution.
- It categorized current VLM-SRN research into **five coupling paradigms**, illustrating diverse interface designs for integrating VLMs into robot navigation systems.
- **Seven major open challenges** were identified, including the need for higher-quality SRN datasets, robust social and spatial reasoning, improved reasoning-to-control grounding, real-time embodied control, and enhanced generalization across environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Hierarchical hybrid architectures are presented as a practical direction, leveraging VLMs for semantic reasoning while relying on specialized planners/controllers for real-time, safety-critical execution.
> - The 'bridging module' is critical for translating high-level, abstract VLM outputs (e.g., social norms, semantic goals) into concrete, planner-facing quantities like cost weights or constraints.
> - Deployable social robot navigation necessitates a shift towards human-centered evaluation metrics beyond geometric success, encompassing social compliance, legibility, human comfort, and cultural considerations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

This work addresses a critical integration challenge within the fields of social robot navigation (SRN) and vision-language models (VLMs). Social robot navigation, distinct from purely geometric path planning, necessitates robots to comprehend human intentions, adhere to social norms, and interpret contextual cues to generate socially compliant behaviors in human-centered environments. Traditionally, robot navigation has relied on metric planning and collision avoidance methods, which are robust for geometric tasks but often lack the semantic reasoning required for complex social interactions.

The emergence of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), often supported by Large Language Models (LLMs), has significantly advanced capabilities in high-level semantic understanding, commonsense reasoning, and natural language interaction. This development opens new avenues for SRN. However, a persistent challenge in the broader robotics landscape is effectively bridging the gap between these high-level, often abstract, VLM-based reasoning capabilities and the real-time, safety-critical demands of low-level robot control and action execution.

Existing surveys in social navigation and VLM-oriented research tend to focus on these areas in isolation. Social navigation surveys typically cover socially aware motion generation, while VLM surveys concentrate on vision-language reasoning. This paper positions itself by systematically examining the "bridging interface"—the mechanisms that translate high-level VLM reasoning into deployable, socially compliant mobile robot motion. It highlights the rapid growth of VLM-enabled SRN research, as indicated by publication trends, and underscores the need for standardized evaluation protocols for deployment-oriented performance. By proposing a unified system perspective and a structured roadmap, this survey aims to integrate these two previously disparate domains, moving VLM-based SRN towards practical and reliable deployment.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary motivation for this survey stems from the accelerating deployment of robots in human-centered environments, such as healthcare facilities, educational settings, and public sidewalks. In these shared spaces, robot navigation extends beyond mere geometric path planning; it demands that robots generate motion that is smooth, legible, and socially acceptable to humans. This includes understanding and respecting proxemic norms, context-dependent social conventions, and human intentions, rather than simply treating humans as dynamic obstacles.

The authors emphasize that a robot's navigation success must be measured not only by efficiency in reaching a destination but also by human-centered outcomes such as user comfort, perceived safety, legibility of intent, and overall acceptance. Empirical studies indicate that robot navigation strategies directly influence human behavior, necessitating a shift in evaluation paradigms.

From a system engineering perspective, achieving such sophisticated social behaviors requires the integration of three core components:
1. **High-level reasoning:** To facilitate semantic understanding and social decision-making.
2. **Low-level planning and control:** To generate dynamically feasible and safe trajectories.
3. **A bridging mechanism:** To translate high-level reasoning into executable actions.

While recent advancements in VLMs and LLMs have considerably enhanced high-level reasoning, and classical or learning-based controllers provide effective low-level planning, the crucial connection between these two levels remains underdeveloped. This gap impedes the translation of semantic reasoning into grounded, socially compliant motion execution, thus hindering the practical deployment of social robots.

Specifically, the survey articulates its objectives as threefold:
1. **To establish a working definition of VLM-based SRN:** This definition frames VLM-based SRN as the process of translating high-level VLM reasoning into executable, safe, and socially compliant robot behavior, explicitly outlining the roles of high-level reasoning, low-level planning and control, and the bridging function.
2. **To propose a structured roadmap for coupling VLMs with social robot navigation systems:** This involves presenting a taxonomy of five paradigms that delineate how VLMs interface with navigation systems, organized by the connections between VLM reasoning and robot motion generation.
3. **To provide a comprehensive analysis of relevant datasets, evaluation platforms, and open challenges:** This aims to consolidate existing resources and identify the principal obstacles that limit the transition of VLM-based reasoning from theoretical concept to real-world deployment.

The overarching goal is to lay a foundational understanding for the development of reliable, socially compliant, and deployable VLM-enabled navigation systems.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The paper adopts a survey methodology, synthesizing existing research to propose a unified system-level formulation for VLM-based Social Robot Navigation (SRN) and categorizing current approaches into a structured roadmap.

**System-Level Formulation of VLM-Based SRN:**
The core of the methodology is a hierarchical abstraction that explicitly links high-level VLM reasoning to low-level planning and control through an intermediate bridging representation. This formulation maintains a separation of concerns: VLMs provide semantic and social structure, while the controller manages dynamic feasibility, safety, and execution.

1. **High-Level VLM Reasoning (Section 2.1):**
 - This module takes visual observations ($I_t$) and textual prompts/language instructions ($\rho_t$) as input.
 - A VLM ($\Phi_\theta$) processes these inputs to generate a high-level semantic signal ($B_t^h$).
 - $B_t^h$ is defined as a general interface space, potentially including:
 - Language reasoning or social-norm explanation ($r_t$).
 - A score or selector over candidate goals, paths, or actions ($q_t$).
 - A semantic or language-queryable map ($M_t$).
 - An intermediate representation like waypoints or attention maps ($z_t$).
 - A high-level or mid-level action ($a_h^t$).
 - Examples of existing systems are cited that instantiate different parts of this interface, such as VLM-based scoring, waypoint prediction, semantic map querying, and VLA action prediction.

2. **Bridging Definition (Section 2.2):**
 - The bridging module ($\Gamma_\omega$) translates the high-level VLM signal ($B_t^h$) and robot-side observations ($o_t$) into quantities directly usable by the planner or controller.
 - The output of the bridge, $\eta_t$, can include:
 - A semantic subgoal ($g_t$).
 - A compact social-context descriptor ($\psi_t$), encoding human occupancy, predicted motion patterns, interaction relations, or social norms.
 - A planner-facing reference behavior ($u_h^t$) derived from VLM action components.
 - Adaptive cost weights ($\lambda_t$).
 - Hard planning constraints ($K_t$).
 - The bridge also addresses the temporal mismatch between slower VLM inference and faster low-level control updates, acting as an asynchronous interface that refreshes $\eta_t$ when new VLM outputs are available.

3. **Low-Level Planning and Control (Section 2.3):**
 - Given $\eta_t$, the low-level planner optimizes an executable trajectory ($\tau_t$) by minimizing a navigation cost function ($C_{\eta_t}(\tau_t; o_t)$) subject to a feasible trajectory set ($F_t(\eta_t; o_t)$).
 - The feasible set incorporates constraints for collision avoidance, social clearance (minimum safety distance from predicted human trajectories), robot dynamics, and hard planning constraints from $K_t$.
 - The cost function is a weighted sum of several terms:
 - Goal-reaching ($C_{goal}$).
 - Obstacle avoidance ($C_{obstacle}$).
 - Socially inappropriate motion ($C_{social}$).
 - Consistency with VLM-derived reference behavior ($C_{vlm}$).
 - Legible intent expression ($C_{legibility}$).
 - Smooth and socially natural motion ($C_{naturalness}$).
 - The weights ($\lambda_t$) for these cost terms are also specified by the bridge.

4. **Closed-Loop Receding-Horizon Execution (Section 2.4):**
 - The first control input from the optimized trajectory ($u_t^*$) is executed.
 - This action leads to a new observation, which updates perception, human-motion prediction, high-level VLM reasoning, and the bridge, completing the closed-loop cycle.

**Coupling VLMs with SRN Systems (Roadmap - Section 3):**
The survey identifies five distinct paradigms for integrating VLMs into SRN, illustrating various interface designs:

1. **VLMs as High-Level Reasoners and Proposal Selectors (Section 3.1):** VLMs interpret semantic state, task progress, or skill choices, often by scoring or selecting from geometrically or learned feasible candidate goals, regions, waypoints, or paths. This paradigm benefits from improved spatial grounding.
2. **VLMs as Evaluators (Section 3.2):** VLMs judge candidate behaviors for consistency with task goals and social context, producing reward-like scores that bias downstream planning or optimization.
3. **VLMs for Semantic Map Grounding (Section 3.3):** VLMs are used to create queryable semantic maps by fusing vision-language features with 3D reconstructions, translating language instructions into map-localized spatial goals for navigation.
4. **VLMs as Intermediate Representations (Section 3.4):** VLM outputs serve as explicit (schemas, programs, waypoints, attention maps) or implicit (latent embeddings) planning representations that guide local planners or lightweight policies, connecting high-level reasoning to downstream control.
5. **VLMs as Action Interfaces (Section 3.5):** VLMs directly generate action tokens, commands, or structured action representations conditioned on visual observations and language instructions, representing a more tightly coupled approach to control.

**Efficient VLMs in Deployable SRN (Section 3.6):**
The survey discusses methods to address computational cost and inference latency, essential for deploying VLMs on resource-constrained robots. This includes studying pre-training choices, prompting strategies, lightweight models, sparse mixture-of-experts architectures, group competitive learning, and multi-action VLM designs.

**Datasets and Evaluation Platforms (Section 4):**
The authors review existing datasets (categorized by viewpoint, temporal horizon, language instruction, social context, and output annotation) and evaluation platforms (simulators and benchmarks). This analysis identifies gaps in datasets that jointly align perception, social annotations, planning representations, action labels, and human feedback. Evaluation platforms are assessed based on evaluation target, social factors, metrics, and application scenarios, highlighting the need for closed-loop human-robot interaction assessment beyond purely geometric metrics.

### 4. Main findings and results

The primary findings and results of this survey are presented as a structured synthesis of the current state of VLM-based Social Robot Navigation (SRN), rather than new experimental data.

1. **Unified System-Level Formulation for VLM-Based SRN:** The survey proposes a conceptual framework that systematically integrates high-level VLM reasoning with low-level planning and control through an explicit "bridging module." This formulation defines the inputs and outputs of each component (VLM reasoning, bridge, and low-level planner) and outlines how they interact in a closed-loop, receding-horizon execution. This framework clarifies the role of VLMs in providing semantic and social context, the bridge in translating these into planner-facing quantities (goals, social descriptors, cost weights, constraints), and the low-level planner in generating dynamically feasible and socially compliant trajectories.

2. **Taxonomy of VLM-SRN Coupling Paradigms:** The survey identifies and categorizes existing approaches into five distinct paradigms, offering a structured roadmap for how VLMs are integrated into SRN systems:
 - **VLMs as High-Level Reasoners and Proposal Selectors:** VLMs are used to understand semantic states, task progress, or make high-level skill choices, often by selecting among candidate paths or actions generated by other modules (e.g., LeLaN, Social-LLaVA).
 - **VLMs as Evaluators:** VLMs provide external judgment on robot behaviors, generating reward-like scores that influence downstream planning or optimization by acting as social cost terms (e.g., VLM-Social-Nav).
 - **VLMs for Semantic Map Grounding:** VLMs contribute to queryable semantic maps by integrating vision-language features with 3D scene reconstructions, enabling language-grounded spatial queries and goal localization (e.g., VLMaps, ConceptGraphs).
 - **VLMs as Intermediate Representations:** VLM outputs are structured as explicit planning representations (e.g., schemas, waypoints, attention maps) or implicit latent embeddings, which then guide local motion planners or lightweight policies (e.g., ViLAM, OLiVia-Nav).
 - **VLMs as Action Interfaces:** VLMs directly generate action tokens or structured commands, coupling high-level reasoning more tightly with low-level execution (e.g., RT-2, NaVILA).

3. **Emphasis on Hybrid Architectures for Deployability:** A key observation derived from the review is that hierarchical hybrid architectures are currently among the most practical directions. These architectures leverage VLMs for their semantic reasoning strengths while reserving specialized planners or controllers for real-time, safety-critical execution. This modularity helps manage VLM latency and computational costs while ensuring dynamic feasibility and safety.

4. **Consolidation of Datasets and Evaluation Platforms:** The survey provides a comprehensive review of datasets and evaluation platforms relevant to VLM-based SRN. It categorizes datasets by their features (viewpoint, temporal horizon, social context, language instructions, output annotations) and evaluation platforms by their targets, social factors, metrics, and scenarios. This consolidation reveals that while resources exist for specific aspects (e.g., pedestrian trajectories, social labels), a single dataset fully combining egocentric robot vision, language instruction/rationale, explicit social reasoning annotations, bridging representations, and executable robot actions is largely absent.

5. **Identification of Key Open Challenges:** The paper meticulously outlines seven major challenges that limit the transition of VLM-based SRN from reasoning to real-world deployment performance:
 - **High-Quality SRN Datasets:** Need for richer, more aligned datasets covering diverse social cues, spatial constraints, causal rationales, and real-world interactions.
 - **Robust Social and Spatial Reasoning:** Fragility of VLM inferences, incomplete language grounding, and limited temporal memory in dynamic, open-world social environments.
 - **Reasoning to Control Grounding:** The underdeveloped connection between high-level VLM outputs and low-level robot control, requiring robust bridging mechanisms like social cost maps or validated reward functions.
 - **Real-Time Embodied Control:** Managing VLM inference latency and computational cost while ensuring high-frequency, continuous control in complex, dynamic environments.
 - **Continual Adaptation:** The necessity for systems to adapt online to human feedback, environmental changes, and dynamic interactions, rather than relying solely on offline-trained models.
 - **Evaluation Beyond Success and Collision:** Need for richer metrics that assess social reasoning, reasoning-action consistency, human acceptance, proxemics, comfort, and culture-dependent norms.
 - **Generalization and Sim-to-Real:** The challenge of generalizing across diverse physical environments, cultures, robot embodiments, and interaction styles, and effectively transferring insights from simulation to real-world deployment.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

This survey holds significant implications for the advancement of social robot navigation and the integration of vision-language models into robotic systems.

1. **Structured Research Direction:** The paper's primary contribution is providing a clear, structured roadmap and a unified system-level formulation for VLM-based SRN. This framework offers researchers a common language and conceptual model to categorize existing work and guide future investigations. By explicitly defining the roles of high-level VLM reasoning, the bridging mechanism, and low-level control, the survey clarifies the interfaces and challenges at each stage, potentially accelerating systematic progress.

2. **Highlighting Critical Gaps:** By consolidating current knowledge and analyzing the limitations of existing datasets and evaluation platforms, the survey clearly articulates the deficiencies in current research. The identified open challenges—ranging from data quality and reasoning robustness to real-time control and ethical considerations like cultural generalization—serve as a focused agenda for the research community, directing efforts towards areas requiring urgent attention.

3. **Promoting Deployability and Reliability:** The emphasis on "deployable" SRN and the necessity of hybrid architectures underscore a shift towards practical, real-world applications. By acknowledging the computational costs and safety-critical nature of robotics, the survey advocates for design principles that combine the semantic power of VLMs with the reliability and efficiency of specialized control modules. This perspective is crucial for moving VLM-based SRN beyond theoretical demonstrations to widespread adoption in shared human spaces.

4. **Advancing Human-Robot Interaction (HRI):** The core objective of SRN is to enable robots to navigate in socially acceptable ways. By proposing how VLMs can imbue robots with a deeper understanding of social norms, human intentions, and contextual cues, the survey lays the groundwork for more intuitive, comfortable, and trustworthy HRI. Robots capable of legible and socially compliant motion can foster greater human acceptance and integration into daily life.

5. **Refined Evaluation Paradigms:** The call for evaluation metrics that extend beyond simple success rate or collision avoidance—to include proxemics, comfort, social compliance, human preference, and reasoning-action consistency—is impactful. This encourages the development of more holistic assessment methods that reflect the nuanced nature of social intelligence, ensuring that robots are not just efficient but also appropriate in human environments.

In summary, this survey provides a foundational reference for the burgeoning field of VLM-based SRN. It synthesizes complex interdisciplinary concepts, offers a practical framework for system design, and outlines a comprehensive research agenda, thereby significantly contributing to the development of intelligent, socially aware, and deployable robots.
