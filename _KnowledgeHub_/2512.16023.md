# CoVAR: Co-generation of Video and Action for Robotic Manipulation via Multi-Modal Diffusion

> [!summary] Summary
> CoVAR develops a multi-modal diffusion framework that co-generates videos and precise robotic actions conditioned on initial observations and language instructions. The system achieved higher action success rates and superior video quality compared to existing methods on various benchmarks, demonstrating improved precision for robotic manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- The scarcity of direct, paired action labels for large-scale video datasets limits the application of powerful video diffusion models in robotic policy learning.
- Two-stage methods for robotic control suffer from heavy dependence on generated video quality and a lack of tight coupling between visual prediction and action inference.
- Existing joint video-action models often require extensive data to train from scratch or adapt pretrained models to a shared latent space, leading to suboptimal performance with limited expert data.

## Method

- Introduces a multi-modal diffusion architecture that extends a pretrained video diffusion model (OpenSora-1.2) with a parallel, dedicated Action Diffusion Transformer, both conditioned on text and initial robot states.
- Employs a Multi-Modal Rectified Flow to learn a simplified, straight-line path from noise to data, accelerating training convergence and enhancing the quality of generated video-action pairs.
- Incorporates a Bridge Attention mechanism that uses separate queries, keys, and values for video and action features to enable effective, low-interference cross-modal information exchange.

## Results

- CoVAR consistently outperformed all joint-model baselines in video generation quality across multiple metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FVD) on Calvin and Libero90 datasets, producing clearer videos with fewer artifacts.
- Achieved substantially higher action success rates on the Calvin dataset (e.g., 1.000 for 'Drawer' tasks) and demonstrated robust performance on a real-world UR5 dataset for fine-grained manipulation (e.g., 0.74 for Screw pick).
- Ablation studies confirmed that the Bridge Attention mechanism and the UNet-based action decoder are critical for performance, with their removal leading to significant degradations in both video quality and action success rates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging the general visual knowledge of a powerful pretrained video diffusion model in parallel with a dedicated action diffusion model is more effective than adapting it to a joint latent space from scratch.
> - The proposed Bridge Attention mechanism is crucial for enabling effective cross-modal interaction between video and action modalities without compromising their distinct representations or leading to information distortion.
> - An action refinement module can successfully convert coarse actions generated from lower-resolution video inputs into precise control signals, which is vital for achieving high success rates in fine-grained robotic manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic learning is rapidly moving towards developing "generalist robotic policies" capable of performing a wide range of tasks in diverse environments. A significant enabler for this goal has been the emergence of large-scale generative models, particularly video diffusion models. These models have demonstrated impressive capabilities in simulating physical interactions, proposing feasible plans, and augmenting novel data, thereby reducing the reliance on massive amounts of manually collected and annotated robotic data.

However, a critical bottleneck in leveraging video diffusion models for robotic policy learning is the scarcity of direct, paired action labels. While video models can generate realistic visual sequences, the absence of corresponding robotic actions prevents their direct use in imitation learning or for executing visual plans. CoVAR directly addresses this challenge by proposing a method for the co-generation of both video and precise robotic actions.

The paper contextualizes its contribution by categorizing existing approaches into two main paradigms:

- **Two-stage Methods:** These methods first generate future video frames (visual plans) using a video generation model, and then employ a separate policy model (often an inverse dynamics model) to infer actions from these generated videos. Examples include Unipi and RoboEnvision.
 - **Limitations:** Their performance is heavily dependent on the quality of the generated videos. They can struggle when critical robot parts are barely visible, and there's a lack of tight coupling between visual prediction and action inference.
- **Joint Models:** More recently, approaches have emerged that aim to learn a shared latent space for both video and actions, performing denoising in this joint space. Examples include PAD, UVA, and UWM.
 - **Limitations:** These models typically require training a joint diffusion model from scratch or adapting a single-modal video diffusion model to a shared latent space. This process often demands massive amounts of data to capture the joint distribution effectively, leading to suboptimal performance when only limited high-quality paired video-action data is available.

CoVAR positions itself as a novel solution that combines the strengths of both paradigms while mitigating their weaknesses. It seeks to **inherit the general visual knowledge of powerful pretrained video diffusion models** (like two-stage approaches) while also **facilitating information sharing and tight coupling between video and action modalities** (like joint models). The innovation lies in its architectural design, which extends a *pretrained* video diffusion model with a *parallel, dedicated* action diffusion model, enabling effective cross-modal interaction without forcing the adaptation of powerful video priors into a constrained joint latent space from scratch. This approach is aligned with the broader trend in multi-modal generative AI, where models aim to generate coherent content across different modalities, but specifically tailors it to the unique challenges of robotic control (e.g., non-spatial alignment of video and action).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of CoVAR is to **simultaneously generate high-quality video frames and corresponding precise robotic actions** for manipulation tasks, conditioned on an initial image observation, the robot's current joint states, and a natural language instruction. This directly addresses the critical challenge of the lack of action annotations in large-scale video datasets, which limits the applicability of powerful video diffusion models for robotic policy learning.

The motivations driving CoVAR are rooted in the limitations of existing methods:

1. **Bridging the Annotation Gap:** Large video datasets are readily available, but rich, paired video-action data for robotics is scarce and expensive to collect. CoVAR aims to automatically provide action labels for generated videos, making large-scale video data fully usable for imitation learning and visual plan execution.
2. **Overcoming Two-Stage Model Deficiencies:** Previous two-stage approaches suffer from a reliance on video quality and a lack of tight coupling between visual and action predictions. CoVAR seeks to improve action accuracy by allowing direct information exchange between modalities during generation.
3. **Improving Joint Model Efficiency and Performance:** Existing joint diffusion models often require training from scratch or extensive adaptation of pretrained video models to a shared latent space, which is data-hungry and can lead to suboptimal results with limited expert data. CoVAR aims for a more efficient approach by leveraging pretrained video knowledge directly while introducing mechanisms for targeted cross-modal interaction.
4. **Enhancing Action Precision:** Particularly for datasets with lower video resolutions, generated actions can be coarse. CoVAR aims to develop mechanisms, such as an action refinement module, to convert these coarse actions into precise controls necessary for successful task execution in real-world scenarios.
5. **Scalability and Generalization:** The ultimate goal is to offer a scalable framework that can leverage the vast potential of large-scale video data to develop more generalist and robust robotic policies.

In essence, CoVAR strives to unlock the full potential of video diffusion models for robotics by providing a framework that generates not just plausible videos, but also executable action sequences, overcoming the current limitations in data availability and model architecture.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CoVAR's methodology revolves around a novel multi-modal diffusion architecture that efficiently integrates video and action generation. The key components and design choices are:

- **Overall Architecture: Multi-Modal Diffusion:** CoVAR extends a powerful, *pretrained* video diffusion model (specifically, OpenSora-1.2) by adding a parallel, dedicated **Action Diffusion Transformer (DiT)**. This is a crucial distinction from joint models that either train a single DiT from scratch or adapt a video DiT to a shared latent space. The parallel design allows the video DiT to retain its versatile pretrained knowledge while the action DiT focuses on learning action distributions. Both modalities are conditioned on text instructions and initial robot states.
- **Multi-Modal Rectified Flow:** To enhance the quality of generated video-action pairs and accelerate training convergence, CoVAR adopts a multi-modal rectified flow model. Rectified flow simplifies the diffusion process by learning a straight-line path from noise to data, which results in faster and more stable learning. The model learns a vector field that maps joint data distributions (video latent and robotic action) to a standard normal distribution, minimizing the difference between the predicted and actual flow.
- **Dedicated Video and Action DiTs:**
 - **Video DiT:** Leverages the pretrained OpenSora-1.2 backbone, inheriting its robust visual generation capabilities from large-scale datasets.
 - **Action DiT:** A parallel DiT specifically for actions. Given the relatively low dimensionality of action data, a lightweight MLP encoder is used to obtain action embeddings, avoiding the computational cost of a VAE common in latent diffusion models. The action DiT is also conditioned on text instructions via cross-attention.
- **Bridge Attention Mechanism:** This is a core innovation for enabling effective cross-modal interaction without interference. Unlike standard attention mechanisms (which use a single set of queries, keys, and values for all modalities) or early fusion (concatenating tokens), Bridge Attention parameterizes **separate queries (q1, q2), keys (k1, k2), and values (v1, v2) for video features and action features**, respectively. These are then concatenated for attention computation and split back. This design allows each modality to maintain its dedicated representation space while still communicating, mitigating issues where one modality might dominate or distort the other's representation.
- **UNet-based Action Decoder:** For converting intermediate features into action sequences, CoVAR employs a UNet architecture as the action decoder. This choice is found to be critical for both accuracy and convergence speed, outperforming simpler MLPs or ResNets used in prior work. The UNet's multi-scale processing capabilities enable richer cross-modal feature representations and more accurate action generation, particularly for capturing hierarchical temporal dynamics.
- **Action Refinement Module:** This module addresses the challenge of precise action generation on datasets with lower spatial resolutions (e.g., 128p), where coarse actions from the main model might be insufficient. It is a transformer-based model conditioned on the initial observation image and textual description. It takes the coarse actions and initial image tokens, processes them through self-attention (to aggregate visual information into action tokens) and cross-attention (for instruction guidance), and finally maps these features to precise robotic actions. This module is applied selectively to low-resolution datasets.

The entire model is optimized by minimizing a loss function that measures the L2 distance between the predicted flow and the target flow for both video and action modalities, as defined by the rectified flow objective.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

CoVAR's effectiveness is rigorously evaluated through extensive experiments on public benchmarks and real-world robotic datasets, demonstrating superior performance in both video generation quality and action accuracy.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Datasets:**
 - **Calvin:** ~20k teleoperated demonstrations, 200x200 resolution.
 - **Libero90:** 90 tasks, 50 expert demos per task, 128x128 resolution (where the Action Refinement Module is applied).
 - **Self-collected Real-world Dataset:** 1K demonstrations for fine-grained manipulation (bowl stacking, picking nuts, screws, dowels) on a UR5 platform, 180x320 resolution.
- **Baselines:**
 - **Joint Models:** UVA, UWM, PAD.
 - **Two-stage Models:** Unipi, RoboEnvision.
 - **Pure Video Diffusion Model:** OpenSora-1.2 (for video quality comparison).
- **Metrics:**
 - **Video Quality:** PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FVD.
 - **Action Accuracy:** Task success rate.

### 5. Main Findings

1. **Superior Video Generation Quality:**
 - On both Calvin and Libero90 datasets, CoVAR consistently outperforms all three joint-model baselines (UVA, PAD, UWM) across all video quality metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FVD).
 - Compared to OpenSora-1.2 (a pure video diffusion model), CoVAR achieves comparable metrics (e.g., PSNR 19.95 vs 19.60 on Calvin, FVD 72.42 vs 61.00 on Calvin), indicating that the integration of the action modality does not significantly degrade video quality. Qualitatively (Fig. 4), CoVAR generates videos with fewer artifacts, greater visual clarity, and more consistent object and robotic arm representations.

2. **Higher Action Success Rate:**
 - **Calvin Dataset:** CoVAR achieves substantially higher success rates across all five task categories (Drawer, Cabinet, Light, Pick, Push), with a perfect 1.000 success rate on "Drawer" tasks, significantly outperforming all joint and two-stage baselines (e.g., Unipi's 0.469 for Drawer). This demonstrates its effectiveness in generating accurate and executable actions.
 - **Libero90 Dataset:** On this low-resolution dataset, CoVAR, *with the action refinement module*, shows significant improvements across "Pick," "Open & Close," and "Combination" tasks (e.g., 0.873 for Pick vs. UVA's 0.676). The ablation study confirms that the refinement module is crucial, as CoVAR without refinement performs comparably or slightly worse than other baselines on this challenging dataset (0.592 for Pick vs. 0.873 with refinement). Qualitatively (Fig. 5), refinement transforms coarse actions into precise trajectories required for successful task completion.
 - **Real-world Dataset:** For fine-grained manipulation tasks (picking tiny nuts, screws, dowels) on the UR5 platform, CoVAR achieves a remarkable success rate (e.g., 0.74 for Screw pick) compared to two-stage baselines (Unipi 0.06, RoboEnvision 0.10). This highlights CoVAR's capability for high-precision action generation in real-world scenarios (Fig. 6).

3. **Ablation Studies (Table V, Fig. 7):**
 - **Bridge Attention (BA):** Replacing Bridge Attention with standard self-attention (SA) or cross-attention (CA) significantly degrades both video quality (e.g., FVD jumps from 133.89 to 137.66 for SA and 145.26 for CA) and action success rate (from 0.68 to 0.32 for SA and 0.20 for CA). This confirms that Bridge Attention is critical for mitigating cross-modal interference and enabling effective information exchange.
 - **UNet Action Decoder:** Removing the UNet-based action head and replacing it with a ResNet architecture leads to a decline in both action accuracy (success rate drops from 0.68 to 0.24) and video quality. This underscores the UNet's importance in learning richer, multi-scale action representations that also positively impact video consistency.
 - **Only Action DiT (w/o video):** Training only the Action DiT without the video DiT results in a drastically low success rate (0.08). This clearly demonstrates that the video modality provides crucial information and pretrained knowledge that is indispensable for the action generation process, validating the multi-modal co-generation approach.

In summary, CoVAR successfully generates high-fidelity video-action pairs, exhibiting robust performance across diverse tasks and datasets. Its architectural innovations, particularly Bridge Attention and the action refinement module, are empirically proven to be essential for its superior performance, especially in precision-demanding robotic manipulation.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

CoVAR represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic learning, particularly in addressing the pervasive challenge of data scarcity and the limitations of current generative models for control. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Key Contributions:**
 - **Scalable Multi-Modal Co-generation:** CoVAR introduces a novel multi-modal diffusion framework that effectively co-generates videos and robotic actions. By extending a *pretrained* video diffusion model with a *parallel, dedicated* action DiT, it efficiently leverages existing visual knowledge without the need to retrain a complex joint latent space from scratch on limited data.
 - **Enhanced Cross-Modal Interaction:** The proposed Bridge Attention mechanism is a crucial architectural innovation. It enables effective, low-interference information exchange between distinct video and action modalities, leading to better alignment and improved accuracy in both generated videos and actions.
 - **Precision for Low-Resolution Data:** The action refinement module offers a practical solution for datasets with lower spatial resolutions, converting coarse initial actions into highly precise control signals essential for delicate manipulation tasks.
 - **Empirical Validation:** Extensive experiments on diverse simulated and real-world benchmarks rigorously demonstrate CoVAR's superiority over existing two-stage and joint-model baselines in terms of video quality and, more critically, robotic task success rates.

- **Broader Implications and Potential Impact:**
 - **Unlocking Large-Scale Video Data for Robotics:** CoVAR provides a scalable framework to effectively utilize the vast, unstructured, and often action-free video data available online. By automatically generating paired actions, it can significantly expand the training data for robotic imitation learning and visual planning, thereby accelerating the development of more generalist robot policies.
 - **Towards More Capable Generalist Robots:** The ability to generate coherent and executable video-action sequences from high-level instructions is a critical step towards enabling robots to understand, plan, and execute complex tasks in open-ended environments. This pushes the boundaries of autonomous systems beyond narrow, pre-programmed behaviors.
 - **Improved Data Augmentation and Synthesis:** For roboticists, CoVAR offers a powerful tool for data augmentation, generating diverse scenarios and action trajectories that might be difficult or dangerous to collect in the real world. This could lead to more robust policies that generalize better to novel situations.
 - **Foundation for Future Research:** The architectural innovations, particularly Bridge Attention, lay a foundation for future multi-modal generative models that need to integrate disparate data streams while preserving modality-specific representations.

- **Limitations and Future Work:**
 The authors acknowledge a key limitation: CoVAR currently operates on **monocular videos**, which restricts its ability to capture full 3D scene geometry. This can be critical for precise robotic manipulation that relies on accurate depth and object pose understanding.

 For future work, they propose **incorporating 3D perception from foundation models**. This would provide more accurate spatial understanding for both video and action learning, potentially leading to even greater precision, robustness, and generalization capabilities in complex 3D environments. Integrating multi-view data or explicit 3D representations could further enhance the model's spatial reasoning.

In conclusion, CoVAR presents a robust and innovative solution to a fundamental problem in robot learning, leveraging the power of diffusion models to generate highly aligned video-action pairs. Its contributions promise to significantly advance the capabilities of learning-based robotic systems by making large-scale visual data directly actionable.
