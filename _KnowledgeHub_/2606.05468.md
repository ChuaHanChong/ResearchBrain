# FlowPRO: Reward-Free Reinforced Fine-Tuning of Flow-Matching VLAs via Proximalized Preference Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> FlowPRO introduces a reward-free, offline reinforced fine-tuning framework for flow-matching Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, consistently achieving superior success rates and faster completion times on bimanual manipulation tasks compared to existing methods. This approach effectively mitigates reward-hacking pathologies in continuous control preference optimization by anchoring implicit reward magnitudes.

## Problem

- Post-training VLA models into robust policies for real-world robotic deployment is challenging due to the high cost of expert demonstrations and task-specific feedback on real robots.
- Current preference-based reinforcement learning (RL) methods, when applied to continuous action spaces, are susceptible to "reward-hacking," leading to policies generating implausible actions.
- Existing supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and DAgger-style approaches inefficiently use failure signals, while reward-based RL requires designing difficult-to-obtain dense reward functions for complex manipulation tasks.

## Method

- **FlowPRO**, a two-stage framework, first fine-tunes a base policy via SFT and then iteratively refines it using RPRO, a novel offline reinforcement learning algorithm.
- RPRO (Robotic Flow-matching ==Proximalized Preference Optimization==) extends PRO to continuous action spaces, using a ==proximal regularizer== to anchor implicit reward magnitudes and prevent reward-hacking.
- A human-in-the-loop teleoperated intervention-and-rollback paradigm efficiently collects paired positive and negative trajectories, which are processed into dense per-state supervision using smooth interpolation.

## Results

- FlowPRO achieved the highest success rates and shortest completion times across four bimanual manipulation tasks, demonstrating statistically significant superiority (p < 10
-3) over all tested baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the RPRO's proximal regularizer is essential, preventing severe performance collapse (e.g., **13%**/**5%** success rates) due to reward-hacking observed in plain DPO.
- Providing dense, per-state supervision via Smooth Interpolation outperformed trajectory-wise preference optimization by 3–7 percentage points in success rate, highlighting the value of fine-grained learning signals.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Anchoring the absolute magnitude of implicit rewards through a proximal regularizer is crucial for preventing reward-hacking in preference optimization for continuous actions.
> - Dense, per-state learning signals, even derived from sparse trajectory-level human corrections, are more effective for policy improvement than diluting feedback over entire trajectories.
> - A strategic batch-mixing schedule that combines new preferences, historical preferences, and initial SFT data is vital for accelerating convergence, preventing catastrophic forgetting, and maintaining base policy capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of generalist robots is increasingly reliant on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which map visual observations and language instructions directly to low-level robot actions. While these models represent a significant advance, a substantial challenge remains in post-training them into policies that are sufficiently robust and reliable for real-world robotic deployment. The difficulty arises from the expense of collecting expert demonstrations and task-specific feedback on real robots, leading to diminishing returns in post-training gains before policies reach desirable reliability levels.

Existing post-training methodologies for VLA models generally fall into three categories, each with inherent limitations that persist in the context of flow-matching VLAs:
1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and DAgger-style approaches:** These methods, including vanilla SFT and interactive DAgger for human correction, are scalable to real hardware. However, they are criticized for their inefficient use of failure signals from autonomous rollouts. SFT discards them entirely, while DAgger primarily uses them to trigger expert intervention rather than as a direct optimization signal for the policy.
2. **Reward- or Value-based Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Approaches in this category necessitate the training of a reliable reward, value, or advantage model. This often becomes a bottleneck, especially in contact-rich manipulation tasks where designing dense and accurate reward signals is inherently difficult.
3. **Preference-based RL:** This family bypasses the explicit reward design by learning directly from human preference data. Methods such as Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and its trajectory-level adaptation, GRAPE (or TPO), have been applied. However, when extended to flow-matching VLAs, trajectory-level contrast can dilute the per-state learning signal. Moreover, DPO-style methods are susceptible to a "reward-hacking" pathology, where the policy generates implausible actions by optimizing the relative preference margin without regard for the absolute quality of actions, leading to undesirable drifts from reasonable behavior.

FlowPRO positions itself within this landscape by specifically addressing the limitations of preference-based RL for flow-matching VLAs, aiming to provide a reward-free and offline reinforced fine-tuning framework that overcomes the reward-hacking issue and extracts a denser learning signal from human feedback.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop FlowPRO, a reward-free offline reinforced fine-tuning framework that enhances the reliability and deployment-readiness of flow-matching Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models on real robots. This overarching goal is motivated by several specific challenges identified in the broader research landscape:

1. **Eliminating the Need for Explicit Reward Design:** A significant motivation is to bypass the engineering bottleneck associated with designing dense and accurate reward functions for complex real-world robotic manipulation tasks. Traditional reward-based RL methods often struggle with this, particularly for contact-rich or long-horizon tasks where defining precise success and failure criteria numerically is difficult and time-consuming. FlowPRO aims to achieve policy improvement entirely from human preferences, avoiding explicit reward modeling.
2. **Addressing Inefficient Use of Failure Signals:** Current supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and DAgger-like interactive methods do not fully exploit the information embedded in observed policy failures. SFT discards failure rollouts, while DAgger uses them only to trigger expert demonstrations, losing the valuable "what not to do" signal. A key objective is to extract a stronger, more direct learning signal from these failure instances by contrasting them with successful human corrections.
3. **Counteracting Reward-Hacking in Preference Optimization:** Existing preference-based RL methods like DPO, when applied to continuous action spaces, can exhibit a "reward-hacking" failure mode. This occurs when the model optimizes the *relative* preference between chosen and rejected actions without anchoring the *absolute* quality of either, leading to policies generating actions that drift far from plausible or effective control. A crucial motivation is to introduce a mechanism that explicitly anchors the implicit reward magnitude, preventing this pathology.
4. **Enhancing Per-State Learning Signal:** Trajectory-level preference optimization, as seen in methods like TPO, can dilute the learning signal by aggregating feedback over entire sequences of actions. The motivation here is to develop a method that provides dense, per-state supervision, ensuring that corrections are applied at a fine-grained action-chunk level rather than spanning an entire trajectory. This is expected to lead to more precise and localized policy improvements.
5. **Developing a Sample-Efficient Data Collection and Processing Paradigm:** Collecting data on real robots is inherently expensive. The project aims to design a human-in-the-loop data collection paradigm that maximizes the utility of each human intervention. This involves generating both positive and negative demonstrations efficiently from a single operator action and converting sparse, trajectory-level corrections into dense, per-state supervision. This includes strategies for effectively mixing newly collected corrections with historical data and the initial SFT dataset to prevent catastrophic forgetting and maintain base policy capabilities.

By addressing these motivations, FlowPRO seeks to offer a practical and effective solution for the post-training of VLA models, enabling them to achieve deployment-grade reliability with manageable human effort.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FlowPRO operates as a two-stage framework for fine-tuning flow-matching VLA models: an initial supervised fine-tuning (SFT) stage followed by an iterative offline reinforcement learning (RL) stage.

**Stage 1: SFT Base Model**
The process begins by training a base policy ($\pi_\theta$) using standard supervised fine-tuning on a task-specific dataset ($D_{SFT}$). This initial policy, once trained, serves as the reference policy ($\pi_{ref}$) for the subsequent RL stage.

**Stage 2: Iterative Offline RL Fine-Tuning**
This stage involves an iterative loop over $K$ rounds, refining the policy through a combination of novel algorithmic and data-centric components.

#### 3.1. Algorithm (RPRO: Robotic Flow-matching Proximalized Preference Optimization)

RPRO is a preference-optimization objective tailored for flow-matching VLA models, extending Proximalized Preference Optimization (PRO) to continuous action spaces.

- **Preference Optimization in Flow Matching:** In flow-matching VLAs, the action head generates continuous action chunks by transporting Gaussian noise to an action via a velocity field ($v_\theta(a_t, t | s)$). The negative log-likelihood of an action ($a$) given a state ($s$) is approximated by a regression loss, $\ell_\theta(s, a)$. This regression loss is used to define an implicit reward proxy:
 $r_\theta(s, a) = \frac{\beta}{2} (\ell_{ref}(s, a) - \ell_\theta(s, a))$, where $\ell_{ref}$ is the flow matching loss under the frozen reference policy ($\pi_{ref}$) and $\beta$ is a temperature parameter.

- **PRO Loss in Flow Matching:** The PRO loss is designed to resolve the likelihood underdetermination (reward-hacking) issue of plain DPO. In continuous action spaces, the hyper-response reward term (representing unobserved actions) in the original PRO formulation is demonstrated to vanish in the population limit. Thus, the RPRO loss ($L_{PRO}(\theta)$) consists of two main components:
 - **Contrastive Optimizer ($L_{con}$):** This term drives the policy to prefer chosen actions ($a_w$) over rejected actions ($a_l$) by maximizing the log-sigmoid of their implicit reward difference: $\log \sigma(r_\theta(s, a_w) - r_\theta(s, a_l))$.
 - **Proximal Regularizer ($L_{reg}$):** This term anchors the absolute magnitude of the implicit rewards for both $a_w$ and $a_l$ around zero (relative to the reference policy). It prevents either $r_\theta(s, a_w)$ or $r_\theta(s, a_l)$ from drifting to arbitrarily large or small values, which is the mechanism that prevents reward hacking.

- **Full RPRO Objective:** To balance preference alignment with maintaining base policy performance and direct learning from positive actions, the full objective combines the PRO loss with a supervised fine-tuning term:
 $L_{RPRO}(\theta) = \lambda_{PRO} L_{PRO}(\theta) + \lambda_{SFT} L_{SFT}(\theta)$, where $L_{SFT}(\theta) = E_{(s,a_w) \sim D} [\ell_\theta(s, a_w)]$ is the flow-matching regression loss on positive actions, and $\lambda_{PRO}$, $\lambda_{SFT}$ are weighting coefficients.

- **Contrastive Gradient Cancellation:** A property of the RPRO objective is that when an identical pair of actions ($a_w = a_l = a$) is presented, the contrastive term's gradient cancels out. Only the proximal regularizer and the SFT term remain active, reinforcing correct behavior and preventing drift from the reference policy. This allows for a unified treatment of preference pairs and SFT-style demonstrations within the same objective.

#### 3.2. Human-in-the-Loop Data Collection

A teleoperated intervention-and-rollback paradigm is used to efficiently collect paired positive ($\tau_w$) and negative ($\tau_l$) trajectories on a real robot.
- **Rollback:** When an operator observes an erroneous or dangerous action, they trigger a rollback. The robot rewinds to an earlier state ($s_{t-\Delta}$), chosen by the operator to precede the error. The executed segment from $s_{t-\Delta}$ to $s_t$ is recorded as the negative trajectory ($\tau_l$).
- **Scene Restoration:** The operator restores the physical scene to $s_{t-\Delta}$ using visual feedback.
- **Human Correction:** The operator then teleoperates the correct behavior from $s_{t-\Delta}$, recording this as the positive trajectory ($\tau_w$).
This process generates naturally paired trajectories from a single intervention, and the variable rollback horizon ($\Delta$) diversifies the initial states of these pairs.

#### 3.3. Training Data Composition

The collected trajectory pairs are processed to create dense per-state supervision for the RPRO loss, and then mixed with existing data.

- **Per-State Pair Construction (Smooth Interpolation):** Since $\tau_w$ and $\tau_l$ diverge, a smooth interpolation strategy synthesizes missing counterparts:
 - **States on Negative Trajectory ($\tau_l$):** For a state $M$ on $\tau_l$, a synthetic positive action chunk ($a_w$) is constructed. This chunk smoothly bridges from $M$ towards the corresponding segment of $\tau_w$ using a cubic B´ezier curve for position, Slerp for orientation, and linear interpolation for the gripper. The actual next chunk from $\tau_l$ serves as $a_l$.
 - **States on Positive Trajectory ($\tau_w$):** For states on $\tau_w$, the actual next chunk from $\tau_w$ is $a_w$, and $a_l$ is set equal to $a_w$. Due to gradient cancellation, this activates only the SFT loss and the proximal regularizer, reinforcing desired behavior.
 - **SFT Demonstration Data ($D_{SFT}$):** For original SFT samples, $a_w$ and $a_l$ are both set to the expert action ($a_{SFT}$), similarly activating only SFT and the proximal regularizer, regularizing these samples against drift from $\pi_{ref}$.

- **Batch Mixing Across Iterations:** Training mini-batches are composed from three distinct sources with fixed proportions:
 - **Round $k=1$:** 80% from the newly collected preference dataset ($D_{k}^{pref}$) and 20% from the initial $D_{SFT}$.
 - **Rounds $k \ge 2$:** 70% from $D_{k}^{pref}$, 15% from the historical preference pool ($D_{<k}^{pref} = \bigcup_{j<k} D_j^{pref}$), and 15% from $D_{SFT}$.
This schedule prioritizes new corrections, replays historical data to prevent forgetting, and maintains base policy capabilities using the original SFT data.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of FlowPRO was conducted on four long-horizon bimanual manipulation tasks (Cosmetic Packaging, Pen-Cap Assembly, USB Insertion, Pencil-Case Packing) using a Dobot XTrainer platform. The tasks were evaluated with two different base policies (PI0 and PI0.5) over 3 iterative fine-tuning rounds. Success rates (SR) and completion times (CT) were reported across 3 independent training seeds, each with 100 randomized rollouts.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Comparison with Baselines (Q1 & Q2)

FlowPRO consistently achieved the highest success rates and shortest completion times across all 8 task-base policy strata (4 tasks x 2 base policies). A Bonferroni-corrected one-sided Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel (CMH) test indicated a statistically significant superiority (p < 10$^{-3}$) against all tested baselines.

- **RPRO vs. TPO (Trajectory-wise Preference Optimization):** FlowPRO, employing state-wise Smooth Interpolation, outperformed the trajectory-wise TPO variant by 3–7 percentage points (pp) in success rate across all strata. This indicates that providing a dense, per-state learning signal at the action-chunk granularity is more effective than diluting the signal over entire trajectories.
- **RPRO vs. PI0.6* (Advantage Conditioning):** RPRO achieved 2–7 pp higher success rates compared to PI0.6*, which relies on conditioning the policy on an "improvement" indicator. This suggests that directly embedding the preference signal within the action-generation loss function, as RPRO does, is more potent than indirect conditioning.
- **RPRO vs. DAgger & DAgger-Buffered (Positive-only Baselines):** RPRO demonstrated an 8–15 pp gain over vanilla DAgger, primarily due to its ability to exploit negative trajectories through contrastive learning, in addition to positive samples. DAgger-Buffered, which used FlowPRO's batch-composition schedule for positive-only data, showed a 2–7 pp improvement over vanilla DAgger. This confirmed that the proposed batch-mixing strategy itself contributes to sample efficiency, beyond the contrastive learning aspect.

#### 4.2. Loss-Component Ablations (Q3 & Q4)

Ablation studies were performed on the PACK task, under both in-distribution (ID) and out-of-distribution (OOD) initial conditions, after a single round of fine-tuning to isolate the contribution of each component of the RPRO objective. CMH tests showed significant differences (p $\le$ 6 x 10$^{-3}$) for all comparisons.

- **RPRO vs. PRO (Ablates $L_{SFT}$):** Removing the SFT term from RPRO (i.e., using only PRO) led to a performance drop from 94%/91% to 88%/81% (ID/OOD). This indicates that the SFT term actively pulls the policy towards positive actions ($a_w$) and helps in accelerating convergence and mitigating catastrophic forgetting, complementing the proximal regularizer's role in preventing policy collapse.
- **RPRO vs. SFT (Ablates $L_{PRO}$):** Removing the PRO contrastive and regularizer terms (i.e., using only SFT) resulted in a drop to 90%/75% (ID/OOD). This highlights that the explicit "push-away" gradient from negative actions provided by $L_{PRO}$ is crucial, especially for out-of-distribution generalization, as SFT alone only learns from positive examples.
- **RPRO vs. DPO+SFT (Ablates $L_{reg}$):** Removing the proximal regularizer ($L_{reg}$) while keeping the DPO contrastive term and SFT term (DPO+SFT) resulted in a performance drop to 87%/70%. Diagnostic implicit-reward curves showed that without $L_{reg}$, the implicit reward for negative actions ($r_l$) drifted unchecked into an unbounded regime, even if the margin ($r_w - r_l$) remained positive. This confirms that $L_{reg}$ is essential for anchoring the absolute reward magnitudes and preventing the generation of implausible control actions.
- **RPRO vs. DPO (Ablates $L_{reg}$ + $L_{SFT}$):** Removing both the SFT term and the proximal regularizer (plain DPO) led to a severe collapse in success rates (13%/5%). The implicit-reward curves showed extreme reward hacking, with $r_w$ itself driving below zero and reaching magnitudes of -10$^2$, indicating that the policy became worse at generating positive actions than the reference policy, despite a positive relative preference margin. This result conclusively demonstrates that RPRO's proximal regularizer (and the SFT anchor) are critical for preventing this fundamental pathology in preference optimization for continuous actions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

FlowPRO presents a contribution to the field of robotic learning by addressing a critical challenge in the deployment of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models on real robots. The significance and potential impact of this work are multifaceted:

1. **Enhanced Reliability of VLA Policies:** The consistent and statistically significant performance improvements demonstrated across diverse, complex bimanual manipulation tasks suggest that FlowPRO can significantly enhance the reliability of VLA policies. This directly addresses a major bottleneck in moving VLA research from simulated environments to practical, real-world robotic applications.
2. **Reward-Free Offline Fine-Tuning:** By providing a purely preference-based and offline fine-tuning framework, FlowPRO bypasses the challenges associated with designing dense reward functions and the complexities of online interaction in traditional reinforcement learning. This simplifies the development pipeline for robotic tasks, especially those involving intricate contact dynamics or long-horizon sequences where explicit reward engineering is prohibitive.
3. **Mitigation of Reward-Hacking:** The algorithmic innovation of RPRO, particularly the explicit proximal regularizer, effectively eliminates the reward-hacking failure mode observed in prior DPO-style methods when applied to continuous action spaces. This ensures that policy improvements are not just relative but also lead to absolute improvements in action quality and plausibility, which is crucial for safe and effective robot operation.
4. **Sample-Efficient Human-in-the-Loop Learning:** The teleoperated intervention-and-rollback paradigm, coupled with Smooth Interpolation and intelligent batch mixing, represents an efficient strategy for collecting and leveraging scarce human supervision. It maximizes the information extracted from each human intervention by generating naturally paired positive and negative trajectories and converting sparse corrections into dense, per-state learning signals. This makes the fine-tuning process more practical and less labor-intensive.
5. **Unified Treatment of Diverse Data Sources:** The gradient cancellation property of RPRO allows for a unified objective to handle both explicit preference pairs and traditional SFT demonstrations. This simplifies the training pipeline and ensures that the base capabilities learned from SFT are preserved while specific failure modes are targeted through preference optimization.
6. **Broader Applicability to Continuous Control:** While demonstrated on bimanual manipulation, the underlying RPRO algorithm's extension of PRO to continuous action spaces in flow-matching models has broader implications for other continuous control domains where preference-based learning is desired.

In summary, FlowPRO offers a robust and effective post-training methodology that can transform VLA models from capable but unreliable prototypes into deployment-grade robotic policies. Its reward-free nature, combined with a principled approach to preference optimization and efficient human-in-the-loop data utilization, contributes a practical and theoretically grounded solution for generalist robot development. Future work on broader generalization and autonomous failure detection could further extend its impact.
