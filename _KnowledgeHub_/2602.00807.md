# Any3D-VLA: Enhancing VLA Robustness via Diverse Point Clouds

> [!summary] Summary
> ANY3D-VLA is a framework that improves Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by explicitly fusing diverse 3D point cloud representations with 2D visual inputs. The approach achieved an average success rate of 62.5% in challenging real-world zero-shot manipulation, representing a 29.2% improvement over the best baseline, and enhances robustness without requiring specialized depth hardware during inference.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often exhibit brittle performance in precise 3D spatial understanding tasks (e.g., small objects, occlusions) due to their reliance on 2D visual inputs.
- Integrating 3D information into VLA models is hindered by 3D data scarcity, significant domain gaps between different 3D data sources (simulators, sensors, estimated), and dependency on expensive depth hardware.
- Existing 3D integration methods for VLA models often lack widely pre-trained point cloud encoders or treat 2D and 3D inputs too independently, impacting scalable training and robust deployment.

## Method

- The ANY3D-VLA framework is a plug-in pipeline that lifts 2D visual inputs into compressed 3D point clouds and fuses these 3D representations with their 2D counterparts at the patch level.
- A hybrid training strategy exposes the model to diverse point cloud sources (simulator ground truth, sensor data, model-estimated depth) to enhance robustness against varying depth quality and mitigate domain gaps.
- A gated residual fusion mechanism is used to combine patch-aligned 3D features with 2D features, allowing 3D information to provide complementary signals while preserving the strengths of pre-trained 2D representations.

## Results

- Achieved an overall average success rate of **62.5%** in challenging real-world zero-shot manipulation tasks, improving over the best baseline (SpatialVLA) by **29.2%**.
- Demonstrated superior performance in real-world post-training, with **93.3%** success on flower arrangement and **86.7%** on condiment cup placement tasks, outperforming baselines.
- Improved performance on public simulation benchmarks, exceeding GraspVLA by **13.9%** in average success rate on LIBERO and increasing tasks completed in a row by **0.71** on CALVIN.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly representing 3D geometry as compressed point clouds, fused with 2D representations, is more effective for fine-grained manipulation than implicit spatial priors or treating depth as an additional 2D channel.
> - Training VLA models with diverse point cloud inputs (hybrid training) acts as an effective data augmentation strategy, significantly improving robustness and generalization across different depth qualities and reducing the sim-to-real gap.
> - State-of-the-art model-estimated depth can often provide more accurate point clouds than commodity depth sensors, enabling VLA models to achieve robust performance without relying on expensive, specialized depth hardware.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent an area of research aimed at developing general-purpose embodied intelligence. These models are typically trained on extensive datasets of action trajectories paired with language instructions, enabling robots to interpret natural language commands and execute corresponding physical actions. While contemporary VLA models demonstrate capabilities in language understanding and semantic reasoning, their spatial understanding is often constrained by their reliance on 2D visual inputs. This limitation can lead to performance degradation in scenarios involving small objects, significant viewpoint changes, or occlusions, where precise 3D spatial relationships are critical.

The research community has pursued various strategies to integrate 3D information into VLA models. Some approaches maintain 2D inputs but incorporate implicit spatial priors through mechanisms such as depth-pretrained encoders or spatial foundation models (e.g., VGGT) that learn geometric features from reconstruction objectives. Other methods directly utilize depth maps as input to enhance spatial perception. A third category of approaches involves the use of point cloud information. However, many existing point cloud-based methods face limitations, including the absence of widely pre-trained point cloud encoders, reliance on non-parametric 3D tokenizers, or treating 2D and 3D inputs relatively independently rather than integrating them through a unified representation. These factors contribute to challenges in scalable training and robust deployment of 3D-aware VLA models, particularly due to the comparative scarcity of high-quality 3D data and the significant domain gaps that exist between 3D data originating from simulators, real-world sensors, and model-based estimations (e.g., differences in noise characteristics, scale distributions, and geometric biases).

This work addresses these challenges by proposing a method that explicitly lifts 2D visual inputs into point clouds and then fuses these 3D representations with their corresponding 2D counterparts. It specifically aims to mitigate the data scarcity and domain gap issues by incorporating diverse point cloud sources during training, thereby seeking to enhance spatial understanding and generalization capabilities for VLA models without relying on expensive depth hardware during real-world deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the spatial understanding capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by effectively incorporating 3D information. This enhancement is intended to improve the robustness and generalization of robotic manipulation, particularly in complex scenarios where current VLA models, which often rely predominantly on 2D visual backbones, exhibit limitations.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations and identified challenges:

1. **Limitations of 2D-only VLA models**: Existing VLA models, primarily using 2D image inputs, demonstrate brittle performance in scenarios requiring precise spatial reasoning, such as manipulating small objects, handling occlusions, or adapting to viewpoint changes. This suggests an inherent deficiency in their ability to fully comprehend the 3D geometry of a scene.

2. **Pilot Study Insights**: A preliminary investigation across various observation spaces and visual representations indicated that explicitly projecting visual inputs into point cloud space yields representations that are more effective at complementing 2D representations compared to implicit spatial priors (e.g., from depth-pretrained encoders or 3D foundation models like VGGT) or directly feeding depth maps as additional 2D channels. The pilot study found that implicit methods often lack precise metric alignment and can suffer from spatial hallucinations for fine-grained manipulation, while treating depth as a 2D channel compromises the inherent topological structure of 3D data, making it difficult for 2D backbones to infer occlusions or absolute scales. This suggested that the *manner* in which geometric information is represented and exploited is crucial.

3. **Challenges in 3D VLA deployment**: Despite the potential benefits of 3D information, its integration into VLA models faces significant practical bottlenecks:
 - **3D Data Scarcity**: Compared to the vast quantities of 2D image data available, large-scale, high-quality 3D data for robot learning is considerably more scarce.
 - **Domain Gap in 3D Data**: 3D data generated from different sources (e.g., high-fidelity simulators, consumer-grade depth sensors, or model-estimated depth from RGB images) exhibit substantial differences in noise characteristics, scale distributions, and geometric biases. This domain gap hinders a model's ability to generalize across various environments and deployment conditions.
 - **Hardware Dependency**: High-quality depth inputs often necessitate expensive and precise depth hardware, which can limit the scalability and cost-effectiveness of real-world deployments.

Motivated by these challenges, the research aims to develop a novel paradigm called ANY3D-VLA. This framework seeks to overcome data scarcity and domain generalization issues by unifying diverse point cloud sources (simulator ground truth, sensor data, and model-estimated depth) within a single training pipeline. The core idea is to train models that are robust to variations in depth quality and source, thereby achieving more reliable 3D geometric reasoning and reducing reliance on specialized hardware during inference.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, ANY3D-VLA, is designed as a plug-in pipeline that enhances existing VLA backbones by integrating diverse point cloud sources and fusing 3D representations with their 2D counterparts.

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture

ANY3D-VLA integrates a Vision-Language Model (VLM) with an action expert, connected via a Progressive Action Generation (PAG) mechanism. The VLM component comprises a trainable large language model (InternLM2 1.8B), a visual observation module, and a trainable projector that maps visual representations into the language space. The action expert utilizes a conditional flow-matching approach to generate continuous end-effector actions.

#### 3.2. Visual Observation Module

The visual observation module is central to ANY3D-VLA, handling the processing and fusion of 2D and 3D visual information.

- **Point-Cloud Construction and 3D Compression**: Given RGB images and optional depth maps, the system first lifts these visual inputs into point clouds in the camera coordinate frame using camera intrinsics. Each pixel (u, v) with metric depth `d` is converted to 3D coordinates (x, y, z). To manage computational load, a 3D compression step is applied. This involves partitioning the workspace into fixed-resolution 3D grid cells (voxels) and aggregating points within each cell, retaining only one representative point per cell. This reduces the point count from approximately 30,000–60,000 to 3,000–8,000, while preserving per-point attributes such as color and estimated surface normals.

- **Vision Encoder**:
 - **3D Encoder**: The compressed point cloud is fed into a pre-trained point cloud encoder, Concerto. This encoder takes point coordinates, colors, and normals as input, producing point-wise 3D features. Most parameters of Concerto are frozen, with only the last few sparse convolution layers fine-tuned.
 - **2D Encoder**: In parallel, an image encoder (e.g., DINOv2 and SigLIP) is used to map RGB images into patch-level 2D tokens. These 2D encoders are typically kept frozen as static feature extractors.

- **Patch-Wise Alignment**: To bridge the 3D and 2D representations, 3D features are aligned to 2D patches. Each 3D point is projected back onto the image plane to obtain its (u, v) pixel coordinates. These coordinates are then used to determine the corresponding 2D patch index. For each patch, a patch-level 3D feature is computed by performing scatter-mean aggregation over the 3D features of all points assigned to that patch. If no points fall into a particular patch, a learnable empty token is used as its 3D feature. This process ensures that 3D understanding, initially performed in native 3D space, is aligned with the 2D patch structure.

- **2D–3D Fusion**: The aligned patch-level 3D features are linearly projected to match the token dimension of the 2D features. The 2D and 3D tokens for each patch are then concatenated. A gated residual fusion mechanism is employed, where a small MLP processes the concatenated tokens to produce a residual vector. This residual vector, scaled by a sigmoid-gated learnable scalar, is added to the 2D token. This design choice aims to preserve the strength of the pre-trained 2D representations while allowing 3D information to provide corrective or complementary signals rather than replacing the 2D information entirely. The fused token sequence, along with language and proprioceptive tokens, is then fed into the VLA backbone.

#### 3.3. Training Strategy

- **Imitation Learning and PAG**: The model follows an imitation learning paradigm, adopting GraspVLA's Progressive Action Generation (PAG) training. It takes RGB images, corresponding point clouds, language instructions, and proprioceptive states as input. The VLM autoregressively predicts intermediate discrete tokens, and a conditional flow-matching action expert generates continuous end-effector actions.

- **Loss Function**: The VLM head and the flow-matching action expert are jointly optimized. Training data is sampled from both the GRIT internet grounding dataset (supervising VLM for bounding box prediction) and a synthetic RGBD dataset (supervising VLM for bounding box and grasp pose tokens, plus the action expert via flow matching). Notably, no explicit reconstruction losses for depth or point clouds are utilized; performance gains are intended to arise solely from the improved spatial observations and representations.

- **Hybrid Point Cloud Training**: To account for variability in depth quality across different real-world deployment scenarios, three training settings are explored:
 1. **Simulator Only**: All training samples use ground-truth metric point clouds from the simulator.
 2. **Hybrid Point Cloud**: The model is trained from scratch on a dataset where, for each trajectory, a point cloud source is selected with a fixed probability. Sources include simulator point clouds (or sensor-based for real-world datasets) and metric point clouds estimated from single RGB frames (e.g., from UniDepthV2, Depth Anything 3, MapAnything). This strategy aims to expose the model to diverse point cloud types, encouraging the 3D encoder and fusion layers to learn geometric patterns robust to depth source imperfections and scale biases.
 3. **Sensor Only**: (Used for real-world post-training datasets) All training samples use sensor-based point clouds.

#### 3.4. Dataset and Benchmarks

A large-scale synthetic RGBD dataset was generated using the Objaverse LVIS subset in Isaac Sim, featuring randomized scene parameters. Depth maps were acquired directly from the simulator or estimated from RGB via models. For real-world evaluation, two novel tasks (flower arrangement, condiment cup placement) with collected demonstrations were used for post-training. The model was also evaluated on public simulation benchmarks: LIBERO (for diverse generalization) and CALVIN (for long-horizon tasks).

#### 3.5. Pilot Study

A controlled pilot study compared five observation space designs in simulation with perfect depth: 2D-only, implicit-depth RGB (depth-pretrained encoder), implicit-3D RGB (VGGT-style encoder), RGBD image-plane (depth as 2D channel), and point cloud–2D patch fusion. This study was crucial for motivating the design choice of explicit 3D structure representation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings from a pilot study, extensive real-world experiments, simulation benchmark evaluations, and ablation studies to support the efficacy of ANY3D-VLA.

#### 4.1. Pilot Study: Observation Spaces and Visual Representations (Simulation)

The pilot study compared five different methods for incorporating spatial information in a controlled simulation environment with perfect depth. The results indicated that the **point cloud–2D patch fusion** paradigm achieved the highest Single-Trial Success Rate (SR) of 61.1%, surpassing the next best method, RGBD image-plane, by 4.3% (56.8%). Implicit methods (Implicit-depth RGB: 55.8%, Implicit-3D RGB: 46.3%) showed more limited gains. This outcome suggested that explicitly representing 3D geometry as compressed point clouds, rather than relying on implicit priors or treating depth as a 2D channel, is more effective for fine-grained manipulation by providing more direct and stable spatial constraints.

#### 4.2. Zero-Shot Comparisons in the Real World

ANY3D-VLA was evaluated for its zero-shot generalization ability and robustness across four challenging real-world test sets: Standard, Scale & Shape Challenge, Viewpoint Challenge, and Appearance-Deprived Challenge.
- **Superior Performance**: ANY3D-VLA consistently outperformed baseline 2D (π
 0.5, GraspVLA) and 3D (SpatialVLA) VLA models across all scenarios. The overall average success rate for ANY3D-VLA (trained with hybrid point clouds, inferred with Depth Anything 3 estimated point clouds, denoted as Setting 2, DA3) reached 62.5%, which was a 29.2% improvement over the best baseline, SpatialVLA (33.3%).
- **Impact of Hybrid Training**: Hybrid point cloud training (Setting 2) generally resulted in higher average success rates compared to training solely with simulator point clouds (Setting 1), indicating its effectiveness in mitigating domain gaps.
- **Model-Estimated vs. Sensor Depth**: Inference using model-estimated point clouds (DA3) often outperformed inference using RealSense sensor-based point clouds under the same training setting. This suggests that state-of-the-art depth estimation models can provide more accurate point clouds than commodity sensors and that the hybrid training strategy leverages this to reduce reliance on specific depth hardware.

#### 4.3. Real-World Post-Training

For adaptation to novel real-world tasks, ANY3D-VLA underwent post-training on limited real-world demonstration data for two tasks: flower arrangement and placing a condiment cup.
- **Task Performance**: ANY3D-VLA demonstrated superior performance on both tasks. With hybrid point cloud training and DA3 inference, it achieved 93.3% success for Task 1 and 86.7% for Task 2, outperforming all baselines and training configurations using only RealSense point clouds.
- **Robustness to Depth Source**: Hybrid training consistently performed better than RealSense-only training. Furthermore, DA3-based inference was generally superior or equivalent to RealSense-based inference, reinforcing the model's robustness to varying depth quality.

#### 4.4. Inference Efficiency and Latency

Point cloud compression (from 30-60k to 3-8k points) significantly reduced computational overhead. While introducing point cloud representations inherently adds latency compared to 2D-only approaches, DA3-based inference achieved 1.7 FPS and RealSense-based inference achieved 2.0 FPS on an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU. This performance, combined with action chunking (chunk size of 4), was deemed feasible for tabletop manipulation tasks.

#### 4.5. Diverse Point-Cloud Inputs as Data Augmentation

A quantitative analysis across different point cloud data compositions (simulator, sensor, model-estimated, and mixtures) showed that models trained with hybrid point clouds consistently matched or exceeded the performance of those trained with a single source. This supported the hypothesis that exposing the model to diverse point cloud inputs acts as an effective data augmentation strategy, enhancing robustness and mitigating the sim-to-real gap.

#### 4.6. Ablation Study

An ablation study on the 2D–3D fusion design was conducted using simulator ground-truth point clouds.
- **3D-only vs. 2D-only**: A 3D-only model achieved a high Grasp SR (91.6%) but a low Single-Trial SR (44.2%), indicating strong manipulation capability but weak semantic understanding. Conversely, the 2D-only model (45.3% Single-Trial SR) was less stable with small or occluded objects.
- **Full 2D–3D Fusion**: Integrating full 2D–3D fusion significantly improved the Single-Trial SR to 61.1%. This result suggests that robust manipulation benefits from the combination of semantic understanding provided by 2D representations and the precise geometric information from 3D representations.

#### 4.7. LIBERO and CALVIN Benchmarks

ANY3D-VLA achieved good results on public simulation benchmarks. On LIBERO, it improved over GraspVLA by 13.9% in average success rate. On CALVIN, it increased the average tasks completed in a row by 0.71 compared to GraspVLA and showed slightly better overall performance than SpatialVLA. Further, incorporating the 3D branch into a strong baseline like π
0.5
consistently yielded performance improvements on both benchmarks, demonstrating the generalizability of the 3D representation enhancement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of embodied artificial intelligence by addressing fundamental limitations in the spatial understanding of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The proposed ANY3D-VLA framework offers several areas of significance and potential impact:

1. **Enhanced Robotic Manipulation Capabilities**: By demonstrating that fusing native sparse 3D structure (compressed point clouds) with 2D visual representations provides more direct and stable spatial constraints, this work offers a path to improving VLA models for fine-grained manipulation tasks. The consistent outperformance of ANY3D-VLA in challenging real-world scenarios (e.g., small objects, occlusions, viewpoint changes, appearance-deprived scenes) suggests that integrating mature point-cloud representations can lead to more robust and precise robot control.

2. **Mitigation of 3D Data Bottlenecks**: The introduction of a hybrid point cloud training strategy directly confronts the challenges of 3D data scarcity and the domain gap between different 3D data sources (simulators, sensors, model estimations). By training with diverse inputs, the model learns geometric patterns invariant to depth quality and scale biases, thereby enhancing its ability to generalize across varied environments and depth acquisition methods. This approach makes advanced 3D-aware VLA capabilities more scalable and accessible.

3. **Reduced Reliance on Specialized Hardware**: A key implication is the reduced dependency on expensive, high-precision depth hardware during real-world deployment. The research demonstrates that ANY3D-VLA can perform effectively even when relying on model-estimated point clouds (e.g., from Depth Anything 3), which can often be more accurate than those from commodity depth sensors and can be derived from standard RGB cameras. This lowers the barrier to entry for deploying sophisticated robotic manipulation systems.

4. **Generalizable and Modular Framework**: ANY3D-VLA is designed as a plug-in pipeline, allowing for its integration with existing VLA backbones. This modularity means that advancements in 2D vision models and 3D point cloud encoders can be readily incorporated, facilitating continuous improvement and broader applicability across different VLA architectures. The successful integration of the 3D branch into a strong baseline like π
 0.5
 on public benchmarks further supports its generalizability.

5. **Foundation for Future Research**: The work reaffirms the importance of point cloud representations for spatial perception in VLA models and lays groundwork for further exploration. Future research could investigate more advanced 3D backbones, optimized scheduling and weighting strategies for diverse 3D data sources, and the integration of active perception or more complex planning-and-control strategies to address remaining limitations (e.g., grasping slippery objects, handling heavy occlusion, collision avoidance).

6. **Potential Societal Impact**: The enhanced robustness of robotic manipulation, particularly under challenging visual conditions, has implications for improving safety and reliability in various deployment settings, including industrial automation, domestic assistance, and assistive care. As acknowledged by the authors, more capable robots may also influence labor markets and introduce new safety considerations, necessitating the incorporation of appropriate safety monitoring, human oversight, and risk assessment during real-world implementation.
