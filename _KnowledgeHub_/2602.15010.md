# BPP: Long-Context Robot Imitation Learning by Focusing on Key History Frames

> [!summary] Summary
> Big Picture Policies (BPP) provides a framework for robot imitation learning that addresses the "history coverage problem" in long-context tasks by semantically filtering continuous observation histories into a compact set of task-relevant keyframes. This approach, leveraging Vision-Language Models, enabled robots to achieve nearly 70% higher success rates on challenging real-world bimanual manipulation benchmarks compared to prior history-conditioned methods.

## Problem

- Most high-performing robot policies in imitation learning are memoryless or rely on very short temporal contexts, limiting their ability to perform complex, non-Markovian tasks requiring long-term memory.
- Naïvely conditioning policies on long sequences of past observations often degrades performance due to "spurious correlations" and a fundamental "coverage problem" where training data cannot adequately represent the vast space of possible histories.
- Existing solutions like regularization or auxiliary objectives often provide inconsistent, task-dependent benefits and fail to address the core issue of distribution shift caused by out-of-distribution (OOD) histories.

## Method

- Introduced Big Picture Policies (**BPP**) which conditions robot actions on the current observation and a compact set of semantically meaningful "keyframes" derived from the past observation history.
- Utilized an off-the-shelf Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically Gemini 3 Pro, as an intelligent keyframe detector, querying it with simple task-specific prompts to identify salient events at a low frequency (1 Hz).
- Implemented "latency masking" during training to account for VLM inference delays, ensuring the policy learns to use only keyframe information that would be realistically available at deployment time.

## Results

- BPP demonstrated an average of **70%** higher success rates on challenging real-world bimanual manipulation tasks (e.g., Mug Replacement, Drawer Search) compared to the best prior history-conditioned approaches.
- In simulation, BPP consistently outperformed all non-oracle baselines and notably surpassed the Oracle baseline in the Variable-Password task, suggesting that semantic abstraction can be more robust than explicit ground-truth state.
- Ablation studies confirmed BPP's robustness to imperfect VLM-detected keyframes and its superior data efficiency, achieving strong performance with less demonstration data than naïve history conditioning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The "history coverage problem," stemming from the exponential growth of observation history space and limited expert demonstrations, is the root cause of failures in long-context robot imitation learning.
> - Semantic compression of observation history into task-relevant keyframes, rather than providing raw, comprehensive sequences, significantly increases input distribution overlap and mitigates spurious correlations.
> - Even strong auxiliary objectives or explicit regularization can sometimes harm policy robustness by encouraging overfitting to features that are specific to in-distribution training histories but do not generalize under OOD conditions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical and persistent challenge in robot imitation learning: extending policies to handle long-context, non-Markovian tasks that require memory.

**Imitation Learning (IL) Foundations:** Imitation learning has become a dominant paradigm for training robotic policies due to its ability to transfer human expertise to robots. State-of-the-art IL approaches, exemplified by models like RT-1, Diffusion Policy, and various vision-language-action (VLA) models (e.g., OpenVLA, Gemini Robotics), have achieved impressive performance on a wide range of tasks. However, the authors correctly point out a significant limitation: most of these successful approaches are largely "memoryless," conditioning actions primarily on the current observation or a very short temporal window. While effective for many benchmarks, this renders them insufficient for complex tasks requiring robots to remember past actions, perceive long-term states, or track multi-stage progress (e.g., searching for objects, multi-step assembly).

**The Challenge of History Conditioning:** The natural extension of IL to memory-intensive tasks involves conditioning policies on sequences of past observations. Yet, prior work has consistently shown that "naïvely" adding history often *degrades* performance. This phenomenon is attributed to "spurious correlations" or "causal confusion," where policies overfit to incidental features in training histories that do not generalize to out-of-distribution (OOD) trajectories encountered during deployment. The core underlying issue, as identified by this paper, is a fundamental "coverage problem": the space of possible observation histories grows exponentially with the horizon, making it impossible to cover adequately with finite training data, especially with human-teleoperated "near-expert" demonstrations.

**Limitations of Prior Solutions:** The paper positions itself in contrast to existing efforts to mitigate spurious correlations. These include:
- **Regularization techniques:** Methods like information bottlenecks or loss reweighting (e.g., focusing on keyframes where actions change significantly).
- **Auxiliary objectives:** Such as Past-Token Prediction (PTP), which self-supervises policies by predicting past actions or observations.
- **Architectural constraints:** Approaches like TraceVLA (visual trace of past trajectory) or MemER (language-based memory retrieval).
The authors argue that while these techniques offer some benefits, they are often inconsistent and task-dependent because they do not fundamentally address the *coverage* problem. They modify the loss function or architecture without changing the input representation's susceptibility to distribution shift.

**Novelty in VLM Application:** Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have rapidly advanced and found diverse applications in robotics, from VLA models directly predicting actions to high-level planners generating language commands, or even reward models for reinforcement learning. BPP carves out a novel niche: using VLMs not for direct action prediction or high-level planning, but as intelligent perception modules to *identify and filter task-relevant keyframes from observation histories*. This semantic compression of history into meaningful events is a distinct and impactful application of VLMs, guiding what the policy should *attend to* rather than what actions it should *take*. This approach aligns with a broader trend in AI of using large pre-trained models for "scaffolding" or "orchestration" rather than end-to-end control, leveraging their powerful semantic understanding capabilities.

By identifying the "coverage problem" as the root cause of long-context IL failures and proposing a VLM-driven keyframe filtering mechanism as a direct solution, BPP makes a significant contribution to making memory-based robot policies practical and robust for real-world, complex tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enable robotic policies to effectively leverage long-term history for successful execution of complex, non-Markovian tasks, overcoming the significant challenges traditionally associated with history conditioning in imitation learning.

The motivations driving this objective can be broken down as follows:

1. **Bridging the Memory Gap in Robot Learning:** Current high-performing robot policies, especially those trained via imitation learning, are predominantly memoryless or rely on very short temporal contexts. This limits their applicability to tasks that inherently require remembering past events, tracking progress, or inferring latent states (e.g., finding an object in a room, multi-stage assembly, remembering an entered password sequence). The ability to reason over history is crucial for true task generalization and robustness in real-world scenarios.

2. **Addressing the Failure Modes of Naïve History Conditioning:** A straightforward approach to endow policies with memory is to condition them directly on sequences of past observations. However, prior research and the authors' own observations consistently show that this "naïve" history conditioning often hurts performance rather than helping. The primary reason is that policies tend to "latch onto incidental features" or "spurious correlations" within the training histories. When the robot deviates even slightly from the expert's trajectory (which is inevitable during deployment), it encounters out-of-distribution (OOD) histories, causing these spurious correlations to break down and leading to catastrophic failures.

3. **Identifying the Root Cause: The Coverage Problem:** The authors meticulously investigate why these spurious correlations arise and persist. They conclude that the fundamental issue is a "coverage problem." The space of all possible observation histories grows exponentially with the task horizon. Expert demonstrations, even if diverse, can only cover a tiny fraction of this vast space. Consequently, policies trained on these limited histories lack robustness when faced with novel, albeit behaviorally similar, OOD histories during deployment.

4. **Challenging the Efficacy of Existing Solutions:** The paper motivates a new approach by critically evaluating existing mitigation strategies, such as regularization techniques, auxiliary objectives (like Past-Token Prediction), and architectural modifications. While these methods can sometimes provide inconsistent, task-dependent benefits, the authors argue they do not fundamentally resolve the underlying coverage problem. They modify *how* a policy learns from history but not *what* history it learns from, failing to address the fundamental distribution shift.

5. **Proposing a Fundamentally Different Approach:** Motivated by these findings, the core objective of BPP is to develop a method that directly tackles the coverage problem by changing the *representation* of history provided to the policy. Instead of raw, comprehensive observation sequences, BPP aims to distill history into a minimal, semantically meaningful set of "keyframes." This "semantic compression" is hypothesized to significantly increase the overlap between training and test-time policy inputs, reduce exposure to spurious correlations, and thus enable robust generalization without sacrificing expressivity. The utilization of powerful, off-the-shelf Vision-Language Models (VLMs) is a key enabler for this keyframe detection.

In essence, the research seeks to move beyond merely improving policy architectures or regularization strategies to instead re-conceptualize how historical context is presented to a robot policy, making it more robust and effective for real-world long-horizon tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of "Big Picture Policies" (BPP) is to replace complex, potentially spurious raw observation histories with a compact set of semantically meaningful "keyframes." This strategy aims to mitigate the history coverage problem by creating a more robust and less susceptible input space for imitation learning.

#### 3.1. Big Picture Policies (BPP) Core Concept

BPP operates on the principle that many non-Markovian tasks depend on identifying a few "behaviorally salient events" (keyframes) rather than the entire raw history. By focusing on these keyframes, trajectories that might differ in incidental details can be represented by the same task-relevant events, thus increasing input distribution overlap between training and deployment.

- **Keyframe Definition:** Keyframes are defined by concise, task-specific criteria that capture significant events (e.g., "a button is pressed," "an object is grasped," "a drawer is opened"). These criteria are typically simple and represent sub-task completions or critical state changes.
- **Keyframe Detection:** A binary keyframe detector, denoted by $\varphi(o_t) \in \{0, 1\}$, identifies whether an observation $o_t$ corresponds to a keyframe. To prevent redundant detections and ensure distinct events, BPP uses a "rising edge" logic (Eq. 1): $\mathcal{K} = \{t : \varphi(o_t) = 1 \land \varphi(o_{t-1}) = 0\}$. This ensures only the *onset* of a detected event is marked as a keyframe, and consecutive positive detections result in a single keyframe.
- **Policy Conditioning:** The robot policy $\pi_\theta(a_t | o_t, \{o_k\}_{k \in \mathcal{K}_t^\Delta})$ conditions on the current observation $o_t$ and the set of available keyframes $\{o_k\}_{k \in \mathcal{K}_t^\Delta}$.

#### 3.2. Systems Design for BPP

The practical implementation of BPP involves leveraging Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for keyframe detection and incorporating latency awareness.

- **VLM for Keyframe Detection:** BPP utilizes an off-the-shelf VLM (specifically, Gemini 3 Pro) as the keyframe detector. The VLM is queried with simple, task-specific prompts (e.g., "Decide whether or not the robot hand has just picked up a mug.") and images (current wrist camera image and previous query image). Queries are made at a low frequency (1 Hz) to manage computational cost. The VLM's powerful semantic understanding allows it to effectively classify salient events.
- **Latency-Aware Training:** VLM inference incurs latency (3-5 seconds in their setup). To ensure the policy learns to act robustly under realistic delays, BPP employs "latency masking" during training. Keyframes available at time $t$ are defined as those detected *prior* to $t - \Delta$, where $\Delta$ is the simulated latency (set to 3 seconds). This trains the policy to rely only on information that would truly be available at inference time, preventing it from overfitting to instantaneous keyframe detection.
- **Policy Architecture:** BPP uses a standard **Diffusion Transformer** policy architecture.
 - **Image Encoders:** ResNet34 is used to encode images from four camera views (top, worm's-eye, two wrist-mounted). Crucially, different weights are used for each camera view, but these weights are *shared across timesteps*. This allows the encoders to adapt to extended histories.
 - **Transformer Decoder:** Image features are flattened into tokens. Proprioceptive state and learnable tokens for diffusion timestep and action denoising are added. These are then processed by a Transformer decoder (7 layers, 512 layer size, 8 attention heads, 0.1 dropout).
 - **Action Prediction:** The policy predicts temporally extended "action chunks" (e.g., 50 timesteps) using a DDPM (Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models) objective. The authors' prior analysis found that longer action chunks help mitigate spurious correlations.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

The methodology includes a rigorous evaluation across both simulated and real-world robotic tasks.

- **Real-World Tasks (ALOHA 2 Platform):** Four challenging bimanual manipulation tasks requiring long-horizon reasoning:
 1. **Mug Replacement:** Swap one mug from a coffee machine for another.
 2. **Marshmallows:** Place two handfuls of marshmallows into a bowl, then press a button.
 3. **Drawer Search:** Search for a key in six drawers across two cabinets, then place it on a table.
 4. **Stacking Puzzle:** Sort and reassemble three colored pieces.
 These tasks involve diverse teleoperated demonstrations, multiple camera views, and proprioceptive state. VLM inference for BPP is performed on Google Cloud (Vertex AI).
- **Simulation Tasks (MuJoCo):** Three tasks explicitly designed to require history conditioning:
 1. **Ingredient-Insertion:** Place two lemons into a bowl (lemons become invisible upon insertion), then press a button.
 2. **Fixed-Password:** Enter a 7-digit password on a 3x3 grid with randomized button positions.
 3. **Variable-Password:** Enter a 4-digit password (with repetitions) using three buttons, with the password provided as a one-hot vector.
- **Baselines:**
 - **Current Observation:** Policy conditions only on the immediate observation.
 - **Naïve History:** Policy conditions on a fixed-length, uniformly-strided window of past observations.
 - **Past-Token Prediction (PTP):** Same as Naïve History, but with an auxiliary objective to predict actions at past timesteps.
 - **Oracle:** A simulated baseline with access to privileged ground-truth state information (e.g., number of lemons inserted, number of buttons pressed), making history conditioning unnecessary.
- **Analysis and Ablations:** Experiments include analyzing the impact of action chunking, the necessity of training image encoders jointly (vs. freezing), the effectiveness of ground-truth state prediction as an auxiliary task, data efficiency studies, and comparing VLM-detected keyframes with oracle keyframes.

This comprehensive approach allows the authors to rigorously demonstrate the limitations of existing methods, validate their hypothesis about the coverage problem, and showcase the superior performance and robustness of BPP.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several critical findings that not only validate the proposed Big Picture Policies (BPP) but also shed light on the fundamental challenges of history-conditioned imitation learning.

#### 4.1. The Criticality of the History Coverage Problem

- **Naïve History's Failure Mode:** The paper confirms that naïvely conditioning on long observation histories, even with expressive policies and action chunking, leads to policies that lack robustness. While action chunking helps mitigate *catastrophic* failures (by forcing more robust feature learning for longer-horizon predictions), a substantial performance gap to an oracle still persists.
- **Inconsistency of Regularization:** A key finding is that even "golden" auxiliary tasks, such as explicitly regularizing the visual encoder to predict ground-truth task state (e.g., number of buttons pressed), *can actually hurt performance* on out-of-distribution (OOD) policy rollouts. This strongly supports the authors' hypothesis that the underlying issue is *limited coverage over the space of possible histories* during training, rather than architectural deficiencies or lack of strong auxiliary objectives. Policies learn features that make correct predictions on in-distribution data but become spurious under distribution shift, leading to worse generalization.

#### 4.2. BPP's Superior Performance Across Tasks

- **Real-World Benchmark Dominance:** BPP consistently and significantly outperforms all baselines on the challenging real-world bimanual manipulation tasks. On average, BPP achieves nearly **70% higher success rates** than the best prior approach (PTP).
 - Qualitatively, BPP policies demonstrate robust progress tracking, such as systematically searching through drawers without revisiting already checked ones, and more reliably retrying after failed grasps.
- **Simulation Excellence, Sometimes Surpassing Oracle:** In simulation, BPP again outperforms all non-oracle methods. Intriguingly, on the Variable-Password task, BPP even **outperforms the Oracle baseline**. The authors attribute this to BPP potentially providing a more learnable and generalizable representation of history compared to the oracle's explicit one-hot encoding, highlighting the power of semantic abstraction.

#### 4.3. Analysis of Baselines

- **Current Observation Policies:** As expected, memoryless policies perform poorly on these non-Markovian tasks, exhibiting repetitive loops and premature termination, as they cannot track task progress.
- **Naïve History Conditioning (Real-World vs. Sim):** In contrast to some simulation results where naïve history provides modest gains, in the real world, it often *hurts* performance relative to current-observation policies. This is attributed to additional sources of spurious correlations (e.g., background variation, sensor noise) present in real-world data, leading to new failure modes like repeated failed grasps or dropping objects incorrectly due to overfitting to training histories.
- **Past-Token Prediction (PTP) Inconsistency:** PTP's effectiveness is highly task-dependent. It shows improvements on tasks like Mug Replacement and Stacking Puzzle, where predicting past actions reliably indicates task state. However, it offers little benefit on Drawer Search or Marshmallows, where the same action sequence (e.g., pulling away from a drawer) can be consistent with multiple underlying task states (e.g., found no key vs. failed grasp). This reinforces the idea that an auxiliary loss's utility depends on its alignment with the true underlying task state inference, and it doesn't solve the fundamental coverage problem.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies and Robustness

- **Data Efficiency:** BPP achieves strong performance with significantly less data than naïve history conditioning. While naïve history can eventually catch up with BPP with a substantially larger number of demonstrations, BPP's ability to reduce exposure to spurious cues makes it far more data-efficient.
- **VLM Detection Robustness:** The performance drop when using VLM-detected keyframes compared to "oracle" ground-truth keyframes is modest (60% vs. 70% success rate on Mug Replacement). This demonstrates that BPP is robust to imperfect VLM detection, and the VLM's latency (mitigated by latency masking) does not severely hinder performance.
- **Architectural Nuances:** Freezing the image encoder (as in some prior work) significantly degrades performance, indicating that the encoder needs to adapt its features for processing historical observations.

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate that BPP successfully addresses the history coverage problem by intelligently compressing history into semantic keyframes, leading to substantially more robust and high-performing robot policies for memory-intensive tasks in both simulation and the real world.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Big Picture Policies" (BPP) framework represents a significant step forward in robot imitation learning, particularly for tasks requiring long-term memory and sequential reasoning. Its significance and potential impact can be distilled into several key areas:

1. **Redefining the Bottleneck in Long-Context IL:** The paper makes a fundamental contribution by rigorously identifying the "history coverage problem" as the primary bottleneck for long-context imitation learning, rather than merely architectural limitations or the need for more complex regularization. This shifts the research focus from refining learning algorithms to intelligently curating and representing the input history itself. This finding has broad implications for how future memory-based robot learning systems might be designed.

2. **Enabling Robustness for Non-Markovian Tasks:** BPP provides a practical and highly effective solution to a long-standing challenge. By successfully enabling robots to track progress and reason over past events in complex, real-world manipulation tasks, BPP expands the repertoire of problems that imitation learning can reliably solve. Tasks like systematic search, multi-stage assembly, or precise ingredient handling, which are ubiquitous in industrial and domestic settings, become much more accessible for robotic automation. The observed 70% average improvement over prior state-of-the-art history-conditioned methods on real-world tasks is a testament to its practical impact.

3. **Novel and Effective Application of Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** BPP showcases a creative and powerful new application for off-the-shelf VLMs. Instead of using VLMs directly for action generation or high-level task planning, BPP leverages them as intelligent "semantic filters" to extract task-relevant "keyframes" from continuous observation streams. This approach capitalizes on the VLM's strong understanding of visual semantics and language instructions to reduce noise and focus the policy's attention on critical events. This paradigm of using large foundation models for data and context *curation* could be transformative for robotics.

4. **Steering Towards Event-Based Robot Learning:** The core idea of BPP—abstracting continuous observations into discrete, semantically meaningful events—points towards a promising future direction: "event-based robot learning." This paradigm could simplify long-horizon task decomposition, improve data efficiency by collapsing diverse trajectories into common event sequences, and enhance explainability. It suggests a future where robots learn not just from raw pixels, but from a structured understanding of milestones and significant occurrences.

5. **Improved Data Efficiency:** BPP's ability to achieve strong performance with significantly less data than naïve history conditioning is crucial. Data collection for real-world robotics is expensive and time-consuming. By making better use of limited expert demonstrations, BPP can accelerate the deployment of intelligent robotic systems.

6. **Future Research Directions and Scalability:**
 - **Automated Keyframe Definition:** The paper notes that current VLM prompting is task-specific but suggests that Large Language Models (LLMs) could potentially generate keyframe criteria automatically, paving the way for truly generalist, history-conditioned robot policies across diverse, open-ended tasks.
 - **Generalization to "Key Segments":** The concept of keyframes could be extended to "key segments" (short sequences of frames capturing an event, like a failed grasp), offering richer context for learning from errors or adapting to new situations. This hints at leveraging BPP's core insight for forms of in-context learning.
 - **Low-Latency Keyframe Detectors:** Addressing the VLM latency limitation through distillation into faster, specialized models is a clear path for future work, enabling BPP's applicability to even more dynamic tasks.

In conclusion, BPP offers a principled and highly effective solution to a fundamental challenge in robot learning, demonstrating how intelligent history representation, empowered by state-of-the-art VLMs, can unlock robust capabilities for complex, memory-intensive robotic tasks. Its impact lies not just in improving performance but in providing a new conceptual framework for building more intelligent and adaptable robotic agents.
