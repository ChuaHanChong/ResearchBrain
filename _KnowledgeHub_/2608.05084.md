# Learning When to Stop: Prefix-Optimal Dynamic Diffusion Policies for Continuous Control

> [!summary] Summary
> The research from Virginia Tech introduces Prefix-Optimal Generative Policies (POGP), a framework for diffusion policies in continuous control that enables dynamic allocation of denoising steps. POGP reduces computational cost by an average of 2.7 times while maintaining over 97.8% of full-chain performance and also improves overall task performance of diffusion policies.

## Problem

- Standard diffusion policies in continuous control are computationally expensive, requiring a fixed, high number of iterative neural network evaluations (K steps) for every action.
- Intermediate actions produced during the denoising chain of standard diffusion policies are not explicitly supervised, making them unsuitable for early stopping or direct execution.
- Existing adaptive compute methods for diffusion policies often use separate halting networks or heuristics, which can be decoupled from the actual action quality and overall policy performance.

## Method

- **POGP** introduces a ==Prefix Value Function (PVF)== and a ==Bellman-style recursion== over the denoising chain to explicitly optimize intermediate actions for quality and to monitor their utility.
- Develops a learned, adaptive stopping criterion derived directly from the PVF, which calculates the marginal gain of further denoising to halt computation when improvement is minimal.
- Utilizes a ==gated joint optimization== strategy that combines environment Q-learning, PVF training, and policy optimization, along with a completion map to refine aggressively truncated actions.

## Results

- POGP achieved an Interquartile Mean (IQM) normalized score of **0.942**, outperforming all 12 baselines and demonstrating a **9.5-point** average improvement over strong diffusion baseline SDAC on full diffusion.
- Reduced average denoising iterations by approximately **2.7x** (from K=20 to ~7 steps) while retaining **≥97.8%** of its full-chain task performance, outperforming D3P with **3.5%** higher performance and **18.2%** fewer iterations.
- Demonstrated adaptive compute allocation in perturbation experiments, using **3-5** steps during steady locomotion and increasing to **15-20** iterations for recovery, while maintaining robustness to unseen hazard distributions and hyperparameter variations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly supervising and valuing intermediate actions within the diffusion chain, through a Prefix Value Function, can improve both computational efficiency (via early stopping) and the terminal performance of the full diffusion policy.
> - A Bellman-style recursion can be effectively applied to the denoising process, allowing for efficient, TD-based learning of prefix values and ensuring a consistent fixed point for intermediate action quality.
> - Dynamic compute allocation can be integrated directly into the policy's value assessment, enabling it to intrinsically determine when more refinement is beneficial, thereby providing a more principled and task-aware adaptive control mechanism.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of reinforcement learning (RL) for continuous control, specifically focusing on the computational efficiency of diffusion policies. Diffusion policies have emerged as a class of policy models capable of representing complex and multimodal action distributions, a challenge for traditional unimodal actors. These policies generate actions through an iterative denoising chain, which typically involves a fixed number of sequential neural network evaluations (K steps) per action.

A significant limitation of standard diffusion policies is their computational cost. The iterative denoising process, executed uniformly for every action, creates a bottleneck that restricts their practical adoption, particularly in real-time or resource-constrained environments like robotic systems. Previous research has addressed this computational challenge through two main avenues:
- **Improving predetermined operating points:** Methods like distillation or consistency models aim to reduce the number of denoising steps required for acceptable performance, optimizing for a fixed, shorter chain length. However, these often do not ensure that *arbitrary intermediate prefixes* of the original diffusion chain are themselves high-value executable actions.
- **Adaptive budget allocation:** Approaches such as D3P (Dynamic Denoising Diffusion Policy) introduce mechanisms, often lightweight adapters, to dynamically adjust the number of denoising iterations at test time. A common issue with these methods is that the intermediate denoising steps are not explicitly optimized to be executable, as the primary training objective focuses on the terminal action. This can lead to a disconnect when inference is prematurely truncated.

The concept of "anytime prediction" and "adaptive computation" from broader machine learning (e.g., in recurrent neural networks or large language models) is a related area, where models can output useful predictions at various computational budgets. However, in continuous control, this concept is distinct because intermediate outputs are not merely internal representations but directly executable actions whose quality impacts downstream return.

This research contributes by introducing a framework that bridges the gap between these approaches. It focuses on explicitly optimizing the quality of intermediate denoising steps, making them executable and providing a principled mechanism for adaptive compute allocation that is integrated with the policy's value assessment rather than being a separate heuristic or module.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the practical applicability of diffusion policies in continuous control by addressing their computational bottleneck. The authors identify that the uniform K-step denoising process, applied irrespective of the task's dynamic requirements, is inefficient. The core motivation stems from the observation that while some control tasks, such as handling disturbances or mode switches, benefit from extensive refinement, many routine actions might be sufficiently determined with fewer denoising steps. Current diffusion policy frameworks do not differentiate between these scenarios, leading to wasted computational resources.

The specific objectives of the proposed Prefix-Optimal Generative Policies (POGP) are detailed as follows:

- **To improve the quality of intermediate actions within the denoising chain:** Standard diffusion policy training supervises only the final, fully denoised action (`a_0`), leaving intermediate actions (`a_t` for `t > 0`) largely unsupervised. Consequently, these intermediate outputs are not guaranteed to be high-quality or executable. A key objective is to provide a training signal that shapes these intermediate outputs, making them suitable for direct execution if the denoising process is halted early.

- **To develop a learned, adaptive stopping criterion for the denoising process:** The goal is to enable the policy to autonomously decide when to stop the iterative denoising, allocating more computation only when needed. This criterion should be derived directly from the policy's understanding of action quality and utility, rather than relying on a separately learned halting network or fixed step reductions, which can be decoupled from the actual action quality.

- **To achieve these goals while maintaining or improving overall task performance:** The introduction of auxiliary objectives or early stopping mechanisms could potentially destabilize training or degrade the performance of the fully-denoised action. A crucial objective is to ensure that the proposed methods not only enable efficiency but also contribute positively to, or at least do not detract from, the final control performance of the policy.

The motivation is rooted in the practical demands of embodied systems, particularly in robotics. For local and edge deployments, optimizing computational effort per action is critical for real-time operation and energy efficiency. An agent perturbed by an external force, for instance, might require more refined actions to recover, while routine locomotion could be managed with less computational overhead. By allowing the policy to adaptively allocate its computational budget, the authors aim to make diffusion policies more versatile and deployable in dynamic, real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Prefix-Optimal Generative Policies (POGP), addresses the computational inefficiency and lack of intermediate supervision in diffusion policies through the introduction of a Prefix Value Function (PVF) and a Bellman-style recursion over the denoising chain.

#### 3.1. Prefix Value Function (PVF)

POGP defines a PVF, `V_t(s, a_t)`, for each intermediate action `a_t` at refinement step `t` (where `t=K` is noise and `t=0` is the terminal action). This function intuitively measures the expected return of executing the action that would result from completing the denoising chain starting from `a_t`. Formally, `V_t(s, a_t) := Q(s, π_full(s, a_t))`, where `π_full(s, a_t)` is the final action `a_0` obtained by completing the full denoising process from `a_t`. In the context of deterministic denoisers used in this work, this simplifies to `V_t(s, a_t) = Q(s, a_0)`. The learning of `V_t` with a function approximator `V_ψ(s, a_t, t)` requires the network to predict the quality of the terminal action from an intermediate state, enabling both intermediate quality shaping and run-time monitoring.

#### 3.2. Prefix Bellman Recursion

To avoid the `O(K^2)` computational cost of naively evaluating `V_t` by running the full chain for every prefix at every gradient step, POGP leverages the temporal structure of the denoising process. A Bellman-style recursion is derived:
`V_t(s, a_t) = h Q(s, a_0) + (1-h) V_{t-1}(s, π_θ(s, a_t, t))`
with a boundary condition `V_0(s, a_0) = Q(s, a_0)`. Here, `h` is a "hazard coefficient" (set uniformly to `1/K = 0.05` across experiments) that balances anchoring the target to the terminal action's Q-value and bootstrapping from the subsequent refinement step. This recursion enables efficient TD-based training of `V_ψ` from a single shared chain per gradient step and ensures a unique fixed point (Theorem 1).

#### 3.3. Joint Optimization with Gated Objectives

POGP jointly trains three components:

- **Environment Critic (Q):** Two Q-networks (`Q_ϕ1,2`) are trained using standard double Q-learning, minimizing `L_Q` based on environment transitions `(s, a, r, s')`. The target `y_Q` incorporates the reward `r` and the discounted Q-value of the next state's optimal action `a'_0`.
- **Prefix Critic (V):** A `V_ψ` network is trained using the Hazard Bellman recursion. A single denoising chain `a_K → ... → a_0` is generated for a randomly sampled `t ∈ {1, ..., K}`. The TD target `y_V` is `h min_i Q_ϕ'_i (s, a_0) + (1-h) V_ψ'(s, a_{t-1}, t-1)`. The loss `L_V` is the mean squared error between `V_ψ(s, a_t, t)` and `y_V`. A crucial aspect is using the terminal action `a_0` from the *same shared chain* for consistency in gradients.
- **Actor (π):** The diffusion policy `π_θ` (the denoiser) is optimized with a gated loss `L_π` that aims to maximize both the terminal action value and maintain high prefix values. The loss is composed of two terms: `-E[min_i Q_ϕ_i (s, a_0)]` and `-w(n) E[V_ψ(s, a_{t-1}, t-1)]`. A gating schedule `w(n)` is employed, where `w(n)` is initially 0 and ramps up via a cosine schedule only after the `V_ψ` RMSE stabilizes below a threshold `τ`. This prevents early noisy `V_ψ` gradients from destabilizing the policy.

#### 3.4. Dynamic Diffusion at Test Time (Learned Early Stopping)

At inference time, the trained `V_ψ` directly provides a stopping criterion. The marginal gain of an additional denoising step is calculated as `∆_t = V_ψ(s, a_{t-1}, t-1) - V_ψ(s, a_t, t)`. Denoising is halted when `∆_t` falls below a relative threshold `ϵ` (`0.01` used) for `m` consecutive steps (`m=2` used), indicating that further refinement is unlikely to yield significant improvement. This rule requires no additional learned parameters.

#### 3.5. Completion Map

When denoising is stopped early at step `T > 0`, the intermediate action `a_T` may contain residual noise. A lightweight, single-layer MLP, `g_ω`, is trained as a "completion map" to project these truncated actions towards their fully-denoised counterparts. The loss `L_g` minimizes the squared Euclidean distance between `g_ω(s, a_T, T)` and `a_0`, where `T` is sampled uniformly during training. This ensures the executed action remains high-quality even with early stopping.

#### 3.6. Network Architecture and Training Protocol

All networks (denoiser, Q-critics, V-critic, completion map) utilize 3-layer MLPs with 256 hidden units and ELU/ReLU activations. Sinusoidal timestep embeddings are concatenated to inputs for time-dependent networks. Training uses the Adam optimizer, a batch size of 256, a 1M-step replay buffer, and a Polyak averaging target network update. Experiments were conducted on MuJoCo environments with a maximum chain length of `K=20`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated POGP across four MuJoCo environments (HalfCheetah-v4, Walker2d-v4, Hopper-v4, Ant-v4) against 12 baseline methods. The findings are summarized below:

#### 4.1. POGP Baseline Performance on Full Diffusion (Q1)

When POGP is evaluated with the full `K=20` diffusion steps, effectively acting as a stand-alone diffusion policy without dynamic stopping, it achieved the highest average performance.
- POGP obtained an Interquartile Mean (IQM) normalized score of 0.942 (with a 95% confidence interval [0.91, 0.97]), surpassing all 12 baselines.
- It recorded the highest return in all four individual environments, for instance, outperforming SDAC (a strong diffusion baseline) by an average of 9.5 points and D3P by an average of 5 points when D3P also runs the full chain.
- Training curves indicated that POGP also trains at a comparable or slightly faster rate than the highest-performing baselines.
This demonstrates that the prefix-based approach, which optimizes intermediate diffusion steps, acts as a beneficial auxiliary objective, leading to improved terminal action performance.

#### 4.2. Adaptive Compute Allocation (Q2)

The primary objective of POGP is to adaptively allocate computational resources.
- POGP reduced the average number of denoising iterations by approximately 2.7 times (from K=20 to an average of ~7 steps) while retaining a high percentage (≥97.8%) of its full-chain task performance.
- Compared to D3P, a state-of-the-art dynamic diffusion baseline, POGP achieved a 3.5% higher average task performance while requiring 18.2% fewer iterations (POGP averaged 6.8 steps vs. D3P's 8.4 steps on HalfCheetah).
- In a perturbation experiment on HalfCheetah, POGP adaptively varied its compute budget: it utilized approximately 3-5 steps during steady locomotion, increased to around 15-20 iterations when a perturbation was applied to recover, and then returned to its minimal compute budget. D3P showed a similar trend but maintained a higher baseline of 7-11 steps and increased less dramatically (to ~12-15 steps) during perturbation.
These results indicate that POGP effectively allocates more computational budget when the task demands it and conserves compute during routine phases, providing a more efficient and responsive control policy.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies were conducted to determine the contribution of individual components of POGP.
- **Shared Chains (S2):** The mechanism of generating `a_0` and `a_t` from the same shared chain for gradient alignment was identified as the most critical stability component, with its removal leading to a substantial 39.7-point drop in return on HalfCheetah.
- **Prefix Learning:** Prefix supervision was found to be the primary source of truncation robustness and adaptive stopping. Without prefix learning, policy retention at 7 denoising steps dropped from 98.8% to 87.7%.
- **Confidence Gate (S3) and Cosine Warmup (S4):** These components, which control the timing and gradual application of the prefix supervision loss, were also shown to be important for stable training. Removing the confidence gate reduced HalfCheetah return from 347.2 to 337.0 and retention at T=5 from 99.1% to 95.4%.
- **Completion Map:** The completion map `g_ω` provided a complementary benefit, particularly for aggressive truncations, increasing retention from 94.8% to 99.0% at two steps when prefix learning was enabled. This suggests it primarily corrects residual noise in early stopping points.

#### 4.4. Generalization and Robustness

- POGP demonstrated robustness to various test-time hazard distributions not seen during training. Even under an "early-biased" truncation scenario, POGP retained 97.4% of its full-chain performance, while baselines dropped below 81%.
- Sensitivity analysis to hyperparameters (hazard value `h`, gate threshold `τ`, prefix weight `w_max`) showed that POGP's performance is robust across a reasonable range of these parameters.

#### 4.5. Chain Length Sensitivity

- POGP's retention advantage over baselines (e.g., SDAC) increased with longer denoising chain lengths (K). For K=5, the retention gap was modest (~2-3 percentage points), but for K=20, it widened to ~15-17 percentage points.
- POGP consistently maintained >97% retention at half-chain truncation across all environments and K values, while SDAC's retention steadily decreased with increasing K.
- Full-chain return for both POGP and SDAC improved monotonically with K, with POGP consistently outperforming SDAC in absolute return at every K.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of POGP represents a notable advancement in the development of efficient and robust diffusion policies for continuous control. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Computational Efficiency:** POGP directly addresses the primary computational bottleneck of diffusion policies by enabling adaptive allocation of denoising steps. The demonstrated 2.7x reduction in compute, corresponding to approximately 66% fewer iterations, while retaining nearly full performance, translates to substantial energy savings and faster control loop frequencies. This is particularly critical for real-time applications, embedded systems, and resource-constrained platforms in robotics and autonomous agents.

- **Improved Intermediate Action Quality and Anytime Capabilities:** By introducing the Prefix Value Function and training it via a Bellman-style recursion, POGP explicitly optimizes the quality of intermediate actions. This ensures that even if the denoising process is halted prematurely, the resulting action is of high quality and executable. This "anytime" property provides a safety guarantee, as the system can respond with a usable action even under unexpected computational interruptions.

- **Principled Adaptive Control:** Unlike previous methods that might rely on separate learned halting networks or heuristic rules, POGP's stopping criterion emerges directly from the learned value function of the policy. This grounding in expected return provides a more principled and task-aware mechanism for dynamic computation allocation, allowing the policy to intrinsically decide when additional refinement is truly beneficial. This connects adaptive computation concepts to reinforcement learning through value-based supervision over diffusion prefixes.

- **Performance Improvement as an Auxiliary Benefit:** The research demonstrates that supervising intermediate denoising steps not only enables early stopping but also acts as a beneficial auxiliary objective that improves the final, full-chain performance of the policy. This suggests that the internal structure of the diffusion process becomes more robust and effective when all its stages are evaluated for utility.

- **Broader Impact on Reinforcement Learning:** POGP contributes to the growing area of adaptive inference-time compute in machine learning, akin to how large language models learn to "think longer" on harder problems. By extending this to continuous control and grounding it in Bellman equations, POGP offers a new perspective on how computational resources can be dynamically managed within an RL agent.

- **Limitations and Future Work:** While POGP demonstrates strong results on MuJoCo locomotion tasks, its applicability to more complex domains like manipulation or real-robot scenarios, and its scalability to very long diffusion chains (e.g., K > 50-100), remain areas for future exploration. The authors also acknowledge a degree of seed sensitivity related to critic learning stability, suggesting a need for more robust gating criteria. Addressing these limitations could further broaden POGP's impact.
