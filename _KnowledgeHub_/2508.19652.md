# Self-Rewarding Vision-Language Model via Reasoning Decomposition

> [!summary] Summary
> Vision-SR1 introduces a self-rewarding reinforcement learning framework for Vision-Language Models (VLMs) that guides visual perception through reasoning decomposition. This approach significantly improves visual reasoning capabilities, reduces visual hallucinations and language shortcuts, and better balances multimodal and text-only reasoning.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) frequently generate visual hallucinations and rely on language shortcuts, diminishing their trustworthiness.
- Current VLM training methods, especially R1-style RL, offer sparse visual signals, causing models to overemphasize linguistic reasoning at the expense of genuine visual understanding.
- External visual supervision is unscalable due to high annotation costs and can introduce biases or lack adaptability to evolving model policies.

## Method

- Decomposes VLM reasoning into distinct ==visual perception== and ==language reasoning== stages, requiring the VLM to first generate a self-contained visual description.
- Implements a ==two-pass self-reward mechanism== where the VLM in a second pass verifies if its own generated visual perception is sufficient to derive the correct answer without re-accessing the image.
- Employs ==Multimodal GRPO== with a composite reward function, incorporating rewards for visual perception fidelity, answer accuracy, and structured output adherence.

## Results

- Vision-SR1 achieved an average performance score of **58.8** (with Qwen2.5-VL-7B) across **24** diverse VLM benchmarks, surpassing Vision-R1 (**57.4**) and SFT-tuned models (**55.1**).
- The self-reward mechanism demonstrably reduces the Language Shortcut Rate (LSR), lowering it from **7.9** to **6.7** for the 7B model, indicating improved visual grounding over linguistic priors.
- The framework helps mitigate "text-only forgetting" in multimodal RL, preserving text-only reasoning capabilities on math tasks and showing larger gains on general knowledge benchmarks compared to Vision-R1.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-rewarding frameworks can effectively provide adaptive and dense supervision for intermediate visual perception in VLMs, addressing scalability and bias issues of external methods.
> - Explicitly decomposing VLM reasoning into visual perception and language reasoning steps fundamentally strengthens visual grounding and reduces reliance on linguistic shortcuts.
> - The Language Shortcut Rate (LSR) serves as a quantifiable metric to diagnose instances where VLMs produce correct answers despite flawed visual perceptions, highlighting spurious correlations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in vision-language models (VLMs) have primarily stemmed from the integration of pre-trained language models and vision encoders, coupled with instruction tuning and reinforcement learning (RL) techniques. Despite these developments, VLMs continue to exhibit critical limitations, notably visual hallucinations—generating descriptions that lack consistency with visual inputs—and language shortcuts, where models rely on textual priors rather than comprehensive visual understanding.

The paper identifies the root cause of these issues in most post-training methods for VLMs: an over-reliance on simple, verifiable answer matching that supervises only final outputs. This approach leaves intermediate visual reasoning without explicit guidance, resulting in sparse visual signals and a tendency for VLMs to prioritize language-based reasoning over visual perception. Existing efforts to introduce intermediate visual supervision typically involve either costly and labor-intensive human annotations or distilled labels from external large models. However, these external supervision methods introduce limitations such as inherent biases, increased latency, scalability challenges across diverse multimodal tasks, and potential reward hacking due to distributional shifts between fixed intermediate signals and continually updated policies.

Furthermore, current R1-style reinforcement learning methods, while improving VLM reasoning abilities, have been observed to encourage "thinking over seeing." This imbalance often leads VLMs to heavily lean on language reasoning while demoting visual perception, making them susceptible to reward hacking and spurious effects. Such improvements may sometimes represent probability shifts towards training and test data styles, favoring language shortcut answers derived from prior knowledge, rather than a genuine enhancement of visual understanding.

This work positions itself to address these limitations by proposing a self-rewarding RL framework. It seeks to provide explicit guidance for visual reasoning without external supervision, thus aiming to mitigate visual hallucinations and reduce language shortcuts more directly and efficiently than prior approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a self-rewarding reinforcement learning framework, Vision-SR1, that enhances the visual reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) without requiring external visual supervision. This objective is driven by the identified shortcomings of existing VLM post-training methods and their associated challenges.

The key motivations and specific goals include:

- **Mitigating Visual Hallucinations:** VLMs frequently generate content descriptions inconsistent with visual inputs. The research aims to reduce these hallucinations by explicitly guiding the model to ground its reasoning in visual evidence.
- **Reducing Language Shortcuts:** Current VLMs often bypass visual understanding, instead relying on text-based priors or common sense to answer questions. A core objective is to decrease this reliance on language shortcuts, ensuring that the model genuinely processes and understands visual information.
- **Providing Explicit Visual Reasoning Guidance:** Most existing methods offer sparse visual signals, supervising only the final output. The paper aims to introduce a mechanism for explicit guidance during intermediate visual reasoning steps, preventing the model from prioritizing language reasoning at the expense of visual perception.
- **Enhancing Computational Efficiency and Scalability:** Existing solutions that rely on human annotations are costly and unscalable, while those using external models introduce computational overhead and latency. A key motivation is to eliminate the need for such external dependencies, making the training process more efficient and scalable by allowing the VLM to self-evaluate its visual reasoning.
- **Decoupling and Balancing Reward Signals:** Simple summation of various intermediate rewards can lead to entangled and sparse learning signals, making it difficult for the policy to attribute credit effectively. The research aims to address this by decoupling visual and language reasoning reward signals, providing more fine-grained feedback to enable balanced optimization of both capabilities.
- **Developing a Self-Contained Verification Mechanism:** Motivated by the limitations of external supervision, the project seeks to establish an internal verification process where the VLM itself assesses whether its generated visual descriptions are sufficient to answer a given query.

By achieving these objectives, Vision-SR1 aims to contribute a more robust, efficient, and visually grounded approach to VLM training, addressing fundamental issues of faithfulness and reasoning quality.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Vision-SR1 introduces a three-stage self-rewarding reinforcement learning method designed to improve VLM visual reasoning. It builds upon Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and operates by reusing the same VLM model across its different stages without requiring additional external models or GPUs.

#### 3.1. Reasoning Decomposition

The method decomposes the VLM's reasoning process into two distinct components: visual reasoning and language reasoning. For a given multimodal question Q = {image, query}, the VLM is prompted to generate a structured output composed of three parts:
- `⟨visual reasoning⟩ c ⟨/visual reasoning⟩`: A self-contained visual description (`c`) intended to capture all visual information necessary to answer the task.
- `⟨think⟩ t ⟨/think⟩`: The language reasoning trace (`t`), which should be derivable using only the query and the visual perception (`c`), without re-accessing the original image.
- `⟨answer⟩ a ⟨/answer⟩`: The final answer (`a`).

This structured generation format (referred to as See-Think) ensures an explicit separation between visual interpretation and subsequent logical deduction.

#### 3.2. Self-Reward for Visual Reasoning (Stage 1 & 2)

To validate the self-containment of the generated visual reasoning `c`, Vision-SR1 employs a self-reward mechanism:
1. **First Pass (Standard Rollout):** The VLM takes the original `(Image, Query)` pair and generates the structured output: `(Visual Perception, CoT Reasoning, Answer)`. An initial answer reward (`r_ans`) is computed by comparing the final answer `a` with the ground truth `a*`.
2. **Second Pass (Self-Reward Rollout):** The *same VLM model* is re-prompted to perform language reasoning. This time, it receives only the `(Query, Visual Perception)` (`q`, `c`) as input, *without* the original image. If the model successfully derives the correct answer `a*` from this text-only input (`ˆa = f_θ(c, q)`), the visual perception `c` is considered faithful, and a self-visual reward (`r_visual`) is assigned (`r_visual(Q, c) = I(ˆa = a*)`). This process leverages the model's own reasoning ability for self-evaluation, eliminating the need for external reward models.

#### 3.3. Reward Composition

The overall reward signal integrates three components:
- **Format Reward (`r_fmt(s)`):** A binary reward (e.g., 0 or 1) indicating whether the model's output adheres to the specified structured format. This is applied to both answer and visual rewards.
- **Answer Reward (`r_ans(Q, a)`):** Comprises the accuracy of the final answer (`r_acc`) and a weighted format reward. It implicitly encourages correct Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning because the answer is generated after the reasoning trace. `r_ans(Q, a) = r_acc(Q, a) + α r_fmt(s)`.
- **Visual Reward (`r_visual(Q, a)`):** Assesses the self-containment of the visual reasoning (`r_vis acc`) and includes a weighted format reward. A reward of 1 is assigned if the VLM can correctly answer the question using only the query and its generated visual reasoning. `r_visual(Q, a) = r_vis acc(Q, a) + α r_fmt(s)`.

#### 3.4. Multi-Reward Policy Optimization (Stage 3)

To address the issue of sparse and entangled learning signals that can arise from simply summing rewards, Vision-SR1 employs a decoupled multi-reward policy optimization strategy:
- **Reward-Specific Log-Probability Tracking:** During both rollout passes, behavioral log probabilities for every token generated (`log π_old(a_ans,t)` and `log π_old(a_visual,t)`) are cached.
- **Group-wise z-Score Advantage:** Separate advantages (`A_ans` and `A_visual`) are computed for each reward component (answer and visual) using GRPO's group-wise z-score normalization. This centering removes question-level biases and focuses on pairwise preferences.
- **Actor Loss (Policy Gradient Loss):** The actor loss combines weighted policy gradients for the two reward signals. `L_actor = - (1/2B) Σ (A_ans,t log π_θ(a_ans,t) + A_visual,t log π_θ(a_visual,t))`.
- **KL Divergence Regularization Loss:** Separate KL penalty coefficients (`β_ans`, `β_visual`) are applied to regularize the policy deviation for each reward component, maintaining closeness to the original pre-trained model.
- **Total Loss Objective:** The total loss `L_total = L_actor + L_KL` integrates these component-specific policy gradients and regularization terms. This decomposition ensures clear gradient paths from each reward to its corresponding reasoning component, facilitating independent optimization of visual and language abilities.

#### 3.5. Computational Efficiency

The two-stage rollout training reuses the same VLM, avoiding the computational overhead of deploying additional reward models on separate GPUs or calling external APIs. The authors report that this two-stage process introduces a modest overhead (approximately 20% increase in training time) compared to standard one-stage GRPO, significantly less than methods relying on external reward models.

#### 3.6. Data Preparation

Vision-SR1 is trained on Vision-SR1-47K, a dataset comprising approximately 47,000 examples collected from 24 open-source VLM benchmarks. This dataset spans three domains: mathematical reasoning (30.5%), commonsense knowledge (30%), and general visual understanding (39.5%).

#### 3.7. Implementation

The method was implemented using Qwen2.5-VL-3B and 7B, and Mimo-7B-VL as base models. The RL phase was trained for 200 steps. The policy model remains frozen during both rollouts when computing the self-reward for visual reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate Vision-SR1 focused on its performance across diverse vision-language tasks, its robustness against language shortcuts, and its impact on internal model mechanisms.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Against Baselines

Vision-SR1 was compared against several baselines, including Vision-R1, Perception-R1, and Visionary-R1, across general visual understanding, multimodal mathematical reasoning, and visual hallucination detection benchmarks.
- **Qwen2.5-VL-7B Backbone:** Vision-SR1 achieved an average score of 52.2 across benchmarks, outperforming the Vision-R1 fair comparison run (50.7). Notably, on MMMU-Pro, Vision-SR1 scored 40.7 compared to Vision-R1's 39.8, and on MMMU, it scored 52.2 against 51.8.
- **Qwen2.5-VL-3B Backbone:** With the smaller backbone, Vision-SR1 similarly outperformed, achieving an average of 48.8 against Vision-R1's 47.1.
- **Mimo-VL-7B Backbone:** Vision-SR1 demonstrated generalization beyond the Qwen-VL family, improving average accuracy from 46.0 (Vision-R1) to 49.5 on the Mimo-VL-7B model.
These results indicate that Vision-SR1 consistently achieved higher performance compared to baseline models across various vision-language tasks.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study on Spatial Reasoning and Language Shortcut Robustness

The effectiveness of Vision-SR1 was also evaluated on datasets specifically designed to probe spatial reasoning (MMSI-Bench, OmniSpatial) and language shortcut (ViLP) robustness.
- Vision-SR1 demonstrated generalization to spatial reasoning benchmarks and improved robustness to visual-language shortcuts.
- For the Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone, the average performance across these specific benchmarks improved from 32.1 (before RL) to 41.5, with notable gains on ViLP (from 45.1 to 52.6) and OmniSpatial (from 27.3 to 44.2). This suggests that explicitly generating visual descriptions, as mandated by Vision-SR1, helps the model to rely more on visual content and less on shortcut behaviors.

#### 4.3. Analysis on Language Shortcut Rate (LSR)

The paper introduced the Language Shortcut Rate (LSR) as a metric to quantify instances where a model produces a correct answer despite an incorrect (not self-contained) visual perception. A lower LSR indicates reduced reliance on language shortcuts.
- Vision-SR1 consistently exhibited lower average LSRs compared to its ablated version (Vision-SR1 w/o self-reward). For the 7B model, the average LSR for Vision-SR1 was 9.8, versus 10.1 for the ablated version. For the 3B model, the averages were 9.4 and 10.4, respectively.
- These findings suggest that the self-rewarding visual reasoning mechanism effectively reduces the model's tendency to utilize language priors to guess correct answers without genuine visual understanding.

#### 4.4. Analysis of Visual Attention Change

An analysis of layer-wise shifts in visual attention, comparing the base model with Vision-SR1, was conducted using the ViLP dataset.
- **Early Layers (0–7):** Post-training led to an increase in visual attention, with a peak gain of +10.2% at Layer 6, indicating enhanced extraction of low-level visual features early in the network.
- **Middle Layers (8–13):** A decrease in visual attention was observed, suggesting a redistribution rather than a uniform increase.
- **Late Layers (14–27):** Visual attention rose again, peaking at Layer 20 (+9.2%), which was interpreted as enhanced visual re-engagement during the visual integration and output generation stages. These changes suggest that Vision-SR1 training re-weights the VLM's visual processing, enhancing perception at critical stages.

#### 4.5. Analysis on Text-Only Reasoning (Appendix)

An evaluation of text-only reasoning capabilities after multimodal RL fine-tuning showed:
- Multimodal RL training generally degraded text-only reasoning performance on mathematical benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH-500), consistent with "text-only forgetting."
- However, Vision-SR1 was more effective than Vision-R1 at mitigating this degradation in math benchmarks and yielded larger gains on general knowledge tasks (MMLU-Pro, SuperGPQA). This indicates that the decoupled optimization signals help preserve text-only competencies while enabling multimodal improvements.

#### 4.6. Ablation Study on Self-Reward (Appendix)

An ablation study confirmed the importance of the visual reasoning self-reward and Multi-Reward Policy Optimization.
- Training a control version of Vision-SR1 without the self-visual reward and decoupled optimization (`Vision-SR1 w/o self-reward`) resulted in overall worse VLM task performance compared to the full Vision-SR1 model. For the 7B model, the average performance dropped from 52.2 to 50.3.
- The structured output prompt alone, without the self-reward mechanism, provided a slight improvement (+1.0 on average) over Vision-R1, suggesting prompt design also contributes to performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Vision-SR1 offers a method for addressing fundamental challenges in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by enhancing their visual reasoning capabilities through a self-rewarding reinforcement learning framework.

One significant aspect of this work is its **computational efficiency and scalability**. By enabling the VLM to self-evaluate its visual reasoning, Vision-SR1 bypasses the need for costly human annotations or resource-intensive external reward models. This circumvents issues of latency, bias, and the additional GPU overhead associated with deploying external models, making VLM alignment training more practical and accessible.

The core contribution of **reasoning decomposition and multi-reward policy optimization** provides a structured approach to VLM training. By explicitly separating visual and language reasoning components and applying decoupled reward signals, the framework offers clearer gradient attribution. This enables more balanced optimization, potentially reducing the entanglement of heterogeneous reward signals often encountered in traditional reward summation methods. This detailed feedback mechanism contributes to more robust model learning, as evidenced by improved performance across diverse tasks.

Vision-SR1 demonstrates a notable impact on **mitigating visual hallucinations and reducing language shortcuts**. These are persistent issues in VLMs where models may generate inconsistent visual descriptions or rely on textual priors rather than genuine visual understanding. The experimental results, particularly the lower Language Shortcut Rate (LSR) and the analysis of visual attention shifts, indicate that the method effectively encourages the model to ground its responses in visual input, leading to more faithful and reliable reasoning. The introduction of the LSR metric itself provides a valuable diagnostic tool for quantifying and understanding these model behaviors.

Furthermore, the generalizability of Vision-SR1 across different VLM backbones (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL and Mimo-VL) suggests its potential for **broader applicability** in the VLM research landscape. The improvements observed across general visual understanding, multimodal mathematical reasoning, and spatial reasoning benchmarks highlight its versatility.

The paper also opens several **future research directions**. These include exploring more efficient generation formats where visual reasoning could be treated as a latent thought, potentially reducing the number of decoded tokens while retaining reward attribution. Additionally, the authors suggest a need for further analysis to disentangle genuine visual grounding from spurious effects or shortcut learning that might still occur in RL training, particularly in areas like mathematical reasoning. This commitment to deeper mechanistic understanding reflects a broader impact on the interpretability and trustworthiness of VLM reasoning.

Overall, Vision-SR1 presents a practical and effective approach to improving the visual reasoning fidelity of VLMs, with implications for their reliability, efficiency, and interpretability in real-world applications.
