# Yume: An Interactive World Generation Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Yume introduces an interactive world generation model that synthesizes dynamic, explorable virtual environments from images or text, featuring intuitive keyboard-driven camera control. The model produces high-fidelity real-world urban scenes, outperforming existing methods in visual quality and instruction following.

## Problem

- Existing video generation models struggle to create interactive and realistic videos, particularly for complex real-world urban scenarios, due to limitations in generalizability and handling diverse dynamic elements.
- Intuitive and stable camera control in video generation is challenging, with prior methods often relying on overly granular absolute camera poses or complex additional learnable modules.
- Complex scene generation in video diffusion models frequently suffers from visual artifacts such as flickering, unnatural textures, and geometric distortions, diminishing perceptual quality and immersion.

## Method

- **Yume** defines a ==Quantized Camera Motion (QCM)== module that discretizes continuous camera trajectories into a predefined set of semantically meaningful, discrete actions, which are then injected as textual conditions into the text-to-video model.
- The model is built upon the ==Wan architecture==, integrating ==Masked Video Diffusion Transformers (MVDT)== and a ==FramePack memory module== to enhance visual consistency, detail, and enable coherent long-form video generation.
- It employs a ==Training-Free Anti-Artifact Mechanism (AAM)== and ==Time Travel Sampling based on SDE (TTS-SDE)== to improve visual quality, plus a co-optimization strategy combining ==adversarial distillation== and a ==cache-accelerating mechanism== to reuse intermediate residual features for faster runtime.

## Results

- **Yume** achieved a substantially higher instruction-following score (**0.657**) than Wan-2.1 (**0.057**) and MatrixGame (**0.271**); ablations showed TTS-SDE further improved this to **0.743**, while model distillation reduced inference time by over **70%** (**583.1s** to **158.8s**).
- The model demonstrated strong performance in generating extended video sequences autoregressively, with subject and background consistency dropping by only **0.5-0.6%** over a 10-second span.
- Qualitative visualizations showed accurate adherence to keyboard controls and generalizability to diverse unreal and real-world edited scenes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Discretizing and textualizing camera motions (Quantized Camera Motion) provides stable, intuitive, and learnable control over video generation without requiring new learnable parameters.
> - Masked processing within video diffusion transformers (MVDT) improves visual detail and temporal consistency, while a training-free Anti-Artifact Mechanism refining high-frequency components during denoising further mitigates artifacts in complex scenes.
> - Jointly optimizing adversarial distillation and cache-acceleration offers a practical approach to achieving significant runtime speedups in video diffusion models, crucial for interactive applications.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Yume operates at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields within generative artificial intelligence: video generation, interactive AI, and virtual world simulation. Its core contribution lies in advancing the capability to create **interactive, realistic, and dynamic virtual worlds** from diverse inputs, specifically focusing on complex real-world scenarios and intuitive user control.

The foundation of Yume is built upon significant advancements in **video diffusion models**, such as those seen in Imagen Video, Make-A-Video, Lumiere, Sora, and Stable Video Diffusion. These models have demonstrated remarkable success in synthesizing high-fidelity and temporally coherent visual content from text or static images. Yume leverages the "Wan architecture," a modern video diffusion transformer, as its backbone, indicating its reliance on the latest architectural innovations in this domain.

A crucial aspect of Yume's contribution is its focus on **camera control in video generation**. While earlier models implicitly generated camera movements, recent works like MotionCtrl, Direct-a-Video, and CameraCtrl have explored explicit conditioning on camera parameters or trajectories. Yume distinguishes itself from these by proposing a **Quantized Camera Motion (QCM)** mechanism. Unlike methods that rely on dense, explicit sequences of absolute camera pose matrices (which can be overly granular and unstable), Yume discretizes camera movements into semantically meaningful actions (e.g., "move forward," "turn left"). This innovative approach aims for more intuitive, stable, and keyboard-driven control, crucial for interactive exploration.

Furthermore, Yume positions itself within the burgeoning field of **navigatable world generation**. Prior efforts in this area include learning world models (e.g., Dreamer series) for agent planning, and more recently, generative models like Genie, GAIA-1, SIMA, Matrix-Game, and WORLDMEM. These models have explored generating controllable 2D worlds, driving scenes, or generalist agents in 3D environments, often leveraging game-based scenarios (e.g., Minecraft datasets for Matrix-Game). Yume explicitly addresses a critical gap: the generalization to **real-world environments**. Existing methods often struggle with the complexity, diversity, and dynamic nature of real-world urban scenarios, or are trained on relatively simplistic game data. Yume's use of the high-quality Sekai-Real-HQ dataset, comprising real-world walking and drone videos, directly tackles this limitation, aiming for unprecedented realism and generalizability beyond synthetic or static scenes.

Finally, Yume contributes to the ongoing efforts in **mitigating generation artifacts** and **accelerating video diffusion models**. Artifacts like flickering and unnatural textures are common challenges in complex scene generation. Yume introduces a "Training-Free Anti-Artifact Mechanism (AAM)" which refines high-frequency components during denoising, a novel approach compared to training-based methods or other training-free noise initialization strategies. For acceleration, it integrates "adversarial distillation" with "cache-accelerating mechanisms," building upon prior work in consistency models and feature caching for diffusion transformers. This comprehensive approach underscores Yume's ambition to push the boundaries of interactive video generation across multiple technical fronts.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the Yume project is to "create an interactive, realistic, and dynamic world" from various inputs, including images, text, or videos. This generated world should allow for "exploration and control using peripheral devices or neural signals."

For its preview version presented in this paper, Yume focuses on a specific, yet highly ambitious, subset of this grand vision:
- **Generating a dynamic world from an input image:** This involves taking a single static image and expanding it into a navigable, moving environment.
- **Enabling exploration via keyboard actions:** The model aims to translate simple, discrete keyboard inputs (e.g., 'W' for forward, 'A' for left) into corresponding camera movements within the generated video, providing an intuitive interactive experience.

The motivation behind these objectives stems from several critical observations and aspirations:
1. **Demand for Immersive Experiences:** There is a growing demand for vast, interactive, and persistent virtual worlds in domains such as world simulation, interactive entertainment, and virtual embodiment. Yume seeks to provide a technological foundation for these applications.
2. **Addressing Limitations of Existing Video Generation:** While video diffusion models have shown remarkable capabilities, they face significant challenges in producing *interactive* and *realistic* videos, particularly in complex *urban scenarios*.
 - **Generalizability to Real-World:** Many existing approaches focus on synthetic or static scenarios, leading to a "domain gap" that limits their application to the real world. Yume aims to bridge this gap.
 - **Intuitive Camera Control:** Current methods often rely on absolute camera motions requiring precise annotations and complex extra learnable modules. Yume seeks a more user-friendly, stable, and interpretable control mechanism.
 - **Handling Urban Complexity:** Urban environments are challenging due to diverse architectural styles, dynamic objects, and intricate details. Existing models struggle to maintain consistent realism, leading to visual artifacts like flickering, unnatural textures, and geometric distortions. Yume aims to mitigate these issues to enhance perceptual quality and immersion.
3. **Realizing Aspirational Applications:** The authors envision "entering a world through a photo, like time travel," enabling users to explore any scene regardless of location, time, or original context. This could potentially allow individuals to "compensate for our real-world regrets or realize our wishes through the virtual world." This highly imaginative motivation underscores the long-term, transformative potential the authors see in Yume.

In essence, Yume is motivated by the desire to push interactive world generation beyond static or game-centric limitations, creating a robust, high-fidelity, and controllable system that can generate dynamic real-world environments from simple inputs, thereby enabling novel immersive experiences.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Yume's methodology is built upon a well-designed framework comprising four interconnected main components: camera motion quantization, video generation architecture, advanced sampler, and model acceleration.

#### 3.1. Data Processing and Camera Motion Quantization

Yume is trained on **Sekai-Real-HQ**, a high-quality subset of the Sekai dataset, which comprises thousands of hours of real-world walking and drone video clips with corresponding camera trajectory annotations.
A key innovation is the **Quantized Camera Motion (QCM)** module. Recognizing that raw, continuous camera trajectories are often imprecise and difficult for models to follow without complex additional modules, Yume discretizes these motions. It defines a predefined set of canonical relative transformations (e.g., "move-forward," "turn-left"). For each segment of actual camera movement, the system calculates its relative transformation and matches it to the closest predefined canonical motion. This process effectively quantizes continuous trajectories into a sequence of semantically meaningful, discrete actions. Crucially, these discrete motions are then parsed into **textual descriptions** and injected as conditions into the text-to-video foundation model. This allows camera pose-controlled video generation without introducing new learnable parameters, making it highly compatible with pre-trained image-to-video (I2V) models and promoting stable, precise control. Beyond direction, Yume also quantifies and controls *motion speed* by extracting translational, directional change, and rotational dynamics indicators, which are also provided as textual conditions.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture

Yume is based on the **Wan architecture**, utilizing its spatio-temporal VAE encoder and a Denoising Diffusion Transformer (DiT) model.
- **Masked Video Diffusion Transformers (MVDT):** To enhance visual quality and consistency, Yume integrates MVDT. Inspired by masked representation learning, MVDT uses an asymmetric network (encoder, side-interpolator, decoder) to process selectively masked input features. During training, it stochastically masks input video tokens, and a side-interpolator, using learnable latent tokens and self-attention, dynamically predicts the masked content. This approach improves detail, smooths inconsistencies, and reduces artifacts by ensuring the model focuses computational resources on visible tokens while accurately inferring masked regions.
- **Image-to-Video (I2V) and Video-to-Video (V2V) Generation:** The model supports both I2V (initializing from a single image) and V2V (using a video's final frame as conditioning). For V2V, a specific training strategy is employed where 30% of iterations use static image conditions (individual images tiled temporally) to prevent the model from overly relying on homogeneous conditioning video motion, thereby improving its ability to generate diverse motion patterns.
- **Long Video Generation:** To address temporal discontinuity in autoregressive generation, Yume adopts a chunk-based autoregressive framework with a modified **FramePack memory module**. This module contextually compresses historically generated frames using progressively higher downsampling ratios for older frames (e.g., (1,2,2) for recent, (1,4,4) for older, etc.) while preserving high-resolution information for recent segments. This allows for theoretically infinite video generation by feeding a compressed history of prior frames as context to predict new segments.

#### 3.3. Sampler Design

Two key innovations enhance sampling quality:
- **Training-Free Anti-Artifact Mechanism (AAM):** This two-stage denoising process is designed to eliminate visual artifacts in complex scenes. First, a standard denoising pass generates an initial latent estimate (`z_orig`). Second, during a refinement pass, especially in the initial steps, AAM combines the low-frequency components of the noised `z_orig` (stable, coarse structure) with the high-frequency components of the current refinement latent (fine details) using a low-pass filter (e.g., Gaussian blur) and its pseudo-inverse. This guides the refinement to preserve overall structure while enhancing sharpness and reducing artifacts without additional training. While effective for I2V, the paper notes its limitation for V2V long-video generation due to the underlying I2V architecture, an area for future work.
- **Time Travel Sampling based on SDE (TTS-SDE):** Inspired by DDNM and OSV, TTS-SDE refines denoising by leveraging "future" state information. For a current timestep, it samples forward a few steps to obtain a more accurate velocity estimate from a "future" state, then uses this to reconstruct the current state. By replacing deterministic ODE-based sampling with SDE (introducing controlled stochasticity), TTS-SDE significantly boosts textual controllability and sharpness, addressing a limitation of pure ODE samplers.

#### 3.4. Acceleration

To improve runtime efficiency, Yume employs a co-optimization strategy:
- **Adversarial Distillation:** A GAN-based framework distills the denoising process, allowing the model to achieve comparable quality in fewer sampling steps (e.g., 14 steps instead of 50). A discriminator is trained to distinguish real samples from generated ones, while the DiT model learns to fool the discriminator while maintaining denoising accuracy.
- **Cache-Accelerating:** This mechanism reduces computational redundancy by reusing intermediate residual features across denoising steps, particularly for "cacheable layers" within the DiT backbone. These layers are identified by an MSE-based importance metric, selecting those whose ablation has minimal impact on output quality, effectively skipping computation for certain blocks in subsequent steps.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Yume's performance was evaluated using the custom Yume-Bench framework, which assesses both visual quality and instruction following, particularly for complex real-world scenes and interactive keyboard inputs.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Results

- **Image-to-Video Generation:** Yume significantly outperformed state-of-the-art (SOTA) models like Wan-2.1 and MatrixGame. In instruction following, Yume scored 0.657, substantially higher than Wan-2.1 (0.057) and MatrixGame (0.271). This highlights Yume's superior capability in translating discrete keyboard inputs into accurate camera movements. Additionally, Yume demonstrated optimal or near-optimal performance across other metrics, including subject consistency, background consistency, motion smoothness, aesthetic quality, and imaging quality, indicating overall superior visual fidelity and realism.
- **Long-Video Generation:** Yume showcased strong performance in generating extended video sequences autoregressively. An 18-second video generation demonstrated reasonable stability over time, with subject and background consistency dropping by only 0.5-0.6% over a 10-second span. During motion transitions (e.g., switching from varied motion to continuous forward), a temporary drop in instruction-following performance was observed (8.6%), attributed to motion inertia from the input video. However, this effect diminished, leading to a significant recovery (22.3%) in instruction-following after 12 seconds, affirming Yume's ability to adapt to new instructions over longer sequences.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Verification of TTS-SDE Effectiveness:** Comparing different samplers, TTS-SDE yielded a notable improvement in instruction following (0.743) compared to Yume-ODE (0.657), while maintaining competitive scores across other metrics. This validates that the stochasticity introduced by SDE, combined with the "time travel" mechanism, effectively enhances the model's textual controllability and improves the sharpness and aesthetic quality of the generated videos.
- **Validating the Effect of Model Distillation:** Distilling the model from 50 to 14 inference steps (a significant reduction) led to substantial acceleration, reducing inference time from 583.1 seconds to 158.8 seconds. While most visual quality metrics remained largely consistent with the baseline, a slight reduction in instruction following (from 0.657 to 0.557) was observed. This suggests a trade-off where fewer steps enhance speed but might slightly weaken the model's text-control capability.

#### 4.3. Visualization Results

Qualitative visualizations (Figures 6 and 7) visually confirmed Yume's capabilities. Figure 6 demonstrates that Yume accurately follows predefined motion paths based on keyboard controls, generating coherent video sequences from an initial image. It also showcases impressive generalizability, producing interactive worlds from diverse unreal scenes (Japanese anime, Pixar style) and real-world edited scenes (e.g., changing weather from sunny to snowy, or time from night to day using external image editing methods like GPT-4o). Figure 7 specifically illustrates the effectiveness of the Anti-Artifact Mechanism (AAM), showing clearer videos with enhanced structural details and the elimination of illogical scenes or aberrant artifacts that appear without AAM.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Yume represents a significant advancement in the field of generative AI, particularly in bridging the gap between static content generation and dynamic, interactive world simulation. Its contributions offer both immediate practical implications and lay a crucial foundation for future research.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Pioneering Interactive Real-World World Generation:** Yume is one of the first models to successfully generate high-fidelity, dynamic, and interactive virtual worlds specifically from real-world image inputs, allowing exploration via intuitive keyboard controls. This moves beyond the limitations of prior work, which often focused on synthetic datasets or lacked robust real-world generalization.
- **Novelty in Control and Architecture:** The introduction of Quantized Camera Motion (QCM) as a text-based condition simplifies control and improves stability without adding complex learnable modules. The Masked Video Diffusion Transformers (MVDT) and the refined FramePack memory module are architectural innovations that enhance visual quality and enable long-form, coherent video generation.
- **Advanced Sampling and Acceleration:** The Training-Free Anti-Artifact Mechanism (AAM) offers a practical solution for improving visual quality in complex scenes without additional training costs. The Time Travel Sampling based on SDE (TTS-SDE) enhances textual controllability and sharpness, while the joint optimization of adversarial distillation and caching provides a critical pathway for real-time interactive applications by dramatically reducing inference time.
- **Open Science Contribution:** The commitment to open-sourcing the data, codebase, and model weights is highly significant. It fosters transparency, reproducibility, and enables the broader research community to build upon Yume's foundations, accelerating progress in this complex domain.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Immersive Entertainment:** Yume could revolutionize interactive gaming, virtual reality (VR), and augmented reality (AR) experiences by allowing users to instantly generate and explore dynamic environments from simple prompts or even personal photos. The "time travel" aspect, as envisioned by the authors, holds immense potential for personalized virtual experiences.
- **Advanced Simulation and Training:** The ability to generate realistic and controllable real-world scenes could be transformative for training autonomous systems (e.g., self-driving cars, robotics) in diverse and dynamic environments, urban planning simulations, and architectural visualization.
- **Creative Content Creation:** Artists, filmmakers, and content creators could leverage Yume to rapidly prototype and generate complex animated scenes, edit environments dynamically, and explore creative concepts that were previously time-consuming or technically challenging.
- **Foundation for Embodied AI:** Yume's focus on interactive exploration sets the stage for more sophisticated embodied AI research, where intelligent agents can perceive, navigate, and interact within dynamic, generated environments. The long-term goal of control via neural signals hints at future brain-computer interface applications.
- **Future Research Catalyst:** By addressing core challenges in interactive video generation and open-sourcing its components, Yume serves as a strong baseline and catalyst for future research in areas like object interaction within generated worlds, improving long-term temporal consistency at higher fidelity, and enhancing runtime efficiency for truly real-time applications.

In conclusion, Yume represents a substantial leap forward in the ambition and capability of generative AI, pushing the boundaries of what is possible in creating interactive, realistic, and dynamic virtual worlds. While the authors acknowledge ongoing challenges related to visual quality, runtime efficiency, control accuracy, and achieving object interaction, Yume has laid a robust and open foundation for these future endeavors.
