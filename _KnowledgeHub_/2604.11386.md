# ComSim: Building Scalable Real-World Robot Data Generation via Compositional Simulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Compositional Simulation provides a scalable method for generating high-quality robot training data by integrating classical simulators' precise action control with neural simulators' visual realism. This approach converts simulated videos into "pseudo-real" data, leading to substantial improvements in robot policy success rates and enhanced generalization to novel object configurations and types. For example, the Shake Bottle task success rate increased from 17/30 (with 20 real demonstrations) to 28/30 (with 10 real and 200 pseudo-real demonstrations).

## Problem

- Acquiring large-scale, high-quality, and diverse robot training data is costly and time-consuming.
- Classical simulators offer scalable data but suffer from a significant "sim2real domain gap," limiting policy transfer to real-world applications.
- Neural simulators can generate visually realistic data but often lack physical consistency and precise action-video alignment, leading to issues like hallucination.

## Method

- Introduces ==Compositional Simulation==, a hybrid "real-sim-real" data augmentation pipeline that leverages both classical and neural simulation capabilities.
- Trains a ==Diffusion Transformer (DiT)== based ==neural simulator== to transform classical simulation videos into "pseudo-real" data, guided by both control dynamics (actions) and visual dynamics (simulated observations).
- Utilizes a small, precisely aligned real-world dataset to train the neural simulator, then generates large-scale, diverse (V_sim, A) pairs using a rule-based classical simulator (RoboTwin), which are subsequently refined by the neural simulator.

## Results

- The neural simulator achieved superior performance across video quality metrics (e.g., PSNR **19.577**, SSIM **0.6484**, FID **147.90**, FVD **15.765**), confirming effective sim2real transfer of visual dynamics while preserving action consistency.
- Robot policies trained with **10** real and **200** pseudo-real demonstrations showed substantial improvements in task success rates, with **28**/**30** for the Shake Bottle task and **29**/**30** for Move Playing-Card Away, outperforming policies trained with only real or traditional simulated data.
- Policies trained with pseudo-real data demonstrated enhanced generalization to novel spatial distributions (e.g., **12**/**30** OOD success for Shake Bottle vs. 0/**30** with traditional sim data) and new objects, indicating the preservation of real-world properties and effective transfer.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining the precise action generation of classical simulators with the visual realism capabilities of neural simulators effectively bridges the sim2real gap.
> - Guiding the neural video generation process with both control dynamics (robot actions) and visual dynamics (simulated observations) is critical for achieving high fidelity in both visual appearance and action accuracy.
> - A relatively small amount of real-world data can effectively train a neural simulator to produce large-scale, high-quality "pseudo-real" data that significantly augments limited real datasets for robust robot policy learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a central challenge in data-driven robotics: the acquisition of large-scale, high-quality, and diverse training data for robotic systems. Recent advancements in foundational models, such as large language models (LLMs) and world models, have broadened robotic capabilities, enabling complex autonomous tasks. However, these models often require extensive datasets of video-action pairs, which are costly and time-consuming to collect manually in real-world environments.

Current approaches to data generation in robotics primarily include:
- **Direct Human Data Collection:** Provides high-quality data but is expensive, time-consuming, and limited in scalability and diversity of real-world scenarios.
- **Classical Simulators (e.g., MuJoCo, Isaac Lab):** Offer scalable data generation with precise action-video pairs and omniscient views. However, they suffer from a significant "sim2real domain gap" due to discrepancies in appearance, sensor characteristics, and contact physics compared to the real world. This gap hinders the direct transfer of policies trained in simulation to real-world applications.
- **Neural Simulators (e.g., based on video generation models):** Aim to bridge the sim2real gap by generating visually realistic videos from input trajectories or actions. While they can produce visually consistent data, they often suffer from issues like hallucination, lacking physical consistency, and weak action-video alignment, making the generated data less suitable for robust policy training.

This paper proposes "Compositional Simulation" as a hybrid approach designed to combine the strengths of classical and neural simulation while mitigating their respective weaknesses. It positions itself as a scalable solution for generating training data that is both accurate in action and consistent with real-world visual and physical dynamics, thereby aiming to reduce the sim2real domain gap more effectively than existing methods. The work integrates aspects of digital twin creation, diffusion models for video generation, and rule-based simulation to achieve this objective.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a scalable and effective method for generating high-quality robot training data that accurately reflects real-world scenarios, thereby improving the performance and generalization capabilities of robotic policy models.

The specific motivations and objectives include:

- **Addressing Data Scarcity and Cost:** Robotic tasks require vast amounts of data, particularly action-video pairs, for training advanced policy models. Manual data collection is prohibitively expensive and cannot cover the immense diversity of real-world environments. The work aims to provide a scalable alternative to reduce reliance on costly real-world data.
- **Mitigating the Sim2Real Domain Gap:** Classical simulators can generate large datasets but the visual and physical discrepancies between simulated and real environments ("sim2real gap") severely limit the transferability of learned policies. A key objective is to reduce this gap, ensuring that policies trained with synthetic data perform robustly in the real world.
- **Overcoming Neural Simulator Limitations:** While neural simulators can generate visually realistic data, they often struggle with maintaining accurate action-video consistency and physical realism, leading to "hallucinations" or physically implausible actions. The research seeks to combine visual realism with precise action control.
- **Developing a Hybrid Data Generation Paradigm:** The authors propose a "Compositional Simulation" concept, advocating for a hybrid approach that leverages the precise action generation capabilities of classical simulators and the visual realism enhancement of neural simulators.
- **Designing a Real-Sim-Real Data Augmentation Pipeline:** A concrete objective is to establish a closed-loop pipeline where a small amount of real-world data is used to inform and train a neural simulator. This trained neural simulator then transforms large-scale classical simulation data into "pseudo-real" data, which, when combined with actual real data, facilitates policy training.
- **Enhancing Policy Performance and Generalization:** The ultimate goal is to demonstrate that policies trained with data generated by the compositional simulation approach achieve higher success rates in real-world tasks and exhibit stronger generalization to novel spatial layouts and object variations compared to policies trained with less refined simulated data or limited real-world data alone.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Compositional Simulation framework integrates classical and neural simulation through a "real-sim-real" data augmentation pipeline.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The problem is formulated as transforming a large dataset of video-action pairs from a classical simulator, D_sim = {(v_i, a_i)}, into a distribution approximating a real-world dataset, D_real = {(v'_j, a'_j)}. This transformation is performed by a neural simulation function N(·) such that N(D_sim) ≈ D_real, while ensuring that the generated action a_i aligns with the original action a'_j, and maintaining 3D scene consistency and video quality.

#### 3.2. Real2Sim Data Collection and Alignment

To train the neural simulator, a dataset of (V_sim, V_real, A) tuples is created, where V_sim and V_real represent videos from simulation and real-world, respectively, for the *same* action sequence A. This requires a digital twin simulation environment strictly aligned with the real-world data collection platform.
Alignment is performed at three levels:
- **Background and Object Alignment:** Desktop and background colors in the classical simulator (RoboTwin) are matched with the real world. Digital twin assets ensure visual consistency and scale matching for objects. This involves parameterizing real-world attributes (e.g., RGB values for surfaces, principal axes for regular objects, direct mapping for special objects like specific bottles/cards) and configuring the simulated environment accordingly.
- **Camera Calibration and Alignment:** Camera parameters (intrinsics and extrinsics) in the simulation are made consistent with those of the real-world camera. This involves using a calibration checkerboard in the real-world coordinate system, detecting 2D pixel coordinates of corners, and solving a Perspective-n-Point (PnP) problem to recover camera position and orientation.
- **Object Position Alignment:** During task initialization, real-world object positions are localized and precisely transferred to the classical simulator. The desktop is conceptualized as a 5 cm x 5 cm lattice, and objects are placed by aligning their center of mass with a lattice node, with orientations selected from a prescribed catalogue (e.g., axis-aligned or specific rotations).

A small set of real-world trajectory data (actions and corresponding videos) is collected via human teleoperation using PiPER Teach Pendants. These real-world trajectories are then replayed in the precisely aligned classical simulator to generate paired simulated videos, forming the (V_sim, V_real, A) dataset. For the experiments, 20 real-world trajectories across 10 tasks were collected, resulting in 200 data pairs for training the neural simulator.

#### 3.3. Sim2Real Compositional Dynamic Video Generation (Neural Simulator Training)

A neural simulator, based on a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture, is trained to transform classical simulation videos into real-world videos while preserving action consistency. The backbone utilized is a Stable Diffusion 1.5 model.
The DiT is conditioned on two types of dynamics:
- **Control Dynamics (actions):** Represents the robot's control signals.
- **Visual Dynamics (simulated observations):** Represents the visual feedback from the classical simulator.
These dynamics guide the video generation process. The scores conditioned on both dynamics are composited during sampling, enabling "Dynamic Guidance" to steer the simulation toward accurate video generation.
The neural simulator is optimized by minimizing a loss function (L_sim2real) that measures the discrepancy between the generated simulated video and the corresponding real-world video, aiming to make f_N(V_sim, A, θ) ≈ V_real. Since actions (A) are inherently aligned in the paired dataset, the focus is on optimizing video consistency.
Training involves fine-tuning the Stable Diffusion 1.5 model on the (V_sim, V_real, A) pairs. A fixed "sim-to-real instruction" text prompt is provided ("Change the image style from the image style of the simulated environment to the image style captured by a DSLR camera."). The model minimizes a combined loss of denoising loss, perceptual loss, and pixel-wise loss. An online inference strategy (FRESCO) is applied for temporal continuity in generated videos.

#### 3.4. Data Generation with Rule-Based Simulation (Large-Scale Data Production)

After the neural simulator is trained, large-scale (V_sim, A) pairs are generated using a classical simulator (RoboTwin). This process leverages:
- **Action Primitives:** A comprehensive set of interaction rules (e.g., grasp, push, align) defining atomic and higher-order robot behaviors.
- **GPT-5 Integration:** Large Language Models (GPT-5) are employed to synthesize executable code composed of these action primitives, ensuring semantic correctness and physical feasibility.
- **Systematic Variation:** Environmental conditions, object initialization states (including out-of-domain spatial arrangements and different objects like various bottle types or card colors), and agent actions are systematically varied to create diverse trajectories covering a broad spectrum of real-world task scenarios.
The resulting simulated videos (V_sim) are then passed through the trained neural simulator (N) to refine their visual characteristics into "pseudo-real" data (V_real), while preserving the original dynamics and action consistency.

#### 3.5. Real World Deployment with Compositional Simulation

The final step involves training a robot policy using a combined dataset:
- A small amount of real-world data (e.g., 10 or 20 demonstrations).
- A large volume of "pseudo-real" data generated by the compositional simulation pipeline.
This combined dataset is used for joint training of a robot policy (e.g., Diffusion Policy). This approach is designed to leverage the broad distribution coverage of the pseudo-real data with the fidelity of real data, aiming to improve policy performance and generalization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents experimental results validating the effectiveness of Compositional Simulation in two main areas: sim2real transfer via neural simulation quality and real-world policy execution.

#### 4.1. Sim2Real Transfer via Neural Simulation

**Quantitative Results:**
The quality of generated "pseudo-real" videos was evaluated against real-world videos using several metrics: PSNR, SSIM, CLIP Score (perceptual similarity), LPIPS, FID, and FVD (distributional similarity and temporal coherence).
- **Ours-Full (Compositional Dynamic Video Generation with joint control and visual dynamics guidance)** consistently achieved the best performance across all metrics (e.g., PSNR 19.577, SSIM 0.6484, CLIP Score 0.8102, FID 147.90, FVD 15.765).
- **Ours-VD (Visual Dynamics only)** showed strong performance (e.g., PSNR 18.153, FVD 22.311), indicating the dominant role of visual dynamics in bridging the sim-to-real gap.
- **Ours-CD (Control Dynamics only)** performed poorly (e.g., FVD 239.13), suggesting that control dynamics alone are insufficient for generating visually coherent and realistic videos.
- **Classical Simulation (Sim)** and baseline video generation models (Stable Diffusion 1.5 based) performed significantly worse than Ours-Full, confirming the existence of the sim2real gap and the limitations of general video generation without domain adaptation.

**Qualitative Results:**
Visual comparisons across four tasks (Move Playing-Card Away in cluttered/colored background, Place Mouse Pad in cluttered/colored background) demonstrated:
- Conventional diffusion baselines and zero-shot models exhibited hallucination artifacts and failed to preserve critical visual semantic information.
- Ours-CD accurately reproduced manipulation actions but generated scenes from the model's prior knowledge, leading to unsatisfactory visual fidelity.
- Ours-VD contributed substantially to sim-to-real transfer, showing good visual realism, but still had flaws in action accuracy (e.g., gripper timing) and physical consistency (e.g., motion blur).
- **Ours-Full** achieved photorealistic visual generation of agents and scenes while leveraging motion guidance from control dynamics to accurately reproduce real-world manipulation actions, demonstrating precise sim-to-real alignment in both perception and action.

#### 4.2. Real World Execution with Compositional World Simulation

Diffusion Policy (DP) models were trained under six different data-mixture regimes for 8 distinct robotic manipulation tasks. The performance was measured by task success rates.

- **Limited Real Data:** DP performed poorly with only 10 real-world demonstrations (e.g., Shake Bottle: 9/30 success). Doubling real data to 20 demonstrations improved success rates (e.g., Shake Bottle: 17/30), highlighting the need for sufficient real-world experience.
- **Traditional Simulated Data:** Pre-training on 200 RoboTwin-simulated demonstrations or jointly training with them provided limited benefits (e.g., Shake Bottle with 200 Sim Pretrain + 10 Real: 12/30; with 10 Real + 200 Sim: 6/30). This indicates that the visual-physical gap of traditional simulators caps their utility for real-world policy transfer.
- **Pseudo-Real Data (Ours):** Jointly training with 10 real-world demonstrations and 200 pseudo-real demonstrations generated by Compositional Simulation yielded a substantial increase in task success rates (e.g., Shake Bottle: 28/30; Move Playing-Card Away: 29/30).
- **Zero-Shot Pseudo-Real:** Even when trained exclusively on 200 pseudo-real demonstrations (without any real-world supervision), the policy achieved non-trivial performance (e.g., Shake Bottle: 10/30; Move Playing-Card Away: 18/30), demonstrating the effectiveness of the generated data.

#### 4.3. Generalization

The study further evaluated the generalization capabilities of policies trained with Compositional Simulation.

- **Generalization to Novel Spatial Distributions:** Policies trained solely on real data or with traditional simulated data exhibited almost zero generalization to out-of-domain (OOD) initial object positions. In contrast, policies trained with pseudo-real demonstrations consistently improved performance across relocated configurations (e.g., for Shake Bottle, 12/30 OOD success for 10 Real + 200 Pseudo Real, compared to 0/30 for 10 Real + 200 Sim). This suggests that the synthesized scenes faithfully reproduce the spatial statistics of real-world environments.
- **Generalization to New Objects:** When evaluating shape- and color-level generalization by substituting new objects (e.g., different bottle types or a red playing card instead of a blue one), traditional simulated demonstrations provided no improvement. However, pseudo-real demonstrations from Compositional Simulation yielded a clear boost in success rates (e.g., for Shake Bottle with new objects, 15/30 success for 10 Real + 200 Pseudo Real, compared to 0/30 for 10 Real + 200 Sim). This demonstrates that the method preserves real-world properties and supports transfer to unseen objects.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Compositional Simulation framework offers a scalable solution for generating training data for robotics, addressing the long-standing challenges of data scarcity, cost, and the sim2real domain gap.

### 6. Significance

- **Novel Hybrid Approach:** The introduction of Compositional Simulation, combining the strengths of classical (action accuracy, scalability) and neural (visual realism, domain adaptation) simulation, represents a distinct paradigm in robot data generation.
- **Effective Sim2Real Transfer:** The research demonstrates that the proposed neural simulator, particularly when guided by both control and visual dynamics, can significantly reduce the visual and physical discrepancies between simulated and real environments, leading to high-fidelity "pseudo-real" data.
- **Enhanced Policy Performance and Generalization:** Policies trained with this "pseudo-real" data, even with limited real-world supervision, achieve notably higher success rates and demonstrate stronger generalization capabilities across varied spatial layouts and novel objects. This suggests that the generated data effectively captures real-world nuances beyond appearance.
- **Cost-Effective Data Generation:** By leveraging a small amount of real-world data to train the neural simulator and then generating large-scale diverse data synthetically, the approach offers a more resource-efficient alternative to extensive real-world data collection.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerated Robot Learning:** The ability to generate vast, high-quality, and diverse datasets could accelerate the development and deployment of more capable and adaptable robotic systems across various applications.
- **Broader Accessibility to Robotics Research:** Reducing the cost and effort of data collection could make advanced robot learning research more accessible to institutions with limited access to extensive real-world robot platforms.
- **Improved Robustness of Embodied AI:** Policies trained with data from Compositional Simulation are shown to be more robust to variations in scene configuration and object attributes, which is crucial for real-world deployment of embodied AI systems.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework could serve as a foundation for extending data generation to richer modalities (e.g., haptic feedback, audio) and broader robotic embodiments (e.g., mobile manipulation). Future work could also explore the use of unpaired data to further enhance capabilities and generalization, thereby advancing compositional simulation as a general data generation strategy.
