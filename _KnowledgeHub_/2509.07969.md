# Mini-o3: Scaling Up Reasoning Patterns and Interaction Turns for Visual Search

> [!summary] Summary
> Mini-o3, developed by ByteDance and The University of Hong Kong, significantly enhances open-source Vision-Language Models for complex visual search tasks by enabling multi-turn, exploratory reasoning and scaling interaction depth at inference. It achieves state-of-the-art performance on challenging benchmarks like VisualProbe-Hard, demonstrating its capacity for deeper thinking paths.

## Problem

- Current open-source Vision-Language Models (VLMs) exhibit monotonous reasoning patterns and allow only a limited number of interaction turns, hindering their performance on difficult visual search tasks.
- Challenging visual problems requiring fine-grained perception, such as small targets or numerous distractors, demand iterative exploration and trial-and-error, which existing open-source models lack.
- There is a notable gap between the advanced multi-turn reasoning and tool-use capabilities of proprietary models (e.g., OpenAI o3) and open-source alternatives.

## Method

- Mini-o3 utilizes an iterative ==Thought-Action-Observation loop==, allowing the VLM to generate internal thoughts, execute grounding actions (e.g., zoom-in), and observe cropped image patches over multiple turns.
- A two-phase training procedure combines Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on a dataset of diverse multi-turn trajectories with Reinforcement Learning (GRPO) using verifiable rewards to optimize policy.
- The approach introduces the challenging ==VisualProbe dataset== for complex visual search and employs a novel =="over-turn masking" strategy== during RL to enable deeper test-time reasoning without excessive training costs.

## Results

- Mini-o3 achieves state-of-the-art performance among 7B-sized open-source models, reaching **48.0%** accuracy on VisualProbe-Hard, **50.4%** on VisualProbe-Medium, and **67.0%** on VisualProbe-Easy, significantly outperforming baselines like DeepEyes.
- The model demonstrates the ability to naturally scale interaction turns at test time; accuracy on VisualProbe-Hard continues to improve as the upper limit on interaction turns increases from **4** to **32** during inference.
- Ablation studies confirm the necessity of cold-start SFT, hard RL data, and over-turn masking for high performance and deep reasoning, with over-turn masking alone improving VisualProbe-Hard accuracy from **32.2%** to **44.4%** and increasing average turns for correct responses.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A "cold-start" Supervised Fine-Tuning phase is essential to bootstrap multi-turn agentic behaviors in VLMs, as base models lack intrinsic capabilities for long-horizon, interactive problem-solving.
> - The "over-turn masking" strategy effectively addresses the premature policy termination problem in RL, allowing models trained with limited turn budgets (e.g., 6 turns) to naturally scale to tens of turns at inference time with improved accuracy.
> - The design of challenging datasets, such as VisualProbe, featuring small targets, numerous distractors, and high-resolution images, is critical for training robust, exploratory visual agents capable of human-like iterative analysis.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields within artificial intelligence: Vision-Language Models (VLMs), tool-augmented agents, and reinforcement learning for advanced reasoning.

**Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** The past few years have seen remarkable progress in VLMs, which integrate visual and textual understanding. Early models like BLIP-2, Flamingo, and LLaVA established the paradigm of combining pre-trained vision encoders with large language models (LLMs). More recent models such as Gemini, GPT-4o, and Qwen2.5-VL have pushed performance further, primarily by scaling model capacity and training on diverse, high-quality multimodal corpora. Mini-o3 builds upon this foundation, specifically using Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct as its base model, to enhance its interactive reasoning capabilities.

**Tool-Augmented Agents:** A significant trend in AI research is the development of "agentic" capabilities, where models can interact with environments, use external tools, and engage in multi-turn reasoning to solve complex problems. This paradigm has been prominently showcased by models like OpenAI's `o3` and `o4`, Kimi-Researcher, and others that leverage toolkits such as web browsing, code execution, and retrieval. In the visual domain, approaches like DeepEyes, Chain-of-Focus, and Pixel Reasoner have equipped VLMs with active perception abilities, allowing them to perform iterative zoom-in and region-of-interest selection. Mini-o3 directly contributes to this sub-area by focusing on *image-centric* tool use within a multi-turn reasoning framework.

**Reinforcement Learning for Reasoning:** Reinforcement Learning (RL) has become instrumental in enhancing the reasoning capabilities of both LLMs and VLMs. Algorithms like REINFORCE, PPO, and GRPO are used to optimize policies with verifiable reward signals, encouraging structured, step-by-step problem-solving. This includes techniques like Chain-of-Thought prompting, which has improved textual reasoning. Mini-o3 adopts GRPO for its RL phase, leveraging its ability to optimize policy based on the correctness of generated trajectories.

**Bridging the Gap:** The paper identifies a crucial limitation of existing *open-source* VLMs in tool-integrated agentic settings: they often exhibit "monotonous reasoning patterns and allow only a limited number of interaction turns," making them "inadequate for difficult tasks that require trial-and-error exploration." While proprietary models like OpenAI's `o3` demonstrate diverse reasoning strategies and deep thinking trajectories, open-source counterparts lag. Mini-o3 directly addresses this gap by providing a "complete recipe" to achieve similar "thinking-with-image" capabilities, thus pushing the frontier of open-source multimodal agents toward more complex and exploratory visual problem-solving. It's a significant step towards democratizing advanced AI agent capabilities previously seen only in closed systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from a critical observation: while existing open-source Vision-Language Models (VLMs) perform adequately on simple visual search benchmarks, they consistently fail on more challenging tasks that necessitate intricate, multi-step exploration and trial-and-error reasoning.

The **key objectives** of the "Mini-o3" project are:

1. **Overcome Monotonous Reasoning Patterns:** To enable VLMs to exhibit diverse reasoning strategies, such as depth-first search, trial-and-error exploration, and self-reflection, rather than rigid, shallow patterns.
2. **Scale Up Interaction Turns:** To extend the interaction depth of VLMs, allowing them to engage in tens of tool-interaction rounds at inference time, significantly beyond the typical few turns observed in existing open-source models.
3. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance on Challenging Visual Search Tasks:** By enabling deeper and more diverse reasoning, the ultimate goal is to solve visual problems that were previously intractable for open-source VLMs.
4. **Provide an Open-Source Recipe:** To offer a complete, reproducible methodology for training VLMs to exhibit "OpenAI o3–style" multi-turn, tool-based behaviors, thereby advancing the capabilities of the broader research community.

The **motivation** is primarily driven by:

- **Limitations of Current Open-Source VLMs:** The paper explicitly points out that models like DeepEyes achieve only 35.1% accuracy on VisualProbe-Hard and typically use image tools for only one turn per example on benchmarks like HR-Bench-4K. This highlights a clear deficiency in handling complex visual scenarios.
- **Gap with Proprietary Models:** In contrast, proprietary models like OpenAI `o3` demonstrate the capability to produce diverse reasoning strategies and deep thinking trajectories (tens of turns). The authors are motivated to bridge this performance and capability gap for open-source models.
- **Need for Exploratory Reasoning:** Challenging visual search problems often require iterative exploration, hypothesis testing, and self-correction, much like human problem-solving. Current open-source models lack this intrinsic exploratory capability, making them insufficient for real-world complexity.
- **Efficiency and Scalability Concerns:** Training for many interaction turns is computationally expensive. The motivation also includes finding efficient ways to train models that can scale their interaction depth at inference time without requiring prohibitively long training-time budgets.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Mini-o3's approach integrates a novel dataset, an iterative data collection pipeline, and a specialized reinforcement learning strategy to scale up reasoning patterns and interaction turns.

#### 3.1. Overall Agentic Pipeline

The system operates on an iterative **Thought-Action-Observation loop**:
- Given an input image and a user query, the policy model generates an internal **Thought** ($T_i$), which is its reasoning process.
- Based on $T_i$ and the interaction history, it produces an **Action** ($A_i$). Actions can be either `grounding` (specifying a zoom-in region with `bbox_2d` and a `source` image) or `emitting a final answer`. This allows the model to act on the original image or any prior observation.
- Executing $A_i$ in the environment yields a new **Observation** ($O_i$), which is typically a cropped image patch. This $O_i$ is then appended to the history and fed back to the model, continuing the loop until a final answer is given or limits are reached.

#### 3.2. Two-Phase Training

Mini-o3 employs a two-phase training procedure:

1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** The initial phase involves fine-tuning the base VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) on thousands of multi-turn trajectories. This "cold-start" SFT is crucial to teach the model the fundamental behaviors of generating valid trajectories with diverse and robust reasoning patterns, as the base model lacks exposure to such long-horizon agentic interactions during pretraining.
2. **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR):** Following SFT, GRPO (Group Sequence Policy Optimization) is applied to optimize the policy. Rewards are computed based on the correctness of responses, often using an external LLM as a judge for semantic matching. This phase refines the agent's policy to maximize successful task completion. Training efficiency is maintained by capping interaction turns at 6 and context length at 32K tokens.

#### 3.3. Training Data Collection

- **Visual Probe Dataset:** Recognizing the need for challenging instances to encourage deep reasoning, the authors constructed **VisualProbe**. This dataset comprises 4,000 training and 500 test visual question-answer pairs, structured into easy, medium, and hard difficulty levels. Key characteristics making it challenging include:
 - **Small targets:** The objects to be found are often diminutive.
 - **Numerous distractor objects:** Many irrelevant elements clutter the images.
 - **High-resolution images:** Requiring precise, iterative zooming.
 These properties mandate iterative exploration and trial-and-error, making it ideal for training robust multi-turn agents.

- **Diverse Cold-start Data Collection:** To bootstrap the multi-turn tool-use capabilities, an iterative pipeline (Fig. 5) is used:
 - A small set of diverse, manually crafted multi-turn exemplars (image, question, per-turn observations, thoughts, actions) are created, showcasing strategies like depth-first search and self-reflection.
 - An existing VLM (with in-context learning ability) is prompted in a few-shot manner to mimic these behaviors, generating thoughts and actions for new queries.
 - Only trajectories culminating in a correct answer are retained. Approximately 6,000 such trajectories are collected from 6 exemplars for SFT.

#### 3.4. Reinforcement Learning Enhancements

- **Lower Down Max Pixels:** To allow more interaction turns within the fixed 32K token context length, the maximum pixel budget per image is reduced from 12M to 2M (or lower). This simple adjustment enables longer thinking paths, crucial for trial-and-error exploration.

- **Over-turn Masking:** This is a crucial innovation to address the challenge of limited training-time turn budgets while aiming for deep test-time reasoning.
 - **Problem:** In vanilla GRPO, trajectories that hit the maximum turn limit or context length are assigned zero reward, leading to negative advantages after normalization. This implicitly penalizes long responses, biasing the model towards answering prematurely (as seen in Fig. 6, "Mini-o3 w/o over-turn masking" results in many fewer turns).
 - **Solution:** The proposed **over-turn masking** introduces a `completion mask M`. For trajectories that are incomplete (hit turn limit or context length), `M_i` is 0; otherwise, `M_i` is 1. Masked advantages `A'_i = M_i * A_i` are computed, ensuring that incomplete trajectories do not contribute negative learning signals. The objective function is also normalized by the number of *completed* generations.
 - **Benefit:** This prevents the model from collapsing into an "answer earlier" strategy. It allows training with a small turn budget (e.g., 6 turns, reducing training time from 10 days to 3 days) while enabling the model to naturally scale to tens of turns at inference time, with accuracy improving as the number of turns increases (Fig. 1).

#### 3.5. Inference

- **Generation with Temperature:** To mitigate repetitive words or sentences during inference (a common issue as context grows with turns), the temperature is set to 1.0. This introduces randomness, reducing repetition without degrading coherence.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on Mini-o3 highlight its superior performance and novel capabilities in multi-turn visual search tasks.

1. **State-of-the-Art Performance:** Mini-o3 consistently achieves state-of-the-art results across various visual search benchmarks when compared to other 7B-sized open-source models:
 - **VisualProbe:** Significantly outperforms baselines, achieving 48.0% on VisualProbe-Hard, 50.4% on Medium, and 67.0% on Easy (compared to DeepEyes' 35.1%, 29.8%, and 60.1% respectively, and Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct's 23.9%, 26.0%, and 39.1%). This demonstrates its strength on challenging, exploratory tasks.
 - **V\*Bench, HR-Bench, MME-Realworld:** Mini-o3 also exhibits competitive or superior performance on these established benchmarks, indicating its generalizability beyond the VisualProbe dataset (e.g., 88.2% on HR-Bench-4K compared to DeepEyes' 83.3%).

2. **Test-Time Scaling of Interaction Turns:** A crucial finding is Mini-o3's ability to naturally scale the number of interaction turns at inference time, even when trained with a limited turn budget (e.g., 6 turns).
 - As shown in Figure 1 (left), accuracy on VisualProbe-Hard *continues to grow* as the upper limit on interaction turns increases from 4 to 32 during testing. This demonstrates that Mini-o3 develops deep thinking paths and can effectively leverage more turns for harder problems.
 - Figure 1 (right) further illustrates that Mini-o3 solves a higher percentage of correct trajectories with deeper thinking paths compared to baselines.

3. **Ablation Study Insights (Table 2):**
 - **Hard RL Data:** Removing challenging RL data significantly drops performance on VisualProbe-Hard by 8.6 points (from 44.4% to 35.8%), confirming its importance for encouraging complex reasoning.
 - **Cold-start SFT:** This phase is essential. Without cold-start SFT, performance collapses (25.4% on VisualProbe-Hard), validating the hypothesis that base models lack intrinsic multi-turn agentic capabilities. The average turns for correct responses also drop from 5.5 to a mere 1.0 without SFT, indicating a severe limitation in exploratory behavior.
 - **Over-turn Masking:** The technique proves critical. Without it, performance on VisualProbe-Hard drops from 44.4% to 32.2%, and the average turns for correct responses reduce from 5.5 to 3.0. This highlights its role in preventing premature termination and fostering deeper exploration (further corroborated by Fig. 6).

4. **Impact of Max Pixels (Table 3):**
 - Both excessively large (12M) and overly small (0.5M) maximum pixel budgets are suboptimal. 12M pixels lead to premature "early stopping" and limited interaction turns (Avg. Turns (All) = 1.0), while 0.5M can cause perceptual hallucinations.
 - Optimal performance is achieved at 2M pixels (48.0% on VisualProbe-Hard), indicating a trade-off between perceptual accuracy and interaction depth.

5. **Effect of Training Turn Budget (Fig. 8):**
 - A lower training turn budget (e.g., 6 turns) leads to faster initial convergence but plateaus earlier.
 - A higher training turn budget (e.g., 12 turns) converges slower but ultimately achieves a superior performance ceiling, demonstrating the potential benefits of longer training horizons if efficiency is not a primary constraint. However, over-turn masking allows a smaller budget to achieve good test-time scaling.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Mini-o3 project makes significant contributions to the field of multimodal AI, with considerable potential impact:

1. **Democratization of Advanced Agentic Capabilities:** By providing a "complete recipe" to reproduce "OpenAI o3–style behaviors" in an open-source framework, Mini-o3 directly addresses the gap between proprietary, highly capable multimodal agents and their open-source counterparts. This is profoundly significant for the broader research community, enabling more researchers to experiment with, build upon, and understand advanced multi-turn reasoning agents without relying on closed APIs.

2. **Enabling Complex Visual Problem Solving:** Mini-o3 significantly expands the frontier of solvable problems for open-source VLMs. Its ability to perform deep, multi-turn reasoning with trial-and-error exploration allows it to tackle challenging visual search tasks that were previously intractable. This is critical for applications requiring fine-grained visual understanding and iterative analysis, such as medical image diagnosis, complex object identification in cluttered scenes, or detailed visual inspection in industrial settings.

3. **Robust Reinforcement Learning for Multimodal Agents:** The proposed **over-turn masking strategy** is a novel and practical contribution to the field of reinforcement learning for long-horizon tasks. It elegantly solves the problem of premature policy convergence caused by penalizing incomplete trajectories, enabling models to scale their interaction depth at inference time despite efficient training with limited turn budgets. This technique can be broadly applicable to other agentic models, both unimodal and multimodal, where training efficiency and test-time scalability are crucial.

4. **Novel Dataset for Exploratory Reasoning:** The **Visual Probe Dataset** serves as a valuable resource for future research. Its explicit design to require trial-and-error exploration, featuring small targets, distractors, and high-resolution images, ensures that models trained on it develop more robust and adaptive reasoning skills. This dataset can drive further innovation in developing agents capable of human-like visual exploration.

5. **Guidance for Future Multimodal Model Development:** The entire training recipe, encompassing the construction of challenging datasets, iterative cold-start data collection, and specialized RL techniques like over-turn masking, offers practical guidance for researchers and practitioners aiming to build more sophisticated and capable multimodal agents. The insights regarding optimal pixel budgets and the trade-offs between training efficiency and performance ceilings also inform future architectural and training decisions.

In essence, Mini-o3 represents a substantial step forward in equipping open-source Vision-Language Models with the sophisticated, flexible, and deep reasoning capabilities necessary to address real-world visual complexities, paving the way for more intelligent and interactive AI systems.
