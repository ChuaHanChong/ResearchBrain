# Mini-o3: Scaling Up Reasoning Patterns and Interaction Turns for Visual Search

> [!summary] Summary
> Mini-o3, developed by ByteDance and The University of Hong Kong, significantly enhances open-source Vision-Language Models for complex visual search tasks by enabling multi-turn, exploratory reasoning and scaling interaction depth at inference. It achieves state-of-the-art performance on challenging benchmarks like VisualProbe-Hard, demonstrating its capacity for deeper thinking paths.

## Problem

- Current open-source Vision-Language Models (VLMs) exhibit monotonous reasoning patterns and allow only a limited number of interaction turns, hindering their performance on difficult visual search tasks.
- Challenging visual problems requiring fine-grained perception, such as small targets or numerous distractors, demand iterative exploration and trial-and-error, which existing open-source models lack.
- There is a notable gap between the advanced multi-turn reasoning and tool-use capabilities of proprietary models (e.g., OpenAI o3) and open-source alternatives.

## Method

- Mini-o3 utilizes an iterative ==Thought-Action-Observation loop==, allowing the VLM to generate internal thoughts, execute grounding actions (e.g., zoom-in), and observe cropped image patches over multiple turns.
- A two-phase training procedure combines Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on a dataset of diverse multi-turn trajectories with Reinforcement Learning (GRPO) using verifiable rewards to optimize policy.
- The approach introduces the challenging ==VisualProbe dataset== for complex visual search and employs a novel =="over-turn masking" strategy== during RL to enable deeper test-time reasoning without excessive training costs.

## Results

- Mini-o3 achieves state-of-the-art performance among 7B-sized open-source models, reaching **48.0%** accuracy on VisualProbe-Hard, **50.4%** on VisualProbe-Medium, and **67.0%** on VisualProbe-Easy, significantly outperforming baselines like DeepEyes.
- The model demonstrates the ability to naturally scale interaction turns at test time; accuracy on VisualProbe-Hard continues to improve as the upper limit on interaction turns increases from **4** to **32** during inference.
- Ablation studies confirm the necessity of cold-start SFT, hard RL data, and over-turn masking for high performance and deep reasoning, with over-turn masking alone improving VisualProbe-Hard accuracy from **32.2%** to **44.4%** and increasing average turns for correct responses.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A "cold-start" Supervised Fine-Tuning phase is essential to bootstrap multi-turn agentic behaviors in VLMs, as base models lack intrinsic capabilities for long-horizon, interactive problem-solving.
> - The "over-turn masking" strategy effectively addresses the premature policy termination problem in RL, allowing models trained with limited turn budgets (e.g., 6 turns) to naturally scale to tens of turns at inference time with improved accuracy.
> - The design of challenging datasets, such as VisualProbe, featuring small targets, numerous distractors, and high-resolution images, is critical for training robust, exploratory visual agents capable of human-like iterative analysis.
