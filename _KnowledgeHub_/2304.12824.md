# Contrastive Energy Prediction for Exact Energy-Guided Diffusion Sampling in Offline Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A new method called Contrastive Energy Prediction (CEP) enables exact energy-guided diffusion sampling by formulating and learning an intermediate guidance term with a contrastive objective. This approach achieves state-of-the-art performance in offline reinforcement learning on D4RL benchmarks and demonstrates flexible, precise control in image synthesis tasks.

## Problem

- Existing energy-guided diffusion sampling methods are heuristic and inexact, failing to guarantee that generated samples accurately follow the desired target distribution.
- Integrating arbitrary, unnormalized energy functions precisely into diffusion models for controllable generation has lacked a robust, theoretically sound approach.
- The inherent intractability of exact intermediate energy guidance terms posed a challenge for principled learning in diffusion models.

## Method

- Derived the first exact mathematical formulation for the ==intermediate energy guidance term== required at each step of the ==diffusion process== (Theorem 3.1).
- Developed **Contrastive Energy Prediction (CEP)**, a novel ==contrastive learning objective== to train a neural network to approximate the intractable exact intermediate energy guidance.
- Integrated the learned exact guidance term into the ==diffusion sampling== process, enabling provably exact adherence to the ==target energy-guided distribution==, and applied it to ==offline reinforcement learning== (via **Q-Guided Policy Optimization (QGPO)**) and ==image synthesis==.

## Results

- The proposed **Q-Guided Policy Optimization (QGPO)**, utilizing CEP, achieved **state-of-the-art** performance on **D4RL** offline reinforcement learning benchmarks, particularly excelling in complex control tasks like **AntMaze** (umaze, medium, large) and surpassing baselines such as Diffuser, Diffusion-QL, and SfBC.
- **Contrastive Energy Prediction (CEP)** delivered results **roughly on par** with traditional classifier guidance (**FID, sFID, Precision, Recall**) for class-conditional image synthesis on **ImageNet**.
- The method enabled novel energy-guided image synthesis for continuous attributes, successfully controlling the **hue** of generated images by defining a corresponding energy function.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The precise intermediate energy `E_t(x_t)` for guiding diffusion at any step `t` is a log-expectation of the original energy `E(x_0)` conditioned on the noisy sample `x_t`.
> - A contrastive learning objective can provably learn the gradient of this log-expectation, offering an exact method to estimate otherwise intractable guidance terms (Theorem 3.2).
> - Prior energy-guided sampling methods were theoretically inexact due to approximations in their objective functions or guidance definitions, a limitation directly addressed by the proposed exact formulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper situates itself within the rapidly advancing field of **deep generative models**, specifically focusing on **diffusion models**. Diffusion models (DMs), following their foundational work by Sohl-Dickstein et al. (2015) and subsequent breakthroughs by Ho et al. (2020) and Song et al. (2021b), have demonstrated unprecedented success across various generative tasks, including high-fidelity image synthesis, video generation, and controllable text generation.

A crucial aspect for applying DMs in real-world scenarios is **guided sampling**, which allows injecting human preferences or external control signals into the generation process. Existing guided sampling paradigms include:
- **Classifier guidance** (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021), which uses a separately trained classifier to guide the diffusion process towards specific classes.
- **Classifier-free guidance** (Ho & Salimans, 2021), which achieves similar conditional generation without an explicit classifier by training a single model that can operate both conditionally and unconditionally.
- Other methods that typically rely on specific conditioning variables.

This paper addresses a more general and challenging setting: **energy-guided diffusion sampling**, where the guidance is defined by an arbitrary (unnormalized) energy function, E(x). The goal is to sample from a distribution `p(x) ∝ q(x)e^(-βE(x))`, where `q(x)` is the underlying data distribution learned by the diffusion model and `β` controls the strength of the energy guidance. This formulation is highly flexible and widely applicable, encompassing scenarios where:
- Direct conditional variables are not readily available or easily defined (e.g., optimizing for a complex reward function in RL, incorporating nuanced human feedback, or generating images based on abstract aesthetic properties).
- The target distribution `p(x)` is implicitly defined and unnormalized, making direct sampling or training a conditional model difficult.

The authors critically highlight a significant gap in the existing literature: previous energy-guided sampling methods (e.g., Janner et al., 2022; Zhao et al., 2022; Ho et al., 2022c; Chung et al., 2022; Kawar et al., 2022) are often heuristic. They either rely on manual definitions of intermediate guidance or employ approximations that are theoretically "inexact," meaning they do not guarantee that the final samples accurately follow the desired target distribution `p(x)`. This paper aims to fill this theoretical void by providing an *exact* formulation and a *provably convergent* learning method for energy guidance.

By addressing this fundamental problem, the work broadens the utility of diffusion models, especially for tasks like offline reinforcement learning (where a Q-function serves as an energy to guide action generation) and highly controllable image synthesis beyond simple class labels. This positions the paper as a significant theoretical and practical advancement in the field of generative AI, particularly concerning controllable generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a method for **exact energy-guided diffusion sampling** that ensures generated samples precisely conform to a target distribution `p(x) ∝ q(x)e^(-βE(x))`. This overarching goal is broken down into several specific aims:

1. **Formulate Exact Intermediate Guidance:** To derive a mathematically rigorous and exact expression for the energy guidance required at each intermediate time step `t` during the diffusion sampling process. This is crucial because previous methods lacked such a guarantee, leading to potentially inaccurate sample distributions.
2. **Develop a Learning Objective for Exact Guidance:** To propose a novel training objective that can effectively learn this intractable exact intermediate energy guidance using a neural network. This objective must be robust and theoretically proven to converge to the true guidance under ideal conditions.
3. **Address Practical Challenges in Learning Guidance:** To tackle issues such as the intractability of the exact guidance term (due to its log-expectation form) and numerical instability during training, especially when dealing with unnormalized energy functions.
4. **Demonstrate Effectiveness in High-Impact Applications:** To validate the proposed method's practical utility and scalability by applying it to complex, real-world tasks where energy-guided sampling is highly relevant. Offline reinforcement learning (RL) and image synthesis are chosen as primary demonstration domains.
5. **Critically Compare with Existing Methods:** To formally analyze why existing energy-guided sampling techniques are inexact and to empirically demonstrate the superior performance of the proposed method.

The motivations driving this research are both theoretical and practical:

- **Theoretical Gap:** The primary motivation stems from the recognized limitation of prior energy-guided diffusion methods. While these methods offer a degree of control, their lack of theoretical guarantees regarding convergence to the desired target distribution `p(x)` leaves a fundamental problem unsolved. The authors seek to provide a principled solution that ensures exactness.
- **Enhanced Controllability:** Energy functions offer a highly flexible mechanism for incorporating diverse forms of human preference or external constraints that might not fit neatly into conditional labels (e.g., text, classes). Examples include reward functions in RL, human feedback scores, or complex feature matching objectives. A method that can precisely integrate such energy functions significantly expands the applicability and controllability of diffusion models.
- **Relevance to Offline Reinforcement Learning:** The formulation `π*(a|s) ∝ μ(a|s)e^(βQ(s,a))` for optimal policies in offline RL directly aligns with the general energy-guided sampling problem. Effectively solving this problem for diffusion policies in offline RL promises a new paradigm for extracting high-performance policies from static datasets, addressing a critical challenge in the field.
- **Scalability to High-Dimensional Data:** The motivation includes developing a method that is not only theoretically sound but also practically scalable to high-dimensional data, such as images, to showcase its general utility beyond academic toy problems.

In essence, the paper is motivated by the desire to put energy-guided diffusion sampling on a firm theoretical footing, thereby unlocking its full potential for flexible and precise control in a wide array of generative AI applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology revolves around deriving and learning an *exact* intermediate energy guidance term for diffusion sampling. The overall approach can be broken down into several key steps:

1. **Exact Formulation of Intermediate Energy Guidance (Theorem 3.1):**
 - The paper begins by formally defining the target distribution `p_0(x_0) ∝ q_0(x_0)e^(-βE(x_0))`, where `q_0(x_0)` is the data distribution learned by a pretrained diffusion model and `E(x_0)` is the user-defined energy function.
 - Through mathematical derivation, the authors show that the marginal distribution `p_t(x_t)` at any intermediate time `t` in the diffusion process (starting from `p_0`) maintains a similar proportional relationship: `p_t(x_t) ∝ q_t(x_t)e^(-E_t(x_t))`. Here, `q_t(x_t)` is the marginal distribution of the forward process starting from `q_0`, and `E_t(x_t)` is the *intermediate energy function*.
 - Crucially, they derive `E_t(x_t) = -log E_{q_{0t}(x_0|x_t)}[e^(-βE(x_0))]`. This reveals that the intermediate energy `E_t(x_t)` is a log-expectation over the original energy `E(x_0)` conditioned on `x_t`.
 - The exact guidance term for sampling from `p_t(x_t)` is then identified as `∇_{x_t} log p_t(x_t) = ∇_{x_t} log q_t(x_t) - ∇_{x_t} E_t(x_t)`. The first term is approximated by the pretrained diffusion model (`-ϵ_θ(x_t,t)/σ_t`), leaving `∇_{x_t} E_t(x_t)` (the intermediate energy guidance) as the unknown, intractable component to be estimated.

2. **Learning Energy Guidance by Contrastive Energy Prediction (CEP):**
 - To overcome the intractability of `∇_{x_t} E_t(x_t)`, the paper introduces **Contrastive Energy Prediction (CEP)**. Instead of directly estimating `E_t(x_t)` via MSE, which is shown to be inexact, CEP trains a neural network `f_ϕ(x_t, t)` to approximate `E_t(x_t)` using a contrastive learning objective.
 - The core idea is to leverage a set of `K` samples. For each batch, `K` original data samples `x_0^(1), ..., x_0^(K)` are drawn from `q_0(x_0)`. These are then perturbed to `x_t^(1), ..., x_t^(K)` at a random time `t`.
 - The training objective (Equation 12) is a cross-entropy loss that minimizes the difference between "soft energy labels" (derived from `e^(-βE(x_0^(i)))`) and "predicted labels" (derived from `softmax(e^(-f_ϕ(x_t^(i), t)))`) across the `K` samples.
 - **Theorem 3.2** formally proves that, given unlimited model capacity and data, the gradient of the optimal `f_ϕ*` learned by CEP (`∇_{x_t} f_ϕ*(x_t, t)`) *exactly* recovers the desired intermediate energy guidance `∇_{x_t} E_t(x_t)`.
 - A self-normalized version of the objective (Equation 13) is also proposed for improved numerical stability, especially when `e^(-βE(x_0))` can have large variations. This variant is shown to be equivalent to the original objective as `K` approaches infinity.

3. **Comparison with Previous Methods:**
 - The paper formally demonstrates that existing energy guidance methods, such as those based on Mean Squared Error (MSE) objectives (Janner et al.) or Diffusion Posterior Sampling (DPS, Chung et al.), are *inexact*. Their approximations of `E_t(x_t)` or `∇_{x_t} E_t(x_t)` do not hold for all `t > 0`, particularly at later diffusion steps, leading to suboptimal sampling. CEP's exactness is a key differentiator.
 - A connection is also drawn to **classifier guidance**, showing that CEP can be seen as a contrastive alternative for conditional sampling, but is more general as it applies to arbitrary energy functions.

4. **Application to Offline Reinforcement Learning (Q-Guided Policy Optimization - QGPO):**
 - The authors formulate the policy optimization problem in offline RL, `max_π E_{s,a~π} [Q_ψ(s,a) - (1/β)D_{KL}(π(·|s)||μ(·|s))]`, where `Q_ψ` is a Q-function and `μ` is the behavior policy. The optimal policy `π*(a|s) ∝ μ(a|s)e^(βQ_ψ(s,a))` fits the energy-guided sampling framework by setting `E(a|s) = -Q_ψ(s,a)`.
 - A key challenge is sampling `K` actions from `μ(a|s)` for a single continuous state `s` for the contrastive loss. They address this with "in-support Contrastive Energy Prediction," where a *support action set* `D_μ_θ(s)` is pre-generated by sampling `K` actions from the learned behavior diffusion model `μ_θ(·|s)` for each state `s` in the offline dataset.
 - The Q-function `Q_ψ` is trained using "in-support Softmax Q-Learning," which adapts the Bellman update to use importance sampling over the generated support action set to estimate the next state-action value.
 - The entire QGPO framework thus involves training three neural networks: a state-conditioned diffusion model `ϵ_θ` for `μ(a|s)`, the Q-function `Q_ψ(s,a)`, and the energy guidance model `f_ϕ(s,a_t,t)` using in-support CEP.

5. **Application to Image Synthesis:**
 - CEP is applied to conditional image synthesis, where the energy function is defined as `-log q_0(c|x_0)` (class likelihood), demonstrating its equivalence and competitive performance with traditional classifier guidance.
 - More generally, it's used for flexible energy-guided image synthesis, exemplified by controlling the *hue* of generated images. An energy function `E(x) = -||h(x) - h_tar||_1` is defined, where `h(x)` is the hue value, allowing for generation of images with specific color appearances (e.g., red, green, blue themed).

This comprehensive methodology, spanning theoretical derivation, a novel learning objective, practical adaptations for specific domains, and rigorous empirical validation, underscores the paper's robust and impactful contribution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents several significant findings and results, encompassing both theoretical guarantees and strong empirical performance across diverse domains:

1. **Exact Formulation of Intermediate Energy Guidance:**
 - **Finding:** The paper provides the first exact mathematical formulation of the intermediate energy function `E_t(x_t)` and its gradient `∇_{x_t} E_t(x_t)` for energy-guided diffusion sampling (Theorem 3.1). This term is precisely defined as a log-expectation, explicitly showing how the initial energy `E(x_0)` "diffuses" over time.
 - **Result:** This formulation lays the theoretical groundwork for truly exact energy guidance, unlike previous heuristic or approximate methods.

2. **Theoretical Guarantee of Contrastive Energy Prediction (CEP):**
 - **Finding:** The proposed Contrastive Energy Prediction (CEP) objective is theoretically proven to converge to the exact intermediate energy guidance `∇_{x_t} E_t(x_t)` (Theorem 3.2), given sufficient model capacity and data.
 - **Result:** This provides a strong guarantee that models trained with CEP can learn to guide diffusion sampling to the desired target distribution `p(x) ∝ q(x)e^(-βE(x))`, resolving the inexactness of prior approaches.

3. **Superior Performance in Offline Reinforcement Learning (QGPO):**
 - **Finding:** When applied to offline RL as Q-Guided Policy Optimization (QGPO), the method significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art algorithms on the challenging D4RL benchmarks.
 - **Result:** QGPO achieves higher normalized scores in most MuJoCo locomotion tasks (e.g., HalfCheetah, Hopper, Walker2d) and demonstrates particularly strong performance in complex navigation tasks like AntMaze (umaze, medium, large), where it substantially surpasses baselines including other diffusion-based RL methods like Diffusers, Diffusion-QL, and SfBC. This highlights the practical value of exact energy guidance in decision-making contexts.

4. **Effectiveness and Versatility in Image Synthesis:**
 - **Finding:** CEP guidance is shown to be an effective method for image synthesis, both in conditional and general energy-guided settings.
 - **Result (Class-Conditional):** In class-conditional image synthesis on ImageNet, CEP achieves quantitative results (FID, sFID, Precision, Recall) that are "roughly on par" with classic classifier guidance. This validates CEP as a robust and theoretically sound alternative for scenarios traditionally handled by classifier guidance.
 - **Result (Energy-Guided):** The paper successfully demonstrates novel energy-guided image synthesis by controlling the *color appearance* of generated images using a continuous hue-based energy function. This exemplifies CEP's flexibility in enabling fine-grained, continuous control over visual attributes not directly represented by discrete class labels.

5. **Empirical Validation of Exactness vs. Inexactness:**
 - **Finding:** Comparative 2D experiments visually and quantitatively confirm that CEP generates sample distributions that more closely match the ground truth target `p(x)` compared to inexact methods like MSE-based guidance and Diffusion Posterior Sampling (DPS). The difference becomes more pronounced with stronger guidance (larger `β`).
 - **Result:** This direct comparison empirically reinforces the theoretical claims regarding the exactness of CEP and the limitations of previous heuristic methods. Ablation studies in offline RL further support this, showing CEP's superior performance over MSE-based and E-MSE guidance variants (Table 6).

In summary, the main findings solidify CEP as a principled and highly effective method for energy-guided diffusion sampling. The theoretical guarantees, coupled with compelling empirical results in complex domains like offline RL and image synthesis, position this work as a significant advancement in the field of controllable generative AI.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work "Contrastive Energy Prediction for Exact Energy-Guided Diffusion Sampling in Offline Reinforcement Learning" holds significant theoretical and practical implications, poised to impact multiple areas of AI:

1. **Fundamental Theoretical Advancement in Diffusion Models:** The most profound impact lies in providing a *provably exact* method for energy-guided sampling with diffusion models. By deriving the precise form of intermediate energy guidance and designing a learning objective (CEP) guaranteed to converge to it, the paper resolves a critical theoretical shortcoming of prior heuristic methods. This elevates energy-guided diffusion from an approximate technique to a rigorous and reliable framework. This contribution builds a stronger scientific foundation for the field, allowing future research to rely on precise guidance rather than rule-of-thumb approximations.

2. **Expanded Controllability and Flexibility of Generative AI:** Energy functions offer a highly flexible and expressive way to encode human preferences, domain-specific constraints, or complex reward signals that are difficult to represent as discrete conditioning variables or text prompts. CEP unlocks the full potential of this flexibility, enabling fine-grained control over generation processes across modalities. This means users or downstream systems can guide diffusion models with arbitrary criteria, fostering more creative, purposeful, and adaptable generative applications.

3. **New State-of-the-Art in Offline Reinforcement Learning:** The application of QGPO in offline RL is a major practical breakthrough. Offline RL is challenging due to the need to derive optimal policies from static, often sub-optimal, datasets without further environment interaction. By precisely leveraging the Q-function as an energy landscape to guide diffusion policies, QGPO achieves state-of-the-art performance on D4RL benchmarks, particularly in complex control tasks like AntMaze. This provides a powerful new tool for developing intelligent agents in data-scarce or safety-critical domains where online interaction is prohibitive. The framework also offers insights into how generative models can be rigorously integrated with decision-making processes.

4. **Unifying Framework for Guided Generation:** By demonstrating that CEP can effectively replace or augment traditional conditional guidance methods (like classifier guidance for image synthesis), the paper presents a more general and unifying framework for controllable generation. This suggests a paradigm shift where diverse guidance signals—from explicit labels to implicit preferences encoded in energy functions—can be handled consistently within a single, theoretically sound approach.

5. **Scalability and Robustness:** The successful demonstration of CEP's efficacy on both low-dimensional 2D data and high-dimensional, complex tasks such as image generation (ImageNet) and continuous control (D4RL) underscores its robustness and scalability. This indicates that the method is not merely a theoretical curiosity but a practical tool applicable to real-world large-scale problems.

6. **Catalyst for Future Research:** This work opens several avenues for future research:
 - **Exploring richer energy functions:** Researchers can now confidently design and integrate more sophisticated energy functions reflecting nuanced artistic styles, scientific properties (e.g., molecular stability, material properties), or user-defined objective functions.
 - **Integration with other DM advancements:** Combining CEP with faster diffusion samplers (like DPM-Solver used in this paper), latent diffusion models, or hierarchical diffusion architectures could yield even more efficient and powerful controllable generative systems.
 - **Applications beyond RL and Image Synthesis:** The general formulation makes CEP applicable to a wide array of domains, including scientific discovery (e.g., inverse design of materials, drug molecules), computational creativity, and human-computer interaction, where generative models can be guided by complex, non-label-based criteria.

In conclusion, "Contrastive Energy Prediction for Exact Energy-Guided Diffusion Sampling in Offline Reinforcement Learning" is a landmark paper that addresses a fundamental theoretical gap in generative AI. By providing an exact and provably convergent method for energy-guided diffusion, it significantly enhances the controllability, flexibility, and applicability of diffusion models, paving the way for more sophisticated and reliable AI systems across research and industry.
