# Multimodal Prompt Optimization: Why Not Leverage Multiple Modalities for MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at KAIST developed a Multimodal Prompt Optimizer (MPO) framework that optimizes both textual and non-textual prompt components for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). This approach consistently achieves superior performance across diverse tasks and modalities while significantly improving the efficiency of prompt discovery, addressing the limitations of text-only prompt engineering.

## Problem

- Existing prompt optimization methods for MLLMs are limited to text-only inputs, preventing these models from fully leveraging their inherent multimodal capabilities.
- Manual prompt engineering is labor-intensive, requires significant expertise, and often leads to suboptimal performance for MLLMs designed to process diverse modalities.
- The restricted search space of text-only prompts fails to exploit the rich contextual signals available in non-textual forms.

## Method

- Introduced the ==Multimodal Prompt Optimizer (MPO)== framework to jointly optimize prompts across textual and non-textual modalities.
- Utilized ==alignment-preserving exploration== through cohesive backpropagation and joint multimodal updates, ensuring semantic consistency between modalities, along with diverse exploration operators (generation, edit, mix) for non-textual prompts.
- Implemented ==Prior-Inherited Bayesian UCB== for efficient prompt selection, which warm-starts evaluations for new candidates based on parent prompt performance, reducing the evaluation budget needed.

## Results

- MPO consistently and significantly outperformed manual prompts and leading text-only automatic prompt optimization methods across **10** diverse datasets, achieving an average accuracy of **65.1%**, compared to **60.0%** for the best text-only method.
- The Prior-Inherited Bayesian UCB strategy reduced the evaluation budget by **70%** compared to a uniform selection strategy, while achieving similar performance levels.
- Visualization of MLLM hidden states demonstrated that full multimodal prompts shift internal representations into distinct regions, indicating richer reasoning influenced by non-textual components.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimizing prompts with both textual and non-textual components unlocks the full potential of MLLMs, yielding substantial performance improvements over text-only approaches.
> - Maintaining cross-modal alignment during prompt generation is critical for producing effective and coherent multimodal prompts.
> - Leveraging prior performance information from parent prompts dramatically increases the efficiency of searching in the vast multimodal prompt space.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Models (LLMs) has marked a significant advancement in artificial intelligence, demonstrating proficiency across a wide array of linguistic tasks. A critical aspect of harnessing their capabilities involves prompt engineering, a process of crafting specific instructions or contexts to guide the model's behavior and maximize performance. However, manual prompt crafting is often labor-intensive and iterative, leading to the emergence of Automatic Prompt Optimization (APO) as a field dedicated to automating the discovery of effective prompts. Existing APO methods typically frame this as an iterative search problem, where candidate prompts are generated, evaluated, and refined.

More recently, the landscape of AI has expanded with the introduction of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). These models extend beyond text to process and reason over diverse non-textual modalities, including images, videos, and even molecular structures. MLLMs represent a significant leap in computational intelligence, enabling applications that require understanding and integration of information from various sources. Despite these advancements, the field of prompt optimization has largely remained confined to the textual domain. Current APO techniques, while effective for LLMs, do not exploit the richer expressive capacity afforded by multimodal inputs in MLLMs. This limitation means that prompts generated by existing methods may be suboptimal, failing to fully leverage the comprehensive information MLLMs are designed to process.

Furthermore, related research explores instance-specific prompting strategies, such as providing bounding boxes or generating intermediate rationales, to enhance reasoning for individual queries. While valuable for single-instance performance, these techniques differ from APO's objective of discovering a single, reusable prompt that boosts performance across an entire task. This paper identifies the gap between the multimodal capabilities of MLLMs and the unimodal nature of existing prompt optimization techniques. It proposes to bridge this gap by introducing multimodal prompt optimization, thereby expanding the search space for prompts to include non-textual components and potentially unlocking further capabilities of MLLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce and address the novel problem of multimodal prompt optimization for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). The authors are motivated by a perceived limitation in the current landscape of prompt optimization, which, despite the rise of MLLMs, remains exclusively focused on textual inputs.

The core motivation stems from the observation that while MLLMs can process information from various modalities (images, videos, molecules, etc.), existing prompt optimization methods are restricted to refining only the textual components of prompts. This text-only approach is identified as a bottleneck that prevents MLLMs from realizing their full potential. For instance, expressing complex visual characteristics of an object might require lengthy and potentially ambiguous textual descriptions, whereas a single image could convey the same information more directly and unambiguously. By limiting prompt optimization to text, current methods are hypothesized to generate less effective prompts that do not fully utilize the rich, complementary signals available in the multimodal space.

The expansion of prompt optimization into the multimodal space, while offering significant opportunities, also introduces distinct challenges that form the specific objectives of the proposed solution:

1. **Navigating an Expanded, Combinatorial Search Space:** The joint space of textual and non-textual prompts (\(T \times M\)) is considerably larger and more complex than the text-only space. A direct, independent optimization of textual and non-textual components risks generating semantically misaligned prompts, where modalities convey conflicting signals. The objective is to develop a prompt update strategy that can efficiently explore this vast combinatorial space while ensuring cross-modal consistency and semantic alignment between the textual and non-textual elements.
2. **Efficient Candidate Selection in a Sparse Space:** With the enlarged search space, high-quality, optimal multimodal prompts become increasingly sparse, making it difficult to identify promising candidates efficiently. Traditional prompt selection strategies, which treat each candidate independently or allocate evaluation budget uniformly, are prone to inefficiency, particularly during the "cold-start" phase for newly generated prompts. The objective is to design an evaluation and selection strategy that can reliably and efficiently identify high-performing multimodal prompts by leveraging available information, thereby reducing wasted computational resources.

To address these challenges, the authors propose a unified framework, the Multimodal Prompt Optimizer (MPO), aiming to jointly optimize both textual and non-textual prompts through an alignment-preserving exploration mechanism and a prior-inheritance-based selection strategy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Multimodal Prompt Optimizer (MPO) framework is designed to optimize prompts across both textual and non-textual modalities. It consists of two primary components: alignment-preserving exploration and prior-inheritance-based selection.

#### 3.1. Problem Definition

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are defined as functions `MLLM: (T ∪ M)* → T`, where `T` is the textual input space and `M` is the non-textual input space. Given a multimodal query `q` and a prompt `p`, the model produces a textual output `y = MLLM(p, q)`. The paper expands the definition of a prompt from text-only (`p = t ∈ T`) to a multimodal pair `p = (t, m) ∈ T × M`, where `t` is the textual prompt and `m` is the non-textual prompt. The objective of multimodal prompt optimization is to discover the optimal prompt `(t*, m*)` that maximizes performance `E(q,a)∼D[f(MLLM(t, m, q), a)]` on a task dataset `D`, where `f` is a task-specific evaluation metric.

The authors highlight two main challenges in this expanded `T × M` search space: (1) maintaining cross-modal consistency (textual and non-textual components should be complementary) and (2) the increased difficulty of candidate selection due to the enlarged and sparser search space.

#### 3.2. Alignment-Preserving Exploration of Multimodal Prompt Space

This component focuses on jointly updating textual and non-textual prompts to ensure semantic consistency during exploration.

##### 3.2.1. Joint Optimization of Multimodal Prompt

MPO employs two mechanisms for cohesive updates:
- **Cohesive Backpropagation:** A failure set `F` (instances where `y ≠ a`) is identified for a given multimodal prompt `p = (t, m)`. Instead of separate error analyses, a unified feedback `∇p = (∇t, ∇m) = MLLM(t, m; F)` is generated in textual form. This feedback encapsulates cross-modal weaknesses, providing a single supervisory signal for both modalities.
- **Joint Multimodal Update:** The unified feedback `∇p` is used to refine the textual prompt `t'` and generate a modality-specific condition `c`. The MLLM produces `(t', c) = MLLM(t, m; F, ∇p)`. This condition `c` then directs modality-specific generators `g` (e.g., text-to-image or text-to-molecule models) to yield an updated non-textual prompt `m' = g(c)`. This approach ensures `m'` is consistent with `t'`.

The optimization process utilizes a beam search strategy. In each iteration, the top-`b` performing multimodal prompts are selected. From these parent prompts, `b^2` new child prompts are generated through cohesive backpropagation and joint multimodal updates.

##### 3.2.2. Exploration Operators

To ensure diverse exploration of the multimodal space, MPO incorporates three distinct operators for generating or modifying non-textual prompts:
- **Generation Operator:** This operator creates entirely new non-textual prompts from scratch. Conditioned only on a generation signal `c_gen` (derived from `MLLM(t, m; ∇p, F)`), it generates `m' = g(c_gen, ∅)`. This is designed to explore novel regions and avoid local optima.
- **Edit Operator:** This operator performs fine-grained refinements of existing non-textual prompts. Given an edit condition `c_edit`, it updates the non-textual prompt by conditioning on the prior prompt: `m' = g(c_edit, {m})`. This is effective for incremental adjustments.
- **Mix Operator:** This operator blends the strengths of multiple multimodal prompts. It uses feedback from `K` parent prompts to generate a mixing condition `c_mix`, which guides the generator to combine their non-textual components: `m' = g(c_mix, {m_i}^K_{i=1})`. This aims to synthesize balanced compositions and explore intermediate solutions.

For generation and edit operators, one parent prompt is randomly selected from the top-`b` prompts. For the mix operator, `K` parent prompts are selected.

#### 3.3. Effective Prompt Selection by Prior-Inherited Bayesian UCB

This component addresses the challenge of efficiently identifying high-quality candidate prompts in the enlarged multimodal space, moving beyond conventional uniform or standard bandit-based allocation strategies that suffer from cold-start problems.

##### 3.3.1. Parent-Child Correlation

The authors hypothesize a positive correlation between the performance of a parent prompt and its children. Empirical analysis confirms a strong positive correlation (Pearson's r = 0.88), suggesting that parent performance can serve as an informative prior for child performance.

##### 3.3.2. Prior-Inherited Bayesian UCB

MPO adopts a prior-inherited Bayesian UCB strategy. Each multimodal prompt `p_i`'s expected score is modeled as a Beta distribution, `Beta(α_i, β_i)`, where `α_i` and `β_i` are pseudo-counts of successes and failures. For a child prompt `p_i` originating from a parent `p_{par(i)}`, its prior is initialized proportionally to the parent's posterior mean `ˆμ_{par(i)}`, scaled by a prior strength hyperparameter `S`:
`α_i = ˆμ_{par(i)} ⋅ S + 1`, `β_i = (1 − ˆμ_{par(i)}) ⋅ S + 1`.
This mechanism provides `S` pseudo-observations, warm-starting the evaluation. In each round, the prompt with the highest UCB score (upper quantile of its Beta posterior) is selected, evaluated on a small batch of data, and its posterior parameters are updated. This adaptive strategy efficiently allocates the evaluation budget. A theoretical proposition (Proposition 3.1) guarantees that this prior-inherited Bayesian UCB reduces the best-arm identification cost when the prior is informative.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of MPO involved extensive benchmarking against manual prompting, few-shot prompting, and leading text-only Automatic Prompt Optimization (APO) methods across diverse modalities.

#### 4.1. Main Results

MPO consistently demonstrated superior performance across all evaluated domains, including image classification (PlantVillage, CUB-200-2011), visual question answering (SLAKE, RSVQA, DrivingVQA), video action recognition (Drive&Act), video anomaly detection (VANE-Bench), and molecular property prediction (Absorption, BBBP, CYP Inhibition). Specifically, MPO achieved an average accuracy of 65.1%, outperforming the best text-only optimizer, ProTeGi (60.0%), and SEE (59.1%). These results suggest that incorporating non-textual signals into prompts provides stronger contextual grounding and enhances task-specific reasoning for MLLMs. MPO also surpassed exemplar-based Few-Shot prompting, indicating its ability to capture richer cross-modal information beyond simple demonstrations.

#### 4.2. Generalizability to Diverse Backbone Models

MPO's robustness was assessed by varying the backbone models for base MLLMs, optimizer models, and modality-specific generators. The framework maintained strong performance across different architectures, with effectiveness increasing with model size (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL (72B)). MPO also outperformed state-of-the-art text-only methods even when using different optimizer models (e.g., Gemini 2.5 Flash, GPT-4o mini, GPT-4o) and generalized well to various modality-specific generators, including lightweight open-source models like SANA1.5 (1.6B) for image generation.

#### 4.3. Analysis on Cross-Modal Alignment

The research investigated the contribution of MPO's alignment-preserving exploration strategy. Comparisons with variants (Sequential optimization, Random Image Prompt, In-Distribution Image Query, OOD Image Query) showed that MPO achieved both the highest cross-modal alignment score (DSG) and the largest performance gains. This finding supports the hypothesis that stronger cross-modal alignment directly translates to improved task performance and that MPO's joint, alignment-preserving updates are crucial for maintaining modality consistency.

#### 4.4. Ablation on Modality Contributions in Prompts

An ablation study on the textual and non-textual components of optimized multimodal prompts revealed that using either modality in isolation (MPO text without image, or human text with MPO image) already surpassed the Human baseline. However, the full multimodal prompt yielded substantially higher performance, indicating a synergistic, mutually reinforcing relationship between the modalities rather than mere additive contributions.

#### 4.5. Effect of Exploration Operators

Both qualitative observations and an ablation study confirmed the complementary roles of the generation, edit, and mix operators. Qualitatively, the generation operator introduced novel compositions, the edit operator fine-tuned local features, and the mix operator blended broader attributes. Quantitatively, combining all three operators within MPO led to the best performance, demonstrating their collective efficacy in comprehensively exploring the multimodal prompt space.

#### 4.6. Selection Strategies

The prior-inherited Bayesian UCB selection strategy was evaluated against Uniform, standard UCB, and an ablated "w/o Prior" variant. MPO achieved comparable performance to the Uniform strategy with only 30% of the evaluation budget, representing a 70% reduction in resource cost. It also consistently outperformed UCB and w/o Prior, reaching their performance levels with 52% and 42% less budget, respectively. This highlights the efficiency of the warm-start enabled by prior inheritance for scaling across large multimodal search spaces.

#### 4.7. Train Dynamics of MPO

Analysis of training dynamics on the CUB dataset showed that while text-only methods like ProTeGi quickly plateaued after a few iterations (e.g., a 1.1-point gain after the third iteration), MPO continued to improve steadily, achieving an additional 6.4-point gain beyond the third iteration. This suggests MPO effectively navigates the multimodal prompt space to escape local optima inherent in text-only strategies.

#### 4.8. Hidden State Visualization

Principal Component Analysis (PCA) of MLLM hidden states revealed that hidden states from text-only methods clustered in a similar, limited semantic space. In contrast, MPO's full multimodal prompt shifted these hidden states into a distinct region, indicating that non-textual components introduce unique information that alters the model's internal representation, enabling richer reasoning pathways.

#### 4.9. Analysis of Prior Strength

Varying the prior strength parameter `S` in the Bayesian UCB strategy showed that an optimal performance was achieved at an intermediate `S`. Small `S` values underutilized parent priors, leading to suboptimal performance, while excessively large `S` values caused over-reliance on parent priors, limiting adaptation to actual child prompt performance.

#### 4.10. Qualitative Result

Qualitative examples demonstrated that MPO's optimized multimodal prompts consistently provide task-critical context in both textual and visual forms. Notably, the textual prompts explicitly instruct the MLLM to leverage non-textual signals (e.g., "Use the hybrid reference image for guidance"), thereby harnessing the model's full multimodal capacity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a novel problem of multimodal prompt optimization, which represents a crucial advancement in the field of Large Language Models. By explicitly defining and addressing the limitations of text-only prompt optimization in the context of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs, the work opens new avenues for enhancing the capabilities and applicability of these powerful models.

The proposed Multimodal Prompt Optimizer (MPO) framework offers a comprehensive solution with several significant impacts:

1. **Unlocking MLLM Potential:** MPO directly tackles the underutilization of MLLMs' inherent multimodal processing capabilities. By enabling the joint optimization of textual and non-textual prompts, it facilitates the discovery of prompts that can provide richer, more contextually grounded signals, thereby allowing MLLMs to perform closer to their full capacity.
2. **Superior Performance Across Diverse Domains:** The consistent and significant performance gains demonstrated across a wide array of tasks and modalities—including image, video, and molecular analysis—suggest that multimodal prompts are empirically more effective than their text-only counterparts. This could lead to more accurate and robust MLLM applications in specialized fields such as medical diagnostics, drug discovery, autonomous driving, and fine-grained scientific classification.
3. **Efficiency in Prompt Engineering:** The prior-inherited Bayesian UCB selection strategy substantially reduces the computational budget required for prompt optimization. This efficiency is critical for scaling prompt engineering to complex multimodal tasks, where the search space is vast and evaluations can be costly. By accelerating the discovery of high-quality prompts, MPO can make advanced MLLM applications more practical and accessible.
4. **Advancement in Automatic Prompt Optimization:** The introduction of alignment-preserving exploration and complementary operators (generation, edit, mix) provides a robust methodology for navigating the challenging multimodal prompt space. This methodological contribution extends the theoretical and practical foundations of Automatic Prompt Optimization beyond the textual domain.
5. **Shifting Paradigms:** This work advocates for a paradigm shift from text-only to truly multimodal prompt engineering. By demonstrating the benefits of jointly leveraging information across modalities, it encourages future research and development in designing MLLM interfaces and optimization strategies that inherently consider and exploit multimodal inputs.

In summary, this research not only identifies a critical gap in current MLLM deployment strategies but also provides a principled and effective solution. The MPO framework has the potential to significantly enhance the performance, efficiency, and interpretability of MLLMs across a broad spectrum of real-world applications, thereby contributing to the broader advancement of artificial intelligence.
