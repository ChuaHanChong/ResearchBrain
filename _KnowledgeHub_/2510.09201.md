# Multimodal Prompt Optimization: Why Not Leverage Multiple Modalities for MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at KAIST developed a Multimodal Prompt Optimizer (MPO) framework that optimizes both textual and non-textual prompt components for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). This approach consistently achieves superior performance across diverse tasks and modalities while significantly improving the efficiency of prompt discovery, addressing the limitations of text-only prompt engineering.

## Problem

- Existing prompt optimization methods for MLLMs are limited to text-only inputs, preventing these models from fully leveraging their inherent multimodal capabilities.
- Manual prompt engineering is labor-intensive, requires significant expertise, and often leads to suboptimal performance for MLLMs designed to process diverse modalities.
- The restricted search space of text-only prompts fails to exploit the rich contextual signals available in non-textual forms.

## Method

- Introduced the ==Multimodal Prompt Optimizer (MPO)== framework to jointly optimize prompts across textual and non-textual modalities.
- Utilized ==alignment-preserving exploration== through cohesive backpropagation and joint multimodal updates, ensuring semantic consistency between modalities, along with diverse exploration operators (generation, edit, mix) for non-textual prompts.
- Implemented ==Prior-Inherited Bayesian UCB== for efficient prompt selection, which warm-starts evaluations for new candidates based on parent prompt performance, reducing the evaluation budget needed.

## Results

- MPO consistently and significantly outperformed manual prompts and leading text-only automatic prompt optimization methods across **10** diverse datasets, achieving an average accuracy of **65.1%**, compared to **60.0%** for the best text-only method.
- The Prior-Inherited Bayesian UCB strategy reduced the evaluation budget by **70%** compared to a uniform selection strategy, while achieving similar performance levels.
- Visualization of MLLM hidden states demonstrated that full multimodal prompts shift internal representations into distinct regions, indicating richer reasoning influenced by non-textual components.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimizing prompts with both textual and non-textual components unlocks the full potential of MLLMs, yielding substantial performance improvements over text-only approaches.
> - Maintaining cross-modal alignment during prompt generation is critical for producing effective and coherent multimodal prompts.
> - Leveraging prior performance information from parent prompts dramatically increases the efficiency of searching in the vast multimodal prompt space.
