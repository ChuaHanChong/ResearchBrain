# Synthesizing High-Quality Visual Question Answering from Medical Documents with Generator-Verifier LMMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Santa Cruz, Fudan University, and Amazon Research developed MedVLSynther, a generator-verifier framework that synthesizes high-quality, rubric-guided medical VQA data from open biomedical literature. This method produced the MedSynVQA dataset, which improved the average accuracy of open-weight medical LMMs on VQA benchmarks by up to 3.27% compared to prior state-of-the-art.

## Problem

- The development of robust medical Visual Question Answering (VQA) systems is hindered by a scarcity of extensive, high-quality, and openly available training datasets.
- Current medical VQA datasets are either small and expert-curated, large but prone to visual ungrounding and inaccuracies due to automated text-only generation, or large but inaccessible due to privacy/licensing.
- This "supervision bottleneck" limits the generalization capabilities and accelerates the training of medical Large Multimodal Models (LMMs).

## Method

- Introduction of **MedVLSynther**, a rubric-guided ==generator-verifier== pipeline leveraging state-of-the-art open-weight Large Multimodal Models (LMMs).
- The framework synthesizes multiple-choice VQA items directly from open biomedical literature, including figure images, captions, and in-text references.
- A two-stage process involves a generator LMM producing VQA items guided by a medical-education rubric and a verifier LMM performing multi-stage, rubric-based quality control.

## Results

- The MedVLSynther framework produced MedSynVQA, a dataset of 13,087 high-quality multiple-choice VQA items, with **93%** of verifier-accepted items deemed acceptable by human medical experts.
- Training open-weight Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) with MedSynVQA significantly improved their average accuracy on medical VQA benchmarks, with MedVLSynther-**7B** achieving a +**3.27%** gain over the previous best open-weight model (MedVLThinker-**7B**).
- The integrated rubric-guided generation and multi-stage verification pipeline consistently enhanced downstream model performance, with the full framework increasing average accuracy to **55.85%** (**3B** model) and **57.56%** (**7B** model).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit rubric guidance during VQA generation and a multi-stage, rubric-based verification are critical for producing high-quality, clinically valid, and image-grounded synthetic medical data.
> - The capacity of the Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) chosen for both the generator and verifier roles directly correlates with the quality of the synthesized dataset and the downstream performance of trained models.
> - Reinforcement Learning with Verbal Rewards (RLVR) is more effective than Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for training medical LMMs, especially when utilizing the high-quality synthetic data.
