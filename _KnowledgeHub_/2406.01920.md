# CODE: Contrasting Self-generated Description to Combat Hallucination in Large Multi-modal Models

> [!summary] Summary
> CODE, developed by researchers at KAIST's Integrated Vision and Language Lab, introduces a training-free decoding method that combats cross-modal hallucination in Large Multi-modal Models by contrasting responses against the model's own self-generated visual descriptions. This approach consistently improves performance on hallucination benchmarks like POPE and MMHal-Bench, demonstrating enhanced factual consistency across various state-of-the-art LMMs.

## Problem

- Large Multi-modal Models (LMMs) frequently generate "hallucinations," meaning responses that are factually incorrect or inconsistent with the provided visual content.
- This cross-modal inconsistency undermines the reliability and trustworthiness of LMMs.
- The persistent issue of hallucination limits the practical deployment of LMMs in real-world applications requiring high factual accuracy.

## Method

- Introduces ==COuntering DEscription Contrastive Decoding (CODE)==, a training-free method, to mitigate hallucination during LMM inference.
- Utilizes the LMM's own ==self-generated visual descriptions== as a contrasting "amateur" reference for next-token prediction.
- Implements ==Dynamic Restriction (DR)== via "α_t" and ==Adaptive Information Constraint (AIC)== via "β_t" to dynamically control information flow and candidate token selection based on prediction divergence.

## Results

- CODE consistently achieved the highest accuracy and F1 scores on discriminative hallucination benchmarks like POPE, e.g., reaching **86.93** Acc and **87.53** F1 with InternVL 1.5.
- It demonstrated notable improvements on generative benchmarks, including a "remarkable gain" in average score on MMVP and an improved overall score (e.g., **95.3** for LLaVA-NeXT 34B) and reduced hallucination ratio (e.g., **25.00** for IXC2-VL) on MMHal-Bench.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of both Dynamic Restriction ("α_t") and Adaptive Information Constraint ("β_t") for CODE's superior performance across models and benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LMMs' self-generated descriptions, despite being comprehensive, are inherently less informative or partially incorrect compared to direct visual input, making them ideal for contrastive decoding.
> - By recursively referring to these self-generated descriptions, the model can identify and restrict inconsistent information during response generation, enabling a self-correction mechanism.
> - Dynamically controlling the contrastive influence and adaptively constraining the candidate token search space based on prediction divergence is essential for enhancing visual grounding and coherence.
