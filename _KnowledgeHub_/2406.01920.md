# CODE: Contrasting Self-generated Description to Combat Hallucination in Large Multi-modal Models

> [!summary] Summary
> CODE, developed by researchers at KAIST's Integrated Vision and Language Lab, introduces a training-free decoding method that combats cross-modal hallucination in Large Multi-modal Models by contrasting responses against the model's own self-generated visual descriptions. This approach consistently improves performance on hallucination benchmarks like POPE and MMHal-Bench, demonstrating enhanced factual consistency across various state-of-the-art LMMs.

## Problem

- Large Multi-modal Models (LMMs) frequently generate "hallucinations," meaning responses that are factually incorrect or inconsistent with the provided visual content.
- This cross-modal inconsistency undermines the reliability and trustworthiness of LMMs.
- The persistent issue of hallucination limits the practical deployment of LMMs in real-world applications requiring high factual accuracy.

## Method

- Introduces ==COuntering DEscription Contrastive Decoding (CODE)==, a training-free method, to mitigate hallucination during LMM inference.
- Utilizes the LMM's own ==self-generated visual descriptions== as a contrasting "amateur" reference for next-token prediction.
- Implements ==Dynamic Restriction (DR)== via "α_t" and ==Adaptive Information Constraint (AIC)== via "β_t" to dynamically control information flow and candidate token selection based on prediction divergence.

## Results

- CODE consistently achieved the highest accuracy and F1 scores on discriminative hallucination benchmarks like POPE, e.g., reaching **86.93** Acc and **87.53** F1 with InternVL 1.5.
- It demonstrated notable improvements on generative benchmarks, including a "remarkable gain" in average score on MMVP and an improved overall score (e.g., **95.3** for LLaVA-NeXT 34B) and reduced hallucination ratio (e.g., **25.00** for IXC2-VL) on MMHal-Bench.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of both Dynamic Restriction ("α_t") and Adaptive Information Constraint ("β_t") for CODE's superior performance across models and benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LMMs' self-generated descriptions, despite being comprehensive, are inherently less informative or partially incorrect compared to direct visual input, making them ideal for contrastive decoding.
> - By recursively referring to these self-generated descriptions, the model can identify and restrict inconsistent information during response generation, enabling a self-correction mechanism.
> - Dynamically controlling the contrastive influence and adaptively constraining the candidate token search space based on prediction divergence is essential for enhancing visual grounding and coherence.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper directly addresses one of the most pressing challenges in the rapidly evolving field of Large Multi-modal Models (LMMs), often referred to as Large Vision-Language Models. LMMs have demonstrated impressive capabilities in understanding visual contexts and generating coherent textual responses, pushing the boundaries of human-computer interaction across modalities. They have successfully integrated and advanced various tasks previously treated as separate, such as image captioning, visual question answering, and object detection, into unified frameworks.

However, alongside these advancements, the critical issue of "hallucination" has emerged. In the context of LMMs, hallucination specifically refers to **cross-modal inconsistency**—generating responses that appear plausible but are factually incorrect or unrelated to the given visual content. This is distinct from hallucination in Large Language Models (LLMs), which primarily focuses on factual inaccuracies stemming from linguistic knowledge. The paper highlights that despite significant efforts in scaling models, improving resolutions, deepening alignment layers, and increasing model sizes, LMMs continue to struggle with this fundamental problem. The unreliability introduced by hallucinations severely hinders the adoption of LMMs in real-world applications where factual accuracy and trustworthiness are paramount.

Existing approaches to mitigate LMM hallucination broadly fall into several categories:
- **Data-associated solutions:** Involving curated datasets or additional training.
- **Scaling model architectures:** Designing more robust models.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based training:** Using feedback systems to refine responses.
- **Reactive methods (Decoding-based approaches):** Intervening during the inference or decoding phase of LMMs to alleviate undesired responses.

This paper’s contribution, COuntering DEscription Contrastive Decoding (CODE), falls squarely within the **reactive (decoding-based) methods**. It builds upon the concept of Contrastive Decoding (CD), which gained traction in LLMs by contrasting outputs from "expert" and "amateur" models to enhance coherence and informativeness. Previous CD-based approaches in LMMs have explored contrasting responses using manipulated visual inputs (e.g., visual contamination, image-biased models, or fine-grained visual information). CODE distinguishes itself by proposing a novel and intuitive approach: leveraging the **LMM's own self-generated descriptions of the visual content as the contrasting "amateur" reference**. The core insight is that while these self-generated descriptions might be imperfect or incomplete, they still represent the model's inherent understanding of the visual evidence, which can be contrasted against the direct visual input to correct inconsistencies.

Therefore, CODE contributes to the cutting edge of LMM research by offering a training-free, adaptable, and demonstrably effective decoding strategy to combat hallucination, specifically addressing cross-modal inconsistencies by using the model's internal understanding as a self-correction mechanism during the inference phase. This positions the work as a practical and significant step towards more reliable and trustworthy LMMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **effectively mitigate the pervasive issue of hallucination in Large Multi-modal Models (LMMs)**, specifically focusing on cross-modal inconsistency between visual content and generated text. The authors acknowledge that despite the impressive capabilities of contemporary LMMs in understanding visual contexts and generating coherent responses, their tendency to produce erroneous and visually ungrounded information remains a significant barrier to real-world deployment.

The motivation for CODE stems from a crucial research question posed by the authors: **"How effectively do contemporary LMMs capture visual evidences in their descriptive responses, and what information must be curbed to produce informative and consistent responses?"**

The core insights driving the development of CODE are:
1. **Inherent Information Density Difference:** Vision information, compared to language, tends to be less redundant for spatial signals. LMMs can generate seemingly comprehensive descriptions from visual inputs.
2. **Imperfection of Self-Generated Descriptions:** A closer examination reveals that these self-generated descriptions often miss fine-grained information or contain hallucinatory instances themselves. They are "less informative than the visual contents themselves," acting as a useful "amateur" counterpart for contrastive purposes.
3. **Self-Correction Potential:** By recursively referring to these self-generated, albeit incomplete, descriptions, the model can identify and restrict the flow of incorrect or inconsistent information during the response generation phase. This self-correction mechanism enhances the alignment of the model's responses with the actual visual evidence.

Therefore, the key objectives are to:
- Introduce **COuntering DEscription Contrastive Decoding (CODE)**, a novel training-free decoding method.
- Utilize **self-generated descriptions as a contrastive reference** to reduce hallucination.
- **Dynamically adjust information flow and next-token predictions** to enhance coherence and informativeness.
- Provide a **simple yet effective decoding strategy** that can be integrated into existing LMMs without requiring additional training or architectural modifications.
- Demonstrate CODE's efficacy across various benchmarks and state-of-the-art LMMs, proving its robustness in mitigating both object-level and contextual hallucinations.

Ultimately, the motivation is to make LMMs more reliable and trustworthy for broader application in critical areas where factual accuracy is paramount.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CODE is a novel, training-free contrastive decoding method designed to mitigate hallucination in LMMs. Its methodology centers on using the LMM's own self-generated comprehensive descriptions as a contrastive reference during the auto-aggressive decoding process.

The approach can be broken down into the following key steps and novel components:

1. **Problem Setup and Preliminaries:**
 - An LMM, denoted as Mθ, generates responses `y_t` for a given visual content `v` and textual query `x`, auto-regressively predicting the next token `y_t` based on `pθ(y_t|v, x, y_<t)`.
 - CODE operates within the Contrastive Decoding (CD) framework, which typically contrasts logits from an "expert" model (or direct input) with an "amateur" model (or distorted input). The general CD formula is `p_cd(y_t | y_<t) = Softmax[(1 + α)logit_θ(y_t | v, x, y_<t) - αlogit_θ_hat(y_t | v_bar, x, y_<t)]`.

2. **Comprehensive Image Description as Visual Counterpart:**
 - Instead of using a separate "amateur" model or twisted visual inputs, CODE leverages the LMM itself to generate a "comprehensive description" (`d`) of the given visual content (`v`). This is achieved by prompting the model with a specific instruction `x_0` (e.g., "Provide a detailed description of the image, covering all visible elements...").
 - This self-generated `d` then replaces the visual input tokens (`<image>` token in LLaVA series) for the "contrasting" logit calculation.
 - **Motivation:** The authors empirically demonstrate that descriptions-only yield sub-optimal performance compared to using actual visual content, confirming that `d` is indeed "less informative" or "partially incorrect/hallucinatory" as a standalone input, thus suitable as the "amateur" counterpart in the contrastive setup.

3. **COuntering DEscription Contrastive Decoding (CODE) Formula:**
 - The next-word prediction probability for CODE is defined as:
 `p_code(y_t | y_<t) = Softmax[(1 + α_t)logit_θ(y_t | v, x, y_<t) - α_t logit_θ(y_t | d, x, y_<t)]`
 - Here, `logit_θ(y_t | v, x, y_<t)` represents the logit from the direct visual input (`v`), and `logit_θ(y_t | d, x, y_<t)` represents the logit from the self-generated description (`d`).

4. **Dynamic Restriction (DR) with α_t (Novelty 1):**
 - Unlike previous CD approaches that use a fixed `α` value, CODE introduces a **dynamic restriction term `α_t`**.
 - `α_t = 1 - D_bd(P_v_t || P_d_t)`, where `D_bd` is the Bounded Divergence, a symmetric and bounded statistical distance measuring the relative entropy between the token distributions `P_v_t` (from visual content) and `P_d_t` (from description) at time step `t`.
 - **Rationale:**
 - If `P_v_t` and `P_d_t` are very close (minimal divergence, `D_bd` approaches 0), then `α_t` approaches 1. This allows for higher amplification of logit variations, promoting stronger adjustments.
 - If `P_v_t` and `P_d_t` are dissimilar (`D_bd` increases), `α_t` decreases towards 0. This restricts the information flow from the variation, compelling the model to rely more on the direct visual information and preventing erroneous predictions when the description is significantly off.

5. **Adaptive Information Constraint (AIC) with β_t (Novelty 2):**
 - To address the challenge of implausible tokens being rewarded, even with low confidence, CODE introduces `V_head`, an adaptive information constraint that filters less relevant tokens from the candidate pool.
 - `V_head(y_<t) = {y_t ∈ V: p_θ(y_t | v, x, y_<t) ≥ β_t max_w p_θ(w | v, x, y_<t)}`
 - The dynamic threshold `β_t` is defined as `β_t = D_bd(P_v_t || P_d_t)`.
 - **Rationale:** This strategy dynamically expands the token searching pool (`V_head`) when the prediction distributions from `v` and `d` are similar but uncertain (i.e., potential false negatives). Conversely, it constricts the pool when divergence is high, filtering out tokens less likely to be correct. Tokens not in `V_head` have their logits set to `-∞` to prevent their selection.

In essence, CODE's methodology combines the principle of contrastive decoding with two dynamic, data-driven mechanisms (dynamic restriction and adaptive information constraint) that intelligently leverage the model's own descriptive capabilities to perform self-correction and enhance the visual grounding of its generated responses without any additional training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to validate CODE's efficacy were extensive, covering a range of cutting-edge LMMs and diverse hallucination benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Evaluation Setup

- **LMMs Tested:** LLaVA-1.5 (13B), Emu2-Chat (14B), InternLM-XComposer2 (7B), LLaVA-NeXT (34B), Yi-VL (34B), and InternVL 1.5 (26B).
- **Baseline Decoding Strategies:** Greedy decoding, Nucleus sampling, Beam search decoding, OPERA (contrastive decoding), and VCD (contrastive decoding).
- **Benchmarks:** Categorized into discriminative (evaluating among given options) and generative (using GPT-aided evaluation for open-ended responses).

#### 4.2. Key Quantitative Results

- **Discriminative Benchmarks:**
 - **POPE (Object Hallucination - Adversarial Split):** CODE consistently achieved the best performance in both accuracy (Acc↑) and F1 score (F1↑) across all 6 LMM baselines compared to other decoding methods. This indicates its strong ability to combat object-level hallucinations. For instance, with InternVL 1.5, CODE achieved 86.93 Acc and 87.53 F1, outperforming all other methods significantly.
 - **MMVP (Sophisticated Visual Assessment - CLIP-blind pairs):** CODE showed a "remarkable gain in average" score across 9 visual patterns. For Emu2-Chat, CODE's average MMVP score was 42.00, compared to 34.67 for Greedy and 34.00 for Nucleus. This highlights CODE's effectiveness in enhancing precise visual understanding and cross-modal consistency for subtle visual differences.
 - **RealworldQA (Real-world Spatial Understanding):** While specific numbers aren't in the main tables for RealworldQA, the spider chart (Figure 3) and additional experiments (Figure 5) confirm CODE achieved "competent performance" in these more challenging, real-world scenarios.

- **Generative (GPT-aided) Benchmarks:**
 - **LLaVA-QA90 (Overall Score Ratio):** CODE generally outperformed other decoding methods. For LLaVA-NeXT (34B), CODE achieved an overall score of 95.3, compared to 90.7 for Greedy and 92.1 for VCD, showing up to +13.7% improvement over other CD methods for certain models. This demonstrates its ability to generate more coherent and informative responses in open-ended conversation, detailed description, and complex reasoning tasks.
 - **MMHal-Bench (Hallucination Severity):** CODE not only improved the overall average score (higher is better for score, lower for hallucination severity) but also effectively mitigated the hallucination ratio (Hal↓). For IXC2-VL, CODE achieved an overall score of 3.46 and a hallucination ratio of 25.00, outperforming baselines which often had higher hallucination ratios (e.g., Greedy 29.17). This validates CODE's robustness in reducing the severity of hallucinations across various question types.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study on CODE's Dynamic Components

- The ablation study demonstrated the individual and combined effectiveness of Dynamic Restriction (DR, `α_t`) and Adaptive Information Constraint (AIC, `β_t`).
- Using either DR (`! ✗`) or AIC (`✗ !`) alone improved performance over fixed hyperparameters (`✗ ✗`).
- The full CODE implementation, utilizing both DR and AIC (`! !`), consistently achieved the best results across models and benchmarks (e.g., MMVP: 34.00 (LV1.5), 42.67 (LV-N), 51.33 (IVL1.5); LLaVA-Bench: 72.6 (LV1.5), 85.1 (LV-N), 85.7 (IVL1.5)). This validates that the dynamic control of information flow and candidate token pool are crucial for CODE's success.

#### 4.4. Computational Analysis

- CODE incurs additional computational load compared to vanilla decoding, but it remains competitive with other CD-based methods.
- It generally exhibits lower throughput (tokens/s) and higher latency (ms/token) than VCD but significantly outperforms OPERA, which even suffers from Out-of-Memory (OOM) errors for larger models (34B). This indicates a practical efficiency trade-off for its improved performance.

#### 4.5. Token-level Case Study

- Detailed analysis showed how CODE adjusts logit values dynamically. For instance, it successfully curbed the logit for a hallucinatory token like "Yoplait," allowing the correct token "Fage" to be amplified and selected, leading to a correct prediction.
- Another case illustrated `β_t`'s role in the Adaptive Information Constraint, preventing implausible tokens (e.g., "four," "dragon") from being rewarded by setting their logits to `-∞` when the confidence is low and distributions diverge.

In summary, CODE consistently and significantly improves LMM performance across a wide range of hallucination detection and evaluation benchmarks, demonstrating its effectiveness in enhancing cross-modal consistency and reducing erroneous outputs without requiring additional training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper, with its introduction of COuntering DEscription Contrastive Decoding (CODE), holds significant implications for the field of Large Multi-modal Models (LMMs) and their broader applications.

#### 5.1. Technical Significance

- **Novel Training-Free Approach:** CODE is a pioneering training-free decoding strategy that effectively mitigates hallucination. This "plug-and-play" nature makes it highly adaptable and easily integratable into existing LMM frameworks without requiring extensive retraining, fine-tuning, or architectural modifications, significantly lowering the barrier to adoption.
- **Innovative Use of Self-Generated Descriptions:** The core innovation lies in leveraging the LMM's own comprehensive descriptions of visual content as a "corrective reference." This "self-correction" mechanism is intuitive and exploits the inherent representational capabilities of the models themselves, distinguishing it from prior contrastive decoding methods that rely on external "amateur" models or altered inputs.
- **Dynamic Control Mechanisms:** The introduction of Dynamic Restriction (`α_t`) and Adaptive Information Constraint (`β_t`), both governed by Bounded Divergence, provides sophisticated, token-wise feedback control. This dynamic adjustment of information flow and candidate token pools ensures more contextual and accurate responses, addressing a key limitation of fixed-parameter contrastive decoding.
- **Robust Performance Across Models and Benchmarks:** The extensive experimental validation across six cutting-edge LMMs and six diverse hallucination benchmarks (discriminative and generative) demonstrates CODE's consistent efficacy. This broad empirical support highlights its generalizability and reliability in tackling various forms of hallucination, from object-level inaccuracies to more complex contextual inconsistencies.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Enhanced Trustworthiness and Reliability:** Hallucination is a major impediment to the real-world deployment of LMMs, as it undermines user trust. By significantly reducing cross-modal inconsistency, CODE directly addresses this challenge, making LMMs more dependable and their outputs more reliable.
- **Broader Real-World Applicability:** Improved trustworthiness translates into wider adoption of LMMs in critical domains. CODE can pave the way for more effective LMM applications in:
 - **Customer Service & Personal Assistance:** Generating accurate product descriptions or providing precise information based on visual queries.
 - **Education:** Creating reliable explanatory content from images or interactive learning experiences.
 - **Autonomous Driving & Robotics:** Ensuring that visual perception (e.g., object recognition, spatial relationships) is accurately translated into actionable commands, minimizing safety risks from misinterpretations.
 - **Healthcare:** Aiding in the interpretation of medical images or generating precise diagnostic descriptions.
 - **Augmented Reality (AR):** Providing accurate contextual information for overlaid digital content.
- **Advancement of Research on Hallucination:** This work contributes valuable insights into the nature of LMM hallucination and offers a powerful new tool for mitigation. It opens avenues for future research, such as exploring more sophisticated bias detection techniques, integrating external knowledge sources, or developing hybrid approaches that combine decoding strategies with other mitigation methods to further reduce the remaining hallucinatory effects. It also highlights the ongoing challenge of LMMs' reliance on language priors, indicating a clear direction for subsequent investigations.
- **Cost-Effectiveness for Deployment:** Being training-free, CODE avoids the substantial computational resources and time typically required for model retraining or fine-tuning, making it a more economical and practical solution for developers and researchers to integrate into existing LMM pipelines.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Directions

The authors acknowledge that CODE, while highly effective, cannot assure 100% removal of hallucinations. Its main limitation stems from the inherent computational overhead associated with any contrastive decoding method compared to vanilla decoding. Furthermore, the effectiveness relies on the "amateur model" (self-generated description) not being *too* deviated or biased from the true answers. In cases where the initial self-generated description is severely inaccurate due to strong language priors, CODE might struggle to correct it fully. This points to future work on integrating external knowledge or more sophisticated mechanisms to verify and correct such biases, ensuring the contrastive reference itself is sufficiently informative for effective self-correction.

Overall, CODE represents a significant stride towards developing more robust and trustworthy LMMs, addressing a critical bottleneck for their widespread adoption and fostering a new direction in hallucination mitigation research.
