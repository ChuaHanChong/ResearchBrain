# GENMANIP: LLM-driven Simulation for Generalizable Instruction-Following Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper presents GENMANIP, a large-scale, photorealistic robotic simulation platform built on NVIDIA Isaac Sim, designed to evaluate instruction-following robotic manipulation policies. It features an LLM-driven scenario generation pipeline and a standardized benchmark (GENMANIP-BENCH) to assess policy generalization, revealing distinct strengths and limitations of modular and end-to-end learning approaches across different task categories.

## Problem

- Existing robotic simulation platforms lack scenario diversity and photorealism, hindering comprehensive evaluation of generalization for instruction-following robots.
- There is an absence of standardized evaluation frameworks and benchmarks specifically designed to compare different policy architectures for natural language instruction following.
- Current robotic manipulation approaches struggle to generalize across varying objects, spatial configurations, and task instructions, particularly for complex, long-horizon tasks.

## Method

- GENMANIP introduces a realistic, large-scale tabletop simulation platform built on NVIDIA Isaac Sim, featuring 10,000 annotated 3D objects and 100 articulated objects.
- An LLM-driven pipeline automatically generates diverse manipulation scenarios using a Task-oriented Scene Graph (ToSG) to bridge natural language instructions with simulation configurations.
- GENMANIP-BENCH provides a standardized evaluation framework with 200 human-curated scenarios to systematically assess the generalization of both modular and learning-based robotic manipulation policies.

## Results

- Modular systems achieved up to 23.0% overall success rate (CoPA with GPT-4.5) but performed poorly on long-horizon tasks (9.07% average success) and showed planning inefficiencies (low SPL scores).
- End-to-end learning methods (e.g., GR-1) demonstrated strong performance scaling with data, reaching 95.0% success on familiar tasks, but completely failed (0.0% success) on novel instructions and unseen objects.
- Error analysis revealed task decomposition failures and grounding errors as primary limitations for modular systems, while learning-based methods suffered from a lack of compositional understanding.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modular systems, while demonstrating zero-shot capabilities and semantic understanding, struggle with efficient low-level planning and long-horizon tasks, achieving only 9.07% success on the latter.
> - End-to-end learning methods show strong data scaling (e.g., GR-1 improving from 33.0% to 95.0% success with more data) but exhibit semantic brittleness, failing completely (0.0% success) on unseen instructions or objects.
> - Future research should explore hybrid architectures combining LLM-driven high-level reasoning with learning-based low-level control to leverage complementary strengths.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

*GENMANIP platform compared to existing robotic simulation environments, showing superior realism and complexity.*

The development of truly generalizable robots capable of following natural language instructions remains one of the most challenging problems in robotics. Current approaches fall into two main categories: modular systems that leverage large language models (LLMs) and foundation models for perception and planning, and end-to-end learning methods that train Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models on vast datasets of robot demonstrations. However, evaluating and comparing these approaches has been hindered by the lack of comprehensive simulation platforms designed specifically for generalization studies.

GENMANIP addresses this critical gap by introducing a realistic, large-scale tabletop simulation platform built on NVIDIA Isaac Sim, specifically designed for evaluating instruction-following robotic manipulation policies. The platform combines photorealistic rendering with an extensive collection of 10,000 annotated 3D objects and employs an LLM-driven pipeline to automatically generate diverse manipulation scenarios.

### 2. The Challenge of Robotic Generalization

Robotic manipulation faces fundamental challenges in generalizing across different objects, spatial configurations, and task instructions. Traditional simulation platforms often fall short in several key areas:

- **Limited scenario diversity**: Existing platforms typically operate in simplified environments with constrained object sets
- **Inadequate instruction-following evaluation**: Most benchmarks focus on fixed task sequences rather than natural language instruction following
- **Lack of systematic comparison frameworks**: Current platforms don't provide standardized evaluation protocols for comparing different policy architectures

The research landscape has evolved around two primary approaches:

**Modular Manipulation Systems** integrate pre-trained foundation models such as Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) and segmentation models like SAM (Segment Anything Model) for hierarchical task and motion planning. These systems leverage the broad knowledge encoded in large models to interpret instructions and adapt to new scenarios.

**End-to-End Learning Methods** train Vision-Language-Action models directly on robotic demonstration data, mapping visual inputs and language instructions to robot actions. Recent advances include RT-1, RT-2, and GR-1, which have shown promising results on specific tasks but struggle with broad generalization.

### 3. GENMANIP Platform Architecture

GENMANIP is built on NVIDIA Isaac Sim and features several key components that enable comprehensive generalization studies:

#### 3.1. Asset Collection and Annotation

The platform includes an extensive asset database:
- **10,000 rigid objects** curated from Objaverse, each annotated with physical and semantic properties using GPT-4V
- **100 articulated objects** from GRUtopia and PartNet-Mobility with manually annotated constraints
- **100 diverse HDRI backgrounds** providing varied environmental conditions

All objects undergo quality filtering and human-in-the-loop corrections to ensure realistic tabletop manipulation scenarios.

#### 3.2. Primitive Skills Implementation

Basic manipulation capabilities are implemented through key poses and motion primitives:
- **Pick and place operations** using pre-grasp, post-grasp, move, and placement poses
- **Articulated object manipulation** including open, close, press, push, pull, and twist actions
- **Grasp pose validation** ensuring physical feasibility of all proposed manipulations

### 4. Task-Oriented Scene Graph for Scenario Generation

*LLM-driven pipeline for generating diverse manipulation scenarios using Task-oriented Scene Graphs (ToSG).*

A core innovation of GENMANIP is the Task-oriented Scene Graph (ToSG), a structured representation that bridges natural language instructions with robotic manipulation scenarios.

#### 4.1. ToSG Components

The ToSG representation includes:
- **Task Instructions**: Natural language descriptions of desired manipulations
- **Scene Graph**: Nodes representing object states and edges defining spatial relationships
- **Goal Conditions**: Target states that define successful task completion

#### 4.2. LLM-Driven Generation Pipeline

GPT-4 automatically synthesizes diverse scenarios through a structured process:

1. **Seed Selection**: Initial objects and task types are sampled from the annotated database
2. **Scenario Generation**: GPT-4 creates coherent task descriptions and spatial arrangements
3. **ToSG Formatting**: Generated scenarios are converted to structured scene graph format
4. **Layout Construction**: Objects are placed in simulation following topological ordering and constraint satisfaction

This automated pipeline enables the generation of thousands of diverse manipulation scenarios across four reasoning categories: spatial, appearance, common-sense, and long-horizon tasks.

### 5. GENMANIP-BENCH Evaluation Framework

*Architecture of the modular manipulation system evaluated on GENMANIP-BENCH, showing the integration of foundation models for perception, planning, and execution.*

GENMANIP-BENCH provides a standardized evaluation framework consisting of 200 human-curated scenarios that systematically assess policy generalization across multiple dimensions.

#### 5.1. Evaluation Approaches

The benchmark evaluates two distinct policy types:

**Modular Manipulation Systems** are abstracted into a unified framework with four key modules:
1. **Affordance Proposal Generator**: Uses SAM2 and Set-of-Mark prompting for object identification
2. **Grid-based Motion Planner**: Employs VLMs with spatial grid overlays for trajectory planning
3. **Trajectory-Action Chainer**: Maps trajectory segments to executable actions
4. **Long-Horizon Planner**: Manages task decomposition and execution monitoring

**Learning-based Methods** include end-to-end policies like GR-1 and ACT, trained with varying data sizes and randomization levels to assess generalization capabilities.

#### 5.2. Evaluation Metrics

Performance is measured using:
- **Success Rate (SR)**: Percentage of successfully completed tasks
- **Success Rate weighted by Path Length (SPL)**: Efficiency-aware success metric
- **ToSG-based Evaluation**: Systematic comparison of final scene states with target conditions

### 6. Experimental Results and Insights

*Performance scaling of end-to-end learning methods (GR-1 and ACT) with increasing training data size.*

The comprehensive evaluation on GENMANIP-BENCH reveals significant insights into the strengths and limitations of current robotic manipulation approaches.

#### 6.1. Modular System Performance

Modular methods demonstrated promising zero-shot capabilities:
- **CoPA (GPT-4.5)** achieved the highest overall success rate of 23.0%
- **Strong performance** on appearance and common-sense reasoning tasks
- **Significant struggles** with long-horizon tasks (average 9.07% success rate)
- **Planning inefficiencies** revealed by low SPL scores (one-sixth to one-tenth of success rates)

*Generalization performance of GR-1 across different levels of environmental variation, showing dramatic performance drops with novel instructions and objects.*

#### 6.2. Learning-based Method Limitations

End-to-end policies showed clear data scaling benefits but fundamental generalization limitations:
- **Strong data scaling**: GR-1 improved from 33.0% to 95.0% success rate with 10x more training data
- **Spatial generalization**: Reasonable performance within familiar spatial arrangements
- **Semantic brittleness**: Complete failure (0.0% success rate) on unseen instructions and objects
- **Layout sensitivity**: Significant performance degradation across different scene configurations

#### 6.3. Error Analysis

*Sankey diagram showing the flow of task types to success/failure modes, revealing that long-horizon tasks are particularly challenging for current approaches.*

Detailed failure analysis revealed:
- **Task decomposition failures** as the primary limitation for long-horizon planning
- **Grounding errors** when identifying target objects in cluttered scenes
- **Motion planning inefficiencies** in modular systems
- **Lack of compositional understanding** in end-to-end methods

### 7. Implications for Future Research

The findings from GENMANIP-BENCH highlight a "dual-system" dynamic in current robotic manipulation approaches, where modular systems excel at semantic understanding and broad adaptability but struggle with efficient low-level planning, while end-to-end methods achieve impressive performance on familiar tasks but fail to generalize to novel instructions or object configurations.

#### 7.1. Key Research Directions

The results suggest several critical areas for future development:

**Hybrid Architectures**: The complementary strengths of modular and end-to-end approaches suggest potential benefits from hybrid systems that combine LLM-driven high-level reasoning with learning-based low-level control.

**Long-horizon Task Decomposition**: Improving the ability of prompt-based models to effectively decompose complex tasks and maintain coherent execution over extended horizons.

**Compositional Generalization**: Developing end-to-end methods that can generalize beyond spatial variations to handle novel semantic compositions and instruction following.

**Efficient Planning Integration**: Bridging the gap between high-level reasoning capabilities and efficient motion planning for more practical robotic systems.

The GENMANIP platform and benchmark provide the research community with essential tools for systematically evaluating and advancing instruction-following robotic manipulation. By offering standardized evaluation protocols and revealing the current limitations of both modular and end-to-end approaches, this work establishes a foundation for developing more capable and generalizable robotic systems.

The open-source nature of GENMANIP will enable researchers worldwide to build upon these findings, fostering collaborative development of the next generation of instruction-following robots capable of operating effectively in diverse, real-world environments.
