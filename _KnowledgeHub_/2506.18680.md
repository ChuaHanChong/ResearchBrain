# DuetGen: Music Driven Two-Person Dance Generation via Hierarchical Masked Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> DuetGen generates synchronized two-person dance motion from music via a two-stage pipeline: a hierarchical VQ-VAE tokenizes unified two-person motion into coarse semantic and fine detail tokens, then two masked transformers generate these tokens from music. Achieves state-of-the-art motion realism, partner synchronization, and music alignment on DD100, beating all baselines in quantitative metrics and user studies.

## Problem

- Prior two-person motion methods model each dancer's motion separately with single-person representations, failing to capture close interpersonal interactions like lifts and twirls.
- Existing motion quantization (single-layer VQ-VAE, residual VQ) tokenizes at one temporal scale, mixing global semantics with fine articulation and yielding suboptimal two-person reconstruction fidelity.
- Duolando, the only prior two-person dance method, only generates reactive follower motion conditioned on a given leader, not simultaneous joint generation of both dancers' motions.

## Method

- ==Unified two-person motion representation== combining both dancers' features per frame, with B's root position expressed relative to A rather than in global coordinates.
- ==Hierarchical two-person VQ-VAE== with bottom and top 1D-convolution encoders producing two discrete token sequences at coarse (semantic) and fine (detail) temporal resolutions, decoder conditioned on music features.
- Two-stage generative ==masked transformers== trained with ==cosine mask scheduling== and ==classifier-free guidance==: first predicts top-level tokens from music, second predicts bottom-level tokens conditioned on music and top tokens, plus a ==trajectory refinement regressor== for root motion.

## Results

- DuetGen achieves individual FID of **1.31** versus GCD's **9.71** and paired-FID of **2.54** versus GCD's **8.11**, and the highest Beat Alignment Score of **0.215** on DD100.
- Hierarchical VQ-VAE reconstruction reaches **36.26mm** MPJPE and **0.17mm** relative distance error, versus **65.09mm** MPJPE and **7.12mm** RDE for the vanilla single-layer VQ-VAE ablation.
- User study of **30** participants rates DuetGen highest on motion quality, music alignment, and partner coordination, scoring above **4/5** and at least **22%** higher than any baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing one agent's position relative to a partner rather than in shared global coordinates makes cross-agent interaction prediction far more tractable for the network.
> - Separating motion tokens by temporal-resolution hierarchy (coarse semantics vs fine detail) generalizes better than residual quantization stacked at a single scale for multi-body motion.
> - Unifying multiple agents' features into one joint token sequence, rather than modeling each agent's tokens separately, is key to learning tightly coupled interaction dynamics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of human motion synthesis has seen rapid advancements, driven by deep learning techniques. This paper situates itself within the intersection of music-driven motion generation and multi-person interaction, addressing a significant gap in the existing literature.

Historically, music-to-dance synthesis primarily focused on **single-person dances**. Early methods relied on motion graphs and dynamic time warping, limited by reliance on pre-existing libraries. The advent of deep learning, particularly RNNs, Transformers, GANs, VAEs, and more recently, diffusion models and GPT-like architectures, has enabled the generation of novel, diverse, and realistic single-person dances (e.g., Bailando, TM2D, MoFusion, EDGE). These works successfully learned intricate movement patterns and music correspondences.

More recently, research has extended to **multi-person motion synthesis**. This area is inherently more complex due to the need to model interactions, synchronization, and individual behaviors within a shared space. Prior work in multi-person scenarios includes:
- **Group Dance Synthesis:** Methods like GCD (Group Choreography using Contrastive Diffusion) focus on synchronized multi-person dance, but often neglect close, intricate interactions between individuals, treating them more as independent entities moving in sync rather than interacting.
- **Reactive Motion Synthesis:** Approaches like Duolando and InterDance have explored scenarios where one character's movements are generated in response to another (leader-follower dynamics). While these introduced partner awareness, they don't simultaneously generate the choreography for both dancers from music alone.
- **Text-Driven Multi-Person Generation:** Methods like InterGen leverage text prompts for generating multi-human motions, but adapting them to music-driven generation is non-trivial and often less effective for continuous, temporally aligned outputs like dance.

DuetGen specifically tackles the challenges of **two-person interactive dance generation from music**, a problem previously unaddressed in its full complexity. It distinguishes itself from prior work by:
- **Simultaneous Generation:** Unlike reactive approaches, DuetGen choreographs both dancers' movements concurrently.
- **Focus on Close Interactions:** It emphasizes modeling intricate interactions (e.g., twirls, lifts, partner supports) crucial for realistic duet dancing, which group dance methods typically overlook.
- **Music as the Sole Driver:** It relies solely on music as input, providing a natural and intuitive interface for choreography.

The paper also builds upon advancements in **motion quantization** using VQ-VAE (Vector Quantized-Variational Autoencoders). The concept of discretizing continuous motion into tokens, inspired by successful applications in speech and natural language processing (e.g., AudioLM, BERT), has shown promise in motion synthesis (e.g., TM2T, T2M-GPT, Bailando). DuetGen extends this by proposing a *hierarchical* VQ-VAE for *two-person* motions, capturing different levels of abstraction (high-level semantics vs. fine-grained details). This hierarchical approach is a key innovation over standard single-layer VQ-VAE or residual quantization methods.

By combining a novel unified representation for two-person motion, a hierarchical tokenization scheme, and masked generative transformers, DuetGen pushes the state-of-the-art in music-to-dance synthesis, opening new avenues for generating complex, interactive human movements in digital environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of DuetGen is to **generate synchronized and interactive two-person dance choreography directly from input music**. This aim is motivated by several inherent complexities and existing limitations in current motion synthesis techniques.

The core motivations and challenges that DuetGen seeks to address are:

- **Complexity of Two-Person Interactions:** Duet dancing is a highly expressive and interactive art form that demands intricate coordination between partners. Unlike single-person dances, where timing and spatial consistency relate to one body, two-person dances require maintaining synchronized motions between two individuals, ensuring cohesive rhythm, tempo, and mutual positioning. This includes modeling lead-follow dynamics, shared momentum, and close-proximity interactions such as twirls, lifts, and partner supports, which are difficult to capture and generate realistically.
- **Simultaneous Choreography from Music:** While previous works have explored generating group dances or reactive partner motions, they either overlook close interactions or do not simultaneously generate the movements for both dancers from a single music input. The goal is to provide a unified framework where music drives the choreography for both individuals, ensuring they move together interactively and harmoniously.
- **Realism and Plausibility:** Generated dances must not only be synchronized with music but also appear physically realistic, avoiding issues like foot sliding, interpenetration, or unnatural movements. This is particularly challenging in two-person scenarios where maintaining spatial relationships and avoiding collisions between bodies is critical.
- **Leveraging Discrete Motion Representations:** Inspired by the success of discrete tokenization in other generative AI domains (e.g., language models), the authors aim to apply and extend this paradigm to the complex domain of two-person dance. The motivation here is that discretizing motion can simplify the generative process by framing it as a sequence-to-sequence prediction problem using powerful transformer architectures.
- **Hierarchical Understanding of Motion:** Dance, especially duet dance, involves movements at various levels of abstraction – from high-level semantics like overall flow and major steps to low-level details like intricate hand gestures or subtle weight shifts. The motivation is to capture this hierarchy in the motion representation to enable more effective learning and generation, allowing the model to first establish the "gist" of the dance and then fill in the "details."
- **Enabling New Creative Applications:** The capability to synthesize high-quality duet dances from music has significant potential in various domains, including entertainment (e.g., virtual concerts, video games), communication media (e.g., personalized avatars, social platforms), virtual education (e.g., dance training), and broader content generation, fostering new forms of digital artistry and interaction.

By tackling these motivations, DuetGen aims to provide the first comprehensive solution for generating interactive two-person dances that are synchronized with music, realistic, and exhibit natural partner coordination.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DuetGen proposes a novel two-stage framework for music-driven two-person dance generation, built upon two core insights: a **unified representation for two-person motions** and a **hierarchical learning strategy** for these motions.

#### 3.1. Data Representation

- **Unified Two-Person Motion Representation:** Instead of treating dancers independently, DuetGen represents both dancers' motions as a single, unified feature vector (`x_i`). This `D`-dimensional vector for each frame `i` combines features for dancer A and dancer B. Key components include:
 - Root translation differences (`t_delta_A` for A, `t_epsilon_B` for B relative to A's current frame). This "relational global positioning" for B is crucial for modeling interactions.
 - Global orientations of root joints (`r_g_A`, `r_g_B`).
 - Root-invariant local joint positions and 6D local joint rotations for both dancers.
 - Local joint velocities and binary foot-ground contact indicators.
 This unified representation helps reduce the modeling complexity of inter-person interactions by treating them as a cohesive whole.
- **Music Features:** Standard music features (MFCC, MFCC delta, Chroma features) are extracted using the Librosa library, sampled to match the motion sequence's temporal resolution.

#### 3.2. Hierarchical Two-Person Motion Quantization (VQ-VAE)

This first stage aims to encode continuous two-person motions into discrete tokens at two levels of abstraction:
- **Coarse-level tokens:** Capture high-level semantic features (e.g., overall body movements, transitions, broad actions).
- **Fine-level tokens:** Represent low-level details (e.g., intricate articulations, subtle movements).

The architecture is a **hierarchical VQ-VAE** (Vector Quantized-Variational Autoencoder):
- **Encoders:** A bottom encoder (`E_B`) processes the raw motion sequence `x_1:N`. Its output is fed into a top encoder (`E_T`), which further downscales the temporal resolution to `N/eta_top`.
- **Top-Level Quantization:** `E_T`'s output (`~z_top`) is quantized by mapping each latent code to its nearest entry in a learnable codebook (`C_top`), yielding `z_top`. This forms the top-level discrete tokens.
- **Bottom-Level Quantization:** The quantized top-level features (`z_top`) are upscaled and concatenated with the output from `E_B`. This combined feature `~z_bot` is then quantized using a separate codebook (`C_bot`), producing `z_bot` – the bottom-level discrete tokens.
- **Decoder:** A single decoder `D` takes both `z_top` and `z_bot` (concatenated) along with the music features `z_m` (to aid convergence) and reconstructs the original motion sequence `x_hat_1:N`.
- **Training Objectives:** The VQ-VAE is trained end-to-end with:
 - Reconstruction loss (`L_r`) and velocity loss (`L_v`) on the motion features.
 - Commitment losses (`L_com`) for both codebooks to encourage encoder outputs to commit to codebook entries.
 - Crucially, **synchronization-specific losses**: `L_fk` (reconstruction loss on global-coordinate joint positions for both persons) and `L_rel` (reconstruction loss on relative joint distances between dancers, with distance-aware weighting to emphasize close interactions). These losses are vital for realistic duet motions.
- **Discrete Representation:** After training, the VQ-VAE effectively converts continuous motion into sequences of integer indices (tokens) `t_top` and `t_bot`.

#### 3.3. Music to Motion Via Two-Stage Masked Transformers

This stage learns to generate the learned motion tokens from music using two generative masked transformers, mirroring the hierarchical structure. The approach is inspired by masked language modeling (e.g., BERT, MaskGIT), where models predict masked tokens given context.

- **Music Encoder:** An encoder `E_M` processes the input music signal `m` to produce music features `z_m`.
- **Masking Strategy:** During training, a varying fraction of tokens in a sequence are randomly masked (replaced with `[MASK]` tokens) using a cosine scheduling function for the masking ratio. A re-masking strategy is also employed to enhance contextual reasoning.
- **First-Stage Transformer (for Top-Level Tokens):** A transformer encoder `theta_t` takes the masked top-level tokens (`t_M_top`), their embeddings, and the music features `z_m` as input. It is trained to predict the discrete distribution of tokens at the masked positions, minimizing the negative log-likelihood.
- **Second-Stage Transformer (for Bottom-Level Tokens):** A separate transformer `theta_b` models the bottom-level tokens. It takes the masked bottom-level tokens (`t_M_bot`), their embeddings, the music features `z_m`, AND the *full sequence of top-level tokens (`t_top`)* as input. This conditioning on `t_top` allows the fine-level transformer to refine details based on the high-level semantic context. It is also trained with a negative log-likelihood objective on masked tokens.
- **Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG):** Both transformers use CFG during training, occasionally dropping music conditioning, to allow for more controllable and diverse generation during inference.

#### 3.4. Inference

The generation process during inference is iterative:
1. **Top-Level Token Generation:** The first-stage transformer `theta_t` iteratively fills an initially empty (fully masked) sequence of top-level tokens (`N/eta_top` length) over `L_top` iterations, conditioned on music features. In each iteration, it predicts probabilities for masked locations and samples tokens. Tokens with low prediction probabilities are re-masked for subsequent iterations, gradually revealing the sequence.
2. **Bottom-Level Token Generation:** Once `t_hat_top` is generated, the second-stage transformer `theta_b` similarly generates the `N/eta_bot` length bottom-level token sequence `t_hat_bot` over `L_bot` iterations. Crucially, this stage is conditioned on both the music features AND the *generated* top-level tokens `t_hat_top`.
3. **Decoding:** The generated `t_hat_top` and `t_hat_bot` are combined and fed into the pre-trained VQ-VAE decoder `D` to reconstruct the continuous two-person dance motion sequence `x_hat`.
4. **Trajectory Refinement Module:** A lightweight 1D convolutional regressor is applied post-decoding. It takes local motion features (e.g., joint positions, rotations, velocities) and root orientations from the generated motion and predicts refined root trajectories. This module helps correct common issues like foot sliding and improves overall physical plausibility, as global root motions can be derived from local body movements.

This multi-stage, hierarchical approach, combined with dedicated representations and masked modeling, allows DuetGen to effectively capture the complex temporal dynamics and spatial interactions required for high-quality music-driven two-person dance generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DuetGen demonstrates state-of-the-art performance in music-driven two-person dance generation, validated through extensive quantitative evaluations and user studies on the DD100 dataset.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Evaluation

- **VQ Reconstruction Quality:** The hierarchical two-person motion VQ-VAE module is highly effective.
 - It achieves superior individual motion reconstruction (e.g., 36.26 mm MPJPE for Person B) and remarkably low relative distance error (RDE) between dancers (0.17 mm), significantly outperforming single-layer VQ-VAE (A2, 7.12 mm RDE) and other ablations.
 - The "relational global positioning" (A1 vs. ours) and "unified two-person motion representation" (A3 vs. ours) are critical; their removal leads to over 30% increase in individual MPJPE and substantial RDE increase, highlighting their role in modeling interactions.
 - Conditioning the VQ-VAE on music features (`z_m`) (A6 vs. ours) improves reconstruction quality, showing that music context helps in more accurate motion quantization.
 - The two-level hierarchy is optimal; a three-level hierarchy (A8) offered only marginal gains or even slight degradation, suggesting increased complexity without proportional benefits.
- **Two-Person Dance Generation Quality:** DuetGen significantly outperforms all re-trained baselines (Duolando, GCD, InterGen, MoFusion) across key metrics on the DD100 dataset.
 - **Motion Quality:** DuetGen achieves the lowest FID (1.31), indicating superior realism and distribution alignment with ground-truth motions. Baselines have significantly higher FIDs (e.g., GCD at 9.71). It also maintains high diversity (Div 15.71), comparable to ground truth. Physical Foot Contact (PFC) is also competitive (1.47), especially with trajectory refinement.
 - **Synchronization between Dancers:** DuetGen achieves the lowest Paired-FID (PFID) score (2.54), demonstrating excellent synchronization and coordination between dancers compared to baselines (e.g., GCD at 8.11). The Contact Frequency (CF) is maintained at 83.2%, very close to the ground truth (82.6%), signifying natural and frequent interactions.
 - **Music-Dance Alignment:** DuetGen achieves the highest Beat Alignment Score (BAS) of 0.215, indicating precise alignment of generated movements with musical beats, outperforming all baselines.
- **Ablation Studies on Generation:** These studies confirm the importance of DuetGen's unique components:
 - Removing hierarchical modeling (A2) or unified/relational representations (A1, A3) leads to significant degradation in realism (higher FID/PFID), validating these core design choices.
 - The trajectory refinement module (A4 vs. ours) provides modest improvements, particularly enhancing foot contact plausibility.
 - Different music feature encodings (A5) or higher hierarchy levels (A8) show sub-optimal performance, reinforcing the chosen design.

#### 4.2. User Study

A user study involving 30 participants consistently rated DuetGen as superior across all three evaluation aspects: motion quality, music-motion alignment, and partner coordination. DuetGen scored above '4' on a 5-point Likert scale for all aspects, achieving at least 22% higher ratings than any other method. This strong perceptual validation reinforces the quantitative findings and highlights the naturalness and realism of the generated dances.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

Visual comparisons (Figure 5) further illustrate DuetGen's strengths:
- Baselines like InterGen exhibit significant spatial drift, and dancers move far apart.
- Duolando and GCD show better partner awareness but still suffer from poor coordination, frequent interpenetration (dancers passing through each other), and unnatural movements.
- Ablations demonstrate specific failures: "w/o relational positioning" (A1) and "w/o unified representation" (A3) result in dancers unaware of each other, while "w/o hierarchical tokenization" (A2) fails to capture fine-grained interactions.
- In contrast, DuetGen produces well-coordinated dances with natural synchronization and spatial coordination, maintaining plausible interactions and close proximity throughout the sequence. The generated dances exhibit novel choreography, indicating the model's ability to generalize beyond simply reproducing training data.

In summary, DuetGen's results unequivocally establish its leading performance in generating high-quality, synchronized, and interactive two-person dances from music, demonstrating significant advancements over prior methods and validating the effectiveness of its novel architectural components.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DuetGen marks a significant advancement in the field of human motion synthesis, particularly for interactive and multi-person scenarios. Its core contributions extend beyond merely achieving state-of-the-art results; they open up new possibilities and have a wide range of potential impacts:

- **Pioneering Music-Driven Interactive Duet Dance:** By being the "first framework for generating interactive two-person dance motions directly from music," DuetGen addresses a critical, yet highly complex, challenge. This establishes a new benchmark and provides a strong foundation for future research in synchronized multi-person movement generation.
- **Enabling Realistic Digital Human Interaction:** The ability to generate natural and synchronized close interactions between virtual characters is crucial for believable digital experiences. DuetGen's success in modeling "twirls, lifts, and partner supports" translates directly into more immersive and engaging virtual environments, whether for gaming, social VR/AR, or virtual performances.
- **Advancements in Generative AI for Motion:** The proposed hierarchical VQ-VAE and two-stage masked transformers represent a novel and effective approach to motion tokenization and generation. The idea of separating high-level semantics from low-level details, and the use of a unified representation for multiple interacting agents, are methodological innovations that could inspire similar approaches in other complex, multi-agent generative tasks. The empirical validation of relational global positioning and the benefits of conditioning the VQ-VAE on music further contribute to the best practices in this domain.
- **New Avenues for Creative Content Generation:** DuetGen empowers creators to easily generate complex duet choreographies from music, democratizing access to sophisticated animation capabilities. This can revolutionize content creation for:
 - **Virtual Entertainment:** Automated choreography for virtual idols, animated films, and interactive stories.
 - **Gaming:** More dynamic and realistic non-player character (NPC) interactions and emotes.
 - **Social VR/AR:** Enabling avatars to perform complex synchronized dances with friends or other users in virtual spaces.
 - **Education and Training:** Providing personalized dance instruction or allowing users to practice with virtual partners.
 - **Artistic Expression:** Offering new tools for choreographers and digital artists to experiment with movement.
- **Bridging the Gap between Academia and Industry:** The collaboration between leading academic institutions (DFKI, MPI for Informatics) and an industry giant (Snap Inc.) showcases a successful model for pushing foundational research towards practical applications. This ensures the developed technologies are not only scientifically sound but also relevant for real-world deployment.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations:
- **Body Shape Variations:** The current model relies on fixed body shapes from the dataset, not addressing variations. Future work could integrate parametric body models (e.g., SMPL-X) to allow for customizable body shapes.
- **Fine-Grained Finger Interactions:** Noisy data in the DD100 dataset prevented modeling intricate finger movements. Future datasets with higher fidelity capture or specialized models for hand/finger animation could address this.
- **Dataset Size:** The 1.9 hours of DD100 data, while the most comprehensive for duet dance, is still relatively small. Future efforts could focus on combining motion capture data with large-scale online video analysis to increase data diversity and volume, enhancing the model's generalization capabilities across more dance styles and interaction types.

Despite these limitations, DuetGen represents a monumental step forward in synthesizing realistic, interactive human movements, particularly for the challenging domain of two-person dance. Its novel methodology and demonstrated performance lay a robust groundwork for the next generation of intelligent agents capable of complex and coordinated physical interaction in virtual worlds.
