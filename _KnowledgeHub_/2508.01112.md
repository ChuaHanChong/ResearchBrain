# MASIV: Toward Material-Agnostic System Identification from Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> MASIV introduces a vision-based framework to infer object dynamics from multi-view videos without explicit material priors. It integrates dynamic 3D geometry reconstruction and dense particle trajectory estimation with learnable neural constitutive models in a differentiable simulator, achieving new performance benchmarks in observable and future state prediction across diverse materials.

## Problem

- Existing vision-based system identification methods rely on predefined material priors and hand-crafted constitutive laws, limiting their application to unknown or heterogeneous materials.
- Flexible neural constitutive models require complete particle state information, which is not directly available from sparse video observations, hindering their use in vision-based settings.
- Learning complex material dynamics from sparse visual data often leads to under-constrained optimization problems, resulting in unstable convergence and physically implausible simulations.

## Method

- A three-phase pipeline combining dynamic 3D Gaussian Reconstruction and Continuum Trajectory Estimation to extract dense 3D geometry and continuous particle motion from multi-view video.
- Integration of learnable neural constitutive models (parameterized by neural networks for elasticity and plasticity) into a differentiable Material Point Method (MPM) simulator.
- An analysis-by-synthesis optimization approach using a novel 'dense geometric guidance' loss, which supervises simulated particle positions with the estimated dense trajectories, complemented by a silhouette loss.

## Results

- MASIV achieves state-of-the-art performance in observable state simulation (e.g., Chamfer Distance 0.210 on PAC-NeRF, 0.13 on Spring-Gaus Synthetic) and consistently outperforms most baselines in future state prediction.
- Ablation studies demonstrate the critical role of dense geometrical guidance, with methods lacking it showing significantly higher errors, and confirm the superiority of trajectory-based supervision over sparse alternatives.
- The framework successfully exhibits strong intra-class (modifying object properties) and inter-class (applying learned properties to novel objects) generalization, producing physically plausible deformations for various material types.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dense geometrical guidance derived from continuum particle trajectories is crucial for robustly training flexible neural constitutive models from sparse visual observations.
> - Neural constitutive models, when combined with differentiable simulation, enable material-agnostic inference of complex material behaviors, improving generalization without expert-defined priors.
> - Bridging the gap between sparse visual input and rich physical state information through dense trajectory estimation allows for stable and physically plausible learning of intrinsic object dynamics.
