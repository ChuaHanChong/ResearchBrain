# MASIV: Toward Material-Agnostic System Identification from Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> MASIV introduces a vision-based framework to infer object dynamics from multi-view videos without explicit material priors. It integrates dynamic 3D geometry reconstruction and dense particle trajectory estimation with learnable neural constitutive models in a differentiable simulator, achieving new performance benchmarks in observable and future state prediction across diverse materials.

## Problem

- Existing vision-based system identification methods rely on predefined material priors and hand-crafted constitutive laws, limiting their application to unknown or heterogeneous materials.
- Flexible neural constitutive models require complete particle state information, which is not directly available from sparse video observations, hindering their use in vision-based settings.
- Learning complex material dynamics from sparse visual data often leads to under-constrained optimization problems, resulting in unstable convergence and physically implausible simulations.

## Method

![[2508.01112-method.png]]
*MASIV's three-phase pipeline: (I) Geometrical Reconstruction extracts dense motion from sparse observations, (II) Material-Agnostic System Identification optimizes neural constitutive models, and (III) Generalization enables novel simulations.*

- A three-phase pipeline combining dynamic 3D Gaussian Reconstruction and Continuum Trajectory Estimation to extract dense 3D geometry and continuous particle motion from multi-view video.
- Integration of learnable neural constitutive models (parameterized by neural networks for elasticity and plasticity) into a differentiable Material Point Method (MPM) simulator.
- An analysis-by-synthesis optimization approach using a novel 'dense geometric guidance' loss, which supervises simulated particle positions with the estimated dense trajectories, complemented by a silhouette loss.

## Results

- MASIV achieves state-of-the-art performance in observable state simulation (e.g., Chamfer Distance 0.210 on PAC-NeRF, 0.13 on Spring-Gaus Synthetic) and consistently outperforms most baselines in future state prediction.
- Ablation studies demonstrate the critical role of dense geometrical guidance, with methods lacking it showing significantly higher errors, and confirm the superiority of trajectory-based supervision over sparse alternatives.
- The framework successfully exhibits strong intra-class (modifying object properties) and inter-class (applying learned properties to novel objects) generalization, producing physically plausible deformations for various material types.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dense geometrical guidance derived from continuum particle trajectories is crucial for robustly training flexible neural constitutive models from sparse visual observations.
> - Neural constitutive models, when combined with differentiable simulation, enable material-agnostic inference of complex material behaviors, improving generalization without expert-defined priors.
> - Bridging the gap between sparse visual input and rich physical state information through dense trajectory estimation allows for stable and physically plausible learning of intrinsic object dynamics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a critical challenge in **vision-based system identification**, a subfield of artificial intelligence aiming to infer the underlying physical laws governing object motion and interaction from visual observations. Traditionally, this task is fundamental to "world modeling," where AI systems construct internal representations of the environment to understand and predict its evolution.

The broader landscape of system identification from videos has seen significant advancements, particularly with the integration of **differentiable rendering** (e.g., Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF), 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)) and **differentiable simulators** (e.g., Material Point Method (MPM)). These techniques allow for end-to-end optimization of physical models by comparing rendered simulations with observed video frames.

However, a major limitation of existing approaches is their heavy reliance on **predefined material priors** and hand-crafted constitutive laws (e.g., specific elasticity, plasticity, or viscosity models). This dependence means that for an AI system to identify the dynamics of an object, it first needs to be told or infer the *type* of material (e.g., "this is rubber," "this is sand"). This significantly restricts their adaptability and generalization ability, especially in "in-the-wild" scenarios where material properties are unknown or heterogeneous.

The paper directly builds upon the concept of **neural constitutive models** (e.g., NCLaw), which offer a more flexible, data-driven way to represent material properties using neural networks, moving beyond fixed mathematical formulations. However, prior neural constitutive models, designed for direct simulation tasks, assume access to *complete particle state information* (positions, velocities, deformation gradients) at every simulation step. Such rich data is simply not available from sparse video observations. This discrepancy creates a significant gap: neural constitutive models offer the desired flexibility, but visual data lacks the necessary completeness to train them effectively in a vision-based setting.

MASIV's contribution lies precisely in bridging this gap. It aims to develop a **material-agnostic** framework that leverages the flexibility of neural constitutive models while operating solely from visual input. It innovates by introducing "dense geometric guidance" to compensate for the sparsity of visual observations, effectively distilling rich motion constraints from sparse pixel data. By doing so, MASIV pushes the boundary toward AI systems that can infer physical dynamics without needing explicit material labels, akin to how humans intuitively perceive and predict object behavior regardless of knowing precise material compositions. This aligns with the long-term goal of building more robust, generalizable, and intelligent agents capable of understanding and interacting with the complex physical world.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of this research is to develop **MASIV (Material-Agnostic System Identification from Videos)**, a novel vision-based framework capable of inferring the intrinsic dynamics of objects from video observations without relying on predefined material priors.

This ambitious objective is driven by several key motivations stemming from the limitations of prior work:

- **Overcoming Material-Specific Limitations:** Existing vision-based system identification methods are fundamentally constrained by their reliance on pre-defined, hand-crafted constitutive laws and material types (e.g., Neo-Hookean elasticity, identity plasticity). This "material-specific" assumption necessitates knowing an object's material type beforehand, severely limiting their applicability to unknown, novel, or heterogeneous materials commonly encountered in real-world "in-the-wild" scenarios. The authors are motivated to create a system that can adapt to any material behavior observed in a video.
- **Mimicking Human Intuition:** Humans possess an remarkable ability to observe an object's motion and intuitively predict its future behavior or understand how it would react to different forces, often without explicit knowledge of its material composition. The research aims to imbue artificial intelligence with a similar capability, enabling it to generalize physical laws to diverse scenarios in a more human-like manner.
- **Leveraging Flexible Neural Constitutive Models:** Recent advancements in neural constitutive models (e.g., NCLaw) offer a promising avenue for representing complex material behaviors without explicit analytical forms. However, these models were designed for simulation with access to *complete particle state information* (positions, velocities, deformation gradients, etc.), which is not directly available from visual data.
- **Addressing Data Sparsity Challenges in Vision-based Settings:** Directly applying neural constitutive models to vision-based system identification, where only sparse pixel-level observations are available, leads to significant challenges. The lack of full particle state information results in an under-constrained problem, causing unstable optimization, poor convergence, and physically implausible simulated behaviors. The authors are motivated to devise a mechanism that can extract sufficient constraints from sparse visual data to stably train these flexible neural models.
- **Enhancing Generalization and Adaptability:** By removing the requirement for material priors, MASIV aims to create a more generalizable "digital twin" of observed objects. This digital twin should be able to simulate novel interactions (e.g., new forces or velocities) for objects of previously unseen material types, thereby improving the fundamental understanding and predictive power of AI systems regarding physical dynamics.

In essence, the paper seeks to bridge the gap between flexible, data-driven material modeling and the inherent sparsity of visual observations, leading to a more robust, adaptable, and generalized approach to understanding the physics of the world from videos.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MASIV framework addresses the challenge of material-agnostic system identification from videos through a meticulously structured, three-phase pipeline: Geometrical Reconstruction, Material-Agnostic System Identification, and Generalization.

**Phase I: Geometrical Reconstruction**
This phase focuses on extracting precise 3D geometry and dense motion information from multi-view video inputs.

- **Dynamic Gaussian Reconstruction (Sec. 3.2):** The process begins by reconstructing the dynamic object using **3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)**. Instead of discrete frames, a set of canonical Gaussians ($G^*$) is maintained and deformed over time by a neural deformation network ($D$). This network maps a given time step to deformation bases for position and scale, allowing the canonical Gaussians to warp and represent the object's appearance and shape at each observed frame. The optimization minimizes L1 and Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM) losses between rendered frames and ground-truth observations, coupled with L1 regularization on Gaussian scales for reconstruction quality. This provides a temporally sparse (frame-wise) dynamic representation.
- **Continuum Trajectory Estimation (Sec. 3.3):** To bridge the gap between sparse Gaussian observations and the continuous nature of physical simulation, the reconstructed Gaussians are "filled" to form a solid continuum of particles ({p_t}). A crucial innovation here is the fine-tuning of the deformation network (now denoted $D'$) to establish *temporally dense particle trajectories* for these continuum particles. By leveraging motion bases and temporal positional embeddings, $D'$ can interpolate particle motion between observed frames, generating particle positions ($x_t$) at every *simulation* time step (N, which is much denser than T observed frames). This dense sequence of particle trajectories serves as "pseudo ground truth" for subsequent physics-based optimization, providing rich motion constraints that are otherwise absent from sparse visual cues. The fine-tuning uses Chamfer Distance to align the deformed continuum particles with the filled continuum from reconstruction.

**Phase II: Material-Agnostic System Identification**
This is the core phase where the intrinsic dynamics are learned without material priors.

- **Neural Constitutive Models (Sec. 3.4):** Unlike prior methods that use expert-designed constitutive laws, MASIV employs fully learnable neural networks to parameterize the elastic ($E_{\theta_e}$) and plastic ($P_{\theta_p}$) constitutive laws. These networks (MLPs) are designed to incorporate physical priors like frame indifference (using rotation-invariant inputs) and undeformed state equilibrium (eliminating bias terms), inspired by NCLaw.
- **Differentiable Simulation with Material Point Method (MPM):** The learned neural constitutive models are integrated into a **Material Point Method (MPM)** simulator. MPM discretizes the object into material points, tracking their states (position, velocity, deformation gradient) and evolving them over time through a time-stepping scheme. The constitutive laws $E_{\theta_e}$ and $P_{\theta_p}$ are used within MPM to compute stress and correct trial deformation gradients, respectively.
- **Analysis-by-Synthesis Optimization:** The framework follows an analysis-by-synthesis approach to optimize the neural constitutive model parameters ($\theta_e, \theta_p$) and the initial velocity ($v_0$). Starting from an initial particle state, the MPM simulator rolls out particle motion ({$\hat{x}_t$}). The simulation is guided by two complementary loss terms:
 - **Geometric Loss ($L_{geo}$):** This is the key innovation, utilizing the *temporally dense particle trajectories* ($x_t$) estimated in Phase I as supervision. Specifically, $L_{geo}$ is an L1 loss comparing the simulated particle positions ({$\hat{x}_t$}) with the estimated trajectories ({$x_t$}) at every *simulation* time step. This dense supervision provides robust constraints for training the neural constitutive models.
 - **Silhouette Loss ($L_{sil}$):** An L1 loss that compares rendered masks from the simulated particles ({$\hat{M}_t$}) with object silhouettes ($M_t$) extracted from the original video frames. This ensures that the simulated object's visual appearance remains consistent with observations.
- **Initialization:** For stable optimization, the neural constitutive models are initialized with parameters from a pre-trained model (e.g., NCLaw trained on complete state data). MASIV then fine-tunes these parameters using its vision-based losses.

**Phase III: Generalization**
Once the neural constitutive models are optimized, MASIV yields a generalizable digital twin. This digital twin can then be used to simulate novel interactions by applying new velocities or forces, effectively predicting the object's behavior under unseen conditions.

By combining explicit geometry reconstruction with dense trajectory estimation, and integrating this with differentiable MPM and learnable neural constitutive models, MASIV effectively grounds complex object dynamics from sparse visual observations in a material-agnostic manner.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

MASIV demonstrates significant advancements in material-agnostic system identification, showcasing superior performance across various metrics and generalization capabilities.

#### 4.1. Observable State Simulation

- **State-of-the-Art on Synthetic Data:** On the PAC-NeRF and Spring-Gaus Synthetic datasets, MASIV achieves superior or on-par performance in terms of Chamfer Distance (CD) compared to existing state-of-the-art methods (PAC-NeRF, Spring-Gaus, NeuMA, GIC). This is particularly notable because MASIV operates without expert-designed physical models or predefined material priors, unlike the baselines. The mean CD for MASIV on PAC-NeRF is 0.210, slightly better than GIC (0.212), and significantly better than PAC-NeRF (0.278). On Spring-Gaus Synthetic, MASIV achieves a mean CD of 0.13, outperforming all baselines (e.g., GIC at 0.17, Spring-Gaus at 0.85).
- **High Fidelity on Real-World Data:** On the Spring-Gaus Real dataset, MASIV achieves state-of-the-art results for visual quality metrics, with a mean PSNR of 41.12 (vs. GIC's 38.11) and a mean SSIM of 0.997 (vs. GIC's 0.996). This indicates that MASIV's more flexible constitutive representation allows for better alignment with complex real-world dynamics.

#### 4.2. Future State Simulation

- **Improved Predictive Accuracy:** MASIV consistently outperforms most baselines (PAC-NeRF, Spring-Gaus, NeuMA) in future state prediction on the Spring-Gaus Synthetic dataset across CD, PSNR, and SSIM metrics. For example, MASIV's mean CD is 1.20, significantly better than PAC-NeRF (17.94), Spring-Gaus (5.16), and NeuMA (170.56).
- **Comparison with GIC:** While generally superior, MASIV slightly underperforms GIC in some future state prediction scenarios. The authors attribute this to GIC's benefit from a pre-known constitutive model (acting as an "oracle" with category-level regularization), which can aid generalization under scarce data. MASIV, being purely data-driven, might require more data for comparable generalization. However, MASIV's advantage in dense geometrical constraints ensures more stable optimization compared to approaches relying solely on sparse pixel supervision (e.g., NeuMA's unstable optimization process).

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Necessity of Geometrical Guidance:** The study unequivocally demonstrates the critical role of dense geometrical guidance. A baseline without any geometrical objective ($L_{geo}$) exhibits significantly higher Chamfer Distance errors across all material types, highlighting that pixel-level supervision alone is insufficient for stable learning.
- **Effectiveness of Dense Trajectory Supervision:** Comparing different forms of geometrical objectives (surface-based, continuum-based, trajectory-based), the trajectory-based supervision ($L_{traj}$) consistently yields the lowest and most stable errors across materials. Sparse supervision methods (surface, continuum) lead to moderate error reduction but show instability and "overshoots" in error, especially for deformable materials like Plasticine and Sand. This is attributed to unconstrained intermediate motion dynamics between sparse observations, leading to physically implausible deformations.
- **Impact of Pre-trained Models:** Fine-tuning pre-trained NCLaw models (which are trained on full particle state data) significantly improves MASIV's performance, demonstrating its robustness to different initializations and its ability to adapt general physical priors to specific visual observations.
- **Impact of Silhouette Loss:** The inclusion of silhouette loss ($L_{sil}$) substantially decreases CD values, confirming its effectiveness in constraining object boundaries and enhancing overall simulation fidelity and shape reconstruction.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Analysis

- **Physically Plausible Dynamics:** Qualitative comparisons show that sparse geometrical cues (surface or continuum) can lead to physically implausible deformations (e.g., inconsistent distortion in the "Cream" object). In contrast, dense trajectory guidance mitigates these issues, producing smoother and more accurate motion reconstructions.
- **Intra-Class Generalization:** MASIV successfully demonstrates intra-class generalization by modifying an object's (PAC-NeRF Bird) dynamic characteristics (softening or hardening) using constitutive models learned from other elastic objects. This shows the ability to apply learned material properties to different instances of the same object class.
- **Inter-Class Generalization:** The framework exhibits strong inter-class generalization. Neural constitutive models trained on different material types (Elastic, Newtonian, Sandy, Non-Newtonian, Plastic) were applied to a novel object (PAC-NeRF Trophy). Each material model induced distinct and physically plausible deformation patterns, confirming MASIV's ability to capture generalizable intrinsic dynamics.
- **Data-Driven Advantage:** An additional quantitative analysis on a multi-sequence dataset (generated with Genesis) shows that MASIV's performance, unlike GIC, improves significantly with more training data. This highlights the inherent data-driven advantage of MASIV's neural constitutive models, which can learn richer, more nuanced material behaviors with increased observations.

In summary, MASIV effectively addresses the critical limitation of material-specific system identification by leveraging neural constitutive models and dense geometrical guidance. Its strong quantitative and qualitative results establish it as a robust and generalizable framework for understanding and predicting physical dynamics from videos.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of MASIV marks a significant step forward in the field of vision-based system identification and physics-informed AI, with several profound implications and potential impacts:

- **Breaking the Material Prior Barrier:** MASIV is the first framework to enable material-agnostic system identification directly from videos. By eliminating the need for predefined material priors (e.g., knowing if an object is elastic, fluid, or plastic), it vastly expands the applicability of physics-based AI to real-world scenarios where material properties are often unknown, complex, or heterogeneous. This represents a crucial advancement towards AI systems that can operate more autonomously and intelligently in diverse physical environments.
- **Enhanced Generalization and Adaptability:** The ability of MASIV to learn flexible, neural constitutive models from visual data directly translates to improved generalization. It demonstrates both intra-class (modifying properties of a known object) and inter-class (applying learned properties to novel objects) generalization. This level of adaptability is vital for creating robust AI agents that can interact with an unpredictable physical world, unlike previous systems limited to narrowly defined material categories.
- **Robustness from Sparse Observations:** A key technical achievement is MASIV's ability to robustly train neural constitutive models from inherently sparse visual information. The novel "dense geometric guidance" (via continuum trajectory estimation) effectively bridges the gap between limited pixel-level observations and the rich state information required by physical simulations. This methodology provides a blueprint for future research in inferring hidden physical states from visual cues.
- **Improved World Modeling for AI:** By enabling AI to understand and predict object dynamics without explicit material labels, MASIV contributes directly to the grand challenge of building comprehensive "world models." Such models are foundational for advanced AI applications, including intuitive physics, predictive control, and complex reasoning about physical interactions.
- **Impact on Robotics and Manipulation:** The ability to infer an object's dynamic properties from video, irrespective of its material type, has immense implications for robotics. Robots could intelligently manipulate novel objects (e.g., grasping, deforming, cutting) by predicting their physical responses purely from visual observation, without needing prior calibration or material databases. This could accelerate the development of more dexterous and adaptable robotic systems.
- **Advancements in Computer Graphics and Simulation:** MASIV's approach can lead to more realistic and flexible simulations in computer graphics. Animators and developers could generate dynamic behaviors for arbitrary objects based on observational data, without needing to manually select or tune complex material parameters. This opens doors for data-driven material synthesis and physics-driven animation that more closely mimic real-world phenomena.
- **Potential for Scientific Discovery:** In the long term, frameworks like MASIV could aid in scientific research by helping to characterize unknown materials or understand complex physical processes from experimental video data. The learned neural constitutive models could offer insights into underlying physical laws or emergent behaviors that are difficult to model analytically.

Despite its significant contributions, the authors acknowledge limitations, such as reliance on multi-view video (restricting single-view applications), assumptions about internal geometries (limiting applicability to complex granular or multiphase fluids), and not explicitly disentangling material properties from external forces. Addressing these will be crucial for MASIV's broader adoption and continued impact. Nevertheless, MASIV represents a powerful conceptual and technical leap towards more general and intelligent AI systems that can intuitively grasp the physics of our world.
