# Analog In-memory Training on General Non-ideal Resistive Elements: The Impact of Response Functions

> [!summary] Summary
> This research provides a theoretical framework for gradient-based training on analog in-memory computing (AIMC) hardware, accounting for non-ideal resistive elements with asymmetric and non-linear response functions. It introduces Residual Learning, a bilevel optimization algorithm proven to achieve exact convergence, significantly outperforming traditional Analog SGD on various neural network models and datasets, while also addressing practical issues like I/O noise and limited response granularity.

## Problem

- The Von Neumann architecture creates a data movement bottleneck, making training large machine learning models computationally and energy-intensive.
- Analog In-Memory Computing (AIMC) offers energy efficiency but faces challenges in training due to non-ideal resistive elements, particularly their asymmetric and non-linear response functions.
- Existing theoretical analyses of AIMC training were often limited to specific cases or empirical observations, lacking a general framework for generic non-ideal hardware.

## Method

- Developed a discrete-time mathematical model for analog updates, decomposing ==non-ideal response== functions into symmetric and asymmetric components.
- Introduced "Residual Learning," a bilevel optimization framework using a main weight array and an auxiliary residual array to correct for asymmetric updates.
- Extended the framework (Residual Learning v2) with a digital buffer for I/O noise filtering and a threshold-based transfer mechanism for limited response granularity.

## Results

- Analog SGD was theoretically shown to exhibit inexact convergence, with an asymptotic error proportional to noise variance and an amplification factor dependent on the asymmetry.
- Numerical simulations on MNIST and CIFAR10/100 datasets demonstrated that Residual Learning achieved test accuracy comparable to Digital SGD, consistently outperforming Analog SGD, which degraded significantly (e.g., below **15%** accuracy) under pronounced asymmetry.
- Residual Learning v2, incorporating noise filtering and granularity handling, further improved robustness, showing enhanced performance on CIFAR100 over the basic Residual Learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Asymmetric and non-linear response functions in resistive elements introduce an "implicit penalty" in Analog SGD, causing it to optimize a modified objective instead of the true one.
> - This implicit penalty leads to "inexact convergence" for Analog SGD, resulting in a persistent, non-diminishing asymptotic error relative to the true optimal point.
> - The proposed Residual Learning framework achieves "exact convergence" by using a bilevel optimization strategy and a "zero-shifted symmetric point" for the auxiliary residual array.
