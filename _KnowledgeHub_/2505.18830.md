# On the Effect of Negative Gradient in Group Relative Deep Reinforcement Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> University of British Columbia and Vector Institute researchers identify Lazy Likelihood Displacement (LLD) in Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)—where the likelihood of correct responses increases marginally or decreases during training—tracing this phenomenon to naive penalization of all tokens in incorrect responses including shared reasoning steps, and develop Negative Token Hidden Reward (NTHR) that selectively penalizes only tokens contributing most to likelihood reduction, achieving consistent performance improvements on mathematical reasoning benchmarks across model sizes from 0.5B to 3B parameters while maintaining computational efficiency.

## Problem

- GRPO suffers from Lazy Likelihood Displacement where correct response likelihood fails to increase or even decreases during training
- Naive penalization treats all tokens in incorrect responses equally, including shared reasoning steps that also appear in correct solutions, unfairly penalizing correct intermediate reasoning
- Existing optimization dynamics in GRPO remain insufficiently understood despite empirical success in LLM reasoning tasks

## Method

- Develop theoretical framework characterizing ==likelihood change dynamics== in GRPO through formal analysis of update equations
- Introduce Negative Token Hidden Reward (NTHR), which uses GRPO's group-based structure to identify influential tokens and selectively penalizes them based on their contribution to likelihood reduction, rather than applying uniform penalization
- Maintain computational efficiency by avoiding inference overhead while preserving data efficiency of online learning

## Results

- GRPO+NTHR consistently outperforms standard GRPO on MATH and AIME mathematical reasoning benchmarks across multiple model scales, using both Qwen and DeepSeek model families
- Method effectively mitigates LLD while maintaining data efficiency compared to masking all negative gradients
- Theoretical analysis validated through empirical comparison with "Pos Only" variant that masks negative gradients entirely

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLD in GRPO extends the misalignment phenomenon previously observed in Direct Preference Optimization methods
> - Penalizing shared reasoning steps or correct tokens within incorrect responses directly causes likelihood displacement
> - Selective token penalization based on gradient contribution proves more effective than uniform or random approaches, with the group relative optimization structure enabling this targeted intervention without requiring fundamental algorithmic changes

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work fits into the broader research landscape at the intersection of:

- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) for Large Language Models (LLMs):** The paper directly addresses the growing trend of using RL to fine-tune LLMs for improved reasoning and task-specific capabilities. It builds upon the foundation laid by algorithms like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO).

- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** GRPO is an algorithm gaining traction within the LLM community, with demonstrated success in enhancing reasoning capabilities in models like DeepSeek-Math, DeepSeek-R1, Med-R1, and Search-R1. This work contributes to a deeper understanding of the inner workings of this algorithm and its potential limitations.

- **Preference Learning:** The paper draws a conceptual connection to DPO and other preference learning methods, highlighting the importance of aligning LLM behavior with desired preferences. It investigates a similar phenomenon to those observed in preference optimization frameworks, where negative gradients can lead to counter-intuitive learning dynamics.

- **Understanding LLM Fine-tuning Dynamics:** This paper contributes to a growing body of work aimed at understanding the intricacies of LLM fine-tuning. It investigates the subtle, and sometimes detrimental, effects of specific optimization strategies on model behavior. It investigates "misalignment" or "likelihood displacement".

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The key objectives of this research are:

- **Identify and Characterize Lazy Likelihood Displacement (LLD) in GRPO:** The primary goal is to empirically demonstrate and formally define the phenomenon of LLD in GRPO, where the likelihood of correct responses either increases marginally or even decreases during training.

- **Analyze the Source of LLD:** The paper aims to provide a theoretical explanation for LLD in GRPO, identifying the specific mechanisms that contribute to this phenomenon. The central argument is that LLD arises from the naive penalization of all tokens in incorrect responses with equal strength.

- **Develop a Method to Mitigate LLD:** The authors aim to create a targeted intervention strategy to address LLD in GRPO without compromising data efficiency or requiring modifications to the online learning paradigm.

- **Empirically Validate the Effectiveness of the Proposed Method:** The paper seeks to demonstrate that the proposed method, Negative Token Hidden Reward (NTHR), effectively mitigates LLD and leads to improved performance on mathematical reasoning benchmarks across different model sizes.

The motivation for this research stems from the observation that, despite the empirical success of GRPO, its optimization behavior remains insufficiently studied. The authors were inspired by recent findings in DPO-based methods where negative gradients were found to lead to "misalignment" or "likelihood displacement".

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a combination of theoretical analysis and empirical validation.

- **Theoretical Analysis:** The authors provide a theoretical analysis of GRPO's update dynamics. They derive an expression (Theorem 4.4) that characterizes the change in the log-likelihood of correct responses during training. This expression reveals that the impact of negative gradients from incorrect responses can lead to LLD.
- **Empirical Analysis:** The authors conduct a series of experiments using math reasoning tasks to assess the impact of negative gradients on the likelihood of generating correct responses. The use model-dataset combinations varying in dataset difficulty and model scale (Qwen-0.5B, Qwen-1.5B, Deepseek-1.5B with MATH and AIME datasets). They compare the log-likelihood gains achieved by GRPO with those obtained by a "Pos Only" variant, where negative gradients are masked.

- **Development of NTHR:** Based on the theoretical analysis and empirical observations, the authors develop a method called Negative Token Hidden Reward (NTHR). NTHR selectively penalizes tokens in incorrect responses that contribute most to lowering the likelihood of correct responses.

- **Empirical Validation of NTHR:** The authors evaluate GRPO+NTHR against baseline methods (standard GRPO and GRPO+Random token dropping) through per-sample training. They demonstrate that GRPO+NTHR mitigates LLD and improves generalization on held-out mathematical reasoning problems. They test the approach with model sizes from 0.5B to 3B.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The main findings and results of the research are:

- **GRPO Exhibits LLD:** The authors empirically demonstrate that GRPO suffers from LLD, an extended form of the misalignment phenomenon observed in DPO.
- **Penalizing Shared Reasoning or Correct Tokens Causes LLD:** The theoretical analysis identifies the penalization of shared reasoning steps or correct tokens in incorrect responses as the cause of LLD.
- **NTHR Effectively Mitigates LLD:** The proposed NTHR method, which selectively penalizes tokens in incorrect responses, effectively mitigates LLD without sacrificing data efficiency.
- **GRPO+NTHR Improves Performance on Math Reasoning Tasks:** GRPO+NTHR consistently outperforms GRPO on math benchmarks across model sizes ranging from 0.5B to 3B. The performance gains are consistently observed.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several significant contributions and has the potential to impact the field of LLM fine-tuning:

- **Deeper Understanding of GRPO:** The paper provides a deeper understanding of the optimization dynamics of GRPO, a widely used algorithm for enhancing the reasoning capabilities of LLMs. By identifying and characterizing LLD, the authors reveal a potential limitation of this algorithm.

- **Novel Mitigation Strategy:** The proposed NTHR method offers a novel and effective strategy for mitigating LLD in GRPO. Unlike previous approaches, NTHR leverages the group-based structure of GRPO to identify influential tokens and selectively penalize them.

- **Improved Performance on Reasoning Tasks:** The empirical results demonstrate that NTHR leads to improved performance on challenging math reasoning tasks. This suggests that NTHR can be used to enhance the reasoning capabilities of LLMs in various domains.

- **Broader Implications for Preference Learning:** The findings of this research have broader implications for the field of preference learning. The identification of LLD in GRPO suggests that similar phenomena may exist in other preference learning algorithms. The NTHR method provides a potential framework for mitigating these phenomena.

- **Practical Applicability:** The authors state that NTHR doesn't introduce inference overhead, and the authors work hard to provide a computationally efficient solution. This makes NTHR a practical technique for improving LLM fine-tuning.

In summary, this research makes a significant contribution to the understanding and improvement of RL-based LLM fine-tuning. By identifying and mitigating LLD in GRPO, the authors have developed a method that has the potential to enhance the reasoning capabilities of LLMs and improve their performance on complex tasks.
