# On the Effect of Negative Gradient in Group Relative Deep Reinforcement Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> University of British Columbia and Vector Institute researchers identify Lazy Likelihood Displacement (LLD) in Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)—where the likelihood of correct responses increases marginally or decreases during training—tracing this phenomenon to naive penalization of all tokens in incorrect responses including shared reasoning steps, and develop Negative Token Hidden Reward (NTHR) that selectively penalizes only tokens contributing most to likelihood reduction, achieving consistent performance improvements on mathematical reasoning benchmarks across model sizes from 0.5B to 3B parameters while maintaining computational efficiency.

## Problem

- GRPO suffers from Lazy Likelihood Displacement where correct response likelihood fails to increase or even decreases during training
- Naive penalization treats all tokens in incorrect responses equally, including shared reasoning steps that appear in correct solutions
- Existing optimization dynamics in GRPO remain insufficiently understood despite empirical success in LLM reasoning tasks
- Standard GRPO can penalize correct intermediate reasoning tokens that happen to appear in incorrect final responses

## Method

- Develop theoretical framework characterizing ==likelihood change dynamics== in GRPO through formal analysis of update equations
- Introduce Negative Token Hidden Reward (NTHR) method that selectively penalizes tokens based on their contribution to likelihood reduction
- Use group-based structure of GRPO to identify influential tokens rather than applying uniform penalization
- Maintain computational efficiency by avoiding inference overhead while preserving data efficiency of online learning

## Results

- GRPO+NTHR consistently outperforms standard GRPO on mathematical reasoning benchmarks across multiple model scales
- Method effectively mitigates LLD while maintaining data efficiency compared to masking all negative gradients
- Performance improvements demonstrated on both MATH and AIME datasets using Qwen and DeepSeek model families
- Theoretical analysis validated through empirical comparison with "Pos Only" variant that masks negative gradients entirely

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLD in GRPO extends the misalignment phenomenon previously observed in Direct Preference Optimization methods
> - Penalizing shared reasoning steps or correct tokens within incorrect responses directly causes likelihood displacement
> - Selective token penalization based on gradient contribution proves more effective than uniform or random approaches
> - Group relative optimization structure enables targeted intervention without requiring fundamental algorithmic changes
