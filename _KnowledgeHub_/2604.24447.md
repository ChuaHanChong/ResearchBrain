# Characterizing Vision-Language-Action Models across XPUs: Constraints and Acceleration for On-Robot Deployment

> [!summary] Summary
> A systematic evaluation of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models on diverse heterogeneous accelerators revealed distinct computational bottlenecks, leading to the development of DP-Cache and V-AEFusion, which achieved up to 6x inference speedup on edge NPUs and 2.9x on GPUs with minimal impact on task success rates. This provides practical guidelines for efficient on-robot deployment.

## Problem

- VLA models show promise for generalist robot control but face challenges for real-time, on-robot deployment due to computational demands, tight cost, and energy budgets.
- Existing VLA model evaluations predominantly use high-end desktop GPUs, overlooking the performance and trade-offs on resource-constrained edge accelerators suitable for mobile robots.
- A lack of systematic understanding of VLA model performance across heterogeneous hardware hinders informed hardware selection and optimization for practical, efficient robotic control.

## Method

- Conducted a systematic model-hardware co-characterization of representative VLA pipelines across a diverse range of heterogeneous accelerators (GPUs, NPUs, **XPUs**).
- Developed a multi-dimensional leaderboard to quantify model-hardware pair performance based on Cost, Energy, and Time (CET), alongside VRAM capacity and control frequency constraints.
- Proposed two training-free latency optimization strategies: DP-Cache to reduce redundancy in iterative diffusion and V-AEFusion for VLM-Action Expert pipeline parallelism.

## Results

- The multi-dimensional CET leaderboard identified 'right-sized' hardware, showing that AGX Orin or Ascend 310P could be optimal for cost-energy priority depending on the VLA model.
- DP-Cache achieved up to **2.09x** speedup for Diffusion Policy on RTX 4090 (with a **3.6%** average success rate drop) and a **6.0x** speedup when combined with compilation on Ascend 310P.
- V-AEFusion provided a **1.3x** speedup for the π0 model on RTX 4090, with a minimal **0.3%** average success rate drop in simulation and **4%** on real-robot pick-and-place tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLA inference exhibits a distinct dual-phase computational imbalance: the VLM backbone is compute-bound, while the iterative Action Expert is memory-bound, leading to severe resource underutilization.
> - Optimal hardware selection varies significantly based on deployment priorities (e.g., Cost-Energy vs. All-Round), demonstrating that 'right-sized' edge accelerators can offer better performance-to-cost operating points than flagship desktop GPUs.
> - Effective VLA acceleration requires composing phase-aware, orthogonal optimizations with a global view, as the efficacy of individual methods varies considerably across different hardware platforms.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have significantly influenced the field of robotics, leading to the development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models aim to enable robots to translate natural language instructions into physical behaviors by integrating perception, language understanding, and action generation capabilities. While VLA models hold promise for generalist robot control, their practical deployment on physical robots faces substantial challenges.

Existing research in VLA models often focuses on algorithmic development and performance evaluation using high-end, desktop-grade GPUs (e.g., NVIDIA RTX 4090). This approach, while facilitating rapid prototyping, overlooks critical deployment constraints such as real-time inference requirements, acquisition cost, and energy consumption, which are paramount for mobile, low-cost, and humanoid robots. The current landscape therefore lacks a systematic understanding of how VLA models perform on heterogeneous edge accelerators, including embedded GPUs, NPUs, and other XPUs, which are more suitable for on-robot deployment due to their constrained power and cost budgets.

Furthermore, existing acceleration efforts for VLA models typically target specific models or high-end platforms, providing limited generalizable insights for practitioners. This paper positions itself within this gap by providing a systematic, cross-accelerator analysis of VLA model deployment. It aims to bridge the disconnect between theoretical model capabilities on powerful hardware and practical real-world deployment on resource-constrained platforms, thereby informing hardware selection and guiding optimization strategies for efficient, economical, and real-time robotic control. The work contributes to the growing need for system-level understanding and hardware-aware optimization in the domain of robotic foundation models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the disparity between the demonstrated capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and the practical challenges associated with their on-robot deployment. While VLA models offer potential for generalist robot control, their real-time inference requirements are often bottlenecked by computational demands, especially when considering the tight cost and energy budgets characteristic of mobile and low-cost robotic platforms. Most prior evaluations rely on powerful desktop GPUs, which obscures the trade-offs and opportunities presented by heterogeneous edge accelerators.

Against this backdrop, the research articulates several key objectives:

1. **Systematic Model-Hardware Co-characterization:** To conduct a comprehensive analysis of VLA deployment by evaluating representative VLA pipelines across a diverse range of heterogeneous accelerators (GPUs, XPUs, NPUs). This objective aims to understand the interplay between VLA model architectures and various hardware platforms.

2. **Development of a Cross-Accelerator Leaderboard:** To establish a multi-dimensional leaderboard that quantifies the performance of model-hardware pairs based on three critical metrics: Cost, Energy, and Time (CET). The motivation here is to move beyond simple latency metrics and provide a holistic view that includes practical deployment considerations, enabling practitioners to identify "right-sized" compute platforms that meet real-time control rates under specified cost and energy constraints.

3. **In-depth Profiling of VLA Inference Bottlenecks:** To uncover the underlying computational characteristics and bottlenecks of VLA inference. The motivation is to move beyond superficial performance metrics by employing fine-grained profiling and Roofline analysis to identify how different phases of VLA models (e.g., VLM backbone, Action Expert) utilize hardware resources and which resources (compute or memory bandwidth) constrain their performance. This detailed understanding is crucial for designing effective optimization strategies.

4. **Development of Latency Optimization Strategies:** To propose and evaluate novel latency optimization methods that are specifically tailored to the identified computational structure and bottlenecks of VLA models. The motivation is to develop practical, training-free acceleration techniques that can significantly improve end-to-end inference speed on various hardware platforms while maintaining task success rates. This includes strategies for redundancy reduction and asynchronous pipeline parallelism.

In essence, the research is motivated by the need to make VLA models viable for real-world robotic applications by systematically addressing the practical hardware and efficiency challenges that limit their deployment beyond laboratory settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a systematic approach involving model-hardware co-characterization, detailed profiling, and the development of targeted optimization techniques.

#### 3.1. Model-Hardware Co-Characterization and Leaderboard Construction

1. **Model and Hardware Selection:**
 - **VLA Models (Consumers):** Models were categorized into "Small Consumers" (e.g., ACT, Diffusion Policy, SmolVLA) and "Big Consumers" (e.g., Gr00t, π0, π0.5, OpenVLA), using a 1-billion-parameter threshold. These models represent different architectural paradigms, including autoregressive and diffusion-based policies, and hierarchical designs.
 - **Hardware Platforms (Producers):** Platforms were classified into "Basic Producers" (e.g., 11th Gen Intel i7-11700 CPU, Ascend 310B), "Standard Producers" (e.g., Intel B60 Pro, AGX Orin, Ascend 310P), and "Ultra Producers" (e.g., Jetson Thor, RTX 4090) based on their theoretical peak FP16/BF16 computing capability (FLOPS). This selection includes diverse architectures from NVIDIA, Intel, and Huawei.

2. **Leaderboard Metrics and Constraints:**
 - The leaderboard evaluates end-to-end inference latency.
 - It augments latency with acquisition cost and energy consumption, summarized as CET (Cost, Energy, Time).
 - Energy consumption is measured using a physical power meter during inference.
 - Model capability is reflected by the average success rate across four LIBERO subtasks (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), which is considered hardware-independent for a given precision.
 - Hard feasibility constraints include VRAM capacity (to prevent Out-Of-Memory errors) and the required control frequency (latency gate).

#### 3.2. VLA Computational Characterization

1. **Fine-grained Profiling:** NVIDIA's Nsight Systems was used to conduct fine-grained profiling of VLA models (specifically π0) on NVIDIA platforms (RTX 4090, AGX Orin, Jetson Thor). This involved analyzing latency breakdown across the VLM backbone and Action Expert stages, along with corresponding Streaming Multiprocessor (SM) utilization.

2. **Roofline Model Analysis:** The Roofline model was applied to characterize the computational properties (compute-bound vs. memory-bound) of the VLM backbone and Action Expert phases. This involved calculating the operational intensity (FLOPs/Byte) for key layers and comparing it against the memory bandwidth and peak FLOPs of the hardware platforms to identify the limiting factor.

#### 3.3. Latency Optimization Development

1. **Fusion of Orthogonal Acceleration Techniques (OpenVLA):**
 - Evaluated existing methods: 4-bit quantization (Dettmers et al., 2023), Cache-VLA (Xu et al., 2025) for prefill redundancy reduction, and Spec-VLA (Wang et al., 2025a) for speculative decoding during the iterative decode phase.
 - Tested combinations of these techniques to assess their synergistic effects on speedup and task success rate.

2. **Model Compilation:**
 - Leveraged `torch.compile` for Just-In-Time (JIT) compilation on NVIDIA GPUs to fuse PyTorch operations into optimized kernels.
 - Utilized the Ascend Tensor Compiler (ATC) on Ascend NPUs to compile the full workflow into a static computation graph for offline inference optimization.

3. **DP-Cache (Diffusion Policy Cache):**
 - Inspired by redundancy reduction in video generation, this method targets the iterative nature of diffusion-based Action Experts.
 - Identified "stable segments" within the diffusion process where the relative L1 distance between consecutive steps is low.
 - Proposed caching and broadcasting computed results within these stable segments to skip redundant intermediate calculations, reducing the total number of denoising steps.
 - The optimal number of cache steps (hyperparameter `S`) was determined.
 - Evaluated on Diffusion Policy, π0 (in simulation), and π0.5 -droid (on a physical Franka arm).

4. **V-AEFusion (VLM-Action Expert Pipeline Parallelism):**
 - Addressed the computational imbalance between the VLM backbone and the Action Expert.
 - Exploited temporal coherence of observations in a closed-loop control setting.
 - Implemented a pipeline parallelism strategy where the VLM processes the current observation while the Action Expert concurrently begins early denoising steps using a "stale" KV cache from the previous timestep. The Action Expert then switches to the "fresh" KV cache from the current VLM output for later, more precise denoising steps.
 - Justified by the high temporal coherence of robotic observations and the coarse-to-fine nature of diffusion generation.
 - An ablation study was conducted to determine the optimal number of "stale steps" (denoising iterations using previous features).
 - Evaluated on π0 on a JAKA S5 dual-arm robot for manipulation tasks and LIBERO simulation.

These methodologies collectively allowed for a deep understanding of VLA performance on diverse hardware and the development of targeted, effective acceleration strategies.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding VLA deployment, computational characteristics, and optimization effectiveness across heterogeneous hardware.

#### 4.1. Leaderboard and Multi-dimensional Analysis (CET)

- **Finding #1: Model size is not a definitive predictor of inference frequency.** Counter-intuitively, models with fewer parameters, such as the Small Consumer (Diffusion Policy), exhibited lower inference frequencies than larger Big Consumers (e.g., π0). This was attributed to the iterative nature of the diffusion action expert, where the Diffusion Policy required 100 denoising steps compared to π0 's 4 steps.
- **Finding #2: Optimal hardware selection varies across VLA models and metrics.** A multi-dimensional analysis incorporating Cost, Energy, and Time (CET) revealed that the flagship RTX 4090 is not always the optimal choice.
 - For "Cost-Energy Priority," the AGX Orin was optimal for SmolVLA, while the Ascend 310P was superior for heavier workloads like Gr00t and π0.
 - For an "All-Round" CET priority, optimal choices diverged: Jetson Thor for SmolVLA, Ascend 310P for Gr00t, and RTX 4090 for π0. This demonstrated that "right-sized" edge accelerators could offer better performance-to-cost operating points.
- **Deployment Guideline:** A three-step selection process was distilled from the leaderboard: (i) select the target model, (ii) apply VRAM and control frequency feasibility filters, and (iii) sort remaining options by the priority metric (latency, cost, or energy). This turns model-hardware pairing into a reproducible procedure, validated with real-world deployment on a Franka arm using π0.5.

#### 4.2. VLA Computational Characterization

- **Finding #3: VLA workflow exhibits a distinct dual-phase computational imbalance, leading to severe resource underutilization.** Profiling revealed a consistent two-phase inference pattern: a VLM backbone phase characterized by high SM utilization (typically >90%) and an Action Expert phase with significantly lower SM utilization (20-40%). Despite lower utilization, the Action Expert often dominated overall latency (e.g., 2× that of the VLM for π0), indicating inefficient resource usage during this phase.
- **Finding #4: VLA robotics inverts traditional generation bottlenecks: the VLM backbone is compute-bound, while the Action Expert is memory-bound.**
 - **VLM Backbone (Compute-Bound):** The VLM backbone, acting as a feature extractor in a single forward pass, showed high operational intensity (e.g., ~840 FLOPs/Byte for π0 's DecoderLayer on RTX 4090), placing it in the compute-bound regime.
 - **Action Expert (Memory-Bound):** The Action Expert, despite using relatively lightweight heads, exhibited low SM utilization and was characterized as memory-bound (e.g., 64.5 FLOPs/Byte on RTX 4090), which falls significantly below the hardware's ridge point. This memory bandwidth bottleneck was exacerbated by its iterative nature (10-100 steps in diffusion policies) and was universal across tested platforms.

#### 4.3. Acceleration Methods

- **Fusion of Orthogonal Acceleration (OpenVLA):** Combining Speculative and Cache methods yielded the highest speedup (1.29×) for OpenVLA, but incurred a 6.6% decrease in average success rate. Quantization (4-bit) alone provided 1.14× speedup but reduced success rate and undermined speculative acceleration due to precision loss.
- **Model Compilation:** JIT compilation (`torch.compile` or ATC) demonstrated varied effectiveness across hardware. For π0, it achieved 2.90× speedup on RTX 4090 and 2.34× on Ascend 310P, but only 1.51× on Jetson Thor.
- **DP-Cache (Diffusion Policy Cache):**
 - For Diffusion Policy on RTX 4090, DP-Cache (S=8) achieved up to 2.09× speedup with a 3.6% average success rate (SR) drop.
 - On a physical Franka robot with π0.5 -droid, DP-Cache (S=4) yielded a 1.28× speedup with a 6% SR drop.
 - Combined with ATC compilation, DP-Cache achieved a 6.0× speedup for Diffusion Policy on the Ascend 310P.
- **V-AEFusion (VLM-Action Expert Pipeline Parallelism):**
 - For π0 on RTX 4090, V-AEFusion achieved a 1.3× speedup with only a 0.3% average SR drop in LIBERO simulation and a 4% drop on real-robot pick-and-place tasks. An ablation study showed optimal performance with 5 stale denoising steps.
- **Finding #5: Phase-aware, orthogonal optimizations must be composed with a global view.** Effective VLA acceleration requires joint optimization of different phases and levers, necessitating a unified framework for integration.
- **Finding #6: Cross-hardware Impact: The same optimization method exhibits varying performance across platforms.** V-AEFusion's speedup varied from 1.32× on RTX 4090 to 1.0× on Ascend 310P. This variation was attributed to computational resource contention on platforms with limited compute capacity, where the combined demands of parallelized VLM and Action Expert could exceed hardware limits.

In summary, the research characterized VLA systems by a two-fold serialization: pipeline-level (VLM to Action Expert) and component-level (iterative diffusion). This informed the development of DP-Cache and V-AEFusion, which provided significant, training-free speedups across diverse hardware.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic foundation models by addressing the practical challenges of deploying Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models on real robots under stringent resource constraints. The findings and methodologies presented have several significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Enabling Practical On-Robot Deployment:** By systematically evaluating VLA models across heterogeneous edge accelerators (GPUs, XPUs, NPUs) and considering cost, energy, and latency, this work provides a crucial framework for informed hardware selection. The proposed "right-sized" hardware recommendations and the interactive leaderboard directly assist practitioners in deploying VLA models on robots that are constrained by budgets and power, moving VLA models from laboratory settings to real-world applications. This can accelerate the adoption of generalist robot control in various industries.

2. **Deeper Understanding of VLA Computational Bottlenecks:** The in-depth profiling and Roofline analysis uncover previously uncharacterized computational imbalances within VLA inference. Identifying the VLM backbone as compute-bound and the Action Expert as memory-bound, particularly the "inverted" bottlenecks compared to traditional VLM/diffusion generation, provides fundamental insights. This understanding is critical for guiding future architectural designs of VLA models and for developing more efficient hardware tailored to their specific computational demands.

3. **Development of Targeted Optimization Strategies:** The introduction of DP-Cache and V-AEFusion offers effective, training-free acceleration methods directly addressing the identified bottlenecks. DP-Cache tackles the redundancy in iterative diffusion, while V-AEFusion mitigates pipeline-level serialization through asynchronous parallelism. These methods demonstrate substantial speedups (up to 2.9× on GPUs and 6× on edge NPUs) with minimal degradation in task capability, providing immediate practical benefits for improving the real-time performance of VLA models.

4. **Guidance for Future Research and Hardware Design:** The findings highlight the need for a multifaceted approach to VLA optimization that considers both algorithmic aspects and hardware-aware designs. The observation that optimization efficacy varies across different hardware platforms emphasizes the importance of co-design principles for future VLA architectures and specialized accelerators. The paper also points to bandwidth-oriented optimization and parallelism-centric designs as pivotal directions for addressing scalability challenges as VLA models and their Action Experts grow in complexity.

5. **Creation of a Reproducible and Expandable Resource:** The development of a cross-accelerator VLA-XPU Leaderboard and its open-source nature (indicated by the example website) fosters transparency and reproducibility. This resource can serve as a living benchmark, allowing the community to add new models and hardware, thereby continually enriching the collective understanding of VLA deployment performance.

In conclusion, this research moves beyond theoretical performance metrics to provide a practical, system-level understanding of VLA model deployment. By identifying bottlenecks and offering tailored solutions, it significantly contributes to making VLA models more viable, efficient, and accessible for real-world robotic applications, thereby influencing both software development and hardware design in the field of embodied artificial intelligence.
