# MARIO: MAth Reasoning with code Interpreter Output -- A Reproducible Pipeline

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Alibaba Group developed MARIO, a reproducible pipeline that enhances Large Language Models' mathematical reasoning through a hybrid approach combining textual analysis with code execution. This framework achieves state-of-the-art results on challenging math benchmarks like MATH and improves solution accuracy by integrating an Outcome Value Model for verification.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle with precise mathematical reasoning, often due to their probabilistic nature which is ill-suited for exact calculations.
- Existing tool-augmented LLMs, while good at computation, sometimes lack common sense or contextual understanding for math word problems, leading to illogical answers.
- There is a need for high-quality, reproducible, and cost-effective protocols for fine-tuning math-specific LLMs and curating suitable datasets.

## Method

- A reproducible pipeline for mathematical reasoning, MARIO, constructs a rich dataset by combining human-reviewed solutions from GSM8K/MATH with self-trained data and MetaMath augmentation, re-formatted into an ==HTML-like structure==.
- A ==three-stage fine-tuning protocol== is used: starting with a math-oriented foundation model (Llemma), followed by Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on correct solutions, and then multi-task LoRA fine-tuning to train an ==Outcome Value Model (OVM)== for solution evaluation.
- During inference, the SFT LLM generates multiple candidate solutions, and the OVM re-ranks them using an ==outlier-free selection algorithm== to identify the most accurate answer.

## Results

- MARIO-7B achieved a **47.0%** accuracy on the MATH dataset, establishing a new state-of-the-art for 7B-parameter models, and showed strong generalization on challenging out-of-domain datasets (OCWCourses: **21.7%**, GaoKao2023-Math-En: **38.2%**).
- The outlier-free OVM selection algorithm improved MARIO-7B's GSM8K accuracy by **+12.8%** (to **82.9%**) and MATH accuracy by **+12.1%** (to **59.1%**) over its base SFT performance.
- Converting training data to an HTML-like format resulted in a **20%** reduction in training loss during initial iterations compared to a key-value (REACT) format.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating textual analysis with code execution enhances mathematical reasoning by providing common sense checks and problem decomposition, leading to more robust solutions than purely code-centric methods.
> - Re-formatting training data to an HTML-like structure can significantly reduce initial training loss, suggesting better alignment with LLM pre-training corpora.
> - An Outcome Value Model (OVM) trained with a multi-task approach and an outlier-free selection algorithm effectively improves solution accuracy by re-ranking generated outputs, outperforming simple majority voting.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models (LLMs) has witnessed remarkable progress in natural language understanding and generation. However, a persistent challenge remains their inherent limitations in precise mathematical reasoning, which often demands exact calculations and symbolic manipulation beyond probabilistic next-token prediction. This paper positions itself at the forefront of addressing this gap by integrating LLMs with external tools, specifically code interpreters.

Historically, the advancement of LLM reasoning capabilities has seen several key paradigms:
- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Prompting:** Introduced by Wei et al. (2022), CoT prompting empirically showed that generating intermediate reasoning steps can significantly enhance LLMs' performance on complex tasks, including mathematical problems. This technique effectively "teaches" the LLM to "think step-by-step." Proprietary models like GPT-4 and Claude-2 widely adopt CoT.
- **Tool-Augmentation (Program-of-Thought/Program-Aided Language):** Recognizing LLMs' weakness in arithmetic, researchers began augmenting them with external computational tools. Seminal works like Program-of-Thought (PoT) by Chen et al. (2022) and Program-Aided Language (PAL) by Gao et al. (2023) pioneered generating code snippets for precise numerical computations. This approach leverages the LLM's language generation ability to write code, which is then executed by an interpreter, providing exact results.
- **Hybrid Approaches:** More recent efforts, such as MathCoder (Wang et al., 2023) and ToRA (Gou et al., 2023), further refined tool augmentation by attempting to blend natural language reasoning with code generation. ToRA, in particular, set new benchmarks by generating predominantly code-centric solutions.

MARIO builds upon these foundations but critically addresses a perceived shortcoming of purely code-centric solutions: the potential to overlook common sense or real-world constraints inherent in math word problems. The authors argue that while code provides precision, it can lack the contextual understanding crucial for certain problems (e.g., quantities cannot be negative, or people cannot be fractional). Their proposed solution integrates "text analyses and code snippets," aiming for a more robust and human-like reasoning process that combines logical reasoning with precise computation. This hybrid approach differentiates MARIO from models that disproportionately rely on code snippets.

Furthermore, the paper highlights the challenge of reproducibility and cost-effectiveness in developing high-performing math-specific LLMs, particularly concerning data annotation. By providing a "tentative, easily replicable protocol" and publicly releasing their resources, MARIO aims to lower the barrier for entry and accelerate research in this specialized domain. The paper also delves into the critical aspect of solution verification, moving beyond simple outcome supervision to more sophisticated multi-task learning for an "outcome value model" (OVM), which assists in selecting the best solutions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the MARIO project are multifaceted:

1. **Bridge the Gap in Mathematical Reasoning for LLMs:** The core motivation stems from the recognition that despite significant advancements in natural language understanding, LLMs still exhibit "shortcomings in mathematical reasoning capabilities," particularly concerning tasks requiring exact calculations. The authors postulate that the fundamental probabilistic nature of LLM training (predicting the next token) inherently struggles with the deterministic demands of mathematics.
2. **Enrich the Data Landscape for Math Reasoning:** To overcome these inherent limitations, the authors aim to create a novel, high-quality math dataset. This dataset is designed to teach LLMs to effectively utilize a Python code interpreter for precise computations while simultaneously incorporating natural language reasoning for problem decomposition and common sense checks.
3. **Develop a Reproducible Fine-Tuning Protocol:** A key objective is to propose and implement an "easily replicable protocol for the fine-tuning of math-specific LLMs." This addresses the practical challenges of reproducing complex LLM training setups, fostering transparency, and enabling wider community participation and advancement.
4. **Integrate Textual Analysis with Code Execution:** The motivation here is to develop a more robust and "common sense" approach to mathematical problem-solving. Unlike purely code-centric solutions that might produce logically inconsistent answers (e.g., negative quantities), MARIO aims to combine the precision of code execution with the contextual understanding and logical reasoning provided by textual analysis.
5. **Enhance Solution Verification:** The authors recognize the difficulty in evaluating the reasoning process itself, beyond just the final answer. They aim to incorporate an "outcome value model (OVM)" to assist in verifying and selecting the most accurate solutions, thereby improving the overall quality of generated outputs, especially when dealing with multiple sampled solutions.

In essence, MARIO seeks to advance the state-of-the-art in mathematical reasoning by providing a comprehensive, reproducible framework that encompasses data generation, model fine-tuning, and robust inference, specifically emphasizing the symbiotic relationship between natural language understanding and precise computational tools.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MARIO project proposes a multi-stage, reproducible pipeline for enhancing LLMs' mathematical reasoning capabilities, focusing on data creation, model fine-tuning, and inference.

#### 3.1. Dataset Construction (MARIO Dataset)

The core of MARIO's approach lies in its meticulously curated dataset, which integrates text analysis and code snippets. The process involves:

- **Foundation Datasets:** The dataset is primarily derived from two well-known math reasoning benchmarks:
 - **GSM8K (Grade School Math 8K):** For this relatively straightforward dataset, GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 are initially prompted (with REACT instructions and demonstrations) to generate solutions incorporating code snippets. A temperature of 0 (greedy decoding) is used for initial attempts, followed by 0.6 for more diversity if needed. Crucially, any remaining unsolved questions or discrepancies (approximately 100) are subjected to **manual human review and correction** of both LLM-generated and original answers, ensuring 100% coverage with correct solutions.
 - **MATH Dataset:** Given its higher difficulty, only GPT-4 is used, and more code snippets (up to 8) are allowed. Since manual annotation for all remaining unsolved problems (28.2%) would be prohibitive, human verification is limited to "easily rectifiable errors." For the more challenging, unsolved problems, a **self-training and knowledge distillation** approach is employed: an initial teacher model (Llemma-34B fine-tuned on the combined GSM8K and partially human-verified MATH data) generates up to 100 samples per unsolved question, from which up to 4 correct solutions are identified. This boosts coverage to 93.8%.
- **Data Augmentation:** To expand scale and variety, approximately 240K novel questions from **MetaMath** (transformations of GSM8K/MATH problems) are incorporated. This provides a rich set of both positive (correct) and negative (incorrect) examples, especially valuable for training the outcome value model (OVM).
- **Re-formatting to HTML:** Crucially, the raw data generated using the REACT template (key-value pairs like "Thought:", "Action:") is **re-formatted into an HTML-like structure** (e.g., `<p>text analysis</p>`, `<code>code snippet</code>`). The authors hypothesize this bridges the gap between the pre-training corpus (which likely contains HTML) and the fine-tuning corpus, leading to lower initial training loss.

#### 3.2. Fine-Tuning Protocol

The fine-tuning pipeline is divided into three stages, building upon a suitable foundational LLM:

- **Foundational Model Selection:** The authors choose **Llemma** (a math-oriented continual pre-training variant of Llama-2) as their base model. This choice is motivated by Llemma's proficiency in both mathematical and coding domains and its confirmed lack of overfitting on GSM8K/MATH.
- **Stage 1: Continual Pre-training (CPT):** This initial stage involves pre-training on a comprehensive corpus covering both mathematical and coding domains (represented by the selection of Llemma).
- **Stage 2: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with Full Parameters:** The entire set of LLM parameters is fine-tuned using the MARIO dataset of *correct solutions*. The model optimizes a cross-entropy loss by masking out question tokens, focusing solely on learning to generate correct solutions.
- **Stage 3: Multi-Task Fine-Tuning with LoRA for Outcome Value Model (OVM):** This stage trains the LLM to discern the validity of a solution by predicting whether the final answer is accurate. A light-weighted binary classifier (linear layer with sigmoid activation) is added alongside the token prediction layer. This is performed using **LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation)**, an efficient fine-tuning method. The model is trained on a mixture of both correct and incorrect solutions (from MetaMath augmentation). This multi-task setting enables the OVM to both generate solutions and evaluate their correctness, maintaining generative capabilities with computational efficiency.

#### 3.3. Inference Strategy

Both the SFT LLM and the LoRA-tuned OVM are capable of generating solutions. The OVM plays a crucial role in enhancing the quality of solutions generated by the SFT LLM, particularly through a re-ranking mechanism:

- **Solution Generation:** The SFT LLM generates multiple candidate solutions (e.g., K=20).
- **Outlier-Free OVM Selection:** Instead of simple majority voting, the OVM is used to evaluate the likelihood of correctness for each sampled solution. An "outlier-free" algorithm is proposed: among groups of sampled solutions yielding the same final answer, the solution with the highest OVM score is selected. If all sampled solutions are unique, the single solution with the highest OVM score is chosen. This helps filter out plausible-looking but incorrect solutions.

The maximum number of code snippets allowed during inference is consistent with training (5 for GSM8K, 8 for MATH and out-of-domain datasets).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The MARIO project demonstrates significant advancements in mathematical reasoning capabilities of LLMs, particularly for challenging problems and out-of-domain datasets.

#### 4.1. Performance of SFT Model

- **Overall Improvement:** MARIO-7B achieved a new state-of-the-art benchmark for 7B-parameter LLMs on the MATH dataset (47.0%) and showed notable generalization ability on challenging out-of-domain (OOD) datasets like OCWCourses (21.7%) and GaoKao2023-Math-En (38.2%). This performance often surpassed proprietary models like Claude-2 and PaLM-2 (in terms of specific size comparisons) and significantly outperformed other open-source models of similar scale.
- **Strength in Complex Problems:** While the MARIO-34B teacher model (fine-tuned on 26.9K dataset) didn't always match the GSM8K performance of some concurrent models like MathCoder or ToRA, it consistently outperformed them on more complex MATH problems and especially on challenging OOD problems. This suggests that MARIO's "text analysis" capability, which breaks down problems into manageable code snippets, is more beneficial for problems requiring deeper logical reasoning beyond simple arithmetic. The 7B model, trained on an larger 82K dataset, replicated this pattern.

#### 4.2. Effectiveness of OVM Model

- **Superior to Majority Voting:** The outlier-free OVM selection algorithm significantly enhanced results compared to traditional majority voting. For MARIO-7B, using OVM selection improved GSM8K accuracy by +12.8% (from 70.1% to 82.9%) and MATH by +12.1% (from 47.0% to 59.1%) over its base SFT performance. Similar substantial gains were observed on OOD datasets. This underscores the OVM's effectiveness in identifying the best solution among multiple samples.
- **Dual Role of OVM:** The OVM, designed for multi-task learning, demonstrated comparable proficiency in generating solutions directly, though with a slight reduction in effectiveness on OOD datasets. This is expected, as the OVM was continually fine-tuned on MetaMath questions derived from in-domain datasets. The ability to both generate and evaluate solutions with a single LLM offers significant practical benefits.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Impact of Data Sources:** Incremental addition of training examples, particularly human-reviewed data and MetaMath augmentation, showed more pronounced advancements on the MATH dataset compared to GSM8K. This is attributed to MATH's higher difficulty, lower initial GPT coverage, reliance on exact matches, and the teacher model's focus on multi-step reasoning.
- **CPT Model and Data Format:**
 - **CPT Model:** Fine-tuning from a superior Continual Pre-training (CPT) model (DeepSeek-MATH-7B compared to Llemma-7B) significantly enhanced math reasoning capabilities, as expected.
 - **Data Format (HTML vs. REACT):** Converting the REACT template data to an HTML-like format resulted in a substantial 20% reduction in training loss during initial iterations. While this only translated to a marginal 1% improvement on the MATH test set after 3 epochs, the lower initial loss suggests potential for further performance gains with careful hyper-parameter tuning, indicating that HTML-like formatting might better bridge the gap between pre-training and fine-tuning corpora.

#### 4.4. Critique of GSM-Hard Dataset

The authors present a strong argument against the use of the GSM-Hard dataset as a reliable benchmark for MATH LLMs. They highlight instances where:
- **Unreasonable Questions:** Modifications to numbers in GSM-Hard can lead to questions that defy common sense (e.g., negative quantities of objects, fractional people).
- **Purely Code-Centric Flaws:** PoT/PAL models often generate code without verifying the sensibility of output, leading to physically impossible intermediate or final answers.
- **Incorrect Ground Truths:** Some GSM-Hard solutions are identical to the original GSM8K despite numerical changes, or are simply incorrect.

MARIO's approach, with its embedded textual analysis and self-verification, explicitly avoids producing illogical answers, which can result in lower reported accuracy on GSM-Hard but reflects a more "intelligent" understanding of real-world constraints. This is a critical insight for the community regarding dataset quality.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MARIO project makes several significant contributions to the field of AI, particularly in the domain of mathematical reasoning with LLMs:

1. **Advancing Mathematical Reasoning in LLMs:** By achieving state-of-the-art results on challenging math benchmarks (MATH, OCWCourses, GaoKao2023-Math-En) with relatively smaller models (7B and 34B parameters), MARIO demonstrates the efficacy of its hybrid text-analysis and code-interpreter approach. This pushes the boundaries of what open-source LLMs can achieve in complex quantitative tasks.
2. **Enhancing Robustness and Common Sense:** The explicit integration of textual analysis alongside code execution allows MARIO to incorporate common sense reasoning, preventing illogical outputs that purely code-centric approaches might generate. This makes the models more reliable and aligned with human understanding of problem constraints.
3. **Promoting Reproducibility and Open Science:** The commitment to an "easily replicable pipeline" and the public release of source code and model checkpoints are crucial for accelerating research. This transparency enables other researchers to build upon MARIO's work, validate findings, and foster a more collaborative and efficient research environment.
4. **Efficient Data Curation Strategies:** The paper provides a practical blueprint for creating high-quality, tool-augmented datasets, combining human review for critical corrections with self-training and knowledge distillation for scalability. The re-formatting to HTML, while seemingly minor, represents an important empirical finding regarding training efficiency and initialization performance.
5. **Effective Solution Verification (OVM):** The development of an outcome value model (OVM) that can both generate and evaluate solutions, coupled with an outlier-free selection algorithm, offers a computationally efficient method for improving answer accuracy. This is particularly impactful for applications where high precision is paramount and generating multiple samples is feasible.
6. **Critical Dataset Evaluation:** The insightful critique of the GSM-Hard dataset highlights a broader issue in AI benchmarking—the quality and real-world applicability of evaluation datasets. This encourages the community to scrutinize existing benchmarks and develop more robust and sensible evaluation criteria for mathematical reasoning.

In summary, MARIO provides a holistic, open-source framework that not only improves LLM performance on mathematical reasoning but also offers valuable lessons on data engineering, fine-tuning strategies, and the importance of integrating common sense with computational precision. Its contributions are poised to facilitate further research and development towards truly intelligent and robust mathematical problem-solving AI systems.
