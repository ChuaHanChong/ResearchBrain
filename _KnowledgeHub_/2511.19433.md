# Mixture of Horizons in Action Chunking

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Renmin University of China, The Chinese University of Hong Kong, and the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill developed the Mixture of Horizons (MoH) strategy for Vision-Language-Action models. MoH integrates multiple action chunk horizons, improving task success rates by up to 3.0% on long-horizon LIBERO tasks and demonstrating adaptive execution with up to 2.5x higher throughput in simulation and real-world robotic manipulation.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models relying on 'action chunking' exhibit sensitivity to the chosen length of the action chunk, referred to as the 'horizon'.
- A fixed action chunk horizon forces a trade-off: longer horizons provide better global foresight but compromise fine-grained local control, while shorter horizons offer precision but lack long-term planning.
- This inherent trade-off limits the generalization capabilities and adaptability of VLA models across diverse robotic manipulation tasks.

## Method

- ==The Mixture== of Horizons (MoH) strategy integrates multiple action chunk horizons within a single VLA policy to concurrently leverage both long-term foresight and short-term precision.
- A shared action transformer processes truncated ground-truth action chunks for different horizons in parallel, with predictions fused via a lightweight gated mixture network and optimized with a balance loss to ensure diverse horizon utilization.
- A dynamic inference mechanism uses cross-horizon consensus to adaptively determine the number of actions to execute at each step, allowing for self-truncating executable chunks for improved stability and efficiency.

## Results

- MoH consistently improved various VLA baselines across LIBERO, RoboTwin2.0, and RoboCasa benchmarks, achieving a new state-of-the-art average success rate of **99%** on LIBERO with the π0.5 policy.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the benefits of MoH stem from fusing diverse temporal structures, not generic ensemble effects, and the balance loss is crucial for preventing horizon collapse.
- The dynamic inference scheme achieved up to **2.5** times higher throughput (executed actions per predicted chunk) while maintaining or surpassing baseline performance, demonstrating adaptive and efficient control in simulation and real-world robotic tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The fixed-horizon trade-off is a fundamental limitation in VLA models, where optimal chunk length varies significantly with task type and temporal demands.
> - Fusing predictions from diverse temporal horizons within a single model effectively mitigates the trade-off, enabling the policy to simultaneously achieve long-term planning and short-term precision.
> - Adaptive action execution based on the consensus among multiple horizons allows for dynamic adjustment of control granularity, enhancing execution stability and inference efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent an advancing area in artificial intelligence, focusing on enabling robots to interpret human instructions and execute complex tasks within physical environments. These models typically integrate large-scale pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) as foundational backbones, extending their capabilities with an action module for embodied control. A common strategy employed in modern VLAs is "action chunking," where the model predicts a sequence of future actions, or an "action chunk," rather than a single next action, conditioned on current observations and linguistic instructions. This approach allows policies to expose temporal structure and support high-frequency control.

Despite the effectiveness of action chunking, the research landscape has identified a critical challenge: the model's performance is sensitive to the chosen length of the action chunk, referred to as the "horizon." Existing VLA models generally adopt a fixed horizon, leading to a recognized trade-off: longer horizons may improve global foresight and planning for long-term tasks, but can compromise fine-grained local control and immediate motor precision. Conversely, shorter horizons facilitate precise control but may lack the necessary long-term foresight for complex, multi-stage tasks. This trade-off inherently limits a model's generalization capabilities and adaptability in dynamic environments.

Prior works, such as ACT (Zhao et al., 2023) and CogACT (Li et al., 2024), have popularized action chunking, making chunked prediction with full-attention transformers a standard component in many VLA policies. However, the selection of an optimal chunk length has largely remained heuristic, with observations suggesting that different horizons are preferable for different task types (Shukor et al., 2025; Li et al., 2024). The current work addresses this specific limitation by proposing a strategy to mitigate this fundamental horizon trade-off, thereby aiming to improve the robustness, generalization, and efficiency of VLA models across diverse robotic manipulation tasks. It seeks to move beyond fixed-horizon policies toward a more flexible and adaptive control paradigm.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from an observed inherent trade-off in VLA models employing action chunking: the selection of a fixed action chunk length, or "horizon," necessitates a compromise between long-term foresight and short-term precision. Experimental observations (Figure 1 in the paper) using a baseline VLA model (π0) on the LIBERO benchmark demonstrated that longer horizons enhance performance on long-horizon tasks, implying improved long-term planning, while shorter horizons facilitate precise control, leading to higher success rates on short-horizon tasks. A single, fixed horizon thus imposes a bottleneck on generalization and flexibility.

Given this foundational challenge, the key objectives of this work are as follows:

1. **Systematic Study of Horizon Effects:** To conduct an empirical investigation into how the choice of action chunk length (horizon) influences VLA model performance, specifically articulating the trade-off between long-term planning capabilities and fine-grained short-term control accuracy. This involves evaluating different fixed horizons across a range of tasks with varying temporal demands.
2. **Development of a Multi-Horizon Integration Strategy:** To design and implement a novel strategy that integrates multiple action horizons within a single VLA model. The goal is for this strategy to concurrently leverage the benefits of long-term foresight (from longer horizons) and short-term precision (from shorter horizons), thereby mitigating the identified trade-off.
3. **Ensuring Plug-and-Play Compatibility and Efficiency:** To ensure that the proposed multi-horizon strategy, termed Mixture of Horizons (MoH), is readily integrable into existing full-attention action modules of VLA policies, regardless of their underlying action prediction mechanism (e.g., flow-based or one-step regression). Furthermore, a critical objective is to achieve this with minimal additional computational overhead during both training and inference, preserving the practical applicability of VLA models.
4. **Enabling Dynamic and Adaptive Inference:** To develop a dynamic inference mechanism that leverages the multi-horizon predictions to adaptively determine the number of actions to execute at each step. This scheme aims to enhance execution stability and inference-time efficiency by committing actions only when there is sufficient cross-horizon consensus, allowing for self-truncating executable chunks.

These objectives collectively aim to enhance the robustness, adaptability, and performance of VLA models for complex robotic manipulation by addressing a fundamental architectural decision in action chunking.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces a Mixture of Horizons (MoH) strategy designed to alleviate the trade-off inherent in fixed-horizon action chunking by integrating multiple horizons within a single VLA policy.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

The paper first contextualizes MoH within existing VLA model paradigms.
- **VLA Models with Action Chunking:** These models observe multi-view inputs, optional history, language instructions, and proprioceptive states to predict an action chunk $A_t = (a_{t,1}, \dots, a_{t,H})$ of length $H$. Action chunking reduces policy calls and enables planning over an extended horizon. Modern VLAs typically use a pre-trained VLM backbone for context encoding, followed by a compact, full-attention action transformer for chunk prediction, which is non-causal.
- **Flow-Matching Policies:** These policies learn a velocity field $v_\theta$ that transports a Gaussian noise chunk $\epsilon$ to a target action chunk $A_t$. The reference path is a linear interpolation $A^{(\tau)}_t = (1 - \tau)\epsilon + \tau A_t$, with ground-truth velocity $u(\epsilon, A_t) = A_t - \epsilon$. The policy is trained to minimize the $L_{fm}$ loss between its predicted velocity and the ground-truth velocity. During inference, an ODE solver integrates $v_\theta$ to yield the final action chunk.
- **One-Step Policies:** These policies directly map the context to the final action chunk in a single forward pass, $\hat{A}_t = g_\theta(\dots)$. They can be implemented either as discretized classification (predicting bin indices with $L_{cls}$ loss) or continuous regression (predicting continuous actions with $L_{reg}$ loss). Both flow-matching and one-step policies can be built on the same full-attention action transformer, with minor adjustments like time embedding for flow-matching or a learnable query token for one-step prediction.

#### 3.2. Mixture of Horizons (MoH) Strategy

The MoH strategy aims to fuse multiple horizons to leverage both long-term foresight and short-term precision.
- **Action Chunk Rearrangement:** A maximum horizon $H$ is fixed, and a set of candidate horizons $\mathcal{H} = \{h_1, \dots, h_N\}$ is defined, where $h_1 < \dots < h_N = H$. For each ground-truth action chunk $A_t = (a_{t,1}, \dots, a_{t,H})$, truncated chunks $A^{(h)}_t = (a_{t,1}, \dots, a_{t,h})$ are constructed for each $h \in \mathcal{H}$. For computational efficiency, each $A^{(h)}_t$ is padded to length $H$, and a horizon-specific attention mask is applied to invalidate positions beyond $h$. This allows a shared action transformer to process all horizons in parallel within a single forward pass, minimizing computational overhead.
- **Gated Mixture:** The shared action transformer produces hidden states $Z^{(h)}_t$ for each horizon. An action head then converts these into horizon-specific predictions $\hat{A}^{(h)}_t$. A lightweight linear gating layer (with approximately 2k parameters) acts as a gating head, generating logits $g_{t,k,h}$ for each step $k$ and horizon $h$. For each step $k$, only horizons $h$ where $k \le h$ are considered valid. The logits are normalized using a softmax-like function to produce attention weights $\alpha_{t,k,h}$. The final fused prediction at step $k$, $\hat{a}_{t,k}$, is then computed as a weighted sum of the horizon-specific predictions: $\hat{a}_{t,k} = \sum_{h \in \mathcal{H}: k \le h} \alpha_{t,k,h} \hat{a}^{(h)}_{t,k}$. This design is plug-and-play for any full-attention action transformer.
- **Balance Loss for Horizon Utilization ($L_{bal}$):** To prevent the gating network from collapsing to a few preferred horizons, a balance loss is introduced. This loss, inspired by mixture-of-experts load-balancing, encourages balanced utilization of all active horizons. It computes the mean squared coefficient of variation (CV$^2$) of the average usage $\bar{\alpha}^{(i)}_h$ for each horizon $h$ within specific temporal intervals (defined by horizon boundaries). Minimizing $L_{bal}$ ensures that all horizons contribute effectively to the final prediction.
- **Training Objective:** The total training objective $L$ is a combination of three loss components: $L_{mix}$ (loss on the fused predictions), $L_{ind} = \sum_{h \in \mathcal{H}} L^{(h)}$ (sum of losses on individual horizon-specific predictions), and $L_{bal}$. The equation is $L = L_{mix} + \lambda_{ind} L_{ind} + \lambda_{bal} L_{bal}$, with $\lambda_{ind}$ and $\lambda_{bal}$ as empirically set weighting coefficients.

#### 3.3. Dynamic Inference via Horizon Consensus

MoH naturally enables a dynamic inference scheme.
- **Cross-Horizon Consensus:** At each timestep, the model outputs horizon-wise predictions $\{ \hat{a}_k \}_{k=1}^H$ along with their mixture $\hat{a}$. Each horizon is treated as a "voter." The $\ell_1$ disagreement $\bar{d}_k$ between the fused action $\hat{a}$ and each horizon's prediction $\hat{a}_k$, weighted by the gating weights $\alpha$, is calculated.
- **Self-Truncating Executable Chunk:** A data-dependent threshold `thres` is established from the mean disagreement of the first `n` steps. The policy then executes the longest action prefix whose disagreement remains below this threshold, while ensuring a minimum number of active horizons `m`. This mechanism allows for the execution of a "self-truncating executable chunk" where only actions with consistent support across horizons are committed, and uncertain actions are deferred to the next replanning iteration. This is intended to improve execution stability and efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the Mixture of Horizons (MoH) strategy involved extensive experimentation across multiple simulation environments and real-world robotic tasks, employing both flow-matching and one-step regression policies as baselines.

#### 4.1. Horizon Trade-off Confirmation

The initial study using the $\pi_0$ baseline on the LIBERO benchmark confirmed a fundamental trade-off: longer horizons (e.g., H=30) yielded better performance on long-horizon tasks, while shorter horizons (e.g., H=10) resulted in higher success rates on short-horizon tasks (Figure 1, Table 7 for $\pi_{0.5}$). This sensitivity highlighted the limitations of a fixed, single horizon.

#### 4.2. Simulation Experiments

MoH was evaluated on LIBERO, RoboTwin2.0, and RoboCasa benchmarks.
- **LIBERO Benchmark:** MoH consistently improved all tested baselines (regression-based $\pi_{reg}$ and flow-matching-based $\pi_0$, $\pi_{0.5}$, StarVLA). For instance, $\pi_{reg}$ with MoH improved average success rate by 1.2% to 96.4%, and $\pi_0$ with MoH by 1.3% to 95.1%. Notably, $\pi_{0.5}$ with MoH achieved an average success rate of 99%, establishing a new state-of-the-art on LIBERO, with particular gains on long-horizon tasks (e.g., +3.0% for $\pi_{0.5}$ on Long suite). This demonstrated MoH's ability to jointly exploit long-term foresight and short-term precision (Table 1).
- **RoboTwin2.0 Benchmark:** On this bimanual benchmark, $\pi_0$ equipped with MoH achieved the highest average success rate and consistently improved over the base $\pi_0$ across most tasks, including both easy and hard (domain-randomized) settings (Figure 4). This indicated that MoH not only accelerated in-distribution convergence but also enhanced robustness and generalization to challenging task configurations.
- **RoboCasa Benchmark:** MoH consistently improved the GR00T baseline across all five selected tasks, yielding an average gain of 3.4% (Table 2). This indicated that MoH's benefits extended to less saturated environments and distinct VLA backbones, demonstrating broad generalizability.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

A series of ablation studies investigated the impact of MoH's components:
- **Effect of Horizon Density:** Varying the stride `d` to construct candidate horizons showed that even three horizons (d=10) improved performance. A moderately dense set (d=3, 10 horizons) achieved optimal overall performance (99.0% for $\pi_{0.5}$), particularly on long-horizon tasks. Increasing density beyond this did not yield further significant gains (Table 3).
- **Effect of Horizon Diversity:** Replacing diverse horizons with 10 identical horizons (all H=30) yielded only marginal improvement, failing to alleviate the long-horizon trade-off. This confirmed that MoH's benefits originate from fusing *diverse* temporal structures rather than a generic ensemble effect (Table 4).
- **Effect of Loss Reweighting:** Applying analogous temporal loss weights to a single-horizon policy, without MoH, improved short-term tasks but *degraded* long-horizon performance, intensifying the trade-off. This indicated that MoH's improvements are not solely due to implicit loss reweighting (Table 4).
- **Simple Average Fusion:** Replacing the gated fusion with naive average fusion still yielded modest improvements, supporting the core concept of multi-horizon integration, with the gating network further enhancing performance (Table 4).
- **Effect of Gating Balance Loss ($L_{bal}$):** Without $L_{bal}$, the gating network tended to favor longer horizons. $L_{bal}$ effectively suppressed this bias, ensuring balanced utilization of all horizons, demonstrating its importance for robust learning of gated fusion (Table 4, Figure 11).

#### 4.4. Dynamic Inference via Horizon Consensus

The proposed dynamic inference scheme consistently outperformed fixed-length prefix execution strategies. It achieved up to 2.5 times higher throughput (average action steps executed per predicted chunk) while preserving or even surpassing the baseline's performance (Figure 6). Qualitatively, it enabled adaptive control, selecting shorter executable chunks near decision points and during fine-grained manipulation, and longer chunks during smooth, low-risk motions (Figure 5, Figure 10).

#### 4.5. Comparison with Temporal Ensemble Strategy

Comparing MoH with Adaptive Temporal Ensemble (ATE) revealed that ATE alone provided marginal gains on short-term tasks but consistently degraded long-horizon performance for $\pi_0$ and $\pi_{0.5}$. This suggested that ATE's smoothing effect could introduce drift for already precise policies. In contrast, MoH improved all four task suites robustly, indicating an enhancement in underlying temporal modeling. Applying ATE on top of MoH provided no additional benefit and slightly harmed long-horizon performance (Table 5).

#### 4.6. Real-world Experiments

Real-robot experiments on a 7-DoF manipulator demonstrated consistent gains across four diverse tasks (two short-horizon, one long-horizon, one fine-grained deformable manipulation task) for both $\pi_0$ and $\pi_{0.5}$ baselines (Figure 7b). MoH led to more decisive motions (T1), accurate grasps and quicker completion (T3), and improved success rates on deformable manipulation (T4, 75% to 90% for $\pi_{0.5}$). It also alleviated observed overfitting issues in $\pi_{0.5}$ on a pouring task (T2). These results confirmed MoH's effectiveness in practical robotic manipulation scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of embodied AI and robotic control by addressing a fundamental limitation in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: the inherent trade-off induced by a single, fixed action chunk horizon. The proposed Mixture of Horizons (MoH) strategy offers a practical and effective solution to this challenge.

The significance of this work can be summarized as follows:
- **Mitigation of the Horizon Trade-off:** MoH effectively resolves the dilemma between achieving long-term foresight and maintaining short-term precision, a trade-off previously identified as a bottleneck for VLA models. By simultaneously leveraging information from multiple temporal scales, MoH enables policies to exhibit both strategic planning and accurate execution within a single framework.
- **Broad Applicability and Efficiency:** MoH is designed as a plug-and-play module that can be seamlessly integrated into any full-attention action transformer, regardless of whether the underlying policy is flow-matching-based or one-step regression-based. Its minimal computational overhead during both training and inference (e.g., ~1.4% increase in training overhead for a sparse configuration, negligible inference latency impact) ensures its practical viability for diverse VLA architectures.
- **Enhanced Performance and Generalization:** The consistent and substantial performance gains demonstrated across multiple challenging simulation benchmarks (LIBERO, RoboTwin2.0, RoboCasa) and real-world robotic tasks highlight MoH's ability to improve task success rates and enhance generalization. Achieving a new state-of-the-art on LIBERO with $\pi_{0.5}$ signifies its capacity to push performance boundaries.
- **Dynamic and Adaptive Control:** The introduction of a dynamic inference scheme via cross-horizon consensus provides a novel mechanism for adaptive closed-loop control. This allows robots to dynamically adjust their execution granularity based on context-dependent confidence, leading to more stable execution and improved inference-time efficiency (e.g., 2.5x higher throughput while maintaining performance). This adaptive behavior is crucial for real-world scenarios where tasks involve varying levels of complexity and risk.
- **Pathway Towards Scalable Foundational Models:** By providing a principal method to create more robust VLA models, MoH offers a pathway towards developing more scalable foundational action models. The insights gained from managing temporal granularity can inform the design of future agentic AI systems, world models, and interactive perception systems, benefiting a broader range of applications in the embodied AI community.

In conclusion, this paper presents a well-motivated, empirically validated, and broadly applicable strategy that significantly advances the capabilities of VLA models in robotic manipulation, offering both performance improvements and a conceptual framework for adaptive temporal control.
