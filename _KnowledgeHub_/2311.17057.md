# ReMoS: 3D Motion-Conditioned Reaction Synthesis for Two-Person Interactions

> [!summary] Summary
> ReMoS is a denoising diffusion model that synthesizes a reactor's full-body and finger motion in two-person interactions, conditioned only on the actor's 3D motion with no text or action labels. A cascaded body-then-hand design uses combined spatio-temporal cross-attention, hand-interaction-aware attention, a distance-weighted reaction loss, and inference-time spatial guidance, reaching state-of-the-art MPJPE and FID on pair-dancing, Ninjutsu, acrobatics and kickboxing.

## Problem

- Existing two-person motion synthesis relies on action labels or text prompts, which are coarse, costly to annotate for fast contact-heavy motion, and awkward for non-experts.
- Prior reactive methods need seed poses of both characters or extra conditioning signals, so they cannot derive synchronized reactions purely from one person's continuous 3D motion.
- Available two-person interaction datasets are small, weakly interactive, and lack finger-level capture, so hand contacts crucial for interaction realism cannot be modeled.

## Method

- Cascaded conditional ==DDPM==: a body stage denoises reactor body joints given actor body motion, then a hand stage conditions on the generated body plus binary hand-interaction masks. This is **ReMoS**.
- **CoST-XA**, a combined spatio-temporal cross-attention forming an NJ x NJ actor-reactor joint attention matrix, plus **H-XA** masking queries and keys to active interacting hands.
- Training uses a ==distance-weighted reaction loss== with exponential decay plus kinematic regularizers, and inference adds a ==spatial guidance== gradient aligning actor and reactor arm joints.

## Results

- On the new ReMoCap Lindy Hop split ReMoS reaches **40.7 mm** MPJPE and **0.12** body FID versus **62.6 mm** and **0.30** for the strongest baseline InterGen.
- Ablations show roughly **20%** MPJPE and **40%** body-FID gain from CoST-XA, and about **50%** hand-FID gain from cascading plus the reaction loss.
- ReMoS also leads on ExPI (**97.9 mm**) and 2C (**59.1 mm**), and **40** users rate its reaction plausibility **3.88/5** versus **3.19** for InterGen.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fine-grained inter-person synchronization can be learned implicitly from one partner's motion alone, so label or text conditioning is not required for reactive synthesis.
> - Attending jointly over space and time beats stacking separate spatial and temporal attention, which loses fine-grained cross-person dependencies when no annotations are available.
> - Motions at different scales benefit from cascaded generation with contact-derived masks, but iterative diffusion sampling stays slow, near 24 s per 50 frames.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 3D human motion synthesis has seen rapid advancements, particularly with the rise of denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs). Existing literature extensively covers single-person motion generation, ranging from text or music-conditioned synthesis to facial expressions, gestures, and human-scene interactions. These methods have achieved high-quality animations for individual digital characters.

However, a significant technological gap persists in effectively addressing the complex dynamics of multi-human interactions. Synthesizing plausible and synchronized motions for two or more interacting people, especially in contact-heavy scenarios, remains a considerable challenge. Previous approaches for multi-person interaction have explored various techniques, including motion graphs, interaction patches, inverse kinematics, GAN-based models, and transformer networks. While these methods have demonstrated some success in generating plausible motions, they often suffer from several limitations:
- **Reliance on Explicit Annotations:** Many approaches require high-level semantic inputs such as action labels (e.g., "high-five," "salsa") or detailed text prompts to guide the motion synthesis. This necessitates cumbersome data collection and does not capture the fine-grained, continuous synchronization often required in dynamic interactions.
- **Limited Scope:** Some methods focus on sparse interactions or specific types of actions, struggling with the diversity and complexity of real-world human-human physical contact.
- **Lack of Fine-Grained Detail:** Crucially, most existing multi-person datasets and synthesis techniques do not adequately address finger-level articulations, which are vital for realism in contact-based interactions (e.g., holding hands in dance, grappling in martial arts).

Recent developments in diffusion models have shown immense potential in generative tasks for single-person motion, and a few concurrent works have begun to apply them to multi-person scenarios. However, even these diffusion-based multi-person methods often retain the dependency on text descriptions or action labels for conditionality.

ReMoS directly addresses this critical gap by focusing on *motion-conditioned reaction synthesis* for two-person interactions without requiring any explicit semantic labels or text prompts. It innovatively leverages the strengths of denoising diffusion models to learn implicit dependencies directly from the actor's 3D motion. Furthermore, ReMoS explicitly tackles the challenge of generating both full-body and fine-grained hand articulations, a feature largely absent in prior work. By contributing the ReMoCap dataset, which includes detailed finger motions in complex scenarios, the authors also provide a crucial resource for future research in this under-explored area. This work pushes the frontier of generative AI for animation, moving beyond isolated characters to realistically modeling intricate social and physical interplay.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of ReMoS is to develop a robust and flexible system for synthesizing realistic, temporally-aligned reactive motions of a second person (the "reactor") given the continuous 3D motion of a first person (the "actor") in a two-person interaction scenario.

This core objective is driven by several key motivations and aims to address specific challenges:
- **Automating Reactive Character Animation:** Animators often face significant overhead in creating synchronized motions for interacting characters. ReMoS aims to alleviate this by allowing animators to define one character's actions and automatically generate plausible, meaningful reactions for the other. This streamlines animation pipelines and fosters creative freedom.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Methods:**
 - **Annotation Dependency:** Current generative methods for multi-person motion often rely on high-level annotations (text prompts, action labels) which are difficult to collect accurately for complex, contact-heavy motions and may not capture fine-grained synchronization. ReMoS aims for an *annotation-free* approach, deriving reactions directly from the actor's 3D motion.
 - **High Dimensionality and Plausibility:** Synthesizing two-person interactions significantly increases the complexity and dimensionality of the motion generation problem. The system must generate diverse yet plausible reactions that adhere to the narrow manifold of realistic human interactions without explicit guidance.
 - **Integrated Full-Body and Hand Motion:** Realism in close interactions, especially contact-based ones, critically depends on accurate hand movements. Modeling both broad full-body movements and intricate finger articulations at vastly different scales, and ensuring their coordination, is a significant technical challenge.
- **Enabling New Applications:** The ability to generate high-quality reactive motions opens doors for numerous applications:
 - **Commercial and Entertainment Media:** More realistic and dynamic character interactions in films, games, and virtual experiences.
 - **Interactive Mixed/Augmented Reality:** Creating more believable virtual companions or opponents that react naturally to user movements.
 - **Social Robotics:** Developing robots capable of generating contextually appropriate reactive behaviors in human-robot interaction.
- **Data Contribution:** Recognizing the scarcity of high-quality datasets for complex, contact-based two-person interactions with finger-level detail, a secondary but crucial objective is to contribute the ReMoCap dataset. This dataset features challenging scenarios like Lindy Hop dancing and Ninjutsu, providing rich data for further research in this domain.

In essence, ReMoS seeks to advance the state-of-the-art in 3D human motion synthesis by providing a robust, data-driven framework for generating nuanced and realistic reactive motions in multi-person contexts, with a specific focus on implicit conditionality and fine-grained detail.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ReMoS employs a novel denoising diffusion-based probabilistic model (DDPM) framework with a cascaded, two-stage generation strategy, specifically tailored for 3D motion-conditioned reaction synthesis in two-person interactions.

#### 3.1. DDPM for Reactive Motion Synthesis

The core of ReMoS is a conditional DDPM. The model learns to reverse a forward diffusion process that progressively adds Gaussian noise to a clean reactive motion sequence (X) over 'T' steps. The goal is to learn the conditional probability distribution P(X|Y), where X is the reactor's motion and Y is the actor's motion. During inference, the model iteratively denoises a noisy sample, conditioned on the actor's motion Y, to generate a plausible reactive motion X.

#### 3.2. ReMoS Framework - Cascaded Generation Strategy

The key innovation lies in a cascaded approach to generate the reactor's full-body joints (X_B) and then its hand joints (X_H) separately (Fig. 3). This strategy acknowledges the different scales and complexities of body and hand articulations.

- **Stage 1: Body Synthesis Module:**
 - This module denoises the reactor's body motion (X_B) conditioned on the actor's body motion (Y_B).
 - It utilizes a transformer decoder block, a common architecture in diffusion models.
 - **Combined Spatio-Temporal Cross-Attention (CoST-XA):** A crucial novel component. Unlike prior methods that often apply spatial and temporal attention sequentially, CoST-XA combines these features. It generates an attention matrix that simultaneously learns inter-dependencies between *each pair of reactor and actor body joints across all frames*. This ensures highly synchronized and fine-grained reactions, particularly vital in the absence of explicit semantic labels.

- **Stage 2: Hands Synthesis Module:**
 - This module denoises the reactor's hand joints (X_H) conditioned on the actor's hand motions (Y_H) *and* the previously synthesized full-body motions (X_B) of the reactor. This cascading ensures that hand movements are coherent with the overall body posture.
 - **Hand-Interaction-Aware Cross-Attention (H-XA):** To encourage the synthesis of localized interaction features, H-XA introduces binary hand-interaction masks (1_H_A, 1_H_R). These masks are dynamically calculated by thresholding the distances between the actor's and reactor's wrist joints to the nearest body joints of the other person. By element-wise multiplying these masks with the query and key values in the cross-attention mechanism, the network is explicitly guided to focus its attention on the hands that are actually interacting, making the generated hand motions more realistic and context-aware.

#### 3.3. Loss Functions and Training Details

ReMoS is trained using a weighted sum of three loss terms:
- **Reconstruction Loss (L_c):** A standard L2 distance between the ground truth and synthesized motion, driving the basic learning.
- **Reaction Loss (L_r):** A novel loss designed to ensure accurate timing and spatial positioning of the reactor relative to the actor. It minimizes deviations in Euclidean distances between corresponding actor-reactor joints, but critically, it uses an *exponentially decaying distance-aware weight*. This weighting mechanism (exp(-d(x,y))) focuses the loss more heavily on joints that are closer and thus more actively interacting, mitigating issues with distant, less relevant joints.
- **Kinematic Loss (L_k):** This term enforces kinematic plausibility, comprising:
 - Joint velocity and acceleration losses (L_vel, L_acc) for temporal smoothness.
 - Bone length consistency loss (L_bone) to maintain skeleton integrity.
 - Foot sliding loss (L_foot) to prevent unrealistic foot-ground interactions.

#### 3.4. Inference-Time Spatial Guidance

To further enhance spatial alignment, particularly for fast, contact-heavy interactions that can sometimes lead to misalignment in the arm joints, ReMoS incorporates an inference-time guidance function. This function (G) provides gradients to nudge the sampling process. It minimizes the distance between the arm joints of the actor and the synthesized reactor, using the hand-interaction masks to focus on the relevant interacting arms. This fine-tuning step improves spatial coherence without altering the learned model weights.

#### 3.5. ReMoCap Dataset Contribution

To support this research and provide a benchmark for future work, the authors introduce the ReMoCap dataset. This dataset is designed for complex two-person interactions, featuring:
- **Diverse Scenarios:** Lindy Hop dancing (fast-paced, swing-style) and Ninjutsu (martial arts techniques), encompassing a wide range of interaction intensities, speeds, and styles.
- **High-Quality Motion Capture:** Markerless multi-view motion capture (69 body joints, 93 DoF) with full-body and *finger-level articulations*, a critical addition for realistic contact.
- **Rich Data:** Approximately 275.7K frames (2.04 hours) of motion data, including multi-view RGB videos, foreground-background masks, and 3D surface reconstructions. This addresses the scarcity of datasets with fine-grained hand interactions.

This comprehensive methodology, combining a cascaded DDPM architecture with novel attention mechanisms, carefully designed loss functions, inference-time guidance, and a new high-quality dataset, forms a robust framework for advanced reactive motion synthesis.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ReMoS demonstrates its superior performance and effectiveness across multiple quantitative metrics, qualitative visualizations, and user studies, establishing it as a state-of-the-art solution for 3D motion-conditioned reaction synthesis.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Evaluation

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** ReMoS consistently outperforms all baselines (InterFormer, MixNMatch, ComMDM, RAIG, InterGen) across diverse datasets, including the newly introduced ReMoCap (Lindy Hop and Ninjutsu), ExPI, and 2C datasets.
- **Accuracy Metrics (MPJPE, MPJVE):** ReMoS achieves significantly lower Mean Per-Joint Positional Error (MPJPE) and Mean Per-Joint Velocity Error (MPJVE), indicating greater spatial and temporal accuracy of the synthesized motions compared to ground truth. For instance, on Lindy Hop, ReMoS records an MPJPE of 40.7 mm, substantially lower than the closest baseline (ComMDM at 59.4 mm). Similar improvements are observed for Ninjutsu (139.2 mm vs. 172.6 mm for InterGen).
- **Distributional Fidelity (FID):** The Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) for both body and hand motions shows a remarkable reduction. For Lindy Hop, ReMoS achieves an FID (body) of 0.12 and FID (hands) of 0.26, a significant improvement over baselines, with the closest baseline (ComMDM) showing 0.32 (body) and 0.53 (hands). This indicates that the distributions of generated motions are much closer to the ground truth.
- **Diversity:** Diffusion-based methods generally exhibit higher diversity scores compared to transformer or GAN-based approaches, and ReMoS maintains competitive diversity, indicating its ability to generate varied yet plausible reactive motions.
- **Ablation Studies Insights:**
 - **Cascaded Strategy & Reaction Loss:** The ablation studies highlight the critical impact of the cascaded generation strategy and the distance-aware reaction loss, contributing to an almost 50% improvement in FID scores for the reactor's hand motion.
 - **CoST-XA:** The Combined Spatio-Temporal Cross-Attention mechanism demonstrated substantial benefits, leading to over 20% improvement in MPJPE and approximately 40% improvement in FID for the reactor’s body motion, underscoring its role in learning fine-grained inter-person dependencies.
 - **Inference-Time Spatial Guidance:** While subtle, the spatial guidance function consistently improves MPJPE and MPJVE, indicating its effectiveness in fine-tuning spatial alignment, particularly for arm joints in dynamic interactions.
- **Efficiency:** ReMoS boasts a relatively efficient model size, with 17.4M trainable parameters, which is fewer than some diffusion-based baselines like RAIG (81.2M) and InterGen (170M), demonstrating a good balance between performance and computational footprint.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

- Visualizations (e.g., Fig. 1, 5a) clearly demonstrate ReMoS's ability to synthesize highly plausible, synchronized, and realistic reactive motions. The generated hand interactions are notably detailed and accurate, crucial for contact-based scenarios like pair dancing or martial arts.
- The reactor's synthesized motion shows superior alignment and naturalness with the actor's motion compared to the outputs of baseline methods.

#### 4.3. User Study

- A user study involving 40 participants evaluated the perceived "Motion Quality" and "Reaction Plausibility" on a five-point Likert scale.
- ReMoS received significantly higher mean scores (3.79 for Motion Quality, 3.88 for Reaction Plausibility) compared to all baselines. Its scores were approximately 78% of the maximum possible, trailing only the ground truth motions (~96%).
- This strong preference from human evaluators validates that ReMoS generates motions that are not only quantitatively accurate but also visually natural and believable as reactive interactions.

#### 4.4. Applications

- ReMoS successfully demonstrated its utility as an interactive motion editing tool. It can perform:
 - **Pose Completion with Controlled Joints:** Animators can manually specify the motion for certain joints (e.g., a hand's wrist joint), and ReMoS automatically synthesizes the rest of the body to complete the pose plausibly.
 - **Motion In-Betweening:** Given start and end keyframes of a reactive motion, ReMoS can generate the smooth and realistic intermediate frames, a highly valuable feature for animators.

In summary, ReMoS delivers state-of-the-art results in generating complex, motion-conditioned reactive 3D human motions, including fine-grained hand interactions. Its performance is validated through rigorous quantitative analysis, compelling qualitative results, and positive user perception, underscoring its robustness and practical utility.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ReMoS research represents a significant advancement in the field of 3D human motion synthesis, particularly for multi-person interactions. Its impact extends across several dimensions:

- **Addressing a Critical Gap:** The work directly tackles the long-standing challenge of synthesizing complex, reactive human-human interactions without relying on explicit, high-level semantic annotations. This moves the field closer to truly *motion-conditioned* generative capabilities, reflecting the continuous and implicit nature of human interactions.
- **Novel Methodological Contributions:** The introduction of the cascaded diffusion framework, the Combined Spatio-Temporal Cross-Attention (CoST-XA) for body synthesis, and the Hand-Interaction-Aware Cross-Attention (H-XA) with dynamically calculated masks for hand synthesis are key innovations. These mechanisms effectively capture both the broad coherence of body movements and the intricate details of contact-based hand interactions, a capability largely absent in prior work. The distance-aware reaction loss further refines the realism of interactions.
- **High-Quality Dataset for Future Research:** The ReMoCap dataset is a valuable and timely contribution. By providing full-body and *finger-level* motion capture data for complex, contact-heavy scenarios like Lindy Hop and Ninjutsu, it offers a rich resource that can catalyze further research in multi-person interaction, especially for fine-grained interaction modeling and physically accurate contact analysis.
- **Enhanced Realism and Immersion:** The ability to generate highly plausible and synchronized reactive motions, including detailed finger articulations, significantly elevates the realism of digital human interactions. This has direct implications for creating more immersive and believable experiences in virtual reality, augmented reality, and various forms of digital media.
- **Practical Applications for Creative Industries:** ReMoS has immediate practical utility for animators, game developers, and creative artists. By automatically generating reactive motions conditioned on a leading character's movement, it can dramatically reduce the manual effort and time required for animating complex scenes. The demonstrated motion editing capabilities, such as pose completion and motion in-betweening, offer powerful tools for interactive content creation, functioning as intelligent generative assistants.
- **Advancing Social Robotics and HCI:** The framework’s capacity to generate realistic reactive behaviors could be instrumental in developing more socially intelligent robots and improving human-robot interaction. Robots that can respond with natural and contextually appropriate physical reactions to human movements would enhance fluidity and trust in collaborative tasks.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work lays a strong foundation for future exploration. The authors point towards scaling the problem to multi-person motion prediction, incorporating scene-aware interactions, and improving inference speed (e.g., using techniques like Pro-DDPM or DDIM). The suggested adaptability to mesh-level inter-person contacts (by adjusting contact thresholds) also opens avenues for more precise physical simulations of skin-to-skin interactions.

In conclusion, ReMoS represents a significant step forward in synthesizing nuanced and realistic digital human interactions. Its methodological innovations and robust performance offer practical benefits for creative industries and lay crucial groundwork for more advanced, intelligent virtual agents and human-robot collaborations in the future.
