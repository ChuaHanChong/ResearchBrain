# Set-of-Mark Prompting Unleashes Extraordinary Visual Grounding in GPT-4V

> [!summary] Summary
> Set-of-Mark prompting enhances GPT-4V's fine-grained visual grounding by augmenting images with unique alphanumeric marks, enabling the model to precisely refer to and output specific image regions. This approach allows GPT-4V to achieve performance on tasks like referring segmentation that often surpasses fine-tuned specialist models in a zero-shot setting.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art large multimodal models (LMMs) like GPT-4V exhibit weak fine-grained visual grounding, struggling to precisely pinpoint or refer to specific objects or regions within an image.
- LMMs cannot easily produce accurate spatial outputs such as coordinates for specific objects or parts within an image.
- Existing visual prompting methods often struggle with referring to multiple objects or are not 'speakable' by LLMs, limiting their utility for true multimodal grounding.

## Method

- The proposed ==Set-of-Mark (SoM) Prompting== technique augments input images with unique, speakable ==alphanumeric marks== generated for semantically meaningful regions.
- Image regions are automatically partitioned using a suite of state-of-the-art open-vocabulary segmentation models, including ==MaskDINO, SEEM, and SAM==, which provide rich granularity.
- A clever mark allocation algorithm ensures unique mark placement within each region, minimizing overlaps, and these marked images are then used with either plain or interleaved text prompts to enable precise human-AI interaction.

## Results

- Set-of-Mark (SoM) prompting dramatically improves GPT-4V's performance on Referring Expression Comprehension (REC) from **25.7** ACC@0.5 to **86.4** ACC@0.5 on RefCOCOg.
- GPT-4V + SoM achieves **75.6 mIoU** for Referring Expression Segmentation (RES) on RefCOCOg, outperforming state-of-the-art fine-tuned specialist models like PolyFormer (**67.2 mIoU**) in a zero-shot setting.
- The method demonstrates strong performance across various tasks, including phrase grounding (**89.2** Recall@1 on Flickr30K) and video object segmentation (**78.8** J&F on DAVIS2017), consistently outperforming other open-source LMMs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Powerful LMMs like GPT-4V possess latent fine-grained visual grounding abilities that can be unlocked by simple, speakable visual prompting techniques.
> - Set-of-Mark bridging visual and language prompting by making visual cues 'speakable' allows for seamless, symbolic integration of visual and textual information in prompts and responses.
> - The quality of initial image partitioning using off-the-shelf segmentation models serves as a bottleneck, indicating that GPT-4V's grounding capability is even stronger when provided with more precise regions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the forefront of the rapidly evolving fields of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Large Multimodal Models (LMMs), particularly in the domain of visual grounding and human-AI interaction.

The past few years have seen unprecedented advancements in LLMs, exemplified by Generative Pre-trained Transformers (GPTs). More recently, the focus has shifted towards LMMs that can process and reason across different modalities, such as text and images. GPT-4V(ision) represents a significant leap in this direction, showcasing remarkable general perception and reasoning capabilities. However, a known limitation of even advanced LMMs like GPT-4V is their relatively weak *fine-grained visual grounding*—the ability to precisely pinpoint or refer to specific objects or regions within an image based on textual queries, or to produce spatial outputs like coordinates.

Existing approaches to enhance LMMs' visual understanding include:
- **Prompt Engineering for LLMs**: Techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and Tree-of-Thought aim to elicit better reasoning from LLMs through sophisticated textual prompts.
- **Visual Prompting**: Prior work in computer vision has explored "visual prompts" (e.g., points, boxes, strokes, or overlaid marks) to guide vision models for tasks like interactive segmentation or highlighting regions. However, these methods often struggle with referring to multiple objects or are not "speakable" by LLMs, limiting their utility for true multimodal grounding.
- **Fine-tuning LMMs**: Many open-source LMMs (e.g., LLaVA, MiniGPT-4) achieve multimodal capabilities through instruction tuning on large datasets, sometimes including fine-grained vision tasks. However, these models often lack the emergent capabilities of proprietary, larger-scale models like GPT-4V.

"Set-of-Mark Prompting" addresses the critical gap where GPT-4V excels at high-level vision-language understanding but struggles with granular spatial precision. It bridges the divide between visual prompting techniques and the language processing capabilities of LMMs by making visual marks "interpretable and speakable" to the model. By doing so, it effectively "unleashes" an inherent but previously latent visual grounding ability in GPT-4V. This work contributes to the growing understanding of how to optimally interact with and harness the full potential of powerful, pre-trained LMMs, moving beyond simple image-captioning to complex, spatially-aware reasoning. It also implicitly highlights a major challenge: the difficulty of assessing and improving proprietary models like GPT-4V due to limited API access and internal knowledge.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from an observed limitation in state-of-the-art large multimodal models (LMMs) like GPT-4V: despite their impressive general perception and reasoning abilities, they exhibit a relatively weak capacity for *fine-grained visual grounding*. This means GPT-4V struggles with tasks that require precise spatial understanding, such as identifying exact locations of multiple objects, referring to specific parts of an image, or generating accurate coordinates. The authors explicitly note, for instance, that GPT-4V "can hardly produce a sequence of accurate coordinates for a dog in the image, or a few traffic lights."

The key objectives of this study are:

1. **Unleash Fine-Grained Visual Grounding**: To develop a novel visual prompting method that can effectively unlock or enhance the precise visual grounding capabilities of LMMs, particularly GPT-4V.
2. **Improve Visual Input Modality**: To propose a new approach that focuses on augmenting the *visual input* (the image itself) rather than solely relying on text-based prompt engineering, enabling the LMM to "see location-by-location."
3. **Ensure Interpretability and "Speakability"**: To design a prompting strategy where the auxiliary visual information (marks) is both interpretable by the LMM's vision component and "speakable" by its language component, allowing the model to incorporate these marks into its textual outputs.
4. **Comprehensive Empirical Validation**: To conduct an extensive empirical study across a wide range of fine-grained vision and multimodal tasks (e.g., open-vocabulary segmentation, referring segmentation, phrase grounding, video object segmentation) to quantitatively and qualitatively validate the effectiveness of the proposed method in a zero-shot setting.
5. **Develop Evaluation Benchmarks**: To create a new suite of evaluation benchmarks tailored to assess the grounding ability of GPT-4V and other LMMs, leveraging off-the-shelf segmentation models to generate marked regions.

In essence, the authors aim to transform GPT-4V from a model that primarily understands the "what" in an image to one that also understands the "where" with high precision, enabling more interactive and task-specific applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology is **Set-of-Mark (SoM) Prompting**, a simple yet highly effective visual prompting technique designed to enhance the visual grounding capabilities of LMMs, specifically GPT-4V. The approach centers on intelligently augmenting the input image with a set of "marks" that provide spatial cues.

The methodology can be broken down into three main stages:

#### 3.1. Image Partition

Given an input image, the first step is to partition it into a set of semantically meaningful regions. This process needs to be automatic or semi-automatic to be practical. The authors achieve this by employing a suite of state-of-the-art, open-vocabulary image segmentation models:
- **MaskDINO**: Used for its strong performance in general image segmentation.
- **SEEM (Segment Everything Everywhere All at Once)**: Chosen for its open-vocabulary capabilities, allowing it to recognize objects beyond a predefined set.
- **SAM (Segment Anything Model)** and **Semantic-SAM**: Utilized for their ability to provide rich granularity, segmenting not only full objects but also parts of objects.

These tools allow users to select the desired granularity and choose between automatic or interactive segmentation modes, generating a set of binary masks, each representing a distinct region.

#### 3.2. Set-of-Mark Generation

Once the image is partitioned into regions (masks), a unique "mark" is generated for each region. Two critical factors are considered:

- **Mark Type**: The marks must be interpretable and speakable by the LMM. The primary choice is **alphanumeric characters** (numbers and alphabets) due to their compactness and GPT-4V's inherent OCR (Optical Character Recognition) capability. Additionally, **boxes** and **mask boundaries** can be used as auxiliary marks. The paper notes the importance of choosing mark types image-dependently to avoid conflicts (e.g., not using numeric marks on an image full of numbers).

- **Mark Location**: Placing marks strategically is crucial to avoid overlaps and confusion, especially in dense images. The authors propose a clever **mark allocation algorithm**:
 1. **Sort by Area**: Regions (masks) are sorted in ascending order of their areas. This prioritizes smaller regions, ensuring their marks are placed first.
 2. **Exclude Covered Regions**: For each mask `k`, any parts already covered by previously processed `k-1` masks are excluded. This helps prevent marks from overlapping or being placed in areas already assigned to other, typically larger, objects.
 3. **Distance Transform**: The modified mask is then fed to a distance transform algorithm. This finds the location *within* the mask that is maximally distant from all boundary points, effectively identifying the "center" of the remaining region. This ensures the mark is placed clearly within its designated area.
 4. **Handling Small Regions**: If a region is too small, the mark might cover most of it. In such cases, the mark is slightly moved off the region, but GPT-4V is still found to build a decent association.
Each mark generated is unique and distinguishable, ensuring proper identification by the LMM.

#### 3.3. Interleaved Prompt

With the marked image (`I_m`) prepared, it can be used with LMMs in two ways:

- **Plain Text Prompt**: Users can ask questions as usual without explicitly referencing the marks. Remarkably, GPT-4V, when provided with a SoM-augmented image, automatically grounds its answers using the visible marks (as shown in Figure 1).
- **Interleaved Text Prompt**: Users can explicitly inject the marks into their textual queries (e.g., "What is in 3?"). Since the marks are interpretable by GPT-4V, this allows for symbolic reference, enabling precise and multi-round human-AI interaction.

Crucially, the ability of SoM-prompted GPT-4V to output paired texts and marks (e.g., "The man in glasses corresponds to region labeled 2") means that for any mentioned mark in its textual response, the corresponding image region (mask) can be traced back. This enables the model to perform a variety of fine-grained vision tasks by bridging `region <-> mark <-> text description`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical study conducted by the authors reveals extraordinary improvements in GPT-4V's visual grounding capabilities when augmented with Set-of-Mark (SoM) prompting. The evaluation covers a wide range of fine-grained vision tasks in a zero-shot setting.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

The key quantitative findings from Table 2 are:

- **Dramatic Improvement for GPT-4V**: Standard GPT-4V struggles significantly with tasks requiring precise spatial output (e.g., Referring Expression Comprehension (REC) on RefCOCOg yields 25.7 ACC@0.5 when directly predicting coordinates). With SoM prompting, GPT-4V's performance on REC jumps dramatically to **86.4 ACC@0.5**. This validates the core hypothesis that SoM "unleashes" an inherent grounding ability.
- **Outperforming Specialist Models**: For **Referring Expression Segmentation (RES) on RefCOCOg**, GPT-4V + SoM achieves **75.6 mIoU**, significantly outperforming the state-of-the-art fully fine-tuned specialist model PolyFormer (67.2 mIoU). On REC, it also beats Grounding DINO (86.1) and PolyFormer (85.8), showcasing its generalist strength.
- **Strong Performance Across Diverse Tasks**:
 - **Open-Vocabulary Segmentation**: On COCO and ADE20K, GPT-4V + SoM achieves **75.7 Precision** and **63.4 Precision** respectively. While MaskDINO (a specialist model) achieves 80.7 on COCO (with GT boxes), GPT-4V's zero-shot performance is competitive, especially considering it processes a full image.
 - **Phrase Grounding (Flickr30K)**: GPT-4V + SoM achieves **89.2 Recall@1**, which is comparable to or slightly surpasses specialist models like GLIPv2 (87.7) and Grounding DINO (90.5).
 - **Video Object Segmentation (DAVIS2017)**: In a semi-supervised setting, SoM-prompted GPT-4V achieves **78.8 J&F**, outperforming other generalist vision models and showing strong cross-frame tracking ability.
- **Superior to Open-Source LMMs**: GPT-4V + SoM consistently outperforms leading open-source LMMs like LLaVA-1.5, MiniGPT-v2, Shikra, and Ferret across the evaluated vision tasks, despite some of these models being fine-tuned on task-specific data.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

- **Mark Type (Table 3)**: Combining numbers, masks, and boxes as mark types improved phrase grounding on Flickr30K from 84.4 R@1 (numbers & masks) to **89.2 R@1**, indicating that richer visual cues can further enhance performance.
- **Golden Mark Location (Table 4)**: Replacing predicted segmentation masks with ground-truth (GT) masks for SoM on RefCOCOg RES significantly boosted performance from 75.6 mIoU to **90.1 mIoU**. This highlights that the quality of the initial image partition (using off-the-shelf segmentation models) is a bottleneck, and GPT-4V's grounding capability is even stronger when provided with perfect regions.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Observations

- **"Golden" Annotations Not Always Golden**: SoM-prompted GPT-4V sometimes corrected errors or ambiguities in human-annotated ground truth datasets, suggesting its potential for data curation and cleaning.
- **Centered Marks Not Always Best**: The default mark allocation, which places marks at the geometric center of a region, sometimes led to confusion in dense or unusually shaped objects. This indicates that GPT-4V's "focus" might not always align with geometric centers, opening avenues for more sophisticated mark placement.
- **Dynamic Mark Type Selection**: The effectiveness of mark types can be context-dependent (e.g., numeric marks on an arithmetic problem image can be confusing). This points to the need for adaptive mark selection in real-world applications.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Set-of-Mark Prompting Unleashes Extraordinary Visual Grounding in GPT-4V" paper presents a highly significant contribution to the field of AI, with several far-reaching implications:

1. **Unlocking Latent Capabilities in LMMs**: The most immediate impact is the demonstration that powerful, black-box LMMs like GPT-4V possess remarkable latent fine-grained visual grounding abilities that can be "unleashed" by a simple yet clever visual prompting technique. This suggests that the full potential of such models may not be apparent through standard interaction methods, opening a new frontier for "prompt engineering" not just in text but also across modalities.

2. **Bridging Visual and Language Prompting**: SoM provides a critical link between the traditionally separate domains of visual prompting (for computer vision models) and language prompting (for LLMs). By making visual cues "speakable" (e.g., using alphanumeric IDs), SoM allows for a seamless, symbolic integration of visual and textual information in prompts and responses. This enables more precise, interactive, and grounded human-AI communication, paving the way for truly multimodal prompt engineering.

3. **Advancing Fine-Grained Vision Tasks**: SoM dramatically improves GPT-4V's performance on complex fine-grained vision tasks like referring segmentation, phrase grounding, and video object segmentation, often surpassing or competing with specialist models in a zero-shot setting. This highlights the potential of generalist LMMs, when properly prompted, to tackle tasks traditionally requiring highly specialized architectures and extensive fine-tuning. This could accelerate development in many vision-related applications.

4. **Enabling New Data Annotation Paradigms**: The ability of SoM-prompted GPT-4V to accurately ground and describe specific regions in an image holds immense potential for large-scale data annotation. As the authors note, the community struggles with the scarcity of fine-grained, semantically rich annotations. GPT-4V with SoM could potentially act as a powerful tool to automatically or semi-automatically generate such data, revolutionizing how datasets for fine-grained vision and multimodal tasks are created. It offers a scalable solution to map rich semantic knowledge to fine-grained spatial domains.

5. **Insights into LMM Architectures and Training**: The paper raises intriguing questions about *why* SoM works so effectively with GPT-4V but not with other open-source LMMs. The authors hypothesize that GPT-4V's scale and sophisticated data curation (possibly including data with labeled figures/charts) might be key. This encourages further research into the internal mechanisms of large LMMs, the impact of scale, and optimal training data strategies for achieving emergent multimodal capabilities, pushing the boundaries towards more capable and perhaps even AGI-like systems.

In conclusion, SoM prompting is a powerful yet simple innovation that significantly enhances the practical utility of LMMs like GPT-4V for tasks requiring precise visual grounding. It not only pushes the state-of-the-art in multimodal AI but also opens exciting new avenues for human-AI interaction, data generation, and fundamental research into the nature of large foundation models.
