# EndoVLA: Dual-Phase Vision-Language-Action Model for Autonomous Tracking in Endoscopy

> [!summary] Summary
> EndoVLA introduces a dual-phase Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model for autonomous tracking in endoscopic procedures using continuum robots. It achieves robust object localization and precise motion prediction for tasks like polyp tracking and circular cutting, demonstrating strong zero-shot generalization to novel environments.

## Problem

- Endoscopic procedures demand high cognitive and physical burden from endoscopists, while existing conventional robotic systems are often brittle and lack generalization.
- Current learning-based methods for medical robotics struggle with data scarcity, complex reward engineering, and poor generalization across diverse *in vivo* scenarios.
- General Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models exhibit a 'semantic gap' when applied to the specific, dynamic, and safety-critical demands of endoscopic environments.

## Method

- Develops **EndoVLA**, a ==VLA== framework built on the ==Qwen2-VL-7B== multimodal large language model, tailored for a commercial Olympus endoscope with 2-DOF control.
- Creates the ==EndoVLA-Motion== dataset comprising 6,000 image-action pairs for three core endoscopic tracking tasks: polyp tracking, abnormal region localization, and circular-marker following.
- Proposes a ==Dual-Phase Fine-tuning (DFT)== strategy that combines ==Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT)== for foundational learning with ==Reinforcement Fine-tuning (RFT)== using verifiable rewards (IoU, Motion Angle, Format) to refine performance and enhance generalization.

## Results

- The DFT model achieved dramatically superior IoU scores for bounding box prediction, reaching **86.1%** for polyp tracking and **48.5%** for circular cutting, representing up to a **340.9% improvement** over single-phase methods.
- DFT demonstrated the highest action prediction precision rates across all tasks, with **94.8%** for polyp tracking and **89.7%** for circular marker following, indicating more accurate motion commands.
- EndoVLA showed strong zero-shot generalization to novel non-endoscopic tasks, successfully tracking a complete sequence of CORL characters (**50%**) and fruit sequences (**100%**), while single-phase approaches largely failed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The Dual-Phase Fine-tuning (DFT) strategy is crucial for achieving robust object localization and precise motion prediction in endoscopic VLA, significantly outperforming single-phase SFT or RFT alone.
> - Reinforcement Fine-tuning with verifiable, objective rewards effectively refines model behavior beyond initial supervised learning, leading to improved performance and generalization capabilities.
> - Accurate and consistent object localization is a fundamental prerequisite for successful motion generation in VLA models applied to complex, dynamic robotic tasks like endoscopic tracking.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of EndoVLA is situated at the intersection of several rapidly advancing research areas: robotic surgery, vision-language-action (VLA) models, and continuum robotics.

**Robotic Surgery and Automation:** Endoscopic procedures, while gold standard for diagnosing and treating gastrointestinal conditions, are highly complex, requiring extensive surgeon training and imposing significant cognitive and physical burdens due to non-intuitive manipulation, limited visual fields, and factors like hand tremors and fatigue. The broader goal in this field is to enhance surgical precision and autonomy, reducing human error and improving patient outcomes. Previous attempts at automation often relied on conventional model-based pipelines (e.g., separate modules for detection, motion planning, control), which proved fragile, required extensive manual tuning, and lacked generalization across diverse anatomical scenes. Learning-based strategies, particularly reinforcement learning (RL) and imitation learning (IL), have emerged to address specific surgical tasks. However, these often demand vast amounts of data, intricate reward engineering, and struggle with generalization across varied scenarios or real-world domain shifts, thus limiting their practical autonomy in safety-critical *in vivo* tasks.

**Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** A more recent paradigm shift in robotics has been the advent of VLA models, often powered by Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). These models integrate visual perception, natural language understanding, and action generation within an end-to-end framework. Inspired by successes in general-purpose robotics (e.g., RT-2, VoxPoser), VLA models promise to interpret high-level surgeon prompts (e.g., "track the polyp"), adapt dynamically, and potentially require less task-specific training than traditional RL/IL. They aim to bridge the semantic gap between human intent and robotic action, offering unprecedented flexibility and generalization.

**Challenges in Endoscopic VLA:** Despite their potential, directly applying VLA models to robotic endoscopy presents unique challenges. Endoscopic environments are highly dynamic, unstructured, and variable (patient-to-patient, region-to-region), characterized by continuous tissue deformation, specular reflections, bleeding, and occlusions. MLLMs, trained on general web-scale datasets, exhibit a "semantic gap" when confronted with the specificity and criticality of *in vivo* endoscopic scenarios. Furthermore, the open-ended nature of general MLLMs can conflict with the deterministic and reliable behavior demanded in safety-critical medical interventions.

EndoVLA directly addresses these challenges by developing a customized VLA solution specifically for continuum robots in GI interventions. It attempts to overcome the limitations of both conventional and earlier learning-based approaches by leveraging the generalization capabilities of VLA while ensuring the reliability and precision required in medical settings through a novel dual-phase fine-tuning strategy and domain-specific data. This work represents a significant step towards more adaptable, intelligent, and autonomous surgical robotic assistants.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of EndoVLA is to **develop a robust, autonomous tracking system for continuum robotic endoscopes in gastrointestinal interventions that can interpret high-level surgeon prompts, reduce cognitive burden on endoscopists, and generalize across diverse and dynamic endoscopic scenes.**

The core motivations driving this research are:

- **Reducing Cognitive Burden:** Endoscopists face significant cognitive load due to the demanding nature of endoscope manipulation, which involves precise navigation, target localization, and treatment execution. Autonomous tracking capabilities for features like abnormal regions or cutting markers can free the surgeon to focus on higher-level decision-making and patient safety.
- **Overcoming Brittleness of Conventional Systems:** Traditional vision-based tracking pipelines are fragile because they rely on separate, manually tuned modules for perception, motion planning, and control. These systems struggle to generalize across variable anatomical scenes and cannot easily incorporate high-level surgical intent, leading to poor adaptability.
- **Addressing Limitations of Existing Learning-Based Methods:** While reinforcement learning and imitation learning have shown promise, they are often data-intensive, require complex reward engineering, and lack the generalization capabilities needed to handle the significant domain shifts encountered in diverse *in vivo* endoscopic environments.
- **Bridging the Semantic Gap for VLA in Endoscopy:** Vision-Language-Action models offer a promising end-to-end approach, allowing natural language prompts to guide robotic actions. However, general VLA models struggle with the unique complexities, dynamic nature, and safety-critical requirements of endoscopic environments, exhibiting a "semantic gap" between their pre-trained knowledge and domain-specific challenges.

To achieve these, EndoVLA focuses on three core tracking tasks essential for autonomous assistance:
1. **Polyp tracking:** Automatically following polyps for inspection or intervention.
2. **Delineation and following of abnormal mucosal regions:** Precisely tracking the boundaries of lesions.
3. **Adherence to circular markers during circumferential cutting:** Maintaining precise alignment with predefined markers during surgical cutting procedures.

The project is further motivated by the need to tackle **data scarcity** and **domain shifts** inherent in medical robotics research, proposing a novel dual-phase strategy to achieve robust performance and generalization with limited, domain-specific data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EndoVLA introduces a novel dual-phase Vision-Language-Action (VLA) framework for autonomous endoscopic tracking using continuum robots.

**Robotic System:** The system utilizes a commercial Olympus endoscope, modified for automatic control. It features two control knobs that provide 2-Degrees-of-Freedom (2-DOFs) movement to the end-effector. Two motors drive these knobs, allowing bending in four perpendicular directions. The system provides a real-time video feed at 30 FPS, with experiments conducted at 400x400 pixels. Motion outputs are converted to motor angles assuming a linear mapping.

**EndoVLA-Motion Dataset:** To address data scarcity for continuum robots, the authors created the EndoVLA-Motion dataset. This dataset comprises 6,000 image-action pairs across the three core tasks: polyp tracking (PP), abnormal region localization (AR), and circular-marker following (CC). Data was acquired from stomach phantoms using teleoperated control. Bounding boxes were initially annotated via YOLOv5 and then manually curated. Each bounding box was mapped to one of five discrete bending motions: upper-right, upper-left, lower-left, lower-right, or "stop" (if the target is within a 28-pixel radius of the image center). The dataset includes structured prompts combining visual scene descriptions (generated by Qwen2-VL) and explicit spatial-to-action mappings.

**EndoVLA Model Architecture:**
- **Backbone:** EndoVLA is built upon the Qwen2-VL-7B multimodal large language model.
- **Input Processing:** At each timestep, an RGB image is processed by a frozen visual encoder, and the instruction prompt by a fixed language encoder. A trainable MLP projector aligns visual features with the LLM's language embedding space.
- **Output:** The model predicts both a bounding box `[x, y, w, h]` for the target and a discrete action `A_t` (one of the five defined motions).
- **Efficient Fine-tuning:** Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is employed to efficiently fine-tune the LLM parameters while keeping most of the base model frozen.

**Dual-Phase Fine-tuning (DFT) Strategy:** This is the core novelty to achieve robust performance and generalization.
1. **Phase 1: Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** The model undergoes initial training using the EndoVLA-Motion dataset. The objective is to minimize prediction errors for both ground-truth bounding boxes and the corresponding discrete action labels. This phase establishes a foundational understanding of visual localization and appropriate motion commands.
2. **Phase 2: Reinforcement Fine-tuning (RFT) with Verifiable Rewards:** To further refine the model's performance beyond supervised learning, RFT is applied using reward signals objectively computed from the model's predictions. The Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm is used to optimize policies across different target-focus configurations.
 - **IoU Reward:** Measures the Intersection over Union between the predicted and ground-truth bounding boxes, encouraging accurate localization.
 - **Motion Angle (MA) Reward:** A binary reward that is 1.0 if the predicted action matches the ground-truth action, otherwise 0.0, directly reinforcing correct motion decisions.
 - **Format Reward:** A binary reward ensuring the model's output adheres to the required `[x, y, w, h]character` format, promoting reliable output parsing.

**Training Details:** The model was trained for a single epoch. SFT used the AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of 2e-4 for approximately 3 hours. RFT then reduced the learning rate to 1e-5 and used a batch size of 4 with a group size of 4 for 4 hours.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated EndoVLA's performance across three endoscopic tracking tasks (Polyp Tracking - PP, Abnormal Region Tracking - AR, Circular Cutting Marker Following - CC) and its generalization to non-endoscopic (general) scenes, comparing single-phase SFT, RFT, and the proposed Dual-Phase Fine-tuning (DFT).

#### 4.1. Motion Prediction with Base Model (Qwen2-VL)

- The raw Qwen2-VL base model showed limited motion prediction accuracy (20-50%).
- Crucially, providing explicit localization instructions (I_d) and especially ground-truth bounding boxes (Img_gt) significantly improved the base model's motion prediction, with improvements up to 43.2% for AR. This confirmed that accurate object localization is vital for effective motion generation.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study of Fine-tuning Strategies (Table 2)

- **Bounding Box IoU (I_b):**
 - SFT performed reasonably well for PP (62.1% IoU) but poorly for the complex CC task (11.0% IoU).
 - RFT showed minor improvements in AR (48.2%) but significantly worse performance for PP (58.0%) and CC (2.8%). This suggests RFT alone, without proper initial SFT, struggles with fundamental localization.
 - **DFT (SFT + RFT) achieved dramatically superior IoU scores:** 86.1% (PP), 79.1% (AR), and 48.5% (CC). This represents improvements of 38.6% (PP), 64.1% (AR), and a remarkable 340.9% (CC) over the best single-phase approach, demonstrating the synergy of the dual-phase strategy in enhancing localization.
- **Action Prediction Precision Rate (PR):**
 - SFT showed strong PR for PP (87.7% with I_b) but struggled with CC (57.1% with I_b).
 - RFT was less consistent, performing poorly for AR (61.3% with I_b).
 - **DFT achieved the highest PR across all tasks:** 94.8% (PP), 92.0% (AR), and 89.7% (CC) with I_b, indicating improved accuracy in selecting the correct motion command.

#### 4.3. Real Robot Evaluation (Table 3)

- **Polyp and Abnormal Region Tracking:**
 - For the simpler PP and AR tasks, DFT achieved 100% success rates in moving closer to the target (c) and significantly higher success rates in positioning the target within the focus region (r) compared to SFT or RFT alone. For example, in AR with direct action (I_a), DFT achieved 57.0% success in positioning within FR, a 185% improvement over SFT.
- **Circular Cutting Marker Following (CC):** This was the most challenging task. Only the DFT model achieved any success, completing 10.0% of the entire circle. SFT and RFT alone had 0% success.
- **Qualitative Results (Figure 3):** Show successful rollouts on real endoscopic phantoms for PP, AR, and CC, with the model accurately predicting bounding boxes and "still" actions when the target is centered.

#### 4.4. Generalization to General Scenes (Table 4 & Figure 4)

- Evaluated on non-endoscopic tasks like sequential character tracking (CORL), fruit sequence tracking, and hole tracking.
- Single-phase RFT performed very poorly (0% success in many cases), often generating excessively large bounding boxes.
- SFT showed some limited success (e.g., 90-100% for individual fruits, 30% for hole tracking) but failed for entire sequences (0% for CORL).
- **DFT demonstrated remarkable zero-shot generalization:** Achieved 50% success for the complete CORL character sequence, 100% for fruit sequence tracking, and 90% success in tracking a hole in the field.
- **Qualitative Results (Figure 4):** Visually confirm successful tracking in diverse, novel scenes, highlighting the model's ability to localize and follow targets based on abstract prompts.

In summary, the main finding is that the **dual-phase fine-tuning (DFT) strategy, combining supervised fine-tuning with reinforcement fine-tuning using verifiable rewards, is critical for achieving robust object localization, precise motion prediction, and significant zero-shot generalization capabilities** in endoscopic robotic tracking tasks, outperforming single-phase approaches across the board.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EndoVLA represents a significant advancement in the field of autonomous robotic endoscopy, offering several key contributions and potential impacts:

- **Enhanced Autonomy and Reduced Cognitive Burden:** By enabling robust autonomous tracking of critical anatomical features (polyps, lesions, cutting markers) and interpreting high-level surgeon commands, EndoVLA can substantially reduce the cognitive and physical load on endoscopists. This allows surgeons to concentrate on diagnostic interpretation, higher-level decision-making, and patient safety, potentially improving overall procedural efficiency and outcomes.
- **Improved Precision and Safety:** The demonstrated accuracy in target localization and motion prediction, especially in challenging tasks like circular cutting, suggests that EndoVLA can lead to more precise and consistent surgical manipulations. This is crucial in delicate endoscopic procedures where even small deviations can have significant clinical consequences.
- **Robustness and Generalization:** The core innovation of the Dual-Phase Fine-tuning (DFT) strategy addresses the critical challenges of data scarcity and domain shifts in medical robotics. By combining the initial learning of visual-action mappings with reinforcement-based refinement using verifiable rewards, EndoVLA achieves strong performance in endoscopic tasks and, notably, remarkable zero-shot generalization to novel, non-endoscopic environments and sequential tasks. This indicates a high level of adaptability, a key requirement for deployment in variable clinical scenarios.
- **Foundation for Future VLA in Continuum Robotics:** EndoVLA establishes a new foundation for Vision-Language-Action models tailored for continuum robots. Continuum robots, with their inherent flexibility, are ideal for navigating complex anatomical pathways, and integrating them with intelligent, language-guided control opens up possibilities for more intuitive and versatile surgical tools beyond traditional rigid-link robots.
- **Development of a Specialized Dataset:** The creation of the EndoVLA-Motion dataset is itself a valuable contribution, addressing the notorious scarcity of multimodal data for continuum robots in medical applications. This dataset will likely serve as a benchmark and resource for future research in this niche but critical area.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
While highly promising, the authors acknowledge several limitations:
- **Dataset Size and Diversity:** The EndoVLA-Motion dataset is relatively small (6k pairs) and phantom-based, lacking the full complexity, pathologies (bleeding, mucosal irregularities), and visual challenges (smoke, mucus, occlusions) of *in vivo* clinical settings. Future work must involve collecting and annotating diverse clinical videos and designing multi-step, multi-target tracking benchmarks to bridge this gap.
- **Environmental and Visual Variability:** The model's robustness in adverse visual conditions (specular reflections, tissue deformation, bleeding) remains unverified.
- **Motion Control Assumptions:** The current discrete action set and linear mapping assumption ignore complex robot kinematics, actuation delays, and varying compliance, which may limit fine-grained manipulation. Future work will explore continuous control outputs and model compression for real-time rates (≥30 Hz).
- **Inference Speed and Temporal Responsiveness:** The current 2 Hz inference speed is too slow for real-time applications (30 Hz video feed). Future work aims for faster inference and the integration of temporal context and uncertainty-aware modeling to anticipate motion, detect out-of-distribution inputs, and implement fail-safe behaviors.
- **Observed Failure Modes:** The paper reports instances of incorrect rotational direction in circular cutting, misidentification of similar targets in zero-shot tests, and a lack of recovery mechanisms when targets move out of the field of view. These highlight the need for more sophisticated control and re-localization strategies.

Despite these limitations, EndoVLA represents a significant step towards realizing truly intelligent and autonomous robotic assistants in endoscopy. Its principled approach to leveraging VLA models with domain-specific fine-tuning and verifiable rewards paves the way for a new generation of surgical robots that can understand, adapt, and operate with unprecedented precision and safety. The ongoing work outlined by the authors will be crucial in transitioning this technology from controlled phantom experiments to real-world clinical deployment, ultimately transforming endoscopic procedures.
