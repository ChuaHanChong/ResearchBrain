# Hierarchical Process Reward Models are Symbolic Vision Learners

> [!summary] Summary
> This research from Adelaide AIML and Ohio State University presents a self-supervised symbolic auto-encoder that translates diagrams into interpretable, hierarchical logic forms of geometric primitives. The system enhances diagram reconstruction, perception, and mathematical reasoning by generating fine-grained, rule-based rewards for multi-level parsing and stabilizing reinforcement learning with novel techniques.

## Problem

- Existing pixel-based vision models struggle to capture the precise geometric primitives and hierarchical relationships inherent in diagrams, leading to limited interpretable understanding.
- Fine-grained supervision methods like Process Reward Models often rely on costly human annotations or unreliable LLM-as-a-judge approaches, hindering scalable development.
- Reinforcement learning for diagram reconstruction suffers from poor exploration and training collapse due to sparse visual rewards and low reward variance in semantically sparse diagrams.

## Method

- A self-supervised ==symbolic auto-encoder== (**SymVAE**) was developed to encode diagrams into a structured latent space of logic forms, which are then reconstructed by a deterministic rendering engine.
- A Hierarchical Process Reward Model (HPRM) was introduced, featuring cost-free, rule-based reward functions (SymHPR) that supervise learning across multiple abstraction levels with explicit geometric dependencies.
- The training pipeline incorporates cold-start pre-training, reinforcement learning with HPRM, and self-supervised learning with visual rewards, stabilized by hard negative contrastive learning and power normalization annealing.

## Results

- SymVAE-**7B** achieved a **98.2%** reduction in MSE for geometric diagram reconstruction and surpassed GPT-4o on chart reconstruction with a **6.5%** improvement in layout accuracy.
- On the MathGlance perception benchmark, SymVAE-**7B** obtained **72.6%** average accuracy, representing a **13.4%** absolute improvement over Qwen2.5-VL-**7B**, with **100%** accuracy in relation identification.
- For mathematical reasoning, SymVAE+CoTs-**7B** achieved **51.8%** overall accuracy on MathVerse and **79.4%** on GeoQA, outperforming Qwen2.5-VL-**7B** and other baselines, indicating improved visual grounding through symbolic representations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing diagrams as hierarchical symbolic logic forms, rather than continuous pixel-level features, enables more interpretable, structured, and accurate understanding for diagrammatic content.
> - Hierarchical Process Rewards, which condition higher-level rewards on the correctness of lower-level primitives, are essential for enforcing step-level parsing consistencies and improving understanding of complex relations.
> - Stabilization techniques, such as hard negative contrastive learning and power normalization annealing, effectively mitigate the training instability of reinforcement learning in domains with sparse rewards like diagram reconstruction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the field of symbolic computer vision, which focuses on interpreting visual information through explicit logical rules and structured representations, in contrast to prevalent pixel-based visual models. The paper addresses the challenge of enabling machine vision systems to understand diagrams—which are "semantically sparse yet structurally rich blueprints"—by parsing them into geometric primitives and their interrelationships.

The authors differentiate their approach from existing computer vision learners, which typically encode images into continuous high-dimensional vectors optimized for semantic similarity. While these models have been successful in natural image understanding, they often struggle to capture the precise geometric primitives and their hierarchical relationships essential for diagram comprehension. The paper specifically extends the auto-encoder paradigm, previously applied to pixel-level latent spaces (e.g., VAEs, MAEs), to operate on symbolic latent spaces.

In the context of diagram parsers, the research contrasts with two main categories: code-based representations (e.g., ChartMimic, VisCodex, ChartCoder) and logic-based representations (e.g., PGDP, AlphaGeometry). The authors argue that code-based methods are often too verbose and ill-suited for geometric diagrams, while existing logic-based methods either lack indicators of shape properties or are tailored for symbolic mathematical deduction rather than vision-grounded perception and reconstruction. This work aims to bridge this gap by producing visual logic forms that encode primitive entities and shape-level information within a hierarchical structure, enabling self-supervised symbolic vision encoding.

Furthermore, the paper integrates and extends the concept of Process Reward Models (PRMs), which have been applied in language reasoning (e.g., Let’s Verify Step by Step, GroundedPRM, VisualPRM) to provide fine-grained supervision for intermediate reasoning steps. This research introduces a Hierarchical Process Reward Model (HPRM) tailored for symbolic vision, supervising learning across multiple abstraction levels and incorporating explicit geometric dependencies, thus addressing the limitations of human annotation costs and potential hallucination in LLM-as-a-judge approaches for reward generation.

Ultimately, this work contributes to the development of neuro-symbolic systems, aiming to combine the reasoning capabilities of deep learning models with the logical rules and interpretability inherent in symbolic AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a self-supervised symbolic auto-encoder capable of understanding and reconstructing diagrams through explicit, interpretable symbolic representations rather than continuous pixel-level features. This overarching goal is driven by several motivations:

1. **Bridging the Gap in Diagram Understanding:** Diagrams, ubiquitous in science, engineering, and education, convey complex structural relations that are fundamentally different from natural images. Current mainstream vision encoders primarily process textures and colors, encoding diagrams into continuous high-dimensional vectors. This pixel-based paradigm is limited for diagrams, where pixel values lack inherent semantic meaning, forcing models to approximate symbolic understanding. The authors propose an auto-encoder that explicitly parses diagrams into geometric primitives (points, lines, shapes) and their interrelationships within a structured latent space, directly addressing this limitation.

2. **Achieving Interpretable and Structured Understanding:** The aim is to move beyond "black-box" models by representing diagrams through explicit logical rules and structured representations. This enables interpretable understanding in machine vision, allowing the system to externalize complex relationships in a compact and human-understandable form, which is crucial for applications requiring high reliability and explainability.

3. **Perceiving Hierarchical Structures:** A key challenge identified is enabling the encoder to perceive not only individual primitives but also their inherent hierarchical structures. For instance, line detection depends on correctly identified points, and shape analysis relies on accurate line parsing. The research is motivated by the need to enforce these step-level parsing consistencies.

4. **Developing Cost-Effective and Reliable Fine-Grained Supervision:** Existing Process Reward Models (PRMs) for fine-grained supervision often rely on expensive human annotations or Large Language Models (LLMs) acting as judges, which can be prone to hallucination. The authors are motivated to introduce a cost-free, rule-based reward mechanism (SymHPR) that generates verifiable metrics for hierarchical multi-step parsing, circumventing these issues. This involves a synthetic logic-form engine to construct structured parsing paths and computes rewards using verifiable metrics like F1 scores and L2 distances.

5. **Stabilizing Self-Supervised Reinforcement Learning:** The paper identifies that vanilla reinforcement learning, when applied to diagram reconstruction, often suffers from poor exploration in the policy space due to sparse supervision and low reward variance. This leads to early reward plateaus and low KL divergence, indicating training collapse. A motivation is to introduce stabilization mechanisms, such as hard negative contrastive learning and power normalization annealing, to balance exploration and exploitation and ensure stable self-supervised training.

6. **Advancing Neuro-Symbolic Architectures:** The ultimate motivation is to develop a neuro-symbolic system that integrates the reasoning capabilities of neural networks with the interpretability and logical rules of symbolic models. This system aims to enable reasoning-grounded visual rewards to adaptively complement textual answer rewards in multimodal reasoning tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology centers on a novel self-supervised symbolic auto-encoder architecture, designed to encode diagrams into structured primitive-level latent spaces and reconstruct them via an executable engine. The approach involves a three-stage training pipeline and stabilization mechanisms for reinforcement learning.

#### 3.1. Symbolic Auto-Encoder Architecture

- **Logic Forms:** The core symbolic representation (`s ∈ S`) consists of compositional geometric primitives:
 - **Points:** Labeled (e.g., 'a', 'b') with normalized spatial positions (x, y).
 - **Lines:** Defined by vertex pairs forming segments (e.g., 'ab').
 - **Shapes:** Constructed from lines or circles, with explicit types (e.g., `RightTriangle`) and properties (e.g., perpendicular lines).
 - **Geometric Relations:** Interactions among primitives (e.g., `IntersectAt`, `Incircle`, `Tangent`, `AngleBisector`). This forms a natural hierarchy where points define lines, lines form shapes, and primitives form relations.
- **Encoder (`fθ: X → S`):** A vision-language model, parameterized by `θ`, generates a structured symbolic representation (`s_hat`) from an input diagram (`x`).
- **Decoder (Rendering Engine `R: S → X`):** A deterministic engine that converts a logic form (`s_hat`) back into a diagram image (`x_hat`). This engine parses the structured logic form and renders the diagram using standard graphics libraries like Matplotlib.

#### 3.2. Three-Stage Training Pipeline

1. **Cold-start Pre-training (SymParser):**
 - The encoder is initially trained using supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on a dataset `D` of paired `(x_i, s_i)` examples.
 - The objective (`L_SFT`) is standard next-token prediction, enabling the model to learn visual composition rules and the formal structure of the symbolic language.
 - **Dataset:** 100K synthetic diagram-logic-form pairs.

2. **Hierarchical Process Reward Modeling (SymHPR):**
 - Following SFT, reinforcement learning via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) further optimizes the encoder.
 - For each input diagram `x`, `K` logic-form candidates (`s_hat^(k)`) are sampled from the current policy. Each candidate receives a reward `r^(k)`.
 - **Task-Specific Reward Functions (`r_logic`):** Evaluate six geometric dimensions by comparing predicted logic forms (`s_hat`) with ground truth (`s`), normalized to:
 - **Point Reward:** F1 score for correctly identified points.
 - **Line Reward:** F1 score for lines, with order normalization.
 - **Shape Reward:** F1 score, requiring an exact match of shape type and constituent vertices.
 - **Shape Indicator Reward:** F1 score, conditioned on correctly predicted shapes (e.g., right angles, parallel sides).
 - **Geometric Relation Reward:** F1 score on relation type-word, abstracting specific objects.
 - **Point-Position Reward:** Exponential of negative average Euclidean distance for correctly detected points.
 - **Hierarchical Reward Designs:** Instead of simple averaging, a blending mechanism (`r'_c = r_c ⋅ [(1 - α_c→p) + α_c→p ⋅ r'_p]`) enforces dependencies. For instance, `line` rewards are conditioned on `point` rewards, `shape` on `line`, and `relation` on `shape`. This ensures rewards are granted only when lower-level detections are reliable.
 - **Dataset:** 9K samples (5K synthetic, 4K extended PGDP).

3. **Self-Supervised Learning (SymVAE):**
 - The rendering engine acts as a decoder, enabling a self-supervised auto-encoding pipeline where the model reconstructs diagrams (`x_hat = R(s_hat)`).
 - The non-differentiable rendering function enables gradient flow through reinforcement learning via a visual reward (`r_vis`).
 - **Visual Reward Functions (`r_vis`):** Compare the original diagram `x` with the reconstructed `x_hat` using three components:
 - **Reconstruction Reward:** Mean Squared Error (MSE).
 - **Structural Similarity:** SSIM.
 - **DINO Similarity:** Cosine similarity between DINOv2 features.
 - Combined as a normalized weighted sum (`w_MSE=0.6, w_SSIM=0.3, w_DINO=0.1`).
 - **Stabilized GRPO:** To address training collapse from low visual reward variance in semantically sparse diagrams:
 - **Hard Negative Contrastive Learning:** Gaussian noise is applied to reconstructed images (`x_noisy`) for half the rollouts during reward computation. Noise standard deviation (`σ_t`) is annealed exponentially.
 - **Power Normalization Annealing:** Group-normalized rewards are transformed by a power function (`r_tilde_i = (...) ^ α_t`), where `α_t` is annealed from 3.0 to 1.0. This sharpens the reward distribution, amplifying differences between good and bad rollouts.
 - **Dataset:** 16K diagram-only samples (Geo170K, synthetic, PGDP).

#### 3.3. Neuro-Symbolic System Design

- For multimodal reasoning, SymHPR converts diagrams into executable logic forms, which are prepended to the question and processed by an LLM reasoner (Qwen2.5-Math-7B).
- An adaptive reward weighting mechanism is introduced, dynamically scaling the visual reward based on mathematical correctness. Visual rewards are downweighted when final answers are incorrect to prevent spurious visual-textual correlations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation across reconstruction, perception, and reasoning tasks demonstrates the effectiveness of the proposed approach.

#### 4.1. Geometric Diagram Reconstruction

- SymVAE-7B achieved the highest performance across all metrics (MSE, LPIPS, SSIM, DINO similarity) on both synthetic and real-world Geo170K diagrams. For geometric diagram reconstruction, it achieved a 98.2% reduction in MSE.
- The symbolic representation paradigm consistently outperformed pixel-level auto-encoders (VAE, VQ-GAN) and Python-code generation models (PyhParser) in terms of reconstruction fidelity and DINO similarity.
- Visualizations revealed that pixel-based methods lose fine structural details, while code-based models struggle with symbolic semantics, generating primitives without coherent shape or relational understanding.

#### 4.2. Chart Reconstruction

- On the ChartMimic Direct Mimic benchmark, SymVAE+Chart-7B surpassed GPT-4o by 0.6% on low-level metrics and demonstrated a 6.5% improvement in layout accuracy.
- The model also outperformed the specialized chart parser VisCodex-8B and significantly exceeded the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B, indicating strong generalization capabilities of symbolic logic-form representations across domains.

#### 4.3. Diagram Understanding

- On the MathGlance perception benchmark, SymVAE-7B achieved 72.6% average accuracy across planar geometry, solid geometry, and graph analysis, representing a 13.4% absolute improvement over Qwen2.5-VL-7B (59.2%).
- The largest gains were observed in relation identification (100.0% accuracy), highlighting the benefit of compositional reasoning over geometric primitives.
- SymVAE-7B also surpassed GPT-4o-mini-high in solid geometry (+2.9%) and graph analysis (+12.1%).

#### 4.4. Mathematical Reasoning

- **MathVerse:** SymVAE+CoTs-7B achieved 51.8% overall accuracy, outperforming Qwen2.5-VL-7B (49.2%), particularly in vision-dominant settings, suggesting improved visual grounding through symbolic representations. Cross-modal attention visualizations confirmed consistent visual focus during reasoning.
- **GeoQA:** SymVAE+CoTs achieved 79.4% accuracy, surpassing Qwen2.5-VL-7B (76.4%) and MultiMath-7B (74.1%). The model's performance without explicit projector alignment matching projector-based variants suggested that symbolic logic forms are inherently compatible with the language reasoning space.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Symbolic Understanding:** Symbolic representations were demonstrated to be superior to pixel-level feature vectors and code-based programming for diagram reconstruction and understanding. PyhParser achieved only 11.4% average accuracy on diagram understanding, failing catastrophically on planar geometry and graphs.
- **Hierarchical Process Reward (HPR):** Hierarchical reward modeling (SymHPR) yielded moderate gains in reconstruction but a significant 3.9% improvement in diagram understanding, particularly for relation identification and object counting, compared to a flat reward variant. This indicated the importance of modeling hierarchical dependencies.
- **Stabilization Strategies:** Both hard negative contrastive learning and power normalization annealing effectively stabilized GRPO training for SymVAE, preventing reward plateaus and poor exploration. Power normalization was found to be superior in final performance and stability.
- **Neuro-symbolic System Designs:** The neuro-symbolic approach, where SymHPR outputs executable logic forms processed by an LLM with adaptive visual-textual rewards, proved effective. This suggested symbolic logic forms are inherently compatible with language reasoning and that RL primarily refines existing reasoning policies.

#### 4.6. Cross-Domain Generalization (Supplementary Material)

- The model demonstrated an ability to transfer primitives from planar geometry to solid-geometry attribution (e.g., assigning colors and material properties to 3D objects).
- Fine-tuning on conversational perception datasets enabled the model to translate logic-form rules into linguistic Chain-of-Thoughts, demonstrating an alignment between symbolic structures and natural language reasoning.
- The approach extended to reconstructing electrical and chemical diagrams, leveraging common underlying geometric primitives.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a method for diagram understanding that moves beyond pixel-level feature extraction towards an interpretable, symbolic, and hierarchical representation. The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

- **Enhanced Interpretability and Verifiability:** By explicitly encoding diagrams into logic forms composed of geometric primitives and their relationships, the system provides a human-readable and verifiable understanding of visual content. This contrasts with opaque, continuous feature vectors of deep neural networks and is particularly valuable in fields like scientific research, engineering design, and education, where clear and verifiable reasoning is paramount.

- **Improved Performance in Specialized Visual Tasks:** The method demonstrates competitive performance across a range of tasks, from precise diagram reconstruction to complex mathematical reasoning and chart parsing. Its ability to excel in tasks requiring fine-grained structural understanding, where general-purpose multimodal models often struggle, highlights the advantages of a symbolic approach for diagrams.

- **Cost-Effective and Scalable Data Generation:** The development of a synthetic logic-form engine that generates structured parsing paths and renders corresponding diagrams offers a method for creating large-scale, high-quality training data without the prohibitive costs and subjectivity associated with manual annotation or unreliable LLM-based supervision. This could accelerate research and development in symbolic vision.

- **Advancement in Neuro-Symbolic AI:** The work contributes to the integration of neural and symbolic AI paradigms. By using neural networks for perception to extract symbolic representations and then leveraging an executable symbolic engine for reconstruction and reasoning, the research exemplifies a neuro-symbolic system that combines the pattern recognition power of deep learning with the logical rigor and interpretability of symbolic methods. This could lead to more robust, explainable, and generalizable AI systems.

- **Broad Applicability in STEM Fields:** Diagrams are fundamental tools across science, technology, engineering, and mathematics. This research could lead to improved automated tools for:
 - **Educational Platforms:** Better understanding and generation of geometric proofs, scientific schematics, and instructional diagrams.
 - **Engineering and Design:** Automated parsing and verification of circuit diagrams, architectural blueprints, and mechanical drawings.
 - **Data Analysis:** More accurate interpretation and reproduction of charts and graphs.
 - **Automated Reasoning:** Enhanced capabilities for solving visual mathematical problems.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** The limitations identified, such as the rule-based design limiting flexibility for novel geometric concepts and challenges with hand-drawn or cluttered diagrams, open avenues for future work. This includes exploring meta-learning for automatic primitive vocabulary discovery, integrating with differentiable program induction for adaptive symbolic space growth, and incorporating uncertainty estimation to improve robustness. The neuro-symbolic cycles mentioned suggest a path toward systems that balance flexible neural pattern recognition with rigorous symbolic verification.
