# Sampling-Based Control via Entropy-Regularized Optimal Transport

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Georgia Institute of Technology introduced Optimal Transport Model Predictive Control (OT-MPC), which leverages entropy-regularized optimal transport to overcome limitations like mode-averaging and mode-seeking in sampling-based control. This approach demonstrated improved success rates in complex robotic tasks and more efficient locomotion compared to prior methods.

## Problem

- Existing sampling-based Model Predictive Control (MPC) methods, such as MPPI, suffer from "mode-averaging" where global averaging of samples can lead to undesirable or unsafe actions in multimodal cost landscapes.
- Algorithms like CEM exhibit "mode-seeking" behavior, prematurely committing to single modes and potentially getting stuck in suboptimal local minima due to aggressive elite selection.
- Current methods, rooted in information-theoretic objectives like KL divergence, lack inherent geometric awareness, preventing effective handling of spatial arrangements crucial in control problems.

## Method

- The research proposes Optimal Transport ==Model Predictive Control== (OT-MPC), which reformulates the variational inference objective from information-theoretic divergences to an entropy-regularized optimal transport (EOT) problem.
- OT-MPC employs the Sinkhorn Coordinate Descent (SCD) algorithm to iteratively update candidate control sequences (particles) by computing an optimal coupling with sampled proposals.
- The method uses a geometric cost function (e.g., squared Euclidean distance) to quantify proximity between particles and proposals, enabling local refinement while preserving diversity across distinct solution modes.

## Results

- OT-MPC achieved significantly higher success rates across various complex robotics tasks, including **93.5%** in hard car obstacle avoidance (vs. **88.5%** for MPPI) and **76%** in 2D Push-T manipulation (vs. **4%** for MPPI).
- Demonstrated superior performance in multi-quadrotor cooperative load carrying, with a **91%** success rate (vs. **22%** for MPPI), and more efficient quadruped locomotion, completing tasks in significantly fewer average steps (**427.8** vs **799.2** for MPPI).
- Exhibited comparable or faster wall-clock times than MPPI in JAX-vectorized environments, as the optimal transport mechanism efficiently extracts more information per sample, reducing the number of computationally expensive rollouts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Replacing information-theoretic divergences with an optimal transport formulation explicitly incorporates geometric information into the sample aggregation process, enabling more nuanced control decisions.
> - The Sinkhorn Coordinate Descent algorithm provides a gradient-free mechanism for updating particles through barycentric projections of proposals, facilitating efficient computation while maintaining theoretical guarantees like monotone descent.
> - Separating the control cost into marginal weights and reserving the transport cost for geometric proximity allows OT-MPC to effectively refine local solutions while simultaneously maintaining coverage of distinct solution modes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses fundamental limitations within the domain of sampling-based Model Predictive Control (MPC), a class of algorithms essential for real-time control of nonlinear and contact-rich robotic systems. Algorithms such as Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) and the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) are widely used due to their compatibility with black-box simulators and learning-based models, as they primarily require only cost evaluations of candidate trajectories.

However, existing sampling-based MPC methods, rooted in a "control-as-inference" framework, derive from information-theoretic objectives (typically KL divergence). These objectives quantify how probability mass is distributed but do not inherently incorporate the spatial arrangement or geometry of the control problem. This leads to two primary pathological behaviors:
- **Mode-averaging:** Observed in methods like MPPI, where the global weighted average of samples ignores their spatial positions, resulting in a control output that blends multiple minima and may lead to undesirable or unsafe actions (e.g., averaging trajectories around an obstacle results in collision).
- **Mode-seeking:** Exhibited by methods like CEM, which commit aggressively to a single mode through elite selection, thereby limiting exploration and potentially getting stuck in suboptimal local minima.

Previous attempts to mitigate these issues have often involved *ad hoc* heuristic modifications, such as annealing schedules, covariance adaptation, or warm-starting techniques. This work distinguishes itself by proposing a foundational shift in the underlying variational principle, moving from information-theoretic divergences to an optimal transport (OT) formulation.

In the broader context of optimal transport applications in robotics, prior work has utilized OT to define control objectives (e.g., steering multi-agent systems, covariance steering, imitation learning) or as an underlying mechanism in areas like diffusion models. This paper, however, employs optimal transport to determine *how* to solve the control problem, specifically in the sample aggregation and update step. It contrasts with approaches like MPOT, which encode the control cost directly into the transport cost matrix, by instead encoding costs through marginal weights and reserving the transport cost for geometric proximity. This structural separation is designed to enable local refinement towards nearby promising proposals while maintaining coverage of distinct solution modes, thereby avoiding the global averaging behavior of MPPI and the aggressive mode-seeking of CEM.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to overcome the inherent limitations of current sampling-based MPC methods, specifically the mode-averaging behavior of MPPI and the mode-seeking behavior of CEM, especially in scenarios characterized by complex, multimodal cost landscapes.

The motivation stems from the recognition that the information-theoretic objectives underlying these traditional methods are "agnostic to the geometry of the control problem." This agnosticism prevents them from effectively handling situations where the spatial arrangement of control sequences is critical. For instance, in obstacle avoidance tasks, MPPI's global averaging can lead a robot to steer directly into an obstacle by blending trajectories that bypass it on different sides. CEM, while avoiding averaging, can prematurely commit to a single, potentially suboptimal, path.

Therefore, the authors aimed to:
1. **Introduce geometric awareness:** Develop a sampling-based control algorithm that explicitly incorporates spatial information into the process of aggregating information from candidate control sequences. This would allow for local refinement of solutions while respecting the geometry of the solution space.
2. **Enable mode preservation and diversity:** Create a mechanism that allows the algorithm to maintain diverse candidate solutions, especially in multimodal cost landscapes, preventing premature collapse to a single mode or harmful averaging between distinct modes.
3. **Ensure compatibility and efficiency:** The proposed method should remain gradient-free, preserving compatibility with black-box simulators and non-differentiable costs, which is a key advantage of existing sampling-based MPC. Furthermore, it should achieve real-time performance through efficient, closed-form updates where possible.
4. **Provide a foundational alternative:** Rather than applying heuristic fixes, the goal was to address the problem at a fundamental level by reformulating the variational inference objective itself, replacing the information-theoretic divergence with an optimal transport objective.

By achieving these objectives, the research seeks to enhance the robustness and reliability of sampling-based MPC for challenging robotic tasks, particularly those involving discontinuous dynamics, contact-rich interactions, and complex decision-making in environments with multiple viable strategies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology is the **Sinkhorn Coordinate Descent (SCD)** algorithm, which is instantiated as **Optimal Transport Model Predictive Control (OT-MPC)** for real-time control. SCD is a gradient-free optimization algorithm designed to evolve a set of `N` particles (candidate control sequences) toward a target distribution `Q*` by coordinating updates with `M` proposals sampled from a reference distribution.

**A. Optimal Control Problem Formulation and Variational Inference**
The optimal control problem (P) involves minimizing a cost function `S(u; x0)` over control sequences `u`. Similar to existing sampling-based MPC, OT-MPC frames this as a variational inference problem, approximating a Gibbs distribution `Q*(u) ∝ exp(-βS(u))P(u)`. While MPPI uses importance sampling to approximate the expectation of `u` under `Q*`, OT-MPC replaces the KL divergence objective with an optimal transport formulation.

**B. Optimal Transport Foundation**
Optimal transport (OT) measures the minimum cost to transform one probability distribution into another, incorporating geometric information through a cost matrix `C`. The specific formulation used is the entropy-regularized optimal transport (EOT) problem:

`Tε(q, p) = minimizeΓ∈Γ(q,p) Σi,j CijΓij − εH(Γ)`

Here, `q ∈ Δ^N` and `p ∈ Δ^M` are marginal probability mass functions for particles and proposals, respectively. `Γ ∈ R^N×M+` is the coupling matrix, and `H(Γ)` is the entropy regularization, which facilitates efficient computation via the Sinkhorn algorithm.

**C. Sinkhorn Coordinate Descent (SCD) Algorithm**
SCD proceeds via alternating optimization between particle positions `z` and the coupling matrix `Γ`:
1. **Particle Representation:** Each particle `zi` represents a candidate control sequence `(u_0_i..., u_tf-1_i)`.
2. **Proposal Distribution:** Proposals `yj` are sampled from a distribution `R(y|z)`. The target marginal `p(y)` for proposals is defined via self-normalizing importance sampling: `pj(y) = Q*(yj) / Σk Q*(yk)`, where `Q*(yj) ∝ exp(-βS(yj))`. Particles `q(z)` are typically assigned uniform weights `qi = 1/N` to encourage exploration.
3. **Cost Function `c(z, y)`:** This function quantifies the geometric proximity between a particle `z` and a proposal `y`. A common choice is the squared Euclidean distance `c(z, y) = ||z - y||^2`.
4. **Coupling Update:** Given fixed particle positions `z`, the optimal coupling `Γ^(k+1)` is computed using the Sinkhorn algorithm (Algorithm 1) based on the cost matrix `Cij = c(z^(k)_i, y_j)`. This step is efficient and yields a "soft" coupling, indicating how each particle `z_i` is related to each proposal `y_j`.
5. **Particle Update:** Given the fixed coupling `Γ^(k)`, each particle `z_i^(k+1)` is updated toward a barycentric projection of the proposals:
 `z*i = Σ_j Γ_ij y_j / Σ_k Γ_ik`
 For quadratic cost functions `c(z, y) = ||z - y||^2`, this update is closed-form and represents a weighted average of proposals, where weights are derived from the coupling. A relaxation parameter `η ∈ (0, 1]` is typically used for stability: `z_i^(k+1) = (1 - η)z_i^(k) + ηb_i`, where `b_i` is the barycenter.

**D. OT-MPC Implementation Details**
- **MPC Cycle:** At each MPC cycle, `K` iterations of SCD are run. New proposals are typically drawn at each iteration. The first control from the lowest-cost particle is then executed. Standard warm-starting is applied.
- **Proposal Distribution `R(y|z)`:** To balance exploration and exploitation, a mixture model is used: `R(y|z) = (1 - ρ) · (1/N) Σ_i N(y; z_i, Σ) + ρ · R_global(y)`. The first component perturbs existing particles for local refinement, while the second provides global coverage. Temporal correlations are incorporated into the perturbation covariance `Σ` for trajectory optimization.
- **Hyperparameter Selection:**
 - `ε` (entropy regularization): Controls the sparsity of the coupling. Smaller `ε` leads to more deterministic assignments, faster convergence, but higher risk of premature commitment. Larger `ε` maintains diversity but slows refinement. A practical range is [0.01, 0.1] times the median pairwise distance.
 - `η` (relaxation parameter): Step size for particle updates. Values around 0.3 to 0.7 balance stability and convergence speed.
 - `N` (number of particles): Determines the capacity to represent multimodality. A heuristic `N << M` is suggested, where `M` is the number of proposals.

**E. Theoretical Properties**
For fixed proposals `y` and a convex `c(z,y)`:
- The objective `L_c_ε(z, Γ; y)` is biconvex in `(z, Γ)`.
- The sequence of objective values `L_c_ε(z^(k), Γ^(k); y)` is non-increasing (monotone descent).
- The iterates `(z^(k), Γ^(k))` converge to a stationary point.

These properties provide guarantees regarding the algorithm's behavior and convergence.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of OT-MPC was evaluated through empirical comparisons against MPPI, CEM, and SV-MPC across a variety of robotics control tasks. Hyperparameters for all controllers were tuned using Optuna to ensure a fair comparison.

**A. Car (Bicycle) Obstacle Avoidance:**
- **Task:** Kinematic bicycle model navigating a dense obstacle field.
- **Results:** OT-MPC achieved higher success rates (99% on Easy, 93.5% on Hard) than MPPI (95% Easy, 88.5% Hard), CEM (70% Easy, 56% Hard), and SV-MPC (93% Easy, 78.5% Hard). The performance gap was more pronounced in the "Hard" setting, indicating OT-MPC's advantage in multimodal cost landscapes.

**B. 2D Push-T:**
- **Task:** Planar manipulator pushing and aligning a T-shaped block, characterized by hybrid contact dynamics and sparse costs.
- **Results:** OT-MPC demonstrated superior performance with a 76% success rate, significantly outperforming MPPI (4%), CEM (46%), and SV-MPC (0%). MPPI's low success rate was attributed to mode-averaging, while SV-MPC failed due to the non-differentiability of contact dynamics.

**C. Quadrotor Dense Obstacle Avoidance:**
- **Task:** 12DOF quadrotor navigating a dense obstacle field.
- **Results:** OT-MPC outperformed MPPI in challenging environments: 100% vs 60% success in "Medium" difficulty, and 92% vs 19% in "Hard" difficulty. MPPI frequently became stuck in local minima, failing to find feasible paths, whereas OT-MPC's multimodal approach mitigated this issue.

**D. Two Quadrotor Cooperative Load Carrying:**
- **Task:** A 27DOF system of two quadrotors cooperatively carrying a suspended load through a narrow opening in a wall.
- **Results:** OT-MPC achieved substantially higher success rates (91% Normal, 75% Hard) compared to MPPI (22% Normal, 10% Hard). The authors attributed MPPI's poor performance to its mode-averaging disrupting the precise coordination required to navigate the bottleneck.

**E. Franka Push-T:**
- **Task:** Full 3D manipulation with a Franka Panda arm pushing a T-block, involving a 34-dimensional system and cubic spline control trajectories.
- **Results:** After extensive hyperparameter tuning for MPPI, both OT-MPC (66%) and MPPI (64%) achieved similar success rates. The authors noted that OT-MPC attained competitive performance with less tuning effort.

**F. Quadruped Locomotion (Unitree Go2):**
- **Task:** Simulated Unitree Go2 quadruped performing locomotion on various terrains (ramps, bridges) without predefined gaits.
- **Results:** Both OT-MPC and MPPI achieved 100% success across different terrain configurations. However, OT-MPC achieved significantly lower average steps (427.8 vs 799.2), indicating more efficient locomotion.

**G. Quadruped Box Pushing:**
- **Task:** Loco-manipulation where the Unitree Go2 quadruped pushes a box to a goal location, expanding the state space to include box dynamics.
- **Results:** OT-MPC performed better with a 78% success rate compared to MPPI's 71%, while also maintaining lower average steps and median distance to goal.

**H. Computational Overhead:**
- For tasks with JAX-vectorized rollouts, OT-MPC exhibited comparable or faster wall-clock times than MPPI on most tasks, despite the additional Sinkhorn step. This was due to Optuna tuning OT-MPC to use fewer proposals per iteration, as the optimal transport coupling extracts more information per sample, reducing the number of computationally dominant rollouts.
- For environments using sequential MuJoCo rollouts (Franka, Go2), the rollouts dominated wall-clock time. The isolated per-iteration cost of the Sinkhorn solve and barycentric update was 1.6 ms for Franka (8 × 800 coupling) and 0.2 ms for Go2 (8 × 50 coupling).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of sampling-based optimal control by addressing core limitations of existing methods through a novel theoretical framework. The introduction of **Optimal Transport MPC (OT-MPC)**, based on **Sinkhorn Coordinate Descent (SCD)**, shifts the paradigm from information-theoretic objectives to an optimal transport formulation that explicitly incorporates geometric information.

The primary impact lies in its ability to mitigate the well-documented issues of mode-averaging (seen in MPPI) and mode-seeking (seen in CEM). By computing an optimal coupling between candidate control sequences and low-cost proposals, OT-MPC facilitates local refinement while simultaneously preserving diversity across multiple distinct solution modes. This capability is particularly crucial for complex robotic tasks, as demonstrated across navigation, manipulation, and locomotion experiments, where multimodal cost landscapes are common. The empirical results consistently show improved success rates for OT-MPC, especially in challenging scenarios like dense obstacle fields, contact-rich manipulation, and coordinated multi-robot control, where traditional methods struggle.

Furthermore, the theoretical properties established for SCD, including monotone descent and convergence guarantees, provide a stronger foundation for the algorithm's reliability. Operationally, despite the additional computational complexity of the Sinkhorn algorithm (O(NM) per iteration compared to O(M) for MPPI), the method achieved comparable or even faster wall-clock times in JAX-vectorized environments. This is attributed to the optimal transport mechanism's ability to extract more information per sample, thereby reducing the total number of expensive rollouts required. This efficiency suggests that OT-MPC can be a viable option for real-time control, providing improved performance without necessarily incurring a prohibitive computational cost.

**Limitations:**
- The `O(NM)` complexity of the Sinkhorn step can become a bottleneck if the number of particles `N` or proposals `M` is very large, though this was managed in the reported experiments by using fewer particles.
- The performance of OT-MPC is dependent on the tuning of the entropy regularization parameter `ε`. Small values risk premature commitment, while excessively large values can slow refinement, indicating a need for careful selection or adaptive scheduling.
- Similar to other sampling-based methods, the exploration capability of OT-MPC is fundamentally limited by the coverage of its proposal distribution. Poor initialization or overly localized sampling strategies could still lead to a collapse of diversity among particles.

**Future Work:**
The authors propose several directions for future research. Beyond the direct application to MPC, SCD could be explored as a general sampling algorithm across various problem classes. Algorithmic extensions include dynamically adapting the proposal distribution, representing particles as Gaussian distributions, or leveraging connections between optimal transport and Laguerre tessellations for semi-discrete algorithms. Additionally, further theoretical analysis is needed to generalize the convergence guarantees of SCD to scenarios where proposals are resampled dynamically, which is common in practical MPC implementations. These avenues suggest a potential for broader impact and continued development of optimal transport-based sampling methods in control and beyond.
