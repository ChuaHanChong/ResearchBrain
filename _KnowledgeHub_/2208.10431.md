# ProtoPFormer: Concentrating on Prototypical Parts in Vision Transformers for Interpretable Image Recognition

> [!summary] Summary
> ProtoPFormer introduces a Vision Transformer-based architecture that integrates global and local prototypes for interpretable image recognition, effectively addressing the "prototype distraction" issue to yield superior classification accuracy and transparent visual explanations concentrated on discriminative object parts.

## Problem

- Deep neural networks, including Vision Transformers (ViTs), often function as "black boxes," lacking transparency in their decision-making processes.
- Directly applying existing prototype-based interpretability methods to ViTs results in a "prototype distraction" problem, with prototypes activating on background elements and scattering in the foreground.
- Current ViT interpretability methods typically provide global feature explanations but struggle to pinpoint specific discriminative parts crucial for fine-grained understanding.

## Method

- A dual-branch **ProtoPFormer** architecture is proposed, utilizing ==global prototypes== to capture holistic object features and ==local prototypes== to focus on partial features.
- A ==Foreground Preserving (FP) mask==, derived from the ViT's class token ==attention rollout==, is introduced to guide local prototypes toward foreground regions and away from background noise.
- A ==Prototypical Part Concentration (PPC) loss== is designed to explicitly encourage local prototypes to centralize their activations and diverge onto distinct, fine-grained visual parts.

## Results

- ProtoPFormer achieved superior top-1 classification accuracy across CUB, Dogs, and Cars fine-grained datasets with DeiT and CaiT backbones, outperforming both prototype-based baselines and non-interpretable vanilla ViT models.
- Qualitative visualizations demonstrated that ProtoPFormer's local prototypes exhibit strong, centrally focused activations on diverse and discriminative object parts, effectively resolving the prototype distraction problem.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the dual-branch architecture with its mutual correction mechanism, along with the Foreground Preserving mask and Prototypical Part Concentration loss, are critical for the observed performance and interpretability gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - ViT's intrinsic architectural components, such as the class token and attention mechanisms, can be effectively leveraged to guide prototype learning and foreground identification.
> - Explicitly enforcing prototype concentration on discriminative parts and foreground elements, through tailored masking and loss functions, is crucial for overcoming the "prototype distraction" problem in ViTs.
> - Combining global contextual understanding with local fine-grained part recognition via a dual-prototype approach enables mutual correction and more robust, interpretable classification decisions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The emergence of deep neural networks (DNNs) has led to substantial advancements across various computational domains, particularly in feature extraction and pattern recognition from large datasets. Despite their performance, DNNs frequently operate as "black boxes," lacking transparency in their decision-making processes. This opacity limits their application in critical fields that require accountability and understanding of AI decisions.

The field of Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) addresses this challenge by developing methods to make DNNs more interpretable. Within XAI, prototype-based networks, such as ProtoPNet, have garnered attention due to their inherent self-explanatory properties. ProtoPNet, primarily developed on convolutional neural networks (CNNs), interprets decisions by identifying discriminative parts of objects using category-specific prototypes and forming predictions based on the similarity of image patches to these prototypes. This approach aligns with human visual reasoning, where objects are often identified by comparing their characteristic parts.

Concurrently, Vision Transformers (ViTs) have emerged as a dominant architecture in computer vision, challenging the supremacy of CNNs with their ability to model long-range dependencies and achieve high performance. Naturally, researchers have explored combining the architectural strengths of ViTs with the interpretability of prototype-based methods. However, directly applying ProtoPNet to ViT backbones has revealed a "prototype distraction" problem. In this scenario, learned prototypes are frequently activated by background elements and exhibit scattered, fragmented activations in the foreground, compromising both interpretability and classification accuracy. Existing ViT interpretability methods, which often rely on attention weights, gradients, or attributions, typically provide global feature explanations but struggle to pinpoint specific discriminative parts crucial for fine-grained understanding.

This research aims to bridge the gap between the powerful long-range dependency modeling of ViTs and the local, part-based interpretability offered by prototype networks. By explicitly addressing the "prototype distraction" problem in ViTs, the proposed ProtoPFormer seeks to leverage the architectural characteristics of transformers to enable prototype-based interpretability effectively, thereby extending the utility of XAI in the context of state-of-the-art vision models. It positions itself as a method that not only enhances the interpretability of ViTs but also improves their performance in fine-grained image recognition tasks, moving beyond the observed trade-off between accuracy and transparency in prior ViT-backed prototype approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind ProtoPFormer stems from a identified limitation: while prototypical part networks (ProtoPNets) have demonstrated effective self-explanatory properties when integrated with convolutional neural networks (CNNs), their direct application to Vision Transformers (ViTs) results in a significant "prototype distraction" problem. This issue manifests as prototypes being disproportionately activated by background regions and exhibiting dispersed, fragmented activations within the foreground, thereby impairing their inherent interpretability and hindering the model's ability to focus on salient prototypical parts.

The key objectives of this research are detailed as follows:

1. **Develop an appropriate and effective prototype-based method for ViTs:** The overarching goal is to design a framework, ProtoPFormer, that can successfully integrate prototype-based interpretability with Vision Transformers for image recognition. This addresses the incompatibility observed when prior CNN-centric ProtoPNet variants are applied to ViT architectures.
2. **Address the "prototype distraction" problem in ViT-backed prototype networks:** Specifically, the research aims to devise mechanisms to concentrate prototypes on discriminative foreground elements and specific prototypical parts, thereby resolving the issue of scattered activations and background interference.
3. **Capitalize on the intrinsic architectural characteristics of ViTs:** The motivation includes leveraging ViT's distinct components, such as the class token (which aggregates global information) and image tokens (which retain spatial location similarities), to inform the design of prototype learning. This ensures that the interpretability mechanism is inherently aligned with the ViT's operational principles rather than being an external add-on.
4. **Introduce complementary global and local prototypes:** A detailed objective is to design two distinct types of prototypes:
 - **Global prototypes:** Intended to capture and highlight holistic features of target objects, providing a broad contextual understanding.
 - **Local prototypes:** Explicitly supervised to concentrate on representative partial features, offering fine-grained visual evidence.
 The motivation for this dual approach is to enable a more comprehensive and robust reasoning process, analogous to how humans process both overall context and specific details.
5. **Implement a two-step concentration process for local prototypes:** This involves:
 - **Foreground preservation:** Utilizing global information to guide local prototypes towards foreground regions and away from background clutter. This step is motivated by the need to filter irrelevant visual information that distracts prototypes.
 - **Prototypical part concentration:** Designing a mechanism to explicitly encourage local prototypes to focus on diverse and distinct visual parts, thereby promoting inter-prototype divergence and centralizing activation scores. This addresses the fragmentation of activations.
6. **Enable mutual correction and joint decision-making:** A core objective is to allow the global and local branches to mutually correct each other's decisions and jointly contribute to the final prediction. This is motivated by the hypothesis that combining insights from both holistic and partial perspectives can lead to more accurate and reliable classification while enhancing the transparency of the decision.
7. **Achieve superior performance and faithful reasoning:** The research aims for ProtoPFormer to not only provide interpretable explanations but also maintain or surpass the classification accuracy of existing prototype-based methods and even non-interpretable ViT baselines. This addresses the historical trade-off between interpretability and performance often observed in XAI models.

In essence, the motivation is to transform ViTs from powerful but opaque models into transparent systems that can logically and visually explain their decisions by highlighting the key global and local features they rely upon, thereby increasing trust and applicability in sensitive contexts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ProtoPFormer addresses the "prototype distraction" problem in ViT-backed prototype networks by introducing a dual-branch architecture comprising global and local prototypes, which are explicitly constrained to focus on relevant visual information. The methodology is structured around leveraging the inherent architectural characteristics of ViTs and implementing a two-step concentration process.

**Overall Architecture:**
The ProtoPFormer architecture processes an input image through a Vision Transformer backbone, which outputs a visual sequence $X$ consisting of a class token $t_c \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times d}$ and image tokens $X_f \in \mathbb{R}^{(n-1) \times d}$, where $d$ is the embedding dimension and $(n-1)$ is the number of image tokens. These are then fed into two parallel branches: a global prototype branch and a local prototype branch.
- **Global Branch:** This branch processes the class token $t_c$. It employs $m_g$ learnable global prototypes $P_g = \{p^{(1)}_g, \dots, p^{(m_g)}_g\}$ to compute similarity scores with $t_c$. These scores are then passed through a fully connected (FC) layer to produce logits $z_g$ for classification. The global branch aims to capture holistic features of the target object.
- **Local Branch:** This branch processes the image tokens $X_f$. It utilizes $m_l$ learnable local prototypes $P_l = \{p^{(1)}_l, \dots, p^{(m_l)}_l\}$ to compute similarity scores with the image tokens. These scores are then max-pooled and passed through an FC layer to generate logits $z_l$ for classification. The local branch focuses on partial and prototypical features.

The final prediction $z_c$ is obtained by a weighted summation of the logits from both branches: $z_c = \lambda_g z_g + \lambda_l z_l$, allowing for mutual correction and joint decision-making.

**Two-Step Concentration Process:**

To address the "prototype distraction" problem and ensure local prototypes focus on discriminative parts, ProtoPFormer implements a two-step concentration process:

1. **Concentration on the Foreground (Foreground Preserving (FP) Mask):**
 The global branch guides the local branch by generating an adaptive binary mask, termed the Foreground Preserving (FP) mask ($\gamma \in \{0, 1\}^{n-1}$), which identifies foreground-related image tokens. This mask is derived using the attention rollout method from the class token's attention matrix.
 - **Attention Rollout:** For a ViT with $L$ encoder layers, the rollout matrix at the $(l-1)$-th layer is used. The rollout attention value $\tilde{A}_{1,i}$ quantifies the influence of the $i$-th token on the class token, serving as an indicator of foreground relevance.
 - **Mask Generation:** A predefined number of top-K tokens with the highest rollout attention values are preserved as foreground tokens (i.e., $\gamma_i = 1$), while others are considered background ($\gamma_i = 0$).
 - **Modified Softmax:** In the $L$-th encoder layer, the softmax normalization for computing attention weights $A_{i,j}$ is modified. It only considers connections to foreground tokens ($\gamma_j=1$) during normalization, effectively cutting off connections between background and foreground tokens. This ensures that subsequent computations in the local branch primarily use foreground-filtered tokens.
 - **Prototype Layer Application:** In the prototype layer of the local branch, only similarity scores generated from these preserved foreground-related tokens are considered for further class prediction, thereby tentatively concentrating local prototypes on the foreground.

2. **Concentration on Prototypical Parts (Prototypical Part Concentration (PPC) Loss):**
 With foreground tokens selected, local prototypes are further explicitly supervised to concentrate on heterogeneous visual parts. This is achieved by modeling the similarity map (reshaped to a 2D array) for each local prototype as a bivariate Gaussian function:
 - **Gaussian Fitting:** For each local prototype, the similarity scores ($s_i$) from foreground-related tokens at their respective positions ($x_i$) on the similarity map are used to estimate the mean ($\hat{\mu}$) and covariance matrix ($\hat{\Sigma}$) of a Gaussian distribution.
 - **PPC Loss Formulation:** A composite Prototypical Part Concentration (PPC) loss, $L_{PPC} = \lambda_\mu L_{\mu}^{PPC} + \lambda_\sigma L_{\sigma}^{PPC}$, is designed:
 - $L_{\mu}^{PPC}$: Encourages inter-prototype divergence by penalizing prototypes of the same class whose estimated centers ($\hat{\mu}$) are too close. It measures the maximum of a predefined threshold $t_\mu$ minus the squared Euclidean distance between centers, if positive.
 - $L_{\sigma}^{PPC}$: Promotes centralization of similarity scores by minimizing the trace of the covariance matrix $tr(\hat{\Sigma})$. This term aims to reduce the dispersion of activations, ensuring prototypes focus on tighter, more specific regions. It is applied only when the trace exceeds a threshold $t_\sigma$.

**Overall Optimization:**
The total optimization objective for ProtoPFormer is to minimize $L = L_{CE} + L_{PPC}$, where $L_{CE}$ is the conventional cross-entropy loss applied to the final combined logits $z_c$. The FC layers in both branches are non-trainable, with fixed weights connecting prototypes to their respective class logits. Unlike some prior ProtoPNet variants, ProtoPFormer does not employ additional cluster and separation losses or a "push" process during training, as the PPC loss is deemed sufficient for promoting prototype diversity and concentration.

The methodology integrates global contextual guidance with local fine-grained supervision, directly addressing the limitations of applying prototype-based interpretability to ViTs while leveraging their architectural strengths.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted for ProtoPFormer evaluated its performance against seven state-of-the-art prototype-based baselines and a vanilla ViT model (Base) across three fine-grained classification datasets: CUB, Dogs, and Cars. Three different Vision Transformer backbones were utilized: DeiT-Ti, DeiT-S, and CaiT-XXS-24.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance Comparison

- **Superior Accuracy:** ProtoPFormer consistently achieved superior top-1 classification accuracy across all three datasets and with all three ViT backbones compared to the baseline methods. For instance, on the CUB dataset with DeiT-Ti, ProtoPFormer achieved 82.26%, outperforming ProtoPNet (81.36%) and the Base ViT (80.57%). Similar improvements were observed on Dogs (e.g., 82.20% with DeiT-Ti vs. 81.05% for Base) and Cars (e.g., 88.48% with DeiT-Ti vs. 86.21% for Base).
- **Addressing Trade-off:** The results indicated that ProtoPFormer not only provided interpretability but also surpassed the accuracy of the non-interpretable Base ViT, suggesting that it effectively mitigates the performance-interpretability trade-off often encountered in XAI models.
- **Incompatibility of CNN-based Baselines:** ProtoPNet, TesNet, and Def.ProtoPNet, which were originally designed for CNNs, showed varying degrees of performance degradation when applied to ViT backbones. This observation underscores their incompatibility with the long-range dependency modeling of transformers and highlights the necessity for ViT-tailored approaches.
- **Efficiency:** ProtoPFormer achieved its performance with only a small number of additional parameters (e.g., 6.33M for ProtoPFormer DeiT-Ti on CUB vs. 5.56M for Base, and significantly less than ViT-Net's 10.42M which had comparable accuracy). This indicates an efficient use of parameters for interpretability.
- **Comparison with ViT-specific Baselines:** Even against ViT-specific methods like CT (ConceptTransformer) and ViT-Net, ProtoPFormer generally showed competitive or superior accuracy while offering advantages. CT required user-defined, time-consuming attribute labels, which ProtoPFormer’s automatically learned prototypes circumvented. ViT-Net achieved comparable accuracy but with a considerably higher parameter count due to its neural tree structure.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Interpretability (Visualization Analysis)

- **Faithful Reasoning Process:** ProtoPFormer demonstrated a transparent reasoning process, as illustrated by example cases. The global and local branches provided complementary predictions, with local prototypes often correcting global misjudgments by identifying specific discriminative parts (e.g., indigo features of a bird's head, belly, and feet). This highlights the "mutual correction and joint decision" strategy.
- **Concentrated Local Prototypes:** Visual comparisons of activated local prototypes across baselines and ProtoPFormer revealed the effectiveness of the proposed concentration mechanisms. Baselines generally exhibited "prototype distraction," characterized by scattered and irregular activations, frequently extending into background regions. In contrast, ProtoPFormer's local prototypes showed strong, centrally focused activations on diverse, discriminative parts of the target objects (e.g., specific bird features, dog's head/belly).
- **Gaussian Function Shape:** Analysis of the Gaussian function shapes fitted to prototype activation maps indicated that ProtoPFormer's local prototypes maintained smaller covariance values, confirming that the Prototypical Part Concentration (PPC) loss successfully promoted tighter, more centralized activations.
- **Holistic Global Prototypes:** Visualizations of global prototypes demonstrated their ability to capture holistic features of target objects, including variations in pose and perspective (e.g., distinguishing between a flying and perching bird, or front and side views of a dog). This confirmed their role in providing a broader contextual understanding.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Mutual Correction and Joint Decision:** Ablation studies confirmed that combining the global and local branches yielded higher total accuracy compared to either branch operating in isolation. This empirically supports the hypothesis that these branches mutually correct each other, leveraging their complementary perspectives for more robust final decisions.
- **Hyperparameter Influence:** Experiments systematically evaluated the impact of various hyperparameters, including the number of kept foreground tokens (K), weighted coefficients ($\lambda_\mu, \lambda_\sigma$) for the PPC loss, and the thresholds ($t_\mu, t_\sigma$) for the PPC loss. The results guided the selection of optimal parameter values that maximized performance and interpretability.
- **Prototype Dimension:** The dimension of prototypes was also analyzed, showing that a medium value (e.g., 192) yielded optimal results, balancing information capture with noise reduction.

In summary, the main findings demonstrate that ProtoPFormer successfully integrates prototype-based interpretability with Vision Transformers, resolving the "prototype distraction" problem through its dual-branch architecture and two-step concentration process. It achieves superior classification performance, provides transparent and faithful visual explanations, and leverages the intrinsic characteristics of ViTs efficiently.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of ProtoPFormer represents a notable advancement in the field of Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI), particularly concerning Vision Transformers (ViTs). Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Bridging the Interpretability Gap for ViTs:** ProtoPFormer successfully addresses a critical challenge in XAI: making advanced ViT models inherently interpretable at a conceptual level. Previously, prototype-based methods, while effective for CNNs, struggled with ViTs due to the "prototype distraction" problem. This work provides a tailored solution that allows prototype-based reasoning to be effectively applied to the long-range dependency modeling capabilities of transformers.
- **Enhanced Trust and Transparency in AI:** By offering both global and local visual explanations, ProtoPFormer provides a more comprehensive and human-understandable reasoning process for ViT decisions. This transparency can significantly increase trust in AI systems, especially in sensitive domains where understanding *why* a decision was made is as crucial as the decision itself.
- **Improved Performance with Interpretability:** Unlike many XAI methods that introduce an interpretability-performance trade-off, ProtoPFormer demonstrates that enhanced interpretability can be achieved while simultaneously improving or maintaining superior classification accuracy compared to both non-interpretable ViT baselines and other prototype-based methods. This outcome suggests a more efficient and harmonized approach to building reliable and transparent AI.
- **Leveraging ViT Architectural Strengths:** The design of ProtoPFormer intelligently capitalizes on the distinct architectural components of ViTs, specifically the class token and image tokens. By dedicating global prototypes to the class token and local prototypes to image tokens, and using the class token's attention rollout to guide foreground concentration, the method extracts interpretability directly from the model's internal workings rather than relying on external post-hoc analyses.
- **Advancement in Fine-Grained Recognition:** The consistent superior performance across fine-grained classification datasets (CUB, Dogs, Cars) highlights ProtoPFormer's utility in tasks requiring precise identification of subtle visual cues. The ability to concentrate on diverse prototypical parts makes it particularly well-suited for such detailed recognition tasks.
- **Foundation for Future XAI Research:** ProtoPFormer introduces novel mechanisms like the Foreground Preserving (FP) mask and the Prototypical Part Concentration (PPC) loss. These concepts could inspire further research into developing more robust and efficient ways to guide deep learning models toward human-interpretable features, not only for ViTs but potentially for other architectures as well.
- **Broadened Application in Critical Domains:** The ability to provide traceable and understandable decisions from ViTs has significant implications for various critical applications.
 - **Medical Image Diagnosis:** Explaining *why* a lesion is classified as malignant by pointing to specific visual characteristics could assist clinicians in making more informed decisions.
 - **Model Debugging:** When a model makes an incorrect prediction, ProtoPFormer's visual explanations could help developers pinpoint which specific visual features or background elements led to the error, facilitating faster and more effective debugging.
 - **Autonomous Systems:** Understanding the visual cues an autonomous vehicle relies on for object detection or scene understanding can be vital for safety and regulatory compliance.

In conclusion, ProtoPFormer contributes a robust and effective framework for interpretable image recognition with Vision Transformers. Its ability to simultaneously enhance performance and transparency positions it as a significant step forward in making advanced AI systems more understandable and trustworthy for practical applications.
