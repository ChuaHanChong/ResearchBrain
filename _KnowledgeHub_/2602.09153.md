# SceneSmith: Agentic Generation of Simulation-Ready Indoor Scenes

> [!summary] Summary
> SceneSmith introduces an agentic framework for generating dense, articulated, and physically feasible indoor scenes directly from natural language descriptions, specifically designed for robotics simulation. The system produces environments with 3-6 times more objects, significantly lower collision rates, and 95.6% static stability compared to prior methods.

## Problem

- Existing robotics simulation environments are often too simplistic, lacking the density, articulation, and physical fidelity of real-world indoor spaces necessary for robust robot training.
- Manually creating complex, physically plausible simulated environments is time-consuming and unscalable, hindering large-scale robot policy development and evaluation.
- Prior scene synthesis methods often prioritize visual realism, neglecting explicit physical properties, inter-object relationships, and static stability essential for direct use in physics simulators.

## Method

- A hierarchical, agentic framework uses a "Designer," "Critic," and "Orchestrator" VLM trio to iteratively construct house-level scenes from natural language, making grounded decisions at each stage.
- Integrated asset generation combines text-to-3D synthesis for static objects and retrieval from articulated object libraries (ArtVIP) to ensure open-vocabulary support and simulation readiness.
- Physical feasibility is enforced through post-processing steps, including non-penetration projection and gravity settling, along with VLM-estimated physical properties for all assets.

## Results

- Generated scenes contain 3-6 times more objects per room (average **71.1**) and **2.6** times more objects in house-level scenes compared to previous methods.
- The framework achieves a low inter-object collision rate of **1.2%** and **95.6%** static stability for objects under physics simulation, significantly outperforming baselines.
- Human evaluations indicate SceneSmith scenes are preferred for realism (**92.2%** win rate) and prompt faithfulness (**91.5%** win rate) against all room-level baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A multi-agent VLM framework can effectively manage the hierarchical decomposition and iterative refinement needed to synthesize complex, multi-room indoor environments from high-level prompts.
> - Integrating asset generation (including text-to-3D synthesis and articulated object retrieval) directly into the scene assembly process is critical for achieving open-vocabulary support and simulation readiness for robotics.
> - Explicit physical property estimation and post-processing steps (non-penetration projection, gravity settling) are crucial for ensuring generated scenes are immediately usable in physics simulators with high static stability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of general-purpose robots, particularly those intended for deployment in diverse human indoor environments, necessitates robust training and evaluation methodologies. Real-world data collection, while essential, is resource-intensive and struggles to scale across the variability of indoor spaces in terms of layout, object composition, clutter, articulation, and physical interaction affordances. Consequently, simulation has emerged as a crucial tool for scalable robot training and evaluation, offering controlled experimentation and safe testing.

However, a significant gap exists between the simplicity of most current simulated environments and the physical complexity and diversity of real indoor spaces. Existing simulation environments often feature sparsely furnished rooms with limited object diversity and largely static scenes. This contrasts with real homes, which exhibit dense object arrangements, articulated furniture, fine-grained clutter, and diverse manipulable objects. For instance, a kitchen cabinet might be densely packed with individually manipulable items possessing plausible physical properties. This disparity limits the effectiveness of simulation-based approaches for preparing robots for real-world conditions, particularly for complex manipulation tasks. While manual environment design can yield high-quality scenes, it is not a scalable solution.

Prior research in scene synthesis has predominantly addressed asset generation (producing individual objects with geometry and physical properties) and scene generation (assembling assets into environments) in isolation. Asset-centric methods focus on object reconstruction or synthesis, while scene-centric approaches typically generate layouts or object arrangements assuming a fixed asset library. Furthermore, many existing scene synthesis methods, whether procedural (e.g., ProcTHOR, Infinigen Indoors), data-driven (e.g., DiffuScene, Mixed Diffusion), or language-guided (e.g., Holodeck, I-Design, LayoutVLM, SceneWeaver), primarily target furniture-level layout and visual realism. They often treat small objects, articulated assets, and accurate physical properties as secondary concerns, which is misaligned with the requirements for robotics simulation where dense arrangements of manipulable objects, hierarchical support relationships, and physically valid configurations are essential.

This work, SceneSmith, positions itself by aiming to bridge this gap. It integrates asset generation and scene assembly to create simulation-ready indoor environments that capture the density, articulation, and physical feasibility required for scalable robot training and evaluation. It builds upon recent advances in agentic AI by employing a multi-agent framework for iterative scene refinement, extending concepts seen in systems like LL3M for asset generation and SceneWeaver for scene synthesis. Crucially, SceneSmith emphasizes the generation of *physically simulatable* scenes, a feature often lacking in prior visual-centric methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the limitations of current simulation environments in adequately preparing general-purpose robots for real-world deployment. As foundational models drive progress in robotics, enabling deployment into arbitrary human homes, robots require the ability to perceive, reason, and act robustly across highly diverse and complex environments.

The specific motivations are:
- **Scalability for Robot Training and Evaluation:** Real-world data collection is expensive, slow, and cannot cover the vast diversity of environments robots are expected to encounter. Simulation offers a scalable alternative, but only if the simulated environments accurately reflect real-world complexity.
- **Mismatch with Real Indoor Spaces:** Existing simulated environments are often too simplistic, featuring sparse furniture, limited object variety, and static scenes. Real homes are characterized by dense object arrangements, articulated furniture (e.g., cabinets with drawers), and fine-grained clutter (e.g., packed shelves), which pose significant challenges for robotic manipulation.
- **Lack of Physical Properties and Interaction Affordances:** Most scene synthesis methods prioritize visual realism over the explicit physical properties (e.g., collision geometry, mass, inertia, friction) and manipulability crucial for robotic interaction. Scenes that lack these properties cannot be directly used in physics simulators.
- **Cost of Manual Design:** Manually designing high-fidelity, physically plausible environments for simulation is time-consuming and labor-intensive, preventing large-scale environment generation.
- **Fragmented Approach to Scene Synthesis:** Prior work has largely separated asset generation from scene assembly, resulting in environments that may look realistic but lack the interactive capabilities or physical coherence required for robotics.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of the SceneSmith project are:
- **Generate Realistic, Simulation-Ready Environments:** To enable the scalable creation of indoor environments that accurately reflect the diversity and physical complexity of real homes.
- **Text-to-Scene Generation:** To develop a framework that can construct room- or house-level indoor environments from natural language descriptions, through a sequence of grounded decisions.
- **Immediate Simulability:** To ensure that the generated scenes are immediately usable in arbitrary physics simulators, complete with explicit physical properties and suitable for robotic interaction.
- **Dense and Diverse Object Placement:** To populate scenes with dense object arrangements, including small manipulable objects and articulated furniture, reflecting real-world clutter and functional relationships.
- **Physical Feasibility and Stability:** To guarantee that generated scenes are physically feasible, with minimal inter-object collisions and statically stable object configurations under physics simulation.
- **Integrated Asset and Scene Generation:** To jointly generate simulation-ready assets (individual objects with geometry and physical properties) and assemble them into coherent, house-level scenes in an end-to-end pipeline.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SceneSmith is a hierarchical, agentic framework designed for constructing simulation-ready indoor environments from natural language prompts. It decomposes scene creation into a sequence of decisions spanning layout, furnishing, object population, and refinement.

#### 3.1. Scene Hierarchy

A scene is represented as a collection of rooms, each comprising architectural geometry (walls, floor, doors, windows) and objects (simulation-ready assets with poses). SceneSmith constructs scenes through a tree of stages:
- **Root Stage:** Generates architectural layout, determining the number of rooms and their structural geometry.
- **Room Population Stages:** Each room is independently populated with objects through successive stages:
 - Furniture placement (floor-standing furniture).
 - Wall-mounted object placement (art, shelves).
 - Ceiling fixture placement (lights, fans).
- **Manipuland Placement Stages:** Selected supporting entities (e.g., furniture surfaces, wall shelves, floor regions) spawn additional branches for adding small manipulable objects.
This hierarchical structure facilitates prompt refinement, where global prompts are refined into room-specific and entity-specific prompts, ensuring local decisions align with the overall scene intent.

#### 3.2. Agentic Trio: Designer, Critic, and Orchestrator

Each stage of the hierarchical construction is implemented as an interaction between three specialized Vision-Language Model (VLM) agents:
- **Designer:** Proposes modifications to the scene state using a set of structured tools for observation, modification, and asset acquisition.
- **Critic:** Evaluates the proposed scene modifications against factors like semantic plausibility, physical feasibility, and alignment with the stage objective, providing scalar scores and natural-language feedback.
- **Orchestrator:** Manages the iterative critique-and-improve loop. It tracks scores, determines whether to accept a proposal or request refinement, and can revert to previous scene states (checkpoints) if quality degrades.

Agents interact through a shared toolkit that includes state observation, visual observation (multi-view renders with annotations), scene modification (placing, moving, removing, scaling objects), asset acquisition (generating or retrieving assets), and feasibility verification (collision, reachability checks). Each stage also exposes specialized tools; for instance, furniture placement includes snapping tools and relational facing queries, while manipuland placement includes tools for creating stacks, filling containers, and creating random piles or controlled arrangements. Agents can invoke multiple tools within a single turn. Persistent, turn-based session memory is maintained for agents within each stage, summarizing past interactions to manage context growth. All agents and VLM calls leverage GPT-5.2 with varying reasoning efforts.

#### 3.3. Asset Generation and Routing

SceneSmith tightly integrates asset generation to support open-set object vocabularies and ensure simulation readiness:
- **Asset Router:** A VLM analyzes asset requests, filtering out inappropriate items for a given stage, decomposing composite requests (e.g., a "fruit bowl" into a bowl and individual fruits), and selecting the appropriate acquisition strategy.
- **Generative Asset Synthesis (Static Objects):** For static objects, a text-to-3D pipeline is used: text description → reference image (GPT Image 1.5) → foreground segmentation (SAM3) → textured 3D mesh reconstruction (SAM3D). Meshes are then canonicalized, scaled, and augmented with collision geometry and estimated physical properties.
- **Articulated Object Library:** For objects with movable parts (e.g., cabinets, drawers), assets are retrieved from ArtVIP, an articulated object library containing pre-authored multi-link models with joint definitions. A two-stage retrieval process uses CLIP similarity and target dimensions.
- **Thin Coverings:** Flat decorative elements (rugs, posters) are generated procedurally using tileable or single AI-generated image textures, falling back to generative models when library retrieval fails.
- **Asset Validation:** All candidate assets undergo VLM-based validation to verify object type, style consistency, completeness, and reasonable proportions. Assets failing validation are regenerated or rerouted.

#### 3.4. Simulation Readiness

To ensure generated scenes are immediately usable for robotics:
- **Volumetric Geometry:** Architectural elements use volumetric geometry to improve robustness against penetration in physics simulation.
- **Physical Property Estimation:** A VLM estimates mass, center of mass, inertia, and friction for all objects by analyzing multi-view renders and assigning material categories. For articulated objects, properties are estimated per-link.
- **Collision Geometry:** Visual meshes are decomposed into convex pieces using V-HACD for efficient contact resolution in physics engines.
- **Physical Feasibility Post-Processing:** After furniture and manipuland placement, a two-stage process enforces physical feasibility:
 - **Non-Penetration Projection:** Nonlinear optimization projects object positions to the nearest collision-free configuration to resolve inter-object penetrations.
 - **Gravity Settling:** The scene is simulated under gravity in Drake to allow unstable objects to settle into statically stable configurations.
- **Fallen Object Removal:** Objects that fall over or off surfaces during simulation are automatically detected and removed.

#### 3.5. Parallel Scene Generation

SceneSmith leverages a GPU worker pool and batch request pipelining to generate multiple scenes concurrently, enabling practical throughput for large-scale dataset creation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SceneSmith was evaluated across 210 diverse room- and house-level prompts using both human user studies and automated metrics, demonstrating its capabilities in generating dense, articulated, and physically feasible indoor environments.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance

- **Object Density:** SceneSmith generated 3-6 times more objects per room than prior methods (average 71.1 objects vs. baselines' 11-23 objects). For house-level scenes, it generated 2.6 times more objects (average 214 vs. Holodeck's 81 objects).
- **Collision Rate:** SceneSmith achieved a low inter-object collision rate of less than 2% (1.2% average) compared to 3-29% for baselines. The mean penetration depth for SceneSmith was 3.8mm, which was 3-12 times shallower than baselines.
- **Static Stability:** 95.6% of objects generated by SceneSmith remained stable under physics simulation, in contrast to 8-61% for baselines.
- **Human User Study:** A pairwise comparison study with 205 participants revealed:
 - **Realism:** SceneSmith achieved a 92.2% average win rate against all room-level baselines (e.g., 88.5% vs. HSM, 88.6% vs. Holodeck).
 - **Prompt Faithfulness:** SceneSmith achieved a 91.5% average win rate against all room-level baselines (e.g., 85.2% vs. HSM, 90.6% vs. Holodeck).
 - **House-Level Scenes:** Against Holodeck (the only multi-room baseline), SceneSmith achieved 80.3% realism and 84.7% prompt faithfulness win rates.
- **Automated Metrics:** SceneSmith showed superior performance on object count (CNT), object attributes (ATR), object-object relationships (OOR), object-architecture relationships (OAR), collision rate (COL), and static stability (STB). Its lower scores on accessibility (ACC) and navigability (NAV) were attributed to the significantly higher object density.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- The removal of generative asset acquisition (NotGenerated), VLM-based asset validation (NoAssetValidation), and visual observations (NoObserveScene) led to the most significant degradation in human preference scores, demonstrating the importance of these components.
- The NoCritic ablation, which removed critic iterations, resulted in similar preference scores but reduced generation cost by 70% and generation time by 51%, albeit producing 24% fewer objects on average. This suggests a cost-quality trade-off for certain applications.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

SceneSmith demonstrated the ability to generate scenes with appropriate object density, realistic arrangements, and faithful adherence to complex prompts, including specific stylistic constraints (e.g., themed rooms). For multi-room scenes, SceneSmith generated plausible room connectivity and architectural layouts (e.g., a hotel scene with logical reception flow and en-suite bathrooms), unlike some baselines that produced less realistic or traversable layouts.

#### 4.4. Robot Policy Evaluation Application

An end-to-end robot policy evaluation pipeline was demonstrated, converting natural-language task descriptions into diverse scene prompts for SceneSmith, executing robot policies in these generated environments, and automatically verifying task completion using a VLM evaluator agent. This pipeline successfully distinguished between a standard pick-and-place policy (16% success) and a deliberately degraded variant (12% success), with the evaluator agent achieving 99.7% agreement with human judgments.

#### 4.5. Robot Simulation Demonstrations

Qualitative demonstrations, including teleoperation of a humanoid robot (RB-Y1) and zero-shot policy rollouts with an externally trained generalist robot policy, indicated that SceneSmith-generated scenes are suitable for interactive robot simulation and support complex manipulation tasks. The zero-shot policy, trained on real-world data and predating SceneSmith, successfully operated within the generated environments without SceneSmith-specific tuning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SceneSmith addresses a critical bottleneck in the field of robotics by enabling the scalable generation of realistic, physically accurate, and simulation-ready indoor environments. The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

- **Accelerated Robot Development:** By providing diverse and complex simulated environments, SceneSmith can significantly accelerate the training and evaluation of general-purpose robots, particularly those designed for human homes. This reduces the need for costly, slow, and labor-intensive real-world data collection and manual environment design.
- **Enhanced Robot Robustness:** The ability to generate environments with dense clutter, articulated objects, and accurate physical properties allows robots to be trained and tested against a wider range of challenging conditions, improving their robustness in perceiving, reasoning, and acting in complex real-world scenarios.
- **Improved Simulation Fidelity for Robotics:** SceneSmith's emphasis on generating scenes that are *immediately simulatable* with minimal inter-object collisions and high static stability marks an advancement in simulation fidelity tailored specifically for robotics applications, where dynamic interactions are paramount.
- **Open-Vocabulary Environment Generation:** The integrated asset generation pipeline, combining text-to-3D synthesis for static objects and retrieval for articulated objects, supports open-vocabulary generation. This enables users to specify a wide array of objects and themes, increasing the versatility and applicability of the generated scenes.
- **Scalable Policy Evaluation:** The demonstrated end-to-end robot policy evaluation pipeline offers a scalable method for automatically assessing robot performance across diverse tasks and environments, reducing reliance on hand-crafted success predicates.
- **Reduced Simulation-to-Real Gap:** By producing environments that are statistically closer to the complexity and physical characteristics of real homes, SceneSmith contributes to narrowing the sim-to-real gap, making policies trained in simulation more transferable to physical robots.
- **Broader Applications:** While primarily focused on robotics, the capabilities of SceneSmith in generating high-fidelity, customizable 3D indoor scenes from natural language could find applications in other fields such as architectural visualization, virtual reality content creation, and game development, where detailed and diverse environments are beneficial.

The authors suggest that these results indicate a point where environment generation may no longer be the primary bottleneck for scalable robot training and evaluation in simulation, fostering further advancements in embodied AI.
