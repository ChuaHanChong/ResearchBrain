# DexH2R: A Benchmark for Dynamic Dexterous Grasping in Human-to-Robot Handover

> [!summary] Summary
> The DexH2R benchmark provides the first real-world dataset for dynamic human-to-robot object handover using dexterous robotic hands, featuring multi-modal data and detailed 3D annotations. An accompanying three-stage solution demonstrates that a Diffusion Policy 3D approach achieves a balanced performance with a 66.3% success rate and 50.1% safety in complex dynamic grasping scenarios.

## Problem

- A scarcity of high-quality, real-world datasets specifically for dynamic human-to-robot handover involving dexterous robotic hands.
- Existing datasets are limited to static grasping, human-to-human interactions, or synthetic data, creating a significant reality gap for complex dexterous robot control.
- Developing robust and adaptive grasping strategies for multi-fingered robot hands in dynamic human-robot interaction remains a challenge due to the lack of suitable training and evaluation data.

## Method

- Introduction of **DexH2R**, the first real-world dataset comprising 4282 dynamic human-to-robot handover trials with 39 participants, 56 objects, and 79,911 successful grasp poses, captured with multi-modal sensors and 3D annotations.
- Development of **DynamicGrasp**, a ==three-stage solution== for human-to-robot handover, encompassing ==grasp pose preparation==, ==approaching motion generation== (using autoregressive or ==diffusion policies==), and goal pose alignment.
- Establishment of a comprehensive benchmark on DexH2R to evaluate dynamic grasping approaches across metrics like success rate, safety, penetration depth, and diversity, including unseen objects and subjects.

## Results

- Grasp pose generation models trained exclusively on DexH2R achieved an **89.38%** success rate for single-force stability, demonstrating the dataset's quality for learning stable grasps.
- The Diffusion Policy 3D (DP3) method achieved the highest safety rate (**50.1%**) and a **66.3%** success rate in the 'Easy Mode' for approaching motion, balancing successful grasping with collision avoidance.
- In the more challenging 'Hard Mode', DP3 maintained a competitive **27.1%** success rate with a **33.7%** safety rate, outperforming other baselines under tighter proximity constraints for goal alignment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The high fidelity of teleoperated grasping data in DexH2R allows for effective training of grasp pose generative models, achieving strong performance without extensive pre-training on synthetic datasets.
> - Integrating 3D point cloud input (Diffusion Policy 3D) provides a more robust and safer approach for dynamic object approaching motions compared to 2D image-based or purely autoregressive methods.
> - A multi-stage approach combining initial grasp preparation, dynamic trajectory generation, and a final goal pose alignment strategy is crucial for successfully executing complex, safe, and physically plausible dexterous handovers.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Human-Robot Interaction (HRI) and collaboration are rapidly evolving fields, with object handover being a cornerstone task for seamless integration of robots into human environments, spanning applications from household assistance and healthcare to industrial automation. Within this landscape, the use of five-finger dexterous robotic hands is gaining increasing attention because they can mimic human hand structures and functionalities, enabling more sophisticated and natural operations compared to simpler two-finger grippers. However, their high degrees of freedom and complex control make dexterous grasping a significantly challenging problem.

The broader research landscape for dexterous grasping and handover can be segmented by the type of interaction and data availability:
- **Static Grasping:** Many existing datasets (e.g., RealDex, ContactPose, DexYCB) focus on a robot grasping stationary objects, which does not capture the dynamic nature of human-to-robot handover.
- **Human-to-Human (H2H) Handover:** Datasets like H2O and HOH provide insights into human interaction patterns but are not directly applicable to robot control without substantial adaptation.
- **Synthetic Handover Data:** Recent efforts like HandoverSim, GenH2R, and MobileH2R attempt to synthesize handover motions and data in simulation environments. While these offer scalability, they suffer from a significant "reality gap" due to a lack of authentic visual data and real motion patterns, especially concerning the intricacies of dexterous robotic hands. They often model givers with simplified human hand models (e.g., MANO) or receivers with parallel grippers, failing to capture the complexity of real human-to-robot (H2R) interactions with dexterous hands.
- **Dynamic Grasping (Limited Scope):** Some studies explore dynamic grasping for grippers or simpler objects (e.g., throwing and catching balls), but real-time adaptability for complex, moving objects with dexterous hands remains an open challenge.

This paper positions itself directly to address a critical void in this landscape: the **absence of high-quality, real-world datasets specifically for dynamic human-to-robot handover involving dexterous robotic hands.** The authors highlight that previous datasets are either static, H2H, or rely on synthetic data/simplified grippers, making them inadequate for developing and evaluating robust, adaptive grasping strategies for complex dexterous hands in real-world H2R scenarios. By introducing DexH2R, the first dataset of its kind, and a benchmark solution, the work aims to bridge the simulation-to-reality gap and drive advancements towards truly human-like and intelligent robot collaboration.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind this research stems from the significant challenges in enabling dexterous robotic hands to naturally and safely receive objects from humans in dynamic environments. The authors identify a crucial limitation in current research: the scarcity of high-quality, real-world datasets that accurately capture the complexities of human-to-robot handover with dexterous hands. Existing datasets either focus on static grasping, human-to-human interactions, or rely on synthetic data that fails to replicate real-world motion patterns and visual fidelity, particularly for multi-fingered robotic hands. This data gap severely impedes the development and evaluation of effective dynamic dexterous grasping methods.

Based on this motivation, the paper sets forth three key objectives:

1. **Introduce DexH2R: A Comprehensive Real-World Human-to-Robot Handover Dataset.** The primary objective is to create and release the first-ever real-world dataset specifically designed for human-to-robot handovers involving a dexterous robotic hand (ShadowHand). This dataset aims to feature a diverse range of interactive objects, dynamic motion patterns, rich multi-modal sensor data (RGB-D, egocentric views), and detailed 3D annotations (human hands, robot hands, objects). A crucial aspect of this objective is to ensure "human-like" robot motions through teleoperation during data collection, which is vital for developing intuitive and trustworthy humanoid robots.
2. **Propose DynamicGrasp: An Effective Solution for Human-to-Robot Handover.** Beyond just providing data, the paper aims to present a practical and effective solution for the human-to-robot handover task, named DynamicGrasp. This solution is inspired by human cognitive and motor strategies for receiving objects, breaking down the process into stages of grasp pose preparation, approaching motion generation, and goal pose alignment. The goal is to equip dexterous hands with human-like intelligence for dynamic grasping.
3. **Establish a Comprehensive Benchmark for Human-to-Robot Handover.** The final objective is to create a robust benchmark using the DexH2R dataset and the DynamicGrasp solution. This benchmark involves evaluating various state-of-the-art dynamic grasping approaches, including autoregressive and diffusion policy methods, providing a thorough comparison, and defining specific evaluation metrics (safety, precision, reliability) to systematically assess performance. The intention is to provide a standardized framework that will catalyze future research and development in this critical field.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology is meticulously structured around two main contributions: the creation of the DexH2R dataset and the development of the DynamicGrasp solution, followed by a comprehensive benchmarking process.

#### 3.1. DexH2R Dataset Collection and Processing

- **Hardware System Setup:**
 - **Vision Capture System:** A sophisticated multi-view setup captures detailed and diverse perspectives. This includes 12 ZCAM E2 cameras arranged in a quarter-sphere for high-quality RGB streams (360-degree coverage) and 4 Azure Kinect cameras at workspace corners for 3D reconstruction and depth data. Additionally, two RealSense D455 cameras provide egocentric RGB-D views from the robotic wrist, offering close-up details during grasping.
 - **Dexterous Manipulation System:** A UR10e robotic arm manipulates a ShadowHand (a 24 DoF tendon-driven dexterous hand). Crucially, a teleoperation system with <50ms latency is used to control the ShadowHand. This human-in-the-loop approach allows the robotic hand to mirror a human operator's movements, ensuring "natural and human-like dexterous motions" and capturing genuine human behavioral habits for the robot receiver.
- **Data Collection:** The dataset comprises 4282 handover trials, involving 39 human participants and 56 diverse everyday objects (various shapes and sizes). Participants were instructed to move objects freely, allowing for diverse dynamic motion patterns. The dataset includes 456K frames and 79,911 successful final grasp poses.
- **Data Processing and Annotation:** To enable detailed analysis, raw visual data underwent precise 3D annotation:
 - **Object Pose:** SAM2 (Segment Anything Model 2) was used for initial object region segmentation from RGB-D streams. Depth fusion located the object, and point clouds were cropped. Global registration (Open3D) estimated initial poses, which were manually corrected, followed by frame-by-frame ICP (Iterative Closest Point) for fine-grained object pose tracking.
 - **Human Hand Pose:** 2D key points were detected from RGB images using MediaPipe, which then estimated MANO (Model of ANimated Objects) parameters for the human hand.
 - All sensor data and 3D annotations are temporally synchronized.
- **Key Characteristics:** DexH2R is distinguished by: (1) **Rich Multi-Modal Perception Data** (18 RGB views, 6 depth views, ego-centric views, high-resolution point clouds, annotated meshes); (2) **Authentic Human-Object-Robot Interaction** (real human handover, precise object dynamics, physically reproducible robot motions); and (3) **Human-like Dynamic Grasping Behavior** (teleoperation-driven robot actions reflecting human habits).

#### 3.2. DynamicGrasp Solution and Benchmark

DynamicGrasp is a three-stage solution inspired by human object reception:

- **Problem Formulation:** The task is defined as predicting a sequence of dexterous hand states (articulation pose `q` and global pose `θh`) for future `Ta` time steps, given historical `To` observations of the environment (RGB image `I`, depth image `D`, object point cloud `Po`, and current hand state `Sh`).
- **Stage 1: Grasping Pose Preparation:**
 - A Conditional Variational Autoencoder (cVAE) (based on PointNet architecture) generates diverse potential grasping poses. It's pre-trained on a large-scale synthetic dataset (DexGraspNet) and then fine-tuned on the real-world DexH2R data, using observed object point clouds as conditions.
 - A multi-step filtering process refines these candidates:
 - **Physical Stability Test:** Poses are simulated in Isaac Gym, applying forces in six directions to ensure the object remains stable within the grasp.
 - **Collision Detection:** Poses that would collide with the human hand are eliminated.
 - **Closest Pose Selection:** The goal pose `Sh,g` closest to the robot's current hand state is selected to ensure smooth transitions.
- **Stage 2: Approaching Motion Generation:**
 - This stage generates a sequence of intermediate hand poses to guide the robot towards the target object. Two technical paradigms are benchmarked:
 - **Autoregressive Approach (MotionNet Adaptation):** MotionNet, originally for static grasping, is extended. It predicts future hand state changes (`ΔS`) over `Ta` steps based on current hand point cloud, vertex velocities, offset to goal pose, and historical interaction data (hand states, object point clouds).
 - **Diffusion Policy Approach (DP and DP3):** Leverages diffusion models to iteratively denoise trajectory distributions, learning a visuomotor policy.
 - **Diffusion Policy (DP):** Uses 2D RGB and depth images (`I_t`, `D_t`) combined with current hand state (`S_t`) as input.
 - **Diffusion Policy 3D (DP3):** Employs 3D point clouds (`P_o_t`) and hand state (`S_t`) as input, allowing for direct spatial reasoning. Both use previous `k` frames as conditioning for denoising future `m` frames.
- **Stage 3: Goal Pose Alignment:**
 - Once the dexterous hand reaches a predefined proximity to the object (global translation difference `d < U`, where `U` is an interpolation threshold, e.g., 5 or 10 cm), this stage takes over.
 - It uses a simple but effective linear interpolation strategy to smoothly align the hand's current pose with the refined goal grasp pose.
 - The process iteratively updates hand and object states, recomputing the distance until it falls below a tight threshold (1cm), while continuously performing collision checks to ensure safety and physical plausibility.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Metrics and Setup

The benchmark evaluates solutions across three categories: Grasping Pose, Approaching, and an overall assessment, using metrics like success rates (succ1, succ6), penetration depth (pen dep, pen fr), diversity (div), trajectory length (traj len), inference frames (infer fr), and safety rate (safe). Experiments were conducted on an A40 GPU server using Isaac Gym for simulation. The DexH2R dataset was split into training, validation, and test sets, including instances with unseen objects and/or subjects to assess generalization. Two modes were defined for approaching motion evaluation: Easy Mode (alignment starts at U=10cm) and Hard Mode (alignment starts at U=5cm), testing global understanding versus fine-grained precision.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments provide valuable insights into the effectiveness of the DexH2R dataset and the performance of various dynamic grasping approaches.

#### 4.1. Evaluation for Grasping Pose Generation

- The study evaluated the grasping pose generation capabilities using the DexH2R dataset, comprising 56 objects and nearly 80,000 teleoperated grasp poses.
- Two baseline models, cVAE and DexgraspAnything, were compared, with and without pre-training on the much larger synthetic DexGraspNet dataset (5,355 objects, 1.32M poses).
- **Key Finding:** Training directly on the real-world DexH2R dataset achieved comparable, and in some aspects even superior, performance compared to models pre-trained on the extensive synthetic DexGraspNet. For instance, DexgraspAnything without pre-training on synthetic data achieved a `succ1` (success rate under a single random force) of 89.38% versus 88.75% with pre-training. Although `succ6` (success rate under six directional forces) was slightly lower without pre-training (50.62% vs. 52.81%), the penetration depth (lower is better) was also slightly higher (1.83cm vs. 1.64cm).
- **Significance:** This result highlights the high quality and effectiveness of the grasping poses within the DexH2R dataset. Despite its focus on dynamic human-robot handover, the teleoperated grasping data provides physically plausible and stable grasp poses that can effectively train generative models, reducing the reliance on vast synthetic datasets for this specific task.

#### 4.2. Evaluation for Approaching Motion Generation

- Three baseline algorithms—MotionNet (autoregressive), Diffusion Policy (DP), and Diffusion Policy 3D (DP3)—were evaluated on the test set, including unseen subjects and objects, under two difficulty modes for goal pose alignment.
- **Easy Mode (U = 10cm):**
 - **MotionNet:** Achieved the highest success rate (71.1%). However, this came at the cost of safety, exhibiting the lowest safety rate (25.9%) and highest penetration depth (0.72cm) and penetration frames (8.3). This suggests an aggressive strategy prioritizing grasping over collision avoidance.
 - **Diffusion Policy (DP):** Showed the lowest success rate (39.4%) but a higher safety rate (42.6%). This indicates a more conservative strategy, often avoiding collisions but failing to complete the grasp within limits.
 - **Diffusion Policy 3D (DP3):** Struck a better balance, with a reasonable success rate (66.3%) and the highest safety rate (50.1%), along with the lowest penetration frames (5.0).
- **Hard Mode (U = 5cm):** This mode emphasized fine-grained precision and collision avoidance in close proximity.
 - **MotionNet:** Success rate dropped significantly (26.6%), and safety remained low (15.4%), with increased penetration. The aggressive nature struggles with precise close-range alignment.
 - **Diffusion Policy (DP):** Success rate plummeted further (8.3%), maintaining a relatively high safety rate (38.5%), indicating its continued conservative behavior.
 - **Diffusion Policy 3D (DP3):** Maintained a comparable success rate to MotionNet (27.1%) but with a significantly higher safety rate (33.7%) and low penetration frames (9.3).
- **Qualitative Comparison (Figure 5):** The paper also includes a qualitative comparison, visually illustrating the different behaviors of the three methods during approaching motions, further supporting the quantitative findings.
- **Overall Analysis:** DP3 emerged as the most robust and balanced approach for dynamic grasping in human-to-robot handover, especially in the challenging "Hard Mode." Its use of 3D point clouds as input likely enables better perception of precise object positions, allowing it to achieve a good balance between successful grasping and collision avoidance. MotionNet, while effective at rapid grasping, sacrifices safety. DP is overly cautious, often failing to grasp. The Goal Pose Alignment strategy plays a crucial role as a corrective mechanism to enhance the reliability and safety of all methods, ensuring physically plausible grasping.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DexH2R research paper makes several significant contributions that promise to profoundly impact the fields of robotics, human-robot interaction, and dexterous manipulation:

1. **Bridging the Reality Gap in Human-to-Robot Handover:** The most substantial contribution is the introduction of DexH2R, the *first real-world dataset for human-to-robot handover involving dexterous robotic hands*. This directly addresses a critical void in the research landscape. Prior work relied heavily on synthetic data or datasets with limited real-world fidelity, which created a substantial "simulation-to-reality" gap. DexH2R, with its authentic multi-modal sensor data, dynamic motions, and precise annotations, provides an invaluable resource for training and validating models that can operate effectively outside of controlled simulation environments.

2. **Enabling Human-like Dexterous Grasping:** The use of teleoperation during data collection ensures that the robotic hand's motions in DexH2R closely mimic human behaviors and habits. This "human-like dynamic grasping behavior" is crucial for developing intuitive and trustworthy human-robot collaboration. By learning from natural human interaction patterns, future robots can become easier for humans to understand, predict, and collaborate with, fostering greater acceptance and efficiency in shared workspaces. This is a foundational step towards genuinely intelligent humanoid robots.

3. **Establishing a Comprehensive Benchmark:** Beyond the dataset, the paper provides an effective solution, DynamicGrasp, and a thorough benchmark for evaluating dynamic dexterous grasping methods in H2R handover. By implementing and comparing state-of-the-art autoregressive and diffusion policy approaches on DexH2R with well-defined metrics (safety, precision, reliability), the authors provide a standardized framework for future research. This benchmark will accelerate progress by offering a common ground for evaluating new algorithms, identifying their strengths and limitations, and driving innovation towards more robust and adaptive solutions.

4. **Advancing Real-World Applications:** The capabilities enabled by this research have far-reaching implications across various domains:
 - **Household Services:** Robots could seamlessly assist in daily chores, retrieving and accepting objects from family members.
 - **Healthcare:** Robots might assist nurses or doctors by accepting instruments or supplies during complex medical procedures, enhancing efficiency and safety.
 - **Industrial Automation:** More adaptable robots could collaborate closely with human workers on assembly lines, accepting parts or tools in dynamic, unstructured environments.
 - **Logistics:** Robots could more flexibly handle object transfers, reducing human burden and improving throughput.

5. **Catalyzing Future Research:** DexH2R and its accompanying benchmark will undoubtedly stimulate new research directions. Researchers can now develop and test novel perception systems, motion planning algorithms, and control strategies directly on realistic data. It paves the way for exploring areas such as proactive anticipation of human intentions, robust handling of occlusions, learning from diverse human interaction styles, and developing more sophisticated safety protocols for dexterous robot operations.

In conclusion, "DexH2R: A Benchmark for Dynamic Dexterous Grasping in Human-to-Robot Handover" represents a pivotal contribution to robotics. By filling a critical data gap and providing a robust evaluation framework, it significantly moves the field closer to realizing natural, safe, and intelligent human-robot collaboration with dexterous robotic hands.
