# PRIMAL3: Pathfinding via Reinforcement and Imitation Multi-Agent Learning - Leveraging LaCAM3

> [!summary] Summary
> PRIMAL3, from researchers at the National University of Singapore and Stanford University, introduces an ultra-large-scale learning-based framework for Multi-Agent Pathfinding (MAPF) that integrates topology-aware communication, expert-guided training, and priority-aware action refinement. It achieves high success rates for up to 10,000 agents in diverse environments, significantly narrowing the performance gap with traditional search-based methods and demonstrating feasibility for up to 100,000 agents.

## Problem

- Learning-based MAPF methods often use local observations, failing to capture topologically structured and relationally heterogeneous critical interactions.
- Policy uncertainty in critical areas, such as bottlenecks or dead ends, leads to inconsistent decisions, hesitation, and suboptimal coordination.
- Existing collision-free action refinement mechanisms ensure single-timestep safety but do not guarantee long-term coordinated progress due to lost priority information or discarded policy preferences.

## Method

- **PRIMAL3**'s ==dual-graph representation learning== module provides agents with a richer understanding of map topology and heterogeneous interactions through 'conflict' and 'following' graphs and topology-aware features.
- ==LaCAM3-guided confidence boosting== during training refines policy decisions by using a strong search-based solver to provide expert actions when the learned policy exhibits uncertainty.
- A ==priority-aware PIBT-based action refinement== module enhances execution safety and temporal consistency by incorporating persistent agent age, learned priorities, and policy-aware fallback preferences.

## Results

- Achieved strong performance on 32x32 random maps, reaching **0.92** success rate for **300** agents, comparable to state-of-the-art search-based methods like LNS2 and LaCAM3.
- Demonstrated ultra-large-scale coordination with **95.0%** success rate for **10,000** agents, exhibiting an effective per-agent failure hazard approximately **16 times** lower than baseline HMAGAT, and successfully completing stress tests with **50,000** and **100,000** agents.
- Ablation studies confirmed the individual contributions of the modified PIBT shielding, LaCAM3 expert guidance, and dual-graph communication, showing significant drops in success rate (e.g., from **93.5%** to **48.0%** without communication) when components were removed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling heterogeneous interaction types (conflict vs. following) with dual graphs and topology-aware features provides a more granular and relevant representation of multi-agent dynamics, improving coordination in complex environments.
> - Leveraging a strong search-based expert (LaCAM3) to selectively guide policy refinement during training addresses policy uncertainty in critical states, without requiring the expert planner at inference time.
> - Integrating persistent priority information and learned policy preferences into action shielding mechanisms enhances temporal consistency and reduces issues like repeated blocking or arbitrary action repairs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multi-Agent Pathfinding (MAPF) is a problem in multi-robot coordination with applications in various domains, including automated warehouses, traffic management, and intersection control. The field of MAPF broadly consists of traditional search-based methods and more recent learning-based approaches.

Traditional search-based MAPF solvers aim to compute collision-free paths for multiple agents on a graph. These methods are categorized by their solution guarantees: optimal (e.g., M*, Conflict-Based Search - CBS), bounded-suboptimal (e.g., Inflated M*, ECBS, EECBS), and unbounded-suboptimal/scalable (e.g., Large Neighborhood Search - MAPF-LNS, MAPF-LNS2; Priority Inheritance With Backtracking - PIBT; and LaCAM/LaCAM3). While these methods offer theoretical guarantees and strong empirical performance on large instances, they typically compute centralized joint plans. Deviations from these plans often necessitate costly re-planning, which can be a limitation in dynamic robotic systems with environmental changes, execution noise, or incomplete observations. Recent work has also explored finite-horizon and closed-loop MAPF formulations to better handle disturbances.

Learning-based MAPF methods approach the planning process as a policy learning task, allowing generalization across different maps and agent configurations. Early methods, such as PRIMAL, combined reinforcement learning (RL) with imitation learning (IL). Subsequent advancements have focused on enhancing observations (e.g., G2RL, MAPPER, DHC, ALPHA), introducing communication mechanisms (e.g., PICO, SCRIMP, DCC, SIGMA, SYLPH), and developing more expressive policy architectures. Recent trends in learning-based MAPF include purely imitation learning approaches using graph neural networks (e.g., MAGAT), integration of distributed expert algorithms as post-processing collision-shielding modules (e.g., methods using PIBT for refinement), and large-scale foundation model-inspired frameworks (e.g., MAPF-GPT, HMAGAT).

Despite progress, learning-based MAPF faces challenges. Existing methods may not fully capture the topologically structured and relationally heterogeneous nature of critical MAPF interactions, where local observations alone may be insufficient. Policy uncertainty, particularly in constrained areas like bottlenecks or dead ends, can lead to inconsistent decisions and suboptimal coordination. Furthermore, while execution-time shielding mechanisms like PIBT ensure collision-free actions, they do not inherently guarantee coordinated long-term progress, as priorities may be reinitialized or policy preferences discarded during fallback action selection.

PRIMAL3 positions itself within this landscape by seeking to address these specific challenges. It aims to develop an ultra-large-scale learning-based framework that integrates advanced communication, expert-guided training, and refined action shielding to overcome the limitations of prior learning-based approaches and narrow the performance gap with traditional search-based solvers, particularly in highly congested and topologically challenging environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an ultra-large-scale learning-based framework, named PRIMAL3, for Multi-Agent Pathfinding (MAPF). The motivation for PRIMAL3 stems from three identified challenges in existing learning-based MAPF methods, particularly concerning coordination in complex and dense environments.

First, the authors observed that critical MAPF interactions are often topologically structured and relationally heterogeneous. Local occupancy observations in many learning-based methods do not explicitly reveal crucial topological features such as cut vertices, dead-end regions, or positions that block other agents. While reference-path overlap can identify potential interactions, it does not differentiate the *type* of coordination required. For instance, agents moving in the same direction may benefit from coherent motion, while those approaching a shared constrained region from conflicting directions require differentiated decisions (e.g., proceeding, yielding, or waiting). Existing homogeneous aggregation methods in communication may blur these relational differences. Therefore, a key motivation was to develop a communication mechanism that accounts for both map topology and heterogeneous agent interactions.

Second, the paper identifies that policy uncertainty is particularly problematic in situations demanding decisive coordination. In open areas, multiple actions might be equally effective, and high policy entropy may have minor consequences. However, near bottlenecks or dead ends, successful coordination necessitates clear right-of-way or commitment to specific avoidance behaviors. Reinforcement Learning (RL) objectives often provide only indirect supervision for such critical decisions, potentially leading to policies that assign similar probabilities to several locally plausible actions. This can result in inconsistent decisions, hesitation, blocking, or oscillatory behavior. This motivated the development of targeted expert guidance to refine the policy specifically when its action preferences remain uncertain.

Third, while collision-free action refinement mechanisms, such as PIBT-based shields, ensure that proposed actions are safe for a single timestep, they do not inherently ensure coordinated progress over longer durations. When decentralized policies propose actions independently, conflicts arise, which these shields resolve. However, if PIBT is reinitialized at every timestep, priority information regarding previous delays is lost, potentially leading to some agents being repeatedly blocked. Moreover, if the shield only receives a discrete action, the policy's relative preferences for alternative actions are discarded, causing fallback decisions to rely on transient priorities or arbitrary tie-breaking. This motivated the creation of a practical shielding mechanism that maintains priority information across timesteps and preserves the learned policy's fallback preferences.

In summary, PRIMAL3 aims to integrate structured communication, expert-guided training, and priority-aware action refinement to overcome these limitations, enabling scalable, robust, and efficient multi-agent pathfinding in challenging, ultra-large-scale environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PRIMAL3 is designed as a learning-based framework for Multi-Agent Pathfinding (MAPF) that integrates reinforcement learning with topological awareness, expert guidance, and action refinement. Its methodology encompasses three main components: dual-graph representation learning, LaCAM3-guided confidence boosting, and priority-aware PIBT-based action refinement.

#### 3.1. A. Dual-Graph Representation Learning

This component aims to provide agents with a richer understanding of map topology and heterogeneous interactions.

1. **Reference Path and Direction Encoding**: For each agent, an individual A\* path from its current position to its goal is computed. These reference paths are padded to a common length, and each transition along the path is assigned a discrete motion direction (stay, right, up, left, down). This provides a foundational estimate of navigation intentions and allows for the classification of pairwise interactions.

2. **Conflict and Following Graph Construction**: Two directed weighted adjacency matrices, A\_conf and A\_foll, are constructed to capture different types of interactions.
 - **Following Graph**: Quantifies "same-direction" overlaps between reference paths, where agents move along compatible paths.
 - **Conflict Graph**: Captures "opposite-direction," "different-direction," or "stationary-blocking" overlaps, indicating competition for shared space.
 - Interaction scores are accumulated based on path overlaps, with temporal discount factors (γ\_conf = 0.9, γ\_foll = 0.5) applied. A higher discount for conflicts emphasizes anticipating future interferences, while a smaller discount for following focuses on immediate cooperation.
 - These dense matrices are then used to select sparse and temporally stable communication partners. Each agent identifies its most relevant conflict and following neighbor, and these fixed neighbors are updated only if the previous interaction disappears. This mechanism creates two sparse communication graphs, G\_conf\_comm and G\_foll\_comm, for each timestep.

3. **Node Feature Construction**: To expose topological constraints, each agent is represented by a seven-dimensional topology-aware feature vector. These features include:
 - Cut-goal indicator (if goal is a cut vertex).
 - Cut-vertex count along the reference path.
 - Hard-blocking count (number of other agents whose path connectivity is severed by agent's goal).
 - Soft-blocking count (number of other agents whose path contains the current vertex of the ego agent).
 - Goal distance (shortest-path distance).
 - Goal-in-dead-end indicator.
 - Agent-in-dead-end indicator.
 These features, derived from precomputed environmental maps (e.g., degree, dead-end, and cut-vertex maps) and A\* paths, provide explicit structural information.

4. **Dual-Graph Communication**: A dual-branch graph communication module processes these two interaction structures:
 - **Following-Graph Aggregation**: For compatible path structures, a multihop aggregation scheme (inspired by Hop2Token) is used. It computes first- and second-order messages based on a degree-normalized adjacency matrix. A feature-wise gate adaptively combines these two message scales to create a following-aware representation (H\_foll), supporting coherent decisions along shared routes.
 - **Conflict-Graph Aggregation**: For agents competing for shared space, masked attention is applied over the conflict graph. The conflict message (m\_conf) explicitly encodes the ego agent's feature, the attended conflict context, their difference, and their element-wise interaction. This preserves relative information to support differentiated decisions.
 - **Adaptive Fusion**: Independent feature-wise gates combine the following and conflict branch outputs with the original agent features to produce a final updated representation (˜x), which is then passed to the policy network.

#### 3.2. B. LaCAM3-Guided Confidence Boosting

This training-only component uses a strong search-based solver, LaCAM3, to refine the policy's decisions, especially in uncertain states.

1. **Entropy-based Uncertainty Detection**: The uncertainty of an agent's policy is quantified using the entropy of its action distribution. An agent is identified as uncertain if its action entropy exceeds a predefined threshold (η = 0.464, derived from a confident reference distribution [0.9, 0.025...]).

2. **Confidence-Triggered Expert Intervention**: Training is divided into two phases. The first is pure RL. In the second phase, LaCAM3 provides coordinated action guidance.
 - **Online-replanning segment**: LaCAM3 is invoked at each timestep. Its expert actions are executed for agents identified as uncertain, while confident agents use their learned policy's actions.
 - **Cached-plan segment**: After a switching timestep, LaCAM3 is invoked once to generate a plan for remaining agents, which is then cached and executed for these agents for the rest of the episode. This exposes the policy to coordinated completion behavior in difficult late-episode states.

3. **Expert-Supervised Policy Refinement**: A behavior cloning (BC) loss is introduced during the second training phase for all agent-timestep pairs where expert supervision is enabled. The expert actions are converted into label-smoothed categorical targets (e.g., 0.9 for the expert action, 0.025 for others) to provide a supervisory signal that encourages decisive, coordinated actions without imposing a hard one-hot target. The total training loss is a combination of the RL loss and this BC loss. LaCAM3 is used exclusively during training, not inference.

#### 3.3. C. Priority-Aware PIBT-based Action Refinement

This module refines proposed actions at execution time to ensure safety and improve coordination consistency.

1. **Persistent Agent Priority Initialization**: The standard PIBT algorithm is augmented with persistent priority information. An environment-side "age" variable for each agent tracks the number of consecutive timesteps it has been unfinished, resetting when it reaches its goal. The initial PIBT priority for an agent is a weighted sum of this persistent age, a learned scalar priority from the neural network, and its normalized shortest-path distance to goal. This ensures that agents waiting longer or those with longer paths receive precedence.

2. **Policy-Aware Action Candidate Ordering**: Instead of only a discrete action, PIBT receives an action-preference vector that preserves the learned policy's relative preferences over fallback actions. The policy's primary selected action is given a score strictly higher than all others, and remaining actions are ranked according to their learned probabilities. If the primary action causes a conflict, PIBT selects among the alternatives based on these preferences, reducing reliance on arbitrary tie-breaking.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of PRIMAL3 involved extensive experiments on various map types, agent densities, and scales, along with ablation studies and real-robot deployments.

#### 4.1. A. Training Setup

Experiments were conducted on a workstation with an AMD Ryzen 9 9950X3D CPU and an NVIDIA RTX 5090 GPU. A curriculum learning approach was used during training, starting with 8 agents and gradually increasing to up to 14 agents. Map sizes were sampled from 10x10 to 30x30. The model was optimized using Adam. For testing, 32x32 random maps (obstacle density ~0.3) and maze maps (obstacle density ~0.5) were used, with a maximum episode horizon of 512 timesteps. Performance was measured by success rate (SR), average episode length (EL), and arrival rate (AR).

#### 4.2. B. Comparison Results (32x32 Maps)

PRIMAL3 was compared against search-based solvers (LaCAM, LaCAM3, LNS2) and learning-based methods (MAPF-GPT, SYLPH, HMAGAT).

- **Random Maps (50 to 300 agents)**: PRIMAL3 demonstrated strong overall performance among learning-based methods. Its success rate remained high, achieving 1.00 for 50 agents and 0.92 for 300 agents. This performance was comparable to state-of-the-art traditional search-based methods like LNS2 and LaCAM3, indicating a substantial reduction in the performance gap. PRIMAL3 consistently outperformed HMAGAT, especially with larger agent populations, and maintained an arrival rate close to 1.0. While its episode length was sometimes higher than HMAGAT's at very high agent counts, this was attributed to successful conflict resolution through waiting or detouring rather than unresolved instances.
- **Maze Maps (8 to 256 agents)**: Maze maps presented a greater challenge due to narrow passages and topological constraints. PRIMAL3 achieved the best or near-best performance among learning-based methods for small to medium scales (SR of 1.00 for 8 agents, 0.935 for 64 agents), surpassing SYLPH, MAPF-GPT, and HMAGAT. At 128 agents, PRIMAL3's SR decreased to approximately 0.5, though its AR remained near 1.0, indicating most agents still reached their goals. At 256 agents, HMAGAT achieved higher SR and AR, but all learning-based methods showed low SR in this extreme congestion, highlighting a common limitation. Search-based solvers, particularly LNS2 and LaCAM3, generally maintained higher performance at larger agent populations in maze environments.

#### 4.3. C. Ultra-Large-Scale Experiments

To assess scalability, PRIMAL3 was tested with 1,000 to 10,000 agents on correspondingly larger maps while maintaining constant obstacle and agent densities. HMAGAT was used as the primary learning-based baseline.

- PRIMAL3 maintained success rates between 95.0% and 99.0% across all evaluated scales, achieving 95.0% even with 10,000 agents. In contrast, HMAGAT's success rate declined from 83.5% at 1,000 agents to 25.0% at 10,000 agents.
- Analysis of the "effective failure load" (λ\_N = -ln SR\_N) showed that HMAGAT's λ\_N grew approximately linearly with the number of agents, implying a constant per-agent failure hazard. PRIMAL3, however, kept λ\_N below 0.052, with an effective per-agent hazard approximately 16 times lower. This difference was attributed to PRIMAL3's task-conditioned and temporally persistent coordination mechanisms, including its sparse communication graphs and the persistence of selected conflict partners.
- Furthermore, PRIMAL3 successfully completed two single-instance stress tests with 50,000 and 100,000 agents, demonstrating coordination feasibility at an unprecedented scale for learning-based MAPF, approaching city-level multi-agent systems.

#### 4.4. D. Ablation Study

Ablation studies on 32x32 maze maps with 64 agents quantified the contribution of PRIMAL3's components:

- **Modified PIBT Shielding**: Removing the modified PIBT shield reduced the SR from 93.5% to 88.0%, indicating its role in providing effective protection against invalid or poorly coordinated actions.
- **LaCAM3 Expert Guidance**: Removing LaCAM3 guidance, leading to pure RL training, further reduced the SR to 77.0%, demonstrating that expert-guided learning improves coordination reliability.
- **Dual-Graph Communication**: Using both conflict and following graphs achieved an SR of 77.0%. Using only the following graph resulted in 71.5% SR, and only the conflict graph yielded 74.0% SR. This indicates that both graphs capture complementary interaction patterns, with the conflict graph being slightly more critical in dense maze environments.
- **No Communication**: Removing both communication graphs led to a substantial degradation, with SR dropping to 48.0%, confirming the importance of inter-agent communication.
- **No Heuristic Features**: Further removal of heuristic features reduced SR to 23.5%, highlighting the importance of this prior information for navigation.
- **Decay Factors Analysis**: Varying the temporal decay factors (γ\_conf, γ\_foll) for graph construction revealed that the conflict decay factor (γ\_conf) was essential, with an optimal value of 0.9. A value of 0 resulted in significantly lower success rates (60.5-68.0%). The following decay factor (γ\_foll) had a milder effect, with 0.5 identified as a suitable value. This supported the design choice of propagating conflict information over longer horizons and following information for short-term consistency.

#### 4.5. E. Real-Robot Experiments

Two proof-of-concept experiments demonstrated PRIMAL3's deployability on physical robots:
- Eight physical robots on a 10x10 random map.
- A mixed-reality experiment with eight physical robots and 92 virtual agents on a 32x32 map.
Both experiments were successfully executed using a low-level controller (P3GASUS) for grid cell movements, indicating the feasibility of simulation-to-real transfer for PRIMAL3.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PRIMAL3 represents a notable development in the field of Multi-Agent Pathfinding (MAPF), particularly for learning-based approaches. Its significance stems from several key advancements and demonstrated capabilities.

Firstly, the framework introduces a novel combination of architectural and training strategies that address persistent challenges in learning-based MAPF. The dual-graph communication mechanism, which distinguishes between path-compatible "following" interactions and path-competing "conflict" interactions, along with topology-aware node features, provides a more granular and relevant representation of multi-agent dynamics than many prior methods. This relational and topological awareness contributes to more effective coordination, especially in environments with complex constraints like bottlenecks and dead ends.

Secondly, the integration of LaCAM3-guided confidence boosting during training is a significant contribution. By leveraging a strong search-based planner as a training-time expert, PRIMAL3 can selectively intervene when the learned policy exhibits uncertainty, thereby improving the reliability of agent decisions in critical situations. This strategy allows the policy to retain the exploratory benefits of reinforcement learning while incorporating targeted expert supervision to resolve ambiguity, without requiring the expert planner at inference time.

Thirdly, the proposed priority-aware PIBT-based action refinement module enhances execution safety and temporal consistency. By incorporating persistent age information, learned coordination priorities, and policy-aware fallback preferences into the PIBT mechanism, PRIMAL3 moves beyond mere one-step collision avoidance. This refinement reduces issues like repeated blocking and arbitrary action repairs, which are common in dense environments and can hinder overall system progress.

The potential impact of PRIMAL3 is considerable, particularly in its demonstrated scalability. The ability to coordinate up to 10,000 agents with high success rates in large-scale simulations, and even completing stress tests with 50,000 and 100,000 agents, positions PRIMAL3 as a framework capable of addressing "city-level" multi-agent coordination problems. This scalability represents an advancement for learning-based MAPF, significantly narrowing the performance gap with traditional search-based solvers, particularly on random maps, and offering robust coordination in complex maze-like environments.

Furthermore, the successful real-robot experiments provide proof-of-concept for the deployability of PRIMAL3 on physical multi-robot systems. This suggests that the framework's learned policies can effectively transfer from simulation to the real world, which is crucial for practical applications in areas such as automated logistics, traffic management, and large-scale autonomous drone operations.

Despite these achievements, the authors acknowledge limitations and outline future work. The reliance on manually designed high-level features (e.g., cut vertices, dead-end regions) for topological awareness suggests a future direction in learning scalable, structured representations directly from raw observations, potentially through auxiliary prediction or teacher-student distillation. Additionally, the computational overhead of repeatedly querying LaCAM3 during training motivates the exploration of more sample-efficient imitation learning methods, such as distilling complete expert trajectories and leveraging offline datasets. Addressing these limitations could further enhance the generality, efficiency, and autonomy of learning-based MAPF solutions, solidifying PRIMAL3's contribution to the development of robust and scalable multi-agent coordination systems.
