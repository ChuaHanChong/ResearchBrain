# Learning constitutive models and rheology from partial flow measurements

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework combines differentiable fluid simulations with a Tensor Basis Neural Network to learn constitutive models and rheology directly from local flow measurements, demonstrating accurate generalization to unseen geometries and robustness to data degradation. This approach further enables the distillation of complex learned constitutive laws into interpretable classical models, effectively performing "digital rheometry" from diverse flow observations.

## Problem

- Traditional rheometry, relying on idealized flows and bulk measurements, often fails to accurately characterize complex fluids in real-world scenarios, leading to models that lack generalizability and predictive power in mixed kinematics or complex geometries.
- Existing data-driven approaches, such as machine learning on bulk rheometer data or Physics-Informed Neural Networks, face limitations including overfitting, lack of transferability, geometry-specificity, or prohibitive computational costs for broad model selection.
- There is a need to bridge the gap between flexible, data-driven constitutive representations and the requirement for physically interpretable rheological models, particularly for in-situ or in-line material characterization.
- Bulk rheological measurements provide an "information bottleneck," offering insufficient data to fully constrain complex constitutive models, which can lead to ambiguous model selection when multiple models fit simple data equally well but diverge in complex scenarios.

## Method

- A fully ==differentiable non-Newtonian fluid solver==, built in ==JAX== with an ==immersed-boundary method==, was developed to simulate complex fluid flows in arbitrary geometries and provide exact gradients of flow observables with respect to model parameters.
- A frame-invariant ==Tensor Basis Neural Network (TBNN)== was embedded directly into the differentiable solver to learn the constitutive stress-strain relationship from local flow measurements, parameterized by a bounded neural network for viscosity.
- A ==differentiable model-fitting== framework was implemented using a ==differentiable ODE solver (Diffrax)== to distill the learned TBNN response or directly fit experimental rheometer data to an ensemble of classical constitutive models, using the ==Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC)== for model selection.

## Results

- The TBNN, integrated with the differentiable solver, successfully learned the underlying stress law of a Carreau–Yasuda fluid from pressure-driven flow in a complex channel, reconstructing velocity fields with **near-perfect agreement** to ground truth.
- The learned TBNN demonstrated strong generalization, accurately predicting flow in an unseen bidisperse porous medium geometry, and showed robustness to significant data degradation, including over **tenfold** decrease in spatial resolution and up to **4%** correlated velocity-field noise.
- The differentiable model-fitting framework accurately distilled the TBNN's learned response into classical constitutive laws and achieved high identification accuracy (e.g., **70%** for Linear PTT) for **five** common rheological models when applied to noisy synthetic bulk rheometer data, effectively recovering their parameters.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Embedding physics-informed neural network constitutive closures, like TBNNs, directly within a differentiable fluid solver provides a strong inductive bias, enabling robust and efficient learning of material laws from potentially sparse or noisy local flow measurements.
> - Learning local, frame-invariant constitutive relationships through TBNNs allows for geometry-agnostic material models that can effectively generalize and predict fluid behavior in unseen flow conditions and complex geometries.
> - The combination of flexible data-driven constitutive modeling with a differentiable framework for fitting classical models and using information criteria (BIC) effectively bridges the gap between predictive accuracy and physical interpretability in rheological characterization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Constitutive laws are fundamental to predicting the behavior of non-Newtonian fluids across various applications, from biological systems to industrial processes. These laws mathematically describe the relationship between fluid stress and deformation, providing essential closure to the universal principles of mass and momentum conservation. Traditionally, the characterization of fluid rheology relies on benchtop rheometers, which measure a fluid's bulk response in simplified, often one-dimensional, idealized flows. This approach facilitates the selection of mathematical model forms and the inference of material parameters.

However, this traditional workflow faces several limitations in accurately capturing the behavior of complex fluids in real-world scenarios. Models calibrated in idealized conditions often fail to predict fluid behavior in complex geometries or under mixed kinematics (combinations of shear and extension), leading to discrepancies. This is partly due to the fact that multiple constitutive laws can yield similar responses under standard test conditions, only to diverge significantly in more complex flow regimes, making model selection ambiguous. Furthermore, traditional rheometry typically provides averaged, low-dimensional data, which may lack the information content necessary to constrain parameters across the relevant range of conditions.

These challenges have driven the development of data-driven approaches. One class of methods applies machine learning to averaged rheometer data, using neural networks (e.g., Rheological Universal Differential Equations – RUDEs, and Rheology Informed Neural Networks – RhINNs) to replace components of classical constitutive laws. While these approaches can interpolate within fitted regimes, they may struggle with generalization due to the limited and sparse nature of bulk rheometry data, potentially leading to non-unique or physically ungrounded solutions, especially when inferring a large number of network weights from scalar measurements.

Another line of research focuses on inferring constitutive laws directly from local velocity fields. Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) have been employed to solve coupled flow and constitutive equations while assimilating experimental data. While powerful for model discovery, PINNs are often geometry-specific and lack transferability due to their reliance on soft physics constraints and specific boundary conditions. Adjoint-based inverse methods offer a rigorous alternative by fitting specific constitutive models (e.g., Carreau model) to velocity data. However, these methods are computationally expensive, requiring the formulation and solution of an adjoint problem for each candidate model, which becomes prohibitive for broad model selection in complex geometries.

This work positions itself to address these limitations by developing an end-to-end framework that integrates a differentiable non-Newtonian fluid solver with a data-driven constitutive model (Tensor Basis Neural Network – TBNN) and a differentiable model-fitting framework. This aims to provide a geometry-agnostic, frame-invariant material representation learned from local flow measurements, while also enabling the distillation of this complex representation into interpretable classical models. The goal is to bridge the gap between data-driven flexibility and physically interpretable rheology, moving towards "digital rheometry" directly within a fluid's operating environment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an end-to-end framework that leverages automatic differentiation through full physics simulations to learn constitutive models and rheology from partial flow measurements. This framework aims to enable form-agnostic stress-strain mappings and subsequently distill these closures into symbolic, interpretable forms.

The motivation for this work arises from several limitations in current rheological characterization practices:

- **Inadequacy of Traditional Rheometry for Complex Flows:** Standard rheological characterization relies on measurements in idealized flow geometries that often fail to replicate the complex kinematics and conditions encountered in industrial and biological settings. This can lead to constitutive models that perform well in simple shear but inaccurately predict behavior in mixed shear-extensional flows or complex geometries, such as those found in micro-vessels or oil extraction.
- **Information Bottleneck of Bulk Measurements:** Benchtop rheometers typically provide averaged, bulk measurements, which often contain insufficient information to fully constrain the parameters of complex constitutive models. This "information bottleneck" can result in ambiguous model selection, where multiple models fit simple data equally well but diverge significantly in predictive power for complex scenarios.
- **Limitations of Existing Data-Driven Approaches:**
 - **Overfitting and Lack of Generalizability:** Data-driven methods trained solely on sparse, low-dimensional rheometry data tend to overfit and struggle to generalize to unseen flow conditions or geometries, potentially leading to non-unique or physically ungrounded solutions.
 - **Geometry Dependence:** Methods like Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs), while powerful for model discovery, often become geometry-specific due to their reliance on soft physics constraints and specific boundary conditions, hindering their transferability.
 - **Computational Expense of Inverse Problems:** Adjoint-based inverse methods, while rigorous, require formulating and solving a new adjoint problem for each constitutive model considered, making broad model selection computationally prohibitive, especially in complex geometries.
- **Need for Interpretability:** While data-driven models can offer high predictive accuracy, they often lack the physical interpretability inherent in classical constitutive laws. There is a need for methods that can bridge this gap, translating complex data-driven closures into physically meaningful parameters and model forms.
- **Enable "Digital Rheometry":** The overarching motivation is to move rheological characterization beyond the laboratory benchtop and into the actual operating environment of the fluid. This "digital rheometry" would enable in-line or on-chip characterization, particularly for materials that are difficult to sample or altered by conventional rheometry.

By developing a framework that learns local, frame-invariant material responses from any flow observable and then distills this into interpretable symbolic forms, the researchers aim to overcome these limitations, providing a foundation for comprehensive and context-specific fluid characterization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research presents a two-part framework for learning constitutive models and rheology, integrating differentiable simulations with data-driven modeling and symbolic distillation.

**Part 1: Learning Constitutive Behavior from Local Flow Measurements using a Tensor Basis Neural Network (TBNN) and Differentiable Simulation**

1. **Differentiable Non-Newtonian Fluid Solver:**
 - **Foundation:** The core of the framework is a fully differentiable non-Newtonian fluid solver implemented in JAX, building upon established components from JAX-CFD and immersed-boundary (IB) methods. This solver enables automatic differentiation through the entire simulation, providing exact gradients of any flow observable with respect to model parameters.
 - **Numerical Scheme:** It employs a staggered Cartesian grid, second-order central differences for diffusive terms, conservative upwind discretization for advection, and a projection step for incompressibility.
 - **Spatially Varying Viscosity:** The solver was extended to handle spatially varying viscosities, a feature crucial for non-Newtonian fluids. It utilizes fully implicit (backward Euler) integration for wall-bounded flows and semi-implicit (IMEX) schemes for unbounded domains to manage stiffness.
 - **Geometry Handling:** An immersed-boundary formulation allows for simulations in arbitrary geometries and boundary conditions, facilitating training on complex or experimentally reconstructed flow domains.
 - **Validation:** The solver was validated against OpenFOAM simulations for classical generalized-Newtonian models (power-law and Carreau–Yasuda fluids) in steady Poiseuille flow, demonstrating quantitative agreement.

2. **Tensor Basis Neural Network (TBNN) Constitutive Closure:**
 - **Frame Invariance:** To learn a geometry-agnostic and physically admissible stress-strain relationship, a TBNN is embedded directly into the differentiable flow solver. The TBNN expresses the stress tensor (σ) as a sum over invariant tensorial bases (B_i) weighted by scalar functions (α_i) of flow invariants (I). This construction inherently guarantees Galilean invariance.
 - **Simplified Basis for GNF:** For the 2D incompressible flow and Carreau-Yasuda (a Generalized Newtonian Fluid) training data used in this study, the full basis set simplifies, focusing on the rate-of-strain tensor (D) and its first invariant (I_1 = tr(D^2)), as rotational components (W, I_2 = tr(W^2)) are negligible for the fluid type investigated. The stress closure takes the form σ = 2 η(I_1) D.
 - **Viscosity Parameterization:** The viscosity η(I_1) is parameterized by a monotone, bounded neural network head, consisting of a mixture of 12 logistic modes. This ensures positivity of viscosity and provides flexibility to capture nonlinear shear-thinning/thickening responses. The mode parameters are generated by a 16-unit Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), resulting in 646 trainable parameters.

3. **Training Protocol:**
 - **Data Generation:** Ground-truth flow fields are generated by forward simulations of a canonical shear-thinning Carreau–Yasuda (CY) model in a pressure-driven constriction–expansion channel containing a semi-circular obstacle. This geometry provides spatially varying shear rates, offering an information-rich dataset.
 - **Objective Function:** The TBNN is trained to minimize a loss function comprising data fidelity (L_data), a scale-invariant shape term (L_shape), and mild regularization terms (L_slope, L_curv) that discourage excessive shear-thinning and overly bumpy viscosity profiles.
 - **Optimization:** Training involves optimizing the TBNN weights (and initially η_∞) using automatic differentiation through the full simulation. A two-stage schedule is used: first adjusting η_∞, then training the network's shape and curvature parameters while η_0 is fixed.
 - **Robustness Evaluation:** The framework's robustness is tested by degrading synthetic training data to mimic micro-PIV conditions, including down-sampling resolution (Hann-windowed averaging) and adding correlated, heteroskedastic Gaussian noise to the velocity fields.

**Part 2: Model Interpretation and Discovery through Differentiable Model Fitting**

1. **Differentiable Model-Fitting Framework:**
 - **Purpose:** This component provides interpretability by enabling the fitting of classical constitutive laws to stress time-series data. It is used both to distill the learned TBNN response into an interpretable model and to directly fit experimental rheometer data.
 - **ODE Simplification:** For one-dimensional shear rheometer data, the partial differential equations (PDEs) of viscoelastic models (e.g., Oldroyd-B, Giesekus, Linear PTT) can be simplified into ordinary differential equations (ODEs), assuming spatial homogeneity and pure azimuthal flow.
 - **Rheometric Simulation:** For a prescribed strain rate (e.g., sinusoidal forcing), the framework integrates the time-only constitutive equations forward to predict all six stress components for a given set of material parameters.
 - **Loss Function and Optimization:** A mean squared error loss function (L) is used to quantify the difference between predicted and measured shear stress. Parameter optimization is performed via gradient descent using a differentiable ODE solver (Diffrax), which provides automatic gradients of the loss with respect to material parameters.

2. **Model Selection using Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC):**
 - **Criterion:** After fitting an ensemble of candidate classical models (Newtonian, Carreau–Yasuda, Oldroyd-B, Giesekus, Linear PTT) to a dataset, the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) is used for model selection. BIC balances the goodness of fit (log-likelihood) with model complexity (number of parameters), favoring more parsimonious models.
 - **Protocol:** Synthetic noisy rheometer data is generated for 100 random instantiations of five common constitutive models under specific sinusoidal shear rate forcings. Each dataset is then fit with all five candidate models, and the best-fit model is identified using BIC. Initial guesses for parameters are set to one, and an ADAM optimizer is used for minimization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the framework's ability to learn, generalize, and interpret constitutive models:

1. **Successful Learning of Constitutive Laws from Complex Flow:**
 - The TBNN, embedded within the differentiable solver, successfully learned the underlying stress law of a Carreau–Yasuda fluid from observations of pressure-driven flow in a complex constriction-expansion channel.
 - During training, the loss decreased by over four orders of magnitude, indicating convergence to a consistent constitutive representation.
 - The velocity field reconstructed by the trained TBNN was visually indistinguishable from the ground truth, with axial velocity profiles at the constriction throat showing near-perfect agreement (Fig. 2).

2. **Generalization to Unseen Geometries and Conditions:**
 - The trained TBNN demonstrated transferability by accurately predicting flow in an unseen bidisperse porous medium geometry under a different pressure gradient (G=7.5, compared to G=5 for training).
 - The relative error between the TBNN-predicted and ground-truth velocity fields was uniformly low across all strain rates, with the smallest deviations observed in the range where the training signal was strongest (10^-1 < γ̇ < 10^1) (Fig. 3).

3. **Robustness to Data Degradation:**
 - The framework proved robust to degraded spatial resolution, maintaining predictive accuracy (quantified by Relative Root-Mean-Squared Error, RRMSE_u) even with an over tenfold decrease in resolution, down to 13x13 interrogation windows (Fig. S4).
 - It also demonstrated robustness to measurement noise, maintaining low RRMSE_u even with correlated, heteroskedastic velocity-field noise as high as 4% of the 95th-percentile flow velocity. This robustness was attributed to the strong physical regularization provided by the differentiable fluid solver, which prevented overfitting to noisy data (Fig. S5, S6).

4. **Successful Model Interpretation from TBNN Closure:**
 - The differentiable model-fitting method successfully distilled the TBNN closure into classical constitutive laws. By probing the trained TBNN with prescribed oscillatory shear-rate forcings ("digital rheometer"), the method accurately identified the Carreau–Yasuda model and recovered its parameters.
 - For the Carreau–Yasuda fluid used in training, the parameters (e.g., shear-thinning exponent 'n' and transition timescale 'k') recovered from the TBNN closely matched the ground-truth values across eight different runs with varying shear-thinning characteristics (Table S1, Fig. 4).
 - The Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) consistently and strongly favored the Carreau–Yasuda model over a Newtonian model, confirming that the more complex model provided a statistically superior description of the TBNN's learned response.

5. **Robust Model Selection from Bulk Rheometry Data:**
 - When applied to noisy synthetic bulk rheometer data, the differentiable model-fitting framework achieved high identification accuracy across five common constitutive models (Newtonian, Carreau–Yasuda, Oldroyd-B, Giesekus, and Linear PTT) (Fig. 6a).
 - For simpler models, identification was successful in nearly all cases. For the more complex Linear PTT model, accuracy was 70%.
 - Misidentification cases for complex models were correlated with specific parameter regimes where the model's response was statistically indistinguishable from a simpler alternative under the applied forcing protocol. This highlighted that model identifiability is contingent on the experimental design and the rheological regimes probed.
 - Parameter estimation for correctly identified models was accurate, with geometric median factors (estimate/true) within 1% for most parameters of Newtonian, Oldroyd-B, and Giesekus fluids (Table S2). Carreau–Yasuda parameters showed larger deviations, particularly for η_∞, which was attributed to insufficient sampling of high-shear behavior by the sinusoidal forcings, consistent with the identifiability limitations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work establishes a framework for learning constitutive behavior directly from flow measurements through differentiable simulations, presenting several significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Foundation for "Digital Rheometry":** The framework creates a foundation for comprehensive rheological characterization directly within a fluid's operating environment, termed "digital rheometry." This means that complex flow measurements, which were previously challenging to interpret for constitutive discovery, can now be rigorously converted into a tool for model selection and parameter inference. This approach can bypass the limitations of traditional benchtop rheometry, particularly where sampling is impractical or alters the material (e.g., emulsions, polymer melts, multiphase suspensions).

2. **Geometry-Agnostic and Transferable Constitutive Models:** By embedding a Tensor Basis Neural Network (TBNN) within a differentiable fluid solver, the framework learns a local, frame-invariant mapping from kinematics to stress. This results in a constitutive representation that is portable across different flow conditions and geometries. Such models can be embedded in new simulations to predict fluid behavior in environments unseen during training, offering a significant advantage over geometry-specific data-driven methods.

3. **Bridging Data-Driven Flexibility and Physical Interpretability:** The two-part nature of the framework effectively combines the flexibility of neural networks in capturing complex, unknown constitutive relationships with the interpretability of classical physical models. The ability to distill a learned TBNN response into an interpretable classical model form, complete with physical parameters and selected via information criteria, provides both predictive power and mechanistic insight.

4. **Enhanced Experimental Design and Optimization:** The fully differentiable nature of the solver offers a path towards optimized experimental design. The gradients used for parameter fitting can also be utilized to quantify the information content of a given forcing protocol or geometry. This capability can facilitate the design of inputs that maximally discriminate between competing constitutive hypotheses or minimize parameter uncertainty, automating what has historically been an intuitive and labor-intensive aspect of rheological characterization. This represents a closed-loop approach between measurement, modeling, and optimization.

5. **Robustness to Real-World Data Challenges:** The demonstrated robustness of the TBNN to coarse spatial resolution and significant measurement noise underscores a practical advantage. The enforcement of governing physics as hard constraints within the differentiable solver provides a powerful inductive bias, reducing the need for extensive datasets and increasing resilience to data imperfections often encountered in experimental velocimetry.

6. **Potential for Broader Constitutive Discovery:** Beyond fitting existing models, the framework holds potential for discovering novel constitutive laws when classical forms are insufficient. Learned responses from data-driven closures could be distilled into compact analytical forms through symbolic regression or mechanistic constraints, advancing the fundamental understanding of complex fluid behaviors.

7. **Applicability Across Engineering and Physical Sciences:** The methodology, being general in its approach to constitutive discovery, has broad applicability across various domains in engineering and physical sciences where understanding and predicting complex material behavior under flow are critical.

Future work includes extending the differentiable solver to incorporate additional physics, such as viscoelastic memory and interfacial multiphase flows, further broadening the scope of its applicability to real-world fluids and operating environments.
