# Learning constitutive models and rheology from partial flow measurements

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework combines differentiable fluid simulations with a Tensor Basis Neural Network to learn constitutive models and rheology directly from local flow measurements, demonstrating accurate generalization to unseen geometries and robustness to data degradation. This approach further enables the distillation of complex learned constitutive laws into interpretable classical models, effectively performing "digital rheometry" from diverse flow observations.

## Problem

- Traditional rheometry, relying on idealized flows and bulk measurements, often fails to accurately characterize complex fluids in real-world scenarios, leading to models that lack generalizability and predictive power in mixed kinematics or complex geometries.
- Existing data-driven approaches, such as machine learning on bulk rheometer data or Physics-Informed Neural Networks, face limitations including overfitting, lack of transferability, geometry-specificity, or prohibitive computational costs for broad model selection.
- There is a need to bridge the gap between flexible, data-driven constitutive representations and the requirement for physically interpretable rheological models, particularly for in-situ or in-line material characterization.
- Bulk rheological measurements provide an "information bottleneck," offering insufficient data to fully constrain complex constitutive models, which can lead to ambiguous model selection when multiple models fit simple data equally well but diverge in complex scenarios.

## Method

- A fully ==differentiable non-Newtonian fluid solver==, built in ==JAX== with an ==immersed-boundary method==, was developed to simulate complex fluid flows in arbitrary geometries and provide exact gradients of flow observables with respect to model parameters.
- A frame-invariant ==Tensor Basis Neural Network (TBNN)== was embedded directly into the differentiable solver to learn the constitutive stress-strain relationship from local flow measurements, parameterized by a bounded neural network for viscosity.
- A ==differentiable model-fitting== framework was implemented using a ==differentiable ODE solver (Diffrax)== to distill the learned TBNN response or directly fit experimental rheometer data to an ensemble of classical constitutive models, using the ==Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC)== for model selection.

## Results

- The TBNN, integrated with the differentiable solver, successfully learned the underlying stress law of a Carreau–Yasuda fluid from pressure-driven flow in a complex channel, reconstructing velocity fields with **near-perfect agreement** to ground truth.
- The learned TBNN demonstrated strong generalization, accurately predicting flow in an unseen bidisperse porous medium geometry, and showed robustness to significant data degradation, including over **tenfold** decrease in spatial resolution and up to **4%** correlated velocity-field noise.
- The differentiable model-fitting framework accurately distilled the TBNN's learned response into classical constitutive laws and achieved high identification accuracy (e.g., **70%** for Linear PTT) for **five** common rheological models when applied to noisy synthetic bulk rheometer data, effectively recovering their parameters.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Embedding physics-informed neural network constitutive closures, like TBNNs, directly within a differentiable fluid solver provides a strong inductive bias, enabling robust and efficient learning of material laws from potentially sparse or noisy local flow measurements.
> - Learning local, frame-invariant constitutive relationships through TBNNs allows for geometry-agnostic material models that can effectively generalize and predict fluid behavior in unseen flow conditions and complex geometries.
> - The combination of flexible data-driven constitutive modeling with a differentiable framework for fitting classical models and using information criteria (BIC) effectively bridges the gap between predictive accuracy and physical interpretability in rheological characterization.
