# MVR: Multi-view Video Reward Shaping for Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> MVR leverages multi-view video-text similarity and a state-dependent reward shaping mechanism to provide robust visual guidance for reinforcement learning agents. This framework consistently improved performance on challenging dynamic locomotion and manipulation tasks by mitigating occlusion and adaptively integrating VLM feedback.

## Problem

- Existing VLM-based reward methods often rely on single, static images, leading to mis-specified guidance for tasks requiring dynamic motions and potentially incentivizing static poses.
- Single viewpoints are prone to occlusions and viewpoint-dependent biases, making it difficult to assess agent behavior comprehensively and requiring manual effort for optimal view selection.
- Linearly adding VLM-derived scores to task rewards without principled shaping can inadvertently alter the optimal policy or create persistent conflicts with the primary task objectives.

## Method

- **MVR** integrates multi-view video observations and ==video-text similarity== to better characterize dynamic behaviors and mitigate viewpoint-dependent biases.
- A state relevance model (`f_MVR`) is learned from multi-view videos using a paired comparison objective (`L_matching`) and state representation regularization (`L_reg`), bridging the semantic gap between states and visual features.
- A state-dependent reward shaping formulation automatically reduces the influence of VLM guidance (`r_VLM`) as the agent's behavior aligns with desired patterns, allowing the primary task reward to dominate later in learning.

## Results

- MVR achieved the best average rank (**1.67** for HumanoidBench, **1.50** for MetaWorld) across **19** challenging locomotion and manipulation tasks, outperforming existing VLM-based methods and task-reward-only baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of multi-view videos, the paired comparison objective, state representation regularization, and the automatic reward shaping mechanism for MVR's robust performance.
- The framework's `r_VLM` term for the 'Sit_Hard' task successfully identified and discouraged visually suboptimal sitting poses that were otherwise tolerated by `r_task`, leading to more stable and robust learned skills.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-view video analysis provides more robust and effective visual guidance for dynamic agent behaviors than single-image approaches, addressing limitations like occlusion and temporal mis-specification.
> - Learning a state relevance model by aligning relative orderings of state sequences with VLM video-text similarity, combined with viewpoint-invariant state representation regularization, is critical for robust reward shaping.
> - An adaptive reward shaping mechanism that naturally decays its influence as the agent improves ensures that VLM-based guidance aids early exploration without long-term conflicts with the core task objective.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reward design is a fundamental aspect of reinforcement learning (RL) for solving complex tasks. Traditional RL relies on quantitatively defined task rewards, which can be challenging to engineer, particularly for behaviors involving nuanced visual cues or dynamic motions. Recent research has explored leveraging Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to augment or generate reward signals. This emerging paradigm often involves computing image-text similarity to guide agents towards states that visually match a textual task description.

However, existing VLM-based reward methods exhibit several limitations. Firstly, many rely on single, static images for similarity computation, which is insufficient for tasks requiring dynamic, rhythmic motions or sequences of visually distinct states. Such approaches can lead to mis-specified rewards, incentivizing agents to repeatedly visit static poses with high similarity scores rather than learning a coherent motion pattern. Secondly, single viewpoints are prone to occlusions, where critical aspects of an agent's behavior may be hidden, leading to viewpoint-dependent biases. Identifying an optimal viewpoint often requires manual effort and domain knowledge. Thirdly, a common practice is to linearly add VLM-derived scores to task or success rewards without explicit shaping, a method that can inadvertently alter the optimal policy or create conflicts with the primary task objectives.

This paper addresses these limitations by proposing Multi-View Video Reward Shaping (MVR), a framework designed to provide visual guidance that is more robust to dynamic behaviors and occlusions. MVR integrates multi-view video observations and a sophisticated state-dependent reward shaping mechanism to overcome the shortcomings of prior VLM-based approaches, positioning itself as an advancement in leveraging large pre-trained models for more effective and natural reward specification in complex robotic tasks. It differentiates itself from other video-based reward learning methods by learning an explicit state-space relevance function from multi-view comparisons of unlabeled online rollouts, and by introducing an automatically decaying shaping term.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance reward design in reinforcement learning by incorporating effective visual feedback from Vision-Language Models (VLMs), particularly for tasks characterized by complex, dynamic motions and visual challenges like occlusion. The motivation stems from observations of human learning, where visual guidance tailored to desired motion patterns plays a significant role in preventing suboptimal behaviors.

The specific objectives and motivations addressed by the MVR framework are detailed as follows:

- **Addressing Mis-specification for Dynamic Motions:** Existing VLM-based rewards, which often rely on single-image similarity, tend to incentivize agents to achieve specific static poses rather than learn continuous, rhythmic movements. For tasks like running, this can lead to an agent repeatedly attempting to optimize a single "running" pose rather than developing an alternating leg cadence. MVR aims to overcome this by using *video-text similarity*, which can better characterize dynamic and sequential behaviors, capturing the temporal dimension of a desired motion pattern.

- **Mitigating Viewpoint-Dependent Bias and Occlusion:** A single camera viewpoint can obscure crucial parts of an agent's body or task environment, leading to an incomplete or biased assessment of its behavior. Selecting an optimal viewpoint typically requires manual expertise. MVR is motivated to counteract this by leveraging *videos captured from multiple viewpoints*. This multi-view approach provides complementary information, allowing for a more comprehensive evaluation of the agent's actions and reducing the reliance on a single, potentially occluded perspective.

- **Developing a Principled Reward Shaping Formulation:** Linearly adding VLM-derived scores to task rewards without a structured shaping mechanism can disrupt the underlying optimal policy of the original task or create persistent conflicts between the VLM guidance and the task objective. The paper aims to introduce a *state-dependent reward shaping formulation* that intelligently integrates VLM-based guidance with task-specific rewards. This formulation is designed to provide dense guidance early in the learning process when the agent's behavior is still unrefined, but *automatically reduce the influence of VLM guidance* as the agent's behavior aligns with the desired motion pattern. This automatic decay is crucial for allowing the task reward to take precedence once the visual objective is largely met, preventing long-term conflicts and potentially avoiding the need for expensive VLM fine-tuning.

- **Learning an Explicit State Relevance Function:** MVR seeks to learn a *state relevance model* (f_MVR) that maps raw state observations (e.g., proprioception, joint angles) to a measure of their relevance to the task. This avoids the need for per-step video rendering for reward computation, which is computationally expensive. The challenge here is bridging the semantic gap between abstract state representations and rich visual features from videos, and learning representations that are robust to viewpoint variations.

In summary, MVR is motivated by the limitations of current VLM-based reward design in RL and strives to create a more effective, robust, and scalable solution by incorporating multi-view video analysis and an adaptive reward shaping strategy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MVR operates as an online reinforcement learning framework designed to generate visual guidance by leveraging multi-view videos and Vision-Language Models (VLMs). The framework integrates this guidance into the RL process through a state-dependent reward shaping mechanism.

**Overall Framework (Figure 2):**
MVR periodically collects trajectories of the agent's behavior from the environment. These trajectories are then rendered into videos from multiple viewpoints. A pre-trained VLM is queried to compute video-text similarity scores and video embeddings based on a textual task description. This information is used to augment a dataset `D` and a reference set `D_ref` (containing high-quality state sequences). Periodically, a state relevance model (`f_MVR`) is updated using `D`. Finally, `f_MVR` and `D_ref` are used to compute a visual feedback term (`r_VLM`), which is combined with the environment's task reward (`r_task`) to shape the agent's learning.

**Learning State Relevance from Multi-View Videos (f_MVR):**
The `f_MVR` model maps an agent's internal state `s` to a scalar relevance score. Learning this function from visual data presents two primary challenges:

1. **Semantic Gap between States and Videos:** Direct regression of VLM similarity scores from low-level state representations (e.g., joint angles) is difficult due to their inherent semantic differences.
 - **Solution: Matching Paired Comparisons (`L_matching`):** Instead of direct regression, MVR aims to preserve the *ordering* of relevance. It leverages the Bradley-Terry (BT) model to define probabilities:
 - `h_vid(o, o') = σ(ψ_VLM(o, ℓ) - ψ_VLM(o', ℓ))` represents the probability that video `o` is a better match for text `ℓ` than video `o'`.
 - `h_state(s, s') = σ(Avg(f_MVR(s)) - Avg(f_MVR(s')))` represents the probability that state sequence `s` is better than `s'`.
 - The `L_matching` objective minimizes the cross-entropy between `h_vid` and `h_state`, effectively aligning the relative rankings of state sequences with those indicated by the VLM's video-text similarity. This objective can use video pairs from different viewpoints as they originate from the same underlying state sequence.

2. **Viewpoint-Induced Bias:** VLM scores for the same behavior can vary significantly depending on the camera angle, introducing spurious correlations.
 - **Solution: Regularizing State Representations (`L_reg`):** `f_MVR` is parameterized as `f_MVR(s) = ⟨g_rel, g_state(s)⟩`, decoupling a state encoder `g_state` (which learns representations) from a relevance vector `g_rel` (which scores relevance).
 - `L_reg` regularizes the state representations `g_state(s)` by aligning their similarity structure with the VLM's video embedding similarity. Specifically, it minimizes the difference between `ψ_VLM(o_i, o_j)` (VLM similarity between videos) and `⟨Avg(g_state(s_i)), Avg(g_state(s_j))⟩` (cosine similarity of averaged state representations). This aims to create viewpoint-invariant state representations.
 - **Overall Objective:** The combined objective for learning `f_MVR` is `L_rel = L_matching + L_reg`.

**Policy Learning with r_VLM (State-Dependent Reward Shaping):**
MVR designs `r_VLM` to guide the agent towards state distributions that yield high video-text similarity, while ensuring that the task reward `r_task` eventually dominates.

1. **Reward Function Formulation:** The agent optimizes a shaped reward `r_MVR(s) = r_task(s) + w * r_VLM(s)`.
2. **Visual Guidance Term (`r_VLM`):** `r_VLM(s) = E_{s'~π_ℓ}[log(σ(f_MVR(s) - f_MVR(s')))]`. This term encourages the current policy's states to be "better" (higher relevance) than states from an "optimal" policy `π_ℓ`.
3. **Reference Set (`D_ref`):** Since `π_ℓ` is unknown, `r_VLM` is computed by sampling `s'` from `D_ref`, a collection of state sequences with the top-k highest VLM-derived similarity scores observed during training. This set is periodically updated, analogous to learning from one's own past best performance.
4. **Automatic Shaping:** A key feature is the *automatic decay* of `r_VLM`. As the agent's policy improves and its state distribution aligns with the high-relevance states in `D_ref`, the expectation term in `r_VLM` approaches zero. This mechanism naturally reduces the influence of the VLM-based guidance, allowing the task reward `r_task` to take precedence and avoid conflicts once the visually desired behavior is achieved.

**Implementation Details:**
- **VLM:** ViCLIP-L (428M parameters) is used.
- **Rendering:** One trajectory out of every nine is rendered into videos (224x224 resolution, 64-frame clips) from a randomly selected viewpoint (from four orthogonal views: 0°, 90°, 180°, 270°).
- **Update Frequencies:** The relevance model `f_MVR` is updated every 100,000 environment steps using a dataset `D` of 20,000 samples. The replay buffer rewards for off-policy RL algorithms (like TQC) are re-calculated after `f_MVR` updates.
- **Hyperparameter:** `w` (weight for `r_VLM`) is selected from {0.01, 0.1, 0.5} via grid search.
- **RL Algorithm:** TQC (Truncated Quantile Critic) is used as the base RL algorithm.
- **Benchmarks:** Evaluations are conducted on 19 tasks from HumanoidBench (humanoid locomotion/posture) and MetaWorld (robotic manipulation), with specific metrics (episodic returns for HumanoidBench, success rates for MetaWorld).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents experimental results demonstrating the efficacy and design principles of MVR across a range of tasks.

**Proficiency for Soliciting Visual Guidance:**
- **HumanoidBench Tasks:** MVR achieved the best performance on five out of nine challenging humanoid locomotion and posture tasks, and the best average rank across all tasks (1.67). It consistently outperformed existing VLM-based methods (VLM-RM, RoboCLIP) and task-reward-only baselines (TQC, DreamerV3). For instance, MVR significantly improved performance on dynamic tasks like "Run" (749.23 vs. 647.87 for TQC) and static tasks like "Stand" (918.55 vs. 576.59 for TQC). VLM-RM showed mixed performance, occasionally outperforming TQC on static tasks but struggling with dynamic ones, while RoboCLIP generally underperformed. This indicates that video-based visual guidance is more effective than image-based methods for both dynamic and static tasks.
- **MetaWorld Tasks:** MVR demonstrated the highest average success rate (0.54 ± 0.22) and the best average rank (1.50) across ten manipulation tasks. RoboCLIP followed, while VLM-RM and task-reward-only baselines generally performed worse. This suggests the importance of visual feedback derived from multi-view videos for manipulation tasks, although the performance margins were smaller than in HumanoidBench, possibly due to shorter episodes and simpler visual contexts.
- **Limitations:** MVR did not outperform TQC on four out of 19 tasks, suggesting that VLM-based guidance might not always align perfectly with the task objective, potentially due to VLMs being trained predominantly on human activities rather than robotics data.

**Model Ablation Studies:**
- **Importance of `L_matching` and `L_reg`:** The variants `w/o reg` (disabling `L_reg`) and `direct` (directly fitting `f_MVR` to VLM scores) exhibited inferior and unstable performance, respectively. This confirms that both the paired comparison objective and the state representation regularization are crucial for learning an effective and robust state relevance model.
- **Video vs. Image Guidance:** The `MVR-CLIP` variant, which used image-text similarity, performed worse than MVR across tasks. This highlights that assessing dynamic agent behaviors with videos is critical for deriving accurate and useful visual guidance.
- **Efficacy of Automatic Shaping:** The `w/o reference` variant, which used raw `f_MVR(s)` as guidance instead of the expectation matching (`Eq. 9`) and its natural decay, was outperformed by MVR for most tasks. This validates the effectiveness of the proposed state-dependent reward shaping formulation and its automatic decay mechanism.
- **VLM Scale:** Comparing ViCLIP-L (larger) with ViCLIP-B (smaller) showed that ViCLIP-L generally yielded better performance on three out of four evaluated tasks, supporting the general trend of scaling laws in foundation models.
- **Reward Components:** Ablation demonstrated that relying solely on `r_VLM` led to learning collapse, underscoring that the VLM signal acts as a complement to `r_task`, not a replacement.
- **Temporal Pooling:** Temporal averaging in MVR proved more stable and effective for dynamic locomotion tasks than attention-based pooling.
- **All-View Rendering:** A variant `MVR-ALL` (rendering and concatenating four synchronized videos) showed some uplift for static tasks but only marginal or no gains for dynamic locomotion, suggesting that naive multi-view concatenation can disrupt temporal consistency for fast motions.

**Influence of Multiple Views:**
- **General Benefit:** For most tasks, increasing the number of views generally improved performance, confirming the hypothesis that multi-view videos provide richer information, especially for complex or dynamic tasks.
- **Task-Dependent Views:** The optimal fixed viewpoint was task-dependent (e.g., side views were more effective for `Sit_Hard` to judge robot-chair distance).
- **Random Sampling Effectiveness:** MVR, which samples viewpoints uniformly at random, achieved competitive or superior results compared to using any single fixed viewpoint, indicating its capability to aggregate multi-view information effectively. On `Run`, `Slide`, and `Sit_Hard`, moving from one view to four orthogonal views pushed mean returns past HumanoidBench success thresholds.

**Case Study: Sit_Hard Task:**
- A t-SNE visualization of states from a TQC agent revealed that while `r_task` and `r_MVR` often aligned, `r_MVR` effectively identified and down-weighted visually suboptimal sitting poses (e.g., sitting on a chair leg, leaning, sitting at the edge) that still received high `r_task` due to minimal robot-chair distance. This highlights `r_MVR`'s ability to shape the reward landscape to discourage visually unstable behaviors that `r_task` alone might tolerate. This visual guidance allows for more efficient learning of stable and robust skills.

**Quantitative Alignment and Automatic Shaping (Appendix B.7):**
- **Reward Correlation:** On `Sit_Hard`, the learned relevance (`f_MVR`) showed a strong Pearson correlation (0.96) with binary success, while being only weakly correlated (0.22) with `r_task`. The shaped reward (`r_MVR`) maintained an even higher correlation with success (0.98). This suggests `f_MVR` captures success cues beyond `r_task`, and `r_MVR` preserves the optimal policy while reinforcing visual priors.
- **Decay:** The average magnitude of `r_VLM` in successful episodes decreased to approximately 23% of its early-training value, empirically confirming the automatic decay mechanism and the handover of control to `r_task`.

**Additional Experiments:**
- MVR retained its performance advantage in pixel-based MetaWorld experiments, operating on proprioceptive inputs augmented with R3M-ViT features.
- In sparse-reward MetaWorld settings, MVR with sparse environment rewards achieved comparable average success rates to TQC with dense rewards, demonstrating its ability to compensate for the lack of dense engineering.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MVR framework represents a notable advancement in the integration of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) into reinforcement learning, particularly for tasks requiring nuanced visual understanding and dynamic motion control.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**

- **Robust Visual Guidance for Dynamic Tasks:** MVR addresses a critical limitation of previous VLM-based reward approaches by effectively incorporating multi-view video-text similarity. This allows the framework to characterize complex, dynamic motions and sequences of states, leading to more appropriate reward signals for tasks like locomotion and continuous manipulation. This is a significant step towards enabling RL agents to learn skills that are inherently temporal and visually rich.
- **Mitigation of Viewpoint Bias and Occlusion:** By leveraging videos from multiple viewpoints, MVR reduces the negative impact of occlusions and viewpoint-dependent biases. This makes the visual feedback more comprehensive and reliable, minimizing the need for extensive human effort in selecting optimal camera angles. The framework's ability to aggregate information from random viewpoint sampling underscores its robustness.
- **Principled and Adaptive Reward Shaping:** The introduction of a state-dependent reward shaping formulation is a core contribution. It intelligently integrates VLM-based guidance with task rewards and features an automatic decay mechanism. This design ensures that visual guidance provides dense feedback early in learning but gradually diminishes its influence as the agent achieves the desired behavior, preventing potential conflicts with the task's primary objective and preserving the optimality of the base task reward. This mechanism enhances stability and efficiency in complex learning environments.
- **Empirical Validation on Challenging Benchmarks:** The consistent superior performance of MVR on challenging humanoid locomotion tasks (HumanoidBench) and diverse manipulation tasks (MetaWorld), compared to existing VLM-based methods and task-reward-only baselines, provides strong empirical evidence for its effectiveness. The ablation studies further validate the necessity and contribution of its individual components, such as paired comparisons and state representation regularization.
- **Towards Automatic Robot Skill Learning:** This work contributes to the broader goal of enabling robots to acquire diverse skills automatically from high-level natural language instructions. By leveraging the rich, generalizable knowledge encoded in pre-trained VLMs, MVR reduces the burden of manual reward engineering, paving the way for more versatile and intelligent robotic systems.

**Potential Impact:**

The proposed framework has the potential to significantly impact the field of embodied AI and robotics by:
- **Accelerating Skill Acquisition:** By providing effective and adaptive visual guidance, MVR can accelerate the learning process for complex robotic skills, reducing the number of interactions required with the environment.
- **Improving Skill Robustness:** The ability to learn from multi-view observations and to shape rewards based on visually salient aspects can lead to agents learning more robust and human-like behaviors, even in challenging and visually ambiguous scenarios.
- **Enhancing Generalization:** As VLMs capture broad semantic understanding, using them for reward shaping could facilitate better generalization of learned skills across variations in environments or robot morphology.
- **Reducing Human Intervention:** The framework moves towards more autonomous reward design, lessening the need for expert knowledge in crafting intricate reward functions, thus streamlining the development of new robotic capabilities.

As this work focuses on simulated benchmarks and does not involve human data or safety-critical systems, it poses no immediate foreseeable ethical, privacy, or security risks. The foundational nature of this research is intended to support the responsible development of advanced autonomous systems.
