# Humanoid Goalkeeper: Learning from Position Conditioned Task-Motion Constraints

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The University of Hong Kong and Shanghai AI Laboratory developed an end-to-end reinforcement learning framework for humanoid goalkeeping, integrating position-conditioned human motion priors via an adversarial scheme. The system achieved an 80.92% success rate in simulation and 70% on hardware using MoCap, demonstrating agile, human-like whole-body ball interceptions across a wide range.

## Problem

- Existing robotic goalkeeping systems are often confined to smaller platforms (e.g., quadrupeds), rely on fixed motion primitives, and have narrow interception ranges, lacking the dynamic whole-body agility required for humanoids.
- Humanoid robots face unique challenges in generating natural, human-like whole-body motions and covering a wide guarding range with equivalent response times due to their many degrees of freedom and complex balance requirements.
- Current control approaches for dynamic humanoid interactions often use separate perception, planning, and control modules, or rely on fixed motion tracking, lacking an integrated, end-to-end learning policy for real-time dynamic interactions.

## Method

- An end-to-end reinforcement learning (RL) framework using PPO in IsaacGym was developed, optimizing for both task success (intercepting the ball) and motion resemblance to human demonstrations.
- Position-conditioned adversarial motion priors (AMP) were incorporated, with region-specific discriminators trained to differentiate between expert human motions and robot-generated motions, encouraging region-appropriate movement styles.
- The system integrates an internal ball estimator trained with noise (e.g., position perturbation, random dropouts) and a post-task stability reward to enhance sim-to-real transfer, robustness to perception uncertainties, and continuous task execution.

## Results

- The proposed Humanoid Goalkeeper achieved an **80.92%** simulation success rate and **67.84%** motion alignment with expert demonstrations, outperforming baselines that lacked explicit task or motion conditioning.
- In hardware experiments, the system achieved a **70%** success rate using a motion capture system and a **46.7%** success rate with an onboard camera, demonstrating functional real-world deployment on a Unitree G1 robot.
- The framework generalized to other dynamic interaction tasks like ball escaping (e.g., 5/5 success for squat escape) and demonstrated the ability to perform continuous goalkeeping without requiring manual resets to default postures.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly optimizing task success and motion resemblance via region-conditioned adversarial priors significantly improves both performance metrics compared to optimizing for either alone, highlighting the benefits of integrated task-motion constraints.
> - Explicit motion and task segmentation (dividing AMP and task rewards by goal region) is crucial for achieving high motion fidelity and task-aligned behaviors, preventing policies from overfitting to specific goal areas.
> - Training with an integrated ball estimator and perception noise is vital for successful sim-to-real transfer, allowing the humanoid to perform robustly with real-world sensors like depth cameras despite field-of-view limitations.
