# PartManip: Learning Cross-Category Generalizable Part Manipulation Policy from Point Cloud Observations

> [!summary] Summary
> The PartManip framework introduces a large-scale benchmark and a two-stage policy learning strategy to achieve cross-category generalizable part manipulation from point cloud observations. This approach outperforms state-of-the-art baselines on various manipulation tasks by leveraging part-based canonicalization and domain adversarial learning, demonstrating effectiveness in simulation and potential for real-world deployment.

## Problem

- Current generalist robotic agents struggle with novel objects and instances, limiting their applicability in unconstrained real-world environments beyond known categories.
- Existing manipulation benchmarks offer category-level generalization but lack cross-category diversity and often rely on privileged information like oracle part segmentation masks.
- The challenge of learning robust manipulation policies for Generalizable Actionable Parts (GAParts) for cross-category generalization, previously identified by GAPartNet, remained unsolved.

## Method

- Introduction of the **PartManip** benchmark, a large-scale and diverse dataset with 494 objects across 11 categories and 1432 tasks, utilizing realistic sparse-view point cloud observations.
- A two-stage policy learning framework consisting of a part-canonicalized state-based expert policy trained with PPO and a vision-based student policy trained via knowledge distillation.
- The vision-based student policy uses ==Behavior Cloning== for pre-training, DAgger for online refinement, a 3D Sparse-UNet backbone, and domain adversarial learning for enhanced cross-category generalization.

## Results

- The PartManip method significantly outperformed state-of-the-art baselines, achieving a **49.1%** success rate on inter-category OpenDoor tasks compared to **26.9%** by the best baseline, and **54.7%** for OpenDrawer compared to **31.2%**.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical contribution of part-based canonicalization, DAgger with pre-training, observation augmentations, and domain adversarial learning to overall performance and generalization.
- Real-world experiments demonstrated the potential for deploying the learned policies on physical robots, showing successful manipulation of novel objects, particularly with the aid of a digital twin system.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Part-based pose canonicalization significantly reduces input variance and enhances the generalization capability of state-based expert policies across diverse object instances and categories.
> - A two-stage knowledge distillation approach (BC pre-training followed by DAgger) effectively transfers skills from an oracle expert to a vision-based student, mitigating issues like distribution shift.
> - Domain adversarial training, integrated into the vision backbone, facilitates the learning of domain-invariant features, which is crucial for improving generalization to unseen object categories.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is continuously striving for more generalizable and adaptable agents that can operate effectively in complex, unstructured real-world environments. This paper positions itself at the forefront of this effort by tackling the critical challenge of **cross-category generalizable part manipulation**.

Historically, generalist agents like GATO and SayCan have demonstrated impressive capabilities, but their manipulation skills often remain confined to known object instances or categories, failing to generalize robustly to novel objects. Benchmarks such as ManiSkill have advanced the state of the art by providing platforms for learning "category-level" manipulation – for example, teaching a robot to open various instances of drawers. However, ManiSkill's generalization is limited *within* a single object category. The next frontier involves generalizing manipulation skills *across* different object categories.

This is where the concept of **part-based manipulation** becomes crucial. Many objects, despite belonging to different categories, share functionally similar "parts" (e.g., a handle on a drawer versus a handle on an oven). If a robot can learn to manipulate these "Generalizable Actionable Parts" (GAParts), it could theoretically generalize skills across a vast array of objects. The foundational work, GAPartNet, pioneered the definition and consistent identification of these GAParts and their poses across diverse object categories. However, GAPartNet explicitly left the critical problem of *learning manipulation policies* for these GAParts unsolved.

"PartManip" directly addresses this gap by introducing the **first large-scale, part-based cross-category object manipulation benchmark**. It moves beyond category-level generalization by requiring agents to manipulate parts that appear on vastly different objects (e.g., opening a door on storage furniture, then on a microwave, then a refrigerator). Furthermore, the benchmark emphasizes **realism** by using sparse-view point clouds as input, eschewing oracle information like part segmentation masks that were often used in previous benchmarks (e.g., ManiSkill). This makes PartManip a much more challenging and relevant testbed for real-world robotic applications.

The paper also fits into the broader areas of **vision-based policy learning** and **knowledge distillation**. Given the complexity and diversity of visual inputs from point clouds, direct reinforcement learning often struggles. Therefore, the work leverages established techniques like learning from expert demonstrations and behavior cloning, combined with advanced vision backbones and domain generalization strategies, to make policy learning feasible and robust. This positions "PartManip" as a significant step towards creating truly adaptive and versatile robotic manipulation systems capable of operating in diverse, unseen environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of this research is to enable robotic agents to **learn a generalizable object manipulation policy that can succeed across different object categories and novel object instances, relying solely on realistic point cloud observations.**

This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

- **Bridging the Generalization Gap in Robotics:** Current generalist robotic agents often struggle with novel objects and instances, limiting their applicability in unconstrained real-world environments. The authors aim to push beyond "category-level" generalization (e.g., opening many types of drawers) towards "cross-category" generalization (e.g., opening doors on various furniture types, appliances, and safes), which is a critical step towards human-level adaptability.
- **Addressing the Policy Learning Challenge for GAParts:** While the concept of Generalizable Actionable Parts (GAParts) proposed by GAPartNet offers a promising pathway for cross-category generalization, the previous work explicitly did not solve the problem of learning robust manipulation policies for these parts. PartManip directly addresses this by proposing and evaluating methods for policy learning based on GAParts.
- **Need for a More Diverse and Realistic Benchmark:** Existing manipulation benchmarks, such as ManiSkill, often have limitations in terms of object diversity and the realism of their observation space (e.g., providing oracle part segmentation masks). To foster research into truly generalizable and deployable policies, there was a clear need for a new benchmark that features:
 - **Large Scale and Diversity:** A significantly larger number of objects and categories, encompassing 11 object categories, 494 objects, and 1432 tasks across 6 task classes.
 - **Realistic Input:** Using sparse-view point clouds, obtained from multiple RGB-D cameras, as the primary visual input, without relying on privileged information like precise part segmentation masks. This makes the learning problem much harder but more relevant to real-world scenarios.
- **Overcoming Challenges in Vision-Based Policy Learning:** Directly applying state-of-the-art reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms to learn vision-based policies in such diverse and complex settings is inherently difficult due to noisy gradients and the vast sampling space. This necessitates a more structured and robust approach to policy learning.

To address these motivations, the authors established specific objectives for their methodology:

- **Develop a robust state-based expert:** By leveraging oracle state information (available in simulation), an expert policy can be trained efficiently, guided by part-based canonicalization and part-aware rewards to reduce task variations and provide accurate guidance.
- **Design an effective knowledge distillation framework:** To transfer the skills from the state-based expert to a vision-based student policy that operates on realistic (partial) observations. This framework needs to be robust to observation distribution shifts and large visual diversity.
- **Incorporate advanced visual feature extraction:** An expressive 3D vision backbone is required to extract informative geometric and task-aware representations from diverse point clouds.
- **Enhance cross-category generalization:** Mechanisms are needed to learn domain-invariant features, explicitly addressing the challenge of applying learned skills to entirely unseen object categories.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors introduce the **PartManip benchmark** and a novel **two-stage policy learning strategy** to achieve cross-category generalizable part manipulation from point cloud observations.

#### 3.1. PartManip Benchmark Design

PartManip is built upon the Generalizable Actionable Parts (GAPart) definitions from GAPartNet, focusing on manipulation tasks that leverage these parts.

- **Task Formulation:** Six classes of manipulation tasks are defined: OpenDoor, OpenDrawer, CloseDoor, CloseDrawer, PressButton, and GraspHandle. These are formulated as Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs) where the robot policy predicts actions based on partial observations.
- **Object Assets:** The benchmark utilizes 494 object instances from GAPartNet, encompassing 11 object categories (e.g., StorageFurniture, Microwave, Oven, Refrigerator, Safe, Table). These objects provide 1432 distinct manipulation tasks. Crucially, the geometry and appearance of objects vary significantly *across* categories, posing a considerable challenge for generalization.
- **Robot and Action Space:** A Franka Panda arm with a parallel gripper serves as the end-effector. The action space is a 6 DoF target pose for the gripper, which is then converted to target joint angles via inverse kinematics for position control.
- **Observation Space (Realistic):** The key aspect of realism is the input to the vision-based policy:
 - Robot states (joint angles, velocities, gripper pose).
 - Colored point clouds ($P_t \in \mathbb{R}^{6 \times 4096}$), back-projected from 3-view RGBD images (multi-camera setup to mitigate occlusion).
 - A fixed point indicator ($c \in \mathbb{R}^3$) specifying the center of the target GAPart at timestep 0. This replaces oracle part segmentation masks used in prior work and requires the policy to visually identify the relevant handle or part from the point cloud.
- **Part Pose-aware Reward Design:** For training, dense rewards are provided in the simulator by accessing ground truth environment states. The reward function combines four components:
 - **Rotation Reward:** Encourages the gripper to align with the part's manipulation axis.
 - **Distance Reward:** Minimizes the distance between the gripper and the target handle center, with a discount factor to relax strict grasping after initial movement.
 - **Part Moving Reward:** Encourages the desired movement of the target GAPart (e.g., opening angle for a door, length for a drawer).
 - **Tips Closure Reward:** Promotes stable grasping by encouraging gripper fingertips to close.

#### 3.2. Two-Stage Policy Learning Framework

Recognizing the difficulty of directly learning vision-based policies in this challenging setting, the authors propose a two-stage approach:

- **Stage 1: Part-Canonicalized State-Based Expert Policy Learning:**
 - **Algorithm:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is used with access to oracle environment states (robot states and precise GAPart bounding boxes).
 - **Part-based Pose Canonicalization:** A novel technique is introduced where all coordinate-dependent state information is transformed from the world frame into the target GAPart's canonical coordinate frame. This involves setting the origin at the part's bounding box center and aligning axes with its edges. This transformation significantly reduces input variance by making observations relative to the part, thereby easing learning and boosting generalization across diverse object instances and categories.
 - **Part-aware Rewards:** The carefully designed dense rewards (Section 4.1) guide the expert training effectively.

- **Stage 2: Augmented State-to-Vision Policy Distillation for Student Policy Learning:**
 - The state-based expert policy cannot be directly deployed in the real world due to its reliance on oracle states. Therefore, its knowledge is distilled into a vision-based student policy that takes realistic partial observations as input.
 - **Pre-training with Behavior Cloning (BC):** The expert first collects an offline dataset of successful observation-action trajectories. The student policy is pre-trained using BC to provide a robust initial policy, which helps prevent the student from falling into out-of-distribution states early in training.
 - **Online Imitation with DAgger:** To mitigate the problem of accumulating errors due to distribution shift in BC, DAgger (Dataset Aggregation) is employed. The student policy interacts with the environment, and if its observations diverge from the training data, the expert provides supervision on these new observations. This online learning process allows the student to continuously improve under its own observed distribution.
 - **Observation Augmentations:** To enhance the generalization ability, point cloud jittering and aggressive color replacement augmentations are applied during DAgger training, increasing the diversity of the visual input.
 - **3D Sparse UNet-based Backbone:** An expressive 3D Sparse-UNet architecture is chosen as the vision backbone to process the colored point clouds. This backbone extracts per-point features, which are then aggregated by a small PointNet and concatenated with robot states before being fed to the actor network. The authors optimized Sparse-UNet for faster inference.
 - **Domain-invariant Feature Learning:** To explicitly address cross-category generalization, a **domain adversarial training strategy** is incorporated. A Gradient Reverse Layer (GRL) and a domain classifier are added to the vision backbone. The domain classifier attempts to predict the object category from the extracted visual features. The GRL then negates the gradient from the classifier, forcing the backbone to learn features that are *indistinguishable* across object categories (i.e., domain-invariant), thereby improving generalization to unseen categories.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments, primarily conducted in simulation with validation in the real world, demonstrate the significant effectiveness of the PartManip benchmark and the proposed methodology.

- **Benchmark Difficulty and Performance:**
 - The tasks CloseDoor and CloseDrawer are relatively easier, with the proposed method achieving high success rates (88.7%-99.6%) even on unseen categories.
 - More complex tasks like OpenDoor, OpenDrawer, PressButton, and GraspHandle are substantially more challenging, with performance generally dropping on unseen object categories. For instance, OpenDoor shows a 68.4% success rate on the training set but 49.1% on the inter-category validation set, highlighting the difficulty of generalization.

- **Superiority Over Baselines:**
 - The PartManip method significantly outperforms several state-of-the-art baselines, including direct PPO, ILAD, Where2Act, and winners from the ManiSkill Challenge (e.g., SilverBullet3D, Shen et al., Wu et al., Dubois et al.).
 - For example, in OpenDoor, the proposed method achieves 49.1% on the inter-category set, substantially higher than the best baseline (SilverBullet3D at 26.9%). Similarly, for OpenDrawer, PartManip achieves 54.7% compared to SilverBullet3D's 31.2%. This demonstrates the strong generalization capabilities of the proposed approach.
 - Baselines often show dramatic performance drops on intra- and especially inter-category validation sets, confirming the difficulty of the benchmark and the limitations of previous methods in truly cross-category generalization.

- **Ablation Study Insights:** The ablation studies validate the importance of each component of the proposed framework:
 - **Part-based Canonicalization (State-based Expert):** This technique is crucial, significantly improving the expert's performance and generalization. For OpenDoor, it boosted the inter-category success rate from 23.4% to 50.7%, indicating its efficacy in reducing task variations.
 - **DAgger + Pre-training (Vision-based Student):** Directly applying PPO to a vision-based policy performs poorly (e.g., 0.2% for OpenDoor inter-category). DAgger alone significantly improves this, and adding pre-training with expert demonstrations further boosts performance by providing a better initialization and handling out-of-distribution states.
 - **Sparse-UNet Backbone:** The expressive capacity of the Sparse-UNet backbone contributes to better overall performance compared to simpler architectures, though it can overfit without regularization.
 - **Observation Augmentations:** Applying point cloud augmentations (jittering, color replacement) helps alleviate overfitting and improves generalization, particularly on unseen instances.
 - **Domain Adversarial Learning (DomAdv):** This component provides a further boost in performance, especially on unseen object categories (e.g., from 48.8% to 54.7% for OpenDrawer inter-category when combined with all other components). This confirms its role in facilitating domain-invariant feature extraction, which is key for cross-category generalization.

- **Real-World Experiments:** The method was validated on novel physical objects in the real world using a Franka Emika robot arm and an RGB-D camera. While direct sim-to-real transfer had a lower success rate due to the sim2real gap, utilizing a digital twin system significantly improved real-world performance. This demonstrates the potential for deploying the learned policies in physical environments, provided sim2real challenges are managed.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "PartManip" paper represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, with several profound implications and potential impacts:

- **New State-of-the-Art in Generalizable Manipulation:** By successfully demonstrating robust manipulation policies that generalize across vastly different object categories from realistic visual observations, PartManip pushes the frontier of generalizable robotics. It shows that learning to manipulate functional "parts" is a highly effective strategy for achieving broader applicability beyond specific object instances or categories. This moves robots closer to the human ability to manipulate novel objects intuitively.

- **Crucial New Benchmark for Research:** The introduction of the PartManip benchmark itself is a major contribution. Its large scale, diverse object categories, and emphasis on realistic sparse-view point cloud inputs (without oracle information) make it an extremely challenging yet invaluable tool for the research community. It sets a new standard for evaluating cross-category generalization, inspiring and facilitating future research into more robust and adaptable robotic policies. The open-sourcing of the benchmark will undoubtedly accelerate progress in this area.

- **Robust Methodological Framework:** The proposed two-stage policy learning framework (state-based expert + vision-based student) offers a robust and effective strategy for tackling complex vision-based control tasks where direct reinforcement learning struggles.
 - **Part-based Canonicalization:** This ingenious method for reducing state-space variance is highly significant. By focusing on the relative pose to the part rather than absolute world coordinates, it simplifies the learning problem and is broadly applicable to other part-centric manipulation tasks.
 - **Domain Adversarial Learning:** The successful integration of domain adversarial learning to achieve domain-invariant feature extraction is crucial for true cross-category generalization. This technique helps ensure that the learned visual representations are robust to appearance and geometric variations across different object types.
 - **Knowledge Distillation Pipeline:** The combination of BC for pre-training and DAgger for online refinement provides a robust pipeline for transferring skills from an ideal expert to a real-world-constrained student, mitigating common issues like covariate shift.

- **Enhanced Real-World Applicability:** By requiring policies to learn from point cloud observations, the work moves robotic manipulation closer to practical deployment. While the sim2real gap remains a challenge, the successful real-world experiments (even with a digital twin) validate the potential for these learned policies to operate on physical robots. This has implications for diverse applications, from intelligent home robots that can handle various household items to industrial robots that can adapt to different product designs on an assembly line.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** The limitations acknowledged by the authors (e.g., further improving performance on truly unseen categories, more robust sim2real transfer without digital twins) highlight exciting avenues for future work. The PartManip benchmark provides a solid foundation for researchers to explore novel architectures, more advanced domain generalization techniques, and improved sim2real strategies, ultimately contributing to the long-term goal of truly general-purpose robotic agents.
