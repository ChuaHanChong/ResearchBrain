# Neural Module Networks

> [!summary] Summary
> Neural Module Networks (NMNs) dynamically build specialized deep networks from reusable modules to answer visual questions, integrating deep learning's representational power with explicit compositional structure. This architecture achieved 55.1% accuracy on the VQA dataset, surpassing previous state-of-the-art, and showed a significant performance gap (90.6% vs. 65.3%) on a new dataset designed for complex compositional reasoning.

## Problem

- Monolithic deep learning models for Visual Question Answering (VQA) often struggle to explicitly exploit the compositional structure inherent in natural language questions.
- Prior VQA approaches either lack structured reasoning (monolithic deep networks) or flexible learning with continuous representations (symbolic parsing).
- Designing a single, fixed deep network topology is suboptimal for handling the diverse and multi-step reasoning required by complex VQA queries.

## Method

- Proposes **Neural Module Networks (NMNs)** which dynamically assemble a ==computation graph== from a library of specialized ==neural modules== for each question.
- Utilizes a ==semantic parser== to transform natural language questions into ==symbolic representations==, which then directly map to the NMN's structure and module instances.
- Employs a set of generic ==module types== (e.g., 'attend', 're-attend', 'combine', 'classify', 'measure') that learn specific visual or logical operations through joint ==end-to-end training==.

## Results

- Achieved **90.6%** accuracy on the SHAPES compositional reasoning dataset, a substantial improvement over the **65.3%** scored by the VIS+LSTM baseline, demonstrating strong performance on complex, multi-step questions.
- Set a new state-of-the-art on the VQA natural image dataset, with the full NMN+LSTM model reaching **55.1%** accuracy on the test server, exceeding previous bests (e.g., **54.1%** for VIS+LSTM).
- An ablation study demonstrated the crucial role of the auxiliary LSTM question encoder, with an NMN-only model dropping to **42.7%** accuracy, underscoring its importance for linguistic context and common-sense knowledge.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling linguistic compositionality by dynamically assembling neural networks improves performance on complex, multi-step visual reasoning tasks.
> - The integration of symbolic semantic parsing (for structural layout) with end-to-end trained deep neural modules (for processing visual features) enables a flexible and powerful hybrid AI approach.
> - Reusable neural modules can learn specialized visual or logical functions entirely from data through joint training, adapting the network's computation to each specific question.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of Visual Question Answering (VQA), which requires sophisticated understanding of both visual scenes and natural language to answer natural language questions about images. The broader landscape of VQA research can be characterized by two primary directions:

1. **Monolithic Deep Network Approaches**: These methods typically represent questions as bags of words or encode them using recurrent neural networks (e.g., LSTMs), then train a single, end-to-end deep network to map the encoded question and image features to an answer distribution. These approaches often treat the entire question-answering process as a "black box" without explicit modeling of compositional reasoning. Examples include work by Malinowski et al. (2015) and Gao et al. (2015).

2. **Symbolic and Semantic Parsing Approaches**: This line of work, often drawing from textual question answering, uses semantic parsers to decompose questions into logical expressions. These expressions are then evaluated against a structured, logical representation of the world, which may be pre-defined or extracted from an image. Notable examples in early VQA include Malinowski and Fritz (2014) and Krishnamurthy and Kollar (2013). While providing compositional structure, these methods often rely on fixed logical inference rules rather than learned, flexible operations, and may struggle to leverage the rich, continuous representations characteristic of deep learning.

The work presented in this paper, "Deep Compositional Question Answering with Neural Module Networks" (NMNs), seeks to integrate the strengths of both paradigms. It aims to combine the representational power and learning capacity of deep neural networks with the explicit, flexible compositional structure afforded by symbolic approaches to semantics. Instead of a single, monolithic network, the NMN approach dynamically constructs specialized deep networks on the fly for each question, using a collection of reusable, jointly-trained neural "modules." This differentiates it from existing recurrent or recursive neural networks that typically apply a single computational module repeatedly, and from memory networks that employ fixed computational graphs. The paper positions NMNs as a novel architecture that allows for heterogeneous computations and mixed message types (e.g., raw image features, attentions, classification predictions) to be passed between modules, a feature the authors state they are unaware of in prior joint training contexts.

Furthermore, the paper connects to work on attention mechanisms in vision and language, where models learn to focus on relevant parts of an image or text for tasks like image captioning or phrase grounding. NMNs build on this by allowing attention to be a fundamental, composable operation within their modular framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objectives and motivations for developing Neural Module Networks (NMNs) for visual question answering are:

1. **Exploiting Compositional Structure**: The authors observe that natural language questions, especially in VQA, are fundamentally compositional. For example, questions like "where is the dog?" and "what color is the dog?" share substructures (identifying the "dog"). Existing monolithic deep learning approaches often treat questions as atomic inputs, potentially failing to leverage these shared, compositional elements effectively. The objective is to design a system that explicitly models and exploits this linguistic compositionality.

2. **Bridging Neural Networks and Symbolic Semantics**: Prior work either relied on the representational power of deep networks without explicit compositional reasoning or used symbolic logic with limited integration of learned, continuous representations. A key objective is to integrate the strengths of both: the data-driven learning and feature extraction capabilities of deep networks with the structured, compositional reasoning offered by symbolic semantic parsing. This aims to move beyond purely logical reasoning over truth values and instead operate entirely within the domain of visual features and attentions.

3. **Developing a Modular and Reusable Architecture**: Recognizing that no single deep network topology is optimal for all computer vision tasks, and that different tasks often require different processing steps, the authors aim to create a framework where the computational structure can adapt to the specific question. The motivation is to treat VQA not as learning a single function, but as a highly multi-task learning setting where each question defines a novel task. This leads to the objective of developing reusable neural "modules" that specialize in particular visual or reasoning primitives (e.g., object recognition, spatial relations, counting) and can be dynamically assembled.

4. **Handling Complex and Multi-Step Questions**: Simple questions might require extracting one piece of information, but complex queries (e.g., "how many objects are to the left of the toaster?") necessitate multiple processing steps and potentially different kinds of computations (e.g., convolution for identification, recurrent architecture for counting). The system is motivated to handle such arbitrary complexity and depth of reasoning by allowing the number and types of processing steps to vary dynamically with the question.

5. **Achieving State-of-the-Art Performance**: A practical objective is to achieve competitive or superior performance on established VQA benchmarks, demonstrating the efficacy of the compositional approach on real-world natural images.

6. **Benchmarking Compositional Reasoning**: Recognizing that many existing VQA datasets contain relatively simple questions, a further motivation is to introduce a new dataset specifically designed to challenge models on complex compositional phenomena, including spatial relations, set-theoretic reasoning, and multi-attribute recognition. This aims to provide a more rigorous testbed for evaluating true compositional understanding.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the methodology is the **Neural Module Network (NMN)**, a framework for dynamically constructing and training deep networks based on the linguistic structure of questions.

#### 3.1. Neural Module Network Architecture

An NMN is defined by a collection of modules $\{m\}$ with associated parameters $\theta_m$, and a network layout predictor $P$ that maps natural language questions to network structures. For a given image $x$ and question $w$, the model first instantiates a network based on $P(w)$, processes the image (and potentially the question again) as input, and outputs a distribution over answers $p(y | w, x; \theta)$.

#### 3.1.1. Modules

The system employs a small set of generic module types, each performing a specific visual or logical operation, operating on three fundamental data types: images, unnormalized attentions, and labels.

- **`attend[c]` (Image → Attention)**: These modules perform convolution on the input image to produce a heatmap or unnormalized attention. Each instance `[c]` (e.g., `attend[dog]`, `attend[red]`) has distinct weights, learning to focus on specific objects or attributes.
- **`re-attend[c]` (Attention → Attention)**: These are multilayer perceptrons (MLPs) with ReLU nonlinearities that transform an existing attention. For example, `re-attend[above]` shifts attention upward, while `re-attend[not]` moves attention away from active regions. Each instance `[c]` has distinct weights.
- **`combine[c]` (Attention × Attention → Attention)**: These modules merge two attention maps into a single one. Examples include `combine[and]` for intersection of attentions or `combine[except]` for difference.
- **`classify[c]` (Image × Attention → Label)**: These modules take an attention map and the input image to produce a distribution over labels. For instance, `classify[color]` would predict the color within the attended region.
- **`measure[c]` (Attention → Label)**: These modules take an attention map alone and map it to a distribution over labels. They are suitable for tasks like determining the existence of an object or counting.

The module names (e.g., "dog", "red", "and") are not pre-programmed but serve as identifiers for distinct parameter sets that learn specific behaviors through end-to-end training.

#### 3.1.2. From Strings to Networks (Layout Generation)

The process of transforming a natural language question into an instantiated neural network layout involves two steps:

1. **Parsing**: Each question is first processed by the Stanford Parser to obtain a universal dependency representation. This parse tree expresses grammatical relations and performs lemmatization (e.g., "kites" to "kite"). A filtering step identifies dependencies connected to the *wh*-word, abstracting the core meaning of the sentence into a simple symbolic form (e.g., "what color is the truck?" becomes `color(truck)`).
2. **Layout Assignment**: This symbolic representation directly determines the structure and module instances of the neural network.
 - **Leaves** in the symbolic representation (e.g., "truck" in `color(truck)`) are mapped to `attend` modules (e.g., `attend[truck]`).
 - **Internal nodes** (e.g., "color" in `color(truck)` or "above" in `is(red, above(circle))`) become `re-attend` or `combine` modules, depending on their arity (number of arguments).
 - **Root nodes** are mapped to `measure` modules for yes/no questions or `classify` modules for other question types.
 For example, "What color is his tie?" generates `classify[color](attend[tie])`, and "Is there a red shape above a circle?" generates `measure[is](combine[and](attend[red], re-attend[above](attend[circle])))`.

#### 3.2. Joint Training with LSTM Question Encoder

The NMN is augmented with a standard single-layer LSTM question encoder (1024 hidden units). This component serves two purposes:
1. **Modeling Syntactic Regularities**: The semantic parser simplifies questions, potentially discarding grammatical cues (e.g., singular vs. plural) that influence the answer. The LSTM provides sentence context to capture these nuances.
2. **Incorporating Common-Sense Knowledge and Dataset Biases**: The LSTM helps model semantic regularities, allowing the system to make reasonable guesses even with poor image data (e.g., "what color is the bear?" implies "brown").
The LSTM also produces a distribution over answers. The final prediction of the complete model is a geometric average of the NMN's output and the LSTM's output distributions, dynamically reweighted based on text and image features. The entire NMN and LSTM system is trained jointly end-to-end.

#### 3.3. Training Details

- The objective is to maximize the likelihood of the data, as the final module in every network outputs a probability distribution.
- Due to the dynamic network structures and varying update frequencies for module weights, AdaDelta (an adaptive learning rate method) was chosen for optimization.
- Crucially, module instances (e.g., `attend[cat]`) are not manually specified or initialized with their intended behavior; they acquire their specialization (e.g., learning to recognize cats) solely through the end-to-end training process based on image-question-answer pairs and parameter tying.

#### 3.4. Datasets and Baselines

- **SHAPES Dataset**: A newly introduced synthetic dataset designed for compositional reasoning. It features simple arrangements of colored shapes and complex yes/no questions involving spatial relations and set-theoretic logic. Image features are extracted by a LeNet jointly trained with the QA model. Baseline: A re-implementation of a VIS+LSTM model similar to Ren et al. (2015), using a LeNet.
- **VQA Dataset**: A large-scale natural image dataset with human-annotated questions and answers. Visual input to the NMN is the `conv5` layer of a pre-trained VGGNet-16, without fine-tuning. Baselines: LSTM and VIS+LSTM models reported in previous work.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents experimental results on two distinct visual question answering tasks: a newly introduced synthetic dataset designed for compositionality and the VQA natural image dataset.

#### 4.1. Experiments on SHAPES Dataset (Compositionality)

The SHAPES dataset was designed to test the model's ability to handle questions with complex, compositional structures, requiring multiple processing steps and reasoning over spatial and logical relations.

- **Superior Performance**: The NMN model achieved an overall accuracy of 90.6% on the SHAPES dataset. This significantly outperformed the VIS+LSTM baseline, which scored 65.3%, and the majority guesser baseline at 63.0%.
- **Performance on Complex Questions**: The NMN demonstrated a substantial advantage on highly compositional questions. For questions requiring a network layout of size 5 (meaning 5 modules in the NMN), the NMN achieved 92.4% accuracy compared to VIS+LSTM's 62.5%. For size 6 questions, NMN scored 85.2% against VIS+LSTM's 61.7%. This indicates the NMN's architecture is better suited for multi-step reasoning.
- **Generalization to Unseen Complexity**: In an additional experiment, an NMN trained on a modified dataset without "size-6" questions (NMN (easy)) still achieved 90.8% accuracy. This outcome suggests that the model can generalize to questions more complicated than those encountered during training by extrapolating learned visual patterns and linguistic structures.
- **Module Behavior**: Qualitative inspection confirmed that individual modules, such as color detectors and attention transformations (e.g., `re-attend[above]`), behaved as expected, demonstrating that the joint training procedure successfully allocated specialized responsibilities to the modules.

#### 4.2. Experiments on VQA Natural Image Dataset

The VQA dataset evaluates the model's ability to answer questions about natural images, often involving complex perceptual challenges.

- **Overall State-of-the-Art Performance**: The full NMN+LSTM model achieved 55.1% accuracy on the VQA test server, surpassing the previously best-published results on this task, including the VIS+LSTM baseline (54.1%) and a simple LSTM (54.1%).
- **Performance by Answer Type**:
 - The NMN+LSTM model performed particularly well on questions answered by an object, attribute, or number (categorized as "Other"), achieving 39.3% accuracy, which was higher than the VIS+LSTM baseline (36.4%).
 - However, its performance on Yes/No questions (77.7%) was slightly lower than the VIS+LSTM baseline (78.9%). The authors suggested this might be attributable to overfitting on Yes/No questions.
- **Ablation Study (Importance of LSTM)**: An ablation experiment showed that the NMN without the LSTM question encoder (NMN-only) performed significantly worse, achieving only 42.7% overall accuracy. This highlights the crucial role of the LSTM in providing sentence context, capturing grammatical cues, and incorporating common-sense knowledge that the semantic parser might lose or that the visual modules alone cannot infer.
- **Error Analysis**:
 - Observed prediction errors included plausible semantic confusions (e.g., "cardboard" for "leather"), normal lexical variation (e.g., "container" for "cup"), and contextually plausible but image-unrelated answers (e.g., describing a horse in a "pen" instead of a "barn").
 - Analysis of parser outputs indicated that while simple property questions were mostly analyzed correctly (80–90%), more complex questions were prone to picking up irrelevant predicates (e.g., `be(people, likely)` instead of `is(people, work)`), suggesting room for improvement by integrating joint learning for the parsing process.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Neural Module Networks (NMNs) represents a significant contribution to the fields of visual question answering and deep learning architecture design, with several potential impacts:

1. **Advancement in Compositional Reasoning**: NMNs provide a novel and effective framework for handling the compositional nature of natural language questions in a visual context. By dynamically assembling specialized neural networks, the approach demonstrates superior performance on questions requiring multi-step reasoning and complex logical or spatial relationships, particularly evident on the SHAPES dataset. This moves VQA beyond simpler recognition tasks towards deeper semantic understanding.

2. **Bridging Symbolic and Neural AI**: The work successfully integrates the structured, interpretable reasoning capabilities derived from semantic parsing (traditionally symbolic AI) with the powerful feature learning and representational capacity of deep neural networks. This hybrid approach offers a pathway to combine the strengths of both paradigms, potentially leading to more robust and explainable AI systems.

3. **Novel Neural Architecture Design**: NMNs introduce a general architecture for discretely composing heterogeneous, jointly-trained neural modules. This paradigm shifts from designing a single, fixed deep network for all tasks to creating an inventory of reusable "neural parts" that can be dynamically assembled into task-specific computation graphs. This modularity could inspire new approaches in various deep learning applications beyond VQA.

4. **State-of-the-Art Performance**: Achieving state-of-the-art results on the challenging VQA natural image dataset, alongside strong performance on a new compositional dataset, validates the practical efficacy of the NMN approach. This sets a new benchmark and demonstrates the potential for complex, structured reasoning in real-world visual tasks.

5. **Enhanced Generalization and Extrapolation**: The demonstrated ability of NMNs to generalize to question structures more complex than those seen during training (on the SHAPES dataset) is impactful. This suggests that the system learns fundamental compositional rules and visual primitives, allowing it to extrapolate its understanding to novel combinations, a key characteristic of intelligent behavior.

6. **Broader Applicability**: While demonstrated for VQA, the authors suggest the NMN architecture has broader usefulness. It could be applied to visual referring expression resolution, question answering over natural language texts or structured knowledge bases, and even generalized signal processing or function approximation tasks. The concept of "visual SQL," where users directly construct queries from learned modules, highlights the potential for greater user control and expressivity.

7. **Future Research Directions**: The paper opens avenues for future work, such as jointly learning the network structures (i.e., the semantic parsing process) with the network parameters, rather than maintaining a strict separation. Improving specific module designs (e.g., for Yes/No questions) and further exploring the theoretical underpinnings of dynamic network construction are also highlighted as important areas.
