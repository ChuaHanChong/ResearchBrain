# VisPhyWorld: Probing Physical Reasoning via Code-Driven Video Reconstruction

> [!summary] Summary
> VisPhyWorld introduces an evaluation framework that assesses Multimodal Large Language Models' (MLLMs) physical reasoning by requiring them to reconstruct observed physical events as executable simulator code. This approach reveals that while MLLMs excel at semantic scene understanding, they significantly struggle with precise physical parameter inference and consistent dynamic simulation, highlighting the need for more grounded physical comprehension.

## Problem

- Existing recognition-style benchmarks for physical reasoning (VQA, VoE) often allow MLLMs to succeed via visual heuristics or statistical correlations, making it difficult to ascertain genuine causal physical understanding.
- MLLMs primarily output text, which does not provide a testable or falsifiable "physical hypothesis" of observed events, hindering diagnostic analysis of their underlying reasoning.
- Current MLLMs lack interpretable and verifiable world representations, making it challenging to audit their physical reasoning process for reliability in critical applications.

## Method

- A novel execution-based framework, **VisPhyWorld**, is proposed where MLLMs generate executable simulation code (object properties, initial states, dynamics) to reconstruct observed videos from keyframes and optional object detection context.
- A deterministic physics renderer executes the generated code, decoupling the **MLLM**'s reasoning from visual rendering and enabling direct inspection and falsification of the inferred physical hypothesis.
- **VisPhyBench**, a benchmark of 209 synthetic 2D/3D physical scenes with varying difficulty, is introduced alongside a multi-metric evaluation protocol encompassing perceptual, semantic, motion, and a holistic AI judge.

## Results

- MLLMs achieve high perceptual and semantic scores (e.g., low LPIPS, high CLIP-Img) but frequently fail to infer accurate physical parameters, leading to visually plausible but physically inconsistent motion in reconstructions.
- The iterative self-repair mechanism improved code generation success rates from 85.3% to 97.9% for P5.js and 97.9% to 99.0% for Three.js, indicating that many initial failures are correctable surface-level issues.
- Pixel-space video generation baselines can achieve competitive perceptual scores but often produce physically implausible motion without diagnostic interpretability; the Gemini-3-Pro (threejs) model achieved the highest holistic score by balancing visual alignment and physical coherence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current MLLMs exhibit a significant gap between strong semantic scene understanding and weak capabilities in inferring precise physical parameters and consistently simulating dynamics.
> - Requiring MLLMs to generate executable code for physical simulation offers a more rigorous and diagnostic evaluation than pixel-space video generation, exposing specific reasoning failures.
> - The use of physics-enabled rendering backends is crucial, as MLLMs struggle to infer and apply fundamental Newtonian dynamics without offloading these principles to specialized engines.
