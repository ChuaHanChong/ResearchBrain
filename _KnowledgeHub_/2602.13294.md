# VisPhyWorld: Probing Physical Reasoning via Code-Driven Video Reconstruction

> [!summary] Summary
> VisPhyWorld introduces an evaluation framework that assesses Multimodal Large Language Models' (MLLMs) physical reasoning by requiring them to reconstruct observed physical events as executable simulator code. This approach reveals that while MLLMs excel at semantic scene understanding, they significantly struggle with precise physical parameter inference and consistent dynamic simulation, highlighting the need for more grounded physical comprehension.

## Problem

- Existing recognition-style benchmarks for physical reasoning (VQA, VoE) often allow MLLMs to succeed via visual heuristics or statistical correlations, making it difficult to ascertain genuine causal physical understanding.
- MLLMs primarily output text, which does not provide a testable or falsifiable "physical hypothesis" of observed events, hindering diagnostic analysis of their underlying reasoning.
- Current MLLMs lack interpretable and verifiable world representations, making it challenging to audit their physical reasoning process for reliability in critical applications.

## Method

- A novel execution-based framework, **VisPhyWorld**, is proposed where MLLMs generate executable simulation code (object properties, initial states, dynamics) to reconstruct observed videos from keyframes and optional object detection context.
- A deterministic physics renderer executes the generated code, decoupling the **MLLM**'s reasoning from visual rendering and enabling direct inspection and falsification of the inferred physical hypothesis.
- **VisPhyBench**, a benchmark of 209 synthetic 2D/3D physical scenes with varying difficulty, is introduced alongside a multi-metric evaluation protocol encompassing perceptual, semantic, motion, and a holistic AI judge.

## Results

- MLLMs achieve high perceptual and semantic scores (e.g., low LPIPS, high CLIP-Img) but frequently fail to infer accurate physical parameters, leading to visually plausible but physically inconsistent motion in reconstructions.
- The iterative self-repair mechanism improved code generation success rates from 85.3% to 97.9% for P5.js and 97.9% to 99.0% for Three.js, indicating that many initial failures are correctable surface-level issues.
- Pixel-space video generation baselines can achieve competitive perceptual scores but often produce physically implausible motion without diagnostic interpretability; the Gemini-3-Pro (threejs) model achieved the highest holistic score by balancing visual alignment and physical coherence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current MLLMs exhibit a significant gap between strong semantic scene understanding and weak capabilities in inferring precise physical parameters and consistently simulating dynamics.
> - Requiring MLLMs to generate executable code for physical simulation offers a more rigorous and diagnostic evaluation than pixel-space video generation, exposing specific reasoning failures.
> - The use of physics-enabled rendering backends is crucial, as MLLMs struggle to infer and apply fundamental Newtonian dynamics without offloading these principles to specialized engines.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge within the field of artificial intelligence, specifically the evaluation of physical reasoning capabilities in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). Existing approaches to assessing physical understanding in MLLMs primarily rely on "recognition-style" protocols such as Visual Question Answering (VQA) and Violation of Expectation (VoE) tasks. These methods evaluate models based on their ability to select correct answers or identify physically implausible events. However, the paper argues that such recognition-based evaluations can be susceptible to models exploiting surface-level visual correlations, learned biases, or dataset-driven guessing, rather than demonstrating genuine, explicit physical understanding. MLLMs, which typically produce textual outputs, further complicate the use of generative video protocols that rely on predictive likelihoods or measures of surprise.

The paper posits that for MLLMs, a more rigorous evaluation requires models to reconstruct and re-simulate physical events. This necessitates committing to an explicit and testable physical hypothesis, rather than merely identifying or describing an outcome. The proposed VisPhyWorld framework introduces an "execution-based" paradigm, moving beyond passive recognition to active reconstruction through code generation. This approach positions the work at the intersection of physical reasoning, MLLM evaluation, and executable world representations in computer graphics and simulation.

Prior work on intuitive physics often involves video prediction benchmarks or VoE paradigms using videos generated from physics engines (e.g., Phyre, Physion, IntPhys). More recent benchmarks extend to generative video models. While some MLLM-based evaluators exist (e.g., VideoPhy, VideoScore) for physical commonsense, they typically frame the evaluation as recognition tasks. VisPhyWorld differentiates itself by requiring an *executable physical hypothesis*, thereby offering a diagnostic interface for physical reasoning by making object states and dynamics explicit and inspectable, rather than only assessing visual realism or animation quality. This contrasts with methods focusing on generating executable code for static visualizations or general animations, where the primary goal is content creation rather than probing underlying physical understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and transparent framework for evaluating whether Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) possess genuine physical reasoning capabilities. The authors identify a significant limitation in existing evaluation protocols:

- **Reliance on Recognition-Style Tasks:** Most current benchmarks, such as VQA and VoE, evaluate physical understanding by asking models to recognize correct outcomes or identify violations. This passive approach allows models to succeed by leveraging superficial visual patterns, learned statistical correlations, or dataset biases, without necessarily demonstrating a deep, causal understanding of physical dynamics. The concern is that correct answers might stem from "memorized priors and surface-level pattern matching rather than genuine causal physical understanding."
- **Lack of Explicit Physical Hypotheses:** MLLMs typically output text, making it difficult to ascertain their underlying physical logic or the specific parameters inferred. Unlike generative world models that produce predictive likelihoods, MLLMs do not readily provide verifiable intermediate representations of their physical understanding. This opacity makes it challenging to diagnose the root causes of errors—whether they relate to object recognition, physical parameter inference, or simulation dynamics.

To address these limitations, the authors are motivated to establish an evaluation paradigm that:

1. **Demands Executable Hypotheses:** Models should be required to generate executable code that reconstructs a physical scene and simulates its future motion. This forces MLLMs to commit to an "explicit, testable physical hypothesis," making their inferred world representation directly inspectable, editable, and falsifiable.
2. **Decouples Reasoning from Rendering:** By generating code for a physics engine, the process of physical reasoning (inferring parameters and dynamics) is separated from the rendering process. This allows for a more controlled evaluation where the consistency of the physical simulation can be assessed independently of visual generation quality.
3. **Provides Diagnostic Capabilities:** Executable code acts as a "reasoning artifact," allowing direct inspection of the model's physical logic. This transparency facilitates diagnosing where and why a model's physical understanding might be deficient (e.g., incorrect initial conditions, faulty dynamics parameters, or misinterpretation of interactions).

The key contributions outlined in the paper align with these motivations:
- **VisPhyWorld Framework:** A novel framework that uses MLLMs to interpret video frames and generate executable simulation code for predicting future motion. This is presented as the first paradigm evaluating physical reasoning in MLLMs through code reconstruction and re-simulation, aiming to provide an interpretable view of a model's physical understanding.
- **VisPhyBench Evaluation Suite:** A unified benchmark comprising 209 evaluation scenes derived from 108 physical templates (both 2D and 3D) with a systematic protocol to assess appearance reconstruction and physically plausible motion. This aims to provide a standardized tool for rigorous evaluation across different aspects.
- **In-depth Analysis of MLLMs:** An experimental analysis demonstrating that while state-of-the-art MLLMs achieve strong semantic scene understanding, they struggle to accurately infer physical parameters and to simulate consistent physical dynamics. This aims to highlight a critical gap where models may rely on "superficial visual pattern matching rather than a grounded understanding of physical causality."

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The VisPhyWorld framework is designed to evaluate physical reasoning in MLLMs by having them interpret visual observations and reconstruct the underlying physical scene as executable code. This process involves several stages:

#### 3.1. Problem Definition

The focus is on 2D and 3D physical scenes involving interactions such as ball collisions and box sliding, represented as sequences of image frames $X = (I_t)_{t=1}^T$.
- **Input:** The MLLM receives two key frames, $I_{start}$ (e.g., first frame) and $I_{later}$ (e.g., a later frame like frame 10). Optionally, a detection context $D$ is provided for $I_{start}$, listing objects with categories, bounding boxes, and coarse attributes (obtained via GPT-5.2 or set to empty if unavailable).
- **Outputs:** VisPhyWorld generates four artifacts:
 - **Textual motion analysis (A):** A natural language description of salient motions and interactions.
 - **Machine-readable first-frame JSON specification (S):** Encoding object layout and inferred parameters.
 - **Executable program (C):** The core output, containing simulation code.
 - **Rendered video ($\hat{X}$):** Obtained by executing program C with a physics engine.

#### 3.2. VisPhyWorld Architecture

The system implements a composite mapping: $(I_{start}, I_{later}, D) \xrightarrow{f_{LLM}} (A, S, C) \xrightarrow{R_{phys}} \hat{X}$.
- **LLM-based Agent:** An MLLM acts as the central agent, taking the visual input and optional detection context to perform motion analysis and generate raw executable code.
- **Physical Renderer ($R_{phys}$):** This component takes the generated code, sanitizes it, and executes it to produce the simulated video. The generated code (C) is treated as an explicit, falsifiable physical hypothesis.
- **Validation and Robustness:** To ensure well-defined evaluation, generated code undergoes lightweight validation before execution. If the initial execution fails, a single automatic repair attempt is made, providing error diagnostics to the MLLM. If both attempts fail, the system falls back to a minimal valid scene template to prevent pipeline crashes.

#### 3.3. Benchmark, Metrics, and Baselines (VisPhyBench)

- **Dataset Construction:** VisPhyBench is built upon and extends the 2D data from the PhyWorld dataset, using the PHYRE engine for the 2D subset. A new 3D subset is curated using Three.js for rendering and Cannon.js for rigid-body dynamics. The benchmark comprises 108 physical templates and 209 videos, each with first-frame JSON annotations and coarse difficulty levels (easy, medium, hard) rated by STEM-trained annotators. Scenes cover diverse object configurations and motion patterns, with fixed orthographic (2D) or perspective (3D) cameras and white backgrounds.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Five families of metrics are employed:
 1. **Reconstruction and perceptual quality:** PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FSIM, VSI, DISTS, computed on temporally aligned frames.
 2. **Visual semantic consistency:** CLIP-based image similarity (CLIP-Img) and DINO feature similarity.
 3. **Text–video and analysis-text consistency:** CLIP text–image similarity (CLIP-Cap) between analysis text and video frames, and ROUGE-L, BERTScore-F1 to compare analysis text with a GPT-generated reference.
 4. **Motion and physical plausibility:** RAFT-based optical-flow diagnostics (End-Point Error (EPE), temporal offset), interpreted jointly with holistic perceptual/physics judgments.
 5. **Subjective overall quality:** A Gemini-2.5-Pro video-video judge (1–10 score with textual justification) and a model-blind human evaluation score (1–5). Pipeline success rate (valid video produced) is also reported.
- **Video Model Baselines:** Pixel-space video generation models like Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) img2vid, Sora-2, Veo-3.1, and Cosmos-Predict2.5-2B are included for comparison, conditioned on the same visual frames and prompts.

#### 3.4. Engine Evaluation and Selection

The researchers evaluated several rendering backends: Three.js, P5.js, SVG, Manim, and Blender.
- **Backend Comparison:** Three.js and P5.js, which integrate rigid-body physics solvers, demonstrated superior reconstruction and motion fidelity by offloading physics computations. SVG and Manim, primarily non-physics-based rendering systems, often produced physically implausible behaviors (e.g., interpenetration), indicating a limitation of MLLMs in inferring and applying Newtonian dynamics without an explicit physics solver.
- **Selection:** Three.js and P5.js were prioritized for the main evaluation to emphasize physically grounded re-simulation. A cross-engine stress test with GPT-5 showed that while absolute visual scores varied, scene-level motion difficulty rankings were stable across engines (Spearman correlation of ρ = 0.84 for RAFT-EPE), indicating that conclusions were not solely due to backend-specific coding artifacts.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on VisPhyBench revealed several key insights regarding the physical reasoning capabilities of MLLMs:

#### 4.1. Overall MLLM Performance and Divergence in Reasoning

- **Strong Semantic Understanding, Weak Physical Grasp:** State-of-the-art MLLMs (GPT-5, GPT-4.1, Gemini-3-Pro, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Qwen3-VL-Plus) demonstrated strong semantic scene understanding, achieving high reconstruction and perceptual scores (low LPIPS, high CLIP-Img, DINO scores). This indicates their ability to recover object identities and global layouts from visual input. However, despite this visual proficiency, they exhibited significant limitations in fine-grained physical comprehension, frequently failing to parameterize simple Newtonian dynamics correctly.
- **Divergence of Visual and Language-Based Reasoning:** The benchmark demonstrated that strong visual reconstruction does not always translate to strong language-based reasoning or accurate physical simulation. For example, Gemini-3-Pro with Three.js achieved high perceptual scores (lowest LPIPS, highest CLIP-Img), but other models like GPT-4.1 with Three.js achieved higher BERT-F1 scores, indicating better language analysis. This highlights that VisPhyBench effectively separates a model’s ability to "see" from its ability to "explain" or "simulate" a scene.
- **Impact of Code Backend:** Three.js-based variants consistently outperformed their P5.js counterparts in reconstruction quality (e.g., GPT-5 with Three.js reduced LPIPS by nearly 40% compared to P5.js). This suggests that the structure and capabilities of the backend interface influence the MLLM's ability to translate visual evidence into an executable simulation.
- **Limitations of Pixel-Space Baselines:** Video generation baselines (SVD, Sora-2, Veo-3.1, Cosmos-Predict2.5-2B) showed competitive performance on some feature semantics but lacked interpretability. Their failures were difficult to attribute to specific physical causes (e.g., friction, restitution) because their generation process does not expose interpretable latent variables. Veo-3.1 achieved reasonable semantic similarity but often exhibited physically implausible motion. Cosmos-Predict2.5-2B had the lowest visual scores and human ratings, struggling with consistent object identities. Code-driven approaches, conversely, maintained competitive semantic and motion scores while providing transparent, executable states for diagnosis.
- **Holistic Physical Plausibility:** The combination of RAFT-EPE (optical-flow discrepancy) and a Gemini-2.5-Pro judge (aggregating visual and physical cues) was crucial. High RAFT-EPE alone could be misleading (e.g., Qwen3-VL-Plus with p5.js had the lowest RAFT-EPE but poor perceptual quality and Gemini score). Genuine physical understanding required both motion agreement and perceptually coherent outcomes, with the Gemini judge providing qualitative justifications for physical violations.

#### 4.2. Robustness and Ablation Studies

- **Detection Context Ablation:** Removing the structured object list (D) as input led to a clear drop in LPIPS and Gemini scores across all models, while other metrics were less affected. This indicates that detection context primarily aids object discovery and state initialization, rather than directly solving physical reasoning.
- **Judge Replacement:** Evaluations using alternative holistic judges (GPT-5.4 and Qwen3-VL-Plus) showed consistent overall rankings, supporting the robustness of the conclusions against the choice of a specific evaluator.
- **Forecast-Only Ablation:** Removing the later input frame ($I_{later}$) resulted in a more pronounced drop in Gemini physical-plausibility scores, even while visual metrics and RAFT-EPE changed modestly. This suggests that $I_{later}$ provides crucial physical context, without which models can generate visually reasonable but less physically accurate motion.
- **Direct Parameter Recovery:** Models made non-trivial errors in inferring physical parameters like restitution, friction, dynamic mass, and initial speed, even while gravity was consistently recovered (as a stable default). Gemini-3-Pro, despite strong visual reconstruction, showed substantial dynamic-mass error. This finding directly supports the conclusion that MLLMs struggle to infer the quantitative parameters necessary for faithful physical simulation, often relying on appearance rather than a deep understanding of underlying physics.

#### 4.3. Case Studies

- **2D Multi-body Interactions (Figure 5):** GPT-5 with Three.js demonstrated strong physical grounding by correctly simulating collision dynamics (Gemini score 10.0, DINO 0.926). Pixel-space baselines like Veo-3.1 achieved high semantic similarity (DINO 0.835) but failed on event logic (Gemini score 2.0), indicating plausible appearance with "hallucinated dynamics." Qwen3-VL-Plus had low RAFT-EPE but was penalized by Gemini for producing static or empty outputs, underscoring that optical-flow errors alone are insufficient for physical assessment.
- **3D Scene with Depth and Occlusions (Figure 6):** Similar conclusions held in 3D settings. Strong appearance matching did not guarantee physically faithful dynamics. Models like Claude-4.5 and Qwen3-VL-Plus exhibited reconstruction deficits, yet their visual-semantic scores did not significantly diverge from other models. This highlighted the dissociation between semantic alignment and correct physical dynamics, and indicated the increased challenge of 3D physical reasoning for current MLLMs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VisPhyWorld framework and VisPhyBench dataset introduce a novel and more rigorous approach to evaluating physical reasoning in Multimodal Large Language Models. By requiring models to generate executable simulation code from visual observations, the work effectively decouples "visual mimicry from physically grounded reasoning."

The key significance and potential impact are:

- **Enhanced Diagnostic Capabilities:** The framework provides a transparent and interpretable diagnostic tool. Unlike black-box recognition or video generation systems, the code-driven approach makes the model's inferred physical hypothesis explicit. This allows researchers to directly inspect, edit, and falsify the underlying physical logic, enabling more precise identification of deficiencies in object discovery, state initialization, parameter inference (e.g., mass, friction, restitution), or contact modeling. This moves beyond opaque "correct/incorrect" judgments to a more granular understanding of model failures.
- **Identification of Key MLLM Limitations:** The research demonstrates that while current MLLMs excel at semantic scene understanding, they "struggle with precise physical parameterization." This reveals a critical gap where models often rely on superficial visual pattern matching rather than a grounded, quantitative understanding of physical causality. The inability to accurately infer latent physical parameters (such as dynamic mass or initial speed) even for simple Newtonian dynamics highlights that impressive visual fidelity does not equate to robust physical intelligence.
- **Guidance for Future AI Development:** The findings suggest that future progress in robust world modeling may benefit from a shift "beyond purely statistical pattern matching in pixel space toward hybrid representations that ground visual perception in verifiable, executable physical laws." This implies that integrating symbolic reasoning, explicit physics engines, or structured code generation more deeply into MLLM architectures could be a promising direction for developing AI systems with genuinely intuitive physics.
- **Standardized and Comprehensive Benchmark:** VisPhyBench, with its 2D and 3D scenes, diverse templates, and multi-metric evaluation protocol, offers a standardized resource for evaluating physical understanding. The inclusion of perception, semantics, language consistency, motion, and subjective quality metrics provides a holistic assessment, reducing the risk of single-metric biases.
- **Promotion of Transparent and Verifiable AI:** By emphasizing executable hypotheses, VisPhyWorld contributes to the broader goal of building more transparent and verifiable AI systems. The ability to audit an AI's physical reasoning through its generated code represents a step towards more trustworthy and explainable AI in domains requiring physical interaction and prediction.

While the current work focuses on synthetic, simulator-driven scenes and relatively simple rigid-body dynamics, its findings pave the way for future research into more complex 3D reasoning, long-horizon interactions, and integration with real-world video data. The identified dissociation between visual recognition and quantitative physical understanding is a fundamental insight that can guide the development of next-generation AI models.
