# From Explicit CoT to Implicit CoT: Learning to Internalize CoT Step by Step

> [!summary] Summary
> The Allen Institute for AI, University of Waterloo, University of Washington, and Harvard University introduce Stepwise Internalization, a training method that enables language models to solve complex reasoning tasks like multi-digit multiplication and grade-school math problems without generating explicit intermediate steps. This approach internalizes reasoning, achieving high accuracy with improved computational efficiency and offering a configurable balance between speed and performance.

## Problem

- Explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, while effective for complex tasks, introduces significant computational overhead due to increased sequence length and slower inference times.
- Existing approaches for implicit CoT, such as knowledge distillation, can be complex and may not fully capture the reasoning capabilities of the original models.
- The computational cost of explicit CoT limits its practical use in resource-constrained environments or real-time applications.

## Method

- The paper introduces ==Stepwise Internalization (SI)==, a ==curriculum-based training== method that gradually removes explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) tokens from training data.
- Models are initially trained with full CoT, then continuously fine-tuned as intermediate steps are progressively removed until only the input and final output remain.
- The approach incorporates techniques such as a ==linear removal schedule==, ==optimizer resetting==, and removal smoothing to ensure stable training dynamics as steps are internalized.

## Results

- A GPT-2 Small model trained with Stepwise Internalization achieved near-perfect accuracy on **9x9** multi-digit multiplication without explicit steps, outperforming standard training.
- The Mistral-7B model using Stepwise Internalization reached over **50%** accuracy on GSM8K math problems without generating intermediate steps, matching or exceeding prior knowledge distillation methods for implicit reasoning.
- The method demonstrates a clear accuracy-speed tradeoff, allowing models to achieve explicit CoT-level accuracy with improved speed or maximize speed by internalizing more steps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Language models can be systematically trained to internalize complex reasoning steps, transitioning from explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) to implicit problem-solving.
> - Gradual and structured removal of intermediate steps, combined with specific training adjustments (like optimizer resets), is crucial for successful internalization.
> - The approach allows for a flexible balance between computational speed and reasoning accuracy, enabling deployment across diverse application requirements.
