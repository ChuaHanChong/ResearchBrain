# From Explicit CoT to Implicit CoT: Learning to Internalize CoT Step by Step

> [!summary] Summary
> The Allen Institute for AI, University of Waterloo, University of Washington, and Harvard University introduce Stepwise Internalization, a training method that enables language models to solve complex reasoning tasks like multi-digit multiplication and grade-school math problems without generating explicit intermediate steps. This approach internalizes reasoning, achieving high accuracy with improved computational efficiency and offering a configurable balance between speed and performance.

## Problem

- Explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, while effective for complex tasks, introduces significant computational overhead due to increased sequence length and slower inference times.
- Existing approaches for implicit CoT, such as knowledge distillation, can be complex and may not fully capture the reasoning capabilities of the original models.
- The computational cost of explicit CoT limits its practical use in resource-constrained environments or real-time applications.

## Method

- The paper introduces ==Stepwise Internalization (SI)==, a ==curriculum-based training== method that gradually removes explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) tokens from training data.
- Models are initially trained with full CoT, then continuously fine-tuned as intermediate steps are progressively removed until only the input and final output remain.
- The approach incorporates techniques such as a ==linear removal schedule==, ==optimizer resetting==, and removal smoothing to ensure stable training dynamics as steps are internalized.

## Results

- A GPT-2 Small model trained with Stepwise Internalization achieved near-perfect accuracy on **9x9** multi-digit multiplication without explicit steps, outperforming standard training.
- The Mistral-7B model using Stepwise Internalization reached over **50%** accuracy on GSM8K math problems without generating intermediate steps, matching or exceeding prior knowledge distillation methods for implicit reasoning.
- The method demonstrates a clear accuracy-speed tradeoff, allowing models to achieve explicit CoT-level accuracy with improved speed or maximize speed by internalizing more steps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Language models can be systematically trained to internalize complex reasoning steps, transitioning from explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) to implicit problem-solving.
> - Gradual and structured removal of intermediate steps, combined with specific training adjustments (like optimizer resets), is crucial for successful internalization.
> - The approach allows for a flexible balance between computational speed and reasoning accuracy, enabling deployment across diverse application requirements.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Introduction

Language models (LMs) have made remarkable progress in handling complex reasoning tasks by using Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, which involves generating explicit intermediate reasoning steps before producing a final answer. While effective, this approach comes with computational overhead due to the increased sequence length required to generate these intermediate steps.

In this overview, we explore the paper "From Explicit CoT to Implicit CoT: Learning to Internalize CoT Step by Step" by Yuntian Deng, Yejin Choi, and Stuart Shieber from the Allen Institute for Artificial Intelligence, University of Waterloo, University of Washington, and Harvard University. The authors introduce a novel approach called Stepwise Internalization that trains language models to perform reasoning tasks without generating explicit intermediate steps, effectively internalizing the reasoning process within the model's parameters.

#### 1.2. Chain of Thought Reasoning

Chain-of-Thought reasoning has become a standard technique for improving language model performance on complex tasks such as mathematical problem solving, logical reasoning, and multi-step decision-making. In explicit CoT, a model is prompted or trained to generate intermediate reasoning steps that break down complex problems into smaller, more manageable components.

For example, when solving a multi-digit multiplication problem like "21 × 43", an explicit CoT approach might involve:
1. Multiply 1 × 3 = 3
2. Multiply 2 × 3 = 6
3. Multiply 1 × 4 = 4
4. Multiply 2 × 4 = 8
5. Combine these partial products: 3 in the ones place, 6+4=10 in the tens place (0 with 1 carried), and 8+1=9 in the hundreds place
6. Final answer: 903

While this approach improves accuracy, it comes at the cost of increased computational resources and slower inference times due to the generation of these intermediate steps.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

#### 2.1. The Challenge with Explicit CoT

Despite its effectiveness, explicit CoT faces several limitations:

1. **Computational Inefficiency**: Generating intermediate steps increases the sequence length, which directly impacts the computational cost and inference time.

2. **Architectural Mismatch**: The authors argue that explicit reasoning might not align with the intrinsic computational strengths of language models, which excel at pattern recognition rather than explicit logical reasoning.

3. **Real-world Applications**: In many real-world scenarios, such as real-time decision-making or resource-constrained environments, the computational overhead of explicit CoT may be prohibitive.

Existing approaches to implicit CoT reasoning often rely on knowledge distillation (KD) techniques, where a student model learns to replicate the outputs of a teacher model that uses explicit CoT. However, these methods can be complex and may not fully capture the reasoning capabilities of the original model.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Stepwise Internalization Approach

The paper introduces Stepwise Internalization (SI), a novel training method that gradually removes intermediate reasoning steps from the training data, allowing the model to internalize the reasoning process. The key idea is to transform the model from explicit to implicit reasoning in a gradual, step-by-step manner.

The process involves:

1. **Initial Training with Explicit CoT**: The model is first trained on data with complete chain-of-thought reasoning steps.

2. **Gradual Token Removal**: The training process then systematically removes tokens from the reasoning chain, one step at a time.

3. **Continuous Fine-tuning**: After each removal, the model is fine-tuned to predict the final output based on the remaining steps.

4. **Complete Internalization**: Eventually, all intermediate steps are removed, and the model learns to predict the final answer directly from the input.

This approach differs from knowledge distillation in that it doesn't require a separate teacher model. Instead, it uses a curriculum-based learning strategy to gradually shift the model's reasoning from explicit to implicit.

#### 3.2. Implementation Details

The implementation of Stepwise Internalization involves several key components:

1. **Linear Removal Schedule**: The authors use a linear schedule to determine how many CoT tokens are removed at each training step:

 $s(t) = \min\left(\lfloor \frac{t}{T} \cdot N_{\text{CoT}} \rfloor, N_{\text{CoT}}\right)$

 where $t$ is the current training step, $T$ is the total number of training steps, and $N_{\text{CoT}}$ is the total number of CoT tokens to remove.

2. **Optimizer Resetting**: To address training instability caused by changes in the loss function, the optimizer's state is reset whenever a CoT token is removed.

3. **Removal Smoothing**: A small random offset is added to the number of tokens to remove to smooth the transition between stages:

 $s'(t) = s(t) + \text{uniform}(-\delta, \delta)$

 This technique helps prevent sudden shifts in training dynamics.

The training process is applied to two reasoning tasks:
- **Multi-digit multiplication**: A synthetic dataset of multiplication problems of varying complexity (from 2×2 to 9×9 digits).
- **GSM8K**: A benchmark dataset of grade-school math word problems requiring multi-step reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Experimental Results

The paper demonstrates impressive results across different model scales and tasks:

1. **Multi-digit Multiplication**:
 - A GPT-2 Small model trained with Stepwise Internalization achieves near-perfect accuracy on 9×9 multiplication.
 - In contrast, standard training without CoT struggles even with 4×4 multiplication.
 - The model learns to internalize the multiplication algorithm without explicitly writing out the intermediate steps.

2. **GSM8K Math Problems**:
 - Stepwise Internalization achieves state-of-the-art results for models not using intermediate steps on the GSM8K dataset.
 - The Mistral-7B model trained with SI achieves over 50% accuracy without generating any intermediate steps.
 - This performance significantly surpasses models trained without CoT and matches or exceeds models using knowledge distillation approaches.

The distribution analysis of errors shows that the models trained with SI predominantly produce either the correct answer (98% of cases) or a single incorrect answer (2% of cases), with very few instances of multiple incorrect answers.

#### 4.2. Accuracy-Speed Tradeoff

One of the significant advantages of Stepwise Internalization is the ability to balance accuracy and computational speed by internalizing different amounts of CoT tokens. The paper demonstrates a clear tradeoff curve:

As shown in the figure, ICoT-SI (Implicit CoT with Stepwise Internalization) offers a range of operating points along the tradeoff curve:
- At one extreme, the model can match the accuracy of explicit CoT with slightly better speed.
- At intermediate points, the model can offer various balances of accuracy and speed.
- At the other extreme (No CoT), the model maximizes speed but sacrifices accuracy.

This flexibility allows practitioners to choose the optimal balance for their specific application requirements.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

The paper includes comprehensive ablation studies to understand the importance of different components in the Stepwise Internalization approach:

The results show that:
1. **Optimizer Reset**: Resetting the optimizer when CoT tokens are removed is crucial for stable training.
2. **Removal Smoothing**: Adding a small random offset to the token removal schedule improves stability.
3. **Removal Direction**: Removing tokens from left to right (the approach used in the full method) outperforms right-to-left removal.
4. **Removal Schedule**: The gradual linear schedule outperforms more aggressive removal strategies.

The paper also investigates the sensitivity to random seeds, showing that the full approach is more robust than unsuccessful training runs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Practical Implications

The Stepwise Internalization approach has several practical implications:

1. **Computational Efficiency**: By eliminating the need for generating explicit reasoning steps, models can perform complex reasoning tasks with reduced computational resources and faster inference times.

2. **Resource-Constrained Environments**: The method is particularly valuable in scenarios where computational resources are limited, such as edge devices or real-time applications.

3. **Model Scaling**: The technique is effective across different model scales, from smaller models like GPT-2 to larger models like Mistral-7B, suggesting broad applicability.

4. **Flexible Deployment**: The ability to trade off accuracy and speed allows for flexible deployment across different use cases with varying requirements.

5. **Training Simplicity**: Compared to knowledge distillation approaches, Stepwise Internalization offers a simpler training procedure that doesn't require maintaining separate teacher and student models.

#### 5.2. Conclusion

The paper "From Explicit CoT to Implicit CoT: Learning to Internalize CoT Step by Step" presents a significant advancement in the field of language model reasoning. The proposed Stepwise Internalization method offers a novel approach to training models that can perform complex reasoning tasks without generating explicit intermediate steps.

The key contributions include:

1. A simple yet effective training method that gradually transforms explicit reasoning into implicit reasoning.
2. State-of-the-art results on mathematical reasoning tasks without generating intermediate steps.
3. A flexible framework that allows for balancing accuracy and computational efficiency.
4. Insights into the training dynamics of implicit reasoning models.

The research opens up new possibilities for efficient deployment of reasoning-capable language models in resource-constrained environments and real-time applications. As language models continue to evolve, techniques like Stepwise Internalization will play a crucial role in making advanced reasoning capabilities more accessible and practical for a wide range of applications.
