# Low-Rank Adaptation for Critic Learning in Off-Policy Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers integrated Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) into off-policy reinforcement learning critics to enhance stability and performance, particularly for large-capacity networks. This approach, which also includes a specialized LoRA formulation for hyperspherical weight normalization, consistently improved agent performance across diverse DeepMind Control and IsaacLab robotics tasks compared to full-parameter and static sparsity baselines.

## Problem

- Large critic networks in off-policy reinforcement learning algorithms (e.g., SAC, TD3) are prone to overfitting and instability.
- The reliance on bootstrapped targets from non-stationary replay data exacerbates optimization challenges for scaled critics.
- Simply increasing critic capacity often fails to yield expected performance benefits, sometimes leading to plasticity loss.

## Method

- ==Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)== was adapted as a structural regularizer for off-policy critics, constraining updates to a low-dimensional subspace by training only small, low-rank adapters while freezing base matrices.
- A specialized **LoRA-WN** formulation was developed to be compatible with ==hyperspherical weight normalization==, preserving the frozen base matrix's geometric structure during updates.
- The LoRA-based critic learning method was integrated and evaluated with ==Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)== and ==FastTD3== algorithms on scaled SimbaV2 architectures.

## Results

- LoRA consistently achieved superior performance on DeepMind Control tasks (**777.2** average return vs. **719.7** for SimbaV2 with larger critics) and demonstrated consistent gains on IsaacLab robotics tasks (**35.6** vs. **32.5** for SimbaV2 on locomotion).
- Pure LoRA training was competitive or outperformed hybrid schemes involving a dense critic warm-up phase, indicating the immediate regularization benefit.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the LoRA-WN module and the frozen base matrix were essential for robust performance, and LoRA generally outperformed static sparsity at matched trainable parameter budgets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LoRA's primary benefit in off-policy RL is its role as an optimization regularizer, improving critic learning in complex, non-stationary settings rather than merely reducing model capacity.
> - Training LoRA from scratch proved highly effective, suggesting that applying the low-rank constraint early on guides optimization trajectories more effectively than a dense warm-up phase.
> - The proposed hyperspherical weight normalization (LoRA-WN) is crucial for the method's performance and robustness, highlighting the importance of maintaining the geometric structure of the frozen base matrix.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Off-policy reinforcement learning (RL) is a prominent paradigm for continuous control, largely due to its ability to reuse past experiences, thereby enhancing sample efficiency compared to on-policy methods. At the core of these algorithms is the critic network, responsible for estimating action values and guiding policy improvement. The quality of this critic is central to the stability, scalability, and performance of off-policy RL.

Prior research aimed at improving off-policy RL has explored several avenues in critic design. Early efforts focused on mitigating estimation bias, exemplified by Double Q-learning. More recent work has investigated critic architectural elements, such as the incorporation of Batch Normalization in CrossQ to improve sample efficiency, the development of residual and normalization-based backbones like SimBa for scaling network capacity, and SimbaV2, which introduced hyperspherical normalization and distributional value estimation for larger critics. Another line of research redefined the critic learning objective, with distributional RL modeling full return distributions, and methods that recast value regression as classification to improve scalability.

Concurrently, there has been a growing interest in scaling model capacity in continuous-control RL. Works such as TD-MPC2 and BRO have demonstrated that larger critics, when combined with robust regularization and exploration strategies, can improve performance and robustness. However, simply scaling dense RL networks often leads to performance degradation. Larger critics in off-policy learning are particularly susceptible to overfitting and instability, primarily because they rely on bootstrapped targets derived from non-stationary replay data. This vulnerability indicates that effective critic scaling requires not only increased model capacity but also appropriate structural regularization to manage these optimization challenges.

In the broader context of machine learning, regularization techniques are widely used to prevent overfitting, including weight decay and dropout in supervised learning. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods, such as Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), have also emerged as implicit regularizers by constraining optimization to a low-dimensional subspace of parameters. In RL, specific regularization techniques have been developed to address unique challenges, such as DroQ using dropout for critic stability and studies demonstrating that static network sparsity can mitigate issues like plasticity loss and gradient interference in large RL networks.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by addressing the need for effective structural regularization in scaling off-policy RL critics. It draws inspiration from PEFT methods but re-purposes LoRA not for fine-tuning pre-trained models, but as a structural regularizer for critics trained *from scratch*. By restricting weight updates to a low-dimensional subspace while maintaining a dense forward pass, the work seeks to improve critic robustness and learning stability in the face of non-stationary and bootstrapped data, thereby enabling more effective scaling of critic capacity in off-policy RL.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the observed challenges in scaling off-policy reinforcement learning critics. While increasing critic capacity can enhance expressiveness and has contributed to the performance of contemporary RL methods, larger dense critics are prone to overfitting and instability. This vulnerability arises from their reliance on bootstrapped targets and non-stationary data within replay buffers, leading to difficulties in optimization and hindering the full realization of increased model capacity. The authors contend that successful critic scaling necessitates not only larger models but also appropriate structural regularization.

Against this background, the key objectives of the paper are:

1. **Develop a Structural Regularizer for Critic Learning:** The central objective is to propose and evaluate Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) as a structural regularization mechanism for off-policy critic learning. Unlike its typical application in fine-tuning large pre-trained models, this work applies LoRA to critics learned *from scratch*. The hypothesis is that by freezing randomly initialized base matrices and optimizing only low-rank adapters, critic updates can be constrained to a low-dimensional subspace, thereby mitigating overfitting.

2. **Evaluate LoRA's Effectiveness in Reducing Critic Loss and Improving Policy Performance:** A core objective is to empirically demonstrate that this low-rank adaptation approach can efficiently reduce critic loss during training and consequently improve overall policy performance on challenging RL tasks. The authors aim to show that LoRA acts as an optimization regularizer that improves the stability of fitting and enables more robust value learning under bootstrapping and distribution shifts.

3. **Demonstrate Generality Across Diverse RL Settings:** The work aims to establish the broad applicability of LoRA regularization. This involves evaluating its effectiveness across different off-policy RL algorithms (Soft Actor-Critic, FastTD3) and various state-of-the-art critic architectures (SimbaV2, BRC/BroNet), as well as on different continuous control benchmarks (DeepMind Control Suite-Hard tasks, IsaacLab robotics tasks). The goal is to show that the proposed method is an architecture-agnostic and generalizable principle.

4. **Analyze the Design Choices and Mechanisms of LoRA as a Regularizer:** Through ablation studies, the authors seek to understand the contributions of specific LoRA design elements, such as the role of the frozen base matrix, the interaction with hyperspherical weight normalization (in SimbaV2), and the trade-off between LoRA rank and trainable parameters compared to other regularization methods like static sparsity. This aims to provide insight into why LoRA functions effectively as a structural regularizer in this context.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology involves integrating Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) into the linear layers of off-policy RL critics as a structural regularizer. This approach is applied across various algorithms and architectures, with specific adaptations for models employing hyperspherical normalization.

**Low-Rank Adaptation Parameterization:**
For a standard linear layer represented as `y = Wx`, where `W` is the weight matrix, the LoRA approach modifies this to `y = (W_0 + ΔW)x`. In this formulation:
- `W_0` is a dense base matrix that is randomly initialized and then *frozen* throughout training.
- `ΔW` is a low-rank update matrix, parameterized as `ΔW = (α_lora/r)BA`. Here, `A` and `B` are trainable adapter factors with dimensions `r x d_in` and `d_out x r` respectively, where `r` is the LoRA rank. `α_lora` is a scaling factor, set equal to `r` in the experiments such that `α_lora/r = 1`.
Gradients are only applied to `A` and `B`, while `W_0` remains fixed. This confines the learning to a low-dimensional subspace defined by the rank `r`. This modification is specifically applied to the linear maps within the critic's residual blocks (e.g., inverted-bottleneck MLP weights in SimbaV2, or equivalent linear maps in BRC's residual blocks). Other components, such as the actor, input embedding, output head, and normalization statistics, are kept as per the original baseline implementations.

**Critic Architectures and Algorithms:**
The method is evaluated across three settings:
1. **SAC with SimbaV2:** Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) is used with the SimbaV2 critic backbone. SimbaV2 employs hyperspherical feature normalization, post-update weight projection, LERP residual blocks, and categorical value learning.
2. **SAC with BRC:** SAC is also tested with the BRC (Bigger, Regularized, Categorical) critic, which uses a BroNet residual MLP backbone with layer normalization and ReLU activations. BRC also utilizes categorical value learning and an ensemble average of two critics.
3. **FastTD3 with SimbaV2:** FastTD3, a high-throughput variant of TD3, is combined with the SimbaV2 critic (without hyperspherical weight normalization in this specific implementation, denoted as SimbaV2⋆). FastTD3 incorporates parallel simulation, large-batch updates, and distributional critics.

**Categorical Critic Learning:**
Both SimbaV2 and BRC leverage categorical value learning. Instead of outputting a scalar Q-value, the critic predicts a categorical distribution over a fixed discrete value support. The scalar Q-value is derived as the expectation of this distribution. Training is performed using a cross-entropy loss between the predicted distribution and a target distribution, constructed by applying the Bellman backup and projecting it back onto the value support (C51 projection).

**LoRA with Hyperspherical Weight Normalization (SimbaV2-specific):**
SimbaV2's original design includes a post-update hyperspherical projection that forces each weight vector to unit norm. Direct application of this to `W_0 + ΔW` would implicitly rescale the frozen `W_0`. To maintain compatibility, a specific projection rule is devised: for each row `w_j` of `W_0` and corresponding `δ_j` of `ΔW`, a scalar `s_j` is computed such that `||w_j + s_j δ_j||^2 = 1`. This `s_j` is absorbed into the LoRA update `B_j`, ensuring the effective weight row is unit-norm while `w_j` remains fixed. To enable this, `W_0` rows are initialized with a norm strictly less than one (e.g., 0.5). LoRA adapter factors `A` and `B` are initialized with small random values to ensure `ΔW` is non-zero at initialization, providing a valid direction for this projection.

**Motivating Example:**
A minimal 15-state chain MDP is constructed to illustrate the benefits of low-rank updates. It incorporates bootstrapped targets, replay-induced distribution shift, and overparameterized value approximation.
- **Critic Architecture:** A two-hidden-layer MLP operating on fixed random features.
- **Training Regimes:**
 - **Static Regression:** Critic trained directly on true Q-values without bootstrapping or distribution shift.
 - **Bootstrapped Off-Policy TD:** Transitions sampled from a fixed replay buffer (collected by a uniform random behavior policy), with TD targets constructed using a Polyak-averaged target network and the target policy (always selecting 'right').
- **Metrics:** True-Q error on the target policy's state distribution and RMS Bellman residual.

**Baselines and Hyperparameters:**
The proposed LoRA method is compared against two baselines in each setting:
1. **Dense:** The original, full-parameter implementation of SimbaV2 or BRC.
2. **Sparse:** A variant employing one-shot random pruning to match the number of trainable critic parameters of the LoRA method.
All methods within a given setting share common hyperparameters for the environment, replay buffer, batch size, update schedule, and optimizer family. LoRA-specific parameters, such as the `A` and `B` adapter factors, are optimized using AdamW with weight decay to control the magnitude of the low-rank residual.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) as a structural regularizer consistently reduces critic loss and improves policy performance across various off-policy RL settings.

**Overall Performance Comparison (Figure 3, Tables 8, 9, 10):**
LoRA generally achieved the best or competitive results compared to dense and sparse baselines across three main experimental setups:

- **SAC + SimbaV2 on DMC-Hard Tasks:**
 - With a smaller critic (L=2, d_h=512), LoRA's average return (701.3) was comparable to the dense SimbaV2 (701.1), significantly outperforming the sparse baseline (607.3).
 - With a larger critic (L=4, d_h=1024), LoRA demonstrated a clearer advantage, achieving an average return of 777.2, which exceeded both the dense SimbaV2 (719.7) and the sparse variant (731.2) by a notable margin. This suggests that LoRA's regularization benefits become more pronounced as critic capacity increases.

- **SAC + BRC on DMC-Hard Tasks:**
 - Utilizing the BroNet architecture with SAC+BRC (L=2, d_h=4096), LoRA-BRC achieved the highest average return (755.1), surpassing dense BroNet-BRC (625.1) and Sparse-BRC (713.4). LoRA obtained the highest mean score on six out of seven tasks, indicating its generality beyond SimbaV2's specific architectural details.

- **FastTD3 + SimbaV2⋆ on IsaacLab Robotics Tasks:**
 - LoRA-SimbaV2 also yielded the best average return on both IsaacLab locomotion tasks (35.6 compared to dense 32.5 and sparse 33.4) and competitive results on manipulation tasks. This demonstrates transferability to different algorithms and environments utilizing massively parallel GPU simulation.

**Critic Loss Profile (Figure 3):**
LoRA generally exhibited a stronger critic-loss profile, often reaching the lowest loss during later stages of training. This suggests that restricting the critic update space contributes to more stable fitting and more robust value learning, counteracting the tendency of unconstrained critics to overfit to noisy, bootstrapped, and distribution-shifted replay samples.

**LoRA from Scratch vs. Dense Warm-Up (Figure 4):**
Pure LoRA training, initiated from scratch, remained competitive and frequently achieved the strongest final returns. Hybrid training schemes, which involved an initial phase of dense training before switching to LoRA, did not systematically outperform pure LoRA. This finding suggests that the regularization benefits of LoRA are derived from constraining the critic's update trajectory throughout the entire learning process, rather than merely acting as a fine-tuning stage.

**Ablation Studies (Figure 5):**
Ablation studies conducted on the SimbaV2 backbone at two model scales (L=2, d_h=512 and L=4, d_h=1024) explored key design choices:

- **Effect of Frozen Base-Matrix Rank (Figure 5a):** Performance was generally robust to the rank of the frozen base matrix (W_0). Smaller critics showed more sensitivity, implying that the fixed base representation's capacity becomes more critical when overall critic capacity is limited. Larger, deeper critics were less affected, likely due to their inherent representational capacity.

- **Effect of Base-Matrix Norm (Figure 5b):** The initialization scale of the frozen base-matrix norm (||w_0||) influenced performance, particularly for the smaller critic. Intermediate sub-unit norms (e.g., 0.5) provided sufficient "headroom" for the LoRA residual and generally resulted in stronger RL performance, especially for the larger critic.

- **Effect of Module Design (Figure 5c):** The proposed normalized LoRA (incorporating the hyperspherical projection for SimbaV2) consistently improved RL performance and robustness. Removing either the weight normalization mechanism (LoRA-NoWN) or the frozen base matrix entirely (NoBase, effectively W=BA) led to substantial performance degradation, particularly for the smaller critic.

- **Parameter Efficiency at Matched Budget (Figure 5d):** When comparing LoRA (varying rank) against static sparsity at a similar trainable parameter budget, LoRA consistently outperformed static sparsity across most evaluated parameter ranges. This indicates that structured low-rank updates provide a more effective parameterization for a given capacity budget than unstructured sparsity.

**Computational Cost Analysis:**
The study notes that SimbaV2, Sparse, and LoRA variants exhibited comparable GPU VRAM usage and training times within the same algorithm. This is attributed to the fact that training time and VRAM consumption in these RL setups are predominantly dictated by environmental interaction, replay buffer sizes, and activation storage, rather than the number of trainable parameters in the critic network itself. Therefore, the motivation for LoRA in this context is primarily its regularization effect, not parameter or memory efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) as an effective structural regularizer for critic learning in off-policy reinforcement learning, addressing a known challenge in scaling deep RL agents. The findings demonstrate that by constraining critic updates to a low-dimensional subspace, LoRA can mitigate overfitting, improve training stability, and enhance overall policy performance.

The significance of this work lies in several aspects:
1. **Addressing Critic Overfitting in Scaled RL:** The paper provides a principled approach to managing the overfitting and instability issues that arise when scaling critic capacity in off-policy RL, particularly due to bootstrapped targets and non-stationary replay data. This is a critical factor for developing more robust and powerful deep RL systems.
2. **Novel Application of LoRA:** Unlike its conventional use as a parameter-efficient fine-tuning method for pre-trained models, this work repurposes LoRA as a structural regularizer for critics trained *from scratch*. This expands the utility of LoRA beyond its established applications and introduces a new strategy for regularization in RL.
3. **Generality and Robustness:** The demonstrated effectiveness of LoRA across diverse off-policy RL algorithms (SAC, FastTD3), network architectures (SimbaV2, BRC/BroNet), and continuous control benchmarks (DMC-Hard, IsaacLab) highlights its broad applicability. This suggests that the low-rank regularization principle is not specific to a particular model design but offers a general mechanism for improving critic learning.
4. **Mechanism of Regularization:** The results, particularly from the motivating example and the performance gains over dense and sparse baselines, suggest that the low-rank constraint acts as an optimization regularizer. It guides the critic's learning trajectory, preventing it from excessively fitting noisy bootstrapped targets or transient replay-buffer artifacts, leading to more stable and robust value estimates.
5. **Implications for Future RL Research:** This work opens a new direction for designing architectures and training schemes that explicitly incorporate regularization for value learning under the challenging conditions of non-stationary replay data. It suggests that controlling critic overfitting is a vital principle for advancing off-policy RL, potentially leading to the development of more stable, scalable, and high-performing RL agents in complex environments.

In summary, this research contributes to the fundamental understanding and practical application of regularization in deep reinforcement learning, offering a robust and generalizable method for improving the stability and performance of scaled off-policy critics.
