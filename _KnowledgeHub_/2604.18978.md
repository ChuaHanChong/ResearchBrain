# Low-Rank Adaptation for Critic Learning in Off-Policy Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers integrated Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) into off-policy reinforcement learning critics to enhance stability and performance, particularly for large-capacity networks. This approach, which also includes a specialized LoRA formulation for hyperspherical weight normalization, consistently improved agent performance across diverse DeepMind Control and IsaacLab robotics tasks compared to full-parameter and static sparsity baselines.

## Problem

- Large critic networks in off-policy reinforcement learning algorithms (e.g., SAC, TD3) are prone to overfitting and instability.
- The reliance on bootstrapped targets from non-stationary replay data exacerbates optimization challenges for scaled critics.
- Simply increasing critic capacity often fails to yield expected performance benefits, sometimes leading to plasticity loss.

## Method

- ==Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)== was adapted as a structural regularizer for off-policy critics, constraining updates to a low-dimensional subspace by training only small, low-rank adapters while freezing base matrices.
- A specialized **LoRA-WN** formulation was developed to be compatible with ==hyperspherical weight normalization==, preserving the frozen base matrix's geometric structure during updates.
- The LoRA-based critic learning method was integrated and evaluated with ==Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)== and ==FastTD3== algorithms on scaled SimbaV2 architectures.

## Results

- LoRA consistently achieved superior performance on DeepMind Control tasks (**777.2** average return vs. **719.7** for SimbaV2 with larger critics) and demonstrated consistent gains on IsaacLab robotics tasks (**35.6** vs. **32.5** for SimbaV2 on locomotion).
- Pure LoRA training was competitive or outperformed hybrid schemes involving a dense critic warm-up phase, indicating the immediate regularization benefit.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the LoRA-WN module and the frozen base matrix were essential for robust performance, and LoRA generally outperformed static sparsity at matched trainable parameter budgets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LoRA's primary benefit in off-policy RL is its role as an optimization regularizer, improving critic learning in complex, non-stationary settings rather than merely reducing model capacity.
> - Training LoRA from scratch proved highly effective, suggesting that applying the low-rank constraint early on guides optimization trajectories more effectively than a dense warm-up phase.
> - The proposed hyperspherical weight normalization (LoRA-WN) is crucial for the method's performance and robustness, highlighting the importance of maintaining the geometric structure of the frozen base matrix.
