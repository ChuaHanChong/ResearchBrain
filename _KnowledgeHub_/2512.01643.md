# ViT$^3$: Unlocking Test-Time Training in Vision

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University and Alibaba Group systematically investigated Test-Time Training (TTT) designs for visual sequence modeling, culminating in the ViT	extsuperscript{3} architecture, which achieves linear computational complexity while matching or surpassing existing linear-complexity models and significantly narrowing the performance gap to quadratic-complexity Vision Transformers across diverse tasks.

## Problem

- Vision Transformers (ViTs) suffer from quadratic computational complexity (O(N	extsuperscript{2})) due to Softmax attention, limiting their application to long visual sequences like high-resolution images or videos.
- Existing linear-complexity (O(N)) attention alternatives often compromise expressive power, leading to reduced performance compared to full Softmax attention.
- The application of Test-Time Training (TTT) to vision tasks is underexplored, lacking systematic understanding of optimal inner model architectures and online training configurations.

## Method

- Conducted a systematic empirical study to explore the design space of **TTT** for vision, investigating various inner training configurations (loss functions, batch size, learning rate) and inner model architectures (capacity, depth, convolutional types).
- Developed ViT	extsuperscript{3} and hierarchical H-ViT	extsuperscript{3} architectures, which replace standard attention blocks with optimized TTT modules incorporating a simplified gated linear unit and depthwise convolution.
- Evaluated the proposed models across image classification, object detection, semantic segmentation, and image generation, comparing their performance and efficiency against state-of-the-art linear and quadratic complexity vision models.

## Results

- ViT	extsuperscript{3} and H-ViT	extsuperscript{3} models consistently matched or outperformed advanced O(N) linear-complexity models (e.g., Mamba) and narrowed the performance gap to O(N	extsuperscript{2}) vision Transformers on ImageNet-**1K** classification (H-ViT	extsuperscript{3}-S achieved **84.4%** Top-1 accuracy).
- On object detection (COCO) and semantic segmentation (ADE20K), H-ViT	extsuperscript{3} models established strong linear-complexity baselines, matching or surpassing VMamba and other prior O(N) methods.
- DiT	extsuperscript{3} models integrated into Diffusion Image Transformers (DiT) reduced FID scores for class-conditional image generation on ImageNet-**1K** (e.g., DiT	extsuperscript{3}-S/2 reduced FID from 68.40 to 62.65), and ViT	extsuperscript{3}-T achieved a **4.6x** speedup and **90.3%** GPU memory reduction over DeiT-T at high resolutions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Inner loss functions for TTT require non-zero mixed second derivatives (e.g., Dot Product, MSE) for effective outer-loop gradient flow, with MAE and Smooth L1 performing poorly.
> - Optimal inner training involves a single epoch of full-batch gradient descent with a learning rate of 1.0; mini-batches or excessive epochs degrade performance or lead to instability.
> - Increasing inner model capacity improves performance, but deeper inner models face optimization challenges, suggesting preference for shallower, well-constrained designs, while convolutional inner modules are particularly effective for vision tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision Transformers (ViT) have become a foundational architecture in computer vision, achieving state-of-the-art performance across various tasks such as image classification, generation, object detection, and segmentation. A fundamental limitation of traditional ViTs, however, is the quadratic computational complexity, O(N$^2$), of their Softmax attention mechanism with respect to the sequence length (N). This quadratic scaling renders them computationally expensive and often prohibitive for processing long visual sequences, such as high-resolution images or videos.

To address this computational bottleneck, the research community has explored several avenues. One approach involves reducing the number of tokens engaged in attention through techniques like locality or sparsity. Another significant direction is the development of linear-complexity, O(N), attention alternatives. Linear attention models, for instance, replace the Softmax kernel with a linear one, enabling a reordering of computations to achieve linear scaling. However, prior research indicates that these linear attention variants frequently exhibit limitations in expressive power, leading to reduced performance compared to Softmax attention, making them less practical for certain applications.

Test-Time Training (TTT) models have emerged as a recent and distinct approach to efficient sequence modeling. TTT reframes the attention operation as an online learning problem, where a compact "inner model" is constructed or adapted from key-value pairs specific to each input sequence during inference. This inner model then processes the queries to produce the output. This paradigm offers increased flexibility in model design and has demonstrated potential for scalable, linear-complexity solutions in various domains, including natural language processing, 3D reconstruction, and video generation.

Despite the promise of TTT, its application to visual sequence modeling remains underexplored. The inherent flexibility of the TTT framework introduces a vast design space, particularly concerning the optimal architecture of the inner module and the configuration of its online training process. Critical design choices, such as the appropriate loss function, learning rate, batch size, and the architecture (e.g., depth and width) of the inner model, lack systematic understanding and practical guidelines within the context of vision tasks. This research aims to bridge this gap by conducting a systematic empirical investigation into these fundamental TTT design choices for visual applications, thereby providing foundational insights and a robust baseline for future advancements.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent limitations of current state-of-the-art vision models in processing long visual sequences efficiently. While Vision Transformers (ViTs) achieve high performance, their core Softmax attention mechanism incurs a quadratic computational cost (O(N$^2$)) with respect to sequence length N. This scaling bottleneck is a significant barrier for applications involving high-resolution images or extended video sequences, where N can be very large. Existing linear-complexity alternatives (O(N)), such as linear attention variants, often compromise expressive power to achieve efficiency, resulting in a performance gap compared to full Transformers.

Test-Time Training (TTT) offers a new paradigm that reformulates attention as an online learning process, constructing a compact inner model from key-value pairs at test time. This approach promises linear computational complexity while potentially retaining greater expressive power through its flexible, learning-based compression mechanism. However, the application of TTT to vision tasks is relatively nascent, and the optimal design principles for TTT modules in this domain are not well-established. This lack of comprehensive understanding represents a critical bottleneck for leveraging TTT's full potential in computer vision.

Against this background, the key objectives of this work are articulated as follows:

1. **Systematic Empirical Study:** To conduct a thorough and systematic empirical investigation into the design space of Test-Time Training models specifically for visual sequence modeling. This involves examining fundamental choices related to both the inner training process (e.g., loss function, learning rate, batch size, number of epochs) and the inner model architecture (e.g., network architecture, model size, depth).
2. **Distill Design Principles:** To synthesize empirical observations and analyses into a set of practical insights and design principles for building effective, yet efficient, visual TTT modules. These insights are intended to guide future development and improve the performance of TTT-based vision models.
3. **Develop a Pure TTT Architecture (ViT$^{3}$):** To develop and propose a novel, pure TTT architecture named Vision Test-Time Training (ViT$^{3}$), which is specifically tailored for visual sequence modeling. This architecture is designed to embody the distilled insights, achieve linear computational complexity, and support parallelizable computation.
4. **Comprehensive Evaluation and Baseline Establishment:** To rigorously evaluate the proposed ViT$^{3}$ model across a diverse range of visual tasks, including image classification, image generation, object detection, and semantic segmentation. The goal is to demonstrate that ViT$^{3}$ can consistently match or surpass existing advanced linear-complexity models (e.g., Mamba, linear attention variants) and effectively narrow the performance gap to highly optimized, but quadratically complex, vision Transformers. This evaluation aims to establish ViT$^{3}$ as a strong baseline for future research in visual TTT models.
5. **Facilitate Future Research:** To contribute a foundational study and a robust baseline that can stimulate and guide further research and improvements in the domain of visual TTT models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of this work involves a systematic empirical study to explore the design space of Test-Time Training (TTT) for visual tasks, culminating in the development of the Vision Test-Time Training (ViT$^{3}$) model.

#### 3.1. Systematic Empirical Study of TTT Designs

The investigation commenced by replacing the attention blocks of a classical vision Transformer architecture, DeiT-S, with TTT layers to establish a baseline. Experiments were conducted primarily on ImageNet-1K. The study systematically varied two fundamental aspects: inner training configurations and inner model designs.

**Inner Training Configuration:** The inner model was initially set as a two-layer MLP with SiLU activation and a width ratio of 1.0.
- **Loss Function:** Five different self-supervised reconstruction objectives were evaluated: Dot Product Loss, Mean Squared Error (MSE), Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), Mean Absolute Error (MAE), and Smooth L1 loss. The analysis focused on the behavior of their mixed second derivatives (∂²L/∂V̂∂V) concerning outer-loop gradient flow to the value projection matrix (W$_{V}$).
- **Batch Size and Epochs:** The impact of mini-batch vs. full-batch gradient descent was investigated. Batch sizes of N (full-batch), N/2, N/3, and N/4 (mini-batch) were tested for a single epoch. Additionally, the effect of multiple epochs (1, 2, 3, 4) of full-batch inner training was examined.
- **Learning Rate:** The inner learning rate was varied from 0.1 to 10.0. An input-dependent, token-wise dynamic learning rate, as proposed in prior work, was also evaluated.

**Inner Model Design:** With inner training parameters fixed (dot product loss, one epoch of full-batch gradient descent, learning rate 1.0), the study proceeded to analyze inner model architectures.
- **Capacity (Width):** The hidden dimension of a two-layer MLP inner model was progressively enlarged from `d` to `4d` to assess the effect of increased capacity.
- **Depth:** Three inner models of varying depths were compared: a single `d×d` linear (FC) layer, a two-layer MLP, and a three-layer MLP. The study also explored constrained designs like SiLU(FC(x)) and SwiGLU with an identity output layer, and investigated the use of residual connections and specific weight initialization strategies.
- **Convolutional Architectures:** The flexibility of TTT was leveraged to test convolutional inner models, including a standard `3×3` convolution and a lightweight `3×3` depthwise convolution.

#### 3.2. ViT$^{3}$ Model Development

Based on the empirical insights derived from the systematic study, the Vision Test-Time Training (ViT$^{3}$) model was constructed.
- **Inner Training:** ViT$^{3}$ adopts a single epoch of full-batch gradient descent with a learning rate of 1.0, optimizing a dot-product loss.
- **Inner Model Components:** The inner model incorporates two modules identified as effective:
 - A simplified gated linear unit: F$_{1}$ = FC(x) ⊙ SiLU(FC(x)), which doubles linear state capacity while maintaining optimization ease.
 - A depthwise convolution: F$_{2}$ = DWConv(x), chosen for its ability to integrate local and global information.
- **TTT Block Construction:** Within each TTT block, a single attention head utilizes F$_{2}$, while the remaining heads employ F$_{1}$. This design serves as a drop-in replacement for standard attention blocks.
- **Architecture Families:** Two main model families were developed:
 - **ViT$^{3}$:** Non-hierarchical architectures, following classical ViT designs.
 - **H-ViT$^{3}$:** Hierarchical 4-stage architectures, consistent with modern vision Transformers.
 - **DiT$^{3}$:** An adaptation for diffusion image Transformers (DiT) for generative tasks.
- **Positional Encoding:** Conditional positional encodings were used to introduce spatial information.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Protocol

ViT$^{3}$ and H-ViT$^{3}$ models were extensively evaluated across various visual tasks:
- **Image Classification:** ImageNet-1K dataset, following Swin Transformer's training protocol (300 epochs, AdamW, cosine decay, RandAugment, Mixup, CutMix, random erasing). Results with MESA training strategy were also reported.
- **Object Detection:** COCO dataset, using Mask R-CNN with 1x and 3x training schedules.
- **Semantic Segmentation:** ADE20K dataset, using the UPerNet framework.
- **Image Generation:** Class-conditional image generation on ImageNet-1K, using DiT$^{3}$ (ViT$^{3}$ blocks replacing Softmax attention in DiT). Metrics included FID-50K, IS, Precision, and Recall.
- **Efficiency Analysis:** Throughput (FPS) and GPU memory usage were compared against DeiT-T across varying image resolutions using an RTX3090 GPU.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded a set of six practical insights regarding the design of Test-Time Training (TTT) modules for vision, which informed the development and evaluation of the ViT$^{3}$ model.

#### 4.1. Six Practical Insights for TTT Designs in Vision

1. **Loss Function Suitability:** Inner loss functions where the mixed second derivative (∂²L/∂V̂∂V) approaches zero are not suitable for TTT. Specifically, MAE loss, whose mixed second derivative is zero almost everywhere, resulted in the worst accuracy (76.5%). Smooth L1 also performed poorly. In contrast, Dot Product, MSE, and RMSE losses, which maintain non-zero mixed second derivatives, achieved approximately 79.0% accuracy, demonstrating that a strong outer-loop gradient signal to the value projection matrix (W$_{V}$) is crucial.
2. **Inner Training Regimen:** A single epoch of full-batch (B=N) gradient descent for inner training proved most effective for vision tasks (78.9% Top-1). Mini-batch gradient descent (B=N/2, N/3, N/4) led to a degradation in performance (78.6% to 78.1%). While multiple epochs (2-3) could slightly improve accuracy (up to 79.2%), they significantly reduced model throughput and could lead to instability with more epochs (e.g., 4 epochs leading to 57.0% Top-1 before divergence). This suggests that non-causal, global updates are preferred for vision, differing from some language modeling results.
3. **Inner Learning Rate Efficacy:** A relatively large inner learning rate, specifically 1.0, was found to be effective (78.9% Top-1). Smaller rates (e.g., 0.1) provided insufficient updates (77.5%), while excessively large rates (e.g., 5.0, 10.0) resulted in training instability. Input-dependent dynamic learning rates, while explored, were generally less effective in vision experiments.
4. **Inner Model Capacity Improves Performance:** Increasing the capacity of the inner model, specifically by enlarging the hidden dimension of a two-layer MLP from `d` to `4d`, consistently improved performance (from 78.9% to 79.6% Top-1). This highlights TTT's advantage over linear attention, as it allows for more complex, nonlinear inner modules that benefit from increased capacity.
5. **Optimization Challenges for Deep Inner Models:** Deeper inner models (e.g., a three-layer MLP) unexpectedly suffered from optimization difficulties, leading to lower performance (77.5% Top-1) compared to shallower ones (two-layer MLP at 78.9% Top-1, or a single FC layer at 79.1%). Analysis revealed that deeper inner modules exhibited higher training loss, indicating underfitting due to optimization bottlenecks in both the inner and outer loops. Constrained designs, such as SiLU(FC(x)) or SwiGLU with an identity output layer, which simplify the inner model, surprisingly yielded better results (79.4% and 79.7% respectively), further supporting the optimization challenge hypothesis.
6. **Convolutional Architectures for Vision:** Convolutional inner modules (standard `3×3` and depthwise `3×3` convolutions) were particularly effective for visual tasks, achieving strong accuracy improvements (79.9% and 80.1% Top-1, respectively). This is attributed to their natural ability to integrate global information (encoded in kernel weights) and local features (via receptive fields), providing a unified mechanism for contextual modeling.

#### 4.2. ViT$^{3}$ Model Performance

Guided by these insights, the ViT$^{3}$ (Vision Test-Time Training) model, incorporating a simplified gated linear unit and a depthwise convolution in its inner module, was developed and evaluated across four visual tasks:

- **Image Classification (ImageNet-1K):** ViT$^{3}$ and H-ViT$^{3}$ (hierarchical variant) consistently matched or outperformed advanced O(N) linear-complexity models like Mamba and various linear attention variants. For example, H-ViT$^{3}$-S achieved 84.4% Top-1 accuracy, competitive with or surpassing larger O(N) models and narrowing the gap to O(N$^2$) vision Transformers. With the MESA training strategy, H-ViT$^{3}$-S further improved to 84.9%.
- **Object Detection (COCO):** H-ViT$^{3}$ models demonstrated strong global modeling capabilities, crucial for high-resolution inputs where N ≫ d. They matched or surpassed state-of-the-art linear-complexity methods (e.g., VMamba, SOFT++) and significantly narrowed the performance gap to highly optimized O(N$^2$) vision Transformers. For instance, H-ViT$^{3}$-S achieved 49.1 APb with a 1x schedule and 50.5 APb with a 3x schedule.
- **Semantic Segmentation (ADE20K):** H-ViT$^{3}$ established a robust linear-complexity baseline, consistently outperforming VMamba, VVT, and SOFT++ variants. H-ViT$^{3}$-S achieved 50.2 mIoU. While still trailing some highly optimized O(N$^2$) Transformers, this task highlights the potential for further capacity boosts by designing more expressive inner modules.
- **Image Generation (ImageNet-1K):** DiT$^{3}$ models, which integrate ViT$^{3}$ blocks into Diffusion Image Transformers (DiT), consistently improved upon the original DiT baselines in terms of FID score across different sizes and patch resolutions. For example, DiT$^{3}$-S/2 reduced the FID from 68.40 to 62.65, demonstrating ViT$^{3}$'s effectiveness in generative tasks.

#### 4.3. Efficiency Analysis

ViT$^{3}$ demonstrated significant efficiency advantages over Softmax attention. At a resolution of 1248x1248 (approximately 6,084 tokens), ViT$^{3}$-T achieved a 4.6x speedup over DeiT-T and reduced GPU memory consumption by 90.3%, underscoring its linear time and memory complexity benefits for long sequence processing.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research significantly advances the understanding and practical application of Test-Time Training (TTT) in computer vision, offering a systematic foundation for building efficient and expressive visual sequence models. The study's core contributions and potential impact are multifaceted:

**Advancement in Linear-Complexity Vision Models:** The paper provides a principled approach to designing O(N) linear-complexity models that can bridge the performance gap with traditional O(N$^2$) Transformers. By moving beyond the limitations of prior linear attention methods, TTT offers a flexible framework that leverages online learning to maintain expressive power while achieving computational efficiency. This is particularly critical for processing increasingly long visual sequences found in high-resolution images, videos, and 3D data.

**Establishment of a Strong Baseline:** The proposed Vision Test-Time Training (ViT$^{3}$) model, meticulously designed based on the distilled insights, serves as a robust and competitive baseline for future research in visual TTT. Its consistent performance across diverse tasks—image classification, object detection, semantic segmentation, and image generation—demonstrates its general applicability and efficacy. This baseline can accelerate development in the field by providing a strong reference point for new TTT designs and methodologies.

**Guidance for Future Research:** The six practical insights derived from the systematic empirical study offer concrete design principles and illuminate several promising directions for future work.
- **Optimization of Deep Inner Modules:** The identification of optimization difficulties for deeper inner models highlights a fundamental challenge. Addressing this bottleneck through novel initialization strategies, architecture designs, or meta-learning techniques could unlock even greater expressive power for TTT.
- **Lightweight and Expressive Inner Models:** Further research into designing inner modules that are both computationally lightweight and highly expressive is encouraged, particularly given the four-fold computational cost of inner training relative to an outer module forward pass.
- **Vision-Specific Inner Training:** While full-batch gradient descent was effective, the paper notes potential for vision-specific mini-batch inner training algorithms, analogous to how sequential processing benefits causal data like language.
- **Broader Design Space Exploration:** The study acknowledges that other design choices (e.g., inner optimizer, data augmentation within the inner loop, different inner model architectures like small Transformers) remain unexplored, opening avenues for further investigation.

**Enabling New Applications:** The significant efficiency gains demonstrated by ViT$^{3}$—including substantial speedups and memory reductions at high resolutions—have direct practical implications. Such models can enable real-time processing of high-resolution imagery, facilitate large-scale video analytics, and power applications on resource-constrained devices where quadratic complexity models are unfeasible. This expansion of practical applicability can drive innovation in various domains, from autonomous systems to medical imaging.

**Conceptual Contribution to Attention Mechanisms:** By reframing attention as an online learning problem, the work offers a new conceptual lens through which to understand and design sequence models. This paradigm shifts the focus from fixed-function attention kernels to adaptable, context-specific neural modules, potentially inspiring novel architectural developments beyond traditional Transformers.

In summary, this research not only provides a comprehensive empirical analysis and practical guidelines for visual TTT but also establishes a strong architectural baseline. It highlights both the immense potential of TTT to deliver efficient yet powerful vision models and outlines clear research trajectories for further enhancing their capabilities and widespread adoption.
