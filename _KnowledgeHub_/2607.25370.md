# Critical slowing down for predicting controller induced loss of control in quadrotors

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Delft University of Technology developed a system model-free Cascaded Bayesian Forecaster (C-BeFore) to predict controller-induced loss of control (LOC) in quadrotors. Leveraging Critical Slowing Down (CSD) theory, the framework provides early warnings up to 0.9 seconds before LOC, outperforming existing recurrent neural network methods and demonstrating robust generalization across different platforms and failure scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing quadrotor safety research focuses primarily on fault detection and fault-tolerant control for rotor failures, often neglecting controller-induced instabilities like "flyaways" or oscillations.
- Current safety methods, such as Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) and Safe Flight Envelopes (SFEs), are computationally expensive, model-dependent, or fail to constrain critical inner-loop dynamics, leaving systems vulnerable.
- Previous machine learning approaches for LOC prediction often lack generalization across diverse scenarios and physical interpretability, limiting their trustworthiness and broader adoption.

## Method

- A Cascaded Bayesian Forecaster (**C-BeFore**) was developed to predict controller-induced quadrotor LOC, operating as a ==system model-free== framework.
- The forecaster utilizes generic ==early warning signals (EWS)== derived from ==Critical Slowing Down (CSD) theory==, specifically the ==lag-1 autocorrelation (AC1)== of processed rotor speed differences.
- Multiple ==Bayesian detectors==, cascaded with increasing detection window sizes, generate ==time-to-LOC forecasts==, leveraging either data-informed or theoretically *assumed* LOC data distributions.

## Results

- The C-BeFore demonstrated superior LOC detection accuracy (**48** true positives out of **49** on DataCan75) and reduced early false positive rates by at least **83%** compared to recurrent neural network baselines.
- Provided an average time-to-LOC forecast ranging from **0.17 to 0.44 seconds**, with some forecasts up to **0.9 seconds** before the LOC event, offering practical lead time for intervention.
- Showed robust generalization by successfully detecting flyaway events on different quadrotor platforms (SuperKnight5, DamselFly) and controller architectures without requiring re-parameterization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Critical Slowing Down (CSD) theory offers a physically interpretable, model-free, and generic framework for anticipating stability loss in complex systems like quadrotors.
> - Reliable LOC forecasts can be generated even without extensive, specific LOC data by using theoretical insights from CSD to construct *assumed* LOC distributions.
> - Cascading Bayesian detectors with varying detection windows allows for accurate time-to-LOC predictions by observing the progressive trend towards instability, providing sufficient lead time for corrective actions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The proliferation of autonomous robots, including unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) like quadrotors, necessitates advancements in safety mechanisms. Existing safety research for quadrotors largely focuses on fault detection and fault-tolerant control (FTC), particularly for rotor failures. Significant progress has been made in enabling rudimentary control despite partial or multiple rotor losses. However, these methods often do not address situations where a fully functional quadrotor loses control due to unstable controller characteristics, such as sensor saturation, structural resonance, or "flyaways" (rapid uncontrolled ascent).

Another approach to ensuring safety involves defining and enforcing safe operating spaces using techniques like control barrier functions (CBFs) to constrain navigational states (e.g., position or attitude). While effective for preventing collisions or geofence breaches, these methods typically do not constrain inner-loop state and control variables like body and motor rotational rates. Consequently, disturbances can still lead to an unrecoverable state through these unrestricted inner-loop dynamics. Knowledge of a quadrotor's control invariant set or safe flight envelope (SFE) could prevent such excursions, but high-dimensional reachable sets are computationally expensive to determine and are inherently model and controller dependent, making them unsuitable for unexpected changes in system dynamics or model mismatches.

Local proximity to loss of control (LOC) has also been explored, with previous work employing neural networks to detect and predict LOC using onboard sensor data. While these methods demonstrated predictive capabilities, they often exhibited limited generalization and lacked physical interpretability due to their black-box nature. This gap highlights the need for explainable early warning signals (EWS) that can anticipate quadrotor LOC.

This research introduces an approach that draws inspiration from the field of ecology, applying the concept of Critical Slowing Down (CSD) to predict LOC in quadrotors. CSD is a generic phenomenon observed across various complex systems (e.g., biological, ecological, physical) where a system's recovery rate from perturbations slows down as it approaches a critical transition. This theoretical foundation offers a model-free and generic framework for anticipating stability loss, positioning this work as a complementary or alternative approach to model-dependent SFEs and data-intensive, black-box machine learning methods, while addressing the limitations of traditional fault-tolerant control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the increasing integration of autonomous robots into society and industry, which necessitates robust safety measures. Studies indicate that increased automation can sometimes correlate with a rise in accidents, many of which are attributed to loss of control (LOC). For quadrotors, while fault-tolerant control has advanced for rotor failures, other critical LOC scenarios, such as those induced by unstable controller behavior, remain a significant challenge. These "controller-induced" LOC events, exemplified by 'flyaways' or oscillations resulting from input-output delays, can lead to catastrophic failures even in otherwise fault-free systems. Predicting such events is difficult, as controller vulnerabilities can be elusive until they manifest.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of this work are:
- **Develop a system model-free forecasting framework:** The central aim is to create a method for anticipating controller-induced (closed-loop unstable) LOC in quadrotors without requiring a specific system model or extensive prior knowledge of the quadrotor's dynamics. This addresses the limitations of model-dependent approaches and aims for broader applicability.
- **Leverage Critical Slowing Down (CSD) theory:** Utilize generic early warning signals (EWS) derived from CSD to identify precursors to LOC. This approach is intended to provide physical interpretability and reduce reliance on large, specific LOC datasets.
- **Achieve effective time-to-LOC forecasts:** The framework is designed to provide sufficient forewarning, aiming for forecasts up to 0.9 seconds before the LOC event occurs, enabling the possibility of corrective actions.
- **Outperform existing state-of-the-art methods:** The research seeks to demonstrate that the proposed approach achieves superior detection accuracy and requires less reliance on specific LOC data compared to current recurrent neural network (RNN) quadrotor LOC forecasters.
- **Demonstrate generalization capabilities:** A critical objective is to show that the developed forecaster can generalize across different controller architectures, quadrotor platforms, and distinct LOC scenarios (e.g., yaw-induced LOC and flyaways) without re-parameterization.
- **Function as an independent safety monitor:** The framework is intended as an "add-on" safety layer that informs the controller of impending LOC, rather than being a controller itself. This allows it to be used in conjunction with existing control systems, including fault-tolerant controllers, to enhance overall safety.

By pursuing these objectives, the research aims to contribute a robust, generic, and interpretable method for enhancing the safety of autonomous quadrotors, particularly against subtle, controller-induced instabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology revolves around the application of Critical Slowing Down (CSD) principles to develop a Cascaded Bayesian Forecaster (C-BeFore) for predicting quadrotor loss of control (LOC).

#### 3.1. Critical Slowing Down (CSD) Theory

CSD describes the phenomenon where a system's recovery rate from small perturbations slows down as it approaches a critical transition (e.g., a bifurcation point where it shifts from one stable state to another or becomes unstable). This slowing recovery is characterized by the dominant eigenvalues of the system approaching zero (for linear systems), indicating a loss of stability. The implications of this slowing recovery extend to nonlinear systems, making CSD a suitable tool for monitoring LOC.
CSD is primarily measured using two indicators: the lag-1 autocorrelation (AC1) and variance (SD). As a critical transition nears, AC1 is expected to approach 1, and SD is expected to approach infinity. This research favors AC1 due to its boundedness (between -1 and 1) and predictable behavior (approaching 1 at a transition), which reduces the need for extensive LOC data.

#### 3.2. Early Warning Signal (EWS) Construction

1. **Quadrotor Variables:** The EWS are constructed from onboard rotor speed measurements ($\omega_1, \omega_2, \omega_3, \omega_4$). These are processed into rolling ($u_p$), pitching ($u_q$), and yawing ($u_r$) rotor speed differences, which represent inputs to the controller and are therefore sensitive to controller-driven instabilities.
2. **Detrending:** Slow-moving trends and biases are removed from the rotor speed differences using a moving average detrender. The size of the moving average window ($W$) is a parameter.
3. **Lag-1 Autocorrelation (AC1) Calculation:** The AC1 is computed as the Pearson correlation coefficient between the detrended signal and its one-sample-lagged version, over a sliding window ($W_{AC}$). The AC1 calculation follows the detrending process. The process has a linear runtime complexity, O(N), for a time-series of N samples.

#### 3.3. Loss of Control (LOC) Data Distributions

To interpret the EWS, two reference distributions are constructed:
1. **Nominal Distribution ($N_{E_k}$):** Represents the EWS behavior during stable, nominal flight conditions.
2. **LOC Distribution ($L_{E_k}$ or $L^A_{E_k}$):** Represents EWS behavior at the moment of LOC. If actual LOC data is available, $L_{E_k}$ is constructed from it. Crucially, if LOC data is unavailable, an *assumed* LOC distribution ($L^A_{E_k}$) can be used, for example, a normal distribution centered near $E_k=1$, based on CSD theory. This highlights the approach's reduced reliance on LOC data.

#### 3.4. Bayesian Loss of Control Detector

The detector's purpose is to identify LOC based on sequences of recent EWS observations ($E_{k,D}$).
1. **Initial Probability of LOC ($P(L)$):** An initial estimate of LOC probability is derived from the median of positive EWS values within a detection window ($D$).
2. **Bayesian Inference:** This initial probability is refined using a Bayesian formulation, yielding a posterior probability $P(L|E_{k,D})$. This incorporates likelihoods $P(E_{k,D}|L)$ (probability of observing current EWS given LOC) and $P(E_{k,D}|\neg L)$ (probability of observing current EWS given nominal behavior).
3. **Wasserstein Distance for Likelihoods:** The likelihoods are determined by comparing the distribution of current EWS observations ($E_{k,D}$) to the reference $L_{E_k}$ (or $L^A_{E_k}$) and $N_{E_k}$ distributions using the Wasserstein distance (WD) metric. Relative similarity scores ($\Phi_L, \Phi_N$) are derived from these WD scores, acting as probability functions for binary classification.
4. **Aggregation:** To mitigate false positives, multiple Bayesian detectors (each using a different EWS $E_k$) are combined, typically by averaging their probabilities, to produce a final $P(L|E)$.
5. **Computational Complexity:** The Bayesian detector has a runtime complexity of $O(D \log D)$ at inference.

#### 3.5. Cascaded Bayesian Forecaster (C-BeFore) Design

The C-BeFore translates sequential detections into time-to-LOC forecasts.
1. **Cascading Detectors:** Multiple Bayesian detectors operate with different, increasing detection window sizes ($D_1 < D_2 < ... < D_d$). The detector with the largest window ($D_d$) is considered the "ground-truth" LOC detection point.
2. **Forecasting Principle:** The core idea is that if a trend towards LOC persists, detectors with shorter windows ($D < D_d$) should produce detections sequentially earlier than those with longer windows.
3. **Forecast Calculation:** When a detector with window $D_w$ triggers, it forecasts when the $D_d$ detector (labeled LOC) will trigger based on the difference in window sizes. The forecast is refined by accounting for inter-window detection errors ($\epsilon$), which capture non-linearities in the approach to LOC. A scaling coefficient ($\alpha$) is derived from these errors.
4. **Final Forecast ($\Delta t_{LOC}$):** The final time-to-LOC forecast is an aggregate of individual forecasts from active detectors, weighted by $\alpha$. The approach is detailed in Algorithm 1 (Detection) and Algorithm 2 (Forecasting).
5. **Computational Complexity:** The overall C-BeFore has a complexity of $O(D \log D)$, which is generally more efficient than RNNs ($O(D H^2)$) for typical quadrotor application window sizes.

#### 3.6. Data Collection and Processing

- **Yaw-induced LOC Data:** Collected from two quadrotors, CineGo and DataCan75, built with off-the-shelf components running Betaflight 4.2, logging rotor speed at 500 Hz. LOC is defined as roll ($\phi$) or pitch ($\theta$) exceeding $\pm 90$ degrees.
- **Flyaway Data:** Collected from SuperKnight5 (Betaflight 4.3, outdoor, structural resonance-induced flyaways) and DamselFly (Indiflight controller, indoor, INDI-induced flyaways due to noise/disturbances). Data logged at 500 Hz (SuperKnight5) or resampled to 500 Hz (DamselFly).
- **Processing:** All raw flight data are resampled to 500 Hz, detrended, and EWS (AC1) are computed. A parameter sweep analysis is used to select optimal detrending and AC1 window sizes, along with detection thresholds for each quadrotor.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Baseline Comparison (DataCan75 Quadrotor)

The C-BeFore was compared against recurrent neural network (RNN) forecasters (BiLSTM, LSTM, CNNLSTM, GRU) previously developed for the DataCan75 quadrotor. The DataCan75 dataset comprised 49 LOC flights and 6 nominal flights.
- **LOC Detection Accuracy:** The C-BeFore demonstrated superior performance, achieving 48 true positives out of 49 LOC flights, with 0 false negatives. It correctly classified 5 out of 6 nominal flights. In contrast, RNNs, while showing no late detections, exhibited a tendency to produce more early false positives (detections before the dangerous maneuver initiated) and less reliable time-to-LOC forecasts.
- **False Positive Rate:** The C-BeFore reduced the early false positive rate by at least 83% compared to the RNN baselines.
- **Time-to-LOC Forecast:** The C-BeFore provided an average time-to-LOC forecast of approximately 0.44 seconds before the LOC event, which is considered sufficient for corrective actions within the quadrotor's control loop frequency (4kHz for DataCan75).

#### 4.2. Forecasting under Data-informed Distributions (CineGo Quadrotor)

The C-BeFore, trained with actual LOC data distributions ($L_{E_k}$), was evaluated on the CineGo dataset (34 yaw LOC flights, 11 non-LOC flights).
- **Detection Performance:** All 34 LOC events were detected prior to their occurrence (100% true positive rate). Zero early false positives were recorded. The system correctly recognized 9 out of 11 nominal flights as non-LOC.
- **Detection Leeway:** On average, LOC detections occurred before roll or pitch attitudes exceeded a magnitude of 40 degrees, with 75% occurring below 50 degrees. These attitude angles are within the range experienced during aggressive nominal maneuvers, suggesting the forecasts are provided well before a critical state is reached.
- **Time-to-LOC Forecast:** The average time-to-LOC forecast was approximately 0.199 seconds.

#### 4.3. Forecasting under Assumed Distributions (CineGo Quadrotor)

To assess the C-BeFore's performance without specific LOC data, the true LOC distributions were replaced with an *assumed* LOC distribution ($L^A_{E_k}$), modeled as a constant distribution near $E_k=1$ (as predicted by CSD theory).
- **Performance Comparison:** The performance of the assumed C-BeFore was observed to be nearly identical to the data-driven C-BeFore. It achieved a 100% true positive rate (34/34 LOC flights), zero early false positives, and correctly identified 9 out of 11 nominal flights.
- **Forecast Timeliness:** While detections and forecasts occurred slightly later than with data-informed distributions, they remained practical and useful. This finding indicates that the approach does not necessitate extensive LOC datasets, which is advantageous in safety-critical applications where such data is often difficult or dangerous to acquire.

#### 4.4. Generalization Capacity (SuperKnight5 and DamselFly Quadrotors)

The CineGo-parameterized *assumed* C-BeFore was applied to detect flyaway events on two different quadrotors (SuperKnight5 and DamselFly) without any re-parameterization, despite differences in quadrotor properties, flight controllers (Betaflight 4.3 vs. Indiflight), and operational environments (outdoor vs. indoor, windy conditions).
- **Flyaway Detection:** The C-BeFore successfully detected all flyaway events on both the SuperKnight5 (4 out of 4 flights) and DamselFly (4 out of 4 flights). This demonstrates the forecaster's sensitivity to controller-induced instabilities across different quadrotor platforms and LOC scenarios, even without significant off-axis oscillations characteristic of the original yaw-induced LOC scenario.
- **Nominal Flight Classification:** For the DamselFly, the C-BeFore correctly classified 12 out of 13 nominal flights as non-LOC.
- **False Positives Analysis:** For the SuperKnight5, some false positive detections occurred, but not during aggressive maneuvers (e.g., 11g punch-outs). Instead, these detections were associated with episodes of rotor speed oscillations, which the authors suggest could be precursors to instability or undesirable behavior.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a forecasting framework that contributes to the field of autonomous robot safety by addressing the prediction of controller-induced Loss of Control (LOC) in quadrotors. The significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Novelty and Genericity:** The work introduces a novel approach for LOC forecasting based on Critical Slowing Down (CSD) theory, a generic phenomenon. This approach is characterized by its system model-free nature, which differentiates it from model-dependent methods like Safe Flight Envelopes (SFEs), and its reduced reliance on extensive LOC data, contrasting with many data-driven machine learning techniques. The generic principles of CSD allow for broad applicability across different systems.
- **Enhanced Safety and Proactiveness:** By providing early warning signals for impending LOC (forecasts up to 0.9 seconds, with practical averages around 0.17-0.44 seconds), the framework offers controllers sufficient time to initiate corrective actions. This shifts the safety paradigm from reactive fault tolerance to proactive LOC prevention, potentially reducing accidents and improving the overall robustness of autonomous quadrotor operations.
- **Superior Performance and Data Efficiency:** The C-BeFore demonstrates superior performance compared to state-of-the-art recurrent neural network (RNN) forecasters, particularly in terms of LOC classification accuracy and reduced false positive rates. Critically, its ability to provide useful forecasts even with *assumed* LOC data distributions highlights its data efficiency, making it valuable in contexts where collecting diverse and extensive LOC data is impractical or dangerous.
- **Robust Generalization:** A significant finding is the framework's ability to generalize across different quadrotor platforms, controller architectures (e.g., Betaflight PID vs. Indiflight INDI), and distinct LOC scenarios (yaw-induced instability vs. flyaways) without requiring re-parameterization. This robust generalization capacity suggests a broad applicability beyond the specific test cases and implies reduced effort for deployment on new systems.
- **Physical Interpretability:** Unlike black-box machine learning models, the C-BeFore's foundation in CSD theory provides a degree of physical interpretability. The early warning signals (e.g., lag-1 autocorrelation approaching 1) have a theoretical basis related to system stability, offering insights into why a LOC event might be imminent.
- **Complementary Safety Layer:** The proposed framework functions as an "add-on" safety monitor, independent of the primary flight controller. This allows it to be integrated with existing (including fault-tolerant) control systems, providing an additional layer of safety without requiring fundamental changes to the control architecture.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations. The approach, while generic, does not inherently provide corrective control actions or identify the specific source of instability, which is a trade-off for its model-free nature. The design of suitable Early Warning Signals (EWS) and the selection of appropriate forecasting parameters can still pose challenges for new systems. Furthermore, the current reliance on rotor speed measurements may limit compatibility with some autopilots, although emerging autopilot features may mitigate this.

Future work outlined by the authors includes applying the approach to anticipate critical transitions in other autonomous systems, simplifying the EWS design process, and, ultimately, integrating the forecasting capabilities with proactive LOC prevention strategies to develop closed-loop recovery mechanisms.
