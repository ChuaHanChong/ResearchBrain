# FOSP: Fine-tuning Offline Safe Policy through World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> FOSP (Fine-tuning Offline Safe Policy through World Models) is a two-stage framework for safely learning and adapting policies from offline data to novel online environments, especially for vision-based robotic control. This method achieved near-zero safety constraint violations and improved task success rates with few-shot fine-tuning on real-world Franka manipulator tasks.

## Problem

- Offline Reinforcement Learning policies often suffer from out-of-distribution (OOD) issues, leading to performance degradation or safety constraint violations in novel environments.
- Online fine-tuning of offline-pretrained safe policies frequently experiences initial performance drops and safety compromises.
- Balancing task performance with strict safety adherence is challenging in high-dimensional, vision-based tasks, particularly during the offline-to-online transition.

## Method

- **FOSP** utilizes a ==two-stage framework==: offline pretraining to learn a robust and safe policy from static data, followed by online fine-tuning for efficient and safe adaptation to novel environments.
- It extends a model-based RL ==world model (DreamerV3)== with a ==cost decoder== to predict future rewards and crucial safety costs from high-dimensional visual observations.
- The framework incorporates ==in-sample optimization== for conservative value estimation, a ==Reachability Estimation Function (REF)== for robust safety constraint enforcement, and a ==safe policy expansion== mechanism for stable offline-to-online transfer.

## Results

- **FOSP** demonstrated superior safety performance in simulations (Safety-Gymnasium), achieving **nearly zero** constraint violations while maintaining competitive reward performance compared to other safe RL algorithms.
- On real-world Franka manipulator 'SafeReach' tasks, **FOSP** successfully adapted to unseen safety regions and target objects with few-shot fine-tuning (**40 gradient steps**), significantly improving success rates and rapidly reducing constraint violations.
- Ablation studies confirmed that in-sample optimization, safe policy expansion, and the Reachability Estimation Function are critical components, each contributing to FOSP's ability to balance reward maximization and safety adherence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating a cost decoder into a world model allows for joint prediction of rewards and safety costs, enabling effective learning of safe environmental dynamics from vision-only offline data.
> - Combining in-sample optimization for conservative Q-value estimation with a Reachability Estimation Function (REF) provides robust safety guarantees by unifying mixed constraints and mitigating OOD action issues.
> - A safe policy expansion approach facilitates a stable transition from offline to online, allowing the agent to safely explore with an adapting policy while benefiting from the prior safety knowledge of the frozen offline policy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has demonstrated capabilities in sequential decision-making. Offline Reinforcement Learning (Offline RL), a subfield, focuses on learning policies from static, pre-collected datasets without requiring active environment interaction. This paradigm is appealing for real-world applications where data collection can be costly, time-consuming, or unsafe. However, offline policies often suffer from out-of-distribution (OOD) issues, limiting their generalization to novel or unseen scenarios and potentially leading to severe performance degradation or safety constraint violations during deployment.

Simultaneously, Safe Reinforcement Learning (Safe RL) is a critical area addressing the challenge of learning optimal policies while adhering to explicit safety constraints. Traditional online Safe RL methods, while effective, involve exploration that can incur costs or risks in real-world environments. Offline Safe RL attempts to learn policies that are safe by design from existing data, but like general Offline RL, it struggles with generalization and ensuring persistent safety in dynamic or unforeseen circumstances.

The intersection of these fields, particularly the offline-to-online transition for safe generalization, represents a significant research gap. Existing offline-to-online RL approaches often observe performance drops during fine-tuning, especially when transitioning from conservative offline policies. Furthermore, many Safe RL methods focus solely on online settings, and those in offline Safe RL primarily deal with soft constraints, which may not guarantee stringent safety. Model-based RL (MBRL) has emerged as a data-efficient approach, capable of building world models to predict future outcomes, thus reducing the need for extensive real-world exploration and potentially enhancing safety in high-dimensional (e.g., vision-based) tasks.

This work contributes to the landscape by aiming to bridge the gap between offline safe policy learning and online fine-tuning for generalization tasks, specifically leveraging the data efficiency and predictive capabilities of world models to address safety-critical, vision-based robotic control. The paper identifies itself as the first to explore offline-to-online RL for safe generalization tasks, positioning itself at the confluence of Offline RL, Safe RL, MBRL, and generalization challenges.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a practical safe RL algorithm that can effectively utilize offline data and rapidly adapt to novel environments while maintaining safety properties. This overarching goal is motivated by several specific challenges and limitations identified in the current research landscape:

1. **Addressing Generalization and OOD Issues in Offline Policies:** Offline policies, while trained on substantial datasets, frequently exhibit poor generalization when deployed in unseen real-world scenarios due to discrepancies between the training data distribution and the real environment (Out-of-Distribution problems). For safety-critical tasks, this can lead to unacceptable safety constraint violations. The motivation is to overcome this limitation by enabling an offline-pretrained policy to safely generalize to novel environments.
2. **Ensuring Safety During Online Fine-tuning:** A straightforward approach of online fine-tuning an offline-pretrained safe policy often leads to degradation in both performance and safety. This degradation can stem from a shift in data distribution during online interaction and the complexity of managing mixed constraints (both soft and hard) in a dynamic setting. The research is motivated by the need for a mechanism that facilitates safe adaptation during online fine-tuning, preventing initial performance drops and minimizing constraint violations.
3. **Leveraging Data Efficiency of Model-Based RL for Vision-Based Tasks:** Vision-based robotic tasks involve high-dimensional observations, making data collection and learning sample-intensive. Model-based RL, through its world models, offers improved sample efficiency by predicting environmental dynamics. The motivation is to exploit this advantage to accelerate the learning process during both offline pretraining and online fine-tuning, particularly for complex vision-based operations where predictions can enhance action safety.
4. **Managing Mixed Constraints and Value Overestimation:** The problem often involves a combination of performance objectives and safety constraints, which can be challenging to balance. Additionally, value overestimation is a common issue in offline RL, exacerbated in model-based settings where both policy and dynamics models are learned from static data. The objective is to introduce methods that conservatively handle value estimation and robustly enforce safety constraints, including both soft behavioral regularizations and hard safety limits, to achieve a favorable trade-off between performance and constraint satisfaction.
5. **Facilitating Seamless Offline-to-Online Transfer:** Current offline-to-online approaches often struggle with the transition phase, leading to initial suboptimality or safety risks. The motivation is to develop a method that provides stable initial performance during fine-tuning while enabling continuous improvement and robust safety guarantees.

In summary, the research aims to provide a comprehensive framework, FOSP, that enhances the generalization of safe policies from offline data to unseen, safety-constrained online scenarios, especially in data-intensive, vision-based robotic applications, by strategically integrating model-based learning, conservative value estimation, and robust safety constraint enforcement across both training phases.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The FOSP (Fine-tuning Offline Safe Policy through World Models) method is structured as a two-stage framework: an offline pretraining phase and an online fine-tuning phase. It builds upon Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) principles, specifically extending DreamerV3, to manage vision-only tasks efficiently and safely.

#### 3.1. World Model Foundation

FOSP extends DreamerV3 by incorporating a cost decoder into its Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM) architecture. The world model learns compressed latent representations (`z_t`, `h_t`) from high-dimensional image observations (`x_t`) and predicts future states, rewards (`r_t`), and crucially, costs (`c_t`). The model is trained end-to-end using a variational free energy loss function (ELBO) with KL balancing, which is augmented to include the cost decoder's likelihood, allowing it to predict safety violations alongside rewards.

#### 3.2. Offline Pretraining Stage

The offline pretraining stage focuses on learning a robust and safe policy from a static dataset (`D`) while mitigating common offline RL challenges.

- **In-Sample Optimization for Critic Networks:**
 - **Value Overestimation Mitigation:** To address value overestimation, particularly prevalent in model-based offline RL due to OOD actions, FOSP employs an in-sample optimization strategy inspired by Implicit Q-learning (IQL). Instead of directly sampling actions from the learned policy for target value calculation, it uses latent state rollouts from the world model.
 - **Value and Critic Networks:** A critic network `Q_phi(s, a)` and a separate value network `V_phi(s)` are maintained. `V_phi(s)` is optimized using expectile regression to approximate the expected Q-value over the policy's action distribution, making it less sensitive to OOD actions.
 - **Target Estimation and Loss:** Returns are estimated using TD(λ) over H-step latent policy rollouts generated by the world model. The critic `Q_phi` is updated using a combined loss: `L1_Q` (based on model-generated rewards from rollouts) and `L2_Q` (a regularization term using real rewards and next-state values from the offline dataset). This combination improves learning efficiency, especially when the world model's reward predictions are still developing.

- **Reachability Estimation Function (REF) for Safety Guarantee:**
 - **CMDP Transformation:** The safety problem is formulated as a Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP). Instead of solely relying on Lagrangian methods, which treat safety constraints as soft penalties, FOSP adopts a feasibility-guided approach.
 - **Feasible and Infeasible States:** The problem is conceptually divided: for feasible states, the goal is to maximize reward while maintaining safety; for infeasible states, the goal is to minimize constraint violations and return to the feasible set.
 - **Unifying Constraints:** The Reachability Estimation Function (REF) `u_pi(s)` is introduced. It estimates the probability of *not* violating a constraint in the future. This function helps to unify both hard safety constraints and soft behavioral regularization by prioritizing reaching or staying in safe regions.
 - **Augmented Lagrangian with Proximal Relaxation:** To manage the mixed hard and soft constraints, an Augmented Lagrangian method with proximal relaxation is used. This allows for an iterative update of Lagrange multipliers (`lambda_k_p`, `mu_k`), ensuring that the policy strongly adheres to safety constraints.
 - **Policy Optimization:** The final policy `pi_psi` is optimized based on an objective function that combines advantage functions for reward (`A_r`) and cost (`A_c`), weighted by the `u_pi` function and the Augmented Lagrangian term (`Phi`). A closed-form solution for `pi*` is derived, expressed as a weighted version of the behavior policy, where weights are determined by `u_pi` and exponential terms of `A_r` and `A_c`. The policy loss includes an entropy regularization term.

#### 3.3. Online Fine-tuning Stage

The online fine-tuning stage aims to adapt the offline-pretrained policy to novel environments efficiently and safely.

- **Safe Policy Expansion:**
 - **Bridging Offline and Online:** To prevent initial performance drops and safety violations, FOSP employs a safe policy expansion approach. Instead of directly fine-tuning the offline policy `pi_psi`, a new policy `pi_psi_1` is optimized while keeping `pi_psi` frozen as a prior.
 - **Probabilistic Action Selection:** During online interaction, actions are probabilistically selected from two sources: the frozen `pi_psi` and the adapting `pi_psi_1`. The selection probability is determined by a softmax distribution based on a normalized `Q_tilde(s, a)` value, which balances cumulative rewards and costs. This mechanism allows the agent to safely explore using the learned online policy while benefiting from the prior safety knowledge of the offline policy.
 - **Online Policy Update:** The new policy `pi_psi_1` is updated using a modified policy loss derived from the safety guarantee formulation (equation 23), which balances reward advantage, cost advantage, and the `u_pi` function, without the behavior policy constraint.
 - **Replay Buffer Integration:** The offline replay buffer is directly integrated as an online replay buffer, simplifying the data management process and allowing new online interaction trajectories to be continuously added for further learning.

In essence, FOSP combines a data-efficient world model with conservative value estimation, a robust safety mechanism based on reachability, and a gradual policy expansion technique, enabling an offline-to-online transfer that aims for improved generalization, stable performance, and maintained safety in vision-based robotic tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of FOSP was evaluated across simulation benchmarks (Safety-Gymnasium) and real-world robotic tasks, addressing its performance in offline pretraining, online fine-tuning, generalization capabilities, and component contributions.

#### 4.1. Performance in Offline Pretraining and Online Fine-tuning

- **Offline Pretraining:** In simulation tasks (PointGoal, PointButton, PointPush, CarGoal), FOSP demonstrated superior performance in reducing costs compared to other safe algorithms like Recover-RL and SafeDreamer. It achieved a balance between task performance and constraint avoidance. While its reward performance was competitive with, or slightly lower than, DreamerV3 (which disregards safety), FOSP maintained nearly zero constraint violations.
- **Online Fine-tuning:** FOSP exhibited robust online fine-tuning capabilities, further reducing costs while improving reward returns. It outperformed the online-only SafeDreamer (OSRP) in terms of safety, maintaining lower constraint violations, though OSRP sometimes achieved slightly higher rewards. This indicated FOSP's ability to bridge offline and online safety effectively while enhancing performance. FOSP's fine-tuning approach also significantly outperformed direct online training from scratch, achieving better reward and cost performance with fewer training steps.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies investigated the contribution of FOSP's individual components:

- **In-Sample Optimization:** Removing this component for value function learning led to decreased offline performance, highlighting its role in mitigating value overestimation.
- **Safe Policy Expansion:** Direct fine-tuning without safe policy expansion resulted in unstable transitions from offline to online, indicating its importance in stabilizing initial performance and avoiding performance drops.
- **Reachability Estimation Function (REF):** Updating the safe actor without the REF led to reduced overall performance while maintaining safety constraints, suggesting its role in balancing objectives.
- **Augmented Lagrangian with Feasible Value Function:** Designing the penalty without the Augmented Lagrangian or using only a feasible value function resulted in less effective compliance with safety constraints, affirming the robust nature of the chosen approach.
- **Dataset Ablation (Coverage):** Experiments on SafetyPointGoal2 revealed that dataset balance impacts performance. A purely safe dataset led to aggressive policies with high rewards but frequent violations, as the dynamics model mistakenly learned zero cost in all states. A purely unsafe dataset resulted in overly conservative policies with low rewards and costs, as the model overlearned cost avoidance. A balanced dataset (e.g., 1:1:1 ratio of safe, unsafe, random data) was found to be crucial for learning an accurate dynamics model and achieving a better trade-off between reward and safety. Adding random data generally helped alleviate world model errors.
- **Dataset Ablation (Size):** Increasing dataset size generally led to better and safer policies, though the marginal gains diminished with larger datasets.

#### 4.3. Safe Generalization Tasks

- **Simulation Generalization:** FOSP successfully generalized from an environment with fewer/simpler unsafe regions (`SafetyPointGoal1`) to one with more types and numbers of unsafe regions (`SafetyPointGoal2`). It showed better performance and safer fine-tuning compared to SafeDreamer in these novel simulation scenarios, maintaining near-zero constraint violations.
- **Real-World Robotic Deployment:** Using a Franka manipulator in "SafeReach" tasks, FOSP demonstrated its ability to adapt to unseen safety regions and target objects with different shapes and sizes (e.g., altering obstacle shapes, target shapes, number of obstacles). With few-shot fine-tuning (40 gradient steps), FOSP significantly improved the success rate and reduced constraint violations, proving its practical value in novel safety-critical scenarios. The robot showed strong adaptability, with safety performance improving rapidly post-fine-tuning, quickly reducing costs incurred in novel environments.

In summary, FOSP consistently exhibited robust performance, achieved near-zero constraint violations, and demonstrated effective generalization from offline-pretrained policies to unseen safety-critical scenarios in both simulated and real-world environments, primarily attributed to its integrated approach of in-sample optimization, reachability-based safety, and safe policy expansion.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The FOSP framework represents a notable advancement in the field of safe reinforcement learning, particularly at the intersection of offline learning, online fine-tuning, and vision-based robotic control. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Pioneering Offline-to-Online Safe Generalization:** FOSP is presented as the initial approach to specifically address safe generalization tasks through an offline-to-online RL paradigm. This bridges a recognized gap in the research landscape, offering a method for transferring safety-aware knowledge learned from static datasets to dynamic, novel deployment environments. This could lead to a broader adoption of RL in real-world safety-critical applications where initial training in a simulator or controlled environment is followed by safe adaptation in diverse real-world conditions.
2. **Enhanced Safety and Performance Balance in Vision-Based Tasks:** The method effectively manages the trade-off between maximizing task performance and strictly adhering to safety constraints across both offline and online training phases. Its application to vision-only tasks, which are inherently high-dimensional and complex, demonstrates its robustness in scenarios relevant to advanced robotics. This could accelerate the development of autonomous systems that operate safely and efficiently using visual inputs.
3. **Practical Real-World Deployment and Few-Shot Adaptation:** FOSP's successful deployment on a real Franka manipulator, without the necessity of sim-to-real transfer and demonstrating safe fine-tuning in unseen safety-constrained scenarios, underscores its practical utility. Its ability to adapt with only a few trials (few-shot fine-tuning) while maintaining safety is a critical feature for real-world robotic applications, where extensive online data collection can be prohibitive. This can reduce the time and resources required to deploy and adapt robotic systems to new operational contexts.
4. **Novel Integration of Methodological Components:** The integration of in-sample optimization, a reachability estimation function to unify mixed constraints, and a safe policy expansion mechanism contributes to a comprehensive solution. This methodological synthesis allows for conservative value estimation, robust safety guarantees, and a stable transition from offline pretraining to online fine-tuning, offering insights that could inform future algorithm designs in safe and efficient RL.
5. **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper lays the groundwork for several future research directions, including:
 - **Addressing More Complex Real-World Tasks:** Expanding FOSP to more intricate robotic tasks (e.g., grasping, manipulation with dynamic obstacles) can further validate its general applicability.
 - **Robustness to Visual Variations:** Exploring methods to handle significant visual changes or occlusions, possibly through semantic information integration or multi-view RL, could enhance its robustness in highly variable environments.
 - **Improving Safety Guarantees:** Incorporating risk-constrained methods could address scenarios with long-tail cost distributions, moving beyond expectation-based safety to more stringent risk measures.
 - **Safe Sim-to-Real Transfer:** FOSP's data efficiency and online fine-tuning capabilities position it as a promising candidate for safe sim-to-real transfer, allowing policies trained in simulators to be safely adapted to real robots.

In conclusion, FOSP offers a significant step towards developing adaptable and safe intelligent agents, particularly in robotics, by providing a robust framework for learning and safely generalizing policies from offline data to novel online environments with high-dimensional observations.
