# DARE-bench: Evaluating Modeling and Instruction Fidelity of LLMs in Data Science

> [!summary] Summary
> DARE-BENCH, a collaboration from the University of Houston and Snowflake AI Research, presents a new benchmark with 6,300 Kaggle-derived tasks designed to evaluate and train large language models for data science, emphasizing process fidelity and verifiable ground truth. Fine-tuning models with DARE-BENCH data led to substantial performance gains, including an over 8x improvement for one model baseline.

## Problem

- Existing LLM benchmarks for data science primarily focus on final answer accuracy, lacking evaluation of process fidelity, instruction adherence, and reproducibility in multi-step workflows.
- A significant scarcity of high-quality, accurately labeled, and executable training data for developing sophisticated LLM agents in data science tasks.
- Current benchmarks often have limited task diversity, neglecting critical domains like time-series forecasting and failing to differentiate between strict instruction-following and open-ended problem-solving.

## Method

- DARE-BENCH introduces a benchmark of 6,300 diverse ==Kaggle-derived== tasks spanning classification, regression, and time-series forecasting, categorized into Instruction Following (IF) and ML Modeling (MM) families.
- An automated data curation pipeline transforms raw datasets into standardized tasks with verifiable ground truth, including noise injection for IF tasks and sandbox validation to ensure reproducible reference outputs.
- The benchmark design explicitly supports scalable training paradigms, providing large-scale training data suitable for Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) using rejection-sampled traces and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR).

## Results

- Fine-tuning the Qwen3-**32B** model with SFT achieved absolute gains of nearly 20 points, representing a **1.83x** improvement over its baseline, while RL fine-tuning on Qwen3-**4B** yielded an over **8x** improvement in total score.
- Proprietary models like Claude-Sonnet-3.7 and GPT-5 generally outperformed open-source baselines on initial evaluations, with Claude-Sonnet-3.7 demonstrating strong overall performance.
- Fine-tuned DARE-BENCH models showed improved generalization by outperforming their baselines on adapted DSBench tasks and achieving competitive results against specialized agents like DataWiseAgent.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective data science agents require training that balances both process-oriented (instruction following) and outcome-oriented (ML modeling) tasks to develop robust and generalizable capabilities.
> - Fine-tuning LLMs on DARE-BENCH's verifiable data, through both SFT and RL, consistently mitigates common failure modes of baseline models, such as incorrect tool argument passing and flawed reasoning in preprocessing.
> - The number of interactive turns and allocated sandbox execution time are critical hyperparameters significantly influencing LLM agent performance in complex data science workflows.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of DARE-bench is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: large language models, AI agents, and automated data science. It critically addresses existing limitations in the evaluation and training of LLMs for complex, multi-step data science workflows.

**LLM Agents:** The paper acknowledges the growing trend of employing LLMs as "data-science (DS) agents" capable of intricate tasks like data reading, transformation, and machine learning modeling, often augmented with tool-augmented code execution. This aligns with broader research in "Agentic LLMs," which explore models' abilities in planning, tool calling (e.g., ReAct, Toolformer), and memory. DARE-bench aims to provide a robust framework to evaluate and enhance these agentic capabilities in a data science context.

**LLMs for Coding and Data Science Benchmarks:** The research landscape has seen a progression of benchmarks for evaluating LLMs on code generation and data science tasks:
- **Foundational Code Benchmarks:** Early benchmarks like HumanEval and MBPP focused on short, self-contained functions with unit tests, establishing a pass/fail signal.
- **Complex Software Engineering:** SWE-bench extended this to real-world GitHub issues requiring multi-file modifications and project-level testing, highlighting the need for more complex reasoning.
- **Emerging Data Science Benchmarks:** More recently, several benchmarks have emerged specifically for data science tasks, including DS-1000 (NumPy/Pandas programming), DSBench, MLE-bench, and DataSciBench (which use Kaggle competitions), DABstep (financial tasks), and DSBC (private datasets). These signify a community shift towards evaluating LLMs on end-to-end data science workflows.

**Gaps Identified by DARE-bench:** Despite these advancements, DARE-bench identifies critical shortcomings in existing data science benchmarks:
1. **Lack of Process-Aware Evaluation:** Many benchmarks primarily evaluate final answer accuracy, overlooking crucial aspects like instruction adherence, process fidelity, and reproducibility. This means they fail to capture *how* an agent arrives at a solution, not just *if* it arrives.
2. **Scarcity of Training Data:** There's a significant bottleneck in obtaining accurately labeled, expert-level, executable data science process traces for training LLM agents. Existing benchmarks often rely on human-processed data or competition leaderboards, limiting scalability and diversity.
3. **Limited Task Diversity:** Some benchmarks neglect critical domains like time-series forecasting or fail to differentiate between strict instruction-following and open-ended problem-solving, both crucial in real-world data science.

**Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR):** A key innovation of DARE-bench is its emphasis on "verifiable ground truth." This design choice explicitly enables and aligns with the advancements in reinforcement learning (RL) using programmatic, verifiable signals for model training at scale. This connects DARE-bench to works like GRPO and DeepSeek-R1, which leverage verifier-enhanced objectives to improve reasoning and code generation without heavy reliance on human preference data. By making evaluation outcomes deterministic and reproducible, DARE-bench provides the necessary "reward signal" for effective RL.

In summary, DARE-bench positions itself as a next-generation benchmark that not only evaluates LLMs on data science tasks but also serves as a critical training resource, specifically designed to overcome the limitations of previous benchmarks by integrating process fidelity, verifiable ground truth, and large-scale, diverse tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for DARE-bench stems from the growing integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) as autonomous or semi-autonomous agents in complex, multi-step data science workflows. This rapid adoption, however, has exposed significant gaps in existing evaluation and training paradigms.

The authors pinpoint two major deficiencies in the current benchmarking landscape:
1. **Inadequate Process-Aware Evaluation:** Current benchmarks predominantly focus on the final output or "final-answer accuracy." They often neglect crucial aspects of the data science process, such as adherence to specific instructions, the fidelity of intermediate steps, and the reproducibility of results. Without evaluating these process-level aspects, it's challenging to build truly reliable and trustworthy data science agents that can operate effectively in real-world scenarios where not just the answer, but *how* it was derived, matters.
2. **Scarcity of High-Quality Training Data:** Developing sophisticated LLM agents for data science demands a wealth of accurately labeled, executable training data, particularly expert-level demonstrations of data science process traces. Relying heavily on human-curated data (e.g., from Kaggle competition notebooks) is expensive, time-consuming, and does not scale sufficiently to meet the training needs of large models. This data bottleneck limits the ability of LLMs to acquire and master domain-specific skills in data science.

To address these critical gaps, DARE-bench sets forth the following key objectives:
- **Introduce a Comprehensive Benchmark:** To provide a new standard for evaluating LLM agents in both machine learning modeling and instruction-following within data science contexts.
- **Ensure Verifiable Ground Truth:** To enable objective and reproducible evaluation by designing all tasks with verifiable ground truth. This eliminates reliance on subjective human or less reliable model-based judges, making evaluations deterministic and suitable for scalable training.
- **Capture Process Fidelity and Instruction Adherence:** To go beyond mere final-answer accuracy by explicitly measuring how well LLM agents follow instructions and adhere to defined data science processes, which is crucial for real-world reliability.
- **Provide Large-Scale Training Data:** To overcome the data scarcity issue by generating a substantial volume of tasks (6,300 Kaggle-derived) that can serve as both evaluation sets and, crucially, as high-quality training data for fine-tuning LLMs.
- **Support Agentic Tools and Diverse Tasks:** To create a benchmark that supports tool-augmented code execution in a sandboxed environment and covers a broad range of data science problems, including different ML task types (classification, regression) and complex domains like time-series forecasting.
- **Facilitate Model Improvement:** To design a benchmark that not only identifies current LLM limitations but also provides the necessary data and evaluation mechanisms to substantially improve model performance through supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR).

By pursuing these objectives, DARE-bench aims to accelerate the development of more capable, reliable, and interpretable LLM agents for data science, thereby unlocking the full potential of AI in automating and assisting complex analytical workflows.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DARE-bench adopts a rigorous and innovative methodology to create a comprehensive and scalable benchmark for LLMs in data science, characterized by its verifiable ground truth, automated curation, and dual role as an evaluation and training resource.

#### 3.1. DARE-bench Structure and Task Families

The benchmark comprises 6,300 tasks derived from Kaggle, partitioned into an approximate 95/5 train/test split. It features three primary data science task families: classification, regression, and time-series forecasting. Each family includes variants designed to probe distinct agent capabilities:

- **Instruction Following (IF) Tasks:** These variants (Classification-IF, Regression-IF) assess an LLM agent's ability to strictly follow a reference workflow, reproducing a procedurally faithful execution. Evaluation is based on strict, binary accuracy against a *simulated reference output* generated from expert-crafted, reproducible code.
- **ML Modeling (MM) Tasks:** These variants (Classification-MM, Regression-MM) focus on outcome-based performance in machine learning. LLM agents are given freedom to develop optimal models, and their predictions are evaluated against the *dataset's original ground truth*.
- **Time-series Forecasting Tasks:**
 - **eXogenous Features (XF):** Retains all original exogenous features in both training and test sets.
 - **Canonical Forecasting (CF):** Constrains the test set to only timestamp and entity columns, making it a more classical forecasting setup.

#### 3.2. Automated Data Curation Pipeline

A novel automated pipeline (Figure 2) systematically transforms raw Kaggle datasets into standardized tasks, ensuring quality and scalability with minimal human involvement:

1. **Dataset Sourcing with Augmented Metadata:**
 - **Source:** Kaggle's official API is used to retrieve tabular datasets with open licenses.
 - **Augmentation:** A lightweight web crawler extracts additional natural language descriptions and column previews from Kaggle webpages, enriching metadata for LLM understanding.

2. **LLM-Assisted Task Design and Feasibility Analysis:**
 - An LLM analyzes dataset previews and descriptions to determine if a well-posed predictive task can be formulated.
 - It identifies target columns (categorical/continuous) and structured features. For time-series, it detects timestamp columns, numerical targets, and temporal frequency. Only feasible datasets proceed.

3. **Post-Process:**
 - **Data Splitting:** Datasets are randomly split into train/test sets, with chronological splitting for time-series data to preserve temporal order.
 - **Noise Injection (for IF tasks):** Approximately 20% of training data for IF tasks undergoes controlled noise injection (e.g., numerical values exceeding valid ranges, unexpected categorical entries) to simulate real-world data quality issues. The test set remains clean for reference.
 - **Time-series Specifics:** LLMs detect entity identifiers to prevent data leakage and perform automatic resampling of irregular time series data to uniform intervals using LLM-suggested aggregation methods.

4. **Finalization:**
 - **Sandbox Validation (for IF tasks):** Each IF task's reference solution code is executed independently within a sandboxed environment. This step ensures that instructions are faithfully executable and that generated predictions are reproducible under a fixed random seed, yielding the "simulated reference output" for ground truth.
 - **Standardization:** Tasks are packaged into a standardized format, including training/testing data, metadata, natural language task description, and reference.

#### 3.3. Task Formulation and Evaluation Metrics

- **Input to LLM Agent (G):** A natural-language question (Q), dataset description (M), training data (D_train), test data (D_test without targets), and access to a code execution tool (T) with maximum runtime (T_max) and interaction budget (K turns).
- **Output:** Executable code (C), which is run in T on D_train to fit a model, and then on D_test to generate predictions (ŷ).
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Instruction-Following (IF) tasks:** Binary (0/1) accuracy by comparing ŷ against y_ref (simulated reference output).
 - **ML Modeling (MM) tasks (and Time-series):**
 - Classification: Macro-F1 score (0.0-1.0) to account for class imbalance.
 - Regression/Time-series: Clipped Coefficient of Determination (R²) defined as `min{1, max{0, R²}}` to ensure scores are in.
 - For tasks with multiple targets, metrics are averaged.

#### 3.4. Key Features of DARE-bench

- **Dual Evaluation Focus:** Assesses both ML modeling (outcome-driven) and instruction following (process-driven).
- **Verifiable Ground Truth:** Objective evaluation through actual labels or reproducible reference solution outputs, removing human/model judgment biases. This is crucial for SFT and RLVR.
- **Training Resource:** The benchmark explicitly offers a large-scale training dataset to facilitate model fine-tuning and alignment.
- **Diversity, Realism, and Practical Constraints:** Kaggle-derived tasks ensure diversity across domains and languages, reflecting real-world challenges (e.g., class imbalance, missing values, noise). Practical constraints like 10-minute execution limits and bounded tool invocation turns simulate real-world efficiency demands.

#### 3.5. Fine-tuning Approaches

DARE-bench's design enables two complementary training paradigms:

- **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** Leverages "rejection-sampled" traces (generated trajectories filtered for validity, quality, and speed) to provide high-quality supervision signals. Four strategies are explored: Fastest-Valid (FV), All-Valid (AV), Best-Valid (BV), and Duo-Valid (DV), differing in how they select successful trajectories.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Utilizes GRPO (with modifications for stability) on DARE-bench training tasks, directly optimizing models with verifiable outcome rewards (based on the evaluation metrics).

This multifaceted methodology ensures DARE-bench serves as a robust, scalable, and versatile platform for both evaluating and enhancing LLM capabilities in data science.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive evaluation conducted on DARE-bench reveals critical insights into the current capabilities and limitations of LLMs in data science tasks, alongside the transformative impact of fine-tuning with its curated data.

#### 4.1. Hyperparameter Sensitivity Analysis

- A sensitivity analysis on `gpt-o4-mini` highlighted the importance of interactive turns and sandbox execution time limits. Performance generally improved with a higher number of interactive turns, with a "dramatic leap" observed when moving from 3-turn to 5-turn configurations.
- The `(5 turns, 200 s)` configuration was selected as a balanced and practical baseline for comprehensive model comparison, offering a strong performance representative within realistic time constraints (approximately 1000s total user wait time).

#### 4.2. Baseline Model Comparison

- **Overall Underperformance:** Even highly capable models, particularly general-purpose LLMs without task-aligned training, "struggle to achieve good performance," especially in machine learning modeling (MM) tasks. The 'time-series-CF' tasks proved exceptionally challenging for all baselines.
- **Proprietary vs. Open-Source:** Proprietary models (e.g., gpt-o4-mini, Claude-Sonnet-3.7, gpt-5) generally outperformed open-source models (Qwen3-32B, Qwen3-4B). Qwen3-4B baseline scored a meager 4.39 total.
- **Top Performers:** Claude-Sonnet-3.7 emerged as the overall top-performing model across several metrics, demonstrating strong capabilities. GPT-5 led specifically in 'classification-IF' and 'regression-IF' tasks, indicating strength in strict instruction following.
- **Generational Disparities:** Claude-Sonnet-4 surprisingly underperformed its predecessor, Claude-Sonnet-3.7, due to its tendency to decompose tasks into excessively fine-grained substeps, leading to frequent timeouts.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Failure Analysis of Baselines

A systematic analysis of failed trajectories identified common bottlenecks:
- **Incorrect Tool Argument Passing:** LLMs frequently generated logically correct Python code but failed to pass necessary arguments (e.g., filenames) correctly to the code execution tool, causing runtime errors.
- **Instruction Following Failures:** Models often ignored explicit constraints, such as executing steps in the wrong order, skipping required transformations, or omitting critical function arguments.
- **Flawed Reasoning in Open-Ended Tasks:** Subtle reasoning issues included misuse of metadata (e.g., hard-coding values instead of dynamically referencing metadata.txt), risky preprocessing (e.g., naive label encoding, poor NaN handling), and unreliable type inference. These led to fragile and error-prone pipelines.
- **Time-Series Task Failures:** Performance on 'time-series-CF' was particularly poor, with models often failing to produce valid output formats or relying on trivial heuristics (e.g., predicting last value or mean), indicating a lack of complex time-series reasoning.

#### 4.4. Impact of Fine-tuning (SFT and RL)

Fine-tuning on DARE-bench artifacts significantly improved model performance:
- **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) on Qwen3-32B:** Achieved "absolute gains of nearly 20 points" in total score, corresponding to a 1.83x improvement over its baseline (from 23.25 to 42.42-42.91 across SFT variants). ModelPerf (quality of predictions on successful attempts) also saw improvements of 5-7%. Different rejection sampling strategies (FV, AV, BV, DV) offered complementary benefits, with AV showing strong overall gains for MM tasks and FV for IF tasks.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) on Qwen3-4B:** Demonstrated even more dramatic relative gains, boosting the total score from a baseline of 4.39 to 37.40 (an over 8x improvement). ModelPerf increased from 54.18 to 62.55.
- **Failure Mitigation:** Fine-tuning effectively mitigated baseline failure modes. For Qwen3-4B, RL reduced code errors by 48% and halved code execution limit errors, indicating improved code correctness and efficiency. Case studies showed fine-tuning resolved issues like incorrect tool arguments and improved metadata utilization (e.g., checking `metadata.txt` for column names instead of guessing).

#### 4.5. Impact of Data Composition (Ablation Study)

An ablation study with SFT-DV on Qwen3-32B confirmed the complementary nature of IF and MM tasks:
- Training exclusively on MM data improved predictive modeling but degraded instruction adherence.
- Training solely on IF data improved instruction adherence but degraded predictive modeling.
- Only the combined approach (IF+MM) successfully integrated both capabilities, achieving a robust balance. This underscores that comprehensive data science agents need exposure to both process-oriented and outcome-oriented tasks.

#### 4.6. External Validation and Comparison

- **Generalization:** Fine-tuned models on DARE-bench showed good generalization by outperforming their baselines on an adapted version of DSBench tasks, validating DARE-bench's ability to enhance performance beyond in-domain tasks.
- **Competitive Performance:** Fine-tuned DARE-bench models (e.g., Qwen3-32B + SFT-DV scoring 42.41 on DSBench) compared favorably to specialized agents like DataWiseAgent (29.17 on DSBench), indicating the framework's effectiveness.

In summary, the findings strongly validate DARE-bench as both an accurate evaluation tool and a critical training resource. It effectively exposes the current limitations of LLMs in complex data science tasks and provides a pathway for substantial performance improvements through targeted fine-tuning, thereby bridging the gap between general-purpose LLMs and highly capable data science agents.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DARE-bench represents a significant advancement in the field of large language models for data science, with a profound potential impact on research, development, and real-world applications of AI agents.

#### 5.1. Addresses Critical Gaps in Benchmarking and Training

The most immediate and crucial significance of DARE-bench lies in its direct attack on the fundamental limitations of existing benchmarks. By focusing on **process fidelity, instruction adherence, and verifiable ground truth**, DARE-bench moves beyond simplistic final-answer evaluations. This comprehensive assessment is vital for developing trustworthy and robust data science agents that not only deliver correct results but also follow prescribed procedures and produce reproducible outcomes, which are paramount in regulated industries or scientific research. The automated and scalable data curation pipeline also directly addresses the **scarcity of high-quality training data**, a major bottleneck in developing capable LLM agents.

#### 5.2. Enables Robust Model Training and Alignment

DARE-bench's design, particularly its **verifiable ground truth** for both instruction-following and ML modeling tasks, makes it uniquely suitable as a training resource. This feature is critical for the effective application of scalable training paradigms like Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). The paper's empirical results, demonstrating dramatic performance boosts (e.g., over 8x for Qwen3-4B via RL) from fine-tuning on DARE-bench, emphatically validate its utility as a training dataset. This capability will accelerate the development of more specialized and performant LLMs for data science.

#### 5.3. Fosters the Development of Generalizable Data Science Agents

By evaluating both the ability to strictly follow instructions and to achieve high predictive performance in open-ended ML modeling, DARE-bench pushes models towards a more holistic understanding of data science. The ablation study, showing that combining IF and MM data is essential for a balanced agent, highlights that agents need both procedural discipline and outcome-driven problem-solving skills. This will lead to the creation of more versatile agents capable of navigating the diverse demands of real-world data science, from executing precise experimental protocols to creatively optimizing model performance.

#### 5.4. Promotes Realism and Reproducibility

The benchmark's reliance on **Kaggle-derived tasks** ensures a high degree of realism and diversity, reflecting the messiness and complexity of real-world data science problems (e.g., class imbalance, missing values, noise). The inclusion of **practical constraints** like execution time limits and turn budgets further aligns the evaluation with user expectations for efficient and practical AI assistance. Moreover, the emphasis on **reproducible evaluation** through sandbox execution and verifiable ground truth enhances the scientific rigor of LLM agent development, allowing for objective comparisons and reliable progress tracking.

#### 5.5. Broadens Research Horizons

DARE-bench opens new avenues for research into LLM agent capabilities. The identified failure modes (e.g., incorrect tool arguments, flawed reasoning in preprocessing, poor time-series handling) provide clear targets for future model improvements. The benchmark's extensibility for new task types (e.g., multimodal inputs, clustering, anomaly detection) and stricter procedural constraints promises to push the boundaries of what LLM agents can autonomously achieve in data science.

In conclusion, DARE-bench is more than just another benchmark; it is a foundational piece of infrastructure for advancing the capabilities of LLM agents in data science. By providing a large-scale, verifiable, and trainable resource, it sets a new standard for evaluating and improving these agents, ultimately accelerating the realization of robust, reliable, and intelligent AI assistance across the entire data science lifecycle.
