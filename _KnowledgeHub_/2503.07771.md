# RoboCopilot: Human-in-the-loop Interactive Imitation Learning for Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboCopilot, developed by UC Berkeley researchers, presents an interactive imitation learning system for robot manipulation, integrating a bilateral teleoperation interface with bimanual robots. The system allows human operators to provide real-time corrections during autonomous execution, significantly improving learning efficiency and policy performance for complex, contact-rich tasks.

## Problem

- Traditional imitation learning methods for robot manipulation suffer from covariate shift, leading to compounding errors when robots encounter states unseen in demonstration data.
- Offline learning from demonstrations is data-inefficient, typically requiring large datasets to achieve robust performance and lacking adaptability to novel situations or real-time error correction.
- Existing teleoperation interfaces often lack intuitive control mechanisms and seamless switching between autonomous and human operation, hindering effective human-in-the-loop learning for complex tasks.

## Method

- **RoboCopilot** introduces a human-in-the-loop interactive imitation learning framework that integrates a compliant, bilateral teleoperation device with a mobile bimanual manipulator.
- The system employs an adapted ==Human-Gated DAgger (HG-DAgger)== algorithm, allowing human operators to provide targeted corrections during autonomous execution, which are then used to update the policy.
- It features a seamless software framework for intuitive switching between autonomous policy execution and human teleoperation, enabling efficient data collection for error correction.

## Results

- RoboCopilot consistently outperformed offline behavior cloning in simulation, with Batched DAgger achieving near-perfect success rates on most tasks.
- In real-world industrial part picking, the system achieved over **90%** success rate after interactive training, up from **60%** with offline methods.
- The system successfully mastered a complex, long-horizon bimanual kitchen task with only 20 demonstrations, requiring 30-**50%** fewer demonstrations than offline behavior cloning for comparable performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Interactive learning with targeted human interventions significantly enhances policy performance and learning efficiency compared to offline behavior cloning, especially for complex manipulation tasks.
> - A kinematically similar, bilateral teleoperation interface with force feedback is crucial for enabling intuitive human control and effective real-time correction of robot behaviors.
> - The integration of a compliant bimanual robot with an interactive learning framework allows for mastering long-horizon, contact-rich bimanual tasks with fewer demonstrations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robot manipulation skills have traditionally been developed through either explicit programming or passive learning from demonstrations. While these approaches have shown success in controlled environments, they often fail when confronted with the complexities and variations of real-world tasks. The RoboCopilot system addresses this challenge by introducing a novel human-in-the-loop interactive imitation learning framework specifically designed for robot manipulation tasks.

This system, developed by researchers at UC Berkeley, integrates hardware and software components to create a seamless interface between human operators and robots. RoboCopilot enables operators to provide demonstrations, monitor autonomous execution, and intervene when necessary—all through an intuitive teleoperation interface that mimics the robot's kinematics while providing force feedback to the user.

The fundamental innovation of RoboCopilot lies in its interactive approach to imitation learning. Rather than relying solely on pre-recorded demonstrations, the system allows humans to provide targeted corrections when the robot makes mistakes, significantly improving learning efficiency and policy performance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

#### 2.1. Limitations of Traditional Approaches

Imitation Learning (IL) has emerged as a promising approach for teaching robots complex skills by observing human demonstrations. However, traditional IL methods face several critical challenges:

1. **Covariate Shift**: When robots execute learned policies, they may encounter states not seen in the demonstration data, leading to compounding errors.

2. **Data Inefficiency**: Offline learning from demonstrations typically requires large datasets to achieve robust performance.

3. **Limited Adaptability**: Pre-recorded demonstrations cannot respond to novel situations or correct the robot's mistakes in real-time.

4. **Teleoperation Barriers**: Existing teleoperation interfaces often lack intuitive control mechanisms and seamless switching between autonomous and human operation.

These limitations have hindered the deployment of robots in complex, real-world manipulation tasks, particularly those requiring bimanual coordination and precise contact interactions.

#### 2.2. Previous Approaches

Prior work has addressed these challenges through various methods:

- **Data Aggregation (DAgger)** algorithms that iteratively collect expert feedback during policy execution
- **Human-Gated DAgger (HG-DAgger)** which allows human operators to decide when to intervene
- **Teleoperation interfaces** that range from direct joint control to VR-based systems

However, these approaches often fail to provide a fully integrated solution that combines an intuitive hardware interface with an effective interactive learning framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. The RoboCopilot System

RoboCopilot addresses these challenges through a comprehensive system integrating:

1. A compliant, bilateral teleoperation device that mimics the robot's kinematics
2. A mobile bimanual manipulator with 7 degrees of freedom (DOF) per arm
3. An interactive imitation learning algorithm based on HG-DAgger
4. A software framework for seamless switching between autonomous and human control

#### 3.2. System Components

The RoboCopilot system consists of several key components:

1. **Policy Learning Network**: A diffusion policy network that learns from demonstration data and is continuously updated through interactive teaching.

2. **Teleoperation Interface**: A custom-designed device that enables intuitive control of the robot arms with force feedback.

3. **Control System**: Software that manages the switching between autonomous execution and human intervention.

4. **Data Collection Framework**: Tools for recording, labeling, and integrating new demonstrations into the training dataset.

#### 3.6. Interactive Learning Framework

The learning framework employed by RoboCopilot builds upon the Human-Gated DAgger (HG-DAgger) algorithm, with several adaptations for robot manipulation:

#### 3.7. Learning Process

1. **Initial Training Phase**: The system is first trained on a small set of human demonstrations to learn a basic policy.

2. **Interactive Teaching Phase**: During autonomous execution, the human operator can take control when needed to correct mistakes or demonstrate optimal behavior in challenging situations.

3. **Policy Update Methods**:
 - **Continual DAgger**: The policy is updated online during interactive sessions, allowing for immediate adaptation to new demonstrations.
 - **Batched DAgger**: All interactive data is collected and then used to train a new policy from scratch, avoiding catastrophic forgetting issues.

The mathematical formulation for the policy learning can be expressed as:

$$\pi_\theta = \arg\min_\theta \mathbb{E}_{(o_t, a_t) \sim \mathcal{D}} [L(\pi_\theta(o_t), a_t)]$$

where $\pi_\theta$ is the policy parameterized by $\theta$, $o_t$ is the observation at time $t$, $a_t$ is the action (either from the human or the policy), and $\mathcal{D}$ is the dataset of demonstrations.

#### 3.8. Control Flow

During execution, the system constantly monitors the robot's performance. When the robot is about to fail or enters an uncertain state, the human operator can:

1. Press a takeover button to gain control
2. Correct the robot's behavior
3. Return control to the autonomous policy

This interaction creates a dataset of targeted corrections that addresses the specific failure modes of the current policy, leading to more efficient learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

The researchers evaluated RoboCopilot through comprehensive experiments in both simulation and real-world settings:

#### 4.2. Simulation Experiments

Using the Robomimic benchmark, the team tested the system on four manipulation tasks:

These tasks evaluate different aspects of robot manipulation, including grasping, placement, and object transport.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiments

Three real-world experiments were conducted to demonstrate the system's capabilities:

1. **Industrial Part Picking**: Picking up metal parts and placing them in a bin

2. **Mobile Industrial Transport**: Navigating to a location, picking up an object, and transporting it

3. **Kitchen Task**: A long-horizon bimanual task involving opening a refrigerator, retrieving an orange, placing it in a pot, and closing the refrigerator

The kitchen task is particularly challenging as it involves multiple steps, requiring coordinated bimanual manipulation and contact-rich interactions.

#### 4.4. Simulation Results

The simulation experiments demonstrated several key findings:

1. Interactive learning with RoboCopilot significantly outperformed offline behavior cloning across all tasks.
2. Batched DAgger achieved the highest success rates, reaching near-perfect performance on most tasks.
3. Continual DAgger showed rapid initial improvement but sometimes suffered from catastrophic forgetting.

#### 4.5. Real-World Performance

In real-world experiments, RoboCopilot showed impressive capabilities:

1. **Industrial Part Picking**: The system achieved over 90% success rate after interactive training, compared to 60% with offline behavior cloning.

2. **Mobile Industrial Transport**: The robot successfully learned to navigate and transport objects with over 85% reliability after interactive training.

3. **Kitchen Task**: The system mastered this complex bimanual task with only 20 demonstrations, achieving consistent performance on a long-horizon task that involves multiple contact-rich interactions.

#### 4.6. Key Performance Metrics

1. **Learning Efficiency**: RoboCopilot required 30-50% fewer demonstrations compared to offline behavior cloning to achieve the same performance level.

2. **Human Intervention**: The need for human intervention decreased significantly as training progressed, indicating successful policy improvement.

3. **Robustness**: The learned policies showed improved generalization to variations in object positions and environmental conditions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Applications and Use Cases

The capabilities demonstrated by RoboCopilot open up several promising application areas:

#### 5.2. Industrial Automation

The system's ability to learn complex manipulation tasks makes it suitable for:
- Small-batch manufacturing with frequent changeovers
- Assembly of complex components
- Material handling in unstructured environments

#### 5.3. Service Robotics

The bimanual manipulation capabilities enable applications in:
- Food preparation and kitchen assistance
- Eldercare and assistance for people with disabilities
- Household chores and cleaning tasks

#### 5.4. Research and Development

RoboCopilot serves as a platform for:
- Studying human-robot interaction
- Developing and testing new imitation learning algorithms
- Exploring multi-modal learning approaches

#### 5.5. Limitations and Future Work

Despite its impressive capabilities, RoboCopilot has several limitations that point to directions for future research:

#### 5.6. Current Limitations

1. **Resource Intensity**: The system requires significant human interaction time, which can be resource-intensive.
2. **Hardware Complexity**: The bilateral teleoperation device adds cost and complexity compared to simpler interfaces.
3. **Policy Generalization**: The learned policies may still struggle with significant environmental variations.

#### 5.7. Future Directions

1. **Multi-modal Learning**: Integrating language instructions with demonstration data for more efficient teaching.
2. **Transfer Learning**: Leveraging pre-trained policies to accelerate learning of new tasks.
3. **Autonomous Improvement**: Developing techniques for robots to identify and request help only in specific challenging situations.
4. **Simplified Hardware**: Creating more accessible teleoperation devices while maintaining intuitive control.

#### 5.8. Conclusion

RoboCopilot represents a significant advancement in interactive imitation learning for robot manipulation. By combining an intuitive bilateral teleoperation interface with an effective interactive learning framework, the system enables robots to learn complex bimanual manipulation skills more efficiently and robustly than traditional approaches.

The key contributions include:

1. A comprehensive hardware-software system design for interactive teaching
2. A bilateral teleoperation interface optimized for human-in-the-loop learning
3. Adapted interactive imitation learning algorithms for robot manipulation
4. Demonstration of successful learning of complex, long-horizon bimanual tasks

As robots continue to move beyond structured industrial settings into more dynamic and unstructured environments, systems like RoboCopilot will play a crucial role in enabling robots to acquire the diverse skills needed for effective operation in human-centric spaces. The blend of human intuition and robot learning capabilities offers a promising path toward more capable and adaptable robotic systems.
