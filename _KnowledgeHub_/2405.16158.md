# Bigger, Regularized, Optimistic: scaling for compute and sample-efficient continuous control

> [!summary] Summary
> Scaling critic networks in continuous control reinforcement learning, when combined with specific regularization techniques and optimistic exploration, yields state-of-the-art sample efficiency. The BRO algorithm, developed by researchers from Ideas NCBR and the University of Warsaw, achieves near-optimal performance in challenging tasks like DeepMind Control's Dog and Humanoid, surpassing leading model-based approaches.

## Problem

- Continuous control Reinforcement Learning has historically focused on smaller network architectures and algorithmic improvements, with limited success from model scaling.
- Naively increasing model capacity in RL often results in performance degradation rather than improvements.
- Model-free algorithms struggle to achieve high sample efficiency and near-optimal performance in complex continuous control environments with high-dimensional state/action spaces.

## Method

- The **BRO (Bigger, Regularized, Optimistic)** algorithm extends ==Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)== by scaling critic network capacity (up to 5M parameters) and training density (==replay ratio==).
- It introduces **BroNet**, a novel critic architecture with ==Layer Normalization== and ==ResNet blocks==, combined with ==weight decay== and ==full-parameter resets== for stable scaling.
- ==Optimistic exploration== is employed using a ==dual actor== setup and non-pessimistic (ensemble mean) ==quantile Q-value== approximations to leverage epistemic uncertainty.

## Results

- The BRO algorithm achieved state-of-the-art performance across **40** complex continuous control tasks, demonstrating **2.5x** greater sample efficiency than the leading model-based algorithm, TD-MPC2, on challenging benchmarks like DeepMind Control's Dog and Humanoid.
- The BroNet architecture enabled stable and effective scaling of the critic network, preventing the performance degradation often associated with naive capacity increases.
- Optimistic exploration, combined with non-pessimistic Q-value estimation and network regularization, contributed to significant performance gains without increasing value overestimation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Skillfully scaling critic network capacity, particularly when combined with robust regularization, is more impactful and computationally efficient than solely increasing replay ratio for continuous control.
> - The novel BroNet architecture, utilizing Layer Normalization and ResNet blocks, is essential for stable training and effective performance scaling of larger critic networks.
> - Replacing pessimistic Q-value estimation with non-pessimistic ensemble means, coupled with strong regularization, improves performance without increasing overestimation, challenging prior assumptions.
> - Overparameterization of the critic network can partially substitute for specific inductive biases, as the impact of some algorithmic improvements diminishes with increased model scale.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Deep learning, particularly in fields such as natural language processing and computer vision, has demonstrated substantial advancements primarily driven by the development and scaling of large neural network models. This trend has also begun to influence Reinforcement Learning (RL), with recent work indicating that model scaling can enhance sample efficiency, particularly in discrete control tasks. However, these scaling approaches have not been directly applicable to continuous action RL, which is prevalent in many physical control tasks.

Traditionally, continuous deep RL has relied on smaller network architectures, with research primarily focused on algorithmic improvements. These improvements have aimed to enhance sample efficiency and address challenges such as value overestimation, exploration, and increasing the number of gradient steps. Prior evidence has also suggested that a naive increase in model capacity can lead to performance degradation in RL.

This work challenges this conventional paradigm by investigating whether significant performance gains in continuous control can be achieved through a combination of parameter scaling and replay scaling, alongside existing algorithmic enhancements. It positions itself by demonstrating that scaling the critic network, when combined with specific regularization techniques and optimistic exploration, can lead to substantial improvements in sample efficiency and overall performance in continuous control, even outperforming leading model-based approaches. The paper suggests that specific inductive biases introduced by domain-specific RL improvements can, to some extent, be substituted by larger, well-regularized critic networks, potentially leading to simpler algorithms.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to ascertain whether significant performance improvements in continuous control can be achieved by combining model parameter and replay scaling with existing algorithmic improvements. This overarching question is motivated by the success of large-scale models in other deep learning domains and the observation that scaling has shown promise in discrete control RL, but its application to continuous action spaces has been limited and often problematic.

The specific objectives and motivations include:

- **Empirical Investigation of Scaling:** To conduct a comprehensive empirical analysis into the interplay of scaling model capacity (specifically the critic network) and domain-specific RL enhancements in continuous deep RL. This involves evaluating a broad range of design choices, including batch size, distributional Q-values, neural network regularizations, and optimistic exploration.
- **Understanding Computational Trade-offs:** To carefully investigate the benefits and computational costs associated with scaling along two axes: the number of parameters in the neural networks and the number of gradient steps (replay ratio). The motivation is to identify which scaling axis yields greater performance gains and computational efficiency, especially in parallelized environments.
- **Development of BRO Algorithm:** To inform the design of a novel sample-efficient model-free algorithm, named BRO (Bigger, Regularized, Optimistic), based on the empirical findings from the scaling investigations. The motivation is to demonstrate that a specific combination of scaling, regularization, and exploration can yield state-of-the-art results.
- **Achieving State-of-the-Art Performance:** To develop an algorithm that significantly outperforms current leading model-based and model-free algorithms across a diverse and challenging set of continuous control benchmarks. A key motivation is to enable a model-free approach to achieve near-optimal policies in notoriously difficult tasks such as the DeepMind Control Dog and Humanoid environments, which have traditionally been challenging for model-free methods.
- **Simplifying Algorithms through Scaling:** To explore the hypothesis that the inductive biases introduced by certain domain-specific RL improvements might be largely substituted by increased critic scaling, potentially leading to simpler overall algorithms.
- **Insights into Architectural Design:** To develop and validate a network architecture (BroNet) that allows for effective scaling of the critic network, addressing the issues of performance degradation observed with naive scaling.

In essence, the research is motivated by the potential for scaling to unlock new levels of sample efficiency and performance in continuous control, shifting the focus from solely algorithmic innovations to also embracing architectural and capacity scaling.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The research employs an extensive empirical methodology to investigate the effects of scaling and other design choices in continuous Reinforcement Learning (RL), culminating in the development of the BRO algorithm.

#### 3.1. Algorithm Base and Key Components (BRO - Bigger, Regularized, Optimistic)

BRO is built upon the Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) algorithm, a stochastic policy, maximum entropy method. It integrates three core design principles:

- **Bigger:**
 - **Scaled Critic Network:** BRO utilizes a critic network with approximately 5 million parameters by default, which is about seven times larger than typical SAC models.
 - **Scaled Training Density:** A default replay ratio (number of gradient updates per environment step) of RR = 10 is used. A faster version, BRO (Fast), uses RR = 2 for increased compute efficiency.
- **Regularized:**
 - **BroNet Architecture:** This novel architecture for the critic network incorporates Layer Normalization (Ba et al., 2016) after each dense layer and utilizes ResNet blocks. This design is crucial for stability and effective scaling.
 - **Weight Decay:** L2 regularization on weights (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2017) is applied.
 - **Full-Parameter Resets:** The network parameters are fully reset periodically (Nikishin et al., 2022; D’Oro et al., 2022) to maintain plasticity and prevent premature convergence.
- **Optimistic:**
 - **Dual Policy Optimistic Exploration:** BRO employs a dual actor setup (Nauman & Cygan, 2023a) where separate policies are used for exploration and temporal difference updates. The exploration policy is optimized towards an optimistic upper-bound Q-value approximation.
 - **Non-Pessimistic Quantile Q-value Approximation:** Instead of using Clipped Double Q-learning (CDQ) which takes the minimum of two Q-value estimates to mitigate overestimation, BRO uses the ensemble mean for Q-value calculation. This approach, combined with network regularization, is stated to unlock performance improvements without value overestimation. Quantile Q-values (Dabney et al., 2018; Moskovitz et al., 2021) are used for value representation, which also enables the estimation of epistemic uncertainty for optimistic exploration.

#### 3.2. Experimental Setup

- **Baselines:** BRO is compared against a range of contemporary and standard RL algorithms:
 - TD-MPC2 (Hansen et al., 2023): A state-of-the-art model-based method.
 - SR-SAC (D’Oro et al., 2022): A sample-efficient SAC implementation.
 - CrossQ (Bhatt et al., 2023): A compute-efficient method.
 - Standard SAC (Haarnoja et al., 2018) and TD3 (Fujimoto et al., 2018).
- **Environments:** The evaluation encompasses 40 diverse and complex continuous control tasks from three benchmark suites:
 - DeepMind Control Suite (Tassa et al., 2018): 15 tasks, including challenging locomotion (e.g., Dog, Humanoid).
 - MetaWorld (Yu et al., 2020): 15 manipulation tasks.
 - MyoSuite (Caggiano et al., 2022): 10 tasks involving physiologically accurate musculoskeletal motor control.
 - Tasks feature high-dimensional state and action spaces (up to 223 state dimensions, 39 action dimensions) and sparse rewards.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Algorithms are run for 1 million environment steps (unless specified otherwise). Performance is reported using interquartile means (IQM) and 95% confidence intervals, calculated using the RLiable package (Agarwal et al., 2021), based on 10 random seeds (except for TD-MPC2, where published results were used).
- **Computational Details:** All algorithms are implemented using JaxRL (Kostrikov, 2021). Hyperparameter selection for baselines is detailed in Appendix D.
- **Ablation Studies:** An extensive empirical analysis, involving training over 15,000 agents, was conducted to understand the impact of various design choices. This included examining the interaction between critic capacity, replay ratio, batch size, target networks, and the specific components of BRO (e.g., removing weight decay, optimistic exploration, or non-pessimistic Q-values). The study also included testing what did not work, such as N-step returns, categorical RL, and various scheduling mechanisms.
- **Generalizability Test:** Additional experiments were conducted to test the effectiveness of BroNet in offline RL settings (AntMaze and Adroit benchmarks) with Behavioral Cloning (BC), Implicit Q-Learning (IQL), and online RL with offline data.

### 4. Main findings and results

The research presents several key findings and results, primarily centered on the efficacy of scaling and architectural design in continuous reinforcement learning:

- **BRO Achieves State-of-the-Art Performance:** The BRO algorithm consistently outperforms existing model-free and model-based algorithms across 40 complex tasks from the DeepMind Control, MetaWorld, and MyoSuite benchmarks.
 - It achieves near-optimal policies in the challenging Dog and Humanoid tasks, a significant milestone for a model-free algorithm, within 1 million environment steps.
 - BRO is 2.5 times more sample-efficient than the leading model-based algorithm, TD-MPC2, in these tasks (Figure 8).
 - BRO achieves over 90% success rates in MetaWorld and MyoSuite, and over 85% of maximal returns in DeepMind Control Suite, within 1M steps.
- **Scaling Critic Networks is Effective and Efficient:**
 - Skillful scaling of the critic model, combined with simple algorithmic improvements, leads to extremely sample-efficient performance (Figure 6).
 - Scaling critic model size demonstrates a stronger impact on performance and is more computationally efficient (due to parallelization) compared to increasing the replay ratio (Figure 6). For instance, a 26M parameter model with RR=1 performs significantly better than a small model with RR=15, while requiring three times less wall-clock time.
 - BRO (Fast), with RR=2 and a 5M network, provides an effective trade-off between sample efficiency and computational speed, matching the sample efficiency of TD-MPC2 and outperforming SAC significantly in wall-clock time.
- **BroNet Architecture is Crucial for Scaling:**
 - The proposed BroNet architecture, which incorporates Layer Normalization after each dense layer and utilizes ResNet blocks, scales more effectively than vanilla dense networks or spectral normalization ResNets (Figure 4).
 - Naive scaling of critic capacity with traditional architectures can degrade performance, highlighting the importance of BroNet's regularization strategies for stable training with larger models (Figure 4, Table 1).
 - In complex environments like the Dog tasks, BroNet significantly outperforms other architectures, maintaining stable gradient norms, Q-values, and TD errors (Table 1).
- **Optimistic Exploration and Non-Pessimistic Q-values:**
 - Deactivating Clipped Double Q-learning (CDQ) and using the ensemble mean for Q-value calculation, rather than the minimum, in conjunction with network regularization, leads to significant performance improvements without increasing value overestimation (Figure 7, Figure 9a). This challenges conventional wisdom in RL.
 - Dual actor optimistic exploration, based on a Q-value upper-bound derived from epistemic uncertainty, contributes to about a 10% performance improvement, particularly in early training stages and for smaller models (Figure 7, Figure 9a).
- **Diminishing Returns of Algorithmic Improvements with Scale:** The impact of certain algorithmic improvements (e.g., smaller batch sizes, quantile Q-values, optimistic exploration) diminishes as the critic model size increases. For the largest 26.3M parameter models, these techniques offer marginal to no further performance gains, suggesting that overparameterization can partially substitute for specific inductive biases (Figure 9a).
- **Other Design Choices:**
 - Full-parameter resets are consistently beneficial for all model sizes.
 - Scaling actor networks is not effective and can sometimes be detrimental (Figure 9a), indicating that prioritizing critic capacity is more advantageous.
 - Target networks provide small but noticeable performance benefits, though their impact varies across tasks and benchmarks (Figure 9b).
 - A smaller batch size of 128 (compared to 256) offers computational benefits with marginal performance impact (Figure 15).
- **Generalizability of BroNet:** The BroNet architecture, when applied naively, showed performance improvements in diverse offline reinforcement learning benchmarks (AntMaze, Adroit) across various algorithms like Behavioral Cloning (BC), Implicit Q-Learning (IQL), and online reinforcement learning with offline data (Figure 11).
- **Ineffective Approaches:** Several techniques typically used in other RL contexts (N-step returns, categorical/HLGauss Q-value representations, various scheduling mechanisms) did not yield improvements in the continuous control settings tested.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

This work presents a significant contribution to the field of continuous Reinforcement Learning by demonstrating that model scaling can be a primary driver of sample efficiency and performance, challenging the traditional focus solely on algorithmic enhancements.

The **significance** of this research stems from several aspects:
- **New Paradigm for Sample Efficiency:** It establishes that judiciously scaling and regularizing the critic network, rather than solely focusing on complex algorithmic changes, can lead to substantial improvements in sample efficiency for continuous control. This suggests a potential shift in research priorities for future RL algorithm development.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** The BRO algorithm sets a new performance benchmark for model-free continuous control, particularly in complex and high-dimensional tasks such as DeepMind Control's Dog and Humanoid, which were previously largely unsolved by model-free methods within standard training limits.
- **Architectural Insights:** The introduction of the BroNet architecture, incorporating Layer Normalization and ResNet blocks, provides a blueprint for designing critic networks that can scale effectively and stably. This addresses the common issue of performance degradation with naive model scaling in RL.
- **Challenging Conventional Wisdom:** The finding that a non-pessimistic Q-value approximation (disabling CDQ) can be effective when combined with strong regularization challenges established practices in addressing Q-value overestimation, suggesting that regularization can mitigate issues previously addressed by pessimism.
- **Understanding Scaling Trade-offs:** The empirical analysis highlights that scaling network parameters can be more computationally efficient in parallelized setups than increasing the replay ratio, providing practical guidance for practitioners aiming to optimize performance and compute.
- **Generalizability:** The observed effectiveness of the BroNet architecture beyond online continuous control, specifically in offline RL settings, suggests its potential as a broadly applicable backbone for various RL problems.

The **potential impact** of this work is multifaceted:
- **Accelerated Research:** By providing a new state-of-the-art and a clear direction for scaling, it could accelerate the development of more capable and generalizable RL agents.
- **Redefined Benchmarks:** The success of BRO in solving many previously hard tasks within 1 million environment steps suggests that current benchmarks may not fully challenge the capabilities of modern algorithms. This could drive the community towards developing new, more demanding standardized benchmarks that accurately reflect contemporary algorithmic advancements and push the boundaries of sample efficiency.
- **Practical Applications:** More sample-efficient and robust continuous control algorithms like BRO could lead to advancements in robotics, autonomous systems, and other domains requiring real-world interaction, where data collection is often costly or time-consuming.
- **Algorithm Simplification:** The insight that increased critic capacity can reduce the need for certain domain-specific inductive biases might lead to simpler, more unified RL algorithms in the future.
- **Future Research Directions:** The paper explicitly outlines future work, including exploring optimization techniques for larger models (quantization, distillation), extending BRO's effectiveness to discrete control, and investigating the interplay between critic scaling and state encoders in image-based RL, thereby guiding subsequent research efforts.
