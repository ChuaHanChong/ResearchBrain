# Sim-and-Real Co-Training: A Simple Recipe for Vision-Based Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper systematically investigates sim-and-real co-training for vision-based robotic manipulation, demonstrating that policies trained on a blend of real and synthetic data achieve significantly improved real-world task performance and generalization to novel objects and positions. The work provides a practical "simple recipe" for effectively integrating diverse simulation data, even with notable discrepancies from reality, across different robot platforms.

## Problem

- Training generalist robot models requires vast amounts of diverse data, which is difficult and expensive to collect in the real world.
- Policies trained solely in simulation often suffer from the "reality gap," hindering direct transfer to physical robots and necessitating extensive human effort for traditional sim-to-real transfer methods.
- A systematic understanding of how to effectively use sim-and-real co-training, including ideal data mixtures and tolerance for simulator-real-world discrepancies, was lacking.

## Method

- The authors propose a "simple recipe" for vision-based robotic manipulation by co-training visuomotor policies via behavioral cloning on a weighted mixture of real-world and synthetic simulation data.
- They differentiate between and utilize two types of simulation data: large-scale, task-agnostic "Prior" data and task-aware "Digital Cousin" data, which is semantically aligned and scalably generated.
- The study systematically investigates the impact of data sources, quantities, co-training ratios, and camera alignment across two distinct robot domains (Panda arm, Fourier GR-1 humanoid).

## Results

- Co-trained policies achieved an average success rate improvement of **38%** over real-only policies across two robot domains and six diverse manipulation tasks.
- Co-training with simulation data significantly enhanced policy robustness to novel object entities and unseen initial positions (e.g., **10%** vs. **80%** success for Humanoid CupPnP on novel objects).
- The benefits of co-training persist even in data-rich real-world settings, with co-trained policies consistently outperforming real-only policies as the number of real demonstrations increased.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sim-and-real co-training consistently and substantially improves real-world task performance and generalization, challenging the conventional need for ultra-high fidelity sim-to-real alignment.
> - Both task-aware "Digital Cousin" data (semantically aligned) and even task-agnostic "Prior" simulation data (pre-existing, loosely aligned) are highly effective in co-training.
> - Sufficient volume of simulation data, tuning the co-training ratio, and approximating camera viewpoint alignment are critical factors for maximizing co-training benefits.
