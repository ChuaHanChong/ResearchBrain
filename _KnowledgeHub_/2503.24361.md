# Sim-and-Real Co-Training: A Simple Recipe for Vision-Based Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper systematically investigates sim-and-real co-training for vision-based robotic manipulation, demonstrating that policies trained on a blend of real and synthetic data achieve significantly improved real-world task performance and generalization to novel objects and positions. The work provides a practical "simple recipe" for effectively integrating diverse simulation data, even with notable discrepancies from reality, across different robot platforms.

## Problem

- Training generalist robot models requires vast amounts of diverse data, which is difficult and expensive to collect in the real world.
- Policies trained solely in simulation often suffer from the "reality gap," hindering direct transfer to physical robots and necessitating extensive human effort for traditional sim-to-real transfer methods.
- A systematic understanding of how to effectively use sim-and-real co-training, including ideal data mixtures and tolerance for simulator-real-world discrepancies, was lacking.

## Method

- The authors propose a "simple recipe" for vision-based robotic manipulation by co-training visuomotor policies via behavioral cloning on a weighted mixture of real-world and synthetic simulation data.
- They differentiate between and utilize two types of simulation data: large-scale, task-agnostic "Prior" data and task-aware "Digital Cousin" data, which is semantically aligned and scalably generated.
- The study systematically investigates the impact of data sources, quantities, co-training ratios, and camera alignment across two distinct robot domains (Panda arm, Fourier GR-1 humanoid).

## Results

- Co-trained policies achieved an average success rate improvement of **38%** over real-only policies across two robot domains and six diverse manipulation tasks.
- Co-training with simulation data significantly enhanced policy robustness to novel object entities and unseen initial positions (e.g., **10%** vs. **80%** success for Humanoid CupPnP on novel objects).
- The benefits of co-training persist even in data-rich real-world settings, with co-trained policies consistently outperforming real-only policies as the number of real demonstrations increased.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sim-and-real co-training consistently and substantially improves real-world task performance and generalization, challenging the conventional need for ultra-high fidelity sim-to-real alignment.
> - Both task-aware "Digital Cousin" data (semantically aligned) and even task-agnostic "Prior" simulation data (pre-existing, loosely aligned) are highly effective in co-training.
> - Sufficient volume of simulation data, tuning the co-training ratio, and approximating camera viewpoint alignment are critical factors for maximizing co-training benefits.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a central challenge in modern robotics: the “data hunger” of increasingly complex robot learning models, especially those aiming for general-purpose manipulation capabilities. The field is rapidly moving towards "generalist robot models" and "robot foundation models"-, which necessitate vast amounts of diverse training data to achieve broad generalization across tasks and environments.

Historically, learning robot manipulation skills relied heavily on real-world data, often collected through costly and time-consuming human teleoperation. While efforts like RT-1 and DROID have pushed the boundaries of real-world data collection, scaling this approach sufficiently for truly generalist systems remains a significant bottleneck due to practical constraints and safety concerns.

Simulation has emerged as a promising alternative, offering the ability to generate virtually infinite data with ease. However, policies trained solely in simulation often suffer from the "reality gap"—discrepancies between simulated and real-world physics, visuals, and dynamics. Traditional approaches to bridge this gap, such as "sim-to-real transfer," involve:
- **Domain Randomization (DR):** Varying simulation parameters (e.g., textures, lighting, physics) to make the policy robust to real-world variations-. This often requires meticulous tuning of randomization ranges.
- **System Identification and Digital Twins:** Precisely modeling real-world physics and visuals in simulation-,-. This demands significant human effort and can be difficult to scale across diverse scenarios.

Recent preliminary studies, have explored "sim-and-real co-training" as a compelling alternative. This strategy involves training policies on a mixture of both simulation and real-world datasets. The key advantage here is that co-training may be more robust to the reality gap, potentially requiring less precise alignment between simulation and reality compared to direct sim-to-real transfer.

This paper directly contributes to this burgeoning area by providing the first systematic and comprehensive study of sim-and-real co-training. It moves beyond preliminary findings to offer actionable insights into *what it takes* to effectively leverage simulation data. By comparing different types of simulation data (task-aware "digital cousins" vs. task-agnostic "prior data") and investigating critical data composition factors, the work seeks to formalize a "simple recipe" for practitioners. This positions the paper as a foundational guide for integrating synthetic data into the training pipelines of future generalist robot models, aiming to unlock their full potential by providing a scalable and practical data augmentation strategy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind this research stems from the acknowledged challenges in acquiring sufficient, diverse data for training robust and generalizable vision-based robotic manipulation policies. While large real-world robot datasets are invaluable, their collection is expensive, time-consuming, and resource-intensive, making it difficult to scale adequately for future generalist robot models. Simulation offers a promising solution due to its capacity for scalable data generation, especially with advancements in generative AI and automated data generation tools. However, the pervasive "reality gap" hinders the direct transfer of policies trained exclusively in simulation to the real world, typically necessitating substantial human effort for alignment.

Against this backdrop, the paper identifies a critical gap in the current understanding of sim-and-real co-training. Although preliminary studies have shown its potential to improve performance over training solely on limited real-world data, a systematic understanding of this strategy is lacking. Specifically, it remains unclear:
- How significant can the differences be between simulation and real-world data for co-training to still be effective?
- What kinds of dataset mixtures and compositions are ideal for maximizing the benefits of simulation data?

Therefore, the **key objectives** of this work are to:
1. **Systematically Investigate Co-training:** Conduct comprehensive experiments to validate the sim-and-real co-training strategy across various simulation and real-world datasets.
2. **Derive a Simple Recipe:** Based on empirical findings, establish a practical and effective set of recommendations for utilizing simulation data to solve vision-based robotic manipulation tasks in the real world.
3. **Quantify Benefits:** Empirically demonstrate how simulation data, even with notable discrepancies, can enhance real-world task performance and policy generalization.
4. **Identify Critical Factors:** Uncover which data composition factors (e.g., task, scene, object similarity, camera parameters, initialization distributions) are most crucial for successful co-training, and to what extent alignment is required.

In essence, the motivation is to bridge the data scarcity problem in real-world robotics by providing a practical, data-driven approach that leverages the abundance of synthetic data while minimizing the extensive human effort traditionally associated with sim-to-real transfer. This aims to accelerate the development and deployment of more capable and generalizable robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a "simple recipe" for vision-based robotic manipulation by systematically studying the effects of co-training policies on a mixture of real-world and synthetic simulation data.

**Core Technical Approach: Co-Training via Behavioral Cloning**
The central methodology involves training a visuomotor policy $\pi_\theta$ using behavioral cloning on a combined dataset of real-world demonstrations ($D_{real}$) and simulation demonstrations ($D_{sim}$). The training objective is to minimize a weighted behavioral cloning action loss:
$L_{total}(\theta; D_{real}, D_{sim}) = \alpha \cdot L(\theta; D_{sim}) + (1-\alpha) \cdot L(\theta; D_{real})$
where $\alpha \in$ is the co-training ratio, representing the probability of sampling from the simulation dataset in each training batch. This weighted sampling strategy allows for flexible control over the relative influence of simulation and real data, which the paper empirically investigates as a crucial hyperparameter. The policy architecture used is a Diffusion Policy implementation, taking RGB images and robot proprioception as input and outputting action sequences.

**Data Sources and Their Composition:**
The study meticulously distinguishes and evaluates three types of data:
1. **Real-World Data (Real):** Limited human demonstrations collected for specific target tasks on physical robots. For the Panda Kitchen domain, 50 demonstrations were collected for each of three tasks (CounterToSinkPnP, CounterToCabPnP, CloseDoor). For the Humanoid Tabletop domain, 20 demonstrations were collected per task (CupPnP, MilkPnP, Pouring). These datasets represent the baseline for real-world learning.
2. **Task-Agnostic Prior Simulation Data (Prior):** Large-scale, pre-existing simulation datasets not specifically designed for the target real-world tasks.
 - **Panda Kitchen:** Utilizes a subset (60k demonstrations across 20 tasks) of the RoboCasa dataset, which focuses on kitchen environments. A key processing step was re-rendering simulation demonstrations to *approximately* match the camera poses of the real-world setup, but no other changes were made, allowing for evaluation of out-of-the-box utility despite discrepancies in object categories, robot configurations, and physical parameters.
 - **Humanoid Tabletop:** A custom prior dataset of 10k demonstrations across 10 single-arm pick-and-place tasks in RoboCasa, also featuring significant discrepancies from real-world tasks and objects.
3. **Task-Aware Digital Cousin Data (DC):** Simulation datasets explicitly created to align semantically with the real-world target tasks. The term "digital cousin" implies proximity but not perfect identity with real-world counterparts.
 - **Alignment Strategy:** Efforts were made to align four key elements: same robot and action space, same task goal/success check, same object categories (though instances could differ), and same environmental fixture categories.
 - **Specific Alignments:** For Panda Kitchen, initial robot states, object categories, and object initialization distributions were adjusted to better match real-world settings. For Humanoid Tabletop, random object selection from the same category as real, and alignment of robot initial pose and camera position were performed.
 - **Scalable Generation:** Leveraging automated synthetic data generation tools, MimicGen for Panda and DexMimicGen for humanoid, to synthesize large quantities of trajectories (e.g., 10k per task for Panda DC from 100 human source demos; 1k per task for Humanoid DC from 10 human source demos), orders of magnitude more than real data.

**Experimental Design and Evaluation:**
The study conducts a series of experiments across two distinct robotic domains (Panda arm in a kitchen, Fourier GR-1 humanoid on a tabletop) and diverse manipulation tasks.
- **Performance Metrics:** Success rate on real-world tasks, evaluated across multiple trials for different data mixtures.
- **Systematic Investigations:**
 - Comparing performance of policies co-trained with various combinations of Real, Prior, and DC data.
 - Evaluating generalization capabilities to novel objects and novel initial positions in the real world.
 - Analyzing the impact of the quantity of real and simulation demonstrations.
 - Studying the effect of the co-training ratio ($\alpha$).
 - Assessing the importance of camera alignment in DC environments.
 - Exploring the effectiveness of co-training in data-rich settings (multi-task humanoid scenario).

This methodical approach, combining diverse data sources, automated generation techniques, and controlled experimentation, allows the authors to systematically derive actionable insights into effective sim-and-real co-training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The comprehensive empirical study yields several significant findings that inform the proposed "simple recipe" for sim-and-real co-training:

1. **Effectiveness of Sim-and-Real Co-Training (Table I):**
 - **Universal Benefit:** Co-training with simulation data consistently and substantially improves real-world task performance compared to training on real-world data alone. Across two robot domains and six diverse tasks, co-trained policies achieved an average success rate improvement of **38%** over real-only policies.
 - **Task-Aware Digital Cousins (DC) are Highly Effective:** Policies co-trained with DC data (which involve semantic and approximate visual alignment) showed a 35.8% higher average success rate than real-only policies. This highlights the strong benefits of even loosely aligned simulation environments.
 - **Task-Agnostic Prior Data is Surprisingly Useful:** Even without explicit alignment efforts (beyond camera re-rendering), co-training with large-scale, task-agnostic prior simulation data yielded significant benefits, improving average success rates by 31.5% compared to real-only training. This is a crucial finding, suggesting that readily available, diverse sim data can be leveraged out-of-the-box.
 - **Additive Benefits:** Combining all three data sources (Real + DC + Prior) generally yielded the best overall performance, demonstrating a synergistic effect.

2. **Generalization Beyond Real Demonstrations (Table II):**
 - **Novel Objects:** Co-training with simulation data significantly enhances a policy's robustness to novel object entities. For example, in the CounterToSinkPnP task, a real-only policy achieved 33% success on unseen objects, while the co-trained policy (Real + DC) achieved 50%. Similar gains were seen in the Humanoid CupPnP task (10% vs. 80%). This underscores that diverse simulation data aids generalization, a critical aspect for real-world deployment.
 - **Novel Initial Positions:** Simulation data also improves robustness to novel object placements. Policies co-trained with DC achieved twice the success rate on objects placed in unseen central positions compared to real-only policies trained on border/corner placements. This indicates that diverse spatial distributions in simulation help cover generalization gaps.

3. **Effectiveness in Data-Rich Settings (Figure 4):**
 - The benefits of sim-and-real co-training are not limited to low-data regimes. Even when the number of real-world demonstrations increases (e.g., up to 400 demos for the MultiTaskPnP task), co-trained policies consistently outperformed real-only policies. This suggests that synthetic data remains a valuable complement, even when more real data becomes available.

4. **Key Elements for Effective Co-Training:**
 - **Sufficient Simulation Data:** Decreasing the quantity of simulation demonstrations (e.g., from 10k to 500 for Panda or 1k to 100 for Humanoid) led to noticeable drops in success rates. This indicates that a large volume of simulation data is crucial.
 - **Tuning Co-Training Ratio ($\alpha$) (Figure 5):** The optimal ratio of simulation to real data is task-dependent and critical for performance. For instance, an $\alpha$ of 0.99 (99% simulation data) yielded the best results for the CupPnP task, but pushing it to 99.5% or 99.9% caused performance drops. This highlights the importance of careful hyperparameter tuning.
 - **Camera Alignment (Figure 6):** For task-aware digital cousins, approximating camera viewpoint alignment with the real-world setup is crucial. Misalignment resulted in significant performance drops (e.g., from 67% to 56% for Panda CounterToSinkPnP, and 95% to 70% for Humanoid CupPnP). However, perfect alignment is not necessary, as demonstrated by the success of non-perfectly aligned setups.

These findings collectively provide strong empirical evidence for the utility of co-training and actionable insights into the key factors for its successful implementation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers a significant contribution to the field of robot learning, particularly for the development of generalist robot models, by providing a validated and actionable strategy for integrating synthetic data. The "simple recipe" derived from extensive experimentation has several profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Practical Guidelines for Practitioners:** The paper addresses a critical need in robot learning for concrete, empirically-backed recommendations on how to effectively use simulation data. The distilled recipe—covering task and scene composition, object diversity, initialization distributions, camera alignment, and hyperparameter tuning—provides practical guidance, significantly lowering the barrier to entry for leveraging synthetic data. This can accelerate research and development in robotics, making it easier for labs and companies to train more capable policies without needing to collect prohibitively large real-world datasets.

2. **Scalable Data Augmentation:** By demonstrating that large-scale simulation data can effectively complement smaller real-world datasets (even in data-rich settings), the work provides a scalable solution to the "data hunger" problem. The ability to generate orders of magnitude more diverse data in simulation using tools like MimicGen and DexMimicGen, and integrate it effectively, is crucial for training complex models that aim for broad generalization. This reduces the dependency on costly and time-consuming real-world data collection campaigns.

3. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** A key finding is that simulation data, especially when diverse, significantly improves the policy's ability to generalize to novel objects and unseen initial positions in the real world. This is a vital step towards building truly robust and adaptable robotic systems that can perform reliably in varied real-world environments, rather than being confined to specific, trained scenarios. This pushes the frontier of robot capabilities beyond "laboratory conditions."

4. **Rethinking the Reality Gap:** The study challenges the conventional wisdom that ultra-high fidelity or perfect alignment between simulation and reality is always necessary for successful sim-to-real transfer. It demonstrates that effective co-training can occur even with "notable differences" and imperfect alignment (e.g., task-agnostic prior data, non-perfectly aligned digital cousins). This paradigm shift can reduce the immense human effort typically spent on meticulous simulation tuning and system identification, making simulation a more accessible and versatile tool.

5. **Foundation for Future Robot Models:** The insights gained from this work directly support the ongoing development of robot foundation models and generalist policies. By showing how to systematically blend real and synthetic data, the paper lays a groundwork for building models that benefit from the best of both worlds: the fidelity of real-world experience and the scalability and diversity of simulation.

**Limitations and Future Directions:** While the recipe is robust for pick-and-place and articulated object manipulation, the authors acknowledge limitations in applying it to tasks involving deformable objects, liquids, or very high-precision insertion, where simulation accuracy remains a challenge. This opens avenues for future research, including exploring data generation from video generation models or world models to bridge these specific reality gaps. The exploration of improving visual realism with Vid2Vid models in the appendix further hints at the potential of generative AI to enhance synthetic data quality, a promising direction for future work.

In conclusion, "Sim-and-Real Co-Training" provides a critical, empirically-validated methodology that promises to significantly advance the development of robust, generalizable, and deployable robotic manipulation skills, making substantial progress towards the vision of generalist robots.
