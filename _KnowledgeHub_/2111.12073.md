# Multi-Person 3D Motion Prediction with Multi-Range Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> Multi-Range Transformers predicts multi-person 3D motion by combining a local-range Transformer encoder per individual with a global-range Transformer encoder over all persons, plus spatial positional encoding and adversarial training; a decoder queries both ranges to forecast trajectory and pose jointly, beating prior SOTA on long-term prediction and scaling to 15-person scenes with automatic interaction grouping.

## Problem

- Prior 3D motion prediction models mostly forecast each person in isolation, ignoring how nearby people's actions and behaviors depend on one another.
- Most prior work fixes the pose center and ignores global body trajectory, treating pose and trajectory as two separate problems.
- Fixed-length input windows used by prior RNN and convolutional predictors lose early interactive information and accumulate error over long-horizon rollouts.

## Method

- **Local-range Transformer encoder** densely encodes each individual's own motion offsets with ==DCT== and ==sinusoidal temporal positional embedding== to capture smooth individual dynamics.
- **Global-range Transformer encoder** sparsely encodes all N persons' motion jointly, computed once per scene, augmented by a proposed ==spatial positional encoding== that measures distance between query and other persons' tokens.
- **Transformer decoder** takes a person's current pose as query against concatenated local-global features, trained jointly with a ==Transformer motion discriminator== via reconstruction plus ==adversarial loss== and progressively growing input length.

## Results

- On CMU-Mocap, MuPoTS-3D and 3DPW, the method achieves lowest MPJPE at 1s/2s/3s horizons, e.g. root+pose error of **0.60**/**1.12**/**1.71** versus **0.97**/**1.73**/**2.62** for LTD on CMU-Mocap.
- Ablations show removing local-range, global-range, discriminator, or spatial positional encoding each degrades MPJPE, and progressively increasing input length beats fixed-length input, e.g. **0.96**/**1.57**/**2.18** versus **0.96**/**1.91**/**2.91** at 1s/2s/3s.
- A **3-layer** Transformer configuration is the sweet spot (**1.57** MPJPE at 2s) versus **1-layer** (**1.71**) and **9-layer** (**4.71**), and DCT ablation shows applying DCT beats no-DCT (**2.18** vs **3.47** at 3s).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Separating per-person dense local dynamics from a sparse, computed-once global social-interaction encoder is an efficient way to scale motion prediction to crowds without redundant computation.
> - Explicit spatial positional encoding based on pairwise distance lets attention implicitly discover social interaction groups, useful wherever agents must be clustered without labels.
> - Progressively growing the training/inference window instead of using fixed-length chunks reduces autoregressive error accumulation, a general fix for long-horizon sequence rollout drift.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The prediction of future human motion in 3D is a field of study with implications for various computer vision applications, including surveillance systems, collision avoidance in robotics, and autonomous vehicles. Prior research in 3D human motion prediction has predominantly focused on forecasting the movements of a single individual. Early approaches often utilized Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) or temporal convolutional networks (TCNs) to model motion sequences. Many of these methods, however, typically fix the pose center, thereby neglecting the global body trajectory, or operate solely on relative joint positions rather than absolute world coordinates. More recent efforts have begun to address the joint prediction of human pose and trajectory in a unified framework, sometimes incorporating scene context.

A parallel area of research has investigated multi-person trajectory prediction, often focusing on the global movements of individuals within a crowd to understand social interactions. Methods like Social LSTM and Social GAN have contributed to this domain by modeling interactions. However, these often deal with coarser representations, such as bounding box trajectories, rather than detailed 3D poses. A subset of recent work has started to explore more fine-grained multi-person pose and trajectory prediction, integrating contextual constraints to model human-human interactions more accurately.

The advent of Transformer models, initially prominent in natural language processing, has extended into computer vision, demonstrating efficacy in tasks ranging from image classification to modeling human motion. Transformers are particularly adept at capturing long-term dependencies, a characteristic relevant to predicting extended motion sequences. Previous applications of Transformers in human motion have focused on single-person contexts, using spatio-temporal attention or motion attention mechanisms.

This paper positions itself at the intersection of these fields by addressing the limitations of existing single-person prediction models and the coarser resolution of many multi-person trajectory prediction methods. It seeks to leverage the long-range modeling capabilities of Transformers to predict detailed 3D multi-person motion, explicitly accounting for individual movements, global trajectories, and complex social interactions simultaneously, and scaling to scenes with multiple individuals.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust framework for multi-person 3D motion trajectory prediction that can forecast detailed future movements for multiple interacting individuals. The authors identify a fundamental limitation in much of the preceding work: the isolated prediction of individual human pose trajectories. Their key motivation stems from the observation that human actions and behaviors are often highly interdependent, especially within a social context where individuals' movements are influenced by the presence and anticipated actions of others. Therefore, a model that predicts motion in isolation is inherently incomplete for social scenarios.

To address this, the authors aimed for a model possessing the following properties:
- **Individual Motion Understanding:** The model should accurately comprehend each person's historical movements to generate future trajectories that are smooth, natural, and physically plausible for the individual. This ensures that the generated motion maintains anatomical correctness and typical human dynamics.
- **Social Interaction Modeling:** Within a group of individuals, the model must discern which agents are interacting and learn to predict their future behaviors based on these dynamic social relationships. This goes beyond simple collision avoidance to capture more nuanced social cues and coordinated actions.
- **Long-Term Prediction Capability:** The model needs to perform predictions over extended future time horizons (e.g., several seconds), which is critical for real-world applications requiring proactive planning and reaction. Existing models often struggle with error accumulation and generating repetitive or "freezing" motions over longer durations.

The motivation for employing Transformer architectures is rooted in their demonstrated effectiveness in modeling long-range dependencies, which is pertinent for both long-term temporal motion prediction and complex spatial interactions among multiple agents. The authors specifically sought to create a model that could not only achieve high accuracy in these prediction tasks but also qualitatively generate diverse and realistic social interactions, and scale to scenarios involving a large number of people without requiring explicit group definitions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces a Multi-Range Transformers framework designed for multi-person 3D motion trajectory prediction. The framework comprises a predictor network (P) and a discriminator network (D), both built upon Transformer architectures.

#### 3.1. Motion Representation

The input to the model consists of `k` time steps of history motion for `N` persons. Each person `n` at time step `k` is represented by a vector `x^n_k ∈ R^{3J}`, which contains the Cartesian coordinates of `J` skeleton joints. Unlike many prior works that center poses at the origin, this method uses absolute joint positions in the world coordinate system, thereby incorporating both pose and global trajectory information. The model predicts future motion as `X^n_{k+1:T}`. Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) is applied to encoder inputs for a compact, smooth representation of motion offsets, and Inverse Discrete Cosine Transform (IDCT) is used for decoder outputs.

#### 3.2. Network Architecture Overview

The predictor network (P) consists of two Transformer-based encoders and one Transformer-based decoder:
- **Local-range Transformer Encoder:** Processes each person's motion individually.
- **Global-range Transformer Encoder:** Models social interactions among all persons in the scene.
- **Transformer Decoder:** Generates the future motion trajectory for each person.

The discriminator network (D) is a Transformer-based classifier that evaluates the naturalness of the generated motion.

#### 3.3. Local-range Transformer Encoder

This encoder focuses on individual motion trajectories to ensure smoothness and naturalness. For each person, it processes the history motion represented as offsets `Δx = x_{i+1} - x_i`. These offsets undergo DCT, a linear layer, and are then combined with sinusoidal positional embeddings to form local motion embeddings (`l_{1:k}`). These embeddings are fed as tokens (`E_loc`) to a stack of `L` Transformer encoder layers. Each layer consists of a multi-head self-attention mechanism, followed by residual connections, layer normalization, and a feed-forward network. The output is a local motion feature (`e_{1:k}`) for each person.

#### 3.4. Global-range Transformer Encoder

This encoder is designed to capture interactions among all `N` persons in the scene. It operates on the absolute joint positions `x^n_{1:k}` for all persons. These are passed through a linear layer and combined with sinusoidal positional embeddings to create global motion embeddings (`g^{1:N}_{1:k}`). These embeddings, forming `E_glob`, are processed by `L` Transformer encoder layers, similar to the local-range encoder, but applied across all persons and time steps. The output (`o^{1:N}_{1:k}`) is then augmented with a Spatial Positional Encoding (SPE) to produce the global motion feature (`f^{1:N}_{1:k}`). This encoder's computation is performed once for the entire scene.

#### 3.5. Positional Encoding

- **Temporal Positional Encoding:** Standard sinusoidal positional embeddings are applied to inputs of both encoders to inject temporal order information.
- **Spatial Positional Encoding (SPE):** A novel technique applied to the outputs of the global-range Transformer encoder. `SPE(x^n_t, x_k)` explicitly calculates the spatial distance between the `k`-th time step pose of the query person (`x_k`) and the `t`-th time step pose of any person `n` (`x^n_t`). This exponential decay function is designed to provide explicit spatial context, facilitating the clustering of persons into interaction groups. The SPE value is added to the global encoder output (`o^n_t`) to derive `f^{1:N}_{1:k}`.

#### 3.6. Transformer Decoder

The decoder takes the concatenated local and global motion features (`H = [e_{1:k}, f^{1:N}_{1:k}]^T`) along with a single static human pose `x_k` (the last observed pose of the person whose motion is being predicted) as input. The static pose `x_k` is used as the query for the multi-head attention mechanism, while `H` provides the keys and values. This design choice, using a single pose query, creates a bottleneck intended to force the Transformer to learn complex relations within the encoders rather than merely repeating past motion. The decoder consists of `L` Transformer layers, culminating in two fully connected layers followed by IDCT. It outputs an offset motion sequence (`[Δx_k, ..., Δx_{T-1}]`), which is then converted into the future 3D motion trajectory (`x_{k+1:T}`). This direct output of a sequence of offsets helps prevent the generation of freezing motions.

#### 3.7. Motion Discriminator

A Transformer-based discriminator (D) is employed to promote the generation of natural and continuous long-term motions. It takes the predicted future motion (`x_{k+1:T}`) as input, processes it through a Transformer encoder (similar to the local-range encoder), and then uses two fully connected layers to classify whether the motion is "real" (from ground truth) or "fake" (generated by the predictor). The discriminator is trained adversarially alongside the predictor.

#### 3.8. Training and Inference Strategy

- **Loss Functions:** The predictor `P` is trained using a combination of a reconstruction loss (`L_rec`, L2 norm of `Δx` offsets) and an adversarial loss (`L_adv`). The discriminator `D` is trained to distinguish between real and generated motions.
- **Progressive Input Length:** An efficient strategy is adopted where the input sequence length progressively increases during training and inference. In training, `x_{1:k}` predicts `x_{k+1:2k}`, then `x_{1:2k}` predicts `x_{2k+1:3k}`, and so on. During inference, this translates to an auto-aggressive approach where `x_{1:k}` predicts `x_{k+1:2k}`, and then the original `x_{1:k}` *plus* the predicted `x_{k+1:2k}` are used as input to predict the subsequent segment (`x_{2k+1:3k}`). This method aims to reduce error accumulation by continuously providing earlier motion history as context.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance

The proposed Multi-Range Transformers model demonstrated competitive performance across various datasets and prediction horizons.
- **MPJPE (Mean Per Joint Position Error):** The model achieved significant improvements in MPJPE without aligning (reflecting both trajectory and pose error) across CMU-Mocap, MuPoTS-3D, 3DPW, and custom mixed datasets (Mix1, Mix2). Specifically, it showed up to 20% improvement compared to prior single-person-based methods (LTD, HRI) and up to 30% improvement over multi-person methods (SocialPool). The performance gains were particularly notable for longer-term predictions (2 and 3 seconds).
- **Root and Pose Error:** The method consistently showed lower root joint position error and aligned pose error compared to baselines, indicating accurate prediction of both global trajectory and fine-grained body poses.
- **Movement Distribution:** An analysis of the L2 distance between the start and end skeleton joint positions in predictions revealed that the proposed method produced movements whose distribution was similar to ground truth. In contrast, baseline methods frequently predicted motions with less overall movement, often resulting in poses that remained in approximately the same spatial location or exhibited "freezing" behavior.
- **User Study:** In a user study conducted via Amazon Mechanical Turk, the model's predictions consistently received higher average scores for "naturalness" and exhibited lower standard deviations across all datasets, suggesting that users perceived the generated motions as more realistic and consistent.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Observations

Visualizations of predicted motions indicated that the Multi-Range Transformers model generated more natural and smoother trajectories that closely resembled real records.
- RNN-based methods like SocialPool quickly exhibited "freezing" motions.
- Other Transformer-based methods (LTD, HRI) sometimes showed "hysteresis," where predicted motion would temporarily revert to a past position before continuing, potentially due to their reliance on directly using past sequences or residual inputs. The proposed method, by querying with a static pose and predicting offsets, mitigated these issues.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Controlled experiments were conducted to evaluate the contribution of individual components:
- **Module Importance:** Removing the local-range encoder, global-range encoder, discriminator, or spatial positional encoding (SPE) individually led to performance degradation, confirming the utility of each component. The local-range encoder was particularly impactful in reducing prediction error. SPE was observed to be more beneficial in scenes with a larger number of people.
- **Decoder Query Length:** Using only a single pose (`x_k`) as the query for the decoder yielded better or comparable performance than using multiple frames or the entire sequence. This supported the hypothesis that a bottleneck query forces the encoders to learn richer, more exploitable representations of motion and interaction.
- **Number of Transformer Layers:** An optimal configuration of 3 Transformer layers was identified. Both fewer (1) and more (6 or 9) layers resulted in diminished performance, suggesting that 3 layers offered a suitable balance for the task.
- **Progressive vs. Fixed Length Inference:** The proposed progressive inference strategy, which continuously incorporates prior predicted steps alongside original history, significantly reduced error accumulation, especially for long-term predictions, compared to a fixed-length input approach.
- **Role of DCT:** The selective application of DCT to local-range encoder inputs and IDCT to decoder outputs proved more effective than applying DCT universally or omitting it entirely. This suggests that while DCT aids in representing smooth motion, the hybrid position-based representation is more suitable for modeling human interaction.

#### 4.4. Attention Mechanism Analysis

An analysis of the decoder's attention scores provided insights into the model's interaction understanding:
- **Dynamic Weighting:** The attention mechanism allowed different query persons to dynamically weigh the past motions of themselves and other individuals differently.
- **Interaction Grouping:** Crucially, persons belonging to the same social interaction group exhibited more similar attention score distributions when querying the global-range encoder outputs. This indicated that the model could implicitly and automatically cluster individuals into interaction groups without explicit labels.
- **Range Specificity:** Attention from the local-range encoder tended to show finer-grained variations, while attention from the global-range encoder demonstrated more consistent patterns within a head, aligning with the dense and sparse sampling principles of the respective encoders. The attention mechanism also indicated a focus on behavioral cues beyond mere spatial proximity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Multi-Range Transformers framework represents a technical advancement in the field of 3D human motion prediction, particularly for multi-person scenarios. By explicitly modeling both individual motion details and complex social interactions through specialized, range-specific Transformer encoders, and by employing a novel query mechanism in the decoder, the research addresses key limitations of prior work. The ability to predict long-term, diverse, and natural multi-person motions, even in crowded scenes (up to 15 persons), with high accuracy, is a notable achievement.

The significance of this work extends to several application areas:
- **Robotics and Autonomous Systems:** Improved motion prediction capabilities can enable more intelligent and safer human-robot interaction, as well as more robust collision avoidance systems for autonomous vehicles operating in environments with pedestrians.
- **Surveillance and Public Safety:** Enhanced predictive models could assist in anomaly detection, crowd behavior analysis, and proactive safety measures in public spaces.
- **Virtual Reality and Gaming:** Generating more realistic and socially aware character animations can improve immersion and interaction in virtual environments.
- **Human-Computer Interaction:** Understanding and anticipating human motion can lead to more intuitive and responsive interfaces.

However, the authors also acknowledge potential ethical considerations, particularly concerning privacy. While the model operates on skeleton data rather than image inputs, raising questions about whether individuals could be identified solely based on their motion patterns. This highlights a broader societal impact concern in human behavior modeling technologies. Despite these concerns, the immediate utility of this research for safety-critical systems offers a compelling potential positive impact. The explicit demonstration of automatic interaction grouping without labels also points to a broader understanding of social dynamics that could be beneficial for various analytical tasks.
