# SSAM: Self-Supervised Association Modeling for Test-Time Adaption

> [!summary] Summary
> The SSAM framework introduces a self-supervised approach for Test-Time Adaptation in Vision-Language Models by dynamically refining the image encoder with lightweight adapters. This method consistently improves model robustness and generalization, outperforming existing TTA methods on cross-domain and out-of-distribution benchmarks by enhancing visual feature discriminability and visual-textual alignment.

## Problem

- Existing Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) methods for Vision-Language Models (VLMs) largely freeze the image encoder, neglecting its crucial role in handling visual distribution shifts.
- Current TTA approaches primarily focus on text prompt tuning or cache-based adaptation, leaving the image encoder rigid in the face of dynamic visual inputs.
- Image encoders must contend with substantial distribution shifts (domain, style, perturbations) during inference, unlike text encoders which operate on relatively fixed semantic concepts.

## Method

- A lightweight, ==architecture-agnostic adapter module== is introduced to dynamically refine the image encoder during test-time, preserving pre-trained VLM knowledge.
- ==Soft Prototype Estimation (SPE)== establishes probabilistic associations between image features and category embeddings to derive robust, label-free visual prototypes.
- ==Prototype-anchored Image Reconstruction (PIR)== and a contrastive alignment loss provide self-supervised signals to optimize adapter parameters, guiding accurate categorical predictions and semantic consistency.

## Results

- SSAM achieved state-of-the-art average accuracy on cross-domain benchmarks, for instance, **64.43%** for SSAM_DPE with ResNet-50 and **72.27%** for SSAM_DMN-ZS with ViT-B/16, surpassing existing TTA methods.
- It demonstrated superior performance on out-of-distribution benchmarks, with SSAM_DPE reaching **50.96%** average accuracy on ResNet-50 and **66.26%** on ViT-B/16, indicating enhanced robustness.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the Prototype-anchored Image Reconstruction loss (L_PIR) and the contrastive alignment loss (L_ca) contribute positively and synergistically to the overall performance improvements.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Actively adapting the image encoder is a critical yet overlooked component for effective Test-Time Adaptation in Vision-Language Models.
> - Self-supervised learning, specifically via prototype-anchored reconstruction and contrastive alignment, can effectively guide image encoder adaptation without explicit test-time labels.
> - The proposed method enhances visual feature discriminability and improves visual-textual feature alignment, leading to more robust and generalized representations for unseen data.
