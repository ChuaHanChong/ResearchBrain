# Towards Accurate Generative Models of Video: A New Metric & Challenges

> [!summary] Summary
> A new metric, Fréchet Video Distance (FVD), and a suite of challenging benchmark datasets, StarCraft 2 Videos (SCV), are introduced to provide more accurate evaluation tools for deep generative video models. FVD demonstrates strong correlation with human perception of video quality, while current models show significant limitations on SCV tasks requiring relational reasoning and long-term consistency.

## Problem

- A lack of adequate evaluation metrics for video generative models, especially for unconditional generation, failing to capture temporal coherence or diversity.
- Scarcity of datasets that offer controllable complexity, suitable for pinpointing specific model weaknesses beyond simple synthetic or overly complex real-world data.

## Method

- Developed Fréchet Video Distance (FVD), a metric building on FID but utilizing a pre-trained Inflated 3D Convnet (I3D) for features to capture both spatial and temporal aspects of video distributions.
- Introduced StarCraft 2 Videos (SCV), a benchmark suite of four controllable scenarios from the StarCraft 2 Learning Environment, designed to test relational reasoning and long-term memory.
- Conducted extensive empirical validation for FVD, including a large-scale human study, to confirm its correlation with human judgment of video quality.

## Results

- Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) is highly sensitive to both static and temporal video perturbations and consistently outperforms PSNR and SSIM in correlating with human judgment of video quality.
- Current state-of-the-art video generation models fail on complex SCV scenarios, particularly those demanding relational reasoning (e.g., Collect Mineral Shards, Brawl) and long-term consistency (e.g., Road Trip with Medivac).
- Differences of 50 FVD points or more typically correspond to perceivable differences in video quality by human observers, providing a resolution guideline for the metric.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective evaluation of video generative models requires metrics that capture both spatial and temporal dynamics, as traditional image-based metrics are insufficient.
> - Current state-of-the-art video generative models struggle significantly with tasks requiring relational reasoning and maintaining long-term temporal consistency.
> - Intermediate complexity datasets, like SCV, are crucial for diagnosing specific failure modes in generative models, bridging the gap between simplistic and overly complex data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The broader research landscape for this paper is the rapidly evolving field of **deep generative models**, specifically their application to **video synthesis**.

Prior to this work, deep generative models, such as Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), had achieved remarkable success in synthesizing high-quality static images. These models demonstrated versatility across various image processing tasks, including super-resolution, image-to-image translation, and representation learning.

The natural and logical "next challenge" identified by the research community, and explicitly by this paper, is to extend these capabilities to **video**. Video generation is inherently more complex than image generation. Beyond merely synthesizing visually plausible frames, a video generative model must also accurately capture and reproduce **temporal dynamics**—how objects move, interact, and evolve over time, accounting for consistency and causality across a sequence of frames. This requires models to learn intricate dependencies that go beyond static pixel correlations.

Despite recent attempts and some progress, video generation models were, and largely still are, in their infancy at the time of this paper's publication (2018-2019). Existing models struggled to synthesize more than a few seconds of coherent video, often producing samples that clearly indicated a lack of a robust underlying dynamics model.

The authors identify two critical bottlenecks hindering progress in this challenging domain:
1. **Lack of Adequate Evaluation Metrics:** Traditional image-based metrics like Peak Signal to Noise Ratio (PSNR) and Structural Similarity (SSIM) are frame-level measures. They require a ground-truth video for comparison, focus primarily on pixel-wise or structural fidelity, and critically, fail to capture the temporal coherence, diversity, or overall distribution of generated videos. For unconditional video generation (where no ground truth exists), these metrics are entirely inapplicable. This made it difficult to objectively compare models, track progress, or even understand specific failure modes beyond subjective visual inspection.
2. **Scarcity of Challenging yet Controlled Datasets:** The existing datasets for video generation were either purely synthetic and simplistic (e.g., Moving MNIST, Stochastic Shapes) or overly complex real-world datasets (e.g., BAIR, Kinetics). The gap between these extremes meant that simple datasets didn't push models sufficiently, while complex real-world data made it hard to pinpoint *why* models failed (e.g., did they fail at capturing object physics, or merely at visual fidelity?). There was a need for datasets that offered controllable complexity and isolated specific challenges like relational reasoning or long-term memory.

This paper directly intervenes in this landscape by addressing these two crucial missing pieces. By proposing a new, more comprehensive metric (FVD) and a challenging, controllable benchmark dataset (SCV), it aims to provide the necessary tools for the community to accurately measure progress, identify weaknesses, and thereby accelerate the development of more sophisticated and capable video generative models. It shifts the focus, in part, from solely *building* models to *evaluating* them effectively, recognizing that robust evaluation is prerequisite for meaningful advancement.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind this research stems from the identified shortcomings in the evaluation of video generative models: the absence of suitable metrics and adequately challenging datasets. Without these, accurately measuring progress, comparing models, and understanding their failure modes becomes exceedingly difficult, thus impeding research.

The paper sets forth two primary, interconnected objectives to address these challenges:

1. **Develop a New, Comprehensive Metric for Video Generative Models:**
 - **Motivation:** Existing metrics like PSNR and SSIM are fundamentally designed for image quality assessment and are applied frame-by-frame. They require a ground-truth reference, fail to capture the temporal coherence of a video sequence, do not account for the diversity of generated samples (a crucial aspect of generative models), and are inapplicable for unconditional video generation tasks (like GANs). A new metric is needed that can holistically evaluate visual quality, temporal dynamics, and sample diversity.
 - **Objective:** To introduce the **Fréchet Video Distance (FVD)**, a metric that builds upon the success of Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) for images but is specifically adapted for videos. This adaptation involves using a feature representation that captures both spatial and temporal aspects of video content, and it should be capable of comparing distributions of videos, not just individual generated sequences against a ground truth.

2. **Introduce a Suite of Challenging, Controllable Benchmark Datasets for Video Generation:**
 - **Motivation:** The current spectrum of video datasets presents a dichotomy: either overly simplistic synthetic data (e.g., Moving MNIST) that doesn't push model capabilities, or highly complex real-world data (e.g., Kinetics) where isolating specific failure modes (like relational reasoning or long-term memory) is difficult due to myriad confounding factors. There's a need for an intermediate step—datasets that are visually rich enough to be challenging but conceptually simple and controllable enough to allow researchers to pinpoint model weaknesses.
 - **Objective:** To create **StarCraft 2 Videos (SCV)**, a benchmark suite derived from the StarCraft 2 Learning Environment. This dataset aims to specifically challenge generative models on tasks requiring relational reasoning and long-term memory, which are critical for understanding complex dynamic scenes, while offering scalability and control over scenario complexity.

In addition to these core objectives, the paper also aims to:
- **Validate FVD:** Conduct extensive empirical studies, including a large-scale human study, to confirm that FVD correlates well with qualitative human judgment of generated video quality. This is crucial for FVD to be adopted as a reliable proxy for human perception.
- **Provide Baselines:** Offer comprehensive benchmark results on the new SCV datasets (as well as existing ones like BAIR and KTH) using state-of-the-art video generation models. These baselines serve as a starting point for future research and highlight the current limitations of models on the proposed challenges.

Ultimately, the motivation is to accelerate research in video generation by providing the community with more appropriate and rigorous tools for evaluation and more targeted challenges for development.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper's methodology centers on the development and rigorous validation of FVD and the construction of the SCV benchmark.

#### 3.1. Fréchet Video Distance (FVD)

- **Foundation in Fréchet Distance:** FVD is based on the Fréchet Distance (or 2-Wasserstein distance), which measures the distance between two probability distributions. For multivariate Gaussians, this distance has a closed-form solution involving the means and covariance matrices of the distributions.
- **Feature Space Embedding:** Similar to FID (Fréchet Inception Distance) for images, FVD requires mapping raw video data into a meaningful feature space.
 - **Key Innovation:** Instead of using an image classification network (like Inception v3 for FID), FVD utilizes an **Inflated 3D Convnet (I3D)**. I3D generalizes the Inception architecture to operate on 3D data (spatial + temporal dimensions).
 - **Pre-training:** The I3D network is pre-trained on large-scale action recognition datasets (specifically, Kinetics-400 and Kinetics-600), which consist of human-centered YouTube videos. This pre-training is crucial because action recognition tasks inherently require the model to learn both visual content and its temporal evolution, making the learned features suitable for evaluating video quality and coherence.
 - **Feature Extraction:** Activations from the final pooling layer or the logits layer of the pre-trained I3D are extracted for both real and generated video samples.
 - **Metric Calculation:** Multivariate Gaussian distributions are fit to these extracted features for both the real data distribution ($P_R$) and the generative model's distribution ($P_G$). The Fréchet distance (Eq. 2 in the paper) is then computed using the estimated means and covariance matrices.
- **Alternatives Explored:** The authors also investigated **Kernel Video Distance (KVD)**, which uses Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) with a polynomial kernel on the I3D features, providing a non-parametric alternative to the Gaussian assumption of FVD. Traditional metrics like PSNR and SSIM were used for comparative analysis.

#### 3.2. StarCraft 2 Videos (SCV) Benchmark

- **Platform:** Leverages the open-source **StarCraft 2 Learning Environment (SC2LE)**, allowing for rendering game play from custom scenarios.
- **Design Principles:**
 - **Intermediate Complexity:** Aims to bridge the gap between simplistic synthetic datasets (e.g., Moving MNIST) and highly complex real-world datasets (e.g., Kinetics).
 - **Targeted Challenges:** Designed to specifically test capabilities like relational reasoning (how objects interact) and long-term memory (consistency over extended periods).
 - **Controllability:** Scenarios are hand-crafted with adjustable hyperparameters (e.g., number of units, map layout) to control complexity and allow for scaling.
 - **Conceptual Simplicity:** While visually complex, the underlying gameplay logic for each scenario is designed to be conceptually straightforward, making it easier to diagnose model failures (e.g., "did the model learn that a unit must be picked up before being dropped off?").
- **Four Scenarios:**
 1. **Move Unit to Border (MUtB):** A simple "unit test" for basic movement and animation, similar to Moving MNIST but visually richer.
 2. **Collect Mineral Shards (CMS):** Requires learning relationships (shard disappears when collected) and optimal pathfinding.
 3. **Brawl:** Highly complex, requiring modeling interactions between multiple entities with different properties (attack, health, animations).
 4. **Road Trip with Medivac (RTwM):** Focuses on long-term dependencies and consistency over extended time horizons (picking up, transporting, and dropping off specific units).
- **Data Generation:** An agent plays scenarios, and replays are saved. Videos are then rendered from these replays at different resolutions (64x64, 128x128), and various frame rates depending on scenario length.

#### 3.3. Experimental Validation

The methodology includes a rigorous set of experiments:

- **Noise Study:** Evaluated FVD's sensitivity to various forms of degradation by adding controlled static (black rectangle, Gaussian blur, Gaussian noise, salt & pepper) and temporal noise (local swap, global swap, interleaving, switching) to real videos. This tested whether FVD could detect subtle distortions.
- **Sample Size Analysis:** Investigated the stability and accuracy of FVD estimates as a function of the number of samples used, crucial for understanding the metric's reproducibility.
- **Large-Scale Human Evaluation:**
 - **Models:** Trained over 3000 variants of several state-of-the-art conditional video generation models (CDNA, SV2P, SVP-FP, SAVP) on the BAIR dataset.
 - **Protocol:** Human raters were presented with pairs of generated videos from different models and asked to judge which was better or if they were indistinguishable.
 - **Scenarios:** "One Metric Equal" (testing if FVD can distinguish models other metrics deem equal) and "One Metric Spread" (testing if FVD's ranking aligns with human ranking across a wide range of quality).
 - **Resolution of FVD:** A specific human study determined the minimum FVD difference that humans could consistently perceive.
- **Baseline Benchmarking:** Evaluated the performance of the chosen state-of-the-art models on BAIR, KTH, and all four SCV scenarios at different resolutions using FVD.
- **Correlation Analysis:** Quantified the statistical correlation between FVD and traditional metrics (SSIM, PSNR) across the large set of trained models.

This multi-faceted approach ensures that both the proposed metric and the new dataset are thoroughly tested and validated against human perception and existing benchmarks, demonstrating their utility and highlighting current research gaps.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study yielded several significant findings, validating FVD as a superior metric and demonstrating the challenging nature of the SCV dataset.

#### 4.1. FVD Performance and Validation

- **Sensitivity to Noise:** The noise study confirmed that FVD is highly sensitive to both **static (frame-level) and temporal perturbations** in videos. Unlike traditional image-based metrics (which struggle with temporal distortions), FVD consistently detected changes introduced by frame reordering, interleaving, or switching between videos. This validates FVD's ability to capture the crucial temporal coherence often missed by PSNR/SSIM.
- **Correlation with Human Judgment:** A large-scale human study, involving over 3000 generated video models, strongly confirmed FVD's correlation with human subjective quality judgments.
 - **Superiority:** FVD consistently outperformed SSIM and PSNR in terms of agreement with human raters. When FVD indicated models were equal, humans largely agreed. Conversely, when other metrics (SSIM, PSNR) failed to distinguish between models, FVD was often able to do so, aligning well with human perception (e.g., 74.9% and 81.0% agreement when SSIM and PSNR deemed models equal, respectively).
 - **Ranking Consistency:** FVD also proved better at inducing a ranking that humans agreed with across a spread of model qualities.
 - **KVD and Avg. FID Comparison:** KVD, while similar, generally performed slightly worse than FVD. A naive extension of FID (Avg. FID), which averages frame-level FID scores, performed markedly worse than FVD, underscoring the importance of capturing *video* features (spatial-temporal) rather than just averaging *image* features.
- **Perceivable Resolution:** The study on FVD resolution revealed that **differences of 50 FVD points or more typically correspond to perceivable differences** in video quality by human observers. Smaller differences might not be consistently distinguishable.
- **Sample Size Impact:** As expected, larger sample sizes lead to more accurate and stable FVD estimations, reducing estimation noise, a critical consideration for fair comparisons.

#### 4.2. StarCraft 2 Videos (SCV) Benchmark Results

- **Current Model Limitations:** State-of-the-art video generation models (CDNA, SV2P, SVP-FP, SAVP) demonstrated significant struggles on the SCV datasets, particularly the more complex scenarios (CMS, Brawl, RTwM).
 - **MUtB (Move Unit to Border):** Models showed moderate success at lower resolutions, but even this relatively simple scenario posed challenges at higher resolutions for weaker models, which sometimes failed to generate the moving unit clearly.
 - **CMS (Collect Mineral Shards):** Models struggled profoundly. While they learned that shards would disappear, they largely failed to accurately model the moving units themselves or capture the correct sequence of shard disappearances, indicating a lack of understanding of the underlying game logic and dynamics.
 - **Brawl:** This scenario proved extremely challenging. Models were unable to capture the multiple interacting game units, often resorting to generating blurry blobs instead of distinct entities and their complex animations and interactions.
 - **RTwM (Road Trip with Medivac):** Models largely failed at this task, unable to model the long-term dependencies and consistency required (e.g., picking up specific units and dropping *the same* units off later). The authors posit this is due to either the long video sequences or the inherent complexity of the scenario's logical consistency.
- **Failure Modes Identified:** The results confirm that current models frequently fail at **relational reasoning** (how entities interact, e.g., unit-shard interaction, unit-unit combat) and **long-term temporal consistency** (maintaining object identities, remembering past states, coherent progression over longer sequences).

#### 4.3. Correlation with Other Metrics

- **PSNR and SSIM:** As anticipated, PSNR and SSIM showed a very high positive correlation (Pearson's r=0.730, Kendall's τ=0.648) with each other, reinforcing that they measure similar aspects of frame-level quality.
- **FVD vs. SSIM:** A weaker, but still statistically significant, negative correlation was observed between FVD and SSIM (r=-0.640, τ=-0.189). This indicates that while SSIM does capture some of the defects FVD identifies, FVD is clearly measuring something distinct and more comprehensive. (A lower FVD value is better, hence the negative correlation with SSIM where higher is better).
- **FVD vs. PSNR:** The correlation between FVD and PSNR was notably weak (r=-0.278, τ=-0.007), suggesting that PSNR has very limited utility in assessing the complex aspects of video generation quality captured by FVD.

In summary, the findings strongly advocate for FVD as a robust, human-aligned metric for evaluating video generative models and highlight the significant remaining challenges in synthesizing complex, temporally coherent, and logically consistent videos.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The contributions of this paper hold substantial significance for the field of video generation and broader artificial intelligence research.

#### 5.1. Establishing a Standard for Video Evaluation

- **Robust and Objective Metric:** FVD provides the community with a much-needed, objective, and statistically sound metric for evaluating the quality of generated videos. Unlike previous frame-level metrics, FVD inherently considers the entire video distribution, capturing both visual fidelity and crucial temporal coherence, as well as diversity of samples.
- **Human Alignment:** The extensive human study unequivocally demonstrates that FVD aligns remarkably well with human perceptual judgment of video quality. This is paramount for any metric seeking to guide research, as human perception is the ultimate arbiter of "good" generated content. Researchers can now trust FVD as a reliable proxy for what humans would find realistic and plausible.
- **Applicability to Unconditional Generation:** A key advantage of FVD is its applicability to unconditional video generation (e.g., with GANs) where no ground-truth video exists for comparison. This opens up new avenues for evaluating models that generate videos entirely from noise, a critical area of research.
- **Accelerated Progress:** By offering a clearer, more consistent, and more informative way to measure progress, FVD will enable researchers to more efficiently compare different generative architectures, training methodologies, and hyperparameters. This precision in evaluation is expected to accelerate the development of more advanced video generative models.

#### 5.2. Guiding Future Research with Targeted Challenges

- **Bridging the Dataset Gap:** SCV successfully fills the critical gap between overly simplistic synthetic datasets and overwhelmingly complex real-world videos. It provides a "unit test" environment where specific challenges can be isolated and rigorously tested.
- **Highlighting Core Problems:** The benchmark results on SCV definitively demonstrate that current state-of-the-art models significantly struggle with fundamental aspects like relational reasoning (understanding how entities interact) and long-term memory/consistency. These are not merely visual fidelity issues but deeper challenges related to understanding underlying physics, logic, and narrative progression within a scene.
- **Directing Research Efforts:** By explicitly showcasing these weaknesses, SCV provides a clear roadmap for future research directions. Instead of broadly aiming for "better video generation," researchers can now focus on developing models specifically designed to handle complex multi-agent interactions, causality, and maintaining coherent states over extended time horizons. The controllable nature of SCV scenarios means researchers can systematically vary complexity and analyze how models break down.

#### 5.3. Broader Impact on AI and Related Fields

- **Foundational for Dynamic AI:** The ability to generate realistic and coherent videos is foundational for numerous AI applications that interact with dynamic environments. This includes **robotics** (for predictive control and planning in simulated environments), **computer graphics** (for efficient content creation and animation), **simulation** (for training autonomous systems), and **data augmentation** (generating synthetic training data).
- **Understanding World Models:** Progress in video generation is intrinsically linked to the development of "world models" – AI systems that can learn and predict the dynamics of their environment. The challenges exposed by SCV, particularly in relational reasoning and long-term consistency, are directly relevant to building more sophisticated and generalizable world models.
- **Beyond Pixel Prediction:** The paper implicitly shifts the focus of video generation research beyond mere pixel-wise prediction towards models that can grasp the semantic and physical principles governing a scene, moving closer to true "intelligent understanding" of visual dynamics.

In conclusion, "Towards Accurate Generative Models of Video: A New Metric & Challenges" is a landmark paper that provides indispensable tools—FVD and SCV—that were critically missing for advancing video generation research. By offering a robust evaluation framework and a targeted benchmark, it paves the way for the next generation of video generative models capable of understanding and synthesizing complex, dynamic, and coherent visual content, with far-reaching implications across various fields of AI.
