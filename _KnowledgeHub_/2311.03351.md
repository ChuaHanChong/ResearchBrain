# Uni-O4: Unifying Online and Offline Deep Reinforcement Learning with Multi-Step On-Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Uni-O4 presents a unified deep reinforcement learning framework that leverages multi-step on-policy optimization to seamlessly integrate offline learning and online fine-tuning, avoiding the conservatism and instability common in prior approaches. The framework achieves state-of-the-art offline performance on 14 out of 20 D4RL benchmarks and demonstrates stable, rapid online adaptation for real-world robot locomotion.

## Problem

- Online reinforcement learning requires extensive, often costly or unsafe, real-time environment interactions.
- Offline reinforcement learning, while data-efficient, suffers from out-of-distribution value estimation and extrapolation errors due to fixed datasets.
- Combining offline and online RL is difficult because offline conservatism impedes online fine-tuning and accurate off-policy evaluation (OPE) is complex.

## Method

- A unified three-stage framework utilizes an on-policy ==Proximal Policy Optimization== (**PPO**) objective for both offline and online learning, avoiding extra conservatism or regularization.
- Ensemble behavior cloning with disagreement regularization learns diverse policies from offline data, effectively addressing multi-modality and data mismatch issues.
- Multi-step policy improvement in the offline phase is enabled by **AM-Q** (==Approximate Model and fitted Q evaluation==), a computationally efficient method for offline policy evaluation.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art offline performance on 14 out of 20 D4RL Gym locomotion, Adroit, and Kitchen tasks.
- Exhibited stable and rapid improvement in offline-to-online fine-tuning, reaching higher asymptotic performance more efficiently than baseline methods without initial drops.
- Successfully enabled real-world robot locomotion on challenging terrains by bridging the sim-to-real gap with minimal interaction, adapting a simulated policy using 180,000 offline steps and 100,000 online steps to achieve 1.62 m/s speed on a mattress.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified on-policy PPO objective enables stable offline-to-online learning transitions without requiring extra conservatism or regularization.
> - Ensemble behavior cloning with a disagreement penalty effectively captures multi-modal behaviors in offline datasets, improving policy diversity and coverage.
> - Reliable offline policy evaluation via AM-Q allows for safe and efficient multi-step policy improvement solely from offline data, providing strong initializations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has demonstrated remarkable successes, yet its application to real-world scenarios remains challenging. The current research landscape often bifurcates into **Online RL** and **Offline RL**.

**Online RL** algorithms learn through direct interaction with the environment, requiring extensive exploration. This makes them prohibitive for many real-world applications, especially in robotics, where interactions can be costly, time-consuming, or unsafe.

**Offline RL**, in contrast, enables agents to learn from a fixed dataset of previously collected interactions, bypassing the need for real-time exploration. While safer and more data-efficient, offline RL faces its own set of challenges, primarily **out-of-distribution (OOD) value estimation** and **extrapolation error**. Policies trained purely offline can struggle to generalize to unseen states or actions, leading to suboptimal performance because they cannot explore beyond the confines of the provided dataset.

A natural and increasingly popular paradigm is to **combine offline and online RL**, typically involving an offline pre-training phase followed by online fine-tuning. This approach, widely successful in other machine learning domains like computer Vision (e.g., ImageNet pre-training) and Natural Language Processing (e.g., BERT), is non-trivial in RL due to several unique challenges:
1. **Conservatism**: Many offline RL algorithms incorporate regularization techniques (e.g., conservatism, policy constraints) to prevent agents from exploiting erroneous OOD value estimates. While crucial for offline stability, this conservatism can hinder online fine-tuning, leading to initial performance drops or asymptotic suboptimality as the agent struggles to escape the "safe" but conservative policy. Previous works (e.g., CQL, IQL, AWAC, PEX, SPOT, Cal-ql, Off2on) have attempted to mitigate this with various policy regularizations, Q-ensembles, or additional value functions, but often suffer from instability or suboptimal convergence.
2. **Off-policy Evaluation (OPE)**: Accurately evaluating new policies based on offline data is complex. Methods like Fitted Q Evaluation (FQE) or Importance Sampling are used, but their accuracy can be limited, especially when the new policy deviates significantly from the data-collecting behavior policy.
3. **Distribution Shift**: The mismatch between the behavior policy that collected the offline data and the estimated policy being learned can lead to difficulties in both value estimation and policy improvement.

The work by Uni-O4 directly addresses these issues by proposing a unified on-policy optimization framework. It builds upon advancements like Behavior Proximal Policy Optimization (BPPO), which adopted PPO for offline learning. However, BPPO still required online evaluation to validate policy improvements, contradicting the pure offline setting. Uni-O4 aims to overcome these limitations by removing the need for extra conservatism, regularization, or frequent online evaluations during the offline phase, enabling a genuinely seamless and stable transition from offline pre-training to online fine-tuning. This positions Uni-O4 as a significant step towards practical and robust RL for real-world applications, particularly in robotics where safety and efficiency are paramount.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind Uni-O4 is to address the inherent inefficiencies and instabilities encountered when combining offline and online reinforcement learning, particularly in the context of real-world robotic applications. The authors pose a fundamental question: **"Can we achieve straightforward yet effective offline and online learning without introducing extra conservatism or regularization?"**

This question drives the following key objectives:

1. **Unification of Learning Paradigms**: Develop a single, unified framework that can seamlessly handle both offline learning from fixed datasets and online fine-tuning through interaction. This aims to eliminate redundant algorithmic designs and facilitate a more flexible learning pipeline, supporting complex scenarios like "online (simulator)-offline (real-world)-online (real-world)" learning.
2. **Elimination of Conservatism and Regularization**: Overcome the "fine-tuning instability" and "asymptotic suboptimality" caused by the conservative regularization typically employed in offline RL. The goal is to achieve robust learning without introducing additional constraints during either the offline or online stages.
3. **Multi-Step Policy Improvement in Offline Settings**: Enable the policy to improve iteratively and significantly in the offline phase, moving beyond single-step updates, without requiring costly and risky online interactions for validation.
4. **Addressing Data Mismatch Issues**: Effectively handle the mismatch between the estimated behavior policy and diverse offline datasets, especially when the dataset contains multi-modal behaviors or varying quality trajectories.
5. **Superior Performance and Efficiency**: Achieve state-of-the-art performance in both pure offline learning and offline-to-online fine-tuning, demonstrating rapid, stable, and high-asymptotic returns.
6. **Practical Real-World Deployment**: Validate the framework's effectiveness on real-world robot tasks, particularly in bridging the sim-to-real gap and enabling rapid deployment in challenging, previously unseen environments with minimal real-world interaction.

By pursuing these objectives, Uni-O4 seeks to create a more direct, stable, and efficient RL solution that is highly amenable to practical deployment, especially in areas like robot locomotion, where safe and efficient adaptation is crucial.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Uni-O4 proposes a three-stage framework—supervised learning, multi-step policy improvement, and online fine-tuning—all built upon the core principle of **on-policy optimization using the PPO objective**. This unified approach is designed to ensure seamless transitions and avoid the complexities introduced by off-policy methods or excessive regularization.

#### 3.1. Overview of Uni-O4 Framework

The core idea is to employ an on-policy PPO objective for both offline and online learning, aligning the objectives across phases. This facilitates the agent's transfer between learning stages without requiring additional conservatism or regularization terms that typically plague offline-to-online methods.

#### 3.2. Ensemble Behavior Cloning with Disagreement Regularization (Supervised Learning Stage)

The first stage focuses on recovering the behavior policy $\pi_\beta$ that generated the offline dataset. Unlike prior works that might use a single behavior cloning (BC) policy, Uni-O4 introduces an **ensemble behavior cloning approach with disagreement regularization**.

- **Motivation**: A single BC policy often struggles to capture the multi-modality of diverse offline datasets and can be biased towards high-density but low-return actions. This leads to a "mismatch issue" between the estimated policy and the true data distribution.
- **Approach**: Uni-O4 learns an ensemble of $n$ policies, $Q_n = \{\hat{\pi}^1_\beta, \dots, \hat{\pi}^n_\beta\}$, to approximate $\pi_\beta$. To foster diversity among these ensemble policies, they are jointly trained using a BC loss augmented by a **negative disagreement penalty**. This penalty encourages each policy $\hat{\pi}^i_\beta$ to diverge from the "combined policy" $f(\{\hat{\pi}^j_\beta\}_{j \in [n]})$, which is chosen as the maximum likelihood over actions: $f(\{\hat{\pi}^j_\beta(a|s)\}) = \max_{1 \le j \le n} \hat{\pi}^j_\beta(a|s)$.
- **Objective (Equation 6)**: The objective for each $\hat{\pi}^i_\beta$ maximizes:
 $J(\hat{\pi}^i_\beta) = E_{(s,a) \sim D} [\log \hat{\pi}^i_\beta(a|s)] + \alpha E_{(s,a) \sim D} [\log \frac{\hat{\pi}^i_\beta(a|s)}{\max_{1 \le j \le n} \hat{\pi}^j_\beta(a|s)}]$
 where $\alpha$ is a hyper-parameter controlling the diversity. This encourages policies to cover different modes of the data, providing a more comprehensive "state-action support" over the offline dataset.

#### 3.3. Multi-Step Policy Ensemble Optimization with Offline Policy Evaluation (OPE) (Offline Policy Optimization Stage)

This stage aims to iteratively improve the policies trained in the supervised learning stage. Uni-O4 performs policy gradient optimization similar to PPO, but crucially, it achieves **multi-step policy improvement safely by querying OPE**, rather than relying on costly online evaluations as BPPO does.

- **PPO Objective (Equation 7)**: For each ensemble policy $\pi^i_k$ (where $k$ denotes iteration), the objective is:
 $J_k(\pi^i) = E_{s \sim \rho_D(\cdot), a \sim \pi^i_k(\cdot|s)} [\min (r(\pi^i)A^{\pi^i_k}(s, a), \text{clip}(r(\pi^i), 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon) A^{\pi^i_k}(s, a))]$
 Here, $\rho_D(\cdot)$ is the state distribution from the offline dataset, $r(\pi^i)$ is the importance sampling ratio, and the clip function constrains policy updates within a trust region. The advantage function $A^{\pi^i_k}(s, a)$ is computed using $(cQ_\tau - cV_\tau)$ rather than GAE, as interaction with the environment is prohibited in the offline setting. $cQ_\tau$ and $cV_\tau$ are fitted Q and V functions derived from Implicit Q-Learning (IQL)'s asymmetric L2 loss (Equations 2 & 3), which are known to approximate optimal values ($Q^*$) under dataset support constraints.
- **Offline Policy Evaluation (AM-Q)**: To safely update and replace behavior policies ($\pi^i_k \leftarrow \pi^i$), Uni-O4 proposes a simple OPE method called **AM-Q (Approximate Model and fitted Q evaluation)**.
 - **Mechanism**: It combines an estimated dynamics model $\hat{T}$ (trained using maximum likelihood on the offline dataset, Equation 5) with the fitted Q-function $cQ_\tau$. AM-Q evaluates a policy $\pi$ by performing H-step Monte-Carlo rollouts using $\hat{T}$ and summing the $cQ_\tau$ values along these trajectories: $cJ_\tau(\pi) = E_{(s,a) \sim (\hat{T}, \pi)} [\sum_{t=0}^{H-1} cQ_\tau(s_t, a_t)]$.
 - **Benefits**: This makes OPE computationally efficient (inference complexity $O(NH)$) and allows for multi-step policy improvement without online interaction. A theoretical bound (Theorem 2) quantifies the OPE bias stemming from transition model approximation.
 - **Policy Replacement**: An ensemble policy $\pi^i_k$ is updated to $\pi^i$ if $cJ_\tau(\pi^i) > cJ_\tau(\pi^i_k)$, ensuring monotonic improvement in an offline context.
- **Policy Selection**: After the offline optimization, one policy from the ensemble is selected for online fine-tuning based on its OPE score, typically the one with the highest evaluated value.

#### 3.4. Seamless Online Fine-Tuning

The policy and value function obtained from the offline pre-training are directly used as initialization for a **standard online PPO algorithm**. Crucially, no extra conservative regularization or replay buffer balancing strategies are introduced during this phase. The unified on-policy design allows for a direct continuation of learning, leveraging the strong initialization from offline training to achieve stable and rapid online performance improvements.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Uni-O4's performance was rigorously evaluated across a wide range of simulated D4RL benchmarks (MuJoCo locomotion, Adroit, Antmaze, Kitchen) and real-world robot tasks, demonstrating significant advancements in both offline and offline-to-online learning scenarios.

#### 4.1. Performance on D4RL Benchmarks (Offline and Offline-to-Online)

- **Offline Performance**:
 - Uni-O4 achieved state-of-the-art results, outperforming all other offline RL algorithms on **14 out of 20 tasks** in the D4RL Gym locomotion, Adroit, and Kitchen tasks.
 - It significantly surpassed "one-step" algorithms (e.g., One-step RL, IQL) by enabling multi-step policy improvement within a trust region, effectively using the clip function and approximate advantage as natural conservative terms.
 - Uni-O4 demonstrated competitive performance against iterative and model-based algorithms, often outperforming them, particularly on the more challenging Adroit and Kitchen tasks.
 - On Antmaze tasks, which involve multi-task learning and sparse rewards, Uni-O4 again outperformed the majority of baselines, achieving a **79.4% improvement** over 26 tasks compared to estimated behavior policies.
 - The results highlight Uni-O4's ability to provide a superior offline initialization, crucial for subsequent online fine-tuning.

- **Offline-to-Online Fine-Tuning Performance**:
 - Uni-O4 exhibited an integration of **stability, consistency, and efficiency** during online fine-tuning.
 - Unlike methods that inherit conservatism (CQL, IQL, AWAC), which struggled to achieve high asymptotic performance, or regularization methods (PEX, SPOT) and Q-ensemble methods (Off2on) that often faced initial performance drops or instability, Uni-O4 showed **steady and rapid improvement** in value functions and normalized returns.
 - It consistently converged to higher asymptotic performance compared to all baseline methods across MuJoCo locomotion and Adroit tasks. The aggregated learning curves clearly demonstrated its ability to fine-tune policies without performance drops or unstable training.
 - Furthermore, Uni-O4 proved significantly more computationally efficient during fine-tuning (e.g., 30 minutes vs. 18 hours for Off2on).

#### 4.2. Real-World Robot Applications

- Uni-O4 was successfully applied in an **online-offline-online framework** to address the sim-to-real gap in real-world legged robot locomotion.
- **Scenario**: A policy pre-trained in a simulator for level terrains struggled on an elastic latex mattress (a challenging, hard-to-model environment).
- **Uni-O4's Solution**:
 1. **Offline Fine-tuning (real-world data)**: By collecting a small dataset (180,000 environment steps) on the mattress before failures, Uni-O4 fine-tuned the policy, enabling the robot to navigate the challenging terrain at a low speed. Baselines like WTW (Walk These Ways) and IQL struggled to adapt.
 2. **Online Fine-tuning (real-world interaction)**: Further online fine-tuning (100,000 environment steps) allowed the robot to achieve higher speeds (1.62 m/s) on the mattress.
- **Significance**: This demonstrated Uni-O4's capability for **rapid deployment and adaptation** in complex, previously unseen real-world environments with minimal interaction, ensuring safety through offline refinement before performance-boosting online interaction.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Insights

- **OPE Accuracy (AM-Q)**: The proposed AM-Q method for offline policy evaluation showed high accuracy. It reached approximately **80% accuracy** in predicting policy improvements and nearly **95% accuracy** when allowing for a 20% estimation error, validating its reliability for multi-step policy improvement in the offline phase.
- **Hyperparameter Analysis**:
 - A minor **disagreement penalty coefficient ($\alpha$)** (e.g., 0.1) yielded better offline performance for the ensemble behavior policies, confirming the benefit of encouraging diversity without excessive divergence.
 - An **ensemble size of 4 policies** was chosen as a good trade-off between performance gain and computational efficiency, significantly outperforming a single policy.
- **Optimality Analysis**: Compared to training PPO from scratch for 3 million steps in online environments, Uni-O4's fine-tuning demonstrated **rapid performance improvement** and reached comparable (or superior) asymptotic optimality faster, validating its stable and efficient fine-tuning capabilities.
- **Policy Ensemble Analysis**: Visualization confirmed that ensemble policies, especially with disagreement penalties, provide a **more comprehensive state-action support** over the offline dataset and are more effective at exploring high-return action regions, mitigating the "main mode bias" of single BC policies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Uni-O4 framework presents a significant advancement in the field of reinforcement learning, particularly at the intersection of offline and online learning. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Unified and Simplified Learning Paradigm**: Uni-O4's core contribution is demonstrating that a single on-policy optimization objective (PPO) can effectively govern both offline and online learning phases. This eliminates the need for complex, often redundant, designs and bridging mechanisms that characterize many existing offline-to-online approaches. The simplicity of this unified approach makes RL pipelines more straightforward to implement and manage.

2. **Overcoming Key Offline-to-Online Challenges**: The paper successfully addresses the long-standing issues of fine-tuning instability and asymptotic suboptimality that arise from the conservative nature of traditional offline RL algorithms. By avoiding extra conservatism or regularization during the transition, Uni-O4 enables seamless and stable fine-tuning, leading to consistent performance gains without initial drops.

3. **Safe and Efficient Multi-Step Offline Improvement**: The introduction of AM-Q as a reliable Offline Policy Evaluation (OPE) mechanism allows for multi-step policy improvement in the offline phase without resorting to risky or costly online evaluations. This significantly enhances the quality of offline-trained policies, providing superior initialization for subsequent online learning and expanding the capabilities of pure offline methods.

4. **Enhanced Data Utilization with Ensemble Policies**: The ensemble behavior cloning with disagreement regularization effectively mitigates the data mismatch problem, particularly in multi-modal datasets. By learning diverse behaviors, the framework can better capture the richness of the offline data, leading to more robust and adaptable initial policies.

5. **Accelerating Real-World Robot Deployment**: Perhaps the most impactful application lies in robotics. The online-offline-online learning pipeline demonstrated with real-world legged robots showcases Uni-O4's ability to bridge the sim-to-real gap. It allows for pre-training in simulators, safe and minimal-interaction fine-tuning on real robots with collected offline data, and subsequent performance enhancement through online interaction. This methodology promises to **reduce deployment time and cost**, enhance safety, and enable rapid adaptation to novel, unsimulatable real-world environments, a crucial step towards ubiquitous robotic systems.

6. **Broader Applicability**: The principles introduced in Uni-O4 can be scaled to various complex fine-tuning settings beyond direct offline-to-online, such as the multi-stage (simulator-real-world offline-real-world online) scenarios, and potentially other domains where large offline datasets and limited online interaction are common.

In conclusion, Uni-O4 offers a practical, efficient, and theoretically grounded solution to a critical problem in modern reinforcement learning. By simplifying the integration of offline and online learning and showcasing its effectiveness in challenging real-world scenarios, it paves the way for more robust and deployable RL agents across various applications.
