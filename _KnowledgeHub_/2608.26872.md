# Self-OPD: On-Policy Distillation for Flow Matching Models without Teacher

> [!summary] Summary
> Self-OPD is a teacher-free on-policy distillation framework for flow matching image generators. At each denoising step it branches the deterministic next state into K stochastic SDE candidates, rolls them out with an ODE sampler, scores them against a deterministic self-reference baseline, and applies an advantage-weighted pull-push velocity loss. Multi-objective alignment fuses normalized rewards before ranking, and it beats RL and teacher-based distillation baselines.

## Problem

- RL alignment methods score only full denoising trajectories, so credit must propagate backward many steps, yielding high-variance gradients and fragile multi-objective alignment.
- Teacher-based on-policy distillation needs a separate specialized teacher trained per objective, which is costly and caps student quality at the teacher's quality and bias.
- Field-level fusion of several teachers blends conflicting gradients in parameter space, routing prompts per teacher so quality holds only on the matched prompt family.

## Method

- ==SDE branching exploration== plus ==self-referenced advantage==: one forward pass gives the deterministic next state, K Gaussian perturbations create branches, and a ==pure-ODE rollout== supplies the baseline reward.
- All-branch ==pull-push distillation loss== with ==direction-aware attenuation gate== bounded in [0,1] and ==SDE-variance normalization==, derived as a Monte Carlo estimate of the ==per-step reward-tilted KL gradient==.
- ==Reward-level fusion== for multi-objective alignment: ==z-scored per-metric rewards== weighted into one composite score used only to rank branches, never differentiated, avoiding gradient conflict.

## Results

- Single-reward training on SD3.5-Medium: GenEval strict **0.9536**, OCR **0.9745**, PickScore **24.47**, HPSv2 **0.4099**, beating Flow-GRPO and GRPO-Guard on every task with identical base and rewards.
- Mixed-reward single model: GenEval **0.9521**, OCR **0.9597**, and on task prompts PickScore **23.87** vs teacher-based DiffusionOPD's **22.72** and HPSv2 **0.3214** vs **0.2676**.
- Ablations: Best-of-K regression trains unstably, unbounded gating collapses GenEval to **0.768**, and warm-start Self-OPD reaches DiffusionOPD parity about **2x faster** than its **97-hour** teacher-plus-student pipeline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Local stochastic exploration around a model's own prediction can replace a teacher for dense per-step supervision, removing the teacher-quality ceiling and its training cost.
> - Fusing objectives at the non-differentiable reward level before ranking samples keeps one generation good on all metrics, unlike blending gradients in parameter space.
> - Negative examples help only when repulsion is attenuated and bounded relative to attraction; unbounded repulsion dominates gradients and collapses training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Flow Matching (FM) models have emerged as a prominent architecture for high-quality visual generation, learning a continuous velocity field to transform noise into data. Despite their generative capabilities, aligning these models with specific downstream objectives, such as accurate text rendering, compositional correctness, or adherence to human preferences, presents a significant challenge.

Existing approaches to address this alignment problem generally fall into two categories:
1. **Reinforcement Learning (RL) methods:** These approaches treat the generation process as a sequential decision-making task and optimize reward signals directly. While teacher-free, they often rely on terminal scores from complete denoising trajectories. This reliance can lead to high-variance gradients due to the need for backward credit assignment over many steps, making multi-objective alignment susceptible to fragility when different rewards favor disparate visual properties. Examples include Flow-GRPO and GRPO-Guard.
2. **On-Policy Distillation (OPD) methods:** These methods, initially successful in Large Language Models (LLMs), have been adapted for flow matching and diffusion models (e.g., Flow-OPD, DiffusionOPD). OPD provides dense, step-wise supervision by having a student model regress toward predictions from a pre-trained teacher model on the student's own generated trajectories. This approach generally improves training stability and sample efficiency.

However, current teacher-based OPD paradigms face two primary limitations. First, the requirement to train or acquire a separate, specialized teacher model for each new objective incurs substantial computational costs. Second, the student model's performance is inherently bounded by the quality and potential biases of its teacher. Furthermore, when attempting multi-objective alignment, combining velocity fields from multiple teachers can lead to conflicting update directions, where improving one metric may degrade others. Some methods handle multiple objectives by decoupling the problem into separately trained, task-specific teachers whose velocity fields are then routed and merged, which may not facilitate a single image achieving high reward across all objectives simultaneously.

This research introduces **Self-OPD** as a response to these limitations. It aims to bridge the gap by providing the dense, step-wise supervision characteristic of OPD while eliminating the need for any external teacher model. This positions Self-OPD as a method that seeks to combine the teacher-free nature of direct RL methods with the stable, dense supervision benefits of OPD, specifically addressing the computational overhead and performance ceiling imposed by teacher models in multi-objective alignment for flow matching.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop and validate a teacher-free on-policy distillation framework for flow matching models, named Self-OPD. This framework aims to provide dense, step-wise supervisory signals by leveraging the student model's internal exploration capabilities, thereby eliminating the reliance on external, pre-trained teacher models.

The motivation behind pursuing this objective stems from several identified limitations of existing generative model alignment paradigms:

1. **High Computational Costs of Teacher Training:** Traditional OPD methods necessitate the training or acquisition of a specialized teacher model for each new alignment objective. This process is computationally intensive and resource-demanding, making it impractical for scenarios involving numerous or frequently changing objectives. The researchers are motivated to remove this bottleneck.
2. **Teacher-Induced Performance Ceiling and Bias:** In teacher-based OPD, the student model's performance is inherently capped by the quality, biases, and data distribution of its teacher. This prevents the student from potentially exceeding the teacher's capabilities or exploring novel, higher-reward regions that the teacher might not cover. Self-OPD seeks to enable the student to surpass such limitations through self-exploration.
3. **Challenges in Multi-Objective Alignment with Field-Level Fusion:** When aligning models with multiple objectives simultaneously, existing teacher-based methods often attempt to fuse information at the velocity field level (e.g., merging gradients from multiple teachers). This approach frequently leads to conflicting update directions (destructive gradient interference), causing a "seesaw effect" where improving one metric degrades others. The goal is to develop a method that allows a single generated image to achieve high rewards across all objectives concurrently, rather than having different images excel in different criteria.
4. **High Variance in Pure RL Approaches:** While some RL methods are teacher-free, their reliance on terminal reward signals for entire generation trajectories can result in high-variance gradients, making training less stable and multi-objective alignment more difficult due to the credit assignment problem. Self-OPD is motivated to retain the stability benefits of dense, step-wise supervision, which OPD offers, but without a teacher.
5. **Desire for Flexible and Black-Box Reward Integration:** The limitations of gradient-based fusion in multi-objective settings also highlight a need for an alignment mechanism that can accommodate black-box or non-differentiable reward functions, which are common in real-world applications (e.g., human preference models).

By addressing these motivations, Self-OPD aims to provide a more efficient, robust, and flexible framework for aligning flow matching models with diverse and potentially multiple downstream objectives.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Self-OPD introduces a teacher-free on-policy distillation framework for flow matching models. Its core methodology involves using the student model's self-exploration to generate step-wise supervisory signals, guided by rewards.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

The work is based on Flow Matching (FM) models, which learn a continuous velocity field $v_\theta(x_t, t)$ to transport a noise distribution to a data distribution. Samples are generated by integrating a reverse-time Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE). To enable stochastic exploration, the reverse ODE is augmented into a Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE). The paper details the Euler-Maruyama discretization for SDE transitions, showing how each step involves a deterministic next-state prediction and an isotropic stochastic perturbation. A key derivation establishes an affine relationship between the deterministic next-state prediction $x_{t_{j+1},\theta}$ and the learned velocity field $v_\theta(x_{t_j}, t_j)$, which is fundamental for converting distillation objectives between transition space and velocity space.

#### 3.2. Self-Referenced Evaluation and Self-Exploration

Self-OPD replaces a teacher with a self-evaluation mechanism:
- **SDE Branching Exploration:** At each on-policy timestep $t_j$, the student first computes a deterministic next-state prediction $x_{t_{j+1},\theta}$. Instead of proceeding deterministically, it generates $K$ stochastic candidate next states, $x^{(k)}_{t_{j+1}}$, by adding independent Gaussian perturbations to this deterministic prediction (Eq. 6). These perturbations are sampled from the SDE transition's noise distribution, with a controllable noise level $\eta$. This allows local exploration around the student's current prediction.
- **ODE Rollout and Self-Reference Evaluation:** Each of the $K$ branched candidates, $x^{(k)}_{t_{j+1}}$, is then rolled out deterministically (using $\eta=0$) to a clean image $\hat{x}^{(k)}_0$. These images are decoded and evaluated using a task-specific reward model $R$, yielding $r^{(k)}$. To provide a stable baseline for comparison, a fully deterministic ODE trajectory is also run directly from the initial parent state $x_{t_j}$, producing a reward $r_{ode}$. The advantage $A_k$ for each branch is calculated by normalizing the difference between its reward $r^{(k)}$ and the self-reference reward $r_{ode}$ by the standard deviation of all branch rewards (Eq. 8). Positive advantages indicate superior local directions compared to the default student path.

#### 3.3. All-Branch Pull-Push Distillation

Instead of merely focusing on the single "best" branch, Self-OPD utilizes feedback from the entire set of $K$ explored branches:
- **Direction-Aware Attenuation Coefficient:** The method introduces $d_k$ (Eq. 9) to modulate the repulsive force from low-advantage branches. If a low-reward branch points in a similar direction as the best branch, a strong repulsion would counteract the desired pull. Thus, $d_k$ is 1 for positive advantage branches and a value between 0 and 1 for negative advantage branches, suppressing repulsion when a negative branch is aligned with the best branch's direction.
- **Effective Branch Velocity:** Based on the affine relationship (Eq. 5), each branched state $x^{(k)}_{t_{j+1}}$ can be mapped back to an effective branch velocity $v^{(k)}$ (Eq. 10). This means injecting Gaussian noise in transition space is equivalent to perturbing the velocity target in velocity space.
- **Self-OPD Objective:** The step-wise loss $L^{(j)}_{\text{Self-OPD}}$ (Eq. 11) is formulated as an "all-branch pull-push" objective. It includes two terms: one that pulls the current velocity $v_\theta$ towards high-advantage branch velocities $v^{(k)}_+$ (for $A_k \ge 0$) and another that pushes $v_\theta$ away from low-advantage branch velocities $v^{(k)}_-$ (for $A_k < 0$), modulated by the direction-aware attenuation $d_k$. The objective also includes a prefactor related to the SDE transition variance, connecting it to a principled per-step KL minimization.
- **Connection to Per-Step KL:** The paper demonstrates that the objective approximates the gradient of the reverse KL divergence between a reward-tilted target distribution $q^*$ and the student's transition kernel $q_\theta$. The expectation is approximated using the $K$ sampled branches, weighted by their advantages.
- **Total Training Objective:** The total loss $L_{\text{total}}$ is a sum of these step-wise losses across all denoising timesteps, weighted by $\alpha_j$. Early timesteps are prioritized to establish global semantic layout, while later timesteps are given less weight.

#### 3.4. Reward-Level Fusion for Multi-Objective Alignment

To address gradient conflicts in multi-objective settings, Self-OPD fuses objectives at the reward level:
- **Composite Reward:** Instead of blending gradients in parameter space, Self-OPD computes a composite reward $r^{(k)}$ for each branch by a weighted sum of z-scored individual rewards (Eq. 18). This composite reward is then used to determine the branch advantages $A_k$.
- **Selector over Trajectories:** This approach means that the composite score is used only for ranking and selecting trajectories, not for direct differentiation. The training target remains a single, concrete trajectory velocity that already scores well across all metrics simultaneously. This bypasses inter-objective gradient conflicts, supports black-box reward models, allows runtime adjustment of trade-offs, and enables the student to potentially exceed teacher performance by exploring joint high-reward regions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating Self-OPD's performance across single-reward and mixed-reward benchmarks, alongside ablation studies and training efficiency analysis.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

- **Single-Reward Training (Table 1):** Self-OPD consistently outperforms prior teacher-free RL fine-tuning methods (Flow-GRPO, GRPO-Guard) across all evaluated tasks. For instance, Self-OPD achieves a GenEval strict score of 0.9536, an OCR accuracy of 0.9745, a PickScore of 24.47, and an HPSv2 score of 0.4099 in its respective specialized variants. This indicates the superiority of step-wise self-distillation over terminal policy gradient optimization.
- **Mixed-Reward Training (Table 2):** A single Self-OPD model trained with mixed rewards demonstrates strong performance across all metrics simultaneously. It matches the highest GenEval strict score (0.9521) and achieves the best OCR accuracy (0.9597). Crucially, when preference metrics (PickScore, HPSv2) are evaluated on the *same test images* used for GenEval and OCR (reflecting the model's ability to jointly satisfy task requirements and aesthetic quality), Self-OPD surpasses teacher-based OPD methods like DiffusionOPD (PickScore 23.87 vs. 22.72; HPSv2 0.3214 vs. 0.2676). This outcome supports the effectiveness of Self-OPD's reward-level fusion paradigm over field-level fusion, as the latter tends to couple preference quality to specific prompt families (Figure 4).

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results (Figures 5, 9, 10)

Qualitative comparisons highlight Self-OPD's ability to:
- Render text with higher fidelity and spell complex phrases accurately.
- Execute complex spatial layouts and counting constraints more precisely, leading to more coherent compositions.
- Generate images with superior aesthetic quality, scene richness, and realistic details compared to both mixed-reward baselines and single-reward specialists, even on task-specific prompts. This aligns with the quantitative advantage in preference scores on task-specific images.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study (Figure 6)

- **Branch Selection Strategy:** The full all-branch pull-push distillation with KL normalization significantly outperforms a "Best-of-K" strategy, which suffered from high gradient variance and non-monotonic training due to abrupt target switching and lack of negative feedback.
- **Repulsion Bounding:** The direction-aware attenuation coefficient, when bounded (d_k $\in$), maintains stable and monotonic improvement. An unbounded version led to unstable training and performance collapse, indicating that repulsion should act as an attenuation gate, not a gradient amplifier.
- **Timestep Weighting:** Prioritizing early-to-mid timesteps accelerates convergence by focusing signals on phases where branching yields more diverse semantic alternatives, compared to uniform weighting.

#### 4.4. Analysis of Training Time (Figure 7)

Self-OPD significantly reduces wall-clock training time compared to teacher-based OPD methods like DiffusionOPD.
- DiffusionOPD requires substantial time (85.8 hours) for teacher model training, during which the student model remains idle, followed by student distillation (11.3 hours), totaling 97.0 hours.
- Self-OPD (from scratch) reaches comparable performance levels (e.g., DiffusionOPD's final OCR of 0.946) in approximately 62 hours, and GenEval (0.915) in approximately 90 hours, both faster than DiffusionOPD's total time.
- When warm-started (training single-reward specialists in parallel, then launching mixed-reward training), Self-OPD achieves these performance levels in roughly 48 hours for OCR and 44 hours for GenEval, making it approximately 2x faster than DiffusionOPD while achieving higher final performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Self-OPD represents a notable advancement in the field of generative model alignment, particularly for flow matching models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Elimination of Teacher Dependency:** Self-OPD removes the need for pre-trained, specialized teacher models. This addresses a significant computational and resource bottleneck in traditional OPD frameworks. By leveraging the student's own self-exploration and self-referenced evaluation, the framework democratizes access to advanced alignment techniques, making them more feasible for researchers and practitioners without extensive access to large-scale pre-trained models or computational resources.
- **Enhanced Multi-Objective Alignment:** The proposed reward-level fusion strategy is a key innovation for multi-objective alignment. By combining objectives at the scalar reward level rather than blending gradients in parameter space, Self-OPD effectively mitigates destructive gradient interference. This enables a single generative model to simultaneously excel across multiple, potentially conflicting, objectives (e.g., text accuracy, compositional correctness, aesthetic quality) in a single image, a capability that teacher-based field-level fusion often struggles with.
- **Improved Training Efficiency and Stability:** The all-branch pull-push distillation objective, coupled with SDE-variance normalization and direction-aware attenuation, contributes to more stable and efficient training. The detailed ablation studies confirm the importance of these architectural choices for robust optimization. The reduced wall-clock training time, as demonstrated, further underscores its practical efficiency compared to teacher-dependent methods.
- **Support for Black-Box Reward Models:** The reward-level fusion paradigm inherently supports black-box or non-differentiable reward functions, which are common in real-world scenarios (e.g., human preference models, external APIs). This flexibility expands the applicability of alignment techniques to a broader range of objectives.
- **Potential to Surpass Teacher Performance:** By allowing the student to explore and discover optimal trajectories independently of a fixed teacher's biases or limitations, Self-OPD theoretically enables the student to exceed the capabilities of any individual pre-trained teacher. This "teacher ceiling" bypass potentially leads to higher-quality and more generalized generative models.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** Self-OPD's teacher-free, self-exploratory approach opens new avenues for research into self-supervised or self-improving generative agents. It suggests that complex alignment objectives might be achievable with less external supervision and more internal guidance mechanisms.

In conclusion, Self-OPD provides a robust, efficient, and flexible framework for aligning flow matching models, marking a step towards more autonomous and capable generative AI systems.
