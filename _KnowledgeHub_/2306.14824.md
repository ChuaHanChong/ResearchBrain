# Kosmos-2: Grounding Multimodal Large Language Models to the World

> [!summary] Summary
> Microsoft Research developed KOSMOS-2, a multimodal large language model that integrates grounding capabilities, allowing it to perceive object descriptions (bounding boxes) and precisely link text to the visual world. The model achieved strong zero-shot performance on phrase grounding (78.7% R@1 on Flickr30k Entities) and referring expression generation (CIDEr 60.3 on RefCOCOg), while largely preserving its existing vision-language and language-only capabilities.

## Problem

- Existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) often lack robust grounding capabilities, making them unable to precisely pinpoint or refer to specific objects or regions within an image.
- Human-AI interaction was limited to high-level textual descriptions, lacking the intuitive "pointing" and "showing" fundamental to human communication about the physical world.
- MLLMs struggled to provide visual answers (e.g., bounding boxes) or to visually resolve coreference ambiguity for objects in an image.

## Method

- Introduced KOSMOS-2, a Transformer-based multimodal large language model that integrates grounding and referring capabilities by representing continuous ==bounding box coordinates as discrete location tokens==.
- Constructed ==GRIT==, a large-scale (91 million images, 115 million text spans, 137 million bounding boxes) grounded image-text dataset, by automatically extracting noun-chunk-bounding-box and referring-expression-bounding-box pairs from existing web-scale datasets.
- Employed a novel =="hyperlink" data format== to explicitly link text spans with their associated location tokens, enabling the model to learn the association between language and visual regions during training.
- Utilized ==instruction tuning with grounded instruction data== to align KOSMOS-2's capabilities with human instructions for tasks requiring visual grounding.

## Results

- KOSMOS-2 achieved **7**8.7%** R@1** zero-shot performance on Flickr30k Entities for phrase grounding, significantly outperforming a previous zero-shot model, GRILL (**1**8.9%** R@1**).
- It demonstrated strong zero-shot referring expression generation with a CIDEr score of **60.3** on RefCOCOg, surprisingly outperforming a finetuned listener-speaker model.
- The model maintained or slightly improved performance on perception-language tasks (e.g., Flickr30k Image Captioning CIDEr **66.7**, up from KOSMOS-1's **65.2**) and largely preserved language-only task proficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly encoding bounding box information as discrete location tokens within the text stream allows a causal language model to learn grounding generatively.
> - Creating a massive, automatically constructed grounded image-text dataset (GRIT) is crucial for training effective grounding capabilities in multimodal large language models.
> - A "hyperlink" format for associating text spans with bounding boxes provides a clear signal for the model to learn multimodal grounding.
> - Grounding capabilities can be integrated into multimodal large language models without significantly degrading their general perception-language or language-only abilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Artificial Intelligence has witnessed a rapid evolution, particularly with the advent of Large Language Models (LLMs) and their subsequent extension to Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). Initially, LLMs like GPT-3 demonstrated remarkable capabilities in understanding and generating human language. The next frontier involved integrating other modalities, primarily vision, leading to MLLMs such as Flamingo, BLIP-2, and KOSMOS-1. These models excelled at tasks like image captioning, visual question answering, and even generating text based on visual inputs, essentially serving as "general-purpose interfaces" that can perceive and respond across text, image, and sometimes audio.

However, a significant limitation of many early MLLMs was their lack of robust *grounding* capabilities. While they could understand and describe visual content, they often struggled to precisely pinpoint or refer to specific objects or regions within an image. For instance, an MLLM might describe "a dog in a field," but it couldn't be instructed to "point to *that* dog" or indicate "the bounding box for *the field*." This gap meant that human-AI interaction was largely confined to high-level textual descriptions, lacking the intuitive "pointing" and "showing" that are fundamental to human communication about the physical world.

KOSMOS-2 directly addresses this critical limitation by introducing and integrating grounding capabilities into an MLLM. It positions itself as a crucial step towards "Embodiment AI" and the "big convergence of language, multimodal perception, action, and world modeling." Embodied AI envisions intelligent agents that can interact with and understand the physical world, requiring not just abstract reasoning but also precise spatial and object-level perception. By enabling MLLMs to perceive object descriptions (like bounding boxes) and ground text to specific visual regions, KOSMOS-2 moves beyond mere description to enable a deeper, more actionable understanding of visual content. This work seeks to unify high-level language understanding with low-level visual perception, laying a foundation for more intuitive human-AI interfaces and potentially intelligent agents capable of performing complex tasks in real-world environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of KOSMOS-2 is to equip Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) with **grounding capabilities**, allowing them to perceive object descriptions (such as bounding boxes) and precisely link text to the visual world.

The motivations behind this objective are multifaceted:

- **Enhanced Human-AI Interaction:** Traditional MLLMs require users to describe objects verbally. Grounding enables a more intuitive interaction paradigm where users can directly "point" to objects or regions in an image (e.g., by providing bounding box coordinates) rather than relying on verbose text descriptions. This significantly streamlines communication and reduces ambiguity.
- **Enabling Visual Answers:** Many vision-language tasks require models to not just generate text but also to visually identify or highlight specific regions. KOSMOS-2 aims to allow the model to respond with visual answers (i.e., bounding boxes), providing more accurate, informative, and less ambiguous responses than text-only outputs. This is particularly crucial for tasks like referring expression comprehension, where the goal is to locate a described object.
- **Resolving Coreference Ambiguity:** By linking noun phrases and referring expressions in generated text responses directly to image regions, KOSMOS-2 can provide more precise and comprehensive answers, resolving potential ambiguities that arise when multiple similar objects are present in an image.
- **Foundation for Embodiment AI:** The ability to ground language to specific visual locations is a fundamental requirement for Artificial Intelligence systems designed to interact with and understand the physical world. KOSMOS-2 aims to lay a critical foundation for the development of Embodiment AI, where AI agents can perceive, reason about, and act within real-world environments.
- **Driving Towards Artificial General Intelligence (AGI):** The authors view grounding as a key step in the "big convergence of language, multimodal perception, action, and world modeling." This convergence is posited as an essential pathway toward achieving Artificial General Intelligence, where disparate AI capabilities are integrated into a holistic, generalized intelligence.
- **Expanding Downstream Applications:** By integrating grounding, KOSMOS-2 aims to unlock a new range of downstream applications that were previously challenging for MLLMs, such as grounded image captioning, grounded visual question answering, and visual dialogue, where the model can respond with specific visual references.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

KOSMOS-2 builds upon the architectural foundation of its predecessor, KOSMOS-1, enhancing it with novel grounding and referring capabilities.

**Model Architecture and Training Objective:**
KOSMOS-2 employs a **Transformer-based causal language model** as its backbone, trained with the standard **next-word (or next-token) prediction task**. This allows the model to generate sequences in an autoregressive manner. Similar to KOSMOS-1, it incorporates a vision encoder (a 24-layer Transformer with 1,024 hidden size) and a resampler module to obtain image embeddings from input images (224x224 resolution). The multimodal large language model component is a 24-layer MAGNETO Transformer. The total trainable parameters amount to approximately 1.6 billion.

**Novelty: Grounding Capability Integration:**
The core innovation lies in integrating grounding and referring capabilities. This is achieved through two main components:
1. **Construction of a Large-Scale Grounded Image-Text Dataset (GRIT):**
 To enable the model to learn grounding, the authors constructed GRIT, a web-scale dataset of Grounded Image-Text pairs, derived from subsets of existing large image-text datasets like COYO-700M and LAION-2B. The pipeline for GRIT creation involves two steps:
 - **Step 1: Generating Noun-Chunk-Bounding-Box Pairs:** For a given image-text pair, noun chunks are extracted from the caption using spaCy. These noun chunks are then associated with image regions (bounding boxes) using a pre-trained grounding model, specifically GLIP. Abstract noun phrases are filtered out, and non-maximum suppression (NMS) is applied to remove overlapping boxes, retaining pairs with confidence scores > 0.65.
 - **Step 2: Producing Referring-Expression-Bounding-Box Pairs:** To handle more complex linguistic descriptions, noun chunks are expanded into referring expressions by traversing dependency relations in the sentence (again using spaCy). Only the longest, most comprehensive expressions are retained (e.g., "a dog in a field of flowers" is kept over "a field of flowers"). The bounding box of the base noun chunk is assigned to the expanded referring expression.
 This process yielded GRIT with approximately 91 million images, 115 million text spans, and 137 million associated bounding boxes, making it significantly larger than existing visual grounding datasets.

2. **Grounded Input Representations:**
 A crucial aspect of KOSMOS-2's methodology is the unified representation of text and visual grounding information.
 - **Discretizing Bounding Boxes:** Continuous coordinates of bounding boxes are converted into sequences of discrete **location tokens**. The image width and height are divided into P (e.g., 32) segments, creating P x P bins. Each bin is represented by a unique location token, added to the model's vocabulary. A bounding box (x1, y1, x2, y2) is represented by concatenating the location tokens of its top-left and bottom-right corner points, enclosed by special tokens: `<box><loc_1><loc_2></box>`. Multiple bounding boxes for a single text span are concatenated with a `<delim>` token.
 - **"Hyperlink" Data Format:** Text spans and their associated location tokens are formatted like a Markdown hyperlink: `<p> text span </p><box><loc_1><loc_2></box>`. This structure explicitly signals to the model that the text span is linked to the specified image region, allowing it to learn the association.
 For multimodal input, the sequence starts with `<s> <image> Image Embedding </image> <grounding>`, where `<grounding>` prompts the model to process grounding information.

**Training Process:**
KOSMOS-2 is trained on a comprehensive mix of data: the newly added **grounded image-text pairs (GRIT)**, monomodal text corpora, original image-caption pairs, and interleaved multimodal data (similar to KOSMOS-1's training mix). The training objective focuses on next-token prediction, with the loss calculated only on discrete text and location tokens. This enables the model to learn to understand and generate location tokens corresponding to image regions. The training initialized with KOSMOS-1's weights, with new location token embeddings randomly initialized.

**Instruction Tuning:**
After initial pre-training, KOSMOS-2 undergoes instruction tuning to align its capabilities better with human instructions. This involves combining:
- General vision-language instruction datasets (e.g., LLaVA-Instruct).
- Language-only instruction datasets (e.g., Unnatural Instructions, FLANv2).
- **Crucially, newly constructed grounded instruction data**: Templates are used to create instruction-response pairs where the model learns to generate location tokens given an expression (e.g., "What is `<p> expression </p>`? It is `<box>...</box>`") or generate expressions given bounding boxes (e.g., "`<p> It </p><box>...</box>` is `{expression}`"). This direct instruction on grounding tasks is key to unlocking its new capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

KOSMOS-2 demonstrates significant advancements in multimodal understanding, particularly in grounding and referring capabilities, while maintaining strong performance on traditional vision-language and language-only tasks.

#### 4.1. Multimodal Grounding (New Capabilities)

KOSMOS-2 was evaluated on two key multimodal grounding tasks in a generation manner, where the model outputs bounding box coordinates as sequences of location tokens.
- **Phrase Grounding (Flickr30k Entities):**
 - KOSMOS-2 achieved impressive zero-shot performance (R@1 of 77.8% on validation, 78.7% on test split).
 - It significantly outperformed a previous zero-shot model, GRILL (which relies on an attached detector), by a large margin (e.g., 78.7% R@1 vs 18.9% R@1 on test).
 - It also surpassed a finetuned VisualBert model by 7.4% R@1.
 - A notable finding is that KOSMOS-2's R@1, R@5, and R@10 scores are very close, indicating that the model generates high-quality locations without needing extensive post-processing or multiple proposals, a strength attributed to its end-to-end generative approach without prior object queries.
- **Referring Expression Comprehension (RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg):**
 - KOSMOS-2 showed promising zero-shot accuracy, particularly on RefCOCOg (60.57% on validation, 61.65% on test), where it significantly outperformed other zero-shot models like GRILL (47.5%).
 - However, its performance on RefCOCO and RefCOCO+ was slightly lower compared to finetuned models. The authors attribute this discrepancy to the data distribution of these datasets, which often use shorter, less complex referring expressions. This suggests an area for future improvement in understanding simpler, more direct human expressions.

#### 4.2. Multimodal Referring (New Capabilities)

This category assesses the model's ability to understand image regions provided as bounding boxes and generate corresponding textual descriptions.
- **Referring Expression Generation (RefCOCOg):**
 - KOSMOS-2 achieved impressive zero-shot performance (CIDEr of 60.3), surprisingly outperforming a finetuned listener-speaker model (SLR, CIDEr 59.2) in a zero-shot setting.
 - When provided with few-shot demonstrations (k=4), KOSMOS-2 showed further improvement (CIDEr of 62.3), highlighting its strong in-context learning ability for this task.

#### 4.3. Perception-Language Tasks (Preserved Capabilities)

KOSMOS-2 was evaluated on standard vision-language tasks without instruction tuning to ensure its new grounding capabilities did not degrade existing performance.
- **Image Captioning (Flickr30k):** KOSMOS-2 achieved a CIDEr score of 66.7, showing a slight *improvement* over KOSMOS-1 (65.2).
- **Visual Question Answering (VQAv2):** KOSMOS-2 achieved a VQA accuracy of 45.6%, which was a marginal decrease compared to KOSMOS-1 (46.7%).
- Overall, the results indicate that KOSMOS-2 successfully integrated grounding without significantly compromising its general perception-language abilities, maintaining competitive performance.

#### 4.4. Language Tasks (Preserved Capabilities)

Evaluations on eight diverse language understanding and generation tasks demonstrated that KOSMOS-2 largely maintained its language-only proficiency.
- **General Performance:** KOSMOS-2 exhibited comparable performance to KOSMOS-1 and a dedicated LLM baseline on tasks like StoryCloze, HellaSwag, Winograd, Winogrande, and PIQA.
- **Minor Fluctuations:** There was a slight decrease in performance on the CB dataset but improvements on BoolQ and COPA.
- This indicates that the extensive training on grounded image-text data did not substantially interfere with its language modeling capabilities, showcasing a successful balance of skill acquisition across different domains.

In summary, the main findings confirm that KOSMOS-2 successfully acquires advanced grounding and referring capabilities, often achieving strong zero-shot performance competitive with or superior to finetuned specialized models. Crucially, it manages to do so while largely preserving the broad language understanding and vision-language capabilities inherited from KOSMOS-1.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

KOSMOS-2 represents a significant step forward in the development of Multimodal Large Language Models, with profound implications for human-AI interaction and the broader trajectory of AI research.

- **Foundation for Grounded MLLMs:** The most immediate significance is that KOSMOS-2 makes **grounding a foundational capability** of MLLMs. By explicitly representing visual regions within the text sequence (via location tokens and the "hyperlink" format) and training on a massive dataset of grounded image-text pairs (GRIT), the model can now intrinsically understand and generate references to specific parts of an image. This moves beyond merely describing visual content to truly "seeing" and "pointing" at it in a language-aware manner.
- **Revolutionizing Human-AI Interaction:** This work enables a more natural and intuitive human-AI interaction. Users can now "point" to objects using bounding boxes for input, and the model can respond with visual answers (bounding boxes) or ground its textual output to precise locations. This drastically reduces ambiguity, especially in complex scenes, and simplifies the user experience compared to purely textual descriptions. It unlocks practical applications like visual dialogue, where conversation can directly reference visual elements.
- **Enabling New Downstream Applications:** The integrated grounding capability facilitates a wide array of new downstream applications. These include grounded image captioning (where captions can link to specific objects), grounded visual question answering (where answers can point to relevant visual evidence), fine-grained object detection with multimodal descriptions, and advanced visual dialogue systems. These applications empower AI to provide richer, more precise, and visually verifiable responses.
- **Laying the Groundwork for Embodiment AI:** The paper explicitly states that this work "lays out the foundation for the development of Embodiment AI." For AI systems to truly interact with and perform tasks in the physical world (e.g., robotics, augmented reality), they must be able to ground abstract language commands to concrete spatial locations and objects. KOSMOS-2's ability to bridge this gap between language and precise visual perception is a critical enabling technology for such embodied agents.
- **Contribution to the "Big Convergence" and AGI:** The authors frame this work as a key step towards the "big convergence of language, multimodal perception, action, and world modeling." By unifying language understanding with precise visual grounding, KOSMOS-2 pushes AI closer to a more holistic understanding of the world, a crucial prerequisite for achieving Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). It suggests a future where AI systems can seamlessly integrate reasoning across various modalities, much like humans do.
- **Open Research Contribution:** The decision to make the code and pretrained models available to the public is highly impactful. This fosters reproducibility, encourages further research, and accelerates the development of more advanced grounded MLLMs and their applications within the broader AI community.

In essence, KOSMOS-2 transforms MLLMs from general descriptions to precise visual understanding, making them more capable, interactive, and aligned with the demands of real-world applications and the long-term vision of truly intelligent systems.
