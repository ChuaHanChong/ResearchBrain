# Kosmos-2: Grounding Multimodal Large Language Models to the World

> [!summary] Summary
> Microsoft Research developed KOSMOS-2, a multimodal large language model that integrates grounding capabilities, allowing it to perceive object descriptions (bounding boxes) and precisely link text to the visual world. The model achieved strong zero-shot performance on phrase grounding (78.7% R@1 on Flickr30k Entities) and referring expression generation (CIDEr 60.3 on RefCOCOg), while largely preserving its existing vision-language and language-only capabilities.

## Problem

- Existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) often lack robust grounding capabilities, making them unable to precisely pinpoint or refer to specific objects or regions within an image.
- Human-AI interaction was limited to high-level textual descriptions, lacking the intuitive "pointing" and "showing" fundamental to human communication about the physical world.
- MLLMs struggled to provide visual answers (e.g., bounding boxes) or to visually resolve coreference ambiguity for objects in an image.

## Method

- Introduced KOSMOS-2, a Transformer-based multimodal large language model that integrates grounding and referring capabilities by representing continuous ==bounding box coordinates as discrete location tokens==.
- Constructed ==GRIT==, a large-scale (91 million images, 115 million text spans, 137 million bounding boxes) grounded image-text dataset, by automatically extracting noun-chunk-bounding-box and referring-expression-bounding-box pairs from existing web-scale datasets.
- Employed a novel =="hyperlink" data format== to explicitly link text spans with their associated location tokens, enabling the model to learn the association between language and visual regions during training.
- Utilized ==instruction tuning with grounded instruction data== to align KOSMOS-2's capabilities with human instructions for tasks requiring visual grounding.

## Results

- KOSMOS-2 achieved **7**8.7%** R@1** zero-shot performance on Flickr30k Entities for phrase grounding, significantly outperforming a previous zero-shot model, GRILL (**1**8.9%** R@1**).
- It demonstrated strong zero-shot referring expression generation with a CIDEr score of **60.3** on RefCOCOg, surprisingly outperforming a finetuned listener-speaker model.
- The model maintained or slightly improved performance on perception-language tasks (e.g., Flickr30k Image Captioning CIDEr **66.7**, up from KOSMOS-1's **65.2**) and largely preserved language-only task proficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly encoding bounding box information as discrete location tokens within the text stream allows a causal language model to learn grounding generatively.
> - Creating a massive, automatically constructed grounded image-text dataset (GRIT) is crucial for training effective grounding capabilities in multimodal large language models.
> - A "hyperlink" format for associating text spans with bounding boxes provides a clear signal for the model to learn multimodal grounding.
> - Grounding capabilities can be integrated into multimodal large language models without significantly degrading their general perception-language or language-only abilities.
