# Unleashing the Reasoning Potential of Pre-trained LLMs by Critique Fine-Tuning on One Problem

> [!summary] Summary
> One-shot Critique Fine-Tuning (CFT) activates latent reasoning abilities in pre-trained large language models using a single problem, outperforming supervised fine-tuning and matching reinforcement learning with 1/15th to 1/20th the computational cost. This approach trains models to critique diverse incorrect solutions, fostering a deeper understanding of reasoning patterns.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods, though effective for reasoning, are computationally expensive (hundreds of GPU hours for one problem) and suffer from unstable training dynamics.
- Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) requires vast, high-quality, and expensive datasets to prevent overfitting and achieve strong generalization, which are scarce for complex reasoning tasks.
- There is a need for a more efficient and stable post-training method to unleash the latent reasoning potential of pre-trained LLMs with minimal data and computational resources.

## Method

- Construct a =='one-shot CFT dataset'== by having 10 diverse 'student' LLMs generate 100 candidate solutions for a single seed reasoning problem.
- Employ 7 high-performing 'teacher' LLMs to provide detailed ==critiques== for each of the 100 candidate solutions, yielding 600 filtered critiques per problem.
- Fine-tune pre-trained LLMs using this compact critique dataset, training the model to generate critiques (problem + candidate solution -> critique) rather than direct solutions.

## Results

- One-shot Critique Fine-Tuning (CFT) improved Qwen2.5-Math-7B's average accuracy on math benchmarks from **32.2%** to **42.2%** (**+10.0 points**).
- CFT achieved performance comparable to or superior to 1-shot RLVR while requiring significantly less compute, using approximately **5 GPU hours** compared to over **120 GPU hours** for RLVR.
- The method consistently outperformed 1-shot SFT and even full-dataset SFT, demonstrating strong generalization across mathematical and logic reasoning tasks (e.g., **+16%** average gain on BBEH logic tasks for Qwen2.5-Math-7B).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning from critiques of diverse incorrect solutions provides a richer and more effective supervisory signal for reasoning than directly learning from correct solutions.
> - A minimal, qualitatively rich dataset derived from a single problem can efficiently activate latent reasoning capabilities in LLMs, outperforming methods requiring large datasets or complex reinforcement learning.
> - Critique fine-tuning offers a significantly more computationally efficient and stable approach compared to reinforcement learning for improving LLM reasoning performance.
