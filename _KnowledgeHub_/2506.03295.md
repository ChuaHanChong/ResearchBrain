# Unleashing the Reasoning Potential of Pre-trained LLMs by Critique Fine-Tuning on One Problem

> [!summary] Summary
> One-shot Critique Fine-Tuning (CFT) activates latent reasoning abilities in pre-trained large language models using a single problem, outperforming supervised fine-tuning and matching reinforcement learning with 1/15th to 1/20th the computational cost. This approach trains models to critique diverse incorrect solutions, fostering a deeper understanding of reasoning patterns.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods, though effective for reasoning, are computationally expensive (hundreds of GPU hours for one problem) and suffer from unstable training dynamics.
- Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) requires vast, high-quality, and expensive datasets to prevent overfitting and achieve strong generalization, which are scarce for complex reasoning tasks.
- There is a need for a more efficient and stable post-training method to unleash the latent reasoning potential of pre-trained LLMs with minimal data and computational resources.

## Method

- Construct a =='one-shot CFT dataset'== by having 10 diverse 'student' LLMs generate 100 candidate solutions for a single seed reasoning problem.
- Employ 7 high-performing 'teacher' LLMs to provide detailed ==critiques== for each of the 100 candidate solutions, yielding 600 filtered critiques per problem.
- Fine-tune pre-trained LLMs using this compact critique dataset, training the model to generate critiques (problem + candidate solution -> critique) rather than direct solutions.

## Results

- One-shot Critique Fine-Tuning (CFT) improved Qwen2.5-Math-7B's average accuracy on math benchmarks from **32.2%** to **42.2%** (**+10.0 points**).
- CFT achieved performance comparable to or superior to 1-shot RLVR while requiring significantly less compute, using approximately **5 GPU hours** compared to over **120 GPU hours** for RLVR.
- The method consistently outperformed 1-shot SFT and even full-dataset SFT, demonstrating strong generalization across mathematical and logic reasoning tasks (e.g., **+16%** average gain on BBEH logic tasks for Qwen2.5-Math-7B).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning from critiques of diverse incorrect solutions provides a richer and more effective supervisory signal for reasoning than directly learning from correct solutions.
> - A minimal, qualitatively rich dataset derived from a single problem can efficiently activate latent reasoning capabilities in LLMs, outperforming methods requiring large datasets or complex reinforcement learning.
> - Critique fine-tuning offers a significantly more computationally efficient and stable approach compared to reinforcement learning for improving LLM reasoning performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of large language models (LLMs), post-training methodologies, and reasoning capabilities. The field has seen tremendous progress in LLM performance on complex tasks, with a particular emphasis on mathematical and logical reasoning.

Traditionally, improving LLM performance after initial pre-training involves two main approaches:
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** This involves training LLMs on large datasets of high-quality examples to align their behavior with specific tasks. While stable, SFT often requires massive, meticulously curated datasets (e.g., millions of examples and billions of tokens for models like Phi-4-Reasoning and AceMath), which are expensive and difficult to acquire, especially for specialized reasoning tasks where ground truth labels are scarce. Overfitting on smaller datasets is also a concern.
- **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR):** Methods like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) or RLVR have shown remarkable success in enhancing LLM capabilities, including reasoning. RLVR allows models to learn through trial-and-error by receiving feedback (rewards) on their generated outputs. Recent breakthroughs, such as 1-shot RLVR (Wang et al., 2025a), demonstrated that even a single training example could significantly "unleash" latent reasoning potential. However, RLVR is notoriously resource-intensive (often requiring hundreds of GPU hours for a single problem) and can suffer from unstable training dynamics, plasticity loss, and dependence on spurious reward signals, as highlighted by other contemporary research (Hochlehnert et al., 2025; Shao et al., 2025).

This paper introduces **Critique Fine-Tuning (CFT) on only one problem** as an alternative post-training paradigm. CFT, previously explored by Wang et al. (2025b), focuses on training models to critique diverse incorrect solutions rather than just generating correct ones. This approach is theorized to expose the model to a wider range of reasoning patterns and error types, thereby fostering a deeper understanding and mitigating overfitting. By combining CFT with the "one-shot" paradigm (training on a single problem), the authors aim to achieve the efficiency benefits of minimal data while addressing the stability and cost drawbacks of RLVR and the data dependency of SFT.

The work directly contributes to the ongoing effort to make LLM post-training more efficient, accessible, and robust. It challenges the assumption that extensive data or complex reinforcement learning setups are always necessary to unlock latent reasoning abilities, especially in smaller to medium-sized LLMs. It proposes that a rich, qualitative feedback signal (critiques) from a single problem can be as effective, if not more so, than quantitative reward signals from RL or large volumes of ground-truth solutions for SFT, particularly for improving reasoning generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop and validate a more efficient and stable method for unleashing the inherent reasoning potential of pre-trained large language models (LLMs), specifically for mathematical and logical reasoning tasks.

The motivation for this objective stems from critical limitations observed in existing post-training techniques:

1. **High Computational Cost of RLVR:** While reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has proven effective in enhancing reasoning, methods like 1-shot RLVR (Wang et al., 2025a) still demand substantial computational resources (e.g., hundreds of GPU hours for a 7B model on a single problem). This makes it prohibitive for many researchers and practitioners.
2. **Instability of RLVR:** RLVR often exhibits unstable or unreliable training dynamics due to issues like transient non-stationarity and plasticity loss. This can lead to unpredictable results and difficulties in reproduction.
3. **Data Scarcity and Overfitting in SFT:** Supervised fine-tuning (SFT), while more stable, typically requires vast volumes of high-quality, task-specific data to prevent overfitting and achieve strong generalization. For many complex reasoning tasks beyond common domains like mathematics, such large-scale datasets are scarce, limiting SFT's applicability and effectiveness.
4. **Unleashing Latent Potential:** Prior work on 1-shot RLVR indicated that base LLMs already possess immense reasoning potential that can be activated with minimal, targeted signals. This paper seeks to find a more efficient *type* of minimal signal.

Given these challenges, the authors pose a critical question: "Is there a more efficient way to unleash the reasoning potential of these powerful base LLMs?" Their key objective is to demonstrate that **Critique Fine-Tuning (CFT) on only one problem** can serve as such a method, providing RLVR-level effectiveness with significantly reduced computational cost and improved stability, while also circumventing the large data requirements of SFT.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, "One-shot Critique Fine-Tuning (CFT)," centers on creating a compact yet information-rich dataset from a single reasoning problem and using it to fine-tune pre-trained LLMs.

#### 3.1. One-shot CFT Dataset Construction

The core of the method lies in ingeniously generating high-quality critique data from a single problem through a "student-teacher" interaction paradigm:

1. **Seed Problem Selection:** The authors selected four representative math problems (π1, π2, π13, π1209) from the DeepScaleR subset, consistent with prior one-shot RLVR studies to ensure fair comparison. These problems vary in difficulty from easy to hard.
2. **Candidate Solutions Generation (Student Models):** For each selected seed problem, 10 diverse open-source LLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct, Qwen3 variants, MiMo, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B, Phi-4-reasoning) were used as "student" models. Each of these 10 models generated 10 different solutions for the single seed problem, resulting in a pool of 100 varied candidate solutions. This step is crucial for capturing a wide range of potential errors and reasoning paths.
3. **Teacher Critique Annotation (Teacher Models):** Seven high-performing, proprietary "teacher" LLMs (e.g., Claude-3.7-Sonnet, GPT-4.1-Mini, GPT-4o) were then employed to provide detailed critiques for each of the 100 candidate solutions. These critiques were instructed to analyze correctness, identify key mathematical concepts, check step-by-step reasoning, and, if incorrect, provide the correct solution. This process generated an initial 700 critiques per seed problem.
4. **Data Filtering and Subsampling:** The generated critiques underwent a filtering process to remove incorrect or inconsistent ones. To maintain uniformity and fair comparison, the remaining data was subsampled to create a unified training set of 600 critiques per seed problem. This results in a highly concentrated, expert-annotated dataset derived from minimal input.

The training data format consists of a user prompt (containing the original problem and a candidate solution) and an assistant response (the teacher-provided critique).

#### 3.2. Training Scheme

The constructed one-shot CFT datasets are then used for fine-tuning pre-trained LLMs:

1. **Training Objective:** Unlike standard Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), which trains a model to directly generate a reference solution (i.e., `(problem) -> (solution)`), Critique Fine-Tuning trains the model to generate a critique given a problem and a candidate solution (`(problem + candidate solution) -> (critique)`). This teaches the model to analyze and identify errors in reasoning, fostering a deeper understanding of the task.
2. **Model Architectures:** The experiments involved a range of Qwen and Llama family models, from 1.5B to 14B parameters, demonstrating the method's applicability across different model scales.
3. **Hyperparameters:** Full-parameter instruction tuning was applied. Consistent hyperparameters were used across experiments for fair comparison: learning rate of 5 × 10^-6, a cosine learning rate schedule, a warmup ratio of 0.1, and a global batch size of 512.
4. **Validation and Checkpointing:** Models were checkpointed every 10 steps during training. The checkpoint achieving the highest validation score (evaluated on a randomly selected 500 math problems from the MATH dataset, excluding benchmark problems) was chosen for final evaluation.

This meticulous approach to dataset creation and fine-tuning ensures a rigorous evaluation of the one-shot CFT paradigm against established methods like SFT and RLVR.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across mathematical and logical reasoning benchmarks consistently demonstrate the effectiveness and efficiency of one-shot Critique Fine-Tuning (CFT).

#### 4.1. Performance on Mathematical Reasoning

- **Significant Improvement over Base Models:** One-shot CFT consistently and substantially improves the reasoning accuracy of base LLMs across all tested scales (1.5B to 14B parameters). For example, Qwen2.5-Math-7B, after one-shot CFT, saw its average accuracy on six standard math benchmarks jump from a sober baseline of 32.2% to 42.2%, a +10.0 point improvement.
- **Outperformance of SFT:** Under the one-shot setting, CFT dramatically outperforms one-shot SFT. For Qwen2.5-Math-7B, one-shot SFT achieved 22.9%, whereas one-shot CFT reached 42.2%. Remarkably, one-shot CFT also surpassed SFT trained on a *full dataset* (25.6%), indicating the superior generalization capability derived from critique-based supervision.
- **Competitiveness with RLVR:** One-shot CFT achieved performance comparable to, and in many cases superior to, one-shot Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (RLVR). For Qwen2.5-Math-7B and Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, one-shot CFT outperformed RLVR by +2.0 and +2.1 points, respectively, demonstrating that critique-based learning can yield similar or better reasoning gains without the associated RL complexities.

#### 4.2. Training Efficiency

- **Dramatic Compute Reduction:** A standout finding is the significantly higher training efficiency of one-shot CFT compared to one-shot RLVR. CFT achieved strong performance with only 5 GPU hours of training, quickly stabilizing. In stark contrast, RLVR required over 120 GPU hours to reach similar performance levels and exhibited greater training fluctuations. This translates to CFT using approximately 1/15 to 1/20 of the compute required by RLVR, making it vastly more practical.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (Seed Examples, Diversity of Solutions)

- **Effectiveness of Seed Examples:** All selected seed problems were effective in improving reasoning. However, seeds of moderate difficulty (e.g., π1) yielded the strongest learning signal, suggesting that a balanced mix of correct and incorrect candidate solutions (and thus, richer critiques) is most beneficial. The method demonstrated robustness to the specific choice of the seed problem.
- **Diversity of Candidate Solutions:** The study confirmed that using a diverse set of solution generators (10 different models) for generating candidate solutions leads to better performance than relying on a single strong or weaker generator. This indicates that exposing the model to a wider array of error types and reasoning patterns through diverse student solutions enhances the effectiveness of critique fine-tuning.

#### 4.4. Performance on Logic Reasoning

- **Consistent Gains on BBEH:** To validate generalization beyond mathematics, one-shot CFT was tested on three challenging BIG-Bench Extra Hard (BBEH) logic reasoning subtasks (Causal Understanding, DisambiguationQA, Time Arithmetic). One-shot CFT consistently and substantially outperformed one-shot SFT across all three tasks, achieving an average gain of 16% on Qwen2.5-Math-7B (e.g., 25.2% vs 13.7% for Causal Understanding trained on in-domain example).
- **Strong Cross-Task Generalization:** The method also exhibited strong generalization capabilities across different logic subtasks, demonstrating its potential for broader reasoning applications.
- **Multi-Task Fine-Tuning:** Merging examples from multiple logic tasks into a single, three-example training set further boosted performance, showcasing scalability to slightly larger, yet still minimal, datasets.
- **Impact of Model Scale:** An ablation study on the DisambiguationQA task showed that CFT improvements hold true across different model sizes, with more pronounced benefits for larger models (e.g., Qwen2.5-14B-CFT achieving 36.6% vs. 9.2% for base). This validates the method's robustness across model scales in logic reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes a highly significant contribution to the field of large language models by introducing and thoroughly validating one-shot Critique Fine-Tuning (CFT). Its potential impact is multi-faceted:

- **Paradigm Shift in Post-Training:** The paper proposes a viable and highly efficient alternative to prevailing post-training paradigms (SFT and RLVR). By demonstrating that learning from critiques of diverse incorrect solutions to a *single problem* can outperform SFT with extensive data and match/surpass RLVR with significantly less compute, it challenges conventional wisdom about data quantity and learning signals.
- **Enhanced Accessibility to Advanced Reasoning:** The dramatic reduction in computational cost (up to 20x less GPU hours than RLVR) and data requirements makes it much more feasible for researchers and practitioners with limited resources to "unleash" the reasoning capabilities of LLMs. This democratizes access to advanced LLM alignment and optimization techniques, potentially fostering innovation in a wider array of settings.
- **Robustness and Generalizability:** The consistent improvements observed across different LLM scales (1.5B to 14B), across two distinct reasoning domains (mathematical and logical), and its robustness to the choice of seed problem highlight the method's general applicability and reliability.
- **Deeper Reasoning Acquisition:** By training models to critique, the method encourages a deeper analysis of problem-solving steps and error patterns, rather than merely memorizing correct solutions. This could lead to more robust and generalizable reasoning abilities in LLMs.
- **Practical Implications for LLM Deployment:** In scenarios where high-quality, large-scale reasoning datasets are unavailable or prohibitively expensive to collect, or where rapid iteration on model capabilities is desired, one-shot CFT offers an immediate, practical solution. This is particularly relevant for specialized domains or for quickly adapting base models to new reasoning challenges.

**Limitations Acknowledged:** The authors transparently state that their method is primarily effective for "relatively weaker language models" or "base models prior to supervised fine-tuning or distillation." For LLMs that have already undergone extensive alignment or are inherently very strong in reasoning, the improvements might be mixed or less consistent. This suggests that CFT is highly effective at activating *latent* reasoning capabilities but might have diminishing returns on already highly aligned or specialized models. Future work is needed to explore adaptations for these more capable models.

**Potential Risks:** The authors also mention potential risks, such as the misuse of enhanced models for generating plausible but incorrect mathematical content or for academic dishonesty. This highlights the ongoing ethical considerations in AI development, necessitating responsible deployment and further research into safeguards and verification mechanisms.

In conclusion, "Unleashing the Reasoning Potential of Pre-trained LLMs by Critique Fine-Tuning on One Problem" presents a compelling and highly impactful approach to improving LLM reasoning. By leveraging rich, qualitative feedback on a minimal dataset, it offers a compute-efficient and robust pathway to unlock latent capabilities, significantly advancing the field of LLM post-training and making powerful reasoning models more accessible.
