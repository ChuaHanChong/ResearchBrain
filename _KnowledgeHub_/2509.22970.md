# Robot Learning from Any Images

> [!summary] Summary
> RoLA converts any single in-the-wild image into a physically accurate, interactive robotic environment, enabling scalable generation of visuomotor demonstrations for training robust robot policies. This framework facilitates robot learning from diverse ambient visual data, allowing successful real-world deployment across various robotic systems.

## Problem

- Robot learning is hindered by the scarcity and high cost of diverse, high-quality, robot-specific training data.
- Existing data collection methods often require specialized hardware setups or controlled lab environments, limiting scalability and data diversity.
- Converting readily available non-robotic visual data into physically accurate and robot-ready demonstrations remains a significant challenge.

## Method

- **RoLA** is a three-step framework: (1) ==physical scene recovery== from a single image using ==generative priors== for objects/background and LLMs for physical properties; (2) large-scale ==visuomotor data generation== in simulation; (3) policy training and sim-to-real deployment.
- A novel ==visual blending== technique combines rendered simulation observations with the real background image using ==z-buffering== to create photorealistic and physically accurate demonstrations.
- Enables automated generation of language annotations using LLMs for training ==Vision-Language-Action (VLA)== models.

## Results

- Policies trained on RoLA-generated data achieved a **72.2%** success rate in manipulation tasks, comparable to multi-view reconstruction methods (**75.5%**).
- RoLA data generation yielded an average **76.2%** task success rate, substantially outperforming retrieval-based (**10.5%**) and pixel-editing (**11.4%**) baselines.
- Successfully deployed policies learned from single images to real **Franka** manipulators and **Unitree humanoid** robots, demonstrating real-world transferability and embodiment generalization.
- Visual blending consistently and significantly improved learning performance, with task success rates (e.g., "Pickplace Banana") increasing from **33.3%** to **80.0%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging generative foundation models and LLMs allows accurate physical scene recovery (geometry, properties, poses) from a single RGB image, circumventing the need for multi-view setups.
> - The combination of physics simulation with efficient visual blending is critical for producing training data that is both physically plausible and visually consistent, effectively bridging the sim-to-real domain gap.
> - Transforming diverse "in-the-wild" images, including those from the Internet, into interactive robotic environments significantly expands potential data sources for generalized robot learning and pre-training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

RoLA addresses one of the most significant bottlenecks in the field of robot learning: the scarcity and cost of diverse, high-quality, robot-specific training data. Traditional methods for collecting such data involve extensive human effort, specialized hardware setups, and considerable time in controlled laboratory environments. This reliance on "on-robot" demonstrations limits the scalability and diversity of data, consequently hindering the generalization capabilities of learned robotic policies.

The current research landscape has explored several avenues to mitigate this data challenge:

- **Real-to-Sim-to-Real Methods:** This prominent paradigm aims to bridge the reality gap by reconstructing real-world environments into digital twins, generating vast amounts of data in simulation, and then transferring learned policies back to real robots. Prior works in this area often rely on multi-view images or videos for 3D scene reconstruction [e.g., 4, 8, 10, 3]. While effective, their critical limitation is the requirement for ad-hoc hardware setups (multiple cameras, structured light sensors, or specific recording procedures), which confines data collection to controlled lab settings and severely limits their scalability to "in-the-wild" images, such as those found on the Internet or captured by everyday cameras. Some single-image reconstruction methods exist [e.g., 1, 2] but typically rely on retrieving existing 3D assets, which constrains their generalization to pre-defined databases.

- **Robot Learning from Unstructured Data:** Researchers have also attempted to leverage the vast amounts of non-robotic visual data (images and videos) available online. This involves extracting actionable knowledge through methods like learning from human-object interactions [e.g., 24, 25], motion retargeting [e.g., 27, 28], or affordance transfer [e.g., 31]. Recent efforts have also focused on pixel-level augmentations, such as changing backgrounds [e.g., 33] or viewpoints [e.g., 34], to synthesize diverse visual demonstrations. However, a major drawback of these pixel-editing approaches is their inability to guarantee physical accuracy or consistency in the generated robotic demonstrations, making them less reliable for training robust policies that must interact with a physical world.

- **Physics-based Scene Generation from Single Images:** A nascent area explores creating physically interactable digital twins from single images [e.g., 40, 41, 42]. While promising for physics-based video generation, these works typically focus on narrow object categories and have limited direct applications in generalized robotics tasks. CAST is a relevant work that also transforms a single image into an interactable digital twin but falls short in modeling the full background and its direct robotic applications.

RoLA positions itself at the intersection of these research fronts, offering a novel solution that overcomes their collective limitations. It fundamentally addresses the challenge of converting "raw pixels" from any single, in-the-wild image into "robot-complete data" – structured robotic scenes and demonstrations that are both physically accurate and photorealistic. By removing the need for multiview reconstruction and specialized hardware, RoLA significantly expands the potential sources of training data to virtually unlimited Internet images and casual camera captures. Its integrated approach, combining advanced single-view physical scene recovery with a novel visual blending strategy, ensures that generated data is not only diverse and abundant but also physically plausible and visually consistent, making it directly usable for training robust robot policies. This work represents a significant leap towards democratizing robot learning by making data generation accessible, scalable, and broad in scope.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind the RoLA framework is to address the critical bottleneck of data quantity and diversity in robot learning. Current methods for collecting robot-specific data are prohibitively expensive and time-consuming, limiting the scalability and generalization capabilities of robot learning systems. While non-robotic visual data is abundant, converting these "raw pixels" into "robot-complete data" (i.e., structured robotic scenes and demonstrations suitable for policy learning) remains a fundamental challenge.

The paper poses a pivotal question: "Can we obtain robot-complete data from non-robotic images under minimal assumptions—ideally from a single image?" RoLA is designed to provide an affirmative answer to this question by fundamentally rethinking the requirements for building robotic environments.

The key objectives of the RoLA framework are:

1. **Eliminate Hardware Reliance:** To remove the dependency on specialized hardware setups (like multi-camera systems or controlled environments) for real-to-sim-to-real pipelines. This allows the use of any in-the-wild image as a source for robotic environments.
2. **Enable Single-View Physical Scene Recovery:** To develop a novel method that can accurately reconstruct the physical properties of a scene (object geometry, physical properties, spatial relationships, camera pose, and robot placement) from just a single RGB image, leveraging generative foundation model priors.
3. **Achieve Photorealistic and Physically Accurate Data Generation:** To generate large-scale visuomotor robotic demonstrations that are both physically plausible (due to physics simulation) and visually photorealistic (through an efficient visual blending strategy), thereby mitigating the sim-to-real domain gap.
4. **Democratize Robotic Data Generation:** To make the creation of robotic training data accessible and scalable, transforming any single image—whether from camera captures, existing robotic datasets, or the vast Internet—into an interactive, physics-enabled robotic environment within minutes.
5. **Support Diverse Robot Learning Applications:** To facilitate a wide range of applications, including:
 - **Scalable Robotic Data Generation and Augmentation:** Providing abundant data for imitation learning policies from robot-captured images.
 - **Training Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** Generating large-scale, automatically annotated data to train more generalizable VLA models.
 - **Leveraging Internet Images for Robot Learning:** Utilizing broader in-the-wild images as priors to improve vision-based robotic manipulation tasks (e.g., pre-training grasping policies).
 - **Real-to-Sim-to-Real Systems for Various Embodiments:** Demonstrating seamless transfer from single-image-derived simulations to real manipulators and humanoid robots.

In essence, RoLA's motivation is to unlock the immense potential of ambient visual data to advance robot learning beyond the confines of specialized data collection efforts, leading to more scalable, diverse, and generalizable robotic intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The RoLA framework is structured as a three-step pipeline: (1) Real-to-Sim: Recovering the physical scene from a single image; (2) Simulation: Generating large-scale robotic data; and (3) Sim-to-Real: Deploying learned policies to real robots.

**Step 1: Recovering the Physical Scene from a Single Image (Real-to-Sim)**

The core challenge is to infer a complete physical scene `S = {O, B}` (objects and background with textured meshes `M`, positions `p`, orientations `q`, physical properties `P`, and camera parameters `C`) from a single input image `I`. This ill-posed inverse problem is tackled by leveraging generative priors.

- **Object and Background Modeling:**
 - **Object:** For each object, RoLA first uses a grounded segmentation model (e.g., Grounded SAM) to generate segmentation masks. These masks isolate object regions, which are then fed into a state-of-the-art image-to-3D generation model (e.g., Wonder3D) to produce a textured 3D mesh (`M_O`).
 - **Background:** An image inpainting model (e.g.,) synthesizes a background-only image (`I_B`). A metric depth prediction model (e.g., Depth Pro) estimates an absolute depth map `D` and camera intrinsics `K` from the original image `I`. Inverse projection converts `D` and `K` into a 3D scene point cloud (`P_S`). `P_S` is partitioned into object (`P_O`) and background (`P_B`) point clouds using the object masks. A background mesh (`M_B`) is then reconstructed from `P_B` using heightmap reconstruction or approximated by simple shape primitives (e.g., planes) for faster simulation.

- **Recovering Scene Configuration:** The scene point cloud `P_S` provides spatial structure and absolute scale. The independently generated object meshes `M_O` are scaled and registered to their corresponding point clouds `P_O` using mesh-to-point registration to establish accurate scene-object relationships. To align the scene with a physics simulator's world frame, a "supported plane" (e.g., tabletop or ground) is identified from `P_S` using ground segmentation and RANSAC. The plane's normal `n` is then mapped to the world Z-axis (gravity axis) using Rodrigues’ formula to derive the scene and camera rotation `R`. The camera translation `t` is updated to center the scene. This process fully recovers the object, background, and camera poses in a physically meaningful world frame.

- **Physical Property Estimation:** To enable realistic physics interactions, properties like mass/density and friction coefficients are estimated. RoLA leverages Large Language Models (LLMs) (e.g., GPT-4o) which, prompted with object categories and image context, infer plausible physical parameters.

- **Robot Placement:** For images from robot-mounted cameras, the robot pose is known. For general non-robotic images, a sampling-based method generates feasible robot base positions. It samples positions such that the robot’s reachable workspace covers object bounding boxes, while avoiding collisions with the scene.

**Step 2: Robotic Data Generation (Simulation)**

Once the physical scene is recovered, RoLA generates visuomotor demonstrations within this environment.

- **Data Collection:** Demonstrations can be collected through various means: direct human control (keyboard/spacemouse), scripted policies (motion planners), or pre-trained manipulation policies (e.g.,). This allows for efficient, large-scale data generation (e.g., 1,500 demonstrations per hour on an RTX4090 GPU). For each demonstration, robot actions `{a_t}`, depth maps `{D_t}`, and visual observations `{I_t}` are recorded from the original camera viewpoint.

- **Visual Blending:** To address the visual domain gap between simulation and reality, a novel visual blending technique is introduced. This method combines the rendered simulated observations `{I_t}` with the real background image `I_B` using z-buffering. A binary mask `M_t` is created by comparing the rendered depth `D_t` with the background depth `D_B`. If `D_t` is closer than `D_B` (minus a small threshold `ϵ`), the pixel comes from the rendered image; otherwise, it comes from `I_B`. This produces photorealistic `{I'_t}` observations that accurately reflect occlusions and preserve the real background, mitigating rendering artifacts and domain shift (Eq. 4).

**Step 3: Robot Learning & Deployment (Sim-to-Real)**

The generated photorealistic and physically accurate visuomotor demonstrations `{I'_t, a_t}` are used for downstream tasks:

- **Policy Training:** Policies, such as diffusion policies, are trained in simulation using this data.
- **Real-World Deployment:** Learned policies are seamlessly deployed to real robotic systems.
- **Data Augmentation:** RoLA can augment existing robotic datasets by generating diverse variations based on their input images.
- **VLA Model Training:** By automatically generating language annotations for the demonstrations using LLMs, RoLA supports the scalable training of generalizable Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.

This comprehensive methodology transforms passive visual data into a dynamic, interactive, and data-rich environment for robot learning, democratizing access to diverse training data.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of RoLA rigorously addresses several key questions, demonstrating its effectiveness across various applications.

- **Q1: Physical Scene Recovery Accuracy:** RoLA's ability to recover an accurate physical scene from a single image was compared against a multi-view reconstruction baseline. For a "pick up banana and put on stove" task, policies trained on RoLA-generated data achieved a 72.2% success rate, closely matching the 75.5% success rate of policies trained with data from multi-view reconstruction. This crucial finding indicates that, despite relying on a single image, RoLA can recover sufficient geometric and physical fidelity to enable effective robot policy learning, making it a highly scalable alternative to more cumbersome multi-view methods.

- **Q2: Robotic Data Generation Quality:** RoLA's data generation quality was benchmarked against retrieval-based (ACDC) and pixel-editing-based (RoboEngine) methods across several manipulation tasks (e.g., "Broccoli into Bowl," "Banana onto Stove," "Carrot onto Burner"). RoLA achieved an average success rate of 76.2%, significantly outperforming ACDC (10.5%) and RoboEngine (11.4%). This substantial improvement underscores that RoLA's combination of physics simulation and visual blending generates more faithful and physically accurate demonstrations compared to methods that rely solely on asset retrieval or pixel-level augmentations, leading to more robust learned policies.

- **Q3: Single-Image Robot Learning and Embodiment Generalization:** RoLA demonstrated successful real-world deployment for policies trained from single images across diverse robotic embodiments.
 - **Robotic Manipulators:** A Franka Research 3 Robot successfully performed tasks like "Object manipulation in cluttered scenes" (picking up a banana into a sink) and "Pouring water" into a cup, with high success rates in randomized trials.
 - **Humanoid Robots:** A Unitree G-1 humanoid robot, leveraging a scene reconstructed from its head-mounted camera, successfully grasped a blue cube with a dexterous hand.
 These experiments confirm RoLA's capability to support single-image, real-world robot learning and its generalizability across different robot types.

- **Q4: Training Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models with RoLA-Generated Data:** RoLA was used to generate over 60,000 visuomotor demonstrations (comparable to BridgeData V2) from 2,000 real-world images, augmented with LLM-generated language descriptions. A VLA model trained from scratch on this data (using Qwen2.5 VLM as backbone) achieved strong generalization in simulation across 10 diverse evaluation tasks (e.g., "pick up the carrot," "put the green pepper beside the lemon"), with high success rates. The VLA model was also successfully deployed to real-world scenarios, demonstrating RoLA's ability to facilitate scalable training for complex vision-language-action capabilities.

- **Q5: Impact of Visual Blending:** An ablation study specifically investigated the contribution of the visual blending technique. Comparing policies trained with visual blending versus direct mesh rendering (without blending), results showed that visual blending consistently and significantly improved learning performance across all tested tasks and robot types (e.g., "Pickplace Banana" saw an 80.0% success rate with blending vs. 33.3% without). This confirms that visual blending is crucial for enhancing the photorealism and consistency of generated demonstrations, which is vital for effective policy learning and sim-to-real transfer.

- **Learning from Internet Images:** As a case study, RoLA demonstrated the potential of leveraging in-the-wild Internet images for pre-training. By generating over 3,000 apple grasping demonstrations from 2,000 Internet images, a vision-based apple grasping prior was learned. When fine-tuned with a small number of real demonstrations (10, 20, or 50), this prior significantly increased real-world apple grasping success rates compared to training without the prior (e.g., 8/10 vs 3/10 success with 50 fine-tuning demos). This highlights the vast, untapped potential of Internet data for generalizable robot learning.

In summary, RoLA successfully demonstrates that single, in-the-wild images can be transformed into high-fidelity, interactive robotic environments for scalable and robust robot learning, enabling applications from imitation learning to complex VLA models and real-world deployment across various robot embodiments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RoLA framework represents a significant advancement in robot learning, addressing fundamental limitations and opening new avenues for research and application. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Democratization of Robot Learning:** By eliminating the need for specialized hardware setups and multi-view data collection, RoLA democratizes access to robotic data generation. Any user with a single camera image (from a phone, existing robotic dataset, or the Internet) can now create interactive, physics-enabled robotic environments. This drastically lowers the barrier to entry for researchers and hobbyists, accelerating innovation in robot learning.

- **Unprecedented Scalability of Data Generation:** RoLA provides a mechanism for generating "massive visuomotor robotic demonstrations within minutes." This capability directly tackles the primary bottleneck of data scarcity, enabling the creation of orders of magnitude more diverse training data than traditional on-robot collection methods. This scalable data generation is crucial for training complex, data-hungry models like Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and for achieving generalized robotic skills.

- **Enhanced Generalizability of Learned Policies:** By leveraging "in-the-wild" images, RoLA can expose learning agents to an unparalleled diversity of object types, scene layouts, appearances, and camera viewpoints. This rich diversity in training data is critical for developing policies that can generalize robustly to novel, unseen real-world scenarios, moving beyond the brittle performance often seen with policies trained on limited, lab-specific datasets. The Internet images case study for apple grasping is a powerful example of how pre-training on diverse, unstructured data can lead to more generalizable real-world performance.

- **Bridging the Reality Gap with Photorealism and Physical Accuracy:** RoLA effectively combines the benefits of physics simulation (for physically accurate interactions) with an innovative visual blending strategy (for photorealistic observations). This dual approach generates data that is both physically plausible and visually convincing, significantly reducing the sim-to-real domain gap. The empirical results demonstrate that this combination leads to superior learning performance compared to methods lacking either physical accuracy or photorealism.

- **Enabling Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models at Scale:** The framework's ability to automatically generate large-scale visuomotor demonstrations, coupled with LLM-generated language annotations, provides a fertile ground for training sophisticated VLA models. This is critical for advancing robots that can understand natural language instructions and execute complex tasks in varied environments, moving towards more intelligent and human-collaborative robotic systems.

- **Versatility Across Robotic Embodiments and Tasks:** RoLA's successful deployment to both robotic manipulators (Franka Research 3) and humanoid robots (Unitree G-1) demonstrates its broad applicability. The tasks range from simple pick-and-place to dynamic manipulation and tool use, highlighting the framework's versatility in supporting different robot types and complex interaction scenarios.

- **Future Research Directions:** RoLA's foundation encourages further research into leveraging vast, uncurated visual data for robot learning. While acknowledging limitations such as reliance on simulator fidelity and occasional shape misalignments from generative priors, the framework's core contribution of scalable and generalizable data generation remains robust. It prompts new questions about how to further refine single-view reconstruction, integrate more sophisticated physics models, and fully harness the diversity of Internet data to pre-train universally applicable robotic skills.

In conclusion, RoLA is a landmark contribution that shifts the paradigm of robotic data generation from resource-intensive manual collection to an automated, scalable, and generalizable process rooted in the ubiquitous availability of visual data. Its impact will likely be felt across the entire robot learning community, accelerating the development of more capable, adaptable, and intelligent robots.
