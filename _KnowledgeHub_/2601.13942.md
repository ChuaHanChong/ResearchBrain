# Glance-or-Gaze: Incentivizing LMMs to Adaptively Focus Search via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology developed Glance-or-Gaze (GoG), a framework that enables Large Multimodal Models to adaptively focus their visual search through a "Selective Gaze" mechanism and dual-stage reinforcement learning. This approach achieves state-of-the-art performance on knowledge-intensive visual question answering by dynamically combining global context with targeted region analysis.

## Problem

- Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) are limited by static parametric knowledge, leading to "knowledge cutoff" and "long-tail amnesia" for recent or obscure visual information.
- Existing search-augmented LMMs use inefficient visual strategies, often relying on indiscriminate whole-image retrieval or converting rich visual details into text, creating information bottlenecks.
- Current methods lack deep iterative reflection, preventing LMMs from adaptively refining search strategies, self-correcting, or performing multi-step visual verification.

## Method

- The ==Glance-or-Gaze (GoG)== framework transforms LMMs into active visual planners through a fully autonomous, dual-stage learning system.
- It introduces a =="Selective Gaze" mechanism== that dynamically decides whether to "glance" at global context or "gaze" into high-value image regions, filtering visual noise.
- A ==two-phase training strategy==, comprising Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for reflective behavior alignment and Reinforcement Learning (RL) for complexity-adaptive policy optimization, instills and refines these capabilities.

## Results

- GoG-3-8B-Think-RL achieves state-of-the-art performance across six diverse benchmarks, with an average **+9.89** gain over the strongest Full-Search Workflow baseline and **+19.97** over the previous MMSearch-R1.
- RL training dramatically increases "Mix Search" behavior (combining multiple search types) from **28.7%** to **76.7%** and improves Crop Selection Accuracy by **4.7-6.8%** and Reflection Rate on Errors from **30%** to **70%**.
- Ablation studies confirm the necessity of the Selective Gaze mechanism for granular evidence verification and validate the effectiveness of complexity-adaptive RL training on challenging samples.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LMMs can transition from passive image perception to active, dynamic visual planning, enabling them to intelligently focus attention and search efforts based on query complexity.
> - A dual-stage training approach effectively instills foundational search behaviors via SFT and then refines adaptive, iterative reasoning and robust policy optimization through RL.
> - Explicitly controlling visual attention with a "Selective Gaze" mechanism provides a physical anchor for reasoning, allowing for granular evidence verification and efficient information filtering before external retrieval.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) have demonstrated capabilities in visual understanding and reasoning through extensive visual-text pre-training. However, a limitation exists in their ability to handle knowledge-intensive queries that involve long-tail entities, evolving information, or require up-to-date facts not present in their static parametric knowledge. This limitation often leads to "knowledge cutoff" or "long-tail amnesia," resulting in factual hallucinations or generic responses.

To address these deficiencies, the research community has explored various approaches to augment LMMs with external search mechanisms. These approaches can be broadly categorized:
1. **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)**: Initial methods extended text-based RAG to the multimodal domain, retrieving context from fixed knowledge bases. These systems often exhibit rigidity and fail to adapt to the dynamic nature of the open web.
2. **Prompt-Engineered Agents**: Subsequently, approaches emerged that leveraged LMMs' planning capabilities through prompt engineering to orchestrate search engines. While offering flexibility, these systems typically operate in a "plug-and-play" manner, without optimizing the LMM's intrinsic search capabilities.
3. **Tool-Integrated LMMs**: More recent advancements incorporate search actions directly into the LMM's training process, aligning internal representations with external retrieval behaviors, often involving reinforcement learning to optimize web navigation policies.

Despite these developments, the paper identifies two critical limitations in current methodologies:
- **Inefficient and noisy visual strategies**: Existing visual search methods often rely on rigid cropping and indiscriminately process all candidate visual regions, leading to substantial visual redundancy and noise. Additionally, they frequently convert visual details into text descriptions, creating an information bottleneck that restricts access to raw visual data.
- **Lack of deep iterative reflection**: Current approaches tend to execute tool calls in a single-pass manner or limit reflection strictly to the textual modality. They lack support for iterative visual verification or self-correction, limiting their ability to adaptively refine strategies for complex visual questions.

This work, Glance-or-Gaze (GoG), aims to bridge these gaps by proposing a fully autonomous framework that transitions from passive image perception to active, complexity-adaptive visual planning. It distinguishes itself by introducing a dynamic selective gaze mechanism to filter visual noise before retrieval and integrating iterative cross-modal reflection, thus moving beyond single-pass or text-only reasoning in search-augmented LMMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework that enables Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) to overcome their inherent limitations with knowledge-intensive queries, particularly those involving long-tail entities, evolving information, or fine-grained visual details not covered by their static parametric knowledge.

The motivation for this objective stems from several identified challenges with existing LMMs and search-augmented approaches:
1. **Addressing Knowledge Cutoff and Long-Tail Amnesia**: LMMs, constrained by their fixed training corpora, frequently fail to provide accurate or up-to-date information for queries concerning recent events, niche concepts, or obscure visual entities. This results in "knowledge cutoff" or "long-tail amnesia," leading to factual inaccuracies or uninformative responses. The research seeks to empower LMMs to access and integrate external, dynamic knowledge effectively.
2. **Mitigating Visual Redundancy and Noise in Search**: Existing search-augmented LMMs often employ indiscriminate whole-image retrieval or rigid cropping. This process introduces significant visual redundancy and noise, potentially overwhelming the model with irrelevant information and hindering its ability to identify pertinent details. A key motivation is to develop a mechanism that can intelligently filter visual information, focusing search on high-value regions.
3. **Enabling Iterative and Adaptive Visual Reasoning**: Current search-augmented LMMs typically perform tool calls in a single-pass manner or restrict their reflection and self-correction to the textual modality. This limitation prevents models from adaptively refining their search strategies or visually verifying information through iterative interaction. The objective is to cultivate a framework that supports multi-step, iterative visual planning and cross-modal reflection, allowing the model to adapt its focus based on query complexity.
4. **Shifting from Passive Perception to Active Visual Planning**: The overarching motivation is to transform LMMs from passive information processors into active agents capable of dynamic visual planning. This involves empowering the model to autonomously decide *when* and *where* to search, and *how* to refine its search based on ongoing reasoning, rather than simply reacting to predefined prompts or static retrieval results.

To achieve these objectives, the Glance-or-Gaze (GoG) framework proposes a Selective Gaze mechanism for dynamic attention allocation and a dual-stage training strategy (supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning) to instill fundamental active selection behaviors and enhance complexity-adaptive reasoning capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Glance-or-Gaze (GoG) framework employs a dual-stage learning architecture to transform LMMs into active visual planners, capable of complexity-adaptive visual reasoning.

#### 3.1. Overview of the Dual-Stage Learning Framework

The framework consists of two sequential stages:
1. **Reflective GoG Behavior Alignment**: This initial stage uses supervised fine-tuning (SFT) to teach the LMM the fundamental paradigm of active visual selection and cross-modal reflection. It addresses inefficiencies and noise in existing visual strategies by instilling the ability to suppress ungrounded generation and proactively trigger "Glance" or "Gaze" actions.
2. **Complexity-Adaptive Reinforcement Learning (RL)**: Building on the SFT stage, this stage further refines the model's planning and reasoning capabilities. It uses reinforcement learning to optimize the selection policy, enabling intelligent, iterative reflection and adaptive focus search based on visual query complexity.

#### 3.2. Stage 1: Reflective GoG Behavior Alignment

This stage focuses on instilling active selection and cross-modal reflection through imitation learning.

##### 3.2.1. GoG-Instruct Data Construction

A specialized dataset, GoG-Instruct, is curated from FVQA and InfoSeek datasets through a three-step pipeline:
1. **Uncertainty-Aware Filtering**: The Qwen3-VL-235B-Instruct model is used to generate answers four times for each query. Only samples where the model answers inconsistently (correct answer count `n < 4`) are retained, as these queries likely require external visual verification. Trivial samples (consistent correct answers) are discarded.
2. **GoG Trajectory Synthesis**: For the filtered queries, reasoning trajectories are synthesized with explicit visual planning structures. Each trajectory follows a sequence: (1) **Glance** for global image analysis, (2) **Decision** for tool invocation or region cropping, and (3) **Gaze** for targeted search execution on selected regions.
3. **Human Verification**: Expert annotators validate each synthesized trajectory. Two criteria are applied: (1) **Answer Accuracy**, ensuring the final response is factually correct, and (2) **Visual Rationality**, confirming that cropped regions are logically relevant to the query. Trajectories failing either criterion are discarded.

The resulting GoG-Instruct dataset comprises 5,750 samples, with 43.5% being search-free instances and 56.5% requiring various search operations (text search, image search, or both).

##### 3.2.2. Training Objective

The base models used are Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct and Qwen3-VL-7B-Think. LoRA adapters (rank 8) are applied across all transformer blocks for parameter-efficient training. The model is trained on multi-turn conversations containing reasoning sequences and structured tool calls using a standard Causal Language Modeling (Causal LM) objective. The training is performed for 3 epochs with a learning rate of 1e-4, a cosine learning rate scheduler, and bf16 mixed precision.

#### 3.3. Stage 2: Complexity-Adaptive Reinforcement Learning

This stage optimizes the planning policy for robustness, addressing the SFT model's potential lack of flexibility for varying query difficulties.

##### 3.3.1. RL Data Construction

Complexity-stratified data is constructed by evaluating the GoG-SFT model on raw queries and categorizing them into two difficulty levels:
- **Level 1**: Queries where the GoG-SFT model has an approximate 50% pass rate. These samples represent decision boundaries for policy optimization.
- **Level 2**: Queries where the GoG paradigm consistently fails, resulting in a lower overall pass rate. Training on this level is designed to force the model to develop more robust and complex reasoning strategies.
The Level 2 data is used as the final RL training set to maximize the model's capability for complex visual reasoning.

##### 3.3.2. Training Objective

Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is employed to optimize the policy on the Level 2 dataset. For each multimodal input, a group of candidate trajectories is sampled from the current policy. Each trajectory receives a reward, and a group-normalized advantage is computed. The objective function includes a clipped surrogate objective and a KL divergence penalty to prevent excessive deviation from the reference model.
The reward function combines two components:
- **Accuracy Score (`r_acc`)**: A binary score (0 or 1) judged by gpt-oss-120b based on semantic correctness against ground truth.
- **Format Score (`r_fmt`)**: A score in validating correct usage of special tokens and structural integrity.
The total reward is a weighted sum of `r_acc` and `r_fmt`. The RL training runs for 15 epochs, with an actor learning rate of 2e-6.

#### 3.4. Integrated Tools

The GoG framework integrates several external tools:
- **Search Tools**:
 - **Image Search Tool**: Built upon SerpAPI. It takes an input image URL and returns a set of visually similar webpages with metadata. The system ranks and extracts up to five results as thumbnail-title pairs.
 - **Text Search Tool**: A pipeline comprising SerpAPI for initial text search, Jina Reader for converting webpage content into structured text, and Qwen3-32B as a summarizer to extract question-relevant information, filtering irrelevant content.
- **Grounding Tool**: Based on Grounding DINO, this tool identifies and returns the top-n bounding boxes most similar to a given image search query (n=5 in implementation). A **Gaze Selection mechanism** further filters these bounding boxes to select 1 to n most question-relevant grounding results, which are then passed to the image search tool.

#### 3.5. Experiment Setup

##### 3.5.1. Benchmarks

GoG is evaluated on six diverse benchmarks:
- **In-distribution (IID)**: InfoSeek (2,000 sampled instances) and FVQA-test.
- **Out-of-distribution (OOD)**: SimpleVQA, MMSearch, DynVQA, and LiveVQA-New (2,000 sampled instances).
All datasets are filtered to retain only English-language samples.

##### 3.5.2. Metrics

An LLM-as-a-Judge approach is used, where gpt-oss-120b assesses the accuracy of generated answers against ground truth.

##### 3.5.3. Baselines

GoG is compared against four categories of baselines:
1. **Direct Answer**: Models respond directly to the image and question without external retrieval (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL, Qwen3-VL, GPT-4o).
2. **Full-Search Workflow**: Retrieval is mandatory for every query, typically involving a two-round prompting strategy with image and text search.
3. **Prompt-based GoG Agents**: Utilize Graph-of-Thought reasoning via prompting (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL, Qwen3-VL).
4. **Search-Equipped LMMs**: Models specifically trained to incorporate search actions, such as MMSearch-R1.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that the Glance-or-Gaze (GoG) framework achieves performance improvements across a range of benchmarks and provides insights into the effectiveness of its components.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Performance

- **Overall Superiority**: GoG-3-8B-Think-RL consistently surpasses all baseline paradigms, exhibiting robust generalization across both in-domain and out-of-domain datasets. It outperforms the strongest Full-Search Workflow and Prompt-based GoG agent by +9.89 and +15.18 on average, respectively. Compared to the previous search-equipped baseline MMSearch-R1, GoG achieves a +19.97 improvement.
- **Adaptive Planning**: While Full-Search workflows improve upon Direct Answer baselines by retrieving external knowledge, they often fall short of GoG due to a lack of learned discrimination to filter irrelevant noise or determine when search is strictly necessary. GoG's active planning paradigm enables this discernment.

#### 4.2. Evolution of Search Behavior with SFT and RL

- **SFT establishes basic search capability**: After Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), both Qwen3-VL-Think and Qwen2.5-VL models primarily rely on single-type searches (62.3% and 50.2%, respectively). A notable proportion of samples (9.0% for Qwen3-VL-Think and 30.4% for Qwen2.5-VL) require no search, indicating that SFT teaches the model when and how to invoke search tools for straightforward queries.
- **RL enhances iterative reasoning**: Following Reinforcement Learning (RL) training, the proportion of "mix search" (combining multiple search types like text, image, and crop) increases significantly for both models—from 28.7% to 76.7% for Qwen3-VL-Think and from 19.4% to 74.0% for Qwen2.5-VL. Concurrently, the "no-search" ratio decreases to below 3%. This shift indicates that RL encourages more complex, multi-step information gathering and iterative verification, cultivating a more deliberate reasoning process.

#### 4.3. Effectiveness of Selective Gaze

- **Improved Crop Selection Accuracy**: RL training sharpens the model's ability to identify answer-relevant image regions. The Crop Selection Accuracy (proportion of selected crops containing answer-relevant information) improves after RL training: Qwen3-VL-Think shows a +6.8% gain (from 42.1% to 48.9%), and Qwen2.5-VL increases by +4.7% (from 46.7% to 51.3%). This indicates that RL refines the model's focus, rather than merely increasing tool use frequency.
- **Enhanced Gaze Correctness and Reflection**: A manual analysis of Gaze operations for GoG-3-8B-Think shows that RL training improves Gaze correctness from 59% to 75%. Furthermore, RL-trained models demonstrate enhanced error awareness: when an initial Gaze is incorrect, the reflection rate (triggering self-correction) increases from 30% for SFT models to 70% for RL-trained models. This suggests that RL instills better-calibrated uncertainty, prompting the model to recognize misguided focus and actively seek alternative regions.

#### 4.4. Effectiveness of Complexity-Adaptive RL Training

- **Benefits of Training on Hard Samples**: An ablation study on data difficulty for RL training demonstrates that training on "Level 2" data (samples where the SFT model's pass rate is < 50%) consistently outperforms training on "Level 1" data (pass rate ≥ 50%). For Qwen2.5-VL-7B, training with Level 2 data yields a +5.94 average gain over Level 1 data, with notable boosts on complex reasoning benchmarks like MMSearch (+5.85) and LiveVQA (+4.75). Similar improvements (+3.49 average gain) are observed for Qwen3-VL-8B-Think. These results validate the strategy of focusing RL optimization on challenging samples, as they provide richer learning signals for policy refinement.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study on Selective Gaze Mechanism

- **Necessity of Selective Gaze**: Removing the Selective Gaze (SG) mechanism, which leads to a regression to a coarse-grained global view without dynamic visual cropping and reflection, results in consistent performance degradation. Average scores drop by 1.52 points for Qwen2.5 and 1.83 points for Qwen3 backbones. This confirms that the "Selective-Gaze-and-Reflect" paradigm is essential, particularly for complex scenarios where target information may be minute or obscured. The SG mechanism facilitates granular evidence verification, grounding the model's reasoning and reducing hallucinations in noisy environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Glance-or-Gaze (GoG) framework introduces a novel paradigm for Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) by shifting from passive image perception to active, complexity-adaptive visual planning. This work has several areas of significance and potential impact:

1. **Enhanced LMM Capabilities for Knowledge-Intensive Tasks**: GoG directly addresses a fundamental limitation of LMMs: their struggle with knowledge-intensive queries involving long-tail entities, evolving information, or fine-grained visual details. By augmenting LMMs with dynamic, reflective search capabilities, the framework enables them to access and integrate external, up-to-date knowledge more effectively, reducing instances of "knowledge cutoff" and factual hallucinations.
2. **Improved Visual Search Efficiency and Precision**: The Selective Gaze mechanism represents a key contribution by enabling dynamic allocation of visual attention. Unlike previous methods that rely on indiscriminate whole-image retrieval or rigid cropping, GoG can intelligently filter visual noise and focus search on high-value regions. This precision reduces visual redundancy and the information bottleneck caused by converting visual data to text, leading to more accurate and efficient retrieval.
3. **Cultivation of Iterative and Adaptive Reasoning**: The dual-stage training strategy, combining Reflective GoG Behavior Alignment via SFT and Complexity-Adaptive Reinforcement Learning, instills the capacity for multi-step, iterative reasoning. This allows the model to adapt its search strategy based on the complexity of the visual query, self-correcting and refining its focus through cross-modal reflection. This capability enhances the model's robustness and flexibility in handling diverse and challenging visual questions.
4. **Advancement in Autonomous Multimodal Agents**: By demonstrating state-of-the-art performance across diverse benchmarks and showcasing emergent complex search behaviors, GoG establishes a promising direction for building more capable and autonomous multimodal search agents. Such agents could dynamically interact with the visual world, decide what information is most relevant, and strategically gather evidence to answer complex inquiries, moving beyond pre-programmed responses.
5. **Robust Generalization Across Domains**: The framework's consistent performance gains across both in-domain and out-of-domain benchmarks underscore its generalization capabilities. This suggests that the learned active planning and selective gaze mechanisms are not merely memorized behaviors but rather transferable skills that enhance visual reasoning in novel contexts.

While the framework primarily focuses on English-language benchmarks and encounters occasional network stability issues inherent to web retrieval, its advancements in adaptive visual search and iterative reasoning contribute to the broader field of LMMs, offering a pathway towards more intelligent and context-aware multimodal AI systems.
