# R1-Reward: Training Multimodal Reward Model Through Stable Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> R1-Reward introduces StableReinforce, a reinforcement learning framework for training multimodal reward models (MRMs) that integrates explicit reasoning. The method improves training stability and enables MRMs to achieve new state-of-the-art performance across multiple benchmarks while demonstrating human-like reasoning and self-correction.

## Problem

- Existing multimodal large language models (MLLMs) face challenges in alignment with human preferences, and their reward models (MRMs) often lack long-term reasoning capabilities.
- Directly applying standard reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms like PPO to train reward models typically leads to significant training instability and potential collapse due to issues like unstable advantage normalization and unconstrained policy updates.
- Traditional reward models primarily output scalar values, lacking transparency and failing to leverage rich human feedback or an explicit reasoning process before making judgments.

## Method

- **R1-Reward** introduces ==StableReinforce==, a refined RL algorithm featuring 'Pre-CLIP' to prevent extreme policy ratio changes and an 'Advantage Filter' to mitigate outliers during advantage normalization, ensuring stable training.
- A novel, multi-component reward function is proposed, which includes a formatting reward, a result reward based on ground truth, and a consistency reward using an external MLLM to supervise the coherence between internal reasoning and the final answer.
- A progressive training strategy is employed, starting with supervised fine-tuning using GPT-4o-generated thought processes for 200K preference samples, followed by RL training focused on 'difficult' samples that require deeper reasoning.

## Results

- R1-Reward establishes new state-of-the-art performance on multimodal reward benchmarks, achieving **71.92%** accuracy on VL-Reward Bench (a **9.3%** improvement over previous best open-source models) and a **14.3%** improvement on Multimodal Reward Bench.
- The StableReinforce algorithm demonstrates significantly faster and more stable policy loss convergence compared to baseline RL methods, while also reducing response length by approximately **15%** during training.
- The model exhibits qualitative 'Aha moments' and self-reflection, correcting calculation errors and re-evaluating questions, demonstrating activation of genuine reasoning and self-correction capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning can be effectively applied to train multimodal reward models, improving their long-term reasoning capabilities beyond traditional supervised methods.
> - Algorithmic refinements like Pre-CLIP and Advantage Filter are crucial for achieving stable reinforcement learning training in the context of reward modeling, addressing common issues like divergence and numerical instability.
> - Designing a multi-component reward function that supervises both the final outcome and the internal 'thought process' (consistency reward) enables the model to develop explicit, interpretable reasoning and self-correction abilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of advanced Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) is a central and rapidly evolving area within AI research. These models, capable of processing and generating content across various modalities (text, image, potentially video and audio), represent a significant step towards more general-purpose AI. A critical challenge in MLLM development is "alignment"—ensuring that model outputs are helpful, harmless, and aligned with human preferences and instructions. Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) has emerged as the dominant paradigm for achieving this alignment, and at its core lies the **Reward Model (RM)**.

Historically, the focus in improving Reward Models (both text-only and multimodal) has largely been on two fronts:
1. **Model Architecture:** Designing more effective neural network structures for RMs.
2. **Training Data:** Curating larger and higher-quality human preference datasets for training RMs.

While these approaches have yielded substantial improvements, the authors identify a critical gap: **limited exploration into using Reinforcement Learning (RL) directly to enhance the *reward modeling capabilities* of MRMs, particularly concerning their long-term reasoning abilities.** Most existing RL applications in MLLMs use RL to improve the *MLLM's generative policy* based on a *fixed* reward model. This paper flips that script, asking: can RL be used to train a *better reward model*?

The paper positions itself by addressing the inherent limitations of standard reward models, which often output scalar values lacking transparency and fail to fully leverage rich human feedback like textual explanations. By reformulating reward modeling as a "rule-based RL task" (where the policy decides which answer is better and the reward is consistency with ground truth), this work enters a novel space. It draws inspiration from recent advancements in rule-based RL for LLMs, such as OpenAI's O1 model and DeepSeek-R1, which aim to incentivize reasoning capabilities. However, it explicitly states that, to their knowledge, no prior work has applied this RL paradigm to *multimodal reward modeling*.

Therefore, this research is significant as it proposes a new direction for MRM improvement, moving beyond static architectures and data scales to leverage the dynamic and reasoning-inducing power of RL directly on the reward model itself. It aims to endow MRMs with "long-term reasoning capabilities" by enabling them to learn an explicit thought process before making a judgment, thereby enhancing interpretability and robustness.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind the R1-Reward paper are multifaceted:

1. **Enhance Multimodal Reward Model (MRM) Performance:** The overarching goal is to significantly improve the accuracy and reliability of MRMs, which are crucial components for aligning MLLMs with human preferences and for downstream applications like data filtering and test-time selection.
2. **Activate Long-Term Reasoning in MRMs:** A key hypothesis is that RL can be used to induce and activate long-term reasoning capabilities within MRMs. Unlike traditional RMs that directly output a score, the authors aim for a model that can explicitly reason about its judgment process (e.g., via a "think" phase) and learn to make consistent, coherent decisions.
3. **Reformulate Reward Modeling as an RL Task:** The paper seeks to formally recast the reward modeling problem as a rule-based reinforcement learning task. In this formulation, the MRM's "policy" decides which of two answers is better, and the "reward" is based on the consistency of this judgment with the ground truth.
4. **Address Instability in Applying RL to Reward Modeling:** A primary practical motivation is to overcome the significant training instability and potential for collapse observed when directly applying existing RL algorithms (like PPO and Reinforce++) to the reward modeling problem. The authors pinpoint specific causes of this instability:
 - **Limitations of PPO/Related Algorithms:** Simple clipping in PPO's loss function often fails to prevent divergence, especially when advantage is negative and policy changes are large.
 - **Instability of Advantage Normalization:** In reward modeling, batches can have very low variance (e.g., mostly correct predictions), leading to extreme advantage values after Z-normalization, causing training instability.
 - **Inconsistency Between Reasoning and Results:** Models might learn to produce correct final answers without coherent or consistent intermediate reasoning, leading to unreliable "black box" behavior. This highlights a need for explicit supervision of the reasoning process.
5. **Develop a Stable and Effective RL Algorithm for MRM Training:** To tackle the instability, the authors aim to propose "StableReinforce," a refined RL algorithm designed specifically for the unique challenges of training reward models.
6. **Create a High-Quality Dataset for RL-driven MRM Training:** To facilitate the RL training, the authors intend to collect and curate a substantial preference dataset, focusing on "difficult" samples to maximize the learning signal for reasoning.

By achieving these objectives, the authors aim to advance the state-of-the-art in MRMs, making them more robust, interpretable, and effective for aligning future generations of MLLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The R1-Reward paper introduces a novel methodology for training Multimodal Reward Models (MRMs) through a stable reinforcement learning (RL) approach, termed **StableReinforce**. The approach addresses the inherent challenges of applying RL to reward modeling through algorithmic refinements, a meticulously designed reward function, and a progressive training strategy.

#### 3.1. StableReinforce Algorithm

StableReinforce refines existing RL methods (specifically building upon Reinforce++) with three key modifications to ensure stable training dynamics:

1. **Pre-CLIP (Policy Loss Refinement):**
 - **Problem:** Traditional PPO loss can lead to numerical instability. Specifically, the ratio `exp(log_probs - old_log_probs)` can overflow when `log_probs` and `old_log_probs` differ significantly. Furthermore, if the advantage is negative, a large ratio can lead to an excessively large negative loss, destabilizing the optimizer.
 - **Solution:** StableReinforce introduces a pre-clipping mechanism. Instead of directly computing `exp(log_diff)`, it first clips the `log_diff` (i.e., `log(new_policy_prob / old_policy_prob)`) within a stable range (e.g., `[log(1e-3), log(1e3)]`) *before* exponentiating to calculate the ratio. This prevents extreme probability ratios, especially when updates are large or advantages are negative, ensuring numerical stability and preventing catastrophic loss values.

2. **Advantage Filter (Advantage Estimation Strategy):**
 - **Problem:** In reward modeling, training batches can become highly imbalanced (e.g., almost all samples yielding a reward of 1). When applying Z-normalization (`(A - μ_A) / σ_A`) to such low-variance distributions, the standard deviation (`σ_A`) can be very small, causing some advantage values to become extremely large or small outliers. These outliers can lead to unstable updates.
 - **Solution:** StableReinforce employs a "3-sigma rule" based advantage filter. After standardizing the advantages, only values within `[-3, 3]` standard deviations from the mean are retained for optimization; others are effectively ignored (set to 0 in the loss calculation). This robustly removes the impact of numerical outliers while preserving the benefits of normalization for the majority of the data.

#### 3.2. Reward Function Design

The paper proposes a novel, multi-component reward function to guide the MRM, moving beyond simple scalar outputs:

1. **Formatting Reward:** Encourages the model to adhere to a specific output format: `<think>Your detailed comparative analysis</think><answer>1/2</answer>`. This ensures the model explicitly generates a reasoning process before a final decision, promoting interpretability.
2. **Result Reward:** The primary reward, aligning the model's final binary decision (1 or 2) with the human preference ground truth.
3. **Consistency Reward:**
 - **Problem:** The model might learn to produce correct final answers without logically sound reasoning (e.g., `<think>...response 2 is better</think><answer>1</answer>`). This can lead to incoherent internal logic.
 - **Solution:** An external Multimodal Large Language Model (specifically Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) acts as a "referee" to evaluate the consistency between the model's self-generated reasoning (`<think>`) and its final output (`<answer>`). This reward component directly supervises the coherence of the reasoning process.
 - **Final Reward Formula:** `Final Reward = Result Reward × (1 + 0.5 × Consistency Reward) + 0.5 × Formatting Reward`. This design is crucial: the Consistency Reward only positively contributes when the `Result Reward` is correct. This prevents the model from being rewarded for consistent but incorrect answers, ensuring accuracy remains paramount while encouraging logical coherence.

#### 3.3. Training Data and Strategy

The training process follows a progressive difficulty strategy:

1. **Dataset Construction:** A comprehensive dataset, `R1-Reward-200K`, is collected, comprising 200,000 preference samples from diverse public multimodal preference datasets (e.g., MM-RLHF, RLAIF-V, VL-Feedback). This ensures data quality, diversity, and balanced answer distribution.
2. **Long-Cot Cold Start (Supervised Fine-Tuning - SFT):**
 - **Motivation:** Directly applying RL to an MLLM not initially trained for reward modeling yields poor and unstable results.
 - **Approach:** GPT-4o (a powerful proprietary model) is used to generate a "thinking process" for each sample in `R1-Reward-200K`, following the defined prompt format (Table 1). This creates initial SFT data, `R1-Reward-200K`, to teach the base model (QwenVL-2.5-7B-Instruct) the fundamental format and task. GPT-4o's "difficulty" (number of attempts to get the correct answer) for each sample is also recorded.
3. **RL Training Data Selection:**
 - **Motivation:** Focus RL on challenging examples where reasoning is critical.
 - **Approach:** Only "difficult" samples are chosen for the RL phase: those where GPT-4o required at least two attempts to get the correct answer, or where it failed to provide the correct answer even after three attempts. This targets the RL training on cases where the distinction between answers is subtle, thus forcing the model to learn more nuanced, long-term reasoning.

By combining algorithmic stability enhancements, a structured reward function, and a focused training data strategy, R1-Reward aims to train a highly capable and interpretable multimodal reward model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The R1-Reward paper presents compelling results demonstrating the effectiveness of their StableReinforce algorithm and training methodology, both quantitatively on standard benchmarks and qualitatively through analysis of the model's behavior.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Performance on Multimodal Reward Benchmarks

R1-Reward consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art (SOTA) models across three major multimodal reward benchmarks:

- **VL-Reward Bench (Table 3):** R1-Reward achieves an average accuracy of **71.92%**, a significant **9.3% improvement** over the previous best open-source model, IXC-2.5-Reward. Notably, R1-Reward achieves this with **200K training samples**, compared to IXC-2.5-Reward's >1 million, highlighting superior data efficiency. It also surpasses leading closed-source models like Gemini-1.5-Pro.
- **Multimodal Reward Bench (Table 4):** R1-Reward demonstrates a **14.3% improvement** over the previous SOTA, achieving the best performance across all dimensions of this diverse benchmark. This benchmark's minimal overlap with their training data underscores R1-Reward's strong generalization ability.
- **MM-RLHF-Reward Bench (Table 5):** This benchmark is particularly challenging. R1-Reward achieves **80.59% Acc** and **54.35% Acc+**. While MM-RLHF-Reward (the previous SOTA) had higher Acc+ (63%), the authors note that MM-RLHF-Reward was trained on a highly aligned dataset. R1-Reward maintains balanced performance across all benchmarks, showing better generalization.

#### 4.2. Inference-Time Scalability (Test-Time Scaling)

Figure 4 illustrates R1-Reward's robust performance improvement with increased inference-time sampling (temperature 1.0):

- **Majority Voting ("Vote"):** Accuracy consistently increases as the number of samples (K) grows, significantly outperforming previous SOTA models, particularly for K < 5. This indicates that even with a few samples, R1-Reward can achieve higher reliability.
- **"Any Correct":** This metric, which counts a success if at least one sampled result is correct, approaches **100% accuracy at K=15**. This remarkable finding suggests that the model intrinsically possesses the "potential to perfectly classify all samples," even if a single greedy decoding attempt doesn't always yield the optimal output. It implies the model has learned the correct underlying logic, and diverse sampling can expose it.

#### 4.3. Training Stability and Efficiency

Figure 2 provides a detailed comparison between StableReinforce and the baseline Reinforce++:

- **Policy Loss Convergence (Figure 2a):** StableReinforce exhibits significantly **faster and more stable convergence** of the policy loss, contrasting sharply with Reinforce++'s tendency to diverge or collapse around step 150. This validates the effectiveness of the proposed stability enhancements (Pre-CLIP and Advantage Filter).
- **Response Length During Training (Figure 2b):** StableReinforce effectively performs **length compression** during training, reducing the average response length by approximately 15% compared to the base model after RL training. This suggests improved efficiency in reasoning, as the model learns to be concise while maintaining accuracy. Reinforce++, again, collapses and shows erratic length behavior.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies and Data Efficiency

Table 6 and Figure 5 highlight the critical role of each component of StableReinforce:

- **Data Efficiency (Table 6):** Training a traditional reward model or MM-RLHF-Reward (with critic generation) on the same 200K dataset yields significantly poorer performance compared to R1-Reward (StableReinforce). This demonstrates that the RL-based approach with StableReinforce vastly enhances reward modeling capabilities, achieving high performance with less data than previous SOTA models. The SFT-only version also outperforms traditional RMs, suggesting the prompt format is effective.
- **Necessity of Consistency Reward:** Removing the Consistency Reward leads to "significant hallucination behaviors" and unstable evaluation outcomes, emphasizing its crucial role in aligning reasoning with the final output.
- **Impact of Advantage Filter and Pre-CLIP (Figure 5):** Ablations show that removing either the Advantage Filter or Pre-CLIP modules results in:
 - Decreased final accuracy (Table 6).
 - Reduced training stability (policy loss becomes more volatile, Figure 5a, 5c).
 - Failure of the model's output length to converge to shorter, efficient values (Figure 5b, 5d).
 - Direct application of Reinforce++ (without these components) causes the model to "crash," with loss becoming NaN and garbled, maximal-length output.

#### 4.5. "Aha Moment" and Self-Reflection (Qualitative)

Figures 3 and 6 provide qualitative examples of R1-Reward's output:

- The model learns to define the goal, analyze input, attempt problem-solving, evaluate responses, and provide a final ranking—a structured "human-like" thought process.
- Crucially, it demonstrates **self-reflective patterns ("Aha moments")**. In Figure 3, it corrects a calculation error after rechecking. In Figure 6, it identifies its initial misunderstanding of the question ("Wait, re-reading the question") and then re-evaluates to arrive at the correct conclusion. This showcases the activation of genuine reasoning and self-correction, which is a hallmark of intelligent behavior and a direct outcome of the Consistency Reward and RL training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The R1-Reward paper makes several significant contributions with far-reaching potential impacts on the field of multimodal AI and reinforcement learning:

1. **Pioneering RL for Reward Model Training:** This work is the first to demonstrate the effective application of reinforcement learning directly to train a multimodal reward model. By reformulating reward modeling as a rule-based RL task, it opens up a fundamentally new paradigm for improving these critical alignment components, moving beyond traditional supervised learning.
2. **Achieving Stable Reinforcement Learning in a Challenging Domain:** The proposed StableReinforce algorithm successfully addresses long-standing challenges of training instability in RL, particularly when applied to reward modeling. The innovations of `Pre-CLIP` and `Advantage Filter` are crucial for robust training, providing generalizable techniques that could benefit other RL applications, especially those dealing with low-variance or highly imbalanced reward signals.
3. **Inducing Long-Term Reasoning and Interpretability:** By designing a multi-faceted reward function that includes a "Consistency Reward" and training a "think-first" format, the paper successfully encourages and supervises the development of explicit, human-like reasoning processes within the MRM. This significantly enhances the interpretability and trustworthiness of reward models, moving them beyond black-box scalar outputs to transparent decision-making agents. The observed "Aha moments" are a testament to this deeper reasoning capability.
4. **Significant Performance and Data Efficiency Gains:** R1-Reward achieves new state-of-the-art results on multiple challenging multimodal reward benchmarks. Crucially, it does so with significantly less training data (200K samples compared to 1M+ for some baselines), demonstrating remarkable data efficiency. This reduces the computational and annotation resources required to train high-performing reward models, making advanced MRMs more accessible.
5. **Enhanced Reliability and Scalability:** The strong test-time scaling performance, particularly with majority voting and the "Any correct" strategy approaching 100%, highlights R1-Reward's inherent reliability and potential for further performance gains through inference-time techniques. This makes MRMs trained with this approach more practical for real-world deployment in selection mechanisms (e.g., best-of-N).
6. **Catalyst for Future Research in RL for AI Alignment:** The paper not only provides solutions but also clearly identifies new open problems and future research directions. These include exploring more advanced test-time scaling methods beyond simple voting and further enhancing foundational capabilities of reward models through improved training strategies. This work lays a solid foundation for integrating more sophisticated RL frameworks into the development of MRMs and, by extension, more aligned and capable MLLMs.

In essence, R1-Reward represents a significant leap forward in the capabilities and development methodology of Multimodal Reward Models, promising more intelligent, interpretable, and efficient alignment for future generations of MLLMs.
