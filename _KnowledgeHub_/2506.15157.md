# Robust Instant Policy: Leveraging Student's t-Regression Model for Robust In-context Imitation Learning of Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A Robust Instant Policy (RIP) is introduced for in-context imitation learning, employing a Student's t-regression model to enhance the reliability of robot trajectory generation by filtering out hallucinated outputs from large language models. The method achieved an 80% task success rate with only 10 demonstrations, outperforming the baseline LLM-based KAT method (54%) and state-of-the-art Diffusion Policy (50%).

## Problem

- Traditional imitation learning methods require large datasets and extensive training, hindering rapid adaptation to new robot manipulation tasks.
- Large Language Model (LLM)-based in-context imitation learning, while data-efficient, is vulnerable to "hallucinations" that cause inaccurate or unreliable robot trajectories.
- Current LLM hallucination mitigation techniques are primarily designed for discrete language data and do not effectively address continuous robot actions.

## Method

- Multiple candidate robot trajectories are sampled by querying an LLM-based instant policy with the same input.
- A Student's t-regression model is employed to robustly aggregate these sampled trajectories, leveraging its heavy-tailed property to downweight outlier trajectories.
- The final robust trajectory is extracted as the mean of the learned Student's t-distribution, mitigating the impact of hallucinated outputs.

## Results

- RIP achieved an **80%** task success rate with 10 demonstrations, a **26%** improvement over the baseline KAT (**54%**) and outperforming Diffusion Policy (**50%**).
- Ablation studies showed RIP using a Student's t-distribution (**80%**) significantly outperformed its Gaussian counterpart (**64%**), demonstrating the effectiveness of robust outlier handling.
- Optimal performance was observed with 5 LLM queries and a Student's t-distribution degrees of freedom set to **1.5**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLM hallucinations pose a critical reliability challenge for continuous robot trajectory generation in in-context imitation learning.
> - The Student's t-distribution's heavy tails enable robust aggregation by effectively downweighting outlier trajectories, a key advantage over Gaussian models.
> - Querying LLMs multiple times and applying robust statistical aggregation can transform noisy, hallucinated outputs into a reliable robot policy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper directly addresses a critical challenge at the intersection of **Imitation Learning (IL)** and **Large Language Models (LLMs)** for **robot manipulation**.

Traditionally, imitation learning allows robots to learn tasks by observing human demonstrations. While powerful, state-of-the-art IL methods, including those based on deep learning architectures like energy-based models and diffusion models (e.g., Diffusion Policy [DP]), often require thousands of demonstrations and extensive model fine-tuning to adapt to new tasks or environments. This high data and computational cost limits their widespread applicability in diverse, real-world robotic scenarios where data collection can be expensive and time-consuming.

A recent paradigm, **In-Context Imitation Learning (ICIL)**, emerged to tackle this data efficiency problem. Inspired by the few-shot learning capabilities of large transformer models in natural language processing (e.g., GPT-3) and vision, ICIL aims to enable robots to instantly adapt to new tasks using only a handful of demonstrations, without explicit policy model updates. Early ICIL approaches involved training large transformer models on diverse robotic datasets to generalize policies. However, the robotics domain often lacks the vast, diverse datasets available in language or vision, limiting the effectiveness of these models for truly novel tasks.

The **Keypoint Action Tokens (KAT)** method represents a significant leap in LLM-based ICIL for robotics. It cleverly bypasses the data scarcity issue by converting robot trajectory data (observations as 3D visual keypoints, actions as 3D gripper poses) into text tokens, effectively allowing off-the-shelf, pre-trained LLMs (like GPT-4o) to be repurposed as instant policies. KAT has shown impressive performance with fewer than 10 human demonstrations, making it a state-of-the-art method in low-data ICIL.

However, the core limitation of LLMs—**hallucinations**—poses a severe challenge in the robotics domain. Hallucinations refer to LLMs generating responses that are plausible but factually incorrect or deviate significantly from the given context. In robotics, even minor inaccuracies or deviations in a generated trajectory due to hallucination can lead to catastrophic task failure. While considerable research exists on mitigating hallucinations in LLMs, these methods primarily focus on discrete language data (e.g.,) and are not directly applicable to the continuous, high-dimensional robot trajectory data.

This paper positions itself precisely to fill this critical gap. It builds upon the success of KAT by acknowledging its vulnerability to hallucinations in generating continuous robot trajectories. The proposed **Robust Instant Policy (RIP)** directly addresses this reliability issue. By introducing a stochastic treatment via the Student’s t-regression model, RIP aims to make LLM-based robot policies robust against hallucinated trajectories, ensuring more reliable and precise robot actions. This work represents a crucial step towards making LLM-powered robots truly dependable for real-world deployment, especially in data-scarce environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the inherent trade-offs and limitations observed in current imitation learning paradigms for robot manipulation:

- **High Data and Training Costs of Traditional IL:** While effective, most state-of-the-art IL methods demand extensive datasets and prolonged model training or fine-tuning for each new task or environment. This is economically and practically unfeasible for rapid deployment in diverse real-world settings.
- **Promise of In-Context IL (ICIL) with LLMs:** Recent advancements in ICIL, particularly methods like KAT that leverage off-the-shelf LLMs by tokenizing robot data, offer a compelling solution. They enable instant policy adaptation with just a few demonstrations, drastically cutting down data and training requirements.
- **Critical Vulnerability: LLM Hallucinations in Robotics:** The core challenge hindering the practical adoption of LLM-based robot policies is the pervasive issue of "hallucinations." LLMs, while powerful, can occasionally generate responses that are plausible but inaccurate or out of context. In the continuous action space of robotics, even subtle inaccuracies in a generated trajectory can lead to task failure, making these policies unreliable for real-world deployment. Existing hallucination mitigation techniques are largely designed for discrete language data and are unsuitable for continuous robot trajectories.

Given these motivations, the **key objectives** of this study are:

1. **To develop a novel robust in-context imitation learning algorithm, named Robust Instant Policy (RIP),** specifically designed to mitigate the adverse effects of hallucinated trajectories generated by LLM-based instant policies.
2. **To enable reliable robot trajectory generation** by introducing a stochastic treatment that can effectively identify and ignore outlier (hallucinated) trajectories.
3. **To leverage the Student’s t-regression model** as the core mechanism for this robust aggregation, capitalizing on its known ability to handle outliers better than traditional Gaussian models.
4. **To quantitatively and qualitatively demonstrate that RIP significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art IL methods,** particularly in low-data scenarios, and provides superior robustness compared to non-robust LLM-based policies.
5. **To identify optimal design parameters** for RIP, such as the number of queries to the LLM and the degrees of freedom for the Student’s t-distribution, to maximize its performance.

In essence, the overarching goal is to transform the promising but unreliable LLM-based instant policies into truly dependable tools for autonomous robot manipulation, thereby accelerating their adoption in practical applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Robust Instant Policy (RIP) proposed in this paper is an innovative approach to enhance the reliability of Large Language Model (LLM)-based in-context imitation learning (ICIL) for robot manipulation. It builds upon the existing Keypoint Action Tokens (KAT) method and introduces a novel stochastic treatment to combat LLM hallucinations.

#### 3.1. Foundation: Leveraging LLMs as an Instant Policy (KAT)

The core idea behind using LLMs as instant policies, as introduced by KAT, is to convert robot sensory observations and actions into a text-token format that LLMs can understand.
- **Observation Space Encoding:** RGB-D images are processed using a state-of-the-art vision transformer, DINO. DINO descriptors are extracted from image patches, and then a nearest-neighbor search identifies K most similar descriptors across demonstrations. These are then projected into 3D space using depth information and camera intrinsics, resulting in a set of 3D visual keypoints (V). For a new observation, similar keypoints (V') are extracted.
- **Action Space Encoding:** Robot end-effector poses are represented as a triplet of 3D points (gripper position, two fingertip positions), along with a gripper state (open/closed). This forms a 10-dimensional action vector (a). A sequence of these actions forms an action trajectory (ξ).
- **LLM Interaction:** Human demonstrations (D) are tokenized into a context [V, A]. When a new task observation (V') is presented, this context and V' are fed to an off-the-shelf LLM (specifically, GPT-4o is used). The LLM then generates a new trajectory of desired end-effector motions (ˆξ) as an instant policy, Φ_KAT([V, A], V') → ˆξ.

#### 3.2. RIP's Robustification Mechanism

The key innovation of RIP lies in its ability to generate a robust trajectory by aggregating multiple LLM outputs while explicitly accounting for outliers (hallucinations).

1. **Multiple Trajectory Sampling:** Instead of relying on a single output, RIP queries the LLM-based instant policy (Φ_KAT) *Q* times with the same input (context [V, A] and new observation V'). This generates a set of *Q* candidate action trajectories, A' = {ˆξ_q}^Q_{q=1}. The intuition is that if the LLM "knows" the task, most sampled trajectories will be consistent; hallucinated ones will diverge.
2. **Trajectory Normalization:** To account for potential variations in trajectory length from the LLM, time steps of all sampled trajectories are normalized to the maximum length, aligning them based on the start and end of the episode.
3. **Student's t-Regression for Robust Aggregation:** The core of RIP is the use of a Student's t-regression model as an action estimator, S_θ(ˆa_t|t;ν).
 - **Why Student's t-distribution?** Unlike a standard Gaussian distribution, the Student's t-distribution has heavier tails. This property makes it significantly more robust to outliers. When fitting the distribution to data, data points far from the mean (i.e., hallucinated trajectories) contribute less to the likelihood and thus have a smaller influence on the estimated mean and variance.
 - **Model Parameters:** The model learns a mean network (μ_θ) and a variance network (σ²_θ) for each time step *t*, parameterized by θ. The degree of freedom (ν) is a crucial hyperparameter that controls the sensitivity to outliers. A smaller ν means higher robustness (heavier tails), while ν → ∞ makes it converge to a Gaussian distribution.
 - **Learning Objective:** The parameters θ of the action estimator are learned by minimizing the negative log-likelihood (Eq. 2), effectively fitting the Student's t-distribution to the ensemble of sampled trajectories A'. This ensures that the learned distribution's mean effectively captures the central tendency while downplaying the influence of outlier trajectories.
4. **Robust Trajectory Extraction:** Once the Student's t-regression model S_ˆθ is learned, the final robust trajectory (ˆξ_RIP) is extracted by simply taking the mean of the learned distribution at each time step: ˆξ_RIP = {μ_ˆθ(t)}^T_{t=1}.

#### 3.3. Grasping-Action-Based Downsampling

A practical detail in the methodology is the way action demonstrations are pre-processed. Instead of uniform downsampling, which might omit crucial gripper actions (opening/closing), the paper proposes masking time steps corresponding to the start/end of episodes and gripper activations. Uniform sampling is then applied *between* these masked segments, ensuring that critical gripper actions are preserved in the downsampled trajectories, which is vital for precise manipulation tasks.

**Summary of Algorithm (Algorithm 1):**
1. Initialize an empty set for aggregated action trajectories, A'.
2. Loop *Q* times:
 a. Query the LLM-based instant policy (Φ_KAT) with context and new observation to get a candidate trajectory ˆξ_q.
 b. Add ˆξ_q to A'.
3. Learn the parameter ˆθ of the Student's t-regression model by minimizing the negative log-likelihood over A'.
4. Extract the robust trajectory ˆξ_RIP by taking the mean of the learned Student's t-distribution at each time step.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of RIP was comprehensive, encompassing both simulated and real-world environments across a variety of daily manipulation tasks. The results strongly support the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed method.

#### 4.1. Comparison to State-of-the-Art Imitation Learning Approaches

- **Quantitative Performance (Low-Data Scenarios):**
 - **Significant Improvement:** With only 10 demonstrations per task, RIP achieved an average task success rate of 80%, a substantial 26% improvement over the baseline LLM-based KAT method (54%).
 - **Outperformance vs. Traditional IL:** RIP also notably outperformed Diffusion Policy (DP) (50%) and KAT-DP (52%), which are state-of-the-art IL algorithms requiring explicit training, whereas LLM-based methods (KAT, RIP) do not require additional training after demonstration.
 - **Robustness Across Demonstration Amounts:** Figure 5b clearly illustrates RIP's sustained superior performance across varying numbers of demonstrations (from 2 to 20). While other LLM-based methods (KAT, RIP_Gauss) plateau or are surpassed by traditional IL methods when more data is available (e.g., 20 demonstrations), RIP consistently maintains the highest success rate, demonstrating its inherent robustness and data efficiency.
- **Validation of Student's t-Distribution (Ablation Study):**
 - **RIP_Gauss Baseline:** The ablation study comparing RIP to RIP_Gauss (RIP using a normal Gaussian distribution, equivalent to Student's t-distribution with ν=∞) further highlighted the Student's t-distribution's advantage. RIP (80%) significantly outperformed RIP_Gauss (64%) by 16%, proving the critical role of the heavy-tailed Student's t-distribution in effectively ignoring outliers and generating robust trajectories.
- **Qualitative Analysis:** Figure 5a visually demonstrates RIP's effectiveness. While KAT sometimes generated hallucinated trajectories that led to task failure, and RIP_Gauss's averaged trajectory was still influenced by these outliers, RIP successfully generated a smooth, reliable trajectory that ignored the outlying (hallucinated) paths, leading to successful task completion.

#### 4.2. Optimal Design Analysis of RIP

The study also investigated the impact of RIP's key design elements on its performance:

- **Number of Queries (Q):**
 - Performance increased with more queries, peaking at Q=5. For Q=2, the success rate was similar to KAT, indicating insufficient reliable trajectories for robust aggregation. Beyond Q=5, performance stabilized, suggesting diminishing returns for additional queries. The paper adopted Q=5 for its main experiments.
- **Degrees of Freedom (ν) for Student's t-distribution:**
 - The success rate was lowest when ν=∞ (equivalent to RIP_Gauss, as it behaves like a Gaussian distribution). As ν decreased, implying greater robustness to outliers, the success rate increased, reaching its maximum at ν=1.5. This validates that a specific level of outlier tolerance is crucial. The paper used ν=1.5.
- **Grasping-Action-Based Downsampling:**
 - This pre-processing technique significantly improved performance in tasks involving precise gripper actions (e.g., Pick Banana, Hang). Using grasping-action-based downsampling increased the success rate by 22% (from 52% to 74%) compared to normal uniform downsampling. This highlights the importance of preserving critical action sequences that involve gripper state changes, as uniform downsampling might omit them, leading to less accurate demonstrations.

#### 4.3. Generalizability

The experiments were conducted on 10 diverse everyday manipulation tasks, including scenarios with "unseen" objects during training (e.g., different cabinets, faucets, sweeping objects), in both simulated (Maniskill3) and real-world (ALOHA) environments. This breadth of evaluation demonstrates RIP's strong generalizability and practical applicability across various robotic tasks and settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Robust Instant Policy (RIP) represents a significant advancement in the field of robot imitation learning, particularly at the confluence of LLMs and robotics. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Enabling Reliable LLM-based Robot Control:** The most direct and crucial impact of RIP is its ability to overcome the critical "hallucination" issue inherent in LLM-based instant policies. By providing a robust method for aggregating noisy LLM outputs and filtering out erroneous trajectories, RIP makes LLM-powered robot manipulation significantly more reliable and trustworthy. This is a game-changer for deploying such systems in real-world, safety-critical applications where even minor errors can have severe consequences.

- **Accelerating Robot Deployment in Low-Data Scenarios:** RIP drastically reduces the data requirements for teaching robots new tasks. Its demonstrated superior performance with fewer than 10 human demonstrations means that robots can be rapidly adapted to novel tasks without the prohibitive costs and time associated with collecting vast datasets or extensive fine-tuning. This efficiency is paramount for agile deployment in dynamic environments, customizable manufacturing, or domestic service robotics.

- **Unlocking the Full Potential of LLMs for Continuous Control:** While LLMs have shown remarkable capabilities in natural language and discrete reasoning, their direct application to continuous robot control has been limited by their occasional unreliability and the challenges of mapping text to precise physical actions. RIP provides a practical framework for bridging this gap, allowing the powerful contextual understanding and generalization abilities of LLMs to be leveraged effectively for continuous robot manipulation.

- **Advancing the Field of In-Context Learning for Robotics:** By refining ICIL, RIP contributes to a paradigm shift from traditional, heavy-computation, data-intensive policy learning to more efficient, adaptable, and "instant" learning. This could inspire further research into developing more sophisticated in-context learning mechanisms that robustly integrate various forms of human knowledge and demonstrations.

- **Foundation for Future Research Directions:** The paper explicitly outlines several promising avenues for future work, which itself highlights the foundational nature of RIP:
 - **Handling Multi-optimal Behaviors:** Extending RIP to a Student's t-mixture model could allow robots to learn from demonstrations that exhibit multiple valid ways to complete a task, mimicking human versatility.
 - **Explicit Hallucination Recognition:** Developing methods to not just mitigate but *identify the root causes* of hallucinations could lead to more interpretable and controllable LLM-robot interfaces.
 - **Addressing Computational Complexity for Online Control:** The current O(l^2) complexity of transformer-based LLMs limits online control. Future work on alternative architectures (e.g., Mamba) for faster inference is critical for real-time robotic applications.

- **Societal and Economic Impact (Society 5.0 Alignment):** Given its support from a national initiative (BRIDGE/Society 5.0), RIP's advancements contribute directly to creating a more automated and intelligent society. By enabling robots to learn and adapt quickly to diverse tasks with minimal human intervention, it paves the way for wider adoption of robots in various sectors, from industry and logistics to healthcare and personal assistance, ultimately improving productivity and quality of life.

In conclusion, RIP is not merely an incremental improvement; it is a crucial step towards making LLM-driven robotics reliable enough for practical use. By turning "instant" into "robust instant," it significantly moves the needle towards truly intelligent, adaptable, and deployable robotic agents.
