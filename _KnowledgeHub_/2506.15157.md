# Robust Instant Policy: Leveraging Student's t-Regression Model for Robust In-context Imitation Learning of Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A Robust Instant Policy (RIP) is introduced for in-context imitation learning, employing a Student's t-regression model to enhance the reliability of robot trajectory generation by filtering out hallucinated outputs from large language models. The method achieved an 80% task success rate with only 10 demonstrations, outperforming the baseline LLM-based KAT method (54%) and state-of-the-art Diffusion Policy (50%).

## Problem

- Traditional imitation learning methods require large datasets and extensive training, hindering rapid adaptation to new robot manipulation tasks.
- Large Language Model (LLM)-based in-context imitation learning, while data-efficient, is vulnerable to "hallucinations" that cause inaccurate or unreliable robot trajectories.
- Current LLM hallucination mitigation techniques are primarily designed for discrete language data and do not effectively address continuous robot actions.

## Method

- Multiple candidate robot trajectories are sampled by querying an LLM-based instant policy with the same input.
- A Student's t-regression model is employed to robustly aggregate these sampled trajectories, leveraging its heavy-tailed property to downweight outlier trajectories.
- The final robust trajectory is extracted as the mean of the learned Student's t-distribution, mitigating the impact of hallucinated outputs.

## Results

- RIP achieved an **80%** task success rate with 10 demonstrations, a **26%** improvement over the baseline KAT (**54%**) and outperforming Diffusion Policy (**50%**).
- Ablation studies showed RIP using a Student's t-distribution (**80%**) significantly outperformed its Gaussian counterpart (**64%**), demonstrating the effectiveness of robust outlier handling.
- Optimal performance was observed with 5 LLM queries and a Student's t-distribution degrees of freedom set to **1.5**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLM hallucinations pose a critical reliability challenge for continuous robot trajectory generation in in-context imitation learning.
> - The Student's t-distribution's heavy tails enable robust aggregation by effectively downweighting outlier trajectories, a key advantage over Gaussian models.
> - Querying LLMs multiple times and applying robust statistical aggregation can transform noisy, hallucinated outputs into a reliable robot policy.
