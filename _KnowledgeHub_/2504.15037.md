# Scaling and Beyond: Advancing Spatial Reasoning in MLLMs Requires New Recipes

> [!summary] Summary
> This position paper argues that multimodal large language models (MLLMs) exhibit critical limitations in spatial reasoning and that simply scaling existing architectures will not resolve this. It proposes a framework for spatial reasoning and identifies specific deficiencies across training data, model architecture, training objectives, and evaluation, advocating for targeted methodological innovations.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) consistently struggle with spatial reasoning, a capability essential for understanding and interacting with the physical world.
- Current MLLM development, often focused on scaling, does not adequately address the qualitative challenges of spatial understanding, limiting their potential in real-world applications like embodied AI and autonomous driving.
- A lack of a precise, comprehensive framework and systematic analysis hinders progress in diagnosing and overcoming MLLMs' spatial reasoning deficiencies.

## Method

- The paper establishes a comprehensive framework for spatial reasoning, decomposing it into =='Relational Reasoning'== (static relationships) and =='Transformation Reasoning'== (dynamic manipulations).
- It advocates for =='new recipes'== across the MLLM pipeline, including specifically designed training data with explicit spatial annotations, architecturally aware components (e.g., advanced positional encodings, spatially-preserving connectors), and spatially-explicit training objectives.
- The work calls for improved evaluation benchmarks that better correlate with real-world performance and the development of more sophisticated multimodal reasoning methods.

## Results

- Analysis of widely used pre-training datasets (e.g., LAION-2B, CC-3M) revealed extremely low frequencies of explicit spatial prepositions (e.g., **0.22%**-**1.44%**), indicating a foundational data gap.
- A case study comparing Qwen2-VL and LLaVA 1.5 demonstrated that current MLLMs primarily focus on object-level semantic information rather than forming a holistic understanding of spatial layouts, consistently failing at complex path planning.
- The paper systematically identified that current MLLM recipes, across data, architecture, training, and evaluation, are fundamentally constrained, concluding that scaling alone is insufficient to achieve human-like spatial intelligence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Spatial reasoning in MLLMs is fundamentally limited by the scarcity of explicit spatial annotations in pre-training data and the inadequacy of current textual representations for complex spatial configurations.
> - Architectural choices like flattening 2D visual information into 1D sequences for LLM backbones and compression in connectors inherently compromise the retention of crucial spatial structure.
> - Existing training objectives and evaluation benchmarks fail to adequately foster and assess robust spatial reasoning, with common techniques like Chain-of-Thought sometimes proving ineffective or even detrimental for spatial tasks.
