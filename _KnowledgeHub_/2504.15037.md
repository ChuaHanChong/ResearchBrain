# Scaling and Beyond: Advancing Spatial Reasoning in MLLMs Requires New Recipes

> [!summary] Summary
> This position paper argues that multimodal large language models (MLLMs) exhibit critical limitations in spatial reasoning and that simply scaling existing architectures will not resolve this. It proposes a framework for spatial reasoning and identifies specific deficiencies across training data, model architecture, training objectives, and evaluation, advocating for targeted methodological innovations.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) consistently struggle with spatial reasoning, a capability essential for understanding and interacting with the physical world.
- Current MLLM development, often focused on scaling, does not adequately address the qualitative challenges of spatial understanding, limiting their potential in real-world applications like embodied AI and autonomous driving.
- A lack of a precise, comprehensive framework and systematic analysis hinders progress in diagnosing and overcoming MLLMs' spatial reasoning deficiencies.

## Method

- The paper establishes a comprehensive framework for spatial reasoning, decomposing it into =='Relational Reasoning'== (static relationships) and =='Transformation Reasoning'== (dynamic manipulations).
- It advocates for =='new recipes'== across the MLLM pipeline, including specifically designed training data with explicit spatial annotations, architecturally aware components (e.g., advanced positional encodings, spatially-preserving connectors), and spatially-explicit training objectives.
- The work calls for improved evaluation benchmarks that better correlate with real-world performance and the development of more sophisticated multimodal reasoning methods.

## Results

- Analysis of widely used pre-training datasets (e.g., LAION-2B, CC-3M) revealed extremely low frequencies of explicit spatial prepositions (e.g., **0.22%**-**1.44%**), indicating a foundational data gap.
- A case study comparing Qwen2-VL and LLaVA 1.5 demonstrated that current MLLMs primarily focus on object-level semantic information rather than forming a holistic understanding of spatial layouts, consistently failing at complex path planning.
- The paper systematically identified that current MLLM recipes, across data, architecture, training, and evaluation, are fundamentally constrained, concluding that scaling alone is insufficient to achieve human-like spatial intelligence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Spatial reasoning in MLLMs is fundamentally limited by the scarcity of explicit spatial annotations in pre-training data and the inadequacy of current textual representations for complex spatial configurations.
> - Architectural choices like flattening 2D visual information into 1D sequences for LLM backbones and compression in connectors inherently compromise the retention of crucial spatial structure.
> - Existing training objectives and evaluation benchmarks fail to adequately foster and assess robust spatial reasoning, with common techniques like Chain-of-Thought sometimes proving ineffective or even detrimental for spatial tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper intervenes critically in the rapidly evolving field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), which represents the cutting edge of artificial intelligence by integrating visual and linguistic understanding. Building upon the success of Large Language Models (LLMs) in text-based tasks, MLLMs (e.g., GPT-4, LLaVA, Gemini, Qwen2-VL) have demonstrated impressive capabilities in general vision-language understanding, ranging from image captioning to visual question answering.

However, amidst the excitement surrounding their performance, the research community has increasingly identified specific weaknesses. A prominent and frequently highlighted limitation is their struggle with **spatial reasoning**. This paper directly addresses this critical deficiency, arguing against the prevailing assumption that simply scaling up existing MLLM architectures and training methodologies will naturally lead to human-like spatial reasoning capabilities. This stance challenges the widely discussed "scaling laws" paradigm, which posits that performance improves predictably with increased model size, data, and compute. The authors contend that spatial reasoning, a fundamental component of general intelligence in cognitive science, is a qualitatively different challenge requiring **"new recipes"** – targeted, fundamental modifications to the MLLM development approach.

The work differentiates itself from previous research by proposing a comprehensive framework for spatial reasoning (decomposed into Relational Reasoning and Transformation Reasoning), which extends beyond general visual understanding or basic textual spatial relations. While some studies have explored MLLMs' ability to process spatial information, this paper provides a more systematic and in-depth analysis of *why* current models struggle and *what specific changes* are needed across various components of the MLLM pipeline.

It connects the abstract problem of spatial reasoning in MLLMs to concrete, high-impact real-world applications such as embodied AI, autonomous driving, remote sensing, medical imaging, and educational applications. This contextualization underscores the practical urgency of addressing this limitation, as the lack of robust spatial reasoning capabilities significantly constrains MLLMs' ability to effectively interact with and understand the physical world. By systematically dissecting the limitations in training data, model architectures, training objectives, evaluation benchmarks, and reasoning methods, the paper offers a structured roadmap for future research, positioning itself as a guiding document for advancing this critical aspect of AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind this position paper stems from the stark contrast between the impressive general performance of current MLLMs and their glaring deficiencies in spatial reasoning. While MLLMs show remarkable proficiency in diverse vision-language tasks, they consistently falter on spatial challenges that are straightforward for humans. This deficiency is not merely an academic concern but a significant impediment to developing AI systems that can genuinely understand and interact with the physical world, thereby limiting their potential in critical real-world applications. The authors argue that merely scaling up existing models and training paradigms will not spontaneously unlock this fundamental cognitive ability.

Against this backdrop, the paper sets forth several key objectives:

- **Establish a Comprehensive Framework for Spatial Reasoning:** A primary objective is to provide a clear and precise definition of spatial reasoning within the context of MLLMs. The authors propose a decomposition into two fundamental capabilities: "Relational Reasoning" (understanding static spatial relationships like position, orientation, and perspective) and "Transformation Reasoning" (reasoning about dynamic manipulations like rotations, translations, and perspective changes). This framework, drawing from cognitive psychology, aims to provide a standardized lens for rigorous analysis and systematic improvement efforts.
- **Elaborate on the Pivotal Role in Real-World Applications:** The paper aims to demonstrate why enhanced spatial reasoning is not just an incremental improvement but a prerequisite for MLLMs to unlock their full potential in downstream tasks. It explicitly links these capabilities to fields like Embodied AI, Autonomous Driving, Remote Sensing, Medical Imaging, and educational tools, highlighting how current limitations restrict MLLMs' ability to perceive, navigate, and interact intelligently with complex environments.
- **Systematically Analyze Current MLLM Methodologies:** A core objective is to conduct a systematic examination of how each component of current MLLM development—from training data and model architecture to training objectives and reasoning mechanisms—influences spatial reasoning capabilities. This deep dive seeks to pinpoint specific weaknesses rather than offering broad generalizations.
- **Identify Critical Limitations and Promising Avenues for Advancement:** Through this systematic analysis, the paper aims to uncover the root causes of MLLMs' spatial reasoning struggles and, concurrently, to identify concrete opportunities and innovative pathways for overcoming these limitations. This involves proposing specific architectural modifications, data collection strategies, and training methodologies.
- **Direct the AI Research Community's Attention:** Ultimately, the paper serves as a strategic intervention, aiming to catalyze research efforts by highlighting the critical yet often underexplored aspects of spatial reasoning. By articulating the challenges and opportunities, the authors seek to inspire the development of "new recipes" that can lead MLLMs toward achieving human-like spatial cognition, thereby contributing to the broader pursuit of artificial general intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a position paper, the methodology primarily involves a comprehensive analytical review and synthesis of existing literature, coupled with a illustrative case study to bolster its arguments. The approach is structured to systematically dissect the problem of spatial reasoning in MLLMs across their developmental pipeline.

1. **Defining Spatial Reasoning Framework:**
 - The authors first establish a foundational definition of spatial reasoning in MLLMs, drawing heavily from cognitive science and psychometrics.
 - They decompose this complex ability into two core components: "Relational Reasoning" (static aspects like relative positions, orientations) and "Transformation Reasoning" (dynamic aspects like rotations, translations, perspective changes). This theoretical grounding provides a structured lens for subsequent analysis.

2. **Component-wise Analysis of Current MLLM Recipes:**
 - The paper then systematically analyzes how existing MLLM components contribute to, or detract from, spatial reasoning capabilities. This is organized into three main sections:
 - **Training Data:**
 - **Pre-training Data:** An empirical analysis of spatial preposition frequency in large-scale datasets (LAION-2B, CC-3M, SBU Captions, ShareGPT4V-PT) is conducted. This quantitative assessment provides concrete evidence for the scarcity of explicit spatial annotations. The limitations of natural language for representing complex spatial configurations are also discussed.
 - **Fine-tuning Data:** A review of recent specialized fine-tuning datasets (e.g., SpatialVLM, SpatialRGPT, SAT, RoboSpatal) is presented, highlighting their progress while also pointing out their limitations, such as the lack of explicit reasoning rationales.
 - **Model Architecture:**
 - **Vision Encoder:** Examination of common vision encoder designs (e.g., ViTs) and their positional encoding mechanisms (APE, RPE), drawing parallels with human cognitive neuroscience (grid and place cells). The challenges posed by 3D encoders and dual-branch designs are also discussed.
 - **Connector:** Analysis of the role of connectors (e.g., LLaVA's linear projection, Q-Former, C-Abstractor) in preserving spatial fidelity during modality alignment, highlighting the trade-off between spatial information retention and computational efficiency.
 - **LLM Backbone:** Investigation of how the language model backbone processes visual tokens, particularly the impact of flattening 2D visual information into 1D sequences (RoPE-1D) and the role of attention masks. Promising developments like M-RoPE are mentioned.
 - **Training Objective:**
 - **Discrete Visual Tokens:** Discussion of the limitations of cross-entropy loss, mismatch in token distributions, and the absence of explicit spatial alignment signals for MLLMs using discrete visual tokens.
 - **Continuous Denoising Process:** Exploration of continuous modeling approaches (e.g., diffusion models) and their challenges in capturing detailed spatial layouts without specific constraints.
 - **Training Modality:** Analysis of how the modality where loss is calculated (text-only vs. interleaved text-image with multimodal supervision) impacts spatial reasoning.

3. **Review of Evaluation Benchmarks:**
 - The paper categorizes and reviews existing spatial reasoning benchmarks for MLLMs into "Static Spatial Reasoning" (e.g., VSR, What’sUp, SpatialVLM, COMFORT) and "Dynamic Spatial Reasoning" (e.g., SAT, iVISPAR, VSI-Bench, SPACE).
 - It synthesizes findings from these benchmarks to illustrate current models' shortcomings in complex, dynamic, and 3D spatial tasks, as well as their struggles with Frames of Reference and the counter-intuitive performance of techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting. A critical gap identified is the lack of correlation between benchmark performance and real-world application performance.

4. **Analysis of Reasoning Methods:**
 - Current reasoning methods are grouped into "Direct reasoning," methods leveraging "reasoning traces" (e.g., CoT, MVoT), and "external tools/agentic reasoning" (e.g., Image-of-Thought, SpatialBot, MM-ReAct). The paper discusses the strengths and limitations of each in the context of multimodal spatial reasoning.

5. **Illustrative Case Study:**
 - To provide concrete examples and insights, the paper includes a case study using two prominent MLLMs, Qwen2-VL and LLaVA 1.5, on two simple maze-related tasks: Path Reading and Planning.
 - The methodology for the case study involves:
 - Evaluating model responses to specific prompts.
 - Analyzing attention maps (averaged across heads and layers) to visualize where models focus their visual attention, drawing inspiration from ADAPTVIS and ViCrop.
 - Testing the impact of visual cues and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting on performance and attention patterns.
 - This case study serves to empirically *illustrate* the theoretical arguments regarding architectural limitations (e.g., positional encoding) and the models' tendency to focus on object-level semantics over higher-level spatial layouts.

Overall, the methodology is a strong, theory-driven, and evidence-supported analytical framework, designed to systematically expose the problem space and advocate for specific research directions rather than presenting novel experimental results across a wide range of tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper’s main findings converge on a central argument: achieving robust spatial reasoning in MLLMs demands a paradigm shift from mere scaling to dedicated architectural, data, and training innovations.

**A. Limitations in Current MLLM "Recipes":**

1. **Training Data Deficiencies:**
 - **Pre-training Data:** Analysis reveals extremely low frequencies of spatial prepositions in widely used pre-training datasets like LAION-2B (0.22%), CC-3M (0.51%), and SBU Captions (1.44%), indicating a significant gap in foundational spatial knowledge. Even ShareGPT4V-PT, with higher spatial descriptions (64.71%), is costly and still lacks dynamic transformation descriptions.
 - **Representation Inadequacy:** Current text-based annotations primarily capture unary or binary relationships, proving inefficient and ambiguous for complex spatial configurations. The paper argues for more sophisticated spatial representation schemes (e.g., topological representations).
 - **Fine-tuning Data:** While recent fine-tuning datasets (SpatialVLM, SAT) have improved, there's a critical lack of large-scale datasets explicitly designed with detailed spatial reasoning rationales, hindering the development of complex spatial reasoning.

2. **Architectural Constraints:**
 - **Vision Encoders:** Existing positional embeddings (APE, RPE) in Vision Transformers struggle with flexibility, extrapolation, and encoding precise spatial information (e.g., distance, rotations). The trade-off between general visual understanding (CLIP) and specialized 3D understanding in dual-branch encoders leads to redundancy and entanglement.
 - **Connectors:** Connectors (e.g., Q-Former) that compress visual features can inadvertently disrupt spatial relationships between image patches, which are crucial for spatial reasoning. A fundamental trade-off exists between spatial information preservation and computational efficiency.
 - **LLM Backbone:** Flattening 2D visual tokens into a 1D sequence for the LLM backbone (with 1D positional encodings like RoPE-1D) inherently compromises the spatial structure. Causal attention masks in LLMs are suboptimal for visual spatial information, which often requires bidirectional processing.

3. **Suboptimal Training Objectives:**
 - **Cross-Entropy Loss:** Standard cross-entropy loss, optimized for semantic alignment, fails to explicitly address the nuances and constraints of spatial reasoning.
 - **Token Mismatch:** Treating visual tokens identically to text tokens, despite their differing distributions, presents challenges for unified modeling.
 - **Lack of Explicit Spatial Supervision:** Current objectives often lack explicit signals for spatial layout understanding. Even diffusion denoising approaches may not effectively capture spatial layouts without additional constraints (e.g., bounding boxes).
 - **Modality of Loss Calculation:** Most MLLMs calculate loss solely on textual tokens, limiting rich cross-modal interactions. Multimodal supervision (loss from both text and visual tokens) has been shown to yield superior performance.

**B. Challenges in Evaluation and Reasoning:**

1. **Evaluation Benchmarks Reveal Deep Flaws:**
 - **Significant Gaps:** Benchmarks for both static (VSR, What'sUp, 3DSRBench) and dynamic (SAT, VSI-Bench) spatial reasoning consistently show substantial performance gaps between MLLMs and human capabilities, especially in 3D environments, complex scenarios, and tasks involving perspective transformations.
 - **Frame of Reference:** MLLMs struggle with robustness and consistency in handling different Frames of Reference (FoR), often preferring egocentric views but failing to flexibly adapt.
 - **Ineffective Techniques:** Counter-intuitively, linguistic prompting techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT), effective in other domains, can be detrimental for spatial reasoning tasks. MLLMs tend to form local "world models" rather than unified global cognitive maps.
 - **Real-World Disconnect:** A critical finding is the lack of empirical and theoretical investigations correlating benchmark performance directly with improved outcomes in real-world applications like Embodied AI.

2. **Limited Reasoning Methods:**
 - **Text-Centric Limitations:** Traditional direct reasoning and text-based reasoning traces (CoT) prove verbose and imprecise for multimodal spatial information.
 - **Underexplored Potentials:** Multimodal Visualization-of-Thought (MVoT) shows promise by extending reasoning traces to multimodal representations but is nascent. Reinforcement learning is largely underexplored for improving spatial reasoning.
 - **Nascent Agentic Reasoning:** While agentic reasoning (e.g., calling external tools for depth estimation) is emerging, specialized agentic workflows tailored for complex spatial reasoning (e.g., scene graph structuring) remain limited.

**C. Case Study Illustrations (Qwen2-VL vs. LLaVA 1.5):**

- **Path Reading:** Qwen2-VL demonstrates more accurate attention to relevant image regions compared to LLaVA 1.5, aligning with its architectural enhancements (2D-RoPE, M-RoPE) and supporting the importance of proper positional embedding. LLaVA 1.5 often shows diffused or incorrect attention.
- **Planning:** Both models fail at complex path planning. Qwen2-VL provides more reasonable (though incorrect) responses and shows some ability to leverage visual cues and CoT (e.g., generating a path while ignoring boundaries), primarily focusing on object-level semantic information. LLaVA 1.5 struggles significantly, often producing repetitive or nonsensical outputs, even with CoT and visual cues.
- **Object-Level Focus:** The case study reinforces that current MLLMs primarily focus on object-level semantic information rather than forming a holistic, higher-level understanding of spatial layouts and relationships, echoing the limitations found in training data.

In summary, the paper robustly demonstrates that current MLLM capabilities for spatial reasoning are fundamentally constrained by limitations across every stage of their design and training. It strongly supports the thesis that scaling alone is insufficient, and a re-evaluation of core architectural and methodological choices is imperative.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This position paper carries significant weight and potential impact on the future direction of Multimodal Large Language Model research, primarily by issuing a compelling call to action and providing a strategic roadmap.

#### 5.1. Re-framing the Scaling Debate

The most profound impact is its direct challenge to the "scaling laws" paradigm. By arguing that spatial reasoning capabilities will not naturally emerge from merely increasing model size or data, the paper redirects the community's focus from brute-force scaling to **targeted, fundamental innovations**. This could lead to a re-prioritization of research efforts towards architectural design, data curation, and training objectives specifically engineered for spatial understanding.

#### 5.2. Bridging Cognitive Science and AI

The paper effectively integrates established frameworks from cognitive psychology (e.g., relational vs. transformation reasoning, grid/place cells) into the technical discussion of MLLMs. This interdisciplinary approach highlights the need for AI systems to mirror human cognitive capabilities more closely, fostering a deeper understanding of intelligence itself and guiding AI development towards more human-like reasoning.

#### 5.3. Unlocking Real-World Applications

By meticulously detailing the limitations in spatial reasoning and connecting them to high-stakes applications like embodied AI, autonomous driving, medical imaging, and robotics, the paper underscores the practical urgency of its message. Addressing these deficiencies would enable MLLMs to transition from passive information consumers to dynamic, interactive agents capable of understanding, predicting, and manipulating the physical world effectively. This directly translates into safer autonomous systems, more precise robotic manipulation, better diagnostic tools, and more intuitive educational aids.

#### 5.4. Providing a Research Roadmap

The systematic analysis across training data, model architecture, training objectives, evaluation, and reasoning methods offers a clear, granular roadmap for researchers. It identifies specific "low-hanging fruit" and grand challenges:
 - **Data:** Calls for richer, spatially explicit annotations beyond basic textual descriptions (e.g., topological representations, explicit reasoning rationales) and multimodal supervision signals.
 - **Architecture:** Advocates for novel positional encoding methods that better capture 2D and 3D space, spatially-aware connectors, and attention mechanisms that go beyond causal masking.
 - **Training:** Emphasizes the need for spatially-aware loss functions and methods to align diverse token distributions.
 - **Evaluation:** Demands benchmarks that are more deeply correlated with real-world application performance and capable of assessing complex dynamic and 3D spatial reasoning.
 - **Reasoning:** Encourages the exploration of multimodal reasoning traces and specialized agentic systems for spatial tasks.

#### 5.5. Catalyzing New Innovations

By highlighting currently underexplored aspects, such as the potential of reinforcement learning for spatial reasoning or the development of specialized multi-agent systems, the paper aims to spark new lines of inquiry and innovation. The case study, despite its illustrative nature, provides concrete visual evidence of attention limitations, which can directly inform architectural improvements.

In conclusion, "Scaling and Beyond" is a pivotal position paper that shifts the narrative around MLLM development. Its significance lies in its rigorous dissection of a core limitation, its human-centric framework, and its comprehensive, actionable roadmap. By advocating for "new recipes" over mere scaling, it seeks to fundamentally reshape how the AI community approaches the challenging yet crucial task of imbuing MLLMs with true spatial intelligence, bringing them closer to general intelligence and broader real-world utility.
