# Contrastive Representation Learning for Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer of Adaptive Humanoid Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Adaptive Robotic Controls Lab and PNDbotics developed a framework for robust sim-to-real transfer of adaptive humanoid locomotion, employing contrastive representation learning to distill environmental awareness into purely proprioceptive policies. This enabled a full-sized humanoid robot to proactively navigate complex terrains, achieving high success rates on 30 cm steps and 26.5-degree slopes with zero-shot transfer.

## Problem

- Achieving stable and adaptive locomotion for humanoid robots in complex, unstructured real-world environments is difficult due to the inherent sim-to-real gap.
- Existing purely proprioceptive policies, while robust to disturbances, are 'blind' to upcoming terrain, limiting proactive adaptation on challenging obstacles.
- Exteroceptive perception-driven control provides proactive capabilities but introduces high system complexity, computational overhead, and potential fragility for real-world deployment.

## Method

- An asymmetric ==Actor-Critic== architecture was designed, where the actor uses only proprioceptive sensory data for real-world deployment, while the critic leverages privileged simulation information.
- A spatial contrastive representation learning module was introduced to distill immediate environmental context from privileged simulation data into the actor's proprioceptive latent state.
- An adaptive gait mechanism was integrated into the policy, allowing it to dynamically modulate gait frequency and foot phases based on its inferred understanding of the terrain.

## Results

- The method demonstrated superior training efficiency in simulation, reaching the highest difficulty terrain levels within 5,000 steps, and maintained over **75%** success rates across various command velocities.
- It achieved robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, enabling a **1.6**-meter humanoid robot (Adam Lite) to successfully ascend 30 cm high steps and traverse slopes up to **26.5**° in real-world conditions.
- Quantitative real-world ablation studies showed the proposed AdaptiveGait significantly outperformed baselines, maintaining over **85%** success on 30 cm stairs and over **90%** success on **26.5**° slopes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Purely proprioceptive policies can achieve proactive and adaptive locomotion by implicitly encoding environmental awareness distilled directly from privileged simulation information through spatial contrastive learning.
> - Direct spatial contrastive learning offers an efficient, end-to-end method to bridge the sim-to-real information gap, bypassing the complexities of auxiliary world models or multi-stage teacher-student frameworks.
> - The synergy between distilled environmental awareness and an adaptive gait mechanism allows a policy to intelligently and proactively adjust its walking rhythm, resolving the trade-off between rigid clocked gaits and unstable clock-free control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a long-standing "grand challenge" in humanoid robotics: achieving stable, adaptive locomotion in complex, unstructured real-world environments while effectively transferring policies trained in simulation to physical robots. This challenge is rooted in the inherent discrepancy between the rich, privileged information available during simulation training (e.g., precise terrain geometry, friction coefficients, robot dynamics) and the limited, noisy proprioceptive data available on a real robot (e.g., joint encoders, IMU).

The existing research landscape for robust humanoid locomotion generally falls into two main paradigms:
1. **Reactive Proprioceptive Control:** This approach focuses on training policies robustly through extensive domain randomization, enabling them to react to disturbances inferred solely from proprioceptive feedback. While robust to unexpected perturbations, these policies are inherently "blind" and cannot proactively anticipate or plan for upcoming obstacles, limiting their performance on highly challenging or discontinuous terrains.
2. **Exteroceptive Perception-Driven Control:** This paradigm integrates external sensors like cameras or LiDAR to provide the policy with direct environmental perception, enabling proactive control and planning. This allows for impressive feats on complex obstacles but introduces significant system complexity, computational overhead, and potential points of failure in the perception pipeline, making real-world deployment challenging.

This paper positions itself as a solution that transcends the limitations of both traditional paradigms by introducing a third, emerging approach: **distilling privileged simulation knowledge into a purely proprioceptive policy**. Within this emerging field, prior works include:
- **Teacher-Student Frameworks:** A privileged "teacher" policy guides a proprioceptive "student," but this often involves multi-stage training and caps the student's performance.
- **End-to-End Representation Learning:**
 - **Auxiliary Prediction/World Models:** Policies learn to explicitly reconstruct privileged information (e.g., terrain maps) from proprioception. This is direct but can add significant overhead from training a separate, complex predictive model.
 - **Temporal Contrastive Learning:** Focuses on aligning historical and future observations to learn predictive dynamics.

The authors distinguish their work by proposing a **more direct and efficient spatial contrastive approach**. Instead of modeling temporal dynamics or reconstructing full environmental states, their method directly aligns the policy's latent proprioceptive representation with the *immediate environmental context* available during simulation. This "integrated, end-to-end method bypasses the inefficiency of auxiliary world models and the complexity of teacher-student frameworks." Furthermore, they integrate this distilled environmental awareness with an **adaptive gait mechanism**, which dynamically modulates gait frequency and phase. While adaptive gait control itself is not new, this paper provides the crucial element: a mechanism for the policy to *intelligently know when and how to adapt* based on its internalized, data-driven understanding of the environment. This synergy aims to resolve the classic trade-off between rigid, clocked gaits and unstable clock-free policies, offering a lightweight, robust, and proactive controller for humanoids.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the fundamental dilemma faced in deploying reinforcement learning-based humanoid locomotion policies in the real world: how to achieve both the robustness of reactive proprioceptive control and the proactivity of complex, perception-driven systems without incurring the heavy deployment costs and fragility of the latter.

The **overarching objective** is to develop a robust, adaptive locomotion controller for full-sized humanoid robots that can effectively transfer from simulation to the real world with zero-shot capabilities, enabling navigation over challenging and unstructured terrains.

More specifically, the **key objectives** are:
- **To imbue a purely proprioceptive policy with proactive capabilities:** This involves developing a mechanism that allows the robot to anticipate and adapt to upcoming terrain features, even without direct exteroceptive sensors at deployment time.
- **To bridge the sim-to-real information gap through knowledge distillation:** The goal is to leverage the privileged environmental information available during simulation training and distill this "awareness" into the actor's latent state, making it predictive of the environment.
- **To introduce a novel contrastive learning framework:** This framework aims to compel the actor's proprioceptive latent state to encode terrain-relevant information by contrasting matching pairs of proprioceptive history and environmental context against non-matching ones.
- **To integrate this distilled awareness with an adaptive gait mechanism:** The policy should be able to dynamically adjust its gait frequency and phase based on its inferred understanding of the terrain, thereby resolving the trade-off between rigid clocked gaits and unstable clock-free policies.
- **To validate the approach with zero-shot sim-to-real transfer:** The developed policy must demonstrate highly robust locomotion on a physical full-sized humanoid robot over challenging terrains in real-world conditions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose an "Adaptive Gait framework" built upon an **asymmetric Actor-Critic architecture** combined with a novel **contrastive representation learning** module and an **adaptive gait mechanism**.

**A. Asymmetric Actor-Critic Network:**
- **Actor Network:** The actor $\pi_{\theta}$ receives an 84-dimensional raw sensor observation ($o_t$), comprising control commands, projected gravity, joint angles and velocities, previous action, body angular velocities, and gait phase. An RNN (RNN$_a$) processes this input along with its hidden state to produce a 256-dimensional intermediate representation ($m_a_t$). This representation is then fed into a small multilayer perceptron (MLP) to output a 26-dimensional action ($a_t$). The action is split into 23 joint-level pose changes ($a_j_t$) and 3 gait adjustment parameters ($a_{\delta_t}$). A PD controller translates these into joint torques. Crucially, the actor operates *solely* on proprioceptive information, making it deployable in the real world without exteroceptive sensors.
- **Critic Network:** The critic network, designed for accurate value estimation during simulation, utilizes a more comprehensive observation ($s_t$) that includes velocity, center-of-mass position, and two 5x5 foot-level height maps. Additionally, it accesses privileged information in the form of a global terrain map ($h_t$, 17x11). A CNN processes $h_t$ to extract a 32-dimensional feature vector ($u_t$). Concurrently, a separate RNN (RNN$_c$) processes $s_t$ to produce a 256-dimensional feature ($m_c_t$). These two feature vectors ($m_c_t$ and $u_t$) are concatenated to form a 288-dimensional vector ($z_t$), which is then fed into a fully connected layer to compute the value function $V(s_t, a_t)$. This asymmetric design allows the critic to guide learning with rich environmental context while keeping the actor lightweight for deployment.

**B. Contrastive Representation Learning:**
This is the core contribution for distilling environmental awareness. Inspired by contrastive learning techniques, the method forces the actor's proprioceptive latent state to encode privileged environmental information.
- **Objective:** To align the actor’s hidden state ($m_a_t$) with the immediate privileged environmental context ($u_t$ and $s_t$) from the *same time step*.
- **Mechanism:** An InfoNCE loss function is employed.
 - A **Source Encoder (MLP$_S$)** maps the actor's hidden state $m_a_t$ to an embedding $I_S_t$.
 - A **Target Encoder (MLP$_T$)** maps the privileged CNN feature $u_t$ (with gradients stopped) and the privileged observation $s_t$ to an embedding $I_T_t$.
- **Loss Calculation:** For each time step $t$, the pair ($I_S_t, I_T_t$) forms a positive pair. The InfoNCE loss maximizes the similarity between positive pairs while pushing apart negative pairs (i.e., $I_S_t$ with $I_T_{t'}$ where $t' \neq t$). This directly compels the actor’s purely proprioceptive latent state to implicitly learn and infer environmental properties by associating its dynamic responses with the underlying causes provided by the privileged context during training.

**C. Training with PPO:**
The entire framework is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). PPO's clipped objective function ensures stable updates by constraining new policies close to old ones. The reward function is a weighted sum of various components (detailed in Table I), including:
- **Locomotion Goals:** Linear/angular velocity tracking, body orientation, body height.
- **Gait Stability:** Feet clearance, smoothness, action rate, and crucially, a **contact pattern reward** that penalizes mismatches between the robot's actual foot contact state and the target contact mask dictated by the adaptive gait clock.
- **Safety/Efficiency:** Joint accelerations, power, torques, joint position/velocity/torque limits, stumble detection, feet slip, ground parallelism.

**D. Adaptive Gait Mechanism:**
To enable proactive adaptation, the action vector $a_t$ is augmented with three additional components: $\delta_0(t)$, $\delta_1(t)$, and $\delta_2(t)$. These parameters allow dynamic modulation of the gait cycle:
- **Gait Frequency Adaptation ($\delta_0(t)$):** $\delta_0(t)$ directly influences the rate of change of a global phase $\phi(t)$, effectively accelerating or decelerating the gait cycle.
- **Foot-Phase Offsets ($\delta_1(t), \delta_2(t)$):** These parameters independently shift the phases of two sinusoidal signals, $s(t)$ and $c(t)$, which are used to drive the stance/swing trajectories of each foot.
- **Gait Phase Mask:** A threshold $\gamma$ applied to $s(t)$ and $c(t)$ determines whether a foot is in a stance or swing phase. This allows the policy to generate dynamic, flexible bipedal walking patterns.
This mechanism, when informed by the contrastive learning-derived environmental awareness, enables the policy to proactively adjust its gait rhythm based on its inferred understanding of the terrain.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the proposed method, "AdaptiveGait (Ours)," was rigorously evaluated through extensive simulations and real-world experiments on a full-sized humanoid robot.

**A. Experimental Platform and Environment:**
- **Robot:** Adam Lite, a 1.6-meter tall, 60-kg humanoid with 25 degrees of freedom (23 controlled) and QDD force-controlled actuators, especially robust in its legs.
- **Simulation:** Isaac Gym with 4096 parallel instances, using PPO for optimization. A detailed physics model of Adam Lite and extensive domain randomization were employed to minimize the sim-to-real gap. Training was efficient, completing in approximately 9 hours on an RTX 4090 GPU. MuJoCo was also used for sim-to-sim validation.
- **Terrain Curriculum:** Included challenging terrains: steps (5-35 cm), slopes (2-34°), and uneven terrain.
- **Baselines and Ablation Studies:** The proposed method was compared against several baselines (DWL, DreamWaQ) and various ablations of its own components (e.g., without contrastive learning, with sequential contrast, with fixed gait cycle, clock-free).

**B. Simulation Results:**
- **Training Progression (Fig. 7, Left):** AdaptiveGait (Ours) demonstrated significantly faster terrain level progression during training, reaching the highest difficulty levels within just 5,000 training steps, outperforming all baselines. This indicates superior learning efficiency and adaptability in simulation.
- **Success Rate vs. Command Velocity (Fig. 7, Right):** Our method consistently maintained superior performance across a wide range of command velocities. While all methods showed declining success rates with increased velocity, AdaptiveGait (Ours) exhibited the most robust performance degradation, maintaining over 75% success rate even at the highest tested velocities. Ablation studies confirmed that both the terrain pathway (adaptive gait) and contrastive learning contribute significantly to overall performance.

**C. Real-World Validation (Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer):**
The true strength of the method was demonstrated through zero-shot transfer to the physical Adam Lite robot, showcasing highly robust locomotion on challenging real-world terrains:
- **Stair and Slope Traversal (Fig. 1, 8, 10):** The robot successfully ascended 30 cm high steps (Fig. 1, 10d) and traversed slopes up to 26.5° (Fig. 8, 10a, 10b), including multi-slope sequences and uneven terrains (Fig. 10c). The robot maintained stable locomotion, exhibiting appropriate foot-lifting behavior without backward slipping.
- **Joint Trajectory and Torque Analysis (Fig. 8, Bottom):** During stair ascent, hip and knee pitch positions showed pronounced peaks corresponding to precise leg-lifting motions, interspersed with stable level-ground locomotion phases. Torque data revealed higher variability during stair climbing, reflecting increased mechanical demands, contrasted with more consistent torque patterns on level terrain indicative of optimized energy usage.
- **Velocity-Torque Scatter Plots (Fig. 9):** Analysis of major leg joints during stair climbing highlighted that hip and knee pitch joints demanded the highest torques, particularly when providing forceful support, which correlated with lower joint velocities. This indicated the policy's learned ability to manage large torques for steep ascents while maintaining stability.

**D. Terrain-Specific Ablation Analysis (Fig. 11):**
Quantitative ablation studies on real-world terrain challenges reinforced the importance of each component:
- **Stair Traversing (Fig. 11, Left):** AdaptiveGait (Ours) maintained over 95% success on moderate stairs (10-15 cm) and an impressive 85% on 30 cm stairs. Removing contrastive learning significantly degraded performance on higher stairs, and baselines (DWL, DreamWAQ) showed substantial drops, falling below 60% in extreme scenarios.
- **Slope Traversal (Fig. 11, Right):** Our approach consistently outperformed all other methods across various slope angles, achieving over 90% success rate even on steep 26.5° slopes.

In summary, the main findings conclusively demonstrate that the proposed contrastive representation learning framework, coupled with an adaptive gait mechanism, enables a purely proprioceptive policy to achieve highly robust, proactive, and adaptive locomotion on a full-sized humanoid robot in complex real-world environments, significantly outperforming existing baselines and ablations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a significant advancement in the field of humanoid locomotion and robot learning, primarily by offering an elegant solution to the long-standing "sim-to-real dilemma" for proactive control.

**1. Resolution of the Reactive vs. Proactive Dilemma:** The most significant contribution is the successful demonstration of a paradigm that imbues a purely proprioceptive policy with proactive capabilities. By distilling privileged environmental awareness from simulation into the actor's latent state through spatial contrastive learning, the robot gains "foresight" without needing complex, fragile, and computationally expensive exteroceptive sensors at deployment. This effectively resolves the trade-off between the robustness of reactive control and the proactivity of perception-driven systems.

**2. Novel and Efficient Knowledge Distillation:** The introduction of a direct, end-to-end spatial contrastive learning framework is a novel and efficient approach to bridging the sim-to-real information gap. Unlike previous methods that might involve complex world models or multi-stage teacher-student frameworks, this method directly trains the policy's latent representation to encode immediate environmental context. This simplicity and efficiency are critical for practical applications.

**3. Intelligent Adaptive Gait Control:** The synergy between the distilled environmental awareness and the adaptive gait mechanism is powerful. The policy learns not just to react, but to *intelligently and proactively* adjust its gait frequency and foot phases based on an inferred understanding of the terrain. This goes beyond traditional rigid or unstable clock-free gaits, leading to more versatile, energy-efficient, and robust bipedal walking patterns across diverse and challenging terrains.

**4. Strong Real-World Validation:** The comprehensive zero-shot sim-to-real transfer validation on a full-sized, high-DOF humanoid robot (Adam Lite) over extremely challenging terrains (e.g., 30 cm steps, 26.5° slopes) is a crucial testament to the practical effectiveness and robustness of the approach. This level of real-world performance is a high bar in humanoid robotics and lends substantial credibility to the research.

**5. Potential Impact:**
- **Reduced Hardware Complexity and Cost:** By minimizing reliance on expensive and failure-prone exteroceptive sensor suites and their associated processing pipelines, this work paves the way for simpler, more robust, and potentially more cost-effective humanoid robot deployments in unstructured environments.
- **Enhanced Autonomy in Unstructured Environments:** Robots endowed with this "distilled awareness" can navigate highly challenging terrains with greater autonomy and adaptability, leading to applications in disaster response, logistics, exploration, and domestic assistance.
- **Generalizability of Method:** The contrastive learning approach for distilling simulation-privileged information could be applicable to other robotic tasks where sensing limitations exist in the real world but rich data is available in simulation.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a strong foundation for future research in areas such as learning more complex, dynamic maneuvers, handling unexpected disturbances informed by this awareness, and exploring longer-term planning or navigation capabilities based on inferred environmental context.

In conclusion, this paper delivers a principled architectural design that achieves both training efficiency and deployment robustness for real-world humanoid locomotion, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable with purely proprioceptive control in complex environments.
