# Embodied Robot Manipulation in the Era of Foundation Models: Planning and Learning Perspectives

> [!summary] Summary
> A survey that organizes foundation-model-based robot manipulation via a unified two-layer abstraction: high-level planning (language, code, geometric, affordance, and 3D-representation methods) that produces structured guidance, and learning-based low-level action modeling (input, latent, and policy learning) that grounds it into executable actions, then synthesizes four future directions.

## Problem

- Prior surveys organize robot manipulation literature by model family, such as VLA, diffusion policy, or generative models, obscuring the functional coupling between planning and action generation.
- High-level planning research (LLM, code, geometric, affordance, 3D) and low-level action modeling research are typically studied separately, missing how structured guidance actually couples with action execution.
- Manipulation benchmarks differ widely in embodiment, environment diversity, and interaction complexity with no standardized evaluation protocol, so reported scores cannot be fairly compared across methods.

## Method

- Formalizes the pipeline as two ==coupled mappings==, an ==f_plan== function producing structured planning artifacts from observations and language, and an ==f_act== function generating executable actions conditioned on that plan.
- ==Six-category high-level taxonomy==: LLM-based task planning, MLLM-based task planning, programmatic (code) planning, geometric constraint-based planning, affordance-based planning, and 3D representation-based planning, each covering distinct planning artifacts.
- ==Three-component low-level taxonomy==: input modeling (2D/3D vision, tactile, force, audio), latent learning (pretrained representations and discrete/continuous latent actions), and policy learning (MLP, Transformer, diffusion, flow matching).

## Results

- Table I contrasts **14** representative methods across planning and action categories, showing RT-1 trained on **130K** demonstrations across **744** tasks versus OpenVLA scaled to **970K** real robot demonstrations.
- Table II compares **10** simulators and benchmarks spanning MetaWorld's **2.5K** demos and **80** objects to RoboCasa's **100K+** demos and **2509** objects, exposing large gaps in scale and diversity.
- Cross-category comparison shows only **one** of **14** highlighted methods (SpatialVLA, **1.1M** demos) targets explicit 3D spatial grounding, confirming that most VLA systems remain confined to 2D RGB perception.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Organizing manipulation literature by functional role, planning versus action, rather than by model family surfaces shared design patterns across VLA, diffusion, and code-generation approaches that family-based grouping hides.
> - Semantically plausible LLM-generated plans routinely violate geometric, kinematic, or contact constraints, so downstream grounding and execution feedback stay necessary even as language reasoning capability improves.
> - No single benchmark spans embodiment diversity, long-horizon complexity, and contact-rich interaction at once, so reported scores should be read against each benchmark's scope, not as universal intelligence measures.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses the rapidly evolving field of embodied intelligence, specifically focusing on robotic manipulation, which is recognized as a complex embodied task. Robotic manipulation requires the integration of perception, semantic understanding, task reasoning, physically grounded action generation, and reliable execution. The advent of large-scale foundation models in computer vision, natural language processing, and multimodal learning has significantly impacted this domain, driving new approaches for data-driven robot control.

Prior research in robotic manipulation has largely relied on deep visuomotor policies, imitation learning, and reinforcement learning. More recently, robotic foundation models have emerged, offering enhanced perceptual and semantic representations and enabling flexible task conditioning through multimodal inputs. This survey positions itself within this context by providing a structured analysis of how these foundation models are integrated into robotic manipulation systems. It differentiates itself from existing surveys that typically organize literature by model families (e.g., vision-language-action models, diffusion policies) by instead emphasizing the functional roles of different components and their interactions across a multi-level control hierarchy. The paper aims to offer a unified perspective on the algorithmic structure of modern robotic manipulation, bridging high-level reasoning with low-level action generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this survey is to systematically examine recent learning-based approaches in robotic manipulation, particularly those leveraging foundation models, through a unified algorithmic abstraction. This abstraction decomposes robotic manipulation into high-level planning and low-level action modeling components.

The motivation for this work stems from the inherent complexity of robotic manipulation, which necessitates a coherent framework to integrate diverse research advancements. Specific motivations and objectives include:

- **Addressing the Multi-faceted Challenge of Robotic Manipulation:** The authors highlight that robotic manipulation is challenging due to the need to seamlessly integrate perception (interpreting sensory data), semantic understanding (comprehending task meaning), task reasoning (breaking down complex tasks), physically grounded action generation (producing executable movements that respect physics), and reliable execution (performing actions robustly). This complexity is further exacerbated by the variety of manipulation scenarios (e.g., basic, dexterous, deformable object, mobile, quadrupedal, humanoid manipulation).
- **Structuring the Role of Foundation Models:** With the rise of foundation models, their application in robotics has been diverse. The paper seeks to clarify their specific contributions by categorizing them into two primary roles:
 - Generating structured planning artifacts (e.g., task plans, code, geometric constraints, affordances, 3D representations) that serve as guidance or inputs for downstream action generation.
 - Directly modeling executable actions and trajectories.
- **Developing a Unified Algorithmic Taxonomy:** The paper aims to move beyond classifications based on model families (e.g., vision-language-action models, diffusion policies) to a functional decomposition. This is intended to enable more coherent comparisons across approaches and reveal common design patterns that might be obscured by a model-centric view. The proposed taxonomy explicitly divides the problem into High-Level Planning and Low-Level Action Modeling, with an implicit Actuation-Level Control.
- **Characterizing Robotic Foundation Models:** The authors establish practical properties for defining a robotic foundation model: diverse pretraining, broad capability scope, and reusability for downstream adaptation. This characterization helps to delineate the specific types of models under consideration within the survey.
- **Identifying Open Challenges and Future Directions:** A crucial objective is to synthesize emerging trends and pinpoint critical areas for future research. This includes addressing issues related to scalability, generalization, data efficiency, multimodal physical interaction, and safety, providing a roadmap for the research community.

In summary, the motivation is to provide a clear, structured, and comprehensive understanding of the current state of foundation-model-based robotic manipulation, to facilitate future research by clarifying the landscape and highlighting key opportunities and challenges.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper presents a comprehensive survey and structured analysis of foundation-model-based robotic manipulation. The core methodology involves:

**A. Developing a Hierarchical Taxonomy:**
The authors propose a three-layer taxonomy to organize robotic manipulation systems:
1. **High-Level Planning (Section II):** This layer is responsible for generating structured guidance or intermediate representations. It determines action intent, temporal organization, and attentional focus.
2. **Low-Level Action Modeling (Section III):** This layer translates observations and task specifications into executable actions or trajectories, grounding high-level reasoning in physical execution.
3. **Actuation-Level Control:** This final layer converts action commands into motor torques for physical robot movement. (This layer is outside the primary scope of the survey but acknowledged for completeness).

The survey primarily focuses on the **High-Level Planning** and **Learning-Based Low-Level Action Modeling**, where foundation models have had the most significant impact.

**B. Defining Robotic Foundation Models:**
A robotic foundation model is characterized by three practical properties:
- **Diverse Pretraining:** Trained on varied robotic and/or multimodal data.
- **Broad Capability Scope:** Enables transferable manipulation across tasks, objects, or environments.
- **Reusability:** Provides reusable representations or policies for downstream adaptation.

**C. Detailed Breakdown of High-Level Planning (Section II):**
This section systematically categorizes approaches based on the type of structured artifact generated:
- **LLM-based Task Planning:** Focuses on using Large Language Models (LLMs) for semantic reasoning, task decomposition, and skill selection, often integrating real-time feedback for dynamic plan revision (e.g., SayCan, Inner Monologue, LLM-Planner).
- **MLLM-based Task Planning:** Explores Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for joint reasoning over vision and language, enabling end-to-end task reasoning or enhanced planning through chain-of-thought and scene graph structures (e.g., PaLM-E, EmbodiedGPT, RoboBrain).
- **Programmatic Planning:** Utilizes LLMs to generate executable code that interacts with robot perception and control APIs, providing explicit structure, conditional logic, and compositionality (e.g., Code as Policies, ProgPrompt, Demo2Code).
- **Geometric Constraint-based Planning:** Focuses on translating language and perception into structured spatial objectives or relational constraints for classical motion planners, enhancing physical grounding (e.g., VoxPoser, ReKep, CoPa).
- **Affordance-based Planning:** Examines how agents infer action possibilities from objects and environments, categorized into geometric, visual, semantic, and multimodal affordance learning (e.g., Ditto, Transporter Networks, CLIPort).
- **3D Representation-based Planning:** Investigates the use of 3D representations (e.g., Gaussian Splatting, Neural Descriptor Fields) as mid-level planning interfaces to transform perception into action-relevant representations (e.g., Splat-MOVER, F3RM, RoboSplat).

**D. Detailed Breakdown of Low-Level Action Modeling (Section III):**
This section organizes learning-based approaches into three components:
- **Learning Strategies (Section III-A):** Describes how policies are acquired, including:
 - **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Model-free (direct interaction, often combined with pretraining) and model-based (leveraging environment dynamics models for imagination or planning).
 - **Imitation Learning (IL):** Learning from expert demonstrations, either from action (Behavior Cloning) or from observation (inferring behavior from trajectories).
 - **Learning with Auxiliary Tasks:** Enhancing policies with self-supervised or weakly supervised objectives (e.g., world modeling, goal extraction, multimodal alignment).
- **Input Modeling (Section III-B):** Details how sensory modalities are processed:
 - **Vision-Action Models:** Direct mappings from 2D or 3D visual observations to motor commands.
 - **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** Integrates semantic grounding (language), spatial reasoning (vision), and action generation, categorized by 2D vs. 3D vision input and model-oriented vs. model-agnostic strategies (e.g., RT-1, RT-2, OpenVLA). This includes sub-categories like Latent Learning for VLA, Action Quantization for VLA, and Dual-/Multi-system VLA.
 - **Tactile-based Action Models:** Incorporating fine-grained tactile feedback for contact-rich manipulation, covering tactile latent learning, tactile-action, tactile-vision-action, tactile-language-action, and tactile-vision-language-action models.
 - **Extra Modalities as Input:** Discusses the inclusion of force and audio sensing.
- **Latent Learning (Section III-C):** Explores how robust and generalizable intermediate representations are learned and utilized:
 - **Pretrained Latent Learning:** Acquiring transferable visual representations from diverse datasets (general images, human egocentric, robotic datasets).
 - **Latent Action Learning:** Learning action abstractions (discrete via quantization or continuous vector representations) that bridge perception and control, including latent dynamics, implicit world modeling, latent diffusion, Koopman-based dynamics, and goal/instruction-conditioned latents.
- **Policy Learning (Section III-D):** Describes how learned representations are decoded into executable actions:
 - MLP-based, Transformer-based, Diffusion Policy (DP), Flow Matching (FM) Policy, and other paradigms (e.g., state-space models, frequency-domain policies).

**E. Identifying Future Research Directions (Section IV):**
Based on the structured review, the authors synthesize four core challenges and directions: building a general robot brain, addressing data and evaluation bottlenecks, achieving richer multimodal physical interaction, and ensuring safety and collaboration.

Through this structured and hierarchical analysis, the paper provides a framework for understanding current capabilities and limitations, fostering systematic comparisons, and guiding future research efforts in robotic manipulation using foundation models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The core contribution of this paper is the presentation of a comprehensive, structured taxonomy of foundation-model-based robotic manipulation, along with a detailed review of current methods and the identification of prospective future research directions.

**A. Unified Algorithmic Abstraction and Taxonomy:**
The primary "finding" is the successful organization of a diverse and rapidly expanding body of literature into a coherent, multi-level framework. The paper establishes a taxonomy that decomposes robotic manipulation into:
- **High-Level Planning (Section II):** Responsible for generating structured guidance. This layer encompasses six distinct categories:
 - **LLM-based Task Planning:** Leverages LLMs for semantic reasoning and task decomposition, often requiring grounding in robotic affordances and real-time feedback.
 - **MLLM-based Task Planning:** Utilizes multimodal LLMs to integrate vision and language for more direct task reasoning, from adapting general MLLMs to developing robotics-specific MLLMs.
 - **Programmatic Planning:** Translates high-level instructions into executable code, providing structured, conditional, and compositional plans.
 - **Geometric Constraint-based Planning:** Infers structured spatial objectives and relational constraints from language and perception for downstream motion optimization, enhancing physical grounding.
 - **Affordance-based Planning:** Focuses on learning action possibilities from objects and environments, spanning geometric, visual, semantic, and multimodal approaches.
 - **3D Representation-based Planning:** Employs 3D scene representations (e.g., Gaussian Splatting, neural descriptor fields) as intermediate interfaces to derive action-relevant spatial information.
- **Low-Level Action Modeling (Section III):** Focuses on transforming observations and task specifications into executable actions. This layer is further categorized into:
 - **Learning Strategies:** Outlines the core paradigms of Reinforcement Learning (model-free and model-based), Imitation Learning (from action and from observation), and Learning with Auxiliary Tasks (world modeling, goal extraction, representation shaping).
 - **Input Modeling:** Describes how diverse sensory inputs are processed, including 2D and 3D Vision-Action models, a detailed taxonomy of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models (covering 2D and 3D vision, model-oriented and model-agnostic strategies, latent learning, action quantization, dual-system VLAs, and methods for efficiency, robustness, and long-horizon generalization), Tactile-based action models, and the incorporation of other modalities like force and audio.
 - **Latent Learning:** Examines the acquisition of robust and generalizable intermediate representations, distinguishing between Pretrained Latent Learning (from general, human egocentric, or robotic datasets) and Latent Action Learning (using discrete or continuous latent representations, often based on dynamics, world models, diffusion, or Koopman operators).
 - **Policy Learning:** Details how representations are converted into actions through different policy architectures, including MLP-based, Transformer-based, Diffusion Policies (DP), Flow Matching (FM) Policies, and other specialized paradigms.

**B. Integration of Foundation Models:**
A key finding is that foundation models are integrated into robotic manipulation systems in two principal ways:
1. **High-Level Guidance:** By generating structured planning artifacts (e.g., symbolic plans, programs, geometric constraints, affordance priors, structured 3D world models) that guide downstream action generation.
2. **Direct Action Modeling:** By directly parameterizing executable actions and trajectories, especially evident in advanced VLA models and diffusion policies.

**C. Identification of Capability Boundaries and Failure Modes:**
Table I comprehensively summarizes representative methods within each category, detailing their core ideas, modalities, data regimes, advantages, use cases, and, crucially, their limitations and characteristic failure modes. This provides a critical overview of what current foundation-model-based manipulation can and cannot reliably achieve. For instance, LLM-based planning may generate physically infeasible plans, programmatic planning can produce unsafe code, and 3D representation-based planning is sensitive to reconstruction errors.

**D. Synthesis of Emerging Trends and Future Challenges:**
The survey concludes by identifying four tightly coupled challenges that represent crucial future research directions:
1. **Building a True Robot Brain:** Moving towards general-purpose architectures capable of robust generalization across unseen task structures, object categories, environments, and embodiments, requiring continual learning, memory, and tight coupling between planning and control.
2. **Data and Evaluation Bottlenecks:** Addressing the scarcity of real-world data, limitations in simulator fidelity, and the need for standardized, reproducible evaluation frameworks that go beyond task-specific benchmarks.
3. **Multimodal Physical Interaction:** Developing systems that can fuse and adaptively integrate a richer variety of sensory streams (touch, audio, thermal) beyond vision and language, and robustly manipulate complex, deformable, or dynamic materials.
4. **Safety and Collaboration:** Ensuring intrinsic safety, supporting safe multi-robot collaboration, and developing autonomous fault detection and recovery mechanisms for real-world deployment, potentially through hybrid learning and classical control paradigms.

Overall, the paper provides a structured map of the research landscape, clarifying the architectural and functional roles of different components and highlighting the progress and persistent challenges in realizing adaptable and trustworthy robotic manipulation with foundation models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey holds significant implications for the field of robotic manipulation and the broader area of embodied artificial intelligence. Its impact can be primarily categorized into guiding research, facilitating understanding, and setting future agendas.

**A. Guiding Research and Development:**
By providing a comprehensive and functionally organized taxonomy, the paper offers a structured view of the design space for foundation-model-based robotic manipulation. This can help researchers:
- **Identify gaps and opportunities:** The detailed breakdown of existing methods, along with their advantages and limitations (as summarized in Table I), can pinpoint underdeveloped areas or identify where specific foundation model capabilities are most needed.
- **Foster cross-disciplinary collaboration:** The unified framework encourages researchers from different sub-fields (e.g., perception, planning, control, natural language processing) to understand how their work fits into the larger robotic manipulation pipeline and how they can leverage or contribute to foundation models.
- **Inform architectural design:** The dual lens of high-level planning and low-level action modeling, coupled with the detailed review of components like latent learning and various policy paradigms, can inform the design of more modular, efficient, and robust robotic systems.

**B. Facilitating Understanding and Comparison:**
The survey's emphasis on functional roles rather than solely model families offers a more intuitive and comparative understanding of different approaches. This allows for:
- **Coherent comparisons:** Researchers can more easily compare methods that solve similar problems at different layers of the hierarchy, regardless of their underlying model architecture (e.g., how different methods achieve high-level task decomposition).
- **Revealing common patterns:** By abstracting away specific model details, the survey reveals recurring design patterns and challenges across diverse learning-based approaches, contributing to a deeper understanding of the field's evolution.
- **Educational resource:** The detailed and organized presentation can serve as a valuable reference for newcomers to the field, providing a foundational understanding of the complexities and current state of robotic manipulation research.

**C. Setting Future Agendas and Addressing Core Challenges:**
The explicit identification of four core challenges (building a true robot brain, data/evaluation bottlenecks, multimodal physical interaction, and safety/collaboration) provides a clear roadmap for future research directions. This is impactful because it:
- **Directs collective effort:** By highlighting areas requiring significant advancements, the paper can help coalesce research efforts around critical, long-term goals rather than incremental improvements on existing benchmarks.
- **Emphasizes real-world relevance:** The focus on scalability, generalization, data efficiency, multimodal robustness, and safety underscores the importance of developing practical and deployable robotic systems that can operate effectively and safely in human environments.
- **Promotes ethical considerations:** The explicit inclusion of "Safety and Collaboration" as a core challenge stresses the increasing importance of incorporating ethical and societal considerations into the design and deployment of advanced robotic systems.

In conclusion, this survey is significant for its comprehensive and structured analysis, offering both a retrospective and prospective view of foundation-model-based robotic manipulation. It provides a valuable reference for the research community, aiming to accelerate progress towards more adaptive, general-purpose, and trustworthy robotic systems capable of operating in complex, open-world environments.
