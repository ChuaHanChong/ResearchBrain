# SIMART: Decomposing Monolithic Meshes into Sim-ready Articulated Assets via MLLM

> [!summary] Summary
> SIMART is a unified multimodal LLM framework that turns a static monolithic mesh into a simulation-ready articulated asset in one stage, jointly predicting part segmentation and URDF kinematics. A Sparse 3D VQ-VAE tokenizes only occupied surface voxels, cutting geometry tokens by about 70%. On SIMART-Bench it beats multi-stage baselines on joint type, axis, and part IoU, and its assets run in Isaac Sim.

## Problem

- Multi-stage articulated-asset pipelines decouple part decomposition, joint inference, and assembly, so errors accumulate and inferred joints end up incompatible with recovered part geometry.
- 3D segmentation priors optimize surface-level consistency rather than articulation, missing mechanically meaningful link boundaries; 2D vision-language prompting transfers unreliably to 3D part boundaries.
- Dense voxel tokenization spends most tokens on empty space, giving cubic sequence growth, memory exhaustion, and heavy downsampling that destroys axis-localization fidelity.

## Method

- ==Unified MLLM== on a Qwen3-VL-8B backbone consumes image, geometry, and text tokens jointly, emitting part-level voxel tokens plus structured URDF metadata: links, joint axes, limits, friction, density.
- **Sparse 3D VQ-VAE** maps a 64-cubed grid through a 3D-UNet into an 8x8x8 latent, assigning unoccupied cells a reserved zero token and quantizing only occupied voxels against a 4096-entry codebook.
- ==Coordinate-aware serialization== of voxel-position-code triplets, plus ==Gaussian-seed graph-smoothing segmentation==, transfers decoded sparse point clouds back onto original mesh faces, preserving input texture and geometry.

## Results

- On in-domain SIMART-Bench items SIMART reaches **0.928** joint-type accuracy, **0.080** axis error, and **0.690** part IoU, versus the strongest baseline's **0.822**, **0.208**, and **0.643**.
- On AI-generated out-of-distribution meshes part IoU rises to **0.777** against **0.618**; language-driven part grounding hits **0.807** IoU versus **0.507** for a segmentation-plus-VLM baseline.
- Ablation: dense tokens average **4138** tokens and hit out-of-memory; zero-token sparsity uses **516** tokens at **0.745** IoU, and adding vision lifts type accuracy to **0.937**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sparsity is an enabler, not an optimization: skipping empty voxels turns an infeasible dense-token generation task into one trainable within fixed context.
> - Reserved zero tokens beat simply dropping empty voxels, suggesting explicit emptiness markers preserve spatial topology that naive sparsification loses.
> - Images disambiguate geometry: objects sharing shape but differing in articulation need visual context, so pure 3D-token pipelines leave accuracy unclaimed.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The generation of high-quality articulated 3D assets is a critical area for advancements in embodied artificial intelligence (AI) and physics-based simulation. Existing 3D generation models predominantly focus on static meshes, leaving a gap in the automated creation of "sim-ready" interactive objects. The manual creation of such assets is resource-intensive, necessitating robust automated solutions.

Previous approaches to creating articulated objects exhibit several limitations. Direct regression of Universal Robot Description Format (URDF) models from images often compromises geometric fidelity, producing outputs that may be too coarse for high-quality simulations. Multi-stage pipelines, which decouple the process into part decomposition, joint parameter inference, and post-hoc assembly, are susceptible to error accumulation. Specifically, part decomposition methods, such as those relying on 2D vision-language models or 3D-native segmentation tools like PartField and P3SAM, may prioritize surface-level consistency over mechanically meaningful link boundaries, leading to parts that appear plausible but lack kinematic affordances. Joint estimation in these pipelines is sensitive to mesh artifacts and restrictive priors, frequently resulting in inferred joints that are incompatible with the recovered part geometry, yielding physically invalid articulations.

Furthermore, while recent 3D generative models can synthesize high-quality static assets, these outputs are typically monolithic and lack kinematic or physical metadata. Attempts to integrate multimodal large language models (MLLMs) for 3D understanding and generation have been constrained by inefficient 3D tokenization. Dense volumetric encodings, as used in approaches like PhysX-Anything and ShapeLLM-Omni, consume a large number of tokens for empty space, leading to prohibitive context lengths and memory overhead. This inefficiency limits their scalability, particularly for complex articulated objects and high-fidelity part-level generation.

SIMART addresses these limitations by proposing a unified MLLM framework. This framework aims to integrate 3D geometric understanding and generation into a single-stage process, directly transforming static 3D meshes into functional, simulation-ready articulated assets. It specifically targets the inefficiency of dense 3D tokenization by introducing a sparse representation, thereby positioning itself as a method that can handle complex geometries with improved computational efficiency while maintaining geometric and kinematic accuracy.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an automated, unified MLLM framework capable of transforming monolithic static 3D meshes into functional, simulation-ready articulated assets. This involves jointly performing part-level decomposition and precise kinematic parameter prediction.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges in the field:
1. **Demand for Sim-Ready Assets:** There is a substantial demand for articulated 3D assets with integrated physical properties and kinematic information for applications in physics-based animation and robotic interactive simulation. However, the majority of existing 3D assets are unarticulated.
2. **Limitations of Manual Creation:** The manual creation of high-quality articulated assets is a labor-intensive and time-consuming process, making it difficult to scale for diverse and large-scale applications.
3. **Deficiencies in Existing Automated Methods:**
 - **Compromised Geometric Fidelity:** Prior methods that directly regress URDF models from images often produce coarse geometric outputs that are unsuitable for high-quality simulations.
 - **Error Accumulation in Multi-Stage Pipelines:** Decomposed approaches, which separate part decomposition from joint parameter inference, tend to accumulate errors across decoupled modules. Part decomposition may not be articulation-aware, and joint estimation can be sensitive to geometric imperfections, leading to physically inconsistent results.
 - **Computational Inefficiency of 3D-Native MLLMs:** Existing MLLMs designed for 3D understanding often rely on dense volumetric tokenization. This approach is computationally expensive due to the large number of tokens consumed by empty space, resulting in high memory overhead and limited scalability for complex articulated objects. This bottleneck hinders the application of MLLMs for detailed part-level generation.
4. **Need for Unified and Efficient Solution:** The researchers identified a need for a unified MLLM paradigm that can simultaneously understand an initial 3D asset and directly generate per-part geometry along with a structured URDF specification (links, joint axes, and limits). A central technical challenge was to develop an efficient, sparse 3D representation to support MLLM-based understanding and scalable, high-fidelity generation without memory exhaustion.

By addressing these points, SIMART aims to provide a robust, automated pipeline for creating physically accurate and kinematically aware 3D assets, which can significantly benefit fields like embodied AI and virtual reality.

### 3. Methodology and approach

SIMART proposes a unified MLLM framework that processes multimodal inputs to jointly perform part-level mesh decomposition and kinematic parameter prediction. The overall pipeline integrates 3D geometric encoding, multimodal reasoning, and structured output generation.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The framework takes multimodal inputs `I = {I_vis, G_geo, T_txt}`, consisting of visual observations, raw geometry data, and language instructions. Its objective is to generate a simulation-ready asset `A = (M_seg, P_sim)`, where `M_seg` is a set of part-segmented meshes and `P_sim` is comprehensive simulation metadata including kinematic parameters (joint types, axes, limits) and dynamic physical properties (scale, friction, density).

#### 3.2. Unified MLLM Architecture

SIMART adopts the Qwen3-VL-8B architecture as its MLLM backbone. This model leverages its pre-training on large-scale image-text datasets and its capabilities for physical world understanding, enabling reasoning about abstract physical attributes and kinematic structures.

- **Input Processing:** The framework processes a heterogeneous sequence of vision, geometry, and text tokens, mapping them into a unified latent space.
 - **Vision Modality:** An RGB image `I_vis` is encoded by a Vision Transformer (ViT) into visual features `F_vis`.
 - **Geometry Modality (Sparse 3D VQ-VAE):** The raw input mesh `G_geo` is first discretized into a 64x64x64 voxel grid.
 - **3D-Unet Encoder:** This encoder maps the voxel grid into a compact latent feature grid `Z` (initially 16x16x16, then aggregated to 8x8x8 with a feature dimension of 64 per token).
 - **Vector Quantization (with Sparse Zero Token):** A key innovation, this stage selectively quantizes only occupied voxels. Unoccupied voxels are assigned a specialized zero token (`e_zero`) from the codebook. This approach reduces the number of informative tokens by approximately 70%, mitigating memory exhaustion.
 - **Coordinate-Aware Tokenization:** Each occupied voxel is serialized into a triplet `⟨voxel⟩ [xyz] [K]`. `[xyz]` is a linearized index representing discrete coordinates within an 8x8x8 grid (e.g., `64x + 8y + z`). `[K]` is the discrete geometry index from the codebook, which has 4,096 entries (the 0-th index reserved for `e_zero`). This sparse tokenization maintains spatial location information for the MLLM.
 - **Text Modality:** Language instruction `T_txt` is embedded into tokens `F_txt` to guide the model towards specific tasks like part grounding or URDF generation.
 - **Concatenation:** The modality-specific features (`F_vis`, `F_geo`, `F_txt`) are concatenated to form the final multimodal sequence for the MLLM.

- **Output Generation:** The MLLM generates a hybrid output sequence containing discrete part-specific voxel tokens and a structured URDF specification.

#### 3.3. Simulator-Ready Assets Process

The final stage synthesizes a functional, simulation-ready asset by integrating geometric components with kinematic metadata.
- **Part Segmentation:** The MLLM-generated part-specific voxel tokens are decoded by the Sparse 3D VQ-VAE decoder into sparse point clouds `S_p`. These point clouds act as seeds for a graph-based surface segmentation algorithm applied to the input mesh `G_geo`. Vertex-wise probability distributions are initialized using a Gaussian kernel, followed by an iterative graph-smoothing operator and majority voting to assign final face labels `M_seg`. The original mesh texture is preserved.
- **URDF Generation:** SIMART directly generates a structured URDF specification (JSON-like description) defining the kinematic chain (parent-child hierarchies, joint configurations), physical attributes (joint limits, material density, surface friction), and global scale.
- **Assembly:** The segmented sub-meshes are combined with this articulated blueprint to produce the final simulation-ready asset `A`.

#### 3.4. Training Details

- **Sparse 3D VQ-VAE Training:** The VQ-VAE is pre-trained on a 500k-object subset (following TRELLIS data distribution) for 60,000 steps per stage across two stages, utilizing 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs. The training objective includes a binary cross-entropy reconstruction loss and a commitment loss.
- **MLLM Fine-tuning:** The Qwen3-VL-8B backbone is fine-tuned for 30,000 steps on a cluster of 32 NVIDIA A100 GPUs. Structured prompt templates unify multimodal inputs during training.
- **Dataset:** A dataset of 39,600 3D objects from PhysXNet and PartNet-Mobility is used, comprising 5,600 articulated models and 34,000 static objects. Articulated models are augmented by rendering each in 20 diverse kinematic states. This forms the basis for two large-scale instruction-following datasets (URDF generation and part grounding), each containing 960k QA pairs.
- **SIMART-Bench:** A new benchmark was curated for evaluation, consolidating In-Domain assets from PartNet-Mobility with Out-of-Distribution (OOD) objects generated by AI models (e.g., Hunyuan3d-V3.1). These assets include expert manual annotations for articulation accuracy.

### 4. Main findings and results

The SIMART framework was evaluated quantitatively and qualitatively on articulated object generation, kinematic awareness, and 3D part understanding tasks.

#### 4.1. Articulated Object and Kinematic Awareness

- **Metrics:** Performance was assessed using Type Accuracy (joint classification correctness), Axis Error (angular deviation of predicted joint axis), Origin Error (L2 distance between predicted and ground-truth joint origins), Intersection over Union (IoU) for geometric decomposition, and Chamfer Distance (CD) for part mesh reconstruction error.
- **Quantitative Results (Table 1):** SIMART demonstrated superior performance across all five metrics on both In-Domain (ID) items from PhysXNet and AI-generated items (SIMART-Bench).
 - **On ID Items:** SIMART achieved a Type Accuracy of 0.928, an Axis Error of 0.080, an Origin Error of 0.111, an IoU of 0.690, and a CD of 0.087. These values represent improvements over existing state-of-the-art methods such as Urdformer, Articulate-Anything, Physx-Anything, and Particulate. For instance, SIMART's Type Accuracy significantly surpassed Articulate-Anything (0.891) and Particulate (0.822). Its Axis and Origin Errors were notably lower than those of Particulate (0.208 and 0.204 respectively).
 - **On AI-generated Items:** SIMART maintained its lead with a Type Accuracy of 0.831, an Axis Error of 0.136, an Origin Error of 0.145, an IoU of 0.777, and a CD of 0.079. The performance on AI-generated objects indicates generalization capabilities beyond existing datasets.
- **Analysis:** Unlike most generative baselines that frequently produce simplified or misaligned geometries, SIMART's direct processing of input meshes via the Sparse VQ-VAE and MLLM-driven part segmentation contributed to superior structural fidelity and kinematic accuracy, closely matching ground-truth configurations. Existing approaches often lacked the ability to process raw mesh inputs directly, leading to poor geometric alignment.

#### 4.2. 3D Part Understanding

- **Metrics:** This task evaluated the model’s ability to ground functional natural language descriptions to specific object components, quantified by IoU (spatial overlap) and CD (geometric fidelity) of the reconstructed parts.
- **Quantitative Results (Table 2):** SIMART outperformed Physx-Anything and a P3SAM+Qwen3-VL baseline on AI-generated items. SIMART achieved an IoU of 0.807 and a CD of 0.018, significantly better than Physx-Anything (IoU 0.067, CD 0.347) and P3SAM+Qwen3-VL (IoU 0.507, CD 0.234).
- **Analysis:** The results suggest that SIMART effectively leverages coordinate-aware tokenization and the MLLM's extensive world knowledge to precisely link functional descriptions with physical coordinates, enabling accurate spatial localization even on novel geometries.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Impact of Sparse 3D VQ-VAE (Table 3):**
 - **Dense Tokenization:** A dense baseline resulted in Out-of-Memory (OOM) errors during fine-tuning for complex, multi-part objects due to the linear scaling of sequence length with the number of parts.
 - **Force Sparse (without zero token):** This configuration, retaining tokens only at occupied voxel coordinates, reduced sequence length but yielded lower performance compared to the zero-token approach.
 - **Zero Sparse (with specialized zero token):** Achieved an average token count reduction of approximately 70% and demonstrated superior performance across kinematic and geometric metrics (e.g., Type Accuracy 0.794, IoU 0.745, CD 0.074).
 - **Full SIMART (+ Vision):** Integrating visual features further enhanced performance across all metrics (e.g., Type Accuracy 0.937, IoU 0.832, CD 0.055), highlighting the role of visual information in resolving geometric ambiguities.
- **Sparse 3D VQ-VAE Reconstruction Quality (Table 4):** The integration of the zero token mechanism significantly improved reconstruction quality, particularly reducing Chamfer Distance, compared to a force sparse baseline without it. While a larger latent resolution (16x8x8) could offer minor fidelity gains, the 8x8x8 latent grid was chosen as an optimal trade-off to avoid substantial memory overhead for the multimodal backbone. Doubling the codebook size (to 8,192 entries) did not yield significant improvements, indicating the sufficiency of the 4,096-entry codebook with the zero token.

#### 4.4. SIMART-Bench

The introduction of SIMART-Bench, incorporating diverse AI-generated objects and manual annotations, addressed the limitations of existing homogeneous datasets, providing a more rigorous standard for evaluating the generalization capabilities of articulated asset generation algorithms.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The SIMART framework introduces a unified MLLM approach for generating simulation-ready articulated 3D assets from monolithic meshes, demonstrating advancements with several significant implications:

1. **Enabling Embodied AI and Physical Simulation:** By automating the transformation of static 3D models into functional, articulated assets with precise kinematic and physical metadata, SIMART addresses a critical need for embodied AI and physics-based robotic simulation. This capability allows for the scalable generation of diverse training scenarios, facilitates interactive learning through real-time dynamic feedback, and provides multimodal observation data for benchmarking advanced vision-language-action (VLA) models.

2. **Addressing Computational Bottlenecks in 3D MLLMs:** The introduction of the Sparse 3D VQ-VAE is a key technical contribution. By reducing token redundancy by approximately 70% compared to dense volumetric representations, it effectively mitigates memory exhaustion issues. This efficiency enables MLLMs to process complex 3D meshes and perform high-fidelity multi-part articulation modeling, which was previously computationally prohibitive. This advancement expands the applicability of MLLMs in complex 3D perception and generation tasks.

3. **Enhancing Geometric Fidelity and Kinematic Accuracy:** SIMART's unified approach, which jointly performs part-level decomposition and kinematic parameter prediction, avoids the error accumulation inherent in multi-stage pipelines. This results in superior geometric decomposition, precise joint parameter estimation (Type, Axis, Origin), and overall structural consistency. The generated assets are thus suitable for demanding applications requiring physical realism.

4. **Improving Generalization Capabilities:** The model's performance on the curated SIMART-Bench, which includes diverse AI-generated and out-of-distribution objects, indicates strong generalization. This suggests that SIMART can process and articulate novel geometries, moving beyond the limitations of category-specific or homogeneously distributed datasets.

5. **Broadening Applications in VR/AR:** Beyond robotic simulation, SIMART extends to immersive VR/AR environments. It facilitates user-driven interactive asset creation, allowing static virtual elements to be transformed into interactive components with realistic kinematic constraints. This enhances the interaction logic of virtual worlds and enables seamless manipulation in mixed-reality scenarios.

6. **Accelerating Data Annotation:** The research identifies the scarcity and inconsistent quality of existing articulated datasets as a primary limitation. SIMART's capability to generate pre-verified articulation predictions offers a foundational tool to accelerate the data annotation loop. This automated pipeline could lead to the creation of larger, more diverse, and higher-quality datasets, further enhancing generative capabilities for simulation-ready articulated objects in the future.

In conclusion, SIMART represents a step towards automated, high-fidelity, and scalable generation of simulation-ready articulated 3D assets, with implications for robotics, virtual environments, and the broader field of 3D content creation.
