# GraspCoT: Integrating Physical Property Reasoning for 6-DoF Grasping under Flexible Language Instructions

> [!summary] Summary
> GraspCoT introduces a 6-DoF robotic grasping framework that integrates physical property reasoning into a multimodal large language model through a Chain-of-Thought mechanism, enabling robots to interpret flexible language instructions. The system demonstrates improved performance over existing methods on a new multi-object benchmark and in real-world scenarios, generating more precise and collision-free grasp poses.

## Problem

- Current language-guided grasping systems underutilize objects' physical properties (e.g., fragility, texture), leading to suboptimal or unsafe grasp planning decisions.
- Robots struggle to interpret flexible, indirect, or multi-target language instructions from humans in complex, multi-object 3D environments.
- A lack of public benchmarks exists for rigorously evaluating multi-object grasp detection under diverse and indirect verbal commands.

## Method

- A unified multimodal LLM architecture processes 3D colored point clouds and flexible language instructions, deeply fusing 3D-aware visual tokens with textual information.
- A three-stage hierarchical Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning pipeline, guided by auxiliary ==Question-Answering (QA)== tasks, identifies target objects, infers physical properties (material, surface, shape) using unsupervised reasoning tokens, and selects appropriate grasp actions.
- The IntentGrasp benchmark was created, using Llama3-70B to generate diverse, flexible, multi-target instructions for existing 3D scenes to train and evaluate the system's ability to handle complex real-world scenarios.

## Results

- GraspCoT achieved a Coverage Rate (CR) of **0.5587** at θ=**0.2** on the IntentGrasp benchmark, outperforming state-of-the-art models by up to **0.2683**, and reduced Earth Mover's Distance (EMD) by **14.1%**.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of CoT reasoning, with its removal leading to a **25.4%** decline in CR@**0.2** and a **9.1%** increase in EMD.
- Real-world experiments demonstrated improved success rates on a Kinova Gen3 robot, showing a **5.6%** advantage for single-target flexible instructions and a **2.7%** enhancement for multi-target sequential grasping tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating hierarchical physical property reasoning (material, surface, shape) via a Chain-of-Thought mechanism substantially enhances an LLM's ability to make precise and contextually appropriate 6-DoF grasp decisions.
> - Auxiliary Question-Answering tasks effectively guide the LLM's reasoning process, allowing it to leverage its extensive common knowledge to infer object properties and actions from flexible and implicit instructions.
> - A unified multimodal LLM architecture provides an effective way to deeply embed 3D visual information with CoT-derived textual reasoning, enabling sophisticated perception and action for complex robotic manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic grasping has evolved significantly, moving from purely geometric approaches to more intelligent, context-aware systems. This paper situates itself at the forefront of this evolution, specifically in the domain of **language-guided 6-DoF grasp detection**.

Initially, robotic grasping focused on static 3D environments and explicit object identification, often relying on robust datasets and classical computer vision techniques for pose estimation (e.g., GraspNet, AnyGrasp). The emergence of large language models (LLMs) and multimodal LLMs (MLLMs) revolutionized the field by enabling robots to interpret natural language instructions, bridging the gap between human commands and robotic actions. Early works in language-guided grasping primarily leveraged LLMs for semantic understanding and task-oriented information, allowing robots to interact with novel object classes or understand explicit commands like "Give me the remote control" (e.g., GraspGPT, SemGrasp).

A more recent trend, which GraspCoT builds upon, involves exploring the reasoning capabilities of LLMs to interpret **implicit or flexible instructions**. This means inferring user intent from indirect cues, such as fetching a remote control in response to "I want to watch TV" (e.g., ThinkGrasp, RT-Grasp, Jin et al.). However, as the authors keenly observe, merely understanding semantics or intentions doesn't fully exploit the vast common knowledge within LLMs. A crucial gap in existing methods is the underexplored integration of **objects' physical properties** into the grasping decision-making process. While some works have touched upon attribute prediction (ShapeGrasp, DeliGrasp), they either rely on geometric decomposition or directly predict precise physical parameters, which can be challenging for current LLMs to accurately infer and apply.

GraspCoT addresses this critical omission by proposing a systematic way to integrate physical property reasoning. Instead of demanding precise physical parameters, it employs structured descriptors for properties like material, surface, and shape, guiding the LLM towards more nuanced grasping strategies. Furthermore, the paper identifies and tackles another significant limitation: the scarcity of public benchmarks supporting **multi-object grasp detection under diverse and indirect verbal commands**. Most existing benchmarks assume explicit, single-target instructions. By introducing the IntentGrasp benchmark, GraspCoT not only presents a novel solution but also provides the necessary data infrastructure to propel future research in this complex, real-world scenario. This work thus represents a significant step towards more adaptable, intelligent, and human-friendly robotic manipulation systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and intelligent 6-DoF grasp detection framework that can effectively interpret and execute grasping tasks based on **flexible language instructions** in complex, multi-object 3D environments.

The motivation for this objective stems from several identified limitations in current robotic grasping systems:

1. **Underexplored Physical Property Reasoning:** While large language models (LLMs) have significantly enhanced robots' ability to understand semantic instructions and infer user intentions, their knowledge regarding the physical properties of objects (e.g., fragility, texture, material strength) remains largely unutilized in grasp planning. The authors highlight that effective grasping goes beyond geometric similarity; it necessitates reasoning about these physical attributes to ensure safe, stable, and appropriate manipulation. For instance, a robot needs to understand that a fragile glass requires a delicate "pinch" grasp on its neck, while a sturdy mug allows for a more forceful "clamp" based on its material and shape. The current LLM-based approaches often overlook this critical dimension.

2. **Handling Flexible and Implicit Instructions:** Human-robot interaction in real-world settings is rarely characterized by explicit, unambiguous commands. Users often issue flexible, indirect, or contextual instructions (e.g., "I'm thirsty," "I need to watch TV") that imply a target object or even multiple targets without explicitly naming them. Existing methods struggle with such referential ambiguity and multi-target scenarios, often assuming single, explicitly defined targets. The motivation is to enable robots to interpret human intent more naturally and adaptively.

3. **Lack of Multi-Target Benchmarks:** A significant practical hurdle for advancing research in flexible instruction-guided grasping, especially for scenarios involving multiple objects, is the absence of suitable public benchmarks. This data gap makes it difficult to train and rigorously evaluate robotic systems on tasks that mimic real-world complexity. The researchers are motivated to fill this void to accelerate progress in the field.

To address these motivations, the core objectives of GraspCoT are:
- To propose a novel framework that systematically integrates physical property analysis into the grasp detection process using a Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning mechanism.
- To leverage the reasoning capabilities of LLMs through auxiliary Question-Answering (QA) tasks to guide this hierarchical reasoning, from target parsing to physical property analysis and grasp action selection.
- To develop a unified multimodal LLM architecture that deeply embeds visual and textual information, optimizing cross-modal feature alignment for precise 6-DoF grasp pose prediction.
- To create a new, large-scale benchmark, IntentGrasp, specifically designed for evaluating multi-object grasp detection under diverse and flexible language instructions.

By achieving these objectives, GraspCoT aims to push the boundaries of robotic grasping towards more intelligent, versatile, and human-centric robotic assistants.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GraspCoT introduces a novel 6-DoF grasp detection framework that systematically integrates physical property reasoning using a Chain-of-Thought (CoT) mechanism, guided by auxiliary Question-Answering (QA) tasks within a unified multimodal Large Language Model (LLM) architecture.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture Overview

The architecture processes colored 3D point clouds and flexible language instructions. It features a dual-branch pipeline: a visual branch for 3D scene understanding and a textual branch for CoT-driven linguistic interpretation. The outputs from both branches are deeply fused within an LLM to predict 6-DoF grasp poses and confidence scores.

#### 3.2. Visual Processing Branch

1. **Multi-View RGB-D Generation:** A colored point cloud is projected into multiple (default four) virtual viewpoints to generate sparse RGB-D images.
2. **2D Feature Encoding:** RGB images are processed by a CLIP encoder, generating 2D patches.
3. **3D Position Embeddings:** Depth data is used to derive 3D positional embeddings.
4. **3D-Aware Visual Tokens:** The CLIP-encoded RGB patches and 3D position embeddings are fused, back-projected into geometrically grounded 3D tokens, and then transformed via voxel pooling and linear projection into 3D-aware visual tokens. This process creates a rich visual representation that is compatible with the LLM's token-based input.

#### 3.3. Physical-Property-Oriented Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Design

The core innovation lies in a three-stage hierarchical CoT reasoning pipeline, facilitated by fill-in-the-blank QA templates:

1. **Target Parsing (Q1/A1):** This initial stage addresses referential ambiguity in flexible instructions. The LLM is prompted to identify which objects need to be grasped and their total number. The target categories are explicitly supervised during training.
 - *Example:* Instruction: "I need to make a mixed salad." -> Target: "apple, banana."

2. **Physical Property Analysis (Q2/A2):** For each identified target object, the LLM is guided to infer its physical properties. These are hierarchically categorized into three primary groups:
 - **Material:** Characterized by hardness (e.g., soft, hard, rigid), strength (e.g., brittle, ductile), and elasticity (e.g., elastic, inflexible).
 - **Surface:** Described by texture (e.g., granular, smooth), roughness (e.g., polished, coarse), and friction (e.g., slippery, adhesive).
 - **Shape:** Analyzed for attributes like symmetry (e.g., symmetric, asymmetric), geometry (e.g., planar, cylindrical), and contour (e.g., curvilinear, angular).
 These properties are expressed using open-world descriptor libraries. Crucially, these physical descriptors (δ_phy) are represented by special "reasoning tokens" that are *unsupervised* during training, leveraging the LLM's extensive corpora knowledge to infer appropriate descriptors without explicit manual annotation.

3. **Grasp Action Selection (Q3/A3):** Based on the inferred physical properties, the LLM selects an appropriate action descriptor (δ_act) from a predefined verb library (e.g., "pinch" for fragile objects, "clamp" for textured surfaces). This selection dynamically links to geometric parameters and contact forces required for the grasp. Similar to physical properties, these action descriptors are unsupervised reasoning tokens.

#### 3.4. Auxiliary QA Task and Grasp Decoding

- **Token Fusion:** Flexible language instructions and the CoT-derived QA templates (with specific reasoning tokens for the blank fields) are encoded into textual tokens. These textual tokens are then jointly embedded with the 3D-aware visual tokens within the LLM.
- **Dual-Task Training:** GraspCoT is trained to perform both the QA task and grasp pose prediction simultaneously. The reasoning tokens, representing unsupervised physical/action descriptors, receive masked supervision with zero loss weighting, effectively prompting the LLM to fill in the blanks based on its knowledge.
- **Grasp Decoding:** After the LLM performs its reasoning and autoregressive generation, all output tokens are passed through a self-attention layer. Two separate heads then operate in parallel:
 - **Regression Head:** Predicts multiple 6-DoF grasp poses (orientation R, position T, gripper width w).
 - **Classification Head:** Predicts confidence scores for each pose.
- **Loss Function:** The total loss (L) is a combination of:
 - `L_QA`: Cross-entropy loss for the supervised QA components.
 - `L_Reg`: L1 loss for grasp pose regression, after aligning predictions with ground truths using the Hungarian matching algorithm.
 - `L_Cls`: Focal loss for confidence score optimization.

#### 3.5. IntentGrasp Benchmark Construction

Recognizing the lack of suitable datasets, the authors created **IntentGrasp**.
- **Source Data:** It is adapted from the Grasp-Anything-6D dataset, containing 1 million 3D colored point cloud scenes with approximately 3 million objects.
- **Flexible Instruction Generation:** The open-source LLM Llama3-70B is used with a carefully crafted prompt template (Fig. 4). This prompt guides the LLM to generate 3-5 linguistically diverse and flexible instructions per scene. These instructions implicitly demand grasping one or more objects without explicitly naming them, focusing on contextual cues and plausible scenarios.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Besides standard Coverage Rate (CR) and Earth Mover's Distance (EMD), GraspCoT proposes an EMD-Weighted CFR (EW-CFR) metric to jointly evaluate geometric consistency and collision avoidance, addressing a limitation of standard CFR.

In essence, GraspCoT's methodology leverages the semantic and reasoning power of LLMs, augments it with structured physical property understanding, and integrates it into a unified multimodal framework, all evaluated on a novel benchmark designed for real-world, flexible interaction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted on the newly introduced IntentGrasp benchmark and in real-world robotic setups consistently demonstrate the superiority and practical applicability of GraspCoT.

#### 4.1. Performance on IntentGrasp Benchmark

GraspCoT significantly outperforms state-of-the-art language-guided 6-DoF grasp detection models, including 3DAPNet and LGrasp6D, across all evaluation metrics under flexible language instructions.
- **Coverage Rate (CR):** GraspCoT achieved a CR of 0.5587 at the strictest threshold (θ=0.2), representing a substantial improvement of 0.2683 over 3DAPNet and 0.2238 over LGrasp6D. This indicates its ability to generate highly precise grasp poses even under stringent accuracy requirements. The performance gap widened with increasing precision demands (from θ=0.4 to θ=0.2), underscoring the method's robustness.
- **Earth Mover’s Distance (EMD):** GraspCoT achieved an EMD of 0.2520, a 14.1% relative reduction compared to LGrasp6D. A lower EMD signifies a better match between the predicted and ground-truth grasp pose distributions, indicating more holistic and diverse grasp pose generation.
- **EMD-Weighted Collision-Free Rate (EW-CFR):** GraspCoT scored 0.4229, outperforming 3DAPNet by 20.1% and LGrasp6D by 9.9%. This novel metric confirms that GraspCoT generates safer and more physically feasible grasps by balancing geometric accuracy with collision avoidance.

#### 4.2. Validation of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning

The ablation study with a "GraspCoT (w/o CoT)" version conclusively validates the necessity and effectiveness of the proposed CoT reasoning pipeline. Removing CoT resulted in a significant performance degradation: a 25.4% decline in CR@0.2 and a 9.1% increase in EMD. This finding highlights that the hierarchical reasoning process is critical for achieving high-precision and robust grasp detection.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies on CoT Components

- **Physical Property Analysis:** Integrating material property analysis alone substantially improved performance (7.1% higher CR@0.3, 22.3% higher CR@0.2, and 6.3% lower EMD compared to w/o CoT). Similarly, integrating surface or shape properties also yielded notable improvements. This suggests that even partial physical property integration significantly aids LLMs in decoding grasp-relevant hidden states.
- **Cumulative Effect:** The cumulative integration of all three physical properties (material, surface, shape) achieved the second-best pose estimation quality. Adding grasp action selection further refined precision, leading to the overall best performance. This step-by-step improvement demonstrates the additive benefits of the hierarchical CoT approach.

#### 4.4. Single- and Multi-Target Grasping Scenarios

CoT reasoning provided substantial improvements in both single-target and multi-target grasping scenarios compared to the w/o CoT version. For single-target tasks, CR@0.2 improved by 36.1% and EMD decreased by 12.7%. For multi-target grasping, CR@0.2 improved by 33.2% and EMD decreased by 11.3%. While CoT showed slightly stronger optimization for single-target cases, it remained highly effective for complex multi-target scenarios, proving its ability to handle flexible instructions involving multiple objects.

#### 4.5. Number of Virtual Views

Experiments on the quantity of virtual views revealed that four complementary views provided the optimal balance between precision gains and computational overhead. Performance plateaued beyond four views, confirming that this configuration is efficient and effective.

#### 4.6. Real-World Experiments

Deployment on a Kinova Gen3 robotic arm with an Intel RealSense D435i depth camera further validated GraspCoT's practicality and robustness in real-world scenarios.
- **Explicit Instructions:** CoT-enhanced GraspCoT achieved a 54.2% success rate, a 2.8% improvement over its non-CoT counterpart.
- **Flexible Instructions:** The CoT version maintained stable performance with a 5.6% advantage in single-target grasping.
- **Multi-Target Sequential Grasping:** For complex multi-target tasks, GraspCoT improved the success rate from 44.0% to 46.7% (a 2.7% enhancement), demonstrating its effectiveness in handling challenging requirements and reducing target omissions.

In summary, the findings robustly confirm that GraspCoT's physical-property-oriented CoT reasoning, combined with its unified multimodal LLM architecture and multi-view processing, leads to significantly more accurate, robust, and contextually appropriate 6-DoF grasp predictions for flexible language instructions in both simulated and real-world environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GraspCoT makes several significant contributions to the field of robotic manipulation and human-robot interaction, with far-reaching potential impacts.

**Pioneering Physical Property Reasoning in Grasping:** The most significant contribution is the systematic integration of physical property reasoning into 6-DoF grasp detection. By enabling LLMs to consider an object's material, surface, and shape properties through a structured Chain-of-Thought process, GraspCoT moves beyond purely geometric or semantic understanding. This allows for more intelligent, context-aware, and safer grasping decisions, crucial for handling diverse objects in real-world scenarios. This paradigm shift will inspire future research to delve deeper into latent physical properties beyond visual cues.

**Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction through Flexible Instructions:** GraspCoT empowers robots to understand and respond to natural, indirect, and flexible human commands. This capability significantly improves the intuitiveness and user-friendliness of robotic systems, making human-robot collaboration more seamless. Instead of rigid, explicit instructions, users can interact with robots more naturally, fostering broader adoption of robots in domestic, healthcare, and service industries where intuitive interaction is paramount.

**Introduction of IntentGrasp Benchmark:** The creation of IntentGrasp is a crucial contribution that addresses a critical gap in public datasets. This large-scale benchmark, featuring multi-object grasping under diverse and indirect verbal commands, provides a standardized and challenging platform for future research. It will accelerate the development and evaluation of new algorithms for complex, real-world grasping scenarios, pushing the boundaries of what language-guided robots can achieve.

**Unified Multimodal LLM Architecture:** The proposed architecture, which deeply embeds 3D-aware visual tokens with CoT-derived textual tokens within a single LLM, represents an elegant solution for multimodal understanding. This unified approach not only enhances cross-modal feature alignment but also reduces system complexity compared to decoupled pipelines. It showcases the growing power of end-to-end MLLMs for complex perception and action tasks in robotics.

**Improved Robustness and Safety in Robotic Grasping:** By explicitly considering physical properties, GraspCoT can generate grasp poses that are not only geometrically accurate but also physically feasible and safe. The higher collision-free rates and more precise pose predictions mean robots are less likely to damage objects (especially fragile ones) or fail tasks, leading to more reliable and trustworthy robotic operations.

**Broad Applicability Across Industries:** The advancements presented in GraspCoT have significant implications for various applications:
- **Domestic Robotics:** Enabling home robots to assist with chores, handle delicate items, or retrieve objects based on natural language requests.
- **Healthcare:** Assisting nurses or caregivers with handling medical instruments or delicate patient items.
- **Industrial Automation:** Improving the flexibility of robots in manufacturing and logistics, allowing them to adapt to variations in object properties and dynamic task requirements.
- **Logistics and Warehousing:** More efficient and damage-free handling of a wider variety of goods.

While the authors acknowledge limitations, such as challenges with slender objects, the foundational work laid by GraspCoT in integrating nuanced physical reasoning and addressing flexible instructions marks a substantial leap forward. It opens exciting avenues for future research, including dynamic scene understanding and continuous learning, ultimately paving the way for more intelligent and adaptable robotic systems capable of performing complex tasks in human environments.
