# Language Is Not All You Need: Aligning Perception with Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Microsoft researchers developed KOSMOS-1, a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) capable of processing arbitrarily interleaved images and text. The model achieves strong zero-shot performance in perception-language tasks, such as 84.7 CIDEr on COCO for image captioning, and demonstrates rudimentary nonverbal reasoning with 22% accuracy on Raven IQ tests.

## Problem

- Traditional Large Language Models (LLMs) are limited to text data, lacking the inherent ability to perceive and interpret visual information from the real world.
- Achieving Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) necessitates a convergence of language, multimodal perception, action, and world modeling, which current text-only LLMs cannot natively support.
- Existing multimodal AI approaches often lack a unified framework for handling arbitrarily interleaved multimodal inputs and struggle with consistent cross-modal transfer and instruction following.

## Method

- **KOSMOS-1**, a ==Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM)== built on a Transformer-based causal language model (==MAGNETO==), was developed to process both text and image inputs in a unified sequence.
- A pre-trained ==CLIP ViT-L/14== model, with its last layer fine-tuned and a ==Resampler== module, was integrated as the vision encoder to efficiently incorporate visual information into the Transformer backbone.
- The model was trained from scratch on a massive, ==web-scale multimodal corpus== comprising monomodal text, cross-modal image-caption pairs, and ==interleaved image-text== data, followed by language-only instruction tuning.

## Results

- Achieved competitive or superior zero-shot performance on perception-language tasks, including **84.7 CIDEr** on COCO for image captioning and **51.0%** accuracy on VQAv2, outperforming smaller Flamingo models.
- Demonstrated nascent nonverbal reasoning abilities with **22%** accuracy on custom Raven IQ tests (**26%** with language-only instruction tuning) and robust OCR-free language understanding, reaching **72.9%** accuracy on Rendered SST-2 with Multimodal Chain-of-Thought prompting.
- Maintained strong performance on language-only tasks and exhibited significant cross-modal transfer benefits, with language-only instruction tuning boosting VQA accuracy by **+4.3 pp** and multimodal pre-training enhancing visual commonsense reasoning in language-only contexts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified Transformer-based architecture effectively aligns multimodal perception with language models, enabling them to process and understand interleaved text and image inputs in a cohesive manner.
> - Language-only instruction tuning, surprisingly, transfers and significantly enhances instruction-following capabilities in multimodal contexts, indicating a generalized understanding of instructions.
> - Multimodal pre-training fosters cross-modal knowledge transfer, where visual information improves language-only commonsense reasoning and learning in one modality benefits others, leading to a more grounded understanding of the world.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the crucial intersection of Large Language Models (LLMs) and multimodal AI, representing a significant stride towards what the authors term "artificial general intelligence (AGI)." The paper explicitly frames its contribution as advancing the trend "from LLMs to MLLMs," highlighting a pivotal shift in the AI paradigm.

**Evolution from LLMs:** For years, LLMs such as GPT-3 (implicitly referenced through [BMR+20]) and PaLM [CND+22] have demonstrated unprecedented capabilities in natural language understanding, generation, and few-shot learning. However, their reliance solely on text data inherently limits their ability to interact with and understand the rich, multifaceted real world. This paper addresses this fundamental limitation by seeking to align multimodal perception (specifically vision) with the powerful reasoning and generative capabilities of LLMs.

**Emergence of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs):** The concept of MLLMs is a rapidly expanding area of research. KOSMOS-1 builds upon foundational work in vision-language pre-training (VLP) and the integration of visual information into language models. The authors cite models like Flamingo [ADL+22] and METALM [HSD+22] as direct inspirations and benchmarks, acknowledging the pioneering efforts in developing visual language models that can learn in-context. More recent concurrent work, such as BLIP-2 [LLSH23], further underscores the active and competitive nature of this field. KOSMOS-1 distinguishes itself by proposing a general-purpose MLLM capable of handling arbitrarily interleaved text and images from scratch, aiming for a unified approach to multimodal input.

**Toward Artificial General Intelligence (AGI):** The paper frames the "big convergence of language, multimodal perception, action, and world modeling" as a "key step toward artificial general intelligence." This aligns with a growing consensus in the AI community that true intelligence requires not just linguistic proficiency but also the ability to perceive, interact with, and model the physical world. KOSMOS-1's ability to perceive images, learn from multimodal contexts, and follow instructions across modalities directly contributes to this grand vision by enabling "knowledge acquisition and grounding to the real world."

**Unifying Interfaces and Applications:** A key aspect of the broader landscape that KOSMOS-1 addresses is the desire for unified AI interfaces. The authors suggest that MLLMs can serve as "general-purpose interfaces" that can "unify various APIs," similar to how graphical user interfaces offer a natural way for humans to interact with computers. This vision implies that MLLMs could directly interpret screen content, extract information from diverse documents (e.g., receipts), and potentially control robots, broadening the application scope of language models significantly into areas like multimodal machine learning, document intelligence, and robotics.

In summary, KOSMOS-1 is a timely and significant contribution that pushes the boundaries of large language models by integrating robust visual perception. It solidifies the trend towards MLLMs as a crucial pathway to more versatile, grounded, and ultimately, more intelligent AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of this research is to develop and evaluate **KOSMOS-1, a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM)** that can bridge the gap between powerful language understanding and real-world perception. The authors succinctly state their goal: "to align perception with LLMs, so that the models are able to see and talk."

This overarching objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Overcoming LLM Limitations for Multimodal Data:** Despite their remarkable successes in natural language processing (NLP), traditional LLMs inherently struggle with multimodal data such as images and audio. They lack the native ability to perceive and interpret visual information, which severely limits their applicability in real-world scenarios that are inherently multimodal. KOSMOS-1 aims to resolve this by endowing LLMs with general perceptive capabilities.

2. **Advancing Towards Artificial General Intelligence (AGI):** The authors posit that "multimodal perception is a necessity to achieve artificial general intelligence." They view a "big convergence of language, multimodal perception, action, and world modeling" as fundamental for AGI, enabling models to acquire commonsense knowledge beyond mere text descriptions and ground their understanding in the physical world. KOSMOS-1 is presented as a significant step in this convergence.

3. **Expanding Applications of Language Models:** By incorporating multimodal perception, KOSMOS-1 aims to greatly widen the application areas of language models. This includes high-value domains like multimodal machine learning, where joint understanding of different data types is crucial; document intelligence, which often requires interpreting visual layouts alongside text (e.g., OCR-free NLP); and robotics, where models need to perceive their environment to execute actions effectively.

4. **Enabling New Capabilities:** The development of KOSMOS-1 is motivated by the desire to unlock novel capabilities not present in unimodal LLMs. Specifically, the authors aim to demonstrate:
 - **Instruction Following and In-Context Learning Across Modalities:** The ability to perform zero-shot and few-shot learning for multimodal tasks using natural language instructions and examples.
 - **Nonverbal Reasoning:** To explore whether MLLMs can exhibit nonverbal reasoning, as diagnosed by tasks like Raven’s Progressive Matrices IQ tests.
 - **Cross-Modal Knowledge Transfer:** To investigate how knowledge learned in one modality (e.g., language) can transfer to another (e.g., vision), and vice-versa, enhancing overall intelligence and commonsense reasoning.
 - **Unified Multimodal Interaction:** To enable multi-turn interactions and complex reasoning over diverse multimodal inputs, such as in multimodal dialogue and visual chain-of-thought prompting.

In essence, the motivation behind KOSMOS-1 is to move beyond text-centric AI, creating a more comprehensive and versatile intelligence that can "see, talk, learn, and reason" across the rich tapestry of human experience, thereby making LLMs more capable, grounded, and applicable to real-world challenges.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The KOSMOS-1 model is designed as a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) built upon a Transformer-based causal language model (decoder-only architecture), capable of processing various modalities in a unified manner.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

- **Unified Input Representation:** The model flattens all inputs (text and image) into a single sequence, delineated by special tokens (e.g., `<s>`, `</s>`, `<image>`, `</image>`). This allows the Transformer decoder to process heterogeneous data seamlessly.
- **Modality Embedding:**
 - **Text:** Standard lookup table for token embeddings.
 - **Images:** A pre-trained CLIP ViT-L/14 model serves as the vision encoder. To enhance training efficiency and convergence, the parameters of the CLIP model are largely frozen, with only the last layer being fine-tuned. A Resampler module [ADL+22] is employed as an attentive pooling mechanism to reduce the number of image embeddings, thus managing computational load.
- **Transformer Backbone:** KOSMOS-1 utilizes **MAGNETO [WMH+22]**, a Transformer variant known for its improved training stability and superior performance across modalities. MAGNETO incorporates an additional LayerNorm in each sublayer (multi-head self-attention and feed-forward network) and benefits from a theoretically derived initialization method for more effective optimization.
- **Position Encoding:** **XPOS [SDP+22]** relative position encoding is integrated to enhance long-context modeling. This allows the model to generalize better to varying sequence lengths, improving both interpolation and extrapolation capabilities.
- **Model Scale:** The MLLM component consists of 24 layers, 2,048 hidden dimensions, 8,192 FFN intermediate size, and 32 attention heads, culminating in approximately 1.3 billion parameters. Including the image representation module (CLIP ViT-L/14), the total number of parameters for KOSMOS-1 is about 1.6 billion.

#### 3.2. Training Data

KOSMOS-1 is trained from scratch on a massive, web-scale multimodal corpus, meticulously curated from diverse sources to ensure robust learning. This corpus comprises three main categories:
- **Monomodal Data (Text Corpora):** Large English text datasets like The Pile [GBB+20] (excluding specific splits) and Common Crawl snapshots (2020-50, 2021-04), along with CC-Stories and RealNews. These data primarily contribute to language modeling capabilities, instruction following, and in-context learning for text-only tasks.
- **Cross-modal Paired Data (Image-Caption Pairs):** Datasets such as English LAION-2B [SBV+22], LAION-400M [SVB+21], COYO-700M [BPK+22], and Conceptual Captions [SDGS18, CSDS21]. These are crucial for aligning visual features with their linguistic descriptions.
- **Interleaved Image-Text Data:** Collected from Common Crawl web pages, this dataset contains arbitrarily interleaved images and text. This type of data is vital for teaching the model to understand the contextual relationship between visual and textual elements in real-world documents and web content, and is particularly important for few-shot learning and complex multimodal tasks.

#### 3.3. Training Objective and Setup

- **Next-Token Prediction:** The model is trained with the standard next-token prediction task, maximizing the log-likelihood of tokens given the preceding context. Notably, only discrete tokens (primarily text tokens) contribute to the training loss, while image embeddings serve as contextual input.
- **Training Scale:** KOSMOS-1 was trained for 300,000 steps, processing approximately 360 billion tokens in total. A large batch size of 1.2 million tokens was used, balanced across text corpora, image-caption pairs, and interleaved data. The AdamW optimizer was employed with specific learning rate schedules and regularization.
- **Language-Only Instruction Tuning:** Following the initial multimodal pre-training, the model undergoes an additional stage of "language-only instruction tuning" using datasets like Unnatural Instructions [HSLS22] and FLANv2 [LHV+23]. This process, involving solely text-based instructions, is crucial for calibrating the model's instruction-following capability. The authors demonstrate that this language-only tuning surprisingly transfers and enhances instruction-following in multimodal contexts.

By combining a robust Transformer architecture with an efficient integration of pre-trained vision encoders and training on a diverse, web-scale multimodal dataset, KOSMOS-1 aims to achieve a comprehensive understanding of both language and perception, fostering cross-modal reasoning and instruction-following capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

KOSMOS-1 demonstrates impressive performance across a wide spectrum of tasks, validating its capabilities as a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) in both zero-shot and few-shot settings. The key findings are summarized below:

#### 4.1. Perception-Language Tasks

- **Image Captioning:** KOSMOS-1 achieved remarkable zero-shot results on COCO Karpathy test (CIDEr 84.7) and Flickr30k test (CIDEr 67.1). Notably, it outperformed larger Flamingo models (3B and 9B) on Flickr30k, despite KOSMOS-1 being a 1.6B parameter model. Few-shot performance further improved with more examples, consistent across datasets.
- **Visual Question Answering (VQA):** In zero-shot VQA, KOSMOS-1 was competitive with Flamingo models on VQAv2 (51.0% accuracy) and outperformed them on VizWiz (29.2% accuracy), a dataset known for its diversity and complexity. Few-shot learning also led to performance gains on these tasks.

#### 4.2. Nonverbal Reasoning (Raven IQ Test)

- KOSMOS-1 exhibited a rudimentary but significant ability in zero-shot nonverbal reasoning, achieving 22% accuracy (26% with language-only instruction tuning) on a custom Raven IQ test dataset, compared to a 17% random baseline. This indicates that MLLMs can perceive abstract conceptual patterns and deduce relationships in visual matrices, marking a novel capability for such models.

#### 4.3. OCR-Free Language Understanding

- The model demonstrated built-in capabilities to read and comprehend text directly from rendered images without external OCR tools.
- It achieved 63.9% ROC AUC on HatefulMemes and 67.1% accuracy on Rendered SST-2, outperforming CLIP ViT-L/14 and Flamingo models (which even used explicit OCR text).
- **Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Prompting:** Applying multimodal CoT prompting (generating a visual rationale first) significantly boosted Rendered SST-2 accuracy to 72.9%, illustrating improved reasoning by leveraging intermediate generated descriptions.

#### 4.4. Web Page Question Answering (WebSRC)

- KOSMOS-1 outperformed a language-only LLM baseline on the WebSRC task (15.8 EM / 31.3 F1 vs. 7.6 EM / 17.9 F1), showcasing its ability to benefit from the layout and style information present in web page images, not just the extracted text.

#### 4.5. Zero-Shot Image Classification

- **ImageNet:** KOSMOS-1 achieved 38.1% top-1 accuracy in constrained zero-shot image classification and 4.0% in unconstrained settings, significantly outperforming GIT [WYH+22].
- **With Descriptions:** Providing in-context verbal descriptions of categories dramatically improved image classification accuracy on a CUB-like bird dataset from 61.7% to 90.0%. This highlights KOSMOS-1's ability to align linguistic concepts with subtle visual features, enhancing interpretability and fine-grained classification.

#### 4.6. Language Tasks

- Despite its multimodal focus, KOSMOS-1 maintained strong performance on language-only tasks. It achieved comparable or even better results than an LLM baseline (trained on the same text corpora), particularly in few-shot settings across tasks like StoryCloze, HellaSwag, Winograd, PIQA, BoolQ, CB, and COPA. This confirms that its multimodal capabilities do not come at the cost of its core language understanding.

#### 4.7. Cross-modal Transfer

- **Language to Multimodal:** Language-only instruction tuning, performed solely on text data, significantly improved KOSMOS-1's instruction-following capabilities across multimodal tasks (e.g., +4.3 VQA accuracy on VQAv2). This indicates a powerful transfer of generalized instruction understanding.
- **Multimodal to Language:** KOSMOS-1 demonstrated superior performance over the LLM baseline on visual commonsense reasoning tasks (RelativeSize, MemoryColor, ColorTerms) even when given only text prompts. This implies that the multimodal pre-training enables the model to transfer visual knowledge to language-only questions about object properties, demonstrating enhanced commonsense reasoning.

In summary, KOSMOS-1 not only integrates vision with language but also demonstrates synergistic benefits, leading to new capabilities in nonverbal reasoning, robust OCR-free understanding, and enhanced cross-modal knowledge transfer, all while maintaining strong language-only performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of KOSMOS-1 marks a significant advancement in the field of artificial intelligence, particularly in the development of multimodal large language models (MLLMs). Its significance and potential impact can be understood through several key aspects:

1. **A Stepping Stone Towards Artificial General Intelligence (AGI):** The paper explicitly positions KOSMOS-1 as a "key step toward artificial general intelligence" through the "big convergence of language, multimodal perception, action, and world modeling." By enabling models to "see and talk" and integrate diverse sensory inputs, KOSMOS-1 contributes to building AI systems that can acquire knowledge and ground their understanding in the real world, a crucial component missing in purely text-based LLMs.

2. **Unlocking New AI Capabilities:** KOSMOS-1 demonstrates several novel capabilities that significantly broaden the scope of AI applications:
 - **Zero-Shot Nonverbal Reasoning:** The performance on Raven IQ tests, even if nascent, signifies the first time a model performs such zero-shot nonverbal reasoning, opening avenues for AI to tackle abstract pattern recognition and fluid intelligence tasks previously considered uniquely human.
 - **Robust OCR-Free Understanding:** The ability to directly comprehend text from images without relying on traditional OCR tools is highly impactful for document intelligence, accessibility, and processing real-world visual information (e.g., signs, receipts, web pages).
 - **Enhanced Commonsense Reasoning:** The demonstration of cross-modal transfer, where visual pre-training enhances language-only commonsense reasoning about object properties, suggests a deeper, more grounded understanding of the world.

3. **General-Purpose Unified Interface:** KOSMOS-1 reinforces the vision of language models as universal interfaces. By handling arbitrary interleaved images and text, and responding to natural language instructions across modalities, it paves the way for a more intuitive and flexible interaction with AI. This could lead to:
 - **Advanced Robotics:** MLLMs could directly perceive the environment and understand human commands for complex robotic tasks.
 - **Revolutionized Document Intelligence:** AI systems could understand entire document layouts, figures, and text holistically, moving beyond character-level recognition to semantic comprehension of visual documents.
 - **More Natural Human-AI Interaction:** Users could interact with AI using a mix of images and text, asking questions about visual content, receiving visually grounded explanations, and engaging in multimodal dialogues.

4. **Proof of Concept for Cross-Modal Knowledge Transfer:** The research compellingly shows that knowledge can effectively transfer between modalities. Language-only instruction tuning improved multimodal instruction-following, and multimodal pre-training enhanced language-only commonsense reasoning. This highlights the synergistic benefits of a unified multimodal approach, where learning in one domain can positively impact others, leading to more efficient and robust model development.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** KOSMOS-1 serves as a strong foundation for future research in several directions. The authors themselves suggest scaling up the model size, integrating speech capabilities, and using KOSMOS-1 as a unified interface to control multimodal generation (e.g., text-to-image generation). This work inspires further exploration into more complex reasoning, richer multimodal understanding, and the development of truly general-purpose AI agents.

In conclusion, KOSMOS-1 represents a substantial leap in MLLM capabilities, moving beyond theoretical discussions to demonstrate practical, compelling performance across a wide array of multimodal tasks. Its success underscores the critical role of aligning perception with language models in the pursuit of more intelligent, versatile, and human-like AI systems, promising to reshape how we interact with and develop artificial intelligence.
