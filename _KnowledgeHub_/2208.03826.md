# Fine-Grained Egocentric Hand-Object Segmentation: Dataset, Model, and Applications

> [!summary] Summary
> EgoHOS is a fine-grained egocentric hand-object segmentation dataset and model: 11,243 in-the-wild frames labeled with per-pixel left/right hand masks, per-hand interacting-object masks, and, uniquely, dense hand-object contact boundaries. A sequential decoder segments hands first and conditions object segmentation on them, while context-aware compositional augmentation pastes hand-object pairs into retrieved plausible backgrounds, lifting hand mIoU to 82.17 and enabling four downstream applications.

## Problem

- Existing egocentric hand segmentation datasets are collected in lab or few-scene settings, so models trained on them fail to generalize to in-the-wild video.
- Large in-the-wild hand-object datasets label only bounding boxes, not per-pixel masks, and no prior dataset labels the interacting object at pixel level.
- Appearance-only segmentation cannot decide whether an object should be segmented, since the same object counts only when a hand is actually contacting it.

## Method

- Dataset of 11,243 frames sparsely sampled from nearly 1,000 videos across four sources, with 20,701 hand and 17,568 interacting-object masks over 100+ activities.
- ==Hand-object contact boundary==: a dense per-pixel label of the contact region, predicted as an explicit relational cue that guides interacting-object segmentation.
- ==Sequential stacked decoder== (==ResNet-18==/==HRNet== or ==Swin-L==/==UperNet==) predicts hands, then contact boundary, then objects; **CCDA** inpaints clean backgrounds and retrieves top-K semantically matched scenes for compositing.

## Results

- Left/right hand mIoU reaches **76.29**/**77.00**, versus **36.30**/**37.51** for a box-detector plus BoxInst pipeline and **10.68**/**33.28** for EgoHand; adding CCDA raises it to **79.73**/**82.17**.
- Binary hand mIoU **85.45** beats the in-the-wild EYTH baseline at **75.94**, and hand-plus-arm mIoU reaches **95.20** versus **33.26** for the EGTEA-trained model.
- Ablation: contact boundary lifts object mIoU from **54.83**/**38.48**/**39.14** to **59.05**/**40.85**/**49.94**, and adding CCDA reaches **62.20**/**44.40**/**52.77** on the three object classes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Segmenting interaction requires modeling the relation, not just appearance; an explicit contact-boundary channel is a cheap, reusable relational supervision signal.
> - Sequential conditioning beats parallel multi-channel decoding when output classes are causally dependent, here objects defined by which hand touches them.
> - Compositing foregrounds into semantically retrieved rather than random backgrounds gives most of the out-of-distribution robustness gain, with returns saturating around 8K-16K composites.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Understanding human behavior through egocentric video analysis is a significant area within computer vision. Prior research in this field has often focused on segmenting hands or detecting objects in egocentric views. Early approaches to hand segmentation utilized color statistics or motion-based methods, while more recent work has leveraged deep learning techniques. Datasets such as EgoHand, EGTEA, and EYTH have contributed to hand segmentation, but often feature limited diversity, constrained lab settings, or coarse labeling. For instance, EgoHand and EGTEA collected data in specific environments, and EYTH, while sampling from YouTube, remained relatively small and focused on binary hand masks. The 100-DOH dataset marked a step towards larger-scale, in-the-wild hand and object interaction data, but provided only bounding box level annotations, limiting fine-grained analysis.

Beyond segmentation, hand-object interaction research encompasses 3D hand and object pose estimation, mesh reconstruction, and activity recognition. Many 3D reconstruction methods, like those by Hasson et al. and Cao et al., rely on 2D hand and object segmentation masks to guide optimization. However, these often utilize general-purpose instance segmentation models (e.g., PointRend pre-trained on COCO) which are not specialized for the unique challenges of egocentric hand-object interaction, such as frequent occlusion and diverse appearances. This often necessitates manual intervention to correct segmentation errors.

This work addresses these limitations by providing a dataset with fine-grained, per-pixel segmentation labels for both hands and interacting objects across diverse real-world scenarios. It also introduces explicit modeling of hand-object contact boundaries, a feature absent in previous datasets. Furthermore, it develops a robust segmentation model that aims to generalize well to out-of-distribution egocentric videos, thereby serving as a foundational tool for enhancing downstream applications where current tools are limited by robustness or representation granularity. The paper positions its contributions as orthogonal to many existing vision tasks, offering a more reliable base for analyzing and modeling human hand-object behaviors.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust, fine-grained egocentric hand-object segmentation system that can generalize effectively across diverse real-world conditions. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and objectives:

1. **Addressing Data Limitations:** The authors identify a significant gap in existing datasets for egocentric hand-object segmentation. Previous datasets either offer hand segmentation without interacting object labels, are confined to lab settings, cover limited scenes or activities, or provide only coarse (bounding box level) annotations. This lack of diverse, high-quality, fine-grained pixel-level data for both hands and interacting objects hinders the development of generalizable segmentation models. The motivation is to create a comprehensive dataset that supports fine-level understanding of hand-object interactions in diverse "in-the-wild" scenarios.

2. **Explicitly Modeling Hand-Object Relationships:** Traditional segmentation methods often treat hands and objects as independent entities. However, in egocentric videos, the interaction between hands and objects is crucial for understanding behavior. The paper is motivated to introduce and explicitly model the "dense contact boundary" between hands and interacting objects. This is intended to provide a direct cue for the presence of an interacting object and to improve segmentation accuracy by offering a clearer separation between contacting regions.

3. **Improving Generalization to Out-of-Distribution Data:** Egocentric videos can vary significantly in terms of illumination, object types, and background clutter. Models trained on limited datasets often struggle to generalize to unseen domains. The motivation is to develop a data augmentation technique that can effectively adapt segmentation models to out-of-distribution data, specifically by generating synthetic but plausible composite images that expand the training diversity.

4. **Serving as a Foundational Tool for Downstream Applications:** The authors recognize that accurate, fine-grained hand-object segmentation is not an end in itself but a critical prerequisite for many advanced vision tasks. They are motivated to demonstrate that a robust segmentation system can enhance or enable various downstream applications that currently suffer from unreliable or coarse hand-object representations. These applications include hand state classification, egocentric video activity recognition, 3D mesh reconstruction of hand-object interactions, and video inpainting to "see through" occluding hands.

In summary, the key objectives are to:
- Create the first large-scale, fine-grained egocentric hand-object segmentation dataset with per-pixel labels, including novel contact boundary annotations.
- Develop a context-aware compositional data augmentation technique to improve model robustness and generalization.
- Introduce a causal segmentation pipeline that leverages explicit hand-object relationship modeling via contact boundaries.
- Validate the utility of their dataset and model by demonstrating performance improvements and enabling new capabilities in various egocentric vision applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on three main components: a new labeled dataset, a segmentation model incorporating a novel contact boundary concept, and a context-aware data augmentation technique.

#### 3.1. Dataset Construction (EgoHOS)

The authors collected a dataset comprising 11,243 egocentric images sparsely sampled from nearly 1,000 videos across multiple sources: Ego4D (7,458 frames), EPIC-KITCHEN (2,121 frames), THU-READ (806 frames), and custom-collected GoPro videos (350 frames). This multi-source approach aimed to achieve diversity in daily activities and scenarios, involving over 100 human subjects and more than 300 activity types, which is significantly larger than previous segmentation datasets in terms of subject and activity diversity.

For each image, per-pixel mask annotations were obtained for:
- Left hand
- Right hand
- Interacting objects, categorized by the hand(s) interacting with them: left-hand object, right-hand object, or two-hand object.
- Two levels of interaction: direct (hand touching object) and indirect (object interacted with without touching). The paper primarily focuses on direct interactions but notes that indirect interaction labels are also provided.
- Both "hand only" and "hand + arm" mask types are provided, allowing flexibility for different application requirements.

A separate out-of-distribution test set of 500 frames was created by sparsely sampling and labeling frames from 30 egocentric YouTube videos to evaluate generalization performance in real-world, unconstrained settings.

#### 3.2. Hand-Object Contact Boundary

A novel concept, the "dense contact boundary," is introduced to explicitly model the relationship between a hand and an interacting object.
- **Definition and Generation:** The contact boundary is defined as the contact region between the hand and the interacting object. It is generated by dilating both the labeled hand and object masks, finding the overlapping region between these dilated masks, and then binarizing this overlap to create a pseudo-ground truth contact boundary.
- **Causal Segmentation Pipeline:** The segmentation model employs a sequential, multi-stage decoding pipeline. It first predicts the left/right hand masks. These predicted hand masks are then concatenated with the RGB image and used as input to predict the contact boundary. Finally, the RGB image, predicted hand masks, and predicted contact boundary masks are concatenated as inputs to predict the interacting object masks. This sequential approach is designed to leverage the information from previous stages, thereby providing explicit guidance for interacting object segmentation and hand-object separation.
- **Model Architecture:** The authors tested both a convolutional architecture (ResNet-18 backbone with HRNet head) and a transformer architecture (Swin-L backbone with UperNet head). For the experiments presented, the ResNet-18/HRNet combination was primarily used. Training utilized standard binary cross-entropy loss for contact boundary prediction.

#### 3.3. Context-Aware Compositional Data Augmentation (CCDA)

To improve robustness and generalization, especially for out-of-domain adaptation, a context-aware compositional data augmentation technique was developed. The pipeline consists of:
1. **"Clean" Background Extraction:** This step generates a pool of background scenes devoid of hands or interacting objects. Two methods are used:
 - A binary classifier identifies frames without hands from a large pool of video frames.
 - An image inpainting model removes existing hand-object regions from images with annotations, yielding a "clean" background.
2. **Context-Aware Image Composition:** For a given image with hand-object segmentation masks, the hand-object regions are first inpainted to create a "clean" query background. This query background is then used to retrieve the top-K semantically similar background scenes from the "clean" background candidate pool, based on deep features extracted by.
3. **Final Composition:** Hand and object foregrounds from the original image are composited onto sampled backgrounds from the retrieved top-K list. This process aims to generate semantically consistent foreground-background combinations, expanding the training data with diverse but plausible scenarios. The quantity of augmented images was determined empirically based on achieving maximum IoU on a validation set.

#### 3.4. Applications

The developed hand-object segmentation model is applied to demonstrate its utility in several downstream vision tasks:
- **Hand State Classification and Activity Recognition:** Hand and object masks are added as auxiliary inputs to classification models (e.g., a classifier with backbone for hand state, SlowFast for activity recognition) to evaluate performance improvement.
- **3D Mesh Reconstruction of Hand-Object Interaction:** The segmentation model is integrated into an existing 3D mesh reconstruction pipeline, replacing its generic segmentation component (100-DOH detector + PointRend). This tests the ability to provide more accurate 2D masks for guiding 3D optimization.
- **Seeing Through the Hand in Egocentric Videos:** The robust per-frame hand segmentation masks are combined with a flow-guided video inpainting algorithm to remove hands from egocentric videos, thereby revealing occluded background content.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed dataset, methodologies, and their impact on various applications.

#### 4.1. Two-Hand Segmentation

The models trained on the proposed EgoHOS dataset consistently outperformed those trained on previous datasets across different hand segmentation tasks, as evaluated on an out-of-distribution YouTube test set.
- **Left/Right Hand Segmentation:** For left/right hand segmentation, the model trained on EgoHOS achieved a mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) of 76.29%/77.00% for left/right hands, significantly surpassing EgoHand (10.68%/33.28%) and 100-DOH + BoxInst (36.30%/37.51%) (Table 2). Qualitative results (Fig. 5) indicated that previous models often misclassified hand categories.
- **Binary Hand Segmentation:** When evaluating binary hand segmentation, the EgoHOS-trained model achieved an mIoU of 83.18%, outperforming EgoHand (56.51%), 100-DOH + BoxInst (69.50%), and EYTH (75.94%) (Table 3).
- **Binary Hand + Arm Segmentation:** For hand + arm segmentation, the EgoHOS-trained model yielded an mIoU of 92.46%, substantially higher than EGTEA (33.26%) (Table 4).
- **Impact of Context-Aware Compositional Data Augmentation (CCDA):** CCDA consistently improved hand segmentation performance across all settings. For instance, in left/right hand segmentation, mIoU increased from 76.29%/77.00% to 79.73%/82.17% with CCDA (Table 2).

#### 4.2. Hand and Interacting Object Segmentation

An ablation study on hand-object segmentation (left hand, right hand, left-hand object, right-hand object, two-hand object) revealed the benefits of the proposed components:
- **Sequential Decoding vs. Parallel Decoding:** A sequential decoding pipeline, where hand masks guide object segmentation, achieved better performance for object masks compared to parallel decoding.
- **Contact Boundary (CB):** Integrating the dense contact boundary as an intermediate guiding signal significantly boosted object segmentation performance. For instance, comparing "Seq. Decode" to "Seq. Decode + CB" (Table 5), left-hand object mIoU increased from 54.83% to 59.05%, right-hand object from 38.48% to 40.85%, and two-hand object from 39.14% to 49.94%. This suggests that explicitly modeling the interaction relationship aids in detecting and segmenting interacting objects.
- **Impact of CCDA:** CCDA consistently improved both hand and object segmentation performance. When combined with sequential decoding and contact boundary ("Seq. Decode + CB + CCDA"), the model achieved mIoU of 87.70% (left hand), 88.79% (right hand), 62.20% (left-hand object), 44.40% (right-hand object), and 52.77% (two-hand object), marking the highest performance in the ablation study (Table 5). The authors suggest CCDA particularly helps object segmentation by allowing the network to learn pixel grouping more effectively in varied backgrounds.
- Qualitative results (Fig. 6, Fig. 7) demonstrated the model's ability to predict dense contact boundaries and segment hands and interacting objects accurately in diverse scenarios.

#### 4.3. Applications

- **Boosting Hand State Classification and Activity Recognition:**
 - **Hand State Classification:** For classifying hand states (portable, stationary, no-contact, self-contact, not-exist), adding hand and object masks as input significantly improved performance. The baseline model using only RGB achieved 78.53%/74.29% accuracy for left/right hand state. Adding hand masks increased this to 84.18%/83.33%, and adding both hand and object masks further raised it to 86.72%/83.33% (Table 6).
 - **Activity Recognition:** For verb/noun classification in EPIC-KITCHEN, adding hand masks to a SlowFast network improved top-1 accuracy from 23.95%/36.77% to 25.98%/37.04% respectively.

- **Improved 3D Mesh Reconstruction of Hand-Object Interaction:** Integrating the EgoHOS segmentation model into a 3D mesh reconstruction pipeline (replacing the 100-DOH + PointRend pipeline) led to improvements. Qualitative comparisons (Fig. 9) showed that the EgoHOS-based pipeline produced more accurate object mesh reconstructions by segmenting complete objects, whereas the previous pipeline often failed to segment full objects or entirely missed interacting objects, leading to inaccurate or absent 3D models.

- **Seeing Through the Hand in Egocentric Videos:** The robust per-frame hand segmentation, when combined with a video inpainting model, enabled a "see-through hand" application. This demonstrated the capability to remove hands from egocentric video frames, revealing previously occluded background content (Fig. 10).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents several contributions that hold significance for the field of egocentric vision and related applications.

Firstly, the introduction of the **EgoHOS dataset** addresses a critical data gap. By providing 11,243 images with fine-grained, per-pixel segmentation labels for both hands and interacting objects, including novel dense contact boundaries, across a wide range of diverse activities and subjects, the dataset offers a more comprehensive resource than previous efforts. Its emphasis on "in-the-wild" scenarios and a dedicated out-of-distribution test set promotes the development of more generalizable models. This dataset can serve as a new benchmark for fine-grained egocentric perception.

Secondly, the methodological contributions, particularly the **dense contact boundary** concept and the **context-aware compositional data augmentation (CCDA)**, offer technical advancements. Explicitly modeling the hand-object contact boundary provides a direct cue for interaction, which was demonstrated to improve segmentation accuracy for interacting objects. The sequential decoding pipeline leveraging this information represents a structured approach to hand-object segmentation. The CCDA technique, designed to generate semantically consistent composite images, helps overcome issues of domain shift and improves model robustness, especially for object segmentation, against unseen illuminations, backgrounds, and object appearances.

Thirdly, the work positions its robust hand-object segmentation model as a **foundational tool** that can significantly impact various downstream applications.
- For **human behavior analysis**, improved hand-object segmentation can enhance the accuracy of hand state classification and egocentric activity recognition, leading to a more nuanced understanding of human actions and intentions.
- In **3D computer vision**, the more reliable 2D hand and object masks generated by this system can improve the accuracy and robustness of 3D mesh reconstruction for hand-object interactions, reducing the need for manual intervention in complex scenes. This is crucial for applications in virtual reality, augmented reality, and robotics where realistic interaction modeling is essential.
- The novel application of **"seeing through the hand"** in egocentric videos, enabled by accurate hand segmentation and video inpainting, has potential implications for future AR systems or any vision system where foreground hand occlusion hinders scene understanding. It could allow for the analysis of occluded visual information, opening new avenues for research into pervasive sensing and context awareness.

Overall, this research advances the state-of-the-art in egocentric hand-object segmentation by providing a rich dataset and robust methodologies. Its contributions are expected to facilitate further research in egocentric perception, human-computer interaction, robotics, and augmented reality, by offering a more reliable and fine-grained basis for understanding and modeling human interaction with the environment.
