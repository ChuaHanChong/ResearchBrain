# TACTIC: Tactile and Vision Conditioned Contact-Centric Control for Whole-Arm Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> TACTIC, a control framework, enables robots to execute robust and safe whole-arm manipulation by integrating multimodal sensing with learned and analytical predictive models. It achieves higher success rates and reduces unsafe force interactions in both simulated and real-world contact-rich tasks, including manikin caregiving.

## Problem

- Existing robot manipulation methods struggle with whole-arm manipulation, which requires sustained, distributed contact and explicit force regulation due to tightly coupled motion and contact forces, and partial observability.
- Purely data-driven approaches often learn from end-effector-centric datasets, leading to brittle and physically inconsistent predictions for diverse multi-link contact configurations.
- Purely analytical models, while safe, typically fail to capture complex, multimodal, and partially observed interactions, limiting adaptive behaviors in contact-rich tasks.

## Method

- Developed **TACTIC**, a ==receding-horizon, sampling-based Model Predictive Control (MPC)== system that leverages multimodal sensing, ==learned latent dynamics==, and ==analytical kinematics==.
- Created a ==contact-centric state representation== by fusing RGB-D vision, distributed tactile sensing, and a ==2D proximity mask== to comprehensively understand near and active contact.
- Introduced ==contact-aware action sampling== using ==contact Jacobian projections== to bias exploration towards force-regulating directions, combined with a ==hybrid predictive model== for robust contact dynamics and force prediction.

## Results

- Achieved an **87.2%** success rate and **39.0** force violations in simulated 3D Maze tasks, outperforming latent-only (**64.1%** SR, **108.3** FV) and kinematics-only (**43.6%** SR, **79.9** FV) variants, as well as offline model-based RL baselines.
- Demonstrated higher success rates in real-world Side Rollover (**12/20** vs **3/20** for Diffusion Policy) and Limb Repositioning (**14/20** vs **5/20** for Diffusion Policy) tasks, while exhibiting fewer force-threshold violations than human expert teleoperation.
- Contributed open hardware designs for a Kinova Gen3 exoskeleton teleoperation interface and a WiFi-based distributed piezoresistive tactile sensing suite.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A hybrid predictive model, combining learned latent dynamics with analytical kinematics, effectively balances physical consistency with the capacity to predict complex, multimodal interactions in whole-arm manipulation.
> - Fusing distributed tactile sensing with a visual proximity mask is crucial for overcoming partial observability, significantly enhancing task success and safety in contact-rich scenarios.
> - Contact-aware action sampling, which biases exploration towards force-modulating directions, substantially improves planning efficiency and reduces unsafe force violations in multi-contact environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in robot learning, including vision-language-action (VLA) models, diffusion-based policies, and world models, have significantly improved robotic capabilities across various manipulation tasks. These methods often leverage large datasets to acquire priors over scenes and skills, enabling desired behaviors with reduced task-specific engineering.

However, a persistent challenge in robot manipulation lies in contact-rich scenarios, particularly "whole-arm manipulation." In such contexts, successful task completion necessitates explicit reasoning about and regulation of interaction forces. Whole-arm manipulation, where a robot deliberately uses multiple arm links to establish, break, and modulate contact, is a common mode of interaction for humans when manipulating large, unwieldy, or deformable objects, or when providing physical assistance. This capability is critical for applications such as assistive caregiving and physical human-robot interaction (pHRI), where distributing forces over larger areas improves stability and user comfort.

Existing approaches to contact-rich manipulation typically fall into two categories: purely data-driven models and purely analytical models. Data-driven methods, while capable of learning complex interaction patterns, often struggle with whole-arm scenarios because available datasets are predominantly end-effector-centric, lacking the diversity of multi-link contact configurations. This can lead to brittleness and physically inconsistent predictions, even with tactile supervision. Conversely, purely analytical models offer safety mechanisms but frequently fail to capture the multimodal, partially observed interactions inherent in complex, contact-rich whole-arm tasks. Consequently, systems relying solely on one paradigm may either capture rich interactions but compromise safety under distribution shift or enforce safety without generating sophisticated whole-arm behaviors.

This work addresses the limitations of both paradigms by proposing a hybrid control framework. It integrates multimodal sensing, learned dynamics, and analytical kinematic structures within a receding-horizon control scheme. This approach aims to enable robust and safe whole-arm manipulation, bridging the gap between data-driven flexibility and model-based physical consistency in complex interaction settings. The research also contributes to the open hardware landscape by releasing designs for a teleoperation interface and a tactile sensing suite, which supports future work in this challenging domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable robots to perform robust and safe whole-arm manipulation. This involves tasks where the robot's entire arm surface makes direct contact with the environment, distributing contact across multiple links as interactions form, slide, and break.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in whole-arm manipulation:

1. **Tight Coupling of Motion and Contact Forces:** In whole-arm manipulation, the robot's arm configuration profoundly influences both its motion and the contact forces it experiences. Small changes in joint angles can significantly alter contact locations, contact types (forming, breaking, sliding), and the magnitude and direction of interaction forces. Generating trajectories that account for this intricate coupling is complex and often leads to non-convex optimization problems.
2. **Partial Observability of Contact State:** Under real-world conditions, parts of the robot arm or the manipulated object can become occluded, making it challenging to fully observe the contact state using visual sensors alone. Traditional manipulation pipelines often assume full observability or rely on end-effector-centric observations, which are insufficient for whole-arm tasks. Distributed tactile sensing is identified as crucial for directly observing contact and inferring interaction forces.
3. **Physical Inconsistency and Data Scarcity in Learned Models:** Many learning-based manipulation pipelines are trained on datasets that primarily feature end-effector interactions. Consequently, they may lack sufficient examples of diverse multi-link contact configurations and force-regulating behaviors. When deployed in whole-arm scenarios, purely learned models can exhibit physically inconsistent predictions (e.g., unrealistic force magnitudes or contact dynamics) under distribution shifts, leading to brittle or unsafe behavior.
4. **Limitations of Purely Analytical Models:** While analytical models offer strong guarantees for safety and physical consistency, they often struggle to capture the complex, multimodal, and partially observed interactions that arise in contact-rich whole-arm manipulation. Relying solely on analytical models can restrict the robot's ability to perform complex, adaptive behaviors.

To address these challenges, the research proposes TACTIC, a control framework grounded in three design principles:

- **Contact-Centric and Multimodal State Representation:** To overcome partial observability and integrate crucial information, the system should fuse RGB-D vision with distributed tactile sensing and a compact 2D proximity representation. This provides a comprehensive understanding of where the arm is near or in contact with the scene.
- **Contact-Aware Action Sampling:** In constrained multi-contact environments, standard action sampling schemes (e.g., for MPPI) may be inefficient at discovering actions that effectively modulate interaction forces. The system needs to bias sampled action sequences towards directions that can regulate forces, leveraging tactile-derived contact locations and corresponding Jacobians.
- **Hybrid and Contact-Aware Predictive Model:** To balance physical consistency with the ability to predict complex interactions, the system should combine a learned, action-conditioned latent dynamics model with analytical kinematics via contact Jacobians. This hybrid approach enables reliable rollouts of future contact configurations and interaction forces, allowing objective functions to trade task progress against whole-arm force regulation.

The paper explicitly outlines five main contributions (C1-C5) that directly support these objectives:
- **C1:** A contact-centric state representation for whole-arm manipulation fusing RGB-D, distributed tactile sensing, and a 2D proximity map.
- **C2:** Contact-aware action sampling for sampling-based MPC, using contact Jacobian projections to bias exploration.
- **C3:** A hybrid predictive model combining learned latent dynamics with analytical kinematics for predicting contact dynamics and forces, and scoring trajectories with force-aware objectives.
- **C4:** Simulation and real-robot experiments with ablations to demonstrate performance and isolate contributions.
- **C5:** Open hardware for a Kinova Gen3 exoskeleton and a WiFi-based distributed piezoresistive tactile sensing suite.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TACTIC (Tactile and Vision Conditioned Contact-Centric Control) is formulated as a receding-horizon, sampling-based Model Predictive Control (MPC) system, specifically instantiating the online optimizer with Model-Predictive Path Integral (MPPI). At each timestep, MPPI samples candidate action sequences, evaluates their costs, and updates a nominal sequence. Only the first action is executed before replanning.

The methodology is structured around three core design principles:

**A. Building a Contact-Centric Representation (C1):**
The system learns an action-conditioned dynamics model from multimodal observations. The raw observation `o_t` includes an RGB image (`I_t`), a depth map (`D_t`), distributed tactile readings (`f_t`), and robot proprioception (`q_t`, `˙q_t`). This is augmented into a contact-centric observation `o_cc_t`.

1. **Proximity Mask (`M_t`):** This is a 2D image-aligned representation indicating regions where the arm is near or in contact with the scene.
 - It is generated by segmenting robot and object masks from the RGB image (using finetuned YOLOv11).
 - Pixels from these masks are unprojected to 3D point sets.
 - For each robot point, the distance to the closest object point is computed. These distances are mapped to pixel intensity (nearer = brighter).
 - Tactile data is fused by rendering a binary taxel indicator image (`M_tax_t`). For active taxels (force > `f_min`), their 3D pose (via forward kinematics) is projected onto the image, and a unit-valued blob is rendered.
 - The final `M_t` is the per-pixel maximum of the visual proximity field and `M_tax_t`, providing explicit visual grounding for impending and active contact.
2. **Encoding Multimodal Observations:** `o_cc_t` is encoded into a latent state `z_t` using modality-specific encoders.
 - A shared DINOv3 backbone encodes `I_t`, `D_t`, and `M_t` (with `D_t` and `M_t` replicated to three channels).
 - A lightweight 1D CoordConv network processes `f_t` (vector of normal forces), allowing it to learn location-dependent contact features.
 - A lightweight MLP embeds proprioception (`q_t`, `˙q_t`), using a sin-cos mapping for joint angles.
 - The latent dynamics model conditions on a history of `L` latent states, `z_t−L+1:t`.

**B. Contact-Aware Action Sampling (C2):**
TACTIC enhances the MPPI sampling process (based on STORM) to improve efficiency in multi-contact settings.

1. **Weighted Normal Contact Jacobian:** For each active contact `k` (where force > `f_min`), a contact Jacobian `J_k,t` and unit contact normal `n_k,t` are computed. The normal Jacobian row `J_n_k,t` maps joint velocities to normal velocity at contact. These are stacked into `J_n_t`. To prioritize significant contacts, each row is weighted by a saturated measured force magnitude (`ρ_k,t = sat(∥f_k,t∥ / F_sat)`), resulting in `f_J_n_t`.
2. **Contact-Conditioned Shaping:** Two operators are defined: `G_force_t` (extracts components of perturbations aligned with force-modulating directions, i.e., `row(f_J_n_t)`) and `G_null_t` (retains nullspace components). Perturbation sequences (`δ^(j)_t+i`) are shaped by `S_t = σ_force,eff G_force_t + σ_null,eff G_null_t`, where `σ_force > σ_null` biases exploration towards directions that affect contact interaction.
3. **Safety Projection:** To prevent actions that drive motion into high-force contacts, sampled action sequences are projected onto a safety cone. For contacts `k` where force (`f_k,t`) exceeds a `F_safe` threshold, a quadratic program (QP) ensures that commanded normal motion (`J_viol_t (μ_t+i + y)`) is non-negative (i.e., away from contact).

**C. Contact-Centric Hybrid Predictive Model (C3):**
The core of TACTIC's planning is a hybrid predictive model `F = {F_k, F_l}` that combines analytical kinematics with learned latent dynamics.

1. **Analytical Kinematics (`F_k`):** Given current joint state and a candidate action sequence, this branch deterministically rolls out joint kinematics, providing predicted joint states (`ˆq_t+i`, `ˆ˙q_t+i`), end-effector poses (`ˆe_t+i`), and contact Jacobians for current active contacts. This ensures physical consistency and enables reasoning about active contact motion.
2. **Learned Latent Dynamics (`F_l`):** This model predicts the evolution of complex, multimodal interactions. It takes a history of latent states (`z_t−L+1:t`) and an action sequence (`u_t:t+H−1`) to output a latent rollout (`ˆz_t+1:t+H`). `F_l` is implemented as a Vision Transformer (ViT) predictor conditioned on actions via adaptive layer normalization (AdaLN). Lightweight decoders (`ψ_m`) map `ˆz_t+i` to task-relevant signals, such as joint position and binary contact probabilities for all taxels.
3. **Training Objective:** Models are trained on offline datasets using a teacher-forced latent consistency loss, augmented with a multi-step rollout loss to reduce drift.
4. **Planning with Hybrid Models:** In each MPC step, `N` candidate action sequences are sampled. For each sequence, `F_k` and `F_l` are rolled out in parallel. The resulting rollouts are then scored by a cost function `J(u_t:t+H−1)`.
5. **Two-Fidelity Rollouts:** To achieve real-time control, a lightweight "draft" predictor (`F_draft_l`) quickly rolls out all `N` particles for approximate costs. Only the top-M candidates (`M ≪ N`) are then re-scored with the full `F_l` for final cost computation. A runtime safeguard monitors `F_draft_l`'s prediction error and can trigger a fallback to the full model for all particles if error exceeds a threshold.

**D. Scoring Hybrid Rollouts:**
The total cost `J` is a weighted sum of three components:

- **`c_kin`:** Penalizes kinematic constraint violations (self-collision, joint limits) and encourages task-space goals (e.g., end-effector pose tracking), along with action magnitude.
- **`c_force`:** Regulates predicted interaction forces relative to a safety limit (`F_safe`). This combines:
 - `c_active`: A spring-based force estimate for currently active contacts, initialized with measured forces and propagated kinematically.
 - `c_pred`: Derived from the learned latent dynamics' decoded binary contacts (identifying contact onset) and joint positions, also using a spring model for predicted compression.
- **`c_goal`:** Encourages goal-reaching in the latent space (`∥ˆz_t+i − z_g∥^2`).
- **Learned Value Function for Long-Horizon Tasks:** For tasks with long horizons, an Implicit Q-Learning (IQL) trained value function `V_θ(ˆz_t+H, z_g)` is added as a terminal cost term to bias sampling towards sequences that maintain task progress beyond the finite horizon.

**E. Open Hardware (C5):**
The research also contributes open hardware designs for:
- A mirrored Kinova Gen3 7-DoF teleoperation exoskeleton interface.
- A WiFi-based distributed piezoresistive tactile sensing suite for the Kinova Gen3 arm, comprising 22 Tekscan FlexiForce sensors.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated TACTIC through both simulation and real-world experiments, conducting ablations and comparisons against alternative methods.

**A. Simulation Experiments (Q1-Q3)**
Simulation studies were designed to isolate the contributions of TACTIC's design choices:

- **Q1: Contact-centric Representation (Tactile + Proximity Mask):**
 - Ablation studies in the 3D Maze environment demonstrated that removing both distributed tactile readings and the proximity mask (`TACTIC-NoTacNoMask`) significantly degraded performance and increased unsafe interactions (63.5% success rate (SR), 84.4 force violations (FV)).
 - Removing only tactile readings but retaining the vision-based proximity mask (`TACTIC-NoTac`) showed intermediate performance (72.1% SR, 55.1 FV).
 - TACTIC with the full contact-centric representation achieved the highest SR (87.2%) and lowest FV (39.0). This indicates that the combination of tactile sensing and the proximity mask is important for task success and safety.

- **Q2: Contact-Aware Action Sampling (CaSa):**
 - Comparing TACTIC against a variant using a base STORM sampler without contact-aware shaping (`TACTIC-NoCaSa`) showed that contact-aware sampling substantially reduced force violations (39.0 FV vs 111.9 FV) and improved motion smoothness (lower jerk, 2.83 vs 5.87).
 - Further ablations confirmed that the QP-based safety projection component of contact-aware sampling contributes to reducing force violations (50.0 FV with QP vs 84.3 FV without).

- **Q3: Hybrid Predictive Model (Analytical Kinematics + Learned Latent Dynamics):**
 - Comparison of latent-only (`TACTIC-L`), kinematics-only (`TACTIC-K`), and hybrid (`TACTIC`) rollouts revealed that TACTIC achieved the best trade-off in the 3D Maze environment.
 - `TACTIC-L` (latent-only) showed a 64.1% SR and 108.3 FV, indicating potential for drift without analytical grounding.
 - `TACTIC-K` (kinematics-only) yielded a 43.6% SR and 79.9 FV, demonstrating that while it provides geometric grounding, it cannot anticipate complex contact state changes.
 - TACTIC (hybrid) achieved the highest SR (87.2%) and lowest FV (39.0), confirming the benefit of combining both predictive components.

- **Hybridization with Pretrained World Models:**
 - TACTIC's hybrid modeling approach was extended to existing pretrained world models (V-JEPA2 on `Reach` and DINO-WM on `Granular`).
 - Adding a kinematics-based goal cost to these models' planners, while keeping the core predictor frozen, improved V-JEPA2 finetuning data efficiency (requiring approximately 34% less data for comparable performance) and enhanced DINO-WM's geometric rollout quality (reducing Chamfer Distance by approximately 26%). This suggests the general applicability of hybrid modeling.

- **Comparison to Alternate Model-Based Approaches:**
 - TACTIC was benchmarked against two offline model-based RL baselines: DreamerV3 and TD-MPC2.
 - On the `Maze` task, TACTIC achieved a higher SR (87.2%) and fewer FV (39.0) compared to TD-MPC2 (65.1% SR, 93.9 FV) and DreamerV3 (75.9% SR, 82.4 FV).
 - On the `Bed Bathing` task, TACTIC outperformed DreamerV3 in both SR (79.5% vs 51.6%) and FV (22.6 vs 63.8).

**B. Real-World Experiments**
Real-world deployment assessed TACTIC's performance under sensing noise, actuation delays, and dynamic contact conditions.

- **Tasks:** Evaluated on a dynamic 3D Maze, Side Rollover (manikin manipulation), and Limb Repositioning (manikin caregiving).
- **Baselines:** Compared against Diffusion Policy (DP) and human expert teleoperation (as a safety reference).
- **Performance:**
 - TACTIC achieved higher success rates than Diffusion Policy on Side Rollover (12/20 vs 3/20 trials) and Limb Repositioning (14/20 vs 5/20 trials).
 - On the Maze task, TACTIC achieved a 3/5 success rate, while DP achieved 2/5. DP's reported zero force violations on Maze were attributed to its tendency to bypass the "maze" by reaching from the top, rather than navigating through it with contact.
- **Safety:** TACTIC demonstrated fewer force-threshold violations compared to matched human expert teleoperation episodes. This indicates that TACTIC's contact-aware planning can regulate interaction forces more effectively than human demonstrations, particularly beyond the training data distribution.
- **Value Function Ablation:** An ablation in the real-world maze environment showed that the terminal value function improved success rate (6/10 vs 2/10 without it).

**C. Open Hardware Contribution**
The research developed and open-sourced the hardware for a Kinova Gen3 exoskeleton teleoperation interface and a WiFi-based distributed piezoresistive tactile sensing suite, facilitating data collection and future research.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents TACTIC, a control framework that advances the field of whole-arm manipulation by addressing critical challenges in contact-rich robotic interaction. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling Robust Whole-Arm Manipulation:** TACTIC provides a principled approach for robots to engage in sustained and distributed contact across multiple arm links. This capability is crucial for tasks involving large, heavy, or deformable objects, which are often unwieldy for end-effector-centric manipulation. By demonstrating effective control in such scenarios, the work contributes to expanding the repertoire of tasks robots can perform.

2. **Enhanced Safety in Physical Human-Robot Interaction (pHRI):** A central aspect of TACTIC is its focus on explicit force regulation and contact-aware planning. The ability to predict and control interaction forces, and to reduce force violations below human expert levels, is paramount for applications like assistive caregiving. This contributes to making robots safer and more acceptable collaborators in close proximity to humans.

3. **Advancement in Hybrid Control and World Models:** The hybrid predictive model, which fuses analytical kinematics with learned latent dynamics, represents a significant contribution to robot learning. It leverages the strengths of both model-based (physical consistency, safety) and data-driven (learning complex interaction patterns from multimodal data) approaches. The demonstration that this hybrid approach can enhance the performance and data efficiency of pretrained world models (like V-JEPA2 and DINO-WM) suggests its broader applicability beyond whole-arm manipulation, potentially improving general robot learning systems.

4. **Improved Planning Efficiency in Constrained Environments:** The novel contact-aware action sampling mechanism efficiently steers the MPC planner towards force-modulating directions. This addresses the computational challenges of exploring high-dimensional action spaces under multi-contact constraints, making real-time control feasible in complex environments.

5. **Practical Real-World Applicability:** The validation of TACTIC on a physical robot performing complex tasks such as manikin repositioning and navigating a dynamic maze provides empirical evidence of its robustness and effectiveness in real-world conditions. This moves the research closer to practical deployment in fields like assistive robotics and logistics.

6. **Open Science and Reproducibility:** The decision to open-source the custom hardware (exoskeleton teleoperation interface and distributed tactile sensing suite) is a valuable contribution to the research community. It lowers barriers for other researchers to replicate experiments, build upon this work, and accelerate progress in whole-arm and tactile-enabled manipulation.

7. **Foundational for Future Research:** TACTIC lays a foundation for future investigations into tighter integration with low-level compliant control, exploration of variable impedance strategies, and adaptation to human subjects in caregiving contexts. The identified limitations, such as reliance on high-fidelity data and sparse tactile coverage, also point towards important directions for scalability and generalization, including cross-embodiment learning and active tactile exploration.

In summary, TACTIC offers a robust and adaptable framework for contact-rich whole-arm manipulation, with direct implications for improving safety and capability in pHRI and general robotic tasks, while also contributing valuable open-source resources.
