# Seeing with You: Perception-Reasoning Coevolution for Multimodal Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Shanghai AI Lab developed PRCO (Perception–Reasoning Coevolution), a dual-role reinforcement learning framework that disentangles visual perception and symbolic reasoning in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). This approach, using role-specific utility rewards, consistently improves accuracy by over 7 points on various multimodal reasoning benchmarks and reduces perception errors by 39.2% on WeMath.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) frameworks for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) suffer from a 'perception bottleneck,' failing to reliably enhance the accuracy of upstream visual evidence extraction.
- Outcome-driven optimization in current RLVR methods uses a shared reward signal from the final answer, leading to blurred credit assignment between perception and reasoning components and primarily benefiting reasoning patterns.
- Diagnostic analysis showed that while reasoning errors decreased, perception errors only marginally improved with prior RLVR approaches, highlighting MLLMs' struggle with accurate visual information extraction.

## Method

- Introduces **PRCO** (==Perception-Reasoning Coevolution==), a ==dual-role RLVR framework== featuring a cooperative ==Observer== (for evidence captioning) and ==Solver== (for reasoning) that share a unified MLLM policy.
- The Observer is trained with a ==utility reward== derived from the Solver's downstream success, incorporating an auxiliary LLM-based ==leakage suppression== mechanism to prevent direct answer shortcuts in captions.
- The Solver is rewarded for correctness and format compliance, utilizing a ==caption-first warmup== phase that initially trains it solely on question and caption, encouraging effective utilization of evidence before regaining full image access.

## Results

- **PRCO** consistently improved accuracy by an average of **7.65 points** (3B backbone) and **7.18 points** (7B backbone) across eight diverse multimodal reasoning benchmarks, outperforming prior RL-tuned baselines.
- Fine-grained error analysis on WeMath and MathVista revealed **PRCO-7B** reduced perception errors by **39.2%** and reasoning errors by **23.8%** on WeMath, directly addressing the identified perception bottleneck.
- Ablation studies validated the importance of dual-role updates, caption-first warmup, and leakage suppression, showing that each component is critical for enhancing utility-driven evidence extraction and effective reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly disentangling perception and reasoning through distinct, role-specific, utility-driven learning signals effectively resolves blurred credit assignment and drives the generation of genuinely useful visual evidence.
> - A co-evolutionary learning paradigm, where an Observer provides evidence captions and a Solver reasons over them, leads to synergistic improvements in both capabilities, fostering more robust and accurate multimodal problem-solving.
> - Strategic training steps, such as a 'caption-first warmup' for the Solver and leakage suppression for the Observer, are crucial for encouraging effective utilization of intermediate evidence representations and optimizing the overall multimodal reasoning pipeline.
