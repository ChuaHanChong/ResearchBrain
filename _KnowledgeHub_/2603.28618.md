# Seeing with You: Perception-Reasoning Coevolution for Multimodal Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Shanghai AI Lab developed PRCO (Perception–Reasoning Coevolution), a dual-role reinforcement learning framework that disentangles visual perception and symbolic reasoning in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). This approach, using role-specific utility rewards, consistently improves accuracy by over 7 points on various multimodal reasoning benchmarks and reduces perception errors by 39.2% on WeMath.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) frameworks for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) suffer from a 'perception bottleneck,' failing to reliably enhance the accuracy of upstream visual evidence extraction.
- Outcome-driven optimization in current RLVR methods uses a shared reward signal from the final answer, leading to blurred credit assignment between perception and reasoning components and primarily benefiting reasoning patterns.
- Diagnostic analysis showed that while reasoning errors decreased, perception errors only marginally improved with prior RLVR approaches, highlighting MLLMs' struggle with accurate visual information extraction.

## Method

- Introduces **PRCO** (==Perception-Reasoning Coevolution==), a ==dual-role RLVR framework== featuring a cooperative ==Observer== (for evidence captioning) and ==Solver== (for reasoning) that share a unified MLLM policy.
- The Observer is trained with a ==utility reward== derived from the Solver's downstream success, incorporating an auxiliary LLM-based ==leakage suppression== mechanism to prevent direct answer shortcuts in captions.
- The Solver is rewarded for correctness and format compliance, utilizing a ==caption-first warmup== phase that initially trains it solely on question and caption, encouraging effective utilization of evidence before regaining full image access.

## Results

- **PRCO** consistently improved accuracy by an average of **7.65 points** (3B backbone) and **7.18 points** (7B backbone) across eight diverse multimodal reasoning benchmarks, outperforming prior RL-tuned baselines.
- Fine-grained error analysis on WeMath and MathVista revealed **PRCO-7B** reduced perception errors by **39.2%** and reasoning errors by **23.8%** on WeMath, directly addressing the identified perception bottleneck.
- Ablation studies validated the importance of dual-role updates, caption-first warmup, and leakage suppression, showing that each component is critical for enhancing utility-driven evidence extraction and effective reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly disentangling perception and reasoning through distinct, role-specific, utility-driven learning signals effectively resolves blurred credit assignment and drives the generation of genuinely useful visual evidence.
> - A co-evolutionary learning paradigm, where an Observer provides evidence captions and a Solver reasons over them, leads to synergistic improvements in both capabilities, fostering more robust and accurate multimodal problem-solving.
> - Strategic training steps, such as a 'caption-first warmup' for the Solver and leakage suppression for the Observer, are crucial for encouraging effective utilization of intermediate evidence representations and optimizing the overall multimodal reasoning pipeline.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has demonstrated utility in enhancing the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) in text-only domains. This success has led to its extension to Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for addressing complex multimodal reasoning tasks. Existing multimodal RLVR approaches primarily focus on outcome-driven optimization, where a single, shared reward signal, based solely on the final answer, is used to update both the perception and reasoning components of the model.

This prevalent outcome-driven approach introduces a challenge related to credit assignment. The shared reward signal can blur the distinction between errors in visual evidence extraction (perception) and errors in subsequent logical deduction (reasoning). Consequently, while such methods often improve reasoning patterns, they may not reliably enhance the accuracy or specificity of upstream visual perception. Previous diagnostic analyses, such as those conducted using GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) on datasets like WeMath, have indicated that while reasoning errors are substantially reduced, perception errors show only marginal improvement over base models. This observation points to a "perception bottleneck" in current multimodal RLVR frameworks.

Prior attempts to address this bottleneck have included introducing additional perception-oriented optimization objectives, employing weighted token-level credit assignment, or explicitly requiring an image-description step before reasoning. However, these methods often still distribute outcome-based rewards to both perception and reasoning tokens, maintaining a coupled training signal that can lead to continued unreliability in visual evidence extraction.

The work presented in this paper, PRCO (Perception–Reasoning Coevolution), aims to address this specific limitation by introducing a dual-role RLVR framework. It seeks to decouple perception and reasoning at the gradient level by providing separate and reliable learning signals for each component, thereby promoting their coevolution. This approach distinguishes itself from prior work by fundamentally altering the reward structure to disentangle the optimization process for these two distinct capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to overcome the "perception bottleneck" observed in existing RLVR frameworks applied to MLLMs. The authors identify that current outcome-driven RLVR methods for MLLMs, which utilize a shared reward based on the final answer, blur credit assignment. This blurring frequently leads to improvements in reasoning patterns without consistently enhancing the accuracy of upstream visual evidence extraction.

The motivation for PRCO stems from a detailed diagnostic analysis, exemplified by applying GRPO to Qwen2.5-VL-7B on the WeMath benchmark. This analysis revealed that training with GRPO substantially reduced reasoning errors (by 24.3%), but perception errors improved only marginally (by 7.6%) compared to the base model. This indicated that while the model became better at reasoning, its ability to accurately perceive and extract visual information remained a significant limitation. The authors attribute this disparity to the outcome-only nature of the shared reward, which fails to provide sufficiently distinct feedback for the perception module.

This observation led the authors to pose a core question: "Can we use reliable, separate learning signals for perception and reasoning to decouple them at the gradient level?" The motivation is to move beyond methods that distribute a single outcome-based reward to both perception and reasoning tokens, which can still result in coupled training signals and suboptimal visual evidence extraction.

To address this, PRCO was developed with the following key objectives:
1. **Disentangle Perception and Reasoning:** To create a framework where the learning processes for visual perception and logical reasoning are distinct, rather than being optimized through a single, shared outcome reward.
2. **Introduce Role-Specific Learning Signals:** To design specific reward mechanisms for each identified role—one for evidence extraction (perception) and another for answer generation (reasoning)—to ensure that each component receives targeted and reliable feedback.
3. **Facilitate Perception–Reasoning Coevolution:** To enable a synergistic improvement where better evidence extraction from the perception module leads to more robust reasoning, and successful reasoning, in turn, provides a clear utility signal to refine perception.
4. **Improve Overall Multimodal Reasoning Accuracy:** To demonstrate that by addressing the perception bottleneck and fostering coevolution, the model's overall performance on challenging multimodal reasoning benchmarks can be consistently and significantly enhanced across various model scales.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PRCO (Perception–Reasoning Coevolution) is introduced as a dual-role RLVR framework designed to disentangle perception and reasoning in MLLMs. It operates with a shared multimodal policy that alternates between two cooperative roles: an Observer and a Solver.

#### 3.1. RLVR Setting

The framework operates within a standard RLVR setup. Training data consists of tuples (Image, Question, Ground-Truth Answer). A rule-based verifier `V(ˆa, a)` determines correctness (0 or 1), and a `FormatScore(ˆa)` checks output format compliance (0 to 1). The approach initiates RL training directly, without reliance on existing chain-of-thought data or prior supervised fine-tuning.

#### 3.2. Dual-Role Interaction Loop

A single policy, `πθ`, is instantiated in two roles through role-specific prompting. For each training instance (I, q, a):
1. **Observer Role:** The policy generates `G_O` candidate evidence captions `{c_k}` for the given image `I` and question `q`. These captions aim to summarize question-relevant visual evidence.
2. **Solver Role:** For each generated caption `c_k`, the policy generates `G_S` candidate answers `{ˆa_k,g}`. The Solver predicts the final answer based on the question `q`, the caption `c_k`, and optionally the image `I_S` (which can be `∅` or `I`).

#### 3.3. Observer: Utility-Driven Evidence Captioning

The Observer's task is to convert high-dimensional visual input into a textual evidence signal by producing a question-conditioned evidence caption. This caption summarizes the visual evidence most relevant to `q`, such as entities, attributes, and relations. The Observer samples `c ∼ πθ(· | I, q, r_O)`, where `r_O` is the Observer prompt.

- **Utility Reward with Leakage Suppression:** Directly evaluating the intrinsic quality of an evidence caption is challenging. Therefore, the Observer is trained using a utility reward derived from the Solver's downstream success. A crucial component is `I_leak(q, c)`, an auxiliary LLM-based leakage checker that identifies if the caption `c` directly contains the final answer to question `q`.
 The Observer reward `r_O_k` for a caption `c_k` is defined as:
 `r_O_k = (1 - I_leak(q, c_k)) * E[V(ˆa, a)]`, where the expectation `E[V(ˆa, a)]` is the empirical mean of verifier scores over `G_S` Solver rollouts conditioned on `(I_S, q, c_k, r_S)`.
 This reward mechanism encourages captions that assist downstream problem-solving while actively discouraging captions that directly leak the answer, ensuring the Observer focuses on evidence extraction rather than answer shortcutting.

#### 3.4. Solver: Evidence-Conditioned Reasoning

The Solver is responsible for producing the final answer by reasoning over the question `q` and the Observer’s caption `c`. It can optionally use the image `I_S`. Conditioning on `c` promotes explicit evidence-driven reasoning, while image input helps in cases where global structure, complex geometric relations, or fine-grained details are difficult to fully convey in text. The Solver samples `ˆa ∼ πθ(· | I_S, q, c, r_S)`, where `r_S` is the Solver prompt.

- **Solver Reward:** The Solver's reward `r_S` combines correctness and format compliance:
 `r_S = λ * r_acc + (1 - λ) * r_format`
 where `r_acc = V(ˆa, a)` (verifier score), `r_format = FormatScore(ˆa)` (rule-based format checker score), and `λ` is a hyperparameter (default 0.9) balancing these components.
- **Caption-first Warmup:** In the initial training phase (first 40 steps), `I_S` is set to `∅`, forcing the Solver to rely solely on `(q, c)`. This encourages the Solver to effectively utilize the caption. After this warmup, `I_S` is switched to `I`, restoring full multimodal grounding while retaining the benefits of caption conditioning. This prevents the Solver from ignoring captions by relying directly on raw image input too early.

#### 3.5. Unified Policy Optimization with Role-Specific Advantages

Both roles share the same policy `πθ` and are optimized jointly. However, their rollouts define different comparison groups for relative optimization.

- **Role-wise Grouping and Advantage Computation:**
 - For the Observer, `G_O` captions `{c_k}` are generated for a given `(I, q)`. The Observer rewards `{r_O_k}` are computed.
 - For each caption `c_k`, `G_S` Solver answers `{ˆa_k,g}` are generated, and their rewards `{r_S_k,g}` are computed.
 - Group-relative advantages (`ˆA`) are computed separately for each role. For a reward group `{r_i}`, `ˆA_i = r_i - mean({r_j})`. Standard deviation normalization is omitted to preserve original relative reward differences within each role.
 - Caption advantages `{ˆA_O_k}` are computed from `{r_O_k}`.
 - For Solver updates, one caption `˜k` is preferentially sampled from captions with non-zero variance in Solver rewards. Solver advantages `{ˆA_S_˜k,g}` are computed from `{r_S_˜k,g}`.
- **Unified Policy Update:** Observer and Solver trajectories, along with their respective advantages, are aggregated into a combined rollout batch. The shared policy `πθ` is optimized using a unified GRPO-style objective:
 `L_dual(θ) = L_GRPO(θ; ˆA_S) + L_GRPO(θ; ˆA_O)`
 where `L_GRPO` is the PPO-style clipped surrogate objective. A KL-divergence penalty is not applied (`β = 0`) to encourage exploration. This unified update mechanism allows for joint improvement of both perception and reasoning through decoupled, role-specific feedback.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of PRCO was conducted on eight challenging multimodal reasoning benchmarks, utilizing Qwen2.5-VL-3B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, and Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct backbones.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

PRCO consistently improved performance across model scales and task categories.
- **Qwen2.5-VL-3B:** PRCO-3B achieved an average accuracy gain of 7.65 points over the base model, outperforming GRPO, DAPO, and other open-source RL-tuned baselines (e.g., PAPO-G-3B, PAPO-D-3B, MMR1-3B-RL, Vision-SR1-3B).
- **Qwen2.5-VL-7B:** PRCO-7B demonstrated an average accuracy gain of 7.18 points over the base model. It achieved the best overall performance and the strongest results across all evaluated benchmarks, surpassing competitive baselines like VPPO-7B, DAPO, and GRPO.
- **Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct:** On this stronger backbone, PRCO also outperformed GRPO and DAPO, further confirming its effectiveness.
The improvements were observed across both math-related benchmarks (MathVerse, MathVision, MathVista, WeMath, DynaMath) and general multimodal reasoning tasks (LogicVista, MMMU-Pro, MMStar), indicating broad generalization.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

A comprehensive ablation study investigated the contribution of PRCO's key components:
- **Effect of Role-wise Updates:**
 - `PRCO w/o Solver` (only Observer caption trajectories): Resulted in a substantial performance reduction compared to full PRCO, but still outperformed the base model. This indicated that utility-driven caption learning alone can improve accuracy and highlights the perception bottleneck.
 - `PRCO w/o Observer` (only Solver answer trajectories): Led to consistent drops across model scales and benchmarks, confirming that utility-driven evidence extraction provides complementary benefits by strengthening the intermediate evidence signal for the Solver.
- **Effect of Caption-first Warmup:** Removing the caption-first warmup degraded performance on both 3B and 7B backbones. This suggested that the warmup phase is crucial for encouraging the Solver to effectively utilize captions. Without it, the Solver might rely on raw visual inputs too early, weakening the learning signal for the Observer and leading to lower-contrast utility rewards for credit assignment.
- **Additional Ablations (PRCO-7B):**
 - `PRCO w/ I_S = ∅` (Solver never sees the image after warmup): Showed the largest performance drop, indicating that while captioning is primary, direct image access remains important for recovering global structure and fine-grained details.
 - `PRCO w/o Leakage Checker`: Decreased overall performance, underscoring the importance of suppressing answer leakage to ensure the Observer focuses on genuine evidence extraction.
 - `PRCO w/ Fixed Utility Estimator`: Underperformed standard PRCO, suggesting that co-evolving utility feedback from the Solver is more beneficial for Observer learning than a fixed estimator.

#### 4.3. Pass@k Performance

On WeMath and MMStar, PRCO-7B exhibited larger gains over baselines (DAPO-7B, VPPO-7B) as the number of sampling attempts (`k`) increased. For example, on WeMath, the gap over DAPO-7B grew from 3.53 points at pass@1 to 7.33 points at pass@32. This trend suggests that PRCO-7B possesses more robust reasoning capabilities and scales better with increased sampling budgets.

#### 4.4. Error Category Analysis

An analysis of Qwen2.5-VL-7B and PRCO-7B on WeMath and MathVista, using GPT-5.1 for error categorization, demonstrated that PRCO reduced both perception and reasoning errors.
- On WeMath, PRCO-7B reduced perception errors by 39.2% and reasoning errors by 23.8% relative to the base model. This reduction in perception errors was notably larger than that achieved by vanilla GRPO (7.6%).
- Similar trends were observed on MathVista. These findings support the hypothesis that separate and reliable learning signals improve question-grounded visual perception and enable more robust reasoning.

#### 4.5. Effect of Rollout Group Size

- **Solver Rollout Group Size (`G_S`):** Increasing `G_S` consistently improved both PRCO and DAPO. PRCO with `G_S = 4` already outperformed DAPO with `G_S = 12`, indicating PRCO's higher optimization efficiency under fixed rollout budgets due to its separate learning signals.
- **Observer Rollout Group Size (`G_O`):** The effect of `G_O` was not monotonic; performance improved from `G_O = 2` to `G_O = 4`, but slightly declined at `G_O = 8`. This suggests that perception may saturate earlier than reasoning, and beyond a certain point, allocating more rollouts to the Solver might be more beneficial.

#### 4.6. Case Studies

Qualitative analysis with attention overlays demonstrated PRCO's operational mechanism. For example, in synthetic object filtering, the Observer accurately enumerated relevant objects with their attributes and spatial cues. In bar-chart reasoning, it transcribed item-wise values, and in table-based option selection, it extracted only question-relevant entries. The Solver then performed the required logical steps based on this structured evidence. Attention heatmaps showed the Observer focusing on question-relevant regions of the image, indicating effective question-grounded visual perception.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of PRCO represents an advance in multimodal reasoning by addressing a fundamental challenge in current RLVR frameworks: the reliable enhancement of visual perception. By proposing a dual-role RLVR framework that disentangles perception and reasoning through role-specific learning signals, PRCO facilitates a coevolutionary process where the Observer improves question-grounded visual evidence extraction, and the Solver learns to reason more effectively with explicit evidence guidance.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Decoupled Optimization:** PRCO provides a mechanism to decouple the optimization of perception and reasoning components, leading to more targeted and efficient learning. This is a departure from previous outcome-driven methods that often blur credit assignment.
- **Improved Perception Accuracy:** The diagnostic analyses and error categorization demonstrate that PRCO substantially reduces perception errors, a key bottleneck identified in prior work. This indicates that direct utility feedback for perception can lead to more accurate and question-relevant visual understanding.
- **Enhanced Reasoning Robustness:** By providing the Solver with structured, high-quality evidence captions, PRCO enables more robust and verifiable reasoning, as evidenced by consistent performance gains and better scaling with sampling budgets (Pass@k).
- **Broad Applicability:** The consistent improvements across various multimodal reasoning benchmarks, including mathematics, geometry, logic, and general tasks, and across different model scales, suggest the broad applicability and generalization capabilities of the PRCO framework.
- **Novelty in Reward Design:** The utility reward for the Observer, coupled with a leakage suppression mechanism, represents a novel approach to supervising intermediate perception outputs in an RLVR setting, where direct ground truth for evidence captions is often unavailable.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Development of More Capable MLLMs:** The framework contributes to the development of MLLMs that are not only skilled at reasoning but also possess a more accurate and contextually aware visual understanding, leading to more reliable AI systems.
- **Foundational for Future Research:** The concept of perception-reasoning coevolution and role-specific reward signals could serve as a foundation for future research in multimodal learning, particularly for complex tasks requiring nuanced visual interpretation and logical deduction.
- **Applications in Specialized Domains:** Improved multimodal reasoning capabilities could have significant implications for domains such as scientific research, medical diagnosis, educational tools involving diagrams, and autonomous systems that require precise interpretation of visual information.
- **Addressing AI Hallucination:** By strengthening the visual grounding of reasoning through explicit evidence extraction, PRCO could contribute to mitigating hallucination in MLLMs, where models generate plausible but factually incorrect information.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Work

- **Generalization to Open-Ended Generation:** The current study focuses on benchmarks with concise and verifiable answers. Further evaluation is needed to assess PRCO's generalization to more open-ended generation settings, where reward signals are often less defined.
- **Auxiliary Supervision Overhead:** The auxiliary supervision for leakage detection and answer verification, while helpful, may introduce additional noise and computational overhead.
- **Lossiness of Captioning:** Representing visual evidence as short captions is inherently lossy. Important aspects like global structure, fine-grained spatial relations, and geometric details might not be fully preserved. Future work could explore richer intermediate representations for visual inputs that transcend the limitations of textual captions.

#### 5.4. Ethical Considerations

The work utilizes publicly available datasets and benchmarks, which may contain social biases, annotation artifacts, or other imperfections. The authors state that PRCO does not introduce additional ethical risks beyond those inherent in training and evaluating multimodal models on existing public data, and they advocate for continued attention to data quality, transparent evaluation, and responsible reporting of model capabilities and limitations.
