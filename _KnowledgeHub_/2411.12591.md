# Thinking Before Looking: Improving Multimodal LLM Reasoning via Mitigating Visual Hallucination

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces the Visual Inference Chain (VIC) framework, a "thinking before looking" paradigm for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) designed to mitigate visual hallucination. The VIC framework consistently improves zero-shot performance across various vision-related tasks, demonstrating its effectiveness in refining MLLM reasoning capabilities, with average hallucination reduction of 15.62% on MMVP and general capability improvements of 9.13% on MME.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are prone to visual hallucination (generating non-existent objects, misinterpreting visual information) during complex reasoning tasks.
- Current multimodal Chain-of-Thought (MMCoT) approaches, operating under a "thinking while looking" paradigm, foster cross-modal biases and stereotypical reasoning due to simultaneous visual and textual processing.
- Hallucinations undermine the reliability and accuracy of MLLM reasoning, hindering their practical application in critical scenarios.

## Method

- The ==Visual Inference Chain (VIC)== framework introduces a =="thinking before looking" paradigm==, decoupling visual and textual inputs through a three-stage process.
- ==VIC Generation== involves a language model creating a sequence of "visual inference steps" (a reasoning chain) solely from the textual question before any image is processed.
- ==VIC Rationale Extraction== uses an MLLM to interact with the image, but its attention is guided by the pre-generated inference steps to extract relevant visual rationales; Answer Inference then synthesizes information with a ==reflection prompt== to ensure coherence and correctness.

## Results

- VIC consistently improved performance on hallucination detection benchmarks, reducing hallucinations by an average of **15.62%** on MMVP and showing notable gains on HallusionBench and POPE adversarial samples.
- The framework enhanced general multimodal capabilities, improving MME performance by an average of **9.13%** and SEED-Bench scores across most subtasks, particularly for complex reasoning.
- VIC demonstrated strong effectiveness with closed-source models (GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro/Flash) but showed performance declines with open-source models like Qwen-VL Plus, indicating current limitations of open-source models in handling multi-modal long-context processing.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling reasoning from immediate visual input, as in the "thinking before looking" paradigm, reduces cross-modal biases and stereotypical reasoning in MLLMs.
> - A multi-stage process involving preliminary textual reasoning, guided visual rationale extraction, and a final reflective inference significantly mitigates visual hallucination.
> - Including a reflection step in the final answer generation helps MLLMs reconsider and re-evaluate extracted rationales, leading to more robust and accurate responses.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a significant challenge in the rapidly evolving field of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs): **hallucination**, particularly in the context of visual-language understanding and reasoning.

The advent of LLMs, such as GPT-4 and Llama, has revolutionized natural language processing by demonstrating remarkable capabilities in world comprehension and complex reasoning, often enhanced by prompting techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT). CoT prompting guides LLMs to break down complex tasks into intermediate, human-like reasoning steps, improving their performance on intricate problems.

However, the integration of visual modalities into LLMs to create MLLMs (e.g., GPT-4o, Gemini) introduces new complexities. While MLLMs have shown exceptional performance across vision-related tasks (e.g., visual question answering, object recognition), they are prone to various forms of hallucination. These hallucinations include generating non-existent objects, misinterpreting visual information, and exhibiting cross-modality biases. Current multimodal CoT (MMCoT) approaches largely operate under a "thinking while looking" paradigm, where reasoning steps are generated concurrently with the visual input. The authors identify this paradigm as a key contributor to heightened hallucination risks, as the immediate visual input can trigger stereotypical, biased reasoning based on prior training data, leading the model to "see" things that aren't there or misinterpret subtle visual cues.

This paper proposes a fundamental shift in this paradigm to "thinking before looking," aligning with human cognitive processes where initial reasoning or planning often precedes detailed perceptual analysis. By doing so, it attempts to mitigate the "tight interweaving of textual and visual inputs" that leads to biased reasoning and hallucinations in existing MMCoT methods. This work positions itself as a crucial advancement in making MLLMs more reliable and accurate, moving beyond merely extending unimodal CoT to multimodal contexts, and instead, fundamentally re-architecting how visual and textual information are processed during complex reasoning. It contributes to the ongoing efforts in the AI community to enhance the trustworthiness and groundedness of advanced AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **mitigate visual hallucination in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs)** during complex reasoning tasks, thereby improving their overall accuracy and reliability.

The core motivation stems from the observation that current multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) approaches, which follow a "thinking while looking" paradigm (generating reasoning steps while simultaneously processing visual input), are highly susceptible to hallucinations. This susceptibility arises because the direct and simultaneous integration of visual and textual inputs can lead to:
1. **Cross-modal biases:** The model's reasoning becomes skewed by misleading visual cues or stereotypes learned from training data.
2. **Stereotypical reasoning:** MLLMs tend to recall similar prior contexts and overlook subtle variations in images, leading to erroneous conclusions.
3. **Undermined reliability:** Hallucinations compromise both the integrity of the reasoning process and the accuracy of the final responses, hindering the practical deployment of MLLMs in critical applications.

To address these limitations, the authors are motivated to explore a novel cognitive paradigm: **"thinking before looking."** This approach aims to:
- **Decouple visual and textual inputs:** By generating an initial reasoning chain based solely on textual context (the question), the model can leverage its extensive accumulated knowledge and forward-looking reasoning capabilities from its language model backbone without immediate visual bias.
- **Reduce cross-modal biases:** By establishing a textual reasoning trajectory first, the subsequent visual analysis is guided by a more objective and contextually relevant plan, minimizing the direct impact of misleading visual elements.
- **Enhance reasoning accuracy:** A more structured, less biased reasoning process is expected to lead to more accurate and factually grounded responses.

In essence, the paper aims to develop a framework that allows MLLMs to anticipate and recognize patterns more efficiently and dynamically adjust reasoning steps, mirroring human cognitive processes where a preliminary plan is often formed before detailed perception, ultimately leading to more robust and less hallucinatory multimodal reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose the **Visual Inference Chain (VIC)** framework, a novel "thinking before looking" approach designed to decouple visual and textual inputs during MLLM reasoning, thereby mitigating hallucinations. The methodology comprises three distinct stages:

#### 3.1. VIC Generation (Thinking Before Looking Phase)

This is the foundational step where the reasoning chain is constructed *before* any visual input is introduced.
- **Input:** The textual question (Q) and a specific VIC prompt (P_vic) are provided to a language model (f). The model `f` can be either a pure Large Language Model (LLM) or a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) operating in a "blind mode" (without image input).
- **Process:** The model generates a sequence of "visual inference steps" denoted as `{s_n}^k_{n=1}`. These steps serve as a forward-looking reasoning strategy derived purely from the textual question. This leverages the LLM's vast accumulated knowledge and pattern recognition abilities to create a preliminary plan for image analysis.
- **Output:** The generated visual inference chain `{s_n}`. The initial `k-1` steps are recognition and reasoning instructions, while the final step, `s_k`, is a format instruction for the eventual answer. This decoupling helps discretize reasoning steps from specific visual inputs, reducing immediate visual bias and potential hallucinations.

#### 3.2. VIC Rationale Extraction

In this stage, the MLLM interacts with the image, but its attention and information extraction are guided by the pre-generated VIC.
- **Input:** The image (I), an extraction-specific prompt (P_extract), and the first `k-1` visual inference steps from the VIC generation phase (`{s_n}^{k-1}_{n=1}`).
- **Process:** An MLLM (f') is employed to process the image, following the detailed instructions provided by the visual inference chain steps. This guided extraction process aims to mitigate the effects of textual bias from the original question, forcing the model to focus on relevant visual information as dictated by the pre-established reasoning steps.
- **Output:** A set of rationales, `R`, which are detailed textual descriptions extracted from the image based on the VIC instructions. The authors explore both single-step and multi-step rationale extraction, finding single-step to be generally more stable and efficient.

#### 3.3. Answer Inference

The final stage synthesizes all information to produce the answer, with a crucial reflective mechanism.
- **Input:** The original question (Q), the image (I), the extracted VIC rationales (R), the final format instruction from the VIC (`s_k`), and a reflection prompt (P_reflect).
- **Process:** The MLLM (f') leverages all these inputs. The reflection prompt is critical here; it encourages the model to reconsider and potentially re-evaluate the correctness of the VIC rationales in light of the original question and image, rather than blindly accepting the rationales as definitive. This adds a layer of robustness.
- **Output:** The final answer (A) to the visual question.

In essence, VIC transforms the "thinking while looking" approach into a structured, three-phase process where conceptual reasoning precedes detailed visual processing, and a final reflective step ensures coherence. This mirrors a more deliberate human problem-solving strategy, leveraging the strengths of LLMs for abstract reasoning and MLLMs for grounded visual understanding, while carefully managing the interaction between modalities to prevent hallucinatory outputs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted comprehensive evaluations of the Visual Inference Chain (VIC) framework across a diverse set of six benchmark datasets and four popular closed-source MLLMs (GPT-4o mini, Gemini 1.5 Flash, GPT-4o, and Gemini 1.5 Pro), comparing VIC against direct model application ("Origin") and zero-shot Chain-of-Thought (CoT).

#### 4.1. Performance on Hallucination Detection Benchmarks

- **MMVP (Multimodal VQA Problems):** VIC significantly reduced hallucinations related to CLIP-based vision encoders. The average improvement was **15.62%**, with Gemini 1.5 Pro seeing a substantial **31.74%** gain and GPT-4o mini an **16.59%** increase. This highlights VIC's effectiveness in overcoming subtle visual misinterpretations.
- **HallusionBench:** VIC showed notable improvements in both Question Pair Accuracy and Figure Accuracy, indicating mitigation of both language hallucinations and visual illusions. Crucially, "Hard Accuracy" (interpreting human-edited images) also improved. Compared to zero-shot CoT, VIC demonstrated significant gains in figure understanding, reduction of illusion errors, and enhanced map identification, suggesting improved robustness against anti-hallucination tasks.
- **POPE (Polling-based Object Presence Evaluation) adversarial samples:** VIC effectively handled these challenging scenarios, improving models' performance by an average of **1.36%**. While zero-shot CoT sometimes exhibited higher precision, VIC maintained a better balance between precision and recall, leading to more reliable overall performance.

#### 4.2. Performance on General Multimodal Capability Benchmarks

- **MME (Multimodal Evaluation):** Across both perception and cognition subtasks, VIC consistently enhanced performance, showing an average improvement of **9.13%** over baseline methods. It demonstrated significant advantages in tasks requiring reasoning such as existence, position, landmark recognition, artwork analysis, numerical calculation, text translation, and code reasoning. Tasks not requiring advanced reasoning (e.g., OCR, color recognition) showed less improvement or similar performance to baselines.
- **SEED-Bench:** Similar to MME, VIC consistently outperformed baselines across all models and most subtasks, particularly excelling in complex reasoning tasks like instance location and spatial relations.
- **MathVista:** While showing an average gain of **3.15%**, VIC's performance was less dramatic here. The authors attribute this to the benchmark's nature, where many questions lack detailed textual explanations and rely heavily on direct visual interpretation (e.g., "Is R0 greater than 0?"), which limits the utility of the "thinking before looking" paradigm when rich textual context is absent.

#### 4.3. Ablation Experiments

- **Different VIC Generators:** The study found that both pure language models (e.g., GPT-4 Turbo, Qwen-Max) and multimodal models operating in blind mode (e.g., GPT-4o mini, Gemini 1.5 Flash) could serve as effective VIC generators. There was no single consistently superior generator; effectiveness varied based on the specific benchmark, task complexity, and compatibility with the target MLLM. This suggests flexibility in generator choice.
- **Single-step vs. Multi-step Rationale Extraction:** The single-step approach for VIC rationale extraction proved more stable and efficient, reducing latency and computational cost without significant performance compromise. While multi-step offered slight advantages in some detail-oriented tasks like HallusionBench, single-step generally provided greater consistency and avoided error propagation.
- **Reflection Prompt:** Including a reflection prompt in the final answer inference stage proved beneficial, encouraging the MLLM to re-evaluate the VIC rationales. Removing it led to a slight performance decrease (e.g., 0.3% on SEED-Bench for Gemini 1.5 Flash), confirming its role in enhancing robustness and accuracy by promoting careful reassessment.
- **Open-Source Models (Qwen-VL Plus):** The VIC framework generally *declined* performance on Qwen-VL Plus across several benchmarks. The authors attribute this to open-source models' limitations in handling multi-modal long-context processing and multi-task execution, capabilities critical for the VIC framework's effectiveness. This highlights that VIC's full potential is currently best realized with robust closed-source models.

**Overall, VIC consistently improved zero-shot performance across various vision-related tasks, demonstrating its effectiveness in mitigating hallucinations and refining the reasoning capabilities of MLLMs, particularly for powerful closed-source models.**

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Visual Inference Chain (VIC) framework represents a significant conceptual and practical advancement in multimodal AI, particularly in addressing the pervasive challenge of hallucination in MLLMs.

**Key Significance:**
1. **Novel Paradigm Shift:** The most significant contribution is the formalization and empirical validation of the "thinking before looking" paradigm. This fundamentally challenges the prevailing "thinking while looking" approach in multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. By demonstrating that generating a reasoning chain solely from textual context *before* engaging with visual input can significantly reduce cross-modal biases and hallucinations, the paper opens a new avenue for research into MLLM cognitive architectures.
2. **Hallucination Mitigation:** VIC offers a robust and effective training-free method for mitigating visual hallucinations. The consistent and substantial performance gains on hallucination-specific benchmarks (MMVP, HallusionBench, POPE) directly address one of the most critical issues hindering the reliability and trustworthiness of MLLMs for real-world applications.
3. **Enhanced Reasoning Robustness:** Beyond just reducing hallucination, VIC demonstrably improves the general reasoning capabilities of MLLMs across diverse tasks (MME, SEED-Bench, MathVista). This suggests that a more structured, de-biased information processing flow leads to more accurate and reliable multimodal understanding.
4. **Cognitive Alignment:** By drawing parallels to human cognition, where initial planning often precedes detailed perception, VIC provides a biologically plausible and intuitively appealing framework for AI reasoning. This alignment could inspire future research into more human-like AI cognitive processes.
5. **Practical Applicability:** The method's zero-shot nature and its effectiveness with powerful closed-source models (GPT-4o, Gemini series) mean it can be readily applied to existing MLLMs without requiring re-training or fine-tuning, offering immediate practical benefits for developers and users. The reflection mechanism further bolsters its real-world utility by allowing for self-correction.

**Potential Impact:**
- **More Reliable MLLMs:** VIC can lead to MLLMs that are less prone to factual errors arising from visual misinterpretations, making them more dependable for critical applications such as medical image analysis, autonomous driving, surveillance, and legal document review with visual aids.
- **Improved Human-AI Collaboration:** As MLLMs become more reliable, human users will trust their outputs more, fostering better collaboration in complex problem-solving scenarios where both visual and textual information are crucial.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** The "thinking before looking" paradigm provides a strong foundation for future work. The authors themselves suggest combining this with "thinking while looking" through a selection mechanism or mutual reflection, hinting at a new generation of hybrid reasoning strategies. This could lead to more sophisticated multi-stage reasoning pipelines for MLLMs.
- **Benchmarking and Evaluation:** The findings regarding the limitations with open-source models (e.g., Qwen-VL Plus) also provide valuable insights for future MLLM development, highlighting the need for enhanced long-context and multi-task handling capabilities in open-source architectures to fully leverage such advanced reasoning frameworks.

While the paper acknowledges limitations (e.g., MathVista's direct visual dependency, open-source model struggles), the Visual Inference Chain represents a significant step towards creating more robust, accurate, and trustworthy multimodal AI systems, profoundly impacting their deployment and capabilities in diverse real-world scenarios.
