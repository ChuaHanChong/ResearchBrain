# Grounded Reinforcement Learning for Visual Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> ViGoRL introduces a method that enhances visual reasoning in Vision-Language Models by explicitly anchoring each reasoning step to specific image coordinates, leading to more structured problem-solving and improved performance across various fine-grained visual tasks. This approach enables models to dynamically refine visual attention, outperforming proprietary models on some benchmarks and fostering human-like cognitive behaviors.

## Problem

- Existing state-of-the-art Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often operate end-to-end, producing answers without exposing intermediate, interpretable reasoning steps or adapting their strategies to task specifics.
- Standard Reinforcement Learning (RL) applications to VLMs frequently result in abstract, ungrounded reasoning, failing to effectively leverage fine-grained visual evidence.
- Current prompt-based methods for VLM interpretability often rely on fixed, hand-crafted prompts, limiting their dynamic adaptability for complex visual reasoning tasks.

## Method

- ViGoRL models reasoning as explicit ==`⟨textual thought, (x, y) coordinate⟩` pairs==, systematically anchoring abstract thoughts to concrete spatial evidence in the image.
- A ==two-stage pipeline== first warm-starts the VLM using ==Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS)== to generate diverse, visually-grounded reasoning traces for SFT, followed by RL (GRPO) to refine these behaviors with task and grounding-specific rewards.
- A novel ==multi-turn RL framework== incorporates an 'interactive microscope,' allowing the model to dynamically request and process high-resolution crops of predicted coordinates for fine-grained visual information.

## Results

- ViGoRL achieved substantial accuracy gains on spatial reasoning tasks (e.g., **12.9** percentage points over vanilla GRPO on SAT-2) and competitive performance on web grounding benchmarks, even outperforming larger proprietary models on fine-grained visual search.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical necessity of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), the MCTS-generated warm-start, and explicit visual grounding for performance improvements and for inducing desired reasoning behaviors.
- Behavioral analysis showed ViGoRL amplified visual cognitive behaviors like region exploration, grounded subgoal setting, and uniquely enabled visual backtracking, leading to human-like interpretability with **72.8%** accurate coordinate references.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly anchoring reasoning steps to image coordinates forces VLMs to engage in a more structured, human-aligned form of cognition, significantly enhancing both visual perception and reasoning capabilities.
> - Combining MCTS-driven warm-starting with RL can effectively induce complex visual cognitive behaviors in VLMs, such as region exploration, grounded subgoal setting, and visual backtracking, which are largely absent in ungrounded models.
> - Dynamic, multi-turn visual feedback, mimicking human visual exploration, provides a powerful mechanism for models to gather and utilize fine-grained visual information, leading to superior performance on detail-oriented tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has advanced significantly, demonstrating capabilities in multimodal tasks through large-scale pretraining. However, existing state-of-the-art VLMs often operate in an end-to-end fashion, directly predicting answers without exposing intermediate reasoning steps beyond basic attention maps. This limits their adaptability to diverse visual reasoning tasks and their interpretability.

Previous work has attempted to introduce structure into VLM reasoning through prompt-based methods, such as chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting in LLMs and its multimodal extensions (e.g., V*, VisualProg, ViperGPT). While these methods decompose tasks into subgoals, they typically generate fixed reasoning chains that do not adapt to the specific visual input, and their reasoning often remains abstract and ungrounded in specific image regions.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has been successfully applied to reasoning chains in text-based domains (e.g., mathematics and coding) to enable models to learn diverse, context-specific reasoning strategies. However, studies have indicated that RL primarily amplifies existing capabilities within a base model rather than inducing entirely new cognitive behaviors. When applied directly to VLMs, prior research suggests that standard RL fine-tuning often leads to abstract, ungrounded reasoning, failing to foster rich, visually-anchored cognitive behaviors like visual search, verification, or backtracking. This observation aligns with findings that explicitly prompting VLMs to reference spatial locations can improve performance and interpretability.

This paper addresses a critical gap: how to embed useful, visually grounded cognitive behaviors into VLMs *before* applying RL, to enable robust visual reasoning. It proposes that explicitly anchoring textual reasoning steps to specific image regions, analogous to human visual decision-making processes, can serve as a foundational cognitive behavior that enhances a VLM's ability to "see better" and "think better." This work moves beyond mere prompt-based reasoning by proposing a learning framework that composes spatially anchored reasoning steps, thereby inducing structured region-level behaviors that support improved generalization in visual tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a vision-language model, named ViGoRL (Visually Grounded Reinforcement Learning), that can perform visual reasoning by explicitly anchoring each reasoning step to specific visual coordinates within an image. This approach aims to imbue VLMs with robust, interpretable, and general-purpose visual reasoning capabilities.

The motivation for this work stems from several observed limitations in current VLMs and their training paradigms:
1. **Lack of Grounded Reasoning:** Current VLMs often fail to reference fine-grained image inputs, leading to abstract and ungrounded reasoning. They tend to treat visual information as static context rather than actively referenced input, spending tokens on abstract thought instead of analyzing specific visual regions. For instance, an analysis of Qwen2.5-VL-3B indicated minimal region exploration and visual verification, with no observed backtracking.
2. **Ineffectiveness of Standard RL:** While RL on chains of thought has benefited LLMs, applying standard RL optimization to VLMs with task-level rewards (e.g., final answer correctness) often exacerbates ungrounded reasoning. This leads to models optimizing for immediate rewards through shortcuts, rather than developing richer visual reasoning skills like subgoal setting or backtracking, which are crucial for complex visual tasks. This suggests that RL alone cannot spontaneously discover systematic, region-level visual strategies if the base model's sampling distribution biases it towards abstract, language-based strategies.
3. **Human-Inspired Cognition:** The hypothesis is that models will "see better" and "think better" when their textual reasoning steps are explicitly grounded in specific image regions. This is inspired by human cognition, where gaze shifts and selective attention are fundamental to gathering and integrating task-relevant information during reasoning. Grounding in models is proposed to function as a spatial attention mechanism, supporting accurate feature binding and deictic reference to simplify multi-step reasoning through localized perceptual anchoring.
4. **Addressing Fine-Grained Visual Challenges:** Tasks involving small or subtle visual elements (e.g., small GUI elements on a high-resolution screen) are particularly challenging for models that process globally resized images. The ability to dynamically zoom into predicted coordinates to gather fine-grained visual information is a key motivation to improve performance in such scenarios.

Therefore, the key objective is to address these limitations by developing a multi-turn RL framework that explicitly trains VLMs to:
- Produce spatially grounded reasoning traces, where each natural language thought is paired with a specific (x,y) location in the image.
- Dynamically guide visual attention to task-relevant regions at each step.
- Iteratively request zoomed-in views of selected regions (via a novel multi-turn RL framework) when fine-grained visual information is necessary.
- Develop robust cognitive behaviors such as region exploration, grounded subgoal setting, visual verification, and backtracking, even without explicit human-provided grounding supervision.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ViGoRL employs a multi-turn Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework that trains Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to reason in a grounded, visually-aware manner. This approach contrasts with typical LLM reasoning, where explicit grounding in external input is not strictly required. The methodology consists of a two-stage pipeline: a warm-start supervised fine-tuning (SFT) phase, followed by a reinforcement learning phase, extended with a multi-turn RL formulation for visual feedback.

**Core Concept: Grounded Reasoning Steps**
The central modification is redefining each reasoning step ($n_t$) as a tuple: $n_t = \langle s_t, (x_t, y_t) \rangle$, where $s_t$ is a textual thought and $(x_t, y_t)$ is the corresponding spatial anchor in the image. A full reasoning trajectory ($\tau$) thus becomes $\tau = [n_1, \dots, n_T, a]$, where $a$ is the final answer. This explicit grounding constrains the model to systematically reference specific image locations as evidence for its reasoning, encouraging iterative exploration, verification, and subgoal formulation.

#### 3.1. Warm-Start Data Generation via Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS)

To address the VLM's initial bias towards abstract, ungrounded reasoning and inject structured, region-level strategies, a warm-start dataset is generated using MCTS with visual grounding.
- **MCTS with Visual Grounding:** MCTS is used to systematically construct diverse, visually-grounded reasoning traces. Each node in the MCTS tree represents a reasoning step $\langle s_t, (x_t, y_t) \rangle$.
 - **Selection:** Nodes are traversed using an Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) policy, prioritizing paths with high estimated value and exploration potential.
 - **Expansion:** A VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-72B acts as a high-capacity teacher) is prompted to sample new grounded steps $\langle s_t, (x_t, y_t) \rangle$ for unexplored nodes, each referencing a distinct image region. Alternatively, it can emit a candidate answer.
 - **Simulation (Rollout):** From expanded nodes, the VLM generates reasoning steps until a terminal answer is produced.
 - **Backpropagation:** A judge scores terminal answers for correctness, and these rewards are propagated up the path, updating node values.
- **Rationale for MCTS:** MCTS enables systematic exploration of the space of grounded reasoning steps, generating richly annotated paths (approximately 30,000 traces from 1,500 prompts). These paths exhibit desired behaviors such as wide exploration (through early branching), subgoal setting, verification, and backtracking (by abandoning failing branches and revisiting alternatives), which are difficult to obtain via linear rollouts or human curation at scale.
- **Linearization for SFT:** The MCTS-constructed search trees are linearized into two types of training examples for supervised fine-tuning:
 - **Direct chains:** Successful trajectories leading directly to the correct answer.
 - **Corrected chains:** Trajectories where an initial rollout fails, triggers a "wait, that seems off" backtracking phrase, and then proceeds to a correct solution.
 These linearized traces are used to fine-tune a base VLM (denoted as $\pi_{\theta_0}$), embedding structured, region-level reasoning strategies.

#### 3.2. Reinforcement Learning with Spatially-Grounded Reasoning Steps

Although the SFT phase initializes the model with grounded reasoning, it may not be optimal for new queries. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is then applied to further refine these grounded behaviors and directly maximize task reward.
- **GRPO Application:** GRPO stabilizes policy learning for long-form trajectories by using group-normalized advantages and clipped token-level PPO-style updates. It optimizes the policy $\pi_{\theta}$ to maximize the expected return $E_{\tau \sim \pi_{\theta}}[R(\tau)]$.
- **Reward Design:** The total reward $R(\tau)$ is a weighted sum: $R(\tau) = \lambda_{fmt} r_{fmt} + \lambda_{task} r_{task}$.
 - $r_{fmt}$: Encourages valid and interpretable output format. Importantly, a +1 reward is awarded only if all coordinate references are valid, along with correct `<think>` and `<answer>` tags.
 - $r_{task}$: Captures task-specific correctness (e.g., binary for SAT-2 and OS-Atlas, decomposed for ICAL).

#### 3.3. Multi-Turn Reinforcement Learning for Visual Feedback

To address scenarios requiring fine-grained visual details (e.g., small GUI elements), a novel multi-turn RL formulation is introduced, enabling dynamic visual feedback.
- **Mechanism:** After predicting a coordinate, the model can "request" a higher-resolution crop ($o_t$) centered at that coordinate. This "interactive microscope" provides detailed visual evidence that might be blurred in the initial global view.
- **Multi-Turn Warm Start:** Single-turn MCTS-derived traces are converted into dialog-style traces for SFT. Each turn involves the model generating a textual thought, emitting a `<tool_call>` with a coordinate for a `crop` function, or providing an `<answer>`. If a `crop` call is made, the environment responds with an `<observation>` containing the zoomed-in crop, and the loop repeats.
- **Multi-Turn Reward Design:** New failure modes are addressed by a composite format reward:
 - $r_{grammar}$: A binary reward (+1) if the dialog adheres to a strict tag automaton (e.g., `<think>` → `</think>` → `<tool_call>` → `</tool_call>` → `<observation>` → `</observation>` → repeat or `<answer>` → `</answer>`).
 - $r_{div}$: A diversity bonus (+0.2) for each sufficiently distinct coordinate in tool calls (at least 10 pixels from previous calls, up to 4 times), encouraging exploration.
 The overall format reward is $r_{fmt} = r_{grammar} + r_{div}$. GRPO is then applied over these full multi-turn dialog trajectories.

**Implementation Details:**
- Training utilizes Qwen2.5-VL models (3B and 7B parameters) on 8 A100 GPUs.
- SFT involves 3 epochs, while GRPO is run for 500 rollout-update iterations.
- Decoding temperature is set to 0.5 for evaluation.
- The system builds on Llama-Factory for SFT and EasyR1 for GRPO.
- Multi-turn specific settings include a max of 5 turns, crop resizing to 384x384, and a crop size of 100x100.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ViGoRL across various benchmarks demonstrates its effectiveness in improving visual reasoning through explicit grounding and multi-turn interaction.

#### 4.1. Performance on Visual Reasoning Tasks (RQ1)

- **Spatial Reasoning:** ViGoRL-3B consistently outperformed baselines on spatial understanding benchmarks. It achieved 62.9% accuracy on SAT-2 (a +16.8 point improvement over the base model and +12.9 over Vanilla GRPO), 48.5% on BLINK, and 67.1% on RoboSpatial. The 7B parameter variant, ViGoRL-7B, further improved SAT-2 accuracy to 67.5%, and BLINK and RoboSpatial to 54.1% and 76.4% respectively, demonstrating scalability. These gains were achieved without compromising general visual-language capabilities.
- **GUI Understanding:** ViGoRL-3B surpassed the base model, SFT direct, and vanilla GRPO on vision-only web interface tasks. On ScreenSpot-Pro, which requires grounding small elements in high-resolution webpage images, accuracy improved by 21.8 points over the base model, 6.1 points over SFT direct, and 2.1 points over vanilla GRPO. ViGoRL-7B achieved 91.0% on ScreenSpot-V2 and 33.1% on ScreenSpot-Pro, outperforming comparable open-source VLMs that were trained on significantly larger GUI grounding datasets. On VisualWebArena, ViGoRL outperformed direct SFT and vanilla GRPO, and surpassed the previous state-of-the-art for its model size (ICAL), despite ICAL having access to textual HTML inputs.
- **Visual Search:** Multi-turn RL with visual feedback significantly enhanced visual search capabilities. On V*Bench, ViGoRL-7B achieved 86.4% accuracy, surpassing proprietary VLMs (e.g., GPT-4o at 66.0%) and advanced VLM tool-using pipelines (e.g., Sketchpad-GPT-4o at 80.3%). This multi-turn approach also yielded a 1.2 percentage point improvement on ScreenSpot-Pro (32.3%) and even more substantial gains (+2.4%) in low-resolution small element detection scenarios (ScreenSpot-Pro-LR), confirming the benefit of dynamic, zoomed-in visual feedback.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies (RQ2)

Ablation studies confirmed the importance of each ViGoRL component:
- **GRPO:** Removing the GRPO stage resulted in a performance drop of 4.1 points on SAT-2 and 3.5 points on BLINK, underscoring the role of RL refinement.
- **MCTS-generated Warm-Start:** Without the structured traces generated by MCTS, the model failed to produce valid outputs, indicating the necessity of scaffolded learning signals.
- **Explicit Grounding:** Disabling explicit grounding during warm-start data generation led to a 3.4-point drop on BLINK, even with MCTS and GRPO, highlighting the direct impact of spatial anchoring.
- **MCTS for Generalization:** Distilling from linear teacher rollouts (without MCTS) preserved in-distribution performance on SAT-2 but significantly degraded out-of-distribution generalization on BLINK (-2.32%), suggesting MCTS fosters more robust reasoning.
- **SFT Direct Pretraining:** Adding an SFT stage to directly predict the answer before the warm-start and GRPO phases yielded only marginal gains (<1 point), reinforcing that the grounded thought processes are the primary drivers of improvement.
- **Pointing vs. Bounding Box:** Training ViGoRL to output bounding boxes instead of fixed-size point crops for visual search showed no difference in V*Bench accuracy, suggesting that fixed crops provide sufficient context for most cases, and the model can handle larger regions through multiple overlapping crops.

#### 4.3. Amplified Visual Reasoning Behaviors (RQ3)

Behavioral analysis using GPT-4o to code VLM reasoning traces on the SAT-2 benchmark revealed substantial amplification of visual cognitive behaviors in ViGoRL:
- **Region Exploration:** ViGoRL explored 3.5 visual regions per task on average, compared to 1.44 (zero-shot) and 1.8 (Vanilla GRPO).
- **Grounded Subgoals:** It demonstrated a 15x higher rate of grounded subgoal setting (1.1 vs. 0.07 in zero-shot).
- **Visual Verification:** It exhibited a 3x higher rate of visual verification (0.39 vs. 0.14 in zero-shot).
- **Backtracking:** ViGoRL uniquely developed visual backtracking behavior (0.47 instances per task), which was entirely absent in all baselines (0.00).
These enhanced behaviors allowed a 3B parameter ViGoRL model to achieve accuracy (0.64) comparable to a 72B parameter zero-shot model (0.65). Ablation studies confirmed these benefits were directly attributable to explicit grounding incentives.

#### 4.4. Human Evaluation of Grounded Reasoning (RQ4)

A human study assessed the spatial accuracy and helpfulness of ViGoRL's grounded reasoning traces:
- **Referent Accuracy:** 72.8% of the model's predicted coordinates were judged as accurately referring to the described image region.
- **Helpfulness:** Participants rated the helpfulness of the highlighted region at 3.35 on a 5-point Likert scale. Helpfulness increased substantially when the prediction was correct (3.81) and decreased when incorrect (2.26).
These findings indicate that accurate spatial grounding improves the interpretability and usefulness of the model’s reasoning process for human users.

### 5. Significance

This research introduces Visually Grounded Reinforcement Learning (ViGoRL), a paradigm that significantly advances the capability of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in visual reasoning tasks. The core insight is that explicitly anchoring each reasoning step to specific visual coordinates transforms how VLMs process and interpret visual information. This "spatial anchoring" compels the model to engage in a more structured, human-aligned form of cognition, moving beyond abstract textual reasoning to foster iterative inspection, verification, and exploration of visual content.

The work's significance lies in several key areas:
1. **Bridging the Gap between Language and Vision:** It demonstrates a method to effectively ground abstract textual reasoning in concrete perceptual evidence, addressing a fundamental challenge in multimodal AI.
2. **Amplifying Cognitive Behaviors:** ViGoRL amplifies crucial cognitive behaviors previously absent or underdeveloped in VLMs, such as goal formulation, visual verification, and effective backtracking. These behaviors are vital for robust problem-solving in complex visual environments.
3. **Improved Generalization and Interpretability:** By fostering structured reasoning, the method leads to better generalization, particularly in out-of-distribution scenarios, and enhances model interpretability by making intermediate reasoning steps physically referable and understandable to humans.
4. **Novel Multi-Turn Interaction:** The introduction of a multi-turn RL framework with dynamic visual feedback (zooming into regions) provides a new mechanism for VLMs to interact with and resolve fine-grained visual details, mirroring human capabilities.
5. **Connection to Cognitive Science:** The findings resonate with established cognitive science theories (e.g., Yarbus, Ullman, Ballard, Treisman, Harnad) that emphasize the role of spatial attention, visual routines, and deictic reference in human problem-solving. This suggests that grounding is not merely a computational optimization but a structural inductive bias that enhances compositionality and goal-directed reasoning.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Positive Societal Impacts:**
 - **Enhanced Transparency:** By producing interpretable chains of thought linked to specific image regions, ViGoRL improves model transparency. This is critical in high-stakes domains like medical imaging, accessibility tools, assistive robotics, and scientific image analysis, where understanding the rationale behind model decisions is paramount.
 - **Improved Human-AI Collaboration:** Making the model’s internal decision process legible and actionable for human users can foster greater trust and facilitate more effective collaboration. The alignment with human intuition for solving visual subproblems could lead to more intuitive and effective AI tools.
 - **Advanced Embodied AI:** The principles of grounded reasoning could be applied to embodied agents, allowing them to reason about and interact with their physical environment in more sophisticated and explainable ways.

- **Potential Negative Impacts:**
 - **Misuse for Surveillance:** The ability of a grounded model to isolate visual elements, track reasoning over time, and justify actions could be misused for detailed surveillance or automated profiling in sensitive contexts.
 - **Over-Trust in Interpretable Outputs:** An interpretable reasoning trace might lead users to over-trust a model, even if its underlying reasoning is superficially plausible but incorrect. This risk is particularly high in visually ambiguous domains or where annotation biases exist.
 - **Amplification of Dataset Biases:** Grounding mechanisms might inadvertently focus the model's attention on biased or stereotyped regions of an image if the training data is not diverse, thereby reinforcing existing disparities.

**Mitigation Strategies:**
To address the potential negative impacts, future development could incorporate:
- **Uncertainty Estimation:** Explicitly marking when the model is uncertain about a grounding decision or reasoning step.
- **Auditing Tools:** Developing tools that allow users to audit the reasoning trace and provide corrections or feedback.
- **Data Diversity:** Continued efforts to diversify training data and evaluate model behavior across demographic and situational axes to ensure fairness.
- **Human-in-the-Loop Oversight:** Implementing human oversight, especially in sensitive deployment settings where grounded outputs inform consequential decisions.

In conclusion, this research presents visually grounded RL as a robust paradigm for imbuing models with general-purpose visual reasoning, offering a path toward more transparent, adaptable, and perceptually aligned AI systems.
