# Lifting Embodied World Models for Planning and Control

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces Lifted World Models (LWM) to improve planning and control for embodied agents with high-dimensional action spaces. The framework integrates a lightweight policy mapping 2D image-space waypoints to low-level actions, achieving a 3.8x reduction in mean joint error for goal-reaching tasks compared to direct low-level planning.

## Problem

- World models for complex, human-like embodied agents struggle with high-dimensional action spaces, which are cumbersome to control.
- Search-based planning methods like CEM are computationally expensive and scale poorly with the high dimensionality of low-level joint actions.
- Existing methods for egocentric, human-like embodiments lack efficient and tractable planning and control mechanisms due to complex low-level action requirements.

## Method

- **LWM** composes a lightweight ==goal-conditioned policy== with a frozen, pre-trained ==low-level world model==.
- A novel high-level ==2D image-space waypoint== action space encodes specific leaf joints (pelvis, hands) — low-dimensional, visually interpretable, suitable for search.
- A ==diffusion policy== translates high-level 2D waypoints into low-level joint actions, enabling efficient ==search-based planning (CEM)== in the lifted space.

## Results

- **LWM** achieved a **3.8x improvement** in mean joint error (MJE), reducing 'all MJE' from **0.704m** to **0.374m** vs. PEVA CEM's **0.616m**.
- Waypoint conditioning reduced 'all MJE' by **8.8 cm** (0.331m → 0.243m); waypoint masking further improved to **0.226m**.
- Lifted CEM outperformed direct low-level planning across varying compute budgets and goal horizons, with successful generalization to unseen environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Abstracting control to a lower-dimensional, visually intuitive waypoint space dramatically improves the efficiency and effectiveness of planning for complex embodied agents.
> - 2D image-space waypoints are an effective goal-conditioning signal for egocentric agents, outperforming high-dimensional goal observations by directly guiding relevant body parts.
> - A lightweight policy trained with waypoint masking enhances robustness, allowing the model to infer target joint positions even when some waypoints are absent or out-of-frame, and generalize to counterfactual scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

World models represent a method for understanding physical interactions by predicting future observations given current states and actions. These models have evolved from applications in constrained settings, such as simple games, to more complex and diverse environments, encompassing actions like text generation, navigation, and detailed whole-body joint control. The paper positions itself within the context of advancements in world models that are increasingly applied to richer embodiments, such as human-like agents.

A significant challenge in developing world models for these complex embodiments is the high dimensionality of their action spaces. For instance, controlling a human-like agent often requires specifying numerous per-joint parameters, which can be difficult to manage. This high dimensionality can render world models cumbersome to control and computationally expensive for planning, particularly when employing search-based methods like the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM), which exhibit poor scaling properties with increasing action dimensionality.

Existing research has explored various strategies for planning with world models, including planning in latent spaces (e.g., PlaNet, TD-MPC2), directly in pixel space (e.g., UniSim, NWM), and using gradient-based optimization methods (e.g., DINO-WM). Some approaches integrate world models for policy training (e.g., the Dreamer series), while others focus on specific embodied agents, such as whole-body conditioned human models (PEVA) or dexterous hand models (DexWM).

This work addresses a perceived gap in the research landscape: the effective control and planning for complex, egocentric, human-like embodiments where existing methods struggle with the high-dimensional action space. The authors propose a novel approach to make this task more tractable by introducing a mechanism to "lift" the abstraction level of low-level world models. This involves integrating a lightweight policy that maps low-dimensional, visually interpretable, and searchable high-level actions to the complex, high-dimensional low-level actions required by the world model. This approach contrasts with methods that rely on high-dimensional goal observations, aiming to provide a more efficient and controllable planning framework for embodied agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the controllability and planning efficiency of world models applied to embodied agents with high-dimensional action spaces. The authors identify a specific problem: for complex embodiments, such as human-like agents, actions are typically defined by a large number of joint parameters. This leads to high-dimensional action spaces, which make world models challenging to control and computationally expensive for planning using search-based techniques like CEM, as their performance degrades significantly with increased action dimensionality and sequence length.

To address this issue, the core motivation is to reduce the effective dimensionality and length of the action sequence that a planner must search over, without requiring modifications to the underlying, pre-trained low-level world model. The paper proposes to achieve this by:

1. **Introducing a "Lifted World Model" (LWM) framework**: This framework aims to predict future observations from a single, high-level action. This is achieved by composing a lightweight, goal-conditioned policy with a frozen, pre-trained low-level world model.
2. **Developing a lightweight policy**: This policy is designed to translate high-level actions into sequences of low-level, high-dimensional joint actions.
3. **Designing a novel high-level action space**: For a human-like embodiment, the high-level action space is defined using 2D waypoints. These waypoints represent 2D projections of 3D goal positions for specific "leaf" joints of the agent (e.g., pelvis, head, hands) onto the current observation frame. The choice of waypoints is motivated by several factors:
 - **Low-dimensionality**: This is critical for improving search efficiency.
 - **Visual interpretability**: Waypoints are intuitive and can be easily specified manually.
 - **Suitability for search**: Their discrete and low-dimensional nature makes them amenable to search-based planning algorithms.
 - **Biological inspiration**: The concept draws parallels to human visuomotor control, where visuospatial goals are translated into joint-level commands.

In essence, the research aims to demonstrate that by "lifting" the control abstraction, it is possible to achieve more effective and computationally efficient planning for complex embodied agents, thereby overcoming limitations posed by their inherently high-dimensional action spaces.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology centers on the development of a Lifted World Model (LWM) that integrates a high-level action-conditioned policy with an existing low-level world model.

#### 3.1. World Model Preliminaries

The base low-level world model, `f_ϕ`, predicts the next observation `o_{t+1}` given a current observation `o_t` and an action `a_t`. It can condition on `K` previous observations. For this work, the authors utilize PEVA, a world model trained on the Nymeria dataset, which predicts egocentric observations based on human joint-angle displacements.

- **Pose Representation**: A human-like embodiment is represented using the XSens model. A pose `p` consists of the 3D position of the pelvis and Euler angles for 15 joints (total 48 dimensions).
- **Action Representation**: An action `a_t` is defined as the change between two consecutive poses, `p_t` and `p_{t+1}`. This includes the change in pelvis 3D position and changes in Euler angles for all joints (also 48 dimensions).

#### 3.2. High-level Action Space: Waypoints

The high-level action `a_HL` is designed to be effective, low-dimensional, and intuitive. It is expressed as a set of waypoints annotated onto the current observation `o_t` to create an annotated observation `o_{ann_t}`.

- **Definition**: A waypoint is the 2D projection of a near-term 3D goal position for a specific leaf joint (pelvis, head, left hand, right hand) of the embodied agent. This yields a high-level action space of 8 dimensions (4 waypoints * 2D coordinates).
- **Ground-truth Waypoints**: During training, these are generated by computing 3D joint positions from a ground-truth goal pose `p_g` using forward kinematics and then projecting these 3D points onto the image plane via the camera matrix.
- **Rationale**: This design choice makes actions visually interpretable and amenable to manual specification or search-based planning, mitigating the exponential scaling issues associated with high-dimensional action spaces. This approach is contrasted with using a high-dimensional goal image `o_g` as a conditioning signal.

#### 3.3. High-level Action Conditioned Policy (`π_θ`)

A lightweight policy `π_θ` is trained to map high-level actions to sequences of low-level actions. At time `t`, the policy predicts a sequence of `T` low-level actions (`a_{LL_{t:t+T-1}}`) conditioned on context observations (`o_t`), context poses (`p_t`), and the high-level action (`a_{HL_t}`).

- **Waypoint Masking**: To enable robustness to sparse inputs and to facilitate inference of target joint positions when waypoints are absent or out-of-frame, waypoints are masked during training. For half of the training time, no waypoints are masked; for the other half, each waypoint is independently masked with a 0.5 probability.
- **Architecture**: The policy is implemented as a diffusion policy, generating actions by denoising in joint action space.
 - The annotated goal image `o_{ann_t}` and current observations `o_t` are encoded using a DINOv3-S encoder.
 - Spatial information is preserved by not pooling the tokens immediately.
 - Pose context `p_t` is linearly projected and added to the corresponding image embeddings.
 - 3D positional embeddings are added to preserve spatial and temporal ordering.
 - A vision transformer processes these tokens, which are then pooled to form a context vector `c_t`.
 - This context vector `c_t` conditions a denoising UNet, which has a larger dimension than a baseline diffusion policy.

#### 3.4. Lifted World Model (LWM)

The LWM `f_HL` is formed by composing the trained policy `π_θ` with the frozen low-level world model `f_ϕ`. The LWM predicts a future observation `o_{t+T}` given current context (`o_t, p_t`) and a single high-level action `a_{HL_t}`.

- **Internal Operation**: First, the policy `π_θ` generates a sequence of `T` low-level actions (`a_{LL_{t:t+T-1}}`). Then, the low-level world model `f_ϕ` autoregressively samples `T` subsequent observations, with each newly generated observation being appended to the context for the next step.

#### 3.5. Search-based Planning with LWM

The LWM enables search-based planning using CEM in the high-level action space.

- **Process**: Given a starting state and a goal observation `o_g`, CEM samples a batch of high-level action sequences (`a_HL`), simulates future observations using the LWM, and assigns a cost based on the perceptual similarity to `o_g`. The samples with the lowest cost are used to update the prior distribution for subsequent iterations.
- **Advantages**: This approach significantly reduces the dimensionality of the search space (8 dimensions per step for `a_HL` vs. 48 for `a_LL`) and the effective sequence length, making planning more computationally efficient and effective. The chosen high-level action is then translated into low-level joint actions by the policy for execution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted using PEVA, a world model trained on the Nymeria dataset, which comprises first-person videos from Project Aria glasses and XSens motion capture data, providing both visual observations and 3D body motion/joint angle data. The performance metric used is Mean Joint Error (MJE) in meters, calculated between the final predicted pose and the ground-truth goal pose for leaf, intermediate, and all joints.

#### 4.1. Goal-conditioned policy

The policy's ability to generate actions based on high-level goals was evaluated through quantitative ablations and qualitative visualizations.

- **Quantitative Results (Table 1)**:
 - A baseline diffusion policy (NoMaD) provided only a minor improvement in MJE (2.1 cm reduction from initial distance).
 - Conditioning on a goal observation `o_g` yielded a further small improvement (1.3 cm).
 - Incorporating architectural enhancements and pose context (`p_t`) led to improved performance across all metrics.
 - **Waypoint conditioning** demonstrated a substantial improvement, reducing "all MJE" by 8.8 cm compared to unconditioned generation (from 0.331 m to 0.243 m).
 - **Waypoint masking** further improved goal-conditioned performance (0.243 m to 0.226 m for "all MJE"), suggesting that the policy learned to infer target joint positions effectively even with incomplete information.
 - Augmenting 2D waypoints with 3D depth information for "3D conditioning" provided only a marginal improvement (at most 2 cm reduction in "all MJE"), indicating that 2D waypoints are largely sufficient.
- **Qualitative Action Generations (Figures 4, 6, 7)**:
 - The policy generated coherent action sequences for both navigation (e.g., walking through a doorway) and interaction (e.g., grasping a doorknob, raising a hand with a camera).
 - It demonstrated generalization to **counterfactual waypoints** not explicitly seen in the training data, responding sensibly to sparse waypoints (e.g., a single head waypoint guiding movement and orientation) and producing actions consistent with larger goals (e.g., climbing onto a bench).
 - The policy exhibited **contextual awareness**, generating different action sequences for identical waypoints when presented with different environmental scenes (e.g., grasping a pot in a kitchen vs. walking in an open room).
- **Waypoint Visibility (Table 2)**:
 - When a target joint was not visible in the current observation, observation-conditioned policies showed a significant MJE increase (26.5 cm).
 - In contrast, waypoint-conditioned policies exhibited a smaller increase (12.7 cm), suggesting they learn more generalized whole-body motion patterns.
 - Waypoint masking further reduced this performance drop for non-visible joints (to 8.8 cm), indicating its benefit in inferring hidden joint positions.
- **Decomposing Action Generation (Table 3)**:
 - The task of motion generation (predicting actions given a ground-truth goal pose `p_g`) resulted in a low MJE of 0.105 m.
 - However, predicting the goal pose `p_g` directly from a goal observation `o_g` resulted in a higher MJE of 0.279 m. This suggests that egocentric goal observations are often insufficient to infer the complete target body pose, as the agent's own body is frequently out of frame. This finding supports the utility of waypoints as a more direct and effective goal-conditioning signal.

#### 4.2. Lifted World Model Planning

The LWM was used for search-based planning with CEM on hybrid navigation and interaction tasks from the Nymeria dataset.

- **Quantitative Results (Table 4)**:
 - Direct planning in low-level joint space using PEVA CEM reduced "all MJE" by 8.8 cm (from an initial 0.704 m to 0.616 m).
 - **Lifted CEM (using 2D waypoints)** achieved a significantly greater reduction, bringing "all MJE" down by 33 cm (to 0.374 m), representing a **3.8x improvement** over PEVA CEM.
 - A policy conditioned only on a goal image `o_g` performed worse than Lifted CEM.
 - Lifted CEM with 3D waypoints yielded an "all MJE" of 0.420 m, performing worse than its 2D counterpart.
- **Qualitative Planning Visualizations (Figure 8, Appendix F)**:
 - Planning with Lifted CEM effectively reproduced ground-truth actions, guiding the agent to desired goal states (e.g., navigating to the right, setting down a bag).
 - The policy integrated within the LWM appeared to generate more natural and plausible body movements compared to direct PEVA CEM planning, which sometimes produced unnatural joint angles.
- **Planning Efficiency (Figure 9)**:
 - Lifted CEM consistently outperformed PEVA CEM across varying computational budgets (different numbers of CEM iterations and samples per iteration).
 - PEVA CEM's MJE sometimes worsened after the initial step, indicating challenges with naive search in high-dimensional spaces.
 - Lifted CEM with 3D waypoints lagged behind 2D waypoints, likely due to the increased action dimensionality (12 vs. 8). This suggests that lifting not only yields better solutions but also does so with less computational cost.
- **Varying Goal Horizons (Figure 10)**: Lifted CEM maintained superior performance over PEVA CEM even when tasks involved different goal horizons, despite the fixed short sequence length of the underlying policy.
- **Varying Initial MJE (Figure 11)**: Lifted CEM generally outperformed PEVA CEM across a spectrum of initial MJE distances, performing less effectively only for very small initial distances (≤ 0.3 m).
- **Unseen Environments (Table 5)**: To assess generalization, a policy was trained on a dataset with specific environments held out. When used to lift the world model, this policy still enabled Lifted CEM to outperform PEVA CEM on tasks within those held-out environments. The performance drop compared to a policy trained on all environments was minor, indicating good generalization capabilities of the lightweight policy design.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces a framework for "lifting" low-level world models to a higher level of abstraction, addressing a critical challenge in controlling and planning for embodied agents with high-dimensional action spaces. The primary significance lies in demonstrating a viable method to enhance the tractability of planning for complex agents without modifying the underlying low-level world model.

Key contributions and potential impacts include:

- **Improved Planning Performance and Efficiency**: The Lifted World Model (LWM) with 2D waypoints achieved a substantial 3.8x improvement in mean joint error for goal-reaching tasks compared to direct planning in low-level joint space using PEVA CEM. This was accomplished with reduced computational cost, making complex planning more feasible.
- **Effective High-level Action Space**: The introduction of 2D image-space waypoints for leaf joints is shown to be an effective, low-dimensional, visually interpretable, and searchable goal-conditioning signal for egocentric embodied agents. This contrasts with high-dimensional goal observations, which were found to be less informative for inferring full body poses.
- **Generalization to Unseen Environments**: The lightweight policy, which translates high-level waypoints into low-level actions, demonstrated robust generalization to environments not included in its training data. This suggests the approach is scalable and potentially applicable to novel settings.
- **Lightweight and Scalable Design**: The methodology requires no additional data collection or annotation beyond what is typically used for training the base world model. This characteristic renders the approach resource-efficient and suitable for scaling to richer embodiments and more complex tasks in the future.
- **Enabling Richer Embodiments**: By simplifying the control interface, this work contributes to making world models more controllable and plannable for increasingly complex and realistic embodied agents, fostering advancements in areas like human-robot interaction and realistic character control.

Limitations identified include the current inability to explicitly control head orientation and the constraint that waypoints cannot specify goal poses entirely outside the current observation frame. However, the authors suggest these are areas for future exploration as world models continue to improve in consistency and generalization.

In conclusion, this research provides a practical and effective strategy for bridging the gap between high-dimensional, low-level world model dynamics and efficient, high-level planning. The "lifting" paradigm presents a promising direction for future work in embodied AI, particularly as models scale in complexity and demand more intuitive and efficient control mechanisms.
