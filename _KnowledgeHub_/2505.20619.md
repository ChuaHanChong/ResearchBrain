# Gait-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning with Multi-Phase Curriculum for Humanoid Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> University College London researchers present a unified, reference-free reinforcement learning framework that enables humanoid robots to perform diverse locomotion behaviors, including standing, walking, running, and smooth transitions, using a single recurrent policy. This approach successfully transferred to a Unitree G1 robot without real-world fine-tuning, demonstrating stable and natural movements.

## Problem

- Reference-based locomotion methods are constrained by the availability and quality of motion capture data, often leading to morphological mismatches and limited interpretability.
- Integrating multiple distinct gaits (e.g., standing, walking, running) into a single controller is complex due to reward interference and architectural overhead, typically requiring separate expert policies or intricate switching mechanisms.
- Reference-free reinforcement learning often struggles to naturally enforce human-like biomechanical traits crucial for balance and energy efficiency without meticulous reward tuning.

## Method

- A unified, recurrent actor-critic policy based on LSTMs is developed to control standing, walking, running, and transitions, conditioned by a one-hot gait ID.
- A gait-conditioned reward routing mechanism dynamically activates only relevant reward terms for the current locomotion mode, mitigating interference between conflicting objectives.
- A multi-phase curriculum progressively introduces locomotion complexity, starting with walking, then adding standing/transitions, and finally running/transitions, to facilitate stable skill acquisition.

## Results

- The learned policy accurately tracks commanded velocities up to 3 m/s and performs stable, smooth transitions between standing, walking, running, and their intermediate states in simulation.
- Analysis confirms that the policy maintains low total angular momentum in the Z-axis (yaw) through coordinated anti-phase motions of the arms and legs, reflecting human-like balance mechanisms.
- The policy, trained solely in simulation, successfully transferred to a physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot, demonstrating stable standing, walking, and smooth walk-to-stand transitions on real hardware without any fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically routing rewards based on the current gait mode is critical for enabling a single policy to effectively co-learn and coordinate diverse and potentially conflicting locomotion behaviors.
> - A biologically inspired, multi-phase curriculum is essential for the stable acquisition of complex, multi-gait skills, preventing convergence failures seen in simultaneous multi-gait training.
> - Integrating biomechanically grounded reward terms, such as those encouraging straight-knee support and anti-phase arm-leg coordination, can effectively promote natural, dynamically stable, and efficient motions without requiring external motion capture data.
