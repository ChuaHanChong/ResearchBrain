# Towards Synergistic, Generalized, and Efficient Dual-System for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University, The University of Hong Kong, AgiBot, and Shanghai AI Lab introduce RoboDual, a dual-system framework for robotic manipulation that combines a large generalist vision-language-action model with an efficient specialist diffusion transformer. This approach enables broader generalization and more precise, real-time control by overcoming the inference latency and adaptation costs of large models, achieving a 13.2% higher success rate on complex language-conditioned tasks and a 15 Hz real-world control frequency.

## Problem

- Specialist robot policies are precise and efficient for specific tasks but lack generalization ability, requiring extensive retraining for new scenarios.
- Generalist robot policies, while broadly generalizable from vast datasets, suffer from high inference latency, expensive adaptation to new embodiments, and difficulty integrating diverse sensory inputs for real-time fine-grained control.
- No single existing policy effectively balances broad versatility, efficient real-time inference, and precise control for robotic manipulation in dynamic, open-ended environments.

## Method

- RoboDual proposes a synergistic dual-system framework, analogous to System-1/System-2 thinking, integrating a high-level generalist with a low-level specialist.
- The generalist, based on a LoRA-tuned 7B-parameter OpenVLA, provides slow, deliberate high-level guidance via discretized action chunks and latent representations.
- The specialist, built on a ==Diffusion Transformer==, uses these generalist outputs along with multimodal sensory inputs (RGB, depth, tactile) for agile, precise, and fast (15 Hz) real-time control, trained with a latency-aware mechanism.

## Results

- RoboDual achieved a **13.2%** increase in success rate for 5 consecutive tasks and an average task length of **3.66** on the CALVIN ABC→D benchmark, showing robustness to free-form language instructions.
- In real-world ALOHA platform experiments, it demonstrated a **+20%** average improvement over competitive baselines across single and multi-instruction tasks, achieving **93.3%** success in position generalization for single tasks.
- The specialist training required only 8 GPU-hours to achieve **+12%** performance gain on CALVIN, and RoboDual achieved a real-world control frequency of 15 Hz, a substantial improvement over OpenVLA's **3.9** Hz.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A synergistic dual-system architecture combining a slow, high-level generalist (VLA model) with a fast, low-level specialist (Diffusion Transformer) can overcome the individual limitations of each, yielding both broad generalizability and precise, efficient control.
> - Training a lightweight specialist policy with generalist guidance and multimodal inputs is remarkably data and computationally efficient, enabling substantial performance gains with minimal resources compared to fine-tuning large generalist models alone.
> - Integrating a shifted-window mechanism and latency-aware training allows the asynchronous generalist and specialist components to operate effectively in real-time, significantly improving control frequency for dexterous manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape: Generalists vs. Specialists in Robotic Manipulation

The field of robotic manipulation is currently grappling with a fundamental tension between two prevailing paradigms: "specialist" and "generalist" policies.

**Specialist policies** represent the traditional approach in robot learning. These policies are typically trained on datasets meticulously curated for a specific robot and a narrowly defined task. Examples include popular methods like Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT) and Diffusion Policy. Specialists excel in precision and efficiency within their designated scenarios, delivering high accuracy for well-defined tasks. However, their primary limitation is a severe lack of generalization ability. They often fail when deployed in environments or on tasks even slightly different from their training data, necessitating extensive retraining for new applications.

In contrast, the recent surge in **generalist policies** aims to address this generalization bottleneck. Inspired by the success of large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs), these approaches leverage vast, heterogeneous datasets, often collected across multiple robot embodiments (e.g., Open-X-Embodiment dataset). Projects like RT-2, Octo, and OpenVLA exemplify this trend, aspiring to transfer "web knowledge" to robotic control. Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, in particular, integrate pre-trained VLMs to facilitate capabilities like common-sense reasoning and instruction following, enabling robots to interpret natural language commands and adapt to diverse scenarios.

Despite their promising generalizability, VLA-based generalists face significant practical limitations:
1. **Inefficient Inference:** Their large model size (often billions of parameters) leads to high computational demands and considerable inference latency. This makes them unsuitable for real-time, fine-grained control required in dynamic manipulation tasks.
2. **Costly Adaptation:** While generalizable, they cannot be deployed entirely "out-of-the-box" in novel environments or on new robot embodiments without adaptation. This fine-tuning process is data-intensive and computationally expensive, negating some of the benefits of broad pre-training.
3. **Limited Modality Integration:** Current generalist models primarily rely on single-frame RGB observations. Integrating additional sensory inputs crucial for manipulation, such as depth or tactile feedback, often requires resource-intensive retraining and risks "catastrophic forgetting" of previously acquired knowledge.

Another related research direction involves **hierarchical control with LLMs**. Here, LLMs are used for high-level task planning, decomposing complex instructions into sub-tasks (e.g., SayCan, PaLM-E). Some approaches use action decoders on LLM latent outputs, or action tokenization (e.g., VQVAE) to bridge LLMs with low-level actions. While RoboDual shares a hierarchical philosophy, it distinguishes itself by not explicitly decomposing tasks or requiring end-to-end optimization of decoupled policies. Instead, it focuses on using discretized actions and latent embeddings as flexible "interfaces" between the two systems.

RoboDual's contribution to this landscape is the proposal of a **synergistic dual-system framework**. This work posits that relying solely on enhancing generalists might not be sufficient. Instead, it seeks to combine the best of both worlds: the broad understanding and generalization of a generalist with the precision, efficiency, and adaptability of a specialist. By drawing an analogy to System-1 (fast, automatic) and System-2 (slow, deliberate) thinking from cognitive science, RoboDual grounds its architectural design in a widely recognized model of intelligence, aiming for a more robust and practical solution for robotic manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind RoboDual stems from the observed limitations of both standalone generalist and specialist policies in robotic manipulation. The authors identify a critical gap: no single policy type adequately addresses the simultaneous demands for broad versatility, efficient inference, and precise, real-time control in dynamic environments.

The **generalist policy**, while capable of high-level reasoning and generalization across diverse tasks and embodiments (leveraging large cross-embodiment datasets), suffers from:
- **High Inference Latency:** Its large model size leads to slow decision-making, making it impractical for fine-grained control in real-time scenarios.
- **Expensive Adaptation:** Despite pre-training, adapting generalists to new embodiments or environments still requires significant data and computational resources for fine-tuning.
- **Limited Sensory Input Integration:** Most generalists primarily use RGB observations, making it challenging to incorporate crucial additional sensory modalities (e.g., depth, tactile) without extensive and costly retraining that risks losing existing knowledge.

Conversely, the **specialist policy** excels at:
- **Efficiency and Precision:** It offers fast, accurate control for specific tasks and domains, often with fewer parameters.
- **Adaptability to Specific Data:** It is well-suited for fine-grained, task-level control using curated datasets.
However, its major drawback is a fundamental **lack of generalization capacity**, rendering it ineffective for open-ended or multi-task applications.

Recognizing these complementary strengths and weaknesses, the **key objectives** of the RoboDual project are to:
1. **Develop a Synergistic Dual-System:** Create a novel framework that intelligently integrates the generalizability of large-scale pre-trained generalist policies with the efficiency and task-specific adaptability of specialist policies.
2. **Enhance Performance and Robustness:** Achieve significantly higher success rates in a wide array of manipulation tasks, including those requiring complex multi-instruction following and broad generalization, compared to single-system baselines.
3. **Improve Inference Efficiency:** Enable real-time, low-latency control suitable for dexterous manipulation, overcoming the "pivotal bottleneck" of high inference latency in large generalist models.
4. **Boost Data Efficiency for Adaptation:** Allow the system to adapt effectively to new tasks or environments with minimal new demonstration data and reduced training costs, making deployment more practical.
5. **Facilitate Multimodal Integration:** Design the specialist policy to flexibly incorporate diverse sensory inputs (e.g., depth, tactile, multi-view RGB) without requiring resource-intensive re-training of the entire generalist model, thus enhancing effectiveness in complex manipulation tasks.
6. **Pave the Way for Practical Deployment:** Offer a cost-effective and widely adaptable solution for leveraging the power of large VLA models in real-world robotic setups.

By pursuing these objectives, RoboDual aims to move beyond incremental improvements to existing models and instead proposes a fundamentally new architectural paradigm for more capable and practical robotic intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoboDual introduces a novel synergistic dual-system framework designed to combine the strengths of large generalist policies with the efficiency and precision of specialist policies. The framework operates on a "System-1/System-2" analogy, where a slow, deliberate generalist provides high-level guidance, and a fast, reactive specialist executes fine-grained control.

#### 3.1. Generalist Policy: The High-Level Planner

The generalist component of RoboDual serves as the meticulous, robust planner. It is built upon **OpenVLA (Kim et al., 2024)**, a state-of-the-art 7B-parameter autoregressive vision-language-action model. OpenVLA's architecture is based on Prismatic-7B, incorporating a fused visual encoder (from backbones like DINOv2 and SigLIP), a projection layer to align visual embeddings with language, and a LLaMA2 large language model. It is pre-trained on a massive corpus of robotic manipulation data (e.g., Open-X-Embodiment, Bridge V2, DROID).

While OpenVLA offers broad generalizability, it requires **LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation)** fine-tuning to adapt to specific robotic setups and novel environments. Crucially, the generalist generates two types of conditioning for the specialist:
1. **Discretized Action Outputs:** Following methods like RT-2 and OpenVLA, robot actions (e.g., 7-DoF) are mapped to discrete tokens within the LLaMA tokenizer's vocabulary. The generalist auto-regressively decodes these tokens. RoboDual extends this to predict **action chunks** with a temporal length *k<sub>g</sub>*, providing longer-range planning but increasing the generalist's inference latency.
2. **Latent Representations:** High-level task and action latents are extracted from the generalist model, encapsulating its understanding of the task and planned actions.

The generalist operates at a slower frequency, providing deliberate, high-level guidance, which then supports multiple faster roll-outs of the specialist.

#### 3.2. Specialist Policy: The Agile Executor

The specialist policy is designed for precise, real-time control, efficiently leveraging both multimodal sensory inputs and the privileged knowledge from the generalist. Its architecture is primarily based on a **Diffusion Transformer (DiT)** (Peebles & Xie, 2023).

- **Base Architecture:** The specialist is composed of stacked DiT blocks, each featuring a causal self-attention layer (for temporal action processing), a cross-attention layer (for fusing conditioning information), and a point-wise feedforward network. A 7-DoF action is treated as a "pixel" with seven channels, projected into a single token, allowing for flexible action chunk prediction of temporal length *k<sub>s</sub>*.
- **Multimodal Sensory Encoders:** RoboDual uses Vision Transformers (ViT) as generalized encoders for all possible input modalities, including RGB, depth, and tactile feedback. A DINO-pretrained model encodes RGB inputs and is frozen during training for efficiency. Other modality encoders are lightweight (six layers, 256 hidden size). A **Perceiver Resampler** (Alayrac et al., 2022) distills key features from observation embeddings, reducing token length and accelerating the denoising process, especially with multiple sensory inputs.
- **Unified Conditioning Mechanism:** The specialist is conditioned on four distinct information sources:
 1. **Proprioceptive States:** Processed through an MLP and combined with a time-step embedding, enabling adaptive sample-wise conditioning using gamma (γ), beta (β) for layer normalization, and an alpha (α) scaling parameter for residual connections.
 2. **Multimodal Sensory Inputs:** Encoded by ViTs and resampled, these embeddings are concatenated with generalist latents and used as keys and values in the cross-attention layer.
 3. **Generalist's Discretized Action Outputs:** These are concatenated with the noised actions of the corresponding time step and projected into a shared latent space. This direct conditioning helps guide the specialist's action prediction.
 4. **Generalist's Latent Representations:** Task and action latents from the generalist are linearly projected to align their hidden spaces, preserving their original positional encoding. These are then combined with resampled observation embeddings for the cross-attention mechanism.
- **Shifted-Window and Latency-Aware Conditioning:** To manage the asynchronous operation of the generalist (slower) and specialist (faster), a "shifted-window" mechanism is used. After the specialist executes a certain number of steps (*τ<sub>s</sub>*), only the most recent *k<sub>g</sub> - τ<sub>s</sub>* generalist actions are retained as context. Furthermore, to enhance robustness to real-world latency variations, the specialist is trained with a **latency-aware training augmentation**. It learns to denoise future trajectories by conditioning on *delayed* generalist outputs (with a variable offset *τ*), simulating and compensating for asynchrony in deployment.

#### 3.3. Training and Inference Protocols

- **Generalist Training:** The generalist model (*g<sub>φ</sub>*) is trained with a discrete token prediction objective, minimizing the negative log-probabilities of next action tokens, consistent with decoder-only LLMs. LoRA fine-tuning is used for adaptation.
- **Specialist Training:** The specialist (*π<sub>θ</sub>*) is trained with an action denoising objective, similar to Diffusion Policy. Given a noisy action trajectory, it predicts the noise injected into ground truth actions. This training is notably lightweight, requiring only 20M trainable parameters and 8 GPU-hours to achieve significant performance improvements over a generalist-only approach. The DDIM scheduler is employed for faster inference. Classifier-free guidance is applied on generalist latents and proprioceptive states to improve controllability.
- **Inference:** In real-world deployment, the generalist produces a single action (e.g., the 8th action) and conditioning latents. The specialist then performs multiple (e.g., eight) rapid inference steps based on this guidance. This enables RoboDual to achieve a control frequency of 15 Hz in real-world setups (compared to OpenVLA's 3.9 Hz), facilitating deployment in dexterous tasks. Action outputs from the specialist are temporally aggregated using an exponential weighting scheme for smoother control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

RoboDual's experimental evaluations demonstrate its superior performance, generalizability, and efficiency across both simulation and real-world robotic tasks.

#### 4.1. Performance Comparison with State-of-the-Art

- **CALVIN Benchmark (Simulation):** RoboDual achieved significant improvements on the CALVIN ABC→D benchmark, a challenging suite for long-horizon language-conditioned manipulation tasks. It increased the average length of completed tasks from 3.27 (3D Diffuser Actor) to **3.66** and elevated the success rate for accomplishing 5 consecutive tasks by **13.2%**. Furthermore, RoboDual exhibited exceptional robustness to free-form language instructions (e.g., GPT-4 enriched prompts), showing minimal performance degradation and nearly doubling the average task length (3.47 vs. 1.78 for LCB) compared to other VLM-based baselines like RoboFlamingo and LCB. This highlights its strong semantic understanding and robustness to variations in conditioning latents.
- **Real-world Robot Experiments (ALOHA platform):** RoboDual consistently outperformed all specialist (ACT, Diffusion Policy) and generalist (Octo, OpenVLA) baselines across both single-instruction and multi-instruction tasks. On average, it showed a **+20% improvement** compared to the most competitive baseline. While Diffusion Policy struggled significantly with multi-instruction tasks (e.g., 20% success on "Put obj into basket"), RoboDual successfully leveraged the generalist's high-level reasoning to guide the specialist's precise control, achieving a minimum success rate of 60% across all evaluated tasks. The paper notes that OpenVLA's high inference latency (3.9 Hz) caused jittering and hindered performance in dexterous tasks, a limitation effectively mitigated by RoboDual.

#### 4.2. Generalizability Across Diverse Scenarios

RoboDual's generalizability was thoroughly evaluated across four axes in real-world settings: position variation, visual distractors, unseen backgrounds, and novel objects.
- RoboDual demonstrated superior performance across all generalization tasks compared to specialist and generalist-only methods. For instance, in "Put block into bowl" requiring position generalization, RoboDual significantly outperformed specialist policies (ACT 46.7%, Diffusion Policy 53.3%) and OpenVLA (26.7%), achieving **93.3%** (single-task) and **86.7%** (multi-task).
- The system also showcased **error-correction capabilities**, such as re-grasping a block after an initial failed attempt, indicating robust low-level adaptation guided by high-level understanding.
- Thanks to the pre-trained VLMs, RoboDual excelled in tasks necessitating high-level generalizability (e.g., "visual distractor" and "novel object"), where specialist-only models struggled.

#### 4.3. Efficiency Analysis: Training, Data, and Inference

Efficiency is a cornerstone of RoboDual's design, and the results strongly support its claims:
- **Training Efficiency:** RoboDual is remarkably cost-effective. While a generalist-only model stabilized performance on CALVIN after approximately 1,400 GPU hours, RoboDual achieved an additional **+12% performance gain** with merely **8 GPU-hours** of training for its 20M-parameter specialist. Even more strikingly, when applied to an *inadequately trained* generalist, just one hour of adaptation with RoboDual yielded greater improvement (+17%) than several days of further training on the VLA alone.
- **Data Efficiency:** RoboDual exhibited exceptional data efficiency. On CALVIN, using only **5% of demonstrations**, RoboDual maintained an average task length of 3.59, significantly outperforming RoboFlamingo (1.35). In real-world experiments, with just **5 demonstrations**, RoboDual achieved a **73.3% success rate** for "put block into bowl," which was more than three times the performance of Diffusion Policy, and where ACT completely failed. This demonstrates the specialist's ability to effectively leverage generalist knowledge and extrapolate with limited in-domain data.
- **Inference Latency:** RoboDual achieved a real-world control frequency of **15 Hz** (on NVIDIA A5000 Ada GPUs). This is a crucial improvement over OpenVLA's standalone performance of only 3.9 Hz within the system, directly addressing the bottleneck of slow inference in large VLAs and enabling deployment in more dexterous tasks.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies: Understanding Synergy

Ablation studies elucidated the individual contributions of RoboDual's design choices to its synergistic performance:
- **Generalist Outputs as Conditioning:** All components of generalist conditioning contributed positively. Discretized trajectories alone provided a significant +0.8 improvement in average task length, while the absence of task latents led to a notable 0.14 performance decline, confirming their importance for high-level task understanding in multi-task learning.
- **Additional Sensory Inputs:** Integrating extra modalities (depth, tactile) and viewpoints (gripper camera) into the specialist model proved to be a cost-effective strategy, enhancing overall performance without the need for VLA fine-tuning or compromising specialist inference efficiency.
- **Conditioning Method:** Among tested methods, **cross-attention** performed best, showing slight advantages over in-context conditioning and proving more computationally efficient, making it the preferred approach for connecting the two models.

These findings collectively underscore RoboDual's ability to effectively combine the strengths of generalist and specialist policies, leading to a highly performant, generalizable, and efficient robotic manipulation system.

### 5. Significance, Potential Impact, and Future Directions

RoboDual represents a significant advancement in robotic manipulation, particularly in bridging the gap between theoretical capabilities of large foundation models and practical deployment requirements. Its core contribution lies in demonstrating that a synergistic dual-system, combining a powerful generalist with an efficient specialist, can overcome the individual limitations of each.

#### 5.1. Significance and Potential Impact

- **Addresses Key Limitations:** RoboDual effectively mitigates the major drawbacks of large generalist policies (high inference latency, expensive adaptation, limited multimodal integration) while simultaneously expanding the generalization capabilities of specialist policies. This is crucial for moving beyond lab-confined demonstrations to real-world deployment.
- **Practical Deployment of VLAs:** By achieving a high control frequency (15 Hz) and demonstrating robust performance across diverse, challenging real-world tasks, RoboDual offers a viable and "cost-effective and widely adaptable solution for the practical deployment of large generalist policies" for dexterous tasks. This makes advanced VLA models accessible for real-time control, which was previously a significant bottleneck.
- **Enhanced Generalizability:** The framework's superior performance in generalization tasks (position variation, visual distractors, unseen backgrounds, novel objects) is a testament to its ability to leverage the generalist's broad understanding. This suggests robots powered by RoboDual could adapt more seamlessly to new environments and unexpected scenarios.
- **Efficiency Paradigm Shift:** The striking training and data efficiency of RoboDual's specialist component (significant performance gains with minimal GPU hours and few demonstrations) could redefine how robot policies are adapted and scaled. Instead of endlessly scaling generalists or retraining from scratch, a lightweight specialist can efficiently absorb knowledge from pre-trained generalists and new data. This paradigm could accelerate research and development in robot learning.
- **Inspiration from Cognitive Science:** The explicit analogy to System-1 and System-2 thinking provides a compelling conceptual framework, potentially inspiring further research into biologically plausible and synergistic AI architectures for robotics.

#### 5.2. Limitations and Future Work

The authors acknowledge several limitations and outline promising avenues for future research:

- **Fixed Inference Time Assumption:** RoboDual currently assumes constant inference times for both generalist and specialist, leading to a fixed number of specialist steps per generalist inference. Future work could explore dynamic allocation or more adaptive synchronization mechanisms.
- **Discretized Action Output Limitation:** The generalist currently outputs only discretized actions. The authors suggest exploring richer "bridges" between the generalist and specialist. This could involve:
 - **Task Decomposition:** Enabling the generalist to perform more explicit task decomposition (as in Ahn et al., 2022), providing a more structured high-level plan.
 - **Affordance Grounding:** Integrating the generalist's ability to identify object affordances (Qian et al., 2024; Lai et al., 2024), offering richer contextual understanding for the specialist.
 - **Image Goal Generation:** Allowing the generalist to generate image goals (Sun et al., 2024), providing visual targets for the specialist's low-level execution.
 These richer interfaces could further enhance the synergy, allowing for more complex behaviors and more robust error recovery.
- **Failure Analysis Insights:** The appendix highlights "Not Following Instruction" as a major failure mode in real-world experiments. This reinforces the need for improving the instruction-following capabilities of the VLA (generalist) and ensuring that its semantic understanding is fully leveraged by the specialist, pointing directly to the need for better "bridges" between the two systems.

In summary, RoboDual makes a substantial contribution by demonstrating a viable path toward building versatile and practical robotic manipulation systems that are both intelligent and efficient. Its success opens doors for the widespread adoption of large foundation models in real-world robotics, setting a new benchmark for performance and efficiency in dual-system robot control.
