# ArtVIP: Articulated Digital Assets of Visual Realism, Modular Interaction, and Physical Fidelity for Robot Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The ArtVIP dataset introduces a collection of open-source, high-fidelity articulated digital assets, complemented by indoor scenes and pixel-level annotations, specifically designed to advance robot learning. These assets achieve superior visual realism, physical fidelity, and modular interaction capabilities, demonstrating improved zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and higher success rates in robot manipulation tasks compared to existing datasets.

## Problem

- Real-world robot data collection and deployment are resource-intensive, necessitating high-quality simulation platforms for embodied AI development.
- Existing open-source articulated object datasets suffer from insufficient visual realism, low physical fidelity of dynamic joints, and lack embedded modular interaction capabilities.
- Current generative or reconstruction methods for articulated objects often yield inconsistent quality, limited geometric generalization, and significant sim-to-real gaps, hindering complex dexterous manipulation tasks.

## Method

- Created **ArtVIP**, an open-source dataset of 992 high-fidelity ==digital-twin articulated== assets and complementary indoor scenes, meticulously modeled by professionals under unified specifications.
- Achieved visual realism through hierarchical mesh modeling with high-resolution PBR textures and pixel-level affordance annotations, and physical fidelity by optimizing collision geometry and position/velocity-dependent joint stiffness and friction parameters.
- Pioneered modular interaction by embedding five canonical behavior primitives directly within assets (e.g., latching, variable damping), enhancing reusability and simplifying scenario setup for robot learning.

## Results

- ArtVIP assets demonstrated superior geometric detail, higher structural fidelity in 3D reconstruction, and closer alignment of CLIP features to real-world objects compared to existing datasets like BEHAVIOR-**1K** and PartNet-Mobility.
- Evaluations confirmed high physical fidelity, with simulated joint motions for various objects (e.g., drawers, microwaves, oven doors) closely matching real-world optical tracking data.
- Robot imitation learning achieved non-zero zero-shot sim-to-real transfer with mixed real-sim data boosting performance, while an RL agent reached a **98%** success rate on trashcan closing with a **0.9886** sim-to-real Pearson correlation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-quality digital assets, meticulously crafted with visual realism and physical fidelity, are crucial for effectively reducing the sim-to-real gap in robot learning.
> - Detailed and physically accurate modeling of joint dynamics, including position- and velocity-dependent parameters, is essential for reproducing complex real-world interactions in simulation.
> - Embedding modular interaction behaviors directly into assets significantly streamlines development workflows and accelerates algorithm iteration by making assets inherently interactive and reusable.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of embodied artificial intelligence (AI) is progressing toward enabling robotic systems to operate in complex, unstructured real-world environments. This shift is increasingly supported by large-scale pretrained models and novel learning paradigms, ushering in a data-centric era where high-quality data is critical for developing scalable and generalizable embodied intelligence.

Real-world data collection and robot deployment are resource-intensive and difficult to scale. Consequently, simulation platforms serve as an efficient alternative, providing environments for imitation learning, reinforcement learning, rapid algorithm deployment, and standardized testing without risks to hardware or safety. The utility of these simulations, however, heavily depends on the quality of their digital assets. High-quality digital assets are crucial for accurately representing the real world and its physical characteristics, thereby reducing the sim-to-real gap and enhancing robot learning algorithm performance. Digital-twin assets, in particular, can facilitate pre-deployment validation and optimization of robotic systems. Furthermore, high-quality assets can act as training data or seed models for synthetic-asset generation methods like 3D reconstruction and domain randomization.

Current robot learning research is moving beyond simple tasks like pick-and-grasp to more complex dexterous manipulation and interaction tasks. This progression demands high-quality articulated-object assets. Existing open-source articulated-object datasets, such as PartNet-Mobility and BEHAVIOR-1K, are reported to have limitations, including insufficient visual realism, low physical fidelity of dynamic joints, and inconsistent quality due to varied sourcing without unified modeling standards. Methods for generating or reconstructing articulated objects often inherit stylistic biases, show limited geometric generalization, or suffer from issues like mesh quality variance and inadequate joint parameter tuning.

This work addresses these identified shortcomings by introducing a new dataset, ArtVIP, which aims to provide high-fidelity, open-source articulated digital assets with enhanced visual realism, physical fidelity, and modular interaction capabilities, specifically designed to bridge the sim-to-real gap for advanced robot learning tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and release ArtVIP, a comprehensive open-source dataset of high-quality digital-twin articulated objects, complemented by indoor-scene assets, specifically tailored for advancing robot learning. This objective is motivated by several critical challenges observed in the current landscape of simulation-based robot learning:

- **Insufficient Visual Realism in Existing Datasets**: Many open-source articulated-object datasets, such as PartNet-Mobility, are reported to suffer from unsmoothed geometric surfaces, low rendering quality, and general lack of photorealism. This visual discrepancy between simulated and real environments impedes the transferability of policies trained in simulation to real-world robots, thus widening the sim-to-real gap. The motivation is to create assets that appear photorealistic, with precise geometric meshes and high-resolution textures, to ensure simulated visual inputs closely resemble real-world observations.
- **Low Physical Fidelity of Dynamic Joints**: Existing datasets are often criticized for imprecise dynamic joint parameters, which means that the simulated motion of articulated parts does not faithfully reproduce real-world kinematics and dynamics. This limitation hinders the training of robots for tasks requiring fine-grained interaction and dexterous manipulation. The motivation is to achieve accurate physical fidelity through fine-tuned collision geometry and joint dynamics (stiffness, damping, friction), enabling realistic physical interactions in simulation.
- **Lack of Modular Interaction Capabilities**: Current datasets generally lack embedded interaction behaviors within assets, requiring researchers to manually program interactive functionalities for each scenario. This increases development overhead and limits asset reusability. The motivation is to pioneer embedded modular interaction behaviors directly within assets, making them inherently interactive and reusable across different scenarios, thereby accelerating algorithm iteration.
- **Limited Simulation Friendliness and Scalability**: Many existing assets are sourced from public repositories without consistent modeling standards, leading to quality inconsistencies, disorganized part hierarchies, and non-standardized coordinate systems, necessitating significant manual preprocessing for simulation use. Furthermore, proprietary datasets (e.g., BEHAVIOR-1K assets being locked into OmniGibson) restrict accessibility and wider community adoption. The motivation is to provide open-source assets in a compatible format (USD), accompanied by detailed production guidelines and pixel-level affordance annotations, to maximize utility across various simulation platforms and foster community adoption and replication.
- **Advancing Robot Learning for Complex Tasks**: As robot learning evolves to tackle dexterous manipulation, precise interaction, and complex everyday tasks, the need for high-quality, interactive, and physically accurate articulated objects becomes paramount. ArtVIP aims to facilitate the learning and evaluation of diverse manipulation skills such as rotating, clicking, pulling, and pressing, which are essential for embodied AI in unstructured real-world environments.

In summary, the key objective is to provide a robust foundation for next-generation robot learning by overcoming the limitations of current datasets through a meticulously crafted, open-source collection of articulated digital assets that prioritize visual realism, physical fidelity, and modular interaction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ArtVIP project adopts a systematic approach to overcome the limitations of existing datasets, focusing on fidelity and usability.

**ArtVIP Collection and Prioritization:**
The methodology prioritizes fidelity over sheer scale, contrasting with approaches that rely on pre-made models from public repositories or immature generative/reconstruction methods. ArtVIP was constructed by professional 3D modelers following unified asset specifications. The dataset includes 9 categories, 37 subcategories, and 992 articulated assets, alongside complementary sim-ready indoor scenes and pixel-level annotations.

**Visual Realism:**
To achieve visual realism, a top-down mechanical modeling approach is employed:
- **Hierarchical Decomposition:** Each articulated object is broken down into three levels: assembly, module, and mesh. An assembly is the complete functional unit, composed of multiple modules and meshes.
- **Coordinate System:** The assembly's base coordinate frame is established at the geometric center of the object's bottom surface.
- **Rigid-Body Modules:** Modelers partition the assembly into Xform-type rigid-body modules based on affordances, functionality, and joint locations. These modules expose dynamic information.
- **Mesh Parts:** Each module contains mesh parts that provide geometric detail, visual appearance, and static physical properties (collision shapes, mass). Strict rules are followed for meshes, textures, and materials to ensure photorealistic appearance. Manifold meshes form the geometric core, optimized for smooth surfaces and reduced computational burden by merging redundant vertices. High-resolution textures are precisely mapped via UV coordinates to capture fine surface characteristics. Materials leverage Physically Based Rendering (PBR) within Isaac Sim's RTX Renderer to accurately simulate light response, roughness, and emissive properties.
- **Assembly and Integration:** Meshes are assembled bottom-up (mesh, module, assembly), and dynamic motion is integrated by connecting modules with joints, preserving intended affordances.
- **Pixel-level Annotations:** Each module is annotated with pixel-level labels for identifying interaction affordances (e.g., armrest, button, door, drawer).

**Physical Fidelity:**
Physical fidelity is addressed through optimized collision modeling and joint dynamics:
- **Collision Modeling:** A balance between physical fidelity, interaction consistency, and computational efficiency is struck by using a mix of convex hulls, convex decomposition, and fine-tuned collision meshes. Simple geometries use Isaac Sim's default convex hull generation. Complex meshes are decomposed into multiple primitive meshes (cubes, cylinders) if affordances are maintained. Built-in convex decomposition tools are used when other methods are insufficient.
- **Joint Optimization:** The standard Isaac Sim joint drive equation, $\tau = K(q) \cdot (q - q_{target}(q)) + D \cdot (\dot{q} - \dot{q}_{target}(q))$, is enhanced. To model complex, real-world joint dynamics (e.g., door closers, light switches), the stiffness $K$ and target terms are parameterized as functions of joint position ($q$) and velocity ($\dot{q}$). This includes modeling friction as dependent on external force and velocity (static, maximum static, dynamic friction) and position-dependent stiffness variations (e.g., high stiffness for static equilibrium, decreasing stiffness during opening, and an exponential surge during final closure for mechanisms like refrigerator doors).

**Modular Interaction:**
A core innovation is embedding customizable behaviors directly within assets:
- **Canonical Behavior Primitives:** Five types of interaction primitives are abstracted and instantiated across 394 assets and over 900 joints:
 - **Latching/magnetic closure:** Automatic self-closing within a capture angle, simulating magnetic or spring-driven mechanisms.
 - **Damping:** Variable damping (e.g., increasing near closure) for smooth starts and stops in sliding or rotational components.
 - **Cross-asset effects:** Trigger-based coupling where one object's state influences another's behavior (e.g., button opening a door).
 - **Within-asset effects:** Instantaneous, internal triggers (e.g., microwave button popping the door open).
 - **Hover/hold position:** Static-friction-mediated holding at any intermediate pose (e.g., oven doors).
- **Enhanced Reusability:** Behaviors are bound to assets at design time. Importing a USD file grants immediate access to these interaction affordances, reducing development overhead and allowing researchers to focus on AI algorithms.

**Simulation Friendliness:**
- **USD Format:** All assets are provided in the modern USD format, ensuring compatibility with various simulation platforms.
- **Workflow Integration:** Compatibility with established robotics workflows is maintained via conversion to legacy formats like URDF or MJCF.
- **Production Guidance:** Detailed production guidelines are provided to facilitate community adoption and replication of the asset creation process.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ArtVIP focuses on demonstrating its visual realism, physical fidelity, and applicability in robot learning tasks.

**Visual Realism Evaluations:**
- **Geometric Detail:** Quantitative comparison of triangle counts (referencing Fig. 4, left) indicates ArtVIP assets possess richer geometric detail compared to BEHAVIOR-1K and PartNet-Mobility across shared object categories. For instance, categories like toilets and refrigerators show ArtVIP significantly surpassing other datasets in triangle count. While this increases detail, profiling confirmed that the frame rate remains above 60 Hz even in complex scenes (e.g., kitchen with 65 articulated joints) on typical hardware.
- **Reconstruction Performance Evaluation:** Experiments using VGGT, a 3D reconstruction method, show that reconstructions from ArtVIP assets exhibit higher structural fidelity and finer detail preservation than those from OmniGibson. This suggests that ArtVIP's realistic geometry and material representations improve the quality and compatibility of sampled images for reconstruction tasks (referencing Fig. 5, left).
- **Feature Distribution Visualization Analysis:** t-SNE visualization of CLIP features extracted from images of ArtVIP, OmniGibson, and real-world objects demonstrates that ArtVIP features align more closely with real-world data. This indicates higher consistency in visual semantics, texture, and material, enhancing ArtVIP's value for sim-to-real transfer in downstream tasks (referencing Fig. 5, right).

**Physical Fidelity and Interaction Evaluations:**
- **Joint Motion Fidelity:** Experiments compare real-world joint motion trajectories (recorded by an optical tracking system) with simulated motions of corresponding digital-twin objects. For a drawer, simulated displacement driven by various forces (1 N to 2.5 N) closely matched real-world measurements, demonstrating high physical fidelity (referencing Fig. 6). Similar evaluations on microwave latch release and oven door closer mechanisms (referencing Figs. 12 & 13) also show close agreement between simulated and real-world kinematics, including velocity changes during physical transitions.

**Applications in Robot Learning:**

- **Imitation Learning in Real-World Environments:**
 - **Setup:** A Franka robotic arm with a Robotiq 2F-85 gripper and four RealSense cameras was used in both real and simulated (Isaac Sim) environments, which were closely matched.
 - **Tasks:** Four challenging articulated-object manipulation tasks were designed: PullDrawer, OpenCabinet, SlideShelf, and CloseOven.
 - **Algorithms:** Action Chunking Transformer (ACT) and Diffusion Policy (DP) were used.
 - **Results (Table 1):**
 - **Zero-shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Models trained solely in simulation (SO) achieved non-zero success rates in the real world (e.g., ACT 39% on PullDrawer), indicating ArtVIP's fidelity effectively reduces the sim-to-real gap.
 - **Real vs. Simulation Data Volume:** With equal data volumes, real-world trained models (RO) generally outperformed simulation-trained models (SO), highlighting persistent sim-to-real challenges despite ArtVIP's quality.
 - **Mixed Data Benefits:** Mixing real and simulated data (RSM) consistently boosted performance, with higher proportions of simulated data leading to better success rates (e.g., DP for SlideShelf improved from 44% with RO to 59% with RSM100+100), suggesting ArtVIP assets align well with real-world data distributions.

- **Comparison with Other Assets via Imitation Learning:**
 - **Setup:** Comparison of ArtVIP and PartNet-Mobility microwave ovens for a door-pull task, using 100 real-world trajectories and 500 simulated trajectories per dataset.
 - **Results (Table 2):** ArtVIP assets yielded stronger zero-shot sim-to-real transfer under Sim-Only (SO) and higher success rates under Real-Sim-Mixed (RSM) settings (e.g., DP achieved 45% SO with ArtVIP vs. 35% with PartNet-Mobility; 83% RSM with ArtVIP vs. 70% with PartNet-Mobility). This supports the conclusion that higher-quality assets (like ArtVIP's) reduce the sim-to-real gap and lead to improved performance.

- **Reinforcement Learning in High-Fidelity Simulators:**
 - **Task:** CloseTrashcan, using a Franka arm.
 - **Method:** A two-stage EAGLE agent was trained in Isaac Sim, with a PPO expert distilled into a visuomotor policy.
 - **Results (Table 3):** The EAGLE agent achieved a 98% success rate after 500k training iterations in simulation. The Pearson correlation coefficient between simulated and real-world performance was 0.9886, indicating a strong linear relationship and supporting ArtVIP's fidelity for RL training and evaluation pipelines.

- **Comparison to Generative Pipelines:**
 - Evaluation of assets generated by methods like SplArt showed lower quality compared to ArtVIP's digital-twin assets. Issues included self-collisions, severe mesh distortions, incorrect joint limits, unrealistic materials, and lack of interior details (referencing Fig. 16).
 - Reconstruction metrics (Chamfer Distance, Axis Angular/Positional Error) reported for generative methods on real-world objects were significantly worse than those reported on synthetic data, indicating a substantial sim-to-real gap for generated articulated assets (referencing Table 13). Additionally, generated assets often had higher triangle counts (e.g., ~100x more than ArtVIP counterparts), reducing simulation frame rates.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ArtVIP represents a significant contribution to the field of robot learning by addressing critical limitations in existing simulation-ready datasets for articulated objects. Its primary significance lies in providing a comprehensive, high-quality, and open-source resource designed to accelerate the development and evaluation of robot manipulation skills.

The potential impacts of ArtVIP include:

- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap**: By emphasizing visual realism through precise geometric meshes, high-resolution textures, and PBR materials, and by ensuring physical fidelity through fine-tuned collision models and enhanced joint dynamics, ArtVIP helps reduce the discrepancy between simulated and real-world environments. This directly facilitates the transfer of policies learned in simulation to physical robots, potentially minimizing or eliminating the need for extensive real-world adaptation.
- **Advancing Complex Manipulation Skills**: The dataset's focus on articulated objects with modular interaction behaviors enables robots to learn and master a wider array of dexterous manipulation tasks, such as rotating knobs, pulling drawers, pressing buttons, and opening various types of doors. This is crucial for developing embodied AI systems capable of interacting with complex, everyday objects in unstructured environments.
- **Enhancing Robot Learning Research Efficiency**: The embedded modular interaction behaviors simplify the process of setting up interactive scenarios, reducing the programming overhead for researchers. Assets are inherently interactive, which can accelerate algorithm iteration and allow researchers to concentrate on core AI challenges rather than asset preparation.
- **Fostering Community Collaboration and Standardization**: ArtVIP's open-source nature, provision in the USD format, compatibility with other formats (URDF, MJCF), and detailed production guidelines promote broad adoption and community contributions. This standardization can lead to more consistent benchmarks and reproducible research, advancing the field collectively.
- **Supporting Diverse Robot Learning Paradigms**: The evaluations demonstrate ArtVIP's utility across various robot learning approaches, including imitation learning and reinforcement learning, showcasing its versatility as a training and evaluation environment. Its high fidelity allows for training policies that exhibit successful zero-shot transfer and benefit significantly from mixed real-sim data.
- **Benchmarking and Development of Generative Models**: By offering a high-quality baseline, ArtVIP provides a standard against which new asset generation and reconstruction methods can be rigorously evaluated. The comparative analysis with generative pipelines highlights the current limitations of automated methods and sets a quality target for future research in this area.

While acknowledging the current bottleneck of human labor for asset modeling, the foundational quality provided by ArtVIP is expected to catalyze future research, particularly in exploring generative methods to automate synthesis, reduce manual effort, and broaden object diversity, ultimately leading to more scalable and generalizable embodied intelligence.
