# A Survey: Learning Embodied Intelligence from Physical Simulators and World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey provides a comprehensive review of advancements in embodied artificial intelligence (AI) from 2018 to 2025, focusing on the synergistic integration of physical simulators and world models. It proposes a five-level grading standard for intelligent robots (IR-L0 to IR-L4) and analyzes how these technologies collectively bridge the simulation-to-reality gap and enhance robot autonomy, adaptability, and generalization in complex tasks.

## Problem

- Bridging the gap between abstract AI reasoning and real-world physical interaction to advance Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).
- Overcoming the simulation-to-reality (Sim2Real) gap, which limits the transferability and robustness of robot policies trained in virtual environments.
- Lack of a unified and systematic framework to assess and guide the development of increasingly intelligent robot capabilities across various dimensions.

## Method

- Conducts an extensive literature review (2018-2025) on the interplay between ==physical simulators== and ==world models== in embodied AI.
- Proposes a novel ==five-level grading system (IR-L0 to IR-L4)== for intelligent robots, evaluating them on autonomy, task handling, environmental adaptability, and societal cognition.
- Provides a detailed comparative analysis of mainstream physical simulators and categorizes the architectural evolution and core functional roles of world models.

## Results

- Defined a **5-level** grading system for intelligent robots (**IR-L0** to **IR-L4**), ranging from basic execution to fully autonomous, context-aware, and socially intelligent agents.
- Systematically compared leading physical simulators (e.g., **Isaac Sim**, **SAPIEN**, **Genesis**) based on their support for advanced physical properties, rendering capabilities, and sensor/joint types.
- Identified **3** core functional roles of world models: **neural simulators** for data generation, **dynamic models** for model-based reinforcement learning, and **reward models** for inferring training signals, with key applications in autonomous driving and articulated robotics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physical simulators (external explicit models) and world models (internal implicit models) play complementary and synergistic roles in enhancing robot autonomy, adaptability, and addressing the Sim2Real gap.
> - Generative world models are evolving into 'neural simulators' capable of generating controllable, high-fidelity synthetic data, paving the way for foundation models in robotics.
> - The proposed IR-L0 to IR-L4 grading system offers a standardized framework for evaluating and guiding the progression of embodied AI systems toward higher levels of intelligence and autonomy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The pursuit of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) increasingly recognizes embodied intelligence as a critical frontier. Traditional AI systems, often operating in symbolic or digital domains, lack the ability to ground their abstract reasoning in the physical world. Embodied intelligence, which emphasizes perception, action, and cognition through physical interaction, bridges this gap, enabling robots to learn robustly and adaptively from real-world feedback.

Within this rapidly evolving landscape, two foundational technologies—physical simulators and world models—have emerged as indispensable enablers. Physical simulators provide controlled, high-fidelity virtual environments for safe and efficient training and evaluation of robotic agents. They allow for the rapid development and testing of complex behaviors without the costs and risks associated with real-world experimentation. Complementing this, world models equip robots with internal, predictive representations of their environment, fostering planning, decision-making, and adaptive behaviors beyond direct sensory input.

This survey distinguishes itself from existing literature by specifically addressing the **synergistic relationship** and **complementary roles** of physical simulators and world models. While previous surveys have often focused on these components individually (e.g., robotics simulators or world models as distinct topics), this paper provides a unified framework, explicitly analyzing how the interplay between external simulation (simulators) and internal modeling (world models) is crucial for bridging the simulation-to-reality (Sim2Real) gap and accelerating the development of generalizable embodied AI systems. It positions itself as a comprehensive guide for researchers navigating the integration of these two powerful technologies on the path toward more capable and autonomous robots, especially humanoid robots and autonomous driving systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind this survey are clearly articulated:

- **Address the AGI Quest through Embodied Intelligence:** The overarching motivation is to contribute to the pursuit of AGI by highlighting embodied intelligence as a central focus in robotics. The authors emphasize that grounding abstract reasoning in real-world understanding and action through intelligent robots is essential.
- **Systematically Review Key Enabling Technologies:** The paper aims to provide a systematic review of recent advances (primarily 2018-2025) in learning embodied AI, specifically through the integration of physical simulators and world models.
- **Analyze Complementary Roles:** A core objective is to analyze how physical simulators (explicit external models) and world models (implicit internal models) play complementary roles in enhancing autonomy, adaptability, and generalization in intelligent robots. This includes discussing their interplay in bridging the Sim2Real gap.
- **Synthesize Progress and Identify Challenges:** The survey seeks to synthesize current progress in the field, outlining the state-of-the-art in robotic capabilities, simulation technologies, and world model architectures. Simultaneously, it aims to identify open challenges and future research directions to guide the community.
- **Propose a Unified Grading System for Robot Intelligence:** Recognizing a lack of a comprehensive framework, a key objective is to propose a novel five-level grading standard (IR-L0 to IR-L4) for intelligent robots. This classification aims to provide a unified framework to assess and guide technological development across dimensions like autonomy, task handling, environmental adaptability, and societal cognition.
- **Provide a Comprehensive Perspective:** Ultimately, the authors are motivated to offer a comprehensive and holistic perspective on the path toward more capable and generalizable embodied AI systems by integrating insights from various sub-domains.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors employ a systematic and structured survey methodology to achieve their objectives:

- **Literature Review and Synthesis:** The core approach involves an extensive review of recent literature (2018-2025) across embodied AI, robotics, physical simulation, and world modeling. The findings from this review are then synthesized to provide a coherent overview of the field.
- **Hierarchical Classification and Categorization:**
 - **Robot Intelligence Levels:** A novel five-level grading system (IR-L0 to IR-L4) for intelligent robots is proposed. This system is defined along four criteria: autonomy, task handling ability, environmental adaptability, and societal cognition ability. This classification serves as an organizing principle to discuss the progression of robotic capabilities.
 - **Robotic Techniques:** The survey categorizes recent advances in intelligent robotics into key areas: robotic mobility (legged locomotion, fall protection/recovery), robotic manipulation (unimanual, bimanual, whole-body control, foundation models), and human-robot interaction (cognitive collaboration, physical reliability, social embeddedness).
- **Comparative Analysis of Simulators:** Mainstream physical simulators (e.g., Webots, Gazebo, MuJoCo, Isaac Gym/Sim/Lab, SAPIEN, Genesis, Newton) are systematically analyzed and compared based on several critical aspects:
 - **Physical Properties:** Support for suction, random external forces, deformable objects, soft-body contacts, fluid mechanisms, Discrete Element Method (DEM) simulation, and differentiable physics.
 - **Rendering Capabilities:** Underlying rendering engines, support for ray tracing, Physically-Based Rendering (PBR), and scalable parallel rendering.
 - **Sensor and Joint Component Types:** Support for various sensors (IMU, force contact, RGB, LiDAR, GPS) and joint types (floating, fixed, hinge, spherical, prismatic, helical).
- **Architectural and Functional Analysis of World Models:**
 - **Architectural Evolution:** World model architectures are reviewed, tracing their evolution from Recurrent State Space Models (RSSMs) and Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) to Transformer-based, Autoregressive, and Diffusion-based generative models.
 - **Core Roles:** The functional roles of world models are analyzed, categorizing them as neural simulators, dynamic models (for model-based reinforcement learning), and reward models.
- **Application-Specific Deep Dives:** The application of world models is explored in two crucial domains: autonomous driving and articulated robots. Within each domain, the roles of world models as neural simulators, dynamic models, and reward models are examined, along with emerging technical trends and implications.
- **Identification of Challenges and Future Perspectives:** Throughout the analysis, the authors systematically identify current limitations, open challenges (e.g., high-dimensionality, causal reasoning, interpretability), and future research directions.

The methodology is comprehensive, moving from a high-level conceptual framework (robot intelligence levels) down to detailed technical comparisons (simulators) and in-depth functional analyses (world models and their applications). The maintenance of an active online repository (`https://github.com/NJU3DV-LoongGroup/Embodied-World-Models-Survey`) further enhances the survey's utility by providing up-to-date literature and open-source projects, serving as a living resource for the community.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey presents several key findings and results, structured around its core contributions:

1. **Proposed Levels of Intelligent Robots (IR-L0 to IR-L4):**
 - **IR-L0 (Basic Execution):** Non-intelligent, program-driven robots for repetitive, deterministic tasks (e.g., industrial welding).
 - **IR-L1 (Programmatic Response):** Limited rule-based reactive capabilities using basic sensors for predefined tasks (e.g., cleaning robots).
 - **IR-L2 (Basic Perception and Adaptation):** Preliminary environmental awareness and autonomous capabilities, responding to changes, and basic task mode transitions (e.g., service robots avoiding obstacles).
 - **IR-L3 (Humanoid Cognition and Collaboration):** Autonomous decision-making in complex dynamic environments, sophisticated human-robot interaction, intent inference, and ethical constraints (e.g., eldercare robots detecting emotional states).
 - **IR-L4 (Fully Autonomous):** Complete autonomy in perception, decision-making, and execution in any environment, with self-evolving ethical reasoning, advanced cognition, empathy, and long-term adaptive learning (e.g., sci-fi movie robots).

2. **Recent Techniques in Robot Learning:**
 - **Control Algorithms:** Comprehensive overview of Model Predictive Control (MPC), Whole-Body Control (WBC), Reinforcement Learning (RL), Imitation Learning (IL), and Visual-Language-Action Models (VLA).
 - **Robotic Locomotion:** Significant advancements in bipedal robots for unstructured environment adaptation (e.g., walking on uneven terrains, stairs) and high dynamic movements (running, jumping, parkour), enabled by force-controlled joints and learning-based methods. Fall protection and recovery techniques (model-based and learning-based) are also highlighted.
 - **Robotic Manipulation:** Progress from unimanual (gripper-based, dexterous hand) to bimanual and whole-body manipulation. Learning-based approaches (RL, IL, foundation models) are key, with advancements in large-scale data collection (e.g., ALOHA series) and visual-language models (e.g., RT-2).
 - **Human-Robot Interaction (HRI):** Classified into Cognitive Collaboration (understanding intent, multimodal intention learning, semantic understanding), Physical Reliability (safe coordination of force, timing, distance through motion planning and compliant control), and Social Embeddedness (adapting to social norms, cultural expectations, and group dynamics).

3. **Analysis of General Physical Simulators:**
 - **Mainstream Simulators:** Detailed review of Webots, Gazebo, MuJoCo, CoppeliaSim, PyBullet, Isaac Gym/Sim/Lab, SAPIEN, and Genesis, including the emerging NVIDIA Newton.
 - **Comparative Analysis:** Provides tables summarizing the support for:
 - **Physical Properties:** Variations in support for suction, random external forces, deformable objects, soft-body contacts, fluid mechanisms, DEM simulation, and differentiable physics, with platforms like Genesis and Isaac Sim leading in advanced capabilities.
 - **Rendering Capabilities:** Differences in rendering engines (OpenGL-based vs. Vulkan/RTX), ray tracing (limited to Isaac Sim/Lab, SAPIEN, Genesis), PBR, and crucial parallel rendering for scaling RL (Isaac Gym/Sim/Lab, SAPIEN, Genesis).
 - **Sensor and Joint Support:** General support for common sensors (RGB, IMU, force contact) but variability in LiDAR, GPS, and specialized joint types like helical.
 - **Advantages and Challenges:** Simulators offer cost-effectiveness, safety, control, and repeatability but face challenges in accuracy, complexity, data dependency, and overfitting. This highlights the motivation for world models.

4. **Recent Advancements of World Models:**
 - **Representative Architectures:** Evolution from Recurrent State Space Models (RSSMs, e.g., Dreamer series) and Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs, e.g., I-JEPA, V-JEPA) to more powerful Transformer-based, Autoregressive, and Diffusion-based generative models (e.g., Sora, GAIA-1/2, DriveDreamer).
 - **Core Roles:** World models are found to serve three critical roles:
 - **Neural Simulators:** Generating controllable, high-fidelity synthetic experiences (e.g., NVIDIA Cosmos, Wayve's GAIA series for autonomous driving, RoboDreamer for robotics).
 - **Dynamic Models:** Enabling planning and decision-making in model-based reinforcement learning (e.g., Dreamer series, iVideoGPT), improving sample efficiency.
 - **Reward Models:** Inferring meaningful training signals from weakly supervised or unlabeled data (e.g., VIPER) by using prediction likelihood as a reward.

5. **World Models for Intelligent Agents (Autonomous Driving & Articulated Robots):**
 - **Autonomous Driving:** World models are transforming autonomous driving by addressing limitations of modular designs. Key trends include generative architecture evolution (autoregressive to diffusion), multi-modal integration for controllable scenario generation, 3D spatial-temporal understanding (e.g., occupancy-based models), and end-to-end integration with driving pipelines.
 - **Articulated Robots:** World models enable neural simulation for training (e.g., NVIDIA Cosmos, RoboDreamer), act as dynamic models for MBRL (e.g., Dreamer series, Plan2Explore), and serve as reward models (e.g., VIPER) for dexterous manipulation and locomotion. Emerging trends include tactile-enhanced WMs, unified WMs for cross-hardware/task generalization, and hierarchical WMs for long-horizon tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey holds significant implications for the future of embodied AI research and development:

- **Roadmap for Embodied AI:** By proposing a five-level grading standard for robot intelligence, the survey provides a clear, progressive roadmap for assessing and guiding the development of intelligent robots. This framework can foster standardized evaluation and accelerate progress toward advanced autonomy (IR-L4).
- **Catalyst for Sim2Real Transfer:** The comprehensive analysis of physical simulators, alongside the deep dive into world models, directly addresses the critical Sim2Real gap. By elucidating the complementary nature of external simulation and internal modeling, the survey offers strategies for leveraging synthetic data to train robust policies for real-world deployment, reducing costs and increasing safety.
- **Advancing Model-Based Reinforcement Learning:** The detailed review of world models as dynamic and reward models underscores their potential to overcome the sample efficiency challenges of traditional RL. This can lead to faster learning cycles and more adaptable agents, particularly in complex and resource-intensive domains like robotics and autonomous driving.
- **Promoting Foundation Models in Robotics:** The discussion on generative world models, particularly diffusion and transformer-based architectures, highlights their role in creating "neural simulators" that can generate diverse, controllable, and high-fidelity training data. This paves the way for the development of generalist foundation models for robotics, similar to those in natural language processing and computer vision.
- **Guiding Future Research:** By synthesizing current progress and clearly outlining open challenges (e.g., high-dimensionality, causal reasoning, interpretability, compositional generalization, data bias, long-term memory), the survey provides a valuable guide for researchers to identify impactful new directions. This includes pushing toward more abstract and semantic understanding in world models, as well as developing robust evaluation and benchmarking methodologies.
- **Interdisciplinary Collaboration:** The diverse authorship from both academia and industry signifies the interdisciplinary nature of embodied AI. This survey encourages further collaboration between computer vision, robotics, machine learning, and hardware development to tackle the complex challenges of building truly intelligent agents.
- **Ethical and Safety Implications:** As robots become more autonomous and capable (approaching IR-L3 and IR-L4), the discussions on interpretability, verifiability, and social embeddedness underscore the growing importance of ethical considerations and safety in their design and deployment.

In conclusion, this survey acts as a crucial synthesizing document, not only summarizing the state-of-the-art but also charting a course for future innovations at the intersection of physical simulation, world modeling, and embodied intelligence. Its comprehensive scope and forward-looking perspectives are poised to significantly influence the next generation of intelligent robotic systems.
