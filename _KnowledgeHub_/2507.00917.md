# A Survey: Learning Embodied Intelligence from Physical Simulators and World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey provides a comprehensive review of advancements in embodied artificial intelligence (AI) from 2018 to 2025, focusing on the synergistic integration of physical simulators and world models. It proposes a five-level grading standard for intelligent robots (IR-L0 to IR-L4) and analyzes how these technologies collectively bridge the simulation-to-reality gap and enhance robot autonomy, adaptability, and generalization in complex tasks.

## Problem

- Bridging the gap between abstract AI reasoning and real-world physical interaction to advance Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).
- Overcoming the simulation-to-reality (Sim2Real) gap, which limits the transferability and robustness of robot policies trained in virtual environments.
- Lack of a unified and systematic framework to assess and guide the development of increasingly intelligent robot capabilities across various dimensions.

## Method

- Conducts an extensive literature review (2018-2025) on the interplay between ==physical simulators== and ==world models== in embodied AI.
- Proposes a novel ==five-level grading system (IR-L0 to IR-L4)== for intelligent robots, evaluating them on autonomy, task handling, environmental adaptability, and societal cognition.
- Provides a detailed comparative analysis of mainstream physical simulators and categorizes the architectural evolution and core functional roles of world models.

## Results

- Defined a **5-level** grading system for intelligent robots (**IR-L0** to **IR-L4**), ranging from basic execution to fully autonomous, context-aware, and socially intelligent agents.
- Systematically compared leading physical simulators (e.g., **Isaac Sim**, **SAPIEN**, **Genesis**) based on their support for advanced physical properties, rendering capabilities, and sensor/joint types.
- Identified **3** core functional roles of world models: **neural simulators** for data generation, **dynamic models** for model-based reinforcement learning, and **reward models** for inferring training signals, with key applications in autonomous driving and articulated robotics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physical simulators (external explicit models) and world models (internal implicit models) play complementary and synergistic roles in enhancing robot autonomy, adaptability, and addressing the Sim2Real gap.
> - Generative world models are evolving into 'neural simulators' capable of generating controllable, high-fidelity synthetic data, paving the way for foundation models in robotics.
> - The proposed IR-L0 to IR-L4 grading system offers a standardized framework for evaluating and guiding the progression of embodied AI systems toward higher levels of intelligence and autonomy.
