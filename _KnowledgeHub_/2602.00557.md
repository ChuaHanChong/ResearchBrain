# ConLA: Contrastive Latent Action Learning from Human Videos for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> ConLA extracts latent robot actions from unlabeled human videos via contrastive disentanglement, separating motion dynamics from visual appearance using action-category and temporal priors before VQ-VAE quantization, then pretrains a VLA policy and fine-tunes on limited real robot data. Pretrained only on human videos, it surpasses a policy pretrained on real robot trajectories.

## Problem

- VQ-VAE-based latent action extraction optimizes visual reconstruction, giving no direct incentive to capture true inter-frame motion dynamics rather than static appearance.
- This reconstruction objective causes shortcut learning where models memorize future visual content, producing latent actions entangled with irrelevant scene features.
- Entanglement is especially severe on human videos due to complex visual variation, limiting transferability of learned motion priors to robot manipulation policies.

## Method

- ==Contrastive Disentanglement Module== splits the ==inverse-dynamics== latent embedding into action and visual halves, routing each through separate heads for targeted supervision.
- ==Action-Centric Contrastive Learning== applies a ==supervised contrastive loss== over action-class labels to cluster same-action latent embeddings across environments and embodiments.
- ==Vision-Centric Contrastive Learning== uses ==reverse-order frame augmentation== and an ==InfoNCE loss== so visual features stay motion-invariant while temporal order still drives the action head.

## Results

- ConLA pretrained solely on human videos (Something-SomethingV2) reaches **64.6%** average success on SimplerEnv, beating LAPA's **52.1%** by **12.5** points and even ACTIONVLA's real-trajectory pretraining (**63.5%**) by **1.1** points.
- Real-world Franka tests across three tasks show ConLA outperforming LAPA by **15.9%** success rate when both pretrain on human videos, and improve generalization to unseen objects (**47.2%** vs **25.8%**).
- Ablations show action-contrast alone lifts LAPA's **52.1%** to **58.4%**, and full ConLA with vision-contrast plus inverse-order augmentation reaches **64.6%**, with gains scaling consistently from **10%** to **100%** of pretraining data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reconstruction-only objectives for latent action learning silently encode appearance instead of motion; explicit disentanglement supervision is needed to prevent shortcut learning.
> - Weak semantic priors already present in data (action-class labels, temporal order) can substitute for costly pose/retargeting supervision to structure unsupervised representations.
> - Once shortcut learning is controlled, human video pretraining can match or exceed real-robot trajectory pretraining, suggesting scale had been undercut by representation quality, not data type.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of robotic manipulation, large-scale machine learning, and computer vision, specifically focusing on the emergent field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The broader landscape is characterized by:

- **Rise of VLA Models:** Inspired by the success of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs), VLA models aim to enable robots to understand language instructions, perceive complex visual environments, and execute corresponding actions. Recent advancements, such as OpenVLA, RT-1, and π0, have shown promising generalization capabilities by pretraining on large-scale robot teleoperation datasets.
- **Data Scarcity in Robotics:** A fundamental challenge in VLA development is the acquisition of sufficiently large, diverse, and well-annotated robot teleoperation datasets. Such data collection is incredibly costly, time-consuming, and difficult to scale across myriad tasks and environments. This limits the generalization potential and broad applicability of current VLA models.
- **The Promise of Human Videos:** In stark contrast to robot data, human demonstration videos (e.g., from the internet) are abundant, diverse, and readily available. They offer a rich source of manipulation behaviors and scene variations that could potentially unlock unprecedented scalability for VLA training.
- **Challenges of Human Videos for Robotics:** Despite their abundance, human videos present significant hurdles:
 - **Lack of Explicit Action Supervision:** They do not contain precise robot joint commands or end-effector trajectories, making direct VLA training impossible.
 - **Domain Gap:** Visual and kinematic differences between human and robot embodiments.
 - **Visual Noise:** Human videos often contain complex backgrounds, varying viewpoints, camera jitters, and irrelevant visual distractions, making it difficult to extract pure action information.

Prior work has attempted to bridge the gap between human videos and robot learning through two main avenues:
- **Explicit Action Extraction:** Methods that rely on hand pose estimators, motion capture, or specific human-robot paired data to retarget human actions to robots (e.g., EgoMimic, HAT, EgoVLA). These approaches, while effective for specific tasks, still require significant data collection efforts and lack general scalability due to their reliance on specific setups or paired data.
- **Latent Action Learning (Unsupervised Inverse Dynamics Models - IDMs):** This line of research, which ConLA directly builds upon, focuses on learning abstract "latent actions" from unlabeled videos. **LAPA (Latent Action Pretraining from Videos)** is a notable precursor, using a VQ-VAE (Vector Quantized Variational AutoEncoder) paradigm to extract motion priors. Other works like CLAM, COMO, and UniVLA also explore latent action spaces.

**ConLA's contribution** specifically addresses a critical limitation of these VQ-VAE-based latent action learning methods: **"shortcut learning."** The authors identify that, because the primary objective of these models is often visual reconstruction, they tend to learn to memorize future visual content or rely on spurious visual cues rather than truly capturing inter-frame dynamics (motion). This results in "entangled" latent representations that are mixed with irrelevant visual features, hindering their transferability to robot policies, especially when dealing with the inherent visual complexity and noise of human videos. ConLA aims to overcome this by proposing a novel contrastive disentanglement mechanism, thereby positioning itself as a significant advancement in leveraging large-scale, off-the-shelf human video data for robust and scalable robot learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the ConLA research is to develop an unsupervised pretraining framework that can effectively learn robotic policies from the vast and diverse repository of human demonstration videos, thereby overcoming the limitations of costly and difficult-to-scale robot teleoperation datasets.

More specifically, the key objectives and underlying motivations are:

1. **Mitigate Shortcut Learning in Latent Action Extraction:**
 - **Motivation:** Existing VQ-VAE-based methods for learning latent actions from videos (like LAPA) often fall prey to "shortcut learning." Instead of learning true inter-frame motion dynamics, the models exploit simpler, spurious visual cues to minimize reconstruction error. This means they might "memorize" future visual appearances rather than understanding how to *generate* that appearance through motion.
 - **Objective:** Design a mechanism that prevents this shortcut learning, ensuring that the learned latent actions genuinely represent motion dynamics.

2. **Disentangle Action Dynamics from Visual Noise:**
 - **Motivation:** Human videos are inherently noisy, containing variations in backgrounds, camera angles, lighting, and object appearances that are irrelevant to the core action being performed. These "visual noise" factors can get entangled with action representations, making them less robust and less transferable across different environments and robot embodiments.
 - **Objective:** Develop a method to isolate pure motion dynamics from these distracting visual elements, creating a cleaner, "action-centric" representation.

3. **Learn Compact and Semantically Consistent Latent Action Representations:**
 - **Motivation:** For latent actions to be useful for generalist robot learning, they must be semantically meaningful and consistent. Similar physical actions (e.g., "move left") should be represented similarly, regardless of the background, object color, or specific human performing the action. Current entangled representations lack this consistency.
 - **Objective:** Guide the learning process to produce latent actions that cluster compactly based on their semantic meaning, enhancing their discriminability and transferability.

4. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Robotic Manipulation Performance Solely from Human Videos:**
 - **Motivation:** If human videos can be effectively utilized, they offer a scalable alternative to robot data. However, previous attempts using human videos have generally underperformed compared to models trained on real robot data, questioning their utility.
 - **Objective:** Demonstrate that by addressing the identified challenges, pretraining *only* on human videos can yield policies that match or even surpass the performance of policies pretrained on real robot trajectories, thereby validating the full potential of human video data for VLA models.

By addressing these objectives, ConLA aims to unlock a vast, scalable data source for robotics, facilitating the development of more generalist, capable, and accessible robot manipulation systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ConLA proposes a three-stage unsupervised pretraining framework designed to leverage human videos for robotic policy learning: 1) Contrastive Latent Action Learning, 2) Latent Action Pretraining, and 3) Action Finetuning.

#### 3.1. Stage 1: Contrastive Latent Action Learning (Core Contribution)

This initial stage is where ConLA introduces its primary innovations to learn high-quality, disentangled latent actions from video data, serving as robust pseudo-labels for subsequent stages.

- **Latent Action Quantization Base:**
 - The process starts by forming video pairs `[O_t, O_t+k]` (current frame `O_t` and future frame `O_t+k` separated by `k` frames) and their corresponding action class label `y`. A "reverse-order augmentation" creates an inverse pair `[O_t+k, O_t]` to capture temporal information.
 - An **Inverse Dynamics Model (Encoder I)**, implemented as a Spatial-Temporal Transformer, takes `[O_t, O_t+k]` as input to extract a latent action embedding `Z`.
 - A **Forward Dynamics Model (Decoder F)**, a Spatial Transformer, takes `O_t` and the quantized latent action `Z_aq` to reconstruct the future frame `ˆO_t+k`.
 - The base objective is the reconstruction error: `||ˆO_t+k - O_t+k||^2`.
 - Importantly, a **warmup phase** (5,000 steps) is conducted where only this reconstruction loss is used. This stabilizes preliminary representations before introducing contrastive learning, which might otherwise lead to model collapse early in training.

- **Contrastive Disentanglement Module:** This is the heart of ConLA, designed to mitigate shortcut learning and disentangle action from visual content.
 - The latent action embedding `Z` from the encoder is *evenly split* into two parts: `Z_a'` (action-related) and `Z_v'` (visual-related).
 - **Action-Centric Contrastive Learning:**
 - `Z_a'` is projected by an `MLP_action` (action head) to `Z_a`.
 - A **supervised contrastive objective (`L_action`)** is applied to `Z_a`. This leverages the action category labels `y` (weak supervision from datasets like Something-SomethingV2 or extracted from instructions). The objective pulls representations of the same action class closer together while pushing representations of different classes apart in the latent space. This direct semantic guidance forces the model to learn truly action-related features, preventing reliance on visual shortcuts.
 - **Vision-Centric Contrastive Learning:**
 - `Z_v'` is projected by an `MLP_visual` (visual head) to `Z_v`.
 - The inverse frame pair `[O_t+k, O_t]` is also fed through the encoder to obtain its latent representation `Z_I`, which is similarly split to yield `Z_I_v'` and projected to `Z_I_v`.
 - An **InfoNCE loss (`L_visual`)** is used to make `Z_v` and `Z_I_v` similar. This exploits the "temporal sensitivity prior": motion information changes drastically when frame order is reversed, but static visual content (background, object appearance) remains relatively stable. By encouraging `Z_v` and `Z_I_v` to be similar, the model is forced to capture content-consistent, motion-invariant features, thus isolating visual appearance from motion.
 - **Combined Optimization:** The overall loss for this stage is `L_total = L_MSE + L_action + L_visual`. This joint optimization ensures both accurate reconstruction and disentangled, semantically consistent latent action representations.
 - Finally, the optimized `Z_a` is quantized using VQ-VAE to produce `Z_aq`, a discrete "action token" from a predefined codebook.

#### 3.2. Stage 2: Latent Action Pretraining

This stage transfers the learned motion priors to a generalist robotic policy.
- **Pseudo-Label Generation:** The encoder trained in Stage 1 is used to infer sequences of `Z_aq` (latent action tokens) for all video pairs in the pretraining dataset. These `(O_t, instruction, Z_aq)` triplets form a pseudo-labeled dataset.
- **Policy Training:** A pretrained Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically based on the 7B Large World Model, is trained autoregressively. Its task is to predict the next `Z_aq` token conditioned on the current visual observation `O_t` and the task instruction.
- **Model Structure:** An additional latent action head (a single-layer MLP) is attached after the VLM's language model head, with a vocabulary size matching the latent action codebook size. During this stage, the vision encoder of the VLM is frozen, while the language model parameters are unfrozen and optimized.

#### 3.3. Stage 3: Action Finetuning

The final stage maps the abstract latent actions to executable robot control signals.
- **Robot Data Integration:** The pretrained policy from Stage 2 is finetuned using a small amount of real robot trajectories that contain ground-truth robot actions `A_t`.
- **Action Head Replacement:** The latent action head is replaced with a new action head designed to predict continuous (but discretized) robot actions in the robot's action space.
- **Optimization:** Similar to pretraining, the VLM's vision encoder remains frozen, while all parameters of the underlying language model are unfrozen and optimized to learn the mapping from latent actions to real robot commands.

This structured approach, with its novel contrastive disentanglement at its core, allows ConLA to effectively learn robust motion representations from complex human videos, paving the way for scalable and generalist robot manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ConLA presents compelling experimental results across both simulation and real-world benchmarks, demonstrating its effectiveness in learning robust robotic policies from human videos.

- **Overall State-of-the-Art Performance:** ConLA consistently achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming previous methods on various tasks.

- **SimplerEnv Simulation Benchmark:**
 - **Superiority over Baselines:** ConLA significantly surpasses prior methods. When pretrained solely on human videos (Something-SomethingV2), ConLA achieves an average success rate of **64.6%**, representing a substantial **12.5% improvement** over LAPA (52.1%) which uses a naive VQ-VAE for latent action learning.
 - **Surpassing Robot Trajectory Pretraining:** A groundbreaking finding is that ConLA, pretrained *only on human videos*, for the first time **surpasses the performance of models pretrained directly on real robot trajectories (ACTIONVLA)** by **1.1%** (64.6% vs. 63.5%). This is a crucial validation of human videos as a viable, and potentially superior, pretraining data source when effectively utilized.
 - **Improvement with Robot Videos:** Even when pretrained on robot videos (BridgeV2), ConLA shows a **3.1% improvement** over LAPA (60.4% vs. 57.3%), indicating its disentanglement mechanism benefits all video types.

- **Real-World Tabletop Manipulation:**
 - **Significant Performance Boost:** In real-world robot tests, ConLA demonstrates a dramatic performance boost when pretrained on human videos, outperforming LAPA by a notable **15.9%** (48.2% vs. 32.3% average success rate).
 - **Enhanced Generalization:** ConLA exhibits strong generalization capabilities, especially under the "unseen object" setting. When pretrained on human videos, ConLA achieves an average success rate of **47.2%** for unseen objects, significantly higher than LAPA's 25.8%. This is attributed to the broader object diversity present in large-scale human video datasets, which ConLA's disentangled representations can better leverage. This highlights the potential of human video pretraining for tasks requiring robust generalization to novel objects.
 - **Validation of Video Pretraining:** Both ConLA and LAPA outperform the `SCRATCH` baseline, confirming the value of pretraining from videos in general.

- **Latent Action Analysis (Qualitative and Quantitative):**
 - **Mitigation of Shortcut Learning:** Qualitative visualizations (Figure 5 and Appendix Figure 8) strongly support ConLA's ability to mitigate shortcut learning. Unlike LAPA, which often produces reconstructions that "memorize" future visual content (e.g., specific background elements or object appearance), ConLA's reconstructions accurately reflect the intended motion (e.g., move left/right) without leaking irrelevant visual information from the source frames. This demonstrates that ConLA indeed captures motion-meaningful latent actions.
 - **Semantically Consistent Representations:** t-SNE visualizations (Figure 6) of the latent action embedding space confirm that ConLA learns a more compact and semantically coherent representation. Actions of the same category cluster tightly together, even if performed in different visual contexts or by different humans. In contrast, LAPA's latent space appears messy and entangled, with similar actions being separated due to visual variations. This consistency is crucial for effective knowledge transfer.

- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Contrastive Disentanglement Module:** The ablation studies confirm the importance of each component of the Contrastive Disentanglement Module.
 - Adding **Action-Centric Contrastive Learning** alone significantly improves performance over the LAPA baseline (52.1% to 58.4%).
 - Further adding **Vision-Centric Contrastive Learning with inverse-order augmentation** yields the full performance gain (up to 64.6%).
 - Removing the inverse-order augmentation from Vision-Centric Contrastive Learning leads to a performance drop (from 64.6% to 57.3%), validating that the temporal prior and disentanglement between motion and content are critical.
 - **Data Scalability:** Experiments on varying scales of human video data (10% to 100% of Something-SomethingV2) demonstrate that ConLA's performance scales positively with increased data size and makes more efficient use of the data compared to LAPA.

In summary, the findings robustly establish ConLA as a highly effective framework for learning disentangled and semantically consistent latent actions from human videos. Its ability to surpass robot-data-pretrained models signifies a major step towards scalable and generalist robotic manipulation using readily available, diverse human demonstrations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ConLA represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic learning, particularly in addressing the critical challenge of data scalability for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Its potential impact is multifaceted:

- **Unlocking the Full Potential of Human Videos for Robotics:** Historically, the vast abundance of human demonstration videos (e.g., from the internet) has remained an underutilized resource for robot learning due to the lack of explicit action labels and the inherent visual complexity. ConLA's ability to effectively extract high-quality, disentangled, and semantically consistent latent actions directly from these videos is a game-changer. It provides a robust methodology to harness this scalable data source, significantly reducing the reliance on costly and hard-to-acquire robot teleoperation datasets.

- **Breaking Performance Barriers:** The demonstration that a model pretrained *solely on human videos* can surpass the performance of models pretrained on real robot trajectories (e.g., ACTIONVLA on SimplerEnv) is a landmark achievement. This finding challenges the conventional wisdom that robot-specific data is always superior and opens new avenues for achieving higher performance ceilings by leveraging the sheer scale and diversity of human demonstrations. It suggests that well-extracted motion priors from human behavior can offer richer, more generalizable knowledge.

- **Enhanced Generalization and Transferability:** By explicitly disentangling action dynamics from irrelevant visual noise and achieving semantically consistent latent representations, ConLA significantly improves the generalization capabilities of robot policies. This is evidenced by its strong performance on unseen object combinations, completely unseen objects, and novel instructions in real-world scenarios. This is crucial for developing truly "generalist" robots that can adapt to new environments and tasks without extensive retraining.

- **Addressing a Fundamental Problem in Representation Learning:** The paper directly tackles and provides an effective solution to the "shortcut learning" problem prevalent in unsupervised representation learning, particularly in reconstruction-based objectives. The contrastive disentanglement mechanism offers a principled way to ensure that learned representations capture the intended abstract concepts (motion) rather than superficial visual cues. This methodology could inspire similar approaches in other domains facing entangled representation challenges.

- **Accelerating VLA Model Development:** By providing a scalable and effective data pretraining strategy, ConLA will likely accelerate the development and deployment of more capable VLA models. Researchers can now leverage readily available human video datasets, allowing for faster iteration cycles and the exploration of larger model architectures and more diverse task sets. This could democratize access to advanced robot learning research.

- **Inspiration for Future Research:** ConLA opens several promising avenues for future work:
 - Exploring more sophisticated methods for automatically extracting fine-grained action category labels from unconstrained videos.
 - Investigating further how to bridge the human-to-robot embodiment gap more seamlessly using the disentangled latent actions.
 - Applying ConLA's principles to even larger and more diverse internet video datasets to assess its scalability limits and further push generalization boundaries.
 - Integrating ConLA with emerging large language and vision models to create even more powerful multimodal robot agents.

In conclusion, ConLA marks a pivotal step in bridging the gap between human observation and robot action. By innovatively addressing the challenges of learning from human videos, it paves the way for a future where robots can learn complex manipulation skills from the vast amount of human knowledge available online, leading to more intelligent, adaptable, and autonomous robotic systems.
