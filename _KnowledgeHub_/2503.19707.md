# Mind the Gap: Benchmarking Spatial Reasoning in Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> University of Edinburgh researchers develop a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating spatial reasoning capabilities in vision-language models (VLMs), revealing that current state-of-the-art models perform near random chance across multiple spatial reasoning tasks while demonstrating particular weakness in mental rotation abilities.

## Problem

- Existing VLM benchmarks don't effectively isolate or assess spatial reasoning abilities
- Lack of standardized evaluation methods for comparing spatial reasoning capabilities across different VLM architectures

## Method

- Created a multi-component benchmark combining ==synthetic==, ==GenAI-generated==, and ==real-world images==
- Developed specific tests for ==spatial relations==, orientation, navigation, and ==mental rotation==
- Evaluated **13** state-of-the-art VLMs using the new benchmark framework

## Results

- Average accuracy across **13** tested VLMs was close to random chance
- Mental rotation tasks proved particularly challenging for all models
- Models demonstrated inconsistent performance across different spatial reasoning components
- Ablation studies showed limitations persist even with different image generation approaches

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLMs show relatively strong performance on basic spatial relations but struggle with complex transformations
> - Success in one spatial reasoning component doesn't translate to robust performance across others
> - Using GenAI-generated images doesn't significantly improve model performance

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

- **Emergence of VLMs:** The paper situates itself within the rapidly evolving field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs). VLMs represent a significant step forward in AI, enabling machines to process and understand information from both visual and textual sources. They have demonstrated impressive capabilities in tasks like image captioning, visual question answering (VQA), and image-text retrieval.
- **Limitations of Existing Benchmarks:** However, the paper argues that existing benchmarks used to evaluate VLMs often fall short in isolating and rigorously assessing spatial reasoning abilities. These benchmarks tend to conflate spatial reasoning with related tasks like object detection and semantic comprehension.
- **Need for Specialized Evaluation:** The authors highlight the need for a more precise and structured evaluation of spatial reasoning in VLMs. They point out that many datasets augment visual data with additional information like point clouds or depth maps, which may not accurately reflect real-world scenarios where VLMs must rely solely on visual input and textual instructions.
- **Human-Level Spatial Reasoning:** The paper emphasizes the importance of achieving human-level spatial reasoning in VLMs. Humans possess an intuitive understanding of spatial relationships, orientations, and transformations, which is crucial for interacting with and navigating complex environments. By identifying and addressing the shortcomings of current VLMs in spatial reasoning, the research aims to drive the development of more capable and reliable AI systems.
- **Relevance to Downstream Applications:** The paper connects spatial reasoning to practical applications such as robotics, autonomous navigation, and augmented reality. VLMs with strong spatial reasoning abilities can interpret visual scenes in a more human-like manner, enabling them to make better decisions in real-world environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

- **Defining Spatial Reasoning:** The primary objective of the paper is to provide a clear and precise definition of spatial reasoning in the context of VLMs.
- **Identifying Components of Spatial Reasoning:** A key aim is to delineate the core components of spatial reasoning, namely:
 - Spatial relations (e.g., object positioning)
 - Orientation and navigation (e.g., directionality)
 - Mental rotation (e.g., invariant object recognition)
 - Spatial visualization (e.g., transformation prediction)
- **Creating a Novel Benchmark:** Another important objective is to develop a novel benchmark specifically designed to evaluate spatial reasoning in VLMs. This benchmark combines programmatically generated, GenAI-synthesized, and real-world images to provide a comprehensive assessment.
- **Evaluating State-of-the-Art VLMs:** The paper aims to evaluate the performance of a range of state-of-the-art VLMs on the proposed benchmark.
- **Identifying Limitations:** A key motivation is to uncover and highlight the limitations of current VLMs in spatial reasoning.
- **Providing a Platform for Future Research:** Ultimately, the paper seeks to establish a solid platform for future research and development in the area of spatial reasoning for VLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

- **Multi-Faceted Approach:** The research employs a multi-faceted approach to understanding spatial reasoning in VLMs.
- **Defining Spatial Reasoning Components:** The authors begin by defining the core elements of spatial reasoning, drawing upon insights from cognitive science and neuroscience.
- **Data Creation:** The authors created a novel benchmark dataset comprised of synthetically generated images (for mental rotation and paper folding tasks), images sourced from existing datasets (Spatial-Obj and SpatialEval for spatial relations and navigation, respectively), and real-world images (EgoOrientBench for orientation). In particular, the creation of the mental rotation dataset contained two sub-datasets, one that was difficult and one that was easy. This allows the authors to control the complexity and difficulty for the VLM.
- **Model Evaluation:** The authors evaluated a set of 13 state-of-the-art VLMs, using the created benchmark dataset. They evaluated a diverse set of models including open-source models (Qwen, Llava, Instruct-Blip, Idefics, SmolVLM, Llama) and closed-source models (GPT-4o and o1).
- **Component-Specific Evaluation:** The benchmark is designed to isolate different spatial reasoning components, allowing for a detailed analysis of VLMs' strengths and weaknesses in each area.
- **Comparative Analysis:** The research compares the performance of different VLMs across the various spatial reasoning tasks.
- **Ablation Study with GenAI Images:** To explore the reasons for the limitations in mental rotation tasks, the authors performed an ablation study using images generated by generative AI models. This study aimed to determine whether the synthetic nature of the original images was a factor in the poor performance of VLMs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- **Poor Overall Performance:** The main finding of the paper is that the average accuracy of the 13 VLMs on the spatial reasoning tasks is close to random chance.
- **Spatial Relations Strength:** The models exhibit relatively strong performance in spatial relations tasks.
- **Mental Rotation Weakness:** Mental rotation tasks prove to be a significant challenge for the VLMs. This indicates the complexity of spatial transformations for VLMs.
- **Inconsistency Across Tasks:** Success in one spatial reasoning component (e.g., the easier MRT version) does not necessarily translate to robust performance across other tasks.
- **Role of Synthetic vs. Natural Images:** The ablation study using GenAI-generated images reveals that the limitations in mental rotation are not solely due to the synthetic nature of the images. While some models show improved performance with GenAI images, this may be attributed to their resemblance to training data or the presence of other visual cues.
- **GenAI images do not improve performance significantly.** These results further strengthen the argument that VLMs exhibit a limitation in spatial reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

- **Highlighting a Critical Gap:** The paper highlights a critical gap in the capabilities of current VLMs: spatial reasoning.
- **Driving Future Research:** By identifying the limitations of VLMs in spatial reasoning, the research provides a clear direction for future research and development.
- **Informing Model Development:** The component-specific evaluation offers insights into the specific areas where VLMs need improvement, which can inform the design and training of future models.
- **Enabling Real-World Applications:** By advancing spatial reasoning in VLMs, the research has the potential to enable a wide range of real-world applications, including:
 - **Robotics:** Robots that can understand and reason about spatial relationships can perform more complex tasks in unstructured environments.
 - **Autonomous Navigation:** Self-driving cars and drones require strong spatial reasoning abilities to navigate safely and efficiently.
 - **Augmented Reality:** AR applications can become more immersive and interactive with VLMs that can accurately understand and manipulate virtual objects in a spatial context.

In conclusion, this research makes a significant contribution to the field of vision-language models by identifying and characterizing the limitations of current models in spatial reasoning. The proposed benchmark and the component-specific evaluation provide a valuable tool for researchers and developers to assess and improve the spatial reasoning abilities of VLMs. The findings of the paper have important implications for a wide range of real-world applications and pave the way for future research in this critical area.
