# D3D-GEN: Robot-Aware Domain-Grounded Interactive 3D World Generation for Social Robotics

> [!summary] Summary
> D3D-GEN generates simulator-ready 3D indoor worlds from a natural-language prompt. A domain agent runs grounded web research to build a provenance-tagged constraint database (building codes, robot clearance rules); a RAG pipeline then turns a room-level scene graph into floorplans and collision-checked asset placements exported as world.yaml for Isaac Sim and Gazebo. Across 450 generated worlds it beats floorplan and population baselines on object density, spacing, and perceptual quality.

## Problem

- Real-world scan datasets yield non-watertight meshes with static, non-articulated geometry, needing heavy manual post-processing before physics engines can simulate them.
- Designer-made asset libraries cover only residential rooms, run to hundreds of gigabytes, and do not transfer to hospitals, offices, airports, or warehouses.
- Procedural, diffusion, and agentic generators use fixed asset databases and generic priors, so layouts ignore jurisdiction-specific codes and robot circulation, charging, and docking needs.

## Method

- ==Domain agent==: ==four-stage pipeline== (domain/country inference, 16 search-grounded research calls over eight topics, corpus structuring with URL-merged deduplication, deterministic database synthesis) emitting schema-validated constraints with provenance.
- ==Text-to-Graph== plus ==RAG floorplan generator==: an LLM builds a ==room scene graph== with adjacency edges, robot-role zones (cleaning, service, charging buffer), then emits room and door polygons assembled into Shapely wall geometry.
- ==Asset population==: ==natural-language nearest-neighbor retrieval== over a 259-asset embedded database (bounding box, materials, affordances, face direction), LLM-proposed poses validated by overlap, boundary, and wall-offset constraints with position-search fallback.

## Results

- 450 worlds generated (150 each residential, office, hospital; 50 per 5/8/10-room request) with exact room-count fidelity; office peaks at **8.9** objects per room versus residential **6.0-6.1**.
- Against four baselines on 18 residential worlds, it reaches **54.56** objects, **7.06** objects/room, **9.19 m** spacing, **7.61** visual, **0.400** VQA accuracy, **8.63** scene rating, beating DiffuScene's **46.60** and **8.33**.
- Domain database build costs **0.60-0.80 million** tokens and **184-221 seconds**, yielding **138-172** constraints; hospital covers **82** room types across **9** jurisdictions at **90%** high confidence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Treat regulatory knowledge retrieval as a generation stage, not a hand-curated asset: provenance-tagged constraints make synthetic environments inspectable, citable, and portable to new domains.
> - Making robot infrastructure a first-class research topic (corridor width, dock clearance, staging area) yields simulator worlds usable without post-hoc edits, unlike perceptually optimized generators.
> - Layout-FID rewards similarity to residential training distributions, so it penalizes domain-grounded layouts; distribution-matching metrics can mislead when the goal is operational viability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied AI and social robotics increasingly relies on simulated environments for the training, validation, and benchmarking of robotic agents. A persistent challenge in this area is bridging the "simulation-to-real" (sim2real) gap, where models trained in simulation often exhibit poor transferability to physical environments. The quality, diversity, semantic soundness, and perceptual realism of these simulated 3D environments are critical factors influencing training outcomes and real-world deployment success.

Current approaches to 3D environment generation generally fall into several categories, each with limitations addressed by D3D-GEN:

- **Real-world scan datasets (e.g., HM3D, Matterport3D)**: These provide high spatial complexity but often result in non-watertight meshes that require extensive manual post-processing for physics engine compatibility. They also frequently lack interactive objects, limiting their utility for tasks requiring object manipulation or social navigation.
- **Designer-created datasets (e.g., 3D-FRONT, Structured3D)**: While offering physically sound CAD assets, these datasets tend to feature sanitized, idealized layouts that do not reflect the clutter and nuances of real human spaces. A significant limitation is their domain specificity, often restricted to residential settings, which impedes the training of robots for diverse environments like hospitals or offices. Furthermore, their large size and fixed asset libraries pose barriers to entry and adaptation for new domains.
- **Procedural generation approaches (e.g., ProcTHOR, Infinigen)**: These methods offer scalability and physical soundness but frequently generate sterile, homogeneous layouts, often limited to simple rectangular room geometries. They generally lack the semantic grounding necessary for complex, domain-specific scenarios.
- **Agentic and diffusion-based methods (e.g., PhyScene, SAGE, DiffuScene, RoomDreamer, HouseDiffusion, LayoutGPT, Holodeck)**: These represent more recent advancements, incorporating techniques like differentiable guidance for object placement or LLM-based reasoning for layout synthesis. While these methods improve aspects such as object quantity regulation, collision avoidance, and navigable space preservation, they predominantly operate over fixed asset databases and predefined spatial patterns. This inherent limitation restricts their capacity to generate environments truly grounded in the specific conventions and operational constraints of a target domain. Critically, many do not incorporate robot-operational constraints as first-class generation objectives, necessitating substantial manual effort to prepare generated environments for robot training or benchmarking.

Parallel research has explored **scene-graph representations** to improve robotic reasoning. Efforts like Domain-Conditioned Scene Graphs, FunGraph, SPADE, and TACS-Graphs demonstrate how structured spatial knowledge can enhance task planning, object affordance reasoning, path planning, and localization. However, these methods typically consume pre-existing scene graphs rather than generating the underlying 3D worlds themselves.

D3D-GEN positions itself within this landscape by addressing the critical need for simulation environments that are not only physically interactive and perceptually realistic but also semantically grounded in specific operational domains and explicitly designed with robot-operational constraints in mind. It aims to bridge the gap by combining autonomous domain knowledge acquisition with a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) pipeline to create diverse, valid, and simulator-ready 3D worlds. The work distinguishes itself by moving beyond generic priors and fixed asset databases to offer a user-defined, domain-generalized approach.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of D3D-GEN is to develop a novel system for generating fully interactive, domain-grounded 3D worlds suitable for the training and validation of embodied AI, particularly for social navigation robotics. This objective is motivated by several identified limitations in existing simulation environment generation paradigms:

1. **Bridging the Sim2Real Gap with Realistic and Diverse Environments**: Robotics research requires simulation environments that facilitate the transfer of learned behaviors to the real world. Current approaches often fall short by either lacking sufficient perceptual realism, physical soundness, or diversity. Agents trained on homogeneous environments develop navigation strategies that generalize poorly to new, out-of-distribution spaces. D3D-GEN aims to generate an unlimited amount of realistic, logically sound worlds that capture the variability necessary for robust agent training.

2. **Achieving Semantic Grounding in Specific Domains**: Existing methods frequently produce generic layouts or scenes restricted to a narrow set of domains (e.g., residential). Real-world deployment contexts, such as hospitals, offices, or public spaces, adhere to distinct legal regulations, spatial conventions, and object distributions. The system is motivated to generate environments semantically grounded in the specific conventions of a target domain, ensuring that layouts and object placements reflect real-world operational viability rather than just visual aesthetics.

3. **Incorporating Robot-Operational Constraints as First-Class Objectives**: A significant gap in prior work is the insufficient integration of robot-specific operational requirements during scene generation. D3D-GEN aims to explicitly include constraints related to robot placement, charging, circulation, and docking from the outset. This ensures that generated environments are immediately "robot-ready," reducing the need for manual post-modification to accommodate robotic agents.

4. **Automating Domain Knowledge Acquisition and Integration**: Manually curating domain-specific rules and conventions for each new environment type is labor-intensive and not scalable. A key motivation is to automate the collection and structuring of domain-specific knowledge by framing database construction as an autonomous generation task. This involves conducting grounded web research and synthesizing quantitative rules from authoritative sources into a machine-readable, citable, and reproducible format.

5. **Enabling Natural Language Interaction for Diverse Users**: Technical interfaces for scene generation often require specialized simulation expertise, excluding domain experts who possess critical knowledge of target environments but lack technical proficiency. D3D-GEN aims to provide a natural language interface, allowing both domain experts and laypeople to describe desired worlds, thereby making the generation process more accessible and user-intent driven.

6. **Overcoming Dependence on Fixed 3D Model Databases**: Many systems rely on large, pre-existing asset libraries that are fixed to specific domains (e.g., 3D-FRONT for residential scenes). D3D-GEN seeks to operate without dependence on any fixed 3D model database, instead dynamically querying a user-provided and extensible semantic asset database. This approach allows for greater flexibility and adaptability to new or emerging domains.

In summary, D3D-GEN aims to balance realism and simulability, provide domain generalization, incorporate robot-specific constraints, and democratize 3D world generation through an accessible, natural language interface grounded in verifiable domain knowledge.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

D3D-GEN employs a multi-staged, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) pipeline to transform natural language prompts into executable, physics-aware multi-agent simulations. The system is composed of three main tightly coupled stages: domain database generation, world generation, and scenario generation.

#### 3.1. A. Domain Database Generation

This stage focuses on autonomously constructing a structured knowledge base of domain-specific constraints. Instead of a static, hand-curated database, D3D-GEN synthesizes this information through grounded web research based on a natural language request.

1. **Database Generation Pipeline**: The database is generated via a four-stage, end-to-end pipeline, each implemented as an LLM call:
 - **Domain & Country Inference**: The user prompt is classified into a canonical triple (domain, country, country\_code), where the domain is one of {residential, office, hospital}.
 - **Grounded Web Research**: For eight predefined topics (e.g., room types, size ranges, adjacency rules, fire safety, accessibility, common objects, robot infrastructure), the pipeline issues Gemini calls with Google Search grounding. This process is repeated for *k* passes (default *k*=2), retrieving public standards, codes, guidelines, and research documents, returning raw notes with embedded URLs.
 - **Corpus Structuring**: Raw notes are chunked, converted into typed documents following a corpus schema, and merged if they share the same URL. Metadata is unified, and constraints are deduplicated.
 - **Database Synthesis**: A deterministic function transforms the structured corpus into the final database. This involves generating stable reference slugs, mapping document types to canonical source types, normalizing constraint values (scalars, ranges, algebraic expressions, lookup tables), and attaching provenance links. The output can be schema-validated.

2. **Database Schema**: The final database adheres to a strict JSON schema, comprising "Metadata" (provenance, generation timestamp, model identity, building-type scope) and "References" (an array of reference objects). Each reference object groups a source document with its extracted constraints, which are polymorphic in value type.

3. **Robot Infrastructure**: A dedicated research topic focuses on robot placement, charging, circulation, and docking constraints. These robot-specific rules (e.g., charging-dock clearance, corridor widths for robot traffic, line-of-sight requirements) are automatically retrieved and injected into subsequent generation prompts, ensuring floorplans are "robot-ready."

4. **Constraint Retrieval**: At floorplan generation time, a RAG retriever selects relevant constraints from the generated database in two steps: building-type classification (matching room labels to domain keywords) and constraint filtering/formatting (rendering selected constraints as natural language bullets for LLM injection).

#### 3.2. B. Generation Pipeline

This multi-staged pipeline leverages both the domain database and an asset database to generate floorplans and object placements.

1. **Asset Database**: This is an automatically processed, multi-format (mainly USDZ) dataset of 3D models, queryable via natural language. For each asset, the database stores annotations such as bounding box, material list, color palette, human-object interactions (HOI) affordance tags, face direction for consistent orientation, semantic tags (domain, category), and a free-text description. These annotations are concatenated into a single text embedding, enabling nearest-neighbor retrieval for natural-language asset queries. The current database covers residential, office, and hospital domains with a total of 259 assets.

2. **Text-to-Graph**: Given a natural language prompt (e.g., "Design an office floorplan for 60 staff"), this module generates a scene graph G = (V, E). Vertices (V) represent rooms with unique identifiers, semantic room-type labels, preliminary asset lists (enriched with room-type keywords, including robot-specific assets), and natural-language descriptions. Edges (E) encode adjacency and door relationships. The scene graph also includes robot-operation semantic annotations on nodes and edges, creating typed regions (e.g., `cleaning_zone`, `service_zone`, `charging_buffer`) and linking them to traversable corridor/door edges.

3. **Floorplan Generation**: The scene graph is fed to a three-phase RAG pipeline:
 - **Preprocess**: The building type and RAG database are retrieved. Relevant constraints from the knowledge base are formatted as natural language and injected into the LLM prompt.
 - **Inference**: The structured prompt with RAG context is submitted to an LLM, which returns a JSON object containing polygon coordinates for rooms and doors.
 - **Post-processing**: The LLM response is parsed to construct Shapely geometries. A `WorldDescription` is assembled, with wall segments derived from room polygons and door openings. Materials are assigned based on room type.

4. **Asset Population**: Following floorplan generation, this three-part pipeline populates the rooms with objects, ensuring collision-free 2D poses.
 - **Asset Retrieval**: The pipeline obtains the building type and maps scene graph assets to real 3D models from the asset database. Room-type priors are used to fill asset gaps. Candidate ranking combines lexical relevance (text similarity), room-type compatibility, and asset sizes.
 - **LLM-guided Placement Proposal**: The placement model receives room boundaries, door geometries, clearance instructions, ranked candidate assets with dimensions, target asset count, and room semantics. It outputs a JSON list of proposed placements, which are validated against hard constraints (no overlapping, compliance with room boundaries, wall offsets). If validation fails, a position search is performed.
 - **Output Canonicalization**: Accepted placements are exported as obstacle entities with unique object identifiers and domain-relative model paths. The populated room list is then serialized into the final `WorldDescription` artifact, aligning with the generated floorplan graph.

Finally, the RAG pipeline exports the generated world in a `world.yaml` file format. This file includes zone geometry, wall material specifications, door dimensions/poses, semantic names (e.g., `patient_room_01`), and populated assets. This structure ensures that Regions of Interest (ROIs) and Points of Interest (POIs) for behavior specifications are grounded in geometrically valid and standards-compliant regions. The system also includes a local web frontend for interactive world generation and feedback.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

D3D-GEN was evaluated on its capacity to generate plausible results in common domains (residential) and extend to less-common transfer domains (commercial office, hospital). A total of 450 worlds were generated across these three domains, with 150 samples for each, further subdivided by requested room counts (5, 8, and 10 rooms). Quantitative metrics were extracted from `world.yaml` files, and room polygon areas were computed using the Shoelace formula.

#### 4.1. A. Domain-Agent Execution Profile and Database Quality

The domain-agent execution profile (Table II) indicates the computational resources and time required for database generation. For residential, office, and hospital domains, total token usage (input + output) ranged from 0.60 MTok to 0.80 MTok, with total execution times between 183.8 seconds and 221.4 seconds. Research (web grounding) was the most time-consuming stage (148.5s to 174.3s).

The generated database quality and size metrics (Table III) demonstrate the system's ability to synthesize structured knowledge:
- Raw notes varied from 80,788 (hospital) to 104,200 (office) characters.
- The system generated 24 to 41 deduplicated corpus documents and final references.
- Total constraints ranged from 138 (residential) to 172 (office).
- Unique room types covered varied significantly, from 12 (residential) to 82 (hospital), indicating the depth of domain knowledge captured for more complex environments.
- Confidence splits showed high proportions of high/medium confidence constraints across domains, with hospital environments achieving the highest confidence (90% high).
- Database file sizes were modest, ranging from 86 KB to 116 KB.

These metrics suggest that the autonomous database generation pipeline is effective in collecting and structuring diverse, domain-specific constraints efficiently.

#### 4.2. B. Quantitative Metrics and Multi-Method Comparison

D3D-GEN was benchmarked against four baselines (HouseDiffusion, Holodeck for floorplan-only; DiffuScene for population-only; ProcTHOR for combined generation) on 18 residential worlds (Table IV).
- **Object Count**: D3D-GEN generated the highest average object count per world (54.56), compared to ProcTHOR (3.73) and DiffuScene (46.60).
- **Objects per Room**: It also showed the highest object density (7.06 objects/room).
- **Object Spacing**: D3D-GEN achieved the highest average object spacing (9.19 m), suggesting effective use of space while avoiding clutter.
- **Perceptual Metrics (Gemini-based)**:
 - **Visual Quality**: D3D-GEN scored highest at 7.61.
 - **VQA Accuracy**: D3D-GEN achieved the highest accuracy at 0.400.
 - **Scene Rating**: D3D-GEN received the highest average scene rating (8.63).
 - **Layout-FID**: D3D-GEN's score (5377.19) was competitive, performing better than HouseDiffusion and ProcTHOR, though not as low as DiffuScene or Holodeck.

Table V provides a configuration-level summary from 50 generated worlds per configuration (domain x room count):
- **Room Count Fidelity**: The system consistently achieved perfect room-count fidelity, matching the requested number of rooms exactly in all cases.
- **Total Area Scaling**: Office and hospital worlds exhibited more substantial total area expansion compared to residential layouts, reflecting larger functional rooms and greater circulation requirements typical of these domains. For instance, a 10-room office world had an average area of 365 m², while a 10-room residential world had 222 m².
- **Topological Complexity**: Hospital environments consistently showed the highest topological complexity, characterized by the largest numbers of doors and wall segments, which aligns with their inherent corridor-loop structures and complex care-workflow constraints.

These quantitative findings indicate that D3D-GEN generates structurally richer and perceptually more realistic worlds compared to the evaluated baselines, while adhering to requested parameters.

#### 4.3. C. Qualitative Comparison with Baselines

Qualitative analysis (Figure 5) highlighted D3D-GEN's ability to address common failure modes observed in baselines.

1. **Floorplan Generation**:
 - D3D-GEN maintained clear adjacency logic and coherent transitions between private and shared spaces, which is beneficial for social navigation tasks.
 - Robot-path semantics were consistent with domain intent: office scenes featured broad cleaning loops through open work areas, while hospital scenes prioritized service routes connecting nursing stations, patient rooms, and supply rooms with fewer unnecessary detours.
 - Baselines often exhibited issues such as incorrect or missing door placements, coordinates not adhering to actual rules, and scalability challenges with increasing room counts. D3D-GEN demonstrated robustness in these areas, generating logically consistent layouts.

2. **Asset Population**:
 - **Object Density**: Office environments were the most cluttered (up to 8.9 objects per room), while residential scenes were comparatively sparse, reflecting real-world trends.
 - **Material Diversity**: Hospital scenes exhibited less material diversity, consistent with functional and hygiene requirements, while residential and office environments used broader palettes.
 - **Functional Consistency**: Object groupings in D3D-GEN were functionally consistent with room semantics (e.g., workstation clusters in offices, treatment-support groupings in hospitals). This was achieved while preserving usable free space for movement.
 - **Robot-Operational Semantics**: Cleaning robots were assigned larger cleanable areas (e.g., open-plan offices, lobby loops), and service robots were assigned targeted service areas near relevant assets (e.g., beds, nurse counters), minimizing cross-traffic with human circulation. This balance between semantic richness and clear space contributes to both visual plausibility and simulation readiness.
 - D3D-GEN showed improved object handling and grouping, as well as better knowledge of asset rotation concerning walls and room boundaries. Robot charging rooms and clearance zones were explicitly designated by D3D-GEN.

Overall, the findings demonstrate D3D-GEN's capability to generate diverse, plausible, and robot-aware 3D worlds that address the shortcomings of prior methods in terms of domain generalization, semantic grounding, and operational viability for embodied AI deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

D3D-GEN presents a notable advancement in the generation of 3D simulation environments for embodied AI and social robotics, offering several significant contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Realism and Domain Generalization**: The system addresses a critical gap by providing a mechanism to generate physically plausible and semantically sound 3D environments across a diverse range of domains (residential, office, hospital). Unlike approaches confined to specific datasets or generic priors, D3D-GEN's ability to autonomously collect and integrate domain-specific knowledge allows for the creation of environments that accurately reflect real-world conventions and regulations. This domain generalization capability is crucial for training robots that need to operate effectively in varied real-world settings.

2. **Improved Simulation-to-Real Transfer**: By generating environments that are not only perceptually realistic but also operationally viable and semantically grounded, D3D-GEN can contribute to mitigating the sim2real gap. Robots trained in these contextually rich and constraint-aware simulations are more likely to develop robust behaviors that transfer effectively to physical deployments. The inclusion of interactive objects and physically sound layouts further enhances the fidelity of the simulated experience.

3. **Robot-Centric Design and Deployment Readiness**: A core impact of D3D-GEN is its explicit integration of robot-operational constraints (e.g., charging zones, circulation pathways, clearance requirements) directly into the generation process. This ensures that the produced environments are "robot-ready" from the outset, significantly reducing the manual effort and expertise typically required to adapt generic 3D scenes for robotic training or benchmarking. This feature positions D3D-GEN as a valuable tool for roboticists developing and evaluating social robots.

4. **Accessibility and Democratization of Environment Generation**: The natural language interface allows domain experts, who may lack specialized simulation expertise, to describe and generate environments based on their in-depth understanding of real-world contexts. This democratization of 3D world creation can accelerate research and development in robotics by making advanced simulation tools accessible to a broader range of stakeholders.

5. **Scalability and Reproducibility**: The autonomous nature of the domain database generation pipeline, combined with its ability to produce dozens of distinct environments for each domain, indicates high scalability. The explicit provenance (source URL, publisher, jurisdiction, confidence level) attached to extracted constraints enhances the inspectability, citability, and reproducibility of the generated knowledge and, by extension, the environments themselves.

6. **Foundation for Advanced Embodied AI Tasks**: The generated worlds are fully interactive and loadable into popular simulators like Isaac Sim and Gazebo. Furthermore, the inclusion of semantic annotations and region-of-interest (ROI) definitions provides a strong foundation for grounding complex navigation and behavior anchoring tasks for multi-agent human-robot interaction scenarios.

While the system currently relies on an existing asset database, the authors acknowledge this limitation and propose future work on integrating procedural generation of articulated assets for arbitrary domains, further enhancing the system's autonomy and scalability. The potential for interactive modification of the domain database in the future could also empower users to refine generated knowledge graphs, tailoring environments even more precisely to specific research needs. D3D-GEN represents a comprehensive framework for creating high-quality, task-specific simulation environments, which can facilitate significant progress in embodied AI research and the deployment of social robots.
