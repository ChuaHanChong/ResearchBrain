# D-Cubed: Latent Diffusion Trajectory Optimisation for Dexterous Deformable Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> D-Cubed is a method that integrates a latent diffusion model with gradient-free guided sampling for dexterous deformable object manipulation. It leverages a task-agnostic play dataset to learn a skill-latent space and demonstrates superior performance compared to existing methods across six challenging manipulation tasks, including qualitative transfer to a real robot.

## Problem

- Dexterous manipulation of deformable objects is challenging due to their high-dimensional state space and complex multi-contact interactions.
- Traditional trajectory optimization methods struggle with the vast search space, numerous non-linear contacts, and sparse or uninformative gradients in deformable manipulation.
- Prior work often requires expensive, task-specific expert demonstrations or relies on noisy/inaccurate gradients from differentiable physics simulators.

## Method

- A task-agnostic "play dataset" of general robot hand motions is collected, and a ==VAE== learns a compact, meaningful ==skill-latent space== for short-horizon action sequences.
- A ==latent diffusion model (LDM)== is trained on these learned skill-latent representations to compose long-horizon skill trajectories, generating diverse and coherent robot hand movements.
- A novel ==gradient-free guided sampling== method, leveraging a ==Cross-Entropy Method (CEM)==-like approach within the LDM's reverse diffusion process, iteratively selects and refines trajectories based on task cost, bypassing simulator gradients.

## Results

- D-Cubed consistently outperformed all competitive baselines across six challenging dexterous deformable manipulation tasks, achieving significantly higher normalized EMD improvements.
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of the skill-latent space for performance and demonstrated that augmenting D-Cubed with simulator gradients did not yield significant improvement, validating the gradient-free approach.
- Qualitative real-world experiments showed successful sim-to-real transfer of an optimized open-loop action sequence to a physical LEAP hand for a "Flip" task.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning a structured "skill-latent space" from a general play dataset effectively prunes the vast action space, allowing for more efficient exploration of meaningful robot motions.
> - A Latent Diffusion Model can effectively compose these learned skills into long-horizon trajectories, capturing the multi-modal distribution of complex manipulation behaviors.
> - Gradient-free guidance during reverse diffusion, specifically a CEM-like approach, can robustly optimize for deformable manipulation tasks, overcoming the limitations of noisy or sparse gradients from differentiable physics simulators.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous robotic manipulation is a field that has seen significant advancements, particularly in tasks involving rigid objects. However, real-world applications frequently require robots to manipulate deformable objects, such as fabrics, soft tissues, or dough. This domain presents substantial challenges due to the complex, high-dimensional state space of deformable objects and the intricacies of multi-contact manipulation with high-degree-of-freedom (DoF) robot hands.

Traditional trajectory optimization approaches, common in rigid object manipulation, often prove inadequate for deformable tasks. The difficulties arise from the vast search space, the large number and non-linearity of contacts, and the limited task information typically provided by cost functions. For instance, a cost function based on the distance to a target shape offers no guidance when the robot is not in contact with the object, leading to sparse or uninformative gradients. Previous attempts to mitigate this by using expert demonstrations are often deemed prohibitively expensive and difficult to scale across various tasks. Reinforcement Learning (RL) also struggles with the extensive sample requirements, especially with the infinite degrees of freedom characteristic of deformable objects.

Recent advancements in generative models, particularly diffusion models, have shown promise in synthesizing data, including trajectories. Classifier guidance, a technique to steer diffusion models using gradients, has been applied to trajectory generation. However, this method faces difficulties when gradients are inaccurate or noisy, a common issue with differentiable physics simulators used for deformable object manipulation.

This work, D-Cubed, positions itself as a method that addresses these specific limitations. It seeks to develop a robust trajectory optimization framework for dexterous deformable manipulation without relying on costly task-specific expert demonstrations or problematic gradient-based guidance. Instead, D-Cubed leverages a latent diffusion model trained on a general, task-agnostic "play dataset" and introduces a gradient-free guidance mechanism, aiming to achieve efficient exploration and effective optimization in these challenging high-dimensional spaces. By focusing on skill-latent representations, it seeks to ensure that even noisy explorations correspond to meaningful robot hand motions, distinguishing it from methods that sample low-level actions directly.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an effective trajectory optimization method for long-horizon dexterous manipulation of deformable objects, overcoming existing challenges in the field.

The key motivations driving this work include:

1. **Addressing the Gap in Deformable Object Manipulation:** While robot manipulation of rigid objects has advanced considerably, the ability to dexterously handle deformable objects remains a significant hurdle. Many real-world applications, from industrial tasks like manufacturing to domestic chores and medical procedures, involve soft or pliable materials. Developing robust solutions for these scenarios is crucial for broader robotic adoption.
2. **Overcoming Trajectory Optimization Limitations:** Traditional trajectory optimization methods struggle with the complexities inherent in deformable object manipulation. These complexities include:
 - **Large Search Space:** The combination of a high-DoF robot hand and the infinite-dimensional state of deformable objects creates an extremely vast space of possible actions and configurations.
 - **Numerous Contacts:** Manipulating deformable objects often involves a high number of intricate, non-linear contacts, which are challenging for optimization algorithms to manage.
 - **Limited Task Information:** Cost functions, typically based on the distance to a target object shape, provide sparse or uninformative feedback, especially when the robot is not in direct contact with the object or when local optima prevent progress. This sparsity hinders gradient-based optimization.
3. **Reducing Reliance on Expensive Expert Demonstrations:** Many prior works address deformable manipulation by requiring expert demonstrations for each specific task. Collecting such demonstrations is time-consuming, difficult, and not scalable to a wide variety of tasks. The authors aim to develop a method that circumvents this requirement.
4. **Leveraging and Improving Diffusion Models for Planning:** Diffusion models have demonstrated strong capabilities in generating diverse and high-quality data. The motivation is to harness this generative power for action trajectory synthesis. However, existing gradient-based classifier guidance methods for diffusion models are susceptible to inaccurate or noisy gradients, particularly those derived from differentiable physics simulators, which are common in deformable object research. This motivates the development of a gradient-free guidance mechanism.
5. **Enhancing Exploration Efficiency:** In high-dimensional action spaces, random sampling of low-level actions is often inefficient as many sampled trajectories may be meaningless. The authors are motivated to structure the exploration by learning a "skill-latent space" that encodes meaningful short-horizon robot motions, ensuring that exploration efforts are directed towards functional behaviors.

In summary, the core motivation is to enable dexterous robots to robustly manipulate deformable objects in complex, long-horizon tasks by developing an efficient, scalable, and gradient-robust trajectory optimization framework based on latent diffusion models and skill-based exploration.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

D-Cubed (Latent Diffusion Trajectory Optimisation for Dexterous Deformable Manipulation) is a novel trajectory optimization method designed to address the challenges of dexterous manipulation of deformable objects. It integrates a latent diffusion model (LDM) with a gradient-free guided sampling strategy based on the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM). The approach consists of three main components: data collection and skill-latent space learning, training a latent diffusion model, and a gradient-free guided sampling method for trajectory optimization.

#### 3.1. Data Collection

To avoid the need for task-specific expert demonstrations, D-Cubed utilizes a single, *task-agnostic play dataset* of robot hand trajectories. This dataset contains a wide range of representative robot hand motions, such as opening and closing the hand, moving individual fingers, and flexing the wrist. The data is collected by mimicking human hand motions, tracking them with a RealSense D435 camera, and re-targeting the human hand pose to a robot hand within the SAPIEN simulator. The collection process is relatively efficient, taking approximately 20 minutes to gather 50,000 data points. This approach aims to provide a diverse foundation of general hand movements that can be reused across multiple manipulation tasks.

#### 3.2. Latent Diffusion Model as Skill Sampler

The large search space of dexterous deformable manipulation makes direct sampling of low-level actions inefficient. D-Cubed addresses this by learning a structured *skill-latent space* and then training an LDM to compose these skills.

1. **Skill-Latent Space Learning (VAE):** A Variational Autoencoder (VAE) is trained to learn a compact skill-latent representation, `z`, for short-horizon action sequences `a_{t:t+H}` (where `H=10`) sampled from the task-agnostic play dataset. The VAE comprises an encoder `q_enc` and a decoder `p_dec`, optimized using the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) objective. The VAE ensures that each skill latent `z` corresponds to a meaningful and smooth low-level robot action sequence, thus making exploration in the latent space more effective than in the raw action space. The VAE encoder and decoder are implemented using 4-layer LSTMs.

2. **Latent Diffusion Model (LDM) Training:** An LDM is subsequently trained to compose these learned skill-latent representations into *long-horizon skill trajectories* `z_{1:T_skill}`, where `T_skill = T/H` is the total number of skills required for a full task horizon `T`. The LDM is trained to predict the clean skill trajectory `z^0` from a noisy version `z^i` at different diffusion steps `i`. This generative model is capable of capturing complex multi-modal distributions present in the play dataset. A transformer architecture (specifically, a variant of NanoGPT) is used as the backbone for the LDM's noise prediction model. The LDM's ability to generate diverse sequences of meaningful skills is crucial for efficiently exploring the task solution space.

#### 3.3. Trajectory Optimisation using Gradient-Free Guided Sampling

To find a performant action trajectory for a specific target task, D-Cubed introduces a novel *gradient-free guided sampling method* that employs a variation of the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) within the LDM's reverse diffusion process. This method is designed to overcome issues associated with noisy or uninformative gradients from differentiable physics simulators.

The reverse diffusion process is viewed as analogous to iterative CEM optimization steps:

1. **Initialization and Exploration:** At the beginning of the reverse process (`i=N`), a batch (B) of noisy skill-latent trajectories `z_{1:T_skill_N}` is sampled from a Gaussian distribution (initially `N(0, I)`). Due to the high variance in early diffusion steps, these samples enable broad exploration of the solution space. Each noisy skill trajectory, when decoded by the VAE, still represents a sequence of meaningful short-horizon robot motions.

2. **Evaluation:** Each of the `B` sampled skill-latent trajectories is decoded into low-level action trajectories using the VAE decoder. These action trajectories are then executed and evaluated within a simulator using a pre-defined task-informed cost function, which is the Sinkhorn Divergence (an approximation of Earth-Mover Distance) measuring the difference between the manipulated and target object shapes.

3. **Selection and Guidance:** From the batch of evaluated trajectories, the one with the *lowest task cost* (`z_{1:T_skill_best}`) is selected. This "best" trajectory guides the subsequent denoising process.

4. **Denoising and Refinement:** The LDM uses this `z_{1:T_skill_best}` as input to predict the mean `μ_i` of the Gaussian distribution for the *next* denoising step (`i-1`). The variance `σ^2_{i-1}` for this distribution is determined by the pre-defined noise schedule of the diffusion process. Intuitively, the LDM gradually removes noise from the selected performant trajectory, refining it towards lower-cost solutions.

5. **Best Mean Update Mechanism:** To prevent degradation in performance if the LDM makes a poor prediction, the mean of the distribution for the next step (`μ_best`) is only updated if the currently predicted mean (`μ_i`) yields a lower cost than the previously recorded best mean. This mechanism, inspired by variants of CEM, ensures that the optimization process continually focuses on improving trajectory performance.

The process iteratively refines the skill trajectory, leveraging the LDM's generative capabilities for exploration and the CEM-like selection for exploitation, until a clean, high-performing action sequence is obtained. For dual-hand tasks, the LDM generates individual trajectories for each arm.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of D-Cubed focused on its effectiveness in generating trajectories for dexterous deformable object manipulation, comparison with baseline methods, and the importance of its design choices. The evaluation was conducted on a public benchmark of six challenging dexterous deformable object manipulation tasks (Folding, Flip, Wrap, Dumpling, Bun, Rope) using a differentiable physics simulator. The primary evaluation metric was the normalized improvement in Earth-Mover Distance (EMD), where a score of 1 indicates a perfect match to the target shape.

1. **Outperformance Against Baselines:**
 - D-Cubed consistently outperformed all competitive baselines across all six tasks by a significant margin.
 - Traditional trajectory optimization methods, including gradient-based (Grad TrajOpt) and sampling-based (MPPI), demonstrated limited success, often yielding scores close to zero. This indicates their struggle with the large search space and sparse task information.
 - Skill-based MPPI, which uses the learned skill-latent space but without the LDM's generative capabilities, also performed poorly. This highlights the crucial role of the LDM in generating *meaningful sequences of skills* that effectively explore the solution space, rather than just using a skill-latent space with unconstrained sampling.
 - Reinforcement Learning (PPO) showed better performance than basic trajectory optimization on some tasks but still fell short of D-Cubed, suggesting its difficulty in exploring vast state spaces for deformable objects.
 - LDM with Classifier Guidance, which attempted to use simulator gradients for guidance, performed poorly. This confirms the hypothesis that noisy and sparse gradients from differentiable physics simulators are ineffective for guiding diffusion models in these complex manipulation tasks, especially when no contact is made.
 - Diffusion-ES, a concurrent gradient-free method based on evolutionary search, also struggled to achieve competitive performance. The paper attributes this to Diffusion-ES's method of small perturbations which limits effective exploration, especially when initial trajectories fail to make object contact. D-Cubed's early-stage noisy skill trajectory sampling promotes broader exploration.

2. **Effectiveness of Gradient-Free Guided Sampling:**
 - D-Cubed's novel gradient-free approach successfully guided the LDM to converge towards high-performing trajectories. The iterative selection of the best trajectory from a batch of noisy samples effectively combined exploration and refinement.

3. **Ablation Studies on Design Decisions:**
 - **Number of Sampled Trajectories:** Increasing the number of trajectories sampled in each reverse diffusion step (exploration budget) significantly improved performance. This indicates that broader exploration within the CEM-like guidance mechanism leads to better solutions.
 - **Additional Gradient Guidance:** When D-Cubed was augmented with classifier guidance using simulator gradients, no statistically significant improvement in performance was observed. This further validates the choice of a gradient-free approach and supports the argument that gradients from differentiable simulators are often sparse and uninformative for these tasks.
 - **Role of Skill-Latent Representations:** The use of a pre-trained skill-latent space substantially improved task performance, particularly on more challenging tasks like "Rope" and "Wrap." While the impact was less dramatic on simpler tasks like "Flip" and "Folding," the overall trend suggests that structuring the action space with meaningful skills is crucial for effective exploration in hard-exploration problems.

4. **Performance Improvement During Diffusion Process:**
 - Analysis of EMD improvement over diffusion timesteps (Figure 7 in Appendix A.1) showed a rapid increase in performance during the initial steps, indicating successful establishment of contact and gross manipulation. This was followed by a more gradual refinement of the object shape in later steps, demonstrating the continuous optimization capability of the method.

5. **Qualitative Real-World Transfer:**
 - A qualitative experiment on a real-world LEAP hand for the "Flip" task demonstrated successful transferability. The open-loop action sequence optimized in simulation enabled the physical robot hand to manipulate the deformable object, achieving a similar deformation and folding as observed in the simulation. This provides preliminary evidence for the sim-to-real potential of the approach.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The D-Cubed research makes several significant contributions to the field of robotic manipulation, with notable potential impacts:

1. **Advancing Deformable Object Manipulation:** This work represents a considerable step forward in enabling robots to dexterously manipulate deformable objects, a long-standing challenge in robotics. By offering a robust method that consistently outperforms existing techniques, D-Cubed expands the range of tasks robots can potentially address. This has implications for industries such as manufacturing (e.g., handling flexible components), healthcare (e.g., surgical tasks involving soft tissues), and consumer robotics (e.g., folding laundry, food preparation).

2. **Reducing Reliance on Costly Expert Data:** A key impact of D-Cubed is its ability to learn from a task-agnostic "play dataset" rather than requiring expensive, task-specific expert demonstrations. This greatly improves the scalability and practicality of deploying robots for new deformable manipulation tasks, as data collection becomes significantly less burdensome. This makes complex dexterous tasks more accessible for learning-based control.

3. **Robust Trajectory Optimization in Challenging Domains:** The introduction of a novel gradient-free guided sampling method within the reverse diffusion process is significant. It provides a robust alternative to gradient-based optimization, particularly valuable in scenarios where gradients are sparse, noisy, or unreliable (e.g., from complex physics simulators or real-world interactions without precise analytical models). This method could be applicable to other high-dimensional control problems where direct gradient signals are problematic.

4. **Efficient Exploration in High-Dimensional Spaces:** The combination of learning a skill-latent space and using an LDM to compose meaningful skill trajectories allows for highly efficient exploration. Instead of sampling arbitrary low-level actions, D-Cubed focuses its search on sequences of coherent robot motions. This structured exploration is critical for solving tasks with vast search spaces, enabling the discovery of effective manipulation strategies that might otherwise be missed.

5. **Paving the Way for Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The qualitative demonstration of transferring an optimized trajectory to a real-world LEAP hand suggests the potential for D-Cubed to bridge the sim-to-real gap. While currently open-loop and evaluated on a simplified task, this successful transfer indicates that the learned latent representations and optimization strategies are robust enough to generalize to physical systems, offering a promising direction for real-world deployment.

**Potential Future Impact and Limitations:**
While significant, the work also highlights areas for future impact and current limitations.
- **Closed-Loop Control:** The current open-loop nature of D-Cubed is a limitation in real-world environments, where discrepancies between simulation and reality, or unexpected events, require reactive adaptation. Future work aiming to distill these trajectories into a reactive policy could lead to truly robust systems.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The time taken to generate trajectories is heavily dependent on simulator speed. Improvements in parallel simulation technologies could further accelerate the optimization process, making D-Cubed more practical for real-time applications.
- **Generalizability to Complex Real-World Physics:** Some benchmark tasks simplify real-world physics (e.g., object interpenetration). Addressing these physical complexities in simulation and validating transfer to a wider range of real-world tasks will be crucial for broader adoption.

Overall, D-Cubed offers a compelling framework for tackling dexterous deformable object manipulation, leveraging the power of generative models and robust optimization techniques to move closer to general-purpose robotic manipulation in complex, unstructured environments.
