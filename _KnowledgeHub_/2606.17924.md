# PearlVLA: Progressive Embodied Action-Plan Refinement in Latent Space

> [!summary] Summary
> PearlVLA introduces a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) framework that progressively refines robot action plans within a model's latent space, guided by imagined future observations from a world model. This approach achieves a 98.7% success rate on the LIBERO benchmark and improves robustness, enabling more anticipatory and efficient robotic manipulation.

## Problem

- Current VLA models face a trade-off between efficient action generation (low latency) and explicit, deliberative planning (anticipation, self-correction).
- Existing efficient methods lack opportunities for internal deliberation, while explicit reasoning through textual chains or visual subgoals incurs significant latency and computational overhead.
- A mechanism was needed to perform sophisticated, 'System 2-style' deliberation for robot control without sacrificing real-time execution efficiency.

## Method

- **PearlVLA** integrates an iterative action-plan refinement loop within the latent space of an OpenVLA-style Vision-Language Model, bypassing external reasoning modules.
- A ==plan-conditioned world query== probes a frozen latent world model to generate action-free future observation latents, which provide feedback for progressive plan refinement.
- An anchored residual update scheme with a scheduled write-back stabilizes the refinement, and a reinforcement learning stage (CRG-PRL) optimizes the latent plan trajectory using group-relative process rewards.

## Results

- Achieved a **98.7%** average success rate on the LIBERO benchmark, outperforming previous state-of-the-art VLA models (e.g., OpenVLA-OFT at **97.1%**).
- Demonstrated improved robustness on LIBERO-Plus (**76.3%** success vs. OpenVLA-OFT's **69.6%**) and strong few-shot generalization on the complex RoboCasa benchmark (**52.9%**).
- Showed that increasing refinement rounds progressively improves success rates (e.g., **96.8%** to **98.5%** for K=0 to K=4), and the system maintains a practical inference throughput of **27.5** Hz.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Progressive, future-guided refinement of action plans entirely within the VLM's latent space can significantly enhance performance and robustness without relying on external or explicit reasoning.
> - An action-free latent world model can effectively provide anticipatory feedback, enabling a policy to 'preview' the implications of its evolving plan.
> - Optimizing the iterative latent refinement process itself using process rewards and group-relative advantages can further improve task success over longer horizons and complex scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied intelligence, particularly concerning Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, aims to enable robots to interpret natural language instructions and execute complex manipulation tasks based on visual observations and robot states. Current VLA models exhibit a trade-off between the efficiency of action generation and the depth of explicit deliberation.

Existing VLA approaches can be broadly categorized. One category prioritizes low-latency control by directly decoding actions from vision-language backbone representations. While efficient, these methods offer limited opportunities for internal deliberation, preventing the policy from anticipating future states or self-correcting plans before execution.

A second category incorporates explicit reasoning mechanisms to enhance planning. These include methods that use textual reasoning chains, predict pixel-level subgoals, or perform action search with a world model. While these techniques can improve planning quality, they typically incur significant latency and computational costs. Textual reasoning and visual subgoals often involve inference steps external to the action decoder, while pixel-level future predictions can be distant from continuous control requirements. Evaluating numerous candidate actions with a world model can also scale poorly with the number of candidates, rollout horizon, and action dimensionality.

Recent advancements in VLA models have explored utilizing future-aware representations, such as predictive features from video diffusion models, as policy context or auxiliary imagination. Simultaneously, the broader field of language models has seen a shift towards latent reasoning, where intermediate computations occur within a model's internal states rather than being fully verbalized as natural language chains. This suggests that explicit, human-readable reasoning might not be a prerequisite for effective deliberation.

PearlVLA addresses this landscape by investigating whether efficient and progressive action-plan refinement can be performed entirely within the latent space of a VLM, without relying on external textual reasoning, pixel-level subgoal reconstruction, or explicit action-space search. This approach positions PearlVLA as a system that internalizes "System 2-style" deliberation—a slower, more deliberate thinking process—within the policy's forward pass, aiming to combine sophisticated reasoning with the execution efficiency required for real-time robotic control. It contrasts with methods that primarily focus on more expressive action decoders or external reasoning chains.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind PearlVLA is to overcome the inherent trade-off in current VLA models between efficient action generation and explicit, deliberative planning. While direct action decoding offers low latency, it lacks foresight and self-correction capabilities crucial for long-horizon and complex manipulation tasks. Conversely, explicit reasoning through textual chains, visual subgoals, or action search, while beneficial for planning, introduces substantial computational overhead and latency, making real-time control challenging.

The key objectives of this research are:

1. **Develop a VLA framework for internal latent deliberation:** To design a mechanism where action-plan deliberation occurs entirely within the latent space of a Vision-Language Model, rather than relying on external reasoning modules or explicit textual/visual representations. This aims to bridge the gap between reactive control and deliberate planning.
2. **Establish closed-loop, future-guided refinement:** To implement a system where a policy can progressively refine its latent action plan by obtaining feedback from an imagined future. This involves generating a plan-conditioned world query, probing a frozen latent world model for an action-free future observation latent, and feeding this information back to guide subsequent plan refinements. The goal is to allow the policy to "preview" the implications of its evolving plan.
3. **Ensure stability and trainability of the refinement process:** To design an anchored residual update scheme and scheduled residual write-back. This is intended to stabilize the iterative refinement loop, prevent latent plan drift, and enable progressive coarse-to-fine adjustments, ensuring that world queries remain within the world model's pre-trained condition space.
4. **Optimize the refinement trajectory with reward-guided learning:** To introduce a reinforcement learning stage, Causal Refinement-Grouped Process-Reward RL (CRG-PRL), specifically designed to optimize the sequence of latent plan edits. This objective aims to enhance the refinement process by using group-relative rewards derived from imagined future frames, without requiring a learned value function or critic, thereby improving task success over longer horizons.
5. **Achieve state-of-the-art performance with efficient execution:** To demonstrate that the proposed PearlVLA framework, by integrating progressive future-guided latent refinement and reward-guided optimization, can achieve high success rates on challenging embodied manipulation benchmarks like LIBERO, while maintaining the low-latency execution path characteristic of direct VLA policies through parallel action-chunk decoding.

Ultimately, the research seeks to enable embodied agents to perform efficient, anticipatory planning by internalizing computation and self-correction within the VLM's latent space.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PearlVLA operates by introducing an iterative action-plan refinement loop within the latent space of a Vision-Language Model (VLM) before action decoding. The framework utilizes a combination of supervised learning and reinforcement learning to optimize this process.

#### 3.1. VLM Backbone and Latent Plan Initialization

PearlVLA is built upon an OpenVLA-style backbone, specifically OpenVLA-7B. This backbone processes multimodal inputs including visual observations (third-person and wrist images), language instructions, and a history of robot proprioceptive states (7-D). A transformer-based proprio encoder embeds the proprioceptive history. The multimodal input sequence `[BOS][vision][proprio][text][meta_query]` is used, with learnable meta-query tokens appended. These meta-query tokens are processed with a prefix-LM attention mask, and their final-layer representations (`z_meta`) are split into two components:
- `z_vis`: Read-only visual grounding tokens, which remain fixed throughout the refinement rounds.
- `~z_0`: Initial writable plan tokens.
To enhance robustness and provide stochasticity for later stages, `~z_0` is perturbed with a small Gaussian noise to obtain `z_0`, the starting point for latent plan refinement.

#### 3.2. Plan-Conditioned World Query

At each refinement round `k`, the current latent plan `z_k` is used to construct a plan-conditioned world query `q_k`. This is achieved by:
1. Projecting the fixed visual grounding tokens (`z_vis`) into the latent world model's condition space using a visual projector (`P_vis`) to produce a `q_anchor` query. This anchor remains constant across refinement rounds.
2. Projecting the current latent plan (`z_k`) into the same condition space using a plan projector (`P_plan`).
3. Combining these projections: `q_k = q_anchor + β_k * P_plan(z_k)`, where `β_k` is a learnable, per-round scalar coefficient that controls the deviation from the anchor.
The world model (`WM_frozen`) used is a lightweight, frozen latent world model (instantiated with UWM). Given `q_k`, the `WM_frozen` predicts an action-free future observation latent `o_k`. This process is action-free, meaning the world model's action channel is unused; it generates a latent future implied by the changing world query `q_k`.

#### 3.3. Future-Guided Latent Refinement

The imagined future `o_k` serves as an update signal for the latent plan. The closed-loop refinement at round `k` involves:
1. `FutureEncoder`: Summarizes the final WM latent output `o_k` into compact future feedback tokens `s_k`.
2. `RefineNet`: A transformer-style residual update module that operates on the plan tokens. It takes `z_k`, `s_k`, and a sinusoidal round embedding `e_k` as inputs. `RefineNet` combines self-attention over `z_k` and cross-attention to `s_k` with round-conditioned modulation to produce a residual correction `δ_k`.
3. Plan Update: The latent plan is updated as `z_k+1 = z_k + w_k * δ_k`, where `w_k` is a per-round residual write-back coefficient, following a decreasing schedule. This schedule facilitates a coarse-to-fine refinement rhythm, allowing larger updates in early rounds and smaller adjustments later.
After `K` refinement rounds, the final refined latent plan `z_K` is decoded in parallel by a query-transformer action head into an `H`-step continuous action chunk for execution.

#### 3.4. Training Objective and Stabilization (Supervised Learning)

The supervised training objective combines several loss components:
- **Action MSE:** `MSE(ˆa, a_⋆)` measures the error between the predicted action chunk `ˆa` (from `z_K`) and the expert action chunk `a_⋆`. A linear warmup is applied to this term.
- **DCT Loss:** `L_dct` is an auxiliary loss based on Discrete Cosine Transform, following practices in frequency-domain action modeling.
- **Teacher Alignment Loss:** `MSE(q_k, q_wm*)` aligns each plan-conditioned world query `q_k` to `q_wm*`, which is the teacher condition vector produced by the frozen WM's observation encoder for the corresponding demonstration state. This ensures `q_k` remains close to the WM's pre-trained condition manifold.
This multi-objective loss ensures that the final latent plan can be decoded into correct actions, while intermediate world queries are valid inputs for the frozen world model.

#### 3.5. Reward-Guided Latent Refinement (CRG-PRL)

To optimize the refinement trajectory beyond supervised training, PearlVLA introduces Causal Refinement-Grouped Process-Reward RL (CRG-PRL):
- **Inner MDP:** The refinement loop is formulated as a `γ=0` inner MDP. The state `x_k = (z_vis, z_k, o_k, k)` includes the current latent plan, imagined future, and round index. The action `a_k = δ_k` is the `RefineNet` residual.
- **Process Reward:** A per-round reward `r_k` is assigned to the imagined future induced *after* the latent plan edit (`z_k+1`). This reward-source future (`˜o_k+1`) is decoded into `H_judge` (3) ordered imagined frames (`O_imgs_k+1`) by the world model's decoder and scored by a frozen Robometer-4B reward model. The reward is derived from the final frame's progress and success predictions.
- **Same-State Local Branching:** To isolate the causal effect of each latent edit, CRG-PRL compares edits within a shared refinement state. For each sample, a deterministic "base path" is run using the current mean policy. At each base state `x_base_k`, `M=8` single-round residual "branches" are drawn, differing only by exploration noise. Each branch triggers one reward evaluation, but its effect is not propagated to subsequent base path rounds.
- **Group-Relative Advantage:** Rewards from the `M` branches at the same `x_base_k` are standardized into a group-relative advantage `~A_k^(i)`. This cancels out state difficulty and per-round reward scale, removing the need for a critic.
- **PPO Objective with Guardrails:** The policy (RefineNet's mean output for the residual) is a fixed-variance Gaussian. A PPO-clipped objective on these group-relative advantages is optimized. Three guardrails are added to keep the optimized operator close to the supervised one: a KL divergence term to the frozen supervised policy, a normalized edit penalty on the write-back magnitude, and a final-action behavior-cloning anchor on the base path.
During CRG-PRL, RefineNet and FutureEncoder are updated, while the VLM backbone, latent WM, projectors, composer, write-back schedule, and action head remain frozen.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

PearlVLA's effectiveness was evaluated on the LIBERO benchmark, along with robustness tests on LIBERO-Plus and few-shot evaluation on RoboCasa.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Performance on LIBERO

- **Superiority over Baselines:** PearlVLA achieves a 98.5% average success rate on the LIBERO benchmark under the suite-specific protocol with supervised training. With the addition of CRG-PRL, this further improves to 98.7%. This outcome surpasses the primary reference model, OpenVLA-OFT (97.1%), as well as other strong VLA baselines, including those utilizing flow-matching or diffusion-based action heads (e.g., VLANeXt at 97.4%, π_0.5 at 97.5%).
- **Consistency Across Suites:** PearlVLA shows improvements across all four LIBERO suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), indicating a generalized benefit of latent refinement.

#### 4.2. Robustness on LIBERO-Plus

- **Improved Generalization:** On LIBERO-Plus, a benchmark designed to test robustness under controlled perturbations (layout, camera, robot state, language, light, background, noise), supervised PearlVLA achieves a total average success rate of 76.3%. This is a notable improvement over OpenVLA-OFT (69.6%), suggesting that latent refinement contributes to better generalization under diverse conditions.

#### 4.3. Impact of Refinement Depth and Reward-Guided Training

- **Progressive Gains with Refinement:** An ablation study on the number of refinement rounds (K) demonstrates that increasing K from 0 (direct decoding) to 4 progressively enhances performance. The average success rate rises from 96.8% (K=0) to 98.5% (K=4), indicating that future-guided latent refinement is a primary driver of the performance gains.
- **CRG-PRL Further Optimizes:** The CRG-PRL stage, applied on top of the K=4 supervised model, further boosts the average success rate to 98.7%, confirming that the refinement trajectory can be effectively optimized beyond purely supervised training signals.

#### 4.4. Action Chunk Horizon Analysis

- **Enhanced Coherence for Longer Horizons:** When the action chunk horizon (H) is extended from 8 to 20, the performance gap between models with and without latent refinement widens. For K=4, the average success rate drops by 1.7 percentage points (from 98.5% to 96.8%), whereas for K=0 (direct decoding), the drop is 3.2 percentage points (from 96.8% to 93.6%). This indicates that future-guided refinement improves action coherence over longer open-loop execution periods, making lower-frequency policy querying more viable.

#### 4.5. Inference Efficiency

- **Practical Throughput:** At its default K=4 configuration, PearlVLA achieves an effective throughput of 27.5 Hz with an action chunk of H=8, which is considered a practical rate for robotic manipulation. Increasing H to 20 boosts throughput to 68.5 Hz with a moderate success rate reduction.

#### 4.6. Latent World Model Instantiation and Substitution

- **Importance of WM Pretraining:** Substituting the UWM (pretrained on scene-similar LIBERO-90 video) with a VPP-style latent world model (pretrained on general video) showed that performance can be maintained above direct decoding but is sensitive to the world model's pretraining data. Post-training the VPP model on LIBERO-90 video helped close the performance gap, suggesting the utility of domain-relevant world model knowledge.

#### 4.7. RoboCasa Few-Shot Evaluation

- **Strong Performance in Challenging Environment:** On the RoboCasa 24-task kitchen benchmark, a more complex and contact-rich environment with few-shot demonstrations (50 per task), PearlVLA (K=4 + CRG-PRL) achieves an average success rate of 52.9%. This performance is comparable to or slightly exceeds specialized flow-matching methods like DiG-Flow (52.6%), despite PearlVLA using a parallel regression head. The benefits of refinement (K=0 to K=4) are also evident here (43.1% to 52.1%).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PearlVLA introduces a significant contribution by addressing the trade-off between efficient action generation and explicit deliberation in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Its key significance lies in the paradigm shift towards *internalized, latent-space deliberation* for embodied action planning, extending concepts of latent reasoning from language models to the domain of robotics.

The research demonstrates that investing computational resources in *latent plan revision* within the VLM's forward pass can yield substantial performance improvements. This approach contrasts with prior efforts that primarily focused on developing more expressive action decoders or relying on external reasoning modules, which often incur prohibitive latency for real-time robotic control. By progressively refining a latent action plan guided by imagined future observations, PearlVLA enables the policy to anticipate the consequences of its actions and self-correct, moving beyond a purely reactive control paradigm.

The empirical results underscore PearlVLA's impact:
- **New State-of-the-Art:** Achieving a 98.7% average success rate on the LIBERO benchmark establishes a new benchmark for VLA performance, outperforming existing methods, including those that employ iterative action decoding.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** The improved performance on LIBERO-Plus, a benchmark for controlled perturbations, suggests that latent refinement contributes to more robust and generalizable policies, which is critical for real-world robotic deployment.
- **Viability for Complex and Long-Horizon Tasks:** The demonstration of improved performance under longer action chunk horizons and in challenging few-shot environments like RoboCasa indicates that PearlVLA's approach enhances action coherence and planning capabilities over extended task durations and in cluttered, contact-rich scenarios. This moves towards making lower-frequency policy querying more practical, improving overall system throughput.
- **Optimizable Deliberation Mechanism:** The success of Causal Refinement-Grouped Process-Reward RL (CRG-PRL) in further optimizing the refinement trajectory highlights that the iterative latent deliberation process is not merely a fixed architectural component but a trainable mechanism capable of continuous improvement.

The potential impact of PearlVLA extends to enabling more reliable, general-purpose, and long-horizon robotic manipulation systems. By internalizing anticipatory planning, robots could become more adaptive and capable of handling complex, unstructured environments with greater autonomy. Future research directions, such as implementing adaptive refinement depths based on task complexity or integrating more diverse action-free video data to scale the frozen latent world model, could further unlock the potential of this latent-space deliberation paradigm.
