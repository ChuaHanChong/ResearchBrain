# A Mechanistic View on Video Generation as World Models: State and Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper offers a mechanistic analysis of current video generation models in relation to world models, introducing a taxonomy based on how they handle state construction and dynamics. It concludes that while showing emergent physical coherence, these models largely lack explicit causal understanding and long-term persistence, highlighting the need for new architectural designs and evaluation methodologies.

## Problem

- Large-scale video generation models exhibit emergent physical coherence but lack the explicit state-space modeling characteristic of traditional world models.
- A significant gap exists between theoretical world model definitions and the 'stateless' nature of contemporary video generation architectures, which struggle with long-range reasoning and causal understanding.
- Existing evaluation metrics for video generation primarily focus on visual fidelity, failing to assess crucial aspects like physical persistence, object permanence, and causal reasoning.

## Method

- The research proposes a novel taxonomy that categorizes video generation approaches based on their mechanisms for 'State Construction' (Implicit vs. Explicit paradigms) and 'Dynamics Modeling' (Causal Architecture Reformulation vs. Causal Knowledge Integration).
- It details methods for implicit state management through memory mechanisms (Compression, Retrieval, Consolidation) and explicit state through latent variable compression (Coupled and Decoupled states).
- It outlines strategies for dynamics modeling, including re-engineering models for causal structures and integrating external causal reasoning via Large Multimodal Models (LMMs).

## Results

- The analysis reveals that traditional evaluation metrics are insufficient for assessing world model capabilities, primarily measuring short-range perceptual realism and visual fidelity.
- Quantitative results from emerging benchmarks, such as Physics-IQ, indicate a substantial gap, with state-of-the-art models achieving only about 24% accuracy on causal understanding tasks.
- Current models primarily capture surface-level correlations and exhibit nascent abilities for physical understanding and planning, indicating they are not yet robust, general-purpose world simulators.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current large-scale video generation models operate predominantly in a 'stateless' manner, relying on direct observation sequences rather than compact internal state representations, which limits long-horizon consistency and causal reasoning.
> - Two primary paradigms for state construction (Implicit and Explicit) address statelessness with varying trade-offs between computational efficiency, information retention, and global consistency.
> - Achieving true causal understanding in video models necessitates either fundamental architectural changes to instill temporal causality or the integration of external, reasoning-capable models like LMMs to guide generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a crucial and rapidly evolving intersection in artificial intelligence: the convergence of **large-scale video generation models** and the theoretical framework of **world models**. The field of video generation has recently undergone a "paradigm shift," moving from producing short, low-resolution clips to rendering "cinematic-quality sequences with unprecedented temporal consistency." Prominent models like OpenAI's Sora, Google's Veo, and Kuaishou's Kling have demonstrated "emergent physical coherence," appearing to implicitly understand concepts like gravity, collision dynamics, and object permanence without explicit programming. This emergent behavior has led to widespread discussion about their potential as "world models."

The concept of a "world model" itself has a rich history, rooted in cognitive science (e.g., mental models by Craik and Johnson-Laird), control theory (state-space models by Kalman), and more recently, Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL). Traditionally, world models are defined by three core elements: **observation**, **state**, and **dynamics**. A key characteristic of classic world models, especially in MBRL, is the reliance on an *explicit State-Space Model (SSM)*. These models construct a compact, internal state representation of the environment, enabling long-range reasoning with constant computational overhead and facilitating clear causal decoupling.

However, the authors identify a significant "gap" between these traditional, state-centric world model theories and contemporary "stateless" video generation architectures. Most large-scale video models, primarily built on transformer architectures, operate in an "open-loop manner," passively observing data without reinforcement learning or policy interaction. Crucially, these models typically do not construct an explicit hidden state; instead, they directly model observation sequences, often requiring large context windows for long-term reasoning. This approach leads to substantial memory and computational burdens as video length increases and obscures causal understanding, as it becomes difficult to discern if an action is generated due to physical understanding or merely a text prompt.

This survey paper positions itself as a critical bridge over this gap. It systematically analyzes how current video generation techniques implicitly or explicitly address the core components of world models—state and dynamics—and proposes a novel taxonomy to clarify their mechanistic underpinnings. By doing so, it aims to guide the field towards developing robust, general-purpose world simulators that move beyond mere visual plausibility to truly capture and simulate the underlying physical and causal regularities of environments. This work is highly relevant for researchers in generative AI, computer vision, reinforcement learning, and embodied AI, offering a principled framework for advancing the capabilities of video models towards more intelligent, interactive, and physically grounded systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the remarkable, yet often underexplored, emergent physical coherence observed in cutting-edge large-scale video generation models. While models like Sora produce visually stunning and temporally consistent videos, their inherent mechanisms differ fundamentally from the classical theories of "world models." The authors seek to move beyond the superficial assessment of visual fidelity to critically examine whether these generative models genuinely function as internal simulators of reality.

The paper articulates three core objectives to achieve this:

1. **Bridging Theoretical and Practical Gaps:** The first objective is to "bridge the gap between high-level theory and practical implementation." This involves grounding the capabilities of modern video models within the rigorous definition of a complete world model, thereby clarifying the position of video models within this broader theoretical framework. This aims to provide a common language and understanding for researchers from diverse backgrounds, from cognitive science to deep learning.
2. **Proposing a Novel Taxonomy:** The central objective is to introduce "a novel taxonomy based on the key constituents of a world model: State and Dynamics." This taxonomy serves as a systematic framework to categorize and understand existing literature and future research directions.
 - For **State Construction**, the paper categorizes approaches into **Implicit Paradigms** (relying on context management, such as KV Cache) and **Explicit Paradigms** (involving latent compression into compact, fixed-size variables).
 - For **Dynamics Modeling**, it categorizes research into **Causal Knowledge Integration** (combining video models with external, reasoning-intensive models) and **Causal Architecture Reformulation** (redesigning video model architectures for inherent causal reasoning).
3. **Advocating for a Shift in Evaluation and Identifying Future Frontiers:** The third objective is to advocate for a crucial transition in the evaluation of video models. Instead of solely focusing on visual fidelity (e.g., perceptual realism), the paper proposes moving towards "functional benchmarks" that rigorously test physical persistence, object permanence, temporal reasoning, and causal understanding. Based on this, it identifies two critical frontiers for future work:
 - **Enhancing Persistence:** This involves developing "data-driven memory and compressed fidelity" to enable consistent long-horizon simulations.
 - **Advancing Causality:** This requires fostering "latent factor decoupling and reasoning-prior integration" to enable models to genuinely understand and simulate causal relationships.

Ultimately, the paper is motivated by the grand vision of transforming video generation models from mere content creation tools into robust, general-purpose "world simulators" capable of supporting complex reasoning, planning, and interactive AI agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This paper is a comprehensive survey, and as such, its methodology is a systematic and analytical review of the existing literature at the intersection of video generation and world modeling. The authors' approach is to deconstruct the concept of a world model into its fundamental constituents – observation, state, and dynamics – and then mechanistically analyze how contemporary video generation models embody or deviate from these principles.

The core of their approach is the development of a **novel taxonomy** that organizes the vast and rapidly growing body of research. This taxonomy is built upon two foundational pillars: **State Construction** and **Dynamics Modeling**.

**I. State Construction:** This pillar addresses how video models represent and manage information about the environment over time, moving beyond simple raw observation sequences.

- **Implicit State - Memory Mechanism:** This paradigm views the "state" not as a compact, abstract variable, but as a dynamically managed context of historical observations. The authors dissect this into three functional primitives:
 - **Compression:** Techniques that condense long historical sequences into compact representations to mitigate computational bottlenecks (e.g., token merging, pyramidal flow matching). This includes `Dynamic Compression` (real-time, content-adaptive merging, like VidToMe, Sparse VideoGen) and `Static Compression` (pre-defined strategies, like FramePack, LoViC).
 - **Retrieval:** Strategies for selectively accessing relevant historical or external context (e.g., sparse attention, key-value lookup). This covers `External Retrieval` (querying historical frames based on physical states or using external reference-augmentation, like WorldMem, Corgi) and `Internal Retrieval` (integrating retrieval directly into attention mechanisms, like MoC, VSA).
 - **Consolidation:** Mechanisms for real-time distillation and updating of the context buffer after new content generation to preserve long-range consistency while maintaining constant computational footprint (e.g., StreamingT2V, EgoLCD).

- **Explicit State:** In contrast, this paradigm constructs the world state through internalized compression, distilling historical context into a globally updating latent variable. This is further divided based on the architectural relationship between the state transition function and the generative backbone:
 - **Coupled States:** Here, the state transition function is intrinsically fused within the generation backbone, meaning the model itself functions as a state-transition system.
 - `Hidden-Variable States`: These are hidden activation vectors updated through internal causal linear systems (e.g., State-Space Models (SSMs), Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), or linear attention, as seen in MALT Diffusion, VideoSSM, SANA-Video).
 - `Parametric States`: A more radical approach where a subset of model parameters are treated as dynamic, input-dependent variables, continuously updated based on the input stream (e.g., TTT-DiT, Titans).
 - **Decoupled States:** In this approach, the state is structurally separated from the internal activations of the visual generation backbone, existing as a standalone representation.
 - `Semantics-oriented State`: Focuses on maintaining high-level consistency in object identity and narrative progression, often using external models like LLMs to evolve latent world descriptions (e.g., Owl-1, Pack and Force).
 - `Geometry-oriented State`: Prioritizes spatial consistency and physical structure by treating 3D representations (e.g., point clouds, 3D Gaussians) as the state, updated through iterative spatial fusion and back-projection (e.g., ViewCrafter, Scene Splatter).

**II. Dynamics Modeling:** This pillar focuses on how video models internalize underlying causal laws to ensure physically plausible and logically consistent temporal rollouts.

- **Causal Architecture Reformulation:** This involves re-engineering video models, particularly diffusion denoisers, into strictly causal structures that respect temporal order. Techniques include `Causal-Masked Video Diffusion` (e.g., AR-Diffusion, MAGI-1), autoregressive generation (e.g., Video-GPT, VideoPoet), and various "forcing" strategies to mitigate exposure bias (e.g., Diffusion Forcing, Self-Forcing).
- **Causal Knowledge Integration:** This paradigm leverages external models with strong causal reasoning capabilities, primarily Large Multimodal Models (LMMs), to guide the generation process. This can manifest as:
 - `Sequential Decoupling`: High-level LMMs act as "planners" (e.g., Owl-1, VideoDirectorGPT), with the video model as a downstream "visualizer."
 - `Unified Coupling`: Understanding and generation capabilities are fused within a single framework, optimizing LMM reasoning and video generation jointly (e.g., UniVideo, BAGEL).

**III. Evaluation and Future Work:** The paper concludes its methodology by proposing a **three-level classification for evaluating video models as world models**: basic Quality, physical Persistence, and Causal Reasoning. This systematic approach allows the authors to summarize the current state, identify critical challenges, and chart a technical roadmap for future advancements.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The comprehensive survey reveals several critical findings regarding the current state and future trajectory of video generation models in their pursuit of becoming robust world simulators:

1. **The "Stateless" Nature of Current Video Models:** A primary finding is that most contemporary large-scale video generation models, while achieving high visual fidelity, largely operate in a "stateless" manner. They rely on directly processing long sequences of observations rather than constructing an explicit, compact internal state representation. This approach, while effective for short-term visual coherence, leads to significant computational and memory burdens for long-horizon generation and makes true causal reasoning difficult.

2. **Divergent Approaches to State Construction:** The paper highlights active research in both "Implicit State" and "Explicit State" paradigms to overcome the limitations of statelessness:
 - **Implicit State (Memory Mechanisms):** Researchers are developing sophisticated memory mechanisms (Compression, Retrieval, Consolidation) to manage raw observation history more efficiently. These methods reduce redundancy and selectively access relevant past information, improving context management and extending temporal horizons. However, they are often heuristic-driven, window-limited, and still context-bound (O(N) to O(N^2) complexity), making them prone to semantic drift or forgetting over very long durations.
 - **Explicit State:** This paradigm seeks to distil history into compact latent variables.
 - **Coupled States** (e.g., Hidden-Variable States in SSMs, Parametric States in TTT-DiT) offer a more integrated approach, often providing constant computational complexity (O(1)). However, balancing efficiency with the retention of fine-grained visual details remains a challenge, as aggressive compression can lead to information loss.
 - **Decoupled States** (e.g., Semantics-oriented States using LLMs, Geometry-oriented States with 3D representations) offer flexibility by separating the state transition logic from the visual generator. Semantics-oriented states excel at narrative coherence but may lack fine-grained spatial reasoning, while geometry-oriented states provide rigorous spatial consistency but struggle with fluid temporal dynamics.

3. **Strategies for Dynamics Modeling and Causality:** The survey identifies two main strategies to instill causal understanding in video models:
 - **Causal Architecture Reformulation:** This involves fundamental architectural changes, such as integrating causal attention masks into diffusion models and employing autoregressive generation. Techniques like Diffusion Forcing and Self-Forcing aim to mitigate exposure bias and improve physical consistency during long temporal rollouts by enforcing strict temporal dependency.
 - **Causal Knowledge Integration:** This approach leverages external, reasoning-capable models (especially LMMs) to guide video generation. LMMs act as "planners" or "directors," providing high-level causal logic, while video models function as "renderers." More advanced efforts aim for unified coupling, where understanding and generation are jointly optimized within a single framework (e.g., BAGEL).

4. **Inadequacy of Traditional Evaluation Metrics:** A crucial finding is that traditional evaluation metrics (e.g., FVD, IS) are insufficient for assessing video models as world models. They primarily measure short-range perceptual realism and visual fidelity, failing to capture critical aspects like long-horizon persistence, object permanence, and adherence to physical laws or causal relationships.

5. **Significant Gap in Causal Understanding:** Quantitative results from emerging benchmarks (e.g., Physics-IQ) demonstrate a substantial gap between visual plausibility and true physical understanding. Current state-of-the-art models may achieve only 24% on Physics-IQ tests, indicating they still primarily capture surface-level correlations rather than internalizing underlying physical dynamics. The ability of models to respond coherently to "interventions" and to be useful for "planning" (e.g., in robotic tasks) is still nascent.

In essence, the paper concludes that while large-scale video models show promising emergent capabilities, they are not yet fully-fledged world models. The challenges of persistence and causality remain substantial, necessitating a fundamental shift in architectural design, learning paradigms, and evaluation methodologies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research paper holds significant importance for the advancement of artificial intelligence, particularly in the realm of generative models and embodied AI. Its potential impact spans several key areas:

1. **Systematization and Conceptual Clarity:** The most immediate and profound impact of this survey is its contribution to systematizing a rapidly evolving and often disjointed field. By introducing a novel taxonomy centered on "State Construction" and "Dynamics Modeling," the authors provide a clear conceptual framework that helps researchers understand, categorize, and compare diverse approaches to video generation and world modeling. This clarity is crucial for fostering structured innovation and preventing redundant efforts in a research area that has seen an explosion of new models and techniques.

2. **Guiding Future Research and Development:** The paper serves as a valuable roadmap for future research. By meticulously identifying the strengths and weaknesses of current paradigms (Implicit vs. Explicit State, Causal Architecture Reformulation vs. Causal Knowledge Integration), it pinpoints critical challenges related to persistence and causality. The suggested future directions – such as developing data-driven memory mechanisms, hybrid compressed states, latent factor decoupling, and integrating reasoning priors – offer concrete pathways for researchers to pursue, accelerating progress towards truly capable world simulators.

3. **Redefining Evaluation Paradigms:** The advocacy for a shift from purely visual fidelity metrics to "functional benchmarks" (Quality, Persistence, Causality) is a critical call to action. This redefinition of evaluation criteria will compel researchers to build models that not only produce aesthetically pleasing videos but also demonstrate genuine understanding of physical laws, object permanence, temporal reasoning, and causal relationships. Such rigorous evaluation is essential for discerning truly intelligent simulation capabilities from mere pattern mimicry.

4. **Enabling Robust General-Purpose World Simulators:** The ultimate vision articulated by the authors is to evolve video generation models into "robust, general-purpose world simulators." Achieving this would have transformative implications:
 - **Embodied AI and Robotics:** A truly predictive and causally aware world model could revolutionize robot learning, allowing agents to "dream," plan, and practice complex actions in simulated environments before deployment in the real world, reducing the need for costly and risky real-world interactions.
 - **Scientific Discovery and Engineering:** Accurate world models could facilitate simulations in fields like physics, chemistry, and engineering, enabling faster prototyping, hypothesis testing, and discovery of new phenomena.
 - **Advanced Content Creation:** Beyond current capabilities, world models could enable the generation of infinitely consistent, causally coherent, and interactive virtual worlds for gaming, film, and virtual reality, allowing for dynamic narratives and highly responsive environments.
 - **Foundation for General Intelligence:** The ability to accurately model the dynamics of a physical world is considered a foundational component of general artificial intelligence. This work contributes directly to building these foundational capabilities.

In conclusion, "A Mechanistic View on Video Generation as World Models" is more than just a review; it is a prescriptive analysis that critically assesses the state of the art, provides a much-needed organizational framework, and sets a clear agenda for future endeavors. By addressing the fundamental hurdles of persistence and causality, this paper significantly propels the field towards realizing the ambitious goal of building AI systems that can not only generate visuals but genuinely understand and simulate the fabric of reality.
