# Conditional Prompt Learning for Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at S-Lab, Nanyang Technological University, developed Conditional Context Optimization (CoCoOp), a method that generates instance-conditional prompts for vision-language models like CLIP. This approach significantly improves generalization to unseen classes by over 8% average accuracy compared to static prompt learning, while maintaining parameter efficiency.

## Problem

- Large pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs) like CLIP are computationally expensive to fully fine-tune for downstream tasks.
- Previous prompt learning methods like CoOp, while parameter-efficient, suffer from poor generalizability to unseen classes within the same dataset.
- Static prompt contexts in CoOp tend to overfit to the base training classes, leading to a significant performance drop on novel categories.

## Method

- The authors introduce ==Conditional Context Optimization (CoCoOp)==, which generates ==instance-conditional prompt contexts== for each input image.
- A lightweight ==Meta-Net== takes the image features from a frozen CLIP image encoder as input and outputs a single 'conditional token'.
- This conditional token is added to the learnable context vectors, dynamically forming the prompt for the text encoder, with both the context vectors and Meta-Net being jointly optimized while the core VLM remains frozen.

## Results

- CoCoOp improves accuracy on unseen classes by over **8%** on average compared to CoOp (**71.69%** vs. **63.22%**) and achieves a higher harmonic mean (**75.83%**) than CoOp (**71.66%**) and zero-shot CLIP (**71.70%**).
- It demonstrates superior cross-dataset transferability, showing clear margins over CoOp on datasets like FGVCAircraft (**+4.47%**) and DTD (**+3.81%**).
- CoCoOp exhibits stronger domain generalizability compared to CoOp across ImageNet variant datasets (ImageNet-Sketch, ImageNet-A, ImageNet-R), affirming robustness to domain shifts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamic, instance-conditional prompts enable better generalization compared to static, class-agnostic prompts in vision-language models.
> - A lightweight neural network (Meta-Net) can efficiently generate instance-specific conditional tokens for prompt learning.
> - Proper initialization of context vectors and longer context lengths contribute to improved generalization performance on new classes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of large-scale pre-trained models, vision-language understanding, and efficient model adaptation techniques. It specifically targets the burgeoning field of **prompt learning**, extending its application from Natural Language Processing (NLP) to the visual domain.

**The Rise of Foundation Models:** The paper acknowledges the emergence of powerful pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs) like CLIP and ALIGN as "foundation models." These models, trained on massive web-scale datasets (e.g., 400 million image-text pairs for CLIP), demonstrate remarkable capabilities in zero-shot image recognition and learning open-world visual concepts. Their power stems from modeling visual concepts using natural language, making them highly transferable.

**The Challenge of Adaptation:** While powerful, these VLMs are enormous in size (hundreds of millions or billions of parameters), making full fine-tuning impractical due to computational cost and the risk of damaging well-learned representations. This necessitates efficient adaptation strategies. Traditional prompt engineering (manual crafting of text prompts) is time-consuming and suboptimal.

**Prompt Learning as a Solution:** Prompt learning, a recent trend originating in NLP, offers a parameter-efficient alternative to fine-tuning. Instead of modifying the entire model, it optimizes a small set of "context" parameters within the prompt structure. The authors' previous work, **Context Optimization (CoOp)**, pioneered continuous prompt learning for VLMs, demonstrating significant improvements over manual prompts with only a few labeled images.

**Addressing CoOp's Limitations (Generalizability):** This paper directly addresses a critical shortcoming of CoOp: its poor generalizability to *unseen classes* within the same dataset. CoOp's static, learned context tends to overfit the "base classes" it was trained on, performing poorly when evaluated on novel categories. This problem echoes the "seen-class bias" issue in Zero-Shot Learning (ZSL), where models struggle to perform well on classes not encountered during training.

**CoCoOp's Novelty:** CoCoOp introduces a significant conceptual leap: **conditional prompt learning**. Unlike CoOp's static prompts, CoCoOp's prompts are dynamic and adapt to each input instance. This instance-conditioning aims to mitigate overfitting to specific classes and improve generalization. The paper highlights this idea's potential novelty even in NLP, suggesting broader implications.

**Relationship to Zero-Shot Learning (ZSL):** While sharing a similar goal of recognizing novel classes, CoCoOp differentiates itself from traditional ZSL methods. Traditional ZSL often relies on learning semantic spaces via attributes or word embeddings. CoCoOp, however, leverages the pre-trained VLM architecture and adapts it through a novel prompting mechanism.

In essence, CoCoOp represents a crucial step forward in the VLM adaptation landscape, pushing the boundaries of prompt learning to achieve more robust and generalizable performance, thereby facilitating the practical deployment of large foundation models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind the development of Conditional Context Optimization (CoCoOp) can be summarized as follows:

- **Efficiently Adapt Pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** The overarching goal is to find highly parameter-efficient methods to adapt powerful, large-scale VLMs like CLIP to diverse downstream image recognition tasks without fine-tuning the entire colossal model. This is crucial for democratizing the use of such "foundation models" given their computational demands and the risk of catastrophic forgetting when full fine-tuning is applied.

- **Overcome the Limitations of Static Prompt Learning (CoOp):** The primary motivation stems from a critical problem identified in the authors' previous work, Context Optimization (CoOp). While CoOp successfully introduced learnable prompts to the vision domain, it exhibited a significant weakness: the learned context vectors were **not generalizable to unseen classes** within the same dataset. This resulted in a substantial drop in accuracy when CoOp was evaluated on novel categories not present during training, indicating an overfitting issue to the "base classes." The authors refer to these as "static prompts" that are "less sensitive to class shift."

- **Improve Generalizability to Unseen Classes:** To directly address CoOp's generalizability shortfall, the key objective of CoCoOp is to enable the prompt learning mechanism to capture more robust and universal visual concepts. The hypothesis is that by making prompts "instance-conditional" rather than static, the model can adapt to the unique characteristics of each input image, thereby becoming less susceptible to overfitting on a fixed set of training classes.

- **Enhance Cross-Dataset Transferability:** Beyond within-dataset generalization, the authors aim for prompts that exhibit stronger transferability across tasks with drastically different visual categories and domains. This would signify a more fundamental improvement in the learned context's ability to adapt to new scenarios.

- **Achieve Stronger Domain Generalization:** The motivation also includes improving the model's robustness to various types of domain shifts (e.g., style changes, real-world variations). A more dynamic, instance-conditional prompt is hypothesized to naturally handle out-of-distribution data better than a static one.

- **Develop a Parameter-Efficient Solution:** While introducing more complexity, a key objective is to ensure that the proposed conditional prompt learning method remains parameter-efficient. This is achieved by designing a lightweight neural network (Meta-Net) that generates the conditional token, rather than requiring a large, complex architecture.

In essence, CoCoOp is motivated by the need to develop a more intelligent and adaptable prompt learning strategy that moves beyond static, class-specific contexts to dynamic, instance-aware contexts, thereby unlocking the full potential of pre-trained VLMs for broad and robust deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CoCoOp (Conditional Context Optimization) builds directly upon the CLIP model and the CoOp framework, introducing a novel "instance-conditional" mechanism to enhance prompt generalizability.

#### 3.1. Foundation: CLIP and CoOp

- **CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training):** CLIP consists of an image encoder (e.g., ResNet or ViT) and a text encoder (a Transformer). It's trained on a massive dataset of image-text pairs using a contrastive loss to learn a joint embedding space. For zero-shot image recognition, CLIP converts class names into text embeddings via a manually designed prompt template (e.g., "a photo of a {class}"). Image features are then classified by their cosine similarity to these class-specific text embeddings.

- **CoOp (Context Optimization):** CoOp automates prompt engineering. Instead of manual prompt templates, it introduces *M* learnable continuous vectors, `{v_1, v_2..., v_M}`, as the context. For a given class `c_i`, the prompt is formed by concatenating these learnable context vectors with the word embedding(s) of the class name: `t_i = {v_1..., v_M, c_i}`. These context vectors are shared across all classes and are learned end-to-end using a cross-entropy loss on a few-shot labeled dataset, while the base CLIP model remains frozen. CoOp's prompts are *static* once learned.

#### 3.2. CoCoOp's Core Innovation: Conditional Context Optimization

CoCoOp addresses CoOp's static nature by making the context *conditional* on each input image.

- **Instance-Conditional Context:** The central idea is that instead of a fixed set of context vectors, CoCoOp learns context that adapts to the specific input image. This dynamic approach aims to generalize better by focusing on the instance rather than overfitting to specific training classes.

- **Meta-Net for Conditional Token Generation:** To implement this parameter-efficiently, CoCoOp introduces a lightweight neural network called **Meta-Net**, denoted as `h_θ(·)`.
 - **Input:** The Meta-Net takes the output features `x` produced by the frozen CLIP image encoder as its input.
 - **Output:** It generates a single "conditional token" (vector) `π = h_θ(x)`. This `π` vector has the same dimension as the word embeddings in the text encoder.

- **Dynamic Prompt Formulation:** The generated conditional token `π` is then *added* to each of the *M* learnable context vectors. So, each context token `v_m` becomes `v_m(x) = v_m + π`.
 - The prompt for the `i`-th class, now conditioned on the input image `x`, becomes `t_i(x) = {v_1(x), v_2(x)..., v_M(x), c_i}`.
 - This means that for every input image, a unique, instance-specific prompt is effectively generated and fed into the text encoder.

- **Classification:** The prediction probability for a class `y` given an image `x` is computed using cosine similarity between the image feature `x` and the text embedding generated by the text encoder `g(t_y(x))`:
 `p(y|x) = exp(sim(x, g(t_y(x)))/τ) / ∑_K exp(sim(x, g(t_i(x)))/τ)`.

- **Training Process:**
 - Both the initial set of *M* context vectors `{v_m}` and the parameters `θ` of the Meta-Net `h_θ` are jointly optimized.
 - The CLIP image and text encoders remain **frozen** throughout the training, making CoCoOp a parameter-efficient adaptation method.
 - A standard cross-entropy loss is used as the objective function.
 - **Meta-Net Architecture:** The Meta-Net is a simple two-layer bottleneck structure (Linear-ReLU-Linear), designed to be lightweight. The hidden layer reduces the input dimension by 16x.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Base Model:** All experiments use the ViT-B/16 backbone of the open-source CLIP model.
- **Context Length & Initialization:** Context length is fixed to 4. Context vectors are initialized with the pre-trained word embeddings of "a photo of a" for both CoOp and CoCoOp (following CoOp's best practices).
- **Few-Shot Training:** Models are trained using 16 labeled images per class from the *base classes*. Results are averaged over three runs.
- **Datasets:**
 - **Base-to-New Generalization & Cross-Dataset Transfer:** 11 diverse image recognition datasets (ImageNet, Caltech101, OxfordPets, StanfordCars, Flowers102, Food101, FGVCAircraft, SUN397, UCF101, DTD, EuroSAT). Classes are split equally into base (for training) and new (for testing generalization).
 - **Domain Generalization:** ImageNet (source) and its four variants (ImageNetV2, ImageNet-Sketch, ImageNet-A, ImageNet-R) as target datasets. Also evaluated on DOSCO-2k.
- **Baselines:** CLIP (zero-shot, manual prompts) and CoOp (static learnable prompts).
- **Training Efficiency Note:** Due to the instance-conditional design, CoCoOp is slower and more GPU memory-intensive than CoOp. A batch size of 1 for 10 epochs was used to manage these constraints.

This methodical approach, leveraging an existing powerful framework and strategically introducing a lightweight dynamic component, allows CoCoOp to effectively tackle the generalizability challenge while maintaining parameter efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across various settings consistently demonstrate CoCoOp's superior generalizability and transferability compared to CoOp, and often outperform the strong zero-shot CLIP baseline.

#### 4.1. Generalization From Base to New Classes (Primary Focus)

- **CoOp's Generalization Gap:** CoOp, while showing significant gains on base classes (82.69% average accuracy) over CLIP's zero-shot performance (69.34%), suffers a drastic drop in accuracy when evaluated on *new, unseen classes* (63.22% average). This creates a substantial gap of almost 20% on average, confirming the overfitting issue. Its harmonic mean (H) performance, a metric for generalization, is very similar to CLIP (71.66% vs 71.70%), indicating that its gains on base classes are offset by failures on new classes.
- **CoCoOp's Improved Generalizability:** CoCoOp significantly closes this generalization gap. Its average accuracy on unseen classes rises to **71.69%**, a substantial improvement over CoOp's 63.22%. While CoCoOp's base accuracy (80.47%) is slightly lower than CoOp's, this minor drop (mostly under 3%) is far outweighed by the considerable gains in new classes.
- **Overall Performance:** CoCoOp achieves an average harmonic mean (H) of **75.83%**, significantly outperforming both CoOp (71.66%) and CLIP (71.70%). This confirms that instance-conditional prompts are much more generalizable and better capture task-relevant elements.
- **Dataset-Specific Improvements:** CoCoOp shows more than 10% accuracy increases on 5 out of 11 datasets for unseen classes. Notably, on ImageNet, CoCoOp (70.43%) even surpasses CLIP (68.14%) on new classes. CoCoOp also performs better than CLIP on new classes for OxfordPets, Food101, and SUN397.

#### 4.2. Cross-Dataset Transfer

- **Enhanced Transferability:** When prompts learned from ImageNet (source) are transferred to 10 other target datasets, CoCoOp demonstrates superior transferability compared to CoOp.
- **Clear Margin:** CoCoOp mostly outperforms CoOp by a clear margin, especially on datasets with distant or more fine-grained/specialized categories like FGVCAircraft (+4.47% gain over CoOp) and DTD (+3.81% gain). This suggests that instance-conditional prompts are more robust to drastic changes in class distribution and task nature.

#### 4.3. Domain Generalization

- **Robustness to Domain Shift:** Both prompt learning methods (CoOp and CoCoOp) significantly outperform zero-shot CLIP in domain generalization, indicating their robustness to domain shifts.
- **CoCoOp's Edge:** CoCoOp generally exhibits stronger domain generalizability than CoOp. While it performs slightly worse on ImageNetV2, it shows better results on ImageNet-Sketch, ImageNet-A, and ImageNet-R. This further validates the strength of dynamic prompts in out-of-distribution scenarios.
- **DOSCO-2k Results (Appendix):** On the DOSCO-2k benchmark (focusing on broader contextual domain shift), CoCoOp beats CoOp on 4 out of 7 datasets, and both learnable methods greatly surpass CLIP, reinforcing their potential for tackling transfer learning problems.

#### 4.4. Further Analysis

- **Class-Incremental Test:** In a scenario where base classes expand to include new, unseen classes, CoCoOp decisively outperforms both CLIP and CoOp (69.13% vs. 65.22% and 65.55% respectively), highlighting its practical utility for evolving recognition tasks without re-training on new data.
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Initialization:** Initializing context vectors with word embeddings ("a photo of a") yields better performance on both base and new classes compared to random initialization, emphasizing the importance of proper initialization.
 - **Context Length:** Longer context lengths (8 and 16 tokens) generally improve performance on new classes, while base accuracy remains relatively stable.
 - **Parameter Size:** The improvements seen with CoCoOp are not merely due to an increased parameter count. When CoOp's context length is increased to match CoCoOp's parameter size, CoCoOp still performs better in generalization, confirming the effectiveness of the conditional design itself.

In summary, the results convincingly show that CoCoOp effectively addresses the generalizability limitations of static prompt learning. Its instance-conditional approach leads to significantly better performance on unseen classes, stronger cross-dataset transfer, and improved domain generalization, making it a more compelling and robust adaptation method for large vision-language models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Conditional Prompt Learning for Vision-Language Models" holds significant implications for the field of artificial intelligence, particularly concerning the deployment and democratized access to powerful foundation models.

#### 5.1. Addressing a Critical Generalizability Problem

The most immediate and impactful contribution of CoCoOp is its successful solution to the weak generalizability problem identified in prior prompt learning methods, specifically CoOp. Overfitting to training (base) classes has been a persistent challenge in machine learning, and CoCoOp demonstrates a principled way to mitigate this in the context of VLM adaptation. By making prompts instance-conditional, the model learns to characterize each input rather than optimize for a fixed set of classes, leading to substantially improved performance on unseen categories. This is a crucial step towards making VLM adaptation truly robust and widely applicable.

#### 5.2. Introducing a Novel Concept: Conditional Prompt Learning

CoCoOp pioneers the concept of "conditional prompt learning" in the vision domain, where the prompt dynamically adapts to the specific input instance. This paradigm shift from static, global prompts to dynamic, local prompts is a fundamental innovation. The authors even suggest its potential novelty and applicability within the NLP community, indicating a broader conceptual impact beyond vision. This opens new avenues for research into more sophisticated and adaptive prompting mechanisms.

#### 5.3. Enhancing Practical Utility and Democratizing Foundation Models

Large pre-trained VLMs are "foundation models" due to their immense capacity and versatility, but their massive size makes full fine-tuning impractical for most users and applications. CoCoOp, by offering a highly parameter-efficient adaptation strategy, helps democratize access to these powerful models. Its ability to perform well with only a few labeled images from base classes and then generalize to unseen classes and new domains means that these models can be more readily adapted to specific, often data-scarce, downstream tasks without extensive computational resources or specialized expertise. This accelerates the adoption of cutting-edge AI in real-world scenarios.

#### 5.4. Stronger Transferability Across Diverse Tasks and Domains

The demonstrated improvements in cross-dataset transfer and domain generalization are particularly significant. The fact that prompts learned from one task (e.g., ImageNet object recognition) can effectively transfer to drastically different visual tasks (e.g., texture classification or fine-grained aircraft recognition) and remain robust to various domain shifts underscores the power of instance-conditional learning. This makes CoCoOp a valuable tool for scenarios where target data distributions vary significantly from the training data.

#### 5.5. Paving the Way for Future Research

The paper openly discusses its limitations, particularly regarding the training efficiency (speed and memory consumption) of CoCoOp due to its instance-conditional design. This transparent acknowledgment serves as a clear direction for future research, inspiring work on more optimized implementations of conditional prompt learning. Furthermore, the success of a lightweight Meta-Net encourages exploration of more advanced architectures for generating conditional tokens. The insights gained regarding context length and initialization also provide practical guidance for future prompt engineering efforts. The potential for scaling conditional prompt learning to even larger model sizes, bigger training datasets, and heterogeneous data mixtures is highlighted, promising even more powerful and versatile adaptation methods in the future.

In conclusion, CoCoOp represents a substantial leap in making large vision-language models more adaptable, generalizable, and transferable. By introducing the elegant concept of instance-conditional prompts, it not only resolves a critical limitation of previous prompt learning methods but also lays a robust foundation for the future development of efficient and effective adaptation strategies for the increasingly prevalent foundation models in AI.
