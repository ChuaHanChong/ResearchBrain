# Scaling Proprioceptive-Visual Learning with Heterogeneous Pre-trained Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> Heterogeneous Pre-trained Transformers (HPT) introduces a modular architecture for robotic policy learning that integrates diverse data from multiple robot embodiments and sources. It demonstrates consistent performance improvements with increased data quantity and diversity, model size, and compute, achieving 10-30% higher success rates in simulations and 20%+ in real-world tasks compared to training from scratch.

## Problem

- Traditional robotic learning approaches require collecting task-specific data for each robot embodiment, leading to high costs and time consumption.
- Policies trained on one robot often fail to transfer to different embodiments due to variations in morphology, sensors, and action spaces.
- The heterogeneity of robotic data, including diverse camera configurations, proprioceptive sensors, and action representations, makes it difficult to train unified generalist models.

## Method

- Introduces **Heterogeneous Pre-trained Transformers (HPT)**, a modular ==stem-trunk-head== architecture designed to process diverse robotic inputs and learn shared representations.
- Embodiment-specific ==stems== tokenize proprioceptive and visual inputs from various robots, feeding them into a shared ==Transformer trunk== that uses ==cross-attention== to fuse information.
- The model is pre-trained on a diverse mixture of real robot data, simulation data, human videos, and multi-embodiment data, enabling it to learn generalizable patterns across different robotic systems.

## Results

- HPT consistently outperforms baselines in transfer learning across multiple simulation benchmarks, achieving **10–30%** higher success rates compared to training from scratch.
- In real robot experiments, pre-trained HPT policies achieve over **20%** improvements in success rates on four diverse manipulation tasks compared to training from scratch.
- Experiments demonstrate clear scaling behaviors, showing lower validation loss with increased data quantity and diversity, larger model sizes (up to over **1 billion** parameters), and more training compute.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robotic learning exhibits clear scaling laws, with performance improving consistently as dataset size, data diversity, model parameters, and training compute increase.
> - Joint training on both proprioceptive and visual data is more effective than training on either modality alone or sequentially.
> - Incorporating diverse data sources, including simulation data and human videos, significantly improves the transferability and final performance of robotic policies across embodiments.
