# Scaling Proprioceptive-Visual Learning with Heterogeneous Pre-trained Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> Heterogeneous Pre-trained Transformers (HPT) introduces a modular architecture for robotic policy learning that integrates diverse data from multiple robot embodiments and sources. It demonstrates consistent performance improvements with increased data quantity and diversity, model size, and compute, achieving 10-30% higher success rates in simulations and 20%+ in real-world tasks compared to training from scratch.

## Problem

- Traditional robotic learning approaches require collecting task-specific data for each robot embodiment, leading to high costs and time consumption.
- Policies trained on one robot often fail to transfer to different embodiments due to variations in morphology, sensors, and action spaces.
- The heterogeneity of robotic data, including diverse camera configurations, proprioceptive sensors, and action representations, makes it difficult to train unified generalist models.

## Method

- Introduces **Heterogeneous Pre-trained Transformers (HPT)**, a modular ==stem-trunk-head== architecture designed to process diverse robotic inputs and learn shared representations.
- Embodiment-specific ==stems== tokenize proprioceptive and visual inputs from various robots, feeding them into a shared ==Transformer trunk== that uses ==cross-attention== to fuse information.
- The model is pre-trained on a diverse mixture of real robot data, simulation data, human videos, and multi-embodiment data, enabling it to learn generalizable patterns across different robotic systems.

## Results

- HPT consistently outperforms baselines in transfer learning across multiple simulation benchmarks, achieving **10–30%** higher success rates compared to training from scratch.
- In real robot experiments, pre-trained HPT policies achieve over **20%** improvements in success rates on four diverse manipulation tasks compared to training from scratch.
- Experiments demonstrate clear scaling behaviors, showing lower validation loss with increased data quantity and diversity, larger model sizes (up to over **1 billion** parameters), and more training compute.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robotic learning exhibits clear scaling laws, with performance improving consistently as dataset size, data diversity, model parameters, and training compute increase.
> - Joint training on both proprioceptive and visual data is more effective than training on either modality alone or sequentially.
> - Incorporating diverse data sources, including simulation data and human videos, significantly improves the transferability and final performance of robotic policies across embodiments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

The field of robotics has long struggled with creating generalizable policies that can work across different robot embodiments, tasks, and environments. While traditional approaches often require training separate models for each specific robot-task combination, recent advances in machine learning have demonstrated the power of large-scale pre-training in domains like natural language processing and computer vision. This paper introduces Heterogeneous Pre-trained Transformers (HPT), a method that applies similar pre-training principles to robotics by learning shared representations across diverse robotic data sources.

The research addresses a fundamental challenge in robotics: how to leverage the growing availability of robotic datasets from various sources—including real robots, simulations, and even human demonstrations—to create more capable and adaptable robotic policies. By developing an architecture that can handle heterogeneous inputs and demonstrating clear scaling behaviors, this work represents a significant step toward robotic foundation models.

### 2. Problem Statement and Motivation

Traditional robotic learning approaches face several critical limitations. First, they typically require collecting task-specific data for each robot embodiment, which is time-consuming and expensive. Second, policies trained on one robot often fail to transfer to different embodiments due to variations in morphology, sensors, and action spaces. Third, the heterogeneity of robotic data—different camera configurations, proprioceptive sensors, and action representations—makes it difficult to train unified models.

The motivation for HPT stems from the success of foundation models in other domains. Just as language models benefit from pre-training on diverse text sources and vision models leverage varied image datasets, the authors hypothesize that robotic policies can benefit from pre-training across heterogeneous robotic data. The key insight is that despite differences in embodiment and task specifics, many robotic behaviors share underlying patterns that can be captured through large-scale pre-training.

### 3. HPT Architecture

The HPT architecture consists of three modular components designed to handle heterogeneous robotic data:

#### 3.1. Stem Components

The stem serves as the interface between different robot embodiments and the shared model. It includes:

- **Proprioceptive Tokenizer**: Processes robot joint positions, velocities, and other internal state information
- **Visual Tokenizer**: Handles camera inputs from various viewpoints and configurations

The key innovation is that stems are embodiment-specific, allowing each robot type to have its own input processing while maintaining a consistent output format of fixed-size token sequences.

#### 3.2. Trunk Architecture

The trunk is a shared Transformer network that processes tokens from all stems. It uses cross-attention mechanisms to fuse proprioceptive and visual information effectively. The proprioceptive tokens serve as queries, while visual tokens provide keys and values, allowing the model to ground visual observations in the robot's internal state.

#### 3.3. Head Components

The heads are task-specific decoders that map the processed trunk representations to action outputs. The paper explores three different head architectures: simple pooling layers, diffusion models, and additional Transformer layers. This modular design allows the same pre-trained trunk to be adapted to different action spaces and control schemes.

### 4. Data and Pre-training Strategy

The pre-training strategy leverages a diverse mixture of data sources:

- **Real Robot Data**: Teleoperated demonstrations from various robot platforms
- **Simulation Data**: Synthetic trajectories from robotic simulators
- **Human Videos**: Demonstrations of manipulation tasks performed by humans
- **Multi-embodiment Data**: Data from robots with different morphologies and capabilities

The training uses supervised learning with a standard cross-entropy loss for action prediction. The key insight is that by training on this heterogeneous mixture, the model learns representations that capture common patterns across different embodiments and tasks.

### 5. Scaling Experiments

The paper conducts extensive scaling experiments to understand how HPT performance varies with different factors:

#### 5.1. Data Scaling

The experiments show that performance improves with both the quantity and diversity of training data. Increasing the number of trajectories leads to lower validation loss, and adding more diverse datasets continues to improve performance even when the total number of trajectories is held constant.

#### 5.2. Model Size Scaling

The results demonstrate that larger models consistently outperform smaller ones, with validation loss decreasing as model parameters increase from millions to over a billion. This suggests that even larger models could potentially achieve better performance.

#### 5.3. Compute Scaling

The experiments reveal that performance scales favorably with training compute, measured both in terms of optimization steps and total data samples processed. Larger batch sizes tend to achieve better final performance, suggesting that the model benefits from seeing more diverse examples in each update.

### 6. Transfer Learning Results

#### 6.1. Simulation Benchmarks

The transfer learning experiments demonstrate clear benefits of pre-training across multiple simulation environments:

- **Fleet-Tools**: Multi-step manipulation tasks requiring tool use
- **Metaworld**: Diverse manipulation primitives and object interactions
- **Robomimic**: Standard benchmarks for robotic manipulation

Across all benchmarks, pre-trained models significantly outperform training from scratch, with improvements ranging from 10-30% in success rates.

#### 6.2. Real Robot Experiments

The real robot experiments validate the simulation results across four tasks:

- **Sweep Leftover**: Cleaning tasks requiring precise manipulation
- **Fill Water**: Pouring and liquid handling
- **Scoop Food**: Deformable object manipulation
- **Switch Insertion**: Fine motor control and alignment

The pre-trained models achieve 20%+ improvements in success rates compared to training from scratch, demonstrating that the benefits transfer to real-world scenarios.

### 7. Key Contributions

#### 7.1. Architectural Innovation

The modular stem-trunk-head architecture successfully handles heterogeneous inputs while maintaining a shared representation space. The use of cross-attention to fuse proprioceptive and visual information proves particularly effective.

#### 7.2. Scaling Validation

The paper provides compelling evidence that robotic learning exhibits similar scaling laws to other domains:

- Performance improves with dataset size and diversity
- Larger models consistently outperform smaller ones
- Additional compute leads to better final performance

#### 7.3. Joint Modality Training

The research demonstrates that jointly training on proprioceptive and visual data is superior to training on either modality alone or adding them sequentially.

#### 7.4. Multi-source Learning

The work shows that incorporating diverse data sources—including simulation and human videos—improves final performance, suggesting that the model learns transferable concepts across domains.

### 8. Implications and Future Directions

The success of HPT has several important implications for the field of robotics:

**Foundation Models for Robotics**: The results support the viability of developing robotic foundation models similar to those in NLP and computer vision. The scaling behaviors suggest that continued investment in larger datasets and models could yield substantial improvements.

**Data Efficiency**: By enabling transfer learning across embodiments and tasks, HPT could significantly reduce the data requirements for deploying robots in new scenarios. This is particularly important for real-world applications where data collection is expensive.

**Standardization**: The success of the modular architecture suggests value in developing standardized interfaces for robotic data, making it easier to combine datasets from different sources.

**Simulation to Reality Transfer**: The ability to learn from simulation data and transfer to real robots opens possibilities for leveraging large-scale synthetic data generation.

Future research directions include exploring different pre-training objectives beyond supervised learning, incorporating temporal dynamics more explicitly, and investigating how to handle even greater diversity in robot morphologies and capabilities. The open-source release of the code and model weights should accelerate progress in these areas.

The work represents a significant step toward more generalizable robotic intelligence, demonstrating that the foundation model paradigm that has transformed other AI domains can be successfully applied to robotics. The clear scaling behaviors and transfer learning benefits suggest that continued scaling of both data and models will be a fruitful path forward for the field.
