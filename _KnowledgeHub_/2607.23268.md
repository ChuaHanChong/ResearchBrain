# Sling2Sim2Real: One-Shot Elastic System Identification for Non-Destructive Slingshot Policy Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework for one-shot, non-destructive elastic system identification enables zero-shot sim-to-real policy learning for slingshot tasks. This method accurately calibrates simulation parameters from a single visuo-haptic interaction, achieving an average projectile landing error of 7.17 cm for soft bands and 19.00 cm for stiff bands, and reducing calibration loss by up to 72.5% compared to existing baselines.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation of elastic objects is challenging due to their high-dimensional, nonlinear, and often visually ambiguous deformation dynamics.
- A significant "sim-to-real gap" exists for elastic objects because their physical parameters (e.g., Young's modulus, damping) are hard to accurately identify for simulation calibration.
- Current system identification methods for deformable objects often require extensive real-world interaction, primarily use visual data, and neglect critical damping effects.

## Method

- A two-stage **Real2Sim2Real pipeline** is developed, featuring ==one-shot elastic system identification== followed by policy learning in a calibrated simulator.
- The system identification employs a single, non-destructive robot interaction to gather ==visuo-haptic data==, including sling point clouds and end-effector forces.
- A ==hierarchical optimization strategy==, combining ==Differential Evolution== for global search and ==covariance-informed multi-start CMA-ES== for local refinement, identifies five key elasticity parameters.

## Results

- Sling2Sim2Real achieved superior zero-shot transfer, with average projectile landing errors of **7.17 cm** for soft bands, **14.17 cm** for medium bands, and **19.00 cm** for stiff bands, consistently outperforming baselines.
- The framework significantly reduced the composite calibration loss (L) by up to **72.5%** compared to the best existing baseline, demonstrating high-fidelity simulation parameter estimation.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the hierarchical optimization strategy and the incorporation of mechanical work loss were essential for robust and accurate elastic system identification.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The combined use of visuo-haptic data, specifically geometric, force, and mechanical work losses, is crucial for accurate and robust elastic system identification.
> - A hierarchical optimization strategy, using global exploration with local covariance-informed exploitation, efficiently navigates the complex and multimodal parameter space of elastic objects.
> - Comprehensive identification of five elasticity-related parameters, including crucial damping effects, enables high-fidelity simulation and robust zero-shot policy transfer.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of Elastic Object Manipulation (EOM), a specialized area within Deformable Object Manipulation (DOM). EOM presents significant challenges due to the high-dimensional, nonlinear, and reversible deformations exhibited by elastic objects. The diverse and often visually indistinguishable physical properties of these objects, such as Young's modulus and damping, result in an expansive state space, making accurate physical modeling, state estimation, and robust control difficult.

Traditional DOM methods, including analytical models or task-specific heuristics, often suffer from limited robustness and generalization. More recent data-driven approaches that learn manipulation policies from sensory observations (e.g., RGB images or point clouds) necessitate extensive data collection and exploration. For elastic objects, the complexity of deformation dynamics further amplifies these data requirements.

To mitigate the cost and safety concerns of real-world data acquisition, researchers increasingly utilize physics simulators. However, a significant "sim-to-real gap" arises when physical parameters are unknown or simplified in simulation, preventing direct transfer of policies learned in simulation to the real world. The Real2Sim2Real paradigm aims to bridge this gap by calibrating simulation parameters to match real-world physics, enabling policy learning in simulation and subsequent transfer to the real system.

The existing Real2Sim2Real methods, particularly for deformable objects, often require extensive real-world interaction data for parameter inference. They frequently assume sufficiently informative observations, which is problematic for elastic properties that are visually indistinguishable. Many prior works primarily focus on estimating soft-body parameters like Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio, often neglecting damping effects, which are critical for the stability and resilience of elastic object behavior. Furthermore, these methods predominantly rely on visual observations, overlooking the direct and complementary cues provided by haptic feedback, especially when visual information is ambiguous or occluded.

This research addresses these specific gaps by proposing a one-shot, non-destructive system identification (SI) framework tailored for elastic objects. It distinguishes itself by:
- Minimizing real-world interaction to a single, non-destructive episode.
- Leveraging both visuo-haptic data for a more comprehensive characterization of elasticity.
- Focusing on a broader set of elastic parameters, including damping, which is crucial for dynamic elastic behaviors.
- Developing an efficient optimization strategy to navigate the complex, multimodal parameter landscape of elastic materials.

By tackling these challenges, the work contributes to advancing the state of the art in EOM, enabling more practical and efficient robot manipulation of elastic objects through high-fidelity simulation and robust sim-to-real transfer.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and efficient framework for learning manipulation policies for elastic objects, specifically for a slingshot task, that can be directly transferred from simulation to the real world. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations and specific sub-objectives:

1. **Addressing Challenges in Elastic Object Manipulation (EOM):** Elastic objects exhibit highly nonlinear and high-dimensional deformation dynamics, which makes their modeling, estimation, and control fundamentally challenging. Despite their practical importance in various applications (manufacturing, healthcare, everyday interactions), EOM has been comparatively underexplored compared to rigid or other deformable object manipulation. The research aims to bridge this gap by enabling effective manipulation strategies for such objects.

2. **Leveraging Simulation for Policy Learning:** Modern physics simulators offer a safe and scalable environment for extensive exploration and policy learning, which is often costly and potentially destructive in real-world settings (e.g., repeated projectile launches). The motivation is to fully exploit the capabilities of these simulators to learn complex manipulation policies.

3. **Overcoming the Sim-to-Real Gap for Elastic Objects:** A major hurdle in simulation-based learning for EOM is accurately calibrating the elastic behavior between the real world and simulation. Elastic properties (e.g., Young’s modulus, damping) are often visually indistinguishable, making it difficult to construct a physically consistent simulated counterpart. This discrepancy leads to a "sim-to-real gap" that can cause policy degradation during transfer. The work aims to precisely calibrate these properties to enable reliable transfer.

4. **Developing Efficient and Non-Destructive System Identification (SI):** Traditional SI methods often require extensive real-world interaction data or may involve destructive trials. For elastic objects, limited interaction opportunities and the multimodal nature of the parameter landscape further complicate accurate identification. A key motivation is to devise an "one-shot" SI method that requires only a single, non-destructive interaction to estimate critical elastic parameters. This reduces the time, cost, and potential damage associated with calibration.

5. **Comprehensive Parameter Identification:** Prior Real2Sim SI studies often focus on a limited set of soft-body parameters (e.g., Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio). However, the stability and resilience of elastic object behavior also strongly depend on damping effects. A specific objective is to identify a more comprehensive set of five elasticity-related parameters, including Young’s modulus, Poisson’s ratio, elasticity damping, dynamic friction, and damping scale, recognizing the crucial role of damping.

6. **Utilizing Multi-Modal Sensory Data:** Most existing approaches predominantly rely on visual observations. The research is motivated to incorporate haptic feedback (interaction forces) in addition to visual deformation data, as haptic cues can provide direct and complementary information for elasticity estimation, especially when visual information is ambiguous.

7. **Enabling Zero-Shot Sim2Real Policy Transfer:** The ultimate objective is that once the simulator is accurately calibrated, a reinforcement learning (RL) policy trained within this high-fidelity simulation can be directly deployed to the real robot without any further real-world trial-and-error or adaptation. This signifies the success of the Real2Sim2Real paradigm.

The slingshot manipulation task, involving rubber bands with diverse yet visually indistinguishable elastic properties, serves as a representative and challenging benchmark to evaluate the proposed framework against these objectives.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, Sling2Sim2Real, is a two-stage Real2Sim2Real pipeline designed for one-shot elastic system identification (SI) and subsequent non-destructive slingshot policy learning.

**A. Overall Framework**
The framework comprises two main stages:
1. **One-shot System Identification (Real2Sim):** This stage focuses on calibrating elasticity-relevant simulation parameters from a single, non-destructive real-world interaction (a controlled pulling and releasing of the sling without a projectile).
2. **Policy Learning (Sim2Real):** After calibrating the simulator, an optimal slingshot policy (π) is learned using Reinforcement Learning (RL) within this high-fidelity simulator. This policy is then transferred directly to the real system without additional real-world refinement.

**B. One-shot System Identification (Real2Sim)**
This stage is formulated as a black-box parameter estimation problem, aiming to minimize the discrepancy between observed real-world interaction data (D_real) and simulated responses (D_sim). It consists of three sequential steps:

1. **Single Data Acquisition:**
 - **Interaction Protocol:** A Franka Emika Panda robot executes a predefined sequence of primitive actions: grasping the sling at its bottom point, pulling it by 40 cm from a U-shaped fork along a specific direction, and then releasing it to excite its elastic dynamics. This interaction is non-destructive, meaning no projectile is launched.
 - **Data Collection:** Synchronized robot proprioceptive signals and visual observations are recorded.
 - **Proprioceptive Data:** End-effector velocity (˙x_real_ee,t) and interaction force (f_real_ee,t) from a wrist-mounted force-torque (FT) sensor are captured.
 - **Visual Data:** Point cloud (p_real_sling,t) representing sling deformation is obtained from an external RGB-D camera.
 - **Data Preprocessing:**
 - Force signals are low-pass filtered (cutoff frequency 0.224 rad/s) to mitigate noise.
 - The sling region is segmented from RGB images using the Segment Anything Model (SAM), and masked depth pixels are projected to 3D to generate the sling point cloud.
 - The point cloud is downsampled to 10^3 points using farthest point sampling, followed by statistical and radius outlier removal.
 - An episode of T=300 time samples (9 seconds) is recorded.

2. **Evolutionary Initialization (Global Exploration):**
 - **Objective:** To find K_top distinct elastic parameter candidates {θ^(1)..., θ^(K_top)} for subsequent local optimizations. The parameter set θ comprises five variables: [Young’s modulus, Poisson’s ratio, elasticity damping, dynamic friction, damping scale].
 - **Algorithm:** Differential Evolution (DE), a population-based evolutionary algorithm, is employed for its global exploration capabilities. Initial parameter populations are generated using Latin Hypercube Sampling.
 - **Fitness Function (Composite Loss L):** The DE algorithm optimizes a composite loss function L = L_geom + L_force + L_work, which quantifies the discrepancy between real and simulated data:
 - **L_geom (Geometric Loss):** Measures the visual mismatch between real and simulated sling geometry using an aggregated Chamfer Distance (CD) between their respective point clouds over time.
 - **L_force (Force Loss):** Minimizes the squared Euclidean distance between the real and simulated end-effector interaction forces over time, directly calibrating the stiffness of the simulated object.
 - **L_work (Work Loss):** Computes the absolute difference in accumulated mechanical work (integral of force-velocity product) between real and simulated interactions. This term improves robustness to noise and temporal misalignments, and helps calibrate both overall stiffness and energy dissipation governed by damping.
 - **Multi-start Initialization Generation:** The search history (H) of all candidate solutions generated by DE is retained. From H, K_top distinct parameter regions are identified using distance-based Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) with a suppression radius δ (K_top=5, δ=0.1). For each selected region center (μ^(k)), an empirical covariance matrix (Σ^(k)) is estimated from its K_NN nearest neighbors (K_NN=15) within H. These (μ^(k), Σ^(k)) pairs serve as multi-start initialization distributions for the next stage.

3. **Covariance-aware Optimization (Local Exploitation):**
 - **Algorithm:** Parallelized CMA-ES (Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy) solvers are run, initialized with the K_top starting points {(μ^(k), Σ^(k))}. CMA-ES is selected for its efficiency in local optimization.
 - **Covariance-informed Search:** Each CMA-ES instance initializes its search distribution by re-parameterizing the search space using the Cholesky decomposition of its assigned covariance matrix (Σ^(k) = L^(k)L^(kT)). This transforms an isotropic latent Gaussian sample (z ∼ N(0, I)) into an anisotropic one (θ_init = μ^(k) + L^(k)z), aligning the initial search with the local curvature and parameter correlations derived from the DE exploration.
 - **Optimization:** Each parallel CMA-ES solver iteratively updates its candidate solutions by minimizing the same composite loss L. Simulation data (D_sim) is generated in Isaac Sim for each parameter set evaluated by the solvers.
 - **Final Output:** The optimal parameter set θ* is selected as the one yielding the lowest loss among all solutions found by the parallel CMA-ES solvers.

**C. Sim2Real Policy Learning**
Upon obtaining a high-fidelity calibrated simulator, the slingshot task is formulated as a one-step Markov Decision Process (MDP).
- **Reference Frame:** An origin (O) is defined at the midpoint between the two fork tips, with the y-axis pointing towards the target and the z-axis vertically.
- **State (S):** Comprises the 2D projectile position (y_p,t, z_p,t) in a plane perpendicular to the fork, and the target displacement (g).
- **Action (A):** Defined by the desired drawn position of the projectile (x_proj ∈ R^2). The projectile's elevation angle is aligned with the stretched sling plane for stable launch.
- **Reward (R):** A dartboard-style function, R = 5e^(-|y_p - g|), where a penalty of -1 is given for unsuccessful launches (e.g., unexpected contact, fall).
- **Policy Training:** An optimal policy π*(s) is trained in the calibrated simulator using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), implemented with the RSL-RL library. The actor and critic networks use hidden dimensions with ELU activations.
- **Zero-Shot Transfer:** The learned policy π* is directly deployed to the real-world slingshot system without any further real-world adaptation or fine-tuning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Sling2Sim2Real focused on assessing its accuracy in zero-shot RL policy transfer for the slingshot task and verifying the effectiveness of its SI components. Experiments were conducted using a real Franka Emika Panda arm, three elastic bands with varying stiffness (Band 1: soft, Band 2: medium, Band 3: stiff), and three target distances (g = {172.5cm, 192.5cm, 212.5cm}).

**A. Benchmark Studies (Quantitative Evaluation)**
- **Overall Performance (Table I):** Sling2Sim2Real consistently demonstrated superior performance, achieving the lowest projectile landing distance errors across all tested rubber band types and target distances compared to five baseline methods. The average landing distance error for Sling2Sim2Real was 7.17 ± 2.68 cm for Band 1, 14.17 ± 2.16 cm for Band 2, and 19.00 ± 2.47 cm for Band 3.
- **Comparison to Baselines:**
 - **Real2Sim2Real** (identifies Young’s modulus and dynamic friction): Frequently resulted in "N/A" indicating failure to launch or missing the target area entirely. Its identified parameters led to higher composite losses (e.g., 4.1798 for Band 1 vs. 1.9785 for Sling2Sim2Real).
 - **Package SI** (Bayesian optimization for Young’s modulus and damping, geometry-based loss): Performed better than Real2Sim2Real but consistently worse than Sling2Sim2Real. Its geometry-based loss struggled to capture stored elastic energy in high-damping materials (Band 1 and 2), leading to higher losses and larger landing errors. For instance, for Band 2, Package SI had an average error of 35.17 ± 4.58 cm compared to Sling2Sim2Real's 14.17 ± 2.16 cm.
- **Effectiveness of Composite Loss (Table II):** The results demonstrated a clear correlation: lower calibration loss (L) directly corresponded to lower landing distance errors. Sling2Sim2Real achieved the lowest composite loss (L) for all band types, with a maximum reduction of 72.5% compared to the next best baseline (Package SI), indicating the high fidelity of its calibrated simulators.
- **Parameter Identification Insights:** Baselines often estimated higher damping scales, which likely reduced the projectile's kinetic energy, requiring greater stretching and potentially leading to launch failures or significant deviations observed as "N/A." Sling2Sim2Real's balanced parameter estimation prevented such issues.

**B. Ablation Studies**
- **Impact of Optimization Strategy (Table III):** Sling2Sim2Real’s hierarchical optimization framework (DE for global exploration, multi-start CMA-ES for local exploitation) outperformed individual optimization techniques (DE_L, BO_L, Adam_L) that used the same composite loss function. This confirms the benefit of combining these strategies to effectively search the complex parameter space.
 - A specific instance at g=212.5cm showed a slightly higher error for Sling2Sim2Real, suggesting that local refinement from DE might not always guarantee optimal performance, possibly due to simulation inaccuracies or the highly nonlinear parameter space across different target configurations. This points to potential future work on target-specific calibration.
- **Impact of Covariance-informed Multi-start and Work Loss (Table IV):**
 - **Covariance-informed Multi-start:** Sling2Sim2Real significantly outperformed all ablation variants that removed either the multi-start scheme (Sling2Sim2Real^(-m)) or the covariance-informed initialization (Sling2Sim2Real^(-c), Sling2Sim2Real^(-c,-m)). This result highlights that both the multi-start approach and the use of covariance information from DE are crucial for efficient and accurate Real2Sim SI.
 - **Mechanical Work Loss (L_work):** Sling2Sim2Real considerably outperformed Sling2Sim2Real^(-L_work) (without mechanical work loss), demonstrating that the inclusion of L_work, particularly its force-based component, substantially improves SI performance by effectively capturing real-to-sim force discrepancies.

**C. Qualitative Studies (Fig. 4)**
Qualitative demonstrations showed that Sling2Sim2Real accurately launched the projectile towards distant targets using unforeseen slings, with smooth and consistent trajectories. In contrast, trajectories from baselines like Package SI and Real2Sim2Real exhibited greater deviations, visually reinforcing the quantitative findings regarding the accuracy of Sling2Sim2Real's calibrated simulator and subsequent zero-shot policy transfer.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Sling2Sim2Real framework offers several significant contributions and has considerable potential impact on the field of robotics and deformable object manipulation:

1. **Advancement in Elastic Object Manipulation (EOM):** EOM has been a particularly challenging domain due to the complex, nonlinear, and often visually opaque properties of elastic materials. This work provides a robust framework that specifically addresses these challenges, moving the field closer to enabling robots to reliably manipulate a wider range of elastic objects.

2. **Efficiency and Safety in Real-World Interaction:** By achieving accurate system identification from a *single, non-destructive* real-world interaction, Sling2Sim2Real drastically reduces the time, cost, and potential damage associated with extensive data collection or repeated destructive trials. This is particularly impactful for tasks involving delicate or costly elastic materials, or for scenarios where real-world failures are undesirable.

3. **Enhanced Real2Sim Fidelity:** The methodology's ability to accurately identify a comprehensive set of five elasticity parameters, including the often-overlooked damping, is a crucial advancement. Damping plays a critical role in the dynamic behavior and energy dissipation of elastic objects. By integrating geometric, force, and mechanical work losses, the framework constructs a high-fidelity simulator that closely mimics real-world elastic dynamics, thereby bridging a significant sim-to-real gap.

4. **Robust Zero-Shot Sim2Real Transfer:** The successful demonstration of zero-shot transfer of policies learned in the calibrated simulator directly to the real robot is a key indicator of the framework's effectiveness. This eliminates the need for costly and time-consuming real-world policy refinement, accelerating the deployment of robotic manipulation solutions.

5. **Novel Optimization Strategy:** The hierarchical, covariance-informed multi-start optimization approach (combining Differential Evolution for global exploration with parallel CMA-ES for local exploitation, guided by covariance) provides a robust and efficient method for navigating the complex and multimodal parameter landscape inherent to elastic materials. This optimization strategy itself could be applicable to other challenging system identification problems.

6. **Broader Applicability:** While demonstrated on a slingshot task, the principles of one-shot, non-destructive, visuo-haptic system identification for elastic materials are broadly applicable. This framework could be extended to other EOM tasks in diverse sectors such as:
 - **Manufacturing:** Handling elastic components (e.g., rubber seals, hoses, fabrics) in assembly lines.
 - **Healthcare:** Robotic assistance in surgery involving soft tissues or elastic medical devices.
 - **Household Robotics:** Manipulating everyday elastic objects like clothing, sponges, or food items.

The framework's ability to generalize across different elastic materials (soft, medium, stiff bands) and target configurations further enhances its practical utility. This research represents a significant step towards enabling more versatile and capable robots that can seamlessly interact with the deformable world.
