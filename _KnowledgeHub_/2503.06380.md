# TI-JEPA: An Innovative Energy-based Joint Embedding Strategy for Text-Image Multimodal Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> TI-JEPA introduces an energy-based Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture for text-image multimodal systems, extending the JEPA framework to align visual and textual data. This approach achieved state-of-the-art results in multimodal sentiment analysis on the MVSA-Single and MVSA-Multi datasets.

## Problem

- The semantic gap between continuous image data and discrete text data, which hinders effective multimodal understanding.
- Need for robust multimodal encoders that can capture intricate semantic relationships across modalities.
- Challenges like "energy collapse" in training joint embedding models, where distinct inputs produce similar outputs.

## Method

- Introduces **TI-JEPA**, an ==energy-based== Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture, to predict masked image patch representations using visual context and an associated text caption.
- Employs separate image and text encoders along with cross-attention modules to integrate features, followed by a predictor to reconstruct target representations.
- Freezes pre-trained image and text encoders, focusing training on the cross-attention modules to learn cross-modal alignment and mitigate energy collapse.

## Results

- TI-JEPA consistently achieved state-of-the-art performance on multimodal sentiment analysis benchmarks.
- TI-JEPA-Large obtained an accuracy of **76.75%** and F1-score of **74.62%** on MVSA-Single, and **77.55%** accuracy and **75.02%** F1-score on MVSA-Multi.
- The approach of freezing pre-trained encoders effectively mitigated energy collapse and contributed to the model's robust performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Extending the JEPA paradigm to a multimodal setting via predictive learning can effectively align text and image features.
> - Strategically leveraging and freezing strong pre-trained unimodal encoders while training cross-attention modules improves stability and avoids energy collapse.
> - An L1 loss on latent representations within an energy-based framework provides a robust objective for cross-modal prediction.
