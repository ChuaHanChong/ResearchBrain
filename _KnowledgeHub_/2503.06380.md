# TI-JEPA: An Innovative Energy-based Joint Embedding Strategy for Text-Image Multimodal Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> TI-JEPA introduces an energy-based Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture for text-image multimodal systems, extending the JEPA framework to align visual and textual data. This approach achieved state-of-the-art results in multimodal sentiment analysis on the MVSA-Single and MVSA-Multi datasets.

## Problem

- The semantic gap between continuous image data and discrete text data, which hinders effective multimodal understanding.
- Need for robust multimodal encoders that can capture intricate semantic relationships across modalities.
- Challenges like "energy collapse" in training joint embedding models, where distinct inputs produce similar outputs.

## Method

- Introduces **TI-JEPA**, an ==energy-based== Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture, to predict masked image patch representations using visual context and an associated text caption.
- Employs separate image and text encoders along with cross-attention modules to integrate features, followed by a predictor to reconstruct target representations.
- Freezes pre-trained image and text encoders, focusing training on the cross-attention modules to learn cross-modal alignment and mitigate energy collapse.

## Results

- TI-JEPA consistently achieved state-of-the-art performance on multimodal sentiment analysis benchmarks.
- TI-JEPA-Large obtained an accuracy of **76.75%** and F1-score of **74.62%** on MVSA-Single, and **77.55%** accuracy and **75.02%** F1-score on MVSA-Multi.
- The approach of freezing pre-trained encoders effectively mitigated energy collapse and contributed to the model's robust performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Extending the JEPA paradigm to a multimodal setting via predictive learning can effectively align text and image features.
> - Strategically leveraging and freezing strong pre-trained unimodal encoders while training cross-attention modules improves stability and avoids energy collapse.
> - An L1 loss on latent representations within an energy-based framework provides a robust objective for cross-modal prediction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in this paper significantly contributes to the rapidly evolving field of **Multimodal Artificial Intelligence**, specifically focusing on **text-image alignment**. This area is critical for developing AI systems that can process and understand information from various sensory inputs, mirroring human cognitive abilities.

Traditionally, Natural Language Processing (NLP) and Computer Vision (CV) have advanced somewhat independently. However, many real-world applications require understanding information presented across multiple modalities simultaneously, such as visual question answering, image captioning, and cross-modal retrieval. The core challenge, which this paper directly addresses, is the inherent **semantic gap** between the continuous, high-dimensional nature of images and the discrete, symbolic nature of text. Bridging this gap effectively is crucial for enabling coherent understanding and interaction between modalities.

The paper positions itself within the broader landscape of **self-supervised learning (SSL)**, a paradigm that has revolutionized representation learning by reducing reliance on extensive labeled datasets. In the multimodal domain, SSL has shown immense promise by leveraging the rich, complementary information available across modalities. Previous works in multimodal SSL have explored techniques like masked language modeling for multimodal inputs (e.g., VilBERT, UNIMO) and contrastive learning (e.g., CLIP, ALIGN) to learn robust joint representations. These methods aim to align text and image embeddings in a shared latent space, making semantically similar pairs closer together.

A key differentiator and direct inspiration for this work is the emergence of **Energy-Based Models (EBMs)** and, more specifically, **Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs)**. EBMs, by learning a scalar energy function that assigns low energy to compatible configurations, offer a flexible framework for modeling complex dependencies. The recent **Image-based Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (I-JEPA)**, introduced by Assran et al. (with LeCun as a co-author), demonstrated a powerful non-generative self-supervised approach that predicts representations of target blocks within the *same* image. TI-JEPA extends this groundbreaking concept from a single-modality (image) context to a **multimodal (text-image)** setting. By adapting the JEPA framework to integrate textual information for predicting visual features, TI-JEPA offers an innovative self-supervised pre-training strategy that differs from purely contrastive or masked autoencoding approaches by focusing on predictive representation learning through energy-based principles. This positions TI-JEPA as a significant step in developing more robust and semantically aligned multimodal representations, potentially overcoming some limitations of existing methods, such as energy collapse, by leveraging the predictive nature of JEPA and pre-trained components.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce and validate **Text-Image Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (TI-JEPA)**, a novel pre-training strategy designed to overcome the semantic discrepancy problem in text-image multimodal fusion.

The core motivations driving this work stem from the existing challenges and emerging opportunities in multimodal AI:

- **Addressing the Semantic Gap:** The fundamental challenge in text-image multimodal systems is the "semantic gap" – the difficulty in establishing meaningful connections between disparate modalities (continuous visual data vs. discrete textual data). Traditional approaches often fall short in capturing the subtle, latent relationships necessary for deep understanding.
- **Leveraging Energy-Based Models (EBMs):** EBMs offer a powerful and flexible framework for learning compatibility between different inputs by associating low energy with correct configurations. The authors are motivated to explore the untapped potential of EBMs, specifically within the context of text-image alignment, given their demonstrated success in other machine learning domains.
- **Extending Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs):** The recent success of I-JEPA in self-supervised image understanding provides a strong precedent. The motivation is to extend this effective non-generative predictive learning paradigm to the multimodal setting, thereby enabling more robust cross-modal representation learning.
- **Developing a Robust Multimodal Encoder:** The aim is to create a pre-training framework that can produce a "robust multimodal encoder" capable of effectively capturing intricate relationships between text and images. This encoder should be dynamic, flexible, and scalable, potentially by integrating existing high-performing pre-trained models.
- **Achieving State-of-the-Art Performance:** A key motivation is to demonstrate that TI-JEPA can achieve competitive, and ideally state-of-the-art, performance on benchmark multimodal tasks, such as multimodal sentiment analysis, and generalize well to other applications like Visual Question Answering (VQA).
- **Mitigating Energy Collapse:** A specific concern in training joint embedding models, including JEPAs, is "energy collapse" – where diverse inputs yield near-identical outputs. The authors are motivated to design TI-JEPA in a way that leverages pre-trained encoders to mitigate this issue, ensuring representational diversity and model stability.

In essence, the paper seeks to push the boundaries of multimodal fusion by proposing a principled, energy-based self-supervised pre-training method that addresses core challenges in cross-modal understanding and aims for superior performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed **TI-JEPA** architecture is an innovative pre-training approach that adapts the Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) paradigm to the multimodal domain of text and images, leveraging energy-based model (EBM) concepts. The core idea is to predict representations of masked image patches by integrating information from both the surrounding visual context and an associated text caption.

#### 3.1. Architecture Overview

TI-JEPA consists of several key components:
- **Image Encoder (f_I):** Processes both the full image (`I`) and a context-masked version of the image (`I_context`) to generate visual embeddings.
- **Text Encoder (f_T):** Converts the image caption (`T`) into a dense semantic representation.
- **Cross-Attention Modules (X and ˜X):** Two blocks of text-to-image (t2i) cross-attention are employed. Block `X` is used for the context pathway, aligning encoded text features with visual features from the masked context image. Block `˜X` is used for the target pathway, aligning encoded text features with visual features from the full image to create target representations.
- **Predictor (g_φ):** Takes the output of the context cross-attention module and mask tokens to predict the representations of the target image patches.

#### 3.2. Training Strategy – Creating Target and Context Blocks

The training process is divided into two stages for generating representations:
- **Target Representation Creation:**
 - The input image `I` is divided into `N` non-overlapping patches, which are processed by the image encoder `f_I` to get `s_I`.
 - The paired text `T` is tokenized into `L` tokens and processed by the text encoder `f_T` to get `s_T`.
 - These `s_I` and `s_T` embeddings are then passed through the target cross-attention module `˜X` to generate the final target representations `s_y`.
 - From `s_y`, `M` blocks are randomly sampled to serve as the "targets" for prediction.
- **Context Representation Creation:**
 - A single "context block" (`I_x`) is randomly sampled from the original image `I`.
 - Crucially, any regions overlapping with the previously sampled target blocks are removed from `I_x` to prevent trivial predictions.
 - The masked context block is processed by `f_I` and then passed through the context cross-attention module `X` along with the encoded text `s_T` to obtain context representations `s_x`.

#### 3.3. Output Prediction and Objective Function

The predictor `g_φ` receives the context vectors `s_x` from the attention module. For each of the `M` target blocks, the predictor also takes a set of learnable "mask tokens" (with positional encoding) corresponding to the patches to be predicted. The predictor then outputs the predicted representations `ˆs_y`.

The objective function is designed to minimize the L1 distance between the predicted representations `ˆs_y(i)` and the true target representations `s_y(i)` across all `M` sampled target blocks. This L1 loss encourages the model to generate accurate feature representations for the masked regions based on the provided context and text.

#### 3.4. Training Details and Hyperparameters

- **Dataset:** Microsoft COCO 2017 training set (118,000+ image-text pairs) was used for pre-training. One random caption was selected per image.
- **Hardware:** Experiments were conducted on two NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1080 Ti GPUs over 300 epochs.
- **Encoders:**
 - **Image Encoder:** ViT-H (16x16 patch, 224x224 resolution), *pretrained with I-JEPA*. This is a crucial detail, as it leverages existing single-modality self-supervised knowledge.
 - **Text Encoder:** `gte-base-en-v1.5` (Transformer++ backbone), supporting 8192 tokens and 768 embedding dimension.
- **Cross-Attention Modules:** Three variants (Small, Medium, Large) were configured with varying numbers of layers, attention heads, and hidden sizes, influencing model capacity (39M to 131M parameters).
- **Predictor:** Inherited from the original I-JEPA predictor, a shallow Vision Transformer with 12 layers and 12 attention heads.
- **Training Configuration:** Batch size 1024, learning rate 0.001, AdamW optimizer with a momentum scheduler (EMA [0.996, 1.0]). Image masking techniques were applied for robustness (Context Mask Scale [0.85, 1.0], Target Mask Scale [0.15, 0.2]). The model used 1 context block and 4 target blocks for prediction.
- **Key Design Choice:** To mitigate "energy collapse" and leverage pre-trained knowledge, the authors strategically **froze both the image and text encoders**, training *only the cross-attention modules*. This approach focuses computational resources on learning cross-modal alignment while preserving the strong feature extraction capabilities of the pre-trained components.

#### 3.5. Downstream Task: Multimodal Sentiment Analysis

To evaluate the pre-trained TI-JEPA, it was fine-tuned for multimodal sentiment analysis using the MVSA-Single and MVSA-Multi datasets.
- **Data Preprocessing:** Inconsistent samples (conflicting image/text sentiment) were meticulously removed, and majority voting was applied for MVSA-Multi to ensure clear sentiment labels. This reduced the datasets to 4,511 (MVSA-Single) and 17,027 (MVSA-Multi) pairs, split into 8:1:1 for train/val/test.
- **Fine-tuning:** A simple linear classification head was added on top of the pre-trained TI-JEPA. This head was trained for 40 epochs using Adam optimizer and cross-entropy loss.
- **Metrics:** Performance was evaluated using Accuracy and F1-score (harmonic mean of Precision and Recall).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experimental evaluation demonstrates the superior performance of the proposed TI-JEPA architecture in the context of multimodal sentiment analysis, outperforming various established single-modal and multimodal fusion models.

#### 4.1. Comparative Performance Highlights

The paper presents a comprehensive comparison of three configurations of TI-JEPA (Small, Medium, Large) against a range of benchmark models, including traditional methods (SentiBank & SentiStrength), deep learning fusion approaches (CNN-Multi, DNN-LR), and more advanced multimodal models (Co-Memory, MVAN, CLMLF, ITIN, CLIP-CA-CG).

- **Overall Superiority:** TI-JEPA consistently achieves state-of-the-art (SOTA) results across both the MVSA-Single and MVSA-Multi datasets in terms of both accuracy and F1-score. This underscores the effectiveness of its energy-based joint embedding and predictive architecture for aligning textual and visual features.

- **TI-JEPA-Large's Dominance:**
 - On MVSA-Single, TI-JEPA-Large achieved an accuracy of **76.75%** and an F1-score of **74.62%**.
 - On MVSA-Multi, it achieved an accuracy of **77.55%** and an F1-score of **75.02%**.
 - These figures represent the highest performance observed among all compared models, unequivocally establishing TI-JEPA-Large as the new SOTA for this task on these datasets. Its F1-score, which balances precision and recall, is particularly indicative of its robust generalization and ability to capture critical multimodal features without overfitting.

- **Strong Performance of Smaller Variants:**
 - **TI-JEPA-Medium** also showcased highly competitive results, with 75.26% accuracy on MVSA-Single and 75.13% on MVSA-Multi. Notably, it outperformed sophisticated models like CLIP-CA-CG and ITIN, despite having fewer parameters than the Large variant. This indicates the efficiency and robustness of the TI-JEPA framework even with reduced model capacity.
 - **TI-JEPA-Small**, the most compact configuration, still demonstrated performance comparable to, or better than, more complex models such as MVAN and Co-Memory. With 73.03% accuracy on MVSA-Single and 73.59% on MVSA-Multi, it offers an efficient alternative for scenarios with computational constraints.

#### 4.2. Efficacy of the Proposed Approach

- **Effective Cross-Modal Alignment:** The results strongly validate the core hypothesis that leveraging an energy-based predictive architecture with cross-attention mechanisms is highly effective for text-image alignment. The model successfully learns to bridge the semantic gap between modalities.
- **Benefit of Pre-trained Encoders and Freezing Strategy:** The paper highlights that strategically freezing the pre-trained image and text encoders, while only training the cross-attention modules, effectively mitigates the problem of "energy collapse." This approach enables the model to retain diverse and meaningful representations from the pre-trained components while focusing learning resources on cross-modal interactions, contributing significantly to stability, scalability, and overall performance.
- **Generalizability for Multimodal Tasks:** The strong performance on multimodal sentiment analysis, a task requiring deep semantic understanding across modalities, suggests that the learned representations are robust and generalize well. This supports the claim that TI-JEPA can serve as a powerful pre-training foundation for a wider range of multimodal tasks.

In summary, the main findings confirm that TI-JEPA is a highly effective and innovative pre-training strategy for text-image multimodal systems, setting a new benchmark for performance in sentiment analysis by skillfully integrating energy-based predictive learning with strategic use of pre-trained encoders and cross-modal attention.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of TI-JEPA represents a significant advancement in the field of multimodal Artificial Intelligence, particularly for text-image understanding. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Novel Pre-training Paradigm for Multimodal AI:** TI-JEPA pioneers the extension of the successful Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) from single-modality (image) to a multimodal (text-image) setting. This offers a novel self-supervised pre-training strategy that deviates from predominant contrastive learning or masked autoencoding methods, potentially opening new research directions in how multimodal representations are learned. The energy-based framework provides a flexible and powerful way to model compatibility between modalities.

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** By achieving state-of-the-art results on challenging multimodal sentiment analysis benchmarks, TI-JEPA demonstrates its practical effectiveness and superior capability in integrating and aligning information from text and images. This SOTA performance suggests that the learned joint embeddings are highly robust and semantically rich, enabling better understanding for downstream tasks.

- **Addressing Key Challenges in Multimodal Learning:** The paper explicitly tackles the "semantic gap" problem between disparate modalities, which is a fundamental hurdle in multimodal AI. Furthermore, its strategic design choice of leveraging pre-trained encoders and freezing them during cross-attention training effectively mitigates the critical "energy collapse" issue, a common problem in joint embedding models. This contribution to model stability and representational diversity is highly impactful for future research and development.

- **Foundation for Broader Multimodal Applications:** The robust multimodal encoder produced by TI-JEPA has significant potential beyond sentiment analysis. As alluded to in the abstract, it could serve as a powerful foundation for a wide range of other complex multimodal tasks, such as Visual Question Answering (VQA), image captioning, cross-modal retrieval, and even multimodal dialogue systems. A strong generalizable cross-modal representation is a prerequisite for advancing these areas.

- **Efficiency and Scalability through Pre-trained Models:** The methodology of using pre-trained image and text encoders, and focusing training only on the cross-attention modules, makes TI-JEPA more efficient and scalable. It allows researchers and practitioners to build upon existing, high-quality unimodal models, reducing the need for expensive training from scratch and making the framework more accessible. This strategic reuse of knowledge enhances the stability of the training process and improves overall performance.

- **Inspiration for Future Research:** The limitations section, while acknowledging current constraints, also outlines clear and impactful future work, such as ablation studies, expansion to more modalities, and tackling advanced tasks like VQA. This implies that TI-JEPA is a foundational step, providing a strong platform for further exploration and development in multimodal AI, encouraging researchers to explore similar energy-based predictive learning paradigms.

In conclusion, TI-JEPA significantly pushes the boundaries of text-image alignment by introducing a principled, energy-based predictive pre-training strategy. Its empirical success, coupled with its elegant architectural choices addressing key challenges, positions it as an important contribution that will likely influence the design of future multimodal AI systems and inspire further research in this rapidly evolving domain.
