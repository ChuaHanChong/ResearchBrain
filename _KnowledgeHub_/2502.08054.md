# COMBO-Grasp: Learning Constraint-Based Manipulation for Bimanual Occluded Grasping

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Oxford developed COMBO-Grasp, a bimanual robotic system that coordinates two arms for occluded grasping. It uses a self-supervised constraint policy and a reinforcement learning-trained grasping policy, enhanced by value function-guided coordination, to achieve robust grasping of diverse objects in real-world settings.

## Problem

- Desired grasp poses for objects are often kinematically inaccessible due to environmental obstructions (occluded grasping).
- Learning bimanual manipulation skills via reinforcement learning is highly sample-inefficient due to expanded action spaces and complex coordination.
- Learning from demonstration for bimanual tasks requires prohibitively extensive and diverse expert data for generalization.

## Method

- A self-supervised method generates data in simulation to train an initial constraint policy for the non-dominant arm using force closure approximation.
- A grasping policy for the dominant arm is trained using PPO, while dynamically refining the constraint arm's pose via value function-guided policy coordination.
- Teacher policies are distilled into vision-based student policies, which are then deployed on real hardware using point cloud observations.

## Results

- COMBO-Grasp achieved superior sample efficiency and higher overall success rates in simulation compared to end-to-end PPO baselines.
- The system demonstrated robust performance in real-world experiments, achieving an average success rate of **68.3%** across 60 trials with diverse seen and unseen objects.
- Value function-guided policy coordination consistently enhanced teacher policy performance in simulation, proving robust across various guidance scaling parameters.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-supervised data collection for the constraint policy effectively addresses the sample inefficiency challenge of bimanual learning without expert demonstrations.
> - Value function-guided coordination dynamically refines the constraint arm's pose, significantly improving cooperation and overall task success by optimizing for the grasping arm's objective.
> - Modular policy training, coupled with distillation into vision-based student policies, enables robust generalization to unseen objects and effective sim-to-real transfer.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation faces challenges when objects are situated such that their desired grasp poses are kinematically inaccessible due to environmental obstructions, a scenario referred to as occluded grasping. Traditional robot manipulation techniques, such as open-loop planning, often assume unobstructed grasp poses and are therefore insufficient for these complex situations. Contemporary approaches leveraging reinforcement learning (RL) or learning from demonstration (LfD) for complex manipulation tasks, particularly bimanual ones, encounter significant hurdles. RL methods are often sample-inefficient due to the expanded action space and intricate coordination requirements of bimanual systems, compounded by the difficulty of designing effective reward functions and achieving robust sim-to-real transfer. LfD, while effective, necessitates extensive expert demonstrations across a diverse range of objects to ensure generalization, a data collection process that is frequently impractical or cost-prohibitive for bimanual non-prehensile manipulation.

Prior research on occluded grasping has explored single-arm extrinsic dexterity, where an object is manipulated against an external constraint (e.g., a wall) to expose a graspable region. Some bimanual systems have also been developed, but these often focus primarily on object reorientation using both arms, still frequently relying on external constraints. The broader domain of bimanual robotic systems has seen advancements in areas like dynamic handovers and assembly tasks, often employing RL or LfD with inductive biases to manage complexity. Strategies inspired by human bimanual coordination, where one hand stabilizes while the other performs precise manipulation, have been investigated. For instance, some works propose learning separate policies for stabilization and manipulation, or dual-loop frameworks where a constraint policy is refined based on manipulation performance, though these often present sample inefficiency.

COMBO-Grasp positions itself within this landscape by addressing the limitations of existing methods through a novel bimanual framework. It focuses on scenarios without reliance on external environmental constraints, instead leveraging one robot arm for active stabilization. The work explicitly tackles the data efficiency challenges of bimanual RL by introducing a self-supervised approach for training a constraint policy and enhances coordination through value function-guided refinement. Furthermore, it aims to achieve effective generalization to unseen objects and robust sim-to-real transfer via teacher-student policy distillation, moving beyond the need for expert demonstrations for bimanual occluded grasping. This research contributes to the development of more autonomous and dexterous robotic systems capable of operating in unstructured environments with complex manipulation requirements.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable robotic systems to perform occluded grasping, a task where the target object's desired grasp poses are initially inaccessible due to collisions with the environment, such as a supporting surface. The motivation stems from several observations and challenges:

- **Prevalence of Occlusion in Real-World Scenarios:** Many objects in everyday environments rest on surfaces (e.g., a keyboard on a desk) such that direct, prehensile grasping is kinematically infeasible. Humans routinely solve these problems through dexterous bimanual manipulation, reorienting or stabilizing objects to create graspable configurations. The ability for robots to replicate this capability is crucial for enhancing their utility in complex, unstructured environments.
- **Limitations of Unimanual and Traditional Approaches:**
 - **Single-arm manipulation:** While some single-arm methods explore extrinsic dexterity (e.g., pushing against a wall), they are limited by the requirement of external environmental features and may not be generalizable to all occluded scenarios.
 - **Open-loop grasping:** Many existing grasp pose prediction models and open-loop planning systems assume clear access to grasp poses and fail when environmental constraints interfere.
- **Challenges of Advanced Learning Methods for Bimanual Tasks:**
 - **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Bimanual manipulation significantly increases the action space, leading to increased task complexity and severe sample inefficiency during RL training. Designing effective reward functions that naturally encourage coordinated bimanual behavior, such as one arm stabilizing while the other manipulates, is non-trivial. Moreover, achieving robust sim-to-real transfer with RL, often reliant on extensive domain randomization, further exacerbates sample inefficiency.
 - **Learning from Demonstration (LfD):** While capable of acquiring complex skills, LfD for bimanual manipulation demands a large volume of expert demonstrations to generalize across diverse objects and scenarios, which is often prohibitively expensive or time-consuming to collect.
- **Inspiration from Human Bimanual Strategies:** Humans frequently employ a "stabilize-and-manipulate" strategy, where a non-dominant hand secures an object while the dominant hand performs finer manipulations. This strategy serves as a key inspiration for designing a more effective bimanual robotic system.

To address these motivations and overcome the identified challenges, COMBO-Grasp sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:

1. **Develop a bimanual robotic system:** The system should coordinate two arms, with one arm stabilizing the object and the other reorienting it for grasping.
2. **Enhance sample efficiency in bimanual skill acquisition:** By avoiding direct end-to-end RL for the entire bimanual task or reliance on extensive expert demonstrations, the system aims to learn efficient coordination.
3. **Introduce a self-supervised method for constraint policy training:** To mitigate the need for expert demonstrations, a method for generating stabilizing poses using simulation and a physics-based signal (force-closure approximation) is sought.
4. **Improve bimanual coordination during RL training:** A mechanism to refine the stabilizing poses predicted by the constraint policy, aligning them with the grasping arm's objectives, is desired to maximize task success.
5. **Achieve robust sim-to-real transfer and generalization:** The system should operate effectively on both seen and unseen objects in both simulated and real-world environments, utilizing vision-based observations.

These objectives collectively aim to develop a more autonomous, dexterous, and practical robotic manipulation system for tackling complex occluded grasping problems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

COMBO-Grasp is a bimanual robotic system designed for occluded grasping tasks, integrating two coordinated policies and a distillation process for real-world deployment. The system uses two Kinova Gen3 robotic arms, each equipped with a Robotiq 2F-85 gripper with soft fingertips, and a RealSense L515 camera for visual observations.

**Task Formulation:** The task involves a left robot arm (dominant) attempting to grasp a target object, while the right arm (non-dominant) stabilizes it. The process begins with the right arm moving to a predicted support pose, followed by the left arm reorienting and grasping the object, and finally lifting it. The problem is formulated as two Markov Decision Processes (MDPs): a fully observable MDP ($M_1$) for training state-based teacher policies in simulation, and a partially observable MDP ($M_2$) for evaluating vision-based student policies in simulation and the real world.

**Action Space:**
- **Grasping Policy (left arm):** Outputs 6-dimensional delta poses (translations and axis-angle rotations). Gripper closure is automated at the end of the episode.
- **Constraint Policy (right arm):** Outputs a 6-dimensional absolute pose (x, y, and a quaternion for orientation). The z-coordinate is fixed as the arm operates on the table plane.
- **Baseline RL Policy (both arms):** Outputs 12-dimensional delta poses (6 for each arm).

**A. Self-Supervised Data Collection for Constraint Policy:**
To avoid manual demonstrations, a self-supervised data collection method is employed in simulation.
1. **Desired Grasp Pose Generation:** Antipodal sampling is used to generate occluded grasp pose annotations for 48 diverse objects from the Google Scanned Objects dataset.
2. **Constraint Pose Sampling:** The right arm's end-effector poses are randomly sampled near the target object, while the left arm remains stationary.
3. **Force Closure Approximation:** A force (25N × object mass) is applied to the object along the desired grasp approach vector. Force closure is considered successful if the object's velocity remains below a predefined threshold after the force application.
4. **Dataset Creation:** Successful sampled end-effector poses, along with corresponding desired grasp poses and object poses, are added to a dataset. This process yields 3K constraint poses per object, totaling 144K samples.

**B. Teacher Constraint Policy Training:**
A state-based teacher constraint policy ($\pi_{teacher\_const}$) is trained using a Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM) on the self-supervised dataset.
1. **Model Architecture:** An MLP-based denoising model with a three-level UNet architecture and residual blocks.
2. **Inputs:** Object pose, desired grasp pose, and a compact latent representation of the object ID (learned via an autoencoder reconstructing object point clouds).
3. **Training:** The model is trained to predict the noise added to a sample during a forward diffusion process, minimizing Mean Squared Error (MSE). The output is the constraint pose for the right arm.
This policy is trained on privileged information available in simulation.

**C. Teacher Grasping Policy Training:**
After the constraint policy is trained (and its weights frozen), a teacher grasping policy ($\pi_{teacher\_grasp}$) is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) in simulation.
1. **Inputs:** Robot proprioceptive states, object pose, object velocity, desired grasp poses, object IDs, object's mass and friction parameters, and operational space controller (OSC) PID gains.
2. **Domain Randomization:** Additive Gaussian noise is applied to low-dimensional observations, and physics parameters (object mass/friction) and controller parameters are randomized to facilitate sim-to-real transfer.
3. **Execution Sequence:** At the start of each episode, $\pi_{teacher\_const}$ generates a constraint pose. The right arm moves to this pose using an inverse kinematics solver. Then, $\pi_{teacher\_grasp}$ controls the left arm to reorient and grasp the object.
4. **Reward Function:** A composite reward function is used, comprising:
 - Position and orientation distance rewards (left end-effector to desired grasp).
 - Action penalty (discouraging large actions).
 - Collision penalty (self-collision and robot-table collision, excluding grippers).
 - Lift reward (incentivizing object lifting to expose the grasp).
 - Sparse grasp success reward.

**D. Value Function-guided Policy Coordination:**
A core contribution is refining the constraint pose generated by $\pi_{teacher\_const}$ during the RL training of $\pi_{teacher\_grasp}$.
1. **Mechanism:** Inspired by classifier guidance in diffusion models, gradients from the value function $V(x)$ (trained jointly with the grasping policy) are leveraged.
2. **Refinement Process:** The denoising process of the constraint policy is modified to incorporate these gradients, steering the generated constraint pose towards maximizing the estimated value function of the grasping policy. This encourages the constraint pose to be more conducive to a successful grasp.

**E. Policy Distillation for Sim-to-Real Transfer:**
To enable real-world deployment using visual observations, the trained teacher policies are distilled into student policies.
1. **Data Collection:** 10K expert demonstrations are collected by rolling out the teacher policies in simulation, including visual observations (point clouds).
2. **Student Grasping Policy:** A diffusion policy (3D Diffusion Policy - DP3) is used, with a DP3 encoder for point clouds and an MLP for state observations. The outputs are concatenated as conditioning input.
3. **Student Constraint Policy:** A Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM)-based policy is employed for its simplicity, with similar DP3 and MLP encoders for point cloud and state (desired grasp pose) observations.
4. **Distillation Process:** Student policies are trained on the collected expert demonstrations. Small perturbations are applied to point cloud observations during distillation to simulate real-world noise.
5. **Real-World Operation:** Point clouds are obtained from a RealSense L515 camera, pre-processed (cropping, outlier removal, farthest point sampling). Object pose is tracked using FoundationPose, and segmentation masks are generated with SAMTrack to extract target object point clouds. Inverse kinematics (CuRobo) and motion planning (MoveIt) are used to control the right arm, followed by the left arm's grasping policy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of COMBO-Grasp was conducted across simulated and real-world environments, addressing questions related to teacher policy success, generalization, and the impact of value function-guided coordination.

**A. Sample Efficiency in Teacher Policy Training (Simulation)**
- **COMBO-Grasp Performance:** COMBO-Grasp demonstrated superior sample efficiency and achieved a higher overall success rate in solving occluded grasping tasks compared to baseline methods.
- **Baseline PPO Limitations:** A PPO policy controlling both arms struggled to achieve comparable performance due to increased task and system complexity. It often exhibited undesirable behaviors, such as exploiting simulator physics imperfections by attempting high-velocity grasps without effective right-arm constraint, making it less transferable.
- **PPO + Constraint Reward:** Adding a distance-based reward to encourage the right arm to act as a constraint improved performance over vanilla PPO by mitigating undesirable behaviors. However, its success was limited by the inherent difficulty of engineering an optimal reward function for cooperative bimanual tasks.
- **Ablations:**
 - **COMBO-Grasp w/ fixed constraint:** Showed mediocre performance, indicating that a static constraint is often suboptimal for the grasping policy across diverse scenarios.
 - **COMBO-Grasp w/o refinement:** Performed worse than the full COMBO-Grasp, highlighting the importance of dynamically refining the constraint pose.
These results collectively suggest that the proposed coordinated policies and their training methodology effectively accelerate learning and lead to higher success rates.

**B. Student Policy Performance in Simulation (Generalization)**
- **COMBO-Grasp Generalization:** The distilled student policies of COMBO-Grasp effectively handled occluded grasping for both seen and unseen objects in simulation.
- **Impact of Grasp Pose Input:** While performance was reduced when the desired grasp pose was omitted as an input, COMBO-Grasp still achieved relatively competent results.
- **Baseline Generalization:** PPO + Constraint Reward showed better generalization to unseen objects than vanilla PPO, primarily because its learned cooperative strategy was more robust than the physics-exploiting behaviors of vanilla PPO. However, its overall performance remained inferior to COMBO-Grasp due to the limitations of its teacher policy's learning.
These findings underscore the importance of a learned, coordinated strategy for generalization to novel objects.

**C. Ablation of Value Function-guided Policy Coordination (Simulation)**
- **Impact of Guidance:** The performance of the teacher policy decreased when the value function-guided policy coordination was not applied (i.e., scaling parameter `w` = 0).
- **Performance Enhancement:** Incorporating value function guidance consistently enhanced the teacher policy's overall performance. This indicates that the initial self-supervised constraint policy occasionally generates suboptimal poses, and the guidance mechanism enables on-the-fly adjustments to improve cooperation and task success. The system was found to be robust to a range of guidance scaling parameters.

**D. Real-World Experimental Results**
- **Overall Performance:** COMBO-Grasp successfully tackled occluded grasping tasks for both seen and unseen objects in real-world environments, achieving an average success rate of 68.3% across 60 trials (6 objects, 10 trials each).
- **Challenges with Specific Objects:** The system encountered difficulties with round objects, which proved challenging to stabilize reliably in real-world conditions.
- **Effect of Desired Grasp Pose Input:**
 - **With grasp pose input:** Achieved an average success rate of 68.3%. It enabled the system to recover from failed non-prehensile manipulation attempts by reattempting the task. For instance, grasping a keyboard had an 80% success rate.
 - **Without grasp pose input:** Achieved a lower average success rate of 58.3%. Without the guidance of a desired grasp pose, the system struggled more to recover from initial failures, such as ineffective pushes, leading to lower success rates for certain challenging objects like the keyboard (40%).
- **Trade-off:** While the desired grasp pose input improved performance by guiding recovery actions, its omission provides increased flexibility by removing the need for real-time object pose estimation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "COMBO-Grasp" contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by addressing the complex problem of occluded grasping, an area where traditional methods often fall short. The significance and potential impact of this work can be understood through several key aspects:

1. **Enabling Dexterous Manipulation in Complex Environments:** The ability to grasp objects despite initial kinematic occlusions is a fundamental requirement for robots operating in unstructured, human-centric environments (e.g., homes, warehouses, service robotics). COMBO-Grasp provides a framework that allows robots to perform more human-like, bimanual manipulation to reorient and grasp objects that are otherwise inaccessible, significantly expanding their operational capabilities.

2. **Improved Data Efficiency for Bimanual Skill Learning:** Learning bimanual coordination through standard reinforcement learning can be prohibitively sample-inefficient due to the high-dimensional action spaces and intricate inter-arm dependencies. Similarly, learning from demonstration often demands vast quantities of expert data. COMBO-Grasp's approach, which combines self-supervised learning for a constraint policy with RL for a grasping policy and leverages value function-guided coordination, offers a more data-efficient pathway to acquiring complex bimanual skills without reliance on extensive expert demonstrations. This addresses a major bottleneck in robotic skill acquisition.

3. **Enhanced Bimanual Coordination and Robustness:** The introduction of a dedicated constraint policy, trained through a novel self-supervised method, and its subsequent refinement via value function-guided policy coordination, is a significant contribution. This mechanism allows the robotic system to dynamically adjust its supporting actions to maximize the success of the grasping arm, leading to more robust and cooperative bimanual manipulation. This contrasts with simpler baselines where coordination is either implicit or relies on challenging reward engineering.

4. **Effective Sim-to-Real Transfer for Vision-Based Policies:** The research demonstrates successful deployment of the learned policies from simulation to the real world through teacher-student policy distillation. This is crucial for practical applications, as training directly in the real world is often impractical. By using vision-based student policies that operate on point cloud observations, COMBO-Grasp provides a robust method for real-world execution, even with varying object geometries and appearances.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** The identified limitations, such as challenges with highly diverse object shapes or round objects, and the potential for closed-loop constraint adjustments, suggest clear avenues for future research. This work provides a strong foundation for developing more generalized and adaptive bimanual manipulation systems.

In summary, COMBO-Grasp advances the state of the art in robotic manipulation by providing a robust, data-efficient, and generalizable solution to occluded grasping using bimanual coordination. Its potential impact lies in enabling robots to operate with greater autonomy and dexterity in real-world environments, performing tasks that require complex object interaction and rearrangement.
