# Reverse Curriculum Generation for Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Florensa et al. introduce Reverse Curriculum Generation, an automatic curriculum learning method for goal-oriented reinforcement learning tasks with sparse rewards. This approach trains an agent by progressively expanding the range of solvable start states from the goal outwards, successfully enabling learning for complex robotic manipulation tasks that were previously intractable for standard reinforcement learning algorithms.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning agents struggle with sparse rewards in goal-oriented tasks, requiring extensive exploration to find any learning signal.
- Existing solutions like manual reward shaping are time-consuming and can lead to suboptimal policies, while expert demonstrations are often unavailable or costly.
- Current curriculum learning methods in RL often lack automatic adaptation for continuous control or face issues with biased exploration.

## Method

- An iterative curriculum generation scheme adapts the start state distribution for policy optimization, learning 'in reverse' from the goal.
- Training is focused on 'good starts' – states where the agent's success probability is within an intermediate range, ensuring informative learning signals.
- New, physically feasible start states are generated by performing short 'Brownian motion' rollouts in the action space from existing 'seed' states.

## Results

- The Reverse Curriculum Generation method consistently demonstrated superior performance over uniform sampling and other baselines across various tasks, successfully enabling learning for complex robotic manipulation like key insertion, which was previously intractable for standard RL.
- Focusing on 'good starts' (intermediate success probability states) significantly accelerated learning compared to expanding the curriculum from all explored states.
- Manual reward shaping was shown to sometimes degrade performance or offer minimal improvement, underscoring the efficacy of the automated reverse curriculum in complex manipulation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Efficient learning in sparse reward environments can be achieved by working backward from the goal, gradually increasing the difficulty of start states.
> - Prioritizing training on states where the agent's success rate is moderate provides the most effective learning signal, accelerating policy improvement.
> - Physically plausible initial states for continuous control environments can be effectively generated by applying short sequences of random actions from known feasible states.
