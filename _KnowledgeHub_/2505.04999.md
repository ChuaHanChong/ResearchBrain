# CLAM: Continuous Latent Action Models for Robot Learning from Unlabeled Demonstrations

> [!summary] Summary
> University of Southern California researchers develop CLAM (Continuous Latent Action Models), a two-stage framework that learns robot policies from unlabeled expert demonstrations by first pretraining a latent action model using self-supervised inverse and forward dynamics, then training policies on relabeled data, achieving 2-3x performance improvements over state-of-the-art methods in both simulated and real robot tasks while demonstrating that continuous latent actions and joint training of action decoders are critical for fine-grained control without requiring expensive action-labeled expert data.

## Problem

- Collecting action-labeled expert demonstrations for robot learning is expensive, time-consuming, and requires specialized skills, limiting the scalability of imitation learning approaches
- Existing methods that learn from unlabeled data often discretize latent action spaces, which is unsuitable for continuous control tasks requiring fine-grained manipulation
- Previous approaches fail to effectively ground learned latent actions to real-world executable actions due to separate training phases

## Method

![[2505.04999-method.png]]
*Overview of CLAM's two-stage approach. Stage 1 (left) involves pretraining a latent action model with both unlabeled and a small amount of labeled data. Stage 2 (right) uses the pretrained model to annotate unlabeled expert demonstrations with latent actions, which are then used to train a policy.*

- Develop a two-stage framework where Stage 1 pretrains a latent action model using self-supervised inverse and forward dynamics on unlabeled data
- Jointly train the latent action decoder with the dynamics models to ensure better grounding of latent actions to real-world actions
- Use continuous latent action representations instead of discrete ones to enable fine-grained robot control
- Train policies in Stage 2 using the pretrained inverse dynamics model to relabel unlabeled expert data with latent actions

## Results

- Achieves 2-**3x** higher success rates compared to state-of-the-art baselines across DMControl, MetaWorld, and real robot manipulation tasks
- Approaches the performance of behavior cloning trained on privileged expert action-labeled data while using only unlabeled expert demonstrations
- Demonstrates that performance scales with the amount of unlabeled expert data, showing the method's potential for leveraging large-scale observation datasets
- Shows successful transfer from simulation to real-world robot tasks, validating the approach's practical applicability for robotics deployment

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Continuous latent actions are essential for robot control tasks, as discretization significantly degrades performance in manipulation scenarios
> - Joint training of the action decoder with the latent action model is critical for learning representations that can be effectively grounded to executable actions
> - Unlabeled expert data combined with small amounts of non-expert action-labeled data can achieve performance comparable to methods using expensive expert action-labeled datasets
> - Image features learned by the pretrained inverse dynamics model provide effective initialization for downstream policy learning

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a critical challenge in robot learning: the need for large, action-labeled datasets. The paper builds upon several existing research areas:

- **Imitation Learning (IL):** The core concept of training robots by imitating expert demonstrations is fundamental to this work. However, it identifies the bottleneck of requiring action labels, a common limitation of traditional IL.
- **Self-Supervised Learning:** The paper leverages self-supervision to overcome the action label bottleneck. Specifically, the approach uses an inverse dynamics model (IDM) and forward dynamics model (FDM) to learn representations from unlabeled data by predicting actions and future states, respectively.
- **Latent Action Models:** The work extends the existing line of research into latent action models, where actions are represented in a lower-dimensional, learned space. By learning a latent action space, the method can leverage unlabeled data to train the action representation without relying on labeled action examples.
- **Learning from Observation (LfO):** LfO is a broader field where robots learn from observing sequences of actions without any form of direct supervision. The paper aims to address one of the primary challenges with LfO: the lack of low-level action labels.
- **Representation Learning:** A key component of the paper is learning effective representations of the environment and robot actions. By learning features using the IDM, the authors hope to train a policy that can effectively use those learned features for action generation.
- **Offline Reinforcement Learning:** The approach learns from a fixed dataset of experience, which aligns with offline RL approaches. This avoids the need for online interaction with the environment during training, which can be costly or unsafe.

The paper's contribution lies in several key areas: (1) demonstrating the importance of *continuous* latent actions for fine-grained robot control, contrary to previous works that often discretize latent action spaces. (2) showing that *jointly training* a latent action model with an action decoder leads to a latent space that is more easily grounded to real-world actions, and (3) showing that performant policies can be learned *without action-labeled expert data*, relying instead on unlabeled expert data combined with a small amount of *non-expert* action-labeled data, addressing the scalability limitations of previous approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a method for training robot policies that overcomes the reliance on large amounts of action-labeled expert demonstrations. The primary motivations are:

- **Scalability:** Action-labeled expert demonstrations are expensive and time-consuming to collect, limiting the scale of training data. The authors aim to leverage the abundance of unlabeled observation data (e.g., videos) to learn robot policies more efficiently.
- **Practicality:** Collecting expert data requires significant human effort and specialized skills. The authors want to reduce the need for expert teleoperation by using play data that can be gathered in a more scalable manner.
- **Generalization:** By learning from diverse, unlabeled data, the authors hope to train policies that are more robust and can generalize to new tasks and environments.

Specifically, the paper aims to answer the following questions:

- How effective is CLAM at learning policies without action-labeled expert demonstrations?
- How important are continuous latent actions and jointly training the action decoder?
- Can CLAM scale to learn capable robot policies in real-world scenarios?

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper introduces "Continuous Latent Action Models" (CLAM), a two-stage approach for learning robot policies from unlabeled data:

- **Stage 1: Latent Action Model (LAM) Training:** A latent action model (LAM) is pretrained using a self-supervised objective. The LAM consists of:
 - A **latent Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM):** Predicts a continuous latent action (z\_t) between two consecutive observations (o\_t, o\_{t+1}).
 - A **latent Forward Dynamics Model (FDM):** Predicts the next observation (o\_{t+1}) given the current observation (o\_t) and the latent action (z\_t).
 - A **Latent Action Decoder:** This component maps the learned latent actions to executable environment actions. Crucially, the action decoder is *jointly trained* with the LAM, which is identified as a key contribution of this work.
 - The LAM is trained to minimize the reconstruction error between the predicted and actual next observations.

- **Stage 2: Latent Action Policy Training:**
 - The pretrained IDM is used to "relabel" the *unlabeled expert* data (D\_{unlabeled-expert}) with latent actions.
 - A latent action policy (π\_θ) is trained to predict the latent actions (z\_t) given the current observation (o\_t) using imitation learning on the relabeled data.
 - During inference, the learned policy predicts latent actions, which are then decoded into environment actions using the action decoder.

The authors compare CLAM to several state-of-the-art baselines and use both simulated and real-world robot experiments to evaluate their approach.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments yielded several significant findings:

- **CLAM outperforms existing methods:** In both simulated (DMControl, MetaWorld) and real-world robot tasks, CLAM significantly outperformed state-of-the-art baselines, often by a factor of 2-3x in terms of task success rate. In some cases, it even approached the performance of behavior cloning with *expert, action-labeled* data (a privileged scenario not available to other methods).
- **Continuous latent actions are crucial:** Discretizing the latent action space, as done in previous works (e.g., LAPO, LAPA), significantly hinders performance in continuous control tasks. CLAM's use of continuous latent actions enables more fine-grained control.
- **Joint training of action decoder is essential:** Jointly training the latent action model and the action decoder greatly improves the ability to ground latent actions to real-world actions, leading to significantly better downstream policy performance.
- **Learning without expert action labels is possible:** CLAM can learn performant policies without relying on any action-labeled *expert* demonstrations. It leverages unlabeled expert data and a small amount of action-labeled *non-expert* (e.g., play) data, making it more scalable and practical.
- **Image feature pretraining helps:** The image encoders of the pre-trained IDM can be used as pre-trained image features for the latent action policy (Stage 2), leading to positive transfer.
- **Scalability:** The paper shows that the performance of the latent action policy scales with the amount of unlabeled expert data (|D\_{unlabeled-expert}|).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance of this work lies in its potential to:

- **Reduce the cost and effort required for robot learning:** By reducing the need for action-labeled expert data, CLAM can make robot learning more accessible and scalable.
- **Enable robots to learn from diverse, real-world data sources:** The ability to leverage unlabeled videos and other observation data opens up new possibilities for training robots from readily available sources.
- **Advance the field of imitation learning:** By addressing the limitations of traditional imitation learning, CLAM contributes to the development of more powerful and flexible robot learning techniques.
- **Accelerate the deployment of robots in real-world applications:** By making robot learning more efficient and practical, CLAM can contribute to the wider adoption of robots in various industries.

The research has a significant impact due to its experimental results that demonstrate a performance increase in both simulated and real-world tasks. The method allows for using cheaper, non-expert data to train a robot, which is more cost-effective and scalable.

The paper is a valuable contribution to the field of robotics, with a clear problem definition, a well-designed approach, thorough experimental validation, and a discussion of limitations and future directions. The work has the potential to significantly impact the development and deployment of robots in various real-world applications.
