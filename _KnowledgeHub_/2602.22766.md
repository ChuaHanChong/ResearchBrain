# Imagination Helps Visual Reasoning, But Not Yet in Latent Space

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Beijing Jiaotong University and Tsinghua University critically investigated the efficacy of latent visual reasoning (LVR) in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) and found that latent tokens exhibit limited causal influence on reasoning outcomes. They propose CapImagine, an explicit text-space imagination method, which demonstrated superior performance, including average improvements of 3.44% on HR-Bench and 2.6% on V* over existing LVR approaches.

## Problem

- The underlying mechanisms driving the effectiveness of Latent Visual Reasoning (LVR) in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) remained poorly understood.
- It was unclear whether MLLMs truly performed deliberative visual reasoning within their latent space or if latent tokens served a more superficial role.
- A lack of rigorous investigation into the causal validity and functional role of latent tokens in existing LVR methods existed.

## Method

- A ==Causal Mediation Analysis== was conducted, modeling the LVR process as an Input → Latent Tokens → Final Answer causal chain, with targeted interventions on both input and latent tokens.
- ==CapImagine== was proposed, a text-space imagination method that converts semantic changes from intermediate visual manipulations into explicit textual captions for MLLMs.
- A high-quality dataset was constructed by extensively rewriting and filtering existing MLLM training data, formalizing ==visual imagination directly in text space==.

## Results

- Causal analysis confirmed latent tokens' high inter/intra-instance cosine similarity and their negligible impact on answer accuracy even after significant corruption, indicating limited causal activity.
- CapImagine consistently outperformed latent-space baselines (e.g., Monet, LVR), achieving average improvements of **3.44%** on HR-Bench and **2.6%** on V*, and over **10 points** on BLINK tasks.
- Dependency analysis on CapImagine showed strong causal influence from its explicit text-form imagination variable, and the method achieved inference speeds comparable to latent-space alternatives while being **twice as fast** as some tool-augmented methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Latent tokens in current LVR methods are largely homogeneous, insensitive to input variations, and progressively collapse into highly identical states, indicating an 'Input-Latent Disconnect'.
> - Drastic alterations to latent tokens resulted in only marginal changes to model accuracy, demonstrating a 'Latent-Answer Disconnect' and suggesting they exert minimal causal influence on the final output.
> - Explicit, text-based imagination maintains diverse, causally active intermediate representations, supporting more faithful and effective visual reasoning than opaque latent-space operations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Visual Reasoning within Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has been advancing, with a focus on enabling models to perform complex tasks requiring detailed visual understanding, such as mathematical reasoning, spatial understanding, and fine-grained visual perception. Current approaches primarily fall into two categories:

- **Visual Reasoning with Tools:** This paradigm involves MLLMs actively engaging with visual content by generating interleaved multimodal reasoning trajectories. These methods employ explicit function calls or rely on a fixed set of tools (e.g., zoom-in, image drawing, retrieval) to incorporate visual semantics into the reasoning process. While effective in expanding perceptual bandwidth, these tools are often rigid and do not fully emulate human-like imagination.
- **Latent Visual Reasoning (LVR):** Introduced as a novel paradigm, LVR aims to mimic human imagination by deliberating through the hidden states, or "latent tokens," within MLLMs. The premise is that aligned input embeddings from different modalities allow LVR models to output latent tokens encoding rich visual semantics, enabling a broader and less constrained form of "Visual Imagination." Various LVR methods have been proposed, typically supervising these latent tokens using visual features or hidden representations from teacher models, and have demonstrated empirical performance on vision-centric tasks.

This paper positions itself as a critical investigation into the internal mechanisms of LVR. Despite the empirical success of LVR methods, the underlying mechanisms driving their effectiveness and the actual role of latent tokens remain unclear. The work aims to demystify whether MLLMs genuinely perform deliberative reasoning in the latent space and, based on its findings, proposes an alternative approach to visual imagination that operates in text space, thereby challenging the necessity and efficacy of current latent reasoning implementations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to systematically investigate the internal mechanisms and actual causal effectiveness of Latent Visual Reasoning (LVR) within Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). This overarching objective is driven by several motivations and broken down into specific research questions:

1. **Demystifying LVR Efficacy:** Despite empirical performance, the fundamental mechanisms by which LVR methods achieve their results, and the precise role and behavior of latent tokens, are not well understood. The authors are motivated to uncover whether MLLMs truly perform deliberate reasoning in the latent space or if other factors contribute to observed performance.
2. **Evaluating Latent Token Validity:** A key motivation is to determine the validity of latent reasoning. The research seeks to ascertain if the latent tokens, which are expected to encode rich visual semantics and facilitate visual imagination, actually contribute meaningfully to the causal reasoning process. This involves examining:
 - Whether latent tokens effectively attend to and vary with the input sequence.
 - Whether perturbations to latent tokens significantly impact the final answer, indicating a causal link.
 - The extent to which latent tokens encode sufficient visual information to support downstream reasoning independently.
3. **Challenging the Necessity of Latent Reasoning:** If the investigation reveals limitations in current LVR implementations, a further motivation is to propose and evaluate alternative paradigms for visual imagination that might offer more faithful, interpretable, and causally effective reasoning. This implicitly questions whether the complexity of latent space manipulation is necessary or if simpler approaches could yield comparable or superior results.
4. **Guiding Future Development:** By providing a rigorous diagnosis of current LVR failures, the study aims to establish a crucial foundation for future improvements in visual reasoning methods. The findings are intended to guide the design of more effective architectures and training paradigms that can truly leverage internal states for visual imagination.

In summary, the motivation stems from a recognized gap in understanding the core functionality of LVR, prompting a rigorous causal analysis to clarify the role of latent tokens and explore alternative, potentially more effective, mechanisms for MLLM-based visual reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a multi-faceted methodology combining Causal Mediation Analysis, detailed probing studies, and the development of a novel text-space imagination method.

#### 3.1. Causal Mediation Analysis of Latent Visual Reasoning (LVR)

The core approach models the visual reasoning process as a causal chain: Input (X) → Latent Tokens (Z) → Final Answer (Y). The study then conducts targeted interventions to analyze the causal links between these components.

- **Investigation of X → Z (Input to Latent Tokens):**
 - **Perturbation:** Instance-level perturbations were applied to the input X, involving altering the entire input sequence.
 - **Measurement:** The resulting changes in latent tokens Z were measured using cosine similarity.
 - **Analysis Types:**
 - *Inter-instance Analysis:* Examined latent tokens at fixed positions across diverse instances and tasks.
 - *Intra-instance Analysis:* Sampled all latent tokens within a single instance, then averaged across instances.
 - **Baselines for Analysis:** Three representative LVR models were used: Monet (distillation-based, general scenario), LVR (image feature supervision, general scenario), and Mirage (image feature supervision, task-specific).
 - **Comparative Analysis:** The behavior of latent tokens was compared against text tokens, image tokens, and inner representations of the MLLM after the input sequence to establish a baseline for informative semantic encoding.

- **Investigation of Z → Y (Latent Tokens to Final Answer):**
 - **Intervention Analysis on Latent Tokens (Z):**
 - **Perturbations (do(Z)):**
 - *Strong Intervention:* Forcing all latent tokens across different positions and instances to be an identical, shared tensor.
 - *Gaussian Noise Injection:* Injecting Gaussian noise into latent tokens (Z_i = Z_i + ε, where ε ∼ N(0, σ²)).
 - *Gaussian Noise Replacement:* Replacing latent tokens entirely with Gaussian noise (Z_i = ε ∼ N(0, σ²)).
 - *Zeroing:* Setting all latent tokens to a small value close to zero (Z_i = μ ≈ 0).
 - **Models Tested:** Monet and Mirage (stage-1 and stage-2 variants).
 - **Measurement:** Performance variations in the final answer (Y) on benchmarks like V*, HR-Bench, MME-RealWorld-Lite, and VSP dataset (for Mirage). The goal was to quantify the causal effect of Z on Y.
 - **Probing Analysis on Latent Tokens (Z):**
 - **Focus:** Monet, as its latent supervision aims to optimize visual signals and textual semantics.
 - **Methodology:**
 - Question-image pairs from V* were sampled, and their corresponding latent embeddings {Z_i} were collected during inference.
 - A set of 30 multiple-choice VQA questions {(Z_i, ˜q_i)} were manually constructed. These derived questions focused on the same image regions as the original queries but probed different attributes of the referenced objects.
 - **Measurement:** Model performance when using *only* the collected latent tokens {Z_i} as input to answer these derived questions, compared to baselines including text-only guessing and using the original image.

#### 3.2. Proposed Alternative: CapImagine

Motivated by the findings from the LVR analysis, the authors proposed CapImagine, a text-space imagination method.

- **Method Design:** Instead of latent variables, CapImagine converts semantic changes from intermediate images into explicit textual captions. This teaches the MLLM to "imagine" visual transformations through a text-space reasoning chain, grounded in concrete intermediate visual evidence.

- **Dataset Construction (based on Monet-SFT-125K):**
 - **Data Rewriting:**
 - For visual search subsets (Visual-CoT, Zebra-CoT) involving zooming, Qwen3-VL-4B was prompted to generate concise captions refocusing highlighted visual semantics from the original question and highlighted image region.
 - For subsets with direct image manipulations (Refocus, CogCoM), Qwen3-VL-4B described visual differences between original and manipulated images, verbalizing key information (e.g., marked values, highlighted text).
 - **Refinement:** An MLLM was employed to globally refine the rewritten reasoning chains to correct inconsistencies and improve fluency.
 - **Data Filtering:** A quality assessment using an MLLM was performed on each training instance (correctness of reasoning, question ambiguity). Instances with flaws were filtered out, retaining 17k high-quality instances.

#### 3.3. Experimental Evaluation of CapImagine

- **Benchmarks:** CapImagine was evaluated on a diverse set of high-resolution visual perception benchmarks (V*, HR-Bench-4K/8K, MME-RealWorld-Lite), abstract visual reasoning tasks (BLINK, Hyperphantasia, STARE), and TableVQA for diagram/table image generalization.
- **Baselines:** A wide range of models were compared:
 - Open-source MLLMs: InternVL3-8B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B (used as CapImagine's backbone).
 - Reasoning with Tool methods: DeepEyes, PixelReasoner.
 - Reasoning through Imagination methods (latent-space): LVR, Monet.
 - Text-space reasoning methods: PAPO, Vision-R1, R1-Onevision.
 - Proprietary model: GPT-4o.
- **Training Details:** Fine-tuning via CoT-SFT using the Monet codebase on 8 A800-80G GPUs.
- **Ablation Studies:** Controlled experiments were conducted to isolate the impact of data rewriting, data filtering, and to compare CapImagine with Monet under strict data parity (using the same 17K curated subset).
- **Dependency Analysis (for CapImagine):** A causal mediation analysis similar to that for LVR was performed on CapImagine's text-form imagination variable Z. This involved inter-instance and intra-instance similarity analysis of hidden states, and explicit manipulation of the reasoning process (corrupting imagination content) to assess its causal impact on the final answer.
- **Efficiency Analysis:** Inference speed (decoding time) of CapImagine was compared against Monet and DeepEyes across different V* categories.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding both Latent Visual Reasoning (LVR) and the proposed CapImagine method.

#### 4.1. Findings on Latent Visual Reasoning (LVR)

The causal mediation analysis uncovered significant limitations in how current LVR methods utilize latent tokens:

- **Input-Latent Disconnect (X → Z):**
 - **Homogeneity and Collapse:** Latent tokens exhibited high cosine similarity across different instances and tasks. This suggests they encode minimal information derived from the specific input images or questions.
 - **Progressive Degeneration:** Within individual instances, latent tokens progressively collapsed into highly similar representations as reasoning continued. The MLLM backbone applied diminishing modifications to these states, leading to convergence toward uniform representations. This contrasts with text/image tokens and inner MLLM representations, which maintained distinct and informative semantics. For instance, LVR models degenerated rapidly, while Monet's latent tokens, initially richer, lost distinctiveness by the fifth step. Mirage showed minimal distinctiveness throughout.

- **Latent-Answer Disconnect (Z → Y):**
 - **Limited Causal Effect (Intervention Analysis):** Drastic alterations to latent tokens (e.g., forcing them to an identical tensor, injecting Gaussian noise, or setting them to near zero) resulted in only marginal changes to the final answers. Performance fluctuations were minimal; in some cases, a slight improvement was observed (e.g., 0.5% on V* for Monet), while others showed only minor degradation (e.g., 1.0% on HR-Bench-4K, 0.7% on MME-RealWorld-Lite). A significant decline only occurred under extreme intervention (setting to near zero) in a specific stage of Mirage, which resulted in repetition. This indicates that latent tokens exert limited causal influence on the final output.
 - **Insufficient Semantic Encoding (Probing Analysis):** When latent tokens were used as the sole input for answering derived VQA questions, their performance was notably weak, even falling below text-only guessing baselines. This suggests that latent tokens encode limited task-relevant visual semantics and are insufficient to support accurate downstream reasoning independently.

These findings collectively indicate that latent tokens in current LVR methods behave more like soft prompts or placeholders rather than active carriers of visual imagination or genuine reasoning, implying that the model likely employs an implicit shortcut that bypasses the intended latent visual reasoning pathway.

#### 4.2. Findings on CapImagine (Text-Space Imagination)

The proposed CapImagine method demonstrated effectiveness and distinct causal properties:

- **Superior Performance:** CapImagine consistently outperformed latent-space baselines, including Monet, across diverse benchmarks.
 - **High-Resolution Perception:** It achieved average improvements of 3.44% on HR-Bench and 2.6% on V*. On MME-RealWorld-Lite, it showed substantial gains where a reproduced Monet exhibited marginal improvements over its base model.
 - **Abstract Visual Reasoning:** CapImagine generalized well to abstract tasks like Jigsaw and multi-view reasoning, surpassing LVR and Monet by over 10 points. On TableVQA, it improved over Monet by 6.1%.
 - It also outperformed other text-space reasoning methods (PAPO, Vision-R1, R1-Onevision) across various benchmarks.

- **Strong Causal Efficacy:**
 - **Input-Imagination Dependency:** Dependency analysis of CapImagine's text-form imagination revealed consistently low inter-instance cosine similarity and substantial intra-instance diversity among consecutive hidden states. This indicates a strong causal dependency between the input (X) and the text-form imagination (Z), where each imagination token encodes distinct semantic content.
 - **Pronounced Impact on Answer:** Intervening on CapImagine's text-form imagination (Z) by deliberately altering its content led to a sharp drop in performance, falling below random-guess levels. This demonstrates a strong and direct causal influence of the explicit text-form imagination process on the final prediction (Y).

- **Impact of Data Rewriting and Filtering:**
 - **Data Rewriting:** Replacing the text-space imagination descriptions with a single `<think_image>` token resulted in consistent performance degradation (e.g., a 3.13% drop on V*), validating the effectiveness of text-driven visual imagination.
 - **Data Filtering:** Training without the comprehensive data filtering step led to further performance decline, demonstrating the necessity of quality control in dataset construction.

- **Comparison under Data Parity:** When Monet was trained on the identical 17K curated subset used by CapImagine, it still significantly underperformed CapImagine, substantiating that explicit text-space reasoning is more effective than current latent-space paradigms even under strictly controlled data conditions.

- **Efficiency:** Despite using relatively long text-form imagination sequences, CapImagine achieved inference speeds comparable to the latent-space method Monet and was nearly twice as fast as the tool-augmented method DeepEyes, demonstrating a favorable trade-off between effectiveness and efficiency.

In conclusion, the findings indicate that while "imagination helps visual reasoning," current implementations of latent-space imagination do not yet effectively harness this potential. Explicit text-space imagination, as demonstrated by CapImagine, provides a more causally effective and higher-performing alternative.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides a critical and systematic examination of Latent Visual Reasoning (LVR), contributing significantly to the understanding and future development of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs).

**Theoretical Contributions:**
- **Diagnosis of LVR Failures:** The study rigorously applies Causal Mediation Analysis to LVR methods, exposing fundamental disconnections: the "Input-Latent Disconnect" (latent tokens are homogeneous and degenerate, not effectively attending to input) and the "Latent-Answer Disconnect" (latent tokens have minimal causal impact on the final answer and encode limited visual semantics). This analysis critically challenges the assumed efficacy of current latent-space implementations for visual imagination and reasoning.
- **Conceptual Clarification:** The findings suggest that current latent tokens behave more like soft prompts or placeholders rather than active carriers of genuine visual imagination, offering a clarified understanding of their actual role.

**Practical Contributions:**
- **Effective Alternative Paradigm:** The introduction of CapImagine, a text-space imagination method, demonstrates that visual reasoning through explicit textual descriptions can significantly outperform existing complex latent-space baselines. This provides a simpler, more interpretable, and causally effective alternative for integrating visual "thoughts" into reasoning chains.
- **Dataset Construction Methodology:** The paper details a systematic process for data rewriting and filtering to create high-quality, text-based visual reasoning datasets, which can serve as a template for future research aiming to verbalize visual transformations.

**Guidance for Future Research:**
- **Re-evaluation of Latent Space Design:** The findings strongly advocate for a re-evaluation of core design choices and mechanisms in LVR. The explicit identification of "representation degeneration" as a root cause for latent space ineffectiveness points towards the necessity of entirely new training paradigms to regularize the latent space for genuine causal reasoning.
- **Foundation for Improved Methods:** By providing both diagnostic tools and a strong, interpretable text-space baseline, the work equips the research community with the means to develop more faithful, interpretable, and causally effective LVR methods in the future. It underscores that while the theoretical capacity of high-dimensional latent spaces is vast, effectively harnessing it for robust, causally sound reasoning remains an open challenge.
- **Shifting Focus for Imagination:** The work suggests that for many visual reasoning tasks, explicit textual imagination can be a more reliable and efficient mechanism than current implicit latent-space approaches, potentially guiding researchers to explore the rich capabilities of language-driven imagination more thoroughly.

In summary, this research provides a critical and constructive analysis, revealing weaknesses in current LVR implementations and proposing a viable alternative that highlights the power of explicit, text-driven imagination. Its impact lies in refining the understanding of visual reasoning in MLLMs and directing future efforts toward developing more robust and transparent AI systems.
