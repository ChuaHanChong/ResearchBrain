# RoboMIND 2.0: A Multimodal, Bimanual Mobile Manipulation Dataset for Generalizable Embodied Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboMIND 2.0 introduces a large-scale, multimodal dataset comprising 310K bimanual and mobile manipulation trajectories collected from six diverse robot embodiments, including tactile sensing and a high-fidelity digital twin. The accompanying MIND-2 dual-system framework, optimized with offline reinforcement learning, enhances generalizable embodied intelligence and improves performance on long-horizon, multi-robot collaborative tasks.

## Problem

- Existing robotic manipulation datasets are limited in scale, diversity (e.g., single-arm, fixed-base, single-embodiment), and multimodal richness (e.g., lacking tactile feedback), hindering the training of generalizable policies.
- Data scarcity and limited diversity impede the development of robust policies for complex bimanual, mobile, and long-horizon tasks.
- A significant gap exists in bridging the sim-to-real performance for robot learning due to insufficient high-fidelity simulated data and assets.

## Method

- Constructed **RoboMIND 2.0**, a dataset featuring 310K dual-arm trajectories from six heterogeneous robot platforms, covering 759 tasks, 129 skills, and 1,139 objects, including ==tactile feedback== and mobile manipulation.
- Developed a high-fidelity ==digital twin== with open-source USD assets and a 20K-trajectory simulated dataset collected in Isaac Sim for effective ==sim-to-real transfer==.
- Proposed **MIND-2**, a ==hierarchical dual-system framework==, combining a high-level ==VLM planner== (MIND-2-VLM) and a low-level ==Vision-Language-Action executor== (MIND-2-VLA) optimized via ==Implicit Q-Learning== (==IQL==).

## Results

- The MIND-2 system with IQL achieved the highest success rates (up to **1.0**) on multi-robot collaborative tasks, outperforming direct VLA fine-tuning and single-task imitation learning methods.
- State-of-the-art VLA models like XR-1, when trained on RoboMIND 2.0, demonstrated strong cross-embodiment and object-level generalization capabilities, consistently performing well across diverse robot platforms and novel objects.
- Integration of tactile sensing led to improved success rates for VLA models, with XR-1 showing more pronounced gains in tasks requiring fine-grained physical interaction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multimodal data, particularly the inclusion of tactile feedback, consistently enhances the success rates and robustness of VLA models in contact-rich and precision-demanding manipulation tasks.
> - A hierarchical dual-system approach (MIND-2), combining a semantic planner with an offline RL-trained VLA executor, effectively addresses long-horizon mobile manipulation and multi-robot collaborative tasks.
> - Co-training with a mix of real and high-fidelity simulated data significantly improves real-world robot performance, with increased simulation data further enhancing results for challenging tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has increasingly adopted data-driven imitation learning to acquire complex skills. However, a significant limitation persists due to the scarcity of large-scale, diverse, and high-fidelity real-world demonstration data. Existing datasets often exhibit diversity along only a single dimension, such as object variety, task types, environments, or robot embodiments. For instance, many prominent benchmarks like Open X-Embodiment, RH20T, DROID, and RoboMIND 1.0 primarily feature single-arm, fixed-base manipulation, lacking bimanual coordination, which is prevalent in human interaction. While some recent efforts, such as AgiBot World and Galaxea Open-World, introduce rich bimanual data and high-resolution tactile sensing, they often rely on a single robot embodiment, limiting their utility for cross-embodiment generalization studies. RoboCOIN expands embodiment diversity but offers sparse task coverage per robot. Additionally, the majority of existing datasets primarily capture visual observations and basic actuation states, omitting crucial physical interaction signals like tactile feedback, which are essential for dexterous manipulation involving contact and slip.

RoboMIND 2.0 addresses these gaps by providing a comprehensive, multi-dimensional dataset designed to enable the training of generalizable embodied intelligence. It contributes to the landscape by offering:
- **Large-scale bimanual and mobile manipulation data:** Moving beyond single-arm, fixed-base scenarios to encompass complex coordinated actions and tasks requiring robot locomotion.
- **Multi-embodiment diversity:** Including six heterogeneous robot platforms, facilitating research into cross-embodiment policy transfer.
- **Multimodal sensing:** Integrating tactile feedback alongside vision and proprioception, allowing for more nuanced understanding of physical interactions.
- **High-fidelity digital twin:** Providing simulated data and assets to bridge the sim-to-real gap and enable cost-efficient data augmentation.
- **Unified collection and annotation:** Ensuring data consistency and providing detailed natural language annotations for language-guided policy learning.

This work positions itself as a foundational resource that aims to overcome the data limitations hindering the development of robust, generalizable, and scalable robot learning policies. It seeks to elevate the capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by providing the diverse and high-quality data necessary for mastering complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks in varied real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop and release RoboMIND 2.0, a large-scale, multimodal, bimanual mobile manipulation dataset, alongside a dual-system robotic framework (MIND-2) designed to leverage this data. The motivation stems from several critical limitations in current robotic manipulation research and existing datasets:

- **Insufficient generalization due to data scarcity and limited diversity:** Existing data-driven imitation learning approaches struggle with generalizing to novel long-horizon bimanual tasks and mobile manipulation in unstructured environments. Current datasets often lack the scale, task complexity, embodiment diversity, or multimodal richness required for robust generalization.
- **Need for bimanual and mobile manipulation capabilities:** Real-world human interaction frequently involves bimanual coordination and mobility. Current datasets are predominantly focused on single-arm, fixed-base manipulation, thereby limiting research into more human-like and versatile robotic behaviors. The authors emphasize the transition from single-arm to bimanual coordinated operation in RoboMIND 2.0.
- **Importance of multimodal sensing, especially tactile feedback:** Dexterous manipulation often requires fine-grained contact understanding (e.g., slip detection, force control) that visual information alone cannot provide. The absence of tactile feedback in most datasets impedes the development of policies capable of robustly handling contact-rich tasks.
- **Bridging the sim-to-real gap:** Collecting large-scale real-world data is resource-intensive, expensive, and time-consuming. High-fidelity simulation environments offer a cost-effective alternative for data generation, but their effectiveness depends on rigorous alignment with real-world physics and appearance. The motivation is to provide digital twins and simulated data that can effectively augment real-world training.
- **Advancing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models:** Despite progress in VLA models, their performance on long-horizon mobile manipulation tasks remains limited due to the lack of appropriate large-scale, real-world datasets. The work aims to provide such a dataset and a framework to validate its utility for training capable and generalizable VLA policies.
- **Standardization and reproducibility:** The authors aim to establish a high-quality, standardized, and reproducible dataset that minimizes noise and bias, providing a solid foundation for training robotic models with strong generalization capabilities across tasks and platforms.

In pursuit of these motivations, the key objectives are:
1. **Construct RoboMIND 2.0:** A comprehensive dataset with approximately 310K dual-arm manipulation trajectories collected from six diverse robot embodiments, covering 759 tasks, 129 skills, and 1,139 objects, including 12K tactile-enhanced sequences and 20K mobile manipulation trajectories.
2. **Develop a high-fidelity digital twin:** Release open-source USD assets and a 20K-trajectory simulated dataset that mirrors real-world tasks to facilitate sim-to-real transfer and scalable training.
3. **Propose MIND-2 system:** A hierarchical dual-system framework, combining a high-level semantic planner (MIND-2-VLM) and a low-level Vision-Language-Action executor (MIND-2-VLA), optimized via offline reinforcement learning to achieve robust long-horizon mobile manipulation.
4. **Conduct extensive empirical validation:** Evaluate the dataset's effectiveness with various imitation learning methods and state-of-the-art VLA models, assess cross-embodiment and object-level generalization, quantify the benefits of tactile modalities, and validate the utility of real-sim data mixing.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this research encompasses two main components: the construction of the RoboMIND 2.0 dataset and the development of the MIND-2 dual-system model.

#### 3.1. RoboMIND 2.0 Dataset Construction

The dataset is designed for multimodal, bimanual mobile manipulation, comprising 310K dual-arm trajectories from six heterogeneous robot embodiments across 759 tasks, 129 skills, and 1,139 objects, accumulating over 1,000 hours of operational experience.

#### 3.1.1. Data Collection

- **Robot Embodiments:** Data was collected from six distinct dual-arm robot platforms: Franka, UR5e, AgileX, ARX, Tien Kung, and Tian Yi. These include fixed-base dual-arm setups (Franka, UR5e), wheeled mobile platforms (AgileX, ARX, Tian Yi), and humanoid robots (Tien Kung, Tian Yi). Some robots were equipped with dexterous hands.
- **Teleoperation Methods:** Diverse teleoperation systems were employed to capture natural human demonstrations:
 - **HACTS:** A low-cost master-slave system used for Franka, UR5e, and Tien Kung, enabling kinematically equivalent bidirectional control.
 - **VR-based:** For ARX, VR headsets captured human arm motions mapped to the robot, and VR controllers commanded the mobile base.
 - **Bilateral Teleoperation (similar to Mobile ALOHA):** Used for AgileX, directly controlling the robot arm. For mobile base data, operators physically pushed the robot to record linear and angular velocities. For Tian Yi, one operator used HACTS for arms, another physically pushed for mobility.
- **Data Format:** Each collected trajectory, including multi-view RGB-D data, robot proprioceptive state (joint positions, end-effector pose), end-effector state, and teleoperation body state, is consolidated into a single HDF5 file. Tactile information from Tashan Tactile sensors (normal force, tangential force, tangential force direction) is included for AgileX mobile manipulation data.

#### 3.1.2. Data Inspection

A centralized, multi-stage data inspection workflow was implemented to ensure high data quality:
- **Rotating-shift schedule and comfortable environment:** To mitigate human factors affecting data quality.
- **Daily reports and preliminary checks:** Raw logs and videos were regularly reviewed, and random subsets were sampled for initial quality checks.
- **Detailed inspection with 12 criteria:** Dedicated staff reviewed each trajectory against specific failure modes, including unintended contact, unsmooth motion, re-grasping, pre-grasp collision, data anomalies, failed placement, trajectory abnormality, excessive speed, visual artifacts, robot arm abnormality, non-standard operation, and target displacement. Timestamps and textual descriptions were recorded for each irregularity.
- **Three-stage workflow:** Initial scan, detailed frame-by-frame review, and data filtering/issue logging. Problematic sequences were discarded, corrected, or recollected.

#### 3.1.3. Data Annotation

- **Natural Language Annotations:** Detailed natural language descriptions are provided for every stage of each mobile manipulation task.
- **Semantic action-based partitioning:** Long-horizon tasks are segmented into phase-specific sub-tasks. Transitions between navigation (e.g., "Go to [location]") and manipulation actions serve as sub-task boundaries. All manipulation actions between two navigation actions are grouped.
- **Automated Annotation:** Annotations are automatically generated using the Gemini 2.5 Pro large language model, then manually refined, ensuring consistency and semantic accuracy. This supports language-guided policy learning and multimodal representation training.

#### 3.1.4. Data Diversity Analysis

The dataset is analyzed across five axes:
- **Embodiment Diversity:** Six platforms with variations in kinematic structure (symmetric/asymmetric dual-arm, humanoid), mobility (static/mobile), perception layout, and teleoperation modality (HACTS, VR, physical guiding).
- **Task Diversity:** 759 dual-arm tasks, organized into fixed-scene and mobile-scene manipulation, covering seven major skill families (grasping/placing, special actions, organizing/sorting, transfer/collaboration, assembly/splicing, opening/closing, stacking/placement). Tasks are distributed across domestic (kitchens, living rooms, supermarkets, children's rooms) and industrial (logistics sorting, biological labs, industrial assembly) scenes.
- **Object Diversity:** 1,139 distinct objects, categorized hierarchically (Food, Daily Necessities, Kitchenware, Stationery, Toys, Others) with fine-grained subcategories, spanning a wide range of visual and geometric variations and interacting in multiple manipulation types.
- **Information Diversity:** Multi-view RGB-D, robot proprioceptive states, end-effector states, and high-resolution tactile information (normal and tangential forces, direction).
- **Simulation Diversity:** Open-sourced digital twin assets (URDF models, scene layouts, sensor configs) created by professional 3D modelers. A 20K-trajectory simulated dataset collected in Isaac Sim, mirroring real-world tasks for Franka and Tien Kung, designed for sim-to-real transfer.

#### 3.2. The MIND-2 Dual-System Model

The MIND-2 system is a hierarchical framework combining a slow, high-level planner with a fast, low-level executor.

#### 3.2.1. MIND-2-VLM (Slow System)

- **Function:** Acts as a "robot brain" to perform high-level task reasoning and generate subtask instructions. It monitors task progress and determines subtask switching.
- **Architecture:** Fine-tuned from the open-source VLM InternVL3-8B.
- **Input:** Multimodal context including three synchronized camera views (front, left wrist, right wrist), an enumerated list of subtasks (task context), and current robot state (proprioception, previously inferred task index).
- **Output:** A structured string indicating the predicted `Task Index` and `Task Progress`, enabling direct parsing by the downstream policy system.
- **Training:** End-to-end fine-tuning on the segmented subtask dataset from RoboMIND 2.0, minimizing cross-entropy loss over the textual output.

#### 3.2.2. MIND-2-VLA (Fast System)

- **Function:** Executes low-level actions based on subtask instructions, current robot state, and visual information.
- **Architecture:** A Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model trained offline.
- **Offline Reinforcement Learning with Implicit Q-Learning (IQL):**
 - **Reward Assignment:** Trajectories are annotated with temporally discounted reward signals. Successful trajectories receive +1 at the terminal step, discounted backward (r_t = γ^(T-t)). Failure trajectories receive -1 at the terminal step, discounted backward (r_t = -γ^(T-t)).
 - **IQL Components:**
 - **State Value Function (Vϕ(s)):** Learned by regressing towards the expected return under the behavior policy using an asymmetric quantile loss (L_V).
 - **Q-function (Qθ(s, a)):** Updated using standard temporal difference (TD) learning with target values from V(s') (L_Q).
 - **Policy (πψ(a|s)):** Updated via advantage-weighted regression, focusing on actions that outperform the average value (L_π) using the implicit advantage A(s, a) = Q(s, a) - V(s).
 - **Benefit of Failure Trajectories:** IQL leverages both successful and failed trajectories, allowing MIND-2-VLA to learn not only optimal behaviors but also to recognize and avoid suboptimal or incorrect actions, enhancing robustness and safety.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluate RoboMIND 2.0 as a benchmark, the effectiveness of the MIND-2 system, tactile sensing, object generalization, and sim-to-real transfer.

#### 4.1. Imitation Learning and Vision-Language-Action Models Experiments: Single-task Imitation Learning Models

- **Models Evaluated:** ACT, Dense Policy, DP3, and UVA.
- **Tasks:** 38 tasks across Franka, UR5e, AgileX, ARX, Tien Kung, and Tian Yi platforms, covering fixed-base, mobile, and humanoid dual-arm manipulation.
- **Findings:**
 - **3D vs. 2D Methods:** 3D-aware methods (DP3, Dense Policy) generally outperformed 2D-based methods (ACT, UVA) on tasks requiring bimanual coordination, attributed to richer spatial modeling.
 - **DP3:** Showed strong performance on fixed-arm robots with rich visual context but struggled with mobile or humanoid platforms due to limited adaptation to varying kinematics and sensorimotor modalities.
 - **Dense Policy:** Exhibited more consistent performance across different robot embodiments and task types, including moderate success on mobile manipulation, indicating better transferability.
 - **Long-horizon tasks:** Both categories of models achieved lower success rates on complex long-horizon collaborative tasks requiring precise handover or intricate spatial reasoning.

#### 4.1.2. Vision-Language-Action Large Models

- **Models Evaluated:** 𝜋0, 𝜋0.5, HybridVLA, and XR-1.
- **Tasks:** The same set of 38 tasks as for single-task IL models.
- **Findings:**
 - **𝜋0:** Showed modest overall performance, achieving moderate success on simple UR5e tasks but consistently failing on bimanual or mobile scenarios, indicating a lack of grounded motor priors for diverse embodiments despite broad semantic understanding.
 - **𝜋0.5:** Demonstrated noticeable gains over 𝜋0, especially on fixed-base dual-arm tasks, but performance still degraded on mobile or humanoid systems.
 - **HybridVLA vs. 𝜋0:** Despite architectural differences in action generation heads, HybridVLA and 𝜋0 achieved remarkably similar task success rates, suggesting that action generation head choice alone may not be as critical as data diversity and embodiment alignment.
 - **XR-1:** Exhibited the strongest overall performance and notable cross-embodiment generalization, consistently achieving high success rates across fixed dual-arm, mobile manipulators, and humanoid robots. This was attributed to its integrated architecture fusing multimodal perception, proprioceptive feedback, and language-conditioned action generation.

#### 4.2. MIND-2 Dual System Experiments

- **AgileX Mobile Manipulation Tasks:** MIND-2 demonstrated significantly improved performance compared to VLA models and single-task imitation learning methods on individual mobile manipulation tasks.
- **Multi-robot Collaborative Tasks:** Three long-horizon tasks were designed involving coordinated work between Tian Yi and AgileX robots in supermarket, industrial, and chemical laboratory environments.
 - **MIND-2 (Post Training):** Directly fine-tuning off-the-shelf VLA models (InternVL3 and 𝜋0.5) on task-specific collaboration data yielded moderate success rates.
 - **MIND-2 (Full-scale Training):** Pretraining on the full-scale mobile manipulation dataset with the fast-slow architecture, followed by fine-tuning on collaboration data, showed consistent improvements, particularly in the Supermarket and Industrial tasks.
 - **MIND-2 (Offline RL):** Further applying Implicit Q-Learning (IQL) to the MIND-2-VLA policy after full-scale training achieved the highest success rates across all three tasks (0.9 in Supermarket, 0.8 in Industrial, 0.6 in Chemical Laboratory). This indicated that offline reinforcement learning effectively refined action execution and enhanced robustness in long-horizon, multi-agent scenarios.
 - **IQL Trajectory Ratio:** Training MIND-2-VLA with IQL performed optimally when the number of failed trajectories matched that of successful ones (1:1 ratio), achieving success rates of 1.0, 1.0, and 0.8 across the three collaborative tasks respectively.

#### 4.3. Effectiveness of Tactile Sensing

- **Models Evaluated:** 𝜋0.5 and XR-1.
- **Tasks:** Four AgileX mobile manipulation tasks.
- **Findings:** Integrating tactile information (tangential and normal forces, direction) as part of the robot’s proprioceptive input consistently improved success rates for both 𝜋0.5 and XR-1. XR-1 showed more pronounced gains, particularly in contact-rich and precision-demanding tasks, suggesting its stronger capability in fusing multimodal proprioceptive signals. This indicates the complementary value of tactile modality for perceiving physical interactions.

#### 4.4. Object Substitution of VLA Models (Generalization)

- **Models Evaluated:** 𝜋0.5 and XR-1.
- **Tasks:** Two UR5e dual-arm manipulation tasks with object variations (color/shape changes, geometry changes, material changes).
- **Findings:** Both 𝜋0.5 and XR-1 demonstrated strong object-level generalization capabilities. They successfully manipulated functionally equivalent but visually or geometrically novel objects, indicating that training on diverse, high-quality data enhances robustness to object variation.

#### 4.5. Real and Simulation Experiments

- **Simulation Benchmark Tests:**
 - **Models Evaluated:** ACT, Diffusion Policy, and XR-1.
 - **Tasks:** Four Tien Kung dual-arm manipulation tasks performed entirely within its digital twin.
 - **Findings:** Policies trained solely on synthetic trajectories showed consistent and plausible performance mirroring expected task difficulty. This validated the realism and internal consistency of the simulated dataset, confirming its suitability as a scalable resource for policy learning.
- **Co-training with Real and Simulation Data:**
 - **Models Evaluated:** ACT, Diffusion Policy, and XR-1.
 - **Training Data Ratios:** Varied ratios of real to simulated data (0:1, 1:0, 1:1, 1:5).
 - **Findings:** All models benefited from co-training with mixed real and simulated data, showing improved real-world robot performance compared to training on real data alone. Furthermore, increasing the amount of simulation data beyond a balanced mix (e.g., 1:1 to 1:5) continued to enhance real-robot performance, particularly for Diffusion Policy and XR-1 on more challenging tasks. This indicates effective sim-to-real transfer and the cost-efficiency of synthetic data augmentation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RoboMIND 2.0 dataset and the proposed MIND-2 framework represent a significant contribution to the field of embodied artificial intelligence and robotic manipulation.

Its primary significance lies in addressing critical limitations of existing datasets, thereby enabling advancements in generalizable robotic policies. By providing a large-scale, multimodal, bimanual, and mobile manipulation dataset across heterogeneous robot embodiments, RoboMIND 2.0 creates a foundational resource for training and evaluating next-generation robot learning models.

The potential impacts include:
- **Accelerating Generalizable Embodied Intelligence:** The multi-dimensional diversity (embodiment, task, object, scene, modality) of RoboMIND 2.0 can facilitate the development of more robust and generalizable policies that can transfer skills across different robot morphologies, adapt to varying environments, and handle novel objects.
- **Advancing Bimanual and Mobile Manipulation:** The explicit focus on bimanual coordination and mobile manipulation tasks addresses a long-standing gap, enabling research into more complex, human-like, and versatile robotic behaviors crucial for real-world deployment in domestic and industrial settings.
- **Promoting Multimodal Perception-Action Research:** The inclusion of synchronized tactile feedback alongside visual and proprioceptive data opens new avenues for research into contact-rich manipulation, fine-grained control, and robust error recovery, leveraging sensory information beyond vision alone.
- **Enhancing Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The release of high-fidelity digital twin assets and a large-scale simulated dataset provides a cost-effective platform for scalable policy training and rigorous evaluation of sim-to-real transfer techniques, reducing the reliance on expensive and time-consuming real-world data collection.
- **Serving as a Comprehensive Benchmark:** RoboMIND 2.0 acts as a standardized benchmark for evaluating and comparing various robot learning approaches, from single-task imitation learning to generalist Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, under consistent real-world conditions. This enables systematic analysis of model strengths and limitations.
- **Enabling Hierarchical and Cooperative Control:** The MIND-2 dual-system framework demonstrates a robust approach for tackling long-horizon and multi-robot collaborative tasks. This hierarchical design, integrating a high-level VLM planner with a low-level VLA executor refined by offline reinforcement learning, shows promise for complex task sequencing and coordinated control of heterogeneous robots.
- **Promoting Robust Learning from Mixed Data:** The empirical validation of offline reinforcement learning using both successful and failure trajectories demonstrates a method for enhancing policy robustness by actively learning to avoid failure modes, which is vital for safe and reliable robot operation. The effectiveness of co-training with mixed real and simulated data further highlights strategies for optimizing data utilization.

Overall, RoboMIND 2.0 and its accompanying findings aim to lower barriers to entry for advanced robot learning research, foster innovation in areas like cross-embodiment generalization and multimodal control, and ultimately contribute to the development of more capable and adaptable autonomous robotic systems.
