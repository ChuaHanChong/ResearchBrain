# ReWiND: Language-Guided Rewards Teach Robot Policies without New Demonstrations

> [!summary] Summary
> ReWiND enables robots to learn new manipulation tasks solely from natural language instructions by training a generalizable reward function from a small set of initial demonstrations. The framework significantly improves policy learning efficiency in both simulation and real-world robotic setups, achieving a 79% success rate on unseen MetaWorld tasks and a 5x improvement in real-world bimanual arm tasks with only one hour of online interaction.

## Problem

- Traditional robot learning methods (Imitation Learning) require extensive, costly human demonstrations for every new task, limiting scalability.
- Reinforcement Learning is hindered by the difficulty of manually designing effective, dense reward functions for complex robot tasks ('reward engineering').
- Prior language-conditioned reward learning often relies on unrealistic assumptions like ground-truth state information, thousands of demonstrations, or impractical online reward model training.

## Method

- **ReWiND** introduces a three-phase framework that learns a ==language-conditioned reward function (R_ψ)== from a small dataset of human demonstrations, augmented with diverse open-source data.
- The reward function is made robust and generalizable through novel techniques including '==video rewinding==' to synthesize failure trajectories for dense feedback, and ==LLM-driven instruction generation== for varied linguistic inputs.
- The learned reward function then guides ==offline policy pre-training== using existing demonstrations and ==online policy fine-tuning== for unseen tasks, enabling learning without new task-specific demonstrations.

## Results

- ReWiND's reward model demonstrated superior generalization, achieving a **30%** relative improvement in Pearson correlation and **27%** in Spearman's rank correlation for reflecting progress in unseen successful demonstrations.
- In simulation (MetaWorld), ReWiND achieved an IQM success rate of **79%** on **8** unseen manipulation tasks after **100k** steps, a **97.5%** improvement over the best baseline (VLC's **40%**).
- On real-world bimanual robot arms, ReWiND improved the pre-trained policy's success rate from **12%** to **68%** after just one hour (**50k** steps) of online RL, a **5x** improvement and **6.7x** better than the best simulation baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A robust reward function must provide dense, informative feedback (even during failures), generalize to unseen tasks and visual inputs, and be robust to diverse robot behaviors and varied language instructions.
> - Synthetically generated 'failure-like' trajectories via 'video rewinding' are crucial for teaching the reward model to provide dense, guiding feedback for imperfect or unsuccessful robot actions.
> - Leveraging frozen pre-trained vision and language encoders combined with a small, causally masked transformer allows for effective reward learning from limited data while promoting generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. ReWiND: Language-Guided Rewards Teach Robot Policies without New Demonstrations – A Comprehensive Report

ReWiND introduces language-guided rewards that teach robot policies without new demonstrations, focusing on its contributions to robot manipulation tasks.

#### 1.1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning is continuously striving to enable robots to learn new tasks efficiently and autonomously, especially in complex, unstructured environments. Traditionally, this has been approached through two main paradigms: **Imitation Learning (IL)** and **Reinforcement Learning (RL)**. IL, while effective, typically requires extensive human demonstrations for every new task, a process that is expensive, time-consuming, and difficult to scale. RL, on the other hand, allows robots to learn through interaction using a reward function, but manually designing these reward functions is a notoriously difficult and time-consuming "reward engineering" challenge, often requiring domain expertise and extensive trial-and-error.

Recent advancements have focused on **language-conditioned reward learning**, aiming to bridge the gap between human instruction and robot reward signals. This nascent area seeks to derive reward functions directly from natural language instructions, thereby alleviating the need for manual reward design or per-task demonstrations. However, as the authors highlight, much of this prior work often operates under restrictive assumptions. Some approaches rely on ground-truth state information (e.g., LLM-generated rewards for simulators) that is rarely available in real-world settings. Others demand thousands of demonstrations, negating the very goal of sample efficiency, or require online training of reward models from scratch, which is impractical for real-world robots due to safety and data collection costs.

ReWiND directly addresses these limitations. It positions itself as a significant step towards **scalable, real-world robot learning** by proposing a framework that learns language-conditioned robot manipulation policies with **minimal human demonstrations** (a "handful" or ~5 per task) and without requiring new demonstrations for unseen task variations. By focusing on learning a robust, generalizable reward function that can provide dense feedback and cope with diverse robot behaviors, ReWiND aims to make RL more feasible for real-world robotic deployment where data is scarce and generalization to novel situations is critical. This work builds upon the foundation of large pre-trained models (vision and language encoders) but critically adapts them to generate effective reward signals for robotics without domain-specific fine-tuning or explicit expert feedback beyond initial demonstrations.

#### 1.2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of ReWiND is to introduce a framework that enables robots to learn new manipulation tasks **solely from language instructions, without requiring per-task demonstrations**. This ambitious goal is driven by several key motivations, stemming from the limitations of existing robot learning paradigms:

1. **High Cost of Demonstrations:** Traditional imitation learning methods necessitate human experts to provide numerous demonstrations for every new task, which is economically prohibitive and time-consuming, especially for diverse real-world applications.
2. **Difficulty of Reward Engineering:** While reinforcement learning offers autonomy, manually designing effective and dense reward functions for complex robot tasks is a substantial challenge, often requiring significant trial-and-error and domain-specific knowledge. Sparse rewards further exacerbate this problem by making exploration difficult.
3. **Limitations of Prior Language-Conditioned Reward Learning:** Existing methods that attempt to leverage language for reward generation often make unrealistic assumptions. These include:
 - Availability of ground-truth state information (not typical in real-world visual-only settings).
 - Need for thousands of demonstrations (defeating sample efficiency).
 - Online training of reward models from scratch (too slow and data-intensive for real robots).

To overcome these challenges, ReWiND aims to develop "intelligent teachers" in the form of robust reward models. The authors outline three crucial properties such desirable reward models should possess:
1. **Dense, Informative Feedback:** The reward model should provide continuous and meaningful feedback, even during failures, to guide the robot's learning process effectively.
2. **Generalization to Unseen Tasks:** The reward model must be able to understand and assign appropriate rewards for tasks it has not explicitly seen during its training phase, adapting to novel visual and language inputs.
3. **Robustness to Diverse Robot Behaviors:** The model should be able to provide consistent and accurate guidance even when the robot exhibits varied or imperfect behaviors, which is common during early stages of learning.

By achieving these objectives, ReWiND seeks to take a significant step towards practical, scalable, and autonomous real-world robot learning.

#### 1.3. Methodology and Approach

ReWiND is a three-phase framework designed to learn unseen, language-specified tasks in a sample-efficient manner. The core idea is to train a language-conditioned reward function from a small demonstration dataset and use it to guide both offline policy pre-training and online fine-tuning.

**Phase 1: Learning a Reward Function ($R_\psi$)**
The primary goal is to train a reward function $R_\psi(\text{o}_{1:t}, z)$ that predicts per-frame progress within an observation sequence $o_{1:T}$ conditioned on a language instruction $z$. The predicted rewards are normalized between. To ensure robustness, $R_\psi$ is also trained to predict zero progress for mismatched observation sequences and instructions. The training objective, $L_{progress}$, directly regresses to $t/T$ for matched sequences and 0 for mismatched ones.

To imbue $R_\psi$ with the desiderata of generalization, policy rollout alignment, and input robustness, ReWiND incorporates several key design choices:

- **Diverse Data Incorporation (D1, D3):** Beyond a small target demonstration dataset ($D_{demos}$), a curated subset of the large-scale **Open-X Dataset ($D_{open-x}$)** is used. This subset focuses on object-centric and directional instructions to improve generalization to new objects and actions not present in $D_{demos}$, and to enhance robustness to diverse task specifications.
- **Video and Language Augmentation (D2, D3):**
 - **Video Rewind:** To address the lack of failure trajectories in human demonstrations, ReWiND automatically generates "failure-like" trajectories. By splitting a successful demonstration video at an intermediate point and then rewinding a few frames, it creates sequences that resemble a robot attempting a task and then failing or dropping an object. The reward model is trained with $L_{rewind}$ to predict decreasing rewards for these rewound segments, thereby learning to penalize mistakes and provide dense feedback even for unsuccessful behaviors.
 - **Instruction Generation:** To improve robustness to varied language inputs, an LLM is used to generate 5-10 additional, semantically similar language instructions for each task in $D_{demos}$. During training, a task's instruction is randomly sampled from all available variants, helping the reward model generalize to different linguistic expressions.
- **Architecture (D1):**
 - **Frozen Input Encoders:** ReWiND leverages powerful pre-trained vision (DINOv2) and language (all-MiniLM-L12-v2) encoders. These encoders are *frozen* during training of $R_\psi$ to prevent overfitting on the small $D_{demos}$ and encourage generalization. Image and language embeddings are projected to a shared dimension.
 - **Cross-Modal Sequential Aggregator:** A small, causally masked transformer processes the projected image and language embeddings. This aggregator learns to fuse information from both modalities to generate per-timestep progress rewards.
 - **Positional Embeddings:** Crucially, positional embeddings are applied only to the *first image* of the sequence. This prevents the model from "cheating" by inferring progress solely from absolute time, forcing it to rely on visual and linguistic cues for temporal understanding, while still providing essential sequential context.

**Phase 2: Policy Pre-training**
After training $R_\psi$, a language-conditioned policy $\pi_\theta(\text{a}_t | o_t, z)$ is pre-trained using **offline Reinforcement Learning (RL)** on $D_{demos}$. The $D_{demos}$ trajectories are relabeled with rewards $\hat{r}_{off,t}$ generated by the learned $R_\psi$, augmented with a success bonus at the end of successful trajectories. IQL (Implicit Q-Learning) is used as the offline RL algorithm, chosen for its effectiveness in robot manipulation and robustness to noisy rewards.

**Phase 3: Learning New Tasks Online**
For an *unseen task variation* specified by a new language instruction $z_{new}$, ReWiND fine-tunes the pre-trained policy $\pi$ through **online RL**. The policy interacts with the environment, and $R_\psi(o_{1:t}, z_{new})$ provides online reward labels $\hat{r}_{on,t}$ for these new rollouts, again complemented by a success bonus from a human supervisor or an assumed success signal. This allows for sample-efficient adaptation to novel tasks using the generalizable reward function.

#### 1.4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments comprehensively evaluate ReWiND across reward correlation, policy learning efficiency, and design choice ablations in both simulation (MetaWorld) and real-world robot setups (Koch bimanual arms).

**Q1: What Makes a Good Reward Function? (Reward Analysis)**

- **Generalization:** ReWiND's reward model demonstrated superior generalization to unseen tasks. In confusion matrices (unseen task videos vs. language instructions), ReWiND produced the most distinct diagonal, indicating accurate alignment. It also achieved 30% relative improvement in Pearson correlation (r) and 27% in Spearman's rank correlation (ρ) over the best baseline (VLC) in reflecting progress in successful, unseen demonstrations. Even without Open-X data (ReWiND w/o OXE), it performed well due to its architectural choices.
- **Policy Rollout Reward Alignment:** ReWiND effectively ranked failure, near-success, and successful policy rollouts. It showed a 74% relative improvement in reward order (Spearman's ρ) and 58% in reward difference over the best baseline (LIV-FT), demonstrating its ability to provide useful feedback for unsuccessful actions, largely attributed to the video rewind technique.
- **Robustness to Varied Inputs:** ReWiND exhibited high robustness to diverse language instructions (e.g., "close the door" vs. "shut the door"). It achieved the highest average Spearman's ρ (0.74), 23% better than VLC, with near-zero variance across instructions, confirming the effectiveness of instruction augmentation.

**Q2: Learning New Tasks with RL (Policy Learning Performance)**

- **Simulation (MetaWorld):** ReWiND significantly outperformed baselines on 8 unseen MetaWorld manipulation tasks. It achieved an IQM success rate of 79% after 100k environment steps, representing a 97.5% improvement over the best baseline, VLC (which achieved 40%). Baselines using sparse rewards or no fine-tuning (Pre-train) achieved near-zero success rates, highlighting the challenge of the task and ReWiND's effectiveness. ReWiND also demonstrated better sample efficiency across various timesteps.
- **Real-World Robot Learning (Koch Bimanual Arms):** On a bimanual Koch v1.1 robot arm setup across five diverse tasks (in-distribution, visual clutter, spatial language), ReWiND improved the pre-trained policy's success rate from an average of 12% to 68% after just one hour (50k environment steps) of real-world RL—a 5x improvement. In contrast, the best simulation baseline, VLC, only improved from 8% to 10%, showcasing ReWiND's superior real-world applicability (6.7x better than VLC).

**Q3: Ablation Study**

- **Datasets:** Removing target environment data ($\text{−Targ. Env Data}$) severely impacted training demonstration alignment and policy rollout ranking, showing $D_{demos}$ is crucial. Removing the Open-X subset ($\text{−Open-X Subset}$) harmed unseen task reward alignment and input robustness, emphasizing the value of diverse, general-purpose data for generalization.
- **Augmentation:** Eliminating video rewinding ($\text{−Video Rewind}$) degraded policy rollout ranking and reduced policy learning success rate by 33%, confirming its role in providing feedback for failures. Removing instruction generation ($\text{−Instr. Generation}$) reduced language input robustness, validating its contribution to handling varied instructions.
- **Architecture:** Adding full positional embeddings ($\text{+Full Pos. Embeds}$) improved unseen demo alignment but worsened policy rollout ranking and led to a 21% inferior relative policy success rate, indicating overfitting and justifying the choice of first-frame-only positional embeddings.
- **Less Training Tasks:** ReWiND's reward model still performed well with only 5 or 10 training tasks instead of 20, though policy success rates decreased, indicating scalability with limited initial data.

**Additional Analysis:** ReWiND showed promising results on a contact-rich "unfold towel" task from the BRIDGE-v2 dataset, accurately reflecting progress and stagnation. Preliminary experiments with Diffusion Steering RL (DSRL) also demonstrated ReWiND's compatibility with more expressive policy classes, improving DP performance on unseen tasks.

#### 1.5. Significance and Potential Impact

ReWiND presents a significant step forward in the field of robot learning, particularly for practical, real-world applications. Its core contribution lies in demonstrating how a robust, language-conditioned reward function can be learned from a **small, initial set of demonstrations** and then effectively generalized to **unseen tasks specified by natural language, without requiring any new task-specific demonstrations or extensive reward engineering**.

The **significance** of ReWiND can be highlighted as follows:

- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap and Scalability:** By learning a reward function that generalizes well across tasks and is robust to diverse robot behaviors, ReWiND addresses a critical bottleneck for deploying robots in varied environments. Its ability to achieve strong performance in both MetaWorld simulation and on real-world bimanual robot arms (a 5x improvement over pre-trained policies) underscores its practical applicability and potential for scaling robotic capabilities.
- **Reducing Human Effort:** ReWiND dramatically reduces the human labor traditionally required for robot learning. It mitigates the need for expensive per-task demonstrations (a core limitation of imitation learning) and complex manual reward function design (a major hurdle in reinforcement learning). This makes robot learning more accessible and economically viable for a wider range of applications.
- **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The innovative techniques, such as **video rewinding** for generating failure trajectories and **LLM-driven instruction generation**, enable the reward model to provide dense feedback and cope with variations in task execution and linguistic description. This leads to robust learning in scenarios where policies might otherwise struggle with sparse rewards or misinterpret novel instructions.
- **Agnostic to Policy Architecture:** ReWiND's reward function can guide various policy architectures, from simple MLPs to action-chunked Transformers and even diffusion policies, highlighting its broad utility as a foundational component for different robot learning systems.

The **potential impact** of ReWiND is substantial:

- **Democratization of Robot Learning:** By simplifying the process of defining new tasks, ReWiND could enable non-expert users to teach robots novel skills through natural language, fostering broader adoption of robotics in various domains, from manufacturing to personal assistance.
- **Faster Adaptation to Novel Environments:** Robots equipped with ReWiND could more quickly adapt to unforeseen circumstances or learn new variations of existing tasks in dynamic environments, which is crucial for real-world autonomy.
- **Foundation for Autonomous Agents:** This work lays a strong foundation for developing truly autonomous agents that can continuously learn and improve in the wild, without constant human intervention for task specification or reward shaping.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors candidly discuss several limitations, which also point to fruitful avenues for future research:
- **Initial Policy Performance:** The current policy architecture is relatively simple. Integrating ReWiND with stronger, pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models could further enhance zero-shot performance and sample efficiency, although fine-tuning such large models remains a computational challenge.
- **Reward Objective Scope:** The progress-based reward objective, while effective, limits the explicit incorporation of *collected* failure trajectories. Future work could explore richer reward objectives or assume human labeling for approximate progress on failures.
- **Frozen Embeddings Trade-off:** Freezing pre-trained vision/language encoders prevents overfitting on small datasets but might underfit robot-specific visual or linguistic nuances not covered by general internet-scale pre-training. Fine-tuning with more robotics data or incorporating intermediate representations could address this.
- **Human Resets and Success Detection:** ReWiND currently relies on human operators for environment resets and success detection during online learning. Integrating with recent advancements in reset-free RL and automatic VLM-based success detectors is a critical step towards fully autonomous learning.

In conclusion, ReWiND represents a significant leap towards making robot learning more practical, scalable, and human-centric by intelligently leveraging language and limited demonstrations to create robust and generalizable reward functions. Its success in both simulation and real-world scenarios promises to accelerate the development of more capable and adaptable robotic systems.
