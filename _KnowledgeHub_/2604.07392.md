# Event-Centric World Modeling with Memory-Augmented Retrieval for Embodied Decision-Making

> [!summary] Summary
> The Event-Centric Retrieval-Based Action (ERA) framework models dynamic environments through semantic events and utilizes memory-augmented retrieval to enable real-time, interpretable decision-making for embodied agents. Evaluated in UAV flight scenarios, the framework achieved a 100% success rate with zero collisions in adversarial conditions and maintained sub-millisecond decision latency on embedded hardware.

## Problem

- Many end-to-end deep learning approaches for embodied AI lack interpretability, making it difficult to understand the decision-making logic and hindering verification in safety-critical applications.
- Existing methods often lack explicit mechanisms to ensure physical consistency of generated actions, potentially leading to physically infeasible or unsafe behaviors.
- Integrating structured reasoning, like case-based reasoning, into dynamic, multi-entity embodied systems for real-time decision-making remains challenging.

## Method

- The **ERA** (Event-Centric Retrieval-Based Action) framework abstracts dynamic environments into a structured list of ==semantic events==, encoded into a compact latent space.
- It employs a ==memory-augmented retrieval== mechanism to query a knowledge bank of physics-informed prior experiences, using an ==Approximate Nearest Neighbor search==.
- Decision-making involves aggregating retrieved solutions, applying a ==Clustered Bayesian Selection== strategy to mitigate multimodal "average-to-collision" issues, and enforcing physics-informed latent dynamics with ==Lyapunov stability constraints==.

## Results

- The ERA framework demonstrated **100%** success rates with **zero collisions** across five adversarial curriculum episodes in UAV flight, showcasing robust navigation in dynamic environments.
- It achieved **sub-millisecond** decision latency on embedded hardware, specifically the Jetson Orin Nano, validating its real-time operability for autonomous systems.
- The physics-consistency regularizer and Lyapunov stability constraints were critical for selecting kinematically feasible maneuvers, preventing high-frequency oscillations and ensuring realistic robot behavior.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing complex, dynamic environments as event-centric semantic abstractions, combined with a latent space encoding, enables efficient and structured reasoning.
> - Integrating physics-informed knowledge and stability criteria directly into the latent space and retrieval process is crucial for ensuring kinematically consistent and safe actions in real-time.
> - A Clustered Bayesian Selection strategy effectively addresses the "average-to-collision" problem in multimodal action selection by aggregating similar, safe maneuvers.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous systems, particularly Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs), are increasingly deployed in complex and dynamic environments, necessitating robust and timely decision-making capabilities. A significant area of research in this field focuses on developing predictive world models that enable agents to anticipate future environmental states and plan accordingly.

Existing approaches often leverage deep learning to create end-to-end mappings from sensory input to control actions, exemplified by methods such as Vision-Language-Action models. While these techniques have demonstrated effectiveness in various scenarios, they frequently exhibit limitations regarding interpretability and the explicit enforcement of physical constraints. The implicit nature of their environmental representations makes it challenging to analyze decision-making processes or verify consistency with underlying physics, which is a critical concern in safety-critical applications.

To address these limitations, prior research has explored several avenues: structured representations to provide clearer environmental models, model-based reasoning for explicit future state prediction, and memory-augmented learning to leverage past experiences. Case-based reasoning (CBR) offers an inherently interpretable paradigm by deriving decisions from previously observed experiences. However, integrating CBR effectively into modern embodied systems, especially those operating in dynamic multi-entity environments, remains an ongoing challenge.

This work contributes to the landscape by proposing an "event-centric world modeling" framework that integrates structured environmental abstraction, memory-augmented retrieval, and physics-informed decision-making. It aims to bridge the gap between the computational efficiency often achieved by end-to-end learning and the interpretability and physical grounding that are crucial for safety and reliability in embodied AI. By explicitly representing dynamic environments as semantic events and using a knowledge bank of prior solutions, the proposed framework positions itself as an alternative to purely implicit or optimization-heavy approaches, seeking to offer enhanced transparency and consistency in autonomous decision-making.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an event-centric predictive representation and decision-making framework for autonomous agents operating in dynamic and safety-critical environments. This framework aims to be computationally efficient, physically grounded, and interpretable.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in existing autonomous decision-making systems:

- **Lack of Interpretability:** Many contemporary end-to-end learning approaches, while effective, function as "black boxes." It is often difficult to understand *why* a particular action was chosen, which hinders debugging, verification, and human trust, especially in safety-critical applications like UAV navigation. The authors aim to provide a transparent link between decisions and prior experiences through a case-based reasoning paradigm.
- **Absence of Explicit Physical Grounding:** Current methods frequently rely on implicit environmental representations, making it challenging to ensure that generated actions are consistent with the underlying physical constraints of the agent and its environment. This can lead to physically infeasible or unsafe behaviors. The research is motivated by the need to incorporate physics-informed knowledge directly into the decision-making process to encourage the selection of kinematically consistent maneuvers and ensure system stability.
- **Computational Efficiency for Real-time Operation:** Autonomous agents in dynamic environments require decision-making frameworks that can operate within strict real-time control constraints. While end-to-end models can be fast, complex model-predictive control (MPC) or extensive online optimization can be computationally intensive. The authors are motivated to develop a framework that is efficient enough for edge-AI hardware deployment.
- **Structured Abstraction of Dynamic Environments:** Representing complex, dynamic environments with multiple interacting entities in a structured and meaningful way is a fundamental challenge. The research aims to abstract the environment into a set of semantic events that capture object-level properties and their dynamics, providing a structured basis for reasoning.
- **Leveraging Prior Experiences:** The ability to learn from and effectively utilize past successful experiences is crucial for robust decision-making. The motivation includes developing a mechanism, such as memory-augmented retrieval, to leverage a knowledge bank of prior solutions, enabling the agent to efficiently adapt to novel situations based on similar observed strategies.

In summary, the core motivation is to enhance the reliability, safety, and transparency of autonomous decision-making systems by integrating structured environmental understanding, experience-based reasoning, and physical consistency constraints within a computationally efficient framework suitable for real-time, embodied AI applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces an **Event-Retrieve-Action (ERA)** framework for world modeling and decision-making. This framework integrates event detection, memory-augmented reasoning, and interpretable decision-making for embodied systems.

#### 3.1. Overview of the ERA Framework

The ERA framework operates through four core stages:
1. **Perception and Feature Extraction:** Raw sensory inputs are processed.
2. **Event Abstraction:** Features are structured into an event list.
3. **Latent Event Code Compression:** The event list is compressed into a latent event code.
4. **Retrieval-Based Decision-Making:** Actions are determined using a physics-informed knowledge bank.
A feedback mechanism records execution outcomes as status codes to refine future decisions via reinforcement learning.

Formally, an environment at time `t` is represented as an event `E_t`, mapped to a latent space `z_t = f(E_t)`. The action `a_t` is a weighted combination of `k` retrieved solutions from a knowledge bank `M`, with weights `w_i` determined by similarity `sim(z_t, z_i)`, a scaling factor `τ`, and a reliability score `r_i` of the stored maneuver.

#### 3.2. Data Representation

- **Event List (`E_t`):** A structured list of detected objects and status, `E_t = {e^i_t}_{i=1}^{N_t}`, where each `e^i_t` is a vector. Components include:
 - `object-id`: Unique identifier for detected entities.
 - `position`: Spatial location (x, y, z).
 - `kinematic state`: Velocity and acceleration.
 - `environment-state`: Global contextual information.
 - `self-status`: Ego agent's internal state.
 - `target-context`: Relative vector to the global goal.
 The event list is updated at a fixed temporal resolution (e.g., 20-50 ms for UAVs) and concatenated with a global state vector `S_global`.
- **Event Code (`z_t`):** A compact `d`-dimensional latent representation of `E_t`, `z_t = f(E_t)`, where `f` is a permutation-invariant encoder (e.g., DeepSets or transformer). Each event code `z_i` in the knowledge bank is paired with a solution maneuver `a_i`.
- **Event Dynamics:** The latent event code evolves according to `z_{t+1} = Ψz_t + Γa_t + ϵ_t`, where `Ψ` is a learned (or pre-constrained) transition operator, `Γ` maps maneuvers into the latent space, and `ϵ_t` is uncertainty. Contractive latent dynamics (`ρ(Ψ) < 1`) are enforced for asymptotic stability, with a Lyapunov stability criterion `||z_{t+1}||^2 - ||z_t||^2 < 0` for the autonomous system. The latent space is also trained to be kinematically informed such that `d_phys(z_t, z_i)` correlates with maneuver suitability.
- **Status Code (`S_t`):** A tuple `(E_t, a_t, r_t, E_{t+1})` capturing the environmental state, executed action, reward/feasibility score, and subsequent state. This facilitates reinforcement learning.

#### 3.3. System Architecture

- **Event Triggering Mechanism:** The system activates when a relevant object or intruder is detected within a predefined spatial threshold (`d_object < d_threshold`) or via semantic triggers.
- **Knowledge Bank (`M`):** A structured memory module storing `(z_i, a_i, r_i)` tuples, derived from human demonstrations, trajectories, or simulation data. It encapsulates object detection, semantic properties, and associated maneuver strategies. A dynamic pruning mechanism removes experiences leading to constraint violations and penalizes those causing proximity warnings.
- **Retrieval Mechanism:** An Approximate Nearest Neighbor (ANN) search (e.g., FAISS IVF-Flat) is performed in the latent space to find the `Top-k` most similar event solution codes `N_k(z_t)`. The final action is a weighted aggregation of these retrieved solutions.
- **Multi-modal Action Selection:** To counter the "average-to-collision" problem, a **Clustered Bayesian Selection (CBS)** strategy is employed. Retrieved maneuvers are grouped into clusters based on directional cosine similarity. The cluster with the highest aggregate weight is selected, and weighted averaging is performed only within that cluster.

#### 3.4. Algorithms

- **Pre-Training Algorithm:** Initializes the knowledge bank `M` using human demonstrations or simulation data. It involves object detection, semantic labeling, maneuver extraction, and encoding events `E` to `z`. An ANN index is built. The loss function `L(θ_1) = λ_m L_metric + λ_i L_imitation` optimizes latent embedding fidelity (`L_metric` based on `D_phys`) and supervised imitation of expert actions (`L_imitation`).
- **Training Algorithm:** Refines the system autonomously. The loss `L(θ_2) = λ_p R_phys(w, z) - λ_r J_perf` balances physical consistency (`R_phys` regularization towards kinematically compatible behavior) and task performance (`J_perf = E[r_t]`). `J_perf` is optimized using policy gradient methods. The process involves perceiving `x_t`, encoding `E_t` to `z_t`, retrieving solutions, computing weights `w_i`, generating `a_t`, executing it, observing outcomes, and appending `S_t` to an experience buffer for off-policy optimization.

#### 3.5. Complexity Analysis

- **Time Complexity:** Dominated by retrieval, `O(d · log |M|)` due to ANN search (FAISS IVF/PQ), where `d` is latent dimensionality and `|M|` is bank size. Event encoding is `O(n)` (n=objects), and action generation is `O(k)`.
- **Memory Complexity:** Primarily `O(|M| · d)` for the knowledge bank.

#### 3.6. Implementation Details

The framework was validated in NVIDIA Isaac Sim, generating an expert dataset using a Virtual Potential Field (VPF) supervisor. An adversarial curriculum with three intruder types (Type-A: stochastic, Type-B: goal-directed, Type-C: static) was used to stress test the system. The environment leveraged NVIDIA Omniverse, with agents modeled as spherical primitives.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the ERA framework encompassed training dynamics, mathematical fidelity, knowledge bank scaling, ablation studies, and hardware deployment on an edge-AI platform.

#### 4.1. Training Dynamics

Training was conducted over 100 episodes using an adversarial curriculum. The dual-objective optimization of performance (`J_perf`) and kinematic loss (`R_phys`) demonstrated stability, with `R_phys` remaining bounded below 0.05. Performance metrics showed robust convergence: initial success was around 75%, increasing to over 95% by episode 80, with consistently decreasing collision and warning rates. A temporary plateau near episode 30 was observed upon the introduction of Type-B intruders.

#### 4.2. Mathematical Fidelity and System Stability

- **Latent Transition Fidelity:** The mean squared error (MSE) between predicted and actual next latent states (`||ˆz_{t+1} - z_{t+1}||^2`) remained consistently below `10^-3`, indicating that the learned latent space supports a well-conditioned linear transition.
- **Contractive Stability:** Empirical validation through Lyapunov energy gradient (`∆V_t`) showed frequent negative spikes, demonstrating the agent's capacity to dissipate system "stress" and maintain contractive stability immediately after encountering threats.

#### 4.3. Knowledge Bank Scaling, Action Alignment, and Performance Saturation

The framework was evaluated with knowledge bank (KB) sizes ranging from 30,650 (Ep 100) to 49,545 (Ep 700) samples.
- **Medium Difficulty Scenarios:** Performance saturated, with the Ep 100 checkpoint achieving 84.71% success, zero collisions, and 3.5 average steps. Larger KBs (Ep 700) stabilized around 81.00% success. This suggests that beyond a certain point, further KB growth provides limited new information for common navigation patterns, potentially introducing redundancy and weakening retrieval precision. Reaction time increased from 6.36ms (Ep 100) to 12.73ms (Ep 700) with KB expansion.
- **Extreme Difficulty Scenarios:** Larger KB sizes yielded non-linear improvements. Success increased from 59.30% (Ep 100) to 61.19% (Ep 700), with the most efficient paths (4.2 average steps) achieved at Ep 600 and Ep 700. This indicates that a larger knowledge base is beneficial for covering rare or highly dynamic situations.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Kinematic Consistency Impact (R_phys):** Removing the kinematic consistency regularizer `R_phys` did not significantly alter success, collision, warning rates, or path efficiency. However, the full ERA model preserved lower reaction latency, particularly in extreme settings (6.36ms vs. 6.90ms). This suggests `R_phys` primarily acts as a stability regularizer and improves action smoothness, rather than directly boosting performance, contributing to efficient real-time control.
- **Bayesian Clustering Analysis (CBS):** CBS's impact was scenario-dependent. In medium difficulty, removing CBS slightly reduced success (0.8471 to 0.8423), increased collisions (0.0000 to 0.0167), and required more steps (4.6 vs. 3.5), indicating improved action coherence and efficiency. In extreme difficulty, the ablated variant *without* CBS achieved higher success (0.6539 vs. 0.5930), lower collision (0.0450 vs. 0.0563), and lower warning rates, but with more steps (6.9 vs. 6.1). This indicates that existing reliability-weighted retrieval and Lyapunov filtering provide substantial protection against unsafe averaging, reducing the marginal benefit of hard clustering in global benchmarks. CBS primarily enhances decisional consistency and trajectory compactness.

#### 4.5. Hardware Deployment

The ERA framework (Ep 100 configuration) was deployed on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano Super (4GB).
- **Computational Performance and Real-Time Latency:** The system achieved an end-to-end latency of 7.649 ms in FP32 mode (130.7 Hz), exceeding the 50-100 Hz requirement for stable autonomous flight. Surprisingly, FP32 outperformed FP16 due to the minimal parameter count (approx. 0.14 MB) and vectorized matrix operations, where half-precision type-casting overhead outweighed throughput gains.
- **Resource Efficiency and Memory Optimization:** Total peak inference memory was 25.28 MB (less than 1% of 4GB RAM). The Knowledge Bank (30,650 samples) occupied 15.43 MB. This demonstrates high memory efficiency and scalability for edge devices. The Lyapunov stability check added only 0.556 ms overhead.

#### 4.6. Comparative Evaluation

The ERA framework was compared against Behavioral Cloning (BC-IL), Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), and Acados (MPC) across easy, medium, hard, and extreme difficulty settings.
- **BC-IL:** BC-IL was the strongest overall baseline in success and safety, with zero collisions in easy to hard settings and the lowest inference latency among learned methods. ERA was competitive in moderate settings and produced more compact trajectories (fewer steps).
- **PPO:** ERA demonstrated substantially stronger reliability than PPO in medium, hard, and extreme environments. PPO exhibited lower success rates, higher warning rates, and significantly larger step counts, suggesting more hesitant or inefficient behavior.
- **Acados (MPC):** Acados had the slowest reaction times across all difficulty levels and consistently worse path efficiency (higher average steps) compared to ERA. ERA achieved a more favorable balance of responsiveness, efficiency, and success in dynamic multi-intruder settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presents a significant contribution to the field of embodied AI, particularly for autonomous agents in dynamic and safety-critical environments. The proposed Event-Centric Retrieval-Based Action (ERA) framework effectively addresses several key challenges that limit the deployment of current autonomous systems.

**Significance:**
- **Enhanced Interpretability and Physical Grounding:** By explicitly representing environments as semantic events and deriving actions through retrieval from a knowledge bank, the framework offers an interpretable decision-making process rooted in case-based reasoning. The incorporation of physics-informed knowledge and Lyapunov stability guarantees directly into the retrieval and training ensures that generated actions are kinematically consistent and contribute to system stability. This is a critical departure from many end-to-end learning models that lack such transparency and explicit physical constraints.
- **Real-time Performance on Edge Hardware:** The experimental evaluation demonstrates that ERA operates within sub-millisecond control loop constraints on edge-AI hardware (NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano). This computational efficiency, coupled with minimal memory footprint, signifies its readiness for real-world deployment on resource-constrained platforms, such as small-scale UAVs.
- **Robustness in Dynamic and Adversarial Environments:** The adversarial curriculum used in training, combined with supervised pretraining on expert demonstrations, yields a policy that is both safety-aware and robust to spatial distribution shifts and increasing environmental complexity. The ability to leverage prior experience from the knowledge bank provides a robust "warm-start" for navigating unseen scenarios.
- **Favorable Trade-offs Compared to Baselines:** The comparative analysis indicates that ERA achieves a balanced performance across safety, efficiency, and real-time responsiveness, outperforming model-predictive control (Acados) in speed and efficiency, and pure reinforcement learning (PPO) in reliability and efficiency for dense multi-agent navigation. While behavioral cloning (BC-IL) can achieve high success rates, ERA offers a more interpretable mechanism and can yield more compact trajectories.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Next-Generation Autonomous UAV Systems:** The ERA framework holds potential for the development of more reliable and safer autonomous UAVs, especially those operating in complex, high-density environments like urban corridors or industrial facilities. Its ability to provide interpretable, physically consistent, and real-time decision-making is crucial for such applications.
- **Enhanced Trust and Verification in AI:** The framework's interpretability and explicit stability guarantees can contribute to increased trust in autonomous systems. This transparency facilitates verification and validation, which is essential for regulatory compliance and public acceptance of AI in safety-critical domains.
- **Foundation for Hybrid AI Architectures:** ERA exemplifies a hybrid approach combining structured world modeling, memory-augmented learning, and control theory principles. This could inspire further research into integrated AI architectures that leverage the strengths of different paradigms to build more robust and versatile autonomous agents.
- **Scalability for Complex Missions:** While the current work acknowledges limitations in knowledge bank scalability beyond `10^5` samples, the demonstrated efficiency and structured approach lay a foundation for developing scalable solutions. Future work on hierarchical indexing or communication-aware coordination could unlock its potential for even more complex, long-duration missions with vast experience repositories.

In conclusion, this research provides a viable and robust framework that bridges critical gaps in embodied AI decision-making, setting a precedent for autonomous systems that are not only effective but also transparent, safe, and deployable in demanding real-world scenarios.
