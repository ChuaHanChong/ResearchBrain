# Event-Centric World Modeling with Memory-Augmented Retrieval for Embodied Decision-Making

> [!summary] Summary
> The Event-Centric Retrieval-Based Action (ERA) framework models dynamic environments through semantic events and utilizes memory-augmented retrieval to enable real-time, interpretable decision-making for embodied agents. Evaluated in UAV flight scenarios, the framework achieved a 100% success rate with zero collisions in adversarial conditions and maintained sub-millisecond decision latency on embedded hardware.

## Problem

- Many end-to-end deep learning approaches for embodied AI lack interpretability, making it difficult to understand the decision-making logic and hindering verification in safety-critical applications.
- Existing methods often lack explicit mechanisms to ensure physical consistency of generated actions, potentially leading to physically infeasible or unsafe behaviors.
- Integrating structured reasoning, like case-based reasoning, into dynamic, multi-entity embodied systems for real-time decision-making remains challenging.

## Method

- The **ERA** (Event-Centric Retrieval-Based Action) framework abstracts dynamic environments into a structured list of ==semantic events==, encoded into a compact latent space.
- It employs a ==memory-augmented retrieval== mechanism to query a knowledge bank of physics-informed prior experiences, using an ==Approximate Nearest Neighbor search==.
- Decision-making involves aggregating retrieved solutions, applying a ==Clustered Bayesian Selection== strategy to mitigate multimodal "average-to-collision" issues, and enforcing physics-informed latent dynamics with ==Lyapunov stability constraints==.

## Results

- The ERA framework demonstrated **100%** success rates with **zero collisions** across five adversarial curriculum episodes in UAV flight, showcasing robust navigation in dynamic environments.
- It achieved **sub-millisecond** decision latency on embedded hardware, specifically the Jetson Orin Nano, validating its real-time operability for autonomous systems.
- The physics-consistency regularizer and Lyapunov stability constraints were critical for selecting kinematically feasible maneuvers, preventing high-frequency oscillations and ensuring realistic robot behavior.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing complex, dynamic environments as event-centric semantic abstractions, combined with a latent space encoding, enables efficient and structured reasoning.
> - Integrating physics-informed knowledge and stability criteria directly into the latent space and retrieval process is crucial for ensuring kinematically consistent and safe actions in real-time.
> - A Clustered Bayesian Selection strategy effectively addresses the "average-to-collision" problem in multimodal action selection by aggregating similar, safe maneuvers.
