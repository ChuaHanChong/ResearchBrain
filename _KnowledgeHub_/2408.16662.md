# Space3D-Bench: Spatial 3D Question Answering Benchmark

> [!summary] Summary
> Space3D-Bench introduces a comprehensive benchmark featuring 1000 human-formulated spatial questions for indoor 3D environments, evaluating AI models' ability to understand complex spatial relationships. An automatic assessment system aligns with human judgment at 97.5% absolute agreement, while a RAG-based baseline achieves 66.8% accuracy, highlighting areas for future improvement.

## Problem

- Foundation models lack robust understanding of spatial relationships and properties within 3D environments, despite their advanced capabilities in other modalities.
- Existing 3D Question Answering (Q&A) datasets suffer from limitations such as narrow focus, limited data modalities, unbalanced question distributions, and coherence issues from automatic generation.
- A gap exists for comprehensive benchmarks that combine multiple modalities with diverse spatial question categories, especially those involving predictions or precise navigable distances.

## Method

- Developed **Space3D-Bench**, a dataset of 1000 human-formulated spatial questions on 13 Replica scenes, categorized into six spatial types (Location, Measurement, Relation, Navigation, Pattern, Prediction).
- Created a VLM-based automatic assessment system using ==GPT4-Vision== to evaluate natural language responses against ground truth, validated for high agreement with human judgment.
- Introduced **RAG3D-Chat**, a modular ==Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG)== baseline system leveraging ==Semantic Kernel== to integrate specialized modules (Image, Text, SQL, Navigation) for answering diverse spatial queries.

## Results

- The VLM-based automatic assessment system achieved **97.5%** absolute agreement with human judgments and **86.4%** weighted agreement, validating its reliability.
- The RAG3D-Chat baseline achieved an overall accuracy of **66.8%** on Space3D-Bench, establishing a starting point for future research.
- The baseline performed best on Navigation questions but struggled most with Prediction questions, particularly those requiring nuanced inference or complex semantic understanding.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human-aligned automated assessment for spatial question answering is achievable using advanced VLMs, demonstrating 97.5% absolute agreement with human judgment.
> - Current state-of-the-art models, even with RAG, achieve a 66.8% accuracy on complex 3D spatial reasoning, indicating substantial scope for improvement.
> - Predictive spatial questions and nuanced semantic understanding (e.g., inferring capacity from objects) remain particularly challenging for AI models, along with ambiguities in natural language interpretation.
