# ReaDy-Go: Real-to-Sim Dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting Simulation for Environment-Specific Visual Navigation with Moving Obstacles

> [!summary] Summary
> ReaDy-Go is a real-to-sim pipeline that reconstructs a target scene as 3D Gaussian Splatting from a monocular video, inserts animatable human Gaussian avatars driven by a motion diffusion prior along planned 2D trajectories, and generates photorealistic dynamic navigation datasets via a Hybrid A* expert planner. Imitation-trained RGB-only policies reach 90% real-world dynamic success, beating mesh-asset and general navigation baselines.

## Problem

- Simulation-trained RGB-only navigation policies degrade on deployment because domain randomization fails to close the sim-to-real distribution gap in visual observations.
- General navigation models need pre-built topological maps, cannot exploit scene-specific layout structure, and underperform environment-specific policies in households, restaurants, or factories.
- Existing Gaussian-Splatting real-to-sim pipelines assume static scenes, or insert non-photorealistic mesh humans needing mesh extraction and physics-engine integration for rendering.

## Method

- **ReaDy-Go** ==Dynamic GS simulator==: ==PGSR== static scene reconstruction unified with ==HUGS== animatable human Gaussian avatars, animated by ==PriorMDM== motion diffusion plus ==SMPLify== fitting from 2D trajectories.
- Robot expert planner for dynamic GS: opacity-filtered voxelization into 2D navigable maps, ==Hybrid A*== with a motion-primitive library, plus reactive replanning against constant-velocity human inflation.
- Human planner samples crossing or parallel start-goal pairs, computes ==A*== seed paths with spline smoothing; a ten-layer residual ==CNN== plus ==MLP== policy regresses expert velocities.

## Results

- Simulation dynamic tasks: **78%**, **78%**, **80%** success across Outside, Lobby, Library versus Vid2Sim's **57%**, **70%**, **68%** and ViNT's **36%**, **55%**, **62%**.
- Real world: **90%**/**70%**/**80%** dynamic success versus Vid2Sim **60%**/**40%**/**60%** and ViNT **30%**/**20%**/**40%**, using **2M** parameters against ViNT's **30M**.
- Zero-shot transfer to an unseen environment after training on **1,200** combined episodes gives **70%** static and **50%** dynamic success at higher reaching times.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Photorealism of the moving obstacle, not just the static background, is the factor that preserves policy success once dynamic agents appear.
> - Environment-specific policies from a few minutes of video beat general navigation foundation models at 15x fewer parameters in target deployment scenes.
> - Pure Gaussian representations for scene and actors avoid mesh extraction and physics engines, but motion realism depends on a learned generative motion prior.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Visual navigation for autonomous robots, particularly policies relying solely on RGB camera input, offers advantages such as reduced hardware complexity and cost. However, a significant challenge in deploying these systems in real-world dynamic environments is their limited robustness due to the "sim-to-real gap"—the discrepancy between simulated training data and real-world conditions. Furthermore, training navigation policies that are specifically tailored to unique target deployment environments (e.g., different household layouts, restaurant settings, or factory floors) remains resource-intensive.

Traditional approaches to training learning-based navigation policies often involve extensive simulation, as large-scale real-world data collection is impractical. While techniques like domain randomization have been employed to mitigate the sim-to-real gap, they may not entirely eliminate distribution differences. An alternative direction involves developing General Navigation Models (GNMs) trained on vast real-world datasets, which show potential for zero-shot deployment across diverse robots and environments. However, GNMs typically require pre-built topological maps and may not fully leverage environment-specific characteristics, potentially leading to reduced success rates compared to policies optimized for a particular setting.

To address the limitations of both simulation-only training and general models, real-to-sim simulation approaches have emerged. These methods aim to reconstruct target environments and generate photorealistic datasets for environment-specific policy training. Recent advancements in 3D Gaussian Splatting (GS) have significantly propelled this area. GS enables high-fidelity rendering, novel view synthesis, and provides an explicit 3D scene representation, making it suitable for generating synthetic data. Prior GS-based works have utilized these advantages for navigation dataset generation and policy training to reduce the sim-to-real gap in static environments. Examples include rule-based planning methods exploiting GS maps for geometric consistency and rendering quality, as well as learning-based navigation policies trained in GS-based environments for transfer to various robot platforms (drones, wheeled robots, legged robots).

Despite these advancements, a significant gap persists: existing GS-based navigation frameworks predominantly assume static scenes or introduce dynamic elements using non-photorealistic simulation assets, such as generic human meshes. Such simplifications limit the ability to learn safe and effective navigation behaviors in the presence of realistic dynamic obstacles and to render photorealistic human appearances within reconstructed real-world environments. This limitation makes it challenging to generate photorealistic navigation datasets for dynamic environments, which are common in real-world robot deployment scenarios. The present work addresses this gap by proposing a GS-based real-to-sim pipeline that jointly models animatable human GS obstacles within reconstructed static GS scenes, generates navigation datasets tailored for environment-specific training in dynamic settings, and trains visual navigation policies robust to both sim-to-real transfer and moving obstacles.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the acknowledged difficulties in deploying visual navigation models in real-world dynamic environments. These difficulties are primarily rooted in two areas:
1. **Sim-to-Real Gap:** Policies trained extensively in simulation often exhibit degraded performance when transferred to real-world scenarios due to discrepancies between simulated and real data distributions.
2. **Environment-Specific Training Challenges:** Robots frequently operate in unique, specific environments (e.g., different homes, offices, or industrial settings). Developing navigation policies optimized for these specific contexts, which can exploit environment-specific characteristics, is beneficial but traditionally expensive due to the cost of creating digital twins or collecting vast real-world data.

While 3D Gaussian Splatting (GS) has offered a promising avenue for real-to-sim simulation by enabling high-fidelity scene reconstruction from RGB videos, prior GS-based approaches have been insufficient for dynamic environments. Existing methods typically either consider only static scenes or incorporate dynamic obstacles using non-photorealistic assets, such as generic human meshes from simulators. This limitation restricts the realism of generated training data and hinders the development of policies that can safely and effectively navigate alongside real-world moving entities, particularly humans. Learning safe navigation in the presence of realistically rendered dynamic obstacles is critical for reliable robot deployment in human-populated spaces.

Therefore, the key objectives of this research are to:
1. **Develop a novel real-to-sim simulation pipeline, ReaDy-Go, capable of synthesizing photorealistic dynamic scenarios.** This involves augmenting a reconstructed static GS scene with dynamic, photorealistic human GS obstacles.
2. **Facilitate the training of robust navigation policies using these dynamically generated datasets.** The aim is for these policies to exhibit improved robustness to the sim-to-real gap and to effectively handle moving obstacles.
3. **Contribute a dynamic GS simulator** that integrates static scene GS with a human animation module, enabling the insertion of animatable human GS avatars and the synthesis of plausible human motions from 2D trajectories.
4. **Establish a navigation dataset generation framework** that leverages this dynamic simulator, along with a robot expert planner specifically designed for dynamic GS representations, and a human planner.
5. **Train visual navigation policies that are robust** to both the transfer from simulation to reality and to the presence of moving obstacles, thereby enhancing their practical applicability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed ReaDy-Go pipeline is designed for photorealistic real-to-sim dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting simulation for environment-specific RGB-only visual navigation with moving obstacles. It takes a monocular video of a static target environment as input and generates photorealistic navigation datasets with moving human obstacles, which are then used to train an environment-specific visual navigation policy. The methodology comprises three main components: a dynamic GS simulator, a navigation data generation framework, and a policy training module.

**A. A Photorealistic Real-to-Sim Dynamic GS Simulator**
The simulator reconstructs a static GS scene and integrates it with a human animation module.
1. **Static GS Scene Reconstruction:** The background environment is reconstructed using PGSR (Planar-based Gaussian Splatting for Efficient and High-fidelity Surface Reconstruction). PGSR compresses 3D Gaussians into flat planes and employs geometric regularization loss terms in addition to a photometric loss. This approach enhances geometric accuracy, reduces Gaussian noise, and improves multi-view consistency, which is beneficial for extracting scene voxels and 2D occupancy grids for planning.
2. **Human Animation Module:** This module places an animatable human GS model into the scene and generates plausible human motion along desired 2D obstacle trajectories.
 - **Animatable Human GS Avatars:** These are extracted from the NeuMan dataset using HUGS (Human Gaussian Splatting). HUGS disentangles a dynamic human and a static scene from video, parameterizes the human GS in a canonical space initialized with the SMPL (Skinned Multi-Person Linear) model, and estimates human Gaussian attributes and linear blend skinning weights to animate the human GS under novel poses.
 - **Human Motion Generation:** PriorMDM (Human motion diffusion as a generative prior) is utilized to predict the body root trajectory and joint angles in the SMPL parameters based on a given 2D trajectory. The 2D trajectory is converted into body root linear and rotation velocities, normalized, and fed into PriorMDM. The predicted 3D body joint positions are then fitted to SMPL parameters via SMPLify and transformed into the target environment's world coordinate frame.

**B. Navigation Data Generation for Dynamic Environments**
This pipeline integrates the dynamic GS simulator with a robot expert planner and a human planner to generate photorealistic navigation datasets, collecting RGB observations, actions, and relative goal positions.
1. **Static Scene Voxelization with Opacity Filtering:** For planning purposes, static scenes are voxelized. A voxel is marked as occupied if it contains part of the 1σ Gaussian ellipsoid. To counter spurious Gaussians (e.g., ground noise), voxels with an accumulated sum of Gaussian opacities below a defined threshold are classified as free space. This process refines free space regions and is crucial for accurate planning. The filtered voxel map is then projected into 2D occupancy maps: a robot navigable map (from ground to robot height) and a human walkable map (from ground to human height). Occupied grids in these maps are inflated by safety margins corresponding to robot or human radii.
2. **Robot Expert Planner for Dynamic GS Representations:** An expert planner is designed to navigate dynamic GS environments and generate kinematically feasible trajectories for ground vehicles. This planner augments the Hybrid A\* algorithm with a motion primitive library.
 - **Dynamic Obstacle Handling:** The robot navigable map is updated at each timestep if a human GS avatar is within the camera's field of view and approaches the planned robot trajectory. To represent the human obstacle accurately, spurious human Gaussian primitives (those with high covariance trace) are removed, and the remaining primitives are downsampled. These points are then projected onto the 2D robot navigable map, and the human-occupied regions are inflated using a safety buffer derived from a 2-second constant velocity prediction to anticipate human movement.
 - **Reactive Replanning:** The Hybrid A\* planner generates paths using discrete motion primitives. If the initial plan becomes unsafe during execution (e.g., minimum distance to a dynamic obstacle falls below a safety margin), a reactive replanning mechanism is triggered.
3. **Human Planner:** This planner generates trajectories for dynamic obstacles. It samples human start and goal positions that encourage interaction-rich scenarios (e.g., trajectories crossing or running parallel to the robot's path). A seed path is computed using a graph-based search (e.g., A\*) on the 2D human walkable map, followed by spline-based optimization for smoothing. The human animation module then uses this 2D trajectory to generate SMPL motion parameters for the human GS avatar.
4. **Data Generation:** For each dynamic scenario, the pipeline records tuples of photorealistic RGB observations, corresponding expert actions (linear and angular velocities), and relative goal positions, creating a dataset for policy training.

**C. Training Visual Navigation Policy**
End-to-end visual navigation policies are trained using imitation learning on the generated photorealistic navigation datasets. The policy architecture consists of an RGB encoder (ten convolutional layers with residual connections) that processes three consecutive frames to incorporate ego history and dynamic obstacle movement. The encoded latent vectors, concatenated with the previous action (linear and angular velocities) and relative goal positions in the robot's local coordinate frame, are fed into shallow fully connected layers. The policy outputs the current action (linear and angular velocities), which is supervised using a mean squared error loss against the expert actions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the ReaDy-Go pipeline's ability to generate photorealistic dynamic simulations and train robust visual navigation policies.

**A. Visualization of ReaDy-Go Dynamic GS Simulation**
Qualitative results demonstrate that ReaDy-Go effectively produces photorealistic real-to-sim dynamic environments. The human animation module generates plausible body motions for human GS avatars along given 2D trajectories without requiring a physics engine. The framework is capable of scalable generation of numerous photorealistic navigation scenarios through the integration of expert and human planners. Novel view synthesis further confirms that geometric consistency is maintained from various viewpoints in dynamic settings.

**B. Visual Navigation Performance in Simulation**
Comparative simulation experiments (Table I) reveal that environment-specific navigation policies trained with real-to-sim data are crucial for effective and safe navigation.
- **Environment-Specific vs. General Models:** ReaDy-Go and Vid2Sim, both trained in real-to-sim target environments, consistently achieve higher Success Rates (SR) and lower Average Reaching Times (ART) compared to general navigation models (GNM, NoMaD, ViNT) across all static and dynamic tasks and environments. This performance advantage is observed despite ReaDy-Go and Vid2Sim policies having significantly fewer parameters (2M vs. 9M-30M for general models). This indicates the efficacy of GS-based real-to-sim training for achieving fewer collisions and faster task completion.
- **Impact of Photorealistic Dynamic Obstacles:** A key finding is the distinct performance difference between ReaDy-Go and Vid2Sim in dynamic environments. While their performance is comparable in static tasks, ReaDy-Go maintains high SR and low ART in dynamic settings, whereas Vid2Sim experiences a substantial performance drop. For example, in the "Outside" environment, ReaDy-Go achieved a 78% SR and 18.68s ART in dynamic tasks, compared to Vid2Sim's 57% SR and 26.88s ART. Since the primary difference between these two methods in the comparison is the representation of dynamic obstacles (photorealistic human GS for ReaDy-Go vs. non-photorealistic human simulation assets for Vid2Sim), these results suggest that photorealistic dynamic obstacles are a critical factor in improving navigation performance in dynamic scenarios.

**C. Real-World Visual Navigation Performance**
Real-world experiments (Table II, Fig. 5, Fig. 6) with previously unseen humans further validate the effectiveness of ReaDy-Go in mitigating the sim-to-real gap.
- **Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer:** ReaDy-Go policies demonstrate comparable success rates in both static and dynamic tasks in real-world deployment as observed in simulation, across all environments. This robustness is maintained even with minor real-world appearance variations (e.g., lighting, texture changes). This indicates that photorealistic datasets generated for dynamic scenarios enhance the sim-to-real transferability of end-to-end visual navigation policies.
- **Environment-Specific Advantage:** Similar to simulation results, environment-specific policies (ReaDy-Go, Vid2Sim) outperform the general navigation model (ViNT) in real-world settings, despite ViNT's significantly larger parameter count.
- **Photorealistic Dynamic Obstacles for Real-World Dynamics:** ReaDy-Go exhibits superior performance in dynamic real-world tasks compared to Vid2Sim. For instance, in the "Outside" environment, ReaDy-Go achieved a 90% SR and 20.68s ART in dynamic tasks, while Vid2Sim reached 60% SR and 28.98s ART. This finding reinforces the importance of photorealistic human GS avatars in training policies that are robust to dynamic obstacles in real-world environments, as evidenced by ReaDy-Go's minimal performance degradation from static to dynamic tasks compared to Vid2Sim.
- **Failure Case Analysis:** Analysis of failure cases (Fig. 5) shows that ViNT experienced more collisions with static obstacles. Crucially, ReaDy-Go was more robust in situations involving dynamic obstacles than Vid2Sim, with fewer dynamic obstacle collisions and fewer static collisions occurring during obstacle detouring.

**D. Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Generalization**
The generalization potential of ReaDy-Go was assessed by deploying a policy, trained on combined datasets from three environments, to a novel, unseen environment. The policy achieved a success rate exceeding 50% in both static and dynamic tasks in the unseen environment (Table III). While the average reaching time was higher than in the training environments, these results suggest that the policy learned general navigation behaviors (e.g., obstacle avoidance and goal reaching). This indicates that ReaDy-Go can contribute to scalable general navigation by facilitating the creation of diverse, large-scale datasets.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents ReaDy-Go, a novel real-to-sim dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting simulation pipeline that addresses critical limitations in visual navigation for robotic systems. The work makes several significant contributions and holds notable potential impact:

1. **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap for Dynamic Environments:** ReaDy-Go is the first GS-based real-to-sim framework for visual navigation that explicitly incorporates photorealistic dynamic human GS obstacles within reconstructed static scenes. This capability is crucial for generating training data that more closely mirrors the complexities of real-world dynamic environments, thereby enhancing the robustness of visual navigation policies during sim-to-real transfer.
2. **Improved Robustness to Moving Obstacles:** The integration of photorealistic human GS avatars and a sophisticated human motion generation module directly contributes to training policies that are more adept at safely navigating in the presence of moving humans. The experimental results consistently demonstrate ReaDy-Go's superior performance in dynamic tasks compared to baselines using non-photorealistic obstacles. This advancement is vital for deploying robots safely and efficiently in human-centric spaces like homes, offices, and public areas.
3. **Cost-Effective and Scalable Environment-Specific Training:** The pipeline enables the generation of high-fidelity, environment-specific dynamic navigation datasets from just a few minutes of monocular video. This approach significantly reduces the traditionally high costs and complexities associated with creating digital twins, performing mesh extraction, or integrating physics engines for simulation. By making environment-specific policy training more accessible and scalable, ReaDy-Go can accelerate the development and deployment of robots tailored for diverse and unique real-world settings.
4. **Advancement in Simulation Fidelity:** The development of a dynamic GS simulator that unifies static scene GS, animatable human GS avatars, and human motion generation without requiring mesh extraction or physics engine integration represents a technical advancement in creating realistic robotic simulation environments. The opacity filtering for voxelization and the dynamic-aware robot expert planner are specific methodological improvements for robust planning within GS representations.
5. **Generalization Potential for Future Robotics:** The demonstrated zero-shot sim-to-real generalization to an unseen environment indicates that policies trained with ReaDy-Go data can learn fundamental navigation behaviors that extend beyond their training environments. This suggests a pathway towards training more generalized navigation models by leveraging ReaDy-Go to easily generate large-scale, diverse datasets from various real-world environments, contributing to the broader goal of more adaptable and autonomous robotic systems.
6. **Foundation for Advanced Navigation Research:** This work provides a foundation for future research. Potential extensions include expanding the diversity and scale of training environments to further strengthen generalization capabilities, integrating safe reinforcement learning techniques to tackle more challenging scenarios (e.g., highly dense dynamic obstacle settings, diverse non-human dynamic agents, or aggressive human motions), and exploring multi-robot navigation within these dynamic GS simulations.

In summary, ReaDy-Go marks a substantial step toward practical and robust visual navigation in dynamic real-world settings, offering a scalable and high-fidelity solution for generating training data that previously presented a significant bottleneck.
