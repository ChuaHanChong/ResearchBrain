# RESTRAIN: From Spurious Votes to Signals -- Self-Driven RL with Self-Penalization

> [!summary] Summary
> A self-driven reinforcement learning framework called RESTRAIN enables Large Language Models to improve reasoning capabilities without human-annotated labels by converting the absence of supervision and model uncertainty into robust learning signals. This approach surpasses existing unsupervised methods and nearly matches the performance of models trained with full gold-label supervision.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for LLM reasoning is limited by the high cost and scalability issues of human-annotated gold data.
- Current RLVR methods become ineffective on tasks where human experts lack definitive answers, hindering models from achieving 'superhuman' performance.
- Prior unsupervised RL methods, such as naive self-rewarding or majority voting, suffer from unreliable signals, training instability, and often discard correct minority solutions.

## Method

- **RESTRAIN** introduces a ==self-penalizing== Reinforcement Learning framework that converts internal model signals into robust learning objectives.
- It employs pseudo-label weighting, assigning soft, frequency-based weights to all unique candidate answers generated per prompt, rather than relying on a single majority vote.
- A negative rollout penalization mechanism applies a uniform negative offset to advantages for prompts where the model exhibits very low self-consistency, discouraging unreliable outputs.
- Prompt-level weighting scales updates based on precomputed consistency signals from a reference policy, ensuring high-confidence prompts contribute more to training.

## Results

- RESTRAIN substantially outperformed unsupervised baselines (TTRL, SRT) on reasoning benchmarks, achieving an average Pass@1 accuracy of **51.0%** for Qwen3-**4B**-Base on DAPO-14k-MATH, an 8.8 percentage point gain over TTRL.
- Its unsupervised performance nearly matched (within **0.4** pp) and in some cases surpassed gold-label supervised GRPO training, particularly on generalization tasks like MMLU_STEM and GPQA-Diamond.
- The framework demonstrated enhanced training stability, preventing model collapse observed in methods like TTRL, and showed strong performance in test-time adaptation across different base models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The absence of gold labels and the presence of model uncertainty can be systematically leveraged as valuable and robust learning signals for LLMs.
> - A soft, frequency-aware weighting over the full distribution of model-generated answers yields more stable and effective learning than hard majority voting.
> - Explicitly penalizing highly inconsistent or low-confidence rollouts is crucial for preventing model collapse and encouraging exploration in unsupervised RL.
> - Pre-calculating and fixing prompt-level weights offline is vital for maintaining training stability and avoiding spurious feedback loops during dynamic policy optimization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper situates itself within the rapidly evolving landscape of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Reinforcement Learning (RL), specifically addressing the limitations of current paradigms for improving reasoning capabilities.

**Background:** Recent years have seen LLMs, particularly when combined with Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), achieve remarkable success in complex chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning tasks. RLVR typically involves human-annotated data or external verifiers to provide reward signals, guiding the model towards correct reasoning steps and final answers. This approach has significantly boosted performance on challenging benchmarks.

**The Bottleneck RLVR Addresses:**
1. **High Cost of Labeled Data:** The primary limitation of RLVR is its heavy dependence on vast quantities of high-quality, human-annotated data. Creating such datasets is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming, hindering scalability.
2. **Performance on Harder Tasks:** On tasks where even human experts struggle to provide definitive answers or consistent feedback, RLVR's effectiveness diminishes. For LLMs to achieve "superhuman" performance, they must be able to improve in environments where curated labels are scarce or non-existent.

**Emerging Solutions (and their limitations):** This recognition has spurred research into "experience-driven learning" or "unsupervised RL," where models learn to self-improve without direct human supervision, drawing signals from unlabeled data or internal mechanisms. Two main directions have emerged:
1. **Self-Rewarding Methods:** Models generate their own reward signals, often by evaluating or ranking their own rollouts (e.g., LLM-as-a-Judge approaches). While these remove dependence on gold labels, their ability to consistently improve performance on complex reasoning tasks has shown limited evidence.
2. **Internal Agreement / Majority Voting:** These methods leverage the model's internal consistency across multiple generated rollouts, often by using a majority-voted answer as a pseudo-label for training (e.g., TTRL, SCPO, SRT). However, these approaches suffer from significant drawbacks:
 - **Reliability and Robustness Issues:** Majority votes can be spurious, especially on challenging tasks or when the model's confidence is low.
 - **Model Collapse:** Over-reliance on potentially flawed majority signals can lead to unstable training and model performance degradation.
 - **Ignoring Minority Solutions:** Crucially, these methods often discard correct solutions if they do not constitute the majority, wasting valuable learning signals and limiting exploration.

**RESTRAIN's Position:** RESTRAIN directly addresses these critical gaps. It builds upon the idea of self-improvement with unlabeled data but introduces a novel *self-penalizing* mechanism to generate *robust* learning signals. Instead of naively accepting majority votes or struggling with inconsistent self-rewards, RESTRAIN systematically turns the absence of gold labels and the presence of model uncertainty into valuable guidance. By robustly penalizing unreliable outputs and inconsistent reasoning, RESTRAIN aims to overcome the "spurious vote" problem, enabling more stable, scalable, and effective unsupervised RL for reasoning, thus pushing the boundaries of what LLMs can learn autonomously. This work is a significant step towards creating truly self-improving AI systems that can operate beyond human supervision.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind RESTRAIN stems from the growing realization that the current paradigm of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for improving Large Language Model (LLM) reasoning, while effective, is fundamentally constrained by its reliance on human-annotated data. The authors identify this dependency as a critical bottleneck for achieving advanced, potentially "superhuman," reasoning capabilities.

**Primary Motivations:**
1. **Cost and Scalability of Labeled Data:** High-quality human-annotated data, especially for complex chain-of-thought reasoning, is exceedingly expensive and difficult to scale. This limits the ambition and scope of current RL-based training for LLMs.
2. **Limitations on Harder Tasks:** As models approach and surpass human performance, they will encounter tasks where even humans lack definitive answers or cannot provide reliable feedback. In such scenarios, current supervised RL methods become ineffective, necessitating a shift towards autonomous improvement.
3. **Inadequacy of Existing Unsupervised RL Approaches:** Prior attempts at unsupervised RL for reasoning, such as simple self-rewarding mechanisms or majority voting schemes, have proven insufficient.
 - Self-rewarding methods often lack the consistency required for complex tasks.
 - Majority voting, while seemingly intuitive, suffers from fragility:
 - **Spurious Majorities:** The most frequent answer might still be incorrect, especially for challenging problems.
 - **Low Self-Consistency:** When models are uncertain, majority votes are unreliable.
 - **Ignoring Correct Minorities:** Valuable correct reasoning paths that are not the majority are discarded, leading to missed learning opportunities and potential model collapse. This "overcommitment to spurious majority votes" is a central problem RESTRAIN seeks to solve.

**Key Objectives of RESTRAIN:**
1. **Develop a Self-Penalizing RL Framework:** The central objective is to design a novel framework that can convert the *absence* of gold labels and the *presence* of model uncertainty into a useful, robust learning signal.
2. **Exploit the Full Answer Distribution:** Instead of relying solely on a single majority-voted answer, RESTRAIN aims to leverage the signals from the model’s entire distribution of generated answers.
3. **Implement Self-Penalization:** Specifically, the framework intends to:
 - **Penalize overconfident rollouts:** Reduce the weight or influence of responses generated with low confidence.
 - **Penalize low-consistency examples:** Down-weight or negatively reinforce prompts where the model exhibits high uncertainty across multiple rollouts.
 - **Preserve promising reasoning chains:** Ensure that potentially correct, but non-majority, reasoning paths are not inadvertently ignored.
4. **Seamless Integration with Policy Optimization:** Ensure that these self-penalization mechanisms can be smoothly integrated into existing and effective policy optimization methods, such as GRPO (Grouped Relative Policy Optimization), to enable continuous self-improvement without explicit supervision.
5. **Demonstrate Scalability and Performance:** Validate that RESTRAIN can deliver substantial performance gains on challenging reasoning benchmarks using only unlabeled data, ideally approaching or even surpassing gold-label supervised training.

In essence, RESTRAIN seeks to unlock a more scalable and autonomous path for LLMs to enhance their reasoning capabilities by transforming internal model signals and uncertainties into powerful, self-correcting learning objectives.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RESTRAIN (REinforcement learning with Self-resTRAINt training) is a self-penalizing Reinforcement Learning framework designed to enable Large Language Models (LLMs) to improve their reasoning capabilities without human-annotated gold labels. It achieves this by transforming the absence of external supervision into internal learning signals, primarily through three core components that integrate seamlessly with existing policy optimization methods like Grouped Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).

**Underlying RL Algorithm: Grouped Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**
RESTRAIN builds upon GRPO, a PPO-style objective that optimizes a policy ($\pi_\theta$) by sampling multiple rollouts per prompt and updating against a fixed reference policy ($\pi_{ref}$). GRPO uses a group-mean baseline for variance reduction and incorporates rewards for correctness against a gold label. RESTRAIN adapts this objective to an unsupervised setting by redefining how rewards and advantages are computed and weighted.

**The Three Core Components of RESTRAIN:**

1. **Pseudo-label Weighting (Section 2.1)**
 - **Problem Addressed:** Traditional majority-voting methods are brittle. They collapse all probability mass onto a single, potentially spurious, majority answer and ignore valuable signals from minority correct answers.
 - **Approach:** Instead of selecting a single pseudo-label, RESTRAIN considers *all* unique answers generated from `n` rollouts for a given prompt `x` as candidate pseudo-labels `{a_j}`.
 - **Mechanism:** Each unique answer `a_j` is assigned a weight `w_j` proportional to its observed frequency `f_j` (`c_j/n`, where `c_j` is the count for `a_j`). This weighting is shaped by a monotonic function `g` (specifically, a Gaussian function centered at `k` with bias `σ`). The GRPO loss is then computed as a weighted sum over these pseudo-labels: `L_GRPO(x; θ) = Σ w_j ⋅ L_GRPO(x, a_j; θ)`.
 - **Benefit:** This creates a "consensus distribution" that softly selects over answer frequencies. High-frequency answers receive proportionally larger weights, suppressing noisy low-frequency predictions, but without entirely discarding them. This provides a smoother, more robust learning signal than hard majority voting.

2. **Negative Rollout Penalization (Section 2.2)**
 - **Problem Addressed:** While pseudo-label weighting handles cases with weak consensus, there are scenarios where self-consistency is extremely low, and *none* of the generated rollouts are likely correct (e.g., when Pass@n is very low, as shown in Figure 2). In such "unreliable supervision" cases, even soft weighting can lead to reinforcing noise.
 - **Approach:** When the model exhibits very low self-consistency for a prompt, RESTRAIN explicitly penalizes *all* rollouts generated for that prompt.
 - **Mechanism:** A majority count `M(x)` for prompt `x` is calculated. If `M(x)` falls below a predefined threshold `κ` (e.g., `κ=3` in experiments), all candidate answers for that prompt are deemed unreliable. In this scenario:
 - Rewards `r_i,j` for all rollouts `y_i` with respect to any pseudo-label `a_j` are set to zero.
 - A uniform negative offset `δ` is applied to the advantages `A_i,j` of all rollouts: `A_i,j = A_i,j - δ`.
 - **Benefit:** This ensures that when the model is highly uncertain, it receives a clear negative signal, discouraging the generation of entirely unpromising or noisy reasoning paths and encouraging exploration for better solutions.

3. **Prompt-level Weighting (Section 2.3)**
 - **Problem Addressed:** Not all prompts are equally "learnable" or reliable. The model's confidence varies across prompts, and treating all updates equally can lead to reinforcing signals from less certain examples.
 - **Approach:** Scale the entire update for each prompt `x` based on its overall consistency.
 - **Mechanism:** A prompt weight `u_x` is precomputed *offline* using the reference policy ($\pi_{ref}$). This weight is derived by applying the monotonic function `g` to the reference model's majority count (`c_ref/n`) for that prompt: `u_x = g(c_ref/n)`. These `u_x` values are then fixed throughout the RL training.
 - **Benefit:** High-confidence prompts contribute more to the training updates, while low-confidence prompts contribute less. Precomputing these weights offline prevents "spurious feedback loops" where the model might inflate its own confidence during dynamic training.

**Final RESTRAIN Loss:**
The final RESTRAIN loss integrates all three components. For each prompt `x`, the loss is computed as:
`L_RESTRAIN(x; θ) = u_x ⋅ Σ w_j ⋅ L_GRPO_penalized(x, a_j; θ)`
where `u_x` is the prompt-level weight, `w_j` are the pseudo-label weights, and `L_GRPO_penalized` incorporates the negative rollout penalization via adjusted advantages (`A_i,j`).

This comprehensive approach allows RESTRAIN to derive robust, multi-faceted learning signals from unlabeled data, addressing the inherent unreliability of naive self-supervision methods and enabling more stable and effective model improvement.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of RESTRAIN on various reasoning benchmarks, using different base models and training datasets, reveals consistently strong performance and addresses critical limitations of prior unsupervised RL methods.

**1. RESTRAIN Significantly Outperforms Unsupervised Baselines:**
- **On DAPO-14k-MATH:** When training on the DAPO-14k-MATH dataset without gold labels, RESTRAIN substantially outperforms TTRL and Self-Rewarded Training (SRT) across multiple math and science benchmarks.
 - For Qwen3-4B-Base, RESTRAIN achieved an average Pass@1 accuracy of 51.0%, compared to TTRL's 42.2% (+8.8 percentage points, a relative increase of 20.9%).
 - Specific gains were remarkable: +140.7% on AIME25, +36.2% on MMLU_STEM, and +19.6% on GPQA-Diamond for Qwen3-4B-Base.
 - Similar trends were observed for the OctoThinker Hybrid-8B-Base model, with RESTRAIN outperforming TTRL by +5.2 pp (17.0% relative) on average.
- **On Synthetic S1k:** Training on a 5k synthetic reasoning dataset also showed RESTRAIN as the strongest label-free approach, exceeding the next-best baseline by at least 7.7 pp on average.

**2. Near-Matching and Surpassing Gold-Label Supervision:**
- **Unsupervised Rivaling Supervised:** A striking finding is that RESTRAIN's performance, without using any gold labels, nearly matches the empirical upper bound set by GRPO trained with full gold-label supervision. For Qwen3-4B-Base, supervised GRPO achieved 51.4% average accuracy, while unsupervised RESTRAIN achieved 51.0% – a mere 0.4 pp difference.
- **Outperformance on Generalization Tasks:** Even more notably, RESTRAIN *surpassed* gold-label GRPO on MMLU_STEM (80.9% vs. 73.7%) and GPQA-Diamond (40.2% vs. 38.7%). This suggests that while supervised training might lead to overfitting on domain-specific patterns, RESTRAIN's reliance on broader distributional signals via self-penalization could enhance cross-domain generalization, especially to science tasks, despite being trained on math-focused data.

**3. Effectiveness in Test-Time Reinforcement Learning (TTRL):**
- RESTRAIN demonstrates strong performance in adapting to test-time data, outperforming other test-time RL methods like TTRL and Entropy-fork Tree Majority Rollout (ETMR) on the Llama3.1-8B-Instruct model.
- It achieved an overall +11.0% gain in average accuracy, with significant margins on AMC23 (+13.0%) and MATH-500 (+13.3%). This indicates its robustness and scalability across different training paradigms.

**4. Enhanced Training Stability and Prevention of Model Collapse:**
- Unlike TTRL, which showed initial improvements but quickly collapsed after about 50 steps, RESTRAIN maintained stable and continuous performance improvement on MATH500 throughout training (Figure 4).
- This stability is attributed to RESTRAIN's frequency-aware soft weighting of pseudo-labels, which smooths the learning signal, curbs overconfident updates, and effectively mitigates sudden performance degradation.

**5. Ablation Studies Confirm Component Necessity:**
- **Pseudo-label weighting is crucial:** Removing this component resulted in the most substantial performance degradation and training collapse, highlighting its foundational role in stable unsupervised learning. Uniform weighting also performed poorly, underscoring the importance of frequency-based weighting.
- **Negative rollout penalization is important:** Omitting this component reduced the average score significantly (from 51.0% to 42.1%), demonstrating its role in handling highly uncertain or noisy prompts.
- **Prompt-level weighting contributes:** While its removal caused a more modest decrease (to 47.7%), it still validated its positive contribution to overall performance and stability. Offline computation of these weights was also shown to be superior to online variants, which tend to collapse.
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** The optimal performance of RESTRAIN depends on carefully tuned hyperparameters, such as `σ` (pseudo-label weighting bias), `δ` (negative advantage offset), and `κ` (majority count threshold). These parameters control the balance between filtering noise and retaining valuable learning signals.

In summary, RESTRAIN presents a robust, stable, and highly effective unsupervised RL framework that not only surpasses existing label-free methods but also competes closely with, and in some cases exceeds, supervised training, marking a significant advancement in self-driven LLM reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RESTRAIN introduces a novel and highly impactful approach to training large language models (LLMs) for reasoning tasks, moving beyond the current limitations of human supervision. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Scalable and Cost-Effective LLM Training:** The most profound impact of RESTRAIN is its ability to enable significant LLM self-improvement *without* relying on expensive, labor-intensive human-annotated "gold" labels. This dramatically reduces the cost and logistical burden associated with training advanced reasoning models, making high-performance LLMs more accessible and scalable for diverse applications and continuous improvement. This is a critical step towards mitigating the data bottleneck in modern AI.

2. **Robustness and Stability in Unsupervised RL:** RESTRAIN directly addresses long-standing challenges in unsupervised reinforcement learning, particularly the issues of spurious majority votes and training instability (model collapse). By introducing a self-penalizing mechanism that leverages the full distribution of model outputs, discriminates against low-confidence rollouts, and accounts for prompt-level consistency, RESTRAIN provides a much more robust and stable learning signal. This prevents the erratic behavior and performance degradation observed in prior methods that rely solely on naive majority voting.

3. **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** The finding that RESTRAIN can surpass gold-label supervised training on generalization tasks (like MMLU_STEM and GPQA-Diamond) despite being trained on math-focused data is highly significant. This suggests that self-penalization encourages the model to learn more fundamental, transferable reasoning patterns rather than overfitting to specific domain-centric cues present in supervised datasets. This can lead to LLMs that are more adaptable and performant across a wider range of unseen tasks and domains.

4. **Advancing Autonomous AI Capabilities:** RESTRAIN pushes the frontier of self-driven AI. By enabling models to learn and refine their reasoning abilities in environments where human feedback is sparse or unavailable, it paves the way for truly autonomous agents. This is crucial for developing AI systems capable of tackling complex, open-ended scientific discovery, creative problem-solving, or operating in environments where humans lack definitive answers (e.g., advanced research, space exploration, highly specialized domains).

5. **Seamless Integration and Broad Applicability:** The framework's seamless integration with existing policy optimization methods like GRPO makes it highly practical. Furthermore, its demonstrated effectiveness in both large-scale training and test-time adaptation suggests broad applicability across various LLM lifecycle stages and use cases, from foundational model refinement to on-the-fly adaptation.

6. **Inspiration for Future Research:** RESTRAIN's success in converting the absence of labels into powerful learning signals will likely inspire further research into novel self-supervision and self-correction mechanisms for LLMs. This could lead to new architectures, intrinsic reward functions, and training methodologies that further reduce reliance on external data and human oversight, accelerating the development of more intelligent and adaptable AI systems.

In conclusion, RESTRAIN represents a significant stride towards building truly self-improving reasoning agents. By making unsupervised RL both effective and stable, it democratizes access to advanced LLM training, unlocks new avenues for generalization, and accelerates the journey towards more autonomous and capable artificial intelligence.
