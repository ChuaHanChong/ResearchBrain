# Learning with Positive and Imperfect Unlabeled Data

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework from Yale University enables learning binary classifiers using positive and imperfect unlabeled data through an iterative constrained optimization approach, demonstrating polynomial-time algorithms for handling distribution shifts and corrupted data while providing theoretical guarantees based on VC-dimension bounds.

## Problem

- Classical Positive-Unlabeled (PU) learning assumes perfect alignment between positive and unlabeled data distributions, which is unrealistic in practice
- Existing approaches fail when unlabeled data is corrupted or exhibits distribution shift
- Prior average-case analyses of positive-only learning require strong parametric assumptions on the data distribution, such as Gaussianity or uniformity, that are often unrealistic for real-world datasets

## Method

- Introduces ==Positive and Imperfect Unlabeled (PIU) Learning== model that handles misaligned distributions
- Develops efficient approximation algorithm using ==constrained L1-regression== and list-decoding techniques
- Solves multiple instances of ==Pessimistic-ERM== optimization iteratively

## Results

- Achieves misclassification error of **ε** with polynomial runtime and sample complexity
- Enables learning from smooth distributions using only positive samples
- Provides the **first algorithm** for detecting truncation in non-product distributions, with applications extending to parameter estimation for exponential family distributions

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sample complexity is characterized by the VC-dimension of the hypothesis class
> - Distribution shifts can be handled by focusing on regions where previous iterations performed poorly
> - Connection to truncated statistics enables parameter estimation under unknown censoring

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research addresses the problem of binary classification from positive-only samples, a variant of PAC learning where a learner receives independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) positively labeled samples and aims to learn a classifier with low classification error. This area of learning theory has historically faced significant challenges. Prior work by Natarajan [Nat87], Gereb-Graus [Ger89], and Shvaytser [Shv90] established that, in a worst-case setting, most "interesting" concept classes, including two-dimensional halfspaces, are not learnable from positive-only examples. This theoretical limitation presents a barrier for numerous real-world applications in fields such as bioinformatics and ecology, where practitioners often rely on heuristic methods due to the scarcity or cost of obtaining negative samples.

To circumvent these worst-case impossibilities, a line of research has explored average-case analyses. These approaches often design computationally efficient algorithms for specific hypothesis classes (ee.g., simplices, halfspaces) under strong assumptions on the underlying data distribution (D*), such as it being uniform on the Boolean Cube or Gaussian. However, these parametric assumptions are frequently unrealistic in practical scenarios. Furthermore, applied sciences have developed various heuristics for positive-unlabeled (PU) learning, sometimes with statistical guarantees, but these typically require exact access to samples from the marginal distribution D*, which may also be unavailable.

This paper introduces a smoothed analysis framework to positive-only learning. Smoothed analysis, pioneered by Spielman and Teng [ST04] for algorithm performance, examines an algorithm's behavior under slight perturbations of worst-case inputs, effectively "smoothing" the input distribution. In learning theory, this paradigm has been adopted by requiring the unknown data distribution D* to be "smooth" with respect to an accessible reference measure D. Previous works, such as those by Haghtalab, Roughgarden, and Shetty [HRS24] and Chandrasekaran et al. [CKKMS24], have applied smoothed analysis to online learning, demonstrating how it can bypass worst-case negative results. This current research extends the smoothed analysis paradigm to the positive-only learning setting, aiming to bridge the gap between the theoretical impossibility of worst-case learning and the restrictive assumptions of average-case learning, thereby providing more broadly applicable theoretical guarantees.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to investigate whether general concept classes can be learned from positive-only samples without imposing strong, often unrealistic, assumptions on the true data distribution D*. This overarching question is motivated by several practical and theoretical considerations:

- **Prevalence of Positive-Only Data**: In many real-world applications, collecting negatively labeled samples is difficult, expensive, or ambiguous. Examples include medical diagnosis (confirmed disease cases are positive, but healthy controls may be unlabeled or hard to ascertain [EN08]), web search and recommendation systems (user engagement indicates positive feedback, while non-engagement is ambiguous [LDLLY03]), and bioinformatics (identifying disease-causing genes where false negatives are high [MV11]).
- **Limitations of Existing Paradigms**:
 - **Worst-Case Negative Results**: Prior theoretical work established that fundamental concept classes, such as two-dimensional halfspaces, are not learnable in the worst-case positive-only setting. This severely limits the applicability of machine learning in critical domains where negative examples are unavailable [JS22].
 - **Strong Assumptions in Average-Case Analysis**: Attempts to achieve learnability through average-case analyses often necessitate strong parametric assumptions on D*, such as Gaussianity or uniformity on a Boolean cube. These assumptions can be unrealistic for diverse real-world datasets. Furthermore, some methods require exact sample access to the underlying marginal distribution D*, which is often not feasible.
 - **Heuristics without Strong Guarantees**: Applied fields frequently resort to heuristic methods for PU learning, which may lack rigorous statistical guarantees or rely on simplified models that do not capture the complexity of real data.
- **The Smoothed Analysis Approach**: The authors propose smoothed analysis as a realistic theoretical framework to overcome these limitations. The core motivation for adopting this framework is to provide robust learning guarantees under milder, more plausible assumptions. Specifically, the work assumes that the true data distribution D* is "smooth" with respect to an *imperfect* yet accessible reference distribution D (Assumption 1: D*(S) ≤ (1/σ) * D(S)^(1/q)). This assumption is significantly less restrictive than requiring D* to conform to a specific parametric family or be perfectly known.
- **Addressing Imperfect Unlabeled Data**: The paper explicitly motivates the smoothed model by discussing scenarios where "perfect" unlabeled examples are difficult or impossible to obtain. This includes:
 - **Source Mismatch**: Positive examples might come from specialized studies, while unlabeled examples are aggregated from broader, potentially different, sources (e.g., validated genes vs. all known genes, specialized hospital data vs. nationwide aggregates). In such cases, the support of the imperfect unlabeled distribution D may be strictly larger than that of D*, making the generalized smoothness assumption natural.
 - **User-Specified Identification Tasks**: Tasks like automated face tagging or email classification, where unlabeled data may be augmented with AI-generated content or collected at different times, introducing complex shifts or corruptions.

By initiating a smoothed analysis of positive-only learning, the research aims to establish a theoretical foundation that supports the development of more practical and robust learning algorithms for general concept classes, applicable across a wider range of real-world contexts than previously possible.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of this research involves applying and adapting the smoothed analysis framework to the challenge of learning from positive-only samples. This entails making specific assumptions about the relationship between the true data distribution and an accessible reference distribution, and then developing novel algorithms that leverage these assumptions.

**Key Assumptions:**

1. **Generalized Smoothness (Assumption 1)**: The true underlying distribution D* is smooth with respect to a known reference distribution D. This is formally expressed as: for any measurable set S, D*(S) ≤ (1/σ) * D(S)^(1/q) for known constants σ ∈ (0, 1] and q ≥ 1. The learner has sample access to D. This assumption is crucial as it bounds the mass of D* on any set S based on D's mass, enabling the transfer of guarantees from D to D*.
2. **Non-Trivial Fraction of Positives (Assumption 2)**: For computationally efficient algorithms, it is additionally assumed that the target concept h* assigns positive labels to a non-trivial fraction of the data under D*, i.e., Pr(x∼D*)(h*(x)=1) ≥ α for some known α > 0. This is a common assumption in efficient positive-only learning algorithms.

**Addressing Challenges with Existing Approaches:**

The paper highlights the limitations of direct applications of existing methods:
- **ERM Insufficiency**: Standard empirical risk minimization (ERM) is insufficient. If D ≠ D*, treating all unlabeled samples as negative forms a mixture distribution M. The optimal hypothesis for this mixed problem may differ significantly from H*, the set of true positive examples.
- **Computational Efficiency Bottlenecks**: Even if D were close to D*, standard agnostic learning algorithms (often based on L1-Regression) require polynomial approximability of the hypothesis class with respect to the feature distribution. For mixture distributions like M, polynomial approximation guarantees may not hold (e.g., M might not be log-concave, and existing results for polynomial approximation are distribution-specific). Furthermore, polynomial approximation might be required in Lq-norm for large q, for which little is known.

**Proposed Methodology: Reduction to Constrained Learning (Pessimistic-ERM)**

Instead of directly reducing to agnostic learning, the authors propose reducing the problem to a series of **Pessimistic-ERM** problems. This approach is inspired by practical heuristics in PU learning.
A Pessimistic-ERM problem is defined as:
`arg min (H∈H) |H ∩ U|/|U| , s.t. , |H ∩ P|/|P| ≥ 1 − ρ`
where P and U are sets of positive and unlabeled samples, and ρ is a tolerance.

1. **Sample-Efficient Algorithm (Algorithm 1: Iterative-Pessimistic-ERM)**:
 - This algorithm (for Theorem 1.1) iteratively solves T = O((σε)^(-q)) instances of Pessimistic-ERM.
 - In each iteration *i*, it defines a "survival set" S_i (initially the entire domain, then the intersection of previous successful hypotheses). The unlabeled samples for the *i*-th instance are U ∩ S_i.
 - The algorithm outputs the intersection of all obtained hypotheses H_1 ∩ ... ∩ H_T.
 - This iterative approach addresses the challenge that D* may have a strictly smaller support than D, preventing low-VC dimension hypotheses from being trivially learned. The intersection mechanism allows the learner to refine its hypothesis by successively shrinking the region of interest.

2. **Computationally Efficient Algorithm (Algorithm 3: Boosted-Constrained-Regression)**:
 - This algorithm (a subroutine for Theorem 3.3) is designed to *approximately solve a single instance* of Pessimistic-ERM.
 - It uses a novel **Constrained-Regression** approach. It finds a degree-k polynomial p(·) that minimizes `sum(|p(x)|)/|U|` subject to a feasibility constraint on positive samples `sum(min{p(x), 1})/|P| >= 1-ρ`.
 - The output hypothesis H is a polynomial threshold function (PTF) `1{p(x) ≥ t}`, where `t` is a suitably chosen threshold (bounded away from 1) to ensure feasibility for Pessimistic-ERM.
 - This approach requires that the concept class H is L1-approximable by low-degree polynomials with a bounded range with respect to the *accessible* reference distribution D. This is a weaker requirement than L2-approximability or approximation with respect to a mixture distribution.
 - To boost performance, the constrained regression is repeated T times, and the best-performing hypothesis on a fresh holdout set of unlabeled samples is selected.

3. **Overall Computational Efficiency (Algorithm 2: Iterative-Constrained-Regression)**:
 - Combines the iterative structure of Algorithm 1 with the efficient approximation of Pessimistic-ERM from Algorithm 3.
 - Addresses additional challenges: maintaining polynomial approximability guarantees for truncated distributions in each iteration and ensuring efficient sampling from truncated distributions (via rejection sampling).

4. **Learning in the List Decoding Model**:
 - For the generalized setting where a list of ℓ reference distributions D1..., Dℓ is provided (one of which satisfies smoothness with D*).
 - The methodology runs Algorithm 2 (Iterative-Constrained-Regression) independently for each D_i in the list.
 - It then filters the resulting hypotheses, keeping those that achieve a certain level of containment for the positive samples. The final output is the intersection of these "good" hypotheses. This avoids the pitfalls of trying to combine the list into a single mixture distribution that might lack desired properties.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several main findings, demonstrating that smoothed analysis can significantly alter the learnability landscape for positive-only learning, from theoretical impossibility to practical feasibility.

#### 4.1. Sample Efficiency of Smoothed Positive-Only Learning (Theorem 1.1)

- **Learnability of VC-classes**: A key finding is that in the smoothed model, all concept classes with finite VC-dimension become learnable, a direct contrast to the worst-case setting where even simple classes like two-dimensional halfspaces are not learnable.
- **Sample Complexity**: The algorithm requires `O( (VC(H) + log(1/δ)) / (εσ)^(2q) )` positive samples (and an equivalent number of samples from D) to guarantee ε-classification error with probability 1-δ.
- **Improper Learning**: The proposed algorithm is an improper learner, meaning it outputs a hypothesis that is an intersection of hypotheses from the original class H. The paper demonstrates that improper learning is necessary in this context (Remark 4.2).
- **Optimal Sample Complexity for q=1 (Theorem 3.7)**: Under an additional assumption (Assumption 3: D*(S) ≥ σ D(S), complementing generalized smoothness), the sample complexity for the special case of q=1 (linear smoothness) is improved to `O( (1/(εσ^2)) * (VC(H) + log(1/δ)) )`. This rate is characterized as optimal for smooth positive-only learning with q=1, matching the optimal rate for realizable PAC learning, which is a simpler problem.

#### 4.2. Smooth Computational Efficiency of Positive-Only Learning (Theorem 3.3)

- **Efficient Learnability Condition**: Efficient learning is possible if the target hypothesis h* (or its corresponding set H*) is approximable by low-degree polynomials with a bounded range (specifically, non-negative polynomials with range `[−C, ∞)`) in the L1-norm with respect to the reference distribution D.
- **Computational Complexity**: The algorithm runs in time `poly(n)` where `n = poly(d^k, 1/ζ, log(1/δ))` samples are used. Here, `k` is the degree of the approximating polynomial, and `ζ` is the approximation error.
- **Approximation with Respect to D**: A notable aspect is that L1-approximability is required only with respect to the accessible reference distribution D, not the inaccessible D*. This is crucial for practical applicability and leverages recent testable learning techniques.
- **Exponential Dependence on 1/ε**: The degree `k` typically scales as `poly(1/ε)`, leading to an exponential dependence on `1/ε` in the runtime. This is not a limitation of the proposed algorithm but is theoretically necessary for any SQ (Statistical Query) algorithm, as indicated by recent lower bounds for related problems [DKPZ24].

#### 4.3. Generalizations and Application to Truncated Statistics

The theoretical framework and algorithms developed for smoothed positive-only learning have direct implications for other challenging problems in learning theory and statistics:

1. **Estimation under Unknown Truncation (Informal Corollary 1.3 / Corollary 5.1)**:
 - The work provides the first polynomial sample and time algorithm for estimating parameters of an exponential family distribution from samples truncated to an unknown set S*.
 - This is a significant improvement over prior works [KTZ19, LMZ24] which required stronger L2-norm approximation of S*. The proposed algorithm only requires S* to be L1-approximable by non-negative polynomials.
 - This enables efficient estimation when S* is an arbitrary function of O(1) halfspaces, and the distribution is sub-exponential (e.g., Gaussians or product exponential distributions), expanding the class of learnable truncation sets.

2. **Truncation Detection (Informal Corollary 1.4 / Corollaries 5.2 & 5.3)**:
 - The paper presents the first algorithm for detecting whether given samples have been truncated (or not) for a broad range of distributions, including non-product distributions.
 - This addresses an open question from [DLNS24a], which was limited to product distributions and required strong assumptions on the knowledge of Q.
 - The algorithm works even with an *approximate* sampling oracle for the untruncated distribution.
 - It allows for detection with general non-convex truncation sets (e.g., functions of O(1) halfspaces), a scenario for which no efficient algorithms were previously known beyond polynomial threshold functions (PTFs).

3. **Learning with a List of Reference Distributions (Informal Theorem 1.6 / Theorem 5.4)**:
 - The main results are generalized to a more complex scenario where instead of direct access to *the* reference distribution D, the learner has access to samples from a *list* of O(1) distributions, one of which satisfies the smoothness condition with D*.
 - The algorithms achieve analogous sample and computational complexity results in this list-decoding model.
 - This is particularly relevant for scenarios involving adversarially corrupted data sources, where list-decodable algorithms can filter out corrupted inputs to identify a plausible reference distribution.

In summary, the main findings establish that under generalized smoothness conditions, positive-only learning becomes tractable both in terms of sample complexity (characterized by VC-dimension) and computational efficiency (characterized by polynomial approximability). These theoretical advancements subsequently enable novel, more general, and faster algorithms for problems in truncated statistics and learning with corrupted data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a notable advancement in the theoretical understanding and practical applicability of learning from positive-only samples. Its significance and potential impact can be delineated across several dimensions:

- **Bridging Theoretical Gaps**: The work directly addresses a fundamental challenge in PAC learning by demonstrating that smoothed analysis can transform a problem with largely negative worst-case results (positive-only learning of general concept classes) into a tractable one. By introducing the generalized smoothness assumption (Assumption 1), the authors provide a middle ground between the overly pessimistic worst-case model and the overly restrictive average-case models, which often assume specific parametric distributions. This conceptual shift offers a more realistic and powerful framework for analyzing learning problems in the presence of incomplete or imperfect data.

- **Broader Applicability of Learning Algorithms**:
 - **General Concept Classes**: The finding that all VC-classes become learnable in the smoothed model significantly broadens the scope of problems that can be tackled with theoretical guarantees, moving beyond the highly specialized concept classes (e.g., specific types of halfspaces or convex bodies) often assumed in prior average-case analyses.
 - **Relaxed Distributional Assumptions**: The algorithms only require smoothness with respect to an *imperfect* reference distribution D, rather than strong parametric assumptions on the true distribution D* or exact knowledge of D*. This makes the approach more robust and applicable to diverse real-world datasets where exact distributional forms are unknown.
 - **L1-Approximability**: The computational efficiency results rely on L1-approximability of the target concept by polynomials with respect to the accessible distribution D. This is a weaker condition than L2-approximability, which was often required previously, thereby expanding the set of efficiently learnable concept classes.

- **Direct Impact on Related Fields**: The new framework yields direct improvements and generalizations in other areas of learning theory and statistics:
 - **Truncated Estimation**: The paper's contribution to truncated estimation with unknown survival sets is substantial. By moving from L2 to L1-approximability, the algorithms can handle more complex, non-convex truncation sets (e.g., arbitrary functions of halfspaces), for which no efficient solutions were previously known. This is critical for robust statistical inference from incomplete data in various scientific and economic domains.
 - **Truncation Detection**: The ability to detect truncation for broad classes of distributions (including non-product distributions) and truncation sets, even with approximate sample access, addresses open problems and enhances the toolkit for data quality assessment and anomaly detection.
 - **Robustness to Data Corruption**: The extension to the "list decoding model" provides a powerful mechanism for learning in scenarios with adversarially corrupted unlabeled data. This is increasingly relevant in an era where data sources can be unreliable or augmented by synthetic content, offering practical pathways for designing more resilient learning systems.

- **Inspiration for Future Research**: The work opens several new avenues for theoretical exploration, including the potential for black-box reductions to agnostic learning, designing algorithms with polynomial dependence on 1/ε for specific concept classes, and characterizing universal rates in the smoothed positive-only setting. The iterative Pessimistic-ERM approach, a novel algorithmic primitive, may also inspire new heuristics in applied settings.

In essence, "Smoothed Analysis of Learning from Positive Samples" offers a paradigm shift for a challenging problem. By developing robust theoretical guarantees under realistic assumptions, it lays a foundation for more powerful, general, and reliable machine learning algorithms in situations where negative data is scarce or unreliable, with significant implications for fields ranging from bioinformatics to econometrics.
