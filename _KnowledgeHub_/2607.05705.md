# IMR: Iterative Mode-World Weighted Regression for Multi-Agent Trajectory Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> EACON researchers developed Iterative Mode-World Weighted Regression (IMR), a multi-agent trajectory prediction method utilizing an iterative decoder and a novel mode-world weighted loss. IMR effectively mitigates mode collapse and enhances prediction accuracy, achieving a `avgBrierMinFDE_6`.`avgBrierMinFDE_6`6 improvement in `avgBrierMinFDE_6` on the Argoverse 2 multi-agent benchmark.

## Problem

- Existing multi-agent motion prediction methods often suffer from 'mode collapse,' failing to capture the full diversity of plausible future trajectories.
- Anchor-based approaches improve mode diversity but frequently compromise prediction accuracy due to reliance on fixed anchors.
- Prior proposal-refinement decoding architectures exhibit reduced effectiveness when initial trajectory proposals are significantly inaccurate, potentially compounding errors.

## Method

- **IMR** employs a ==graph attention network== encoder to model implicit relationships and multi-source interactions among agents based on 3D spatial relationships.
- An ==iterative decoder== progressively refines trajectory predictions over multiple steps, utilizing ==segmented decoding== that directly outputs position coordinates rather than offsets.
- A novel ==mode-world weighted regression loss== combines mode-wise and world-wise regression components to balance mode diversity and prediction accuracy.

## Results

- IMR achieved a `avgBrierMinFDE_6` of **1.59** on the Argoverse 2 multi-agent benchmark, a **0.06** improvement over the previous state-of-the-art QCNeXt (**1.65**).
- The method demonstrated competitive performance on the Argoverse 2 single-agent benchmark, achieving a `brier-MinFDE_6` of **1.63**, comparable to leading methods.
- Qualitative analysis confirmed that the mode-world weighted regression loss successfully mitigated mode collapse and improved prediction accuracy, producing diverse and closely aligned trajectories compared to individual mode-wise or world-wise losses.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The iterative decoder's recurrent and segmented structure, with direct coordinate output, enhances robustness against error accumulation and effectively leverages intermediate information for continuous refinement.
> - The mode-world weighted regression loss successfully encourages the model to generate both diverse and accurate multi-agent trajectories by considering both individual agent modes and coherent joint world states.
> - Directly outputting position coordinates, rather than offsets, prevents early prediction errors from irrevocably corrupting subsequent decoding stages.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

Multi-agent motion prediction is a fundamental component for the development and reliable operation of automated vehicles. Accurate prediction of future trajectories for various road entities, such as other vehicles, bicycles, and pedestrians, enables real-time environmental perception and dynamic analysis. This capability is crucial for enhancing path planning, improving obstacle avoidance, and mitigating accident risks, thereby contributing to overall driving safety and smoother navigation in complex traffic environments.

Within this domain, existing approaches generally fall into two categories: prediction-based methods and anchor-based methods. Prediction-based methods, exemplified by works such as QCNet and Forecast-MAE, aim to directly predict future trajectories. However, these methods frequently encounter a limitation known as "mode collapse," particularly in complex scenarios where multiple plausible future trajectories exist for an agent. Mode collapse refers to the model's tendency to predict only a single, or a very limited number of, future trajectories, thereby failing to capture the full diversity of potential actions an agent might take. This can lead to an incomplete understanding of surrounding intentions and potentially inadequate safety assessments for automated vehicles.

Conversely, anchor-based approaches, such as MTR and TNT, are designed to address the issue of mode collapse by utilizing a predefined set of anchor trajectories or behavioral primitives. While these methods can offer improved mode diversity by predicting multiple distinct future paths, they often achieve this at the expense of prediction accuracy. The reliance on fixed anchors can sometimes limit their ability to precisely fit novel or nuanced trajectories. Consequently, there exists a trade-off between achieving sufficient mode diversity and maintaining high prediction accuracy in multi-agent motion forecasting. The development of methods that can simultaneously enhance both aspects remains an active area of research.

Furthermore, state-of-the-art methods on prominent benchmarks, such as Argoverse 2, have employed various decoding architectures. For instance, QCNeXt, a prior leading method, utilizes a proposal-refinement decoding architecture. This approach involves generating initial trajectory proposals which are then iteratively refined to improve accuracy. A recognized limitation of this refinement strategy is its reduced effectiveness when initial proposals deviate significantly from the true trajectories. In such cases, the refinement layers may struggle to accurately capture the necessary offsets, potentially compounding errors rather than correcting them. This points to a need for more robust decoding mechanisms that can generate accurate predictions even without highly precise initial estimates.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address the identified limitations in multi-agent motion prediction, specifically the trade-off between mode diversity and prediction accuracy. The authors aim to develop a method that can mitigate mode collapse while simultaneously improving the precision of trajectory predictions.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical observations:
1. **Addressing the Mode Diversity-Accuracy Trade-off:** Previous prediction-based methods suffer from mode collapse, leading to an insufficient representation of diverse future behaviors, which can result in inadequate safety assessments by autonomous systems. Anchor-based methods, while improving diversity, tend to compromise prediction accuracy. The central motivation is to bridge this performance gap by proposing a method that can enhance mode diversity without sacrificing prediction accuracy. This involves developing a mechanism that can generate multiple, plausible future trajectories while ensuring that these predictions are also highly accurate.
2. **Enhancing World Ranking and Top-1 Confidence:** Beyond generating diverse modes, it is crucial for an autonomous system to accurately assess the likelihood of these different modes and rank them appropriately. Improving "world ranking" refers to the model's ability to assign higher confidence to the most probable joint future state of all agents (a "world"), and "top-1 confidence" refers to the accuracy of the single most probable predicted trajectory. The proposed loss function is specifically designed to improve these aspects, which are vital for decision-making in autonomous driving.
3. **Improving Trajectory Decoding Robustness and Accuracy:** The paper identifies a limitation in previous proposal-refinement decoding architectures, such as that used in QCNeXt. When initial trajectory proposals are significantly inaccurate, subsequent refinement layers may struggle to correct large errors, potentially leading to persistent inaccuracies. This motivates the development of a decoding strategy that is less susceptible to initial errors and can progressively improve accuracy. The goal is to develop a decoder that can generate accurate trajectory coordinates directly rather than relying on error-prone offset refinements.
4. **Maximizing Information Utilization:** To further enhance prediction accuracy, the authors are motivated to design a decoder that effectively utilizes intermediate information generated throughout the prediction process. This means propagating encoded features, decoded outputs, and predicted trajectories from earlier stages or iterations to subsequent ones, allowing the model to continuously learn and refine its predictions based on accumulated context.

In summary, the core motivation is to enhance the safety and reliability of autonomous vehicles by providing a more comprehensive and accurate understanding of multi-agent future behaviors, addressing both the spectrum of possibilities and the precision of those possibilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, named Iterative Mode-World Weighted Regression (IMR), integrates several components: a scene representation and encoder, an iterative decoder, and a novel mode-world weighted regression loss function.

#### 3.1. Scene Representation and Encoder

The method begins by representing the historical trajectory of each agent at every timestep as an independent element. A polar coordinate system is constructed with each element as the origin, and agents within a predefined radius are selected as interaction objects. For each pair of interacting agents, 3D spatial relationships, including relative distance, azimuth, and orientation, are computed to form a dynamic attention weighted matrix.

A graph attention network-based encoder is employed to model these elements and their interactions. Each agent element is represented as a node in a graph structure, and the connections between nodes denote interaction weights. This design aims to capture implicit relationships and multi-source interactions among different agents in the scene.

#### 3.2. Iterative Decoder

The core of the trajectory generation mechanism is an iterative decoder, structured to progressively refine predictions over multiple steps. The decoder takes the agent and map features from the encoder as input.

- **Iterative Structure:** The decoder executes `N` iterations. In each iteration, the predicted trajectory from the previous iteration is processed by a temporal attention layer and then combined with the decoding features to serve as input for the subsequent iteration. This recurrent process allows for continuous optimization of the trajectory predictions based on accumulated information.
- **Segmented Decoding:** Each iteration comprises `S` decoding stages. These stages are designed to progressively decode trajectory segments. After predicting each segment, it is concatenated with previous segments to form a partial or complete trajectory. This segmented processing approach breaks down the complex task of predicting a full trajectory into smaller, more manageable sub-tasks. The knowledge of previously predicted segments dynamically influences the prediction of subsequent segments.
- **Direct Coordinate Output:** A notable design choice is that each decoding stage directly outputs the position coordinate values of the predicted trajectory, rather than offsets relative to the previous stage or initial proposals. This design aims to prevent error accumulation; an incorrect prediction in a previous decoding stage does not propagate and irrecoverably corrupt subsequent stages, as each stage predicts absolute coordinates.
- **Interaction Block:** As detailed in Figure 2, each interaction block within an iteration consists of `L` stacked interaction layers, producing a predicted trajectory through a prediction head. The feature propagation for iteration `i` involves:
 - `F_0` (initial input features).
 - `F_i = F'_{i-1} + LSTM(y_{i-1})` for `i > 0`, where `F_i` and `F'_{i-1}` are input/output features of iteration `i` and `i-1` respectively, and `y_{i-1}` is the predicted trajectory from the previous iteration.
 - Within each iteration `i`, decoding features `F'_j_i` are generated through hierarchical multivariate Graph Attention Networks (GAT) layers, utilizing agent (`A`) and map (`M`) encoding features.

#### 3.3. Mode-World Weighted Regression Loss

To address both mode diversity and prediction accuracy, a mode-world weighted regression loss is proposed. This loss function is designed to generate world-wise confidence scores, where each "world" comprises the joint trajectories of all agents and a corresponding confidence score.

- **Combined Loss Components:** The final regression loss (`L_reg`) is a weighted sum of a mode-wise regression loss (`L_mode_reg`) and a world-wise regression loss (`L_world_reg`), controlled by weights `ω_mode` and `ω_world` respectively:
 `L_reg = ω_mode * L_mode_reg + ω_world * L_world_reg`
- **Mode-wise Regression:** For mode regression, a winner-takes-all strategy is applied. The "winner" is defined as the best mode-wise trajectory for each individual agent. The selection of the optimal mode-wise trajectory (`m`) is determined by a weighted combination of the minimum average displacement error (ADE) and the minimum maximum displacement error (MDE), with weights `ω_ADE` and `ω_MDE`:
 `m = ω_ADE * ADE + ω_MDE * MDE`
 Laplace loss is used as the regression loss function and focal loss as the classification loss function.
- **World-wise Regression:** This component of the loss focuses on the overall consistency and accuracy of the joint predictions across all agents within a predicted "world." By weighting both mode-wise and world-wise losses, the model is encouraged to produce diverse yet coherent and accurate multi-agent predictions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the proposed IMR method was evaluated through experiments on the Argoverse 2 multi-agent and single-agent motion forecasting benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Implementation Details

The predicted trajectory length was set to 6 seconds. The model utilized a hidden size of 128, 6 iterations (`N=6`), and 3 decoding segments per iteration (`S=3`). Each interaction block contained 3 stacked interaction layers (`L=3`). The initial decoding feature was randomly initialized. The model predicted 6 worlds (`K=6`). Loss weights `ω_mode` and `ω_world` were set to 0.5 and 1, respectively. Training was conducted for 60 epochs using AdamW optimizer on the Argoverse 2 dataset, with a batch size of 32, an initial learning rate of 5x10^-4, and cosine annealing decay. An ensemble of 10 models, each trained with different random seeds, was used, and their 60 predicted worlds were integrated using the weighted k-means algorithm.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Results

- **Argoverse 2 Multi-Agent Motion Forecasting Benchmark:**
 The IMR method achieved competitive performance against other methods. Notably, it demonstrated a 0.06 improvement in `avgBrierMinFDE_6` compared to the previous state-of-the-art method, QCNeXt. This metric reflects the combined accuracy and confidence of the diverse predictions. The `avgBrierMinFDE_6` for IMR was 1.59, while QCNeXt achieved 1.65.
 For other metrics, IMR reported `avgMinFDE_6` of 1.08, `avgMinFDE_1` of 2.29, `actorMR_6` of 0.15, `avgMinADE_6` of 0.51, `avgMinADE_1` of 0.92, and `actorCR_6` of 0.01. These results indicate comparable or improved performance in terms of minimum final and average displacement errors, and comparable miss rates, especially in the top-1 scenario, compared to other benchmark methods.

- **Argoverse 2 Single-Agent Motion Forecasting Benchmark:**
 The proposed method also demonstrated competitive performance on the single-agent benchmark. IMR achieved an `MinFDE_6` of 1.08, `MinFDE_1` of 3.54, `MinADE_6` of 0.57, `MinADE_1` of 1.42, `MR_6` of 0.12, `MR_1` of 0.51, and `brier-MinFDE_6` of 1.63. These results position IMR as a first-tier performer, comparable to methods like LOF which reported `brier-MinFDE_6` of 1.63.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

A visual comparison of different regression losses (mode-wise, world-wise, and the proposed mode-world weighted loss) was provided for two cases on the Argoverse 2 validation set.

- **Mode-wise Loss:** Predictions generated using only mode-wise loss exhibited high diversity. However, these predicted trajectories often showed inconsistencies in length with the ground truth, leading to lower overall accuracy (Figs. 3a, 3d).
- **World-wise Loss:** Predictions based solely on world-wise regression loss demonstrated significant mode collapse. This was evidenced by the absence of predicted trajectories matching the ground truth, indicating a failure to capture diverse plausible future paths (Figs. 3b, 3e).
- **Mode-World Weighted Regression Loss (Proposed):** In contrast, the proposed mode-world weighted regression loss successfully mitigated mode collapse while simultaneously improving prediction accuracy. The visualized results showed predictions that were both diverse and closely aligned with the ground truth trajectories (Figs. 3c, 3f). This qualitatively supports the claim that the combined loss addresses the diversity-accuracy trade-off.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The IMR method makes contributions to the field of multi-agent motion prediction through its novel components and demonstrated performance. The proposed mode-world weighted regression loss represents a significant step towards resolving the long-standing trade-off between mode diversity and prediction accuracy in motion forecasting. By combining both mode-wise and world-wise considerations, the loss function encourages the model to generate a richer set of plausible future trajectories for multiple agents while simultaneously ensuring that these predictions are precise. This mitigation of mode collapse, coupled with improved world ranking accuracy and top-1 confidence, provides autonomous systems with a more comprehensive and reliable understanding of the environment. The `avgBrierMinFDE_6` improvement on the Argoverse 2 multi-agent benchmark specifically indicates enhanced performance in accurately predicting multiple future trajectories and assigning appropriate confidences.

Furthermore, the iterative decoder contributes to improved prediction accuracy by adopting a recurrent and segmented approach. By continuously refining trajectories across iterations and generating direct coordinate outputs rather than offsets, the decoder becomes more robust to initial prediction errors and effectively leverages intermediate information. This design addresses a limitation observed in previous proposal-refinement architectures, potentially leading to more stable and accurate trajectory generation. The consistent performance on both multi-agent and single-agent benchmarks suggests the generalizability of the proposed decoding and loss mechanisms.

The ability to accurately predict diverse multi-agent behaviors is critical for the safety and operational efficiency of automated vehicles. By providing a more nuanced understanding of potential future states, the IMR method can support more robust path planning, more effective obstacle avoidance strategies, and ultimately, safer decision-making in complex driving scenarios. The method's first-place ranking on the Argoverse 2 multi-agent motion forecasting benchmark validates its current state-of-the-art capabilities in this domain.

However, the paper also identifies a current limitation: the iterative decoding approach incurs high computational complexity. This characteristic may restrict its applicability in real-time autonomous driving systems, which demand predictions within tight latency constraints. Future research may need to focus on optimizing the computational efficiency of the iterative decoder without compromising its predictive performance to facilitate its practical deployment in real-world autonomous vehicles. Despite this, the conceptual and empirical advancements presented by IMR offer valuable insights and a strong foundation for further development in multi-agent trajectory prediction.
