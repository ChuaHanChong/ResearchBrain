# Efficient Reasoning via Reward Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from City University of Hong Kong and Huawei Research developed a Conciseness Reward Model (CRM) and an innovative Conciseness Reward Function (CRF) to enable Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) to generate more efficient and accurate solutions. This approach delivered an 8.1% improvement in Pass@1 accuracy and a 19.9% reduction in token length on the Qwen2.5-7B model, successfully mitigating common issues like length and training collapse.

## Problem

- Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) frequently exhibit "overthinking," producing verbose and redundant reasoning steps that increase computational costs and latency.
- Existing length penalty methods designed to encourage conciseness often lead to "length collapse," where reasoning quality suffers, or "training collapse," indicating a breakdown in the learning process.
- The absence of reliable and scalable methods to quantitatively evaluate reasoning path conciseness without extensive human annotation hindered the development of efficient LRMs.

## Method

- A ==Conciseness Reward Model (CRM)== was trained using an innovative LLM-powered data augmentation pipeline, scoring reasoning paths based on factors like repetition, irrelevance, and token length.
- A novel ==Conciseness Reward Function (CRF)== was designed, incorporating an explicit dependency where conciseness is rewarded only when the reasoning outcome is correct, preventing ==reward hacking==.
- The CRF integrated an ==annealing coefficient== for training stability and a difficulty coefficient to adapt conciseness expectations based on problem complexity, applied within a Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework.

## Results

- The method achieved an **8.1%** absolute improvement in Pass@1 accuracy and a **19.9%** reduction in average token length compared to original GRPO on the Qwen2.5-7B backbone.
- Consistent performance gains were observed across diverse LLM backbones, including Llama3.1-8B and Mistral-7B-v0.1, confirming the framework's generalizability.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that the explicit dependency within the CRF is essential for robust training, preventing a **9.8%** drop in outcome reward compared to a simple weighted sum approach.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An explicit dependency between outcome correctness and conciseness in the reward function is crucial for preventing "length collapse" and "training collapse," common pitfalls of simpler length penalties.
> - Leveraging powerful LLMs for data augmentation and conciseness scoring provides a scalable and robust alternative to human annotation for training effective reward models.
> - The proposed Conciseness Reward Function theoretically reduces the variance of the stochastic gradient estimator and improves convergence properties, leading to more stable and efficient reinforcement learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of Large Language Models (LLMs), Reinforcement Learning (RL), and efficient AI. It directly addresses a critical and emerging challenge in the deployment and scaling of sophisticated LLMs for complex reasoning tasks.

**Background:**
Recent advancements have shown that Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), as pioneered by works like DeepSeek-Math (Shao et al., 2024) and Tulu 3 (Lambert et al., 2024), significantly enhances the reasoning capabilities of LLMs. This has led to the development of powerful "Large Reasoning Models" (LRMs) such as DeepSeek-R1 (Guo et al., 2025) and OpenAI o1 (OpenAI, 2024). These models excel in tasks requiring multi-step logical deduction, particularly in domains like mathematics and coding.

**The Problem of "Overthinking":**
Despite their enhanced reasoning abilities, LRMs often suffer from a phenomenon termed "overthinking" (Chen et al., 2024; Luo et al., 2025; Sui et al., 2025). This manifests as the generation of verbose, redundant, or irrelevant reasoning steps, which, while sometimes correct, substantially increases computational costs (both for inference and training) and slows down response times. Efficient reasoning is therefore a critical area of research, aiming for both concise reasoning paths and strong reasoning capabilities.

**Limitations of Prior Solutions:**
Existing efforts to mitigate overthinking commonly involve incorporating length penalties into the reward function during RL training. Examples include methods like Kimi 1.5 (Team et al., 2025), cosine scale reward (Yeo et al., 2025), ALP (Xiang et al., 2025), and LC-R1 (Cheng et al., 2025). These approaches penalize longer responses in an attempt to encourage conciseness. However, the authors identify two critical shortcomings in these methods:
1. **Length Collapse:** The generated token length drastically declines, but the LLM's logical reasoning capacity is impaired, leading to "reward hacking" (Skalse et al., 2022) where models prioritize shortness over correctness or meaningful reasoning.
2. **Training Collapse:** Both the reward and the number of tokens decline drastically, indicating a breakdown in the training process.

**Contribution to the Landscape:**
This paper directly tackles these identified issues, proposing a novel framework that not only aims for efficient reasoning but also robustly avoids length and training collapse. By introducing a dedicated conciseness reward model and a carefully designed reward function with explicit dependencies, the authors push the boundaries of RL-based LLM optimization beyond simplistic length penalties. Their work contributes to the broader goal of developing more robust, efficient, and truly intelligent reasoning AI systems, moving closer to practical deployment in complex problem-solving scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed inefficiencies and critical failures in existing Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) when attempting to achieve conciseness. While RLVR has successfully augmented LLMs with powerful reasoning abilities, the "overthinking" phenomenon has emerged as a significant practical bottleneck.

**Core Motivation:**
The fundamental motivation is to develop a method that enables LLMs to perform **efficient reasoning**, meaning they can generate both **concise reasoning paths** and maintain **strong reasoning capabilities** simultaneously. The authors are particularly driven by the failures of current approaches that use simple length penalties, which often lead to detrimental outcomes like "length collapse" (where models prioritize brevity over correct reasoning, resembling reward hacking) and "training collapse" (where both accuracy and length degrade). Addressing these specific vulnerabilities is central to their work.

#### 2.1. Specific Objectives

Based on this motivation, the authors set out to achieve several specific objectives:

1. **Identify and Characterize Collapse Phenomena:** To formally recognize and describe the "length collapse" and "training collapse" issues prevalent in existing methods that attempt to mitigate overthinking in LRMs. This objective involves diagnosing why these failures occur with current length-penalty-based rewards.
2. **Develop a Conciseness Reward Model (CRM):** To construct a robust and reliable model capable of quantitatively evaluating the conciseness of a given reasoning path. This CRM should overcome the limitations of human annotation (cost, inconsistency, lack of domain expertise) by leveraging advanced LLMs for data augmentation and scoring.
3. **Design a Novel Conciseness Reward Function (CRF):** To formulate a new reward function that effectively integrates the conciseness score with the verifiable outcome reward. A critical design objective for this function is to establish an *explicit dependency* between outcome correctness and conciseness, thereby proactively mitigating reward hacking.
4. **Provide Theoretical Justification:** To theoretically demonstrate the advantages of the proposed CRF, specifically in terms of **variance reduction** in gradient estimation and **improved convergence properties** during reinforcement learning optimization.
5. **Empirically Validate Performance:** To conduct extensive experiments across multiple challenging mathematical benchmark datasets and with different LLM backbones. This objective aims to empirically prove the method's effectiveness in enhancing reasoning accuracy while simultaneously reducing token length, thus confirming its superior trade-off between efficiency and effectiveness compared to state-of-the-art baselines.
6. **Ensure Generalizability and Reproducibility:** To show that the proposed framework is compatible with various LLM architectures and RL algorithms, and to make the implementation code and datasets publicly available to foster reproducibility and further research.

By achieving these objectives, the authors aim to offer a more stable, effective, and efficient solution for training advanced reasoning models, pushing the frontier of practical and reliable AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework for efficient reasoning is structured into a two-stage pipeline: first, training a Conciseness Reward Model (CRM), and second, integrating this CRM into the Reinforcement Learning (RL) process via a novel Conciseness Reward Function (CRF).

#### 3.1. Overview of the Framework

The framework takes Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) as an example, a representative RLVR algorithm. The core idea is to enhance the reward signal with a conciseness component derived from a specialized reward model, applied judiciously during policy updates.

#### 3.2. Conciseness Reward Model (CRM)

Acknowledging the limitations of human preference data (resource-intensive, inconsistent, lack of expertise), the authors propose an innovative pipeline for training a CRM:
1. **Data Selection:** The **DeepMath-103K** dataset (He et al., 2025) is chosen. This dataset is noted for its high difficulty problems, diverse mathematical topics, distinct reasoning paths (from DeepSeek-R1), and decontamination, making it suitable for training a sophisticated reward model.
2. **Data Augmentation and Labeling:**
 - **Generation of Solution Variants:** For each mathematical question and its original DeepSeek-R1 solution, two distinct versions of solutions are generated by **Qwen2.5-Math-72B-Instruct**: one designed to be more concise and another to be more redundant. This step creates a diverse set of reasoning paths.
 - **Conciseness Scoring:** The **Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct** model is then employed to *score* the conciseness of these reasoning paths. Scores range from 0.1 to 1, considering factors like avoidance of repetition, elimination of irrelevant steps, and minimization of token length, all while preserving solution quality. A detailed prompt template is provided in Appendix A, indicating the structured approach to scoring.
3. **Data Filtering for Discriminability:** To ensure the quality of the training data, only highly redundant solutions with lower conciseness scores and highly concise solutions with higher scores are retained. This filtering step aims to maximize the discriminative power of the training examples.
4. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** A smaller model, **Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct**, is used as the backbone for the CRM. It is fine-tuned via SFT on the curated dataset of 65,878 samples to learn to predict conciseness scores.

#### 3.3. Conciseness Reward Function (CRF)

After the CRM is trained, it can provide a conciseness score ($c_i$) for any generated reasoning path $o_i$. The key innovation lies in how this score is integrated into the RL objective to avoid the "collapse" issues.
1. **Explicit Dependency:** Unlike previous methods that simply add a length penalty, the CRF introduces an *explicit dependency*: the conciseness score is applied only if the answer ($R_{o_i}$) is correct. If the answer is incorrect ($R_{o_i}=0$), the conciseness score does not contribute to the reward. This design is crucial for mitigating reward hacking, as the model cannot gain high rewards by simply generating short, incorrect answers. The reward formulation is: $\hat{R}_i = R_{o_i} \cdot [1 + \alpha \cdot c_i \cdot (s + d_q)]$.
2. **Annealing Coefficient ($s$):** To stabilize training, an annealing coefficient $s = \exp(-\frac{\text{step}}{T})$ is introduced. This coefficient decays with increasing training steps, gradually reducing the influence of the conciseness component over time. This helps the model initially focus on conciseness while ensuring overall performance is not compromised in later stages.
3. **Difficulty Coefficient ($d_q$):** Recognizing that harder questions may naturally require longer reasoning paths, a difficulty coefficient $d_q = \exp(\frac{|\{i|R_{o_i}=1, i=1 \cdots G\}|}{G})$ is proposed. This coefficient is based on the accuracy of the group answers for a given question (lower group accuracy implies higher difficulty). It ranges from 1 to $e$, meaning harder questions (lower group accuracy) result in a smaller conciseness penalty (larger $d_q$), allowing for more detailed explanations when needed.
4. **Integration with GRPO:** The $\hat{R}_i$ replaces the binary outcome reward in GRPO's advantage calculation ($\hat{A}_{i,t}$). The authors also state its adaptability to other state-of-the-art RL algorithms like DAPO.

#### 3.4. Theoretical Justification

The paper provides two propositions to theoretically underpin the benefits of the CRF's design:
- **Proposition 1 (Variance Reduction):** If the conciseness reward ($c_i$) is positively correlated with the outcome reward ($R_{o_i}$), the modified GRPO objective (with CRF) exhibits reduced variance of its stochastic gradient estimator compared to the original objective. This implies more stable and reliable policy updates.
- **Proposition 2 (Improved Convergence Constants):** Building on variance reduction, the modified objective function is shown to achieve a convergence rate of $O(1/\sqrt{T})$ with improved constants, suggesting faster and more efficient learning.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Baselines:** Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), ARM (Adaptive Reasoning Model), GRPO (binary reward), GRPO + Cosine scale reward (Cos), GRPO + Kimi 1.5 reward (Kimi).
- **LLM Backbones:** Qwen2.5-7B, Llama3.1-8B, and Mistral-7B-v0.1. For Llama and Mistral, an "R1-like training" (SFT followed by RL post-training) is used to address a "cold-start" problem observed with direct RL training.
- **Datasets:** DeepMath-103K for CRM and RL training; five mathematical benchmarks (MATH-500, AIME2025, OlympiadBench, AMC-23, GPQA-Diamond) for evaluation.
- **Metrics:** Pass@1 accuracy (for effectiveness) and average token length (#Tok) (for efficiency).

This comprehensive methodology demonstrates a thoughtful and rigorous approach to tackle a complex problem in LLM efficiency, combining novel model training with theoretical grounding and extensive empirical validation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across various LLM backbones and mathematical benchmarks provide strong evidence for the effectiveness and efficiency of the proposed framework.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance (RQ1)

- **Baseline GRPO vs. SFT:** On Qwen2.5-7B, the original GRPO significantly improves accuracy over SFT (+14.2% Pass@1) but at the cost of a substantial increase in token length (+43.9%). This highlights the "overthinking" issue.
- **Length Penalty Baselines (Cos, Kimi):** While Cosine reward and Kimi 1.5 reward successfully reduce token length (by 37.8% and 43.2% respectively compared to GRPO), this comes at a severe cost to accuracy (Pass@1 decreases by 14.2% and 8.7%). This directly illustrates the "length collapse" and "training collapse" phenomena identified by the authors.
- **ARM:** Even in "short CoT mode," ARM shows an accuracy improvement (+5.9%) but with a massive increase in token length (+75.1%), indicating it struggles with true conciseness.
- **Proposed CRF:** The CRF framework achieves a remarkable balance, demonstrating an **8.1% accuracy improvement** (Pass@1) and a **19.9% reduction in response token length** compared to the original GRPO on Qwen2.5-7B. This signifies a superior trade-off between effectiveness and efficiency, successfully addressing the overthinking problem without sacrificing reasoning quality.

#### 4.2. Compatibility with Different LLM Backbones (RQ2)

The method's generalizability is confirmed on Llama3.1-8B and Mistral-7B-v0.1, where an "R1-like training" (SFT followed by RL post-training) was used due to a "cold-start" problem observed with direct RL.
- **Llama3.1-8B:** CRF yields a **3.1% increase in accuracy** and a **39.0% decrease in token length** compared to the original GRPO.
- **Mistral-7B-v0.1:** CRF achieves a **9.1% increase in accuracy** and a **17.7% decrease in token length** compared to the original GRPO.
These results validate that the proposed method is effective and compatible with diverse LLM architectures, reinforcing its robustness.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study (RQ3)

The ablation study on Qwen2.5-7B validates the importance of each component in the CRF:
- **"w/o Dep" (without explicit dependency):** Replacing the explicit dependency with a simple weighted sum (common in prior works) causes a significant **9.8% drop in outcome reward**. This strongly supports the authors' claim that explicit dependency is critical for mitigating reward hacking and achieving stable training.
- **Other components:** Removing the annealing coefficient ("w/o Ann"), difficulty coefficient ("w/o Dif"), or both ("w/o Ann&Dif") consistently leads to performance decreases (3.9%, 4.9%, and 8.3% respectively). This confirms the effectiveness of the annealing strategy and the difficulty-related design in stabilizing training and adapting response length.

#### 4.4. Hyper-parameter Analysis (RQ4)

Analysis of the hyper-parameter $\alpha$ (weight for the conciseness score) shows that an optimal value exists. For Qwen2.5-7B, $\alpha=1$ achieves the best accuracy with relatively short token length. Similar optimal values were found for Llama and Mistral.

#### 4.5. Case Study (RQ5)

A qualitative case study involving a simple mathematical inequality demonstrates the practical advantages of CRF:
- **GRPO:** Generates verbose and redundant reasoning steps.
- **GRPO+Cos:** Exhibits "reward hacking," providing only the final answer without any reasoning, resembling rote memorization.
- **GRPO+Kimi:** Generates a shorter path than GRPO but is still less concise than CRF.
- **GRPO+CRF:** Produces a concise, logically sound reasoning path that correctly solves the problem efficiently. This case study visually confirms CRF's ability to achieve true efficient reasoning while avoiding the pitfalls of other methods.

In summary, the main findings robustly demonstrate that the proposed CRM and CRF effectively address the overthinking problem in LRMs, achieving significant improvements in both reasoning accuracy and token efficiency, while also mitigating critical issues like length collapse and training collapse that plague existing approaches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Efficient Reasoning via Reward Model" holds significant implications for the development and deployment of Large Language Models, particularly those tasked with complex reasoning. Its contributions span theoretical advancements, practical improvements, and broad applicability.

#### 5.1. Novel Identification and Solution to Critical Problems

The paper's primary significance lies in being the *first to formally identify and effectively address the "length collapse" and "training collapse" issues*. These phenomena represent critical vulnerabilities in existing RL-based methods for mitigating LLM overthinking. By pinpointing these specific failure modes and developing a targeted solution, the authors advance the state-of-the-art in robust RL for LLMs. The innovative CRM training pipeline, which leverages powerful LLMs for data augmentation and conciseness scoring, offers a scalable way to generate high-quality preference data without relying solely on expensive and inconsistent human annotation.

#### 5.2. Strong Theoretical Foundation

The theoretical justifications provided in Propositions 1 and 2 are a key strength. Proving **variance reduction** and **improved convergence constants** for the proposed Conciseness Reward Function (CRF) offers a deeper understanding of *why* the method is more stable and efficient. This theoretical backing elevates the work beyond an empirical hack, providing a solid scientific basis for its superior performance and offering insights for future research in RL reward design. The explicit dependency between outcome reward and conciseness score is theoretically shown to be crucial for these benefits.

#### 5.3. Practical Impact on Efficiency and Performance

- **Cost Reduction and Speed:** The demonstrated **19.9% reduction in response token length** (on Qwen2.5-7B) translates directly into significant computational cost savings and faster inference times for LLMs. This is crucial for making advanced reasoning capabilities more economically viable and responsive in real-world applications.
- **Enhanced Reasoning Quality:** Crucially, this efficiency gain does not come at the expense of accuracy. Instead, the method achieves an **8.1% improvement in accuracy** (Pass@1 on Qwen2.5-7B). This indicates that by encouraging concise and relevant reasoning, the model's overall problem-solving ability is actually strengthened, rather than just shortening arbitrary output.
- **Robustness against Reward Hacking:** The explicit dependency in CRF effectively mitigates reward hacking, a pervasive problem in RL where agents exploit reward functions in unintended ways. By ensuring conciseness is only rewarded when the answer is correct, the model is incentivized to produce logically sound and accurate, yet brief, solutions.

#### 5.4. Generalizability and Adaptability

The successful application of the framework across different LLM backbones (Qwen, Llama, Mistral) demonstrates its broad generalizability. This suggests that the principles of the CRM and CRF are not tied to a specific model architecture but can be broadly applied to enhance various LRMs. Furthermore, its stated adaptability to other state-of-the-art RL algorithms (like DAPO) and different human-specified objectives implies a wider potential for impact beyond mathematical reasoning.

#### 5.5. Fostering Open Science and Future Research

The commitment to making the implementation code and datasets publicly available is a significant contribution to the open-source community. This will enable other researchers to reproduce the results, build upon the framework, and explore its applications in new domains, accelerating progress in efficient and robust LLM development.

In conclusion, this research provides a robust and theoretically sound solution to a pressing challenge in the field of LLMs. By enabling more efficient and accurate reasoning, it paves the way for more sophisticated, cost-effective, and reliable AI systems that can tackle complex problems in various scientific, engineering, and everyday domains. The implications are profound for both the fundamental understanding of LLM capabilities and their practical deployment at scale.
