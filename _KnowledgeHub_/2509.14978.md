# PA-MPPI: Perception-Aware Model Predictive Path Integral Control for Quadrotor Navigation in Unknown Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Zurich developed Perception-Aware Model Predictive Path Integral (PA-MPPI) control for quadrotors, enabling autonomous navigation in unknown environments without external reference trajectories. This method integrates a novel perception-aware cost function that guides exploration into unknown regions when the goal is occluded, achieving 100% success rates in complex simulated and hardware-in-the-loop tasks while significantly reducing navigation time and energy consumption compared to existing approaches.

## Problem

- Autonomous quadrotor navigation in unknown, cluttered environments presents challenges in path planning within non-convex free space.
- Existing Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) methods primarily focus on local trajectory tracking and lack global map awareness or active exploration capabilities, limiting their use when global reference paths are unavailable or invalid.
- Traditional hierarchical planning and gradient-based methods struggle with non-convexity, dynamic feasibility, and online environment mapping in real-time.

## Method

- The **PA-==MPPI==** controller tightly integrates a real-time depth sensing and 3D occupancy grid mapping module (ROG-Map) with the MPPI framework.
- A novel, adaptive cost function is introduced, which dynamically switches between an 'Exploration Phase' (when the goal is occluded) and an 'Approaching Phase' (when the goal is in line of sight).
- The 'Exploration Phase' cost includes a unique `perception` term that utilizes ray tracing to reward trajectories that explore unknown regions along the path to the goal and penalize paths leading to known obstacles, actively biasing the quadrotor towards frontiers.

## Results

- PA-MPPI achieved **100%** success rates in navigating complex synthetic environments (e.g., C-shaped walls, holes) in both simulation and hardware-in-the-loop experiments.
- The system consistently outperformed a state-of-the-art baseline (SUPER) by achieving significantly shorter navigation times, higher average velocities, and notably lower energy consumption (e.g., **249.5** J vs. **286.8** J in a 3.0m C-wall task).
- PA-MPPI successfully served as a robust action policy for a navigation foundation model (NoMaD), correcting infeasible high-level goal proposals by actively exploring and bypassing unexpected obstacles in cluttered indoor scenes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly incorporating perception objectives and map information into the MPPI cost function allows the controller to transition from local tracking to proactive exploration and reference-free navigation.
> - MPPI's sampling-based optimization inherently handles non-convex obstacle fields, generating dynamically feasible and energy-efficient trajectories through full quadrotor dynamics.
> - A tightly integrated control stack running at 50 Hz, combining real-time depth sensing, 3D mapping, and perception-aware MPPI, enables robust and autonomous flight in previously unmapped environments.
