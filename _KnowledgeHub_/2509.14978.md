# PA-MPPI: Perception-Aware Model Predictive Path Integral Control for Quadrotor Navigation in Unknown Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Zurich developed Perception-Aware Model Predictive Path Integral (PA-MPPI) control for quadrotors, enabling autonomous navigation in unknown environments without external reference trajectories. This method integrates a novel perception-aware cost function that guides exploration into unknown regions when the goal is occluded, achieving 100% success rates in complex simulated and hardware-in-the-loop tasks while significantly reducing navigation time and energy consumption compared to existing approaches.

## Problem

- Autonomous quadrotor navigation in unknown, cluttered environments presents challenges in path planning within non-convex free space.
- Existing Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) methods primarily focus on local trajectory tracking and lack global map awareness or active exploration capabilities, limiting their use when global reference paths are unavailable or invalid.
- Traditional hierarchical planning and gradient-based methods struggle with non-convexity, dynamic feasibility, and online environment mapping in real-time.

## Method

- The **PA-==MPPI==** controller tightly integrates a real-time depth sensing and 3D occupancy grid mapping module (ROG-Map) with the MPPI framework.
- A novel, adaptive cost function is introduced, which dynamically switches between an 'Exploration Phase' (when the goal is occluded) and an 'Approaching Phase' (when the goal is in line of sight).
- The 'Exploration Phase' cost includes a unique `perception` term that utilizes ray tracing to reward trajectories that explore unknown regions along the path to the goal and penalize paths leading to known obstacles, actively biasing the quadrotor towards frontiers.

## Results

- PA-MPPI achieved **100%** success rates in navigating complex synthetic environments (e.g., C-shaped walls, holes) in both simulation and hardware-in-the-loop experiments.
- The system consistently outperformed a state-of-the-art baseline (SUPER) by achieving significantly shorter navigation times, higher average velocities, and notably lower energy consumption (e.g., **249.5** J vs. **286.8** J in a 3.0m C-wall task).
- PA-MPPI successfully served as a robust action policy for a navigation foundation model (NoMaD), correcting infeasible high-level goal proposals by actively exploring and bypassing unexpected obstacles in cluttered indoor scenes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly incorporating perception objectives and map information into the MPPI cost function allows the controller to transition from local tracking to proactive exploration and reference-free navigation.
> - MPPI's sampling-based optimization inherently handles non-convex obstacle fields, generating dynamically feasible and energy-efficient trajectories through full quadrotor dynamics.
> - A tightly integrated control stack running at 50 Hz, combining real-time depth sensing, 3D mapping, and perception-aware MPPI, enables robust and autonomous flight in previously unmapped environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous navigation for quadrotors in unknown environments is a critical capability for various practical applications, including search-and-rescue, infrastructure inspection, and exploration. This domain presents significant challenges related to path planning in non-convex free space, adherence to quadrotor-specific dynamics and objectives, and the need for online environment mapping and exploration.

Traditional approaches often employ a hierarchical planner-controller architecture, where a high-level planner generates trajectories that a lower-level controller tracks. While this decouples complex planning from fast control, it can lead to suboptimal or dynamically infeasible plans due to approximations of quadrotor models and costs at the planning stage. Gradient-based optimization methods offer simultaneous planning and control but struggle with the non-convexity and non-smoothness introduced by complex obstacles and raw depth observations. Learning-based policies, while demonstrating high-speed flight, typically lack explicit safety constraint handling and adaptability to novel environments.

Model Predictive Control (MPC) methods, particularly Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control, have emerged as a promising alternative. MPPI utilizes sampling-based optimization, allowing it to navigate non-convex and non-smooth environments while directly optimizing control inputs based on full quadrotor dynamics and specific costs (e.g., energy consumption). However, existing MPPI implementations have primarily focused on local trajectory tracking, operating within a small neighborhood of a predefined reference trajectory. They generally lack the capability for global map awareness, exploration of unknown regions, and planning alternative paths when faced with large, unforeseen obstacles. This limitation restricts their utility in truly unknown environments where a global reference path is unavailable or frequently becomes invalid.

This work addresses the aforementioned limitations of standard MPPI by integrating "perception awareness." Previous research on perception-aware navigation has focused on objectives like acquiring task-relevant information, maximizing visibility of features, or reducing localization uncertainty. High-level planners have also explored unobserved space as potentially hazardous, guiding exploration towards frontiers. However, these often involve separate planning modules. This paper introduces a novel perception-aware MPPI formulation that directly incorporates exploration objectives into the controller's cost function, thereby extending MPPI's capabilities beyond local tracking to enable reference-free navigation and active exploration in unknown, cluttered environments. It aims to bridge the gap between reactive, local control and proactive, explorative path generation within a unified, real-time control framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable quadrotors to autonomously navigate to a goal in unknown environments without relying on external reference trajectories, a capability crucial for practical missions and the deployment of advanced navigation foundation models. To achieve this, the authors identify and aim to address three key challenges:

1. **Path Planning in Non-Convex Free Space:** Cluttered environments often present non-convex obstacle fields, making gradient-based optimization challenging for trajectory generation. The work seeks to leverage MPPI's sampling-based nature to inherently handle such complexities.
2. **Satisfying Quadrotor-Specific Dynamics and Objectives:** Planned trajectories must be dynamically feasible and optimize for quadrotor-specific costs, such as energy consumption or control effort. The objective is to directly incorporate full quadrotor dynamics into the optimization to generate realistic and efficient trajectories.
3. **Exploring Unknown Regions and Expanding the Map:** In unknown environments, the quadrotor must build a map online using onboard perception. A core motivation is to integrate exploration into the navigation strategy to actively map unknown areas that are relevant for finding a path to the goal, rather than merely avoiding known obstacles.

The motivation behind introducing "Perception-Aware MPPI" (PA-MPPI) stems from the limitations of existing MPPI methods. Standard MPPI is effective for tracking control and local optimization but lacks the ability to explore or find alternative paths when its immediate path to a goal is blocked by large, unknown obstacles. The authors hypothesize that by making MPPI "perception-aware"—meaning adapting trajectory planning based on perceptual objectives—it can overcome this limitation. Specifically, when the direct path to the goal is occluded, the system needs to bias trajectories towards perceiving unknown regions. This active exploration would expand the mapped traversable space, increasing the likelihood of discovering and exploiting alternative routes to the target.

Furthermore, a practical motivation is the increasing use of navigation foundation models, which often output high-level goal poses or waypoints. These outputs might not be directly reachable or dynamically feasible for a quadrotor in an unknown environment due to scale ambiguity or unforeseen obstacles. An adaptive, perception-aware control policy like PA-MPPI could serve as a robust action policy to bridge the gap between these high-level commands and safe, real-world execution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed PA-MPPI controller integrates a novel perception and mapping module with the MPPI framework to facilitate autonomous navigation in unknown environments without external reference trajectories.

**Control Stack Overview:** The system operates as a tightly integrated control stack. A perception and mapping module continuously builds a 3D occupancy grid from onboard depth sensor observations. This occupancy grid serves as input to the PA-MPPI controller, which then optimizes and outputs control sequences (collective thrust and body angular velocities) that are directly executed by the quadrotor. The entire system is designed to run at 50 Hz for real-time operation.

**Quadrotor Dynamics:** The quadrotor's state is defined by its position, orientation (quaternion), linear velocity (in world frame), and angular velocity (in body frame). The dynamics model accounts for collective thrust and body torque, along with gravity and inertial forces. A low-level flight controller tracks zero-order hold control inputs from PA-MPPI, with a motor thrust clipping mechanism to ensure feasible total thrust and body rates. Forward Euler integration is used for simulating the dynamics within the MPPI rollout.

**MPPI Formulation:** PA-MPPI operates within the standard MPPI framework:
1. **Sampling:** At each timestep, $N$ parallel trajectories of $H$ steps are generated by adding multivariate Gaussian noise to a nominal control input sequence.
2. **Rollout:** Each perturbed control sequence is rolled out from the current quadrotor state using the defined quadrotor dynamics model.
3. **Cost Evaluation:** Each sampled trajectory is evaluated by summing a per-step stage cost $\ell$ and a terminal value function $\bar{V}$ at the end of the horizon.
4. **Weighted Averaging:** The optimal control sequence is calculated as an exponentially weighted average of all sampled control sequences, where the weights are inversely proportional to the trajectory costs (lower cost implies higher weight). A temperature parameter $\lambda$ influences the weighting distribution.
5. **Execution and Shifting:** The first action of the averaged sequence is executed. To allow for longer prediction horizons with the same number of simulation steps, the prediction time step $\Delta t_{pred}$ can be larger than the control step $\Delta t_{ctrl}$. The remaining actions in the sequence are then linearly interpolated, shifted by $\Delta t_{ctrl}$, and down-sampled to form the nominal control input for the next iteration. A simple terminal value function penalizes high velocities and angular rates to encourage hovering.

**Perception & Environment Mapping:**
- A depth sensor continuously captures depth images.
- Point clouds are reprojected from these depth images into the world frame using the camera pose.
- These point clouds are then inserted into a **ROG-Map** (Robocentric Occupancy Grid Map). This system efficiently aggregates observations into a 3D occupancy grid, where each voxel can be in one of three states: occupied (1), free (0), or unknown (-1).
- The mapping pipeline processes depth images and updates a 5x5x2m grid with 0.1m voxel resolution at 50 Hz.

**Optimization Cost Definition:** The core innovation lies in the definition of the stage cost $\ell$, which adapts based on whether the goal is within the quadrotor's direct line of sight.

**Phase 1: Goal Occluded (Exploration Phase)**
The cost function is $\ell = \ell_{goal} + \ell_{goal,H-1} + \ell_{act} + \ell_{collision} + \ell_{perception}$.
- $\ell_{goal}$: Encourages movement closer to the goal compared to the start of the horizon, with a lower weight ($c_{goal} = 0.125$).
- $\ell_{goal,H-1}$: A higher weight for the goal distance at the last step of the horizon ($c_{goal,H-1} = 10$) to prevent greedy behavior and encourage detours.
- $\ell_{act}$: Penalizes the magnitude and change of control inputs (collective thrust and angular velocities) to ensure smooth, dynamically feasible maneuvers.
- $\ell_{collision}$: A large binary penalty ($c_{collision}$) if the quadrotor's position lies in an occupied or unknown voxel. This enforces avoidance of known obstacles and prevents entering unknown regions for safety.
- $\ell_{perception}$: This novel cost is active only when the goal is occluded and consists of two main components:
 - **Goal Alignment:** $c_{PoI} \cdot (1 - \langle \hat{x}_WB, \hat{d}_{goal} \rangle)^2$. This term encourages aligning the quadrotor's forward (x) axis with the direction to the goal, maximizing visibility of the Point of Interest (PoI). It becomes inactive when the quadrotor is sufficiently close to the goal (distance below $c_{thresh} = 0.5$m).
 - **Ray Tracing for Exploration:** This component utilizes a ray tracing algorithm (3D Digital Differential Analyzer - DDA) from the quadrotor's current position to the goal. It identifies the first voxel the ray encounters that is not free.
 - If this voxel is **occupied** ($\text{G}(\text{r}(t^*)) = 1$), a positive cost $c_{occupied}$ is assigned, penalizing trajectories that lead to a blocked path.
 - If this voxel is **unknown** ($\text{G}(\text{r}(t^*)) = -1$), a negative cost $c_{unknown}$ is assigned, actively rewarding trajectories that explore unknown regions along the path to the goal, thus guiding the quadrotor towards frontiers.
 - Ray tracing is performed only at the last step of the open-loop trajectory ($k = H-1$) due to computational cost.

**Phase 2: Goal in Direct Line of Sight (Approaching Phase)**
The cost function is $\ell = \ell_{goal} + \ell_{act} + \ell_{collision} + \ell_{progress} + \ell_{vel}$.
- $\ell_{goal}$: Higher weight for goal distance ($c_{goal} = 5.0$) to encourage rapid approach.
- $\ell_{progress}$: Rewards fast movement when a clear path to the goal is available.
- $\ell_{vel}$: Penalizes high velocity, encouraging the quadrotor to slow down as it approaches the goal, ensuring a controlled arrival and hovering.
- $\ell_{perception}$ is inactive in this phase, as exploration is no longer needed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates PA-MPPI's performance through simulated and hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) experiments using an Agilicious quadrotor platform. The HIL setup leverages a motion capture system for ground truth state and the Flightmare simulator for real-time depth image rendering.

**A. Synthetic Scene Experiment:**
Three distinct synthetic scenes (C-shaped wall, hole in a wall, and four walls) were designed to test navigation capabilities around obstacles and through narrow passages, with varying difficulty levels.

- **Comparison with EGO-Planner:** An initial validation demonstrated that a gradient-based planner like EGO-Planner struggled and failed to converge in harder settings, highlighting the challenge of non-convex environments for such methods.
- **Comparison with SUPER (State-of-the-Art Baseline):** PA-MPPI was benchmarked against SUPER, a safety-assured MAV navigation planner for unknown environments, which replans at 10 Hz with a geometric controller for tracking.
 - **Success Rate:** Both PA-MPPI and SUPER achieved a 100% success rate across all difficulty levels in the C-wall, hole, and 4-wall scenarios in simulation. Real-world HIL experiments for PA-MPPI also maintained 100% success.
 - **Performance Metrics (Simulation & HIL):**
 - **Total Time:** PA-MPPI consistently performed better, achieving significantly shorter navigation times compared to SUPER. For example, in the C-wall (3.0m), PA-MPPI completed tasks in 5.14s (simulated) vs. SUPER's 6.00s. Similar trends were observed for the hole (e.g., 3.23s vs. 4.36s for 0.5m hole) and 4-wall scenarios (e.g., 5.78s vs. 7.57s for 1.5m walls).
 - **Average Velocity:** Correspondingly, PA-MPPI exhibited higher average velocities, indicating more efficient and faster trajectories.
 - **Trajectory Distance:** Trajectory distances for PA-MPPI were generally on par with, or slightly better than, SUPER. For instance, in the C-wall (3.0m), PA-MPPI had 5.87m vs. SUPER's 6.48m.
 - **Energy Consumption (Simulation):** PA-MPPI demonstrated significantly lower energy consumption across all tasks. For the C-wall (3.0m), PA-MPPI consumed 249.5 J compared to SUPER's 286.8 J. This is attributed to PA-MPPI's inherent sampling of dynamically feasible trajectories and direct optimization for control effort, leading to smoother and less aggressive maneuvers than SUPER's hierarchically planned paths that often required more rotational effort to track.
 - **Qualitative Observations:** Visualizations showed PA-MPPI's perception cost guiding the quadrotor to positions that enable navigation around obstacles and map the environment towards the goal. SUPER's approach, while safe, sometimes resulted in longer or dynamically less efficient trajectories due to its two-stage planning and optimization process.

**B. Robustness Study:**
PA-MPPI's robustness was tested in simulations under external wind disturbances (1.0 m/s, 2.0 m/s, 3.0 m/s) in the most challenging settings of the synthetic scenes.
- **Results:** PA-MPPI maintained a high success rate at low wind speeds (e.g., 90-100% at 1.0 m/s). As wind speed increased (2.0 m/s and 3.0 m/s), the success rate decreased (e.g., down to 60-70% at 2.0 m/s and 40-50% at 3.0 m/s). Failures were typically due to collisions or being blown into unknown areas, indicating limitations in compensating for strong external disturbances with the current control authority.

**C. PA-MPPI Combined with Navigation Foundation Model:**
PA-MPPI was evaluated as an action policy for NoMaD, a vision-language-action (VLA) navigation foundation model, in two indoor scenes from the Habitat Matterport dataset.
- **Scene 1 (Missed Door):** NoMaD's proposed trajectory attempted to enter a room but missed the door. PA-MPPI successfully explored, mapped the unknown regions, navigated through the door, and reached the goal.
- **Scene 2 (Obstacle Collision):** NoMaD's trajectory started near a ping-pong table and attempted to pass directly through it. PA-MPPI successfully detected and avoided the obstacle, demonstrating its ability to handle infeasible goal proposals from high-level models in cluttered, unknown environments.
These experiments showcased PA-MPPI's real-world applicability for robust, reference-free navigation, making it suitable for deployment with high-level navigation cues that may not be dynamically or geometrically feasible.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in autonomous quadrotor navigation by extending Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control to unknown and cluttered environments without requiring external reference trajectories. The introduction of a novel perception-aware cost function is central to this development.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**

- **Enhanced MPPI Capabilities:** The PA-MPPI framework extends the utility of MPPI beyond local tracking. By incorporating map information and perception objectives directly into the cost function, it enables MPPI to proactively explore unknown areas and plan alternative paths around large obstacles, a capability previously lacking in standard MPPI implementations.
- **Integrated Sensing, Mapping, and Control:** The tightly integrated framework, combining real-time depth sensing, 3D occupancy grid mapping (ROG-Map), and high-performance MPPI control, demonstrates a cohesive solution for autonomous navigation. Running at 50 Hz, it achieves real-time operability on physical hardware.
- **Performance Parity with State-of-the-Art:** Through rigorous simulated and hardware-in-the-loop experiments, PA-MPPI demonstrated navigation performance comparable to, and often exceeding, SUPER, a current state-of-the-art planner for safety-assured MAV navigation. Notably, PA-MPPI achieved faster navigation times, higher average velocities, and significantly lower energy consumption, attributed to its inherent dynamic feasibility and direct optimization over quadrotor dynamics.
- **Robustness against Unforeseen Obstacles and Infeasible Goals:** The perception cost's ability to guide exploration towards frontiers and adapt to newly mapped obstacles enhances the quadrotor's robustness. This is particularly impactful for real-world scenarios where environments are complex and unpredictable.
- **Enabling Foundation Models in Robotics:** The demonstration of PA-MPPI as a robust action policy for navigation foundation models like NoMaD addresses a critical challenge in deploying such AI models in real physical environments. It allows the translation of high-level, potentially abstract or infeasible, goal poses into safe and dynamically feasible quadrotor trajectories, bridging the gap between perception-language systems and physical action.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Search and Rescue:** PA-MPPI could enhance the autonomy and efficiency of quadrotors in hazardous and unknown environments (e.g., collapsed buildings, disaster zones), allowing them to explore and map faster and more reliably.
- **Infrastructure Inspection:** It could facilitate autonomous inspection of complex structures (e.g., bridges, industrial facilities) where GPS is unavailable, and environments are often cluttered and poorly mapped.
- **Exploration:** The perception-aware exploration strategy directly contributes to more effective autonomous exploration missions in unknown or confined spaces.
- **General Robotics Navigation:** The principles of perception-aware costs integrated into sampling-based MPC could be adapted to other robotic platforms and navigation tasks, contributing to more generalized autonomous capabilities.
- **Human-Robot Interaction:** By improving the robustness and safety of autonomous navigation, PA-MPPI could lead to more trustworthy and reliable robotic systems in collaboration with humans.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge certain limitations and outline future research directions. The limited field-of-view (FoV) of depth sensors can necessitate initial maneuvers for observation, suggesting potential improvements with LIDAR integration. The current work considers navigation within fixed 3D boundaries, and extending to larger-scale navigation would require leveraging features like the ROG-Map's local map sliding. The planning horizon of PA-MPPI is constrained by computational resources, which limits the complexity of navigable scenes. Future work aims to investigate the use of appropriate terminal value functions to mitigate this limitation and conduct a deeper study into the path-planning limits and strategies for overcoming them.
