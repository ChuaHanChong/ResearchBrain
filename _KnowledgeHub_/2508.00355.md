# TOP: Time Optimization Policy for Stable and Accurate Standing Manipulation with Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Zhejiang University developed the Time Optimization Policy (TOP) framework for humanoid robots, enabling stable and accurate standing manipulation by adaptively adjusting the speed of upper-body motions. The system achieved a 95.30% success rate on unseen motion datasets and demonstrated improved precision and stability in real-world tasks.

## Problem

- Existing humanoid control methods (whole-body, decoupled) face a trade-off between stability, precision, and time efficiency during standing manipulation.
- Rapid upper-body motions induce momentum changes that can destabilize the robot, compromise balance, or reduce tracking accuracy.
- Current decoupled control often executes upper-body motions at a fixed speed, failing to adapt to dynamic consequences and the lower body's balancing capabilities.

## Method

- ==The Time Optimization Policy== (**TOP**) framework proposes a three-stage approach: Motion Prior Training (VAE for upper-body motion representation), Decoupling Policy Training (RL for lower-body balance with PD for upper-body), and TOP Training.
- The TOP module, trained via supervised reinforcement learning, dynamically predicts a sequence of future timestamps (
Δt) for upper-body motion clips, adapting their speed.
- Action chunking and a hybrid loss function (supervised + PPO) are used to ensure smooth 
Δt adjustments and stable policy learning for the TOP module.

## Results

- The TOP framework achieved a **95.30%** success rate on unseen challenging motion datasets, outperforming baselines (e.g., Exbody at **92.46%**) in stability and precision while adaptively managing time cost.
- The system demonstrated lower upper-body joint position error (E_upper_jpe of **0.0269**) and better projected gravity stability (E_g of **2.729**) compared to other methods.
- Real-world experiments validated precise base (<2mm error) and end-effector (<6mm error) tracking, robust push recovery, and successful completion of complex manipulation and loco-manipulation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing upper-body motions using a VAE provides a structured, generalized latent space that enhances coordination awareness for the lower-body controller.
> - Adaptively optimizing the time trajectory of upper-body motions is crucial for balancing stability, precision, and time efficiency, mitigating the dynamic burden on the lower body.
> - A decoupled training approach, separating the motion prior, lower-body balance policy, and time optimization policy, allows for modular development and improved performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robots are recognized for their potential in performing anthropomorphic loco-manipulation tasks across various domains, including industrial assembly and home service. A fundamental requirement for realizing this potential is the development of robust and precise standing control, particularly during manipulation.

Existing approaches to humanoid control generally fall into two categories: whole-body controllers and decoupled upper/lower-body controllers. Whole-body controllers, such as those based on Model Predictive Control (MPC), can generate precise motions but often face challenges in maintaining stability and robustness in real-world environments. Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based whole-body controllers have demonstrated improved dynamic robustness but frequently struggle with accurate tracking of complex, high-dimensional upper-body trajectories, potentially leading to suboptimal behaviors or unpredictable actions.

To address these limitations, a decoupled control architecture has emerged. In this paradigm, a PD controller typically manages the high-dimensional upper body to ensure precise trajectory tracking, while an RL policy controls the lower body to provide robust balance against external perturbations. This decoupled approach has shown promise in enhancing both stability and precision for standing manipulation tasks.

However, a recurring limitation across these methods, including many decoupled controllers, is their tendency to execute upper-body motions without fully accounting for the robot's dynamic capabilities and the momentum changes induced by these movements. Fast upper-body motions can destabilize the robot, compromise balance, and reduce tracking precision. Conversely, excessively slow motions, while improving stability, sacrifice time efficiency. This work specifically targets this challenge by proposing a method to optimize the time trajectory of upper-body motions, aiming to balance stability, accuracy, and efficiency simultaneously.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a control framework that enables humanoid robots to perform standing manipulation tasks with enhanced stability, accuracy, and time efficiency. The motivation for this work stems from several challenges identified in the current state of humanoid robot control:

Firstly, current humanoid control methods often face a trade-off between stability, precision, and time efficiency. While precise control of high-dimensional upper-body joints is crucial for intricate manipulation tasks, achieving this without compromising overall robot balance, especially during rapid movements, remains a significant hurdle. Existing whole-body controllers may struggle with real-world robustness, and whole-body RL approaches can lack precision for complex upper-body tasks.

Secondly, even with the adoption of decoupled control architectures—where a PD controller handles upper-body precision and an RL policy ensures lower-body balance—a critical oversight persists. These methods tend to execute upper-body motions at a fixed or predefined speed without adequately considering the dynamic consequences of momentum generated by these movements. Rapid upper-body motions can induce substantial momentum changes, potentially exceeding the balancing capabilities of the lower-body controller, leading to instability, loss of balance, or reduced tracking accuracy. This often results in the robot being unable to execute the desired motions as precisely or robustly as intended.

Thirdly, there is a recognized need for a mechanism that can adaptively determine the appropriate speed for upper-body motions. While slower motions can mitigate momentum effects and improve stability, they do so at the expense of task completion time. Conversely, prioritizing speed can destabilize the robot. Therefore, a method is required to intelligently adjust the timing of motion execution based on the robot's current state and dynamic capabilities, rather than solely focusing on strengthening the lower-body's disturbance resistance.

To address these motivations, the authors propose a novel Time Optimization Policy (TOP) with the following specific objectives:
- To train a standing manipulation control model that can ensure balance, precision, and time efficiency concurrently.
- To adjust the time trajectory of upper-body motions to reduce the dynamic burden on the lower body caused by fast movements.
- To integrate a structured representation of upper-body motions, a decoupled control strategy, and the TOP module into a cohesive framework.
- To validate the effectiveness of the proposed approach through both simulation and real-world experiments, demonstrating its adaptability and efficiency in practical standing manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, termed Time Optimization Policy (TOP), is designed to ensure stable, accurate, and time-efficient standing manipulation by adjusting the time trajectory of upper-body motions. The approach is structured into three sequential stages: Motion Prior Training, Decoupling Policy Training, and TOP Training.

**A. Motion Prior Training**
This initial stage focuses on learning a structured representation of diverse upper-body motions to enhance coordination awareness for the subsequent lower-body controller.
- **Model:** A Variational Autoencoder (VAE) architecture is utilized.
- **Input:** The VAE is trained on kinematic states of upper-body motions, `m_t`, which include the base position (set constant for encoding), 6D base orientation, 15 upper-body joint positions (from two 7-DoF arms and one waist joint), and their corresponding velocities.
- **Motion Windows:** Sequences of `2W+1` frames, termed motion windows `M_t` (with `W=30`, representing 0.6 seconds), are extracted from distinct motion clips and fed into the VAE. The input data, excluding orientation, is normalized using mean and standard deviation.
- **Encoder and Decoder:** An encoder `E_ϕ` maps the motion window `M_t` to a 64-dimensional latent space `z_t`, modeled as a multivariate Gaussian distribution. A decoder `D_θ` then reconstructs the motion window `M'_t` from `z_t`.
- **Loss Function:** A β-VAE objective is employed, incorporating a reconstruction loss `L_rec` (Equation 3 and 4) that measures differences in rotation matrices, joint positions, velocities, and base position. A KL weight of 0.002 and a cyclical schedule are used to prevent KL collapse, combined with cosine annealing for stable convergence. The goal is for the latent space `z_t` to capture the features of primitive motion blocks suitable for generalization.

**B. Training Decoupling Policy**
This stage trains a robust lower-body balance policy while the upper body executes motions precisely via a PD controller.
- **Lower-body Controller:** A balance policy is trained using a Legged Gym-based reinforcement learning framework with PPO.
- **Upper-body Controller:** A PD controller directly executes the current upper-body motion `m_t` with a fixed timestamp `Δt` between motion clips (e.g., `Δt = 0.01s`).
- **Goal-Conditional Policy:** The lower-body policy `π_ϕ(a_t|s_t, g_t)` is goal-conditional, where `g_t` is the goal at time `t`, comprising the target upper-body motion clip `m_t` and the latent code `z_t` from the VAE encoder. This provides the lower-body policy with instantaneous kinematic reference and contextual information about past and future upper-body motions.
- **Observation Space `s_t`:** Includes whole-body joint positions `q_t` and velocities `˙q_t`, base orientation `θ_t` and angular velocity `ω_t`, the previous lower-body action `a_{t-1}`, and the goal `g_t`.
- **Action Space `a_t`:** Lower-body joint actions, actuated by a PD torque controller `τ_t = k_p(a_t - q_t) + k_d ˙q_t`.
- **Reward Design:** The reward function (Table II) is designed to shape standing behavior, penalizing deviations in base linear/angular velocity, base orientation, and accelerations, while rewarding stability and contact.
- **Curriculum Learning:** To manage exploration burden, a training curriculum gradually increases the amplitude `α_i` of target motion clips (Equation 5) during training.
- **Domain Randomization:** Applied to enhance robustness and sim-to-real transfer, including variations in CoM position, motor delays, torque noise, and upper-body position/velocity noise.

**C. Time Optimization Policy (TOP) Training**
The final stage introduces the TOP to dynamically optimize the timestamps between motion clips, aiming to reduce momentum-induced disturbances while maintaining accuracy and efficiency.
- **Model:** A reinforcement learning policy `π_θ` is trained to predict a sequence of future timestamps `Δt_seq_t = (Δt_t..., Δt_{t+N})`.
- **Inputs to TOP:** The latent variable `z_t`, current observation `s_t`, and history observation `h_t`.
- **Motion Interpolation:** When `Δt_t` is adjusted by TOP, the original motion `m_t` is replaced by an interpolated motion `m'_t = linear_interpolate(m_{t-1}, m_{t+Δt_t})` to smooth the motion trajectory according to the new timing.
- **Action Chunking:** To ensure smooth `Δt` adjustments and avoid jerky transitions, the policy predicts a horizon of `N=10` future timestamps. An exponential weighting scheme `w_i = exp(-k * i)` (with `k=0.5`) is applied to aggregate predictions, favoring recent actions while maintaining temporal consistency.
- **Supervised Reinforcement Learning:** TOP is trained using a hybrid loss `L_total = λ_sup L_sup + λ_RL L_RL`.
 - `L_sup` (Equation 6) is a supervised loss that uses original `Δt` values as initial guesses, guiding the agent towards high-quality policies and reducing exploration. This loss is omitted if the robot falls, allowing the policy to prioritize slowing down.
 - `L_RL` (Equation 7) is the standard PPO reinforcement learning loss.
 - Weighting factors are `λ_sup = 0.1` and `λ_RL = 0.5`, with `L_sup` dominating early in training.
- **Reward Design for TOP:** The TOP reward function (Table I) shares similarities with the lower-body RL rewards, encouraging accurate motion execution, balance, and penalizing large `Δt` values while rewarding `Δt` smoothness.
- **Decoupled Training Justification:** TOP is trained separately from the lower-body controller. This modularity allows independent updates to the controller without retraining TOP and prevents TOP performance from directly impacting the controller itself.
- **Constraints:** `Δt` values are limited to the range `[0.01s, 0.1s]`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of the proposed TOP framework was evaluated through experiments in both simulation and real-world environments using a full-size humanoid robot (1.65m tall, 60kg, 41 DoFs). The training dataset (M) consisted of the GRAB human motion dataset, while testing utilized an additional unseen dataset (T) of 16,000 complex motion clips.

**A. Evaluation of Motion Priors and Decoupling Policy**
- **Generalization of Motion Priors:** The VAE model demonstrated its ability to capture features of short-horizon motion blocks. Linear interpolation in the latent space between motions from the training dataset (M) and the unseen dataset (T) allowed for the successful reconstruction of intermediate, generalized motions (Figure 3). This indicates the VAE's capacity to represent and reconstruct motions in proximity to similar windows within the dataset.
- **Robustness of Decoupling Policy:** The lower-body balance policy, when trained without the TOP module, exhibited robustness in maintaining balance while precisely tracking upper-body motions. On the unseen and more challenging dataset T, which featured larger amplitude and faster motions, the policy achieved a success rate exceeding 80%. Occasional failures were noted in extreme cases of fast motions or significant momentum changes (Figure 4), which underscores the motivation for the TOP module.

**B. Evaluation of TOP (Comparative and Ablation Results)**
A comprehensive comparison was conducted against several baselines (Fixed Root, Exbody, OmniH2O, Mobile-Television, NMPC+WBC) and through ablation studies (Table III). Metrics included Time Cost, Success Rate, Precision (upper joint position error `E_upper_jpe`, upper end effector position/orientation error `E_upper_eepe`/`E_upper_eeoe`), Stability (projected gravity `E_g`, lower joint acceleration `E_lower_acc`, lower action difference `E_lower_action`), and Smoothness (upper joint acceleration `E_upper_acc`, upper action difference `E_upper_action`).

- **Comparative Performance:** The "Ours (TOP)" method achieved a success rate of 95.30%, outperforming all baselines (e.g., Exbody at 92.46%, OmniH2O at 94.08%, Mobile-Television at 85.79%). While it incurred a higher average time cost (40.5s per 1500 clips compared to 15.0s for most baselines), this was attributed to its adaptive slowing of motions. Critically, TOP demonstrated improved precision across all upper-body error metrics (e.g., `E_upper_jpe` of 0.0269 vs. 0.0354-0.0376 for baselines) and better stability (`E_g` of 2.729 vs. 3.267-3.831 for baselines).
- **Impact of TOP:** Ablation studies showed that without TOP, using fixed `Δt` values (0.01s, 0.03s, 0.05s) yielded lower success rates and worse precision/stability. For instance, "Ours w/o TOP `Δt = 0.01s`" had a success rate of 82.43%. TOP's ability to adaptively select `Δt` between [0.01s, 0.1s] allowed it to balance efficiency with stability. It outperformed fixed-slow `Δt` policies by achieving similar precision/stability at a lower time cost (e.g., "Ours w/o TOP `Δt = 0.05s"` had a time cost of 75.0s for comparable precision).
- **Importance of Motion Priors:** The "Ours w/o motion prior" ablation resulted in a lower success rate (89.33%) and degraded stability/precision, confirming the necessity of the VAE-based motion representation.
- **Effectiveness of Action Chunking:** "Ours w/o acting chunking" also showed slightly reduced performance (93.16% success rate), indicating the benefit of predicting a sequence of `Δt` values for smoother transitions.

**C. Real-world Performance and Manipulation Experiments**
- **Accuracy:** Real-world tests using a motion capture device showed low base errors (e.g., <2mm for M dataset motions) and end-effector errors (e.g., <6mm for M dataset motions), with errors increasing for more dynamic unseen motions (Table IV, Figure 6). This indicated good tracking performance in physical deployment.
- **Adaptive Behavior:** In manipulation tasks, TOP adaptively adjusted motion speed. For simple tasks, it maintained original speeds for efficiency. For complex or dynamically challenging tasks (e.g., "dance with arms"), it actively slowed down motions to mitigate momentum, enhancing balance and accuracy (Figure 8).
- **Task-Specific Metrics:** Quantitative analysis of manipulation tasks (Figure 12, Table VII) demonstrated high success rates (90-100% for simple tasks, ~85% for complex ones) and practical precision (e.g., cup placement offset of 22.5mm, oven handle rotation error of 4.8°).
- **Push Recovery:** The system demonstrated robustness against external pushes from various directions in real-world tests (Figure 10).
- **Loco-Manipulation:** Further experiments showed the framework's generalization to loco-manipulation tasks (e.g., loco-button-pressing, loco-open-fridge), where the robot performs upper-body tasks while omnidirectionally walking (Figure 11).

**D. Sensitivity, Stability, and Efficiency Analyses**
- **Sensitivity to k:** A sensitivity analysis of the action chunking decay coefficient `k` (Figure 14) identified `k=0.5` as providing the best balance between joint error, projected gravity, time cost, and success rate.
- **VAE Parameters Sensitivity:** An ablation study on VAE parameters (Table IX) showed that a window size of 30 frames and a latent dimension of 64 provided the best trade-off for reconstruction quality and generalization.
- **Theoretical Stability (ZML):** Analysis using the Zero Moment Line (ZML) confirmed dynamic stability, with the distance `d` between the ZML projection and the center of the support polygon consistently remaining below 0.32, indicating the ZMP stayed within the support polygon (Figure 17).
- **Momentum Management:** The TOP effectively smoothed out abrupt base movements by adaptively slowing arm motions when momentum was excessive, thereby preventing destabilization (Figure 18).
- **Training and Inference Efficiency:** The total training time for the proposed pipeline was approximately 20 hours on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU, which is comparable to or better than existing baselines (Table X). The total inference time for all modules was approximately 5ms on both high-end GPU and edge computing platforms, enabling a control frequency of 100 Hz suitable for real-time applications (Table VIII).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of humanoid robotics by addressing a core challenge in standing manipulation: the simultaneous achievement of stability, accuracy, and time efficiency. The introduction of the Time Optimization Policy (TOP) provides a method for humanoid robots to adaptively adjust the speed of upper-body motions, directly mitigating momentum-induced disturbances that can lead to instability or reduced precision.

The significance of this work lies in several aspects:
- **Enhanced Performance:** The proposed framework demonstrates improved success rates, higher tracking precision for upper-body movements, and better overall stability compared to several existing whole-body and decoupled control methods. This advancement suggests a step towards more capable and reliable humanoid operation in dynamic environments.
- **Adaptive Control:** The TOP module’s ability to automatically determine appropriate motion speeds based on the robot's dynamic state represents an improvement over fixed-speed execution strategies. This adaptive capability allows the robot to balance the competing demands of task completion time and dynamic stability.
- **Modular and Robust Design:** The decoupled architecture, combined with the VAE-based motion prior and domain randomization during RL training, contributes to the robustness and generalization capabilities of the system. Its validation in both simulation and real-world scenarios, including complex manipulation tasks, push recovery, and loco-manipulation, indicates its practical applicability.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The reported low inference latency of the system suggests its feasibility for real-time deployment on standard robotic hardware.

The potential impact of this work extends to areas requiring dexterous and stable humanoid robot operation, such as industrial assembly, logistics, and service robotics. By providing a framework that enables more stable and precise manipulation, it could facilitate the deployment of humanoids in tasks that currently require human intervention or more specialized robotic solutions.

Future directions, as indicated by the authors, include incorporating motion generation modules for more natural standing adjustments and exploring adaptive task-oriented time optimization policies. These extensions could further enhance the autonomy and versatility of humanoid robots in dynamic and interactive environments.
