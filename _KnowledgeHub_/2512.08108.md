# Scalable Offline Model-Based RL with Action Chunks

> [!summary] Summary
> MAC introduces a model-based offline reinforcement learning approach that uses action chunks and flow-based generative policies to tackle complex, long-horizon tasks. It achieves state-of-the-art performance on challenging manipulation benchmarks, outperforming prior model-based methods and many model-free algorithms.

## Problem

- Scaling offline model-free reinforcement learning to complex, long-horizon tasks due to pathologies in off-policy temporal difference value learning.
- The inherent trade-off in model-based value expansion between needing long rollouts to reduce value function bias and short rollouts to mitigate compounding model errors.
- Modeling complex, high-dimensional, and potentially multi-modal action distributions in offline reinforcement learning settings.

## Method

- Develops ==action-chunk dynamics== and reward models that predict future states and rewards over `n` steps, significantly reducing autoregressive model calls and error accumulation.
- Employs expressive flow action-chunk policies with rejection sampling to generate value-maximizing action sequences while staying within the data distribution.
- Utilizes policy distillation to train an efficient one-step MLP action-chunk policy, accelerating the computationally intensive flow sampling process.

## Results

- Achieves state-of-the-art performance on challenging long-horizon goal-conditioned robotic manipulation tasks from OGBench (**100M** transitions), outperforming prior model-based and many model-free methods.
- Demonstrates that action chunking significantly reduces compounding model prediction errors, and the flow-based rejection sampling policy is critical for tasks with complex behavioral distributions.
- Identified a limitation where MAC, along with other model-based RL approaches, struggles on contact-rich robotic locomotion tasks due to difficulties in modeling highly erratic dynamics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Action chunking effectively mitigates compounding model prediction errors over extended imaginary rollouts, which is crucial for tackling long-horizon tasks.
> - Combining expressive flow-based policies with rejection sampling provides a robust mechanism to generate diverse, value-maximizing action chunks while preventing model exploitation.
> - On-policy model rollouts, combined with multi-step returns facilitated by action chunks, enable deeper value expansion and significantly reduce bias accumulation in long-horizon tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of **offline reinforcement learning (RL)**, **model-based RL**, and **generative modeling**. The broader goal of offline RL is to learn effective policies from static datasets without further interaction with the environment, a crucial step for real-world applications where online data collection is costly or dangerous. However, scaling offline RL to "complex, long-horizon tasks" remains a significant challenge, particularly for traditional model-free methods, which often struggle with pathologies of off-policy temporal difference (TD) value learning (Park et al., 2025b).

The authors position **model-based RL** as a promising alternative, specifically focusing on **model-based value expansion** (Feinberg et al., 2018). This paradigm involves training a dynamics model and then using it to generate imaginary rollouts for on-policy value function estimation, which then informs policy updates. The rationale is that on-policy value learning and generative modeling have individually shown strong scalability to long-horizon tasks (Berner et al., 2019; Harvey et al., 2022; Guo et al., 2025).

The core challenge in model-based value expansion is a "tricky trade-off":
- **Long rollouts (large `n`)** are desirable to reduce bias in bootstrapped value targets, as bias accumulation hinders long-horizon RL.
- **Short rollouts (small `n`)** are necessary to mitigate the compounding errors that accumulate in dynamics models when generating autoregressive predictions over extended horizons.

MAC directly addresses this trade-off by introducing **action-chunk models and policies**. This approach falls within a line of research on "horizon reduction" in RL, which seeks to shorten effective horizon lengths to combat the "curse of horizon" (Liu et al., 2018; Park et al., 2025b). Previous techniques include n-step returns, hierarchical policies, trajectory modeling, and skill-based action abstraction. Specifically, MAC builds on prior works that use action-chunk multi-step dynamics modeling (Asadi et al., 2019; Lambert et al., 2021; Zhao et al., 2024; Zhou et al., 2025), but distinguishes itself by integrating this into a full actor-critic, on-policy value learning framework, without relying on additional planning or full trajectory generation as often seen in other methods.

Furthermore, MAC leverages recent advancements in **expressive generative models**, specifically **flow matching** (Lipman et al., 2023, 2024), to model complex, multi-modal action-chunk distributions. This differentiates it from methods that rely on simpler Gaussian policies or traditional diffusion models for trajectory generation, positioning it at the forefront of combining robust generative modeling with efficient RL algorithms. The use of rejection sampling also provides a principled way to prevent "model exploitation" from out-of-distribution actions, a common pathology in offline model-based RL (Kidambi et al., 2020; Yu et al., 2020).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a **scalable recipe for tackling complex, long-horizon tasks in offline reinforcement learning using a model-based approach**.

The core motivations stem from two critical challenges in current offline RL methodologies:

1. **Limitations of Model-Free Offline RL on Long-Horizon Tasks:** Prior work (Park et al., 2025b) has indicated that standard model-free offline RL often struggles to scale to complex, long-horizon problems, largely due to pathologies inherent in off-policy, temporal difference (TD) value learning, which can lead to significant bias accumulation.
2. **The Dilemma in Model-Based Value Expansion:** While model-based RL offers a promising alternative due to its reliance on on-policy value learning (which has shown better horizon scalability) and dynamics modeling, it faces a fundamental trade-off:
 - **Large rollout horizon (`n`):** Desirable for reducing bias in bootstrapped value targets (`γ^n * V(s_{t+n})`). A smaller `n` leads to more biased updates, accumulating errors over long horizons.
 - **Small rollout horizon (`n`):** Necessary to minimize compounding errors in the learned dynamics model. Autoregressive predictions over many steps inevitably accumulate model inaccuracies, degrading the quality of imaginary rollouts.

The authors hypothesize that action-chunk models and policies, combined with expressive generative models (flow matching), can resolve this dilemma. By predicting a future state from a *sequence* of actions (an "action chunk") rather than a single action, the number of recursive model calls is substantially reduced, mitigating compounding errors and enabling much longer imaginary rollouts (e.g., over 100 environment steps) without sacrificing the benefits of deep value expansion.

Additional motivations include:
- **Modeling complex action distributions:** Action chunks are high-dimensional and potentially multi-modal, requiring expressive policies beyond simple Gaussians.
- **Preventing model exploitation:** Generating actions that are too far from the data distribution can lead to catastrophic failures in the learned model. A robust mechanism to stay "in-distribution" while still maximizing value is needed.
- **Simplifying hyperparameter tuning:** Traditional model-based RL often requires careful tuning of uncertainty penalization coefficients; the proposed rejection sampling approach aims for greater robustness.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, **Model-Based RL with Action Chunks (MAC)**, integrates several key components to achieve scalable offline model-based RL:

1. **Action-Chunk Dynamics and Reward Models:**
 - Instead of a single-step dynamics model `p(s_t+1 | s_t, a_t)`, MAC trains a multi-step action-chunk dynamics model `p_ψ(s_t+n | s_t, a_t:t+n-1)`. This model directly predicts a future state `n` steps ahead given the current state and a sequence of `n` actions. This design is crucial for reducing the number of autoregressive model calls, thereby mitigating compounding errors over long imaginary rollouts.
 - A corresponding action-chunk reward model `r_ψ(s_t, a_t:t+n-1)` is trained to predict the discounted sum of rewards over the `n`-step chunk.
 - These models are trained by minimizing mean squared error (MSE) against actual `n`-step transitions and rewards sampled from the offline dataset `D`. The dynamics model `p_ψ` is typically a deterministic MLP, though the authors note it could be more expressive for stochastic environments.

2. **Flow Action-Chunk Policies with Rejection Sampling:**
 - To utilize the action-chunk dynamics model in an actor-critic framework, an action-chunk policy `π(a_t:t+n-1 | s_t)` is required. Direct training of a reward-maximizing policy for high-dimensional, potentially multi-modal action chunks is challenging in offline RL.
 - MAC employs an **expressive behavioral action-chunk policy** trained using **flow matching**. A state-dependent velocity field `v_θ` is learned to transform a noise distribution (e.g., Gaussian) into the target action-chunk distribution from the dataset. The policy `π_θ(s_t, z)` then generates an action chunk by following the flow starting from noise `z`.
 - **Rejection sampling** is used to define the final policy: given a state `s_t`, `M` action chunks are sampled from the behavioral flow policy `π_θ(s_t, z_i)`. The action chunk that maximizes the learned action-chunk value function `Q_φ(s_t, a_chunk)` is selected as the policy's output: `π(s_t) = argmax_{a^(i)} Q_φ(s_t, a^(i))`. This approach inherently ensures that sampled actions are "in-distribution" (due to the flow BC policy) and also value-maximizing, thus preventing model exploitation and handling complex action distributions.

3. **Policy Distillation for Efficiency:**
 - Directly sampling from the flow policy `π_θ` during training is computationally intensive, as it involves `N_samples * N_flow_steps` queries to the velocity field.
 - To accelerate training, an additional **one-step MLP action-chunk policy `π_ω`** is trained to distill the output of the flow policy `π_θ`. This `π_ω` requires only a single network call per sample, reducing the computational cost of rejection sampling significantly.

4. **Value Learning:**
 - Value functions `V_φ(s)` and `Q_φ(s, a_chunk)` are trained using **on-policy model rollouts**. Initial states are sampled from the dataset, and then `H` action chunks are generated using the rejection-sampling policy. The action-chunk dynamics and reward models are used to synthesize subsequent states and rewards, creating "imaginary trajectories" of total length `nH`.
 - `V_φ` is updated using `nH`-step returns from these imaginary rollouts, regressing towards targets `∑_{i=k}^{H-1} γ^(i-k)n * r_t+in + γ^(H-k)n * V_bar_φ(s_t+Hn)`. This leverages the full `nH` horizon for value expansion, mitigating bias.
 - `Q_φ` is trained to support the rejection sampling process, using a standard Bellman-like update: `Q_φ(s_t, a_t) = r_t + γ^n * V_φ(s_t+n)`.
 - New model rollouts are generated for each training epoch to ensure on-policyness and adapt to the evolving policy and value functions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

MAC demonstrates significant performance improvements across challenging offline RL benchmarks, particularly on long-horizon tasks.

1. **Superior Performance on Large-Scale, Long-Horizon Tasks:**
 - On highly challenging, long-horizon goal-conditioned robotic manipulation tasks from OGBench (e.g., humanoidmaze-giant, cube-octuple, puzzle-4x5) with 100M transitions, MAC achieves state-of-the-art performance among model-based RL algorithms.
 - Crucially, previous model-based approaches often failed to achieve non-trivial performance on these tasks, likely due to error accumulation from single-step models or limitations of planning-based methods without full actor-critic loops.
 - MAC even outperforms state-of-the-art model-free RL algorithms (e.g., SHARSA) on four out of six tasks, particularly on long-horizon manipulation tasks like `cube-octuple` and `puzzle-4x5`.

2. **Robustness and Effectiveness on Standard Benchmarks:**
 - MAC also achieves the best average performance on four out of five standard reward-based manipulation environments from OGBench (e.g., `cube-single`, `cube-double`, `scene`, `puzzle-4x4`) with 1M transitions.
 - It shows substantially better performance than all other methods on relatively longer-horizon environments within this category, highlighting its generalization capabilities.

3. **Ablation Studies Confirm Component Importance:**
 - **Action Chunking Mitigates Error Accumulation:** Experiments show that longer action chunks substantially reduce compounding model prediction errors over extended rollouts. A standard one-step model's errors diverge rapidly, underscoring the necessity of multi-step prediction for long horizons (Figure 2).
 - **Action Chunk Length Impact:** An appropriate action-chunk length is crucial. While chunking improves performance significantly (especially on long-horizon `cube-octuple`, which couldn't be solved without it), excessively long chunks can degrade performance due to the increased difficulty of learning the model and Q-function for high-dimensional action spaces (Figure 3).
 - **Flow Rejection Sampling is Critical:** Ablations demonstrate that using an expressive flow-based policy with rejection sampling is vital. Replacing it with a Gaussian action-chunk policy or a gradient-based policy extraction (like FQL) severely degrades performance, especially on tasks with complex, multi-modal behavioral distributions (Table 3).
 - **Distillation of `π_θ` is also important:** Directly training `π_ω` without distillation from the multi-step `π_θ` also showed significant performance degradation in OGBench tasks (Table 14), suggesting `π_θ` captures important behavioral complexity.

4. **Limitations Identified (Negative Results):**
 - Despite its strengths, MAC, along with all other model-based RL approaches, struggles on contact-rich robotic locomotion tasks (e.g., `humanoidmaze-giant`). The authors attribute this to the difficulty of modeling highly erratic and discontinuous dynamics in such environments, where model errors accumulate severely even with action chunking. This suggests a need for more expressive generative models or latent dynamics models for these specific domains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MAC represents a significant advancement in offline model-based reinforcement learning, offering a scalable and robust solution to the long-horizon challenge. Its primary impact areas include:

1. **Breaking the Horizon Barrier in Offline RL:** By effectively mitigating compounding model errors through action-chunking and reducing value function bias via long model rollouts, MAC significantly extends the practical horizon of offline RL, enabling agents to learn complex, multi-step behaviors from static datasets. This is a crucial step towards deploying RL in real-world scenarios where long-term planning and sequential decision-making are paramount.

2. **Advancing Model-Based RL Design:** MAC provides a principled recipe for combining the strengths of model-based value expansion with modern generative modeling techniques. It shows that by carefully designing the dynamics model (action-chunked) and the policy (expressive flow-based with rejection sampling), the inherent trade-offs in model-based RL can be effectively managed.

3. **Leveraging Expressive Generative Models in RL:** The successful integration of flow matching for behavioral policy learning demonstrates the power of state-of-the-art generative models in addressing challenges like multi-modal action distributions and preventing model exploitation in offline settings. The distillation technique to accelerate this process also highlights practical considerations for scalability.

4. **Addressing Practical Challenges:** The method's robustness to hyperparameters (with fixed `n` and `H` across tasks) and its inherent mechanism to prevent model exploitation make it simpler to tune and more reliable than many prior offline model-based RL algorithms that rely on complex uncertainty quantification and penalization schemes.

5. **Benchmarking and Future Directions:** The comprehensive evaluation on challenging OGBench tasks, including the 100M-transition datasets, provides a new benchmark for evaluating the horizon scalability of offline RL algorithms. The identified limitation in contact-rich locomotion tasks also points to clear avenues for future research, such as exploring more advanced latent dynamics models or incorporating stronger physics priors, to make model-based RL universally robust.

In conclusion, MAC offers a compelling vision for scalable offline reinforcement learning, bridging the gap between theoretical capabilities and practical performance on complex tasks. Its innovative use of action chunks and flow-based generative policies sets a new standard for model-based approaches and lays important groundwork for future research in robust and data-efficient decision-making.
