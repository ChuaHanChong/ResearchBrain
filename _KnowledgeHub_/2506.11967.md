# Visual Pre-Training on Unlabeled Images using Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Annotation Bootstrapping, developed by UC Berkeley researchers, recasts visual pre-training as a reinforcement learning problem, framing image transformations as a Markov Decision Process to learn representations from unlabeled images. This approach improves performance, showing gains on diverse datasets like COCO and EpicKitchens where invariance-based methods struggle, and achieving up to 4% higher ImageNet probing performance over vanilla CLIP in a weakly-supervised setting.

## Problem

- Current self-supervised learning (SSL) methods relying on 'invariance' (e.g., SimCLR, DINO) struggle with 'in-the-wild' datasets where random crops may capture semantically different content.
- Combining weak supervision (e.g., text captions) with self-supervised losses in vision-language pre-training (VLP) often requires complex tuning and can degrade performance if invariance assumptions do not hold.
- Self-distillation methods for SSL can be unstable and prone to representation collapse.

## Method

- The method frames visual pre-training as a ==Markov Decision Process (MDP)==, where image views are states, controllable transformations are actions, and semantic annotations provide rewards.
- It learns a general ==value function (Q-function)== to predict the maximum expected future likelihood of eliciting specific semantic annotations across sequential image transformations.
- The approach decouples reward learning from ==value bootstrapping==, using an existing self-supervised or weakly-supervised objective to define the reward function and optimizing the Q-function to predict ==Bellman targets==.

## Results

- Annotation Bootstrapping (AB) consistently improved representations across diverse datasets, including ImageNet, CC12M, COCO, and EpicKitchens.
- AB variants (AB_SimCLR, AB_DINO) alleviated performance degradation seen with invariance-based methods on non-object-centric datasets like COCO and EpicKitchens for tasks such as object detection and action recognition.
- AB_CLIP achieved substantial gains (e.g., **4%** on ImageNet probing) over vanilla CLIP and other combined CLIP+SSL methods, particularly on COCO where other methods degraded performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual pre-training can be effectively re-framed as a reinforcement learning problem using value-based methods, offering a fresh theoretical lens to approach representation learning.
> - Learning equivariant representations, where features change predictably with transformations, is more suitable for diverse, non-object-centric "in-the-wild" image data than strictly invariant representations.
> - The framework allows for a clear separation between defining 'what to learn' (via reward function and semantic annotations) and 'how to learn from unlabeled data' (via value function), providing flexible guidance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a critical challenge in modern machine learning: leveraging vast amounts of unlabeled data to learn powerful representations, particularly for highly unstructured modalities like images. This paper operates at the intersection of **Self-Supervised Learning (SSL)**, **Vision-Language Pre-training (VLP)**, and **Reinforcement Learning (RL)**.

**Current Landscape of Visual Pre-training:**
- **Self-Supervised Learning (SSL):** Modern large-scale machine learning heavily relies on SSL to acquire representations from unlabeled data. Dominant approaches include:
 - **Reconstruction-based methods** (e.g., Masked Autoencoders (MAE), BEiT): Predict masked or corrupted pixels/features. While scalable, they often yield representations that require significant fine-tuning for downstream tasks and are sensitive to data curation.
 - **Consistency-based methods** (e.g., SimCLR, DINO, BYOL): Learn features invariant to various image augmentations (crops, color changes). These methods aim to produce more semantic features. However, they face challenges like high batch size requirements (contrastive methods), stability issues (self-distillation methods), and reliance on carefully designed inductive biases (e.g., crop invariance). Crucially, the paper highlights that the "invariance" bias of these methods can be detrimental when applied to "in-the-wild" datasets (like web crawls or videos) where random crops might capture vastly different, non-semantically equivalent content.
- **Vision-Language Pre-training (VLP):** Methods like CLIP, SLIP, and SiLC combine weak supervision (textual captions scraped from the internet) with self-supervised objectives. While effective for learning robust representations and improving data efficiency, they contend with challenges such as the appropriate weighting of different loss terms and the noise inherent in web-mined text-image pairs.

**The Novel Contribution:**
This paper introduces a novel perspective by **directly casting visual pre-training as an RL problem**. While prior work has explored connections between contrastive learning and goal-conditioned RL, this work takes the analogy further by explicitly training a **general value function** for visual representation learning. This formulation draws inspiration from **Temporal Difference (TD) learning** in RL, where sparse rewards are propagated back through states. In this analogy:
- **Image views and augmented versions** become "states" in a dynamical system.
- **Image transformations** (e.g., cropping, augmenting) become "actions" that transition between states.
- **Semantic annotations** (e.g., text captions, object categories) provide "rewards" indicating the presence of desired information.

By adopting an RL framework, the authors aim to overcome limitations of existing SSL and VLP methods. Specifically, the value function formulation naturally supports learning **equivariant representations** (where features change predictably with transformations) rather than strictly invariant ones, which is more suitable for diverse, non-object-centric image data. It also offers a principled way to incorporate supervised or weakly-supervised signals (via the reward function) without needing to carefully tune data augmentation strategies or balance loss terms, providing a "simple lever" to guide feature learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of this research is to develop a robust and flexible framework for visual pre-training on unlabeled images by drawing a direct analogy to reinforcement learning. The authors are motivated by several key observations and challenges in the existing landscape of self-supervised and vision-language learning:

1. **Bridging the Gap between SSL and RL:** The authors observe a strong conceptual similarity between self-supervised image pre-training (learning features that associate crops with "nearby" views) and value-based RL algorithms (learning to associate observations with future states and rewards). Their primary objective is to formalize and complete this analogy, proposing a method that directly casts pre-training as an RL problem.
2. **Overcoming Limitations of Invariance-Based SSL:** Traditional consistency-based SSL methods (like SimCLR, DINO) rely on the inductive bias of "invariance" to image augmentations and crops. This assumption often breaks down when applied to "in-the-wild" images (e.g., web crawls, video frames) that are not object-centric or contain complex scenes. Random crops in such datasets may capture entirely different semantic content. The objective is to develop a method that learns more **equivariant representations**, allowing features to change predictably rather than remaining identical across different views.
3. **Leveraging Weak Supervision Flexibly:** While vision-language pre-training (e.g., CLIP) has shown success in leveraging textual captions, combining these weakly supervised signals with self-supervised losses can be challenging, often requiring careful weighting of loss terms. The authors aim to provide a more "natural alignment" and "simple lever" to shape feature learning using curated images or weakly labeled captions, integrating them seamlessly through the reward function in the RL framework. This allows for decoupled learning, where semantic knowledge from curated data can guide representation learning on vast unlabeled datasets.
4. **Enhancing Learning Stability and Interpretability:** Self-distillation methods (like DINO, BYOL) can be unstable and prone to representation collapse. By framing the problem as a value prediction task, the authors seek to leverage the theoretical guarantees of RL, such as stable fixed-point solutions for value functions, thereby improving training stability. Furthermore, unlike opaque losses in some self-distillation approaches, value function predictions offer a degree of interpretability, as they represent the expected "value" of reaching certain semantic annotations.
5. **Improving Generalization to Diverse Unlabeled Data:** The ultimate goal is to learn improved visual representations from highly unstructured and diverse unlabeled image data (like web crawls and video data) that generalize well to a broad range of downstream tasks, including object detection, action recognition, and various classification problems, where existing methods often falter.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of "Annotation Bootstrapping" involves casting the learning from unlabeled image datasets as a reinforcement learning problem, specifically using a **value-based approach** inspired by Q-learning and Temporal Difference (TD) learning.

#### 3.1. The Image-Transformation MDP

- **States (Observations):** Views or crops of an image, denoted as *x*.
- **Actions:** Controllable, automated image transformations that change the view (e.g., zooming out, panning, rotating, cropping to a sub-view). In practice, this is implemented as the relative coordinates of a target bounding box with respect to a source bounding box (`bb_1->2`).
- **Transition Dynamics:** Induced implicitly by the chosen image augmentation strategy (e.g., random cropping). Sampling two views `x1` and `x2` from an image `I` (by cropping `bb1` and `bb2`) defines a transition `(x1, bb_1->2, x2)`. The environment is considered stochastic because applying an action from a state can lead to different subsequent views (e.g., zooming out from a dog's close-up could reveal grass, pavement, or a dog bed).
- **Rewards:** The likelihood of eliciting specific semantic annotations `p(annotation|x)`. These annotations represent "semantic concepts of interest" (e.g., "a kite," "a boy playing," or even representations of augmented views or prototypes). The agent's task is to maximize the expected likelihood of eliciting these annotations along the sequential transformation process.

#### 3.2. Annotation Bootstrapping Algorithm

The method learns a **general value function** `Q*(x, a, annotation)` that estimates the maximum expected future reward (likelihood of annotations) for taking action `a` from state `x` with respect to a specific `annotation`. This is achieved by iteratively optimizing a Bellman fixed-point equation:
`Q*(x, a, annotation) = E[r(x', annotation) + γ * max_a' Q*(x', a', annotation)]`
where `r(x', annotation)` is the immediate reward (likelihood of annotation in `x'`), and `γ` is the discount factor.

**Practical Implementation Details (Algorithm 1 & Figure 2):**
1. **Joint Learning of Reward Function and Value Function:**
 - **Reward Loss (`L_reward`):** An existing self-supervised or weakly-supervised objective (e.g., InfoNCE loss) is used to train a model `p(annotation|x)` that serves as the reward function.
 - **AB_CLIP:** Uses CLIP as the base, where annotations are text captions, and `p(annotation|x)` is the similarity between image and text embeddings.
 - **AB_SimCLR:** Uses SimCLR, where annotations are augmented views of the image, and `p(annotation|x)` is the similarity between original and augmented image embeddings.
 - **AB_DINO:** Uses DINO, where annotations correspond to prototype clusters, and `p(annotation|x)` is the probability of an image belonging to a cluster.
 - **Value Loss (`L_AB`):** Optimizes the Q-function to predict the Bellman target.
2. **Model Architecture (Figure 6):**
 - **Vision Encoder:** A Vision Transformer (ViT-S/16) processes image crops.
 - **Annotation Representation:** Depends on the reward type (e.g., a text transformer for CLIP, a head on the visual backbone for SimCLR/DINO).
 - **Value Function (`Q_AB`):** Implemented as a lightweight transformer decoder atop the image backbone. It processes "action tokens" (discretized relative bounding box coordinates `bb_1->2`) and cross-attends to the visual embeddings to produce an "image-action" representation. This representation is then used to predict the distribution over annotations.
3. **Training Process:**
 - A batch of unlabeled images is sampled. For each image, `n` random crops (`x_i`, `bb_i`) are generated.
 - For any two views `x_i` and `x_j` from the same image, `x_i` is considered the current state, and `x_j` is the next state resulting from action `bb_i->j`.
 - The model for `x_i` (and action `bb_i->j`) is trained to match a target value estimate derived from `x_j`. This target is a combination of the immediate reward `p(annotation|x_j)` and the bootstrapped future value `max_a' Q_ema(x_j, a', annotation)`.
 - **Target Network (EMA):** A lagging Exponential Moving Average (EMA) copy of the model parameters (`ema_model`) is used to generate stable target predictions, a common practice in RL (target networks) and self-distillation methods.
 - The loss `L_AB` is typically Binary Cross-Entropy between the predicted Q-values and the Bellman target.
4. **Computational Efficiency:** Through token packing and batching, the value loss is computed efficiently across all `n^2` pairs of views.

#### 3.3. Key Distinctions and Connections

- **Soft Distillation / Pseudo-labeling:** Annotation Bootstrapping transfers knowledge *from one image view to another image view* (for the *same model*), rather than transferring knowledge *from one model to another* about the *same image*. This is crucial for improving the current model's representation.
- **Consistency via Self-Distillation (DINO, SwAV):** While similar in predicting distributions across crops, Annotation Bootstrapping learns **equivariant representations** (predictions change predictably with transformations) instead of **invariant** ones (predictions are identical). This makes it more suitable for complex scenes where crops are semantically distinct.
- **I-JEPA:** Similar architecture (predicting representations using positional/action tokens), but I-JEPA predicts representations of states "one transformation away," whereas value functions inherently consider "longer horizons" due to recursion. Also, the value prediction task has guaranteed fixed-point solutions, unlike some asymmetric predictor designs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that Annotation Bootstrapping (AB) consistently improves visual representations learned from diverse unlabeled datasets, particularly excelling where traditional invariance-based methods struggle.

1. **Improved Representations Across Datasets:**
 - **ImageNet (Well-curated, Object-centric):** AB (AB_SimCLR, AB_DINO) is synergistic with base self-supervised losses, improving performance over training the base losses for longer. The gains are most pronounced for probes that attend to encoded tokens (e.g., MAP pooling, larger decoder) rather than single global tokens, suggesting richer feature learning.
 - **CC12M (Web-crawl, Weakly-curated):** Similar improvements observed, confirming effectiveness on larger, less curated web data.
 - **COCO & EpicKitchens (Non-object-centric, Scene/Video Data):** This is a key strength. Traditional crop-consistency methods (SimCLR, DINO) degrade significantly on these datasets due to their poor fit for the invariance inductive bias. AB variants (AB_SimCLR, AB_DINO) alleviate these deficiencies, leading to better features than running the base losses alone. While still slightly worse than MAE, AB shows significant relative improvement on object detection/classification and action recognition for these datasets.

2. **Decoupling Reward Learning and Bootstrapping:**
 - A significant finding is the ability to decouple the data distributions for reward learning and value bootstrapping. When rewards are learned from a curated dataset like ImageNet (to define clear semantics) and the value function is trained by bootstrapping on a different unlabeled dataset (like COCO or EpicKitchens), performance significantly improves for in-domain tasks (object recognition, localization, action recognition). This implies that the semantic guidance can come from a "clean" source, while the value function propagates this understanding through "noisy" unlabeled data. The results show AB_DINO on ImageNet+COCO/EpicKitchens outperforms DINO on any mixture ratio of these datasets.

3. **Effectiveness in Weakly-Supervised Setting (AB_CLIP):**
 - When annotations are textual captions (e.g., from CC12M), AB_CLIP significantly outperforms vanilla CLIP and other methods that combine CLIP with self-supervised objectives (e.g., SLIP, SiLC). AB_CLIP shows substantial gains (e.g., ≥4% on downstream ImageNet probing) and scales consistently with increased compute.
 - On COCO, where other combined CLIP+SSL methods (SLIP, SiLC) degrade performance, AB_CLIP is the *only* method that improves over vanilla CLIP. This supports the idea that the "invariance" bias of traditional SSL can be detrimental when combined with weak supervision if it doesn't align with the data's true structure. AB, in contrast, consistently improves over the base CLIP loss.
 - Analysis of gradient alignment shows that AB_CLIP's self-supervision is better aligned with semantic features relevant to CLIP compared to SLIP or SiLC.

4. **Analysis of Learning Dynamics and Design Choices:**
 - **Bootstrapping Objective Optimization:** The model successfully optimizes the bootstrapping objective, with prediction accuracy for annotations increasing through training, even for "distant" crops. AB_CLIP finds the prediction more challenging than AB_DINO, reflecting DINO's tendency to make predictive distributions more similar across crops.
 - **Crop Choice:** Training with more distant (lower IoU) source and target crops generally provides a more useful learning signal, though AB_DINO is more robust to closely related crops than vanilla DINO.
 - **Ablation Study:**
 - Removing the EMA (target) network nullifies performance gains, highlighting its criticality for stability, as in other bootstrapping methods.
 - Removing the base reward loss severely degrades performance, emphasizing the need for grounding annotation distributions in a semantically meaningful space.
 - Replacing bounding box action tokens with empty mask tokens (forcing an invariance objective) also degrades performance, confirming the benefit of learning equivariant representations.
 - Adding augmentations slightly degrades performance for AB_SimCLR, indicating that the core value objective handles some variability inherently.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work "Visual Pre-Training on Unlabeled Images using Reinforcement Learning" presents a significant conceptual and practical advancement in visual representation learning, with several important implications:

1. **Novel Paradigmatic Shift:** The most profound contribution is the successful casting of self-supervised visual pre-training as an RL problem. This re-framing offers a fresh theoretical lens to approach representation learning, moving beyond traditional statistical objectives to leverage the powerful optimization and generalization principles of value-based reinforcement learning. This could inspire further exploration of RL algorithms in other areas of unsupervised learning.
2. **Robustness to Data Inductive Biases:** By naturally leading to **equivariant representations** rather than strict invariance, Annotation Bootstrapping addresses a critical weakness of existing self-supervised methods on "in-the-wild" datasets (e.g., web crawls, videos, complex scenes). This enhanced robustness means that the learned features are more useful for real-world, less curated data, which is abundant but challenging for current techniques. This opens up larger, previously underutilized datasets for effective pre-training.
3. **Flexible and Guided Feature Learning:** The RL framework allows for a clear separation between defining "what to learn" (via the reward function and semantic annotations) and "how to learn from unlabeled data" (via the value function's bootstrapping mechanism). This "simple lever" of the reward function provides an intuitive way to inject specific semantic knowledge, whether from small curated datasets or weak supervision (like captions), without requiring complex tuning of data augmentation strategies or loss weighting. This flexibility is a major practical advantage for researchers and practitioners.
4. **Improved Stability and Interpretability:** Leveraging the theoretical properties of value functions from RL (e.g., guaranteed fixed-point solutions) can lead to more stable training compared to some self-distillation methods prone to collapse. Furthermore, the learned value functions offer a degree of interpretability by predicting semantic properties across image transformations, potentially providing insights into what the model has learned about the visual world.
5. **Accelerating Progress with Unlabeled Data:** As current large language and vision models begin to saturate readily available weakly-labeled data, the ability to effectively learn from vast, truly unlabeled image and video data becomes the next frontier. Annotation Bootstrapping offers a promising avenue to unlock the potential of these largely untapped data sources, facilitating the development of even more powerful and general visual foundation models.
6. **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper lays the groundwork for numerous future research directions, including exploring other RL algorithms (e.g., policy gradient methods) for representation learning, scaling the approach to even larger models and datasets, and developing non-contrastive versions of bootstrapping that could leverage a wider range of weak supervision signals beyond image-text pairs or self-generated augmentations. The connection to "active perception" also suggests potential for embodied AI applications where an agent learns visual understanding through interaction.
