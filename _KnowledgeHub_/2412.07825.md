# 3DSRBench: A Comprehensive 3D Spatial Reasoning Benchmark

> [!summary] Summary
> 3DSRBench is introduced as a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating large multi-modal models' (LMMs) 3D spatial reasoning, using diverse natural and synthetic images with open-vocabulary entities. The benchmark demonstrates that current LMMs exhibit limited 3D understanding and lack robustness to varied camera viewpoints, performing well below human accuracy.

## Problem

- Existing spatial reasoning benchmarks primarily focus on 2D relationships or rely on synthetic data, limiting real-world applicability.
- Current 3D datasets are often confined to specific domains or object categories, with VQA generation methods introducing biases.
- A rigorous evaluation of LMMs' true 3D spatial comprehension, including object-centric positions, orientations, and multi-object interactions, was lacking.

## Method

- Developed **3DSRBench**, a benchmark featuring 12 question types across 4 categories, utilizing manually annotated ==VQA== pairs from MS-COCO natural images and open-vocabulary entities.
- Included a synthetic multi-view split to specifically assess LMM robustness to common versus uncommon ==6D camera viewpoints==.
- Implemented robust evaluation strategies like ==CircularEval== to mitigate order bias and a novel ==FlipEval== to address left/right spatial biases, ensuring reliable 3D understanding assessment.

## Results

- Evaluated LMMs scored between **52.0%** and **60.3%** on 3DSRBench-real, significantly below human performance of **95.7%**.
- Performance drops ranging from **9.5%** to **32.2%** were observed across LMMs when tested on uncommon camera viewpoints in the synthetic split.
- Failure analyses indicate LMMs often lack explicit 3D representations, relying on potentially misleading 2D cues and struggling with multi-step 3D computations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - State-of-the-art LMMs demonstrate limited 3D spatial reasoning, with top models achieving only around 60% accuracy compared to human performance of 95.7%.
> - LMMs show poor robustness to uncommon camera 6D viewpoints, experiencing performance degradation up to 32.2%.
> - Standard LMM scaling laws yield diminishing returns for 3D spatial reasoning, suggesting architectural and data strategies are more impactful than mere size increases.
