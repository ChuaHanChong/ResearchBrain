# Regret-Guided Search Control for Efficient Learning in AlphaZero

> [!summary] Summary
> Regret-Guided Search Control (RGSC) enhances AlphaZero's learning efficiency by identifying and prioritizing "high-regret states" for self-play restarts. This approach led to an average 77-89 Elo point improvement over baselines and demonstrated continued performance gains on already highly proficient models in games like Go, Othello, and Hex.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning agents, such as AlphaZero, require extensive self-play (millions of games) to achieve mastery, exhibiting lower learning efficiency compared to human players.
- Standard AlphaZero training paradigms inefficiently extract learning signals by consistently restarting self-play from initial states and uniformly updating all positions based on final outcomes.
- Existing search control methods, like Go-Exploit, treat all potential restart states uniformly, failing to prioritize states with higher learning potential and becoming less effective as the agent improves.

## Method

- The Regret-Guided Search Control (**RGSC**) framework extends ==AlphaZero== by integrating a specialized regret network and a Prioritized Regret Buffer (PRB).
- A regret network, comprising both ranking and value heads, is used to identify "high-regret states" where the agent's Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) value significantly deviates from the actual game outcome.
- The PRB stores these high-regret states, and new self-play games are preferentially started from sampled states within this buffer, with higher-regret states being prioritized via a softmax distribution.

## Results

- RGSC consistently outperformed AlphaZero and Go-Exploit baselines, achieving an average 77 Elo points improvement over AlphaZero and 89 Elo points over Go-Exploit across 9x9 Go, 10x10 Othello, and 11x11 Hex.
- When applied to an already well-trained 9x9 Go model, RGSC further increased its win rate against KataGo from **69.3%** to **78.2%**, demonstrating continued performance gains where baselines showed minimal progress.
- Ablation studies confirmed the regret ranking network's superiority in selecting states with substantially higher true regret compared to the regret value network, and analysis showed regret values for states in the PRB consistently decreased upon repeated revisits.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly identifying and prioritizing states where the agent demonstrates significant MCTS mis-evaluations (high-regret states) for self-play restarts significantly accelerates learning efficiency and robustness.
> - A regret ranking network, which focuses on differentiating relative regret scores, is more effective for identifying critical learning opportunities than a direct regret value prediction network due to the dynamic and imbalanced nature of regret targets.
> - Using an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) to update regret values for states in the PRB ensures a gradual decrease in regret, allowing for sustained practice on challenging positions until true mastery is achieved.
