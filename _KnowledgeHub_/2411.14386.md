# Learning Humanoid Locomotion with Perceptive Internal Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai AI Laboratory developed a Perceptive Internal Model (PIM) that enables humanoid robots to learn robust and agile locomotion over challenging terrains. This approach allowed both Unitree H1 and Fourier GR-1 robots to climb 15 cm stairs and perform dynamic jumps, demonstrating efficient sim-to-real transfer and cross-platform generalization.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots are inherently unstable and have high degrees of freedom, making stable and robust locomotion on challenging terrains difficult with "blind" policies.
- Prior perceptive methods for humanoids often suffered from computational overhead, robustness issues with raw sensor data, or significant sim-to-real transfer gaps.
- Existing approaches struggled to enable humanoids to consistently navigate highly challenging obstacles like tall stairs or continuous uneven terrain.

## Method

- Introduces the Perceptive Internal Model (PIM), which incorporates onboard, continuously updated elevation maps directly into the robot's state estimation.
- Employs a two-stage reinforcement learning process using PPO to optimize both the policy and the PIM, leveraging a comprehensive reward system.
- Includes techniques like an action curriculum and symmetry regularization to enhance training efficiency and promote natural gait.

## Results

- Successfully enabled humanoid robots (Unitree H1) to continuously climb stairs with an unprecedented height of 15 cm and perform dynamic jumps over high platforms and gaps.
- The policy demonstrated effective zero-shot transfer to real-world Unitree H1 and Fourier GR-1 robots, traversing diverse challenging terrains.
- Training completed in approximately 3 hours on an RTX 4090 GPU, with the PIM approach showing higher state estimation accuracy and faster learning curves compared to a non-perceptive internal model.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating perceptive information directly into the robot's internal model (PIM) for state estimation significantly improves locomotion robustness and training efficiency compared to merely providing perception as a policy input.
> - Using on-board elevation maps provides a compact, robust, and computationally efficient perceptual representation suitable for real-time control and effective sim-to-real transfer.
> - The approach demonstrates cross-platform generalizability, enabling the same training framework to effectively control humanoids with different morphologies (Unitree H1 and Fourier GR-1).

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of legged robot locomotion has seen significant advancements in recent years, largely driven by reinforcement learning (RL) techniques and successful simulation-to-real (sim-to-real) transfer. This paper positions itself at the forefront of this evolution, specifically addressing the unique challenges posed by humanoid robots.

**Current Landscape:**
- **Quadruped Dominance:** "Blind" policies, relying solely on proprioceptive information (e.g., joint positions, velocities, robot orientation), have proven highly effective for quadruped robots. These policies enable robust and agile locomotion across diverse, even challenging, terrains.
- **Humanoid Challenges:** In contrast, humanoid robots possess higher degrees of freedom (DoF) and inherently unstable morphology (bipedal, higher center of mass). This makes achieving stable and robust locomotion significantly more difficult with "blind" policies.
- **Need for Perception:** The consensus in the field is that incorporating perceptual information (exteroception) is essential for humanoids to navigate complex and dynamic environments effectively.
- **Prior Humanoid Approaches and Their Limitations:**
 - **"Blind" Policies for Humanoids:** Many recent efforts in humanoid locomotion have still followed the "blind" policy paradigm or adopted multi-stage training processes. While successful on flat surfaces or mildly uneven terrains (e.g., low stairs), these often lead to instability or failure on more challenging obstacles.
 - **Reliance on Prior Trajectories:** Some approaches utilize prior motion trajectories or computationally expensive multi-stage training to achieve human-like gaits, but these often lack the generalizability or robustness needed for truly diverse and challenging terrains.
 - **Perceptive but Limited:** While some works, like "Humanoid Parkour Learning", exploit perceptive information, they often struggle with terrains requiring fine-grained foot placement, such as continuous stairs, or introduce significant computational overhead and sim-to-real gaps. The challenge of simulating extensive hardware noise for perception transfer can compromise accuracy.
 - **Direct Perception Encoding:** Previous methods that directly encode raw depth maps or point clouds suffer from high computational costs for rendering in simulation, sensitivity to sensor noise and movement, and difficulties in mitigating the domain gap between simulated and real-world sensor data.

**How This Work Advances the Field:**
This paper directly addresses the limitations of existing humanoid locomotion methods by proposing a novel **Perceptive Internal Model (PIM)**. It aims to integrate perception seamlessly and efficiently, allowing humanoids to handle significantly more challenging terrains than previously demonstrated.

- **Efficient Perception Integration:** Instead of raw sensor data, PIM leverages **onboard, continuously updated elevation maps**. This processed representation is more robust to sensor noise and movement, provides a clear view of the terrain underfoot, and crucially, avoids the computational cost of depth map rendering in simulation, making training much faster.
- **Perception for State Estimation:** A key innovation is not just using perception for foothold planning, but integrating it directly into the **internal model (PIM)** for more accurate *state estimation*. This stands in contrast to approaches where perception is merely an additional input to the policy.
- **Cross-Platform Generalization:** The demonstration of the method's effectiveness across two different humanoid robots (Unitree H1 and Fourier GR-1) marks a significant step towards a generalized solution, which is rare in this highly hardware-specific domain. This suggests the proposed approach is robust to variations in robot kinematics, dynamics, and sensor configurations.
- **Sim-to-Real Efficiency:** The method's design, particularly the use of elevation maps and the PIM, reduces the "perception gap" during sim-to-real transfer, allowing for **zero-shot deployment** without additional training stages to simulate hardware noise.

By combining an efficient perception representation with a robust internal model and a streamlined training process, this work pushes the boundaries of what humanoid robots can achieve in complex, unstructured environments, moving beyond merely flat or mildly uneven surfaces to master obstacles like high stairs and gaps with a natural gait.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable humanoid robots to achieve stable, robust, and agile locomotion across diverse and challenging terrains, overcoming the inherent limitations of their complex morphology and high degrees of freedom.

**Specific Objectives:**

1. **Integrate Perception Effectively:** Develop a method to incorporate perceptual signals into the humanoid locomotion policy without introducing additional disturbances, compromising robustness or generalizability, or incurring significant computational costs.
2. **Enhance Terrain Adaptability:** Enable humanoid robots to navigate significantly more difficult and precise terrains than previously possible, specifically targeting continuous climbing of stairs (e.g., 15 cm height), traversing gaps, and stepping onto high platforms.
3. **Improve State Estimation:** Leverage perceptual information not just for direct policy input but also to improve the accuracy of the robot's internal state estimation, thereby enhancing overall stability and adaptability.
4. **Achieve Training Efficiency:** Design a system that allows for rapid policy training in simulation (e.g., within a few hours on a high-end GPU) to accelerate research and development cycles.
5. **Ensure Sim-to-Real Transferability:** Develop a perception mechanism that minimizes the "perception gap" between simulation and the real world, allowing for zero-shot deployment of the trained policy on physical hardware.
6. **Demonstrate Cross-Platform Generalization:** Validate the proposed approach on multiple, morphologically distinct humanoid robot platforms to demonstrate its robustness and potential for general applicability.

**Motivation:**

The motivation stems from the inherent difficulties in controlling humanoid robots, contrasting sharply with the progress seen in quadrupeds:
- **Inherent Instability:** Humanoid robots are bipedal and possess a high center of mass, making them inherently unstable compared to their quadruped counterparts. This instability is exacerbated on uneven or challenging terrains.
- **High Degrees of Freedom (DoF):** The large number of joints in humanoids (e.g., 20+ DoF for locomotion alone) leads to a vast action and observation space, making robust control significantly harder for traditional "blind" policies.
- **Limitations of Current Methods:** Existing "blind" policies, while effective on flat surfaces, quickly fail on substantial obstacles. Methods that do incorporate perception often suffer from:
 - **Computational Overhead:** Direct encoding of raw sensory data (depth maps, point clouds) requires intensive rendering in simulation, slowing down training.
 - **Robustness Issues:** Raw sensor data can be noisy and sensitive to camera movement, affecting policy stability.
 - **Sim-to-Real Gap:** Bridging the gap between simulated and real camera data is challenging, often requiring additional training phases or compromising perception accuracy.
 - **Lack of Fine-Grained Control:** Many perceptive approaches cannot handle tasks requiring precise foot placement or intricate whole-body coordination for challenging obstacles.
- **Demand for Capable Humanoids:** The rise of the humanoid robot industry necessitates control methods that enable these robots to operate effectively in diverse, unstructured human environments, which are rarely flat or predictable.
- **Foundational Research:** The authors aim to contribute a foundational algorithm that can serve as a building block for future humanoid control methods, addressing critical challenges in robust, adaptive, and efficient locomotion.

In essence, the paper is motivated by the gap between the significant potential of humanoid robots and their current limitations in navigating complex real-world terrains, seeking to bridge this gap through efficient and robust perceptive control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology is the **Perceptive Internal Model (PIM)**, which extends the existing Hybrid Internal Model (HIM) by deeply integrating perceptual information. The overall framework is designed as a sequential decision problem solved using reinforcement learning, specifically Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).

#### 3.1. Framework Overview

The system takes proprioceptive and perceptive information as observations to determine actuator movements for desired velocities. The framework (depicted in Fig. 2) constructs a PIM for state estimation, and the policy is optimized with PPO.

#### 3.2. Observations

The policy's observations (`o_t`) are divided into two parts:
- **Non-Perceptive Part (`o_n_t`):** Includes desired velocity commands (`c_t`), proprioceptive information (joint position `θ_t`, joint velocity `˙θ_t`, base angular velocity `ω_t`, gravity direction `g_t`), and the action from the previous timestep (`a_t-1`).
- **Perceptive Part (`o_p_t`):** Consists of an elevation map (`p_t`) around the robot.
The critic, unlike the policy, has access to privileged information like the robot's linear velocity (`v_t`) during training for more accurate state value estimation.

#### 3.3. Preliminary: Hybrid Internal Model (HIM)

PIM is built upon HIM, which is known for its accurate state estimation and sim-to-real transfer capabilities. HIM estimates the robot's next-timestep linear velocity and proprioceptive information in a latent space.
- **Linear Velocity Estimation:** Trained via simple regression against ground truth linear velocity from the simulator.
- **Proprioceptive Prediction:** Trained using contrastive learning, similar to SwAV, where `o_n_t+1` and `o_n_t-H:t` from the same trajectory form a positive pair, and others form negative pairs.

#### 3.4. Perceptive Internal Model (PIM)

PIM enhances HIM by incorporating perceptive information directly into the state prediction.
- **Elevation Maps:**
 - **Real-world implementation:** Employs an elevation map module using either a Mid-360 LiDAR (for odometry and point cloud) or a Realsense T265 (odometry) + D435 (point cloud) setup.
 - **Key features:** Requires robust odometry and ground point clouds. Crucially, the map's z-axis is aligned with the gravity direction. Point clouds are filtered to preserve only ground points.
 - **Robustness:** LiDAR-based setup proved more robust during rapid movements.
- **Elevation Sampling:** From the constructed elevation map, 96 points are sampled in a 0.8m × 1.2m square centered on the robot. Their relative z-coordinates to the base link are used as the perceptive input. This provides a compact and relevant representation of the terrain under and around the robot's feet.
- **Terrain-based PIM:** The central innovation. Instead of only using proprioceptive history for state estimation, the current perceptive observation (`o_p_t`) is concatenated with the proprioceptive history (`o_n_t-H:t`). This provides richer information for predicting the next-step state, recognizing terrain's crucial role in robot state transitions.

#### 3.5. Action Curriculum

To simplify early training, a curriculum is introduced for the action space. Joints less critical for locomotion (e.g., arm and waist joints) initially have their range set to zero, with their range gradually increased as training progresses. This helps the policy focus on core locomotion first.

#### 3.6. Symmetry Regularization

A symmetry regularization technique, similar to, is used to promote a harmonious gait. This involves applying specific operators (`G_n_o`, `G_p_o`, `G_a`) to flip proprioceptive observations, perceptive observations, and actions, respectively, with respect to the robot's x-z plane. During PIM optimization, data is augmented with `G_o`. During policy optimization, two additional Mean Squared Error (MSE) losses are added: one for the policy output's symmetry and another for the value function's symmetry, ensuring that symmetric inputs yield symmetric outputs/values.

#### 3.7. Training Process

The training follows an iterative, two-stage optimization process:
1. **Policy Optimization:** At each timestep, `o_n_t-H:t` and `o_p_t` are fed into the PIM to estimate the next-step linear velocity (`v_hat_t+1`) and a latent variable (`l_t`) predicting proprioceptive information. These, along with `o_t`, are then fed into the policy network to produce action `a_t`. During this stage, the PIM is frozen, and only the policy and value networks are updated using PPO.
2. **PIM Optimization:** After policy optimization, collected trajectories are used to update and optimize the PIM itself. This ensures the internal model continually improves its state prediction capabilities based on the robot's learned behaviors.

#### 3.8. Reward Functions

A comprehensive set of reward functions guides the robot's learning, encouraging desired behaviors:
- **Standard Locomotion Rewards:** Linear and angular velocity tracking, penalties for vertical linear velocity and lateral angular velocity, orientation control, joint accelerations, joint power, body height maintenance, action rate, smoothness, and joint limits.
- **Humanoid-Specific Rewards:**
 - **Feet Lateral Distance:** Penalizes feet being too close to maintain a stable stance.
 - **Feet Ground Parallel:** Encourages the robot's feet to remain parallel to the ground by penalizing variance in height of sampled points on the feet.
 - **Feet Stumble, Torques, Joint Velocity/Tracking Error, Arm/Hip/Waist Joint Deviation, No Fly, Feet Slip, Feet Contact Force, Contact Momentum:** These further refine gait, balance, and whole-body coordination.

This multi-faceted methodology, combining a novel perceptive internal model with efficient training techniques and tailored reward design, aims to enable robust and agile humanoid locomotion in complex environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research effectively validates the proposed Perceptive Internal Model (PIM) across various challenging scenarios and robot platforms, demonstrating significant improvements in humanoid locomotion capabilities.

#### 4.1. PIM vs. HIM (Effectiveness of Perception in State Estimation)

- **Direct Comparison:** The authors directly compare PIM (which uses perception for state estimation) with HIM (which does not, even if the policy itself uses perception for locomotion).
- **Superiority of PIM:** As shown in Fig. 5, PIM consistently achieves:
 - **Higher Training Efficiency:** It learns effective locomotion faster.
 - **Higher Estimation Accuracy:** The estimation loss (error between estimated and real velocity) is lower for PIM, indicating more accurate internal state prediction.
 - **Enhanced Terrain Traversal:** PIM enables robots to traverse significantly more difficult terrain levels, demonstrating that incorporating perception into the internal model for state estimation is crucial for pushing performance boundaries.

#### 4.2. Perceptive Locomotion on Stairs

- **Unprecedented Height:** The method successfully enables the humanoid robot (Unitree H1) to continuously climb stairs with a height of **15 cm** (Fig. 6-a). This significantly surpasses previous state-of-the-art results in the literature, which typically achieved a maximum of 10 cm.
- **Precision Requirement:** The success on 15 cm stairs, especially when the stair width (30cm) is equal to the robot's foot length, highlights the critical need for and achievement of precise foot placement. This precision prevents the foot from getting stuck or causing loss of balance, directly demonstrating the effectiveness of PIM's accurate state prediction and perception.

#### 4.3. High Platform & Gap Traversal

- **Dynamic Response:** The Unitree H1 robot successfully performs consecutive jumps onto high platforms and over gaps (Fig. 6-b).
- **Rapid Adaptation:** This demonstrates the policy's ability to respond rapidly to changing perceptual information and execute extreme parkour actions, showcasing the strength of perceptive-integrated state estimation even for less perception-accuracy-demanding but dynamically challenging tasks.

#### 4.4. Cross-Platform Validation

- **Generalizability:** The same training process was successfully applied to two distinct humanoid robot platforms: **Unitree H1** and **Fourier GR-1** (Fig. 4). The GR-1, with its different ankle DOF (two DOF powered by parallel joints vs. H1's one tandem DOF), height, mass, and joint configuration, still managed to traverse various challenging terrains (Fig. 6-c).
- **Robustness to Design Variations:** This finding is a crucial result, highlighting the method's robustness to significant variations in robot morphology and design. It suggests the potential for PIM to be a generalized solution for humanoid locomotion across a range of hardware. To the authors' knowledge, this is the first perceptive locomotion framework demonstrated to work well across different robot platforms.

#### 4.5. Whole-body Movement

- **Emergent Coordination:** Without any prior knowledge, imitation learning, or explicit human motion data, the robot exhibits coordinated upper-body movements to maintain balance, particularly during extreme actions like jumping.
- **Natural Gait:** During flat-ground locomotion, the robot avoids unnatural gaits (e.g., walking with the same hand and foot).
- **Dynamic Balance:** An example is provided where, during a jump, the robot swings its left arm heavily forward as its right leg jumps (Fig. 6-b, fourth image), indicating an emergent, human-like strategy for dynamic balance.
- **Reward Design Effectiveness:** This demonstrates that effective reward design combined with accurate model prediction (achieved by PIM) can implicitly generate complex, human-like whole-body movements.

In summary, the results convincingly show that PIM is not only efficient and accurate in simulation but also translates effectively to real-world hardware, enabling humanoids to perform complex and dynamic maneuvers on terrains previously considered beyond their capabilities, and exhibiting robust generalization across different robot designs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Learning Humanoid Locomotion with Perceptive Internal Model" paper presents a significant step forward in the field of humanoid robotics, with the potential for broad impact across research and application domains.

#### 5.1. Foundational Algorithm for Humanoid Control

- The paper explicitly states its hope that PIM can serve as a "foundational algorithm for the development of future humanoid control methods." This ambition is well-supported by its demonstrated capabilities.
- By effectively integrating perception into the robot's internal state estimation, PIM addresses a critical bottleneck that has limited humanoid performance on complex terrains. This provides a robust and efficient blueprint for future control policies.

#### 5.2. Enabling Advanced Humanoid Capabilities

- **Unprecedented Terrain Mastery:** The ability to consistently climb 15 cm stairs and navigate gaps and high platforms marks a substantial improvement over previous methods. This significantly expands the range of environments humanoid robots can operate in, moving them closer to deployment in real-world, unstructured settings (e.g., construction sites, disaster zones, logistics facilities).
- **Dynamic and Agile Movement:** The successful execution of "parkour" like actions (consecutive jumps) without specific pre-programmed motions highlights the policy's emergent agility and dynamic balance, essential for robust navigation in unpredictable environments.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Efficiency and Accessibility in Research

- **Rapid Training:** Policy training completed in just 3 hours on an RTX 4090 GPU is exceptionally fast for such a complex task. This drastically reduces the computational resources and time required for iterating on policies, accelerating research and development.
- **Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The fact that the policy can be deployed on hardware in a "zero-shot" manner (without additional training phases on real robots) is a major breakthrough. This eliminates the often-laborious and expensive process of bridging the sim-to-real gap, making hardware experimentation more feasible for a wider range of researchers and reducing development costs.
- **Reduced Reliance on Hardware Noise Modeling:** By using elevation maps that are robust to sensor noise and directly querying ground truth heights in simulation, the method bypasses the need for complex and often inaccurate simulation of hardware noise, simplifying the training process and improving real-world performance.

#### 5.4. Cross-Platform Generalization

- Demonstrating the same training process yields effective policies for both Unitree H1 and Fourier GR-1 robots is a highly significant finding. It suggests that the PIM framework and its principles are not robot-specific but offer a generalizable solution.
- This generalizability is crucial for the wider adoption and impact of humanoid robotics, as it means control strategies might be transferrable across different manufacturers and robot designs, reducing the need for ground-up development for each new platform.

#### 5.5. Insights into Emergent Behavior

- The observation that the robot develops coordinated whole-body movements for balance without explicit prior knowledge or imitation learning underscores the power of sophisticated reward design and accurate state prediction. This finding contributes to our understanding of how complex, human-like behaviors can emerge from reinforcement learning, potentially informing future research in embodied intelligence.

#### 5.6. Addressing Key Limitations

- The work effectively tackles the challenge of integrating perception robustly without degrading performance, addressing a major drawback of previous perceptive control methods.
- It moves beyond the limitations of "blind" policies for humanoids, acknowledging and leveraging their unique needs for exteroceptive sensing.

In conclusion, "Learning Humanoid Locomotion with Perceptive Internal Model" delivers a robust, efficient, and generalizable solution for challenging humanoid locomotion. Its potential impact lies in accelerating research, lowering the barrier to entry for hardware deployment, and ultimately enabling humanoid robots to perform increasingly complex and valuable tasks in the real world.
