# Qantara: Bridge-Flow Training for Multi-Paradigm JEPA Control

> [!summary] Summary
> T-Tech developed Qantara, a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) of approximately 21 million parameters, that supports goal-conditioned latent planning, behavior-cloning, and video-inverse composition from a single trained model. The system achieves competitive performance on the LeWM-suite, including a new state-of-the-art on OGBench-Cube with 93.7% SR, while offering goal-blind behavior cloning and video-inverse paths that run 15-65 times faster than latent planning.

## Problem

- Existing JEPA world models are typically limited to a single inference paradigm during training, restricting their flexibility for diverse real-world control tasks.
- The choice of control strategy (e.g., planning vs. direct imitation) must be made before training, which is suboptimal given dynamic, real-world deployment constraints like latency or goal observation availability.
- Different inference paradigms query the model with distinct combinations of clean and noisy inputs, making a single, fixed objective function insufficient for robust multi-paradigm support.

## Method

- Developed **Qantara**, a ~21 million parameter transformer-based ==JEPA world model== that jointly learns latents and their generating actions from pixel data.
- Employed a joint ==bridge-flow matching== training objective, using a ==Brownian-bridge interpolant== for latent states (anchored by previous clean latent) and ==noise-to-data flow matching== for actions.
- Introduced an ==edge-aligned (τ_a, τ_z) sampling== strategy during training, focusing learning capacity on the four inference-relevant edges and a diagonal mode to enhance multi-paradigm support and stability.

## Results

- **Qantara** achieved an average **91.2%** success rate on the LeWM-suite for goal-conditioned planning, setting a new state-of-the-art on OGBench-Cube with **93.7%** SR, surpassing previous JEPA models.
- A single trained Qantara checkpoint effectively supported **three** distinct inference paradigms (planning, behavior cloning, video-inverse composition) without requiring any retraining.
- Goal-blind behavior cloning and video-inverse paths demonstrated **15–65 times** faster inference (**17–24 ms/step**) compared to latent planning (**0.4–1.2 seconds/step**), offering substantial efficiency gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Edge-aligned sampling is critical; it significantly improves performance across inference paradigms compared to uniform sampling, by concentrating training where it is most relevant.
> - A 'corner co-regularization effect' exists where diverse training modes collectively stabilize the shared model trunk, even for inference paths not directly covered by a specific mode.
> - The combination of a residual state-head, zero-initialized final-layer weights, and specific Brownian-bridge parameter values (γ ∈ {1, √2}) is crucial for training stability and robust performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) form the foundation of a category of latent world models utilized for control tasks from raw pixel data. A notable characteristic of existing JEPA world models is their commitment to a single inference paradigm during training. This commitment typically restricts a trained checkpoint to either trajectory optimization within a learned dynamics model or direct behavior cloning, precluding its use for other paradigms without re-training. This limitation means that the choice of control strategy (e.g., planning for complex tasks vs. direct imitation for speed) must be made prior to training, rather than at deployment where real-world constraints become apparent.

Prior research in visuomotor control via world models can be broadly categorized along two axes: the representation of future states (JEPA latent space versus pixel-video space) and the number of inference paradigms supported by a single checkpoint (one versus many).
- **Single-paradigm JEPA world models** typically operate in a sub-billion parameter scale. Examples include PLDM, DINO-WM, and LeWM, which are designed for action-conditional latent dynamics and planning through trajectory optimization. V-JEPA-2-AC represents another approach, where a large video JEPA is post-trained with an action head for direct behavior cloning. These models, despite their capabilities, are constrained by their single-paradigm design.
- **Multi-paradigm models operating in pixel space** typically involve billion-class parameter scales. Models such as PAD, UVA, UWM, DUST, and Cosmos Policy jointly denoise observations and actions, enabling them to serve as forward dynamics models, inverse dynamics models, and behavior policies from a single set of weights. These models address the multi-paradigm challenge but do so at a significantly larger computational scale and by operating directly in pixel space.

The specific niche addressed by Qantara is the empty cell representing **sub-billion parameter JEPA world models that support multiple inference paradigms**. Qantara aims to bridge this gap by providing a single, relatively small (~21 million parameters) JEPA checkpoint capable of serving latent planning, behavior cloning, and inverse dynamics. This positioning offers a balance between the efficiency of latent-space models and the versatility of multi-paradigm control, contrasting with both the single-paradigm JEPA models and the larger, pixel-space multi-paradigm architectures.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the practical challenges encountered during the deployment of visuomotor control systems for robotics. Robots operate under varying inference-time constraints, such as per-step latency requirements, the complexity of the action search space, or the availability of a goal observation. These constraints are often dynamic and cannot be fully anticipated during the model's training phase. A single, versatile learned predictor that can adapt to these changing constraints would offer substantial practical benefits. For instance, the same model could be rolled out as a forward dynamics simulator for planning when a sufficient search budget is available, or it could be queried as a direct behavior-cloning policy when low latency is critical.

The central problem identified is that existing JEPA world models, when trained from raw pixels for control, invariably commit to a single inference paradigm. A checkpoint optimized for one specific mode (e.g., planning) cannot readily serve others (e.g., behavior cloning) without requiring additional retraining or modifications. This design rigidity limits the adaptability and deployment flexibility of current JEPA-based controllers. The underlying obstacle is that each inference paradigm (planning, behavior cloning, inverse dynamics) interrogates the predictor at a distinct combination of clean and noisy inputs on the action and state axes. An objective function tailored for one specific combination might consequently under-train or neglect the others, leading to suboptimal performance or instability when attempting to apply the model to an unanticipated paradigm.

Against this background, the key objectives of the Qantara project are threefold:
1. **Develop a Multi-Paradigm JEPA World Model**: To present Qantara as an end-to-end pixel JEPA world model, specifically a sub-billion parameter architecture, that defers the choice between planning and imitation to inference time. This is achieved through a joint training objective that pairs a Brownian-bridge interpolant on the state axis with noise-to-data flow matching on the action axis, combined with a novel edge-aligned sampling strategy for noise levels.
2. **Demonstrate Comprehensive Performance and Efficiency**: To evaluate Qantara's capability to serve three distinct inference paradigms (goal-conditioned latent planning, behavior-cloning action sampling, and video–inverse composition) from a single checkpoint without retraining. This includes demonstrating competitive performance against state-of-the-art single-paradigm JEPAs on established benchmarks (like the LeWM-suite) with the planning path, while also showcasing the effectiveness and significantly lower inference cost (15–65 times faster) of the behavior-cloning and video–inverse paths.
3. **Investigate Co-regularization Effects**: To identify and quantify the impact of different training modes and architectural choices on model stability and performance across inference paradigms. Specifically, to investigate if and how the multiple training modes required for multi-paradigm support contribute to the overall stability and generalization of the shared model trunk, even for paths not directly covered by a specific mode.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Qantara is designed as a joint model of latents and their generating actions, trained offline using trajectories of pixel observations and actions without explicit rewards or task labels.

**Model Architecture**:
Qantara comprises two main components:
- **Encoder (Eϕ)**: This component maps each input pixel frame `o_t` to a `d`-dimensional latent representation `z_t`.
- **Predictor (fθ)**: This transformer-based model parameterizes the joint distribution `pθ` of actions and future latents along a trajectory. The trajectory is organized into a sequence of blocks, where each block bundles a latent `z_t` with the action `a_t` that produced it. The predictor factors `pθ` autoregressively across blocks, meaning it predicts `(a_t, z_{t+1})` given the preceding context `(z_≤t, a_<t)`. Within each block, attention is bidirectional, allowing for joint denoising of action and state tokens without a fixed conditioning order.

**Joint Bridge-Flow Matching for Training**:
During training, the predictor processes a sequence divided into an observed clean context and a noised continuation to be denoised. Each action and state token is assigned a scalar noise level `τ` ∈ (where `τ=1` signifies a clean endpoint and `τ=0` a source endpoint), which is fed to the predictor via sinusoidal time-encoding and AdaLN modulation.
- **Action Axis Interpolant**: For actions, there is no inherent anchor point at `τ=0`. Therefore, a standard noise-to-data flow-matching interpolant is used: `˜a_t = τ_a_t * a_t + (1 - τ_a_t) * ε_a`, where `ε_a` is Gaussian noise. This approach means the model learns to map from task-agnostic Gaussian noise to the action manifold.
- **Latent State Axis Interpolant**: For latents, the previous clean latent `z_t` serves as a natural anchor at `τ=0`. The model employs a Brownian-bridge interpolant between `z_t` and the target `z_{t+1}`: `˜z_{t+1} = (1 - τ_z_{t+1}) * z_t + τ_z_{t+1} * z_{t+1} + γ * sqrt(τ_z_{t+1} * (1 - τ_z_{t+1})) * ε_z`. This choice ensures that the `τ_z=0` source input (`z_t`) directly matches what the planner provides at deployment, reducing the need for the model to learn a complex mapping from arbitrary noise.

**Training Objective**:
The transformer produces hidden vectors for action and state tokens. A state head emits a residual on the previous clean latent (`^z_{t+1} = z_t + head_z(h_z_{t+1})`), initialized such that the predictor is an identity mapping. An action head emits an action velocity (`v_a_t = head_a(h_a_t)`). The training losses are:
- **Brownian Bridge Loss (L_z_B)**: Measures the squared difference between the predicted and target clean latents: `E ||^z_{t+1} - z_{t+1}||^2`.
- **Flow Matching Loss (L_a_FM)**: Measures the squared difference between the predicted action velocity and the target velocity: `E ||v_a_t - (a_t - ε_a)||^2`.
- **Sketched-Isotropic-Gaussian Regularizer (SIGReg)**: Applied to the batch of clean latents to prevent latent space collapse, a common issue in self-supervised learning.
The total objective function is a weighted sum of these three components: `L = λ_z * L_z_B + λ_a * L_a_FM + λ_SIG * SIGReg`. All components are trained jointly from scratch without pre-trained representations or stop-gradients.

**Inference Paradigms from a Single Checkpoint**:
Qantara's design allows the same trained checkpoint (~21M parameters) to support three qualitatively different inference paradigms, each querying specific regions of the `^2` noise-time square (`(τ_a, τ_z)`).
1. **Behaviour-cloning Sampling (Policy Edge)**: This paradigm samples `a_t` directly. The state slot of block `t+1` is fixed at `z_t` (clean, `τ_z=0`), and the action slot starts as Gaussian noise. `K` Euler steps are performed using the action head to denoise `~a_t` along the `τ_a: 0 → 1` path. This mode is goal-blind.
2. **Video–Inverse Composition (Video and Inverse Edges)**: This approach approximates the behavior policy by factoring it through the predicted next latent.
 - **Video Edge (`τ_a=0, τ_z: 0 → 1`)**: The action slot is filled with pure Gaussian noise (zero information about `a_t`). The predictor functions as an action-free latent dynamics model, predicting `^z_{t+1}`.
 - **Inverse Edge (`τ_z=1, τ_a: 0 → 1`)**: With both `z_t` and `^z_{t+1}` treated as clean endpoints, `K` Euler steps are used to recover `a_t` from Gaussian noise, representing the inverse-dynamics action that bridges `z_t` to `^z_{t+1}`. This mode is also goal-blind.
3. **Latent Planning (Forward Edge)**: Given a goal observation `o_g` encoded to `z_g`, the predictor is repurposed as a forward dynamics simulator. The system searches for an action sequence `a*` that minimizes the terminal cost `||^z_H - z_g||^2`. Each rollout step queries the forward edge (`τ_a=1, τ_z: 0 → 1`), where the candidate clean action `a_t` and previous latent `z_t` are provided, and `^z_{t+1}` is read from the residual head. The Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) is used for action search, combined with receding-horizon Model Predictive Control. This mode is goal-conditioned.

**Edge-aligned (τ_a, τ_z) Sampling**:
To optimize training capacity, the paper introduces an edge-aligned sampling strategy for `(τ_a, τ_z)` during training. Instead of uniformly sampling over the `^2` square (which would concentrate capacity in regions not queried at inference), training is focused on the four edges corresponding to the policy, forward, video, and inverse inference dispatches. A fifth diagonal mode (`τ_a = τ_z`) is also included for co-regularization. Each batch row is replicated across these five modes. A per-block monotone-decreasing `τ`-chain (cumulative product of i.i.d. uniforms) and a random clean-prefix length are used to ensure comprehensive coverage of possible rollout scenarios.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Qantara, a ~21M-parameter JEPA world model, was evaluated on the LeWM-suite across four environments (Two-Room, Push-T, OGBench-Cube, Reacher-Hard) and demonstrated its multi-paradigm control capabilities.

**Goal-conditioned Planning Performance on LeWM-suite (Table 1)**:
Qantara achieved an average success rate (SR) of 91.2 across three train seeds, demonstrating competitive or superior performance compared to previous end-to-end pixel-based world models (PLDM, DINO-WM, LeWM).
- **OGBench-Cube**: Qantara established a new state-of-the-art with 93.7 ± 0.7 SR, representing a +7.7 SR improvement over DINO-WM (the prior SOTA) and +19.7 SR over the published LeWM numbers.
- **Two-Room**: Qantara achieved 100.0 ± 0.0 SR, matching DINO-WM and exceeding the published LeWM score by +13.0 SR.
- **Push-T**: Qantara scored 90.1 ± 1.1 SR, surpassing DINO-WM by +16.1 SR, but trailing the published LeWM score by -5.9 SR. However, when compared to a reproduction of LeWM within Qantara's pipeline, Qantara achieved a +17.0 SR lead.
- **Reacher**: Qantara achieved 80.9 ± 1.8 SR, outperforming DINO-WM by +1.9 SR but trailing the published LeWM score by -5.1 SR. Similar to Push-T, Qantara outperformed its LeWM reproduction by +16.6 SR.
The observed discrepancies with published LeWM scores for Push-T and Reacher, alongside Qantara's performance against the LeWM reproduction, suggest protocol-level differences in evaluation rather than an intrinsic inferiority of Qantara's method.

**Multi-Paradigm Inference from a Single Checkpoint (Table 2)**:
The research demonstrated that a single Qantara checkpoint can effectively serve all three inference paradigms without retraining, each with distinct performance and efficiency characteristics.
- **Push-T**:
 - CEM (goal-conditioned): 90.1 ± 1.1 SR.
 - Behaviour-cloning (goal-blind): 82.1 ± 1.0 SR.
 - Video–inverse (goal-blind): 83.2 ± 0.6 SR.
 On Push-T, the goal-blind BC and video–inverse methods notably surpassed a goal-conditioned behavior cloning (GCBC) baseline (75 SR), attributed to the target information being visible in the pixel observations.
- **Two-Room & Cube**:
 - CEM (goal-conditioned): 100.0 ± 0.0 SR (Two-Room), 93.7 ± 0.7 SR (Cube).
 - Behaviour-cloning (goal-blind): 69.1 ± 0.4 SR (Two-Room), 70.8 ± 0.3 SR (Cube).
 - Video–inverse (goal-blind): 69.3 ± 0.8 SR (Two-Room), 72.8 ± 0.6 SR (Cube).
 For these environments, where the goal state is not visually rendered, the goal-blind dispatches achieved reasonable success rates by reproducing expert continuations within the environmental success tolerances.
- **Reacher**: For the precision-dependent Reacher environment, goal-blind BC and video–inverse dispatches exhibited near-noise performance (6.1/4.8 SR), indicating that planning remains the reliable approach for tasks requiring precise goal matching without direct visual goal input.
- **Inference Efficiency**: The goal-blind BC and video–inverse paths operated at 17–24 ms per environment step, representing a 15–65 times speedup compared to CEM's 0.4–1.2 seconds per step, highlighting a key benefit for latency-sensitive applications.

**Ablation Studies on Design Choices (Table 3, App. D, E, G)**:
The study investigated the impact of several design choices on Qantara's performance and stability:
- **Edge-aligned Sampling**: Replacing the five edge-aligned training modes with uniform sampling across `^2` (at matched compute) significantly degraded performance. Push-T CEM dropped by 36.8 SR (90.1 to 53.3), Push-T video–inverse by 33.5 SR (83.2 to 49.7), and Reacher CEM by 23.4 SR (80.9 to 57.5). This supports the importance of concentrating training capacity on inference-relevant regions.
- **Mode-set Ablation**: Removing any single mode (`policy`, `inverse`, or `joint`) from the five-mode sampling set destabilized Push-T training across *all three inference paths*, including those not directly querying the removed mode's region. For example, dropping the `policy` mode caused CEM performance to collapse with a population standard deviation exceeding 15 SR (27.2 ± 32.5 SR). This finding suggests a **corner co-regularization effect**, where the training modes collectively stabilize the shared model trunk, rather than merely improving performance on their specific inference paths. Only dropping the `video` mode preserved all three Push-T paths' performance and stability.
- **Output Residual and Zero-Initialization**: The combination of a residual state-head form (`^z_{t+1} = z_t + head_z(...)`) and zero-initialized final-layer weights was found to be crucial for stability. Ablating this residual (head emits `^z_{t+1}` directly) regressed the suite average by 5.6 SR and Push-T by 15.2 SR, often leading to training instability.
- **Brownian Bridge Parameter (γ)**: While `γ=1` (stochastic-interpolant canonical) and `γ=√2` (Schrödinger-bridge canonical) yielded similar robust performance, off-canonical values (`γ ∈ {0, 0.5, 2}`) consistently led to training instability and collapse on Push-T and, for `γ=2`, on Two-Room.
- **Predictor Dropout**: Removing dropout (`p=0`) from the predictor improved the suite average from 84.9 to 88.3 SR, leading to its adoption as the default setting.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Qantara represents a significant step in the field of visuomotor control by addressing a previously unfilled niche: a sub-billion parameter Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture capable of multi-paradigm control from a single checkpoint. This capability fundamentally alters the deployment paradigm for JEPA world models, moving them from single-purpose planners to flexible multi-paradigm controllers.

**Key Contributions and Implications**:
- **Enhanced Deployment Flexibility**: Qantara enables deferring the critical choice between planning-based control and imitation-based control to inference time. This allows robots to dynamically adapt their control strategy based on real-time operational constraints such as latency requirements, available computational budget, or the presence/absence of explicit goal observations. For instance, a robot could leverage rapid behavior-cloning for routine tasks where speed is paramount, and switch to more computationally intensive planning for novel or complex goal-reaching scenarios.
- **Efficiency and Scale**: By demonstrating multi-paradigm capability within a ~21M-parameter model, Qantara provides a significantly more efficient alternative to existing pixel-space multi-paradigm models, which typically require billion-scale parameters. The 15–65x speedup of goal-blind paths further underscores its practical utility for low-latency applications.
- **Novel Training Insights**: The research yielded important findings regarding the training methodology:
 - **Edge-aligned Sampling**: The effectiveness of concentrating training capacity on inference-relevant regions of the noise-time square is a validated principle for optimizing multi-task training objectives.
 - **Corner Co-regularization Effect**: The discovery that dropping certain training modes destabilizes performance across *all* inference paths, even those not directly covered by the dropped mode's region, highlights a crucial co-regularization role of diverse training signals. This suggests that exposing the shared model trunk to a variety of inference-relevant contexts (e.g., policy, inverse, joint) fosters a more robust and generally capable representation, even if a deployment scenario primarily relies on only one specific paradigm (e.g., planning). This finding has broader implications for multi-task learning in deep neural networks.
 - **Brownian Bridge for State Axis**: The use of a Brownian-bridge interpolant anchored by the previous clean latent `z_t` on the state axis aligns the training process with the inference queries of a planner, improving efficiency and stability by avoiding the need to learn mappings from arbitrary noise.
- **Robustness of Design Choices**: The ablation studies systematically validated the criticality of architectural elements like the residual state-head with zero-initialization for training stability and the sensitivity to the Brownian-bridge parameter `γ`.

**Limitations**:
The paper acknowledges several limitations that suggest future research directions:
- **Single-Block Denoising**: The current implementation operates by denoising one block per control step. Extending this to chunked multi-block inference could enable longer-horizon planning and more efficient processing.
- **Simulation-Only Evaluation**: The evaluation was conducted solely in simulation using near-expert offline datasets. Real-robot transfer and pre-training with natural video data (as explored in larger models like V-JEPA-2-AC) remain untested aspects.
- **Parameter Scale**: The recipe's effectiveness is demonstrated at the ~21M-parameter scale. Its scalability to significantly larger JEPA backbones (e.g., those found in V-JEPA-2-AC) has not been investigated.

In conclusion, Qantara advances the state of JEPA world models by demonstrating a compact, multi-paradigm controller that offers flexibility and efficiency at deployment time. Its novel training approach and insights into co-regularization effects contribute to a deeper understanding of how to train robust and versatile latent-space world models.
