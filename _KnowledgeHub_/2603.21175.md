# Reward Sharpness-Aware Fine-Tuning for Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced Reward Sharpness-Aware Fine-Tuning (RSA-FT) to combat reward hacking in text-to-image diffusion models, enhancing alignment with human preferences. This method robustifies reward gradients by applying perturbations in both image and parameter spaces, leading to consistent quantitative and qualitative improvements across various RDRL frameworks and diffusion architectures.

## Problem

- Aligning Text-to-Image (T2I) diffusion models with human preferences is challenging, despite their ability to generate high-quality content.
- Reward-centric diffusion reinforcement learning (RDRL) methods often suffer from 'reward hacking,' where reward scores increase without corresponding improvements in perceptual quality or true human preference.
- Reward hacking is hypothesized to stem from the non-robustness or 'sharpness' of the reward model's gradients, allowing models to exploit minor imperfections.

## Method

- Proposed Reward ==Sharpness-Aware== Fine-Tuning (**RSA-FT**) to mitigate reward hacking by robustifying reward model gradients through 'flattening' the reward landscape.
- Implemented this flattening by considering worst-case perturbations in both the input image space (adversarial training-like) and the diffusion model's parameter space (Sharpness-Aware Minimization-like).
- Combined these two perturbation types into a joint optimization objective, creating a 'doubly robust' reward optimization framework that functions as a plug-and-play module for existing RDRL methods.

## Results

- RSA-FT consistently improved all RDRL baselines (DRaFT-K, AlignProp, ReFL, DRTune) across diverse diffusion models (SD1.5, SDXL, SD3, Flux.1-dev), boosting metrics like HPSv2.1 (e.g., AlignProp + RSA-FT on HPD increased HPSv2.1 from **24.93** to **32.02**).
- Qualitative analyses showed that RSA-FT-enhanced models generated images that were visually sharper, more coherent, and exhibited fewer reward-induced artifacts (e.g., distorted text, malformed body parts).
- Ablation studies confirmed that both image-space and parameter-space perturbations independently contribute to performance gains, with their joint application yielding the most substantial improvements.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reward hacking in RDRL is empirically linked to the 'sharpness' of the reward model's landscape, with higher sharpness correlating negatively with true human preference.
> - Robustifying the reward model's gradients through local perturbations in either image or parameter space can effectively alleviate reward hacking without necessitating reward model retraining.
> - A synergistic effect is observed when combining both image-space and parameter-space perturbations, leading to more significant and consistent improvements in generative quality and human preference alignment.
