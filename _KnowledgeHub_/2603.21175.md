# Reward Sharpness-Aware Fine-Tuning for Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced Reward Sharpness-Aware Fine-Tuning (RSA-FT) to combat reward hacking in text-to-image diffusion models, enhancing alignment with human preferences. This method robustifies reward gradients by applying perturbations in both image and parameter spaces, leading to consistent quantitative and qualitative improvements across various RDRL frameworks and diffusion architectures.

## Problem

- Aligning Text-to-Image (T2I) diffusion models with human preferences is challenging, despite their ability to generate high-quality content.
- Reward-centric diffusion reinforcement learning (RDRL) methods often suffer from 'reward hacking,' where reward scores increase without corresponding improvements in perceptual quality or true human preference.
- Reward hacking is hypothesized to stem from the non-robustness or 'sharpness' of the reward model's gradients, allowing models to exploit minor imperfections.

## Method

- Proposed Reward ==Sharpness-Aware== Fine-Tuning (**RSA-FT**) to mitigate reward hacking by robustifying reward model gradients through 'flattening' the reward landscape.
- Implemented this flattening by considering worst-case perturbations in both the input image space (adversarial training-like) and the diffusion model's parameter space (Sharpness-Aware Minimization-like).
- Combined these two perturbation types into a joint optimization objective, creating a 'doubly robust' reward optimization framework that functions as a plug-and-play module for existing RDRL methods.

## Results

- RSA-FT consistently improved all RDRL baselines (DRaFT-K, AlignProp, ReFL, DRTune) across diverse diffusion models (SD1.5, SDXL, SD3, Flux.1-dev), boosting metrics like HPSv2.1 (e.g., AlignProp + RSA-FT on HPD increased HPSv2.1 from **24.93** to **32.02**).
- Qualitative analyses showed that RSA-FT-enhanced models generated images that were visually sharper, more coherent, and exhibited fewer reward-induced artifacts (e.g., distorted text, malformed body parts).
- Ablation studies confirmed that both image-space and parameter-space perturbations independently contribute to performance gains, with their joint application yielding the most substantial improvements.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reward hacking in RDRL is empirically linked to the 'sharpness' of the reward model's landscape, with higher sharpness correlating negatively with true human preference.
> - Robustifying the reward model's gradients through local perturbations in either image or parameter space can effectively alleviate reward hacking without necessitating reward model retraining.
> - A synergistic effect is observed when combining both image-space and parameter-space perturbations, leading to more significant and consistent improvements in generative quality and human preference alignment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work is situated within the field of generative artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on Text-to-Image (T2I) diffusion models and their alignment with human preferences. Recent advancements in diffusion models have led to the generation of high-quality content, but aligning these models with human preferences remains a significant challenge.

Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) has been successful in aligning large language models (LLMs) with human preferences. This success has inspired the development of reward-centric diffusion reinforcement learning (RDRL) methods for diffusion models. RDRL aims to fine-tune diffusion model parameters by maximizing a reward signal derived from a reward model (RM), which is typically trained on human annotations as a scalable surrogate for direct human evaluation. This approach allows generative models to produce outputs that are more aligned with subjective human judgments.

However, RDRL methods are susceptible to "reward hacking," a phenomenon where the reward score of generated images increases without a corresponding improvement in perceptual quality or alignment with true human preferences. This issue limits the reliability and effectiveness of RDRL. The paper draws an analogy between reward hacking and adversarial attacks observed in deep learning classifiers, where small, imperceptible input perturbations can drastically alter model predictions. Prior research has indicated that non-robust classifiers degrade sample quality under guidance, while robust classifiers can mitigate this.

The current work addresses the problem of reward hacking in RDRL by drawing inspiration from adversarial robustness and generalization techniques like Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) and randomized smoothing. It integrates these concepts to develop a method that enhances the robustness of reward model gradients, thereby mitigating reward hacking without requiring the retraining of the reward model itself. This positions the research as an effort to improve the foundational stability and reliability of human preference alignment in T2I diffusion models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address and mitigate the problem of reward hacking in reward-centric diffusion reinforcement learning (RDRL). Reward hacking is characterized by an increase in the reward model's score for generated images that does not correspond to actual improvements in perceptual quality or alignment with human preferences. This phenomenon compromises the utility of RDRL frameworks, as models can learn to exploit imperfections in the reward model rather than genuinely improving their generative capabilities.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations and analogies:
- **Vulnerability of RDRL:** The authors observe that RDRL remains vulnerable to reward hacking, which is a significant barrier to achieving reliable alignment of diffusion models with human preferences.
- **Non-Robustness of Reward Model Gradients:** The paper hypothesizes that reward hacking arises from the non-robustness of reward model gradients. Specifically, the reward landscape with respect to the input image can be "sharp," meaning small perturbations in the input image or model parameters can lead to substantial changes in the reward score. This sharpness allows the diffusion model to find "adversarial" directions that inflate the reward score without genuine quality improvement.
- **Analogy to Adversarial Attacks:** The authors draw a parallel between reward hacking and adversarial attacks on classifiers, where models are sensitive to subtle input perturbations. Just as non-robust classifiers can lead to degraded sample quality under classifier guidance, non-robust reward models can lead to reward hacking in RDRL.
- **Impracticality of Retraining Robust Reward Models:** While adversarial training can make classifiers more robust, applying this to reward models for human preference alignment is deemed impractical due to the extensive expressive models and labeled data required.
- **Inspiration from Robustness Techniques (Randomized Smoothing & SAM):** The research draws inspiration from methods like randomized smoothing, which enhances classifier robustness without retraining by smoothing predictions, and Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM), which improves generalization by promoting flatter loss landscapes in the parameter space. These techniques suggest that robustifying a model's gradients or its landscape can improve its reliability.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives are:
1. To empirically demonstrate that reward hacking in RDRL is linked to the sharpness of the reward model's landscape.
2. To develop a method that exploits gradients from a robustified (flattened) reward model without requiring its explicit retraining.
3. To introduce techniques that leverage parameter perturbations of the diffusion model and perturbations of its generated samples to achieve this flattening.
4. To demonstrate that these methods, both independently and jointly, alleviate reward hacking and improve the robustness and human preference alignment of RDRL.
5. To create a framework, RSA-FT (Reward Sharpness-Aware Fine-Tuning), that is simple, broadly compatible with existing RDRL frameworks, and consistently enhances their reliability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this research focuses on mitigating reward hacking in RDRL by exploiting gradients from a robustified, or "flattened," reward model. This approach is built upon the hypothesis that reward hacking is linked to the sharpness of the reward landscape.

#### 3.1. Analyzing Reward Sharpness

The initial step involves hypothesizing and empirically validating that reward models generalize best where their reward landscape is locally flat, while sharp regions indicate misalignment with true human preferences.
- **Quantification:** Reward sharpness ($S_1$) is quantified as the expected difference between the reward at a given input and the minimum reward within a local neighborhood around that input. This is approximated by a one-step update, considering a worst-case perturbation in the direction of the negative reward gradient: $S_1 \approx E_{x \sim D} [r(x) - r(x - \rho \frac{\nabla_x r(x)}{\|\nabla_x r(x)\|})]$.
- **Empirical Validation:** Stable Diffusion 1.5 was fine-tuned with DRaFT-K using HPSv2 as the reward model. Reward sharpness ($S_1$) was tracked alongside proxy evaluators for human preference (PickScore and ImageReward). A negative correlation was observed, where higher sharpness corresponded to lower preference quality (Pearson r\_corr = -0.802 for PickScore and -0.669 for ImageReward). This supports the hypothesis that sharp reward landscapes lead to poorer generalization and reward hacking.

#### 3.2. Reward Sharpness-Aware Fine-Tuning (RSA-FT)

Based on the analysis, RSA-FT is proposed to fine-tune the diffusion model using a flattened reward function. The core idea is to maximize a "flattened reward" $\tilde{r}_d(x, c) := \min_{d(x, x') < \rho} r(x', c)$, which seeks the minimum reward within a local neighborhood defined by a distance metric $d(\cdot, \cdot)$. Two specific instantiations of this flattening are explored:

- **Image-Space Perturbation (Adversarial Training-like):** This approach flattens the reward landscape by considering worst-case perturbations in the input image space.
 - The objective is to maximize $E_{c, x_T \sim N(0,I)} [\min_{\|\delta\|<\rho} r(x_0(x_T, c; \theta) + \delta, c)]$.
 - In practice, this inner minimization is approximated by a one-step adversarial perturbation $\delta_{x_0} = -\rho \frac{\nabla_{x_0} r(x_0(x_T, c; \theta), c)}{\|\nabla_{x_0} r(x_0(x_T, c; \theta), c)\|}$. The diffusion model is then updated to maximize the reward of the adversarially perturbed image $r(x_0(x_T, c; \theta) + \delta_{x_0}, c)$.

- **Parameter-Space Perturbation (SAM-like):** This approach flattens the reward landscape by considering worst-case perturbations in the diffusion model's parameter space.
 - The objective is to maximize $E_{c, x_T \sim N(0,I)} [\min_{\|\epsilon\|<\rho_\omega} r(x_0(x_T, c; \theta + \epsilon), c)]$.
 - Similar to SAM, the inner minimization is approximated by a one-step perturbation $\epsilon_\theta = -\rho_\omega \frac{\nabla_\theta r(x_0(x_T, c; \theta), c)}{\|\nabla_\theta r(x_0(x_T, c; \theta), c)\|}$. The model is then updated to maximize the reward from these perturbed parameters $r(x_0(x_T, c; \theta + \epsilon_\theta), c)$, with the gradient computation for $\epsilon_\theta$ detached from the outer optimization.

- **Joint Perturbation (RSA-FT):** The paper proposes combining both image-space and parameter-space flattening, arguing that they offer complementary benefits. The final joint objective is to maximize $E_{c, x_T \sim N(0,I)} [r(x_0(x_T, c; \theta + \epsilon_\theta) + \delta_{x_0}, c)]$. This formulation aims to enforce smoothness in both the image and parameter spaces, leading to a "doubly robust" reward optimization.

#### 3.3. Algorithm Implementation

Algorithm 1 outlines the RSA-FT procedure:
1. Sample noise $x_T$ and condition $c$.
2. Generate image $x_0$ using the current diffusion model parameters $\theta$.
3. Compute the input-space perturbation $\delta_{x_0}$ using the gradient of the reward model with respect to $x_0$.
4. Compute the weight-space perturbation $\epsilon_\theta$ using the gradient of the reward model with respect to $\theta$.
5. Update $\theta$ by performing gradient ascent on the reward of the doubly perturbed output: $r(x_0(x_T, c; \theta + \epsilon_\theta) + \delta_{x_0}, c)$.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Backbones:** Stable Diffusion v1.5 (SD1.5), SDXL, Stable Diffusion 3 (SD3), and Flux.1-dev.
- **RDRL Baselines:** ReFL, DRaFT-K, AlignProp, and DRTune.
- **Reward Model:** HPSv2 model for training.
- **Datasets:** DrawBench and HPSv2 benchmark test set (HPD).
- **Evaluation Metrics:** HPSv2, PickScore, and ImageReward (model-based proxies for human preference), complemented by a limited human evaluation.
- **Hyperparameters:** Perturbation radii $\rho$ and $\rho_\omega$ were found to be optimal at $10^{-2}$ through ablation studies.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the efficacy of RSA-FT in mitigating reward hacking and improving the performance of RDRL frameworks.

#### 4.1. Reward Sharpness and Human Preference

- Empirical analysis established a negative correlation between reward sharpness and human preference quality. Specifically, higher sharpness in the HPSv2 reward model was associated with lower scores from independent preference models (PickScore and ImageReward), with Pearson correlation coefficients of -0.802 and -0.669, respectively. This supports the hypothesis that sharp reward landscapes are indicative of misalignment with true human preferences and contribute to reward hacking.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Improvements with RSA-FT

- **Consistent Enhancement across Baselines (SD1.5):** RSA-FT consistently improved all RDRL baselines (Draft-LV, AlignProp, ReFL, DRTune) on both DrawBench and HPD datasets when using SD1.5. For instance, AlignProp, when combined with RSA-FT on HPD, saw HPSv2.1 increase from 24.93 to 32.02 and ImageReward from 0.032 to 0.528. This indicates that RSA-FT not only boosts performance but also effectively mitigates reward hacking, leading to improvements across multiple preference metrics rather than metric-specific overfitting.
- **Generalization to Larger Backbones (SDXL, SD3, Flux):** The method demonstrated consistent performance gains when applied to larger backbones such as SDXL, SD3, and Flux.1-dev, even at higher resolutions (1024x1024). For example, on SDXL (Drawbench), DRTune's HPSv2.1 improved from 30.04 to 31.58, and ImageReward from 0.804 to 0.944. This suggests broad compatibility across different architectures (U-Net, MMDiT Transformers) and scales.
- **Multi-Reward Scenarios:** RSA-FT also showed robustness and consistent gains in multi-reward optimization settings (e.g., combining HPSv2, Aesthetics, and PickScore), outperforming RDRL baselines in dual- and triple-reward configurations.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- Ablation studies confirmed that both image-space perturbation (AT-like) and parameter-space perturbation (SAM-like) independently contribute to performance improvements. Their combination within RSA-FT yielded the most significant gains, demonstrating a synergistic effect in robustifying reward optimization.
- Hyperparameter sensitivity analysis for perturbation radii ($\rho$ and $\rho_\omega$) indicated that values around $10^{-2}$ provided optimal performance, and the method exhibited relatively low sensitivity within a reasonable range.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results

- Qualitative comparisons (Figure 1, 6, 8, 9, 10) illustrate that models enhanced with RSA-FT generate images that are visually sharper, more coherent, and appear more natural to human perception.
- RSA-FT reduced common reward-induced artifacts such as distorted text or malformed body parts, leading to improved text-image alignment and overall perceptual quality across diverse prompts and RDRL frameworks.

#### 4.5. Fairness and Safety Considerations

- Analysis of distributional divergence using FID and KID metrics showed that RDRL with RSA-FT exhibits divergence from the pretrained model comparable to or lower than standard RDRL baselines. This suggests that the method does not exacerbate existing biases or safety concerns relative to current RDRL approaches.

#### 4.6. Human Preference Study

- A limited-scale human evaluation involving 17 annotators indicated a preference for images generated with RSA-FT compared to baseline RDRL methods in terms of both visual quality and text alignment. However, the authors explicitly state that this study is "limited in scale and not statistically powered for definitive conclusions," serving as supporting evidence rather than a comprehensive assessment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces Reward Sharpness-Aware Fine-Tuning (RSA-FT) as a principled approach to address reward hacking in reward-centric diffusion reinforcement learning (RDRL), offering several significant contributions and potential impacts:

- **Principled Solution to Reward Hacking:** The work establishes a theoretical connection between reward hacking in RDRL and adversarial non-robustness, identifying the sharpness of the reward landscape as a root cause. By proposing to flatten this landscape through both input- and parameter-space perturbations, RSA-FT offers a theoretically grounded solution to a known challenge in RDRL. This perspective can guide future research into the robustness of reward models.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Alignment:** RSA-FT consistently improves the alignment of generated images with human preferences across various RDRL frameworks and diffusion backbones. By mitigating reward hacking, it ensures that increases in reward scores correspond more reliably to genuine improvements in perceptual quality and text-prompt alignment, leading to more trustworthy and higher-quality generative models.
- **Broad Compatibility and Plug-and-Play Integration:** A key advantage of RSA-FT is its compatibility with diverse existing RDRL frameworks (e.g., DRaFT-K, AlignProp, ReFL, DRTune) and diffusion backbones (SD1.5, SDXL, SD3, Flux.1-dev). It functions as a plug-and-play module, meaning it can be seamlessly integrated into current pipelines without requiring architectural modifications or extensive retraining of reward models. This broad applicability facilitates its adoption and potential for widespread impact.
- **Improved Generative Quality:** The qualitative results demonstrate that RSA-FT leads to visually superior generations, characterized by increased sharpness, coherence, and reduction of artifacts often associated with reward-driven optimization. This directly translates to more practical and aesthetically pleasing outputs from T2I models.
- **Efficiency:** The method robustifies the reward model without requiring its retraining, which can be computationally intensive and require extensive labeled data. Instead, it leverages gradients from a flattened version of the *existing* reward model, offering a relatively efficient approach to enhance robustness.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work opens avenues for future exploration, such as extending the framework to other RL paradigms (PPO, DPO), investigating alternative smoothing techniques (e.g., Gaussian averaging), and exploring selective sharpness-aware weighting for further robustness and interpretability.

**Limitations Acknowledged by the Authors:**
- The primary reliance on model-based metrics (HPSv2, PickScore, ImageReward) as proxies for human preference, which are acknowledged to be imperfect.
- The human evaluation conducted was limited in scale (17 annotators) and not statistically powered for definitive conclusions, serving as supporting evidence rather than comprehensive validation of human alignment.
- The current approach uniformly applies flattened rewards, and future work could explore selective weighting.
- The framework currently focuses on single-reward model scenarios, though the authors suggest its extension to multi-reward settings.

Despite these acknowledged limitations, the introduction of RSA-FT provides a valuable contribution to the field of generative AI by offering a systematic and effective method to enhance the robustness and reliability of reward-centric diffusion model fine-tuning.
