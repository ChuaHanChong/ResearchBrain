# ARM: Adaptive Reasoning Model

> [!summary] Summary
> The Adaptive Reasoning Model (ARM) enables large reasoning models to adaptively select appropriate reasoning formats based on task difficulty, reducing token generation by an average of 30% and up to 70% on easy tasks while maintaining strong performance. This approach, using an adapted reinforcement learning algorithm, mitigates the "overthinking" problem and achieves a ~2x training speedup.

## Problem

- Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) suffer from an "overthinking" problem, applying elaborate "Long Chain-of-Thought" (Long CoT) reasoning uniformly across all tasks regardless of difficulty.
- This indiscriminate application leads to excessive token generation, substantial computational overhead, increased inference latency, and can sometimes degrade performance.
- Existing solutions often rely on manual token budget estimations or require specialized, length-constrained model training, which are neither practical nor adaptively efficient.

## Method

- ARM employs a two-stage training framework: Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) to understand four reasoning formats (Direct Answer, Short CoT, Code, Long CoT), followed by Reinforcement Learning (RL).
- It introduces ==Adaptive Group Relative Policy Optimization (Ada-GRPO)==, an RL algorithm that overcomes =="format collapse"== by encouraging exploration of diverse reasoning formats through a ==format diversity scaling factor==.
- ARM supports three inference modes: Adaptive (autonomous selection), Instruction-Guided (user-specified format), and Consensus-Guided (efficient formats first, then Long CoT if disagreement).

## Results

- ARM reduces token cost by an average of **~30%** and up to **~70%** on easy tasks, while maintaining reasoning performance comparable to models that exclusively use Long CoT.
- The model effectively learns to adapt its reasoning strategy, primarily using efficient formats for easy commonsense tasks and shifting to elaborate formats like Code and Long CoT for complex problems.
- Ada-GRPO achieves approximately a **~2x** speedup in training time compared to traditional GRPO by encouraging the generation of shorter, more efficient responses during the RL roll-out stage.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Reasoning Models can be trained to dynamically adjust their reasoning depth and token usage based on task complexity, moving beyond a uniform reasoning approach.
> - Reinforcement learning algorithms can be specifically adapted, via mechanisms like diversity scaling, to prevent 'format collapse' and encourage the balanced utilization of multiple problem-solving strategies.
> - Integrating and adaptively selecting from a palette of reasoning formats allows AI models to achieve an optimal balance between high accuracy and computational efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical and contemporary challenge in the field of large language models (LLMs) and, more specifically, large reasoning models (LRMs). The emergence of LRMs like OpenAI-o1 and DeepSeek-R1 has revolutionized complex problem-solving by leveraging "Long Chain-of-Thought" (Long CoT) reasoning, where models generate extensive intermediate steps to arrive at a solution. This approach, often enhanced by Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) and test-time scaling, has demonstrated impressive performance on challenging tasks.

However, a significant drawback, termed the "overthinking" problem, has become apparent. These powerful models, primarily trained on tasks requiring intensive reasoning, tend to apply Long CoT uniformly across *all* tasks, regardless of their actual difficulty. This leads to excessive and unnecessary token generation, incurring substantial computational overhead (inference cost) and, in some cases, even introducing noise that can degrade performance. Existing attempts to mitigate this issue often rely on manual token budget estimations or require specialized, length-constrained model training, which are not always practical or adaptive.

ARM directly intervenes in this landscape by proposing a novel solution that enables models to *adaptively* control their token usage and reasoning depth based on task complexity. Instead of forcing a single reasoning strategy (like Long CoT), ARM integrates multiple reasoning formats of varying efficiency (Direct Answer, Short CoT, Code, and Long CoT) and learns to select the most appropriate one. This positions ARM as a significant advancement towards more efficient and autonomous AI, moving beyond the "one-size-fits-all" approach of current LRMs. It aims to bridge the gap between high-performance reasoning models and practical, cost-effective deployment, a crucial step for broader adoption of advanced AI. The work builds upon and improves reinforcement learning techniques, specifically adapting Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to overcome its "format collapse" limitation, thereby enhancing the model's ability to diversify reasoning strategies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the Adaptive Reasoning Model (ARM) are:

- **Address the "Overthinking" Problem:** The core motivation is to tackle the inefficiency of current LRMs that indiscriminately apply elaborate reasoning (Long CoT) to all tasks, including simple ones. This "overthinking" leads to wasted computational resources, increased inference latency, and sometimes even reduced accuracy due to superfluous information.
- **Enable Adaptive Reasoning Format Selection:** To counter overthinking, ARM aims to develop a reasoning model capable of autonomously selecting the most suitable reasoning format (from a predefined set of efficient and elaborate options) based on the inherent difficulty and nature of the task at hand. The goal is to be efficient for easy tasks and thorough for complex ones.
- **Balance Performance and Computational Efficiency:** A key objective is to maintain or even improve the high reasoning performance characteristic of LRMs while significantly reducing token generation costs. This involves finding an optimal trade-off where accuracy is not sacrificed for efficiency, and vice-versa.
- **Improve Reinforcement Learning for Diverse Reasoning:** The authors are motivated to overcome limitations in existing RL techniques, specifically the "format collapse" observed in GRPO, which tends to favor a single, high-performing format. They aim to develop an adapted RL algorithm (Ada-GRPO) that encourages exploration and maintenance of diverse reasoning strategies.
- **Enhance AI Autonomy and User Control:** By enabling adaptive selection without human intervention, ARM aims to contribute to more autonomous AI systems. Additionally, providing "Instruction-Guided" and "Consensus-Guided" modes offers users more flexibility and control over the reasoning process when specific outcomes (e.g., maximum efficiency or maximum performance) are desired.
- **Accelerate Training:** A secondary but significant motivation is to improve the training efficiency of reasoning models by leveraging the learned adaptive behavior during the roll-out stage, reducing the overall computational time required for RL.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ARM employs a novel two-stage training framework to achieve adaptive reasoning format selection:

**Stage 1: Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) for Reasoning Formats Understanding**
- **Objective:** To equip a base language model with a foundational understanding of various reasoning formats and their structures.
- **Data:** A diverse dataset of 10.8K questions (AQuA-Rat) is used, with each question annotated with solutions in four distinct reasoning formats. GPT-4o and DeepSeek-R1 are used to generate Code and Long CoT rationales, respectively, which are then filtered for quality and correctness.
- **Reasoning Formats:**
 - **Direct Answer:** The most efficient, providing only the final answer without intermediate steps.
 - **Short CoT:** Offers a brief reasoning chain leading to the answer, effective for certain problems.
 - **Code:** Leverages code-based reasoning, which provides a structured and verifiable approach.
 - **Long CoT:** The most elaborate format, involving detailed, iterative reasoning, self-reflection, and backtracking, suited for highly complex tasks. Special tokens (e.g., `<Code></Code>`) encapsulate the reasoning for each format.
- **Training Details:** SFT is applied using LLAMAFACTORY, with a batch size of 128, a learning rate of 2e-4, cosine learning rate scheduler, LoRA for parameter efficiency, and DeepSpeed ZeRO-3 for optimization.

**Stage 2: Reinforcement Learning (RL) for Encouraging Efficient Format Selection**
- **Objective:** To enable the SFT-trained model to dynamically select the most appropriate reasoning format based on task difficulty while maintaining accuracy.
- **Algorithm: Adaptive Group Relative Policy Optimization (Ada-GRPO):** This is a key methodological innovation, adapting the traditional GRPO algorithm to address the "format collapse" problem.
 - **Traditional GRPO Limitation (Format Collapse):** Standard GRPO primarily optimizes for accuracy. In a multi-format setting, if Long CoT consistently yields the highest accuracy, GRPO would increasingly reinforce its use, leading to an over-reliance on Long CoT and the suppression of more efficient formats.
 - **Ada-GRPO Solution:** Ada-GRPO introduces a "format diversity scaling factor" ($\alpha_i(t)$) that amplifies rewards for less frequently sampled reasoning formats. This encourages exploration and prevents premature convergence on a single format.
 - **Format Diversity Scaling Factor ($G/F(o_i)$):** Upweights rewards for formats that appear less often within a sampled group of outputs, promoting their selection.
 - **Decay Factor ($decay_i(t)$):** Gradually reduces the influence of the diversity scaling factor over training time. This is crucial to prevent long-term misalignment, ensuring that after initial exploration, the model can still converge towards higher-accuracy formats for truly complex tasks without perpetually over-rewarding less accurate but diverse options.
 - **Training Speedup:** By encouraging the selection of more efficient formats (Direct Answer, Short CoT, Code) during the roll-out stage, Ada-GRPO generates shorter responses, which significantly reduces the computational cost of the RL training phase, leading to approximately a 2x speedup compared to GRPO.
- **RL Datasets:** To prevent data leakage and ensure generalization, three additional datasets are used exclusively for RL: CommonsenseQA (CSQA), GSM8K, and MATH, covering diverse difficulty levels.
- **Training Details:** RL is performed using the VeRL framework, with a batch size of 1024, 8 rollouts per prompt, a maximum rollout length of 4096 tokens, a mini-batch size of 180, a KL loss coefficient of 1e-3, and 9 epochs.

**Inference Modes:** ARM supports three distinct reasoning modes:
1. **Adaptive Mode (Default):** ARM autonomously selects the most appropriate reasoning format for each task.
2. **Instruction-Guided Mode:** Users can explicitly specify the desired reasoning format using special tokens (e.g., `<Long CoT>`), ideal when the appropriate format is known for a batch of tasks.
3. **Consensus-Guided Mode:** ARM first generates answers using the three efficient formats. If they agree, that answer is adopted. If they disagree, ARM defaults to Long CoT to resolve the task, prioritizing accuracy at a higher token cost.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents compelling findings that demonstrate ARM's effectiveness and efficiency:

- **Mitigation of "Overthinking" and Enhanced Efficiency:** Current LRMs (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, DS-R1-Distill) suffer from "overthinking," consuming excessive tokens even on easy tasks without proportionate performance gains. ARM, in contrast, significantly reduces token cost—by an average of ~30% and up to ~70% on easy tasks—while maintaining comparable performance to models exclusively using Long CoT. For example, ARM-7B achieves comparable performance to DS-R1-Distill-7B but uses only 27.8% of the tokens on average.
- **Adaptive Format Selection by Task Difficulty:** ARM effectively learns to adapt its reasoning strategy based on task difficulty. For easy commonsense tasks (CSQA, OpenBookQA), it primarily uses efficient formats like Direct Answer and Short CoT. As task difficulty increases to medium (GSM8K, MATH, SVAMP, BBH) and hard (AIME’25), ARM gradually shifts towards more elaborate formats like Code and Long CoT. This is visually confirmed by the analysis of format distribution across task difficulties (Figure 2).
- **Comparable Performance to GRPO-trained Models:** ARM trained with Ada-GRPO achieves accuracy comparable to models trained with traditional GRPO, which heavily relies on Long CoT. This indicates that Ada-GRPO successfully prevents format collapse and promotes efficiency without compromising the model's overall reasoning capabilities, even for complex tasks where Long CoT is genuinely needed.
- **Significant Training Speedup:** Ada-GRPO leads to approximately a ~2x speedup in training time compared to traditional GRPO. This is attributed to the reduced average response length generated during the roll-out stage, as Ada-GRPO encourages the selection of more efficient and shorter reasoning formats.
- **Effectiveness of Ada-GRPO:** The format diversity scaling factor and decay mechanism in Ada-GRPO successfully address the "format collapse" issue present in traditional GRPO, where Long CoT would quickly dominate. Ada-GRPO ensures adequate exploration of all reasoning formats, leading to a better balance between effectiveness and efficiency (Figure 1b).
- **Versatile Reasoning Modes:**
 - **Adaptive Mode:** Demonstrates a superior balance between high accuracy and efficient token usage, autonomously selecting appropriate formats.
 - **Instruction-Guided Mode:** Performs well when users can accurately specify the optimal reasoning format, showing that ARM retains strong capabilities across individual formats (e.g., Inst\_Long CoT performs slightly better than GRPO).
 - **Consensus-Guided Mode:** Prioritizes performance by leveraging consensus among efficient formats and defaulting to Long CoT in case of disagreement. This mode effectively adapts to task complexity, resorting to Long CoT more frequently for harder tasks (e.g., 100% for AIME’25) and less for easy tasks (e.g., 12.9% for CSQA), albeit at a higher token cost.
- **Robustness Across Backbone Models:** While instruction-tuned and base models show similar performance after RL, the DeepSeek-R1-Distill backbone improves performance on hard tasks due to distilled knowledge, though it can surprisingly perform worse on easy tasks with increased token cost, highlighting the persistent "overthinking" issue in strong reasoning models. The method also generalizes to LLaMA-based backbones.
- **Superior to Length-Penalty Strategies:** Unlike length-penalty methods (e.g., L1, THINKPRUNE) that suffer performance degradation when token budgets are underestimated or waste tokens when overestimated, ARM maintains stable performance while adaptively managing token usage, demonstrating a more intelligent efficiency-effectiveness trade-off.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Adaptive Reasoning Model (ARM) presents a significant advancement in the development of efficient and adaptable large language models, with several important implications and potential impacts:

- **Towards Truly Autonomous and Intelligent AI:** ARM moves AI systems closer to human-like cognitive flexibility, where reasoning depth is dynamically adjusted based on task demands. This is a crucial step for building more genuinely intelligent and autonomous agents that can intelligently allocate computational resources, mimicking selective attention and problem-solving strategies.
- **Enhanced Practicality and Cost-Effectiveness of LRMs:** By significantly reducing token generation and inference costs, ARM makes powerful reasoning capabilities more practical and economically viable for real-world applications. This can lead to faster deployment, reduced operational expenses for AI services, and broader accessibility of advanced LLM functionalities, particularly for enterprises and research labs with limited computational budgets.
- **Improved User Experience and Control:** The inclusion of Instruction-Guided and Consensus-Guided modes offers valuable flexibility. Users can explicitly direct the model's reasoning style when certainty exists about optimal approaches or leverage a robust consensus mechanism for high-stakes decisions, providing a balance between automation and human oversight.
- **Novel Reinforcement Learning Paradigm:** Ada-GRPO's approach to overcoming "format collapse" in multi-objective RL settings is a notable methodological contribution. This innovation could inspire future RL research aimed at balancing multiple desiderata (e.g., accuracy, efficiency, diversity) in complex generative tasks, moving beyond single-metric optimization.
- **Faster AI Development Cycles:** The ~2x training speedup achieved by Ada-GRPO translates directly into shorter development cycles for new reasoning models. This enables researchers and developers to iterate faster, experiment with more ideas, and bring advanced AI capabilities to market more quickly.
- **Reduced Environmental Footprint:** The reduction in token usage directly correlates with a decrease in computational energy consumption. As AI models scale, this efficiency gain contributes to a more sustainable development path for large AI systems.
- **Addressing the "Overthinking" Bottleneck:** ARM provides a robust solution to a widely recognized problem in LRMs. By demonstrating that less reasoning can sometimes be more effective (and always more efficient) for simpler tasks, it shifts the paradigm from brute-force reasoning to intelligent, adaptive problem-solving.

In conclusion, ARM represents a critical step towards building smarter, more efficient, and more versatile AI reasoning models. Its adaptive capabilities, coupled with a novel training framework, offer a compelling solution to the "overthinking" problem, promising to unlock new applications and accelerate the responsible scaling of advanced AI.
