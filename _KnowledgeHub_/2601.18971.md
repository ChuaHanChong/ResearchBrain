# A Switching Nonlinear Model Predictive Control Strategy for Safe Collision Handling by an Underwater Vehicle-Manipulator System

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Georgia Institute of Technology and New York University Abu Dhabi developed a three-mode switching Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) strategy for Underwater Vehicle-Manipulator Systems (UVMS). This framework enables UVMS to proactively use its manipulator arm to safely deflect from unavoidable collisions, preventing damage to sensitive vehicle components and facilitating a return to mission operations.

## Problem

- Autonomous Underwater Vehicle-Manipulator Systems (UVMS) lack robust strategies for safely managing unavoidable collisions in dynamic and unpredictable environments.
- Traditional collision avoidance methods and safety guarantees can fail under unforeseen events or when avoidance maneuvers are kinematically or dynamically infeasible.
- The absence of proactive collision handling capabilities risks significant damage to expensive UVMS hardware and potential environmental harm when contact is inevitable.

## Method

- A three-mode switching Nonlinear ==Model Predictive Control== (NMPC) strategy is developed, dynamically adapting control objectives for nominal operation, contact initiation, and collision deflection.
- The UVMS manipulator is strategically utilized to proactively make controlled contact with obstacles and exert forces to deflect the vehicle away from more damaging impacts.
- UVMS links are modeled as superellipsoids and obstacles as planes to enable online distance and closing speed calculations for collision prediction and constraint formulation.

## Results

- Simulations confirmed the NMPC strategy successfully detected impending collisions and either avoided them or switched to active collision handling when necessary.
- The UVMS manipulator was effectively used to initiate controlled contact with an obstacle and exert forces for vehicle deflection, protecting sensitive body parts.
- The manipulator demonstrated a capacity to exert larger stopping forces than the vehicle's thrusters alone, reducing speed and momentum during interaction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The manipulator can act as an 'intelligent bumper,' enabling controlled, voluntary contact with obstacles to deflect the vehicle and protect sensitive components.
> - A proactive, multi-modal NMPC framework allows the UVMS to switch between task execution, controlled contact initiation, and active collision deflection based on collision inevitability.
> - Integrating a detailed hydrodynamic model and specific underwater conditions enhances the realism and applicability of the collision handling strategy for UVMS.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research operates within the expanding field of autonomous marine applications, specifically focusing on advanced control strategies for Underwater Vehicle-Manipulator Systems (UVMS). Historically, interest in marine robotics has been bifurcated into survey missions (data collection) and intervention tasks (physical interaction). While survey missions, often utilizing Autonomous Underwater Vehicles (AUVs), are well-established with control strategies like PID controllers for position tracking, intervention tasks are considerably more complex due to the need for precise control and decision-making during physical interaction. Traditionally, these tasks have relied heavily on remotely-operated UVMS. The emergence of autonomous capabilities for intervention tasks is a recent and active area of research.

A significant challenge in autonomous robot operation, particularly in dynamic and complex environments such as underwater, is ensuring safety in the presence of obstacles. Decades of research have addressed collision avoidance, primarily through path planning algorithms. However, these methods often provide trajectories with uncertain feasibility regarding robot dynamics. More recent advancements have introduced control barrier functions (CBFs) and Lyapunov functions to offer safety guarantees, but these may fail in emergency scenarios or under unforeseen circumstances (e.g., "force majeure" events).

This paper addresses a critical gap in the existing literature: the capability to safely *handle* collisions when avoidance is no longer possible or insufficient. Previous works have explored aspects of this problem. Wosch et al. (2000) considered emergency scenarios for terrestrial mobile manipulators, implementing a collision handling algorithm, but their approach focused on detection and mitigation *after* collisions occurred, raising questions about proactive safety. Chen et al. (2024) investigated emergency avoidance for mobile manipulators facing fast-moving obstacles, highlighting the utility of whole-body dynamics. Palejiya and Tanner (2006) proposed a switching controller for mobile robots, transitioning between velocity control for navigation and force control for collision recovery upon contact sensing. Berkane et al. (2019) laid mathematical foundations for switching between trajectory tracking and collision avoidance.

This work positions itself as an extension and novel integration of these concepts, specifically tailored for UVMS operating in complex underwater environments. It moves beyond purely reactive collision handling or sole collision avoidance, introducing a *proactive* strategy where the robot anticipates unavoidable contact and strategically uses its manipulator to deflect itself away from the obstacle before sensitive parts of the vehicle are damaged. This approach builds upon the inspiration from high-level task MPC (Nikou et2 al., 2019) and whole-body control for collision avoidance (Chen et al., 2024), adapting switching control ideas (Berkane et al., 2019; Palejiya and Tanner, 2006) to a pre-contact, collision handling paradigm for UVMS. The integration of a detailed hydrodynamic model and specific underwater operating conditions distinguishes this research within the broader field of robotic collision management.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the increasing deployment of autonomous systems for active intervention tasks in underwater environments. While the autonomy of Underwater Vehicle-Manipulator Systems (UVMS) offers significant advantages, it also introduces challenges related to operational safety, particularly regarding potential collisions with environmental obstacles. Existing collision avoidance strategies, while effective under nominal conditions, face inherent limitations when confronted with unpredictable events, highly dynamic environments, or situations where avoidance maneuvers are kinematically or dynamically infeasible within the system's operational constraints. In such emergency scenarios, a rigid autonomous UVMS could sustain significant damage or damage the environment.

The paper identifies a critical need for a robust and adaptive control strategy that can manage unavoidable collisions in a safe and controlled manner, thus extending the operational envelope of UVMS without compromising their integrity or mission success. The authors specifically highlight that traditional safety guarantees from methods like Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) might fail under "force majeure" events, necessitating an additional layer of redundancy and protection.

Based on this motivation, the key objectives of the research are:

- **To develop a switching Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) strategy:** The core objective is to design a multi-modal control system capable of dynamically adapting its behavior based on the proximity and inevitability of a collision. This involves formulating distinct control objectives and constraints for different operational phases.
- **To enable safe collision handling for an UVMS:** The strategy aims to prevent severe damage to the vehicle, especially its sensitive areas, when a collision cannot be entirely avoided. This includes managing the interaction forces and reorienting the vehicle.
- **To leverage the manipulator for active collision handling:** A central objective is to utilize the UVMS's manipulator arm not just for intervention tasks, but strategically as a "pushing" mechanism. When avoidance fails, the manipulator is intended to make controlled contact with the obstacle, generate reactive forces, and actively deflect the vehicle away from a more damaging collision.
- **To integrate proactive collision detection with reactive handling:** The system is designed to detect imminent collisions *before* impact and trigger a mode switch to initiate contact on pre-defined terms, rather than waiting for a sensed impact. This proactive approach aims to provide better control over the collision event.
- **To ensure seamless transitions between operational modes:** The control strategy needs to manage the switching between nominal task execution, collision preparation, and collision deflection, ensuring stability and continuity of control without chattering or abrupt changes.
- **To facilitate a return to nominal operation:** After a collision handling event, the objective is for the UVMS to safely disengage from the obstacle and return to its primary mission or a safe state, demonstrating resilience and continued operability.

In essence, the research seeks to provide UVMS with an "intelligent bumper" capability, transforming a potentially damaging event into a controlled interaction that protects the system and allows it to continue its mission.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is a three-mode switching Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) strategy designed for collision detection, avoidance, and handling in Underwater Vehicle-Manipulator Systems (UVMS). This approach allows the system to adapt its control objectives and constraints dynamically based on the proximity and inevitability of a collision.

**A. UVMS Modeling:**
A detailed and accurate model of the UVMS dynamics is central to the NMPC framework.
- **System Components:** The UVMS is modeled as a mobile manipulator, consisting of a manipulator with $N_l$ links attached to a fully actuated AUV with 6 thrusters.
- **Dynamic Formulation:** Kane’s method is employed to derive the highly nonlinear dynamics, incorporating:
 - Inertial and gravitational forces.
 - Hydrodynamic effects (added mass, buoyancy, fluid acceleration, profile drag).
 - Control forces from thrusters.
 - Contact forces.
- **State Vector:** The vehicle state, $h$, includes AUV position, orientation, linear and angular velocities, along with manipulator joint angular positions and velocities.
- **Kinematics:** Homogeneous transfer matrices define the forward kinematics, providing the End-Effector (EE) position and rotation matrix. A geometric Jacobian, $J_v$, is used for differential kinematics to relate joint velocities to EE velocities.
- **Contact Force Integration:** Contact wrenches expressed in the local EE frame are converted into generalized contact forces acting on the UVMS's degrees of freedom, ensuring compatibility with Kane's method.

**B. Superellipsoid Body Modeling:**
To facilitate online collision detection and proximity calculations, UVMS links are modeled as superellipsoids, while obstacles are simplified to planes. This choice provides analytical solutions for:
- **Distance Calculation:** Determining the distance ($d_j$) between each UVMS link and the obstacle.
- **Closing Speed Calculation:** Computing the relative velocity ($\dot{d}_j$) between the UVMS bodies and obstacles. These quantities are crucial for collision prediction and constraint formulation.

**C. Switching NMPC Strategy - Three Modes:**
The core of the methodology is a self-triggering switching NMPC with three distinct modes, each with a qualitatively different optimization variable vector ($\Psi$) and objective function:

1. **Mode I (Task-Oriented Nominal Operation):**
 - **Objective:** Minimize task error and control effort over a prediction horizon ($N_p$), given by $J_I = \sum_{i=1}^{N_p} e^T(k+i)W_s e(k+i) + \sum_{i=1}^{N_p} U^T_k W_u U_k$.
 - **Constraints:** Adherence to dynamic constraints and collision avoidance with obstacles.
 - **Optimization Vector:** $\Psi_I = [\tilde{u} \quad \tilde{h}]^T$, where $\tilde{u}$ are control inputs and $\tilde{h}$ are robot states.

2. **Mode II (Contact Initiation):**
 - **Trigger Condition:** The set of feasible solutions for Mode I becomes empty, indicating an unavoidable collision within the prediction horizon.
 - **Objective:** Establish contact between the End-Effector (EE) and the obstacle by the end of the prediction horizon. The objective function $J_{II} = \frac{1}{d^0_{k|k+N_p} + \epsilon} + w\|V^{ee}_{k|k+N_p}\|$ prioritizes minimizing the distance to contact and the EE velocity at contact.
 - **Method:** Utilizes Algorithm 1, an adaptive timestep and variable prediction horizon length algorithm. This allows the controller to optimize the timestep ($dt$) in addition to control inputs and states.
 - **Constraints:** Collision avoidance is still maintained for other parts of the vehicle, but contact is specifically permitted and required for the EE at the horizon end.
 - **Optimization Vector:** $\Psi_{II} = [\tilde{dt} \quad \tilde{u} \quad \tilde{h}]^T$.

3. **Mode III (Collision Deflection):**
 - **Trigger Condition:** The EE has successfully established contact with the obstacle (as indicated by $N_p^{(k-1)} = 1$ in Mode II, meaning contact is imminent or established).
 - **Objective:** Minimize the relative velocity between the AUV body and the obstacle to push the vehicle away, given by $J_{III} = \sum_{i=1}^{N_p} w_i \dot{d}^0_{k|k+i}$. Weights $w_i$ are designed to emphasize negative (approaching) relative velocities.
 - **Method:** The manipulator exerts the necessary force. Algorithm 2 is employed for initializing an appropriate contact strategy. This involves solving an optimization problem (16) to minimize negative normal force ($F^w_x$) while respecting manipulator torque limits. Additionally, inverse kinematics (17) are solved to find optimal manipulator postures that maintain EE contact with the obstacle, given the AUV's state, minimizing posture change.
 - **Constraints:** Maintain continuous EE contact with the obstacle, adhere to UVMS dynamics, manipulator joint limits, and friction constraints (20).
 - **Optimization Vector:** $\Psi_{III} = [\tilde{u} \quad \tilde{h} \quad \tilde{F}_w]^T$, where $\tilde{F}_w$ includes contact forces and torques.

**D. Mode Switching & Initializations (Algorithm 3):**
- **Logic:** A linear mode switching sequence (I → II → III → I) is implemented. Switching from Mode I to II occurs when Mode I becomes infeasible. Switching from Mode II to III happens when contact is established/imminent at the end of the horizon. Switching from Mode III back to I occurs when the UVMS body is no longer approaching the obstacle ($\dot{d}_0 > 0$).
- **Chattering Avoidance:** The linear progression and the assumption of guaranteed Mode I feasibility after Mode III termination mitigate chattering.
- **Initialization:** The performance of NMPC is sensitive to initial guesses. The standard receding horizon procedure is used, augmented by Algorithm 1 (for $dt$ and horizon length in Mode II) and Algorithm 2 (for contact force and manipulator posture in Mode III) to generate robust initial guesses for the optimization variables.

**E. Constraints:**
The OCP formulations incorporate various constraints:
- **Dynamics Constraints ($C_{Dyn}$):** Ensure the optimized state trajectory adheres to the UVMS dynamic model over the prediction horizon (18).
- **End-Effector Contact ($C_{eq~EE}$):** Enforces contact between the EE and the obstacle, critical for Modes II (at horizon end) and III (throughout) (19).
- **Friction Constraints ($C_{eq~Frict}$):** Models friction at the EE-obstacle contact point, ensuring physical realism (20, 21).
- **Safe Distance Constraints ($C_{Dist}$):** Maintains a safe distance between all UVMS bodies (except the contacting EE) and the obstacle, preventing unintended collisions (22).
- **Bounds:** Standard limits on control inputs, states, and contact forces.

**F. Final Algorithm Flow (Fig. 1):**
The overall algorithm describes a continuous loop where the optimal control problem is solved at each timestep. To improve performance, the calculated optimal control inputs and contact forces are used to recalculate the state trajectory from the dynamic model, which then feeds into the initialization algorithm for the subsequent timestep. The implementation uses MATLAB's `fmincon` solver.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the proposed switching Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) strategy was evaluated through virtual experiments conducted in a MATLAB simulation environment. The kinematic and dynamic model of the UVMS was developed using the symbolic math toolbox, based on Kane's method, to ensure accuracy in representing the highly nonlinear system dynamics.

The simulations covered a range of scenarios and conditions to demonstrate the algorithm's robustness and capability:
- **Diverse Initial Conditions and Missions:** The UVMS was tested from various starting points and with different mission objectives, showcasing the controller's adaptability.
- **Environmental Factors:** The presence of ocean currents was simulated, illustrating the system's ability to operate in challenging and realistic underwater environments.
- **Manipulator Configurations:** Different configurations of the manipulator were explored, highlighting the controller's flexibility in utilizing the manipulator for collision handling.
- **Control Algorithm Parameter Changes:** The impact of variations in control algorithm parameters was assessed, providing insight into its sensitivity and tuning.

A specific scenario highlighted in the paper involves a "dire situation" (Case 4B from supplementary materials) where the vehicle is on an immediate collision course, leading to the instantaneous triggering of Mode II (Contact Initiation). In this and other simulated cases, the key findings include:

- **Successful Collision Detection and Avoidance/Handling:** The algorithm consistently demonstrated the capability to successfully detect impending collisions. It could either navigate the UVMS to completely avoid the obstacle when feasible, or, critically, switch modes to handle the collision when avoidance was impossible.
- **Strategic Use of Manipulator for Deflection:** When avoidance was not an option, the control algorithm effectively utilized the manipulator. The manipulator was deployed to make a controlled initial contact with the obstacle and then exert forces to push the vehicle away, thereby deflecting it from a more damaging impact.
- **Protection of Sensitive Vehicle Areas:** The strategy successfully guided the interaction such that contact was made by the manipulator End-Effector (EE), preventing sensitive areas of the main vehicle body from sustaining damage.
- **Momentum and Speed Reduction:** Telemetry data (e.g., Fig. 3 for control forces and Fig. 4 for UVMS states) from the simulations demonstrated that the transfer of contact force through the manipulator joints effectively reduced the vehicle's speed and momentum relative to the obstacle. This proactive engagement allowed for a controlled deceleration and reorientation.
- **Superior Force Exertion:** The simulations indicated that the manipulator could exert a significantly larger stopping force compared to what the vehicle's thrusters alone could generate, underscoring the advantage of its whole-body interaction capability for emergency braking and deflection.
- **Controlled Volitional Contact:** The concept of "voluntary contact initiation, on better terms" was demonstrated. The UVMS, under the proposed control strategy, proactively seeks contact with the environment (via its manipulator) before an uncontrolled or damaging collision occurs, mitigating the escalation of the situation.
- **Functional Controller Architecture:** The simulations verified that the developed controller architecture, with its distinct modes and switching logic, operates as intended, enabling the UVMS to transition through phases of normal operation, contact initiation, and deflection.

The availability of telemetry and animations (DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.18357280) provides concrete evidence of these findings and allows for visual and quantitative validation of the algorithm's performance in various simulated scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presents a significant contribution to the field of autonomous underwater robotics and general mobile manipulator safety, particularly for systems engaged in intervention tasks. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Safety and Robustness for UVMS:** The primary impact is the provision of an additional layer of safety for Underwater Vehicle-Manipulator Systems. By introducing a proactive collision *handling* strategy that supplements traditional collision *avoidance* methods, the research addresses the critical scenario where avoidance is no longer feasible. This significantly increases the robustness of UVMS operations in unpredictable and constrained underwater environments, reducing the risk of damage to the expensive hardware and preventing potential harm to the environment.
- **Extended Operational Envelope:** The ability to safely manage unavoidable collisions expands the operational envelope of UVMS. Robots can now be deployed in environments with higher obstacle density, tighter clearances, or greater environmental uncertainty without a proportional increase in risk. This opens possibilities for more complex and delicate underwater intervention tasks where incidental contact might occur.
- **Non-Invasive Redundancy:** The proposed controller architecture is designed to be non-invasive during nominal robot operation. This means it can be integrated as an emergency redundancy layer "on top" of existing task-oriented UVMS controllers, offering a fallback mechanism without interfering with primary mission objectives under normal circumstances. This modularity facilitates practical implementation in current and future UVMS platforms.
- **Strategic Utilization of Manipulator Capabilities:** The work demonstrates a novel and intelligent use of the UVMS manipulator. Beyond its traditional role in manipulation tasks, the manipulator is strategically leveraged as an active physical interface for controlled interaction with the environment, acting as a "protective arm" to deflect the vehicle. This expands the perceived utility and design considerations for future UVMS manipulators.
- **Foundation for More Adaptive Robotic Systems:** The concept of "voluntary contact initiation on better terms" is a key paradigm shift. Instead of solely avoiding contact, the system proactively chooses the terms of engagement to mitigate escalation. This principle could inform the design of more adaptive and resilient autonomous systems that can intelligently interact with their environments rather than strictly navigating around them.
- **Broader Applicability to Other Robotic Platforms:** While demonstrated for UVMS, the core switching control architecture and the principle of using a manipulator for collision deflection are not limited to underwater vehicles. The authors suggest its applicability to other mobile manipulator platforms (e.g., terrestrial mobile manipulators) and even humanoid robots operating in complex and dynamic human-centric environments, where controlled physical interaction with the surroundings could be a safety mechanism.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** The paper identifies several promising future research directions, such as reformulating Mode III for even more extreme scenarios, integrating machine learning (Neural Networks via Reinforcement Learning) for more efficient initialization, and developing custom NMPC solvers. These suggestions indicate the potential for continued advancements based on this foundational work.

In summary, this research enhances the practical feasibility and safety of autonomous underwater intervention by providing a sophisticated, proactive collision handling strategy. It moves beyond traditional avoidance by empowering UVMS with the ability to manage inevitable interactions intelligently, thereby broadening their utility and reliability in complex real-world applications.
