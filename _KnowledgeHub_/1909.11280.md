# Human-in-the-loop Robotic Manipulation Planning for Collaborative Assembly

> [!summary] Summary
> A human-in-the-loop dual-arm assembly system splits subtasks: the robot picks, regrasps and hands over workpieces while the human performs fine operations like aligning and screwing. Handover poses are selected by scoring human arm manipulability, and a multi-modal motion planner adds a gravity-torque soft-finger constraint with per-object relaxation. A dual-UR3 robot and a person assembled a seven-board cabinet without in-hand slip.

## Problem

- Fully autonomous assembly cells need bespoke fixtures and end-effectors, are costly to reconfigure, while general-purpose perception and fine-manipulation pipelines remain too brittle to deploy robustly.
- Human-robot collaboration work focused on force control, intention prediction, or handover controllers alone, never coupling the choice of handover pose to the robot's underlying manipulation planning.
- Soft-finger contact constraints were used mainly to score statically stable grasps, not enforced during motion planning, so long heavy objects still rotate in-hand during transport.

## Method

- Human comfortableness estimator samples workpiece poses near the assembly target, plans human grasps by treating the hand as a 1-DoF gripper, and ranks them by inverse condition number of the arm ==Jacobian==.
- ==Gravity-torque constraint== inside a multi-modal manipulation planner: an end-effector inclination inequality from a slip model comparing gravity torque against soft-finger friction torque, discarding violating samples during search.
- Constraint-relaxation pre-processing plus a two-stage handover pipeline: sweep inclination to find each grasp's critical angle, then keep poses sharing grasps between the suction-tool robot-robot handover and the human handover.

## Results

- Unconstrained flipping rotated a long board in-hand and failed the task, while the constrained planner completed the same **180-degree** flip and all **seven** board handovers without slip.
- Relaxation limits adapted per object and grasp: plus-minus **62 degrees** transverse versus **40 degrees** longitudinal on one board, **22 degrees** for the large lateral board, unconstrained **90 degrees** for the **0.22 kg** board.
- Ergonomic Assessment Worksheet comparison against solo assembly eliminated forward torso bending of **20** to **60 degrees**, decreased shoulder work, made reaching and picking more comfortable, and increased human idle time.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Splitting tasks by comparative advantage, robots reposition and humans align, buys deployable autonomy without first solving perception and contact-rich fine control end to end.
> - Physics-derived constraints should be relaxed to their true critical value per object and grasp; uniformly tight constraints create narrow passages that starve sampling-based planners.
> - Selecting handover poses by human arm manipulability moves ergonomic load off the person, so downstream human effort belongs inside the robot's planning objective.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The escalating cost of human labor in manufacturing has driven demand for robotic automation. However, fully autonomous and general-purpose robotic systems still encounter challenges when performing fine or flexible assembly operations. Prior industrial robotic solutions, often requiring extensive system integration and specialized fixtures, are costly and difficult to reconfigure for new tasks. Academic research into fully AI-equipped autonomous systems, encompassing object recognition, motion planning, and fine manipulation, has shown promise but lacks the robustness and practicality for widespread industrial implementation.

This paper positions itself within the field of Human-Robot Collaboration (HRC), which aims to leverage the respective strengths of humans and robots. Previous HRC research has explored various aspects:
- **Force Control:** Early studies focused on controlling manipulators to collaboratively handle objects with humans using force sensors and model-based methods (e.g., Kosuge et al., Harada et al.).
- **Learning Methods:** More recent efforts have used learning from human demonstrations to acquire physical collaborative behaviors (e.g., Rozo et al.).
- **Task and Motion Planning:** Researchers have developed algorithms for predicting human intentions or movements to enable collision-free and coordinated robotic assistance (e.g., Luo et al., Chen et al., Maeda et al., Wisley). These studies often concentrate on inferring human intentions or optimizing handover qualities.

Within the domain of robotic motion planning, the work relates to:
- **Constrained Motion Planning:** Traditionally, this involves optimizing redundant degrees of freedom for inverse kinematics problems using end-effector constraints (e.g., Task-Oriented Constraints, Task Space Region).
- **Multi-modal Motion Planning:** This area addresses planning across different modes of operation or configuration spaces, often for object reorientation tasks requiring regrasps or other transitional states (e.g., Koga et al., Hauser et al., Wan et al.). More recent extensions include heterogeneous planning combining prehensile and nonprehensile grasps or multi-hand handovers.
- **Soft-Finger Contact Constraints:** These constraints model the ability of soft contacts to exert friction torque, which is critical for grasp stability. Prior work has used these models to find stable grasps for robotic hands (e.g., Cutkosky et al., Harada et al.).

This work distinguishes itself by integrating these aspects into a human-in-the-loop system specifically for collaborative assembly. Unlike studies focusing solely on independent autonomous robots or on force control, this paper emphasizes strategic subtask distribution between humans and robots. It proposes a manipulation planning method that simultaneously considers human ergonomics during handover and physical constraints arising from manipulated objects (specifically soft-finger contact to prevent in-hand slipping). The authors state that this work is the first to combine handover planning and manipulation planning while incorporating the human into the planning loop.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from two challenges in modern manufacturing: the rising cost of human labor and the limitations of fully autonomous robotic systems in flexibly executing fine assembly operations. While robots are proficient in repetitive and precise tasks, they often lack the adaptability and dexterity of humans for complex manipulations like alignment, fixing, and screwing. Conversely, tasks such as repetitive pick-and-place or reorientation can be physically demanding for humans.

Against this background, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **Develop a Human-in-the-loop Robotic System for Collaborative Assembly:** The central objective is to create a system where humans and robots work synergistically, leveraging their respective advantages to complete assembly tasks more effectively than either could alone.
2. **Optimize Subtask Distribution:** Specifically, the system aims to assign pick-and-place tasks and workpiece provision to the robot, while humans handle fine manipulation operations (e.g., aligning, fixing, screwing). This distribution is intended to exploit robotic strengths in structured movements and human dexterity in complex, sensitive tasks.
3. **Propose a Constraint-Based Incremental Manipulation Planning Method:** A core technical objective is to design a manipulation planner for the robot that can generate motion sequences. This planner must account for various constraints encountered during object manipulation, particularly those related to object stability and the interaction with the human.
4. **Integrate Human Ergonomics into Manipulation Planning:** A significant objective is to ensure that the robot's actions, especially during workpiece handover, prioritize human comfort. This involves determining optimal handover poses that minimize physical strain for the human collaborator, considering their grasp comfort and arm manipulability.
5. **Address Object Manipulation Challenges through Soft-Finger Contact Constraints:** The planner aims to overcome issues like in-hand object slipping, which can occur when manipulating long or heavy objects with general-purpose grippers. This requires incorporating soft-finger contact models and gravity torque considerations into the motion planning process to maintain grasp stability.
6. **Enhance Assembly Efficiency:** By allowing the robot to prepare subsequent workpieces while the human is engaged in fine assembly tasks, the system seeks to accelerate the overall assembly process.
7. **Demonstrate and Validate Performance:** The final objective is to empirically evaluate the developed system and planning method through a real-world collaborative cabinet assembly task, comparing its performance against alternative approaches (e.g., human-only assembly).

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed system comprises two main interconnected components: a Human Comfortableness Estimation component and a Robot Manipulation Planning component.

**A. System Overview and Workflow**
The system initiates with a target assembly pose as input. The workflow proceeds iteratively for each workpiece:

1. **Human Comfortableness Estimation:**
 - **Goal Pose Sampling:** Candidate goal poses for each workpiece are sampled within the collaborative workspace. These poses are biased towards the final target assembly position but also involve random rotations and translational offsets to explore variations (Eq. 1).
 - **Human Grasp Planning:** For each sampled workpiece pose, potential human grasps are identified by treating the human hand as a 1-DoF gripper, using a previously published algorithm. The stability of these human grasps is estimated considering soft-finger contact constraints (Eq. 2, 3), accounting for frictional force, moment, pressure, and eccentricity to prevent slipping.
 - **Human Arm Comfortableness Evaluation:** Inverse Kinematics (IK) is used with a model of the average Japanese worker's upper body kinematics to check for human arm reachability for each feasible grasp. For IK-feasible grasps, comfortableness is quantified using the inverse of the condition number of the human arm's Jacobian matrix (Eq. 4), which provides a dimensionless measure of manipulability.
 - **Handover Pose Selection:** The workpiece pose associated with the most comfortable human grasp (highest manipulability value) is selected as a candidate robot-to-human handover pose. A set of satisfying handover poses, where the maximum comfortableness value exceeds a threshold, is maintained (Eq. 8).

2. **Robot Manipulation Planning:**
 - **Multi-modal Planning Goal:** The selected comfortable handover pose from the human comfortableness component serves as the goal for the robot's manipulation planning.
 - **Manipulation Graph Construction:** The robot's planner constructs a manipulation graph in the robot's grasp space. This graph enables the system to automatically determine if intermediate states (e.g., tool usage, robot-robot handover) are necessary to move the workpiece from its initial pose to the handover pose.
 - **Constrained Motion Planning:** Motion planning is performed by searching this graph while adhering to various constraints, including collision avoidance and specific constraints related to object stability.
 - **Soft-Finger Contact Constraint for Slip Prevention:**
 - **Slip Modeling:** The planner models potential in-hand object slipping caused by gravity torque (T_g, Eq. 6) exceeding the friction torque (2T_f) provided by the robotic fingertips. This can lead to angular acceleration and changes in the object's in-hand pose.
 - **Gravity Torque-Based Constraint:** To prevent slip, a constraint is imposed on the robot's end-effector inclination (θ). This angle, measured between the end-effector's opening direction and the gravity vector, is a primary factor influencing gravity torque. The safest inclination is 0° (end-effector opening perpendicular to gravity), while 90° is the most precarious.
 - **Constraint Relaxation:** To avoid overly strict constraints that might narrow the planning space, a relaxation strategy is employed. In a pre-processing step, the system determines a safe range (0° ± θ_c) for the inclination angle. This θ_c (critical inclination angle) is the point at which the gravity torque, calculated for gradually increasing inclinations, first exceeds the maximum friction torque the gripper can provide (Fig. 6a.2). During motion planning, any sampled robot pose must satisfy this relaxed constraint.
 - **Tool Usage:** For thin objects like cabinet boards, a suction cup tool is used for initial pick-and-place. One robot arm manipulates this tool, while the other manipulates the boards.

3. **Handover Procedures:**
 - **Robot-Robot Handover:** One robot arm, typically holding the suction cup, picks up a board and moves it to an intermediate "robot-robot handover pose." The other arm then receives the board from this pose. The rotation of the suction tool, the handover pose, and the receiving grasp are optimized together.
 - **Robot-Human Handover Refinement:** The set of human-comfortable handover poses (from step 1) is further refined. The system checks if the robot can achieve "shared grasps" for both robot-robot and robot-human handovers (Eq. 9). From the remaining candidate poses, the one closest to the target assembly pose (measured by Q_S, Eq. 10) is selected as the final robot-human handover goal. If a path to this pose is unfeasible (e.g., due to collision), the next nearest pose is considered.
 - **Execution and Detection:** Once the robot brings the board to the desired robot-human handover pose, it waits for human reception, detected by force/torque sensors at the robot wrists. Upon detection of force changes, the robot opens its gripper to release the board.

**B. Hardware and Modeling**
The experimental setup consists of a dual-arm robot system composed of two Universal Robots UR3 arms, each equipped with Robotiq FT300 6DoF F/T sensors and Robotiq 2F-85 parallel grippers. Hand cameras are mounted for visual detection of workpieces and the suction cup tool. Human kinematics are modeled based on average Japanese worker dimensions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the proposed human-in-the-loop collaborative assembly system and its planning methodology was evaluated through a real-world demonstration of assembling a cabinet, comprising seven boards of varying dimensions and masses (Table I).

**A. System Effectiveness and Robustness**
- **Successful Cabinet Assembly:** The dual-arm robot successfully executed the entire collaborative assembly sequence, involving seven pick-ups, robot-robot handovers, and robot-human handovers. The robot consistently provided workpieces to the human collaborator in ergonomically optimized poses (Fig. 10).
- **Adaptive Constraint Handling:** The system demonstrated adaptive application of the gravity torque-based constraint for slip prevention. For example:
 - A medium-sized board grasped along its transverse axis allowed a relaxation angle of θ = 62°.
 - The same board grasped along its longitudinal axis required a stricter relaxation of θ = 40°.
 - For a large, heavy board (the second board), which was particularly prone to slip, the constraint checking component imposed a tighter safe range of 0° ± 22°. The planner successfully generated a motion sequence to flip this board without slip under this strict constraint (Fig. 11a).
 - For a lighter board (the third board), no constraint was necessary, permitting a full inclination range of 0° ± 90° and allowing the robot greater motion freedom (Fig. 11b).
- **Navigation in Constrained Workspaces:** The planner exhibited the capability to find collision-free motions for boards even when the workspace became narrow due to partially assembled cabinet structures (e.g., for the fourth and sixth boards, Fig. 11c).

**B. Impact on Human Ergonomics**
A comparative analysis, based on Ergonomic Assessment Worksheet (EAWS) criteria, was conducted between the human-robot collaboration approach and a human-alone assembly method (Table II). The results indicated a reduction in human physical workload:
- **Eliminated Bending Forward:** The robot presented boards at comfortable heights and orientations, removing the need for the human to bend.
- **Decreased Shoulder Work:** By pre-orienting and flipping boards, the robot reduced the strenuous shoulder movements typically required for positioning.
- **More Comfortable Reaching and Picking:** The robot's optimized handover poses facilitated more comfortable and less demanding reaching and picking actions for the human.
- **Increased Idle Time:** The robot's shared workload resulted in an increase in human idle activity, suggesting a reduced overall physical demand.
- **Consistent Fine Manipulation:** Tasks designated for the human, such as screwing and fine alignment, remained unchanged in terms of their physical requirements, as these were the human's specialized subtasks.

**C. Overall Performance**
The experimental results collectively indicate that the proposed system and its manipulation planning method are effective in facilitating human-robot collaborative assembly. The system provides efficient robotic assistance, maintains object stability through intelligent constraint handling, and enhances human comfort, thereby improving the overall user experience and potentially expediting the assembly process.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of human-robot collaboration and robotic manipulation planning with several significant implications:

- **Enhanced Practicality of Human-Robot Collaboration:** The work offers a pragmatic approach to integrate robots into assembly lines without requiring fully autonomous AI systems that are currently impractical. By intelligently distributing subtasks, it leverages the strengths of both human dexterity for fine operations and robotic precision for pick-and-place, making collaborative assembly more feasible and robust.
- **Improved Safety and Reliability in Robotic Manipulation:** The integration of soft-finger contact constraints and a gravity torque-based slip avoidance mechanism directly addresses a critical challenge in robotic manipulation: the in-hand slipping of irregularly shaped, heavy, or thin objects. This methodology enhances the reliability of robot operations, potentially reducing errors, material damage, and the need for manual intervention during assembly.
- **Elevated Human Ergonomics and Worker Well-being:** A key impact is the direct consideration of human comfort and ergonomics during the planning process. By optimizing handover poses to reduce physically demanding actions such as bending, excessive shoulder work, and awkward reaches, the system can significantly alleviate physical strain on human workers. This has the potential to decrease fatigue, minimize work-related injuries, and improve overall worker satisfaction and health in manufacturing environments.
- **Increased Efficiency and Adaptability in Manufacturing:** By allowing the robot to prepare subsequent workpieces concurrently with human fine assembly tasks, the system can accelerate the overall assembly process. Furthermore, the developed planner, being reconfigurable and adaptive to different workpieces and assembly states, offers a more flexible solution compared to rigid, costly, and difficult-to-reconfigure traditional industrial robot workcells. This adaptability can reduce setup times and costs for new products.
- **Bridging Research Gaps:** The research fills a gap by uniquely combining elements of human ergonomics, constrained multi-modal manipulation planning, and stable soft-finger grasping within a human-in-the-loop collaborative framework. The authors state this is the first study to combine handover and manipulation planning while considering the human in the loop, establishing a new baseline for future research.

The anticipated future impact of this work includes relaxing the labor-intensive nature of many manufacturing processes. Furthermore, the foundation laid by this research, particularly in modeling human comfort, opens avenues for more personalized and adaptable collaborative systems, with future work planned to update human models online using vision sensors to cater to a wider range of users.
