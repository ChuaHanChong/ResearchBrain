# A survey of the Vision Transformers and their CNN-Transformer based Variants

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey paper provides a comprehensive review and systematic taxonomy of Vision Transformers (ViTs) and particularly emphasizes Hybrid Vision Transformers (HVTs), which combine Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Transformers. It classifies HVTs into seven distinct integration patterns, outlining their design rationales, performance trends, and widespread applicability across diverse computer vision tasks.

## Problem

- Pure Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) excel at local feature extraction but often struggle to capture long-range dependencies and global contextual information in images.
- Traditional Vision Transformers (ViTs) effectively model global relationships but typically require vast amounts of training data due to weaker inductive biases and can be computationally expensive.
- The rapid proliferation of hybrid CNN-Transformer architectures necessitated a structured overview to understand their diverse designs and integration strategies.

## Method

- The authors conducted a systematic review, establishing a foundational understanding of ViT components and general ViT architectures.
- A detailed and novel taxonomy was developed for ==Hybrid Vision Transformers (HVTs)==, classifying them into ==seven distinct integration patterns== based on how CNN operations integrate with self-attention mechanisms.
- The paper provides empirical comparisons, discussing the strengths, weaknesses, and performance metrics of various models, alongside an overview of their applications, challenges, and future directions.

## Results

- The survey presents a comprehensive taxonomy of general ViT architectures into **five main categories** (e.g., patch-based, knowledge transfer-based, shifted window-based).
- A detailed taxonomy for HVTs is established, classifying them into **seven integration patterns** with illustrative examples (e.g., DETR for early-layer, CoAtNet for sequential, MaxViT for hierarchical).
- It demonstrates the widespread applicability of both ViTs and HVTs across almost all major computer vision domains, including image recognition, segmentation, generation, and medical image analysis, documenting their successful deployment in these areas.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Hybrid Vision Transformers (HVTs) offer a powerful solution by combining CNNs' inductive biases for local feature extraction with Transformers' capacity for global, long-range dependency modeling.
> - The integration of CNNs and Transformers can occur in diverse ways, including early-layer, lateral-layer, sequential, parallel, hierarchical, attention-based, and channel boosting-based methods, each with distinct architectural implications and performance characteristics.
> - HVTs generally aim for improved state-of-the-art performance, better efficiency, reduced data requirements, and enhanced generalization across benchmarks compared to pure CNNs or ViTs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This survey paper is situated at a critical juncture in the rapidly evolving field of computer vision, specifically addressing the convergence of two dominant deep learning paradigms: Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Transformers. Historically, CNNs revolutionized computer vision by leveraging their inherent inductive biases, such as locality, translational equivariance, and hierarchical feature learning, to effectively process visual data. They excel at capturing local patterns and feature motifs from images. However, their limited receptive fields, especially in early layers, can sometimes hinder their ability to model long-range dependencies and global contextual information across an entire image.

The emergence of Transformer architectures, initially successful in Natural Language Processing (NLP) with their self-attention mechanism, marked a significant shift. Vision Transformers (ViTs) adapted this mechanism to images by treating image patches as sequences of tokens, allowing them to capture global relationships between distant parts of an image effectively. While ViTs demonstrated impressive performance, often surpassing CNNs on large datasets, they typically require vast amounts of training data due to their weaker inductive biases (less prior knowledge about image structure). This makes them less generalizable than CNNs on smaller datasets and often more computationally expensive.

This paper specifically addresses the *hybridization* trend, where researchers combine the strengths of both CNNs and Transformers to create **Hybrid Vision Transformers (HVTs)**, also known as CNN-Transformer architectures. This approach aims to leverage CNNs' proficiency in extracting local, low-level features and incorporating image-specific inductive biases, while simultaneously utilizing Transformers' capacity for modeling global, long-range dependencies. The authors argue that this hybrid approach offers a powerful solution to overcome the individual limitations of pure CNNs (lack of global context) and pure ViTs (data hunger, lack of local inductive bias).

The work fits into the broader landscape by systematically reviewing and categorizing this burgeoning class of hybrid models. While other surveys might focus on CNNs, general ViT architectures, or specific applications, this paper uniquely emphasizes the architectural designs and integration strategies of HVTs. It highlights the growing recognition that a synergistic combination of these two powerful paradigms is essential for developing more robust, efficient, and versatile computer vision models capable of handling complex visual tasks across varying data scales.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this survey is the rapid proliferation and promising performance of Hybrid Vision Transformers (HVTs), often referred to as CNN-Transformer architectures, in diverse computer vision applications. As these hybrid models combine the strengths of both convolutional operations and the self-attention mechanism, they offer enhanced capabilities in capturing both local and global image representations. Given this swift growth, the authors identify a critical need for a structured and comprehensive overview of these evolving architectures.

The key objectives of this paper are explicitly outlined as follows:

1. **Provide a Taxonomy of Vision Transformer Architectures:** To systematically classify and organize the recent developments in general Vision Transformers (ViTs), offering a foundational understanding of their core architectural modifications.
2. **Uniquely Emphasize Hybrid Vision Transformers (HVTs):** To specifically focus on the emerging trend of architectures that integrate both convolutional layers and transformer modules. This is a distinguishing objective, setting the survey apart from others that might concentrate on individual ViT architectures or pure CNNs.
3. **Offer a Detailed Taxonomy of HVTs:** To thoroughly classify these hybrid approaches based on their core architectural designs and the specific ways they incorporate convolution operations in combination with self-attention mechanisms.
4. **Discuss Key Architectural Features:** To elaborate on fundamental components and characteristics of these architectures, including attention mechanisms, positional embeddings, multi-scale processing strategies, and the role of convolution.
5. **Showcase Potential and Future Directions:** To highlight the demonstrated potential of hybrid vision transformers in achieving exceptional performance across various computer vision tasks and to shed light on promising future research avenues in this rapidly evolving field.

In essence, the authors are motivated to bring order and clarity to a complex and fast-moving research area, providing a valuable resource for researchers and practitioners to understand, categorize, and build upon the advancements in hybrid vision models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a survey paper, the methodology employed by the authors is primarily a systematic review and categorization of existing literature rather than empirical experimentation. The approach is structured to provide a comprehensive and hierarchical understanding of Vision Transformers (ViTs) and, more specifically, Hybrid Vision Transformers (HVTs).

The methodological steps taken by the authors include:

1. **Foundation Building (Section 2):** The paper begins by establishing a foundational understanding of the core components of ViT architectures. This includes detailed explanations of patch embedding, various types of positional embeddings (Absolute, Relative, Convolutional), the self-attention mechanism, Multi-Head Self-Attention (MSA), and the structure of transformer layers (Feed-forward network, Residual connections, Normalization layer). This ensures readers have the necessary background before delving into complex architectures.

2. **Taxonomy of General ViTs (Section 3):** The authors develop a taxonomy for general ViT architectures, classifying them into five main categories based on their architectural modifications:
 - Patch-based approaches (e.g., T2T-ViT, TNT-ViT, DPT, CrowdFormer)
 - Knowledge transfer-based approaches (e.g., DeiT, TaT, TinyViT)
 - Shifted window-based approaches (e.g., Swin Transformer, CSWinTT)
 - Attention-based approaches (e.g., CaiT, DAT, SeT)
 - Multi-transformer-based approaches (e.g., CrossViT, Dual-ViT, MMViT, MPViT)
 Each category is briefly described with examples of prominent models.

3. **In-depth Analysis and Taxonomy of Hybrid Vision Transformers (HVTs) (Section 3.6):** This constitutes the core methodological contribution. The authors meticulously classify HVTs, also known as CNN-Transformer architectures, into seven distinct categories based on *how* convolution operations are integrated with self-attention mechanisms within the ViT framework:
 - **Early-layer integration:** CNNs are used at the beginning of the architecture for initial feature extraction (e.g., DETR, LeViT, CPVT).
 - **Lateral-layer integration:** CNN layers or blocks are placed at the end of the transformer network or as post-processing layers (e.g., DPT (decoder), LocalViT).
 - **Sequential integration:** Convolutional and transformer blocks are stacked vertically in a sequence (e.g., CoAtNet, CMT, BoTNet).
 - **Parallel integration:** CNN and transformer branches process data concurrently, with their outputs fused (e.g., Conformer, Mobile-Former, BossNAS).
 - **Hierarchical integration:** Models adopt a multi-stage, hierarchical design similar to CNNs, with unified blocks integrating both components (e.g., MaxViT, CvT, Visformer, ViTAE, ConTNet).
 - **Attention-based integration:** CNNs are incorporated directly within the attention mechanism itself to enhance locality (e.g., EA-AA-ResNet, ResT, CeiT).
 - **Channel boosting-based integration:** Auxiliary CNN and ViT learners generate "boosted" channels to augment representation learning (e.g., CB-HVT).
 For each integration pattern, the paper provides illustrative examples of specific architectures and explains their design rationale.

4. **Empirical Comparison (Section 3.7):** To provide practical insights, the authors include tables (Table 3 for ViTs, Table 4 for HVTs) summarizing the strengths, weaknesses, underlying rationale, and reported performance metrics (e.g., Top-1 Accuracy on ImageNet, mAP on COCO) for key architectures within their respective taxonomies. This comparative analysis helps in understanding the trade-offs and effectiveness of different design choices.

5. **Applications Overview (Section 4):** The survey details the widespread adoption of both ViTs and HVTs across a broad spectrum of computer vision tasks, including image/video recognition, image generation, segmentation, restoration, feature extraction, medical image analysis, object detection, and pose estimation. Specific examples of models and their contributions in each application area are provided.

6. **Challenges and Future Directions (Sections 5 & 6):** The paper concludes by identifying existing challenges (e.g., inductive bias, computational complexity, hardware requirements, interpretability, efficient feature merging, data dependency) and proposing promising future research directions (e.g., interpretability techniques, lightweight architectures, optimized integration methods, multi-modality, hand-crafted operators).

This systematic and detailed approach allows the authors to offer a comprehensive, well-structured, and insightful review of a complex and dynamic research landscape.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The main findings and results of this survey paper are primarily presented through its comprehensive taxonomies, detailed architectural descriptions, and a synthesis of the observed performance and trends in the field of Vision Transformers (ViTs) and Hybrid Vision Transformers (HVTs).

**1. Comprehensive Taxonomy of Vision Transformers:**
The survey successfully categorizes recent ViT architectures into five distinct classes:
- **Patch-based approaches:** Emphasize varied or iterative patch extraction mechanisms (e.g., T2T-ViT, TNT-ViT, DPT) to improve feature representation by focusing on tokens and handling objects of different sizes.
- **Knowledge transfer-based approaches:** Utilize knowledge distillation to train smaller, more efficient ViT models from larger "teacher" models (e.g., DeiT, TaT, TinyViT), making them suitable for resource-constrained environments.
- **Shifted window-based approaches:** Employ techniques like shifted windowing (e.g., Swin Transformer) to balance computational efficiency with the ability to capture global relationships over large images.
- **Attention-based approaches:** Modify the self-attention mechanism to enhance feature representation, focus on important regions, or reduce computational cost (e.g., CaiT, DAT, SeT).
- **Multi-transformer-based approaches:** Integrate multiple ViT branches or components to capture multi-scale or multi-view features effectively (e.g., CrossViT, Dual-ViT, MPViT).

**2. Detailed Taxonomy of Hybrid Vision Transformers (HVTs):**
The most significant finding is the novel and granular taxonomy of HVTs, classifying them into seven major integration patterns that combine CNN and Transformer components:
- **Early-Layer Integration:** CNNs are used at the initial stage (stem block) for low-level feature extraction and tokenization, providing inductive bias from the outset (e.g., DETR, LeViT, CPVT). This often leads to better performance, especially on smaller datasets.
- **Lateral-Layer Integration:** CNN components are used as a final processing step or decoder, often to convert global features into dense predictions (e.g., DPT for segmentation, LocalViT which enhances FFN with depth-wise convolutions).
- **Sequential Integration:** CNN and Transformer blocks are stacked one after another (e.g., CoAtNet, CMT, BoTNet), allowing for progressive learning of local and global features. This often balances generalization and capacity.
- **Parallel Integration:** CNN and Transformer branches operate in parallel, processing data simultaneously and fusing their outputs to combine local and global contexts (e.g., Conformer, Mobile-Former). This aims for a good balance between efficiency and representation power.
- **Hierarchical Integration:** Architectures adopt a multi-stage, hierarchical design where integrated CNN-Transformer blocks are repeated, mimicking the progressive downsampling of CNNs while incorporating self-attention (e.g., MaxViT, CvT, Visformer, ViTAE, ConTNet). This allows for both local and global feature extraction across the network.
- **Attention-Based Integration:** Convolutional operations are embedded directly within the self-attention mechanism to introduce locality (e.g., EA-AA-ResNet, ResT, CeiT). This enhances the attention module's ability to model local dependencies.
- **Channel Boosting-Based Integration:** CNN and ViT-based auxiliary learners generate "boosted channels" to enrich feature representation, capturing diverse local (CNN) and global (ViT) patterns (e.g., CB-HVT).

**3. Empirical Performance Trends:**
The empirical comparison tables (Table 3 and 4) highlight that HVTs generally aim to achieve state-of-the-art performance across various benchmarks (e.g., ImageNet Top-1 Accuracy, COCO mAP, F-Score) while often improving efficiency, reducing data requirements, or enhancing generalization compared to their pure CNN or pure ViT counterparts. Models like MaxViT and CvT, for instance, demonstrate strong performance, indicating the effectiveness of hierarchical integration of local and global contexts.

**4. Widespread Applicability:**
The survey comprehensively documents the successful application of both ViTs and HVTs across almost all major computer vision domains, including:
- Image/Video Recognition (e.g., fine-grained 3D object recognition, face-periocular identification, vehicle recognition).
- Image Generation (e.g., GANs with hybrid generators for image-to-image translation, MRI reconstruction, facial image super-resolution).
- Image Segmentation (e.g., medical image segmentation, crack detection, land cover classification).
- Image Restoration (e.g., blind deblurring, image super-resolution, denoising, image fusion).
- Feature Extraction (e.g., crop disease identification, recaptured scene image identification, defect detection).
- Medical Image Analysis (e.g., segmentation, disease detection, reconstruction).
- Object Detection (e.g., ViT-FRCNN, MDCT for remote sensing, UAV object detection).
- Pose Estimation (e.g., multi-person pose estimation, human joint estimation).

**5. Identification of Challenges and Future Directions:**
The authors identify key challenges, such as the difficulty in instilling inductive biases (e.g., rotation invariance) in ViTs, high computational and memory overhead, hardware resource demands, interpretability issues, efficient merging of local and global features, and the need for large training datasets. Based on these, future directions include enhancing interpretability, developing lightweight architectures (e.g., via knowledge distillation), exploring optimal integration methods for specific tasks, and leveraging multimodality and multitasking capabilities.

In summary, the core findings underscore that the hybrid paradigm is not merely an incremental improvement but a fundamental shift towards more powerful and versatile deep learning models in computer vision, offering a balanced approach to capture both fine-grained local details and overarching global context.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey paper holds significant importance for the advancement of research and practical applications in computer vision, particularly at the intersection of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Vision Transformers (ViTs). Its potential impact spans several dimensions:

**1. Structured Knowledge and Research Guidance:**
By providing detailed taxonomies for both general ViTs and, crucially, for Hybrid Vision Transformers (HVTs), the paper offers a much-needed structured framework for a rapidly evolving field. For new researchers, it serves as an invaluable roadmap, simplifying the complex landscape of various architectural modifications and integration strategies. It enables them to quickly grasp the fundamental concepts, identify key models, and understand the underlying rationale behind different design choices. This structured knowledge can accelerate research by helping to pinpoint unexplored areas, identify gaps in current approaches, and inform the design of novel architectures.

**2. Facilitating Model Selection and Application Development:**
For practitioners and developers, the survey acts as a comprehensive reference guide. The empirical comparisons, highlighting strengths, weaknesses, and performance metrics of various models, are highly practical. This information can significantly aid in selecting the most suitable HVT architecture for specific computer vision tasks, considering factors like dataset size, computational resources, and the balance required between local and global feature extraction. This enables more informed decision-making in deploying efficient and effective solutions across diverse applications, from medical imaging to autonomous systems.

**3. Driving Innovation in Hybrid Architectures:**
The paper's emphasis on the *synergy* between CNNs and Transformers is a powerful message. It reinforces the idea that combining the inductive biases of CNNs with the global modeling capabilities of Transformers leads to more robust, generalizable, and high-performing models. By systematically breaking down *how* this hybridization occurs (e.g., early-layer, parallel, hierarchical integration), the survey actively encourages further innovation in integration methodologies. It challenges researchers to explore more sophisticated ways of merging these paradigms, potentially leading to entirely new architectural designs that unlock unprecedented performance.

**4. Addressing Limitations and Setting Future Agendas:**
The clear articulation of current challenges faced by ViTs and HVTs—such as computational cost, data hunger, interpretability, and optimal feature merging—is critical. By defining these challenges, the survey sets a compelling research agenda for the community. The proposed future directions, including the development of lightweight architectures, enhanced interpretability tools, and the exploration of hand-crafted operators in conjunction with deep learning, directly address these limitations. This proactive identification of problems and potential solutions will guide future efforts towards creating more efficient, transparent, and deployable AI systems.

**5. Unique Contribution to Survey Literature:**
In contrast to existing surveys that might focus on pure CNNs or general ViTs, this paper's unique and explicit emphasis on hybrid CNN-Transformer architectures fills a crucial gap in the scientific literature. It consolidates scattered knowledge about this emerging and highly promising area, making it a definitive resource for anyone interested in the future of deep learning in computer vision.

In essence, this survey is not just a review; it's a foundational document that organizes a complex research space, highlights the most promising directions, and empowers both new and experienced members of the scientific community to push the boundaries of computer vision with more powerful and intelligent hybrid models.
