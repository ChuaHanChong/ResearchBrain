# Meta-Optimization and Program Search using Language Models for Task and Motion Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> Meta-Optimization and Program Search (MOPS) introduces a hierarchical meta-optimization framework for Task and Motion Planning, leveraging large language models for high-level discrete constraint selection and numerical optimizers for precise continuous parameter refinement. This approach enables a robot to successfully perform complex manipulation and drawing tasks requiring high precision, outperforming existing language-conditioned baselines.

## Problem

- Classical Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) methods rely on human-engineered fixed mappings, limiting flexibility and often leading to suboptimal or infeasible plans due to the abstraction of continuous parameters.
- Prior foundation model (FM)-based TAMP approaches struggle with fine-grained spatial and numerical precision, leading to reliance on simplified atomic skills and inefficient parameter handling, restricting their applicability to complex, high-precision tasks.
- Developing robotic agents capable of robustly and flexibly interacting with dynamic environments over long horizons requires joint reasoning over abstract plans and precise low-level continuous controls, presenting a significant interface challenge.

## Method

- **MOPS** frames language-conditioned TAMP as a three-level ==meta-optimization== problem, iteratively refining a ==Non-Linear Program (NLP)== that defines robot motion by jointly optimizing its structure and parameters.
- A Large Language Model (LLM) at Level 1 performs ==program search== to select discrete constraint sets for the NLP and provides initial continuous parameter guesses, receiving iterative feedback from lower levels.
- A ==black-box optimizer== (Level 2) refines continuous constraint parameters to minimize extrinsic task costs, while a ==gradient-based trajectory optimizer== (Level 3) solves the fully parameterized NLP to generate smooth, collision-free robot trajectories.

## Results

- MOPS consistently outperformed baseline methods like Code-as-Policies and PRoC3S across all six tasks in both block pushing and geometric drawing environments, particularly in scenarios demanding high precision.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the continuous parameter optimization (Level 2, with CMA-ES performing best) and the LLM's outer feedback loop (Level 1) were crucial for MOPS's superior performance, reducing variance and improving consistency.
- The method was successfully deployed on a real Franka Panda robot for both drawing and block pushing tasks, demonstrating its practical viability for real-world applications despite requiring careful physical calibration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Formulating TAMP as a meta-optimization problem over trajectory optimization programs, where both discrete constraint selection and continuous parameters are optimized, provides a more expressive and flexible interface for language-instructed robotics.
> - The hierarchical combination of LLMs for high-level semantic reasoning and dedicated numerical optimizers for low-level continuous parameter refinement effectively mitigates LLM limitations in geometric and numerical precision.
> - An iterative feedback loop, allowing the LLM to refine its constraint proposals based on performance outcomes from numerical optimization and simulation, significantly enhances system robustness and problem-solving reliability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Intelligent robotic interaction with dynamic environments necessitates agents that can perform both high-level planning and precise low-level control. Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) is a long-standing framework designed to address this challenge by integrating symbolic planning with continuous trajectory generation. Over decades, classical TAMP approaches have enabled robots to solve various tasks, from table-top manipulation to architectural construction, by introducing a symbolic domain to link discrete decisions with continuous motions.

However, classical TAMP methods often face limitations due to their reliance on a fixed mapping between symbolic skills and underlying trajectory optimization, which must be engineered by human experts. This fixed mapping can restrict flexibility and solution quality, as specific timings, scalings, and other continuous parameters are abstracted away during the symbolic planning phase. Such rigid separation can lead to plans that are locally optimal or, in some cases, infeasible.

More recently, the advent of foundation models (FMs), particularly large language models (LLMs), has offered alternative approaches to TAMP, bypassing the need for hand-crafted symbolic predicates. These FM-based methods have explored various strategies, including selecting control policies from pre-trained repertoires (e.g., PaLM-e), generating code to control robots (e.g., Code-as-Policies, CaP), or directly predicting joint states. While FMs have demonstrated capabilities in solving a range of tasks, many prior FM-based approaches are built upon simplifying assumptions. A common constraint is their reliance on atomic skills, which often stems from the limited fine-grained spatial and numerical reasoning abilities of current foundation models. This can restrict their applicability to tasks requiring high precision in placement or low tolerance for errors.

A notable development in addressing this precision gap is the work by Curtis et al. (PRoC3S), which frames language-instructed TAMP as a constraint satisfaction problem. This approach employs LLMs to reason over parameterized skills, with parameters subsequently sampled to satisfy specified constraints. Nevertheless, this method has been noted to be sample-inefficient, relying on rejection sampling, and depends significantly on the user's ability to specify all relevant constraints comprehensively at the outset.

The work presented in this paper, Meta-Optimization and Program Search (MOPS), positions itself within this landscape by seeking to enhance the interface between high-level FM-based planning and low-level robot control. It aims to overcome the limitations of both classical TAMP (regarding flexibility and parameter abstraction) and prior FM-based methods (regarding numerical precision and sample efficiency) by introducing a hierarchical meta-optimization framework. This framework leverages the semantic reasoning of LLMs for constraint selection while employing numerical optimization methods to refine the continuous parameters of these constraints, thereby bridging the gap between abstract language instructions and precise robotic execution.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the acknowledged difficulties in developing robotic agents that can robustly and flexibly interact with the real world over long horizons. Such interaction demands joint reasoning over abstract, high-level plans and precise, low-level continuous controls. The paper identifies a significant challenge in the optimal interface between these two levels, particularly within the context of Task and Motion Planning (TAMP).

The specific problems and limitations motivating this work can be summarized as follows:

- **Limitations of Classical TAMP:** Traditional TAMP methods, while robust, often trade expressivity for tractability. They typically fix the mapping between symbolic skills (high-level decisions) and the underlying trajectory optimization (low-level control). This mapping requires significant expert design, which restricts the flexibility of the method and ties solution quality directly to the designer's insights. Furthermore, the abstraction of specific timings, scalings, and other continuous parameters during high-level symbolic planning can lead to plans that are suboptimal or even infeasible in continuous space.
- **Limitations of Prior Foundation Model (FM)-based TAMP:** Recent advances in FMs have offered a promising alternative by allowing natural language instruction for robotic tasks. However, these approaches often rely on simplifying assumptions:
 - **Lack of Fine-Grained Spatial and Numerical Reasoning:** Current FMs tend to struggle with the geometric and numerical precision required for many complex manipulation tasks. This often leads to their reliance on atomic, simplified skills, which are insufficient for tasks demanding precise placement or tight tolerances.
 - **Inefficient Parameter Handling:** Methods that generate parameterized skills, such as PRoC3S, typically rely on uniform sampling to obtain continuous skill parameters. This approach is sample-inefficient, often resembling rejection sampling, and still places a considerable burden on human users to specify all relevant constraints upfront.
 - **Suboptimal Abstraction Levels:** Some FM approaches either over-abstract (e.g., merely chaining simplified skill primitives) or under-abstract (e.g., direct joint angle prediction), failing to establish an effective interface between high-level semantic understanding and low-level physical interaction.

The key objectives of this research are to address these identified limitations by proposing a novel TAMP technique that:

1. **Establishes a Refined Interface:** Develop a method that uses program search over trajectory optimization problems as a structured interface between a foundation model's high-level reasoning and robot control's low-level execution. This aims to provide a more expressive yet precise link than prior abstraction layers.
2. **Enhances Numerical Precision:** Integrate a zero-order method to optimize numerical parameters generated by the foundation model. This directly addresses the known limitations of LLMs in geometric and numerical reasoning, allowing for the refinement of continuous constraint parameters for improved task success.
3. **Frames TAMP as Meta-Optimization:** Introduce a perspective where the TAMP problem is treated as a meta-optimization task. In this framework, both the discrete motion constraints and the continuous parameters within those constraints, as well as the resulting trajectories, are explicit targets for optimization. This allows for an iterative refinement process that jointly improves the plan's structure and its execution details.

By pursuing these objectives, the authors aim to enable more efficient, robust, and precise robotic manipulation for complex tasks specified through natural language instructions, moving beyond the limitations of existing TAMP methodologies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Meta-Optimization and Program Search (MOPS), frames language-conditioned Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) as a meta-optimization problem. It employs a hierarchical and iterative framework that interleaves three distinct optimization levels, aiming to jointly refine both the structure and parameters of a Non-Linear Program (NLP) that defines the robot's motion.

### 4. Problem Formulation (Section 2.1)

MOPS operates within a language-conditioned TAMP setting, where the task goal (G) is specified in natural language. The system optimizes two types of decision variables:
- **Continuous trajectory parameters (x ∈ R^(T ×n)):** Representing the robot's joint states over time.
- **Mixed-integer constraint parameters (α ∈^k × R^j):** Comprising binary selectors (α_i ∈ {0, 1}^k) that indicate which constraints from a predefined library should be enforced, and continuous parameters (α_c ∈ R^j) that specify numerical values for these constraints (e.g., grasp poses or placement locations).
This formulation contrasts with classical TAMP, which typically searches over action sequences; MOPS uses Large Language Models (LLMs) to search over a set of constraints that define a motion NLP. The constraint functions, g(·, α) for inequalities and h(·, α) for equalities, are assumed to be (piecewise) continuously differentiable in x, though they can be nonlinear and non-convex.

The overall objective is to minimize a total task cost J(x, α), which is defined as an integral over time of a continuous, differentiable trajectory cost f(x(t), α) (e.g., squared accelerations) and an extrinsic, black-box cost Ψ(x(t), α) that quantifies task success. By decoupling these costs, specialized solvers can be applied to each term.

### 5. MOPS: Three-Level Meta-Optimization (Section 2.3)

MOPS breaks down the complex mixed-integer optimization problem into three interconnected levels, executed in an iterative loop:

1. **Level 1: Language Model Program Search:**
 - **Objective:** To optimize the selection of discrete constraint sets (α_i) that will be enforced within the NLP. The LLM acts as an optimizer for this semantic search.
 - **Mechanism:** A foundation model (LLM) is prompted with:
 - A textual description of the environment's state space (S) and initial state (s_0).
 - A list of available parameterized constraint functions.
 - Two in-context examples for guidance.
 - **Output:** The LLM is tasked with generating two components:
 - A Python function, `gen_plan`, which effectively defines a parameterized NLP generation function. This function selects the constraints (α_i) based on the task goal and will yield a solvable trajectory once its continuous parameters (α_c) are instantiated.
 - A function, `gen_initial_guess`, which provides an initial set of values (α_init_c) for the continuous constraint parameters.
 - **Role in Loop:** This level is responsible for structural (discrete) changes to the problem formulation, continuously refining the constraint proposals based on feedback from lower levels.

2. **Level 2: Constraint Parameter Optimization (Black-Box Optimization - BBO):**
 - **Objective:** To refine the continuous constraint parameters (α_c) for a given, fixed set of discrete constraints (α_i) provided by Level 1, with the goal of minimizing the extrinsic task cost Ψ.
 - **Mechanism:** A generic black-box optimizer (e.g., CMA-ES, probabilistic hill climbing) is employed. This optimizer iteratively proposes new values for α_c.
 - **Evaluation:** Each proposed set of α_c parameters is evaluated by instantiating the NLP (with fixed α_i), solving it for a trajectory x (via Level 3), and then simulating x to compute the extrinsic cost Ψ. Since Ψ is often fast to evaluate, even if non-differentiable, this allows for efficient exploration and optimization of α_c.

3. **Level 3: Gradient-Based Trajectory Optimization:**
 - **Objective:** To generate a smooth, collision-free robot trajectory (x) that satisfies all task constraints, given a fully parameterized NLP (i.e., with both α_i and α_c fixed).
 - **Mechanism:** This level solves the instantiated NLP: `min_x ∫_0^T f(x(t), α) dt` subject to `g(x(t), α) ≤ 0` and `h(x(t), α) = 0`.
 - **Solver:** A second-order Augmented Lagrangian method is used for this gradient-based optimization, leveraging its efficiency for continuous optimization problems.
 - **Output & Feedback:** The result is a smooth joint-state trajectory x. This trajectory is then rolled out in a physical simulator to calculate the full task cost J(x, α), which includes the extrinsic cost Ψ. This full cost is then propagated back to higher levels to inform the meta-optimization loop.

### 6. Closing the Loop

The three levels are iterated for a predefined number of steps or until convergence. Crucially, feedback from the lower levels is provided to the foundation model at Level 1. This feedback includes information such as the lowest trajectory cost achieved, the final state after trajectory execution, and a target cost for convergence. This closed-loop mechanism allows the LLM to continuously improve its constraint proposals, learning from the outcomes of the numerical optimization and simulation, thus acting as a black-box optimizer for the overall program structure.

### 7. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated MOPS against two prominent language-conditioned TAMP approaches: Code-as-Policies (CaP) and PRoC3S, across two distinct robotic manipulation domains.

### 8. Experimental Setup

- **Environments:**
 - **Pushing Environment:** Tasks involved a Panda robot pushing cuboids into configurations such as a circle, a straight line, or around an obstacle. This environment incorporated static and dynamic obstacles, increasing planning complexity.
 - **Drawing Environment:** Tasks required a Panda robot to draw geometric shapes (five-pointed star, regular pentagon, hash character) on a tilted whiteboard. This challenged the system to produce visually accurate symbols despite perspective distortion from a top-down camera view, a more complex setup than previous work.
- **Baselines:**
 - **Code-as-Policies (CaP):** An optimization-free approach where the LLM directly generates complete policy code, including high-level decision logic and low-level helper functions.
 - **PRoC3S:** An approach that closes the plan-generation loop but employs uniform sampling to determine continuous action parameters.
- **Evaluation Parameters:** Experiments involved 1,000 to 1,500 sampling/optimization steps per LLM query and a maximum of two feedback iterations (three total FM prompts). Performance was measured as 1 - log-normalized error, with higher values indicating better performance. All results were averaged over five independent runs.

### 9. Performance Comparison

- **Overall Superiority of MOPS:** MOPS demonstrated superior or at least equivalent performance compared to both CaP and PRoC3S across all six tasks in both the Pushing and Drawing domains (Figure 1 and 4).
- **Challenges for Baselines in Drawing Domain:** In the Drawing environment, optimization-free methods like CaP largely failed to solve tasks. This was attributed to the high precision required for geometric drawing, which the LLM's initial guesses for action sequences and precise motions lacked.
- **MOPS Improvement Over Sampling:** MOPS significantly improved upon sampling-based methods like PRoC3S, particularly evident in tasks requiring precise numerical parameters. For instance, in the Pushing domain, MOPS was the only method capable of achieving complex multi-block configurations (Figure 3), where the dimensionality of the search space for numerical parameters made random sampling approaches inefficient.

### 10. Ablation Studies

To understand the contributions of individual components within MOPS, ablation studies were conducted:

- **Role of Continuous Parameter Optimization (Level 2: BBO):**
 - Comparison was made between Random Sampling (RS), CMA-ES, and Probabilistic Hill Climbing (HC) for optimizing continuous constraint parameters.
 - **CMA-ES** consistently performed best across most tasks, efficiently identifying appropriate constraint parameters to minimize task cost (Figure 5).
 - **Random Sampling** showed reasonable performance when the LLM's initial guess was already good, but it failed to achieve substantial improvements on more challenging tasks, indicating that a dedicated optimization step is critical.
 - The study confirmed that a formal optimization method at Level 2 significantly enhances the LLM's initial guesses.

- **Importance of the LLM Feedback Loop (Level 1: Meta-Optimization):**
 - The outer feedback loop, where the LLM continuously refines constraint proposals based on performance feedback, was found to be crucial for reliable problem-solving.
 - While an initial constraint set could sometimes suffice, the variance in performance across runs was notably high without feedback. After one or two rounds of feedback, the variance decreased, and performance consistently improved (Figure 6).
 - In the Drawing environment, although the LLM often provided a correct initial number of lines, the feedback loop proved vital in scenarios where the zero-shot guess was suboptimal (e.g., incorrect number of lines or inaccurate initial positions).

- **LLM Ablation Study (Appendix A.5):**
 - An evaluation of various LLMs and VLMs for code generation showed that models like DeepSeek-R1, GPT-4-turbo, and GPT-4o-mini achieved high success rates (100%) in generating correct code following instructions.
 - Several other models (e.g., Qwen2 variants) had a 0% success rate, commonly failing by hallucinating functions or omitting key instructions.

### 11. Real-World Experiments (Appendix A.4)

MOPS was successfully deployed on a Franka Panda robot for both drawing and block pushing tasks. While the method transferred effectively to the real robot, it required careful calibration, highlighting the difference between full state information in simulation and limited access in the physical world.

### 12. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces Meta-Optimization and Program Search (MOPS), presenting a method that advances the field of Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) by addressing limitations inherent in both classical and contemporary foundation model (FM)-based approaches.

### 13. Significant Contributions

- **Improved TAMP Interface:** MOPS offers a novel perspective on TAMP by formulating language-conditioned problems as a meta-optimization over trajectory optimization programs. This approach shifts the paradigm from searching over action sequences to searching over constraint sequences, providing a more expressive and flexible interface between high-level language instructions and low-level robot control.
- **Enhanced Precision via Multi-Level Optimization:** The three-level optimization architecture is a key contribution. By combining the semantic reasoning capabilities of an LLM for discrete constraint selection (Level 1), a zero-order black-box optimizer for continuous parameter refinement (Level 2), and a gradient-based solver for trajectory generation (Level 3), MOPS effectively leverages the strengths of each component. This hierarchical structure specifically overcomes the known challenges of LLMs with geometric and numerical precision, leading to significantly improved task success rates compared to methods relying on sampling or direct code generation.
- **Robustness and Efficiency:** The iterative feedback loop, where the LLM refines its constraint proposals based on performance outcomes from lower optimization levels, enhances the robustness of the system. This continuous self-improvement mechanism allows the system to converge to better solutions and handle complex tasks more reliably, as demonstrated by the ablation studies on the feedback loop's importance.
- **Applicability to Complex Tasks:** MOPS has been shown to perform effectively on challenging object manipulation (pushing blocks into specific formations, obstacle avoidance) and drawing tasks requiring high visual and geometric accuracy. Its ability to solve such tasks, where baselines struggled due to lack of precision or efficiency, indicates its potential for broader application in complex, real-world robotic scenarios.

### 14. Potential Impact

- **Advancement in Language-Instructed Robotics:** This work contributes to bridging the gap between natural language instructions and precise robotic execution. By providing a more robust and accurate method for translating high-level goals into executable robot motions, MOPS can facilitate more intuitive and flexible human-robot interaction.
- **Foundation for Future TAMP Systems:** The meta-optimization framework presented could serve as a foundation for developing future TAMP systems that can dynamically adapt and learn optimal planning strategies. The explicit optimization of both the program structure (constraints) and its parameters provides a powerful mechanism for complex problem-solving.
- **Improved Real-World Deployment:** The successful deployment of MOPS on a physical robot platform, despite requiring careful calibration, indicates its practical viability. As state estimation techniques and LLM capabilities evolve, such a framework could become increasingly critical for robots operating in unstructured or partially observable environments.

### 15. Limitations and Future Work

The paper acknowledges several limitations, which also suggest avenues for future research:
- **Hyperparameter Tuning:** MOPS requires tuning additional hyperparameters for the inner optimizers. Future work could explore integrating these hyperparameters into the LLM's output or employing adaptive tuning mechanisms.
- **Task-Specific Cost Functions:** The method relies on predefined task-specific cost functions (Ψ). Research into learning or predicting these cost functions could enhance generality.
- **Constraint Granularity:** The current experiments aggregate multiple constraints. Future advancements in VLMs may enable reasoning at the individual constraint level, potentially offering finer control.
- **Reliance on Full State Knowledge:** The method currently assumes full state knowledge of the scene. Integrating visual language models (VLMs) or advanced state estimation techniques could relax this requirement, making the system more applicable to real-world scenarios with partial observability.

In conclusion, MOPS presents a notable step forward in robotic manipulation by integrating LLMs with numerical optimization methods in a hierarchical, iterative framework. It demonstrates a practical approach to achieving precision and flexibility in language-instructed robot tasks, paving the way for more intelligent and capable robotic agents.
