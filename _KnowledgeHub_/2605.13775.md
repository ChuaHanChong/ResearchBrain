# RoboEvolve: Co-Evolving Planner-Simulator for Robotic Manipulation with Limited Data

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboEvolve introduces a self-evolving framework that co-evolves a VLM planner and a VGM simulator to acquire robotic manipulation skills from limited, unlabeled data. The system demonstrates enhanced task success, particularly for complex multi-stage tasks, and achieves greater data efficiency compared to methods relying on extensive manually annotated datasets.

## Problem

- Scarcity of high-quality, task-aligned physical interaction data for robotic manipulation, hindering scalability.
- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) as planners often generate physically infeasible trajectories due to semantic-spatial misalignment.
- Video Generation Models (VGMs) as simulators frequently produce "physical hallucinations" without sufficient task-aligned interaction data for training.

## Method

- RoboEvolve employs a co-evolutionary loop integrating a VLM planner and a VGM simulator, inspired by Complementary Learning Systems (CLS) theory.
- It features a dual-phase learning cycle: "Daytime Learning" for online exploration and "Nighttime Learning" for offline consolidation, leveraging both successes and "near-miss" failures.
- A semantic-controlled multi-granular reward function guides training, and a progress-based curriculum enables autonomous scaling to complex tasks.

## Results

- The simulator (S) showed consistent improvements, with relative Task Success gains of 40.0% on Level-1, 49.6% on Level-2, and 55.9% on Level-3 tasks compared to a static baseline.
- The VLM planner (P) performance improved by an average of 36.4 absolute points on EB-ALFRED and 24.6 points on EB-Habitat over the base Qwen3-VL model.
- Achieved high data efficiency, surpassing a static SFT baseline trained on 25K manually annotated trajectories with only 300 unlabeled seed images (generating 7.6K synthesized trajectories).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLMs and VGMs can mutually reinforce each other: VLMs guide VGMs with diverse semantic prompts, while VGMs provide physical feasibility feedback to refine VLM plans.
> - Systematically learning from "near-miss" failure cases is crucial for stabilizing policies and preventing physical hallucinations, rather than solely focusing on successful outcomes.
> - An interleaved dual-phase learning process (exploration and consolidation) prevents model degradation and supports robust continual learning through timely rectification.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of embodied robotic intelligence is significantly hindered by the scarcity of high-quality, task-aligned physical interaction data. Traditional methods for collecting robot trajectories are resource-intensive, requiring extensive human demonstrations or precise annotations, which limits their scalability. This research addresses this fundamental bottleneck by proposing a novel approach that leverages recent advancements in large language and generative models.

Currently, two paradigms show promise but have inherent limitations when applied to robotic manipulation:
1. **Vision-Language Models (VLMs) as Planners:** VLMs excel at semantic understanding and generating high-level plans. However, their plans often lack grounding in physical realities, leading to semantic-spatial misalignment and physically infeasible trajectories. Integrating robust verification for VLM plans typically necessitates substantial manual supervision, which is impractical for large-scale applications.
2. **Video Generation Models (VGMs) as Simulators:** VGMs offer a scalable alternative for synthesizing interaction data, potentially reducing the need for costly physical data collection. Nevertheless, due to the limited availability of task-aligned interaction data for their own training, VGMs frequently produce "physical hallucinations"—visually plausible but physically incorrect or impossible trajectories—which restricts their utility for embodied learning.

Previous research efforts have explored VLM/LLM self-play for policy evolution, but these approaches often prioritize successful outcomes, overlooking valuable insights from failures. Similarly, VGM-based reinforcement learning, while capable of uncovering physical insights, also typically relies on pre-annotated, task-specific datasets and can be inefficient due to a focus on successful trajectories.

This work posits that VLMs and VGMs can mutually reinforce each other. VLMs can provide diverse, semantically grounded task prompts and judgments to guide VGMs, while VGMs can simulate physical feasibility and offer feedback to refine VLM planning. RoboEvolve introduces a framework designed to directly integrate a VLM planner and a VGM simulator into a co-evolutionary loop. This system aims to address the aforementioned limitations by systematically leveraging both successful and failed interaction attempts and operating on limited, unlabeled data, a departure from prior data-hungry or success-biased paradigms. The approach is inspired by the Complementary Learning Systems (CLS) theory from cognitive science, suggesting that effective learning involves both exploratory and consolidative processes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a scalable and autonomous learning framework for robotic manipulation that mitigates the dependence on extensive, manually annotated interaction data. The system, named RoboEvolve, aims to enable a robotic agent to acquire complex manipulation skills from a limited set of unlabeled seed images.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges in the field of embodied AI:
1. **Data Scarcity for Robotic Manipulation:** The collection of high-quality robotic trajectories, especially those with precise annotations or human demonstrations, is notoriously expensive and time-consuming. This data bottleneck impedes the progress and scalability of robotic manipulation systems.
2. **Limitations of Existing Foundation Models in Embodied Contexts:** While VLMs and VGMs have advanced semantic understanding and visual synthesis, their direct application to physical robotics is constrained. VLMs, despite their planning capabilities, often generate plans that lack real-world physical grounding. VGMs, though capable of generating videos, struggle to produce physically consistent trajectories without sufficient task-aligned interaction data, frequently suffering from "physical hallucinations."
3. **Need for Mutual Reinforcement:** The authors hypothesize that the strengths of VLMs (semantic reasoning, task prompting) and VGMs (physical simulation, trajectory generation) can be combined to overcome their individual weaknesses. A VLM could guide a VGM towards semantically meaningful and physically grounded trajectories, while a VGM could provide critical feedback to refine a VLM's plans based on physical feasibility.
4. **Inefficiency of Success-Oriented Learning:** Existing self-evolving systems, particularly in language domains, predominantly focus on successful outcomes, often discarding "failure cases" as noise. The authors are motivated to explore how insights from unsuccessful attempts can be systematically harnessed to stabilize and improve policy optimization, drawing inspiration from cognitive science's Complementary Learning Systems (CLS) theory, which highlights the role of consolidation in learning.
5. **Autonomous Skill Acquisition and Scaling:** The goal is to design a system that can autonomously scale from simple atomic actions to complex, multi-stage tasks without external human supervision or predefined reward functions, operating purely on unlabeled visual input. This aims to shift the paradigm from data-hungry model fitting to autonomous self-improvement.

In summary, the core research question guiding this work is: "How can we design a collaboration system that couples a VLM planner and a VGM simulator to supervise and evolve each other, effectively leveraging both successes and failures, while scaling from limited, unlabeled data?"

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoboEvolve is a self-evolving framework that integrates a VLM planner (P) and a VGM simulator (S) within a co-evolutionary loop. The methodology is inspired by the Complementary Learning Systems (CLS) theory, which posits that effective learning emerges from an interplay between exploratory and consolidative processes.

The framework operates through a dual-phase evolution loop:

#### 3.1. Scene-Grounding Task Initialization

To initiate the learning process from unlabeled seed images, RoboEvolve first transforms raw images into structured task representations:
- **Structured Scene Parsing:** The planner P analyzes an initial seed image `I` to extract objects `{o_k}`, their spatial relations (e.g., `on`, `in`, `near`), and affordance priors (e.g., `pickable`, `openable`). To enhance robustness against perceptual errors, a self-consistency voting mechanism is applied, where `m=8` independent parsing samples are generated, and only majority-consistent entities and relations are retained.
- **Task Template Instantiation:** The structured scene representation `S(I)` is mapped to 13 fundamental task templates (e.g., "pick-and-place", "stacking"), following the BridgeData V2 taxonomy. P then instantiates and composes these primitives into structured plans.
- **Atomic-Action Difficulty Scoring:** Each instantiated plan `π = <a1..., an>` is decomposed into atomic actions `a_i` (e.g., "grasp", "lift"). The difficulty `D(π|I)` is quantified as the cumulative cost of its constituent actions, where each `a_i` has a unit cost `c(a_i) = 1`. This metric guides the progressive curriculum evolution.

#### 3.2. Daytime Learning: Online Exploration

This phase focuses on active exploration and physically grounded behavioral discovery:
- **Simulator Daytime Training:** The simulator S's physical fidelity is improved. For an initialized task `τ`, S generates `K` video trajectories `{V_1..., V_K}`. S is optimized using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to maximize a reward `R(V)` that evaluates the semantic and physical alignment of the trajectory. The advantage `A_k` for GRPO is computed as `R(V_k)` minus the average reward of the group.
- **Planner Daytime Training:** The planner P is evolved to handle longer-horizon tasks. P generates multiple candidate plans `{π_1..., π_K}`. A selective simulation strategy is employed: `π*`, the most consistent plan chosen via self-consistency voting, is executed in S through segment-wise simulation, where each segment's difficulty is constrained to S's current capability (≤ `D`). P is optimized using GRPO, incorporating `R(S(π*))` as a physical feedback term. A binary gate `1[π=π*]` prevents P from adopting executionally infeasible logic while insulating it from potential simulator hallucinations.
- **Semantic-controlled Multi-Granular Reward `R(·)`:** Unlike coarse visual-language alignment, this reward explicitly prioritizes semantic faithfulness and captures subtle manipulation failures. It is defined as `R(V) = I_sem * (s_F + ws * s_S + s_E)`.
 - `I_sem`: A semantic-alignment indicator. P acts as a critic, revising the original goal `G` to `G'` if the trajectory `V` conflicts. `Sim(G, G')` serves as `I_sem`, proportionally suppressing physical scores upon semantic deviation.
 - `s_F`: Frame-level consistency (binary, 1 if object persistence and spatial smoothness are maintained across all frames).
 - `s_S`: Segment-level execution (normalized binary detection of action `a_i` within `Seg_i`).
 - `s_E`: Episode-level success (binary, 1 if the final task goal is achieved).
 These discretized components provide robust supervision and clear failure criteria.

#### 3.3. Nighttime Learning: Offline Consolidation

This phase consolidates experiences by leveraging both successes and failures to refine P and S:
- **Simulator Nighttime Training:** S suppresses physical hallucinations by learning from "near-miss" failures. Video preference pairs `(V+, V-)` are constructed. `V+` consists of high-reward trajectories (s_E=1, I_sem=1), representing physically consistent executions. `V-` consists of hard negatives that satisfy some validity criteria (e.g., s_F=1 or ws*s_S=1) but fail the overall task (s_E=0). S is optimized using a Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) objective `L_Nighttime(S)`, contrasting these pairs to distinguish plausible but invalid motions from physically sound ones.
- **Planner Nighttime Training:** P extracts rich preference signals across three cognitive dimensions:
 - **Planning-level (D_P):** Given an initial image `I`, P learns to prioritize logical consistency, preferring the consensus plan `π*` over suboptimal candidates (runner-up or randomly corrupted plans).
 - **Understanding-level (D_U):** Given a high-reward video `V+`, P enhances visual grounding by favoring the validated goal `G` over erroneous back-translations.
 - **Transition-level (D_T):** Given only the initial and final frames `(f1, fT)` from `V+`, P internalizes causality by predicting the correct underlying intent `π*` over misidentified intents.
 P is refined by minimizing a cumulative DPO objective `L_Nighttime(P)` over these hierarchical preference pairs.

#### 3.4. Dual-Phase Curriculum Evolution

To ensure stable and progressive learning, RoboEvolve incorporates a progress-based curriculum:
- The difficulty space is discretized into `B` bins based on `D(π|I)`.
- The system tracks the success rate `S(b)` and learning progress `P_k(b) = S_k(b) - S_k-Δ(b)` for each bin `b`.
- An Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) strategy, `b*k = argmax_b(P_k(b) + λ * sqrt(log(sum_j n_k(j)) / (n_k(b)+1)))`, is used to select the optimal difficulty for the next exploration phase, where `n_k(b)` is the sampling count and `λ` is an exploration weight. This mechanism shifts the sampling budget towards higher-complexity tasks as simpler tasks reach saturation, enabling continuous scaling without manual intervention.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluate RoboEvolve's effectiveness, data efficiency, and continual learning capabilities by comparing it against static paradigms and state-of-the-art methods on established benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Effectiveness Against Static Paradigms (RQ1)

- **Simulator Evaluation (Table 1):** RoboEvolve demonstrated consistent improvements for the simulator (S), particularly for complex tasks. Compared to a static SFT cold-started baseline, RoboEvolve's relative Task Success gains amplified with increasing task complexity: 40.0% on Level-1, 49.6% on Level-2, and 55.9% on Level-3 tasks (e.g., 0.505 success rate on Level-3 for RoboEvolve versus 0.341 for the SFT baseline). RoboEvolve achieved the highest VBench scores, Task Success Rates, and User Preference across all difficulty levels.
- **Planner Evaluation (Table 2):** For the VLM planner (P), RoboEvolve elevated the performance of the Qwen3-VL base model by an average of 36.4 absolute points on EB-ALFRED and 24.6 absolute points on EB-Habitat. The framework showed notable improvements in spatial and long-horizon reasoning, achieving competitive results against domain-specific expert models without task-specific training.
- **Qualitative Results (Figures 3, 4, 12, 13):** Visual comparisons indicated that static baselines struggled with object distortions and premature task termination, especially in multi-stage instructions. A "Daytime-Only" variant, which lacked nighttime consolidation, exhibited severe physical hallucinations, such as object disappearance. In contrast, RoboEvolve consistently generated trajectories that maintained structural integrity, semantic alignment, and continuous physical plausibility through complex executions.

#### 4.2. Curriculum Scaling Analysis (RQ2)

- **Interleaved vs. Sequential Phases (Figure 5, Left & Middle):** Ablation studies revealed the importance of the dual-phase, interleaved evolution loop. Variants that relied on isolated phases (e.g., "Daytime-only") quickly saturated and accumulated uncorrected physical hallucinations. A sequential approach ("D+N"), where nighttime consolidation occurred only after all daytime exploration, resulted in irreversible policy degradation and significantly lower Task Success. The interleaved sleep-wake cycles of RoboEvolve were shown to provide timely rectification, preventing model collapse during continuous exploration by penalizing out-of-distribution behaviors through "near-miss" failures.
- **Continual Learning (Figure 5, Right):** RoboEvolve demonstrated robust continual learning capabilities. The progressive curriculum successfully guided the system through difficulty stages from D=1 to D=4, maintaining monotonic performance gains across all metrics for both S and P. The system autonomously advanced to higher-complexity tasks only when foundational skills were mastered, avoiding catastrophic forgetting of simpler actions and enabling seamless mastery of increasingly complex, composite tasks.

#### 4.3. Data Efficiency Analysis (RQ3)

- **Performance vs. Seed Images (Figure 6):** RoboEvolve exhibited extreme data efficiency. Scaling the number of initial unlabeled seed images from 300 to 1000 consistently led to monotonic performance gains for both the simulator and the planner. Starting with only 300 unlabeled seeds, which generated approximately 7.6K synthesized trajectories, RoboEvolve surpassed the Level-3 Task Success of the SFT baseline trained on the entire BridgeData V2 dataset (approximately 25K manually annotated trajectories) and also outperformed its P's EB-ALFRED performance. This indicates that RoboEvolve autonomously synthesizes high-density, task-relevant supervision that is more effective than larger quantities of static, manually annotated data.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies (Appendix C)

- **Semantic-Controlled Multi-Granular Reward (Table 4):** The semantic-alignment indicator (I_sem) was identified as the most critical component, with its removal leading to a substantial performance drop (11.0 to 11.6 absolute points on reasoning benchmarks). All physical granularity components (frame-level, segment-level, episode-level) also contributed to performance, with episode-level success (s_E) having the most pronounced impact among physical checks.
- **Selective Simulation Strategy (Table 5):** Removing the selective simulation strategy resulted in a 6.0 absolute point average degradation in planner performance on EB-ALFRED, particularly affecting visual and long-horizon capabilities. This highlights its role in preventing overfitting to simulator hallucinations and managing computational costs.
- **Hierarchical Preference Optimization (Table 6):** All three preference levels (Planning-level D_P, Understanding-level D_U, Transition-level D_T) were found to provide orthogonal benefits. Omitting D_P or D_U led to a 7.0 absolute point average drop in success rate, impacting logical consistency and visual grounding, respectively. D_T also contributed to complex and visual reasoning.
- **Curriculum Hyperparameter (Table 7):** Analysis of the curriculum exploration hyperparameter `λ` demonstrated that an optimal value (λ=0.10) enabled efficient difficulty progression, unlocking multi-stage capabilities and leading to positive backward transfer where higher-level task mastery reinforced foundational skills. Suboptimal `λ` values either stagnated learning or delayed progression, yielding inferior overall performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboEvolve presents a framework for addressing the data scarcity challenge in robotic manipulation by integrating a VLM planner and a VGM simulator within a mutually reinforcing co-evolutionary loop. The system's operation, based on a cognitive-inspired dual-phase mechanism of daytime exploration and nighttime consolidation, allows it to learn robustly from limited, unlabeled seed images.

The significance of this work lies in several key aspects:
- **Data Efficiency:** RoboEvolve demonstrates that autonomously synthesized, high-density supervision generated from a small number of unlabeled seed images can achieve performance comparable to or surpassing that derived from massive, manually annotated datasets. This suggests a potential reduction in the prohibitive costs associated with human data collection and annotation in robotics.
- **Mutual Reinforcement of Modalities:** The framework effectively bridges the gap between high-level semantic planning (VLM) and low-level physical execution simulation (VGM), leveraging their complementary strengths to mitigate their individual weaknesses (semantic-spatial misalignment and physical hallucinations).
- **Learning from Failures:** By systematically mining and consolidating "near-miss" failure cases during its nighttime learning phase, RoboEvolve departs from conventional success-biased learning paradigms. This mechanism contributes to policy stabilization and prevents the accumulation of uncorrected errors during exploration.
- **Robust Continual Learning:** The autonomous progressive curriculum enables the system to scale from simple atomic actions to complex, multi-stage tasks without catastrophic forgetting, indicating a capacity for open-ended skill acquisition.

The potential impact of RoboEvolve on the field of embodied AI is notable:
- **Scalability for General-Purpose Physical Intelligence:** By providing a path for autonomous self-improvement and skill acquisition with minimal initial data, RoboEvolve contributes to overcoming a fundamental bottleneck in developing more generalized and capable robotic systems.
- **Reduced Reliance on Human Supervision:** The framework's ability to operate largely without human annotations or external reward signals could accelerate the development and deployment of robotic manipulation solutions across various domains.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** While the current framework operates in a generative visual domain, its successful co-evolutionary mechanism provides a foundation for future integration with real-world robotic hardware. Future work could involve connecting this pipeline to world action models (WAMs) for seamless translation of synthesized plans into low-level sensorimotor control, integrating dedicated trainable reward models, and enhancing scene-grounding capabilities with specialized visual/spatial grounding models. This could pave the way for robots that can learn and adapt complex manipulation skills in dynamic, real-world environments with increasing autonomy.
