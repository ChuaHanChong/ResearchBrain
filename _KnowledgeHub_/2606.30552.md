# Training Vision-Language-Action Models with Dense Embodied Chain-of-Thought Supervision

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Renmin University of China introduce ZR-0, a 2.6-billion-parameter Vision-Language-Action model that enhances cross-embodiment transfer by leveraging dense Embodied Chain-of-Thought (ECoT) supervision during pre-training. ZR-0 achieved an average success rate of 97.8% on LIBERO, 69.3% on RoboCasa GR-1 Tabletop, and 88.70% on RoboTwin 2.0 (Clean scenes), while also demonstrating improved performance on real-world xArm tasks compared to prior methods.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models struggle with effective cross-embodiment transfer due to the heterogeneity of robot platforms' low-level state and action spaces.
- Prior format-level solutions for unifying robot control interfaces often fail to achieve true semantic alignment, hindering the learning of shared, transferable representations.
- A lack of scalable methods to capture and supervise the high-level cognitive processes (scene perception, planning) that are largely embodiment-agnostic across different robots.

## Method

- Developed **ZR-0**, a 2.6-billion-parameter VLA model with a ==dual-stream architecture== comprising a Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct-initialized ==VLM (System 2)== and a ==Diffusion Transformer-based action expert (System 1)==.
- Introduced dense ==Embodied Chain-of-Thought (ECoT)== supervision, generated from an automated VLM-based pipeline, on the large-scale ==ProcCorpus-60M== dataset to explicitly guide the VLM in capturing high-level, transferable cognitive processes.
- Designed the architecture to utilize ECoT supervision during training for representation learning, but ==bypass its generation entirely during inference== for efficiency (approx. 90 ms latency per action chunk on NVIDIA A6000 GPU).

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **97.8%** on LIBERO (single-arm), including a **4.0-point** improvement over π0.5 on long-horizon tasks, demonstrating enhanced capabilities in sequential multi-goal execution.
- Obtained an average success rate of **69.3%** on RoboCasa GR-1 Tabletop (humanoid), surpassing JoyAI-RA by **6.1 points**, and competitive performance on RoboTwin 2.0 (bimanual) with **88.70%** (Clean scenes) despite using a smaller pre-training corpus.
- Outperformed π0.5 on real-world xArm tasks with an average task progress score of **76.0** vs. **67.8**, particularly showing a **27.9-point** improvement on 'Push Blocks' tasks due to enhanced text recognition and reasoning capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-level cognitive processes in manipulation tasks (scene perception, object identification, task planning) are largely embodiment-agnostic and represent crucial transferable knowledge for cross-embodiment pre-training.
> - Dense, structured Embodied Chain-of-Thought (ECoT) supervision can effectively align the internal representations of a VLM across heterogeneous robot embodiments, fostering general principles of interaction.
> - A dual-stream model allows for the integration of high-level cognitive reasoning (VLM) with low-level continuous action control (action expert), where the VLM's rich features can guide the action expert while maintaining inference efficiency by decoupling ECoT generation from real-time action prediction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of embodied artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on the development of generalist robots capable of performing diverse manipulation tasks across various physical platforms. The paradigm of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models has emerged as a prominent approach, leveraging large-scale pre-training techniques successful in natural language processing and computer vision to equip robots with transferable physical commonsense and manipulation skills. These VLA models aim to learn from aggregated robotics datasets to facilitate efficient adaptation to new tasks, scenes, and robot embodiments.

A significant challenge within this paradigm is achieving effective cross-embodiment transfer. Robot platforms exhibit substantial heterogeneity in their kinematic configurations, control interfaces (e.g., joint position vs. end-effector pose), base types, and sensor setups. These differences lead to disparate low-level state and action spaces, where dimensions may carry distinct physical meanings across robots. Prior approaches have largely addressed this through format-level solutions such as zero-padding, per-embodiment normalization, or attempts to define unified action spaces by assigning fixed semantic roles to dimensions. However, these methods often enable joint training without fully resolving the deeper issue of semantic alignment—ensuring that the model learns shared, abstract, and truly transferable representations rather than merely fitting embodiment-specific patterns within a common architecture.

The paper posits that while low-level control is embodiment-specific, the high-level cognitive processes underlying manipulation—such as scene perception, object identification, task planning, and sub-task decomposition—are largely shared across different robotic platforms. This embodiment-agnostic reasoning represents the transferable knowledge crucial for successful cross-embodiment pre-training.

Within the VLA model landscape, ZR-0 builds upon dual-stream architectures that combine a pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM) with an action expert. This follows a trajectory set by models like $\pi^0$, $\pi^{0.5}$, and GR00T N1, which have moved towards continuous action prediction via methods like flow matching, in contrast to earlier approaches (e.g., RT-2, OpenVLA, FAST) that relied on discrete action tokenization and autoregressive generation. ZR-0 differentiates itself by integrating dense Embodied Chain-of-Thought (ECoT) supervision into the VLM stream to specifically enhance cross-embodiment representation learning. This also aligns with efforts in vision-language data co-training, which seeks to mitigate data scarcity and catastrophic forgetting in VLM-based robot learning by incorporating auxiliary vision-language data. The ECoT framework in ZR-0 focuses on generating embodied reasoning data directly from robot trajectories at scale, thereby providing fine-grained, manipulation-context-specific supervision designed for cross-embodiment alignment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to advance cross-embodiment transfer in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by facilitating the learning of semantically aligned, transferable representations. The authors identify a fundamental challenge in current VLA models: despite efforts to accommodate diverse robot platforms, the inherent differences in their low-level state and action spaces impede the transfer of learned skills across embodiments. Existing solutions, primarily focusing on format-level unification, do not adequately address the deeper need for semantic alignment.

The core motivation for the proposed approach stems from the observation that the high-level cognitive processes involved in manipulation tasks—including perceiving the scene, identifying objects, reasoning about task progress, planning sequences of actions, and decomposing complex tasks into simpler sub-tasks—are largely independent of the specific robot hardware. A robot picking up a cup, for instance, engages in a similar cognitive trajectory regardless of its degrees of freedom or control interface. The authors hypothesize that this embodiment-agnostic reasoning constitutes the crucial transferable knowledge that large-scale cross-embodiment pre-training should capture.

Based on this observation, the key objectives and motivations are:

- **To leverage Embodied Chain-of-Thought (ECoT) reasoning as a dense supervision signal:** The motivation is to explicitly guide the VLM during training to capture and articulate these high-level, transferable cognitive processes. By providing structured reasoning annotations, ECoT aims to align the VLM's internal representations across heterogeneous embodiments, ensuring that the model learns general principles of interaction rather than platform-specific patterns.
- **To develop a dual-stream architecture (System 1/System 2):** This design is motivated by the need to integrate high-level cognitive reasoning with low-level continuous control. System 2 (the VLM) is responsible for processing complex visual and linguistic inputs to generate structured ECoT reasoning, thus capturing the embodiment-agnostic understanding of the task and scene. System 1 (the Diffusion Transformer-based action expert) is then motivated to translate these high-level representations into precise, embodiment-specific continuous action chunks. The coupling between these systems aims to enable a rich flow of information from reasoning to action.
- **To optimize for inference efficiency while retaining representational benefits:** A practical concern with generating explicit textual reasoning is the computational overhead during inference. The authors are motivated to design the architecture such that ECoT supervision can be utilized extensively during training to enhance learned representations, but entirely bypassed during inference. This is achieved through a specific cross-attention mechanism that allows the action expert to attend only to the VLM's input prompt features, making ECoT generation unnecessary for real-time operation.
- **To utilize a large-scale, ECoT-enriched dataset:** The successful application of dense ECoT supervision requires a substantial and diverse training corpus. The motivation is to pre-train ZR-0 on a dataset like ProcCorpus-60M, which offers dense ECoT annotations across numerous frames and heterogeneous embodiments. This provides the necessary scale and diversity to enable the model to learn broadly applicable, transferable representations.
- **To integrate general vision-language data:** To prevent "catastrophic forgetting" of broad visual and linguistic capabilities inherent in pre-trained VLMs, the authors are motivated to mix general-purpose vision-language datasets into the pre-training corpus. This co-training strategy aims to preserve the VLM's general understanding, thereby improving ZR-0's robustness and ability to interpret diverse instructions in novel scenes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors introduce ZR-0, a 2.6 billion parameter end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model. ZR-0 is designed to facilitate cross-embodiment transfer through dense Embodied Chain-of-Thought (ECoT) supervision and a dual-stream architecture.

**Model Architecture:**
ZR-0 adopts a dual-stream architecture, conceptually inspired by the System 1 / System 2 cognitive framework:
- **System 2 (Vision-Language Model - VLM):** This component is initialized from Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct. Its role is to process natural language task instructions and visual observations (from multiple camera views, resized to 224x224) to generate a structured ECoT reasoning sequence during training. The VLM’s last-layer hidden states, representing rich visual and linguistic features, are then passed to the action expert. The ECoT supervision aims to enhance the VLM's learned representations for downstream action prediction.
- **System 1 (Diffusion Transformer-based Action Expert):** This component is responsible for generating continuous action chunks. It takes the VLM's extracted features and the current robot state vector as input. The action expert architecture comprises a state encoder, an action encoder, a stack of Diffusion Transformer (DiT) blocks, and an action decoder (all implemented as MLPs).
 - **DiT Blocks:** These blocks consist of a repeating pattern of one self-attention layer followed by three cross-attention layers. Self-attention layers enable bidirectional interaction between state and action tokens. Crucially, the cross-attention layers allow state and action tokens (as queries) to condition on the VLM's output features (as keys and values). An attention mask is applied to ensure the action expert attends only to the VLM’s input prompt features (task instruction and images), deliberately *excluding* the ECoT tokens during cross-attention. This design choice is fundamental to enabling ECoT generation to be skipped entirely during inference. The 1:3 ratio of self-attention to cross-attention is employed to increase cross-modal interaction, allowing for more comprehensive integration of information from the VLM.

**Pre-Training Data:**
ZR-0 is pre-trained on **ProcCorpus-60M**, a large-scale ECoT-enriched robotic dataset.
- **Scale and Diversity:** ProcCorpus-60M aggregates approximately 60 million frames (equivalent to about 1,000 hours) from over 400,000 trajectories. This data is sourced from various real-robot and simulated datasets, including Open X-Embodiment, DROID, and RH20T, ensuring diversity across embodiments, tasks, scenes, and behaviors.
- **Dense ECoT Annotations:** A key feature is the dense ECoT annotation, covering 96.8% of all frames, generated through an automated VLM-based pipeline. This dense, structured supervision across heterogeneous embodiments is intended to facilitate the learning of aligned, transferable representations.
- **Components of ECoT:** Each ECoT annotation is a structured sequence with six components, each serving a specific training purpose:
 - **Scene Description:** Textual description for object recognition.
 - **Progress Assessment:** Reasoning about task progress and a binary completion indicator for task understanding.
 - **Future Plan:** Natural language description of remaining objectives for temporal reasoning and long-horizon planning.
 - **To-Do Actions:** Structured decomposition into atomic, imperative, embodiment-agnostic sub-tasks, serving as a mechanism for cross-embodiment alignment.
 - **Target Objects:** Bounding box annotations for visual grounding and directing spatial attention.
 - **Discrete Actions:** Embodiment-specific discrete action tokens (from the FAST tokenizer) to bridge high-level reasoning with low-level control.
- **Mixing General Vision-Language Data:** To maintain the VLM's general visual perception and language understanding and to mitigate catastrophic forgetting, general-purpose vision-language datasets (e.g., CapsFusion, Pixmo) are mixed into the pre-training corpus. These datasets are used to train only the VLM via standard language modeling, without involving action prediction.

**Training Objective:**
ZR-0 is optimized using a joint loss function:
- **Next-Token Prediction Loss (L_ntp):** A standard autoregressive language modeling loss used for ECoT generation, updating only the VLM parameters.
- **Denoising Vector Field Prediction Loss (L_fm):** For continuous action chunk prediction, based on flow matching. The model is trained to approximate the denoising vector field, mapping a noisy action chunk (constructed by adding Gaussian noise to the ground-truth action chunk) back to the original action chunk. This loss propagates gradients through both the action expert and the VLM. The sampling of the flow matching timestep $\tau$ from a Beta(1.5, 1.0) distribution emphasizes noisier timesteps during training.
- **Overall Loss:** A weighted sum, L = L_ntp + α L_fm, where α controls the trade-off.

**Inference:**
At inference time, ZR-0 receives task instructions, image observations, and robot state. It initializes a noisy action chunk from Gaussian noise and refines it iteratively via forward Euler integration over a series of denoising steps to produce the final action chunk. Crucially, ECoT sequences are *not* generated during inference. The cross-attention mask design ensures that the VLM's features from the input prompt are sufficient for the action expert, enabling the benefits of ECoT training without incurring the latency of autoregressive text generation. This results in an approximate inference latency of 90 ms per action chunk on an NVIDIA A6000 GPU.

**Implementation Details:**
The model has 2.6 billion parameters (2.1B in VLM, 0.5B in action expert). During pre-training, the action chunk length is 32, global batch size 1,024, and α=5. Robot states and actions are padded to 64 dimensions, with loss masking for padded dimensions. Min-max normalization is applied to each dimension. AdamW optimizer with cosine learning rate schedule, bfloat16 mixed precision, gradient clipping, DeepSpeed ZeRO, Flash-Attention 2, and gradient checkpointing are used for efficient training. For post-training on specific benchmarks, a smaller batch size (64), α=1, and varied action chunk lengths (H=10 or 16) are used.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ZR-0 was conducted across multiple simulation benchmarks and real-world experiments, covering single-arm, bimanual, and humanoid embodiments. The primary metric used was success rate (SR) for simulation and a task progress score for real-world tasks.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

ZR-0 demonstrated competitive performance on all three simulation benchmarks, with results supporting the effectiveness of ECoT-supervised pre-training for cross-embodiment adaptation.

- **LIBERO (Single-Arm):**
 - ZR-0 achieved an average success rate of 97.8%, outperforming other VLA models like $\pi^{0.5}$ and GR00T-N1.7.
 - The most notable performance difference was observed on the LIBERO-10 suite, which involves long-horizon tasks. ZR-0 achieved 96.4% success, a 4.0-point improvement over $\pi^{0.5}$, indicating enhanced capabilities in sequential multi-goal execution.
 - Performance on the Spatial, Object, and Goal suites was also high, aligning with the trend of saturation observed in recent methods for these less complex generalization axes.

- **RoboCasa GR-1 Tabletop (Humanoid):**
 - ZR-0 obtained an average success rate of 69.3%, surpassing the next best method, JoyAI-RA (63.2%), by 6.1 points.
 - Significant improvements were observed in pick-and-place tasks (e.g., CuttingboardToTieredbasket: 80% vs. 36% for JoyAI-RA; PlacematToPlate: 88% vs. 38%). This is attributed to the prevalence of pick-and-place primitives in the pre-training corpus and the benefits of ECoT-supervised representation alignment.
 - However, ZR-0 underperformed on "Close" tasks (e.g., BottleToCabinetClose: 39% vs. 84%; CanToDrawerClose: 47% vs. 90%), suggesting that skills less represented in the pre-training data benefit less from ECoT alignment.

- **RoboTwin 2.0 (Bimanual):**
 - ZR-0 achieved average success rates of 88.70% (Clean scenes) and 87.98% (Randomized scenes).
 - This performance was slightly better than LingBot-VLA, despite ZR-0 utilizing a substantially smaller pre-training corpus (approximately 1,000 hours vs. 20,000 hours for LingBot-VLA).
 - The model demonstrated high success rates (near 100%) on several tasks, including GrabRoller, HandoverMic, and ShakeBottle.
 - Strong performance was observed in bimanual coordination tasks (e.g., HandoverBlock: 93/87%; PickDualBottles: 97/98%).
 - ZR-0 exhibited robustness to visual variations, with only a 0.72-point average drop between Clean and Randomized settings, which was smaller than that observed in Motus (1.64 points) and $\pi^{0.5}$ (5.98 points). This suggests improved generalization to diverse visual conditions, potentially due to co-training with diverse vision-language data.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments (xArm Platform)

ZR-0 was compared with $\pi^{0.5}$ on four xArm tasks, each designed to evaluate distinct capabilities.
- ZR-0 achieved an average task progress score of 76.0, outperforming $\pi^{0.5}$ (67.8) by 8.2 points.
- **Push Blocks:** ZR-0 scored 94.0, a 27.9-point improvement over $\pi^{0.5}$ (66.1). This gain is attributed to VL data co-training and ECoT preserving the VLM's text recognition capabilities essential for OCR-based reasoning.
- **Clean Table:** ZR-0 scored 73.4 compared to 63.3 for $\pi^{0.5}$. This improvement aligns with ECoT’s "To-Do Actions" component, which aids in long-horizon task decomposition.
- **Pick & Place:** ZR-0 scored 66.7 versus 56.7 for $\pi^{0.5}$. This gain is linked to ECoT's "Scene Description" and "Target Objects" components, which enhance spatial reasoning and visual grounding.
- **Hang Cups:** $\pi^{0.5}$ (85.0) outperformed ZR-0 (70.0) on this task. This suggests that tasks requiring fine-grained motor precision might depend more heavily on the scale of action supervision during pre-training than on high-level reasoning alone.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study on the LIBERO benchmark investigated the effect of ECoT-supervised pre-training. A baseline model, "w/o ECoT PT," which skipped ECoT-supervised pre-training and directly fine-tuned on LIBERO, resulted in an average success rate of 95.7% compared to ZR-0's 97.8%. This 2.1-point drop confirms that ECoT-supervised pre-training is instrumental in learning transferable cross-embodiment representations, leading to more effective downstream adaptation. The ECoT framework provides structured supervision for scene understanding, task progress, planning, and object grounding, resulting in stronger initial representations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of ZR-0 and its evaluation demonstrate several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of embodied AI and robotics:

- **Validation of Embodied Chain-of-Thought (ECoT) Supervision:** The work provides evidence that dense ECoT supervision can effectively align cross-embodiment representations within VLA models. By guiding the model with structured, high-level cognitive reasoning, ECoT offers a scalable and embodiment-agnostic approach to learn transferable skills, moving beyond format-level solutions for cross-robot generalization.
- **Enhanced Cross-Embodiment Transfer:** ZR-0's consistent performance across diverse robotic platforms—single-arm (LIBERO, xArm), bimanual (RoboTwin 2.0), and humanoid (RoboCasa GR-1 Tabletop)—from a single pre-trained checkpoint indicates a step towards truly generalist robots. This capability can reduce the need for platform-specific training, potentially accelerating the deployment of robotic systems in varied environments.
- **Improved High-Level Reasoning and Task Understanding:** The detailed components of ECoT (Scene Description, Progress Assessment, Future Plan, To-Do Actions, Target Objects) explicitly train the VLM in critical aspects of scene understanding, long-horizon planning, sub-task decomposition, and visual grounding. This leads to more robust policy learning and better instruction following, as evidenced by real-world gains in tasks requiring OCR-based reasoning and complex sequential actions.
- **Practical Inference Efficiency:** The architectural design that allows for skipping ECoT generation at inference time is a notable practical advancement. It ensures that the representational benefits accrued during ECoT-supervised training can be utilized in real-time robotic control without incurring the typical computational overhead of explicit textual reasoning. This balance between training effectiveness and inference efficiency is crucial for real-world applications.
- **Robustness to Visual Variations:** The strong performance in domain-randomized environments (RoboTwin 2.0) suggests that ZR-0 exhibits improved robustness to visual changes, such as clutter, lighting, and backgrounds. This is likely a result of the diverse pre-training data and the co-training strategy with general vision-language datasets, contributing to the model's ability to generalize in varied real-world conditions.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper identifies several promising directions for future work, which could amplify the impact of this research:
 - **Scaling Data:** Acknowledging the current pre-training corpus size relative to other leading VLA models, scaling up the diversity and volume of robot data could further expand the range of manipulation primitives and enhance fine-grained motor precision.
 - **Leveraging Human Egocentric Video:** The embodiment-agnostic nature of ECoT reasoning opens an avenue for annotating vast human egocentric video datasets, potentially unlocking a new source of rich manipulation behavior data without requiring robot-specific action labels.
 - **Efficient Annotation Strategies:** Research into intelligent selection of informative frames for ECoT annotation could reduce the significant computational resources currently required for dense annotation, making this powerful supervision method more accessible and scalable.

In conclusion, ZR-0 demonstrates the potential of structured reasoning supervision as a method for cross-embodiment representation learning in VLA models, contributing to the development of more capable and adaptable generalist robots.
