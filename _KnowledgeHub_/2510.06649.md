# Local Reinforcement Learning with Action-Conditioned Root Mean Squared Q-Functions

> [!summary] Summary
> Action-conditioned Root mean squared Q-Functions (ARQ) introduces a backpropagation-free local reinforcement learning method leveraging a vector-based goodness function and explicit action conditioning for Q-value estimation. The method achieves superior performance over existing local RL methods and surpasses several backpropagation-based algorithms, including DQN, TD-MPC2, and SAC, across MinAtar and DeepMind Control Suite benchmarks.

## Problem

- Deep learning's reliance on backpropagation presents limitations regarding biological plausibility, such as the need for synchronous computations and weight symmetry.
- Most biologically plausible, backprop-free learning algorithms have primarily focused on supervised tasks, leaving a gap for their application in reward-centric Reinforcement Learning (RL) environments.
- Previous local RL approaches, like Artificial Dopamine (AD), predict Q-values using dot-products, constraining output dimensionality to the action space size and limiting the expressivity of local learning modules.

## Method

- **ARQ** extends the ==Forward-Forward== (FF) algorithm paradigm to Reinforcement Learning by adapting its goodness function for temporal difference learning-based Q-value estimation.
- It introduces a vector-based goodness function, specifically the Root Mean Squared (RMS) of a hidden vector after mean subtraction, allowing Q-values to be extracted from hidden vectors of arbitrary size.
- Actions are explicitly conditioned as inputs to the network, enabling the model to produce representations specifically tailored to each state-action pair, rather than implicitly handling actions through indexing or fixed outputs.

## Results

- ARQ consistently outperformed Artificial Dopamine (AD) and Deep Q-Network (DQN) on all MinAtar games, achieving, for example, 87.84 average returns on Breakout compared to AD's 63.76 and DQN's 27.09.
- On the DeepMind Control Suite, ARQ demonstrated superior returns to AD in all tasks and matched or exceeded the performance of backpropagation-based methods like TD-MPC2 and SAC in most games, scoring 516.23 on Hopper Hop, outperforming SAC's 319.46.
- Ablation studies confirmed that action conditioning at the input led to substantial performance improvements (e.g., ~**50%** increase in average returns on Breakout) and generated more Q-value correlated hidden representations, while the RMS goodness function also yielded superior performance over other tested non-linearities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Local, backpropagation-free learning mechanisms, when designed with vector-based value estimation and explicit action conditioning, can achieve competitive or superior performance in RL tasks compared to both other local methods and established backpropagation-based algorithms.
> - Explicitly conditioning actions at the input is crucial for local RL models, leading to more state-driven representations with stronger correlations to predicted Q-values, thereby improving representational efficiency.
> - The Root Mean Squared (RMS) goodness function maintains healthier and more expressive activation scales throughout training, contributing to more stable and performant learning compared to other goodness functions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of deep learning has largely advanced through the backpropagation algorithm, which, despite its success, faces limitations concerning biological plausibility, such as the requirement for synchronous computations and weight symmetry. This has motivated research into "backprop-free" alternatives for training deep neural networks. Recent contributions in this area include methods like Direct Feedback Alignment and notably, Hinton's Forward-Forward (FF) algorithm. The FF algorithm operates using two forward passes for layer-wise contrastive learning, eliminating the need for backpropagation and addressing some biological plausibility concerns.

However, the majority of research on these backprop-free methods has primarily focused on supervised learning tasks, such as image classification. A significant gap exists in applying these biologically plausible learning mechanisms to domains where learning signals are generated more naturally, such as Reinforcement Learning (RL). RL, particularly temporal-difference (TD) methods, is considered a natural candidate due to its alignment with biological learning processes, evidenced by reward-guided evolution in biological brains and the neural implementation of TD signals (e.g., dopamine).

Previous work has begun to bridge this gap. Guan et al. (2024) introduced Artificial Dopamine (AD), a backprop-free Q-learning framework that incorporates top-down and temporal connections. While AD represents a step towards local RL, its Q-function estimation relies on a dot-product that explicitly predicts action values, limiting its output dimensionality to the size of the action space. This constraint can restrict the flexibility and expressivity of the local learning modules.

This paper positions itself within this evolving landscape by proposing a new method, Action-conditioned Root mean squared Q-Functions (ARQ), which extends the FF paradigm to RL. ARQ aims to enhance the capabilities of local, backprop-free RL by introducing a vector-based value estimation method inspired by FF's goodness function, coupled with explicit action conditioning. The work seeks to demonstrate that such biologically grounded, local learning mechanisms can not only be viable but also competitive or superior to established backpropagation-based and other local RL algorithms. By doing so, it contributes to the broader effort of exploring biologically plausible alternatives to backpropagation, particularly in the context of reward-driven learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce and evaluate Action-conditioned Root mean squared Q-Functions (ARQ), a novel value estimation method designed for local, backprop-free Reinforcement Learning (RL) using temporal difference learning.

The motivation for developing ARQ stems from several key considerations:

1. **Bridging the Gap in Biologically Plausible RL:** Most existing backprop-free learning algorithms, including the Forward-Forward (FF) algorithm, have predominantly been applied to supervised learning settings. There is a recognized need to extend these biologically plausible mechanisms to RL, where learning signals (rewards) are inherently and naturally generated through interaction with an environment. The authors argue that reward-centric environments and temporal difference (TD) methods are biologically meaningful, aligning with how biological brains have evolved and learn.

2. **Enhancing Expressivity and Flexibility in Local RL:** Prior efforts in local RL, such as Artificial Dopamine (AD) (Guan et al., 2024), predict Q-values using a dot-product mechanism that constrains the output dimension of each local cell to the size of the action space. This design limits the capacity and flexibility of the local learning modules. ARQ aims to overcome this limitation by:
 - **Introducing a vector-based goodness function:** Inspired by FF's local goodness function, ARQ proposes to extract value predictions from a hidden vector of arbitrary size, rather than being restricted to the action space dimension. This allows for more neurons at the output layer of a local cell without sacrificing the backprop-free property.
 - **Implementing action conditioning at the model input:** Unlike methods that output action-specific predictions by indexing over a fixed output layer (e.g., DQN) or implicitly handling actions within an attention mechanism (e.g., AD), ARQ explicitly incorporates action candidates as input to the model. This design is hypothesized to enable the network to produce representations that are specifically tailored to each state-action pair, thereby increasing the network's capacity to learn complex state-action dynamics.

3. **Investigating the Potential of Decentralized Value Estimation:** The authors draw inspiration from neuroscientific evidence suggesting that neurons might perform decentralized, independent value estimations. ARQ's design, where each "cell" or module makes an independent Q-value estimation, serves as an exploration of whether this biological phenomenon has practical implications for artificial intelligence.

4. **Demonstrating Competitive Performance with Biologically Inspired Methods:** A key motivation is to demonstrate that simple, biologically grounded local learning mechanisms, when appropriately designed, can achieve competitive or even superior performance compared to both existing backprop-free local RL methods and conventional backpropagation-based algorithms. This empirical validation seeks to encourage further research into the intersection of RL and biologically plausible learning.

In summary, the core motivation is to develop a more expressive and flexible backprop-free local RL algorithm, grounded in biological principles, that can achieve high performance in challenging RL tasks, thereby contributing to both the advancement of RL and the understanding of biologically plausible learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this work centers on the development of Action-conditioned Root mean squared Q-Function (ARQ), a novel local value estimation method for reinforcement learning that operates without backpropagation.

**Core Principles:**
ARQ integrates two main components:
1. **A goodness function for value prediction:** Inspired by the Forward-Forward (FF) algorithm's concept of "goodness" (a measure of compatibility between input and label), ARQ adapts this idea to estimate Q-values.
2. **Action conditioning at the model input:** Actions are explicitly provided as inputs to the network, allowing for state-action specific representations.

**ARQ Formulation:**
Instead of using the sum of squares of activations as in the original FF goodness function, ARQ employs a modification. Given a hidden vector `y` produced by a neural network, the estimated Q-value, Q_θ(s, a), is computed using the Root Mean Squared (RMS) function of the vector `y` after mean subtraction. This is mathematically expressed as:
μ_y = E_{y_i ∈ y} y_i
Q_θ(s, a) = √(E_{y_i ∈ y} (y_i - μ_y)²)
The authors note that this is equivalent to the standard deviation of the hidden vector, and its purpose is to prevent goodness values from "exploding" as the number of units scales, thereby stabilizing the learning process.

**Learning Objective:**
To train the network, ARQ utilizes the standard Q-learning mean squared error (MSE) objective function, analogous to Deep Q-Networks (DQN). The loss function L_θ is defined as:
L_θ = [R_t + γ max_{a'} Q_θ(S_{t+1}, a') - Q_θ(S_t, A_t)]²
This objective drives the network to reduce the temporal difference error, updating weights based on immediate rewards and bootstrapped future value estimates.

**Action Conditioning:**
A key design choice in ARQ is how action information is incorporated. Instead of a network taking only the state `s` and producing an output vector where each entry corresponds to the value of a discrete action (as in DQN or the original AD), ARQ explicitly conditions the network on both the state `s` and the action `a` as inputs. The network then outputs a single scalar prediction for Q(s, a).
- For discrete action spaces, actions are represented as one-hot vectors.
- For continuous action spaces, "bang-bang" discretization is applied to convert continuous actions into binary vectors, which are then passed as input.

**Implementation Architecture (Building on Artificial Dopamine - AD):**
ARQ is implemented on top of the Artificial Dopamine (AD) architecture (Guan et al., 2024), which is itself a backprop-free local RL network. An ARQ network consists of multiple stacked cells. Each cell at depth `l` performs the following operations:
1. **Input Concatenation:** It takes inputs from the raw state observation `s_t`, the activation of the layer below `h_{l-1,t}`, the activation of the layer above from the previous timestep `h_{l+1,t-1}`, and critically, the **action candidate `a_t`**. This forms the concatenated input vector `X`.
 X = concat(s_t, h_{l-1,t}, h_{l+1,t-1}, a_t)
2. **Hidden Activation:** A ReLU activation is applied to a linear transformation of `X` to produce the hidden activation `h_{l,t}`.
 h_{l,t} = ReLU(W_h X)
3. **Attention-like Mechanism:** An intermediate vector `y_{l,t}` is computed using an attention-like mechanism involving two linear projections of `X` (W_att1, W_att2) and `h_{l,t}`. This allows for non-linearity and flexible information integration without backpropagation across cells.
 y_{l,t} = tanh(Xᵀ Wᵀ_att2 W_att1 X) h_{l,t}
 A crucial distinction from AD is that ARQ allows `y_{l,t}` to have an arbitrary dimension `d`, unlike AD which is constrained by the number of actions `n_a`.
4. **Q-value Estimation:** The final Q-value for the state-action pair is derived by applying the RMS function to `y_{l,t}`.
 μ_y = E_{y_i ∈ y_{l,t}} y_i
 Q(s_t, a_t) = √(E_{y_i ∈ y_{l,t}} (y_i - μ_y)²)
Gradients are confined within each cell, ensuring the backprop-free nature of the architecture.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Benchmarks:** MinAtar (miniaturized Atari games) and DeepMind Control (DMC) Suite (physics-based continuous control tasks). Both are chosen for their low-dimensional inputs and suitability for evaluating local methods.
- **Baselines:**
 - **Local RL:** Artificial Dopamine (AD).
 - **Backprop-based RL:** Deep Q-Network (DQN) for MinAtar; TD-MPC2 and Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) for DMC Suite.
- **Network Architectures:** 3-layer fully-connected networks (MLPs) with specific hidden dimensions (e.g., 400-200-200 for MinAtar, 128-96-96 for DMC).
- **Training Details:** Standard RL practices including a replay buffer, target network, epsilon-greedy policy with linear decay, Adam optimizer, fixed learning rate (1e-4), and a batch size of 512. Training ran for 4 million steps across 5 random seeds.

**Ablation Studies:**
The paper includes several ablation studies to investigate the contributions of ARQ's design choices:
- **Effect of Action Conditioning:** Compared ARQ and AD performance with and without action conditioning at the input. Visualized hidden activations using PCA.
- **Effect of Goodness Nonlinearities:** Compared the RMS function with mean, mean squared (MS), and variance (Var) goodness functions. Analyzed distributions of L2 norms of hidden activations and scalar goodness values.
- **Scaling Effects:** Compared AD and ARQ performance at various parameter scales to ascertain if improvements were merely due to increased model capacity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the performance of ARQ across various benchmark environments and its underlying mechanisms through ablation studies.

**Overall Performance:**
- **MinAtar Benchmark:** ARQ consistently outperformed Artificial Dopamine (AD) across all MinAtar games (Freeway, Breakout, SpaceInvaders, Seaquest, Asterix). Notably, ARQ also surpassed the conventional backpropagation-based Deep Q-Network (DQN) in all these games. For instance, on Breakout, ARQ achieved 87.84 ± 11.10 average returns compared to AD's 63.76 ± 17.70 and DQN's 27.09 ± 11.48. On SpaceInvaders, ARQ scored 544.99 ± 88.10, significantly higher than AD's 363.49 ± 36.92 and DQN's 188.03 ± 31.62.
- **DeepMind Control (DMC) Suite:** ARQ achieved superior returns compared to AD in all tested DMC tasks (Walker Walk, Walker Run, Hopper Hop, Cheetah Run, Reacher Hard). Furthermore, ARQ demonstrated competitive performance against and, in most games, exceeded the performance of strong backpropagation-based methods such as TD-MPC2 and SAC. For example, in Hopper Hop, ARQ scored 516.23 ± 34.72, outperforming AD's 470.95 ± 78.14 and SAC's 319.46 ± 62.42, while remaining competitive with TD-MPC2's 348.55 ± 53.30.

**Game-Specific Analysis:**
- On MinAtar games like Breakout and SpaceInvaders, which involve sequential actions or "combos," ARQ's performance advantage over DQN and AD is attributed to the temporal coherence provided by top-down connections (inherited from AD) and enhanced action conditioning.
- In Seaquest, where policies can be multi-modal (e.g., attacking enemies vs. refilling oxygen), ARQ's ability to capture these distinct policy structures more effectively than state-conditioned AD is hypothesized to explain its superior performance.

**Ablation Study: Effect of Action Conditioning at Input:**
- Action conditioning was found to be a critical component for both AD and ARQ. Substantial performance improvements were observed when actions were conditioned at the input rather than implicitly handled or indexed at the output.
- For ARQ, this improvement was particularly significant, leading to an approximate 50% increase in average returns on Breakout (e.g., ~85 with AC vs. ~55 without AC).
- Representation analysis using PCA on hidden activations revealed that without action conditioning, activations clustered primarily by action identity, showing little correlation with predicted Q-values. With action conditioning, representations became more state-driven and exhibited a positive relationship with Q-values, suggesting the model could allocate its capacity more effectively towards value-relevant information.

**Ablation Study: Effect of Goodness Nonlinearities:**
- Comparing different goodness functions (Root Mean Squared, Mean, Mean Squared, Variance), the RMS function used by default in ARQ yielded superior performance on MinAtar Breakout and SpaceInvaders.
- Analysis of activation magnitudes showed that RMS maintained healthier and more expressive activation scales throughout training. In contrast, the mean squared function produced extremely large initial goodness values, which subsequently suppressed activation norms, potentially leading to a compressed representation space.
- Despite these differences, all tested goodness functions resulted in superior performance compared to AD, indicating the versatility of the vector-based value estimation approach.

**Ablation Study: Scaling and Parameter Count:**
- Experiments comparing AD and ARQ with varying total parameter counts demonstrated that ARQ consistently outperformed AD across all scales (0.5x, 1x, 1.5x, 2x relative to original AD parameter count). This suggests that ARQ's effectiveness is due to its design principles (vector-based goodness, action conditioning) rather than merely an increase in model capacity.

**Neuron Sensitivity and Specialization:**
- Visualizations of hidden layer activations in Breakout revealed that neurons exhibited larger magnitudes for "correct" state-action pairs. For instance, when the ball approached the left side of the brick, neurons showed the largest magnitude when the action candidate was "move left."
- Observations indicated that different neurons showed varying degrees of activation for different action candidates, implying a form of specialization where individual neurons might be responsible for recognizing specific categories of positive signals or optimal action choices.

In summary, ARQ demonstrates robust and competitive performance across a range of RL tasks, often surpassing both other local methods and backpropagation-based baselines. Its effectiveness is attributed to the combination of a vector-based RMS goodness function, explicit action conditioning at the input, and the architectural benefits derived from local, backprop-free learning with temporal and skip connections.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents Action-conditioned Root mean squared Q-Function (ARQ) as a contribution to the field of reinforcement learning and biologically plausible machine learning. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Advancement in Biologically Plausible Reinforcement Learning:** The primary significance of ARQ lies in its successful application of a backpropagation-free, local learning mechanism to reinforcement learning. While many backprop-free methods have been confined to supervised learning, ARQ demonstrates the viability and competitive performance of such approaches in reward-centric environments. This is important for advancing the understanding of how learning could occur in biological systems, which do not rely on global error signals like backpropagation.

2. **Challenging the Dominance of Backpropagation:** ARQ's performance, which consistently surpasses state-of-the-art local RL methods and, in many cases, exceeds conventional backpropagation-based algorithms like DQN, TD-MPC2, and SAC on the tested benchmarks, suggests that backpropagation may not be an exclusive prerequisite for achieving high performance in certain RL tasks. This provides empirical evidence that alternative, biologically inspired learning rules can be effective, potentially opening new research avenues for designing more efficient or robust learning systems.

3. **Enhanced Expressivity and Flexibility in Local Learning Architectures:** By introducing a vector-based goodness function (RMS) that extracts value predictions from hidden vectors of arbitrary size, ARQ removes the constraint on output dimensionality imposed by previous local RL methods like AD. This allows local cells to maintain richer representations. Coupled with explicit action conditioning at the model input, ARQ enables the network to generate representations specific to each state-action pair, thereby increasing the expressive capacity of individual local modules. This methodological innovation could influence future designs of decentralized and local learning systems.

4. **Insights into Neural Learning Mechanisms:** The findings, particularly the ablation studies on action conditioning and goodness functions, offer insights into how local networks process information. The observation that action conditioning leads to more "state-driven" and "Q-value correlated" representations suggests a more efficient allocation of representational capacity. The analysis of neuron activations showing specialization for "correct" state-action pairs hints at mechanisms of decentralized value estimation and feature specialization, aligning with some neuroscientific hypotheses about the "hedonistic neuron" and distributed neural representations of value.

5. **Catalyst for Future Research:** This work encourages further exploration at the intersection of RL and biologically plausible learning. By demonstrating the practical implications of decentralized value estimation and the effectiveness of local, TD-based updates, ARQ could inspire researchers to investigate novel architectures and learning rules that are both biologically plausible and performant. This could lead to new paradigms for designing artificial intelligence that are more aligned with biological intelligence, potentially offering advantages in areas like sample efficiency, robustness, or continual learning.

In summary, ARQ provides a robust and empirically validated approach to backprop-free RL, contributing to the theoretical understanding of local learning and offering a practical, high-performing alternative to existing methods. Its impact lies in pushing the boundaries of biologically plausible AI and potentially guiding the development of more sophisticated, decentralized learning systems.
