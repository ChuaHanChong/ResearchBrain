# Fine-Grained Preference Optimization Improves Spatial Reasoning in VLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> SpatialReasoner-R1, a Vision-Language Model, introduces enhanced capabilities for fine-grained, multi-step spatial reasoning from 2D images. The model achieved new state-of-the-art accuracy on the SPATIALRGPT-BENCH dataset, demonstrating a 15.8% improvement in quantitative spatial tasks through novel fine-grained Direct Preference Optimization (fDPO) and Multi-Model Monte Carlo Tree Search (M3CTS) for data generation.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) demonstrate limitations in performing fine-grained, multi-step spatial reasoning from 2D images, particularly for precise object relationships and quantitative estimations.
- The scarcity of high-quality, structured Long Chain-of-Thought (LongCoT) supervision data specifically designed for complex spatial reasoning hinders the development of robust VLMs in this domain.
- Traditional preference optimization methods (e.g., DPO) apply uniform learning signals across entire responses, which is suboptimal for multi-component reasoning where different segments (e.g., visual descriptions vs. logical inferences) require distinct optimization strategies.

## Method

- Introduced ==SpatialReasoner-R1==, a VLM based on the Sa2VA architecture, designed to generate structured ==LongCoT responses== that decompose complex spatial queries into verifiable steps.
- Developed ==Multi-Model Monte Carlo Tree Search (M3CTS)== to generate a diverse and high-quality OPEN SPATIAL REASONING dataset, leveraging multiple VLMs and fine-grained spatial rewards to create LongCoT preference pairs.
- Proposed ==fine-grained Direct Preference Optimization (fDPO)==, an off-policy preference learning algorithm that applies ==segment-specific preference granularity== to optimize distinct components of spatial reasoning (description and inference) within LongCoT.

## Results

- SpatialReasoner-R1 fDPO 8B achieved state-of-the-art performance on the SPATIALRGPT-BENCH dataset, with a **9.4%** improvement in average accuracy over the strongest baseline, SpatialRGPT-8B.
- The model demonstrated a **15.8%** improvement in accuracy on spatial quantitative tasks and a **2.9%** improvement on spatial qualitative tasks compared to SpatialRGPT-8B.
- fDPO significantly outperformed standard DPO, showing a relative gain of **9.0%** on quantitative tasks and **4.1%** on qualitative tasks, validating the effectiveness of segment-specific optimization for complex reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing complex spatial reasoning into explicit, verifiable LongCoT steps, guided by a robust, fine-grained spatial reward mechanism, substantially improves VLM capabilities beyond superficial visual understanding.
> - A multi-model Monte Carlo Tree Search (M3CTS) approach effectively addresses data scarcity by generating high-quality, diverse, and logically consistent LongCoT data for specialized domains like spatial reasoning.
> - Applying adaptive, segment-specific preference optimization (fDPO) significantly enhances model alignment and performance for multi-step reasoning tasks by differentiating the learning signals for distinct semantic components of a generated response.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper positions itself at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields within artificial intelligence: Vision-Language Models (VLMs), Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, spatial understanding, and preference-based alignment.

**VLMs and Spatial Reasoning:** Current VLMs have made significant strides in multimodal understanding, excelling in tasks like image captioning and visual question answering. However, as the paper notes, their capabilities in *fine-grained spatial reasoning* remain limited. This limitation is particularly evident in scenarios requiring multi-step logic, precise spatial alignment, understanding complex object arrangements, and handling occlusions. This gap is a critical bottleneck for real-world applications such as robotics, autonomous driving, and augmented reality, where robust spatial understanding is paramount for decision-making. Existing efforts to enhance spatial understanding include fine-tuning VLMs on spatial VQA datasets and leveraging external 3D foundation models, but these often fall short when dealing with complex, multi-step inference. This work directly targets this recognized deficiency.

**Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and Long Chain-of-Thought (LongCoT):** Historically, early VLMs often used direct-response paradigms, leading to superficial understanding. The advent of CoT prompting introduced step-by-step reasoning, improving transparency and performance. However, standard CoT traces are often too concise for intricate spatial logic. The paper highlights LongCoT as a more promising avenue for richer, more interpretable reasoning paths. This work builds upon the LongCoT paradigm, emphasizing that merely generating longer chains isn't enough; the reasoning needs to be *structured, fine-grained, and precisely supervised* for spatial tasks.

**Preference-Based Learning and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO):** Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and its more efficient variant, Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), have become standard for aligning large language models (LLMs) and VLMs with human intentions and desired behaviors. DPO, in particular, offers a robust method to learn from preference pairs without explicitly training a separate reward model. However, existing DPO methods typically treat the entire response or reasoning process as a single unit, applying uniform optimization parameters. The authors argue that this overlooks the *semantic roles* of different segments within a complex reasoning chain. This paper introduces a novel *fine-grained* DPO (fDPO) to address this limitation, tailoring optimization to specific components like descriptive grounding and logical inference, which represents a significant advancement in preference-based alignment techniques.

**Multi-LLM Guided Reasoning:** Leveraging multiple LLMs in conjunction with search algorithms like Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) has been explored for complex text-based reasoning and, more recently, for multimodal tasks (e.g., CoMCTS). This work adapts MCTS (dubbed M3CTS) specifically for generating high-quality *spatial reasoning* data. By combining multi-model collaborative exploration with a novel fine-grained spatial reward mechanism, it addresses the scarcity of high-quality, structured LongCoT supervision data for spatial reasoning, a common challenge in specialized domains.

In essence, this research bridges critical gaps by offering a holistic approach to complex spatial reasoning in VLMs, integrating structured LongCoT generation, fine-grained reward design, and a novel preference optimization strategy, thereby pushing the boundaries of what VLMs can achieve in demanding spatial understanding tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations driving this research stem from the acknowledged limitations of current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in handling complex spatial reasoning tasks.

1. **Develop a VLM capable of fine-grained, multi-step spatial reasoning:** The primary goal is to introduce `SpatialReasoner-R1`, a novel VLM explicitly designed to overcome the struggle of existing models with intricate spatial queries that demand multi-step logic and precise spatial alignment directly from 2D images.
2. **Generate high-quality, structured supervision for spatial reasoning:** To train such a model, there is a crucial need for rich, logically consistent Long Chain-of-Thought (LongCoT) reasoning trajectories tailored to spatial understanding. The paper aims to develop a systematic method, `Multi-Model Monte Carlo Tree Search (M3CTS)`, for curating this diverse and high-quality data.
3. **Propose an optimized training method for multi-step reasoning components:** Recognizing that traditional preference optimization methods may not adequately handle the distinct challenges of descriptive grounding versus logical inference within a LongCoT, the authors aim to introduce `fine-grained Direct Preference Optimization (fDPO)`. This method is designed to apply segment-specific preference granularity, allowing for differentiated learning updates for distinct semantic components of reasoning.
4. **Establish a comprehensive spatial reward mechanism:** To guide both data generation (M3CTS) and model optimization (fDPO), a robust system for evaluating candidate reasoning paths is necessary. The objective is to design a `fine-grained spatial reward` mechanism that assesses responses based on visual consistency, spatial grounding precision (leveraging depth information), and logical coherence.

**Motivation:**

The motivation for this work is multifaceted:

- **Addressing Critical Real-World Challenges:** The paper highlights the direct implications of poor spatial understanding in VLMs for high-stakes applications such as robotics, autonomous driving, and augmented reality. These domains critically depend on robust spatial understanding for effective decision-making, where current VLM limitations pose significant operational barriers.
- **Overcoming Superficial VLM Understanding:** Early VLMs, and even many current ones, operate on a direct-response paradigm, often leading to shallow understanding without explicit reasoning. While Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting improved this, standard CoT is often too brief or abstract to capture the nuances of fine-grained spatial logic, necessitating more detailed LongCoT.
- **Enhancing Interpretability and Trustworthiness:** Generating structured, step-by-step reasoning paths (LongCoT) directly from 2D images not only improves accuracy but also enhances the interpretability of VLM decisions. This transparency is crucial for debugging, understanding model failures, and building trust in AI systems.
- **Improving Alignment for Complex Tasks:** Traditional Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) applies a uniform learning signal, which can be inefficient or even detrimental when different parts of a response (e.g., visual description vs. logical inference) have varying complexity and require distinct optimization priorities. The motivation for fDPO is to create a more effective alignment strategy for multi-step, semantically segmented reasoning.
- **Tackling Data Scarcity in Specialized Domains:** High-quality, human-annotated LongCoT data for complex spatial reasoning is scarce. M3CTS is motivated by the need to efficiently generate diverse and logically consistent reasoning trajectories at scale, thereby providing the necessary supervision for preference-based training without extensive manual effort.

In summary, the research is motivated by a critical need to endow VLMs with more sophisticated, interpretable, and precise spatial reasoning abilities to unlock their potential in complex, real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces `SpatialReasoner-R1`, a novel VLM, and a comprehensive training framework comprising `Multi-Model Monte Carlo Tree Search (M3CTS)` for data generation, `fine-grained spatial rewards` for evaluation, and `fine-grained Direct Preference Optimization (fDPO)` for model alignment.

#### 3.1. SpatialReasoner-R1 Architecture

SpatialReasoner-R1 is built upon the `Sa2VA architecture` (which is based on `InternVL2.5`), a VLM designed to take a text instruction, visual prompts (masks), and an image as input. It generates `LongCoT` reasoning responses, which decompose spatial reasoning into clear, verifiable steps, unlike typical direct-response VQA models. The architecture integrates an Image Encoder, Text Encoder, Prompt Encoder, and a Text Decoder to produce the desired structured output.

#### 3.2. Multi-Model Monte Carlo Tree Search (M3CTS) for LongCoT Data Generation

To address the scarcity of high-quality spatial reasoning data, M3CTS is introduced to generate diverse and logically consistent LongCoT trajectories. This framework is inspired by existing MCTS applications for LLMs and operates in four key stages:

1. **Expand:** At each reasoning step, M3CTS expands the current state by generating multiple candidate reasoning states. This is done concurrently using `multiple VLMs` (e.g., Gemini 1.5 Pro, GPT-4o, Qwen2.5VL-72B, and an initial SFT-trained SpatialReasoner-R1) to ensure diversity. A structured output format is enforced to enable consistent parsing.
2. **Simulate:** Each candidate reasoning step `s_k,t` is evaluated using fine-grained spatial rewards across three criteria:
 - **Visual Description Accuracy:** How accurately the description in `s_k,t` reflects the visual content.
 - **Spatial Consistency:** Whether spatial relations are consistent with both the RGB image and a generated depth map (using Depth Anything).
 - **Logical Reasoning Coherence:** The logical flow and justification of inferences.
 Each criterion is scored (+1 for accurate, 0 for ambiguous/partially accurate, -1 for inaccurate) by two multimodal models (Gemini 1.5 Pro and Qwen2.5VL-72B). Only high-quality paths with an aggregated non-negative score are preserved.
3. **Backpropagate:** Scores from the simulation phase are recursively propagated upwards through the search tree. This updates value estimates `V(s_k,t)` and visit counts `N(s_k,t)` for parent nodes, performing credit assignment.
4. **Select:** The Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) strategy is used to select the most promising candidate state for further exploration, balancing exploitation of high-value paths and exploration of less-visited nodes.

This process generates the `OPEN SPATIAL REASONING dataset`, consisting of 400K VQA preference pairs (`y_p`, `y_l`). For each pair, a pool of eight candidate answers is generated from multiple VLMs and evaluated by the fine-grained spatial reward mechanism. The highest-scoring response becomes `y_p` (preferred), and the lowest becomes `y_l` (less-preferred). An additional 100K negative pairs are created by perturbing only the conclusion keywords of high-quality positive samples, creating tightly controlled negative examples.

#### 3.3. Fine-Grained Spatial Rewards

This mechanism is central to both M3CTS and fDPO, providing comprehensive evaluation of reasoning paths:

- **Visual Consistency Reward (R_vc):** Evaluates the `description (R_desc)` segment based on existence of objects, attribute accuracy, completeness of details, and appropriateness to the query. Scores range from 0 to 4.0.
- **Depth-Guided Spatial Reward (R_sp):** Measures fine-grained spatial understanding for both `R_desc` and `R_reason` components. It leverages a ground-truth depth map and includes:
 - **Uncertainty Weight (W_u):** Adjusts scores for spatial expressions with qualifiers (e.g., "approximately"), ranging from 0.8 to 1.0.
 - **Context-Aware Weight (W_c):** Emphasizes spatial relations directly relevant to the query, ranging from 0.8 to 1.0.
 The final `R_sp` scores are weighted correctness scores of all spatial assertions, validated against RGB and depth images.
- **Logical Coherence Reward (R_lc):** Evaluates the `reasoning (R_reason)` segment for structural integrity and logical correctness. It assesses factual consistency with image/depth, logical flow of steps, correct application of rules (physical, spatial, logical), and conclusion validity. Scores range from 0 to 4.0.

The preference differentials for fDPO are computed from composite rewards: `score(R_desc) = R_vc + R_sp,desc` and `score(R_reason) = R_lc + R_sp,reason`.

#### 3.4. Fine-Grained Direct Preference Optimization (fDPO)

fDPO is a novel off-policy preference learning algorithm that addresses the limitations of traditional DPO by introducing segment-level preference granularity:

1. **Response Segmentation:** Each `LongCoT` response `R` is segmented into a `description (R_desc)` and a `reasoning (R_reason)` component.
2. **Segment-Wise Preference Differentials:** The preference signal for each segment is quantified by calculating the score difference between the preferred (`R_p`) and less-preferred (`R_l`) responses for their respective `R_desc` and `R_reason` parts.
3. **Adaptive Trade-off Parameters (β_desc, β_reason):** Unlike standard DPO's uniform `β`, fDPO introduces separate, adaptively tuned `β` parameters for `R_desc` and `R_reason`. This allows the model to prioritize deeper logical inference while maintaining visual and attribute accuracy, acknowledging that reasoning is often harder to optimize than description.
4. **Dynamic Segment Weights (w_s):** fDPO computes dynamic weights `w_desc` and `w_reason` based on the preference score differential (`ΔR_s`) of each segment. This ensures that learning focuses on components exhibiting a larger preference differential, i.e., harder-to-learn segments. A hyperparameter `λ` controls the sensitivity of these weights.
5. **Segment-Specific β:** These weights are mapped to adjustment factors applied to a base `β` parameter, yielding `β_s = β * (1 + α(2w_s - 1))`, where `α` controls the scaling amplitude. This dynamic mechanism balances optimization based on segment-specific learning difficulty.
6. **Overall Optimization Objective:** The final fDPO objective (`L_fDPO(θ)`) combines the segment-specific preference margins, weighted by their respective `β_s` parameters, to update the model's policy.

This comprehensive approach allows `SpatialReasoner-R1` to be trained effectively on high-quality, fine-grained spatial reasoning data, optimizing distinct components of the reasoning process for superior performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate the significant advancements achieved by SpatialReasoner-R1, particularly when trained with the proposed fine-grained Direct Preference Optimization (fDPO).

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Spatial Reasoning Performance

- **SPATIALRGPT-BENCH:** SpatialReasoner-R1, specifically the 8B model trained with fDPO (SpatialReasoner-R1 fDPO 8B), established a new state-of-the-art (SoTA) on the challenging SPATIALRGPT-BENCH dataset.
 - It achieved a remarkable 9.4% improvement in average accuracy over the strongest baseline, SpatialRGPT-8B.
 - For spatial qualitative tasks (e.g., Below/Above, Left/Right), fDPO 8B achieved 95.59% accuracy, surpassing SpatialRGPT-8B (92.69%) by 2.9%.
 - For spatial quantitative tasks (e.g., Direct Distance, Vertical Distance), fDPO 8B achieved 77.30% accuracy, significantly outperforming SpatialRGPT-8B (61.42%) by 15.8%.
- **Efficiency:** The parameter-efficient SpatialReasoner-R1 fDPO 4B variant also showcased strong performance, outperforming larger general-purpose VLMs like InternVL2.5-78B, highlighting the effectiveness of the fine-tuning strategy independent of massive scale.

#### 4.2. Impact of Fine-Grained Direct Preference Optimization (fDPO)

- **Superiority over Standard DPO:** fDPO demonstrated clear advantages over standard DPO.
 - On spatial qualitative tasks, fDPO 8B achieved a relative performance gain of 4.1% compared to SpatialReasoner-R1 DPO 8B (95.59% vs. 91.48%).
 - On spatial quantitative tasks, fDPO 8B achieved an even more substantial relative gain of 9.0% compared to SpatialReasoner-R1 DPO 8B (77.30% vs. 68.22%).
 This empirically validates the core hypothesis that segment-specific optimization is crucial for improving complex, multi-step reasoning.
- **Comparison to SFT:** Both DPO and fDPO trained models significantly outperformed their Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) counterparts, indicating the value of preference-based learning in aligning models for spatial reasoning.

#### 4.3. General Vision-Language Understanding

- SpatialReasoner-R1 fDPO 8B not only excelled in specialized spatial reasoning tasks but also maintained strong and often superior performance on a suite of general vision-language benchmarks (MME, POPE, SEED-BENCH, AI2D, SQA-TEST, MMMUV, MMSTAR, HALLUSIONBENCH). For instance, it showed improved results on MME (1667/503 vs 1667/348 for SpatialRGPT-8B) and POPE (89.71% vs 85.50%), suggesting that its specialized training for spatial reasoning generalizes well to broader multimodal understanding without significant trade-offs.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Insights

- Qualitative examples (Figure 4) vividly illustrate SpatialReasoner-R1's advanced capability. It generates coherent, multi-step logical chains that closely match ground truth, breaking down complex estimations (e.g., horizontal distance between objects) into transparent, verifiable steps.
- In contrast, baseline models often exhibit less precise or less interpretable reasoning paths, making inconsistent assumptions or failing to provide step-by-step deductions, thereby underscoring the value of the LongCoT and fDPO approach.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- Ablation studies on fDPO's hyperparameters, Alpha (`α`) and Lambda (`λ`), provided insights into optimal settings.
 - `α` (controls maximum scaling amplitude of segment-specific `β`) showed that a moderate value (e.g., 30%) effectively amplifies learning signals for fine-grained spatial distinctions, while too high or too low values degrade performance.
 - `λ` (controls sensitivity of segment weights to preference differentials) also found an optimal moderate value (e.g., 0.6), demonstrating that balanced sensitivity is key to responsive learning without aggressive re-weighting.

In conclusion, the findings robustly establish SpatialReasoner-R1 with fDPO as a new leader in spatial reasoning, demonstrating significant gains over prior methods and highlighting the efficacy of fine-grained preference optimization for complex, multi-step VLM tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Fine-Grained Preference Optimization Improves Spatial Reasoning in VLMs" carries substantial significance and potential impact across several dimensions of AI research and application.

#### 5.1. Advancement in VLM Capabilities

The most immediate significance is the substantial improvement in Vision-Language Models' ability to perform *fine-grained, multi-step spatial reasoning*. This addresses a critical weakness in current VLMs, which often struggle with complex spatial relationships, occlusions, and precise quantitative estimations. By introducing SpatialReasoner-R1, the authors demonstrate that VLMs can move beyond superficial visual understanding to engage in sophisticated geometric and relational inference.

#### 5.2. Novel Contributions to Preference Learning

The proposed `fine-grained Direct Preference Optimization (fDPO)` is a significant methodological advancement in the field of preference-based alignment. By segmenting reasoning paths into semantically distinct components (description and reasoning) and applying adaptive, segment-specific optimization parameters, fDPO offers a more nuanced and effective way to guide model learning. This principle of "functional-level preference granularity" could be generalized to other complex generative tasks where different parts of a generated response require varied optimization strategies, leading to more stable and precise model alignment.

#### 5.3. Enabling Structured and Interpretable Reasoning

The emphasis on `Long Chain-of-Thought (LongCoT)` reasoning, coupled with structured output formats, enhances the interpretability and transparency of VLM decisions. Instead of just providing an answer, SpatialReasoner-R1 offers a step-by-step explanation, making it easier for humans to understand how the model arrived at its conclusion. This is crucial for building trust in AI systems and for debugging errors, particularly in safety-critical applications.

#### 5.4. Addressing Data Scarcity through Multi-Model MCTS

The `Multi-Model Monte Carlo Tree Search (M3CTS)` framework offers an innovative solution to the challenge of acquiring high-quality, annotated data for specialized, complex tasks like spatial reasoning. By leveraging multiple VLMs and fine-grained spatial rewards, M3CTS can efficiently generate diverse and logically consistent reasoning trajectories at scale. This data generation pipeline is a valuable contribution that could be adapted for other domains requiring structured, multi-step reasoning, reducing reliance on costly manual annotation.

#### 5.5. Potential for Real-World Applications

The enhanced spatial reasoning capabilities have direct and profound implications for various real-world applications:
- **Robotics:** Robots require precise spatial understanding for navigation, object manipulation, and interaction with their environment.
- **Autonomous Driving:** Accurate perception of distances, relative positions of vehicles and pedestrians, and understanding of road layouts are fundamental for safe and effective autonomous navigation.
- **Augmented Reality (AR):** AR applications rely on robust spatial anchoring and understanding of the physical environment to seamlessly integrate virtual objects.
- **Visual Analytics and Healthcare:** Improved spatial reasoning can aid in interpreting complex visual data, such as medical images, by identifying subtle spatial patterns or anomalies.

#### 5.6. Broader Research Directions

The success of SpatialReasoner-R1 and its underlying methodologies opens several avenues for future research. The authors themselves suggest extending fDPO to tasks like GUI navigation and reasoning segmentation, and exploring implicit grounding (without explicit region representations) or extending the framework to 3D or embodied contexts. This work thus serves as a strong foundation for future advancements in multimodal AI.

#### 5.7. Consideration of Broader Impacts and Limitations

The paper thoughtfully includes a "Broader Impacts" section, acknowledging both the positive potential (safer, more transparent AI) and potential risks, such as incorrect inferences in safety-critical domains and the propagation of biases from foundation models. This responsible AI perspective is important. Furthermore, the explicit discussion of limitations, such as the reliance on explicit region representations and the current focus on 2D reasoning, provides a clear roadmap for subsequent research.

In conclusion, this paper provides a significant leap forward in equipping VLMs with advanced spatial reasoning abilities through a well-conceived and empirically validated framework. Its methodological innovations and demonstrated performance establish a new benchmark and pave the way for more capable, interpretable, and practical multimodal AI systems.
