# CoTracker: It is Better to Track Together

> [!summary] Summary
> CoTracker is a transformer-based point tracker that jointly tracks many 2D points across a video, using attention between tracks to exploit their statistical dependencies, proxy tokens for memory efficiency, and unrolled sliding-window training. It tracks up to 70k points on one GPU and beats prior trackers by large margins on standard benchmarks.

## Problem

- Most point trackers (TAPIR, PIPs++) treat tracked points as statistically independent, ignoring dependencies from shared object membership that could improve accuracy.
- Existing trackers process points separately in parallel, so they cannot exploit context from other points when a target point becomes occluded or leaves the frame.
- Joint dense tracking is normally computationally prohibitive since attention across both time and many tracks scales quadratically, blocking large-scale simultaneous point tracking.

## Method

- ==Joint transformer tracking==: attention layers factorized across time and track dimensions let **CoTracker** exchange information between tracks within a sliding window, exploiting inter-point dependencies.
- ==Proxy tokens==: learned fixed tokens act as registers that regular tracks cross-attend instead of each other, cutting track-attention cost from quadratic to near-linear and enabling 70k simultaneous tracks.
- ==Unrolled windowed training==: the model is trained as a recurrent network over overlapping sliding windows with a discounted multi-iteration loss, enabling long-horizon tracking through occlusions beyond training length.

## Results

- On TAP-Vid-DAVIS (first protocol) CoTracker reaches AJ **62.2** versus TAPIR's **56.2**, a roughly 6-point improvement, and OA **89.3** versus **86.5**.
- Removing joint tracking on Dynamic Replica drops occluded-point accuracy from **37.6** to **28.8** (a **30.6%** relative loss), far more than visible-point accuracy loss, confirming joint tracking mainly helps occlusions.
- Proxy tokens raise trackable points **7.4x** (**9.4k** to **69.2k**) on an 80GB GPU and cut inference time **7x**, while disabling unrolled training drops DAVIS AJ from **62.2** to **44.6**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modeling correlations between tracked points, not just individual point appearance, is a powerful generalizable signal for occlusion-robust tracking, echoing why joint estimation beats independence assumptions elsewhere.
> - Register-style proxy tokens are a general recipe for scaling attention-based set processing to tens of thousands of elements without quadratic blowup.
> - Training a windowed model recurrently via unrolling teaches long-horizon consistency that a single-window objective cannot, letting inference generalize far beyond the training sequence length.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "CoTracker" addresses a fundamental problem in computer vision: **point correspondence in videos**. This area is broadly divided into two main tasks: optical flow and point tracking.

**Optical flow** aims to estimate the dense, instantaneous motion (velocities) of all points within a video frame. Modern approaches, like RAFT, have leveraged deep learning and transformer architectures to achieve impressive accuracy. However, optical flow is typically designed for short-range motion prediction and suffers from error accumulation (drift) when integrated over extended periods, making it unsuitable for long-term tracking through occlusions.

**Point tracking (or Tracking Any Point - TAP)**, on the other hand, focuses on estimating the motion of a sparse set of points over an extended duration. Historically, many point trackers, including recent state-of-the-art models like TAPIR and PIPs++, have treated individual points as statistically independent entities. While these methods have improved tracking robustness, especially through partial occlusions, they often struggle with long-term occlusions or when points leave and re-enter the camera view, as they lack a mechanism to infer the motion of an invisible point based on its visible neighbors.

"CoTracker" bridges this gap by reintroducing and significantly advancing the concept of **joint point tracking**. While earlier works, such as Particle Video, explored joint tracking, "CoTracker" is the first to effectively bring modern transformer-based architectures and deep learning techniques to this paradigm at scale. The authors hypothesize that accounting for the inherent dependencies between tracked points (e.g., points on the same rigid object will move coherently) can dramatically improve tracking accuracy and robustness. This stands in contrast to the prevailing independent tracking methods and positions CoTracker as a novel approach that leverages a critical, yet often overlooked, aspect of point motion.

Furthermore, the paper draws inspiration from related fields:
- **Multi-frame optical flow:** While these methods extend flow to multiple frames, they are generally not designed for the long-term occlusion handling that point tracking requires.
- **Visual object tracking (VOT):** VOT systems predict the state of objects and have explored context utilization. CoTracker adapts the idea of using "context" (via support points) from VOT, but applies it to arbitrary points, not just predefined objects.
- **Transformer architectures:** Building on the success of transformers in various vision tasks (e.g., image recognition, object detection like DETR, and optical flow like Flowformer), CoTracker adapts them for the spatiotemporal dependencies in point tracking.

By demonstrating the superior performance of joint tracking through rigorous experimentation and introducing architectural innovations for scalability, CoTracker pushes the boundaries of point tracking, making it more robust and versatile for downstream applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of CoTracker is to develop a highly accurate, robust, and scalable transformer-based model capable of tracking a large number of 2D points over long video sequences. This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Exploiting Point Dependencies:** The core motivation stems from the observation that points in a video often exhibit strong statistical dependencies (e.g., points on the same rigid object move coherently, or points on a deforming object move in a correlated manner). Most existing state-of-the-art point trackers, such as TAPIR and PIPs++, track points independently, ignoring this valuable contextual information. CoTracker aims to demonstrate that explicitly modeling these dependencies through a "joint tracking" mechanism can lead to significant performance improvements.

2. **Enhanced Robustness to Occlusions and Out-of-View Scenarios:** A major challenge in long-term point tracking is maintaining a track when a point becomes occluded or temporarily leaves the camera's field of view. Independent trackers struggle in these situations because they lose direct visual cues. The authors hypothesize that joint tracking, by leveraging information from surrounding visible points, can enable more accurate inference of an occluded or out-of-view point's trajectory, thus improving long-term consistency.

3. **Scalability for Quasi-Dense Tracking:** Traditional transformer-based models face a quadratic computational and memory complexity with respect to the number of tokens (points). To enable joint tracking of a *large* number of points simultaneously (approaching quasi-dense tracking), CoTracker needed innovations to make the transformer architecture memory- and computationally-efficient.

4. **Long-Term Tracking in Arbitrarily Long Videos:** Real-world videos can be very long. CoTracker aims to be an online algorithm capable of processing arbitrarily long videos causally (frame-by-frame or in short windows), without requiring access to future frames. This necessitates a mechanism for propagating tracking information robustly over extended durations.

5. **Contextual Awareness for Sparse Queries:** Even when a user is only interested in tracking a few points, providing additional contextual information from "support points" (e.g., a grid of surrounding points) can improve the tracking accuracy of the target points. CoTracker aims to explore and leverage this concept.

In essence, CoTracker seeks to build a new paradigm for point tracking that is not only more accurate and robust than previous methods but also scalable and practical for a wide range of applications by embracing the interconnected nature of point motion.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CoTracker formulates point tracking as a progressive refinement problem within a transformer-based framework, operating on a sliding window of video frames. Its core innovations revolve around enabling efficient joint tracking and robust long-term performance.

#### 3.1. Transformer Formulation and Iterative Refinement

- **Input and Output:** The model takes a video sequence (T RGB frames) and the starting locations/times of `N` points. It outputs estimated 2D point tracks (`P̂`) and their visibility flags (`v̂`).
- **Feature Extraction:** A convolutional neural network (CNN) extracts dense, multi-scale, d-dimensional appearance features `ϕ(It)` from each video frame, downsampling the image by a factor `k=4`.
- **Token Representation:** Tracks are encoded as input tokens `G`, which are a concatenation of:
 - Estimated position (`P̂`)
 - Visibility flag (`v̂`)
 - Time-dependent track features (`Q`), initialized by sampling image features at starting locations and updated by the network.
 - Spatial correlation features (`C`), similar to RAFT, obtained by comparing `Q` with image features `ϕs(It)` around `P̂` across multiple scales and offsets.
 - Positional encodings for relative displacement, initial location, and time.
- **Iterated Transformer Application:** The transformer `Ψ` is applied `M` times iteratively. Each iteration refines the estimated `P̂` and updates the track features `Q`. Visibility `v̂` is predicted only once after the final iteration.

#### 3.2. Key Architectural Innovations

- **Joint Point Tracking:** The central idea is to allow information exchange between different tracks. The transformer `Ψ` employs attention layers that operate across both the time dimension (within a single track) and the track dimension (across all tracks at a given time). This cross-track attention allows the model to exploit dependencies between points (e.g., points on the same object).
- **Proxy Tokens for Scalability:** To address the `O(N^2)` complexity of full self-attention when tracking a large number of `N` points, CoTracker introduces `K` fixed, learnable "proxy tokens" (where `K << N`). Regular tracks cross-attend to these proxies, but not to each other. This reduces the track-dimension attention complexity to `O(NK + K^2)`, significantly improving memory efficiency and allowing the model to track up to 70k points quasi-densely on a single GPU. These proxy tokens act similarly to registers or query tokens in other transformer architectures, summarizing information that can then be broadcast back to individual tracks.
- **Windowed Inference and Unrolled Training:**
 - **Windowed Inference:** For videos longer than the maximum window length `T`, the video is processed in partially overlapping sliding windows (e.g., `T/2` overlap). Track predictions from the end of one window are used to initialize the start of the next, enabling causal, online processing of arbitrarily long videos.
 - **Unrolled Training:** The model is trained in an "unrolled" fashion, treating the sequence of overlapping windows as a recurrent network. The loss function sums prediction errors over multiple transformer applications and windows, encouraging the model to propagate information consistently across long temporal spans. This is crucial for maintaining tracks through prolonged occlusions or when points leave the view.
- **Support Points:** To provide richer contextual information, especially when only a few target points are queried, CoTracker allows for the addition of "support points" during inference. These additional points, forming regular global or local grids around target points, are not necessarily benchmark points but help the joint tracker by providing more context about camera motion, object boundaries, or scene structure.

#### 3.3. Training and Evaluation

- **Training Data:** Primarily TAP-Vid-Kubric, a synthetic dataset, but also PointOdyssey for a specific version.
- **Loss Function:** A combination of `L1` loss on predicted positions (with a discount factor for early iterations) and cross-entropy loss for visibility flags.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** TAP-Vid metrics (Occlusion Accuracy (OA), `δvis_avg`, Average Jaccard (AJ)), and PointOdyssey/Dynamic Replica metrics (`δavg`, `δvis_avg`, `δocc_avg`, Survival rate).

This sophisticated blend of transformer architecture, memory-efficient attention, and recurrent training strategies enables CoTracker to achieve its goal of robust, scalable, and accurate joint point tracking.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

CoTracker demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across multiple benchmarks, validating its core hypothesis of "tracking together" and the efficacy of its architectural innovations.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance

- **Superior Accuracy on TAP-Vid Benchmarks:** CoTracker substantially outperforms prior trackers (TAP-Net, OmniMotion, PIPs, MFT, PIPs++, TAPIR) on TAP-Vid-DAVIS and TAP-Vid-RGB-Stacking. For example, on DAVIS First protocol, CoTracker achieves an AJ of 62.2, `δvis_avg` of 75.7, and OA of 89.3, significantly higher than TAPIR (56.2 AJ, 70.0 `δvis_avg`, 86.5 OA). This shows strong generalization from synthetic (Kubric) training data to real-world (DAVIS) videos.
- **Exceptional Occlusion Handling:** The improvements are particularly pronounced for tracking occluded points. On Dynamic Replica, CoTracker's `δocc_avg` is 37.6, a substantial leap from TAPIR (27.2) and PIPs++ (28.5). This directly supports the motivation that joint tracking helps in understanding scene motion even for invisible points.
- **Improved Long-Term Tracking:** On PointOdyssey, CoTracker achieves a survival rate of 55.2%, outperforming PIPs++ (47.0%), despite using a much smaller sliding window (8 frames vs. 128 frames for PIPs++). This highlights the effectiveness of unrolled training in propagating information over very long sequences.
- **Competitive on Challenging Datasets:** Even on TAP-Vid-Kinetics, which features discontinuous video segments (a challenge for causal online trackers), CoTracker performs comparably to TAPIR, an offline method with a specialized matching module.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

- Figure 6 illustrates that CoTracker produces visually cleaner and more coherent tracks compared to PIPs++ and TAPIR, especially in challenging scenarios with occlusions or complex motion. It effectively avoids points getting "stuck" or moving chaotically.
- Figure 7 demonstrates CoTracker's ability to track points even when they leave the camera's view, a critical capability for robust long-term tracking.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Importance of Joint Tracking:** Disabling cross-track attention ("no joint") leads to a significant performance drop (e.g., 55.6 AJ vs. 62.2 AJ on DAVIS First). This definitively confirms that exploiting dependencies between tracks is crucial, especially for `δocc_avg` (+30.6% improvement with joint tracking).
- **Importance of Unrolled Training:** Training without unrolling windows results in a massive performance degradation (-18 AJ points on DAVIS First), underscoring its necessity for long-term consistency.
- **Effect of Proxy Tokens:** Proxy tokens are key for scalability. They allow CoTracker to track 7.4 times more points (70k vs. 9.4k) on an 80GB GPU while also making inference approximately 7 times faster for the maximum number of points. An optimal configuration of 64 proxy tokens was identified.
- **Optimal Support Point Configurations:** Adding any support points (global, local, or both) improves performance for tracking individual target points. Local context provides more benefit than global, and their combination yields the best results, demonstrating the value of contextual information for specific target points.
- **Feature Stride and Window Size:** Higher resolution features (smaller stride) significantly improve accuracy. The optimal training and inference sliding window size was found to be `T=8` for the Kubric dataset used.

#### 4.4. Efficiency

- CoTracker achieves better accuracy than both TAPIR and PIPs++, and while it is slower than TAPIR for a given number of points, it is faster than PIPs++. Critically, it offers superior scalability in terms of the number of points that can be tracked concurrently on a single GPU, thanks to proxy tokens.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

CoTracker represents a significant advancement in the field of 2D point tracking, offering a robust, accurate, and scalable solution that addresses key limitations of prior work. Its significance and potential impact are multi-faceted:

- **Paradigm Shift in Point Tracking:** By conclusively demonstrating that "it is better to track together," CoTracker challenges the prevailing independent tracking paradigm. It provides strong evidence that explicitly modeling spatiotemporal dependencies between points via attention mechanisms dramatically enhances performance, especially in challenging scenarios involving occlusions and out-of-view motion. This could inspire a new wave of research focusing on more holistic, context-aware tracking systems.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Long-Term Consistency:** CoTracker's ability to track points accurately through long occlusions and when they temporarily leave the frame is a major breakthrough. This makes it suitable for applications that demand high temporal coherence over extended periods, moving beyond the short-term limitations of many existing trackers.
- **Scalability for Quasi-Dense Applications:** The innovation of proxy tokens is crucial for practical utility. It allows CoTracker to track a very large number of points (up to 70k) quasi-densely on a single GPU, enabling high-resolution motion estimation previously unattainable without significant computational resources or architectural compromises. This opens doors for applications requiring fine-grained motion understanding across entire video frames.
- **Enabling Downstream Computer Vision Tasks:** Point tracking is a fundamental low-level computer vision task that underpins numerous higher-level applications. Improved tracking accuracy and robustness offered by CoTracker can have a cascading positive impact on:
 - **3D Reconstruction:** More accurate correspondences lead to better camera pose estimation and denser, more accurate 3D models.
 - **Video Editing and Synthesis:** Precise point tracks facilitate tasks like object removal, motion transfer, and special effects.
 - **Motion Analysis and Understanding:** Better tracks enable more insightful analysis of object and scene dynamics for robotics, autonomous driving, and sports analytics.
 - **Video Object Segmentation:** Consistent point tracks can help propagate object masks more reliably across frames.
 - **Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM):** Robust point features are critical for tracking camera motion and building maps.
- **Online and Causal Operation:** CoTracker operates causally in a sliding-window fashion, making it suitable for online processing and real-time applications where access to future frames is not feasible. This is a critical advantage for many practical deployments.
- **Flexible and Generalizable Architecture:** The transformer-based design is inherently flexible, allowing for future extensions and integrations, such as explicit 3D reconstruction, as hinted by the authors. The model's ability to generalize from synthetic training data to real-world benchmarks also speaks to its robustness.

**Limitations Acknowledged by Authors:**
Despite its strengths, the authors candidly point out certain limitations:
- CoTracker occasionally makes "human-like" tracking mistakes that are still challenging for machines.
- Generalization to complex real-world scenes with challenging visual phenomena like reflections and shadows can be imperfect, as the model is primarily trained on synthetic data. Specifically, it sometimes tracks shadows along with objects, which may or may not be desirable depending on the application. Addressing these generalization gaps will likely involve training on more diverse and realistic datasets.

In conclusion, CoTracker pushes the frontier of point tracking, offering a powerful, efficient, and versatile tool for understanding motion in videos. Its innovations are likely to be influential in both academic research and industrial applications, paving the way for more intelligent and robust video analysis systems.
