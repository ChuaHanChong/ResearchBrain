# 3D-ViTac: Learning Fine-Grained Manipulation with Visuo-Tactile Sensing

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Columbia University developed 3D-ViTac, a system combining a novel, low-cost flexible tactile sensor with a unified 3D visuo-tactile representation to enhance robotic manipulation. The system achieved significantly higher success rates on tasks requiring fine-grained force control and robust handling under occlusion, outperforming vision-only approaches.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation often relies primarily on vision, struggling with occlusions, fine-grained force control, and complex in-hand adjustments.
- Existing high-resolution tactile sensors are often bulky, rigid, or expensive, while low-cost options lack sufficient information density or flexibility for dexterous tasks.
- Effectively fusing disparate visual (global) and tactile (local) modalities while preserving their 3D spatial relationships remains a challenge for learning algorithms.

## Method

- Development of a novel, ==low-cost== (approx. $20/pad), flexible, and dense (16x16 array) ==resistive tactile sensor== integrated into a soft, 3D-printed gripper.
- Proposal of a ==unified 3D visuo-tactile representation== that concatenates 3D visual point clouds with 3D tactile point clouds (including continuous readings) and modality indicators.
- Leveraging a ==conditional denoising diffusion model== with a ==PointNet++== backbone to learn policies based on this unified 3D representation from bimanual teleoperated demonstrations.

## Results

- The 3D-ViTac system achieved significantly higher success rates across challenging bimanual tasks (e.g., **85%** for Egg Steaming, **90%** for Hex Key Collection) compared to vision-only baselines (e.g., **50-65%**).
- Qualitative analysis confirmed that tactile sensing provided precise force control, enabled handling of visual occlusions, and improved task transition confidence.
- Ablation studies showed that dense (16x16) and continuous tactile signals outperformed sparser or binary tactile information, and that tactile sensing effectively compensated for limited visual information.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating vision and touch through a unified 3D representation is crucial for robust, fine-grained robotic manipulation, especially under occlusion and for fragile objects.
> - Low-cost, flexible, and dense tactile sensors can provide critical continuous force and contact pattern information, complementing visual data.
> - Real-time tactile feedback during teleoperation improves the quality and efficiency of human demonstrations, leading to more robust learned policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is continuously striving to enable robots to perform tasks with human-like dexterity, robustness, and adaptability. While significant progress has been made, particularly with advances in deep learning and imitation learning, a critical gap remains in equipping robots with the full spectrum of sensory capabilities that humans naturally possess. Humans rely heavily on both visual and tactile perception—vision for global context and object localization, and touch for fine-grained contact information, force control, and in-hand manipulation.

Traditionally, robotic manipulation has primarily relied on visual inputs (e.g., RGB images, depth maps, point clouds). While effective for many tasks, vision alone inherently struggles with challenges such as visual occlusion (where objects or contact points are hidden from camera view), discerning fine-grained contact forces (crucial for handling fragile objects), and understanding complex in-hand object states (e.g., subtle slips or rotations within the gripper). Existing tactile sensors, while addressing some of these issues, often present their own set of limitations. Many high-resolution optical tactile sensors are bulky or rigid, making them unsuitable for fine-grained, compliant interactions or integration into thin robot fingers. Conversely, low-cost tactile sensors are often too sparse to provide meaningful information. Commercial solutions can be prohibitively expensive, hindering widespread adoption and scalability.

Furthermore, integrating disparate modalities like vision (global, semantic) and touch (local, physical) presents a significant algorithmic challenge. Previous approaches to visuo-tactile manipulation have often treated these modalities separately or used low-resolution tactile signals, failing to explicitly capture the rich 3D spatial relationship between visual observations and tactile contacts. Similarly, the growing area of bimanual manipulation, while offering new avenues for complex tasks, remains largely vision-centric, limiting human-level flexibility and dexterity.

3D-ViTac directly addresses these critical limitations. By developing a novel, low-cost, flexible, and dense tactile sensor, the authors overcome a major hardware barrier. More importantly, their proposed unified 3D visuo-tactile representation tackles the modality fusion problem by explicitly integrating visual and tactile data within a common 3D space. This novel approach allows the learning policy to leverage the complementary strengths of both senses, enabling robots to perform tasks that are currently beyond the capabilities of vision-only systems, particularly those involving fragile objects, significant occlusions, and complex in-hand adjustments. This work represents a significant step towards creating robots that can interact with the physical world with a richer, more human-like sensory understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind 3D-ViTac stems from the observation that human dexterity in manipulation tasks fundamentally relies on the seamless integration of visual and tactile information. Tasks ranging from grasping a delicate egg to precisely manipulating a utensil require a continuous interplay between what we see and what we feel. The authors highlight this by contrasting the global, geometric information provided by vision with the detailed, local contact information derived from touch. Replicating this multi-modal sensing capability in robots is seen as crucial for unlocking more advanced and robust manipulation skills.

However, the pursuit of such robotic capabilities faces two primary challenges:
1. **Tactile Hardware and System Limitations:** Existing tactile sensors are often too bulky or rigid for fine-grained, compliant manipulation. High-resolution sensors can be expensive, while low-cost alternatives typically lack sufficient information density. The need for a tactile sensor that is cost-effective, flexible, dense, and capable of stable continuous signal transmission is paramount.
2. **Distinct Nature of Tactile and Visual Modalities:** Visual data provides a broad, semantic overview, while tactile signals are local and physical. This inherent disparity makes it difficult for traditional models to effectively fuse and interpret the combined data, especially in a way that preserves their critical 3D spatial relationships.

In response to these challenges, the key objectives of the 3D-ViTac project are clearly defined:
- **Develop a novel, flexible, and dense tactile sensor array:** This aims to provide detailed and extensive coverage of physical contacts, overcoming the limitations of existing solutions in terms of cost, flexibility, and information density. The specific goal is a 16x16 sensor array per finger, covering a larger area of the robot end-effector.
- **Propose a unified 3D visuo-tactile representation:** Instead of processing modalities separately, the objective is to fuse visual and tactile data into a single 3D representation space. This representation must explicitly preserve the 3D structures and spatial relationships between visual points and tactile contacts, introducing an inductive bias beneficial for contact-rich tasks.
- **Integrate this representation with imitation learning policies:** Specifically, the goal is to leverage diffusion policies, known for their ability to generate complex action sequences, conditioned on the unified multi-modal input.
- **Demonstrate enhanced performance in challenging real-world manipulation tasks:** The system's effectiveness is to be validated through concrete hardware experiments, showcasing its ability to perform precise manipulations, safely interact with fragile items, handle tasks under significant visual occlusion, and execute long-horizon in-hand manipulation tasks, significantly outperforming vision-only approaches.

By addressing both the hardware and algorithmic challenges of multi-modal sensing, 3D-ViTac aims to significantly expand the scope and robustness of robotic manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The 3D-ViTac system integrates custom hardware for multi-modal sensing with a sophisticated learning algorithm for dexterous bimanual manipulation.

#### 3.1. Sensor and Gripper Design

The core of the hardware innovation lies in the **Flexible Tactile Sensors**. These sensor pads are based on resistive sensing matrices, converting mechanical pressure into electrical signals. Crucially, they are designed with a thickness of less than 1mm, allowing for seamless integration onto various robotic manipulators. The authors install these sensors on a fully 3D-printed **soft, fin-shaped gripper made from TPU material**. This integration offers several advantages: the soft gripper increases contact area for stability and consistent contact pattern reflection, and its mechanical compliance aids in handling fragile objects while complementing the policy's action compliance.

Each finger of the manipulator is equipped with a 16x16 sensor array, totaling 256 sensing units per pad. The resolution is set at 3mm² per sensor point, providing dense contact information. The tactile pads employ a triple-layer design: a piezoresistive layer (Velostat) sandwiched between two orthogonally aligned sets of conductive yarns (electrodes), encapsulated by Polyimide films with adhesive for robust electrical contact. The resistance of the Velostat layer changes with applied pressure. An Arduino Nano captures these analog signals, which are then transmitted to a computer via serial communication at approximately 32.2 FPS. The manufacturing cost for one sensor pad and its reading board is impressively low, at about $20.

#### 3.2. Multi-Modal Sensing and Teleoperation Setup

The system utilizes a **bimanual teleoperation setup** where human operators control two puppet robots using master devices. During data collection, synchronized multi-modal information is gathered at a consistent frequency of 10 Hz. This includes data from the custom tactile sensors, multi-view RGBD cameras (Realsense 455 for global view, Realsense 435 for detailed views), and robot proprioception (target actions and current joint states). Timestamping and offline alignment ensure temporal consistency across modalities. A key feature for data quality is the **real-time visualization of tactile information** on the operator's screen, enabling humans to assess contact adequacy and gather higher-quality demonstrations.

#### 3.3. Learning Visuo-Tactile Dexterity

The learning problem is formulated as **imitation learning** for contact-rich robot skill trajectories, where a policy π maps observations (O) to actions (A). The approach consists of two critical parts:

- **Dense Visuo-Tactile Representation:** This is the algorithmic cornerstone for fusing the distinct modalities. Instead of separate processing, visual and tactile data are projected into a unified 3D space.
 - **3D Visual Point Cloud (P_visual):** Multi-view depth observations are merged for comprehensive scene coverage, cropped to the work region, and then down-sampled using Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) to a fixed size (N_vis = 512) for computational efficiency while preserving geometric detail. These points are transformed into the robot's base frame, with an additional empty feature channel for shape matching with tactile data.
 - **3D Tactile Point Cloud (P_tactile):** This represents the positions of individual tactile sensing units in 3D space, with their continuous tactile readings (sensing values) as a feature channel. The positions are calculated in real-time using forward kinematics based on the robot's joint states. The number of tactile points (N_tac) is 256 per sensor pad, multiplied by the number of fingers equipped with sensors (2 for single arm, 4 for bimanual tasks).
 - **Unified Representation:** P_tactile and P_visual are concatenated (o = P_tactile U P_visual). Each point is augmented with a one-hot encoding to indicate its modality (visual or tactile). This unified representation provides the policy network with explicit spatial relationships and structural information between vision and touch, serving as a powerful inductive bias for contact-rich manipulation.

- **Policy Learning:** The decision module is formulated as a **conditional denoising diffusion model**. The network uses a **PointNet++ architecture as its backbone**, which is well-suited for processing unordered point cloud data. Conditioned on the unified 3D visuo-tactile representation (o), the diffusion model learns to denoise random Gaussian noise into the desired action sequence (a), represented as a sequence of robot joint states. The PointNet++ backbone includes hierarchical feature extraction through multiple set abstraction layers and uses fully connected layers for final feature processing.

This comprehensive methodology, spanning hardware design and advanced learning algorithms, enables 3D-ViTac to effectively tackle the complexities of real-world, contact-rich robotic manipulation tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents compelling experimental results demonstrating the superior performance of 3D-ViTac across various challenging real-world manipulation tasks.

#### 4.1. Tactile Sensor Hardware Characterization

Detailed characterization of the custom tactile sensors revealed their crucial physical properties. The relationship between applied force and tactile reading signals showed an approximately linear region on a logarithmic scale of force (between 1N and 9N), indicating reliable and continuous force sensing. The sensors exhibited consistency across the entire 16x16 array under different loads, confirming their stable functionality. Furthermore, an additional experiment (in Appendix) on 6-DoF object pose estimation using only tactile information highlighted that these sensors embed valuable information about object geometry and local contact patterns, vital for in-hand reorientation and robust grasping.

#### 4.2. Performance on Challenging Manipulation Tasks

3D-ViTac was evaluated on four demanding real-world tasks, categorized by the type of tactile information they primarily benefit from:

1. **Tasks Requiring Fine-Grained Force Information:**
 - **Egg Steaming:** Opening a tray, grasping and placing a fragile egg without damage, and securing a cooker cover.
 - **Fruit Preparation:** Grasping and placing a plate, bimanual opening of a plastic bag, and grasping/placing fragile grapes without damage.

2. **Tasks Requiring In-Hand State Information:**
 - **Hex Key Collection:** Grasping a hex key, bimanual in-hand adjustment of its position, and precise insertion into a hole.
 - **Sandwich Serving:** Grasping a serving spoon, tilting a pot with the left hand, retrieving a fried egg with the spoon, and serving it on bread.

#### 4.3. Comparison with Baselines (Table 1)

Across all tasks and their constituent steps, the proposed 3D-ViTac system (PC. w/ Tactile Points) consistently and significantly outperformed all baselines. These baselines included vision-only policies (RGB Only, PC. Only) and policies that fused vision with tactile images (RGB w/ Tactile Image, PC. w/ Tactile Image). For instance, in the "Whole Task" success rates:
- **Egg Steaming:** 3D-ViTac achieved 85% success, compared to 50% for RGB Only and 55% for PC. Only.
- **Fruit Preparation:** 3D-ViTac achieved 80% success, against 45% for RGB Only and 45% for PC. Only.
- **Hex Key Collection:** 3D-ViTac reached 90% success, while RGB Only managed 45% and PC. Only 65%.
- **Sandwich Serving:** 3D-ViTac recorded 85% success, versus 60% for RGB Only and 65% for PC. Only.

This stark difference highlights the critical role of dense, spatially explicit visuo-tactile information for robust manipulation.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Analysis and Ablation Studies

- **Benefits of Integrating Touch:**
 - **Precise Force Control:** Tactile sensors provide feedback on contact presence and applied force, preventing common failures like dropping fragile objects due to insufficient grip or damaging them due to excessive force. Vision-only policies often misapply force or fail to secure stable grasps.
 - **Handling Visual Occlusion:** Detailed contact patterns from touch enable the policy to overcome heavy visual occlusions. For example, in-hand adjustment of the Hex Key and precise maneuvering of the spoon in the Sandwich Serving task were significantly improved, where vision-only baselines frequently failed due to obscured views.
 - **Task Transition Confidence:** Tactile feedback provides crucial confidence signals for progressing between task stages, preventing the robot from getting stuck (e.g., inside a plastic bag) when visual information is ambiguous or noisy.

- **Ablation Study on Varying Levels of Visual Information (Table 2):** Experiments showed that tactile sensing effectively compensates for increased visual occlusion. The performance of 3D-ViTac with a single camera and tactile points (0.80 success rate in Egg Steaming, 0.70 in Hex Key Collection) was comparable to or even better than multi-camera vision-only policies (0.55 and 0.65 respectively). This underscores the ability of touch to provide crucial information when visual input is limited.

- **Ablation Study on Varying Levels of Tactile Information (Fig. 6):** This study validated the importance of dense and continuous tactile signals. Policies trained with 16x16 continuous tactile data consistently outperformed those with sparser (8x8, 4x4) or binary tactile signals, especially for tasks requiring precise in-hand orientation. This confirms that simply detecting contact is insufficient; detailed force and contact pattern information is essential.

- **Demonstration Data Quality with Tactile Feedback (Fig. 5):** The real-time visualization of tactile signals during teleoperation improved the quality and efficiency of human demonstrations. New users completed tasks significantly faster with tactile feedback, indicating that this feature aids human operators in generating higher-quality training data, thereby contributing to more robust learned policies.

In summary, the results unequivocally demonstrate that 3D-ViTac's combination of advanced tactile hardware and intelligent multi-modal data fusion leads to substantial improvements in robotic manipulation, particularly in real-world scenarios characterized by fragility, occlusion, and the need for dexterous in-hand interactions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The 3D-ViTac system represents a significant advance in robotic manipulation, bridging critical gaps in both hardware and learning methodologies. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Validation of Multi-Modal Perception:** The paper conclusively demonstrates the indispensable role of integrating both vision and touch for achieving fine-grained, robust, and dexterous manipulation in robots. It empirically proves that combined visuo-tactile input significantly surpasses vision-only approaches, particularly in challenging, contact-rich scenarios.
- **Practical Tactile Sensing Solution:** By introducing a low-cost, flexible, dense, and continuous tactile sensor, 3D-ViTac addresses a long-standing bottleneck in robotic hardware. This innovation makes high-resolution tactile sensing more accessible and adaptable for integration into various robot grippers and end-effectors, moving beyond bulky or rigid traditional designs.
- **Novel Data Fusion Strategy:** The unified 3D visuo-tactile representation is a key algorithmic contribution. By explicitly preserving the 3D structure and spatial relationships between visual and tactile points, the method provides a strong inductive bias that facilitates effective learning for contact-rich tasks, unlike prior approaches that treat modalities separately or fuse them superficially.
- **Robustness in Real-World Scenarios:** The extensive real-world experiments on fragile object handling, manipulation under occlusion, and complex in-hand adjustments highlight the system's ability to operate effectively in practical, unstructured environments where uncertainties are common. This increases the applicability of robotic systems beyond constrained laboratory settings.
- **Improved Imitation Learning:** The integration with diffusion policies and the demonstration that real-time tactile feedback enhances human demonstration quality underscore the system's impact on the entire imitation learning pipeline, making data collection more efficient and the resulting policies more robust.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Enhanced Robotic Dexterity:** This work lays the foundation for robots with significantly improved dexterity, enabling them to handle delicate objects, perform assembly tasks requiring fine motor skills, and adapt to unforeseen contact conditions with greater precision and safety. This could revolutionize applications in manufacturing, healthcare (e.g., assisting surgeries, handling medical instruments), and service robotics (e.g., cooking, elderly care).
- **Democratization of Tactile Sensing:** The low cost and simple manufacturing process of the tactile sensors can significantly lower the barrier to entry for researchers and developers, fostering wider adoption and innovation in tactile robotics. This could lead to a proliferation of tactile-aware robots and accelerate research in haptic perception.
- **Advancement in Multi-Modal Learning:** The effective visuo-tactile fusion methodology provides a strong benchmark and inspiration for future research in multi-modal learning for robotics. It encourages the development of AI models that can better integrate and reason about heterogeneous sensory inputs, moving closer to human-like perception.
- **Safer Human-Robot Interaction:** The ability to precisely control force and detect contact empowers robots to interact more safely with their environment and, crucially, with humans. This is essential for the widespread deployment of robots in shared workspaces and domestic settings.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Work

The authors acknowledge a primary limitation: the expense and complexity of collecting real-world multi-modal data, particularly with the added tactile modality. A significant missing piece is a robust simulation for their custom tactile sensor, which currently limits the ability to leverage simulation for data scaling, randomization, and training, thereby potentially restricting generalizability. Future work is aimed at addressing this by enhancing the system's capabilities to better exploit advances in physical simulation, which would further improve the generalizability and robustness of the learned policies. This would allow for sim-to-real transfer learning, alleviating the heavy reliance on real-world data collection.

#### 5.4. Conclusion

Overall, 3D-ViTac provides a compelling solution for advancing robot manipulation by synergistically combining innovative hardware and intelligent learning algorithms, paving the way for more capable and adaptable robotic systems.
