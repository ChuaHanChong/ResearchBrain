# VL-Calibration: Decoupled Confidence Calibration for Large Vision-Language Models Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> VL-Calibration is a reinforcement-learning framework that teaches vision-language models to verbalize two separate confidences, one for visual perception and one for reasoning, combined by harmonic mean. Lacking perception labels, it builds a pseudo-label from image-perturbation KL-divergence plus token entropy, and reweights token advantages by visual certainty. Across thirteen benchmarks it cuts expected calibration error from 0.421 to 0.098 while raising accuracy.

## Problem

- Verbalized confidence methods built for text-only models optimize one holistic score against binary answer correctness, so perceptual and reasoning error sources stay conflated and unlocalizable.
- Vision-language reasoning is dominated by language priors, so intrinsic visual uncertainty is overshadowed and overall confidence ends up overconfident on ungrounded outputs.
- Sampling-based uncertainty estimators cost many generations, while logit or hidden-state estimators ignore visual grounding entirely, leaving no supervision signal for perception confidence.

## Method

- ==Decoupled confidence inference==: the policy emits a visual rationale with confidence, then a reasoning chain with confidence, fused by ==harmonic mean== so the weaker score dominates the holistic estimate.
- ==Intrinsic visual certainty estimation==: a log-ratio of ==KL-divergence== between outputs on original versus 0.8-ratio patch-masked images and mean token entropy, z-scored and sigmoid-mapped into a ==Brier visual-certainty reward==.
- ==Token-level advantage reweighting== inside ==GRPO== amplifies negative advantages on low-visual-certainty rationale tokens and softens them on well-grounded tokens, with reweighting coefficient 0.1.

## Results

- On Qwen3-VL-4B, average ECE falls **0.421** to **0.098** and AUROC rises to **0.763**; accuracy beats the strongest baseline by **2.3%**, and by **3.0%** at 8B.
- Ablations show decoupling alone matches RLCR (ECE **0.164** versus **0.167**); entropy plus KL together give **0.121**, and adding token reweighting reaches accuracy **0.727**, ECE **0.098**.
- The certainty estimator beats Self-Certainty and VL-Uncertainty at predicting judged caption quality (AUROC **0.746**, SRCC **0.496**); confidence gap on image-removed DynaMath widens to **0.616** versus **0.405**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Splitting a confidence score along a model's actual error sources only helps if each part gets its own supervision; relabeling the output format changes nothing.
> - Sensitivity to input perturbation and internal entropy are complementary uncertainty signals: entropy alone collapses training, perturbation KL alone lets entropy explode.
> - Perception-quality pseudo-labels can be harvested from a model's own logits, removing the need for ground-truth grounding annotations or expensive multi-sample uncertainty estimation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) have demonstrated advanced capabilities in multimodal reasoning by integrating visual perception with logical inference. However, a significant challenge impeding their application in critical domains such as healthcare or law is their propensity for hallucinations and the generation of incorrect responses accompanied by high certainty. This overconfidence issue, where models inaccurately assess their own predictions, necessitates robust confidence calibration.

Prior research has explored verbalized confidence calibration, primarily in text-only Large Language Models (LLMs). Methods like SaySelf and RLCR train models to output confidence scores alongside answers, often using techniques such as distilled rationales from larger models or reinforcement learning (RL) with Brier Score-based rewards. While these methods have shown effectiveness in text-centric tasks, their direct application to LVLMs encounters fundamental limitations.

The multimodal nature of LVLMs introduces complexities not present in text-only models. An incorrect prediction in an LVLM can originate from either a failure in visual perception (e.g., misinterpreting an image) or an error in reasoning despite correct perception. Existing single, holistic confidence scores conflate these distinct error sources, preventing precise error localization and effective uncertainty quantification. Furthermore, LVLM reasoning is frequently influenced by language priors, which can overshadow genuine visual uncertainty, leading to inaccurate overall confidence assessments.

Uncertainty estimation methods for LLMs and LVLMs generally fall into sampling-based approaches (e.g., Self-Consistency, VL-Uncertainty), which aggregate multiple generations but incur high computational costs, and internal-state methods (e.g., Self-Certainty), which leverage internal model signals but may overlook the critical aspect of visual grounding.

This work positions itself within this landscape by addressing these specific shortcomings. It proposes a novel approach to confidence calibration for LVLMs by explicitly decoupling confidence into visual and reasoning components. This decoupling aims to provide a more granular and interpretable understanding of LVLM uncertainty, moving beyond holistic scores to identify the specific source of potential errors, thereby enhancing model trustworthiness and applicability in high-stakes environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the confidence calibration and reasoning accuracy of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) by providing a more granular and interpretable mechanism for expressing uncertainty. This overarching goal is broken down into several specific objectives, motivated by the inherent challenges of confidence estimation in multimodal contexts:

1. **Decouple Confidence into Visual and Reasoning Components:** A key objective is to move beyond a single, holistic confidence score. The motivation for this is rooted in the observation that LVLM errors can arise from distinct sources: misinterpretation of visual input (perceptual failure) or flawed logical inference based on the visual input (reasoning error). A single confidence score conflates these sources, making it difficult to diagnose the root cause of an incorrect prediction. By decoupling confidence into separate visual and reasoning scores, the model can explicitly indicate the origin of its uncertainty, enabling more precise error localization.

2. **Supervise Visual Confidence without Ground-Truth Perception Labels:** Unlike overall answer correctness, obtaining ground-truth labels for the correctness of visual perception or intermediate visual rationales is challenging and resource-intensive. Therefore, a critical objective is to develop a method for effectively supervising the model's verbalized visual confidence ($c_{vis}$) in the absence of explicit, human-annotated ground truth for visual perception. This aims to ensure that the visual confidence score accurately reflects the model's actual certainty about its visual understanding.

3. **Focus Optimization to Suppress Ungrounded Hallucinations while Preserving Valid Perception:** Standard reinforcement learning (RL) credit assignment often treats all errors equally, which can be inefficient for multimodal models. The motivation here is to develop a mechanism that differentiates between types of errors, particularly penalizing ungrounded hallucinations (errors arising from low visual certainty) more severely. Conversely, the method should be designed to preserve and reinforce correct visual perceptions, even if accompanied by minor reasoning errors. This objective aims to guide the model towards more reliable visual grounding and prevent it from generating confidently incorrect visual descriptions.

In summary, the motivation stems from the need to make LVLMs more reliable and transparent, particularly in applications where trust and accuracy are paramount. By understanding *why* an LVLM might be uncertain (visually or logically), and by providing better supervisory signals for visual understanding, the research seeks to improve both the correctness of predictions and the faithfulness of confidence estimates.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The VL-Calibration framework addresses the challenges of confidence calibration in LVLMs through a reinforcement learning (RL) approach that explicitly decouples confidence and introduces novel supervisory signals.

#### 3.1. Decoupled Confidence Inference

The core idea is to reformulate LVLM reasoning into two distinct phases: visual perception and logical reasoning. The policy ($\pi_\theta$) is instructed to generate structured rationales, each followed by a specific verbalized confidence score. The generation trajectory ($\tau$) is defined as:
$\tau = (z_{vis}, c_{vis}, z_{reas}, c_{reas}, y)$
where $z_{vis}$ is the visual rationale (e.g., image dense caption), $c_{vis}$ is the normalized visual confidence token ($\hat{c}_{vis} \in$), $z_{reas}$ is the reasoning chain, $c_{reas}$ is the normalized reasoning confidence token ($\hat{c}_{reas} \in$), and $y$ is the final answer.

To derive a single holistic confidence score ($\Phi$) for the answer $y$, the harmonic mean of the decoupled scores is employed:
$\Phi(\hat{c}_{vis}, \hat{c}_{reas}) = \frac{2 \cdot \hat{c}_{vis} \cdot \hat{c}_{reas}}{\hat{c}_{vis} + \hat{c}_{reas}}$
The harmonic mean is chosen for its conservative property, ensuring that the holistic confidence is significantly reduced if either visual or reasoning confidence is low.

#### 3.2. Visual Certainty Estimation

A critical challenge is the absence of ground-truth labels for visual perception ($z_{vis}^*$ and its correctness $1_{z_{vis} \equiv z_{vis}^*}$). To address this, an "Intrinsic Visual Certainty Estimation" method is proposed, which combines two complementary dimensions:

- **Visual Grounding ($D_{KL}$):** This metric quantifies the model's reliance on the input image. It is computed as the KL-divergence between the model's output distributions when given the original image ($I$) versus a perturbed version ($I'$). Perturbation involves random patch masking with a ratio of 0.8. A high $D_{KL}$ indicates that the model's generation is sensitive to visual content, implying strong grounding.
- **Internal Certainty ($H$):** This metric assesses the model's internal confidence in its generated visual description. It is calculated as the average token entropy over the visual rationale ($z_{vis}$). Lower entropy signifies a sharper probability distribution, indicating higher internal certainty.

These two metrics are integrated into a log-ratio formulation to derive the Visual Certainty Score ($S_{vis}$):
$S_{vis} = \log(D_{KL} + \epsilon) - \log(H + \epsilon)$
This formulation rewards responses that are both visually responsive and internally confident, while providing optimization stability by compressing the numeric range.

#### 3.3. Reinforcement Learning with Certainty-Aware Calibration

The framework utilizes the GRPO (Generalized Reinforcement Learning with Policy Optimization) algorithm, extending its reward structure and credit assignment mechanism.

- **Reward Shaping:** The reward function $R(\tau, y^*)$ is composed of three terms:
 $R(\tau, y^*) = \lambda_{acc} R_{acc} + \lambda_{cal} R_{cal} + \lambda_{vis} R_{vis}$
 - **Accuracy Reward ($R_{acc}$):** A binary reward for the semantic equivalence of the generated answer $y$ with the ground truth $y^*$.
 - **Holistic Confidence Calibration Reward ($R_{cal}$):** A Brier score term that penalizes the squared difference between the holistic confidence $\Phi$ and the binary correctness of the answer.
 - **Visual Certainty Reward ($R_{vis}$):** A novel Brier score term designed to supervise visual confidence. It measures the squared difference between the model's verbalized visual confidence ($\hat{c}_{vis}$) and a pseudo-label derived from the normalized Visual Certainty Score ($\tilde{S}_{vis}$). The raw $S_{vis}$ is batch-wise z-score standardized and then mapped to $$ via a sigmoid transform, with a stop-gradient operator to stabilize training.
 This term explicitly anchors $\hat{c}_{vis}$ to the model's actual perceptual certainty.

- **Token-Level Advantage Reweighting (TAR):** Standard GRPO treats all tokens equally. TAR modifies the advantage ($\hat{A}_t$) calculation for tokens within the visual rationale ($z_{vis}$) to address ungrounded hallucinations. If the model makes an error (negative advantage $\hat{A}_t < 0$) under low visual certainty ($\tilde{S}_{vis}(t) \to 0$), the penalty is amplified. Conversely, if the model is well-grounded ($\tilde{S}_{vis}(t) \to 1$), the penalty is softened. This mechanism encourages the model to generate visually grounded rationales and penalizes blind guessing.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

The model is trained on VL-Calibration-12K, a subset of the ViRL-39K dataset. Experiments are conducted on Qwen3-VL (4B, 8B, 30B) and InternVL3.5-4B-MPO models to evaluate efficacy across different scales and architectures. Baselines include various inference-stage and training-stage calibration methods. Evaluation metrics include Accuracy (ACC), Expected Calibration Error (ECE), and Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (AUROC), assessed across thirteen diverse visual reasoning benchmarks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that VL-Calibration consistently improves both confidence calibration and visual reasoning accuracy across various model scales and architectures.

**Overall Performance and Calibration:**
- **Reduced Expected Calibration Error (ECE):** VL-Calibration significantly reduces ECE. For Qwen3-VL-4B, ECE decreased from a baseline of 0.421 to 0.098. For Qwen3-VL-8B, ECE was reduced from 0.401 to 0.071. This indicates that the model's predicted confidence scores are more closely aligned with its actual accuracy.
- **Improved Accuracy:** The method simultaneously boosts average reasoning accuracy. On Qwen3-VL-4B, accuracy increased by 2.3% over the strongest baselines, and on Qwen3-VL-8B, it improved by 3.0%. This suggests that explicit calibration not only makes the model more honest about its uncertainties but also aids in better reasoning, particularly on complex visual reasoning benchmarks like DynaMath and MathVerse.
- **Enhanced AUROC:** AUROC scores, which measure the model's ability to discriminate between correct and incorrect predictions, also showed consistent improvements (e.g., from 0.524 to 0.763 for 4B and 0.553 to 0.764 for 8B), further confirming better calibration.

**Generalization Across Models and Benchmarks:**
- **Model Scale and Architecture:** VL-Calibration demonstrated generalization to larger models (Qwen3-VL-30B) and different architectures (InternVL3.5-4B-MPO). On Qwen3-VL-30B, AUROC improved to 0.767 and ECE reduced to 0.082, with accuracy boosting from 0.652 to 0.803. For InternVL3.5-4B, it outperformed the strong RLCR baseline in both accuracy (0.689) and ECE (0.103).
- **Out-of-Distribution Tasks:** The method showed consistent gains on multi-disciplinary benchmarks, such as MMMU-Pro (+2.2% accuracy) and A-OKVQA (ECE reduced from 0.112 to 0.017), indicating its robustness and applicability to diverse reasoning challenges.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Necessity of Decoupled Supervision:** Simply decoupling the confidence output without explicit visual certainty estimation (i.e., using RLCR with decoupled outputs) yielded negligible improvement, demonstrating that explicit supervision for visual confidence is crucial.
- **Effectiveness of Visual Certainty Estimation (VCE):** Incorporating either visual grounding (KL-divergence) or internal certainty (entropy) individually reduced ECE compared to the base model. Their combination, however, yielded the best performance and was empirically observed to prevent optimization pathologies like entropy collapse or explosion, ensuring robust training.
- **Contribution of Token-Level Advantage Reweighting (TAR):** Applying TAR on top of VCE further improved overall performance, boosting accuracy to 0.727 and minimizing ECE to 0.098. This confirms the benefit of fine-grained, uncertainty-aware reweighting for effective calibration.

**Analyses of Components:**
- **Validation of Visual Certainty Estimation:** The proposed VCE showed superior correlation with expert perception judgment (Gemini-3-pro-preview) compared to other uncertainty estimation methods, achieving higher AUROC (0.746), SRCC (0.496), and Kendall’s Tau (0.370). It also demonstrated robustness across various perturbation types and mask ratios.
- **Training Dynamics:** Training with VCE exhibited faster initial convergence, with ECE reducing to 0.1 within 100 steps, highlighting its effectiveness as a supervisory signal.
- **Impact of Decoupled Confidence:** The decoupled visual confidence mechanism substantially lowered confidence for visually incorrect responses while maintaining it for correct ones. It also created a larger "Confidence Gap" between visually answerable and unanswerable problems, indicating a better understanding of visual input limitations. A 2D heatmap visualization confirmed that visual and reasoning confidence scores are distinctly distributed, supporting the rationale for decoupling.
- **Qualitative Analysis of TAR:** Visualization of token uncertainty showed that TAR targets not only visually grounded content tokens but also logical connectives, suggesting its ability to influence critical parts of the reasoning process.

In summary, the main findings confirm that VL-Calibration effectively addresses the problem of overconfidence and hallucination in LVLMs by providing a more nuanced, decoupled approach to confidence calibration, which in turn enhances both the reliability and the accuracy of multimodal reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VL-Calibration framework presents several significant contributions and holds substantial potential impact on the development and application of Large Vision-Language Models.

**Enhanced Trustworthiness and Reliability:** The core significance lies in its ability to significantly improve the confidence calibration of LVLMs. By reducing the Expected Calibration Error (ECE) and improving AUROC, the model's verbalized confidence scores become more faithful indicators of its actual correctness. This directly addresses the critical issue of overconfident hallucinations, which is a major barrier to deploying LVLMs in sensitive and high-stakes domains.

**Improved Error Diagnosis and Interpretability:** The explicit decoupling of confidence into visual and reasoning components provides a more granular and interpretable understanding of LVLM uncertainty. This allows users and developers to identify whether a potential error stems from a perceptual misunderstanding of the visual input or a logical flaw in the reasoning process. This diagnostic capability can inform targeted model improvements, debug specific failure modes, and build greater trust in model outputs.

**Simultaneous Accuracy Gains:** Unlike some calibration methods that may trade off accuracy for better calibration, VL-Calibration demonstrates consistent improvements in reasoning accuracy across various benchmarks. This indicates that the process of disentangling and calibrating visual and reasoning confidence does not merely make the model "more honest" about its errors but actively contributes to better overall task performance, suggesting a deeper understanding and processing of multimodal information.

**Novel Supervisory Signal for Visual Confidence:** The introduction of an intrinsic visual certainty estimation method, which combines KL-divergence under image perturbation and token entropy, provides a practical solution to supervising visual confidence without requiring explicit, often unavailable, ground-truth labels for intermediate visual rationales. This method's strong correlation with human judgment of visual perception quality further validates its utility and can inform future research in self-supervision for multimodal understanding.

**Robustness and Generalizability:** The demonstrated effectiveness of VL-Calibration across different model scales (4B to 30B) and architectures (Qwen3-VL, InternVL3.5) and its generalization to out-of-distribution benchmarks underscore the robustness and broad applicability of the proposed paradigm. This suggests that the core principles of decoupled confidence and certainty-aware learning are not specific to a particular model family but offer a widely applicable approach.

**Facilitating Deployment in High-Stakes Domains:** By providing more reliable confidence estimates and reducing ungrounded hallucinations, VL-Calibration directly contributes to making LVLMs safer and more dependable for real-world applications. Industries such as healthcare (e.g., medical image analysis, diagnostic support), legal review, autonomous systems, and scientific research, where errors can have severe consequences, could benefit significantly from models that accurately reflect their uncertainty.

In conclusion, VL-Calibration represents an advancement in multimodal AI, offering a principled approach to tackle the interpretability and reliability challenges of LVLMs. Its potential impact lies in fostering more trustworthy, accurate, and transparent AI systems capable of operating effectively in complex and critical environments.
