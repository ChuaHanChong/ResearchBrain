# HumanoidVLN: A Physics-Grounded Simulator and Benchmark for Vision-Language Navigation Across Diverse Humanoid Embodiments

> [!summary] Summary
> HumanoidVLN is an Isaac Sim benchmark and simulator for vision-language navigation on bipedal humanoids. It couples per-robot RL locomotion policies with interchangeable PD or MPC path trackers, curates 87 navigability-filtered artist and 3D Gaussian Splatting scenes, and generates 933 human-verified instruction episodes via multi-agent annotation. Zero-shot evaluation of four VLN models across four embodiments yields a top mean success rate of 43.55%.

## Problem

- Prior VLN simulators move robots by kinematic teleportation, bypassing bipedal locomotion dynamics, gait stability, step reachability, and center-of-mass constraints that govern real humanoid feasibility.
- Physics-capable benchmarks treat humanoids as one interchangeable robot type sharing a single control proxy, ignoring diversity in lower-body degrees of freedom, height, and camera placement.
- Scene suites scale count without navigability curation, and automated VLM instruction annotation hallucinates spatial relations while rendering video under idealized, shake-free camera conditions.

## Method

- ==Hierarchical control stack==: a per-embodiment ==RL locomotion policy== commands joint torques, while a ==PD tracker== (discrete-action models) or ==MPC tracker== (continuous-action models) converts navigation plans into velocity references.
- ==Real2Sim pipeline== trains ==gsplat== with unbiased depth rendering plus ==depth-normal consistency==, extracts collision meshes by ==TSDF fusion==, and retains only scenes exceeding 100 square meters of traversable floor.
- ==Dual Generator-Reviewer plus Paraphraser multi-agent annotation==: two heterogeneous VLMs infer route graphs from egocentric keyframes, a reviewer verifies against scene-graph and trajectory priors, humans correct residual errors.

## Results

- JanusVLN leads with **43.55%** mean success rate and **48.38** nDTW across four embodiments; StreamVLN trails at **23.63%** success and **36.67** nDTW under physical execution.
- Embodiment matters strongly: Unitree H1 averages **20.84%** success versus **32-37%** for others, and its fall rate reaches **64.5%** (StreamVLN) and **71.0%** (NaVILA) versus **2.7-10.0%** elsewhere.
- A **20**-episode sim-real pilot with DualVLN on Unitree G1 gives navigation-error correlation **r = 0.935**, mean absolute difference **0.68 m**, and trajectory similarity **0.782 plus-minus 0.188** nDTW.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Success rate alone hides physical viability: a model can navigate accurately while falling in most episodes, so stability metrics like fall rate belong in embodied benchmarks.
> - Model rankings are not embodiment-invariant; the same checkpoint swings nearly 19 success points across morphologies, so single-robot evaluation overstates generalization.
> - Continuous action interfaces paired with MPC tracking gave the lowest falls, suggesting action-space granularity, not just perception quality, governs locomotion stability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) aims to enable embodied agents to interpret and execute natural language instructions within complex environments. Significant advancements have been made in humanoid robotics, perception, control, and machine learning, driving progress in this area. However, the application of VLN to humanoid robots presents specific challenges that existing benchmarks have largely not addressed.

Current VLN simulators, such as Habitat-Sim, AI2-THOR, and AirSim, typically model robot motion through kinematic teleportation, bypassing the physical complexities of locomotion. While some recent efforts, like VLN-PE and VLNVerse, leverage NVIDIA Isaac Sim for physical realism, they tend to treat humanoids as interchangeable robot types, often employing a single control proxy across varying morphologies. This approach overlooks the unique bipedal locomotion constraints, diverse morphologies (e.g., varying degrees of freedom, heights), and egocentric observation distortions caused by locomotion-induced camera dynamics inherent to humanoid platforms.

Furthermore, traditional VLN benchmarks often rely on static environments like Matterport3D, which offer limited realism and lack curation for navigability, particularly for bipedal robots that require large, obstacle-sparse traversable areas. Scene reconstruction methods like 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), as used in works like SAGE-3D and GSA-R2R, have improved visual fidelity, but often without explicit navigability filtering or a fully real-to-simulation pipeline.

Instruction generation in existing benchmarks faces limitations as well. Fully automated, VLM-based annotations can suffer from spatial hallucinations, while purely manual annotation is resource-intensive. Idealized video rendering conditions (stable camera, uniform lighting) in existing benchmarks also contrast with the dynamic visual input experienced by real humanoids.

HumanoidVLN positions itself as a specialized benchmark addressing these gaps by providing a physics-grounded simulator specifically designed for diverse humanoid embodiments. It incorporates advanced control architectures tailored to bipedal dynamics, curates scene environments for practical navigability, and employs a multi-agent, human-in-the-loop annotation framework for generating realistic, grounded instructions that account for physical execution. This work is distinguished by its joint focus on embodiment diversity, navigability-curated scenes, and reliable grounded instruction generation, which are critical for advancing VLN in real-world humanoid applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for HumanoidVLN stems from three critical gaps identified in existing Vision-Language Navigation research concerning humanoid robots. The paper aims to address these limitations through specific solutions.

**Motivation 1: Lack of Humanoid-Aware Simulation.**
Existing VLN simulators often simplify robot movement by using kinematic teleportation, which bypasses the complex physical constraints of bipedal locomotion. While some platforms like NVIDIA Isaac Sim incorporate physics, they frequently treat humanoids generically, using a single control mechanism across diverse morphologies. However, humanoid robots exhibit significant variations in lower-body degrees of freedom (10 to 12 DoF) and height (1.17 m to 1.8 m). These morphological differences directly influence gait stability, step reachability, and center-of-mass dynamics, making a generic approach inadequate for realistic simulation.
- **Objective:** Develop an Isaac Sim platform with a hierarchical control architecture. This platform is designed to support diverse humanoid morphologies, employing a reinforcement learning (RL) policy for low-level locomotion and interchangeable Proportional-Derivative (PD) or Model Predictive Control (MPC) controllers for high-level path tracking. This setup grounds navigation in actual bipedal dynamics, ensuring that simulated trajectories are physically executable by the specific robot under test.

**Motivation 2: Insufficiently Curated Scene Environments for Navigability.**
Standard VLN benchmarks often rely on environments like Matterport3D, which offer limited realism and typically lack curation for the specific needs of physically embodied robots. More recent efforts have improved scene quality and count, but without systematically filtering for navigability. Humanoid robots, in particular, require large, obstacle-sparse traversable areas to execute bipedal locomotion effectively. Environments that do not meet this criterion can introduce topological bottlenecks that are infeasible for real-world bipedal navigation.
- **Objective:** Curate a suite of high-fidelity scene environments with verified traversable areas. This involves selecting scenes with navigable floor areas exceeding 100 m² and utilizing a Real2Sim pipeline based on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) to construct simulation-ready environments from multi-view captures, reducing reliance on artist-authored assets.

**Motivation 3: Unrealistic and Ungrounded Instruction Data.**
Existing benchmarks often generate instruction data under idealized conditions, such as stable camera views and uniform lighting. In contrast, real humanoid operation involves significant camera shake induced by bipedal gait and dynamic lighting variations, which affect egocentric observations. Furthermore, while automated VLM-based annotation can scale data collection, it may suffer from spatial hallucinations. Manual annotation, while reliable, is often prohibitively expensive for large datasets.
- **Objective:** Collect egocentric video data directly from the humanoid simulator, capturing locomotion-induced camera dynamics. Subsequently, propose and implement a Dual Generator-Reviewer + Paraphraser Multi-Agent Annotation (MAA) framework. This framework iteratively generates, verifies against scene and trajectory priors, and paraphrases instructions, augmented by a human-in-the-loop verification pass to ensure spatial grounding and realism.

In summary, the key objectives are to provide an embodiment-diverse, physics-grounded simulation platform, a suite of navigability-curated real-to-sim scenes, a robust and human-verified instruction dataset, and a framework for cross-embodiment physics evaluation that collectively address the limitations of existing VLN benchmarks for humanoid robotics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The HumanoidVLN platform integrates several components to create a physics-grounded simulator and benchmark for vision-language navigation with diverse humanoid embodiments.

**Simulator Platform (Section III):**
The platform is built on NVIDIA Isaac Sim, leveraging its rigid-body dynamics and high-DoF articulation capabilities.
- **Supported Embodiments:** Four distinct humanoid configurations are supported to represent current bipedal robot diversity: Unitree G1 (12 DoF, 1.32 m height, 1.25 m camera height), Unitree H1 (10 DoF, 1.80 m height, 1.72 m camera height), Internal-A (12 DoF, 1.61 m height, 1.54 m camera height), and Internal-B (12 DoF, 1.17 m height, 1.11 m camera height). Each robot configuration loads independently.
- **Hierarchical Control Architecture:** Each robot employs a two-level control system. The low level uses a per-embodiment Reinforcement Learning (RL) locomotion policy that commands joint torques to achieve stable bipedal gaits while respecting joint limits and center-of-mass (CoM) dynamics. The high level consists of either a Proportional-Derivative (PD) controller or a Model Predictive Control (MPC) path tracker. These controllers translate high-level navigation plans into velocity and heading references for the RL policy. The MPC tracker is used for continuous-action VLN models, and the PD tracker for discrete-action models, ensuring physically executable trajectories.
- **VLN Model Interface and Data Recording:** The platform facilitates a plug-and-play interface for VLN models, which receive head-camera RGB streams and instructions, then emit actions translated into tracker references. It also records egocentric observation streams (RGB video, depth, IMU) directly from the walking robot, capturing real-world phenomena like camera shake and dynamic lighting. Compatibility is demonstrated with models such as NaVILA, DualVLN, StreamVLN, and JanusVLN.

**Real2Sim Scene Pipeline (Section IV):**
The environments are designed for humanoid navigability and realism.
- **Scene Sources and Navigability Curation:** Environments are derived from artist-designed indoor scenes and 3DGS reconstructions. To ensure bipedal traversability, only scenes with manually verified traversable floor areas exceeding 100 m² are retained. The final suite comprises 87 scenes across 17 indoor classes and 6 application domains (e.g., Residential, Food & retail), with a median navigable area of 266 m².
- **3DGS Reconstruction:** A customized 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) pipeline is developed using the open-source gsplat library. This pipeline directly processes multi-view captures of real environments to produce photorealistic scenes. To obtain high-fidelity collision meshes for physics simulation, the gsplat training pipeline is modified to render unbiased depth and enforce depth-normal consistency. Collision meshes are extracted post-training via TSDF fusion and packaged with the trained Gaussians in USDZ format. Scene registration and Gaussian training are bootstrapped from COLMAP, with coordinate system alignment to Isaac Sim's frame.
- **Physics-Aware Episode Sampling:** For each scene, the 3D collision mesh is sliced at robot body height to create a 2D occupancy map, dilated by the largest robot’s footprint. Start–goal pairs are randomly sampled, and A* paths are generated. The robot then executes these paths under its full hierarchical control stack. Episodes where the robot cannot stably complete the path (e.g., falls) are resampled, ensuring all recorded videos contain genuine locomotion-induced camera dynamics.

**Instruction Generation (Section V):**
A Multi-Agent Annotation (MAA) framework with human-in-the-loop verification is employed.
- **Multi-Agent Annotation (MAA) with Evidence-Scoped Roles:**
 - **Goal Grounding and Route Generation:** Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B identifies the goal landmark. Two distinct large language models, Gemma-4-31B-it and InternVL3.5-38B, independently decompose the route into motion interpretation, landmark grounding, route construction, and instruction realization, relying solely on egocentric keyframes. Each generates a structured route graph `R = ⟨(a_i, ℓ_i, s_i, o_i, m_i)⟩`, containing action, landmark, side-of-path, ordinal, and turn magnitude.
 - **Reconciliation and Verification:** The two route graphs are compared for navigation-critical attributes. Non-conflicting elements are merged, and contradictions are sent to a reviewer agent (Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B). The reviewer verifies the reconciled route against trajectory metadata, the A* route on the occupancy map, and a spatial-semantic scene graph. Per-frame semantic masks ensure evidence is restricted to visible objects. Both deterministic geometric checks and VLM reasoning are used for verification.
 - **Instruction Realization and Paraphrasing:** The verified route is converted into one fine-grained instruction. GPT-5.5 then generates three stylistic variants (Formal, Natural, Casual) from this text. Each variant is re-parsed into the route representation and accepted only if its navigation-critical attributes match the verified route.
- **Human-in-the-Loop Verification:** Three trained annotators review all generated instructions, examining egocentric video and route visualizations to verify action order, landmarks, spatial relations, and stopping conditions. Errors are corrected, and ambiguous instructions are flagged. A 20% subset undergoes independent review by a second annotator, with disagreements resolved by a third.
- **Benchmark Composition:** The final HumanoidVLN benchmark includes 933 episodes across 87 scenes. Each episode features a spatially grounded fine-grained instruction and three stylistic variants. It is used exclusively for zero-shot evaluation, meaning no evaluated model is trained or fine-tuned on HumanoidVLN.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents an evaluation of state-of-the-art VLN models on the HumanoidVLN benchmark across diverse humanoid embodiments, alongside validation of the scene reconstruction pipeline and a pilot sim-to-real study.

**A. Cross-Embodiment Evaluation (Q1 & Q2)**
Experiments evaluated four VLN models (NaVILA, StreamVLN, DualVLN, JanusVLN) in a zero-shot setting across four humanoid embodiments (Unitree G1, Unitree H1, Internal-A, Internal-B). Performance was measured using Success Rate (SR), Oracle Success Rate (OS), Navigation Error (NE), Success weighted by Path Length (SPL), Normalized Dynamic Time Warping (nDTW), and Fall Rate (FR).

- **Overall Model Performance (Q1):**
 - **JanusVLN** achieved the highest average SR (43.55%) and nDTW (48.38%), suggesting a benefit from its explicit 3D spatial representation in physics-grounded execution.
 - **DualVLN** showed strong path adherence with the second-highest average nDTW (43.39%) among the tested models.
 - **NaVILA** exhibited the largest gap between OS and SR (17.39 points), indicating less reliable final stopping decisions.
 - **StreamVLN** recorded the lowest average SR (23.63%) and nDTW (36.67%).

- **Embodiment Effects (Q2):**
 - **Unitree H1** consistently demonstrated the lowest performance across all models, with an average SR of 20.84% compared to 32–37% for other robots. This suggests that the H1's morphology (1.80m height, 10 DoF) or its specific locomotion policy presented greater navigation challenges.
 - The largest cross-robot SR differences were observed for JanusVLN (18.90 points) and DualVLN (14.98 points), highlighting the interaction between VLN models and robot morphology.
 - **Internal-A** achieved the highest average SR (36.66%), while **Unitree G1** had the highest average SPL (25.19), indicating that robot performance varies across different metrics.
 - **Fall Rate (FR)** analysis revealed significant model–embodiment interactions. Fall rates were low for Internal-A, G1, and Internal-B (2.7–10.0%) but markedly increased for Unitree H1, reaching 64.5% for StreamVLN and 71.0% for NaVILA. This suggests that SR alone does not fully capture navigation stability under physical execution, and certain robot-model combinations are more prone to physical instability. **DualVLN** achieved the lowest average Fall Rate across embodiments, potentially due to its continuous action interface.

**B. 3DGS Reconstruction Quality (Q3)**
Qualitative assessment of the 3DGS reconstruction pipeline demonstrated improvements in geometric coherence.
- Vanilla gsplat produced noisy and inconsistent surface normals, particularly in textureless areas, making it unsuitable for collision meshes in physics simulations.
- Adding depth-normal consistency improved local surface coherence but still showed artifacts in large planar regions.
- The full pipeline, combining unbiased depth rendering with depth-normal consistency, resulted in substantially smoother and more geometrically consistent surfaces while preserving scene details. This improvement directly led to higher-quality TSDF fusion results and more reliable collision meshes for Isaac Sim, enabling the use of reconstructed environments without requiring artist-authored assets.

**C. Real-World Correlation (Q4)**
A pilot sim-to-real study was conducted using DualVLN on a Unitree G1 robot in two reconstructed scenes (pantry and studio room), totaling 20 paired episodes.
- **Endpoint Error:** Per-episode navigation error showed a strong correlation between simulation and reality (Pearson r = 0.935; Spearman ρ = 0.911). The mean absolute navigation error difference was 0.68 m, with a mean signed difference of 0.04 m, indicating minimal systematic endpoint bias.
- **Trajectory Similarity:** Paired trajectory similarity averaged 78.2 ± 18.8 nDTW, with scene-level means of 0.803 in the studio and 0.761 in the pantry.
- These results indicate that the reconstructed scenes largely preserve both navigation difficulty and executed trajectory structure, suggesting that agent behavior in simulation can be consistent with real-world performance. The study acknowledges its limited scope to two scenes and one VLN checkpoint.

**D. Dataset Statistics**
The benchmark comprises 933 episodes across 87 scenes. Paths have a median length of 9.97 m, and instructions contain a median of 50 words. A modest path–instruction correlation (r = 0.29) indicates that instruction length is not solely determined by trajectory length. The 87 scenes span 17 indoor classes across six application domains, with a median navigable area of 266 m².

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

HumanoidVLN addresses several critical limitations in the field of Vision-Language Navigation, particularly concerning the deployment of VLN models on physical humanoid robots. The work contributes to the advancement of embodied AI by providing a specialized, physics-grounded framework.

**Significance:**
- **Physics-Grounded Humanoid Simulation:** HumanoidVLN introduces a simulator and benchmark that explicitly accounts for the complex physical dynamics of bipedal locomotion and the morphological diversity of humanoid robots. This contrasts with many existing VLN benchmarks that use simplified kinematic models, thus enabling more realistic evaluation of VLN agents in scenarios relevant to real-world robot deployment.
- **Embodiment Diversity:** By supporting multiple distinct humanoid configurations with tailored hierarchical control stacks, the benchmark facilitates studies into how robot morphology and control strategies influence VLN performance and stability. This allows for a deeper understanding of the interaction between VLN models, controllers, and physical embodiments.
- **Navigability-Curated Environments:** The pipeline for generating navigability-curated scenes from artist-designed assets and 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstructions ensures that environments are physically traversable for bipedal humanoids. This addresses a common issue in other benchmarks where environments may contain areas infeasible for physical robots.
- **Realistic and Grounded Instructions:** The Multi-Agent Annotation (MAA) framework, combined with human-in-the-loop verification and egocentric video capture, generates instructions that are spatially grounded, realistic, and reflect the perceptual challenges (e.g., camera shake) faced by actual humanoids. This improves the fidelity of language guidance.
- **Systematic Performance Evaluation:** The benchmark provides a standardized platform for zero-shot evaluation of VLN models, including metrics like Fall Rate, which are crucial for assessing physical stability in addition to traditional navigation accuracy. This highlights the limitations of current models in physically complex scenarios and across different robot embodiments.
- **Sim-to-Real Validation:** The pilot sim-to-real study provides initial evidence that the reconstructed scenes and simulated robot behaviors align with real-world performance, indicating the potential for effective transfer of learned policies.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Advancement in Humanoid VLN:** HumanoidVLN can serve as a foundational tool for research into more robust and physically aware VLN models specifically designed for humanoid robots. It will drive the development of agents capable of navigating complex, real-world environments while respecting physical constraints.
- **Improved Robot Design and Control:** Insights gained from cross-embodiment evaluations can inform the design of future humanoid robots and their control systems, helping to identify morphological features or control strategies that enhance navigation performance and stability.
- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** By providing high-fidelity, physics-grounded simulations with validated real-world correlations, the benchmark contributes to narrowing the notorious sim-to-real gap, making it more feasible to develop and test robot learning algorithms in simulation before deployment on physical hardware.
- **Standardization of Evaluation:** HumanoidVLN offers a new standard for evaluating VLN models in realistic humanoid contexts, promoting rigorous comparison and progress in the field.
- **Development of Novel AI Capabilities:** The detailed data and complex challenges presented by HumanoidVLN could spur the development of new AI architectures and learning paradigms that are inherently capable of understanding and interacting with the physical world, moving beyond purely semantic or kinematic interpretations.
