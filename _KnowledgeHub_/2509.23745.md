# LocoFormer: Generalist Locomotion via Long-context Adaptation

> [!summary] Summary
> LocoFormer, developed by Skild AI, presents a generalist locomotion model capable of controlling diverse, previously unseen legged and wheeled robots by leveraging long-context adaptation. It achieved an average normalized displacement of 0.96 on 10 unseen robots in simulation and demonstrated adaptive behaviors to severe real-world disturbances such as limb damage and locked wheels.

## Problem

- Current robot locomotion controllers are highly specialized, requiring extensive retraining or fine-tuning for new robot morphologies or minor variations.
- Robots exhibit brittleness, failing catastrophically when encountering unexpected disturbances like mechanical failures, limb damage, or changes in payload.
- Traditional sim-to-real transfer is laborious, demanding meticulous system identification and parameter tuning for each specific robot.

## Method

- Pre-training with massive-scale ==reinforcement learning== on procedurally generated robots, incorporating aggressive domain randomization to create a vast distribution of MDPs.
- Utilizing a ==Transformer==-XL architecture to significantly extend the policy's context length, enabling the policy to retain memory across multiple trials and episodes.
- Defining a unified observation, action, and reward space to allow a single policy to control diverse robot morphologies.

## Results

- Achieved an average normalized displacement of 0.96 on 10 unseen robot morphologies in simulation, comparable to 0.99 for expert policies, with few-shot adaptation improving it to 0.98.
- Demonstrated zero-shot real-world adaptation on Unitree robots to severe, unseen disturbances including locked limbs, wheel failures, and added payloads.
- Successfully learned to walk on stilts and locomote on its knees after the lower legs were removed, adapting to significant morphological alterations beyond its training data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Long-context memory provided by the Transformer-XL architecture is essential for cross-embodiment generalization and adaptation in diverse robotic tasks.
> - The model implicitly learns to form stable, robot-specific internal representations and adaptation strategies online, without requiring explicit morphological inputs.
> - Multi-episodic adaptation, allowing the policy to learn from past failures over extended periods, is a powerful mechanism for developing robust and intelligent robotic behaviors.
