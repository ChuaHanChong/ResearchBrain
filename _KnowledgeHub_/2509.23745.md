# LocoFormer: Generalist Locomotion via Long-context Adaptation

> [!summary] Summary
> LocoFormer, developed by Skild AI, presents a generalist locomotion model capable of controlling diverse, previously unseen legged and wheeled robots by leveraging long-context adaptation. It achieved an average normalized displacement of 0.96 on 10 unseen robots in simulation and demonstrated adaptive behaviors to severe real-world disturbances such as limb damage and locked wheels.

## Problem

- Current robot locomotion controllers are highly specialized, requiring extensive retraining or fine-tuning for new robot morphologies or minor variations.
- Robots exhibit brittleness, failing catastrophically when encountering unexpected disturbances like mechanical failures, limb damage, or changes in payload.
- Traditional sim-to-real transfer is laborious, demanding meticulous system identification and parameter tuning for each specific robot.

## Method

- Pre-training with massive-scale ==reinforcement learning== on procedurally generated robots, incorporating aggressive domain randomization to create a vast distribution of MDPs.
- Utilizing a ==Transformer==-XL architecture to significantly extend the policy's context length, enabling the policy to retain memory across multiple trials and episodes.
- Defining a unified observation, action, and reward space to allow a single policy to control diverse robot morphologies.

## Results

- Achieved an average normalized displacement of 0.96 on 10 unseen robot morphologies in simulation, comparable to 0.99 for expert policies, with few-shot adaptation improving it to 0.98.
- Demonstrated zero-shot real-world adaptation on Unitree robots to severe, unseen disturbances including locked limbs, wheel failures, and added payloads.
- Successfully learned to walk on stilts and locomote on its knees after the lower legs were removed, adapting to significant morphological alterations beyond its training data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Long-context memory provided by the Transformer-XL architecture is essential for cross-embodiment generalization and adaptation in diverse robotic tasks.
> - The model implicitly learns to form stable, robot-specific internal representations and adaptation strategies online, without requiring explicit morphological inputs.
> - Multi-episodic adaptation, allowing the policy to learn from past failures over extended periods, is a powerful mechanism for developing robust and intelligent robotic behaviors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic locomotion has made significant strides, particularly with the advent of sim-to-real transfer paradigms using reinforcement learning (RL). Contemporary methods have enabled robots to achieve impressive agility and robustness on specific terrains or for particular embodiments, ranging from quadrupeds (e.g., Unitree Go1, Boston Dynamics Spot) to humanoids. These specialist controllers, however, typically rely on extensive manual tuning for their designated platform and operate within narrow distributions of morphological and dynamic parameters. While some policies incorporate domain randomization and short-context adaptation (on the order of hundreds of milliseconds), they often fail catastrophically when faced with substantial morphological changes, severe disturbances, or entirely novel robot bodies. This brittleness necessitates painstaking system identification for each new robot and limits the versatility of deployed solutions.

"LocoFormer" directly addresses these limitations by proposing a *generalist, omni-bodied* locomotion model. It positions itself as a significant departure from specialist controllers, drawing inspiration from the success of large language models (LLMs) which achieve in-context adaptation through web-scale pre-training and long-context processing. The paper explicitly contrasts its approach with prior work that is "myopic with short context lengths," arguing that such limited history prevents effective adaptation across a wide task distribution.

This work contributes to several emerging research themes:
- **Foundation Models for Robotics:** By aiming for a single policy that generalizes across diverse embodiments, LocoFormer aligns with the vision of creating generalist AI agents capable of multiple skills on various hardware platforms, akin to general-purpose models in language and vision.
- **Cross-Embodiment Learning:** Unlike methods that condition policies on explicit kinematic information or rely on graph neural networks to encode morphology, LocoFormer seeks to implicitly learn adaptation strategies from raw experience. It extends previous work on generalized controllers for specific robot types (e.g., quadrupeds) to a much broader and more diverse set of morphologies, including bipeds, quadrupeds, and their wheeled variants.
- **Long-Context Reinforcement Learning:** The core innovation of extending context length by orders of magnitude, even spanning episode boundaries, places this work in the frontier of exploring how long-term memory and sequential reasoning can enable more sophisticated adaptive behaviors in RL, paralleling findings in meta-RL and in-context learning.
- **Robustness and Resiliency:** By demonstrating adaptation to severe real-world disturbances like limb damage, locked wheels, and added payloads, LocoFormer pushes the boundaries of how resilient robots can be in unpredicted, adverse situations, a critical requirement for real-world deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a **generalist omni-bodied locomotion model, named LocoFormer, that can robustly control previously unseen legged and wheeled robots and adapt to significant changes in their morphology and dynamics at test time.**

The motivation for this ambitious goal stems from several critical challenges in current robotic locomotion:
- **Lack of Generalization:** Existing controllers are highly specialized, requiring re-training or extensive fine-tuning for each new robot, even those with minor variations. This severely limits their scalability and practical utility.
- **Brittleness to Disturbances:** Robots often fail catastrophically when faced with unexpected events like motor failures, broken limbs, or changes in payload. Biological systems, in contrast, demonstrate remarkable adaptability to injury or growth.
- **Cumbersome Sim2Real Transfer:** The process of transferring policies from simulation to the real world often involves painstaking system identification and parameter tuning, which is time-consuming and prone to errors. A policy that can adapt to out-of-distribution (OOD) dynamics at test time would significantly streamline this process.
- **Inefficiency of Short-Context Adaptation:** Previous adaptive policies with short memory windows (hundreds of milliseconds) struggle to learn complex, long-term adaptation strategies necessary for highly diverse tasks and morphology changes.
- **Towards Foundation Models:** The authors are motivated by the vision of creating "foundation models" for robotics, where a single, broadly trained model can acquire and apply a wide range of skills, reducing the need for task-specific engineering.

By achieving these objectives, LocoFormer aims to pave the way for more autonomous, resilient, and versatile robotic systems that can operate effectively in dynamic and unpredictable real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LocoFormer's methodology hinges on two fundamental design choices: massive-scale reinforcement learning on procedurally generated robots with aggressive domain randomization, and extending context length significantly using a Transformer-XL architecture.

#### 3.1. Pre-training to Learn in Context

- **Formulation for In-Context Learning:** The policy is trained to maximize expected cumulative reward across episodes, where each episode consists of multiple "trials." Crucially, memory persists across these trials, allowing the policy to learn from prior experiences (e.g., falls) within the same episode to improve subsequent trials. This contrasts with standard MDPs where each trial is independent. The objective maximizes return over *k* trials within an episode, where *k* is sampled.
- **Task Generation:** A large space of diverse robot morphologies is procedurally generated in simulation. This includes bipeds, quadrupeds, and their wheeled counterparts. Beyond basic morphologies, aggressive randomization is applied to physical parameters such as inertia, control gains, and joint limits. This creates a vast distribution of MDPs, forcing the policy to learn general adaptation strategies rather than specialized solutions.
- **Unified Observation, Action, and Reward Space:** To control varied robots with a single policy, a unified joint space is defined as a superset of joints found in most contemporary legged robots. The policy outputs target joint positions in this unified space, which are then mapped to the specific robot's active joints. Rewards combine morphology-agnostic terms (e.g., linear/angular velocity command tracking, base height penalties) with morphology-specific elements, encouraging efficient locomotion while discouraging self-damage.
- **Large-Scale RL in Simulation:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is used for training. To emphasize adaptation, the formulation includes an "adaptation time budget" (*u* seconds) within an episode, allowing an arbitrary number of trials within this time. Training proceeds in two phases: an initial phase with shorter trial durations and adaptation times to acquire adaptive behaviors, followed by extended durations to prepare for real-world deployment.

#### 3.2. Policy Architecture with Long-Term Memory

- **Transformer-XL (TXL) Backbone:** High-frequency control tasks generate extensive histories. A vanilla Transformer's quadratic complexity with sequence length would be prohibitive. LocoFormer employs the Transformer-XL architecture, known for its segment-level recurrence mechanism. TXL allows the current segment to attend to hidden states from previous (cached) segments without backpropagating gradients through them, thereby enabling long-range dependencies while managing computational cost. This results in an effective context length that scales as O(N * L), where N is the number of layers and L is the segment length (e.g., 18 seconds of memory at 50 Hz control frequency with 6 layers and 128 segment length). Attention masks are used to prevent memory from spanning episode boundaries when not intended.
- **Inference Acceleration:** To make real-time deployment feasible, the KV-cache mechanism is utilized during inference. This reduces the computational complexity from quadratic to linear with respect to the timestep *t*, by only recomputing attention for the current token and reusing cached keys and values from previous timesteps. The KV-cache maintains the most recent 2*L - 1 timesteps, matching the maximum history length seen during training.
- **Observation Encoding and Action Decoding:** Observations at each timestep are first encoded into an embedding via an MLP. These embeddings are fed into the TXL, whose outputs are then decoded into actions and value estimates by separate actor and critic MLP heads.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

LocoFormer demonstrates remarkable performance in both extensive simulation evaluations and challenging real-world deployments.

#### 4.1. Simulation Evaluation

- **Performance on Unseen Robots:** LocoFormer was evaluated on 10 previously unseen real-world robots (including various bipeds, quadrupeds, and wheeled robots like Unitree G1, H1, A1, Boston Dynamics Spot, etc.) across 1,000 randomized simulation environments with rough terrains.
 - **Zero-shot:** LocoFormer achieved an average normalized displacement of 0.96 (out of 1.0), performing competitively with expert policies (average 0.99) trained specifically for each robot.
 - **Few-shot:** With a brief 5-second adaptation window, performance further improved to an average of 0.98, demonstrating the policy's ability to refine its strategy from initial attempts. For instance, on the TRON1 biped, few-shot adaptation yielded a 10% performance gain.
 - **Baselines Comparison:** LocoFormer significantly outperformed a GRU-based policy (average 0.37) and a "Conditioning" Transformer policy (average 0.78) that received kinematic information but had limited context, highlighting the crucial role of both the Transformer architecture and long-term memory for cross-embodiment generalization.
- **Adaptation Performance:** Under intensified domain randomization (double the training range), LocoFormer showed continuous improvement across multiple seconds of interaction (at least two orders of magnitude longer than prior work). Longer adaptation times consistently led to better survival rates and performance across all morphology types.
- **Representation Dynamics:** Analysis of the policy's internal representations (average output of a Transformer layer) revealed that while embeddings were initially similar across different humanoid variants, they progressively diverged over time, forming distinct clusters by 5 seconds. This indicates that LocoFormer dynamically builds stable, embodiment-specific internal models online without explicit morphological inputs.

#### 4.2. Real-World Evaluation

LocoFormer was deployed zero-shot on various Unitree robots (G1, H1, Go2, Go2-W), including novel bipedal configurations of Go2, without any tuning or retraining. The experiments demonstrated emergent adaptive behaviors to severe, unforeseen disturbances:

- **Leg Locking (A, E):** When a knee joint on a Go2 quadruped was locked, effectively converting it into a three-legged robot, LocoFormer initially became unstable but adapted within 2-3 seconds to shift its weight and achieve stable three-legged locomotion, an unseen scenario during training. Similar adaptation occurred with one or two legs locked on a wheeled quadruped.
- **Adaptation Across Trials (B):** For highly unstable scenarios (e.g., bipedal Go2 with no ankle motor and single point of support), LocoFormer failed in initial trials. However, by retaining memory of failed trajectories, it progressively improved in subsequent trials, achieving stable bipedal walking by the third attempt and robustness to pushes and weights.
- **Wheel Locking and Payload Change (C):** When the wheels of a Go2-W were programmatically locked, LocoFormer detected the change and quickly switched from a rolling gait to a walking gait. When wheels were unlocked, it reverted to rolling. Similarly, it adjusted its gait to maintain balance when external mass was added.
- **Walking on Stilts (D):** With stilts attached, significantly increasing effective leg length and altering kinematics/dynamics beyond training data, LocoFormer adapted its coordination (step timing, foot placement) within a few unstable steps to walk forward stably.
- **Cutting Lower Legs (F):** In an extreme test, the calf of the robot was cut, removing 4 degrees of freedom and shortening the limbs. LocoFormer initially struggled but, after 7-8 seconds of adaptation, discovered that large amplitude thigh joint swings were required and successfully resumed locomotion on its knees.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

LocoFormer represents a significant advancement towards generalist robot locomotion and holds several key implications for the future of robotics:

- **Towards Foundation Models for Robotics:** The work demonstrates a viable path toward creating "foundation models" for low-level robotic skills. By pre-training a single model on a vast distribution of procedurally generated robots and enabling long-context adaptation, LocoFormer shows that generalist policies can emerge, reducing the need for specialized controllers and accelerating robot development.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Resilience:** The ability of LocoFormer to adapt to severe, unforeseen morphological changes (e.g., limb damage, stilts) and dynamic perturbations (e.g., locked wheels, added mass) makes robots significantly more robust and resilient in real-world scenarios. This is crucial for deployment in unstructured, unpredictable, or hazardous environments where failures can be costly.
- **Streamlined Sim2Real Transfer:** LocoFormer's capacity to handle aggressive domain randomization during training and adapt to OOD dynamics at test time suggests a more robust sim2real pipeline. It can potentially reduce the arduous process of system identification and parameter tuning, making it easier to deploy new robots.
- **Reduced Engineering Effort:** A generalist policy that can zero-shot transfer to new robot bodies implies a substantial reduction in the engineering effort and development time currently spent on designing and tuning controllers for each specific robot.
- **New Paradigm for Adaptive Control:** The emphasis on long-context, multi-episodic adaptation introduces a powerful paradigm for robot learning. This ability to "learn from mistakes" over extended periods (human-timescales) is a hallmark of intelligent behavior and could be crucial for acquiring complex skills.
- **Applicability to Other Robotic Skills:** The authors explicitly state that this "simple, yet general recipe" could be used to train generalist controllers for other low-level robotic skills beyond locomotion, especially where large-scale data can be generated in simulation. This opens avenues for broader applications in manipulation, navigation, and other domains.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as the resource-intensive training and the reliance on hand-crafted procedural generation, LocoFormer undeniably pushes the boundaries of robotic adaptability and generalization, offering a compelling glimpse into a future of more versatile and intelligent autonomous systems.
