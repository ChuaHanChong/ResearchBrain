# Unpacking Failure Modes of Generative Policies: Runtime Monitoring of Consistency and Progress

> [!summary] Summary
> Sentinel, a runtime monitoring framework developed by Stanford University researchers, detects unknown failures of generative robot policies by combining a statistical temporal action consistency (STAC) detector for erratic behavior with a Vision Language Model (VLM) for task progression assessment. This framework identifies 18% more failures than individual detectors, demonstrating high accuracy in both simulated and real-world mobile manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Generative robot policies, trained via imitation learning, are prone to unpredictable failures when operating in out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios, posing a barrier to scalable and safe robot deployment.
- Existing OOD detection or uncertainty quantification methods are often insufficient for the multimodal action distributions and closed-loop, temporal nature of generative policy failures.
- The diverse failure modes of generative policies, ranging from rapid erratic actions to slow, incorrect task progression, require a comprehensive detection strategy that single metrics cannot provide.

## Method

![[2410.04640-method.png]]
*Sentinel, a runtime monitor for generative robot policies: calibrated on successful rollouts, it flags unknown deployment failures via (a) statistical temporal action consistency (STAC) and (b) online VLM video QA.*

- Introduces **Sentinel**, a runtime monitoring framework that employs a ==divide and conquer== strategy by running two complementary detectors in parallel: ==STAC== for erratic failures and a VLM for task progression failures.
- The **STAC** (==Statistical Temporal Action Consistency==) detector quantifies erratic policy behavior by measuring the statistical consistency of the generative policy's action distributions over time using ==Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD)== or ==KL-divergence==, calibrated solely on successful policy rollouts.
- A ==Vision Language Model (VLM)== is used in a zero-shot, ==Chain-of-Thought Video Question Answering== setup to assess high-level task progress over longer durations, identifying failures where the policy consistently takes correct but ultimately non-task-completing actions.

## Results

- **Sentinel** achieved high overall balanced accuracy across all tested domains (e.g., **91%** in Close Box simulation, **95%** in real-world Push Chair) and detected **18%** more failures than using either detector alone.
- STAC demonstrated high accuracy and fast detection for erratic failures (e.g., **>90%** balanced accuracy in PushT, **96%** in Close Box), outperforming various baselines in temporal consistency detection.
- VLMs effectively identified task progression failures where STAC struggled (e.g., **85%** True Positive Rate for Claude 3.5 in Cover Object), with VLM performance significantly boosted by video context and prompt ensembling.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A "divide and conquer" strategy with specialized, complementary detectors (fast for erratic, slower for task progression) is crucial for robustly monitoring the diverse failure modes of generative robot policies.
> - Statistical distances (like MMD or KL-divergence) applied to action distributions are essential for effectively detecting erratic behavior in multimodal generative policies, outperforming non-distributional baselines.
> - Vision Language Models can provide valuable high-level, contextual reasoning for task progression failures in a zero-shot manner, particularly when provided with video context rather than single images.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in robot learning, particularly through imitation learning and generative modeling, have enabled robots to acquire complex behaviors from human demonstrations. Generative policies, exemplified by methods like Diffusion Policy, can learn from diverse, multimodal datasets and exhibit promising generalization capabilities. However, a significant bottleneck for their scalable deployment in real-world, uncontrolled environments is their inherent fragility when encountering scenarios that deviate from their training data (out-of-distribution, OOD scenarios). This unpredictability can lead to unpredictable failures, safety hazards, and a lack of trustworthiness, thus limiting the widespread adoption of learned robot policies.

The broader research landscape in robot learning is increasingly focused on robustness, generalization, and safety. Existing approaches to address OOD conditions include:
- **OOD Detection:** Methods that quantify dissimilarity from training data through reconstruction errors or embedding similarities. However, these often provide a per-sample signal and may not fully characterize closed-loop policy failures arising from time-correlated prediction errors or account for action multimodality.
- **Uncertainty Quantification:** Techniques that estimate epistemic uncertainty in model predictions. While valuable, these can be computationally expensive or ill-suited for the iterative nature of generative models like diffusion policies.
- **Post-hoc Correction:** Approaches that retrain policies on OOD states with human supervision after failures have occurred. While important, these do not prevent failures in real-time.
- **Runtime Monitoring:** Algorithms that actively monitor policy behavior during deployment to detect failures. Previous works have explored consistency checks across sensor modalities or time, but often assume known failure modes or specific sensor setups.

This paper addresses a critical gap by proposing a runtime monitoring framework specifically designed for *generative robot policies* and their *diverse failure modes*. Unlike prior work, it explicitly considers:
- **Multimodal Action Spaces:** Generative policies can produce highly varied actions, making traditional single-point comparisons difficult. The paper focuses on comparing *action distributions* over time.
- **Closed-Loop Policy Failures:** Failures in robotics are often temporal and compounding, requiring detection methods that reason over trajectories, not just individual states or actions.
- **Zero-Shot Detection of Unknown Failures:** The framework is designed to detect novel failure types without requiring labeled failure data, relying only on successful demonstrations and task descriptions for calibration.
- **Complementary Failure Categories:** It introduces a novel taxonomy of failures (erratic vs. task progression) and assigns specialized detectors, acknowledging that no single metric can capture all failure types effectively.
- **Integration of Vision Language Models (VLMs):** Leveraging the powerful contextual reasoning capabilities of state-of-the-art VLMs for high-level task progress assessment, a relatively new and rapidly developing area in robotics.

By tackling these challenges, this work contributes directly to making robot policies more reliable and deployable, moving towards the vision of generalist and robust robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a **runtime monitoring framework, named Sentinel**, capable of detecting unknown failures of generative robot policies during deployment.

The motivation for this objective stems from several key challenges in current robot learning paradigms:
- **Vulnerability of Learned Policies:** Robot policies, particularly those trained via imitation learning, are prone to unpredictable failures when operating in conditions that deviate from their training data (out-of-distribution scenarios). This fragility is a major barrier to scalable and safe robot deployment.
- **Complexity of Generative Policies:** Modern generative policies, such as diffusion policies, learn from heterogeneous and multimodal demonstration data, enabling them to produce diverse robot actions. However, this inherent multimodality leads to more stochastic and complex runtime behaviors, making their failure modes more diverse and harder to characterize with traditional monitoring techniques.
- **Inadequacy of Existing Detection Methods:** Current OOD detection or uncertainty quantification methods often provide only an instantaneous signal, failing to capture the temporal, closed-loop nature of policy failures in robotics. They may also not be suitable for the computational characteristics of generative models or the diversity of their failure modes.
- **Need for Early Warning Systems:** To mitigate the consequences of robot failures (e.g., collisions, damage, task incompletion, safety hazards), real-time detection and early warnings are crucial, complementing post-hoc correction or retraining strategies.

To address these motivations, Sentinel's specific objectives are:
1. **Formulate a failure detection paradigm for generative policies** that accounts for their unique characteristics, particularly their multimodal action distributions and closed-loop operation.
2. **Propose a novel temporal consistency detector (STAC)** to identify "erratic failures" – situations where the policy exhibits unstable or conflicting action distributions over time, requiring fast detection.
3. **Propose the use of Vision Language Models (VLMs)** for "task progression failures" – scenarios where the policy consistently takes actions that do not lead to task completion, requiring a broader contextual understanding over longer durations.
4. **Demonstrate the synergistic benefit of combining these complementary detectors** to achieve higher overall accuracy and robustness in detecting diverse failure modes in both simulated and real-world robotic mobile manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Sentinel operates as a runtime monitoring framework that employs a "divide and conquer" strategy by categorizing generative policy failures into two complementary types: erratic failures and task progression failures, each addressed by a specialized detector.

**Overall Framework (Sentinel):**
Sentinel runs two detectors in parallel:
1. **Statistical Temporal Action Consistency (STAC) Detector:** Designed for rapid detection of erratic failures.
2. **Vision Language Model (VLM) Detector:** Designed for slower, contextual reasoning to detect task progression failures.
The system issues a "failure" warning if either detector flags an issue. This parallel operation leverages the strengths of each detector, especially their differing response times.

#### 3.1. Statistical Temporal Action Consistency (STAC) for Erratic Failures

- **Motivation:** Generative policies, while capable of producing diverse actions, should ideally exhibit temporal consistency in nominal operations. Erratic behavior, like jittering between conflicting action modes or sudden policy changes, indicates a failure. Such failures often require immediate detection due to potential safety hazards.
- **Mechanism:** STAC quantifies erratic policy behavior by measuring the statistical consistency of the generative policy's action distributions over time.
 - **Action Sequence Prediction:** The generative policy (e.g., a diffusion policy) predicts a sequence of actions (an "action chunk") for `h` future timesteps. After executing the first `k < h` actions, the policy is re-evaluated. This creates an overlap of `h-k` predicted actions between consecutive policy inference steps `t` and `t+k`.
 - **Statistical Distance:** STAC compares the marginal action distribution `π(a_{t+k:t+h-1|t} | s_t)` (actions predicted at time `t` for `t+k` to `t+h-1`) with `π(a_{t+k:t+h-1|t+k} | s_{t+k})` (actions predicted at time `t+k` for the same window). Since analytical computation is hard for diffusion policies, it uses empirical statistical distance functions, such as:
 - **Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) with RBF kernels:** Measures the distance between the mean embeddings of two distributions in a Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space.
 - **KL-divergence via Kernel Density Estimation (KDE):** Approximates the KL-divergence between the two action distributions by fitting KDEs on sampled action sequences.
 - **Cumulative Score:** A temporal consistency score `η_t` is computed as the cumulative sum of these statistical distances over the policy rollout up to timestep `t`. This captures both sudden large deviations and consistent small deviations (jitter).
 - **Calibration:** A failure detection threshold `γ` is calibrated using a small dataset of *successful* policy rollouts (`D_τ`). `γ` is set to a high quantile (e.g., 95th percentile) of the terminal cumulative scores from these successful trajectories. This ensures a low False Positive Rate (FPR), providing a statistical guarantee (via conformal prediction) that false alarms are unlikely on nominal successful trajectories.
- **Computational Cost:** STAC is designed to be computationally light, sampling batches of actions in parallel on a GPU, making it suitable for fast, real-time detection.

#### 3.2. Vision Language Models (VLMs) for Task Progression Failures

- **Motivation:** Some failures, like confidently placing an object in the wrong location or stalling, involve temporally consistent but ultimately incorrect behavior. Detecting these "task progression failures" requires a higher-level understanding of the task goals and the robot's progress, which is challenging for low-level action consistency metrics. Such failures are less time-sensitive than erratic ones.
- **Mechanism:** Sentinel leverages the contextual reasoning abilities of high-capacity VLMs.
 - **Video Question Answering (Video QA):** The detection is formulated as a Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Video QA task. The VLM is provided with:
 - A natural language description of the task.
 - The robot's history of observed images (as a video) up to the current timestep `t`.
 - Information about elapsed time and task time limit to gauge progress rate.
 - **CoT Prompting:** The prompt instructs the VLM to perform a multi-step reasoning process: generate task-relevant questions, answer them with fine-grained visual details, analyze progress based on answers and time, and finally, output an "ok" or "failure" classification. This encourages robust reasoning.
 - **Zero-Shot:** The VLM is used in a zero-shot manner, meaning it does not require fine-tuning on robot failure data. It relies on its pre-trained knowledge to understand robot tasks and identify off-nominal behavior.
 - **Prompt Ensembling (Optional):** In some visually challenging domains (e.g., simulation), multiple prompt variants (e.g., including a successful reference video or goal images) are used, and predictions are combined via majority voting to increase reliability.
- **Computational Cost:** VLM inference, especially with state-of-the-art models, can incur significant latency (e.g., ~14s for GPT-4o). This makes VLMs less suitable for rapid erratic failure detection but acceptable for task progression failures that unfold over longer timescales. The VLM is typically queried at a lower frequency than STAC.

**Calibration and Combination Strategy:**
Sentinel's strength lies in assigning specialized detectors to complementary failure categories. STAC handles the "fast" erratic failures, leveraging its statistical rigor and low computational cost. The VLM handles the "slow" task progression failures, utilizing its high-level reasoning capabilities. The final failure signal is the logical OR of both detectors' outputs, meaning a failure is flagged if either detector raises a warning. This strategy ensures broad coverage of diverse failure modes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate Sentinel's efficacy in detecting unknown failures of generative robot policies across diverse mobile manipulation domains in both simulation and the real world.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Environments:**
 - **Simulation:** PushT (planar pushing, high action multimodality), Close Box (bimanual mobile manipulation, erratic failures from object scaling), Cover Object (bimanual, task progression failures from object pose shift).
 - **Real-World:** Push Chair (mobile manipulator, nonprehensile pushing, dynamics shift).
- **Generative Policies:** Diffusion policies were trained for each task.
- **OOD Scenarios:** Generated by randomizing object scale, dimensions, or pose beyond training data.
- **Baselines:** A comprehensive suite including embedding similarity (Policy Encoder, CLIP, ResNet), reconstruction error (Diffusion Reconstruction), DDPM loss, diffusion output variance, and temporal non-distributional minimum, with temporal variants where applicable.
- **Calibration:** All methods were calibrated using only a small set (M=50 for sim, M=10 for real) of *successful* in-distribution policy rollouts.
- **Metrics:** True Positive Rate (TPR), True Negative Rate (TNR), Mean Detection Time (DT), Accuracy, and Balanced Accuracy.

#### 4.2. STAC Performance (Erratic Failures)

- **High Accuracy and Low FPR:** STAC consistently achieved high balanced accuracy, particularly for erratic failures. For example, in the PushT domain (high multimodality), STAC (MMD) was the only method to exceed 90% balanced accuracy. In Close Box, it attained 96% accuracy (Table 1), effectively differentiating OOD successes from failures.
- **Outperforms Baselines:** STAC significantly outperformed baselines across various categories (embedding similarity, reconstruction, output variance). Embedding-based methods often conflated OOD states with policy failure, leading to high false positives.
- **Value of Statistical Distances:** Ablations showed that using statistical distance functions (MMD, KL-divergence) was crucial for STAC's performance. Non-statistical distance measures (Temporal Non-Distributional Minimum) performed poorly due to the inherent stochastic multimodality of generative policies (Fig. 5).
- **Fast Detection:** STAC achieved much faster detection times than VLM-based methods (e.g., 5.47s for Sentinel vs. 21.20s for GPT-4o Video QA in Close Box, Table 1), highlighting its suitability for urgent erratic failures.
- **Robustness to Prediction Horizon:** STAC's performance was robust to variations in the policy's prediction horizon (h), while showing some sensitivity to very small execution horizons (k) (Fig. 8, Table 4).

#### 4.3. VLM Performance (Task Progression Failures)

- **Necessity of Video Context:** VLMs performed poorly (low TNR) when provided with only a single image (Image QA), struggling to differentiate progress from failure. Video QA, which allowed reasoning across time, drastically improved TNR (e.g., 91% for GPT-4o Video QA in Close Box, Table 1).
- **Complementary Role:** When STAC struggled to detect task progression failures (e.g., 12% TPR in Cover Object where failures were smooth but incorrect), the VLM (e.g., Claude 3.5 Sonnet Prompt Ensemble with 85% TPR) effectively captured them (Fig. 6, Table 6).
- **Slower Detection Time:** As expected, VLM detection times were significantly slower than STAC, aligning with their role in less time-sensitive task progression failures.
- **Model and Prompt Sensitivity:** VLM performance varied across models (GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Gemini 1.5 Pro) and domains. Prompt ensembling techniques (e.g., including successful example videos or goal images) significantly boosted VLM reliability and reduced false positives, particularly in challenging simulation environments (Table 6).

#### 4.4. Sentinel's Combined Efficacy

- **Increased Failure Detection:** By combining STAC and the VLM, Sentinel detected 18% more failures than using either detector alone. It achieved 100% detection of erratic failures in Close Box (Table 1) and significantly boosted detection for task progression failures (Fig. 6).
- **High Overall Accuracy:** Sentinel consistently achieved high balanced accuracy across all domains. For example, in Close Box, Sentinel (STAC MMD + GPT-4o Video QA) achieved 91% accuracy (Table 1), and in Cover Object, Sentinel (STAC MMD + Claude 3.5 Sonnet Prompt Ensemble) reached 88% accuracy (Table 6).
- **Acceptable FPR:** Despite combining detectors via logical OR, Sentinel maintained an acceptable FPR. For instance, in detecting task progression failures, the combination led to only a 7% increase in FPR while significantly boosting TPR (Fig. 6).

#### 4.5. Real-World Validation

- **Strong Real-World Performance:** Sentinel demonstrated strong performance on the real-world Push Chair task. Calibrated with only 10 successful rollouts, STAC achieved 80% TPR and 90% TNR. The VLM (GPT-4o Video QA) performed even better (90% TPR, 100% TNR), possibly due to a smaller domain gap for visual reasoning in real-world images. Overall, Sentinel achieved a 95% detection accuracy, highlighting its practical applicability (Table 2).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Sentinel framework represents a significant advancement in the field of robust robot learning and deployment, with several profound implications:

- **Enabling Scalable and Safe Robot Deployment:** By providing a robust, real-time mechanism for detecting unknown policy failures at deployment time, Sentinel directly addresses a critical barrier to the widespread use of learned robot behaviors in uncontrolled, real-world environments. This increases trustworthiness and paves the way for more autonomous and capable robotic systems.
- **Specialized Detection for Generative Policies:** The work explicitly accounts for the unique characteristics of modern generative policies, particularly their multimodal action distributions and closed-loop behavior. By introducing a complementary two-pronged detection strategy, it demonstrates that a "one-size-fits-all" approach is insufficient for the diverse and complex failure modes exhibited by these powerful policies.
- **Leveraging Foundational Models for Robot Safety:** The effective integration of Vision Language Models (VLMs) for high-level task progression monitoring highlights the growing utility of large foundational models in enhancing robotic system intelligence and safety, beyond their typical application in planning or high-level instruction. This zero-shot approach to VLM monitoring is particularly impactful as it does not require extensive failure data for training.
- **Improved System-Level Performance:** The empirical results conclusively show that combining specialized, complementary detectors (fast STAC for erratic behavior and slower VLM for task progression) leads to a superior overall failure detection system compared to individual detectors or various baselines. This "divide and conquer" strategy offers a blueprint for designing more comprehensive monitoring systems.
- **Practical Calibration:** The reliance on only successful policy rollouts for calibration (and a conformal prediction guarantee for STAC's low FPR) is highly practical, as collecting diverse failure data is often challenging and costly in real-world robotics.
- **Future Directions for Robot Learning:** This work opens several avenues for future research, including:
 - **Monitoring High-Capacity Policies:** Applying Sentinel to monitor even more complex and generalist policies, such as those arising from large-scale datasets.
 - **Informing Data Collection:** Using detected failures to intelligently guide data collection for policy improvement or adaptation.
 - **Accelerating Policy Learning:** Integrating failure detection signals to enable faster and more robust policy learning through informed exploration or targeted correction.

**Limitations Acknowledged by Authors:**
The authors candidly discuss limitations, which are crucial for guiding future work:
- **Non-Exhaustive Failure Categories:** The proposed categories (erratic and task progression) might not cover all possible failure modes. Expanding or refining these categories could offer broader coverage and more efficient detection.
- **Lack of Formal Guarantees for All Failures:** While STAC has a conformal prediction guarantee for low FPR, the overall system (especially with the VLM component) does not offer formal guarantees on detecting *all* failures. Achieving such guarantees would likely require failure data for calibration.
- **Increased False Alarm Risk:** Combining detectors via a union of predictions (Logical OR) can, in the worst case, increase the overall FPR. More sophisticated combination schemes might be explored to manage this trade-off.
- **Focus on Detection, Not Prediction:** The framework focuses on detecting failures *as they occur*, rather than predicting them *before they occur*. While critical for runtime intervention, predicting failures proactively remains a separate, important challenge.

Despite these limitations, Sentinel represents a robust and empirically validated framework that significantly pushes the boundary of reliable robot deployment by intelligently monitoring the complex behaviors of generative policies.
