# Scaling Vision Transformers to Gigapixel Images via Hierarchical Self-Supervised Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Chen et al. introduce the Hierarchical Image Pyramid Transformer (HIPT), an architecture designed to process gigapixel whole slide images by learning features hierarchically across multiple magnifications. This model, employing a hierarchical self-supervised learning approach, achieved superior performance in cancer subtyping and survival prediction tasks compared to existing weakly-supervised methods.

## Problem

- Processing gigapixel Whole Slide Images (WSIs) for computational pathology is challenging due to their immense size (up to 10 billion pixels), leading to significant memory constraints and computational costs for traditional deep learning models.
- Relevant biological features in WSIs span multiple scales, from subcellular components to tissue-level architecture, which existing Multiple Instance Learning (MIL) methods often fail to capture comprehensively.
- Applying Vision Transformers (ViTs) directly to gigapixel images is computationally prohibitive due to the quadratic complexity of self-attention with respect to input size.

## Method

- The ==Hierarchical Image Pyramid Transformer (HIPT)== processes WSIs in a multi-stage pipeline, featuring Cell-Level (ViT256-16), Patch-Level (ViT4K-256), and Region-Level (ViTWSI-4096) aggregation layers.
- HIPT maintains a constant sequence length (M=256) in its initial two stages by dynamically adjusting patch sizes at each resolution, enabling it to scale to gigapixel images while managing computational complexity.
- A ==hierarchical self-supervised learning== strategy pretrains the lower levels (ViT256-16 and ViT4K-256) using ==DINO== on a large dataset of 10,678 unlabeled TCGA WSIs, allowing the model to learn meaningful representations across scales.

## Results

- HIPT outperformed state-of-the-art weakly-supervised methods on cancer subtyping tasks for **BRCA, NSCLC, and RCC**.
- The model demonstrated robust performance on cancer subtyping and survival prediction even with limited training data, attributing this to its hierarchical pretraining approach.
- Using **K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN)** on the pretrained HIPT features achieved competitive performance on slide-level classification tasks without fine-tuning, indicating the quality of the learned representations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - HIPT effectively learns visual features across multiple scales, from individual cells (16x16 pixels) to tissue organization (4096x4096 pixels), mirroring how pathologists examine slides.
> - A hierarchical self-supervised learning approach, leveraging DINO, enables the model to learn generalizable and robust representations from large unlabeled WSI datasets, even clustering different tissue types effectively without stain normalization.
> - Attention mechanisms within HIPT demonstrate specialization at different levels, with cell-level attention focusing on cellular components and patch-level attention identifying higher-level tissue structures like the tumor-stroma interface.
