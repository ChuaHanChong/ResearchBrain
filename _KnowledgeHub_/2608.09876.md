# Energy-Structured Latent World Models with Neural Time Fields for Physically Constistent Open-World Motion Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> ELWM is an energy-structured latent world model whose latent state carries explicit energy and momentum, evolving through a port-Hamiltonian transition with dissipation and control ports learned from RGB-D and inertial interaction histories. PC-NTF feeds its predicted traversal delays into an Eikonal arrival-time field to rerank navigation candidates, raising success from 81.3% to 89.7% and halving physical collisions.

## Problem

- Existing latent world models optimize unconstrained future representations, leaving dissipation and interaction response entangled in task- and embodiment-specific transition weights rather than reusable physical knowledge.
- Neural time fields such as Active NTFields derive guidance from static scene geometry alone, so the same path is preferred despite payload or actuation changes.
- Prior physics-structured models evaluate only inside their own model or policy, exposing no planner-consumable quantity that links predicted execution consequences to global navigation cost.

## Method

- **ELWM** encodes RGB-D, inertial and trajectory history into a latent phase state with ==Cholesky-parameterized inertia==, a learned stored-energy function, and a ==skew-symmetric port-Hamiltonian transition== carrying dissipation and control ports.
- Training uses only ==multi-step future-latent prediction== against encoder targets plus LeJEPA's ==SIGReg isotropic-Gaussian regularizer== to prevent collapse, integrated by an ==implicit-midpoint discrete-gradient scheme== for the non-separable energy.
- **PC-NTF** adds a physics latent decoder mapping latents to traversal speed, then reranks shared ==NTF== candidates by ==geometric arrival time== at the predicted terminal pose plus a physics-conditioned traversal delay.

## Results

- Against a capacity-matched generic world model, ELWM cuts 0.8-s motion NRMSE from **0.36** to **0.29** and pose error **10.8%**, with action-intervention ratio rising **1.009** to **1.193**.
- PC-NTF lifts navigation success **81.3%** to **89.7%**, SPL **0.64** to **0.73**, drops physical collisions **12.1%** to **5.8%** and Eikonal residual **0.083** to **0.031** versus ANTF.
- Ablations: removing the physics condition degrades NRMSE **1.012** to **2.347** and SPL **0.661** to **0.483**; energy-balance residual stays **1.88e-7** with latency **193ms** versus MPC's **372ms**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structuring a latent space with explicit energy, dissipation and control ports makes physical regularities reusable across scenes and payloads without any differentiable simulator supervision.
> - Coupling a world model to a planner works best through a physically dimensioned scalar, a traversal delay, rather than injecting latent mechanics into the PDE itself.
> - Caution: gains hold only for planar differential-drive navigation with an isotropic speed assumption; anisotropic friction and contact-rich motion remain untested, and latency is 5x geometry-only planning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a foundational challenge in embodied AI: physically consistent motion planning. This area is critical because generated robot trajectories must adhere to real-world execution dynamics to be safely and effectively deployed.

**Current Landscape:**
1. **Latent World Models:** These models are a prominent approach for predicting environmental dynamics by encoding multi-modal observation and interaction histories into latent states. They enable action-conditioned rollouts without needing to reconstruct every future observation. Recent advancements extend their use to motion planning and navigation. However, a limitation of many existing latent world models is their tendency to learn unconstrained future representations where physical regularities (e.g., dissipation, interaction response) remain implicit. This entanglement hinders the formation of reusable physical knowledge, making them less reliable for complex, unpredictable open-world navigation where dynamic interactions are crucial.
2. **Neural Time Fields (NTFs):** NTFs provide an efficient method for motion planning by interpreting time-to-go as a continuous arrival-time function, typically constrained by the Eikonal equation. Active Neural Time Fields (ANTF) further enhance this by learning arrival-time maps online, primarily deriving guidance from static geometric maps. While effective for global geometric pathfinding, ANTF and similar methods do not inherently account for dynamic physical interactions such as changes in robot payload, actuation limits, or varied traversal responses.

**The Gap Addressed by This Work:**
The core challenge identified by the authors is the disconnect between the predictive capabilities of latent world models and the requirements for physically consistent, dynamically feasible motion planning. Existing world models often lack explicit physical structure, preventing them from providing planners with causally interpretable physical quantities. Conversely, neural time fields, while geometrically efficient, do not intrinsically integrate real-time physical dynamics.

This work aims to bridge this gap by introducing a method that explicitly embeds physical structures into latent spaces within a world model and then integrates these physics-informed predictions into a neural time field framework. This approach seeks to enable motion planning that is both geometrically guided and dynamically aware, adapting to changing physical conditions in open-world environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to achieve physically consistent motion planning for robots navigating open-world environments. This overarching goal is motivated by several identified limitations in current embodied AI approaches:

1. **Lack of Explicit Physical Consistency in Latent World Models:**
 - **Motivation:** Generated trajectories in embodied AI are only useful if a robot can execute them under its current dynamics. Existing latent world models, while adept at predicting future states, typically learn "unconstrained future representations." This means that crucial physical principles like energy dissipation, interaction responses, and actuation limits are implicitly absorbed into task-, environment-, or embodiment-specific transition weights. This implicit learning prevents the formation of reusable physical knowledge and a clear causal physical structure.
 - **Objective:** To develop a novel latent world model that explicitly carries energy and momentum in its latent state, ensuring strictly causal and physically consistent transitions. This explicit structuring is intended to make physical dynamics reusable and transferable.

2. **Inadequate Integration of Physical Dynamics into Motion Planning:**
 - **Motivation:** While latent world models can provide predictions, these predictions alone do not directly translate into how a robot should progress toward a navigation goal. Motion planners require a global value representation to connect local actions to the overall navigation cost. Neural Time Fields (NTFs) offer this by efficiently organizing global geometric guidance based on arrival time. However, traditional NTFs primarily derive this guidance from static geometry. They lack the capability to adapt to dynamic physical conditions, such as changes in payload or actuation, which can alter traversal responses significantly.
 - **Objective:** To create a motion planning system that effectively utilizes the physically informed predictions from the structured latent world model to condition arrival-time decisions within a neural time field framework. This system should go beyond mapping states or poses to Eikonal propagation speed, incorporating candidate-dependent physical consequences.

3. **Ensuring Reliability and Adaptability in Open-World Navigation:**
 - **Motivation:** Open-world navigation is characterized by unpredictability, where robots must adapt to unforeseen physical interactions not solely determined by scene geometry. The limitations of current world models (implicit physics) and NTFs (static geometry dependence) compromise reliability and adaptability in such dynamic settings, potentially leading to unsafe or inefficient trajectories.
 - **Objective:** To develop a comprehensive framework (ELWM + PC-NTF) that intrinsically bridges the gap between predictive world models and safe, dynamically feasible motion planning. This framework aims to enable open-world decisions that dynamically adjust to the robot's interaction response, payload, dissipation, actuation limits, and overall dynamic state.

In summary, the authors aim to enhance the reliability and efficiency of robot navigation by structuring learned latent dynamics to be physically consistent and by integrating these dynamics into a global geometric planning framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research proposes the Energy-Structured Latent World Model (ELWM) combined with Physics-Conditioned Neural Time Fields (PC-NTF) to achieve physically consistent open-world motion planning. The methodology is structured in three main components: ELWM, PC-NTF, and their integration into a closed-loop navigation system.

**System Overview:**
The overall inference loop operates as follows:
1. **History Encoding:** An interaction history $H_t$ (RGB-D, inertial data, executed trajectories) is fed into ELWM to produce a latent physical state $s_t$.
2. **Candidate Generation:** A Neural Time Field (NTF) component uses the current robot state ($r_t$), task goal ($g$), and global arrival-time field ($T_A$) to propose a set of nominal motion candidates $U_t^{(i)}$.
3. **Physics-Conditioned Evaluation:** PC-NTF utilizes $s_t$, the candidate actions $U_t^{(i)}$, and $T_A$ to predict the physical consequences of each candidate and re-rank them.
4. **Action Execution:** The action from the highest-ranked feasible candidate ($u_{exec,t}$) is executed.
5. **Feedback Loop:** The observed transition updates the interaction history $H_{t+1}$, closing the loop.

**Energy-Structured Latent World Model (ELWM):**
ELWM is designed to learn transferable physical regularities by explicitly structuring its latent state with energy and momentum principles.
1. **Latent Physical State:** An encoder $E_\theta$ maps the recent proprioceptive and navigation interaction history $H_{phy,t}$ into a latent physical state comprising:
 - $M_t$: Slow physical regularity dynamics (e.g., payload, ground response, actuation delay).
 - $Q_t, V_t \in \mathbb{R}^{d_z/2}$: Latent generalized coordinates and velocities derived from relative motion features.
 - A latent inertia $G_t$ is parameterized by a Cholesky factor $c_t$ and robot meta-information $\mu_r$. Momentum $P_t = G_t V_t$.
 - The port-Hamiltonian (pH) latent phase state is defined as $Z_t = [Q_t, P_t]$.
2. **Latent Stored Energy:** The model formalizes latent stored energy $E_\theta(Q_t, P_t; M_t, \mu_r)$ as the sum of kinetic energy $K_\theta = \frac{1}{2} P_t^\top G_t^{-1} P_t$ and potential energy $U_\theta = U_\theta(\rho(Q_t), M_t, \mu_r)$, where $\rho(\cdot)$ is a feature map for relative quantities. This ensures consistent velocity-momentum relations.
3. **Port-Hamiltonian Transition:** The latent state transition is formalized using a port-Hamiltonian framework:
 - $\dot{Z}_t = \left[ J - R_\theta(Z_t, M_t, \mu_r) \right] \nabla_Z E_\theta + B_\theta(Z_t, M_t, \mu_r) u_t$.
 - $J$ is a skew-symmetric operator, $R_\theta \succeq 0$ represents dissipation, and $B_\theta$ is the control port.
 - $u_t = E_u(a_t, o_t, \mu_r)$ is the latent control input.
 - This construction ensures that the latent energy change $\frac{dE_\theta}{dt}$ is equal to the injected system latent work minus non-negative dissipated power, adhering to energy balance principles. The implicit midpoint method is used for discrete-gradient updates in the non-separable energy function.
4. **Training:** ELWM is trained using a future prediction objective: $L_{ELWM} = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^K w_k d_z (\hat{Z}_{t+k} - Z^+_{t+k})_2^2 + \lambda_{sig} R_{SIGReg}$.
 - Targets $Z^+_{t+k}$ are encodings of future histories.
 - $R_{SIGReg}$ is an isotropic-Gaussian embedding regularizer (LeJEPA) to prevent latent collapse.
 - Gradients propagate through the structure-preserving rollout into $E_\theta, R_\theta, B_\theta, E_u$.
 - Energy residuals $\epsilon_{energy}$ and symmetry-masked momentum residuals are logged for diagnostics.

**PC-NTF System Construction:**
PC-NTF integrates ELWM's physical priors with neural time fields for a complete navigation system.
1. **Physics Latent Decoder:** A decoder maps ELWM's future latent predictions into planner-facing representations:
 - $\Gamma_t^{(i)} = A_{WM}(H_t, U_t^{(i)}) = [\hat{\tau}_t^{(i)}, \hat{x}_{t+H}^{(i)}, \Delta T_{WM}^{(i)}, \rho_t^{(i)}]$.
 - $\hat{\tau}_t^{(i)}$: decoded observable motion.
 - $\hat{x}_{t+H}^{(i)}$: predicted terminal state.
 - $\Delta T_{WM}^{(i)} \geq 0$: traversal delay, representing additional time due to slower-than-commanded progress.
 - $\rho_t^{(i)}$: physical tracking, safety, and control consequences.
 - This decoder is trained using Huber loss against reference physics-speed envelopes $s_t^*$.
2. **NTF Construction and Physics Condition:**
 - Standard NTF estimates travel time $T(x,g)$ using the Eikonal equation $S(x) \|\nabla T(x,x_g)\|^2 = 1$. The speed $S(x)$ is typically geometry-derived.
 - PC-NTF enhances this by conditioning the arrival time with ELWM's predictions. For each candidate action $i$, PC-NTF evaluates the total travel time as $T^{(i)} = T(\hat{x}_{t+H}^{(i)}, x_g) + \Delta T_{phys}^{(i)}$.
 - $T(\hat{x}_{t+H}^{(i)}, x_g)$ is the remaining geometric time from the predicted terminal location (from NTF).
 - $\Delta T_{phys}^{(i)}$ is the physics-conditioned traversal delay supplied by ELWM's decoder, representing physical regularities and predicted motion costs. This effectively penalizes geometrically attractive motions that are physically difficult to execute.
3. **Motion-Planning and Navigation System:**
 - At each replanning step, NTF proposes feasible candidates. ELWM predicts their physical consequences, and PC-NTF evaluates them using the modified $T^{(i)}$ objective.
 - Candidates with poor tracking, speed-limit violations, high uncertainty, or abrupt control changes are penalized. The first action of the lowest-cost feasible candidate is executed.
 - The system operates with ELWM and NTF trained separately and frozen during evaluation, preserving NTF's global guidance while integrating ELWM's local physical consequences.
 - The effective propagation speed $s_{eff,t}$ is modified based on ELWM's readouts (e.g., higher dissipation or payload reduces $s_{eff,t}$), influencing the Eikonal equation and steering planned paths away from physically challenging regions.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Environments:** iGibson indoor scenes, held-out HM3D scenes (formalized to iGibson), and a terrain-variation suite (carpet, tile, gravel, payload changes). Train/test splits were based on scene, surface, and robot configuration.
- **Baselines:** Active Neural Time Fields (ANTF), a capacity-matched generic latent world model (Generic WM) with MPC controller, and the complete ELWM+PC-NTF system.
- **Metrics:**
 - **World Model Axis:** 0.8-s motion-prediction normalized root mean square error (NRMSE@0.8s), pose error, twist error, action intervention ratio, slow-M intervention ratio.
 - **Planning Axis:** Success rate (SR), success weighted by path length (SPL), physical collision rate, Eikonal residual, planning latency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation was structured along two axes: world-model prediction and motion planning.

#### 4.1. Physical Consistent World Model Prediction (ELWM)

The evaluation demonstrated the predictive capabilities and consistency of ELWM, particularly against a generic latent world model.
- **Observable Motion Prediction:** ELWM reduced the 0.8-second motion-prediction NRMSE from 0.36 (Generic WM) to 0.29, representing a 19.4% reduction. Pose error decreased by 10.8%, and twist error by 1.8%.
- **Action Dependence:** ELWM exhibited a higher action-intervention ratio (1.193 compared to 1.009 for Generic WM), indicating that its rollouts are more strongly dependent on the executed control actions. A smaller positive difference in slow-M intervention (1.033 vs. 1.062 for Generic WM) suggests that the $M_t$ component of ELWM effectively identifies reusable physical regularities.
- **Energy Consistency:** The trained ELWM achieved an energy-balance residual of 1.88 x 10^-7, and its predicted dissipated power was consistently non-negative across all evaluated rollout steps. This empirically verified that the numerical transition in ELWM adheres to the imposed dissipative port-Hamiltonian identity.
- **Generalization to Held-Out Scopes:** ELWM showed substantial improvements in prediction on unseen physical regimes and scenes:
 - On held-out physical regimes, motion NRMSE decreased from 7.753 (random initialization) to 0.705, a 90.9% reduction. Latent MSE was reduced by 49.5%.
 - On held-out scenes, motion NRMSE decreased from 7.699 (random initialization) to 0.697, a 91.0% reduction. Latent MSE was reduced by 49.6%. These matched reductions indicate ELWM's effective learning and generalization.

#### 4.2. Motion Planning and Navigation (PC-NTF)

The PC-NTF system, which integrates ELWM's physics-conditioned predictions with Neural Time Fields, was compared against ANTF and a Generic World Model with MPC.
- **Navigation Success Rate (SR):** PC-NTF improved the success rate from 81.3% (ANTF) and 87.1% (Generic WM + MPC) to 89.7%.
- **Success Weighted by Path Length (SPL):** PC-NTF achieved an SPL of 0.73, an improvement over ANTF's 0.64, and comparable to Generic WM + MPC's 0.74.
- **Physical Collision Rate:** PC-NTF significantly reduced the physical collision rate from 14.4% (ANTF) and 6.5% (Generic WM + MPC) to 5.8%.
- **Eikonal Residual:** PC-NTF demonstrated a lower Eikonal residual of 0.031, indicating better adherence to the Eikonal equation compared to ANTF (0.083) and Generic WM + MPC (0.143).
- **Planning Latency:** PC-NTF had a planning latency of 193.2 ms, which is higher than ANTF (35.3 ms) but considerably lower than Generic WM + MPC (371.7 ms). This indicates a balance between physical awareness and computational efficiency.

#### 4.3. Scalability and Ablation Studies

- **Scalability:** ELWM exhibited stable and parameter-robust learning behavior. Across varying hyperparameter settings (learning rate, weight decay, SIGReg coefficient), ELWM consistently reached lower loss regions and maintained lower training loss, validation loss, and future-latent consistency error compared to Generic WM. This suggests that the physics-structured transition contributes to stable learning irrespective of specific parameter combinations.
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - Removing the physical condition ("PC-NTF w/o physical condition") or shuffling the physical regularity component $M_t$ ("PC-NTF w/ shuffled M_t") led to significant performance degradation across NRMSE, SPL, and physical collision rate. This highlights the importance of ELWM's learned physical conditioning.
 - Using a generic world model instead of ELWM ("PC-NTF w/ generic WM") resulted in better performance than ANTF but inferior to the full ELWM + PC-NTF system, particularly in NRMSE and latency. This further supports the benefit of ELWM's structured dynamics over generic predictive models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that contributes to the field of embodied AI by addressing the challenge of physically consistent motion planning in dynamic, open-world environments. The significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Bridging the Gap between Prediction and Planning:** The work fundamentally bridges the conceptual and practical gap between predictive latent world models and safe, dynamically feasible motion planning. By embedding explicit physical structures into the latent space of ELWM, the model learns physical regularities in a way that is reusable and interpretable for a motion planner, which is often a limitation of conventional latent world models.

2. **Enhanced Physical Consistency and Reliability:** ELWM's energy-structured latent state, explicitly carrying energy and momentum with defined dissipation and control ports, ensures strictly causal and physically consistent predictions. This contributes to more reliable robot behavior, particularly in open-world scenarios where unmodeled dynamics can lead to failures. The demonstrated low energy-balance residual and improved prediction accuracy on held-out data support this claim.

3. **Improved Open-World Navigation Performance:** The PC-NTF system, by integrating ELWM's physics-conditioned predictions with Neural Time Fields, yields measurable improvements in navigation performance. The increase in success rate and SPL, alongside a significant reduction in physical collision rates, indicates that the robot can navigate more effectively and safely. This is particularly relevant for autonomous systems operating in complex, dynamic environments.

4. **Adaptability to Dynamic Conditions:** The framework allows motion planning decisions to adapt to changes in robot-environment dynamics, such as payload variations, altered dissipation, or actuation limits. By conditioning the arrival-time field with candidate-dependent physical consequences, PC-NTF ensures that the planner's choices are informed by real-time physical realities rather than just static geometric information. This adaptability is crucial for robust performance in real-world applications.

5. **Modular and Efficient Integration:** The approach of separately training ELWM and NTF, with PC-NTF acting as an interface, offers a modular design. This prevents the need for retraining the global time field for every new physical interaction condition, contributing to computational efficiency compared to methods that might require full model retraining or extensive online optimization for dynamic changes. While incurring a higher latency than geometry-only NTFs, it achieves lower latency than MPC-based approaches using generic world models.

6. **Foundation for Future Research:** The demonstration that explicit physical structures in latent spaces can lead to tangible benefits in complex tasks like motion planning lays a foundation for future research. The authors suggest extensions to more complex scenarios such as anisotropic, state-space, and contact-rich motion planning, and evaluation across broader robot configurations and real-world environments. This indicates the potential generalizability and applicability of the energy-structured latent modeling paradigm.

In conclusion, this work offers a method for addressing critical challenges in embodied AI by providing robots with a more profound understanding and integration of physical dynamics into their planning processes, which has implications for the deployment of intelligent autonomous systems.
