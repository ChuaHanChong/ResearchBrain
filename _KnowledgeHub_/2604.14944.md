# HRDexDB: A Paired Human-Robot Dataset for Cross-Embodiment Dexterous Grasping

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Seoul National University and RLWRLD introduce HRDexDB, a paired human-robot dataset for dexterous grasping that features synchronized, multi-modal data for human and four types of robotic hands interacting with 100 objects. This dataset provides high-fidelity 3D hand and 6D object pose annotations, alongside tactile signals, to facilitate cross-embodiment transfer and advance interaction-centric perception models.

## Problem

- The significant "embodiment gap" between human and robotic hands, and even among different robotic hands, makes it difficult to directly transfer human manipulation skills to robots.
- Existing datasets lack paired human-robot demonstrations for dexterous manipulation across multiple robotic embodiments, hindering research on cross-embodiment learning.
- Accurate 3D hand and 6D object pose tracking during dexterous manipulation is challenging due to severe occlusions and self-occlusions in real-world interaction scenarios.

## Method

- Developed **HRDexDB**, a multi-modal dataset featuring 2.1K grasping trials and 24M frames across 100 objects, with ==paired human-robot sequences== from human subjects and four diverse dexterous robotic hands.
- Implemented a ==multi-modal capture system== with 21 exocentric RGB views and egocentric cameras, alongside a ==two-stage paired acquisition protocol== where human grasps are recorded then ==teleoperated== by robots.
- Designed a high-fidelity ==state reconstruction pipeline== using ==MANO== for human hand pose, and a ==model-based 6D tracking== approach (==FoundationPose== + ==SAM3== masks) that leverages RGB-D and ==cross-view geometric consistency==.

## Results

- Human-to-robot contact map transfer using HRDexDB data increased real-world grasp success rates for the Inspire hand from **66.7% to 73.3%** and for the Allegro hand from **63.3% to 80.0%**.
- A latent-space grasp retrieval model achieved **R@1** accuracies of **36.36%** for Human to Inspire hand and **24.24%** for Human to Allegro hand retrieval, improving grasp optimization seed-level success from **3.39% to 12.24%** for Inspire-F1.
- HRDexDB served as a challenging benchmark, causing existing hand pose estimation models to show higher errors than on FreiHAND, but finetuning the MegaPose refiner with HRDexDB robot-grasp data yielded a **3.99% relative improvement** in object 6D pose estimation (ADD metric).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct imitation of human contact patterns is suboptimal for robots; learning robot-specific contact strategies from paired human-robot data significantly enhances real-world grasp success.
> - HRDexDB facilitates the learning of shared latent representations that enable effective cross-embodiment grasp retrieval and provide beneficial initializations for downstream grasp optimization tasks.
> - Dexterous interaction scenarios in HRDexDB pose a greater challenge for state-of-the-art 3D hand and 6D object pose estimation models compared to less interactive datasets, highlighting the need for more robust perception systems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics aims to enable robots to perform complex manipulation tasks, ideally at a level comparable to human dexterity. A significant challenge in achieving this goal is the "embodiment gap," which refers to the differences in morphology, kinematics, and actuation between human hands and various robotic hands. This gap extends even across different robotic hand designs, leading to distinct feasible contact patterns and grasp strategies. Bridging this gap is crucial for transferring human manipulation knowledge to robots.

Existing research has explored several avenues, often relying on datasets to train and evaluate robotic systems. These datasets can broadly be categorized into:

- **Human-Object Interaction (HOI) Datasets:** These datasets focus on human hands interacting with objects, providing data for understanding human articulation, object motion, and contact. Examples include FreiHAND, HO-3D, DexYCB, ARCTIC, and GigaHands. While these datasets are rich in human manipulation data, they generally lack corresponding robotic data or the necessary information for direct robot application, such as multi-view RGB, 3D annotations, or tactile signals, and are primarily human-centric.
- **Robot-Object Interaction (ROI) Datasets:** These datasets facilitate robot learning by providing diverse manipulation demonstrations. Large-scale efforts like Open X-Embodiment and DROID often feature low-degrees-of-freedom grippers, while others like AgiBot World and RoboMIND include dexterous hands. However, these datasets typically focus solely on robotic manipulation and do not provide direct correspondence with human grasp motions, especially across multiple dexterous robotic embodiments. RealDex, for instance, focuses on a single robotic hand without separate human demonstrations.
- **Human-Robot Correspondence (HROI) Datasets:** A more recent category attempts to link human demonstrations with robotic executions to address the embodiment gap. RH20T and H&R provide task- or frame-level alignment, but primarily utilize parallel-jaw grippers, limiting their relevance for dexterous manipulation. DexWild collects aligned human and multi-finger robot demonstrations but has limitations in tracking under occlusion and lacks dense behavioral alignment.
- **Synthetic Dexterous Grasp Datasets:** Datasets like DexGraspNet and GenDexGrasp/MultiDex generate stable grasps in simulation or scale grasp motion generation across human and robotic hands. While valuable for generating grasp priors, they are synthetic and do not capture real-world interaction trajectories or the complexities of real-world sensing.

HRDexDB addresses limitations within this landscape by providing a novel, paired cross-embodiment dataset. It uniquely offers synchronized, multi-modal data for both human and diverse robotic hands manipulating shared objects, complete with high-fidelity 3D hand and object pose annotations and tactile signals. This dataset aims to bridge the gap by offering closely aligned human and robot execution data, which is largely absent in previous work, thereby enabling new research in cross-embodiment transfer and interaction-centric perception.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to advance robotic dexterity towards human-level performance by addressing the challenges of transferring manipulation strategies across different embodiments. The authors identify several motivations for introducing HRDexDB:

1. **Bridging the Embodiment Gap:** The fundamental motivation stems from the inherent differences between human and robotic hands (morphology, kinematics, actuation), as well as variations among different robotic hands. Direct imitation of human demonstrations by robots is often suboptimal or infeasible due to these disparities. The work seeks to facilitate understanding how robots can learn from human manipulation and adapt grasp strategies for their specific embodiment.
2. **Addressing Data Gaps in Cross-Embodiment Dexterous Manipulation:** Existing datasets are limited in their ability to support research on cross-embodiment transfer.
 - Most human-centric datasets lack corresponding robot data.
 - Robot-centric datasets rarely provide direct correspondence with human manipulation or cover multiple dexterous robotic hand embodiments.
 - Current human-robot paired datasets often use simpler grippers, lack markerless RGB observations, tactile signals, or sufficiently precise 3D motion information.
 - Synthetic datasets provide priors but lack real-world interaction complexities.
 The lack of paired, multi-embodiment captures of comparable grasping behavior over shared objects with detailed multi-modal information is a significant limitation for robot learning research.
3. **Providing High-Fidelity Spatiotemporal Ground Truth:** Acquiring high-precision 3D hand and object motion during dexterous manipulation is challenging due to self-occlusions and hand-object occlusions. A key motivation is to provide accurate spatiotemporal 3D ground-truth motion for both the manipulating agent (human or robot hand) and the manipulated object, which is essential for training and evaluating perception and control systems.
4. **Enabling New Benchmarks for Cross-Embodiment Learning and Perception:** By providing a comprehensive, paired dataset, the authors aim to establish a foundational benchmark. This benchmark would allow researchers to:
 - Develop and evaluate methods for human-to-robot transfer, such as adapting human contact patterns or learning shared latent representations for grasps.
 - Test and improve markerless 3D hand pose estimation and 6D object pose estimation under the challenging conditions of dexterous interaction, where occlusions are prevalent.
 - Facilitate research into the role of tactile sensing in dexterous manipulation across different robotic embodiments.

In summary, the motivation is to create a unique, high-quality, and comprehensive dataset that directly supports research into how dexterous manipulation skills can be effectively transferred from humans to diverse robots, and how perception systems perform in complex interaction scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors developed HRDexDB as a paired, multi-modal dataset for dexterous grasping, encompassing both human demonstrations and robotic executions across multiple hand embodiments. The methodology involved a specialized capture system, a two-stage data acquisition protocol, and a multi-modal state reconstruction pipeline.

#### 3.1. Dataset Overview

HRDexDB contains 2.1K grasping trials and 24M frames involving 100 diverse objects. It includes paired sequences from human subjects and four dexterous robotic hands: Allegro Hand V4, Allegro Hand V5 Plus, Inspire Hand RH56DFTP, and Inspire Hand RH56F1.

The dataset records the following synchronized modalities for each trial:
- **Visual Observations:** 21 exocentric RGB views and 2 egocentric RGB views.
- **Kinematic States:** MANO pose parameters for human hands and joint states ($q_{robot}$) for robotic hands.
- **Reconstructed Geometry:** 3D hand motion (from MANO parameters) and object 6D pose trajectories ($T_{object} \in SE(3)$).
- **Contact Signals:** Tactile force signals ($F_{tactile}$) from tactile-enabled robotic hand fingertips.
- **Annotations:** Grasp success/failure labels ($y \in \{0, 1\}$).

All spatial quantities are aligned within a unified world coordinate system, defined by the calibrated multi-camera platform.

#### 3.2. Multi-Modal Capture System and Paired Data Collection

- **Capture System:** The hardware setup features a 21-camera RGB rig arranged on a three-sided metal frame, providing dense multi-view capture designed to mitigate occlusions during dexterous manipulation. Two additional egocentric cameras are used: an over-the-shoulder rig for robotic trials and a custom stereo helmet for human demonstrations.
- **Robot Teleoperation:** Robotic hands are controlled via teleoperation. An Xsens inertial motion-capture suit captures wrist and arm motions, while MANUS gloves capture finger motions, mapping the operator's movements to the robot arm and hand.
- **Paired Acquisition Protocol:** A two-stage protocol ensures comparable human and robot grasps under identical object and workspace conditions:
 1. A human subject performs a natural grasp on a target object. The multi-view recordings are used to reconstruct the human hand motion and object trajectory.
 2. A teleoperator observes the recorded human demonstration and executes a semantically corresponding grasp with one of the robotic embodiments. This protocol aims to preserve the human grasp intent while allowing for embodiment-specific adaptations in morphology, kinematics, and timing.

#### 3.3. Multi-Modal State Reconstruction

The synchronized recordings undergo a processing pipeline to reconstruct 3D states in the unified world coordinate system.

- **Human Hand Reconstruction:**
 - The MANO parametric hand model is used to represent human hand geometry and articulation.
 - 2D hand keypoints are detected in each of the 21 calibrated exocentric views using HaMeR.
 - These 2D keypoints are triangulated to obtain 3D joint positions.
 - MANO pose parameters are optimized for each frame to fit the 3D joint positions, similar to the multi-view fitting strategy of GigaHands.
 - Subject-specific hand shape is calibrated by aligning silhouettes with masks generated by SAM3.
 - Temporal filtering is applied to reduce noise and jitter in the reconstructed motion.

- **Object 6D Tracking:**
 - A model-based 6D tracking pipeline is employed to obtain accurate object poses ($T_{object} \in SE(3)$).
 - A designated calibrated stereo camera pair estimates dense depth maps using FoundationStereo.
 - SAM3 generates object masks to precisely localize the manipulated object within the images.
 - Given the RGB-D observations and pre-scanned object CAD models, FoundationPose performs 6D pose estimation. The object's initial pose in the first frame is established via global registration, and subsequent poses are refined through temporal tracking to maintain consistency.
 - To counteract drift in stereo-based tracking, which relies on a single viewpoint, cross-view geometric consistency is enforced. This involves rendering the estimated object mesh into all calibrated camera views and minimizing silhouette misalignment across views.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors demonstrate the utility of HRDexDB through several benchmarks: human-to-robot transfer (contact map transfer and grasp retrieval) and perception benchmarks (3D hand pose estimation and 6D object pose estimation).

#### 4.1. Human-to-Robot Contact Map Transfer

This task investigates learning robot-specific contact strategies from paired human-robot grasps, moving beyond direct human contact imitation.
- **Methodology:** A model, conditioned on a human grasp (represented by a contact map $C_h$ and part map $P_h$) and PointNet++ object features, predicts robot-specific contact $C_r$ and part $P_r$ maps. This is supervised with L1 loss on $C_r$ and cross-entropy loss on $P_r$. The predicted maps are then used as objectives for a physics-aware grasp optimization pipeline (CEDex) to synthesize robot grasps.
- **Results (Table 2):**
 - **Simulation:** The `Transferred-Contact` method showed improved grasp success rates compared to `Human-Contact` (optimizing directly from human contact maps). For the Inspire hand, success increased from 54.6% to 55.6%. For the Allegro hand, success increased from 60.2% to 65.8%.
 - **Real Hardware:** `Transferred-Contact` demonstrated more substantial improvements. For the Inspire hand, success increased from 66.7% to 73.3%. For the Allegro hand, success increased from 63.3% to 80.0%.
- **Conclusion:** HRDexDB supports learning robot-specific contact strategies that adapt human grasp intent to the target robot morphology, leading to higher real-world grasp success.

#### 4.2. Latent-Space Robot Grasp Retrieval

This task evaluates the ability of HRDexDB to facilitate learning a shared latent representation that aligns functionally and geometrically similar grasps across different embodiments.
- **Methodology:** A CLIP-style multi-branch retrieval model was developed, using separate point-cloud encoders for human and robot hands and a shared object encoder. The model was trained with a symmetric contrastive loss to align paired cross-embodiment grasps in a shared embedding space. Evaluation included retrieval accuracy and the utility of retrieved grasps as initializations for a downstream grasp optimizer (BODex).
- **Results (Table 3 & 4):**
 - **Retrieval Accuracy (Table 3):** The model achieved retrieval accuracies substantially above random. For Human to Inspire hand retrieval, R@1 was 36.36%, R@3 was 81.82%, and R@5 was 100.00%. For Human to Allegro hand, R@1 was 24.24%, R@3 was 63.64%, and R@5 was 72.73%. Retrieval between Inspire and Allegro hands also showed performance (R@1: 8.18%). This indicates that the dataset enables learning an embodiment-aware latent representation.
 - **BODex Refinement (Table 4):** Retrieval-based initialization strategies improved grasp optimization success compared to a vanilla BODex and kinematic retargeting. For Inspire-F1, `Retrieval-top1` achieved 12.24% seed-level success (compared to 3.39% for Vanilla), and `Retrieval-top5` achieved 75.76% episode-level success (compared to 69.70% for Vanilla). For Allegro-v5, `Retrieval-top1` achieved 21.33% seed-level success (compared to 16.24% for Vanilla), and `Retrieval-top5` achieved 93.94% episode-level success (compared to 84.85% for Vanilla).
- **Conclusion:** HRDexDB facilitates learning cross-embodiment grasp representations that can effectively retrieve robot grasp priors from human queries and provide beneficial initializations for grasp optimization.

#### 4.3. Benchmarking 3D Hand Pose Estimation

This benchmark evaluates the performance of state-of-the-art 3D hand pose and mesh estimation methods under the challenging conditions of dexterous manipulation in HRDexDB.
- **Methodology:** Several state-of-the-art hand reconstruction models (WiLoR, HaMeR, Hamba, MeshGraphormer, FrankMocap) were evaluated on HRDexDB and compared against their performance on FreiHAND. Additionally, the utility of HRDexDB data as a training signal was tested by finetuning models with combined datasets.
- **Results (Table 5 & 6):**
 - **Challenge Level (Table 5):** All evaluated models exhibited consistently higher errors (e.g., higher PA-MPJPE and PA-MPVPE) on HRDexDB compared to FreiHAND. For instance, WiLoR had a PA-MPJPE of 5.94mm on HRDexDB versus 5.71mm on FreiHAND, indicating that HRDexDB presents a more challenging benchmark for hand pose estimation.
 - **Training Utility (Table 6):** When 6,000 HRDexDB samples were added to the finetuning set for HaMeR and WiLoR, both models showed improved performance (lower PA-MPJPE and PA-MPVPE) on the FreiHAND dataset. For HaMeR, PA-MPJPE improved from 6.108mm to 6.027mm.
- **Conclusion:** HRDexDB provides a challenging benchmark for 3D hand pose estimation under dexterous interaction and offers complementary training data that can improve the generalization and accuracy of existing models.

#### 4.4. Benchmarking Object 6D Pose Estimation Methods under Human, Robot Grasping

This benchmark evaluates RGB-based object 6D localization methods in interaction-centric scenes provided by HRDexDB.
- **Methodology:** Three RGB-based object 6D localization methods (FoundPose, GigaPose, PicoPose) were evaluated on both human and robot grasp frames. For refinement-based variants, top coarse hypotheses were refined using MegaPose. The utility of HRDexDB for adaptation was tested by fine-tuning the MegaPose refiner with HRDexDB robot-grasp data.
- **Results (Table 7 & 8):**
 - **Interaction Challenge (Table 7):** All methods generally performed worse (higher ADD and lower AR$_{MSSD}$) under robot grasping compared to human grasping. For example, FoundPose's ADD was 6.91cm for human grasps versus 8.74cm for robot grasps, with a corresponding drop in AR$_{MSSD}$. This suggests that robotic hands introduce additional ambiguities (e.g., overlapping rigid links) for object localization.
 - **Adaptation Utility (Table 8):** Fine-tuning the MegaPose refiner with 5.3K HRDexDB robot-grasp annotations (in addition to GSO synthetic samples) improved its performance on a held-out robot-grasp environment. The fine-tuned refiner showed a 3.99% relative improvement in ADD (cm) and a 10.2% relative improvement in ADD-S (cm).
- **Conclusion:** HRDexDB offers a valuable benchmark for evaluating object 6D pose estimation methods in challenging interaction scenarios and can be used to adapt and improve pose refinement models for robot-object interactions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

HRDexDB represents a contribution to the fields of robotics, computer vision, and machine learning, particularly in the domain of dexterous manipulation and human-robot interaction. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

1. **Enabling Cross-Embodiment Robot Learning:** The dataset directly addresses a critical gap in existing resources by providing paired human and multi-robot dexterous grasping sequences on shared objects. This enables researchers to investigate and develop methods for effectively transferring complex manipulation skills from humans to diverse robotic hands, which is a central challenge in robotics.
2. **Foundational Benchmark for Dexterous Manipulation:** HRDexDB serves as a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating human-to-robot transfer algorithms, grasp synthesis methods, and embodiment-aware grasp representations. The demonstrated improvements in contact map transfer and latent-space grasp retrieval highlight its utility in developing more robust and adaptable robot control strategies.
3. **Advancing Interaction-Centric Perception:** The dataset's high-fidelity, multi-view, and multi-modal data, coupled with accurate 3D hand and 6D object annotations, provides a challenging environment for perception tasks. The benchmarks confirm that human and robot manipulation introduce unique challenges for hand and object pose estimation due to severe occlusions. HRDexDB can facilitate the development of more resilient perception systems capable of operating in cluttered and dynamic interaction scenes.
4. **Complementary Training Data:** The results indicate that HRDexDB data provides complementary information for training existing hand pose and object pose estimation models. Its inclusion in training sets can lead to improved generalization and accuracy, particularly for interaction-heavy scenarios where other datasets may fall short.
5. **Facilitating Future Research:** By publicly releasing this dataset, the authors aim to foster further research in dexterous manipulation, robot learning, and human-robot collaboration. The planned expansion to 1,000 objects and more complex tasks suggests a long-term resource for the community. The detailed capture and reconstruction methodology can also serve as a blueprint for similar data collection efforts.
6. **Addressing Tactile Sensing:** The inclusion of tactile signals for tactile-enabled robotic hands, while currently heterogeneous, lays the groundwork for future research into integrating touch information for robust dexterous manipulation, potentially leading to more nuanced and sensitive robotic control.

While acknowledging limitations such as tactile heterogeneity and challenges in defining precise trajectory correspondence, HRDexDB offers a novel and valuable resource that can accelerate progress towards achieving human-level dexterity in robotic manipulation and bridging the embodiment gap.
