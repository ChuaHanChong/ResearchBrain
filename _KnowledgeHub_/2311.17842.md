# Look Before You Leap: Unveiling the Power of GPT-4V in Robotic Vision-Language Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University, Shanghai AI Laboratory, and Shanghai Qi Zhi Institute developed VILA, a robotic planning framework that leverages GPT-4V directly for unified vision-language reasoning. VILA enables robots to perform complex, long-horizon tasks by understanding visually grounded commonsense, interpreting multimodal goals, and integrating natural visual feedback, achieving significantly higher success rates than prior LLM-based methods in real-world and simulated environments.

## Problem

- Traditional robotic task planning methods relied on pre-defined action primitives and explicit environmental models, limiting scalability and flexibility in open-world scenarios.
- Previous LLM-based robotic planners lacked direct perception of the physical world, relying on external, often disconnected, affordance models or perception APIs to provide real-world grounding.
- Existing planning systems struggled with incorporating visually-grounded commonsense knowledge (e.g., object occlusions, contextual object attributes) and adapting to dynamic environments with natural visual feedback.

## Method

- ==The VILA== framework leverages GPT-4V as a unified Vision-Language Model to directly integrate perceptual data into the reasoning process for robotic task planning.
- It operates in a closed-loop execution process where the VLM is prompted with the current visual observation and high-level language instructions to generate step-by-step plans.
- Primitive skills are assumed to be available, and the system iteratively executes the first step of the plan, updates the visual observation, and re-prompts the VLM until the task is complete.

## Results

- VILA achieved an **80%** average success rate in 8 real-world tasks (vs SayCan's **13%**, Grounded Decoding's **20%**) and higher success across 16 simulated tasks (avg **80-84.4%**) vs all baselines (CLIPort, LLM-only, Grounded Decoding).
- It demonstrated strong capabilities with multimodal goals, including **80%** success for sushi arrangement from a real image and **100%** success arranging jigsaw pieces from a child's drawing.
- Closed-loop VILA (with visual feedback) showed improved robustness in dynamic tasks, achieving **90%** success in 'Stack Blocks' with noise (vs. **20%** for open-loop) and **100%** in 'Pack Chip Bags' with human intervention.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct integration of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like GPT-4V enables inherent grounding of commonsense knowledge in the visual world, allowing for a deeper understanding of spatial layouts and object attributes.
> - VLMs facilitate versatile goal specification, capable of interpreting pure language, goal images, drawings, or multimodal combinations, enhancing human-robot interaction flexibility.
> - Natural integration of visual feedback directly into the VLM's planning loop makes robotic systems robust to dynamic environments, enabling adaptation, recovery from disturbances, and active exploration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of large language models (LLMs), vision-language models (VLMs), and robotic task planning. It addresses a critical limitation in current robotic systems: the ability to perform complex, long-horizon tasks with real-world grounding and adaptability.

Historically, robotic task planning has relied on symbolic planning methods (e.g., STRIPS, SHOP) and optimization-based approaches within the Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) framework. These methods often require meticulously pre-defined action primitives and explicit environmental models, limiting their scalability and flexibility in open-world scenarios.

More recently, the advent of large language models (LLMs) like GPT-3, GPT-4, and Llama 2 has revolutionized various AI fields, including robotics. Researchers quickly recognized LLMs' immense knowledge base and reasoning capabilities for generating high-level, step-by-step plans from natural language instructions. However, a fundamental challenge emerged: LLMs lack direct perception of the physical world. They are "blind," unable to inherently understand environmental states, object properties, or spatial relationships. To compensate, LLM-based planners typically rely on external "affordance models" or "perception APIs" (e.g., object detectors, value functions) to provide real-world grounding. This setup, exemplified by works like SayCan and Grounded Decoding, functions as a two-stage process: perception feeds information to the LLM, which then generates a plan. The paper critiques this "blind person with a sighted guide" analogy, arguing that such independent modules suffer from unidirectional information flow and struggle to convey task-dependent or nuanced visual information crucial for robust planning.

The emergence of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), such as GPT-4V(ision), Flamingo, and BLIP-2, marks a significant paradigm shift. These models unify vision and language processing into a single architecture, enabling joint reasoning over multimodal inputs. This work leverages this very capability, proposing a direct VLM-based approach to robotic planning.

VILA distinguishes itself from other VLM-in-robotics efforts:
- **RT-1 and RT-2**: While these models also integrate VLMs into robotics, their primary focus is on low-level visuomotor control (directly mapping observations to robot actions) and demonstrating generalization through large-scale data. VILA, in contrast, targets high-level task planning, generating a sequence of abstract steps.
- **PaLM-E**: Similar in its multimodal nature, PaLM-E is a large embodied multimodal language model. However, it requires extensive training on a mixture of robotics and general visual-language data, implying retraining for new environments. VILA, on the other hand, operates in a "zero-shot" open-world setting, leveraging the pre-trained knowledge of GPT-4V without requiring additional training data or in-context examples for new tasks.

Therefore, VILA represents a novel application of powerful, pre-trained, general-purpose VLMs like GPT-4V to high-level robotic task planning. It aims to overcome the inherent grounding limitations of LLMs by directly integrating visual perception into the planning loop, thereby enabling a more intuitive and robust understanding of commonsense knowledge in the physical world, a crucial step toward more adaptable and intelligent embodied agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **imbue robots with the capability of physically-grounded, long-horizon task planning** by directly integrating perceptual data into the reasoning process. The authors aim to move beyond the limitations of existing LLM-based planners, which, despite their reasoning prowess, suffer from a lack of true world grounding and an over-reliance on external, often disconnected, affordance models.

The key motivations driving this work can be summarized as follows:

- **Bridging the Perception-Reasoning Gap:** Traditional LLM-based planners treat vision and language as separate modalities, where visual information is first processed by external perception modules and then fed as textual descriptions to the LLM. The authors argue that this separation hinders effective joint reasoning. Their core motivation is to create a *unified multimodal system* where perception and language processing are synergized, allowing the model to "see" and "think" concurrently, much like humans.
- **Leveraging Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for Grounding:** Recent advancements in VLMs, particularly models like GPT-4V, have demonstrated an unprecedented ability to understand and reason across both images and text. The authors are motivated to harness this inherent multimodal grounding capability of VLMs to enable robots to understand complex real-world environments more profoundly, without the need for independent, often unreliable, external affordance models.
- **Enhancing Commonsense Knowledge in the Visual World:** A significant limitation of prior LLM-based planners is their struggle with commonsense knowledge that is intrinsically tied to visual understanding, such as spatial layouts (e.g., object occlusions, containment) and fine-grained object attributes (e.g., sharpness of scissors varying with context). The researchers aim to develop a planner that can seamlessly integrate this visually grounded commonsense knowledge into its decision-making.
- **Enabling Flexible Multimodal Goal Specification:** Human tasks are often communicated through a blend of language and visual cues (e.g., showing a picture of a desired arrangement). Existing systems primarily rely on language instructions. A motivation is to allow robots to interpret diverse forms of goal specifications, including images, drawings, or even pointing gestures, alongside or in lieu of language.
- **Achieving Robust Closed-Loop Planning with Natural Visual Feedback:** Real-world environments are dynamic, necessitating constant feedback and the ability to replan. Current LLM-based methods often convert visual feedback into language, which can be cumbersome and lossy. The authors are motivated to enable robots to naturally incorporate visual feedback directly into the planning loop for robust operation and recovery from unforeseen events.
- **Developing an Open-World, Zero-Shot Planner:** The ultimate goal is to create a versatile task planning method that can operate in open-world scenarios, handling a wide array of everyday manipulation tasks without requiring task-specific training data or extensive prompt engineering (i.e., operating in a zero-shot manner).

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VILA (Robotic Vision-Language Planning) is a novel approach for long-horizon robotic planning that directly leverages Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to generate sequences of actionable steps.

**Core Idea: VLM as the Unified Planner**
At its heart, VILA posits that a VLM, specifically GPT-4V, can serve as an inherently grounded, unified multimodal system for robotic planning. Unlike previous LLM-based approaches that separate perception (via external affordance models) from language reasoning, VILA prompts the VLM directly with both the current visual observation (an image) and a high-level language instruction. This allows for joint reasoning over visual and linguistic information.

**Closed-Loop Execution Process (Algorithm 1 & Figure 2):**
1. **Input:** The system receives an initial visual observation of the environment ($x_t$) and a high-level language goal ($L$) describing the task.
2. **VLM Query:** The VLM (GPT-4V) is prompted with the current visual observation, the high-level instruction, and the history of previously executed steps.
3. **Plan Generation:** The VLM generates a step-by-step plan ($p_{1:N}$), which is a sequence of short-horizon language instructions (e.g., "pick up blue container"). The paper highlights that GPT-4V's inherent "chain-of-thought" reasoning assists in this process.
4. **Step Execution:** The first step ($l_t = p_1$) from the generated plan is selected. A corresponding primitive skill ($\pi_{l_t}$) is then executed by the robot. The paper explicitly states that the acquisition of these primitive skills is *not* the focus of this work; they are assumed to be available as predefined script policies or learned policies (e.g., through RL or BC).
5. **Observation Update & Iteration:** After executing the skill, the environment state changes, leading to a new visual observation ($x_{t+1}$). The executed step ($l_t$) is added to the "finished plan" history. The process then repeats from step 2 until a termination token (e.g., "done") is generated by the VLM. This iterative loop enables robust planning in dynamic environments by continuously incorporating visual feedback.

**Key Properties of VILA:**

- **Comprehension of Commonsense Knowledge Grounded in the Visual World:** By directly processing images, VILA can understand complex spatial layouts (e.g., object occlusions, containment within drawers, needing to remove obstacles first) and object attributes (e.g., a "scissors" being a "hazardous item" or an "art tool" depending on the context of the "art class" instruction and surrounding visual cues). This contrasts with LLMs that struggle to infer such details from textual input alone.
- **Versatile Goal Specification:** VILA extends beyond simple language instructions. It can accept:
 - Pure language instructions.
 - Goal images (e.g., an internet photo of a desired sushi arrangement, a child's drawing for a puzzle).
 - Multimodal inputs (e.g., an image of a desk + language instruction to swap two specific items). This allows for more natural and flexible task communication.
- **Natural Visual Feedback Integration:** Unlike methods that convert visual feedback to language, VILA directly uses the VLM to interpret updated visual observations. The VLM acts as both a scene descriptor and a success detector, enabling it to recognize changes, assess progress, and dynamically replan in response to environmental changes or failed skill executions.

**Experimental Setup:**

The efficacy of VILA is evaluated through extensive experiments in both real-world and simulated environments.

- **Real-World Manipulation Tasks:**
 - **Hardware:** Franka Emika Panda 7-DoF arm with a parallel jaw gripper, Logitech Brio color camera mounted on a tripod.
 - **Tasks:** 16 diverse long-horizon manipulation tasks, categorized into:
 - 8 tasks requiring commonsense knowledge (spatial layouts, object attributes).
 - 4 tasks demonstrating flexible multimodal goal specification.
 - 4 tasks requiring dynamic visual feedback.
 - **Evaluation:** 10 different environmental variations per task to assess robustness to changes in scene configuration and lighting.
 - **VLM/Prompting:** GPT-4V from OpenAI API, operating in a *strict zero-shot* mode (no in-context examples, only high-level instructions and simple constraints).
 - **Primitive Skills:** Script policies (teleoperated or kinematically taught) for "pick up," "place," "pour," "open," and "close" actions, applied consistently across VILA and baselines.
 - **Baselines:** SayCan and Grounded Decoding (GD), both relying on Llama 2 70B (due to OpenAI API limitations for token probabilities) and an open-vocabulary detector (OWL-ViT) for affordances.
- **Simulated Tabletop Rearrangement Tasks:**
 - **Environment:** RAVENS environment with a UR5 robot.
 - **Tasks:** 16 tasks, categorized into "Blocks & Bowls" (8 tasks) and "Letters" (8 tasks). Some tasks are "seen" (used for few-shot prompting/training baselines) and others "unseen."
 - **Prompts:** GPT-4V (zero-shot for Letters, few-shot for Blocks & Bowls due to domain gap with abstract shapes).
 - **Primitive Skills:** Script policies accessing ground-truth object states for fair comparison.
 - **Baselines:** CLIPort ("Short" for single-step, "Long" for high-level instructions), LLM-only (Llama 2, GPT-4), and Grounded Decoding (GD) with Llama 2 (using CLIPort's logits or ground-truth affordances).

This comprehensive methodology allows for a rigorous comparison against state-of-the-art approaches and a thorough demonstration of VILA's unique advantages.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of VILA across both real-world and simulated environments yielded compelling results, demonstrating its superior performance compared to existing LLM-based and imitation learning methods.

**Real-World Manipulation Tasks:**

- **Superior Commonsense Knowledge Understanding:** VILA achieved an average success rate of **80%** across 8 tasks requiring rich commonsense knowledge (spatial layouts, object attributes), significantly outperforming SayCan (13%) and Grounded Decoding (20%).
 - For tasks like "Take Out Marvel Model" (requiring obstacle removal) and "Righteous Characters" (contextual understanding of object attributes), SayCan and GD failed almost entirely, while VILA maintained high success rates (70-80%).
 - Qualitative examples (Figure 3) vividly illustrate VILA's ability to deduce necessary precursor actions (e.g., clearing items from a plate before picking it up) and contextualize object attributes (e.g., scissors as tools for art vs. hazardous items).
- **Reduced "Understanding Error":** The failure breakdown analysis (Figure 4) revealed that VILA drastically reduced "understanding error" (errors in comprehending complex spatial layouts and object attributes) compared to baselines. While baselines struggled with perception errors and understanding errors, VILA's direct visual grounding minimized these issues, leading to the lowest overall error rate.
- **Flexible Multimodal Goal Specification:** VILA demonstrated strong capabilities across 4 tasks with diverse goal types (Table II), including:
 - Arranging sushi based on a **real image** (80% success).
 - Arranging jigsaw pieces based on a **child's drawing** (100% success).
 - Picking vegetables based on a **pointing finger** in an image (100% success), requiring interpretation of abstract visual cues.
 - Tidying a desk based on a **combination of image and language** instructions (60% success).
- **Robust Visual Feedback Integration:** In 4 dynamic tasks requiring real-time adaptation (Table III), closed-loop VILA (with visual feedback) dramatically outperformed its open-loop variant (without feedback).
 - "Stack Blocks" (with injected noise leading to primitive skill failures): Closed-loop VILA achieved 90% success vs. 20% for open-loop.
 - "Pack Chip Bags" (with human intervention reverting progress): Closed-loop VILA achieved 100% success vs. 0% for open-loop.
 - "Find Stapler" (stapler hidden in varying locations): Closed-loop VILA achieved 90% success vs. 30% for open-loop, demonstrating effective re-planning and exploration (Figure 6).
 - "Human-Robot Interaction" (robot waiting for human action): Closed-loop VILA achieved 80% success vs. 20% for open-loop.

**Simulated Tabletop Rearrangement:**

- **Consistent Outperformance:** VILA consistently achieved significantly higher success rates across all 16 simulated tasks (Blocks & Bowls, Letters, both seen and unseen categories) compared to all baselines (CLIPort, LLM-only, and Grounded Decoding).
 - Overall average success rate for VILA was **80%** for Blocks & Bowls and **84.4%** for Letters (Table IV).
 - In contrast, CLIPort methods struggled with generalization, especially to "Long" horizon tasks and "Unseen" tasks.
 - LLM-only baselines (Llama 2, GPT-4) performed poorly due to lack of grounding (avg 13.1% & 13.8% for Blocks & Bowls; 18.8% & 18.8% for Letters).
 - Grounded Decoding (GD), while outperforming LLM-only, still lagged significantly behind VILA (e.g., 21.3% for Blocks & Bowls, 45.6% for Letters), further validating VILA's core hypothesis about the benefits of synergistic vision-language reasoning.

In summary, the results strongly support the paper's central claim: direct integration of perceptual data into a unified VLM for reasoning and planning (VILA) leads to a profound understanding of commonsense knowledge, enables versatile goal specification, and facilitates robust closed-loop planning, culminating in significantly superior performance in diverse and challenging robotic manipulation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VILA framework represents a significant leap forward in robotic task planning, with profound implications for the development of more intelligent, adaptable, and general-purpose robotic systems.

**Key Significance:**

- **Paradigm Shift in Robotic Planning:** VILA champions a paradigm where perception and high-level reasoning are unified within a single VLM, moving beyond the traditional modular approaches where LLMs rely on external, often disconnected, perception pipelines. This fundamental shift allows for truly "grounded" reasoning, mimicking how humans perceive and plan.
- **Unlocking Visually-Grounded Commonsense:** The work definitively demonstrates that VLMs, particularly GPT-4V, can imbue robots with a much deeper understanding of commonsense knowledge related to spatial layouts, object attributes, and environmental constraints. This ability to reason about occlusions, containment, and context-dependent object functions is crucial for robots operating in unstructured, open-world environments.
- **Enhanced Practicality through Versatile Goal Specification:** The capability to accept multimodal goals – including raw images, drawings, and even gestures alongside language – significantly broadens the usability and accessibility of robotic systems. It enables more intuitive human-robot interaction and allows for task specifications that are difficult or impossible to articulate purely through language.
- **Robustness via Natural Visual Feedback:** Integrating visual feedback directly into the VLM's planning loop makes robotic systems inherently more robust. Robots can adapt to dynamic environments, recover from unforeseen disturbances, and actively explore to complete tasks, a critical feature for real-world deployment.
- **Zero-Shot Generalization in Open Worlds:** The successful performance of VILA in zero-shot settings across a wide array of diverse tasks (real and simulated, seen and unseen) is highly significant. It suggests that pre-trained VLMs, with their vast internet-scale knowledge, can be powerful "universal planners" that do not require extensive domain-specific data collection or fine-tuning for every new task or environment. This significantly reduces development costs and accelerates deployment.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Accelerated Development of General-Purpose Robots:** By providing a highly capable and adaptable task planning backbone, VILA could accelerate the development of truly general-purpose robotic assistants for homes, warehouses, and factories, capable of performing a wide variety of tasks without explicit reprogramming.
- **More Intuitive Human-Robot Interaction:** The flexible multimodal goal specification and natural visual feedback mechanisms pave the way for more intuitive and seamless collaboration between humans and robots, where humans can communicate tasks in a more natural, human-centric manner.
- **Foundation for Embodied AI Research:** VILA offers a strong baseline and a new direction for embodied AI research, encouraging further exploration into how large foundation models can bridge the gap between abstract reasoning and physical interaction.
- **Applications Beyond Manipulation:** The principles demonstrated in VILA could extend to other robotic domains, such as navigation, assembly, and human care, where understanding complex environments and adapting to dynamic situations is paramount.
- **Inspiration for Future VLM Development:** The work highlights the specific strengths and remaining limitations of current VLMs for embodied tasks, which can inform future research into more physically grounded and action-oriented multimodal models.

While the paper acknowledges limitations (dependence on existing primitive skills, black-box nature of VLMs, potential for better prompt engineering), VILA marks a crucial step toward building truly intelligent and autonomous robots that can "look before they leap" and reason effectively in our complex, visual world.
