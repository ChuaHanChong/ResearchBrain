# IN-RIL: Interleaved Reinforcement and Imitation Learning for Policy Fine-Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Davis and Toyota InfoTech Labs introduce IN-RIL, an algorithm that interleaves imitation learning with reinforcement learning during policy fine-tuning, achieving up to 76% improvement in success rates on robotic manipulation tasks while maintaining training stability through novel gradient separation mechanisms.

## Problem

- Traditional RL fine-tuning after imitation learning often suffers from instability and poor sample efficiency
- Existing methods struggle to balance the benefits of IL (stability) with RL (exploration) during the fine-tuning phase

## Method

- ==Alternating IL== and RL updates during fine-tuning using a controlled interleaving ratio
- Implementing gradient separation through gradient surgery and network separation to prevent destructive interference
- Developing theoretical guarantees for convergence and sample efficiency

## Results

- Improved success rates from **12%** to **88%** on RobomimicTransport tasks compared to baseline IDQL
- Consistent performance improvements across 14 robotics tasks in multiple benchmarks
- Optimal interleaving ratios between 5-15 IL updates per RL update
- Demonstrated compatibility with multiple state-of-the-art RL algorithms including DPPO and IDQL

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Interleaving IL and RL updates throughout training provides better stability than sequential approaches
> - Gradient separation mechanisms are crucial for preventing conflicting updates between IL and RL objectives
> - The method works as a plug-in enhancement for various RL algorithms including both on-policy and off-policy approaches

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

- The paper addresses a well-known challenge in robotics policy learning: the trade-offs between imitation learning (IL) and reinforcement learning (RL). IL offers stability but struggles with generalization, while RL promotes exploration and generalization but can be unstable and sample-inefficient, especially in sparse-reward environments.
- The hybrid approach of IL-based pre-training followed by RL fine-tuning is already a popular paradigm. This paper builds upon this by addressing the limitations of the RL fine-tuning phase, which often suffers from instability and poor sample efficiency.
- The paper distinguishes itself from existing approaches that use demonstrations in replay buffers or policy regularization by proposing an *interleaved* approach, IN–RIL, where IL and RL updates are alternated throughout the fine-tuning process.
- The proposed gradient separation mechanisms (gradient surgery and network separation) are relevant in the context of multi-objective optimization, where preventing destructive interference between different objectives is a key challenge.
- The work connects to broader trends in robot learning, including diffusion policies, action chunking, and residual policy learning.
- Related work includes research on offline RL, where expert demonstrations are used to train RL policies. IN-RIL differs in that it doesn't necessarily require reward annotations in the IL data and can be applied to both on-policy and off-policy RL methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

- **Main Objective:** To develop a more efficient and stable fine-tuning method that synergizes the benefits of IL (stability) and RL (exploration) for robotics policy learning.
- **Specific Goals:**
 - To introduce a novel fine-tuning approach, IN–RIL, that interleaves IL and RL updates.
 - To develop gradient separation mechanisms to prevent destructive interference between IL and RL gradient updates.
 - To provide theoretical analysis to explain why IN–RIL outperforms conventional RL fine-tuning.
 - To demonstrate the effectiveness of IN–RIL as a general plug-in compatible with various RL algorithms.
- **Motivation:** The motivation stems from the limitations of existing IL+RL pipelines, where the RL fine-tuning phase often suffers from instability, performance collapse, and poor sample efficiency. The authors argue that the stability of IL and the exploration benefits of RL can be better combined through an interleaved approach.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

- **IN–RIL Framework:** The core of the approach is the IN–RIL algorithm, which alternates between RL updates (to maximize reward) and IL updates (to mimic expert demonstrations) during the fine-tuning phase.
- **Gradient Separation:** To address the potential conflict between IL and RL objectives, the authors propose two gradient separation mechanisms:
 - **Gradient Surgery:** Projects IL and RL gradients onto orthogonal subspaces to minimize interference.
 - **Network Separation:** Introduces a residual policy network updated by RL gradients, while the base policy continues to leverage IL, effectively separating the learning of different aspects of the policy.
- **Experimental Setup:**
 - Evaluation on 14 robotics tasks across FurnitureBench, OpenAI Gym, and Robomimic benchmarks.
 - Use of both on-policy (DPPO, residual PPO) and off-policy (IDQL) RL algorithms.
 - Comparison against state-of-the-art RL fine-tuning methods.
 - Ablation studies to analyze the impact of the interleaving ratio (m) and the gradient separation mechanisms.
- **Theoretical Analysis:** Mathematical analysis is presented to justify the effectiveness of IN–RIL and to derive conditions for its superiority over RL-only fine-tuning. The analysis includes convergence guarantees and sample efficiency conditions. The analysis relies on standard assumptions regarding the pretraining performance, data coverage, smoothness properties of the loss functions, and gradient estimation quality.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- **Superior Performance:** IN–RIL consistently outperforms RL-only fine-tuning across a diverse set of robotics tasks, including both manipulation and locomotion.
- **Improved Sample Efficiency:** IN–RIL achieves faster convergence and requires fewer environment interactions than RL-only methods.
- **Enhanced Stability:** IN–RIL mitigates performance collapse and instability during fine-tuning, particularly in sparse-reward environments.
- **Plug-in Compatibility:** IN–RIL can be seamlessly integrated with various state-of-the-art RL algorithms, acting as a general-purpose fine-tuning method.
- **Ablation Study Results:**
 - The interleaving ratio (m) has a significant impact on performance. Values between 5 and 15 generally work well.
 - Gradient separation mechanisms are crucial for preventing destructive interference between IL and RL objectives.
- **Significant Performance Boosts:**
 - On RobomimicTransport, IN–RIL boosted success rates from 12% (with IDQL) to 88%.
 - On FurnitureBench, IN–RIL significantly improved performance on challenging multi-stage assembly tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

- **Addresses a Key Challenge:** IN–RIL provides a practical solution to the problem of unstable and sample-inefficient RL fine-tuning in robotics.
- **Potential for Real-World Applications:** The improved performance and stability offered by IN–RIL could enable the development of more robust and efficient robotics systems for various applications, including manufacturing, healthcare, and logistics.
- **Generalizability:** The plug-in nature of IN–RIL makes it easily adaptable to different RL algorithms and robot platforms.
- **Theoretical Contribution:** The theoretical analysis provides a deeper understanding of the benefits of interleaved IL and RL, offering insights for designing more effective hybrid learning algorithms.
- **Future Directions:** The authors suggest exploring adaptive mechanisms to dynamically adjust the interleaving ratio based on gradient alignment and extending the approach to domains beyond robotics.
- **Broader Impact:** By making robot learning more efficient and reliable, IN-RIL could contribute to the widespread adoption of robotics in various industries and improve the capabilities of autonomous systems.

In conclusion, "IN–RIL: Interleaved Reinforcement and Imitation Learning for Policy Fine-Tuning" presents a significant contribution to the field of robotics policy learning. The proposed IN–RIL algorithm, along with its gradient separation mechanisms, offers a promising approach for addressing the limitations of conventional RL fine-tuning methods. The thorough experimental evaluation and theoretical analysis provide strong evidence for the effectiveness of IN–RIL and highlight its potential for enabling more robust and efficient robotics systems.
