# IN-RIL: Interleaved Reinforcement and Imitation Learning for Policy Fine-Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Davis and Toyota InfoTech Labs introduce IN-RIL, an algorithm that interleaves imitation learning with reinforcement learning during policy fine-tuning, achieving up to 76% improvement in success rates on robotic manipulation tasks while maintaining training stability through novel gradient separation mechanisms.

## Problem

- Traditional RL fine-tuning after imitation learning often suffers from instability and poor sample efficiency
- Existing methods struggle to balance the benefits of IL (stability) with RL (exploration) during the fine-tuning phase

## Method

- ==Alternating IL== and RL updates during fine-tuning using a controlled interleaving ratio
- Implementing gradient separation through gradient surgery and network separation to prevent destructive interference
- Developing theoretical guarantees for convergence and sample efficiency

## Results

- Improved success rates from **12%** to **88%** on RobomimicTransport tasks compared to baseline IDQL
- Consistent performance improvements across 14 robotics tasks in multiple benchmarks
- Optimal interleaving ratios between 5-15 IL updates per RL update
- Demonstrated compatibility with multiple state-of-the-art RL algorithms including DPPO and IDQL

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Interleaving IL and RL updates throughout training provides better stability than sequential approaches
> - Gradient separation mechanisms are crucial for preventing conflicting updates between IL and RL objectives
> - The method works as a plug-in enhancement for various RL algorithms including both on-policy and off-policy approaches
