# Robust Optimization for Mitigating Reward Hacking with Correlated Proxies

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces a robust optimization framework to mitigate reward hacking in reinforcement learning by training agents against worst-case reward functions within a defined uncertainty set. This approach, including a general Max-Min formulation and a feature-linearized variant, yields policies with superior worst-case returns and enhanced stability across varying proxy-true reward correlations compared to existing methods.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning agents often optimize imperfect proxy rewards, leading to "reward hacking" where agents achieve high proxy returns through unintended, undesirable behaviors.
- Existing mitigation methods are sensitive to the specific proxy reward chosen, which can be noisy or uncertain, and lack strong theoretical guarantees against a broad range of reward variations.
- Current black-box RL policies hinder interpretability, which is crucial for human oversight, trust, and risk mitigation in safety-critical applications.

## Method

- Formulates reward hacking as a robust ==max-min== policy optimization problem, where the policy maximizes its return against the worst-case reward from an r-correlated uncertainty set.
- Derives a tractable max-min objective function by employing a change-of-measure technique and solving the inner minimization for a closed-form adversarial reward.
- Introduces a Linear Max-Min variant that assumes a linear reward structure, leveraging feature whitening to obtain an interpretable, element-wise closed-form solution for adversarial feature weights.

## Results

- Both Max-Min and Linear Max-Min policies consistently achieved superior worst-case performance across five benchmark environments (e.g., Traffic, Pandemic, Glucose) compared to the ORPO baseline and its improved variant, ORPO*.
- The Max-Min method demonstrated higher average rewards and lower performance variance across a wide range of proxy–true reward correlation levels, indicating strong resilience to reward misspecification.
- The Linear Max-Min approach provided interpretable adversarial feature weights, diagnosing that features like 'headway' in the Traffic environment or 'early-stage transitions' in Pandemic were most exploited by the adversarial reward.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimizing against a worst-case reward within an r-correlated uncertainty set provides stronger theoretical guarantees against reward hacking compared to optimizing a fixed proxy.
> - The derived adversarial reward in both general and linear cases reveals which reward components or features are most vulnerable to exploitation by the agent, aiding interpretability.
> - Accurate estimation of the Radon-Nikodym derivative (occupancy ratio) through improved discriminator training is crucial for the stability and effectiveness of robust policy optimization algorithms.
