# Towards Dexterous Embodied Manipulation via Deep Multi-Sensory Fusion and Sparse Expert Scaling

> [!summary] Summary
> The DeMUSE framework introduces a Diffusion Transformer for robotic manipulation that deeply integrates RGB, depth, and 6-axis force feedback with sparse expert scaling. This approach achieves an 83.2% success rate on the augmented MetaWorld MT50 benchmark and demonstrates superior active compliance in real-world contact tasks.

## Problem

- Current vision-centric embodied AI paradigms often overlook crucial non-visual physical feedback like geometric depth and 6-axis force, which are vital for precise manipulation.
- Statistical disparities and representation imbalance among heterogeneous sensory signals (e.g., high-dimensional vision vs. sparse force data) hinder effective multimodal alignment and contribution to self-attention mechanisms.
- Traditional decoupled approaches for sensory forecasting and action generation result in temporal misalignment, compromising physical consistency in high-precision, long-horizon manipulation tasks.
- Dense scaling of large models for embodied intelligence leads to prohibitive computational costs and inference latencies, making real-time robotic control impractical.

## Method

- DeMUSE (Deep Multimodal Unified Sparse Experts) employs a Diffusion Transformer architecture for omni-modal embodied synthesis, using a unified denoising stream to synchronously generate future latent trajectories and continuous action chunks.
- Adaptive Modality-specific Normalization (AdaMN) dynamically synthesizes modality-specific scale and offset factors for each sensor type, addressing statistical heterogeneity and ensuring critical signals are not suppressed.
- A Sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) with a perennially active shared branch replaces standard Feed-Forward Networks in Transformer blocks, allowing efficient scaling of model capacity for physical priors while maintaining low inference latency.

## Results

- DeMUSE achieved an **83.2%** overall success rate on the augmented MetaWorld MT50 benchmark, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines like RDT-1B (**77.9%**) and RT-2 (**52.2%**).
- In real-world environments, DeMUSE demonstrated rapid active compliance (action corrections within 80ms) and stable force maintenance (around 10N) in contact-intensive tasks, significantly outperforming baselines which suffered from hazardous force surges.
- Ablation studies confirmed that sparse MoE variants, like MoE-4E, reduced computation by **42.6%** while achieving higher success rates (**83.2%**) compared to dense counterparts (**78.5%**), validating the efficiency and efficacy of sparse scaling.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Deep fusion of heterogeneous sensory inputs (RGB, depth, 6-axis force) within a unified denoising flow is critical for internalizing physically consistent latent dynamics and achieving precise, dexterous manipulation.
> - Modality-specific normalization (AdaMN) is essential for stabilizing multimodal transformers, preserving the distinct dynamics of diverse sensory cues, and ensuring better world-modeling fidelity and execution precision.
> - Sparse Mixture-of-Experts with a shared branch provides an efficient method to scale model capacity, allowing experts to specialize in different aspects (e.g., disentangling physical contact from spatial geometry) without increasing inference latency, crucial for real-time robot control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of general-purpose embodied agents capable of seamless interaction with the physical world represents a grand challenge in Artificial Intelligence. This paper positions itself squarely within this landscape, building upon recent advancements while critically addressing their limitations.

**Current Trends and Limitations:** The field has witnessed remarkable progress with Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models (e.g., RT-2, π0.5) and Diffusion-based policies (e.g., Diffusion Policy, RDT-1B). VLA models excel in semantic generalization through large-scale pre-training, while Diffusion Transformers (DiT) demonstrate exceptional expressive power in capturing complex action distributions. World models (e.g., DreamerV3, WOW) have also advanced by learning environmental dynamics in shared latent spaces. However, the authors argue that these state-of-the-art approaches are predominantly "vision-centric," treating physical interaction primarily as a visual task. This paradigm often overlooks crucial non-visual physical feedback, such as geometric depth and 6-axis force, which are indispensable for precise and physically consistent manipulation.

**Identified Gaps:** The paper highlights two intrinsic hurdles preventing deeper multi-modality integration:
1. **Multimodal Alignment Failure:** Heterogeneous sensory signals (e.g., high-dimensional vision vs. sparse force data) possess significant statistical disparities. This imbalance hinders effective alignment within joint distributions and suppresses the contribution of critical signals to self-attention mechanisms.
2. **Physical Consistency in Multimodal Dynamics:** Traditional decoupled approaches, which separately forecast sensory evolution and generate actions, often lead to temporal misalignment. This lack of synchronization between "latent imagination" and "motor execution" compromises performance in high-precision, long-horizon tasks.

**DeMUSE's Position and Contribution:** DeMUSE directly tackles these challenges. Inspired by the "multi-sensory processing logic of the biological brain," particularly the cerebrum's role in synthesizing diverse sensory inputs, DeMUSE proposes a "cerebral-style" deep synthesis framework. Unlike previous works that might treat depth or force as mere auxiliary constraints (described as "cerebellar-like reactive characteristics"), DeMUSE aims for *deep integration* where RGB, depth, and 6-axis force tokens "co-evolve within a unified denoising flow."

The paper also engages with the broader trend of efficient model scaling. While scaling laws have shown effectiveness in embodied intelligence, dense scaling often leads to prohibitive computational costs and inference latencies, making real-time robotic control impractical. DeMUSE integrates Sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures, a solution increasingly explored in large language models and diffusion models, to expand model capacity without sacrificing real-time performance.

In essence, DeMUSE pushes the boundaries of embodied AI by advocating for and demonstrating a truly multimodal foundation model that deeply integrates vision, geometry, and force, explicitly addressing the architectural and learning challenges inherent in such integration for dexterous physical manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching motivation behind DeMUSE is to overcome the limitations of current vision-centric embodied AI paradigms and achieve truly dexterous, physically consistent manipulation in complex, unstructured environments. This grand challenge necessitates a fundamental shift towards deep integration of heterogeneous multimodal sensory inputs.

The key objectives that drive the DeMUSE framework are:

1. **To realize deep multi-sensory integration:** The primary goal is to move beyond mere auxiliary use of non-visual sensors and instead deeply integrate RGB images, geometric depth, and 6-axis force feedback into a unified computational flow. This integration aims to create a holistic understanding of the physical world for an embodied agent.
2. **To develop a unified architecture for embodied synthesis:** DeMUSE seeks to build a single Diffusion Transformer architecture capable of simultaneously synthesizing environmental evolution (predicting future states) and generating precise action sequences, ensuring physical consistency between them.
3. **To address multimodal alignment failure:** A critical objective is to mitigate the statistical heterogeneity and representation imbalance among diverse sensory signals (e.g., high-dimensional vision vs. sparse force data). This involves ensuring that all critical signals align effectively and contribute meaningfully to the decision-making process.
4. **To ensure physical consistency and temporal coherence:** The framework aims to synchronize latent imagination (sensory forecasting) with motor execution (action generation) through a unified denoising process. This is crucial for high-precision, long-horizon tasks where decoupled paradigms typically fail.
5. **To enable efficient model scaling for physical priors:** DeMUSE aims to expand the model's capacity to store vast amounts of "physical priors" and learn complex multimodal dynamics without compromising the low inference latency required for real-time robotic control. This involves overcoming the computational bottlenecks associated with dense model scaling.
6. **To contribute to multimodal interaction data:** Recognizing the scarcity of high-quality multimodal interaction data, the authors aim to augment existing benchmarks and develop robust real-world data acquisition pipelines to facilitate further research in dexterous manipulation.

By achieving these objectives, DeMUSE aims to lay the groundwork for more robust, general-purpose embodied foundation models that can navigate and interact with the physical world with a level of dexterity and understanding currently lacking in AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DeMUSE (Deep Multimodal Unified Sparse Experts) is a Diffusion Transformer-based framework designed for omni-modal embodied synthesis, employing a unified denoising stream to generate future latent trajectories and continuous action chunks synchronously.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation and Joint Denoising Objective

The framework models the co-evolution of environmental states and agent actions within a unified representation space. It uses an offline dataset of observations `o_t = (I_t, D_t, f_t)` (RGB, depth, 6-axis force) and actions `a_t`. All sensory modalities are projected into a shared D-dimensional latent space and serialized as tokens. The core concept is a **Joint Denoising Process** where a joint objective `w_0 = [z_t+1:t+H, a_t:t+K-1]` (future visual evolution and action sequence) is perturbed, and the model learns a conditional denoiser `ϵ_θ(X | y, k)` to recover the clean target. `X` is a unified token sequence including current observations and the noisy target. This design ensures cross-modal dependencies and physical consistency.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture (Figure 2)

DeMUSE consists of three main parts:
- **Heterogeneous Embedding Layer:** Modality-specific encoders map raw inputs into a unified token space. RGB images are encoded via a VAE, while depth maps, 6-axis force, and action signals are projected through dedicated linear layers. The input sequence concatenates clean context with noisy target variables for both visual and action modalities.
- **Multimodal Backbone:** A stack of `L` Transformer blocks processes the concatenated tokens for cross-modal global integration.
- **Joint Prediction Head:** Estimates the noise `ˆϵ` to drive the reverse diffusion process.

#### 3.3. Key Architectural Innovations

- **Adaptive Modality-specific Normalization (AdaMN):** This mechanism addresses statistical heterogeneity across modalities. Instead of a monolithic normalization, AdaMN employs dedicated expert layers `ϕ_m` for each modality `m`. These layers dynamically synthesize modality-specific scale (`γ_m`) and offset (`β_m`) factors, conditioned on a global context `c = MLP(y, k)` (language instruction `y` and diffusion timestep `k`). This ensures each modality retains its activation specificity and is not suppressed by statistical averaging, enhancing multimodal alignment and providing flexibility for missing modalities.
- **Sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) with Shared Expert:** To scale model capacity without increasing inference latency, the standard Feed-Forward Network (FFN) layers within the Transformer blocks are replaced with a sparse MoE layer. This design includes a perennially active **shared branch** (`E_shared`) to stabilize representation and capture universal physical priors. Gated experts `{E_i}` specialize in modality-specific dynamics. The routing mechanism uses **top-1 routing** for efficiency (selecting the single best expert per token). An auxiliary load-balancing loss is applied to non-action tokens to prevent expert collapse while preserving specialized expert allocation for precise action signals.

#### 3.4. Learning Objectives and Inference

- **Joint Learning Objective:** DeMUSE is optimized by minimizing a multi-objective function `L`, combining Mean Squared Error (`L_mse`) and a Variational Lower Bound (`L_vb`) for both visual and action modalities. Force and depth signals serve as fixed physical conditions and do not contribute to loss gradients, focusing the model on generative dynamics under these constraints. An auxiliary load-balancing loss (`L_aux`) is applied to perceptual modalities (excluding action tokens) to prevent expert collapse and ensure balanced expert utilization.
- **Inference and Action Chunking:** To ensure temporal coherence and mitigate jitter, action chunking synchronizes the visual prediction horizon `H` with the action execution stride `K`. Exponential Moving Average (EMA) parameters are used as a temporal filter for smooth control. DDIM sampling with 16 steps is employed for optimal fidelity and real-time efficiency.

#### 3.5. Training Protocol

A two-stage training paradigm is adopted:
1. **Large-scale Pre-training:** On a subset of the Open X-Embodiment (OXE) dataset, using pre-trained DiT-XL/2 weights for the backbone. MoE layers are initialized with a sparse upcycling protocol (shared expert from original FFN, routed experts with Gaussian perturbation).
2. **Domain-specific Fine-tuning:** On augmented MetaWorld MT50 and custom real-world datasets, adapting the model to specific observation noise and contact dynamics.

#### 3.6. Datasets and Experimental Setup

- **Enhanced Heterogeneous Simulation:** An augmented MetaWorld MT50 benchmark was developed, integrating high-precision, synchronized 6-DoF force and depth signals. Force signals are transformed to the end-effector frame, smoothed, and normalized. Depth maps undergo median filtering.
- **Real-world Platform and Data Pipeline:** A UR10 robot arm, OnRobot RG2-FT force-controlled gripper, and Intel RealSense D435i camera form the hardware suite. A robust event-driven asynchronous data pipeline with dual-timestamping ensures synchronization of heterogeneous sensor rates and mitigates clock drift. 100 expert trajectories per task were collected for four representative real-world tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of DeMUSE demonstrates significant advancements in dexterous embodied manipulation across both simulation and real-world environments, validating the necessity of deep multi-sensory integration.

#### 4.1. Comprehensive Performance

- **State-of-the-Art Success Rates:** DeMUSE achieved an impressive 83.2% overall success rate on the augmented MetaWorld MT50 benchmark, outperforming all compared state-of-the-art baselines, including Diffusion Policy (29.2%), RT-2 (52.2%), PAD (72.4%), ForceVLA (75.9%), and RDT-1B (77.9%). The gains were particularly pronounced in "Medium" and "Hard" tasks, with DeMUSE exceeding the strongest baselines by 7.1 and 6.5 percentage points, respectively.
- **Precision in Contact-Intensive Tasks:** In tasks requiring intricate physical interaction, such as HAMMER-V2 and BASKETBALL-V2, DeMUSE achieved success rates as high as 96.0%, indicating its superior ability to capture critical contact dynamics through omni-modal deep fusion.
- **Real-World Robustness:** In physical environments, DeMUSE achieved a 72.5% overall success rate across four challenging tasks (Sweeping, Dispense Sanitizer, Tool Drawer Org., Fill Cup), significantly outperforming RDT-1B (59.0%) and Diffusion Policy (26.5%). This demonstrates DeMUSE's exceptional robustness and ability to bridge the sim-to-real gap.
- **Qualitative Consistency:** Visualizations of the joint denoising process (Figure 3) showed high alignment between the model's predicted future visual evolution and actual execution results, confirming that DeMUSE internalizes physically-consistent latent dynamics for precise intervention.

#### 4.2. Multimodal Synergy and Active Compliance

- **Superior Contact Dynamics (Figure 4):** In the "Dispense Sanitizer" task, DeMUSE demonstrated stable active compliance, triggering action corrections within approximately 80ms of contact and maintaining a stable force of around 10N. Baselines, in contrast, suffered from hazardous force surges (>15N). This rapid and stable response is attributed to the AdaMN mechanism prioritizing sparse force signals.
- **Terminal State Precision (Figure 5):** For long-horizon tasks like "Fill Cup," DeMUSE achieved remarkable terminal accuracy (0.33 ± 0.02 liquid level), leveraging the synergy between vision and 6D force for enhanced anticipatory capacity and precise system shutdown.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Significance of Multimodal Integration (Table 4):**
 - Adding force feedback (RGB-F) yielded a 9.0% improvement in success rate for "Hard" MetaWorld tasks, underscoring its criticality for dexterous manipulation.
 - Depth integration showed marginal gains in simulation but was significantly more pronounced in real-world trials, providing essential geometric grounding against sensory noise.
 - Vision-only policies (RGB-only) suffered substantial performance degradation (-27.0%) in real-world scenarios due to environmental noise, highlighting DeMUSE's ability to mitigate perceptual distribution shifts through stable geometric and physical feedback.
- **Deep Fusion vs. Conditional Modulation (Table 4):** Directly comparing "Ours (Full)" with "Conditioned" (where depth and force are only injected via AdaMN as modulation parameters, not as self-attention tokens), revealed that mere feature modulation (77.5% SR) performs similarly to RGB-only. This decisively validates that deep fusion, where modality tokens co-evolve within a global receptive field, is indispensable for internalizing consistent physical laws.
- **Role of AdaMN (Table 5):** AdaMN achieved superior LPIPS (0.35 vs. 0.41 for Standard AdaLN) and higher success rates (83.2% vs. 78.6%). This confirms that AdaMN's modality-specific affine recalibration is essential for stabilizing multi-modality transformers, preserving distinct dynamics of force and geometric cues, and ensuring better world-modeling fidelity and execution precision.
- **Scaling Analysis and MoE Specialization (Figure 6):**
 - Sparse MoE variants significantly improved efficiency. MoE-4E (1.57B parameters, 135 GFLOPs) achieved an 83.2% success rate, representing a 42.6% compute reduction compared to a dense 1.53B variant (235 GFLOPs) that only reached 78.5% SR.
 - This non-linear gain is attributed to MoE-induced modality-wise specialization, allowing experts to disentangle physical contact dynamics from spatial geometry.
 - MoE-4E was identified as the optimal configuration, balancing performance with inference efficiency and training stability.

In summary, DeMUSE consistently demonstrates superior performance and robustness, primarily due to its innovative deep multi-sensory fusion architecture, which includes AdaMN for modality alignment and a sparse MoE for efficient scaling. The ablation studies compellingly support the necessity and effectiveness of each proposed architectural component.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Towards Dexterous Embodied Manipulation via Deep Multi-Sensory Fusion and Sparse Expert Scaling" carries significant implications for the future of embodied AI and robotic manipulation. DeMUSE represents a substantial step forward in realizing general-purpose, physically intelligent agents.

#### 5.1. Advancement in Embodied AI and Robotic Dexterity

The most immediate impact is the profound enhancement of robotic dexterity. By moving beyond conventional vision-centric paradigms, DeMUSE enables robots to perform complex manipulation tasks with unprecedented precision and robustness in dynamic, unstructured environments. This deep integration of RGB, depth, and 6-axis force allows robots to develop a more nuanced "physical common sense," critical for contact-rich interactions that were previously challenging. This advancement could accelerate the deployment of robots in areas requiring fine motor skills, such as advanced manufacturing, delicate assembly, surgical assistance, and complex service robotics.

#### 5.2. Towards Unified Foundation Models for Robotics

DeMUSE paves the way for truly unified, full-modality embodied foundation models. By demonstrating that synchronized perception-action synthesis, leveraging heterogeneous sensory inputs, can be achieved within a single Diffusion Transformer architecture, the paper offers a blueprint for future generalist robot policies. Such models hold the promise of learning broad physical priors and generalizing across a wide array of tasks and environmental conditions, much like large language models generalize across linguistic tasks.

#### 5.3. Novel Architectural Solutions for Multimodal Deep Learning

The introduction of **Adaptive Modality-specific Normalization (AdaMN)** and the **Sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) with shared experts** provides innovative solutions to two fundamental challenges in multimodal AI:
- **AdaMN** effectively addresses the representation imbalance and statistical heterogeneity inherent in diverse sensory signals. This mechanism is crucial for ensuring that sparse but critical feedback (like force) is not overshadowed by high-dimensional data (like vision), making multimodal fusion more robust and effective. Its adaptive nature also offers flexibility for varying sensor configurations.
- The **sparse MoE with shared experts** demonstrates how to efficiently scale model capacity for complex physical priors without incurring prohibitive inference latency. This is a critical factor for real-time robotic control, ensuring that advanced intelligence does not come at the cost of responsiveness. This architectural pattern could be adopted more widely in other resource-constrained, real-time AI applications.

#### 5.4. Addressing Data Scarcity in Multimodal Robot Learning

The contribution of an augmented MetaWorld MT50 benchmark and a robust real-world data acquisition pipeline is highly impactful. Data scarcity, especially for synchronized, high-fidelity multimodal interaction data, is a significant bottleneck in robot learning research. By providing these resources, the authors enable other researchers to build upon their work and accelerate progress in the field.

#### 5.5. Bridging Neuroscience and AI

The explicit inspiration drawn from the "multi-sensory processing logic of the biological brain" (specifically, the cerebrum's role in synthesizing heterogeneous signals) highlights a growing trend of cross-disciplinary insights. This biological grounding suggests that mimicking natural intelligence's multimodal integration strategies can yield more capable artificial systems.

In conclusion, DeMUSE represents a significant leap towards more intelligent and dexterous robotic agents. Its contributions in deep multimodal fusion, architectural innovations for efficiency and alignment, and practical data generation efforts will likely catalyze further research and development, ultimately leading to more adaptable and capable robots that can seamlessly interact with the complex physical world.
