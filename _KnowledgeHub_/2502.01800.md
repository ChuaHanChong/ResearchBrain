# Flow-based Domain Randomization for Learning and Sequencing Robotic Skills

> [!summary] Summary
> GoFlow replaces hand-tuned domain randomization with a normalizing-flow neural sampling distribution over simulator parameters, trained jointly with a PPO actor-critic via entropy-regularized reward maximization plus a self-paced KL term. The expressive flow captures multimodal, correlated feasible regions, yielding higher policy coverage across six simulated domains and 9/10 real gear-insertion successes, while doubling as an out-of-distribution detector for belief-space planning.

## Problem

- Hand-specified uniform or Gaussian randomization ranges demand delicate tuning: too broad traps training in local minima, too narrow leaves policies brittle at deployment.
- Prior learned distributions use simple parametric families (multivariate Gaussians, independent beta distributions, expanding uniform intervals) that cannot express multimodal or inter-parameter-dependent feasible regions.
- System-identification methods fit dynamics from real rollouts, but such hardware data is costly or unavailable, and adversarial schemes wrongly assume every sampled environment is solvable.

## Method

- **GoFlow** parameterizes the environment distribution as a ==neural spline flow== that seeds episode-level simulator parameters for an ==asymmetric PPO actor-critic== whose critic receives privileged parameters.
- The flow maximizes a joint objective: expected return under the sampled distribution, plus ==differential entropy== for coverage, minus a ==self-paced KL penalty== against the previous distribution for stability.
- Reward and entropy terms are ==importance-sampled== from a uniform proposal to prevent collapse; flow density thresholded against the privileged value function yields ==belief-space preconditions== for a ==BFS planner==.

## Results

- Across Cartpole, Ant, Quadcopter, Quadruped, Humanoid and Gears in IsaacLab, GoFlow matches or exceeds the coverage of FullDR, NoDR, ADR, LSDR and DORAEMON over **five seeds**.
- Real Franka gear insertion succeeds **9/10** trials for GoFlow versus **6/10** for full randomization, **5/10** each for ADR, LSDR and DORAEMON, and **3/10** without randomization.
- On the 2D toy domain GoFlow recovers the multimodal, correlated energy landscape that baselines miss, and degrades more gracefully than them as randomization ranges widen.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Expressivity of the sampling distribution matters: feasible parameter regions are often off-center, irregular and correlated, which axis-independent or unimodal families structurally cannot represent.
> - A learned training distribution is reusable at deployment as an out-of-distribution detector, telling a planner when to gather information instead of acting blindly.
> - Flexibility costs stability: flow training shows higher variance, and planning preconditions still need manually tuned density and value thresholds per environment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses critical challenges in **reinforcement learning (RL) for robotics**, specifically the **sim-to-real gap**. RL policies are often trained in simulated environments due to the inefficiency, safety concerns, and cost associated with real-world data collection. However, discrepancies between simulation and reality (e.g., inaccuracies in physics models, sensor noise, material properties) often lead to learned policies that fail to transfer effectively to physical robots.

**Domain Randomization (DR)** has emerged as a leading technique to bridge this gap. By randomizing various environment parameters (e.g., object masses, friction coefficients, sensor noise) during simulation training, policies learn to be robust to variations, thereby improving their generalization to unknown real-world conditions. Traditionally, DR involves manually defining sampling distributions (e.g., uniform or Gaussian distributions with fixed ranges and variances) for these parameters. This manual tuning is a delicate and labor-intensive process:
- **Too broad a distribution:** Can lead to training on "unsolvable" environments, hindering policy convergence or leading to suboptimal local minima.
- **Too narrow a distribution:** Results in policies that are not sufficiently robust and fail to generalize to the full range of real-world uncertainties.

This paper positions itself within a growing body of research that seeks to **automate the discovery of optimal domain randomization distributions**. Existing approaches in this learned DR landscape include:
- **Adversarial methods:** These approaches (e.g., Mehta et al., 2020) aim to find challenging environment configurations that push the policy to its limits, but often assume all sampled environments are solvable.
- **Curriculum-based methods:** These strategies (e.g., Mozifian et al., 2019; OpenAI et al., 2019; Tiboni et al., 2024) progressively expand the complexity or diversity of the training distribution while maintaining policy success. They typically use simpler parameterized distributions like multivariate Gaussians or independent Beta distributions, or rely on boundary sampling.

The key innovation presented in this paper is the use of **normalizing flows** for the neural sampling distribution. Normalizing flows are powerful generative models capable of transforming simple base distributions into highly complex, multi-modal target distributions, offering greater flexibility and expressivity than the simpler parametric models used in prior learned DR methods. This allows for capturing intricate dependencies and non-Gaussian characteristics in the optimal randomization space, which simpler models cannot.

Furthermore, the paper extends beyond merely learning robust policies. It delves into **multi-step manipulation planning under uncertainty and partial observability**. This is a critical area because many real-world robotic tasks are long-horizon and require sequential decision-making, where perception and environmental parameters are uncertain. The paper leverages the learned sampling distributions and privileged value functions to act as "out-of-distribution detectors" or "belief-space preconditions" within a planning framework. This enables the robot to actively gather information when its current belief about the world state indicates a low probability of policy success, linking low-level skill learning with high-level cognitive planning. This integration represents a significant step towards more autonomous and robust robotic systems capable of reasoning about their own limitations and acting to overcome them.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are twofold:

1. **To develop and demonstrate a novel learned domain randomization method, GoFlow, that utilizes normalizing flows to automatically discover highly flexible and expressive sampling distributions for environment parameters.** The motivation here is to overcome the limitations of manually specified or simpler parameterized distributions. Manual tuning is time-consuming and often suboptimal, while existing learning-based methods using simpler distributions may fail to capture complex, multi-modal, or interdependent relationships between environment parameters, leading to policies that are not robust enough. GoFlow aims to maximize the diversity of parameters while ensuring the environments remain solvable, thereby leading to highly generalizable policies.
2. **To explore how these learned sampling distributions, coupled with a privileged value function, can be effectively used for out-of-distribution detection in an uncertainty-aware, multi-step manipulation planner.** The motivation stems from the recognition that even highly robust policies have limits to the uncertainties they can tolerate. For long-horizon tasks, robots need to reason about their capabilities and actively seek information when conditions fall outside the policy's expected success region. This objective aims to bridge the gap between low-level skill learning (via RL and DR) and high-level strategic planning, enabling robots to make more informed and adaptive decisions in partially observable real-world scenarios.

In essence, the paper aims to make RL policies more robust and generalize better to real-world conditions by enhancing the domain randomization process, and then to leverage the artifacts of this improved training (the learned distribution and value function) to build more intelligent, uncertainty-aware robotic systems capable of complex, multi-step tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper introduces **GoFlow**, a novel approach to learned domain randomization, and then demonstrates its application in multi-step manipulation planning.

#### 3.1. GoFlow: Flow-based Domain Randomization

GoFlow's core idea is to jointly optimize the reinforcement learning policy and a neural sampling distribution using a normalizing flow.

- **Joint Optimization Objective:** GoFlow optimizes the following objective function:
 `max_{p,π} { E_{ξ~p} [J_ξ(π)] + αH(p) - βD_KL(p_old || p) }`
 - **`E_{ξ~p} [J_ξ(π)]`**: This term maximizes the expected return of the policy `π` over environments sampled from the learned distribution `p(ξ)`. This encourages the policy to perform well across the randomized domain.
 - **`αH(p)`**: This is an entropy regularization term, maximizing the differential entropy `H(p)` of the sampling distribution `p(ξ)`. This encourages `p(ξ)` to be broad and explore a diverse range of environment parameters, enhancing generalization. `α > 0` controls its weight.
 - **`- βD_KL(p_old || p)`**: This term is a KL divergence regularization, minimizing the divergence between the current sampling distribution `p(ξ)` and the previous one `p_old(ξ)`. This acts as a self-paced learning mechanism, preventing abrupt changes in the distribution and ensuring training stability. `β > 0` controls its weight.

- **Normalizing Flows for `p(ξ)`:** GoFlow uses **Neural Spline Flows** (Durkan et al., 2019) to parameterize the sampling distribution `p(ξ)`. Normalizing flows are powerful generative models that transform a simple base distribution (e.g., a standard normal distribution) into a complex target distribution via a sequence of invertible, differentiable functions. This provides a highly flexible and expressive way to model `p(ξ)`, allowing it to capture complex, multi-modal, and interdependent relationships between domain parameters, unlike simpler parametric distributions (e.g., Gaussians or Betas).

- **Training Process (Algorithm 1):**
 1. **Policy Training:** In each iteration, a batch of environment parameters `ξ_train` is sampled from the current normalizing flow `p_ϕ(ξ)`. These sampled environments are used to train the policy `π_θ` using the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm.
 2. **Privileged Information for Critic:** The critic network, which estimates the state-value function `V_ψ(s_t, ξ)`, is given access to the environment parameters `ξ` (privileged information). This helps the critic provide more accurate and lower-variance value estimates, stabilizing policy training, while the actor network `π_θ(a_t|s_t)` only observes the state `s_t`, ensuring realism.
 3. **Sampling Distribution Update:** After the policy update, the normalizing flow parameters `ϕ` are updated. Crucially, the reward and entropy terms for the flow optimization are estimated using **importance sampling from a uniform distribution `u(ξ)`** over the entire parameter space. This is a critical design choice, as it prevents the learned distribution `p_ϕ` from collapsing onto small, well-performing regions and encourages global exploration of the parameter space, even for regions not yet mastered by the current policy. The KL divergence term, however, samples from `p_old(ξ)` for stability. These terms form a loss function that is differentiated to update `ϕ`.

#### 3.2. Application to Multi-step Manipulation Planning

GoFlow extends its utility beyond skill learning by integrating the learned sampling distribution and the privileged value function into a **belief-space planning framework**.

- **Belief-Space Preconditions:** The learned sampling distribution `p_ϕ(ξ)` and the privileged value function `V_ψ(s, ξ)` are used to define **belief-space preconditions** for high-level robotic skills (e.g., "insert gear"). A precondition `Pre_π` for a skill `π` is defined as the set of belief states `b` where the expected value of the policy is greater than a success threshold `J_T`, and importantly, where the environment parameters `ξ` are sufficiently covered by the learned flow (`p_ϕ(ξ) > ϵ`). This `ϵ` threshold ensures that the robot only trusts the value estimates in regions of the parameter space that were adequately sampled during training.
 `Pre_π = { b ∈ B | E_b [1_{V_ψ(s,ξ)>J_T} · 1_{p_ϕ(ξ)>ϵ}] > η }`
 where `η` is the desired probability of success. This formulation allows the robot to assess if its current belief state (uncertainty about environment parameters) is conducive to successful skill execution.

- **Belief Updating:** To operate in belief space, the system requires a probabilistic state estimation system that outputs posterior distributions over unobserved environment variables. The paper employs **Bayes3D** (Gothoskar et al., 2023), a probabilistic object pose estimation framework, to infer posterior distributions over object pose from RGB-D observations. This system captures uncertainty in pose estimates due to distance, occlusion, or symmetries, providing a rich belief state for the planner.

- **Simple Belief-Space Planner:** The paper demonstrates the concept using a **simple Breadth-First Search (BFS) belief-space planner**. This planner considers a set of high-level skills, including the GoFlow-trained manipulation policies (e.g., `insert_yellow_gear`) and an "inspection" action. The inspection action reduces uncertainty in the pose estimate by moving the camera closer to the object. The planner leverages the computed preconditions: if a skill's precondition is not met (i.e., the current belief state indicates too much uncertainty for successful execution), the planner can explore alternative actions, such as the inspection action, to reduce uncertainty until the precondition for a manipulation skill is satisfied. This enables active information gathering.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents compelling evidence for the efficacy of GoFlow through extensive simulated and real-world experiments.

- **Superior Domain Coverage in Simulation:**
 - GoFlow consistently matches or outperforms all baselines (FullDR, NoDR, ADR, LSDR, DORAEMON) across six diverse simulated robotics domains: Cartpole, Ant, Quadcopter, Quadruped, Humanoid, and the contact-rich Gears task.
 - The primary metric for comparison is "coverage," defined as the proportion of the total sampling distribution for which the policy achieves a reward higher than a predefined threshold (`J_T`).
 - A significant finding is GoFlow's particular strength in environments where the optimal or feasible regions of the domain parameter space are "off-center, irregularly shaped, and have inter-parameter dependencies." Unlike simpler learned DR methods (LSDR, DORAEMON, ADR) that rely on Gaussian, Beta, or expanding uniform distributions, GoFlow's use of normalizing flows allows it to accurately model and explore these complex, multi-modal distributions (as visually demonstrated in the illustrative 2D domain in Figure 2).
 - Appendix experiments further support this, showing that GoFlow degrades more gracefully with increased parameter ranges compared to baselines, and its dominance in coverage holds for wide ranges of success thresholds.

- **Enhanced Sim-to-Real Transfer:**
 - For the real-world contact-rich gear insertion task, GoFlow-trained policies demonstrate superior robustness and sim-to-real transfer compared to other baselines.
 - In 10 real-world trials of pink gear insertion, GoFlow achieved a 9/10 success rate, significantly outperforming FullDR (6/10), NoDR (3/10), DORAEMON (5/10), LSDR (5/10), and ADR (5/10). This directly validates GoFlow's ability to learn policies that generalize well to real-world uncertainties.

- **Enabling Uncertainty-Aware Multi-step Planning:**
 - The paper successfully demonstrates the integration of GoFlow's learned distributions and privileged value functions into a belief-space planning framework for a multi-step gear assembly task.
 - The robot, initially uncertain about object poses, uses the learned preconditions to determine if a direct insertion policy is likely to succeed. When uncertainty is too high (e.g., for smaller gears or those further away), the planner intelligently chooses an "inspection" action to gather more information, thereby reducing pose uncertainty (as visualized by the belief posteriors).
 - This demonstrates how the learned artifacts from GoFlow can enable active information gathering, allowing the robot to autonomously decide when it has "insufficient information" and needs to act to improve its world model before attempting a manipulation skill. This effectively extends the applicability of learned short-horizon skills to complex, long-horizon problems under partial observability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Flow-based Domain Randomization for Learning and Sequencing Robotic Skills" paper makes significant contributions to the fields of reinforcement learning and robotics, with several potential impacts:

- **Advancement in Learned Domain Randomization:** GoFlow's primary significance lies in pushing the boundaries of automated domain randomization. By leveraging the expressive power of normalizing flows, it moves beyond simpler parametric distributions, allowing for the discovery of more complex, multi-modal, and interdependent domain parameter distributions. This directly addresses a critical bottleneck in sim-to-real transfer: the need for sophisticated environmental modeling and robust policy learning. The demonstrated superior coverage and real-world transfer highlight its potential to reduce manual effort in tuning DR parameters and achieve higher generalization for robotic skills.
- **Enhanced Sim-to-Real Transfer for Complex Tasks:** The robust performance of GoFlow, particularly in contact-rich manipulation tasks like gear insertion, signifies a leap forward in applying RL to challenging industrial and real-world problems. Contact-rich tasks are notoriously difficult due to unpredictable dynamics and high sensitivity to environmental variations. By enabling more effective sim-to-real transfer in such scenarios, GoFlow can accelerate the deployment of intelligent robotic systems in manufacturing, assembly, and other sectors requiring high precision and adaptability.
- **Bridging Skill Learning and High-Level Planning:** The integration of learned sampling distributions and value functions as belief-space preconditions for multi-step planning is a crucial conceptual and practical advancement. It provides a principled way to:
 - **Quantify Policy Robustness:** The learned distributions inherently encode the regions of environment parameters where a policy is robust.
 - **Enable Uncertainty-Aware Decision-Making:** Robots can use this knowledge to reason about their own limitations and the trustworthiness of their sensory inputs, allowing them to proactively gather information (e.g., inspect objects) when conditions are outside the learned policy's comfort zone.
 - **Facilitate Long-Horizon Autonomy:** This framework moves towards more intelligent and autonomous robotic systems that can chain learned skills, adapt to unforeseen circumstances, and complete complex, multi-step tasks by actively managing uncertainty.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** While the paper acknowledges limitations like potential training instability and the need for manual tuning of certain thresholds, it provides a strong foundation for future work. This includes exploring more robust training schemes for normalizing flows in RL contexts, automating threshold selection, and integrating these learned artifacts into more sophisticated belief-space planning algorithms (e.g., POMDP solvers). The concept of using a learned distribution as an "out-of-distribution detector" for robotic policies is highly impactful and applicable across various domains beyond manipulation.

In conclusion, GoFlow represents a significant step towards developing more intelligent, adaptable, and robust robotic systems capable of operating autonomously in complex and uncertain real-world environments by improving both the learning of individual skills and their integration into higher-level decision-making processes.
