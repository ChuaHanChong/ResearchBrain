# ADAPT: Agile Diffusion Action Priors for Robust and Steerable Online Text-Driven Humanoid Control

> [!summary] Summary
> ADAPT is an end-to-end framework for interactive text-driven humanoid whole-body control. It trains a text-conditioned diffusion policy on retargeted, physically tracked state-action trajectories with frame-level language labels, then adds a constrained residual RL correction and a noise-steering module for downstream tasks. On a Unitree G1 it reaches 0.984 success under rapid prompt switching at 2 ms inference, with smooth transitions and style-preserving goal reaching.

## Problem

- Two-stage text-to-motion pipelines generate kinematic references separately from tracking, causing dynamics mismatch and infeasible references when commands switch rapidly.
- End-to-end language-action policies train on sequence-level captions, giving no supervision at transition boundaries and poor responsiveness to on-the-fly prompt changes.
- Diffusion controllers steered by test-time classifier guidance are computationally costly at inference and unstable under noisy or out-of-distribution guidance signals, blocking real humanoid deployment.

## Method

- **ADAPT**'s ==diffusion skill prior==: 8-layer ==causal transformer== denoises joint future state-action trajectories from ==CLIP== text plus proprioceptive history, per-frame noise levels, 2 ==DDIM== steps with ==classifier-free guidance==.
- ==Constrained residual RL==: ==PPO== learns lower-body-masked corrective actions on the frozen prior, with residual-scale warmup and a ==self-tracking reward== toward the diffusion-predicted next state.
- ==Noise-steering RL==: a PPO policy outputs task-conditioned initial diffusion noise, adapting the frozen prior to goal reaching while preserving text-specified motion style.

## Results

- ADAPT attains **0.984** success under prompt switching versus **0.764** for a two-stage DART pipeline, **0.522** for offline TextOp, **0.923** for LangWBC, with best smoothness and foot sliding.
- Ablating residual RL drops success from **0.984** to **0.804**; removing the lower-body spatial constraint raises success to **0.997** but collapses R@1 from **44.60** to **26.19**.
- Noise steering lifts goal-reaching success from **18.0%** to **97.1%** and cuts falls from **34.7%** to **2.9%**, reaching **95.5%** on the unseen 'jog' style.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Frame-level rather than clip-level language labels expose a policy to intra-sequence skill transitions, which is what enables responsive on-the-fly prompt switching.
> - Unconstrained residual RL buys robustness by silently overriding the commanded behavior; spatial masking and self-tracking rewards are what keep semantics intact.
> - Freezing a generative prior and learning only in its input-noise space adapts it to new objectives cheaply, avoiding expensive test-time guidance and retraining.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses the challenge of enabling humanoid robots to execute diverse whole-body motions based on natural language commands in a closed-loop, interactive manner. This domain requires continuous mapping of robot states and language commands to actions while maintaining balance, motion quality, and smooth transitions between skills.

Existing approaches to language-driven humanoid control generally fall into two categories, each with limitations:

- **Two-stage methods:** These pipelines, exemplified by works such as SONIC, Kimodo, and TextOP, first generate kinematic motion references from text. A separate low-level controller then tracks these references. The primary limitation of this approach is the potential for dynamics mismatch. When commands change rapidly, the generated kinematic references may become dynamically infeasible, posing challenges for the low-level controller to track accurately. This can lead to instability or failures in real-time deployment. SafeFlow attempts to mitigate this by adding a safety gate, but the fundamental separation remains.

- **End-to-end methods:** Approaches like LangWBC and SENTINEL directly map language and robot states to actions, thereby circumventing the dynamics-mismatch issue. However, these methods are often trained on datasets with coarse, sequence-level language annotations. This provides insufficient temporal granularity at the boundaries between different behaviors, limiting the policy's exposure to rapid intra-sequence command changes during training. Consequently, these methods are typically designed for executing a commanded behavior over an extended horizon rather than for responsive, on-the-fly prompt switching.

Recent advancements in diffusion models have established them as a policy class for robot control due to their capacity to represent multimodal action distributions and their stable scaling to high-dimensional action spaces. Prior work, such as UniPhys and BeyondMimic, has explored steering diffusion policies using test-time classifier-based guidance. However, this guidance can be computationally expensive during inference and may lead to unstable motion, particularly with noisy or out-of-distribution signals, making real humanoid deployment challenging due to latency and robustness requirements.

This paper positions ADAPT as an end-to-end framework that integrates a diffusion-based policy with fine-grained language supervision to overcome these limitations. It aims to achieve real-time interactive text-driven control with rapid prompt switching on a physical humanoid, and to provide a reusable text-conditioned motion prior, distinguishing it from both two-stage and existing end-to-end methods, as well as prior work on diffusion policy steering.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The research outlines several key objectives driven by the aforementioned limitations in the field of language-conditioned humanoid control:

1. **Develop an End-to-End Closed-Loop Control Framework:** The primary objective is to create an end-to-end system that directly maps language commands and current physical states to low-level control actions. This approach is intended to mitigate the dynamics mismatch issues inherent in two-stage pipelines.

2. **Enable Robust Interactive Control with Rapid Prompt Switching:** The framework aims to allow the humanoid robot to continuously respond to changing natural language commands. This includes maintaining balance, generating natural whole-body motions, and executing smooth transitions between diverse skills, even when commands are switched rapidly. The motivation here is to move beyond methods designed for sustained behaviors to achieve genuine interactive responsiveness. This is addressed by using fine-grained, skill-level language supervision from datasets like BABEL, which exposes the policy to skill transitions during training.

3. **Learn a Diverse Repertoire of Whole-Body Skills Directly from Language:** A core goal is to train a diffusion-based action prior that can learn a multimodal distribution of natural whole-body skills. This prior should be capable of directly executing these skills based on language commands, enabling a broad range of behaviors.

4. **Improve Long-Horizon Robustness and Smooth Transitions:** While behavior cloning provides a foundation, it can suffer from distribution shift and instability during prolonged execution or abrupt command changes. A specific objective is to enhance the robustness of the learned diffusion policy through a lightweight post-training mechanism, specifically a residual reinforcement learning (RL) policy, to ensure stable and smooth operation in dynamic environments.

5. **Provide a Reusable Text-Conditioned Motion Prior for Downstream Task Adaptation:** Beyond interactive control, the research aims for the learned diffusion policy to serve as a general and reusable motion prior. This prior should be adaptable to various downstream tasks, such as goal reaching, without requiring retraining of fundamental humanoid behaviors. This adaptability should also preserve the language-specified motion styles.

6. **Achieve Real-Time Deployment on Physical Humanoid Hardware:** A practical motivation is to ensure that the proposed framework operates efficiently enough to enable real-time, closed-loop control on a physical humanoid robot, demonstrated on the Unitree G1. This requires managing inference latency and ensuring sufficient robustness for real-world execution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ADAPT is designed as an end-to-end framework integrating a text-conditioned diffusion policy with reinforcement learning (RL) modules for robustness and task adaptation. The methodology encompasses three main stages: dataset curation, diffusion policy pretraining, and RL-based post-training.

#### 3.1. Dataset Curation

The foundation of ADAPT is a physically grounded offline dataset of text-labeled humanoid state-action trajectories.
- **Motion Source:** The process begins with human motion data from AMASS and fine-grained frame-level motion annotations from BABEL.
- **Retargeting and Tracking:** AMASS motions are retargeted to the Unitree G1 humanoid morphology using GMR. These retargeted motions are then tracked in a physics simulation environment (IsaacLab) using a pre-trained tracking policy, specifically from TextOp.
- **Data Collection:** Rolling out the tracking policy generates humanoid trajectories, which are paired with observations (o_t), actions (a_t), and the corresponding frame-level text labels (ℓ_t) from BABEL. Only successfully tracked sequences are retained to ensure the quality of action labels.
- **Domain Randomization:** To improve robustness and reduce overfitting, domain randomization is applied during data collection. This involves perturbing contact properties (static and dynamic friction, restitution), joint calibration, actuator dynamics (stiffness and damping scales), and mass distribution (torso CoM shift). This exposes the tracking policy to a broader distribution of executable demonstrations, beneficial for subsequent behavior cloning.

#### 3.2. Diffusion-based Skill Prior Pretraining

A text-conditioned diffusion model is pretrained using the curated dataset to learn a diverse repertoire of humanoid skills.
- **Modeling Approach:** Instead of solely predicting the next action, the model learns the joint distribution of future actions and future proprioceptive states. This state-action formulation enables the model to predict both control commands and the expected evolution of the humanoid state.
- **Trajectory Construction:** Training clips of length T=20 frames are used, where the first H=5 frames serve as historical context and the remaining frames form the prediction horizon. Proprioceptive observations (o_t) include root linear velocity (v_t), angular velocity (w_t), projected gravity (g_t), joint positions (q_t), and joint velocities (dq_t), along with the previous action (a_{t-1}).
- **Diffusion Training:** A diffusion model is trained to denoise the future segment of state-action trajectories, conditioned on the clean history context and the text command. The model predicts a velocity target (v_k) and is trained with a velocity field regression loss. The denoising network is an 8-layer causal transformer decoder with 512 hidden dimensions and 8 attention heads. Independent noise levels are assigned per frame to align with autoregressive rollout during inference.
- **Language Conditioning:** Text commands (ℓ) are encoded using a frozen CLIP text encoder. For classifier-free guidance at inference, the model is jointly trained with a 10% probability of replacing the text embedding with a null embedding.
- **Real-time Considerations:** Root linear velocity entries in the history context are zeroed out during training to prevent policy dependence on potentially noisy real-time velocity estimates.
- **Closed-loop Execution:** At test time, the model operates as a receding-horizon closed-loop controller. Given the current history and text command, it iteratively denoises a future trajectory using two DDIM denoising steps with a classifier-free guidance scale of 2.5. The first predicted action is executed, the history window is updated, and the process repeats at 50 Hz.

#### 3.3. Residual RL-based Action Correction

To address robustness issues arising from pure behavior cloning, especially during rapid prompt switching or long-horizon tasks, a lightweight residual reinforcement learning (RL) module is trained.
- **Mechanism:** This module predicts a small corrective action (∆a_res_t) on top of the action (a_diff_t) predicted by the frozen diffusion policy. The final action is computed as a_t = a_diff_t + α * m ⊙ ∆a_res_t, where α is a residual scale and m is a binary mask.
- **Inputs:** The residual policy conditions on the proprioceptive history, the diffusion-predicted action (a_diff_t), and the diffusion-predicted next state (o_hat_t+1).
- **Constraints and Regularization:**
 - **Spatial Constraint:** Corrections are restricted to the lower body (hip, knee, ankle, and waist joints) via mask (m) to focus on balance improvements while preserving upper-body motion semantics.
 - **Residual Scale Warmup:** The residual scale (α) is linearly warmed up from 0 to 0.05 over 100,000 environment steps to allow the policy to learn small, stabilizing adjustments before stronger interventions.
 - **Self-Tracking Reward:** A reward term encourages the physical state resulting from the corrected action to remain close to the diffusion-predicted next state (o_hat_t+1). This regularizes the residual policy to preserve the intended motion semantics of the diffusion prior.
- **Training:** The residual policy is optimized using PPO. During training, text prompts are randomly switched every 5–10 seconds to expose the policy to diverse skill transitions. The reward function includes terms for self-tracking, residual action regularization, termination penalties, foot-sliding penalties, and action smoothness.

#### 3.4. Reusable Prior for Downstream Task Adaptation

The pretrained diffusion policy is adapted for downstream tasks by steering its sampling process.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of modifying the diffusion policy, a separate steering policy (π_ϕ) is trained to output a task-conditioned noise vector (z_steer_t). This noise vector is then used as the initial input noise for the frozen diffusion policy. The diffusion policy, conditioned on history and text, then generates an action based on this steered noise: a_steer_t = π_θ(· | τ_hist, ℓ, z_steer_t).
- **Inputs:** The steering policy conditions on the task goal (g), proprioceptive history (τ_hist), and text command (ℓ).
- **Training:** The steering policy is trained using PPO. This approach is demonstrated on a goal-reaching task where the humanoid must navigate to a target location while maintaining a language-specified motion style (e.g., "jog"). The reward system encourages progress towards the target, successful arrival, and standing once the goal is reached. Text commands for locomotion are randomly sampled during training to ensure the steering policy functions across diverse styles without relearning locomotion behaviors.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments presented evaluate ADAPT's performance across interactive text-driven control, ablations of its components, and its reusability for downstream tasks.

#### 4.1. Interactive Text-Driven Control

ADAPT was benchmarked against existing two-stage and end-to-end methods on the Unitree G1 humanoid using a pool of 130 diverse commands. Rollouts of 20 seconds included prompt switches every 5–10 seconds.
- **Robustness and Success Rate:** ADAPT achieved a success rate of 0.984 (no fall), which significantly surpassed two-stage baselines like DART (0.764) and Offline TextOp (0.522). It also outperformed the end-to-end CVAE-based LangWBC (0.923). This indicates ADAPT's capacity for robust online, closed-loop operation.
- **Motion Quality:** ADAPT exhibited low action smoothness scores (0.0086 overall, 0.0107 during transitions) and low foot sliding (0.055), suggesting smooth and natural motion execution.
- **Semantic Alignment:** The full ADAPT model showed competitive R@k scores (R@1: 44.60%, R@3: 64.95%, R@5: 71.26%) and a human alignment score of 50.0%. While the behavior cloning (BC)-only variant of ADAPT achieved higher R@1 (59.50%), the full model, which includes residual correction, demonstrated a beneficial trade-off, accepting a modest reduction in semantic alignment for a substantial gain in physical robustness and safety during deployment.
- **High-Dynamic Skills:** ADAPT maintained high success rates for high-dynamic motions such as running and jumping, areas where two-stage pipelines typically struggle due to dynamically infeasible kinematic references.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Performance evaluation on a real Unitree G1 (Table 2) showed reliable transfer for locomotion skills. Failures predominantly occurred in motions requiring prolonged single-leg support (e.g., kicking) or repeated highly dynamic jumping, with the robot often recovering to a standing posture before complete loss of balance.

#### 4.2. Residual RL and Sampling Ablations

Ablation studies provided insights into the contributions of the residual RL design and diffusion sampling configurations.
- **Impact of Residual RL:** The residual RL module was shown to be essential, improving the overall success rate from 0.804 (BC-only diffusion policy) to 0.984.
- **Spatial Constraint:** Removing the spatial constraint on residual corrections resulted in the highest success rate (0.997) but led to a significant degradation in R-Precision (R@1: 26.19%), indicating semantic collapse where the residual policy overrode the commanded motion. This highlights the importance of the spatial constraint in preserving the diffusion prior's motion semantics.
- **Warmup and Self-Tracking:** Both the residual scale warmup and the self-tracking reward regularized residual learning. Without warmup, R@1 decreased to 42.22%, suggesting that unconstrained early corrections could perturb the pre-trained prior. The absence of self-tracking led to decreased R@3 and R@5, indicating poorer semantic alignment. The full residual design achieved the optimal balance between robustness and semantic preservation.
- **Sampling Steps:** Using two DDIM steps for inference provided the best balance between performance and latency (2 ms). Increasing to five steps offered no consistent gain in semantic alignment (R@3 was lower) while doubling inference time to 4 ms and reducing success. A single step substantially degraded both alignment and robustness.

#### 4.3. Prior Steering for Goal Reaching

The steering policy (Section 3.4) was evaluated on a goal-reaching task, assessing its ability to adapt the frozen diffusion prior while preserving text-specified motion styles.
- **Task Completion and Robustness:** Across various motion styles, the steering policy achieved a high task completion success rate (97.1% aggregated) and significantly reduced the fall rate from 34.7% (random noise samples) to 2.9% (steered noise). This indicated that noise steering effectively adapted the prior to downstream objectives and enhanced policy robustness.
- **Style Preservation and Generalization:** The steering module successfully guided the humanoid to target locations while preserving language-specified styles. It also generalized effectively to an unseen text prompt, "jog" (not present in the diffusion policy's training data), achieving 95.5% success and a 4.5% fall rate, comparable to seen styles. This demonstrated the reusability and adaptability of the framework.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ADAPT framework contributes to the field of humanoid robotics by addressing several critical limitations in text-driven control.

- **Advancement in Interactive Humanoid Control:** ADAPT presents an end-to-end, diffusion-based solution for interactive, text-conditioned humanoid whole-body control that directly addresses the challenges of dynamics mismatch in two-stage methods and coarse temporal granularity in previous end-to-end approaches. Its capacity for robust online operation with rapid prompt switching on a physical robot (Unitree G1) represents a step toward practical, deployable humanoid controllers.

- **Unified Framework for Control and Adaptation:** The work demonstrates a unified framework that supports both direct language-conditioned skill execution and adaptation to downstream tasks. This modularity, utilizing a frozen diffusion prior with lightweight RL modules for specific enhancements (robustness via residual RL, task adaptation via noise steering), offers an efficient paradigm for developing complex humanoid behaviors.

- **Enhanced Robustness and Real-Time Performance:** The integration of constrained residual RL significantly improves the closed-loop robustness of diffusion policies, making them suitable for real-time deployment on hardware. The optimization for fast inference (2 ms with 2 DDIM steps) underscores the feasibility of using diffusion models in latency-critical robotic applications.

- **Effective Skill-Level Language Grounding:** The use of fine-grained, skill-level language supervision (from BABEL) is demonstrated to be crucial for developing policies that can handle responsive and smooth transitions between diverse behaviors, contributing to more natural and controllable robot interactions.

- **Potential for Broader Applications:** The steerable prior approach opens avenues for combining language-defined motion styles with diverse task objectives (e.g., navigation, manipulation), facilitating the creation of more versatile and intelligent humanoid agents. This reusability reduces the need to retrain foundational behaviors for every new task.

- **Directions for Future Research:** The paper identifies limitations, such as occasional bias of high-dynamic motions toward safer behaviors by the residual controller and the trade-off between model capacity and inference efficiency. These points suggest future work in semantic preservation rewards, model distillation, or asynchronous control pipelines to further enhance performance and generalization without compromising real-time responsiveness.
