# ProxiDex: Learning Dynamics-Guided Proximity Policy for Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> ProxiDex is a dexterous imitation-learning framework that treats hand-object proximity as an interaction state. It reconstructs occlusion-free hand-object point clouds, converts distances into region-level proximity cues, learns action-conditioned latent dynamics coupling forward prediction with proximity decoding, and gates proximity tokens by manipulation phase, falling back to predicted latents under occlusion. It reaches 83.9% average simulated success and 72.6% on real robots.

## Problem

- Vision-based teleoperation and visuomotor policies lose fingertip contact regions to hand occlusion, while tactile sensors add hardware-specific modalities, costly calibration, and poor cross-platform scalability.
- Most dexterous imitation policies map observations to actions end-to-end, treating contact signals as auxiliary inputs without modeling how actions change hand-object proximity over time.
- Latent dynamics pretraining on observation features prioritizes salient motion like object-pose change, overlooking fine-grained hand-object distance evolution, and its latents lack explicit physical meaning.

## Method

- ==Interaction-aware data pipeline==: SAM3D object reconstruction with metric-scale calibration plus FoundationPose++ tracking builds hand-object interaction point clouds; Gaussian-kernel distances give region-level proximity, also shown as VR teleoperation feedback.
- ==Forward-inverse latent interaction dynamics==: a DiT forward dynamics model predicts future observation latents from actions, while an inverse differential module decodes latent differences into VAE proximity latents.
- ==Trajectory-adaptive proximity policy==: a causal-masked Transformer diffusion policy gates proximity tokens by predicted interaction-phase probability; at inference, dynamics-consistency checking switches to autoregressive dream mode when prediction error exceeds threshold.

## Results

- Simulation across Adroit, DexArt, and three IsaacLab tasks: **83.9%** average success versus CordViP **81.0%**, AFRO **76.9%**, DP3 **71.8%**; Pen reaches **84.3%** versus CordViP **76.2%**.
- Real robot: **72.6%** average versus CordViP **62.9%** and DP3 **40.0%**; unseen objects **85/120** versus **77/120**, perturbations **67/90** versus **56/90**, best on contact-rich twist and flip.
- Ablations: removing interaction point clouds or proximity cuts average success **7.5** and **7.0** points; removing forward dynamics, inverse module, or closed-loop checking costs **4.0**, **2.3**, and **1.1**; **2-s** occlusion recovery is **80%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Geometric hand-object proximity from reconstructed meshes rivals fingertip tactile arrays for pre-grasp and contact-establishment cues without calibration burden, though it cannot measure contact force.
> - Anchoring latent dynamics to a physically meaningful target, such as proximity decoded from latent differences, makes representations sensitive to fine contact changes that generic future-feature prediction overlooks.
> - A learned forward model doubles as a runtime consistency checker, bridging brief perception dropouts, but open-loop dream mode stays reliable only about 3.5 seconds before drift accumulates.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

A multi-fingered robot must adjust its hand around an object, yet its own hand can hide the fingertip–object relationships the controller needs for grasping, insertion, and object turning. Tactile sensors can provide contact information, but they often require specialized hardware and calibration. ProxiDex takes a different route. It reconstructs the hand and object as geometry and turns their separation into a proximity cue. It then teaches the policy how that proximity state changes under actions. The controller's input shifts from "what can the camera see right now?" to "where are the hand and object relative to one another, and how is that relationship changing?" This is geometry-derived proximity, not a measurement of contact force. In simulation, the paper reports a modest average success advantage over a close baseline that already uses interaction geometry. It also tests recovery when the camera is briefly occluded.

Immersive teleoperation with proximity feedback, and dexterous manipulation in simulation and the real world.

### 2. From reconstructed geometry to a changing interaction state

The pipeline has four steps:

1. Reconstruct the target object from an initial image and calibrate its scale.
1. Track the object's 6D pose.
1. Combine sampled points from the object model with points from the robot hand model, transformed into the robot base frame.
1. Compute hand-to-object distances, map them to proximity strengths, and aggregate these by hand region.

The resulting interaction point cloud preserves modeled hand–object geometry. The region-level proximity vector gives a compact cue about near-field interaction. A palm-region threshold supplies a coarse approach-versus-interaction phase label. The cue is hardware-agnostic in the sense that it is computed geometrically rather than read from a specific tactile device. It depends on reconstruction and pose tracking, and it is not force sensing.

Learning then centers on how this state evolves. A forward dynamics model (FDM) predicts future observation features from the current encoded state and action sequence. An inverse differential module (IDM) reads changes in those features and estimates the corresponding proximity latent. That estimate is trained to align with a latent encoding of the region-level proximity measurements. A phase predictor then lets the diffusion policy rely more on global scene information during approach and strengthen local proximity cues during interaction.

### 3. What the comparisons show

The closest baseline is CordViP, which also uses reconstructed hand–object interaction point clouds. The benchmark covers ten simulated tasks: three Adroit, four DexArt, and three custom IsaacLab tasks, with 30 demonstrations per task. On overall mean success rate, ProxiDex scores 83.9% (±1.8) versus 81.0% (±1.9) for CordViP. This is a modest aggregate advantage, not a win on every task. CordViP scores higher on Faucet (56.5% versus 53.4%), and both methods score 93.0% on Laptop. The comparison is also between complete systems. It is not a controlled test that attributes the entire difference to proximity dynamics alone.

A second test places a cuboid occluder in the depth-camera view for 2, 4, and 6 seconds on the Adroit Pen task. Full ProxiDex achieves 80% and 72% recovery after 2- and 4-second occlusions, then falls to 35% at 6 seconds. Without IDM, recovery is 71% and 59% at 2 and 4 seconds. The authors report that removing FDM causes the largest drop. These results support resilience to brief perception failures, not indefinite operation without vision.

### 4. Proximity helps, but is not touch

ProxiDex's contribution is to treat hand–object proximity as a state that evolves under action, and to use those dynamics in policy learning and inference. Dynamics-based takeover helps bridge short visual failures, but prediction errors accumulate during longer occlusions. Performance also depends on object reconstruction and pose tracking. The geometry-based cues cannot supply real contact forces or precise fingertip force distributions.
