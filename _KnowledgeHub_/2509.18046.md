# HuMam: Humanoid Motion Control via End-to-End Deep Reinforcement Learning with Mamba

> [!summary] Summary
> HuMam, an end-to-end deep reinforcement learning framework, integrates a Mamba encoder for humanoid locomotion control, demonstrating enhanced training stability, improved sample efficiency, and reduced actuation costs. This approach increased peak task performance by 5.8%, required 42.5% fewer samples to achieve a high return threshold, and reduced average joint torques by 9.6% compared to a feedforward neural network baseline.

## Problem

- Developing reliable locomotion controllers for humanoid robots is challenging due to their large mass, high center of gravity, and complex coupled upper-lower body dynamics.
- Existing end-to-end deep reinforcement learning methods for humanoids often suffer from training instability, inefficient feature fusion from diverse inputs, and high actuation costs.
- Conventional neural network backbones may not effectively exploit structural information in heterogeneous observations, while computationally intensive sequence models are impractical for real-time robotic deployment.

## Method

- The **HuMam** framework employs a single-layer Mamba encoder as a lightweight backbone to efficiently fuse robot-centric states (e.g., joint positions, velocities, body orientation) with external guidance (e.g., footstep targets, gait phase).
- A hierarchical control structure is used where the high-level policy outputs desired joint position targets, which a low-gain proportional-derivative (PD) controller then tracks at a higher frequency for smooth torque generation.
- A multi-term reward function for ==Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)== is designed to encourage stable, accurate, smooth, and energy-efficient gaits by penalizing excessive forces, promoting smooth swing motions, and maintaining desired body postures.

## Results

- HuMam achieved a **5.8%** increase in peak task performance and a **5.5%** increase in mean returns during extended training compared to a feedforward network baseline.
- The framework demonstrated superior learning efficiency, requiring **42.5%** fewer environment samples to reach a return of 260, and improved training stability by reducing cross-seed learning curve deviation by **35.4%**.
- Learned policies reduced average joint torques by **9.6%** and lowered average power consumption for standing by **39%**, leading to more energy-efficient and physically economical locomotion across various tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A Mamba encoder can effectively process and fuse multimodal observations in a state-centric manner for humanoid locomotion, capturing structured dependencies with computational efficiency compared to traditional networks or heavier sequence models.
> - Integrating a Mamba encoder promotes energy-efficient control through smoother representations and enhances robustness to observation noise and overall training stability.
> - A well-structured multi-term reward function combined with a hierarchical control scheme involving low-gain PD controllers is crucial for achieving balanced and energy-efficient bipedal gaits in complex humanoid systems.
