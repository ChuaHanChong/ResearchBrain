# HuMam: Humanoid Motion Control via End-to-End Deep Reinforcement Learning with Mamba

> [!summary] Summary
> HuMam, an end-to-end deep reinforcement learning framework, integrates a Mamba encoder for humanoid locomotion control, demonstrating enhanced training stability, improved sample efficiency, and reduced actuation costs. This approach increased peak task performance by 5.8%, required 42.5% fewer samples to achieve a high return threshold, and reduced average joint torques by 9.6% compared to a feedforward neural network baseline.

## Problem

- Developing reliable locomotion controllers for humanoid robots is challenging due to their large mass, high center of gravity, and complex coupled upper-lower body dynamics.
- Existing end-to-end deep reinforcement learning methods for humanoids often suffer from training instability, inefficient feature fusion from diverse inputs, and high actuation costs.
- Conventional neural network backbones may not effectively exploit structural information in heterogeneous observations, while computationally intensive sequence models are impractical for real-time robotic deployment.

## Method

- The **HuMam** framework employs a single-layer Mamba encoder as a lightweight backbone to efficiently fuse robot-centric states (e.g., joint positions, velocities, body orientation) with external guidance (e.g., footstep targets, gait phase).
- A hierarchical control structure is used where the high-level policy outputs desired joint position targets, which a low-gain proportional-derivative (PD) controller then tracks at a higher frequency for smooth torque generation.
- A multi-term reward function for ==Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)== is designed to encourage stable, accurate, smooth, and energy-efficient gaits by penalizing excessive forces, promoting smooth swing motions, and maintaining desired body postures.

## Results

- HuMam achieved a **5.8%** increase in peak task performance and a **5.5%** increase in mean returns during extended training compared to a feedforward network baseline.
- The framework demonstrated superior learning efficiency, requiring **42.5%** fewer environment samples to reach a return of 260, and improved training stability by reducing cross-seed learning curve deviation by **35.4%**.
- Learned policies reduced average joint torques by **9.6%** and lowered average power consumption for standing by **39%**, leading to more energy-efficient and physically economical locomotion across various tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A Mamba encoder can effectively process and fuse multimodal observations in a state-centric manner for humanoid locomotion, capturing structured dependencies with computational efficiency compared to traditional networks or heavier sequence models.
> - Integrating a Mamba encoder promotes energy-efficient control through smoother representations and enhances robustness to observation noise and overall training stability.
> - A well-structured multi-term reward function combined with a hierarchical control scheme involving low-gain PD controllers is crucial for achieving balanced and energy-efficient bipedal gaits in complex humanoid systems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of reliable locomotion controllers for humanoid robots is a significant challenge within the field of robotics. Humanoids are designed for deployment in human-centric environments such as industrial assistance, healthcare, disaster response, and space exploration. However, their physical characteristics, including larger mass, higher center of gravity, and complex coupled upper-lower body dynamics, make locomotion control demanding and safety-critical compared to wheeled or quadrupedal systems.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a promising approach for developing sophisticated locomotion policies for legged robots. Early successes demonstrated that policies trained in simulation, often incorporating dynamics randomization, could transfer to real-world quadrupeds, enabling agile skills like precise velocity tracking and self-righting without manual gait generators. Subsequent work further improved robustness in varied natural environments using purely proprioceptive policies and leveraged massively parallel GPU simulators for rapid policy acquisition and sim-to-real transfer. Techniques like Rapid Motor Adaptation have also introduced learned adaptation modules for online adjustment to novel conditions. For stable periodic gaits, RL research has explored reward shaping with clock signals and parameterized reference motions.

While significant progress has been made with quadrupeds, applying RL to humanoid platforms presents amplified difficulties due to factors such as larger link masses, high-gear transmissions, and reduced backdrivability, which increase safety risks and complicate contact-rich exploration. Prior research in humanoid RL has primarily focused on high-fidelity simulations. Approaches have included demonstration-augmented rewards to bias exploration towards human-like gaits, curriculum learning for omnidirectional walking policies, and hierarchical designs combining learned high-level policies with model-based low-level regulators. Efforts have also addressed precise stepping tasks through curriculum-driven learning and real-time perception.

The current work situates itself within this context by addressing specific limitations in existing end-to-end RL approaches for humanoid locomotion. These limitations include training instability, inefficient feature fusion from heterogeneous inputs (e.g., robot states and external guidance), and high actuation costs. Existing conventional feedforward backbones may not effectively exploit structural information in observations, while computationally intensive sequence models like Transformers impose practical trade-offs. This paper introduces a novel approach by incorporating a Mamba encoder, a lightweight state-space model, as a fusion backbone, representing the first known application of Mamba in an end-to-end humanoid RL controller to mitigate these challenges.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an end-to-end deep reinforcement learning framework for humanoid locomotion that provides enhanced training stability, improved sample efficiency, and reduced actuation costs. The framework aims to enable humanoids to perform diverse locomotion tasks with high performance and energy economy.

This overarching objective is driven by several key motivations and challenges identified in the current landscape of humanoid motion control:

- **Compact Perception-Action Mapping:** End-to-end reinforcement learning offers the advantage of directly mapping robot perceptions to actions, potentially simplifying the control architecture. However, practical implementations often encounter issues.
- **Training Instability:** RL policies for complex systems like humanoids can be difficult to train robustly, leading to inconsistent performance across different training runs or extended training durations.
- **Inefficient Feature Fusion:** Humanoid locomotion control requires processing a diverse set of inputs, including internal robot states (e.g., joint positions, velocities, body orientation) and external guidance (e.g., target footsteps, gait phase). Conventional neural network backbones (e.g., feedforward networks) may not effectively integrate these heterogeneous inputs to capture their structured dependencies, leading to suboptimal control policies. Conversely, more advanced sequence models like Transformers, while effective for complex sequences, can be computationally demanding, which is a significant constraint for real-time robotic deployment.
- **High Actuation Cost:** Learned policies often result in jerky movements or excessive torque usage, leading to high power consumption and accelerated wear on actuators. This is a critical concern for the practical, long-duration deployment of humanoids with finite onboard power resources.
- **Need for Foresightful and Resource-Aware Controllers:** Effective humanoid locomotion demands controllers that can anticipate future states to coordinate precise foot placement and whole-body balance, while also being mindful of the robot's onboard computational and actuation limits.

To address these motivations, the authors propose **HuMam**, a state-centric end-to-end RL framework. The core motivations for its design choices include:
- **Leveraging Mamba for Fusion:** Introducing a single-layer Mamba encoder to efficiently fuse robot-centric states with oriented footstep targets and a continuous phase clock. Mamba's architecture is hypothesized to offer better feature mixing and capture structural dependencies with lower computational overhead compared to traditional feedforward networks or heavier sequence models.
- **Promoting Energy-Efficient Control:** Designing a multi-term reward function for Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) that implicitly encourages stable, accurate, smooth actuation, and energy-efficient gaits by penalizing excessive forces, promoting smooth motions, and maintaining desired body postures.
- **Ensuring Stable Actuation:** Employing a hierarchical control structure where the high-level policy outputs joint position targets, which are then tracked by a low-gain proportional-derivative (PD) controller. This approach aims to yield smooth torques and maintain a well-conditioned learning environment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research presents HuMam, an end-to-end reinforcement learning framework designed for humanoid locomotion control. The methodology encompasses a Markov Decision Process (MDP) formulation, a novel Mamba encoder backbone for observation processing, a multi-component reward function, and policy optimization using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).

#### 3.1. Overview of the Framework

The HuMam pipeline collects robot-centric states (e.g., joint positions and velocities, body orientation) and external states (e.g., target footsteps, gait phase clock signals) as observations. These heterogeneous features are projected into a latent embedding and then processed by a single-layer Mamba encoder. This encoder is intended to mix features using state-space dynamics without requiring explicit temporal accumulation, aiming for computational efficiency. The encoded representation subsequently feeds into policy and value heads, which are optimized using PPO. A hierarchical control scheme is adopted, where the high-level policy generates desired joint positions at 40 Hz, while a low-gain PD controller operates at 1000 Hz to convert these into executable joint torques, effectively incorporating PD tracking behavior. The learning process is guided by a six-term reward function designed to encourage stable and energy-efficient bipedal gaits.

#### 3.2. Problem Formulation as a Markov Decision Process (MDP)

The humanoid motion control task is formalized as an MDP, defined by the state space, action space, transition dynamics, reward function, and discount factor.

- **Observation Space ($s_t$):** At each time step, the agent receives a combined observation of robot-centric and external states.
 - **Robot State ($s_{robot,t}$):** Includes 12 joint positions and velocities of the actuated leg joints, as well as the root orientation (roll and pitch) and angular velocity (roll, pitch, yaw rates). The global yaw angle is excluded as footsteps are relative to the root frame.
 - **External State ($s_{external,t}$):** Comprises the next two planned footsteps, each specified by a 3D position (x, y, z) and a heading angle ($\theta$) relative to the root frame. A two-dimensional continuous clock signal, represented as [sin(2$\pi\phi/L$), cos(2$\pi\phi/L$)] where $\phi$ is the phase variable and $L$ is the cycle period, encodes the gait phase to avoid discontinuities.

- **Action Space ($a_t$):** The action consists of 12 desired joint position targets for the robot’s actuated leg joints. These targets are applied as offsets from a nominal half-sitting posture. A low-gain PD controller then converts these position targets into joint torques, operating at a higher frequency (1000 Hz) than the policy (40 Hz).

- **Reward Function ($R_t$):** A composite six-term reward function is designed to balance stability, accuracy, and efficiency. The weights for these terms are fixed: $\alpha_{force}=0.15, \alpha_{vel}=0.15, \alpha_{step}=0.45, \alpha_{orient}=0.05, \alpha_{height}=0.05, \alpha_{upper}=0.05$.
 - **Foot Force ($R_{force,t}$):** Penalizes the squared L2 norm of normalized ground reaction forces, encouraging stable contact.
 - **Foot Velocity ($R_{vel,t}$):** Penalizes the squared L2 norm of the swing-foot velocity, promoting smooth swing motions.
 - **Step Reward ($R_{step,t}$):** An exponential function rewarding accurate foot placement, measured by the squared L2 distance between the swing-foot position and the next target.
 - **Orientation ($R_{orient,t}$):** An exponential function rewarding alignment of the root orientation with a desired target, using quaternion difference.
 - **Body Height ($R_{height,t}$):** An exponential function maintaining a nominal root height.
 - **Upper-Body ($R_{upper,t}$):** An exponential function penalizing sway of the head relative to the root, promoting upper-body stability.

#### 3.3. Mamba Encoder Backbone

The core of HuMam is a single-layer Mamba encoder used as a lightweight backbone for processing multimodal observations.
- **Tokenization:** Robot-centric and external states are concatenated, then mapped into two modality-specific embeddings ($z_{robot,t}, z_{ext,t}$) of dimension $d$ using a lightweight projection module. These are then concatenated to form a token sequence $U_t \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times d}$.
- **State-Space Encoding:** The Mamba encoder applies gated state-space dynamics to each token. This formulation is characterized by an internal latent state that evolves based on current token inputs and a recurrent-like structure, but without explicit temporal recurrence across successive time steps $t$. It is described as having a convolutional view with exponentially decaying kernels, which enables efficient feature mixing.
- **Output Representation:** The processed output tokens are aggregated into a compact feature vector ($h_t$), which serves as the shared input for the policy and value networks.
- **Claimed Advantages:** The Mamba encoder is claimed to promote energy-efficient control through smoother representations, ensure computational efficiency due to its near-linear complexity, and improve robustness to observation noise and stability through selective gating dynamics.

#### 3.4. Policy Optimization with PPO

Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is employed for policy training due to its stability in continuous control tasks.
- **Clipped Surrogate Objective:** PPO maximizes a clipped surrogate objective function that limits the magnitude of policy updates, preventing unstable changes.
- **Advantage Estimation:** Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) is used to reduce variance in advantage estimates.
- **Critic Optimization:** The value function (critic) is optimized by minimizing the squared error between its predictions and empirical returns.
- **Exploration:** An entropy bonus is added to the overall loss to encourage exploration during training.
- **Overall Loss Function:** Combines the clipped policy loss, value loss, and entropy bonus with respective weighting factors.

#### 3.5. Environment Setup and Evaluation

Experiments are conducted on the JVRC-1 humanoid robot model in the mc-mujoco physics simulator. The robot has 12 actuated leg joints. Five locomotion scenarios are used for training and evaluation: forward walking, backward walking, curved walking, standing, and lateral walking. Footstep targets are either manually constructed or generated by a Humanoid Navigation ROS footstep planner for curved paths. The robot is initialized in a half-sitting posture with minor noise, and episodes terminate upon falling (root height below 0.60 m) or self-collision.

#### 3.6. Implementation Details

The policy network uses a single-layer Mamba encoder with a hidden size of 128, and compact MLP projection heads. Training uses PPO hyperparameters including an episode horizon of 400 steps, 4,800 samples per iteration, a minibatch size of 64, a discount factor ($\gamma$) of 0.99, and a GAE parameter ($\lambda$) of 0.95. Domain randomization is applied to parameters like dynamics, sensors, initial state, and target jitter.

#### 3.7. Evaluation Metrics

Performance is assessed across four dimensions:
- **Task Performance:** Peak episode returns and mean returns over the final 10% of training.
- **Learning Efficiency and Stability:** Number of environment samples to reach performance thresholds, learning curve deviation, and late-stage training variance.
- **Reward-Based Performance:** Weighted per-step reward, decomposed into the six individual reward components.
- **Joint Torque Profiles:** Average and peak torques across all actuated leg joints.
- **Energy Efficiency:** Energy cost in Joules per meter (J/m), average power consumption (W), and normalized power per unit body mass (W/kg).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated HuMam against a Baseline model that uses a conventional feedforward network under identical training conditions. The results demonstrate that HuMam consistently improves learning efficiency, training stability, overall task performance, and control economy.

#### 4.1. Performance vs. Baseline

HuMam consistently achieved higher returns during training, exhibiting a steeper initial learning curve and a higher asymptotic plateau. Quantitatively, HuMam improved the peak performance from 269.85 to 285.50 (a 5.8% increase) and raised the mean return over the final 10% of training from 263.03 to 277.50 (a 5.5% increase). These results indicate that HuMam reaches higher performance ceilings and sustains better outcomes during extended training.

#### 4.2. Effect on Learning Efficiency and Stability

HuMam demonstrated superior learning efficiency and stability compared to the Baseline.
- **Efficiency:** To reach a return of 240, HuMam required 15.8 million environment samples, which is 13.2% fewer than the Baseline's 18.2 million samples. For a higher threshold of 260 returns, HuMam achieved this in 20.2 million samples, a 42.5% improvement over the Baseline's 35.0 million samples.
- **Stability:** HuMam reduced cross-seed learning curve deviation from 12.08 to 7.81 (a 35.4% reduction) and late-stage training variance from 102.10 to 39.87 (a 61.0% reduction). These metrics suggest more reliable and consistent training dynamics across different random seeds.

#### 4.3. Effect on Reward-Based Performance

Analysis of the six reward components across diverse tasks showed that HuMam achieved an average per-step reward of 0.737, surpassing the Baseline's 0.728.
- HuMam consistently improved foot force regulation (0.132 vs. 0.128) and velocity smoothness (0.129 vs. 0.124), indicating better control over ground contact and swing leg dynamics.
- Slight improvements were also observed in upper-body stability and body height regulation, contributing to more balanced whole-body motion.
- While the Baseline showed marginally higher step-placement accuracy in some tasks (e.g., curved and forward walking), HuMam maintained a better balance across all reward terms, suggesting a more holistic control strategy rather than over-optimizing a single component.

#### 4.4. Effect on Joint Torque Profiles

For the forward walking task, HuMam demonstrated a reduction in joint torques. On average, HuMam reduced joint torques by 9.6% in magnitude and 9.1% in peak load compared to the Baseline.
- Specific joints, such as the left hip pitch (L_HIP_P) and right ankle pitch (R_ANKLE_P), showed substantial reductions in average torque (e.g., -37.6% and -40.2%, respectively) and peak torque (e.g., -21.2% for L_HIP_P).
- Although a few joints experienced slightly higher peak torques, the overall trend indicated a more balanced torque distribution and reduced excessive peaks, which contributes to physical efficiency and stability.

#### 4.5. Effect on Energy Efficiency

HuMam exhibited lower power consumption and improved economy of movement across various tasks.
- **Standing:** Average power draw was reduced from 57.48 W to 35.08 W (a 39% decrease), corresponding to a reduction in normalized power per unit mass from 0.92 W/kg to 0.56 W/kg.
- **Dynamic Locomotion:** Energy cost (J/m) was significantly reduced for lateral walking (963 J/m vs. 1395 J/m) and forward walking (421 J/m vs. 673 J/m). Backward walking also showed improvement (853 J/m vs. 952 J/m).
- **Curved Walking:** HuMam consumed slightly more energy (1303 J/m vs. 1055 J/m) but maintained a lower power-per-mass (1.53 W/kg vs. 2.11 W/kg), indicating potentially smoother dynamics.
These results suggest that HuMam's policies lead to smoother joint actuation and fewer unnecessary corrective torques, directly reducing the energy required for locomotion.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents HuMam, an end-to-end reinforcement learning framework that makes several contributions to humanoid motion control.

#### 5.1. Novelty and Technical Contributions

The primary significance of this work lies in its introduction of a single-layer Mamba encoder as the fusion backbone for an end-to-end humanoid RL controller. This represents the first known application of Mamba in this specific domain. The research demonstrates that Mamba can effectively process multimodal observations (robot-centric states and external guidance) in a state-centric manner, capturing structured dependencies among features without requiring explicit temporal accumulation. This design offers a lightweight alternative to conventional feedforward networks and computationally heavier sequence models, addressing critical challenges related to inefficient feature fusion and computational overhead in real-time robotic systems.

#### 5.2. Performance and Efficiency Gains

The empirical validation on the JVRC-1 humanoid robot across diverse locomotion tasks (forward, backward, lateral, curved walking, and standing) consistently showed that HuMam outperforms a strong feedforward baseline. The gains are multifaceted:
- **Improved Learning Dynamics:** HuMam achieved higher final and peak returns, required fewer environment samples to reach performance thresholds, and exhibited significantly lower cross-seed variance. These improvements suggest faster, more efficient, and more reliable training processes, which are crucial for developing complex robotic skills.
- **Enhanced Control Economy:** The learned policies demonstrated better physical efficiency, characterized by lower energy consumption per meter, reduced average power draw, and decreased power-per-mass. This is further supported by the analysis of joint torque profiles, showing reductions in both average and peak torques across actuated leg joints, leading to smoother actuation. These factors are critical for extending the operational duration and overall lifespan of humanoid robots in real-world scenarios.
- **Balanced Locomotion Quality:** The detailed reward component analysis indicated that HuMam achieves a better balance across multiple locomotion objectives, including foot-ground interaction, swing smoothness, foot placement accuracy, and whole-body stability, without over-optimizing any single component.

#### 5.3. Broader Implications and Future Directions

The findings suggest that state-space models like Mamba can serve as an effective, efficient, and stable backbone for compact, end-to-end policy learning in complex robotic systems. This opens avenues for developing more capable and practical humanoid controllers. The demonstrated improvements in energy efficiency and control stability directly address significant barriers to the real-world deployment of humanoids.

Future work outlined by the authors includes crucial steps towards practical application: hardware deployment and sim-to-real transfer for JVRC-class platforms, which will involve latency profiling and safety monitoring. Expanding the observation space to include onboard perception (e.g., vision or event cues) while maintaining the state-centric interface is also planned. Further research will integrate online footstep planning with feasibility checks, investigate multi-contact behaviors (e.g., stairs, uneven terrain) under tighter power budgets, compare Mamba with other lightweight backbones under identical computational constraints, and incorporate safety constraints to enhance reliability in unstructured environments.
