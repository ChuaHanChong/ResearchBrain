# Hybrid Latent Reasoning via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Hybrid Latent Reasoning via Reinforcement Learning (HRPO) enables Large Language Models (LLMs) to integrate continuous latent representations with discrete token generation through a novel reinforcement learning framework. This approach allows compact Qwen2.5-1.5B and 3B models to achieve strong performance on knowledge and STEM benchmarks, surpassing larger RAG baselines and other RL methods without requiring Chain-of-Thought annotations.

## Problem

- Existing latent reasoning methods for LLMs often face incompatibility between continuous hidden representations and discrete token generation, leading to degraded output quality.
- Many current LLM reasoning approaches heavily depend on resource-intensive explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) annotations for training, limiting scalability and broad applicability.
- Prior attempts to leverage LLMs' intrinsic reasoning capabilities through latent states often incurred high training costs and did not fully exploit the potential of pre-trained models.

## Method

- Developed a ==Hybrid Reasoning with Gating mechanism== that projects continuous hidden states into the embedding space using a differentiable mapping and blends them with discrete token embeddings via a ==learnable gate==.
- Introduced ==Hybrid Reasoning Policy Optimization (HRPO)==, an on-policy reinforcement learning framework that trains LLMs to autonomously develop hybrid reasoning based solely on outcome-based rewards, bypassing the need for CoT supervision.
- Utilized LoRA for efficient fine-tuning and initialized the gating mechanism to prioritize discrete token embeddings, ensuring initial generative coherence while progressively incorporating more informative latent features.

## Results

- HRPO consistently achieved superior exact match scores on knowledge benchmarks, with the Qwen2.5-3B HRPO model outperforming the Qwen2.5-7B RAG baseline by **4.5%** on average.
- The method attained state-of-the-art results for smaller LLMs on reasoning-intensive STEM datasets, enabling the Qwen2.5-3B HRPO model to match the performance of several larger (≥ **7B**) LLMs on four out of five datasets.
- Analysis confirmed the efficacy of HRPO's hybrid design for stability and faster convergence, showing an increasing ratio of latent representations used during training, resulting in shorter completion lengths and exhibiting novel cross-lingual reasoning patterns.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A carefully designed gating mechanism effectively bridges the gap between continuous latent representations and discrete token generation, allowing for stable and coherent hybrid reasoning in LLMs.
> - Reinforcement learning provides an effective way for LLMs to intrinsically learn and leverage sophisticated internal reasoning patterns without explicit Chain-of-Thought supervision, promoting autonomous intelligence.
> - The dynamic and learnable integration of latent representations can lead to more concise and efficient reasoning trajectories, with emergent properties like cross-lingual reasoning observed in the generated outputs.
