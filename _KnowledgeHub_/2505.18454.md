# Hybrid Latent Reasoning via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Hybrid Latent Reasoning via Reinforcement Learning (HRPO) enables Large Language Models (LLMs) to integrate continuous latent representations with discrete token generation through a novel reinforcement learning framework. This approach allows compact Qwen2.5-1.5B and 3B models to achieve strong performance on knowledge and STEM benchmarks, surpassing larger RAG baselines and other RL methods without requiring Chain-of-Thought annotations.

## Problem

- Existing latent reasoning methods for LLMs often face incompatibility between continuous hidden representations and discrete token generation, leading to degraded output quality.
- Many current LLM reasoning approaches heavily depend on resource-intensive explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) annotations for training, limiting scalability and broad applicability.
- Prior attempts to leverage LLMs' intrinsic reasoning capabilities through latent states often incurred high training costs and did not fully exploit the potential of pre-trained models.

## Method

- Developed a ==Hybrid Reasoning with Gating mechanism== that projects continuous hidden states into the embedding space using a differentiable mapping and blends them with discrete token embeddings via a ==learnable gate==.
- Introduced ==Hybrid Reasoning Policy Optimization (HRPO)==, an on-policy reinforcement learning framework that trains LLMs to autonomously develop hybrid reasoning based solely on outcome-based rewards, bypassing the need for CoT supervision.
- Utilized LoRA for efficient fine-tuning and initialized the gating mechanism to prioritize discrete token embeddings, ensuring initial generative coherence while progressively incorporating more informative latent features.

## Results

- HRPO consistently achieved superior exact match scores on knowledge benchmarks, with the Qwen2.5-3B HRPO model outperforming the Qwen2.5-7B RAG baseline by **4.5%** on average.
- The method attained state-of-the-art results for smaller LLMs on reasoning-intensive STEM datasets, enabling the Qwen2.5-3B HRPO model to match the performance of several larger (≥ **7B**) LLMs on four out of five datasets.
- Analysis confirmed the efficacy of HRPO's hybrid design for stability and faster convergence, showing an increasing ratio of latent representations used during training, resulting in shorter completion lengths and exhibiting novel cross-lingual reasoning patterns.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A carefully designed gating mechanism effectively bridges the gap between continuous latent representations and discrete token generation, allowing for stable and coherent hybrid reasoning in LLMs.
> - Reinforcement learning provides an effective way for LLMs to intrinsically learn and leverage sophisticated internal reasoning patterns without explicit Chain-of-Thought supervision, promoting autonomous intelligence.
> - The dynamic and learnable integration of latent representations can lead to more concise and efficient reasoning trajectories, with emergent properties like cross-lingual reasoning observed in the generated outputs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of two rapidly evolving fields within AI: **latent reasoning in Large Language Models (LLMs)** and **Reinforcement Learning (RL) for LLM fine-tuning**.

Traditional LLM reasoning often relies on **autoregressive discrete decoding**, typified by Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting. CoT involves generating explicit, natural language intermediate steps, making the reasoning process transparent but potentially inefficient and reliant on high-quality annotation data.

In contrast, **latent reasoning** aims for internal computation using continuous hidden representations, promising more informative features and potentially greater efficiency by operating within the model's internal state rather than through discrete tokens. Early work in this area includes analyzing latent space computation in transformers, using latent representations for in-context learning, and injecting latent variables into language modeling (e.g., recurrent depth approaches, special pause tokens). More recently, methods like Coconut and CODI have explored feeding previous hidden states as inputs or distilling CoT traces into continuous thoughts. However, these methods often face challenges:
1. **Incompatibility:** Continuous latent representations can conflict with the discrete nature of autoregressive token generation, leading to degraded output quality when directly fed back.
2. **CoT Dependence:** Many existing latent reasoning approaches still rely heavily on explicit CoT traces for training, negating some of the efficiency benefits.
3. **High Training Costs:** Tailored training paradigms for latent representation learning can be computationally expensive and may not fully leverage the inherent reasoning capabilities of pre-trained LLMs.

Simultaneously, **Reinforcement Learning** has become a pivotal technique for aligning and enhancing LLMs, notably through Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF). Algorithms like PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization) and its variants (e.g., GRPO, SimPO, DPO) are widely used to fine-tune LLMs based on explicit or implicit reward signals, improving instruction following, safety, and factual accuracy.

This paper addresses the critical gap by proposing **Hybrid Latent Reasoning via Reinforcement Learning (HRPO)**. It explicitly tackles the shortcomings of previous latent reasoning methods by:
- **Bridging the discrete-continuous divide:** Introducing a gating mechanism to seamlessly integrate continuous hidden states with discrete token embeddings during generation, preserving LLM coherence.
- **Leveraging intrinsic LLM capabilities:** Using RL to enable LLMs to *autonomously develop* latent reasoning, reducing reliance on expensive, external CoT annotations.
- **Improving efficiency:** Providing a scalable and training-efficient framework that adapts existing LLMs for latent reasoning without extensive multi-stage training from scratch.

By doing so, HRPO positions itself as a significant advancement, moving beyond simple CoT distillation or direct hidden state feedback. It attempts to unlock a more sophisticated and flexible reasoning paradigm within LLMs, potentially leading to more powerful and efficient models, especially those with smaller parameter counts. The work builds upon recent RL techniques (like GRPO for advantage estimation) but innovatively applies them to the unique challenge of fostering hybrid reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **enable Large Language Models (LLMs) to perform sophisticated internal, latent reasoning by leveraging their intrinsic capabilities through reinforcement learning, without requiring explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) annotations.**

This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations and specific aims:

1. **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Latent Reasoning:** Prior attempts at latent reasoning, while promising, suffer from significant drawbacks. Many are "incompatible with LLMs, as their continuous paradigm conflicts with the discrete nature of autoregressive generation." This leads to degraded generation quality when hidden states are directly fed back. HRPO aims to resolve this by creating a hybrid reasoning paradigm that respects both continuous and discrete representations.

2. **Mitigating Reliance on CoT Traces:** A major bottleneck for many reasoning methods is the need for extensive, often manually curated, CoT traces for training. This is resource-intensive and limits applicability. The authors explicitly state that "these methods rely on CoT traces for training and thus fail to exploit the inherent reasoning patterns of LLMs." HRPO's design, particularly its RL-based optimization, is motivated by the desire to "mitigate the need for chain-of-thought annotations and expensive multi-stage training."

3. **Harnessing Inherent LLM Reasoning:** The paper posits that LLMs possess "inherent reasoning patterns" that are overlooked by current latent reasoning approaches. HRPO seeks to "exploit the intrinsic capabilities of LLMs via reinforcement learning" to naturally encourage the model to develop and utilize these internal reasoning mechanisms.

4. **Preserving Generative Capabilities and Interpretability:** A crucial goal is to ensure that the integration of continuous latent reasoning does not compromise the LLM's ability to generate coherent and interpretable text. The introduction of a "learnable gating mechanism" and its specific initialization are designed to "maintain LLMs’ generative capabilities" while progressively integrating richer latent features. The ambition is for HRPO-trained LLMs to "remain interpretable."

5. **Developing a Scalable and Efficient Solution:** The existing methods "incurr high training costs." HRPO aims to provide a "scalable and training-efficient solution" by being a "plug-and-play" component compatible with any LLM architecture, using optimized implementations and techniques like LoRA.

In essence, the researchers are motivated to build a more natural, efficient, and powerful reasoning framework for LLMs that goes beyond superficial CoT prompting, allowing LLMs to truly "think" in a hybrid space combining their token-level and latent-space knowledge, while remaining practical for deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology is **Hybrid Reasoning Policy Optimization (HRPO)**, an innovative reinforcement learning (RL) framework designed to integrate discrete token generation with continuous latent representations. This involves two main components: a novel **Hybrid Reasoning with Gating** mechanism and an **RL-based Policy Optimization** strategy.

#### 3.1. Hybrid Reasoning with Gating

The challenge lies in incorporating continuous hidden states (outputs of a Transformer's decoder layers) as inputs for subsequent reasoning steps, given that these states often lie outside the model's token embedding manifold. Directly feeding them back can degrade generation quality.

1. **Projection of Hidden States:** HRPO addresses the manifold mismatch by projecting the final-layer hidden state $\hat{h}_t$ into the embedding space. Instead of a direct projection, it uses the model's output probabilities $p_{t+1}$ (derived from the LM head with a temperature $\tau$) to compute a weighted interpolation over the entire vocabulary's embedding matrix ($W_e$). This yields $h_{t+1}$, a "sampling-free mapping" that is differentiable and aligns with the model's native input space, ensuring semantic continuity.

2. **Gating Mechanism:** While interpolation helps, it removes stochasticity and can inject noise. To maintain generative capabilities and control the blending, HRPO introduces a **learnable gating mechanism** (inspired by gated recurrence models). This gate blends the embedding of the sampled discrete token ($\hat{e}_{t+1}$) with the projected continuous hidden state ($h_{t+1}$).
 - The hybrid input $e_{t+1}$ is a weighted sum: $e_{t+1} = a_t \odot \hat{e}_{t+1} + \sqrt{1 - a_t^2} \odot (i_t \odot h_{t+1})$, where $a_t$ is a scaling factor for token embeddings, and $i_t$ and $r_t$ are sigmoid-activated gates controlling the integration of $h_{t+1}$.
 - Crucially, the gating parameter $\Lambda$ (which determines $a_t$) is initialized such that $a_t \rightarrow 1$. This means that in the initial training phases, the inputs predominantly originate from the sampled discrete token embeddings, thereby preserving the LLM's inherent generative coherence. As training progresses, the gate learns to incorporate more informative features from the previous hidden states.
 - This hybrid reasoning is activated only during designated reasoning phases (e.g., between `<think>` tokens), while the final answer is generated via standard autoregressive decoding.

#### 3.2. Hybrid Reasoning Policy Optimization (HRPO)

HRPO trains the LLM using an online reinforcement learning approach, eliminating the need for CoT supervision.

1. **RL Objective:** The policy (parameterized by $\theta$) is optimized to maximize the expected reward for input $x$ and the sampled hybrid outputs $y$ (discrete tokens) and $H$ (hidden representations). The reward function is simple and outcome-based: 1 for a correct final answer (within the answer span) and 0 otherwise.

2. **Hybrid Rollouts and Advantage Calculation:** To obtain a low-variance advantage estimate, HRPO generates $g$ hybrid rollouts for each input query. For each rollout $i$, the reward $r_i$ is computed. Advantages ($\hat{A}_i$) are then calculated by standardizing these rewards within the group of $g$ rollouts (i.e., $\hat{A}_i = \frac{r_i - \text{mean}(r_{1 \dots g})}{\text{std}(r_{1 \dots g})}$). This group-level standardization is inspired by methods like GRPO.

3. **Policy Gradient Update:** The policy is updated using a REINFORCE-style gradient (Equation 6). This objective adapts to integrate both token-level and latent representations, with hybrid trajectories yielding higher returns receiving larger advantage estimates.
 - A KL-divergence term is included as a regularizer ($\beta \nabla_\theta D_{KL}[\pi_\theta || \pi_{ref}]$) to maintain training stability and prevent aggressive policy shifts from the reference model ($\pi_{ref}$). Importantly, for the KL term, log probabilities for the reference model are computed using *solely token IDs* for stability.
 - The authors explicitly state they omit likelihood ratio clipping (common in PPO) because their "conservative learning schedule" rarely encounters situations where it's needed.
 - The method is strictly on-policy, meaning each hybrid trajectory is used for a single gradient update, respecting the tight coupling between hidden representations and model parameters.

#### 3.3. Implementation Details (Appendix A)

- **Efficiency:** HRPO leverages LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) for efficient fine-tuning and utilizes optimized kernel implementations.
- **Hyperparameters:** Specific initialization ranges for $\Lambda$ (controlling $r_{min}$ for the initial hidden ratio) and temperature $\tau$ for projection are defined. `r_min` tuning allows adjustment of the initial proportion of hidden states, while $\tau$ controls the output distribution sharpness.
- **Prompt Formatting:** A minimalist `####` delimiter is used to separate the hybrid reasoning span from the final answer, penalizing premature answer generation.
- **Context for QA:** For knowledge tasks, E5 embeddings are used to retrieve top-3 Wikipedia documents as context.

In essence, HRPO provides a robust and flexible framework for an LLM to learn *how* to blend its symbolic, discrete reasoning with its implicit, continuous internal "thoughts," driven purely by outcome-based rewards.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive evaluations across diverse benchmarks demonstrate HRPO's superior performance and reveal intriguing insights into hybrid latent reasoning.

#### 4.1. Performance on Knowledge Benchmarks (Table 1)

- **Outperformance:** HRPO consistently delivers the strongest exact match (EM) scores among all baselines, including supervised fine-tuning (SFT), Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), and other RL-based methods (PPO, GRPO), using smaller Qwen2.5-1.5B and 3B Instruct models.
- **Rivaling Larger Models:** HRPO with Qwen2.5-3B achieves an average EM of 0.380, surpassing the strongest Qwen2.5-7B RAG baseline (0.335) by 4.5%. The 1.5B HRPO also achieves 0.337 EM, outperforming 1.5B PPO by 3.0%.
- **Consistent RL Advantage:** HRPO consistently outperforms other RL methods like PPO and GRPO across both 1.5B and 3B backbones, suggesting its hybrid approach is more effective.
- **Task-Specific Gains:** HRPO shows particularly strong gains on terse, incomplete queries (NQ) and multi-hop questions (2WikiMQA), implying its ability to leverage richer internal context for complex reasoning.

#### 4.2. Performance on STEM Benchmarks (Table 2)

- **State-of-the-Art for Smaller Models:** HRPO achieves the strongest results on reasoning-intensive STEM datasets (GSM8k, MATH, MATH500, MMLU-ST, ARC-C) with compact Qwen backbones. The 3B HRPO model achieves an average accuracy of 0.700, matching the performance of several larger (≥ 7B) LLMs on four out of five datasets.
- **Significant Improvement over Baselines:** HRPO significantly outperforms SFT, Distilled CoT, PPO, and GRPO. For instance, 3B HRPO improves upon 3B GRPO by 1.3% on average, and 1.5B HRPO improves upon 1.5B GRPO by 1.0%.
- **Strongest MATH Performance:** HRPO registers the highest accuracies for sub-7B models on challenging mathematical reasoning tasks like MATH (0.613) and MATH500 (0.630).
- **Benefit for Smaller Models:** The performance gains of HRPO are more pronounced for the smaller 1.5B backbone, suggesting its effectiveness in unlocking reasoning capabilities in more constrained models.

#### 4.3. Analysis of HRPO (Section 4.3 & Appendix B)

- **Efficacy of Hybrid Design:** Comparisons of different latent reasoning strategies (Figure 3) show that HRPO's hybrid approach (gating + interpolation) is crucial. Direct hidden state feedback fails entirely, and pure interpolation (without gating) suffers from reward collapse, highlighting the stability and faster convergence of HRPO.
- **Dynamic Latent Representation Usage:**
 - **Hidden Ratio:** The ratio of latent representations in the input steadily increases during training (Figure 4), demonstrating that the gating mechanism learns to incorporate more continuous features over time.
 - **Effect of `r_min` (Lambda Initialization):** Lower `r_min` (implying a higher initial hidden ratio) generally benefits knowledge tasks. For STEM tasks, performance shows a bimodal trend, benefiting from either very low or very high initial `r_min` (Table 3, Table 7), suggesting models either rely heavily on tokens or latent states.
 - **Completion Lengths:** Lower `r_min` can lead to significantly shorter completion lengths, especially on knowledge tasks (Figure 4), indicating that richer latent context allows for more concise reasoning.
- **Robustness to Temperature `τ`:** HRPO is robust to the choice of temperature $\tau$ used in projecting hidden states (Figure 5, Table 8). Smaller $\tau$ values (0.3-0.5) tend to yield faster convergence and higher rewards, while both small and large $\tau$ can lead to shorter completions.
- **Statistical Significance:** Additional sampling-based evaluations on STEM tasks confirm that HRPO's improvements over PPO and GRPO are statistically significant (p < 0.05) across most datasets, particularly for the 3B backbone (Table 9).

#### 4.4. Hybrid Latent Reasoning Patterns (Figure 6 & Appendix C)

- **Interpretable Trajectories:** Despite using continuous latent representations, HRPO-trained models produce "readable trajectories by interpreting the tokens even without any CoT supervision."
- **Cross-lingual Reasoning:** Intriguingly, HRPO exhibits cross-lingual patterns, integrating tokens from different languages (e.g., English and Chinese) within its reasoning process (Figure 6), suggesting latent representations can generalize across linguistic boundaries.
- **Compact Responses:** HRPO often delivers concise yet accurate responses, especially for simpler queries, demonstrating improved efficiency from encoding richer context in hidden representations, leading to fewer decoding steps.
- **Error Analysis:** Identified error patterns include cross-lingual mistakes, format violations, repetitive loops, and truncated reasoning, suggesting areas for future refinement.

In summary, HRPO successfully demonstrates that an RL-based hybrid approach can effectively integrate discrete and continuous reasoning, leading to substantial performance gains on diverse tasks, particularly in smaller LLMs. It also unveils novel and efficient reasoning patterns not commonly observed in traditional LLM approaches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Hybrid Latent Reasoning via Reinforcement Learning" paper presents a highly significant advancement in the field of Large Language Models, with profound implications for both research and practical applications.

1. **Paradigm Shift in Latent Reasoning:** HRPO marks a crucial step forward by being the "first reinforcement learning-based approach for hybrid reasoning." It successfully unifies the benefits of continuous latent representations with discrete token generation, addressing the long-standing incompatibility issue that hindered previous latent reasoning methods. This hybrid paradigm could become a new standard for how LLMs perform internal computation.

2. **Unlocking Intrinsic LLM Capabilities:** By leveraging RL to enable LLMs to autonomously develop latent reasoning, HRPO mitigates the dependency on costly and extensive Chain-of-Thought (CoT) annotations. This democratizes access to advanced reasoning capabilities, as models can learn to "think" without explicit human-designed thought processes, making reasoning more accessible and scalable across diverse tasks and languages.

3. **Enhanced Performance and Efficiency, Especially for Smaller Models:** The consistent outperformance of HRPO over strong baselines (SFT, RAG, PPO, GRPO) on both knowledge-intensive and complex STEM benchmarks is highly impactful. Crucially, HRPO allows smaller LLMs (1.5B and 3B Qwen models) to rival or even surpass the performance of much larger (7B) models and their RAG-augmented counterparts. This means that advanced reasoning capabilities can be achieved with more compact, resource-efficient models, reducing computational costs for training and inference, and enabling deployment on less powerful hardware or edge devices. This has significant implications for making LLMs more accessible and sustainable.

4. **Insights into LLM Reasoning and Interpretability:** The analysis of HRPO's emergent behaviors provides valuable insights. The demonstration of readable (interpretable) reasoning trajectories, even without CoT supervision, is a major contribution, addressing a common concern with "black box" continuous representations. Furthermore, the discovery of cross-lingual reasoning patterns and shorter, more compact completions highlights the flexibility and efficiency gains possible through hybrid reasoning. These findings open new avenues for understanding and engineering LLM internal mechanisms.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** HRPO establishes a strong foundation for future work. The identified limitations (computational overhead, on-policy design, transparency of continuous representations) provide clear directions for follow-up research. Exploring off-policy extensions, simpler architectural designs, and advanced techniques to improve both interpretability and efficiency of continuous latent spaces will be key. The cross-lingual reasoning observation, in particular, suggests promising avenues for multilingual LLM development and understanding.

In conclusion, HRPO represents a significant stride towards creating more intelligent, efficient, and versatile language models. By elegantly blending discrete and continuous reasoning via reinforcement learning, it not only pushes the boundaries of LLM performance but also offers a deeper understanding of how these models can learn to reason more effectively and autonomously.
