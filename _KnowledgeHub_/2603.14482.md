# V-JEPA 2.1: Unlocking Dense Features in Video Self-Supervised Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> V-JEPA 2.1, developed by FAIR at Meta, introduces innovations in self-supervised video learning to produce high-quality dense visual features while maintaining robust global understanding. The model achieves new state-of-the-art performance across diverse benchmarks, including a 35% relative improvement in short-term object interaction anticipation and significantly improved depth estimation with an RMSE of 0.307 on NYUv2.

## Problem

- Previous V-JEPA models excelled at global video understanding but produced fragmented local spatial structures, limiting their effectiveness for dense vision tasks requiring fine-grained detail.
- Self-supervised learning lacked a unified framework capable of simultaneously learning representations that preserve dense spatio-temporal structure and support high-level global understanding.
- The self-supervision loss in prior V-JEPA versions, applied only to masked regions, potentially caused context tokens to act as global aggregators rather than encoding local, detailed information.

## Method

![[2603.14482-method.png]]
*The V-JEPA 2.1 architecture. It processes both images and videos through modality-specific tokenizers, uses an MLP to fuse multi-level encoder features, and applies a dense predictive loss on both masked and context tokens.*

- **V-JEPA 2.1** introduces a ==Dense Predictive Loss== to supervise both masked and unmasked tokens, using a dynamically weighted scheme to encourage local continuity and fine-grained feature learning.
- Implemented ==Deep Self-Supervision==, applying the predictive objective hierarchically across multiple intermediate encoder layers to improve representation quality throughout the network.
- Employed ==modality-specific tokenizers== (2D for images, 3D for videos) and a learnable modality token, along with expanded data (VisionMix-163M) and model scaling (up to ==ViT-G==), to facilitate effective multi-modal training.

## Results

- Achieved new state-of-the-art in short-term object interaction anticipation (**7.71 mAP** on Ego4D, **~35%** relative improvement) and action anticipation (**40.8 Recall@5** on EPIC-KITCHENS-100, **+2.8%** over V-JEPA 2).
- Improved robotic arm grasping success rate by **20%** and enabled **1**0x** faster** navigation planning (**5.687 ATE** on Tartan Drive) due to enhanced depth understanding and robust features.
- Set a new state-of-the-art **RMSE of 0.307** on NYUv2 depth estimation and achieved competitive results on semantic segmentation (47.9 mIoU on ADE20K) and video object segmentation, demonstrating significantly more coherent and fine-grained feature maps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly supervising unmasked (context) tokens, in addition to masked ones, is crucial for developing high-quality dense spatial and temporal features within the JEPA framework.
> - Applying self-supervision at multiple intermediate layers significantly enhances overall representation quality and reduces the reliance on multi-scale evaluation for downstream tasks.
> - Combining modality-specific processing with large-scale, diverse multi-modal data and high-resolution training phases leads to robust, generalizable visual world models for both images and videos.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of "world models" capable of perceiving, predicting, and planning in physical environments represents a significant area of research in artificial intelligence. A core component of such models is the ability to learn robust representations that accurately summarize the current state of the world from raw perceptual inputs. Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) from video has emerged as a promising approach in this domain, leveraging large volumes of unlabeled data to capture essential scene properties like geometry, dynamics, and physical interactions.

A persistent challenge within this landscape is the simultaneous learning of representations that maintain dense spatio-temporal structure—critical for fine-grained tasks such as localization, geometry estimation, and object tracking—while also supporting global scene understanding necessary for high-level recognition, action anticipation, and complex dynamics modeling.

Previous advancements, particularly the V-JEPA (Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture) family of models, have demonstrated strong capabilities in global video understanding, excelling in tasks requiring the modeling of motion and temporal dynamics. These models have shown potential for prediction and planning in embodied AI agents. However, their learned representations have exhibited limitations in extracting fine-grained local spatial structure, often appearing noisy or fragmented in qualitative visualizations, which can hinder performance on dense vision tasks.

In contrast, other prominent SSL methodologies, such as DINO, have successfully produced high-quality dense features suitable for tasks like object detection and semantic segmentation. However, these approaches are primarily image-based, limiting their ability to directly learn and represent temporal dynamics from video sequences.

This research addresses the observed disparity by aiming to develop a self-supervised video learning framework that bridges this gap. The objective is to enhance the V-JEPA framework to learn representations that simultaneously offer high-quality dense spatio-temporal features and robust global semantic understanding, thereby advancing the utility of video-based self-supervised learning for comprehensive world modeling.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce V-JEPA 2.1, a family of self-supervised models designed to learn high-quality, dense representations for visual scenes in both images and videos, while simultaneously preserving strong global scene understanding capabilities.

The motivation for this work arises from several observations and identified limitations in existing self-supervised learning approaches for video:

1. **Bridging the Dense vs. Global Feature Gap:** Prior V-JEPA models demonstrated proficiency in global video understanding, action prediction, and planning, particularly concerning motion and dynamics. However, an analysis of their feature maps, including qualitative visualizations (e.g., PCA on patch features) and performance on dense downstream tasks (e.g., semantic segmentation, depth estimation), revealed that V-JEPA 2 features were often noisy, showed fragmented local spatial structure, and were not easily amenable to extracting fine-grained local information. This contrasted with image-based models like DINO, which excel at dense feature extraction but lack inherent temporal understanding. The core motivation was to overcome this limitation and enable V-JEPA to produce features that are both spatially structured and semantically coherent at a fine-grained level, in addition to their existing global understanding.

2. **Addressing the "Context Token Aggregation" Hypothesis:** The authors hypothesized that the observed lack of local structure in V-JEPA 2 features stemmed from its original loss function. The V-JEPA objective primarily applies the prediction loss only to *masked* tokens, providing no explicit incentive for the *visible context* tokens to encode local information. Instead, the model could devote computational resources to aggregating global information within these context tokens, effectively making them act as global aggregators rather than preserving local detail. This hypothesis motivated the introduction of explicit self-supervision on unmasked regions to encourage spatial and temporal grounding for all tokens.

3. **Enhancing Representation Quality Across Tasks:** The goal was not merely to improve dense features in isolation but to achieve a unified representation that benefits a broad spectrum of downstream applications. This included tasks requiring precise spatio-temporal localization (e.g., short-term object interaction anticipation, depth estimation, semantic segmentation, video object segmentation) as well as those demanding high-level semantic reasoning (e.g., action anticipation, action recognition, video question answering, robotic planning). The motivation was to create a more versatile and robust foundation model for physical world modeling.

4. **Scaling and Modality Unification:** The research was also motivated by the continuous exploration of scaling effects in SSL, both in terms of model capacity and data volume and diversity. Specifically, there was a motivation to unify training across images and videos in a more native and computationally efficient manner than previous approaches, which often involved treating images as static video frames.

In summary, the key objective was to significantly advance the capabilities of self-supervised video models by developing V-JEPA 2.1, an architecture and training recipe that learns universally applicable, high-quality, dense spatio-temporal features alongside strong global scene understanding, driven by a refined predictive loss and architectural innovations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

V-JEPA 2.1 builds upon the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) framework, specifically the V-JEPA family, which learns representations by predicting masked segments of an input (image or video) in a learned latent space. The core V-JEPA 2.1 methodology incorporates four key ingredients: a Dense Predictive Loss, Deep Self-Supervision, Multi-Modal Tokenizers, and effective model and data scaling.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA)

JEPA models consist of an encoder (Eθ) that processes inputs (both a masked/corrupted view `x` and an uncorrupted view `y`) into latent representations, and a predictor (Pϕ) that predicts the representation of the uncorrupted input from the corrupted input's representation. A stop-gradient operator and an exponential moving average (EMA) of the encoder weights for the uncorrupted view are used to prevent representation collapse. The original V-JEPA objective (Eq. 1) applies an L1 loss only to the masked patch indexes (M) between the predictor's output and the `sg(Eθ(y))` target.

#### 3.2. V-JEPA 2.1 Key Algorithmic Innovations

- **Dense Predictive Loss:**
 - **Context Loss (Lctx):** This is the central innovation. Unlike the original V-JEPA, V-JEPA 2.1 applies self-supervision to *both* masked and visible context patches. The `Lctx` (Eq. 2) is a weighted L1 loss applied to the context tokens (C), predicting `sg(Eθ(y))` from `Pϕ(Eθ(x), ∆y)`. The total loss becomes `Ldense = Lpredict + Lctx`.
 - **Weighted Scheme:** A dynamic weighting scheme is introduced for `λi` in `Lctx` (Eq. 3). `λi` is inversely proportional to the square root of the minimum spatio-temporal distance between a context token `i` and its closest masked token. This weighting emphasizes local continuity between masked and context areas, aiming to balance dense and global task performance. A progressive warm-up schedule for `λ` is also used to stabilize training.

- **Deep Self-Supervision:**
 - The self-supervised objective is applied hierarchically at multiple intermediate encoder layers, not just the final output.
 - The `x`-encoder outputs representations from several intermediate blocks, which are concatenated along the channel axis and then fed to a lightweight MLP to reduce dimensionality.
 - The predictor then processes this multi-level fused representation and produces predictions corresponding to these multiple encoder levels. Both `Lpredict` and `Lctx` are applied at each of these intermediate levels. This provides a rich training signal throughout the network.

- **Multi-Modal Tokenizers:**
 - To support native joint training over images and videos, V-JEPA 2.1 employs modality-specific patch embedding layers. A 2D convolution (`16x16` kernel) is used for images, and a 3D convolution (`16x16x2` kernel) for videos. This avoids the suboptimal approach of temporally duplicating images to fit a 3D conv.
 - Learnable modality-specific tokens are added to both encoder and predictor inputs, conditioning the processing based on the input modality and helping disentangle static appearance cues from temporal motion information.

#### 3.3. Model and Data Scaling

- **Data Scaling (VisionMix163M Dataset):**
 - The pretraining dataset is expanded and diversified. The 1M-image ImageNet subset from V-JEPA 2 is replaced with the 142M-image LVD-142M dataset, curated for broader visual diversity.
 - Video sampling strategy is shifted to promote more dynamic content, increasing SSv2 sampling weight and YT-1B contribution.
 - Distributed training is used, with separate workers assigned to images and videos, aggregating gradients before model updates. The image/video ratio is controlled via batch sizes (128 video clips, 2,304 images per global batch).

- **Model Scaling:**
 - The encoder model size is scaled from a ViT-L (300 million parameters) to a ViT-G (2 billion parameters) to explore the benefits of increased capacity.

- **High-Resolution Cool-Down:**
 - A second training stage is introduced after the primary phase. During this "cool-down" phase, the learning rate is decayed, and input resolutions are increased (video clips: 64 frames at 384x384; images: 512x512). This helps refine features at higher detail.

#### 3.4. Architecture (Figure 4 Overview)

The architecture integrates these components:
1. An input (image or video) is processed by a modality-specific patch embedding.
2. Mask corruption drops random patch tokens.
3. The `x`-encoder processes visible context tokens, outputting multi-level embeddings.
4. An MLP fuses these multi-level representations, reducing dimensionality.
5. Context tokens are concatenated with learnable mask tokens (with spatio-temporal position information).
6. The predictor processes this combined sequence, producing multi-level predictions for all tokens.
7. Training minimizes both the `Lpredict` (on masked tokens) and `Lctx` (on context tokens) at multiple intermediate representation levels, supervised by the `y`-encoder's multi-level outputs.

#### 3.5. Model Distillation

A protocol for distilling the large ViT-G model into smaller variants (ViT-B, ViT-L) is established. This involves using the frozen ViT-G as a teacher, a smaller student encoder, a simplified predictor (12 blocks, last-layer loss only), and maintaining an EMA of the student encoder for the final model.

#### 3.6. Empirical Evaluation

Ablation studies are conducted on a ViT-L architecture to assess the impact of individual components on dense (ADE20K semantic segmentation, NYUv2 depth estimation) and global (Something-Something-v2 action classification) tasks. Final performance is evaluated using linear probing for dense tasks and attentive probing for global recognition, anticipation, and robotics tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

V-JEPA 2.1 demonstrates advancements in learning dense, high-quality spatio-temporal representations while maintaining strong global scene understanding across a variety of benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Unlocking Dense Features

- **Qualitative Visualizations:** PCA visualizations of V-JEPA 2.1 patch features (Figure 1, 3, 14, 15) exhibit spatially structured, semantically coherent, and temporally consistent representations. This contrasts with V-JEPA 2, which showed noisy and fragmented local structures. The addition of the context loss `Lctx` was instrumental in achieving these coherent feature maps, where similar semantic parts (e.g., dog heads, car wheels) map to the same PCA components.
- **Depth Estimation:** V-JEPA 2.1 ViT-G achieved a state-of-the-art linear-probe monocular depth estimation performance of 0.307 RMSE on NYUv2, outperforming DINOv3 ViT-7B (0.309 RMSE) and significantly improving upon V-JEPA 2 (0.642 RMSE). On KITTI, it achieved 2.461 RMSE. Qualitatively, V-JEPA 2.1 produced sharper, more coherent, and fine-grained depth maps compared to V-JEPA 2.
- **Semantic Segmentation:** V-JEPA 2.1 ViT-G achieved competitive performance, with 85.0 mIoU on PASCAL VOC12, 73.5 mIoU on Cityscapes, and 47.9 mIoU on ADE20K. These represent substantial gains over V-JEPA 2 (e.g., +23.4 points on ADE20K), indicating improved capture of object boundaries and category-level semantics.
- **Video Object Segmentation (VOS):** V-JEPA 2.1 achieved 69.0 J&F on DAVIS-17 and 72.7 J&F on YouTube-VOS, ranking as the second-best performing model and demonstrating strong temporal consistency in its features, crucial for stable object tracking across frames.

#### 4.2. Enhanced Global Understanding and Anticipation

- **Short-Term Object Interaction Anticipation (Ego4D):** V-JEPA 2.1 ViT-G achieved an absolute state-of-the-art of 7.71 mAP, representing approximately a 35% relative improvement over prior methods like STAformer. This gain was attributed to improved understanding of upcoming actions (25.8 APb+V) and time-to-contact predictions (20.20 APb+δ), alongside precise 2D object detection (50.7 APb).
- **Action Anticipation (EPIC-KITCHENS-100):** V-JEPA 2.1 ViT-G achieved a new state-of-the-art performance of 40.8 Action Recall@5, a +2.8% improvement over V-JEPA 2, indicating enhanced ability to forecast future actions from partial temporal context.
- **Action Recognition (Something-Something v2):** V-JEPA 2.1 ViT-G achieved a top-1 accuracy of 77.7%, setting a new absolute state-of-the-art for this motion-centric task, surpassing V-JEPA 2 (77.3%) and other strong visual encoders.
- **Image Classification (ImageNet-1K):** The model achieved competitive performance with 85.5% accuracy.
- **Video Question Answering (VidQA):** When trained with a Llama 3.1 8B LLM, V-JEPA 2.1 ViT-G achieved competitive results across various VidQA benchmarks (e.g., 83.1 accuracy on PerceptionTest), showing comparative improvements over V-JEPA 2 on tasks requiring rich visual semantics and temporal understanding.

#### 4.3. Robotics Applications

- **Robotic Arm Planning (Grasping):** When deployed on real Franka Panda robot arms for grasping tasks, V-JEPA 2.1 demonstrated a +20% improvement in success rate compared to V-JEPA 2 AC. This was attributed to V-JEPA 2.1's improved depth understanding.
- **Navigation Planning (Tartan Drive):** V-JEPA 2.1 enabled more accurate planning (5.687 ATE, SOTA) with a 10x faster planning speed compared to previous work like NWM, by supporting efficient training of a Conditional Diffusion Transformer.

#### 4.4. Impact of Individual Components (Ablation Studies)

- **Dense Predictive Loss (Context Loss):** Initially improved dense tasks significantly (22.2 → 33.9 mIoU on ADE20K, 0.682 → 0.473 RMSE on NYUv2) but led to a degradation in global tasks (72.8 → 62.5 on SSv2). The weighted scheme and warm-up stabilized training and balanced performance.
- **Deep Self-Supervision:** This component recovered global understanding capabilities (72.0 on SSv2, 80.8 on IN1K) while further improving dense tasks (38.6 mIoU on ADE20K, 0.463 RMSE on NYUv2). It also reduced the need for multi-scale evaluation with probes.
- **Data Scaling (VisionMix163M):** Training on a more diverse and extensive database yielded improvements across all tasks (e.g., 38.6 → 40.8 mIoU on ADE20K).
- **Multi-Modal Tokenizer:** Showed a positive effect on dense task performance (e.g., 40.8 → 41.4 mIoU on ADE20K) while maintaining stable global task performance.
- **Model Scaling (ViT-L to ViT-G) & High-Resolution Cool-Down:** Scaling to ViT-G (2B parameters) and introducing a high-resolution cool-down phase systematically improved performance across all downstream tasks, with notable gains in depth estimation (0.365 → 0.307 RMSE on NYUv2).

#### 4.5. Model Distillation

Distilling the ViT-G model into smaller variants (ViT-L, ViT-B) yielded significant improvements over training from scratch, approaching the performance of the larger ViT-G teacher model (e.g., ViT-L SSv2 accuracy improved from 74.2% to 76.5%).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The V-JEPA 2.1 research represents an advancement in the field of self-supervised learning for visual representations, particularly for video data. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

1. **Unified High-Quality Representations:** The primary contribution is the successful development of a self-supervised model that learns representations concurrently supporting high-quality, dense local features and robust global semantic understanding across both images and videos. This addresses a previously identified limitation where existing models often excelled in one area but not the other (e.g., DINO for dense image features, V-JEPA for global video dynamics). This unification provides a more comprehensive foundation for visual understanding.

2. **Advancement in World Modeling:** By unlocking fine-grained, spatially structured, semantically coherent, and temporally consistent features, V-JEPA 2.1 provides a stronger "state estimation" capability for world models. This is crucial for agents operating in physical environments, enabling them to better perceive, predict, and plan. The demonstrated improvements in robotic arm planning (grasping success rate) and navigation planning (faster and more accurate trajectories) highlight its direct impact on embodied AI.

3. **Broad Applicability Across Vision Tasks:** The model's strong performance across a diverse range of downstream benchmarks—including short-term anticipation, action anticipation and recognition, monocular depth estimation, semantic segmentation, and video object segmentation—underscores its versatility. This suggests that the learned V-JEPA 2.1 features can serve as a robust backbone for various practical applications in computer vision.

4. **Architectural and Training Innovations:** The specific algorithmic contributions, including the Dense Predictive Loss (with its weighted context loss), Deep Self-Supervision, and Multi-Modal Tokenizers, provide concrete methodologies for improving representation learning in complex data domains like video. These innovations offer insights into effective design principles for self-supervised learning objectives.

5. **Scalability and Efficiency:** The research confirms the benefits of data and model scaling within this framework, demonstrating that larger models trained on more diverse datasets yield consistent performance gains. Furthermore, the effectiveness of the distillation protocol allows for the creation of smaller, more computationally efficient models (ViT-L, ViT-B) that retain a significant portion of the larger model's capabilities, making the technology more accessible for deployment. The 10x faster planning speed in robotic navigation is a direct consequence of more efficient and effective latent representations.

6. **Fostering Future Research:** The public release of V-JEPA 2.1 code and pretrained models is intended to accelerate further research in physical world modeling. This includes future work on scaling model and data sizes, exploring more advanced world modeling aspects using these dense prediction capabilities, and unlocking new applications in robotics and autonomous agents that require precise, pixel-level state estimation and manipulation in real-world environments.

In summary, V-JEPA 2.1 significantly pushes the boundaries of self-supervised video understanding by integrating dense feature learning with global comprehension, offering a more complete perceptual foundation for future AI systems, especially in robotics and interactive agents.
