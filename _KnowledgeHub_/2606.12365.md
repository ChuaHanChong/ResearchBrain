# Ambient Diffusion Policy: Imitation Learning from Suboptimal Data in Robotics

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at MIT developed Ambient Diffusion Policy, an imitation learning method that integrates suboptimal robot data by selectively allowing its contribution to training at specific noise levels. This approach improves policy performance and robustness, outperforming baselines like data filtering and co-training on tasks ranging from maze navigation to large-scale robotic manipulation.

## Problem

- High-quality robot data is expensive and difficult to acquire, while abundant suboptimal data is often discarded or improperly utilized by existing imitation learning methods.
- Current data filtering techniques waste potentially useful information, and simple co-training often introduces bias from detrimental features present in suboptimal datasets.
- Existing methods struggle to distinguish between useful signals and detrimental noise in heterogeneous robotics datasets, requiring complex strategies or high proportions of quality data.

## Method

- Introduces **Ambient Diffusion Policy**, which selectively allows ==suboptimal data== to contribute to training within a Diffusion Policy framework based on the diffusion noise level.
- Leverages the hierarchical learning properties of diffusion models, where high noise levels correspond to learning global planning and low noise levels correspond to fine-grained motion primitives.
- Utilizes a classifier-based annotation method to determine `t_min` and `t_max` parameters for suboptimal samples, defining specific noise ranges where they are admissible for training.

## Results

- Ambient Diffusion Policy consistently outperformed data filtering and co-training baselines across controlled experiments (noisy trajectories, sim-to-real, task mismatch) and large-scale Open X-Embodiment datasets.
- Achieved smoother trajectories and higher success rates; for instance, in a 2D maze, it yielded **99.5%** success with 31.0 average squared acceleration, compared to co-training's **99.4%** success with 62.2.
- Demonstrated scalability and reduced sensitivity to dataset re-weighting on Open X-Embodiment, achieving **83%** task completion for table cleaning with 50 high-quality demos, outperforming co-training (**71%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robot action data exhibits a spectral power law, which induces a global-to-local hierarchy in action diffusion; high noise levels mask local details, while low noise levels reveal fine-grained dynamics.
> - The spectral power law and derived locality property of optimal denoisers justify noise-dependent data usage, enabling the policy to learn useful features from suboptimal data while ignoring detrimental ones.
> - Selectively using suboptimal data at appropriate noise levels acts as a novel co-training axis, allowing policies to learn shared high-level plans from diverse data or shared local motion primitives from misaligned data.
