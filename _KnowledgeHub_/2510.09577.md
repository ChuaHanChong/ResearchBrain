# Dyna-Mind: Learning to Simulate from Experience for Better AI Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> The DYNA-MIND framework provides a two-stage training approach that teaches AI agents to integrate and improve internal world simulations from experience, thereby enhancing their reasoning and decision-making in complex environments. This method yields superior performance, reaching 92.5% success on ALFWorld and 40.7% on AndroidWorld in-distribution tasks, while generating simulation-guided reasoning more efficiently.

## Problem

- AI agents, particularly those based on large language models, perform poorly on complex, long-horizon interactive tasks requiring multi-step planning and dynamic environmental interaction.
- Agents lack the cognitive ability of 'vicarious trial and error,' or mentally simulating future states and consequences before acting, which is crucial for adaptable decision-making.
- Current solutions for integrating world models are either computationally expensive during inference or rely on synthetic data, which can propagate errors and biases.

## Method

- A two-stage training framework, DYNA-MIND, is proposed to infuse and improve ==simulation ability== directly into AI agents' reasoning processes.
- Stage 1, Reasoning with Simulations (RESIM), generates high-quality, simulation-guided reasoning traces from real environment interactions and distills them into a policy model via supervised fine-tuning.
- Stage 2, Dyna-GRPO, refines the agent's internal simulation and policy through online reinforcement learning by using ground-truth future states to enable self-correction.

## Results

- DYNA-GRPO achieved **92.5%** task success on ALFWorld and **82.5%** on Sokoban, surpassing other online reinforcement learning methods and strong commercial models.
- In AndroidWorld, the framework reached **40.7%** success on in-distribution tasks and **22.9%** on out-of-distribution tasks, a notable improvement over baselines like GPT-4o (**5.1%**).
- Distilled models leveraging RESIM generated high-quality, simulation-guided reasoning traces with 11 times fewer tokens on average compared to DeepSeek-R1's traces, demonstrating efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly teaching AI agents to generate structured reasoning traces with future state anticipations, grounded in real environment dynamics, significantly enhances performance in interactive tasks.
> - The 'vicarious trial and error' ability, inspired by human cognition, is crucial for improving long-horizon planning and decision-making in AI agents.
> - Refining internal simulations and policies using online reinforcement learning with intermediate state feedback from real environments yields more accurate world models than synthetic data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of large language models (LLMs) as autonomous agents, reinforcement learning (RL), and cognitive science-inspired AI. The broader landscape sees impressive strides in LLM reasoning for domains like mathematics and coding, yet a significant gap remains in their performance for long-horizon, interactive tasks such as web navigation, computer control, and complex problem-solving. Existing AI agents, often built upon LLMs or visual language models (VLMs), struggle with tasks requiring multi-step planning and dynamic interaction with unpredictable environments.

The paper critically observes that current AI agents lack "vicarious trial and error"—a concept borrowed from human cognition literature, where individuals mentally simulate alternative futures to evaluate consequences before acting. This cognitive ability, attributed to the neocortex, is argued to be essential for adaptability and superior decision-making, a capability conspicuously absent or underdeveloped in most AI agents.

Current approaches to developing AI agents can be broadly categorized:
1. **Reactive agents:** Directly prompt (V)LMs to make decisions based on immediate observations. These lack planning and struggle with complex, long-horizon tasks.
2. **Search-based methods (e.g., Tree of Thoughts, MCTS):** Augment (V)LMs with algorithmic search capabilities (BFS, DFS, MCTS) to explore possible futures. While effective, these methods often introduce substantial inference-time overheads due to repeated interactions with the environment or complex heuristic design.
3. **Hierarchical/Multi-agent systems:** Orchestrate multiple specialized agents to tackle complex tasks. These can also be computationally intensive and require intricate coordination mechanisms.
4. **Training (V)LM agents:** Early methods rely on supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with human or synthetic data. More recently, reinforcement learning (RL) with verifiable rewards has gained traction (e.g., DeepSeek-R1), prompting models to reason before acting. However, the specific aspects of reasoning crucial for long-horizon tasks remain underexplored.

"Dyna-Mind" differentiates itself by specifically addressing the integration and *improvement* of "world simulation" ability directly into the agent's reasoning process through *training*, rather than solely relying on inference-time search or external modular systems. It builds upon ideas from "Dyna algorithms" (Sutton, 1991), which combine model-based and model-free RL, but aims to internalize the world model within the agent's reasoning instead of keeping it as a separate component. The work also directly contrasts with prior attempts like "Dyna-Think" (Yu et al., 2025b), which generate synthetic simulation data via LLMs, risking the propagation of errors and biases. By learning from *real experiences*, Dyna-Mind seeks to provide more faithful world dynamics and grounded reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind Dyna-Mind stems from a critical observation: despite remarkable progress in AI reasoning for structured tasks, contemporary AI agents—particularly those based on (V)LMs—fall short in complex, interactive, and long-horizon tasks. The authors empirically demonstrate this shortfall (Figure 1a), showing that a strong reasoning model like DeepSeek-R1 performs well in a structured environment like Sokoban (where its simulation ability is high), but significantly declines in a more complex domain like ALFWorld, where its simulation accuracy and task success rate drop sharply. This highlights a fundamental limitation: the inability to accurately model and simulate future states in dynamic environments.

Inspired by cognitive neuroscience research on human "vicarious trial and error"—the mental simulation of potential actions and their consequences—the authors argue that AI agents need this capacity to truly excel in complex environments. This cognitive parallel suggests that integrating robust simulation capabilities directly into an agent's reasoning process is essential for achieving expert-level performance in real-world scenarios.

The key objectives of this paper are therefore to:
1. **Infuse simulation ability into AI agents' reasoning:** Explicitly teach (V)LM agents to generate structured reasoning traces that include anticipations of future states, grounding their reasoning in faithful world dynamics.
2. **Improve simulation and decision-making through online learning:** Develop a method to continuously strengthen an agent’s internal simulation and policy based on real environment feedback, leveraging both outcome rewards and intermediate states.
3. **Overcome limitations of synthetic data:** Avoid reliance on LLMs to *generate* synthetic simulation data, which can introduce errors and biases, by learning directly from real experiences.
4. **Enhance agent performance in long-horizon, planning-intensive tasks:** Demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed framework across various benchmarks, including synthetic and realistic environments, to validate its impact on task success.

In essence, the paper aims to bridge the gap between impressive symbolic reasoning in LLMs and the practical demands of embodied and interactive AI by equipping agents with an internalized, experience-driven world model for proactive planning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Dyna-Mind is a two-stage training framework designed to teach (V)LM agents to integrate and improve simulation abilities within their reasoning process. The framework operates on the principle that learning from real environment interactions provides more accurate world dynamics compared to relying on synthetic data.

#### 3.1. Stage 1: Reasoning with Simulations (RESIM)

The first stage, Reasoning with Simulations (RESIM), focuses on creating high-quality imitation learning data where the agent's reasoning explicitly incorporates planning with simulations. This is achieved by systematically constructing rich reasoning traces from expanded search trees derived from actual environment interactions.

1. **RESIM Data Collection:**
 - Given a current state *s*, RESIM first employs a "rollout model" (πθ) to generate a set of *b* rollouts, each exploring up to *d* future steps. This rollout model can be a finetuned LLM or a prompted generic LLM.
 - A "value function" (Vν) is then used to estimate the quality (e.g., likelihood of success) of these partial rollouts. This can also be a finetuned value model or an LLM acting as a judge.
 - Crucially, these rollouts traverse *real environment dynamics*, providing ground-truth future state information.
 - Finally, a separate generic (V)LM aggregates these rollouts and their associated values into a single, structured reasoning response, *aReSim*. This response is designed to first independently summarize each partial rollout (including ground-truth future states), then combine these summaries into a coherent reasoning trace, and finally select the best plan and immediate action for execution. The goal is to produce reasoning that explicitly lays out potential futures and evaluates them.

2. **RESIM Distillation:**
 - The generated *aReSim* responses encapsulate an entire search tree within their reasoning. This makes them ideal targets for supervised fine-tuning (SFT).
 - The policy model is trained to directly generate *aReSim* given an input state *s* and historical context. This process teaches the model to perform simulation-guided reasoning *autonomously*, without needing algorithmic search support during inference. This is key to enabling efficient, internalized simulation.

#### 3.2. Stage 2: Dyna-GRPO

The second stage, Dyna-GRPO, addresses the computational expense and offline nature of RESIM by further refining the model's simulation and decision-making abilities through online reinforcement learning (RL). It modifies the standard Group-based Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm to incorporate textual signals from future states.

1. **Challenge with Standard RL:** Traditional RL algorithms like GRPO optimize policies based solely on scalar rewards, offering no direct signal for refining the internal reasoning process or world model simulations within the agent.

2. **SIMROLLOUT (Simulation Refinement Rollout):**
 - To inject future-state information into RL, Dyna-GRPO introduces SIMROLLOUT. At each state *st*, the agent first samples an action *a* from its current policy πθ.
 - It extracts a multi-step plan from *a* and *executes this plan in the real environment* to obtain ground-truth next-states.
 - The agent is then prompted *again* to *refine* its original response *a* (resulting in *arefine*) by leveraging these real future states. This process teaches the agent *how to correct its internal simulations* based on actual outcomes.

3. **DYNA-GRPO Training:**
 - DYNA-GRPO iteratively alternates between two improvement phases:
 - **Simulation Improvement:**
 - Performs SIMROLLOUTs to collect *refined trajectories* (τ'refine) with future-state information, and *unrefined trajectories* (τ') that are the original actions leading to refined responses.
 - Also performs standard policy rollouts (τ) with the current policy.
 - The policy is updated using GRPO on a combination of standard rollouts (τ) and the *refined policy trajectories* (τ') from SIMROLLOUT.
 - Crucially, it further updates the policy using τ'refine to explicitly improve the model’s *simulation refinement ability*. This is achieved via a modified advantage function (Arefine) that specifically rewards refinements that lead to task success *and* outperform the average reward of standard policy rollouts. This incentivizes the agent to learn to leverage future information for better planning.
 - **Policy Improvement:** Performs standard policy rollouts without future-state access and optimizes the policy using GRPO. This ensures the agent effectively integrates its learned simulation ability into its autonomous decision-making.

By combining experience-driven imitation learning with online, simulation-aware reinforcement learning, Dyna-Mind provides a comprehensive approach to developing AI agents that can internally simulate and plan more effectively in complex environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates Dyna-Mind on three benchmarks: two synthetic text-based environments (Sokoban and ALFWorld) and one realistic visual environment (AndroidWorld).

#### 4.1. Text Games (Sokoban and ALFWorld)

**Inference-time Performance (Table 1):**
- **RESIM's Superiority:** RESIM inference achieves near-perfect performance on Sokoban (96.4% success) and significantly strong performance on ALFWorld (87.7% success). This substantially outperforms strong commercial and research models like GPT-4o, Claude-3.7-Sonnet, DeepSeek-V3, and DeepSeek-R1.
- **DeepSeek-R1's Context-Dependence:** While DeepSeek-R1 achieves similar near-perfect performance on Sokoban, it struggles more in ALFWorld (62.5%). This highlights that its "world modeling" capability is environment-dependent; it can accurately simulate simpler environments like Sokoban but not more complex ones like ALFWorld. RESIM, by design, uses ground-truth simulations from search trees, allowing it to maintain high performance across both.

**Stage 1 Training (DISTILL(RESIM)) (Table 1):**
- **Effective Distillation:** Distilling RESIM-generated traces (DISTILL(RESIM)) into a smaller Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct model yields strong performance. On Sokoban, it's comparable to DISTILL(R1) (71.9% vs. 72.5%), but on ALFWorld, DISTILL(RESIM) significantly outperforms DISTILL(R1) (78.9% vs. 59.4%).
- **Efficiency:** DISTILL(RESIM) outputs 11 times fewer tokens on average compared to DISTILL(R1), demonstrating that high-quality, simulation-guided reasoning can be learned in a more compact and efficient manner. This suggests RESIM's traces effectively teach the core simulation ability without unnecessary verbosity.

**Stage 2 Training (DYNA-GRPO) (Table 1):**
- **Reinforced Performance:** Continuing training with DYNA-GRPO on the DISTILL(RESIM) model leads to further significant improvements. On Sokoban, it reaches 82.5% success (vs. 71.9% for DISTILL(RESIM) SFT-only and 79.1% for GRPO). On ALFWorld, it achieves 92.5% success (vs. 78.9% for DISTILL(RESIM) SFT-only and 87.0% for GRPO).
- **Outperformance of Baselines:** DYNA-GRPO consistently outperforms other online RL methods like RLOO and standard GRPO, as well as Dyna-Think, while maintaining compact output token lengths. This validates its effectiveness in refining both the policy and internal simulation during online learning.

**Measuring Simulation Ability (Table 2):**
- **Correlation:** A strong positive correlation (r) is observed between Simulation Score and task success rate across models and environments, supporting the hypothesis that simulation ability is crucial.
- **Improvement:** Both DISTILL(RESIM) and especially DYNA-GRPO demonstrably improve the Sim Score alongside task performance, indicating that the methods successfully integrate and enhance the agent's capacity for accurate internal simulation. For example, on ALFWorld, DISTILL(RESIM) increased Sim Score to 0.37 (from 0.18 for REACT(Qwen2.5-7B)), and DYNA-GRPO further raised it to 0.43.
- **SIMROLLOUT Validation (Table A1):** Experiments confirm that providing ground-truth next-state information (SIMROLLOUT) significantly improves task success rates for all tested models, especially stronger ones like GPT-4o and GPT-4.1. This highlights the inherent difficulty of accurate world modeling for LLMs and the value of external feedback for refinement.

#### 4.2. AndroidWorld (Realistic Benchmark)

**Performance (Table 3):**
- **RESIM Inference:** RESIM inference achieves 34.4% success, a notable improvement over strong baselines like REACT(Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct) (19.5%) and GPT-4o (5.1%).
- **Stage 1 (DISTILL(RESIM)):** Distillation of RESIM traces to smaller Qwen2.5-VL models (7B and 32B) successfully transfers a significant portion of the performance. DISTILL-32B(RESIM) reaches 32.8% on ID tasks, demonstrating the transferability of simulation-guided reasoning to models with fewer parameters.
- **Stage 2 (DYNA-GRPO):** DYNA-GRPO further boosts performance on AndroidWorld, achieving 40.7% on ID tasks and 22.9% on OOD tasks (for DISTILL-32B(RESIM) + DYNA-GRPO), outperforming its GRPO counterpart and even the RESIM inference-only results. This confirms the framework's efficacy in complex, realistic, visual environments.

**Error Analysis (AndroidWorld):**
- While significant improvements are shown, RESIM's performance in AndroidWorld is not near-perfect like in Sokoban. This is attributed to limitations of the underlying rollout model (Qwen2.5-VL-72B), specifically its incomplete understanding of GUI interfaces, certain button functionalities, and its inability to recover effectively after multiple mistakes. This suggests that the quality of the base VLM remains a bottleneck in highly complex, visual environments.

In summary, the main findings strongly support the central thesis: integrating and improving simulation ability is critical for AI agents tackling long-horizon tasks. Dyna-Mind effectively achieves this through its two-stage framework, leading to marked performance gains and more efficient reasoning traces across diverse environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Dyna-Mind framework represents a significant step forward in the development of more capable and adaptive AI agents for complex, interactive environments. Its primary significance lies in providing a principled, two-stage methodology to explicitly integrate and enhance "world simulation" capabilities directly within the reasoning process of (V)LM agents, moving beyond reactive behaviors or costly inference-time search mechanisms.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**
1. **Cognitive Alignment:** By drawing inspiration from human cognition's "vicarious trial and error," Dyna-Mind offers a biologically plausible approach to AI agent design, suggesting a path towards more human-like intelligence in planning and decision-making.
2. **Effective Simulation Learning (RESIM):** RESIM provides a robust mechanism to generate high-quality, simulation-guided reasoning traces grounded in *real environment interactions*. This is a crucial advancement over methods that rely on LLM-generated synthetic data, which can be prone to errors and biases. By directly learning from expanded search trees built from actual experiences, agents acquire more faithful world dynamics.
3. **Online Refinement and Integration (Dyna-GRPO):** DYNA-GRPO introduces an innovative approach to online reinforcement learning by leveraging both scalar outcome rewards and detailed *intermediate state feedback*. This allows for continuous, experience-driven refinement of both the agent's policy and its internal world model. The SIMROLLOUT mechanism, in particular, empowers agents to learn to correct their own internal simulations based on real outcomes, a vital meta-learning capability.
4. **Efficiency and Compactness:** The distillation process in Stage 1 enables smaller models to learn complex, simulation-guided reasoning in a more compact form (e.g., 11x fewer tokens than DeepSeek-R1's traces), which is critical for deployment in resource-constrained environments.
5. **Broad Applicability:** Demonstrated effectiveness across diverse environments—from synthetic grid-world games (Sokoban) and text-based embodied tasks (ALFWorld) to realistic visual GUI control (AndroidWorld)—underscores the generality and robustness of the Dyna-Mind framework.

**Potential Impact:**
- **More Capable Generalist Agents:** This work could lead to the development of more robust, adaptive, and autonomous AI agents capable of tackling open-ended, long-horizon tasks in real-world applications such as:
 - **Web navigation and automation:** Agents that can reliably browse, extract information, and perform tasks on the internet.
 - **Computer/phone control:** Agents that can operate digital devices by understanding GUI, planning actions, and recovering from errors.
 - **Scientific discovery and research:** Agents assisting in complex data analysis, experimental design, and knowledge synthesis.
 - **Embodied AI:** Robots and virtual agents that can plan and act effectively in physical or simulated worlds.
- **Reduced Inference Overhead:** By internalizing simulation capabilities through training, Dyna-Mind can reduce the need for computationally expensive inference-time search algorithms or modular systems, making agents more efficient and scalable.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework opens new avenues for research into better world modeling, experience replay mechanisms, and the integration of cognitive science principles into AI. Further work can explore enhancing the foundation models for better GUI understanding, incorporating more advanced search algorithms (like MCTS) in data generation, or studying agent-user-environment interactions.

**Ethical Considerations:**
The paper also includes an ethics statement, acknowledging the potential for misuse of highly capable autonomous AI agents (e.g., for scams, misinformation). This highlights a critical, broader impact: as AI agents become more proficient at simulating and interacting with environments, the importance of developing robust guardrails and ethical guidelines for their deployment becomes paramount.

In conclusion, Dyna-Mind provides a compelling argument and a concrete framework for integrating mental simulation into AI agent reasoning. By enabling agents to "vicariously trial and error" based on real experience, it paves the way for a new generation of AI systems that are more intelligent, adaptive, and effective in navigating the complexities of the digital and physical worlds.
