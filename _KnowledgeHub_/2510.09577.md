# Dyna-Mind: Learning to Simulate from Experience for Better AI Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> The DYNA-MIND framework provides a two-stage training approach that teaches AI agents to integrate and improve internal world simulations from experience, thereby enhancing their reasoning and decision-making in complex environments. This method yields superior performance, reaching 92.5% success on ALFWorld and 40.7% on AndroidWorld in-distribution tasks, while generating simulation-guided reasoning more efficiently.

## Problem

- AI agents, particularly those based on large language models, perform poorly on complex, long-horizon interactive tasks requiring multi-step planning and dynamic environmental interaction.
- Agents lack the cognitive ability of 'vicarious trial and error,' or mentally simulating future states and consequences before acting, which is crucial for adaptable decision-making.
- Current solutions for integrating world models are either computationally expensive during inference or rely on synthetic data, which can propagate errors and biases.

## Method

- A two-stage training framework, DYNA-MIND, is proposed to infuse and improve ==simulation ability== directly into AI agents' reasoning processes.
- Stage 1, Reasoning with Simulations (RESIM), generates high-quality, simulation-guided reasoning traces from real environment interactions and distills them into a policy model via supervised fine-tuning.
- Stage 2, Dyna-GRPO, refines the agent's internal simulation and policy through online reinforcement learning by using ground-truth future states to enable self-correction.

## Results

- DYNA-GRPO achieved **92.5%** task success on ALFWorld and **82.5%** on Sokoban, surpassing other online reinforcement learning methods and strong commercial models.
- In AndroidWorld, the framework reached **40.7%** success on in-distribution tasks and **22.9%** on out-of-distribution tasks, a notable improvement over baselines like GPT-4o (**5.1%**).
- Distilled models leveraging RESIM generated high-quality, simulation-guided reasoning traces with 11 times fewer tokens on average compared to DeepSeek-R1's traces, demonstrating efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly teaching AI agents to generate structured reasoning traces with future state anticipations, grounded in real environment dynamics, significantly enhances performance in interactive tasks.
> - The 'vicarious trial and error' ability, inspired by human cognition, is crucial for improving long-horizon planning and decision-making in AI agents.
> - Refining internal simulations and policies using online reinforcement learning with intermediate state feedback from real environments yields more accurate world models than synthetic data.
