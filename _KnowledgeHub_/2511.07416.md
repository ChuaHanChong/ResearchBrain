# Robot Learning from a Physical World Model

> [!summary] Summary
> A new framework, PhysWorld, enables robots to learn and execute complex manipulation tasks in a zero-shot manner by generating physically feasible actions from task-conditioned videos. It rebuilds the physical world from generated visual data, leading to an 82% average success rate in real-world tasks and significantly reducing grasping failures from 18% to 3%.

## Problem

- Generated videos, while visually plausible, lack physical accuracy, making direct translation to robot actions often infeasible or inaccurate in the real world.
- Existing robot learning methods from generated videos either still require substantial real-world robot demonstrations or suffer from physically ungrounded, pixel-level imitation.
- The high cost and labor intensity of collecting large-scale real-world robot data limit the scalability and generalizability of robotic manipulation systems.

## Method

- Introduces **PhysWorld**, a framework that generates ==task-conditioned videos== and then reconstructs a physically interactable ==digital twin== of the scene from the visual outputs.
- Employs ==geometry-aligned 4D reconstruction==, textured mesh generation with generative priors, and physical property estimation from the generated monocular videos.
- Leverages an ==object-centric residual reinforcement learning== approach to learn robust manipulation policies within the reconstructed physical world model.

## Results

- Achieved an **82%** average success rate across **10** diverse real-world manipulation tasks, outperforming baseline zero-shot visual imitation methods by a substantial margin (**+15** percentage points over RIGVid).
- The explicit physical world model reduced grasping failures from **18%** to **3%** and completely eliminated tracking failures observed in purely visual approaches.
- Object-centric learning demonstrated superior robustness (up to **90%** success) compared to embodiment-centric learning, with residual reinforcement learning accelerating policy convergence significantly.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - While video generation offers vast visual demonstrations, explicit physical world modeling is essential to bridge the gap between visual plausibility and physical feasibility for robotic tasks.
> - High-quality 3D geometry and physical properties can be effectively reconstructed from monocular, generated videos by integrating 4D reconstruction with generative priors.
> - Focusing on object motions as learning targets and using residual reinforcement learning within a physical simulation environment leads to more robust and efficient policy acquisition.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning is rapidly evolving, with a growing emphasis on leveraging large-scale data and generalizable AI models to enable robots to perform complex tasks. A particularly promising direction involves using generative models, especially video generation models, to synthesize visual demonstrations for robotic manipulation. These models, trained on vast internet datasets, can produce photorealistic videos of tasks being completed, offering an "infinite" source of potential training data for robots without the need for costly and labor-intensive real-world data collection.

However, a critical gap exists in translating these visually plausible, but physically ungrounded, generated videos into executable robotic actions. Previous approaches to robot learning from generated videos fall into a few categories:
1. **Inverse dynamics/policy learning with real data:** Some methods train action models using generated video frames, but still rely on large amounts of real robotic demonstrations for alignment and physical grounding-. This negates the zero-shot benefit of video generation.
2. **Direct pixel-level imitation:** Other approaches directly extract robotic actions by following visual cues (e.g., optical flow, sparse tracks, object poses) from generated videos-. The fundamental limitation here is that pixel-level imitation inherently "neglects physics." Generated videos prioritize visual fidelity, not physical accuracy or constraint satisfaction. Consequently, directly retargeting pixel motions often leads to physically inaccurate or infeasible manipulations in the real world. This "physical feasibility bottleneck" is the core problem PhysWorld aims to address.

The paper argues that despite their generalization power, video generation models only provide *visual plausibility*. For robots operating in the physical world, *physical accuracy* is paramount. This work enters the landscape by explicitly introducing a "proxy physical world model" built *from* generated videos. This approach bridges the gap by transforming implicit visual guidance from videos into physically executable robotic trajectories. It sits at the intersection of video generation, 3D reconstruction, and robot reinforcement learning, aiming to achieve zero-shot generalizable robotic manipulation without relying on real robot data collection. It builds upon ideas from "real-to-sim-to-real" pipelines but innovates by reconstructing the physical scene *from generated videos* rather than real-world multi-view captures or ad-hoc laboratory setups.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable **zero-shot generalizable robotic manipulation from video generation** by integrating physical world modeling. The authors introduce **PhysWorld**, a framework designed to overcome the limitations of current approaches that struggle to translate visually generated demonstrations into physically accurate robotic actions.

The core motivations are:

1. **Leveraging the power of video generation:** Recent advancements in generative models allow for the synthesis of photorealistic, task-conditioned videos. These videos represent a vast, readily available, and diverse source of "demonstrations" that could revolutionize robot learning if properly utilized.
2. **Addressing the physical feasibility bottleneck:** The main challenge is that generated videos, while visually compelling, lack physical grounding. Directly translating pixel motions to robot actions often results in inaccurate or infeasible real-world manipulations due to neglected physical constraints. Robots operate in a 3D physical world, not just a 2D visual one.
3. **Eliminating the need for real-world data:** Collecting large-scale real-world robot demonstrations is expensive, time-consuming, and labor-intensive, limiting the scalability and generalization of robot learning. PhysWorld aims to completely bypass this requirement.
4. **Improving manipulation accuracy and robustness:** By explicitly modeling the physical world, the framework seeks to provide realistic physical feedback, allowing robots to learn from generated videos in a physically consistent manner, thereby substantially improving manipulation accuracy and robustness compared to purely visual imitation methods.
5. **Achieving zero-shot generalization:** The goal is for a robot to perform a novel task by simply observing a generated video and receiving a language prompt, without any prior training on that specific task or environment with real robots.

In essence, the paper aims to develop a system that can "understand" the physics implied by a generated video, reconstruct that physical reality, and then learn to execute the task within that reconstructed physical world, thereby making the visually guided action physically realizable.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PhysWorld is a unified framework that couples video generation with physical world reconstruction and object-centric residual reinforcement learning. It takes a single RGB-D image and a language task prompt as input and outputs physically feasible robotic actions. The methodology can be broken down into three main stages:

**A. Task-Conditioned Video Generation**
- **Model:** PhysWorld supports various image-to-video models with text control (e.g., Veo3, Tesseract, CogVideoX, Cosmos-2B). In this work, Veo3 is primarily used due to its high output quality.
- **Process:** Given an initial RGB-D image ($I_0$) and a task command, the video generation model synthesizes a sequence of future frames ($\{I_1, \ldots, I_T\}$) depicting the completion of the task.

**B. Physical World Modeling from Video Generation**
This is the core innovation that transforms pixel-level guidance into physically grounded representations.
- **Geometry-aligned 4D Reconstruction:**
 - Initial depth estimation: MegaSaM is used to produce temporally consistent depth estimates ($\{D'_0, \ldots, D'_T\}$) for each generated video frame.
 - Metric calibration: Since MegaSaM's estimates are not metrically scaled, the real-world captured depth image ($D_0$) is used to calibrate the generated depths. A robust regression objective is solved to find a global scale ($\alpha$) and shift ($\beta$) that aligns $\alpha D'_0 + \beta$ with $D_0$. This calibration is applied to all frames, yielding metric-aligned depth maps ($\{D_t\}$) and dynamic point clouds ($\{P_t\}$).
- **Textured Mesh Generation:** Generated videos provide only partial views. To obtain complete 3D meshes suitable for physics simulation:
 - Object-background separation: The initial image ($I_0$) and its point cloud ($P_0$) are segmented into individual objects and background. Missing regions in the background (occluded by objects) are filled using image inpainting.
 - Object mesh generation: For each individual object crop, an image-to-3D generator produces a canonical textured mesh ($M_o$).
 - Background mesh reconstruction: For the background, a novel "object-on-ground" assumption is used. Occluded background regions are inferred as either planar supporting surfaces or extending to infinity. Camera rays are cast through occluded pixels to compute intersections with the inferred supporting plane or scene boundaries, filling in the background point cloud ($P_b$). The background mesh ($M_b$) is then reconstructed via height-map triangulation and textured with the completed background image.
 - Assembly and Alignment: All object and background meshes ($\{M_o, M_b\}$) are assembled into a complete scene by aligning and resizing them to match the observed point cloud ($P_0$) through registration.
- **Physical Scene Reconstruction and Alignment:** To make the meshes physically interactable in a simulator:
 - **Physical Property Estimation:** Vision-language models (VLMs) are queried with object categories to estimate physical parameters like mass and friction coefficients, which are then assigned to each object and the background.
 - **Gravity Alignment:** The scene is transformed from the camera frame to the world frame, aligning the estimated ground plane normal (via RANSAC) with the world's up-axis to ensure physically plausible simulation under gravity.
 - **Collision Optimization:** To prevent initial penetrations, object placements are optimized relative to the background mesh. This involves voxelizing the background into a signed distance field (SDF) and minimizing a vertical translation for each object to ensure a minimum clearance using gradient descent.
 - **Result:** A physically interactable "digital twin" of the scene is created.

**C. Object-Centric Learning from the Physical World Model**
This stage leverages the physically grounded model to learn robotic policies.
- **Learning Targets (Object Poses):** Instead of embodiment motions (prone to hallucination), the method focuses on object motions. FoundationPose is used to recover per-frame object pose trajectories ($\{x_{o_t}\}$), which serve as supervision.
- **Residual Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
 - **Motivation:** Standard grasping models and motion planners often fail in complex tasks due to compounding errors. Pure RL requires long training times and careful reward design.
 - **Approach:** A residual RL approach combines baseline actions from grasping and motion planning with a learned residual policy. The executed action ($a_t$) is the sum of a baseline action ($a^{base}_t$) and the residual policy's output ($\pi_\theta(o_t)$).
 - **Observation Space ($o_t$):** Includes current end-effector and object poses ($x^{ee}_t, x^{obj}_t$), normalized time ($\tau_t$), target object pose from the video ($x_{o_t}$), and baseline actions (grasp proposal $x_{grasp}$, pre-grasp offset $d_{pre}$, planned end-effector pose $x^{base}_t$).
 - **Action Space:** The policy outputs a residual translation ($\Delta p_t$) and rotation ($\omega_t$) which refine the baseline end-effector pose command.
 - **Rewards:** Simple, generalizable rewards are used:
 - **Object Tracking Reward ($r^{trk}_t$):** Encourages the robot to align the object with its target pose from the video (exponential decay based on position and orientation error).
 - **Grasp Reward ($r^{grasp}_t$):** Penalizes excessive distance between the end-effector and the object when grasping, ensuring a stable grasp.
 - **Planning Reward ($r^{plan}_t$):** Assigns a negative reward if inverse kinematics or motion planning fail, discouraging infeasible actions.
 - **Algorithm:** PPO is used to train the residual policy within the physical world model. The residual formulation significantly accelerates convergence by guiding exploration.

The entire framework allows for zero-shot manipulation by first generating a visual demonstration, then converting it into a physically interactable scene with specific object motion targets, and finally training a robot policy to robustly execute these motions under physical constraints using residual reinforcement learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments aim to answer three key empirical questions regarding video generation, physical world modeling, and learning strategies.

**Q1: Video Generation Enables Generalizable Robotic Manipulation**
- **Qualitative Evaluation:** PhysWorld was tested on 10 diverse real-world manipulation tasks (e.g., "Wipe the whiteboard," "Pour the tomato from the pan onto the plate"). Qualitative results (Figure 5) demonstrate that the task-conditioned generated videos provide rich visual guidance across varied scenes, enabling zero-shot robotic manipulation in the real world without additional robot data.
- **Video Generation Quality Impact:** A comparison of four image-to-video models (Veo3, Tesseract, CogVideoX1.5-5B, Cosmos-2B) showed that **Veo3 achieved the highest usable video ratio (70%)**, indicating its superior quality for generating task-consistent videos from which object poses could be robustly recovered (Table I). Models fine-tuned on robotic data (e.g., Tesseract) tended to outperform generic generators, emphasizing the need for high-quality, task-consistent video outputs.

**Q2: World Modeling Improves Manipulation Robustness**
- **Physical Scene Reconstruction Quality:** Figure 3 visually confirms that the method effectively reconstructs geometry-consistent and physically interactable scenes from monocular generated videos by integrating 4D reconstruction with generative priors.
- **Effectiveness of World Modeling:** PhysWorld was quantitatively compared against three zero-shot methods without physical world modeling (RIGVid, Gen2Act (modified), AVDC) across 10 real-world tasks with 10 rollouts each.
 - **PhysWorld achieved the highest average success rate (82%),** significantly outperforming the next best method, RIGVid (67%) (Figure 4). This substantial improvement highlights that learning within a physical world model provides crucial corrective feedback, reducing compounding errors common in grasping and planning, particularly during critical phases like picking, insertion, and pouring.
 - The results also showed that using **object poses as tracking targets significantly outperformed methods relying on point tracks or optical flows**, which suffer from drifting under occlusion and motion blur.
- **Failure Mode Analysis:** A detailed breakdown of failure cases (Figure 6) revealed that introducing the physical world model **drastically reduced grasping failures (from 18% to 3%) and eliminated tracking failures (from 5% to 0%)** compared to RIGVid. The main remaining failure mode for PhysWorld was reconstruction errors (7%), primarily due to the challenges of reconstructing complete, metrically accurate geometry from monocular, generated videos in occluded regions.

**Q3: Object-Centric Learning Enhances Policy Effectiveness**
- **Object-Centric vs. Embodiment-Centric Learning:** A comparison on tasks like "Put the book in the bookshelf" and "Put the shoe in the shoebox" (Table II) showed that **object-centric learning (90% and 80% success rates, respectively) was remarkably more robust than embodiment-centric learning (30% and 10%)**. The reason is that generated videos often contain hallucinated or inconsistent human hands, while object motions are more stable and reliably estimated, leading to better transfer to robots.
- **Residual RL vs. RL from Scratch:** Training for the "Pour the tomato from the pan onto the plate" task (Figure 7) demonstrated that **residual RL converged much faster (within a few hundred iterations) and achieved higher object tracking rewards** compared to training an RL policy from scratch under the same physical world model. The baseline grasp-and-plan actions effectively constrain the exploration space, allowing the residual policy to quickly learn corrective adjustments guided by physical feedback.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PhysWorld represents a significant step forward in robot learning, particularly in leveraging the rapidly advancing capabilities of generative AI for robotic control. Its core significance lies in bridging the critical gap between visually plausible generated demonstrations and physically feasible robotic actions.

**Key Contributions and Impact:**

1. **Enabling Zero-Shot Generalizable Manipulation:** The most impactful aspect is the ability to perform novel, complex manipulation tasks in the real world without any task-specific real robot data collection. This eliminates a major bottleneck in robot deployment and accelerates the development of general-purpose robots.
2. **Physically Grounded Learning from Synthetic Data:** By introducing an explicit physical world model derived from generated videos, PhysWorld ensures that the robot's actions are not just visually guided but also adhere to physical laws. This leads to significantly improved accuracy and robustness compared to prior purely visual imitation approaches.
3. **Robust 3D Reconstruction from Monocular Generated Videos:** The novel methods for geometry-aligned 4D reconstruction, textured mesh generation with generative priors, and physical scene alignment are crucial technical advancements that enable the creation of high-fidelity digital twins from limited, imperfect synthetic visual data.
4. **Effective Object-Centric Residual Reinforcement Learning:** The adoption of object-centric learning targets and a residual RL framework significantly enhances the policy's effectiveness, learning speed, and robustness. By focusing on objects rather than potentially hallucinatory embodiment motions, and by refining strong baselines, the system efficiently acquires reliable control policies.
5. **Scalability and Accessibility:** By reducing the reliance on real-world data and expert demonstrations, PhysWorld opens avenues for more scalable and accessible robot learning. Researchers and developers can potentially prototype and train complex manipulation skills purely in simulation derived from generated content.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Accelerated Robot Skill Acquisition:** New manipulation skills could be developed and deployed much faster.
- **Broader Range of Robotic Applications:** Robots could be quickly adapted to perform a wider variety of tasks in unstructured environments, such as logistics, manufacturing, and even household chores.
- **Reduced Development Costs:** The elimination of extensive real-world data collection could significantly lower the cost and time involved in training robotic systems.
- **Advancement of Generative AI for Robotics:** This work demonstrates a concrete application of video generation models that pushes beyond mere visual content creation, proving their utility as powerful tools for robot learning.

**Limitations and Future Work:**

The authors acknowledge that physical world modeling is bounded by the fidelity of physical simulators and may introduce additional sim-to-real gaps. While the results suggest the benefits outweigh these limitations, further improvements in simulation accuracy and robust sim-to-real transfer remain critical. The current reconstruction method from monocular, generated videos can lead to misaligned geometry in occluded regions, which could be mitigated by incorporating multi-view reconstruction if environmental data is available.

Future work includes synthesizing physically accurate videos using this framework to potentially train even better robotic video generation models, creating a virtuous cycle between generative AI and robot learning. Exploring other forms of motion supervision beyond object poses could also expand the framework's capabilities. Overall, PhysWorld lays a strong foundation for future research in physics-informed robot learning from rich, synthetic visual data.
