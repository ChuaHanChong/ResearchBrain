# Latent Policy Steering with Embodiment-Agnostic Pretrained World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Latent Policy Steering (LPS) introduces an embodiment-agnostic pretrained World Model (WM) that utilizes optical flow as a universal action representation, improving visuomotor policy performance in low-data robot learning by effectively leveraging diverse data sources. The approach, developed at Carnegie Mellon University, achieved a 70% relative improvement over baselines in real-world tasks with 30-50 target-embodiment demonstrations.

## Problem

- Training high-performing robot visuomotor policies typically requires extensive, high-quality, embodiment-specific training data, which is resource-intensive to collect.
- Existing approaches struggle with "embodiment gaps" and "mismatched action spaces," making it difficult to transfer policies or data across different robot platforms or human demonstrations.
- Large pretrained models are computationally expensive to fine-tune and often require substantial target-specific data to generalize effectively to new tasks or robots.

## Method

- Proposing ==optical flow== as an ==embodiment-agnostic action representation==, enabling the aggregation and utilization of data from various robots, simulations, and human demonstrations.
- Pretraining a ==World Model== using this optical flow representation on diverse multi-embodiment data to learn general dynamics, followed by finetuning the WM and a ==diffusion-based base policy== with limited target-embodiment data.
- Developing **Latent Policy Steering (LPS)**, which employs a ==robust value function== trained to penalize deviations from expert data in the ==latent space==, to evaluate and select optimal action plans generated by the base policy during inference.

## Results

- LPS-mix* demonstrated a **70%** relative improvement over the Behavior Cloning baseline with **30-50** target-embodiment demonstrations in real-world pick-and-place and complex long-horizon tasks, and a **44%** improvement with **60-100** demonstrations.
- Pretraining the WM with optical flow (LPS-sim(flow)*) yielded superior results compared to pretraining with end-effector (EEF) pose actions, highlighting the advantage of embodiment-agnostic representation.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of the robust value function, as variants without explicit penalization of out-of-distribution states (LPS-vanilla, LPS-bootstrap) performed worse than the Behavior Cloning baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Similar skills performed across different embodiments produce visually analogous motion patterns, which can be effectively captured by off-the-shelf representations like optical flow.
> - World Models are well-suited for leveraging sub-optimal and diverse data efficiently because their primary function is to model system dynamics rather than directly learn optimal actions.
> - A value function for policy steering must be explicitly robust to out-of-distribution states encountered during inference, actively penalizing deviations from expert data to maintain stability and guide the robot effectively.
