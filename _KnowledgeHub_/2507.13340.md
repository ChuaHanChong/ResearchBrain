# Latent Policy Steering with Embodiment-Agnostic Pretrained World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Latent Policy Steering (LPS) introduces an embodiment-agnostic pretrained World Model (WM) that utilizes optical flow as a universal action representation, improving visuomotor policy performance in low-data robot learning by effectively leveraging diverse data sources. The approach, developed at Carnegie Mellon University, achieved a 70% relative improvement over baselines in real-world tasks with 30-50 target-embodiment demonstrations.

## Problem

- Training high-performing robot visuomotor policies typically requires extensive, high-quality, embodiment-specific training data, which is resource-intensive to collect.
- Existing approaches struggle with "embodiment gaps" and "mismatched action spaces," making it difficult to transfer policies or data across different robot platforms or human demonstrations.
- Large pretrained models are computationally expensive to fine-tune and often require substantial target-specific data to generalize effectively to new tasks or robots.

## Method

- Proposing ==optical flow== as an ==embodiment-agnostic action representation==, enabling the aggregation and utilization of data from various robots, simulations, and human demonstrations.
- Pretraining a ==World Model== using this optical flow representation on diverse multi-embodiment data to learn general dynamics, followed by finetuning the WM and a ==diffusion-based base policy== with limited target-embodiment data.
- Developing **Latent Policy Steering (LPS)**, which employs a ==robust value function== trained to penalize deviations from expert data in the ==latent space==, to evaluate and select optimal action plans generated by the base policy during inference.

## Results

- LPS-mix* demonstrated a **70%** relative improvement over the Behavior Cloning baseline with **30-50** target-embodiment demonstrations in real-world pick-and-place and complex long-horizon tasks, and a **44%** improvement with **60-100** demonstrations.
- Pretraining the WM with optical flow (LPS-sim(flow)*) yielded superior results compared to pretraining with end-effector (EEF) pose actions, highlighting the advantage of embodiment-agnostic representation.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of the robust value function, as variants without explicit penalization of out-of-distribution states (LPS-vanilla, LPS-bootstrap) performed worse than the Behavior Cloning baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Similar skills performed across different embodiments produce visually analogous motion patterns, which can be effectively captured by off-the-shelf representations like optical flow.
> - World Models are well-suited for leveraging sub-optimal and diverse data efficiently because their primary function is to model system dynamics rather than directly learn optimal actions.
> - A value function for policy steering must be explicitly robust to out-of-distribution states encountered during inference, actively penalizing deviations from expert data to maintain stability and guide the robot effectively.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses challenges in robot visuomotor policy learning, particularly in low-data regimes and when leveraging diverse datasets. Robot learning, especially through imitation learning or behavior cloning (BC), traditionally requires substantial amounts of expert demonstration data. This data is often specific to a particular robot, task, or environment, necessitating repeated collection for different embodiments.

Recent advancements have focused on building generalist robot policies by training on large datasets from multiple robots and environments. Approaches such as cross-embodiment training, modular architectures with common trunks and varying action heads, and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models aim to improve generalization. However, these large models can be computationally expensive to finetune for specific tasks and may still require considerable amounts of task-specific data to achieve high performance. A key challenge in this area is the "embodiment gap," where differences in robot morphology, proprioception, and action spaces make it difficult to transfer knowledge directly across diverse robots or from human demonstrations.

Another line of research involves using World Models (WMs) to learn environment dynamics. WMs can be utilized for planning by predicting future states and evaluating action sequences. Policy steering is an extension of this concept, where a value function and optionally a WM are used to refine the outputs of a base policy. This can help compensate for policy failure modes or enforce constraints.

The current work aims to integrate the strengths of these approaches by developing a method that effectively leverages diverse, multi-embodiment data (including human videos) for pretraining, even when specific action labels are disparate or absent. It addresses the embodiment gap by proposing an embodiment-agnostic action representation and enhances policy performance in low-data scenarios by employing a robust value function within a policy steering framework. This positions the work at the intersection of scalable robot learning from diverse data, world model-based planning, and robust policy improvement in data-scarce environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to improve the performance of learned robot visuomotor policies in low-data regimes by effectively leveraging diverse, multi-embodiment data.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges in current robot learning paradigms:
- **Data Dependency:** Visuomotor policies, particularly those learned through behavior cloning, are heavily reliant on the size and quality of the training dataset. Collecting sufficient expert demonstrations is a time-consuming and labor-intensive process.
- **Embodiment Gaps:** While large-scale robot and human datasets are becoming more available, existing methods struggle to leverage them effectively due to significant embodiment gaps and mismatched action spaces. Each robot has specific proprioception and action spaces, making direct transfer or combined training challenging. This often leads to pretrained representations that are heavily dependent on the embodiments included in the dataset and may generalize poorly to new ones.
- **Finetuning Costs:** Even with large pretrained models, achieving high success rates on new tasks typically requires substantial additional finetuning data, which defeats some of the purpose of pretraining for low-data scenarios.
- **Sub-optimal Data Utilization:** Many available datasets, especially those from "play" or general human videos, may contain sub-optimal demonstrations or lack explicit action information, making them difficult to directly use for policy learning.

The authors' main insight is that similar skills performed across different embodiments often produce visually similar motions. These visual similarities can be captured by off-the-shelf action representations, such as optical flow, which are embodiment-agnostic. Furthermore, World Models (WMs) are identified as suitable for leveraging sub-optimal data because their primary focus is on modeling environmental dynamics rather than directly learning expert policies.

Given these motivations, the specific objectives are:
1. To propose and validate optical flow as an embodiment-agnostic action representation to pretrain a World Model across a variety of robots and human embodiments. This aims to enable the use of accessible and cost-efficient data regardless of the source embodiment.
2. To develop Latent Policy Steering (LPS) as a method to finetune the embodiment-agnostic World Model to a target embodiment and subsequently improve a base policy. A critical aspect of LPS is the development of a robust value function designed to mitigate inference-time distribution shift by training on both expert states and states the policy is likely to visit during inference.
3. To demonstrate the effectiveness of this approach in enhancing policy performance on long-horizon manipulation tasks in both simulated and real-world environments, particularly in scenarios with limited target-embodiment demonstration data. This includes showing that pretraining WMs on abundant data from other embodiments can significantly boost performance for new target robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, Latent Policy Steering (LPS), integrates pretraining an embodiment-agnostic World Model (WM) with a finetuning process that includes training a robust value function and steering a base policy during inference. The approach consists of three main stages:

#### 3.1. A. Flow-as-Action: Embodiment-Agnostic World Modeling

The core idea for cross-embodiment transfer is to use an embodiment-agnostic action representation. The paper proposes **optical flow** as this representation, based on the observation that visually similar motions occur across different embodiments when performing similar skills (e.g., picking up an object). Optical flow quantifies visual motion patterns between consecutive image frames.

1. **World Model Architecture:** The World Model utilizes the objective and architecture of Dreamer v3, which is a recurrent state-space model (RSSM). This model learns to predict future latent states from current observations and actions.
2. **Optical Flow Encoder:** During pretraining, an optical flow encoder is added to the WM. This encoder is a convolution-based network that processes optical flow images and outputs an *n*-dimensional vector. The dimensionality *n* is set to match the action space dimension of the target embodiment (∥A∥).
3. **End-to-End Training:** The WM is pretrained end-to-end on a large, cross-embodiment dataset (C) where actions are represented by these encoded optical flow vectors. This forces the network to extract salient motion features and to disregard embodiment-specific details (e.g., robot morphology, noise) that are irrelevant to the common visual motion patterns. By focusing on dynamics modeling, the WM can effectively learn from diverse and potentially sub-optimal data.

#### 3.2. B. Target Embodiment Finetuning

After pretraining, the WM is adapted to a specific target embodiment and a base policy is trained.

1. **Base Policy Training:** A base visuomotor policy (π) is trained from scratch using a small expert dataset (E) collected on the target robot embodiment. This policy learns to map observations (x_t) to actions (a_t). The paper mentions using a diffusion policy for this purpose.
2. **WM Finetuning:** The pretrained WM is finetuned on the target embodiment dataset (E). Crucially, the optical flow encoder is replaced with direct input of normalized robot actions, which are also ∥A∥-dimensional. While this introduces a distribution shift from the optical flow representation used during pretraining, the authors found this direct approach to be effective and simpler than attempting to explicitly align the representations. The WM is finetuned using the same Dreamer v3 objective, learning to predict future states based on actual robot actions.
3. **Robust Value Function Learning:** A robust value function V(s_t) is trained jointly with the finetuned WM. This value function estimates discounted future rewards from a given latent state. Its primary design goal is to handle states the policy is likely to visit during inference, which may deviate from the expert dataset, and to encourage the robot to stay close to the expert data distribution.
 - **Procedure (Algorithm 1):**
 - An expert demonstration sequence (x_t:t+h, a_t:t+h, r_t:t+h) is sampled from E.
 - A predicted action plan (a'_t:t+h) is sampled from the base policy π given the initial observation x_t.
 - The WM is used to predict latent state sequences for both the expert actions (s_t:t+h) and the policy's predicted actions (s'_t:t+h).
 - A similarity metric (cosine similarity) is computed between the expert latent states and the policy's predicted latent states (sim(s_t:t+h, s'_t:t+h)).
 - An additional reward (sim - 1)/2 is added to the environmental reward. This reward penalizes deviations of the policy's predicted states from the expert data distribution.
 - The value function is then optimized using a lambda-return objective (from Dreamer v3), training on a concatenation of both expert states and states predicted by the policy (S_all = concat(s_t:t+h, s'_t:t+h)) and their corresponding rewards (R_all = concat(R_λ, R'_λ)). This procedure ensures the value function is robust to inference-time distribution shift by "foreseeing" potential deviations and penalizing them.

#### 3.3. C. Latent Policy Steering (LPS)

During inference, LPS improves the base behavior cloning policy by evaluating multiple candidate action plans.

1. **Candidate Plan Sampling:** Given the current observation, the base policy generates B candidate action plans, each with a horizon *h*.
2. **WM Prediction and Evaluation:** For each candidate plan, the finetuned WM predicts the corresponding future latent states. The robust value function then computes state values for these predicted states.
3. **Plan-Level Value:** A plan-level value is calculated for each candidate plan as a discounted weighted average of its predicted state values. This weighted average, with heavier weights on future states, is intended to reduce noise in value function predictions.
4. **Best Plan Selection:** The action plan with the highest plan-level value is selected and executed for the specified horizon. The process then repeats with replanning. This mechanism steers the base policy towards actions that the robust value function deems more desirable, i.e., closer to the expert data distribution and leading to higher rewards.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates Latent Policy Steering (LPS) in both real-world environments and a simulated benchmark (Robomimic), comparing it against baselines and providing ablation studies.

#### 4.1. A. Real-world Experiments

The experiments involved a Franka robot on four tasks: "Put-radish-in-pot" (pick-and-place), "Sweep-salad-off-the-board," "Scoop-beads-with-spoon," and "Fold-towel-to-triangle" (the latter three requiring tool use or deformable object manipulation). The target datasets consisted of 30-50 or 60-100 demonstrations.

- **Comparison with Baselines:** LPS-mix*, which used a WM pretrained on a mixture of simulation, real-world robot, and human data, demonstrated substantial improvements.
 - With 30-50 target-embodiment demonstrations, LPS-mix* achieved an average success rate of 36.2% across tasks, representing a 70% relative improvement over the Behavior Cloning (BC) baseline (21.2%). LPS* (LPS without pretraining) performed similarly to BC (21.2%).
 - With 60-100 demonstrations, LPS-mix* reached an average success rate of 73.8%, a 44% relative improvement over BC (51.2%). LPS* also showed improvement to 62.5%.
- **HPT Comparison:** The HPT baseline, an embodiment-dependent pretrained policy finetuned on the target dataset, performed poorly, with average success rates of 5.0% (30-50 demos) and 13.8% (60-100 demos). This suggests that while HPT was pretrained on a large-scale dataset, its embodiment-dependent nature made finetuning in low-data regimes challenging, reinforcing the need for embodiment-agnostic representations.
- **Task Complexity:** LPS-mix* showed improvements across various task complexities, including those involving tool-use and deformable objects.

#### 4.2. B. Ablation Studies in Simulated Benchmark (Robomimic)

Ablation studies were conducted on four Robomimic tasks (Lift, Can, Square, Transport) using a Franka robot, primarily focusing on 30-50 expert demonstrations.

- **Performance with Different Pretraining Embodiments:**
 - With 50 demonstrations, LPS-mix* (pretrained on mixed data) generally outperformed other LPS variants and achieved a 10.6% relative improvement over the BC baseline, averaged across tasks (63.4% vs. 57.3%).
 - LPS-sim* (pretrained on simulation data) and LPS-real* (pretrained on real-world robot data) also showed improvements over BC.
 - LPS demonstrated particular effectiveness on the "Transport" task, a long-horizon bimanual task with a 14-dimensional action space, showing a 34% relative improvement over BC with sufficient demonstrations.
 - With only 30 demonstrations, the improvement from LPS variants over BC was less significant, suggesting that LPS benefits from a base policy that can generate diverse plans (multimodal), which may be harder to achieve with very limited data.
- **Scaling with Number of Embodiments (Figure 5):** Increasing the number of embodiments in the pretraining dataset (e.g., from simulation only to mixed sim, real-world robot, and human data) led to improved performance. This trend supports the utility of embodiment-agnostic action representations and WMs in leveraging more diverse pretraining data. Human video data from "play" also contributed to a competitive pretrained WM, attributed to potentially higher quality and diversity of human demonstrations within a shorter timeframe.
- **Optical Flow vs. End-Effector (EEF) Actions (Table III):** When comparing WM pretraining using optical flow versus end-effector (EEF) pose as an action representation (on a dataset of 3 simulated robots), optical flow (LPS-sim(flow)*) yielded better average success rates (35.9% vs. 34.4% for 30 demos; 62.4% vs. 59.1% for 50 demos). This indicates an advantage for optical flow in enabling embodiment-agnostic learning, particularly as it can also incorporate human data, which EEF actions cannot.
- **Robust Value Function Ablations (Table IV):**
 - **LPS-vanilla:** This variant, which trained the value function only on expert data rewards, performed worse than BC (62.9% vs. 65.2% average success rate on Robomimic Can, Lift, Square, Transport with 100 demos). It lacked robustness to out-of-distribution states.
 - **LPS-bootstrap:** This variant trained the value function on both expert and policy-predicted states but without penalizing deviations from the expert data. It also performed worse than BC (64.3%). It treated out-of-distribution states optimistically.
 - The full LPS, which included the reward penalizing distribution shifts, significantly outperformed both ablated variants and BC (68.7%), demonstrating the necessity of the specific robust value function design for the WM to effectively benefit the policy during inference.
- **Inference Horizon (Figure 6):** LPS generally improved policy performance for action prediction horizons up to 16. However, at longer horizons (e.g., 24), the performance decreased and became worse than BC. This was attributed to increased noise in the reward capturing distribution shifts over longer horizons, rendering the value function less effective.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of robot learning, particularly for addressing the challenges of data scarcity and embodiment generalization.

The core significance lies in:
1. **Enabling Cross-Embodiment Knowledge Transfer:** By proposing optical flow as an embodiment-agnostic action representation, the work provides a mechanism to effectively leverage diverse data sources, including readily available human videos and data from heterogeneous robot platforms, for pretraining a World Model. This mitigates the traditional embodiment gap, allowing robots to benefit from a broader spectrum of collected data without requiring direct action space compatibility.
2. **Improving Policies in Low-Data Regimes:** The demonstration of significant performance improvements (e.g., 70% relative improvement in real-world tasks with 30-50 demonstrations) highlights the method's ability to enhance visuomotor policies when only a limited amount of target-embodiment data is available. This can reduce the time and cost associated with robot skill acquisition.
3. **Robustness to Distribution Shift:** The introduction of Latent Policy Steering (LPS) with its robust value function explicitly trained to account for and penalize deviations from the expert data distribution is crucial. This addresses a common failure mode in learned policies, where deviations during inference can lead to unpredictable behavior and task failure. By guiding the policy back towards known successful states, LPS contributes to more reliable robot operation.
4. **Practical Applicability:** The method's effectiveness on real-world manipulation tasks, including those requiring tool use and interaction with deformable objects, suggests its potential for practical deployment in complex robotic scenarios. The ability to utilize "data from play" also opens avenues for more accessible and cost-effective data collection.

Potential impacts include:
- **Accelerated Robot Skill Acquisition:** Robots could learn new skills faster and with less expert human intervention by leveraging existing large, diverse datasets.
- **More Generalizable Robot Systems:** The embodiment-agnostic pretraining paradigm moves towards more generalist robot models that are less constrained by specific hardware configurations.
- **Reduced Data Collection Costs:** The ability to use cost-efficient or publicly available multi-embodiment data can lower the barrier to entry for many robotics researchers and developers.
- **Enhanced Policy Reliability:** The robust value function and policy steering mechanism can contribute to the development of more stable and safer robot policies, particularly in uncertain environments.

The identified limitations, such as optical flow's sensitivity to occlusion and viewpoint, and noisiness with non-static cameras, indicate areas for future research. However, the work establishes a foundation for building scalable action representations and augmenting them with embodiment-specific details to further enhance performance and address current constraints.
