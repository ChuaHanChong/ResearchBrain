# Mind the Gap: Examining the Self-Improvement Capabilities of Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This work gives a modular mathematical framework for LLM self-improvement: sample many generations, verify them with the same model, reweight or filter, then distill. Its central metric, the generation-verification gap, isolates verification precision from model-update confounders. Across seven model families and four tasks the relative gap grows monotonically with pre-training flops, while iterative gains saturate within two or three rounds.

## Problem

- Reward models trained on human labels are costly and give little signal in superhuman domains, while stronger-model annotation fails at the capability frontier.
- Reported self-improvement gains conflate verification quality with finetuning confounders such as answer formatting and output length, so they may not reflect real capability.
- Existing studies cover a single model family or one verification prompt, leaving scaling behaviour, task dependence and iteration limits essentially unmeasured.

## Method

- ==Generation-verification gap== (**GV-Gap**): accuracy of the self-verification-reweighted generation distribution minus the base distribution, plus a relative variant normalizing each prompt by its headroom to maximum utility.
- ==Three-desiderata decomposition== (improvable generation, informative verification, high-fidelity update) yielding the bound that post-update utility gain is at least the gap minus ==distillation error==.
- Four verification mechanisms under ==rejection sampling==: ==Multiple Choice== token probability, ==Chain-of-Thought Score== from 1 to 10, ==CoT-Binary==, and single-elimination ==Tournament== pairwise comparison, with quantile or global thresholds.

## Results

- With CoT-Score the relative gap rises roughly linearly in log pre-training flops across Qwen-1.5, Qwen-2, Qwen-2.5, Llama-2 and Yi-1.5; **0.5B** models and Llama-2 **7B** show non-positive gaps.
- Cross-verification: a Qwen-2 0.5B generator gains **24.91%** with a **72B** verifier versus **3.39%** self-verified, and gaps shrink as generator size grows; Natural Questions gaps stay below **1%**.
- Sudoku improves only at **72B** (gap **16.99%** over **8.82%** accuracy, about **200%**); iterative gaps reach zero by round three; AND-ensembling MC with CoT-Score lifts Qwen-2 1.5B from **5.27%** to **7.68%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Measure verification precision separately from the distillation step: apparent post-finetuning gains often come from output-format alignment rather than improved reasoning.
> - Self-improvement needs a generation-verification complexity separation the model can actually exploit; factual recall offers none, and Sudoku's separation stays unusable below frontier scale.
> - Iterative self-verification collapses generation diversity, with pass@k falling at large k, so preserving diversity matters more than adding rounds.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical and rapidly evolving area within the field of large language models: **self-improvement**. It positions itself at the intersection of synthetic data generation, self-training, LLM alignment, and test-time inference optimization.

The current paradigm in LLM development heavily relies on vast datasets, and as human-generated data becomes saturated or costly, synthetic data, often generated by LLMs themselves, has emerged as a crucial complement. However, this practice is not without pitfalls. Prior work (e.g., Bertrand et al., 2023; Gerstgrasser et al., 2024) has demonstrated that naive self-training on model-generated data can lead to **performance degradation or "model collapse"**. This necessitates mechanisms to ensure the quality and reliability of synthetic data.

One promising mitigation strategy is the incorporation of a "reliable" verifier (Gillman et al., 2024). While external reward models (Lightman et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024a) or stronger, frontier models (Chang et al., 2023; Havrilla et al., 2024) can serve this purpose, they often incur prohibitive costs or are impractical when the model itself is at the capability frontier. This motivates the concept of **LLM self-verification** – where a model verifies its own generations, leveraging the intuition that "verification is easier than generation."

Numerous empirical successes have been reported in self-improvement algorithms (Zelikman et al., 2022; Huang et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2022b), often involving iterative cycles of generation, self-verification/reranking, and distillation. This also ties into **improving test-time inference quality** by enabling models to self-critique and refine outputs with additional computation.

However, the authors identify a significant gap in the existing literature: a **lack of fundamental understanding** of LLM self-improvement. Much prior work focuses on specific model families or verification mechanisms, leading to limited generalizability and potential confounding factors. This paper directly addresses this gap by initiating a "comprehensive, modular and controlled study," aiming to dissect the phenomenon rather than just demonstrate isolated successes. It shifts the focus from proposing new self-improvement algorithms to a rigorous, systematic analysis of the underlying mechanisms and boundaries, particularly through the lens of a newly formalized metric: the generation-verification gap.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the widespread but often empirically-driven application of self-improvement in LLM development and deployment, coupled with a significant deficit in foundational understanding. The authors articulate their key objectives:

1. **Develop a Comprehensive and Modular Framework:** To move beyond anecdotal empirical successes, the paper aims to provide a structured, mathematical formulation of the self-improvement process. This involves dissecting the process into its core components: generation, verification, and model update. By modularizing, they seek to isolate the contribution and dynamics of each step.
2. **Formalize a Core Evaluation Metric:** A central objective is to introduce and rigorously define the "generation-verification gap (GV-Gap)" (and its relative variant) as the primary metric for quantifying self-improvement. This metric is designed to capture the "precision" of a model's self-verification capabilities, offering a more accurate measure of true improvement signal than simply observing performance difference after a model update, which can be confounded by other factors.
3. **Conduct a Controlled and Systematic Study:** The paper aims to perform an extensive empirical investigation across diverse model families, tasks, and verification mechanisms. This comprehensive approach is motivated by the need to identify generalizable patterns and scaling properties, rather than findings limited to specific setups. This addresses the limitation of much prior work that focused on narrow experimental conditions.
4. **Uncover Scaling Properties of Self-Improvement:** A key objective is to investigate how self-improvement capabilities scale with fundamental model properties, particularly pre-training compute (flops). Understanding these scaling laws can provide crucial guidance for future LLM training strategies.
5. **Analyze Iterative Self-Improvement Dynamics:** Beyond a single round, the research seeks to understand the behavior and limitations of iterative self-improvement processes, including factors like saturation and changes in generation diversity.
6. **Deep-Dive into Verification Mechanisms:** Given the flexibility of the verification step, a specific objective is to conduct a fine-grained study of different verification methods to understand their individual characteristics, correlations, and potential for enhancement through ensemble approaches.

In essence, the overarching motivation is to transform the understanding of LLM self-improvement from a collection of empirical observations into a principled, quantifiable science, thereby providing actionable insights for designing more effective and reliable LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors design a rigorous and modular experimental framework to systematically study LLM self-improvement, focusing on rejection sampling as the primary update mechanism while also touching upon RL-based updates.

#### 3.1. Self-Improvement Framework Dissection

The core methodology revolves around a three-component framework:
1. **Response Generation:** Given a prompt `x` from a task-specific distribution `μ_task`, a generative model `f` produces multiple candidate responses `y`. The objective is to maximize the expected utility `J(f) = E_{x~μ, y~f(·|x)}[u(x,y)]`, where `u` is the ground truth utility function (e.g., correctness).
2. **Verification with Proxy Utility:** Instead of direct access to `u`, a proxy utility function `bu_g(x,y)` is constructed by a "verifier" model `g` (often `g=f` for self-improvement). This `bu_g` provides a score or judgment on the quality of a generated response `y` for a given prompt `x`.
3. **Model Update via Reweighting:** The model `f` is updated based on the self-verified responses. This involves creating a reweighted distribution `f[w(bu_g)]` where `w` is a weight function that maps the proxy utility score to a weight. The updated model `f_t+1` is then trained to approximate this reweighted distribution.
 - **Rejection Sampling (Primary Focus):** The weight function `w(s)` is an indicator function `1[s ≥ τ]`, filtering out generations below a certain threshold `τ`. The model is then fine-tuned on this filtered, higher-quality data.
 - **KL-regularized RL Update (Explored in Iterative Study):** The proxy utility `bu` is treated as a reward in an RLHF-style update, with `w(s) = exp(s/β)`.

#### 3.2. Key Metrics

- **Generation-Verification Gap (GV-Gap):** Defined as `gap(f, g) := J(f[w(bu_g)]) - J(f)`. This quantifies the absolute performance improvement if generations were perfectly reweighted by the verifier's score. When `f=g`, it's `gap(f)`, the self-improvement GV-Gap.
- **Relative Generation-Verification Gap (Relative GV-Gap):** Defined as `gap_rel(f, g) := (E_{y~f[w(bu_g)]}[u(x,y)] - E_{y~f(·|x)}[u(x,y)]) / (U_max - E_{y~f(·|x)}[u(x,y)])`. This normalizes the gap by the remaining potential for improvement, making it suitable for comparing models at different base performance levels.

#### 3.3. Verification Mechanisms Investigated

The study systematically evaluates three distinct types of verification, with detailed prompt descriptions provided in the appendix:
- **Multiple Choice (MC):** The LLM labels responses as "Correct" or "Incorrect," using the probability of "Correct" as a continuous score.
- **Chain of Thought (CoT):** The LLM provides a score (1-10 for CoT-Score, 0/1 for CoT-Binary) and a justification (Chain of Thought), parsing the score from the output.
- **Tournament (To):** A batch of generations undergoes a single-elimination tournament via pairwise comparisons by the verifier, yielding a refined distribution.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Environment:** All experiments are conducted using `lm-evaluation-harness` for consistent evaluation.
- **Sampling Parameters:** `p=0.9`, `t=0.7` (with ablations for `t=0.5` and `t=1`), `max_length=512`, `4-shot in-context samples`.
- **Generation and Verification Count:** For each prompt, 128 responses are sampled, and 1 verification is sampled per response.
- **Model Families:** A wide range of base models are used to avoid post-training confounders: Qwen-1.5, Qwen-2, Qwen-2.5, Llama-2, Llama-3, Llama-3.1, and Yi-1.5. This allows for a robust analysis of scaling properties.
- **Tasks:**
 - **Reasoning/Problem Solving:** GSM8K (math word problems), MATH (more complex math).
 - **Factual Knowledge:** Natural Questions (trivia/QA).
 - **Procedural/Logical Reasoning:** Sudoku (4x4 puzzles).
- **Infrastructure:** vLLM for efficient inference, Nvidia A100 GPUs for inference and training.

By meticulously defining the framework, metrics, and experimental conditions, the authors ensure a controlled study that can isolate the effects of various factors on self-improvement, allowing for a deeper, more generalizable understanding of the phenomenon.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings across scaling properties, task types, iterative processes, and verification mechanisms:

#### 4.1. Scaling Properties of Generation-Verification Gap

- **Small Models Cannot Self-Improve:** Models with low pre-training flops (e.g., Qwen-1.5 0.5B, Llama-2 7B) often exhibit non-positive GV-Gap. This suggests a minimum threshold of reasoning and instruction-following capability is required for self-improvement.
- **CoT Verification is More Stable:** Chain-of-Thought (CoT) verification methods consistently yield positive gaps for medium-to-large models, unlike Multiple Choice (MC) verification, which can result in non-positive gaps even for some medium-sized models. This points to the robustness of CoT for eliciting verification capabilities.
- **Relative GV-Gap Scales Monotonically with Pre-training Flops:** A key discovery is that for effective verification methods (e.g., CoT-Score), the *relative* generation-verification gap increases monotonically, and potentially linearly with the logarithm of pre-training flops. This demonstrates a clear scaling law for self-improvement, similar to performance scaling laws. The *absolute* GV-Gap, however, does not show such a clear trend.
- **Cross-Verification Dynamics:**
 - **Verifier Capability:** When the generator is fixed, the GV-Gap increases as the verifier model's capacity (pre-training flops) increases. Stronger verifiers provide more informative signals.
 - **Generator Capability:** When the verifier is fixed, the GV-Gap decreases as the generator model's capacity increases. This is because larger models generate fewer and more subtle errors, making the improvement signal harder to find.

#### 4.2. Unimprovable Tasks

- **Factual Recall Tasks (Natural Questions):** For tasks primarily requiring factual recall, the GV-Gap remains near zero or negative across all models, despite non-trivial generation accuracy. This supports the hypothesis that self-improvement provides little benefit when verification is as hard as generation (i.e., contingent on pre-trained knowledge).
- **Sudoku Puzzles:** Despite generalized Sudoku being a canonical example where verification is computationally easier than generation (P vs. NP-hard), only the largest models (e.g., Qwen-1.5/2 72B, Llama 3.1 70B) show significant self-improvement (50-300% accuracy gain). Smaller models fail to improve, suggesting that even "easier" verification tasks require a baseline level of inherent reasoning and planning capabilities that smaller models lack or haven't learned through pre-training.

#### 4.3. Iterative Self-Improvement

- **Rapid Saturation:** The GV-Gap (and overall accuracy improvement) quickly saturates to near zero within two or three rounds of iterative self-improvement, regardless of model capacity. This indicates that self-improvement, without new external information, extracts most of its available signal quickly.
- **Model-Independent Saturation Rate:** The rate at which saturation occurs is remarkably consistent across models of different capacities.
- **Effective Diversity Degradation:** While initial rounds of self-improvement increase `pass@k` for small `k` (meaning more good answers are generated among the top few), `pass@k` for larger `k` (overall diversity) decreases. This suggests that the self-improvement process, by focusing on high-confidence correct answers, might inadvertently reduce the model's ability to explore diverse, potentially correct, solution paths, potentially leading to convergence on incorrect solutions for some questions.

#### 4.4. Fine-grained Study on Verification Mechanisms

- **Consistent Trends Across Models:** For a given verification method, the relationship between the gap and the filtering threshold (e.g., quantile for MC, score for CoT-Score) remains consistent across different models. This implies that optimal verification settings derived from smaller models might be transferable to larger, more expensive models.
- **Significant Non-Overlaps:** Different verification methods exhibit generally low correlations in their outputs and induced gaps. This suggests they are capturing distinct aspects of correctness or confidence. MC verification tends to have higher variance in gaps and saturates faster than CoT methods.
- **GV-Gap Not Correlated with Generation Accuracy:** Surprisingly, the magnitude of the GV-Gap for any method does not positively correlate with the model's baseline generation accuracy. This reinforces the relative gap as a more appropriate metric for assessing self-improvement potential.
- **Ensemble Verification Benefits:** Combining different verification methods using simple logical AND operations generally improves self-improvement performance, particularly when the individual methods already yield non-trivial gaps. This leverages the non-overlap property of different verification signals.

These findings collectively provide a much-needed empirical foundation for understanding the capabilities and limitations of LLM self-improvement, highlighting the critical role of model scale, task characteristics, and verification strategy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step towards a more systematic and principled understanding of LLM self-improvement, moving beyond mere empirical observation. Its findings carry substantial significance and potential impact across various aspects of LLM research and development:

#### 5.1. Theoretical and Fundamental Understanding

- **Formalization of GV-Gap:** The introduction and comprehensive study of the Generation-Verification Gap (GV-Gap) provide a crucial, disentangled metric for analyzing self-improvement. This shifts the focus from confounded performance gains post-fine-tuning to the intrinsic signal available for improvement.
- **Discovery of Scaling Laws:** The observation that the *relative* GV-Gap scales monotonically with pre-training flops provides a fundamental insight into LLM capabilities. This "scaling law for self-improvement" parallels established scaling laws for model performance and suggests that self-correction is an emergent property tied to model scale, offering a predictability framework for future model development.
- **Demystifying Self-Improvement Boundaries:** By identifying "unimprovable tasks" (like factual recall for base models) and the need for a minimum reasoning threshold (for Sudoku), the study delineates the inherent limits of the current self-improvement paradigm. This guides researchers to understand when self-improvement is likely to be beneficial and when alternative strategies are needed.

#### 5.2. Practical Implications for LLM Development

- **Pre-training and Post-training Guidance:** The scaling properties of the GV-Gap can inform pre-training strategies. If larger models inherently possess a greater self-correction signal, this could justify further scaling or specific architectural designs that enhance this capability. For post-training (fine-tuning), the findings suggest that the efficacy of self-improvement methods is highly dependent on the base model's capacity and the task domain.
- **Optimizing Synthetic Data Generation:** Understanding the GV-Gap allows for more informed strategies for generating and filtering synthetic data. Developers can prioritize domains where a significant gap exists and select verification methods that maximize this gap for effective distillation.
- **Efficient Verification Strategy:** The consistency of optimal verification thresholds across models and the benefits of ensemble methods suggest practical heuristics for hyperparameter tuning. Instead of costly tuning on large models, insights from smaller models can be leveraged. The non-overlap property between verification methods also encourages the development of diverse verification strategies.
- **Test-time Inference Enhancement:** The study's framework inherently connects to improving test-time performance by leveraging self-verification for reranking or refinement. A better understanding of the GV-Gap can lead to more compute-optimal strategies for inference, where the model effectively trades computation for increased accuracy without external supervision.

#### 5.3. Avenues for Future Research

The paper explicitly opens several promising directions:
- **Deeper Scaling Law Studies:** Extending the observational scaling analysis to a more rigorous, predictive scaling law could provide concrete empirical guidelines for designing LLMs.
- **Compute-Optimal Self-Improvement:** Investigating the trade-offs between model size, generation count, verification complexity, and final accuracy to identify compute-optimal configurations for self- and cross-improvement across different tasks.
- **Mitigating Diversity Degradation:** Addressing the observed decline in effective generation diversity during iterative self-improvement is crucial for sustained gains. Research into techniques that maintain diversity while improving quality could unlock further performance improvements.
- **Compositional Verification:** The success of simple ensemble methods suggests that more sophisticated algorithms for combining distinct verification signals could yield even greater benefits. This could involve learning to weigh different verifiers or building hierarchical verification systems.
- **Predicting "Self-Improvability":** Developing metrics or heuristics to predict a model's potential for self-improvement on specific tasks *before* extensive experimentation would be highly valuable.

In conclusion, "Mind the Gap" provides a robust analytical framework and a wealth of empirical insights that contribute significantly to the scientific understanding of LLM self-improvement. By pinpointing key factors, formalizing metrics, and uncovering scaling phenomena, it not only offers practical implications for current LLM practices but also lays a strong foundation for future research into unlocking the full potential of self-correcting AI systems.
