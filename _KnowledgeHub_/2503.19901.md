# TokenHSI: Unified Synthesis of Physical Human-Scene Interactions through Task Tokenization

> [!summary] Summary
> TokenHSI introduces a physics-based character control framework that unifies diverse human-scene interaction skills, including dynamic manipulation, within a single transformer network. By employing task tokenization and a shared proprioception token, it achieves higher success rates on foundational skills and demonstrates efficient, skill-preserving adaptation to novel scenarios like complex skill compositions and varied terrains.

## Problem

- Most physics-based character controllers are highly specialized for single interaction tasks, making it difficult to integrate multiple skills for complex scenarios.
- Existing unified controllers often lack robust dynamic manipulation capabilities and struggle to generalize to novel scenes or object variations.
- Adapting pre-trained policies to new tasks typically requires inefficient full fine-tuning, limiting practical applicability and often leading to catastrophic forgetting.

## Method

- **TokenHSI** utilizes a transformer-based policy with separate task-specific goal tokenizers and a shared proprioception tokenizer for the character's internal state.
- A single unified policy is trained on diverse foundational human-scene interaction skills (locomotion, contact, dynamic manipulation) using reinforcement learning with attention masking.
- Policy adaptation is enabled by largely freezing the pre-trained transformer and action head, while training only lightweight modules like new task tokenizers and zero-initialized adapter layers for novel scenarios.

## Results

- The unified multi-task policy achieved higher success rates across foundational skills compared to individually trained specialists.
- TokenHSI maintained a **99.2%** success rate on challenging composite tasks (e.g., "Climb + Carry"), outperforming baselines which saw significant performance drops.
- It successfully adapted learned skills to irregular object shapes and varied terrains, surpassing other policy adaptation methods in both performance and efficiency.
- The framework enabled fluent execution of complex long-horizon tasks, completing significantly more sub-tasks (**3.79** **0.14**) than training from scratch (**0.82** **0.06**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The introduction of a shared proprioception token significantly enhances motor knowledge sharing, leading to improved multi-task performance and robust adaptation.
> - A transformer architecture with task-specific tokenization and attention masking effectively unifies diverse human-scene interaction skills, including dynamic manipulation, within a single policy.
> - Zero-initialized adapter layers allow for efficient and stable policy adaptation to novel tasks and environments while preserving previously learned skills.
