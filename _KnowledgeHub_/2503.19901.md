# TokenHSI: Unified Synthesis of Physical Human-Scene Interactions through Task Tokenization

> [!summary] Summary
> TokenHSI introduces a physics-based character control framework that unifies diverse human-scene interaction skills, including dynamic manipulation, within a single transformer network. By employing task tokenization and a shared proprioception token, it achieves higher success rates on foundational skills and demonstrates efficient, skill-preserving adaptation to novel scenarios like complex skill compositions and varied terrains.

## Problem

- Most physics-based character controllers are highly specialized for single interaction tasks, making it difficult to integrate multiple skills for complex scenarios.
- Existing unified controllers often lack robust dynamic manipulation capabilities and struggle to generalize to novel scenes or object variations.
- Adapting pre-trained policies to new tasks typically requires inefficient full fine-tuning, limiting practical applicability and often leading to catastrophic forgetting.

## Method

- **TokenHSI** utilizes a transformer-based policy with separate task-specific goal tokenizers and a shared proprioception tokenizer for the character's internal state.
- A single unified policy is trained on diverse foundational human-scene interaction skills (locomotion, contact, dynamic manipulation) using reinforcement learning with attention masking.
- Policy adaptation is enabled by largely freezing the pre-trained transformer and action head, while training only lightweight modules like new task tokenizers and zero-initialized adapter layers for novel scenarios.

## Results

- The unified multi-task policy achieved higher success rates across foundational skills compared to individually trained specialists.
- TokenHSI maintained a **99.2%** success rate on challenging composite tasks (e.g., "Climb + Carry"), outperforming baselines which saw significant performance drops.
- It successfully adapted learned skills to irregular object shapes and varied terrains, and enabled fluent long-horizon task execution, completing significantly more sub-tasks (**3.79** **0.14**) than training from scratch (**0.82** **0.06**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The introduction of a shared proprioception token significantly enhances motor knowledge sharing, leading to improved multi-task performance and robust adaptation.
> - A transformer architecture with task-specific tokenization and attention masking effectively unifies diverse human-scene interaction skills, including dynamic manipulation, within a single policy.
> - Zero-initialized adapter layers allow for efficient and stable policy adaptation to novel tasks and environments while preserving previously learned skills.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The synthesis of diverse and physically plausible Human-Scene Interactions (HSI) is a long-standing and critical challenge in both computer animation and embodied AI. The ability of virtual characters or robots to naturally interact with their environment is fundamental for creating immersive digital experiences and developing intelligent autonomous agents.

Historically, approaches to HSI have largely fallen into two main categories:

1. **Data-driven Kinematic Generation:** These methods leverage large-scale motion datasets and generative models (e.g., diffusion models, VAEs) to synthesize high-quality, realistic human motions and interactions. While visually appealing, they often overlook physical plausibility, leading to motions that may violate real-world physics or lack robust interaction capabilities. Examples include generating grasping poses or text-to-motion synthesis.
2. **Physics-based Character Control:** These methods prioritize physical realism by using physics engines to simulate character dynamics. Early approaches involved hand-crafted controllers or trajectory optimization, which were often offline, computationally expensive, and difficult to generalize to new scenarios or complex tasks. The advent of Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) has revolutionized this area, enabling the learning of complex motor skills for physically simulated agents, from locomotion to highly specific manipulation tasks (e.g., basketball dribbling, solving a Rubik's Cube).

**Limitations of Prior Physics-Based DRL Approaches:**

Despite the advancements brought by DRL, existing methods face significant limitations that TokenHSI aims to address:

- **Task Specialization:** Most DRL-based controllers are highly specialized, trained for a single interaction task (e.g., sitting, climbing, carrying) or a very limited set of skills. This creates a fragmented ecosystem where integrating multiple skills for complex, real-world tasks (like "sitting down while carrying an object") becomes exceedingly difficult or requires cumbersome task-specific engineering.
- **Limited Generalization and Adaptability:** Even "unified" controllers that claim to handle diverse skills often perform poorly in novel scenes or when faced with variations in object shapes or terrain. Adapting these pre-trained policies to new scenarios typically requires inefficient full fine-tuning of all parameters, which is costly and skill-damaging. Furthermore, many prior unified controllers are primarily designed for static scenes and lack robust manipulation skills, limiting their applicability to dynamic interactions.
- **Lack of Unified Skill Integration:** Previous attempts at unification (e.g., UniHSI, MaskedMimic) might use shared architectures or masking mechanisms, but they often struggle to incorporate a wide variety of skill *types* (e.g., following, contact-based interactions, and dynamic manipulation) into a single coherent policy while maintaining efficient adaptability.

**TokenHSI's Position:**

TokenHSI enters this landscape as a novel framework explicitly designed to overcome these limitations. It positions itself as the *first physics-based character control framework* capable of:

- **Unifying diverse HSI skills** (including dynamic manipulation like carrying, alongside locomotion and contact-based tasks like sitting and climbing) within a *single transformer network*.
- Maintaining **flexible and efficient adaptability** to novel scenarios and challenging new tasks (e.g., skill composition, object/terrain variations, long-horizon tasks) without requiring full fine-tuning.

By introducing the concept of "task tokenization" and a shared proprioception token within a transformer architecture, TokenHSI aims to achieve better knowledge sharing across skills and more robust generalization, thereby narrowing the gap between highly specialized simulated characters and the general-purpose capabilities of real-world humans.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching motivation behind TokenHSI stems from the desire to create more versatile and adaptable simulated human characters, mimicking the proficiency of real-world humans as general-purpose agents. Humans can seamlessly perform a wide variety of complex interaction tasks and adapt effortlessly to novel contexts, a capability largely absent in current physics-based character controllers.

The paper identifies two critical gaps in existing research that TokenHSI aims to bridge:

1. **Lack of a Unified Controller for Diverse Skills:** Current methods typically develop separate controllers for each specific interaction task. This fragmentation makes it challenging to tackle complex HSI tasks that inherently require the integration and coordination of multiple skills (e.g., "sitting down while carrying an object"). Furthermore, existing unified controllers often have an incomplete skill set, focusing on static interactions and lacking dynamic manipulation capabilities.
2. **Limited Generalization and Inefficient Adaptation to Novel Scenarios:** Prior approaches often perform well only in in-domain settings and struggle to generalize to new environments or object geometries. The process of adapting learned skills to novel tasks is often inefficient, requiring costly fine-tuning of the entire policy, which limits practical applicability.

Based on these motivations, TokenHSI sets forth the following key objectives:

- **Unified Skill Learning:** To establish a single, comprehensive controller (a unified policy) capable of learning and integrating a wide array of diverse human-scene interaction skills—including locomotion, contact-based interactions (sitting, climbing), and dynamic manipulation (carrying)—within a single network architecture.
- **Flexible and Efficient Policy Adaptation:** To develop an effective methodology that allows the pre-trained unified policy to quickly and robustly adapt its learned skills to novel tasks and environments without the need for extensive fine-tuning of the entire model. This includes adapting to new object shapes, varied terrain, and performing complex, long-horizon task compositions.
- **Enhanced Knowledge Sharing:** To design a policy architecture that facilitates effective motor knowledge sharing across different tasks, thereby boosting multi-task training performance and improving generalization capabilities. This is primarily addressed through the innovative use of a shared proprioception token and a masking mechanism within a transformer network.
- **Scalability and Extensibility:** To create a framework that can not only handle the unification of known diverse HSI skills but also allows for the flexible introduction of new skills or modifications to existing ones by training only lightweight additional modules.

By achieving these objectives, TokenHSI aims to significantly improve the versatility, adaptability, and extensibility of physically plausible human-scene interaction synthesis, bringing simulated characters closer to the general-purpose nature of humans in real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TokenHSI proposes a novel transformer-based policy, building upon the transformer's ability to handle variable-length inputs, to achieve both multi-skill unification and flexible policy adaptation. The methodology is structured into two primary stages: Foundational Skill Learning and Policy Adaptation.

#### 3.1. Foundational Skill Learning

The first stage focuses on training a single, unified transformer policy to simultaneously master multiple fundamental HSI skills in a multi-task environment.

- **Policy Architecture:**
 - **Tokenizers:** The core innovation lies in tokenizing observations. Instead of a single character-goal state space, TokenHSI uses separate task-specific tokenizers ($T_{task}$) for goal states and a dedicated, shared **proprioception tokenizer ($T_{prop}$)** for the character's internal state ($s_t$). Each tokenizer converts its input into a 64-dimensional feature vector.
 - **Transformer Encoder ($\phi$):** A 4-layer transformer encoder fuses these tokenized observations using a self-attention mechanism. Crucially, the proprioception token and a learnable embedding (output token $e$) *always* participate in the attention computations, fostering knowledge sharing across tasks.
 - **Action Head (H):** An MLP network that receives the processed tokens and outputs the 32-dimensional action ($a_t$) (joint target rotations for PD controllers).
- **Foundational Skills:** Four diverse HSI tasks are chosen for simultaneous learning on flat ground:
 - **Following:** Tracking a 2D target path.
 - **Sitting:** Approaching and sitting on a target object.
 - **Climbing:** Approaching and climbing onto a target object.
 - **Carrying:** Moving a box from an initial to a target location. This is a dynamic manipulation skill, often overlooked in prior unified controllers.
- **Multi-task Policy Training:**
 - **Environment Setup:** Training occurs in a multi-task environment where tasks are assigned based on a predefined probability distribution (e.g., 10% for following, 30% for others).
 - **Reward Function:** The policy is trained using goal-conditioned reinforcement learning (Proximal Policy Optimization - PPO) to maximize a reward function comprising a style reward (for natural motion) and a task reward (for task completion).
 - **Task Tokenization and Masking:** During training for a specific task, a one-hot label ($l_t$) acts as an attention mask within the transformer encoder, effectively suppressing features from non-target tasks. The shared proprioception token, however, always remains active. This design encourages motor knowledge sharing across tasks and boosts multi-task performance.
 - **Conditional Discriminator:** A motion discriminator ($D$), conditioned on the one-hot task label, is employed to ensure the policy learns task-relevant motor skills and avoids unnatural motions (e.g., squatting during path-following).

#### 3.2. Policy Adaptation

The second stage demonstrates TokenHSI's ability to flexibly and efficiently adapt the pre-trained unified policy to novel and more challenging HSI tasks.

- **Core Adaptation Strategy:**
 - **Freezing Pre-trained Components:** Key components trained in the foundational stage—the shared proprioception tokenizer ($T_{prop}$), the learnable embedding ($e$), the transformer encoder ($\phi$), and the action head (H)—are largely frozen. This preserves learned foundational skills and ensures efficiency.
 - **Lightweight Trainable Modules:** Adaptation is achieved by introducing and training only a few lightweight modules:
 - **New Task Tokenizers ($T_{new}$):** For new observation types or new tasks.
 - **Zero-initialized Adapter Layers ($\xi_A$):** Small, trainable bypass layers are integrated into the action head (H). These layers initially have zero weights, meaning they don't affect the pre-trained policy's output until trained, ensuring graceful adaptation and preventing catastrophic forgetting.
 - **New Discriminator:** A new motion discriminator (D) is trained from scratch for the specific adaptation task.
- **Challenging Adaptation Scenarios:**
 - **Skill Composition:** Combines multiple foundational skills (e.g., sitting while carrying a box). This involves reusing and freezing the relevant foundational task tokenizers ($T_s, T_c$) and introducing an additional trainable $T_{new}$ to perceive the combined task state.
 - **Object Shape Variation:** Adapts the carrying skill to irregular objects (e.g., chairs, tables). Since the observation information is similar to a box, the pre-trained carrying task tokenizer ($T_c$) is directly fine-tuned.
 - **Terrain Shape Variation:** Generalizes locomotion and carrying skills from flat ground to uneven terrain (e.g., stairs). This involves fine-tuning existing task tokenizers ($T_f$ or $T_c$) and introducing a new trainable $T_{new}$ to encode a height map (environmental observation).
 - **Long-horizon Task Completion:** Enables sequential execution of multiple skills in complex, cluttered environments. This is achieved by making all skills environment-aware via a height tokenizer and iteratively fine-tuning each task tokenizer in the complex scene. A Finite State Machine (FSM) is used during inference for automated task switching.

This two-stage methodology, with its emphasis on a shared proprioception token and efficient, lightweight adaptation mechanisms for a transformer policy, allows TokenHSI to learn a broad range of HSI skills and generalize effectively to novel and complex scenarios.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

TokenHSI demonstrates significant improvements in versatility, adaptability, and extensibility across a wide array of HSI tasks, both foundational and complex.

#### 4.1. Evaluation on Foundational Skill Learning

- **Superior Performance:** TokenHSI's unified multi-task policy achieves higher success rates and maintains comparable error rates across all four foundational skills (Following, Sitting, Climbing, Carrying) when compared to individually trained "specialist" policies. This highlights its capability to generalize effectively across different skill categories.
- **Importance of Shared Proprioception Token ($T_{prop}$):** An ablation study ("Ours (w/o $T_{prop}$)") reveals a general decrease in success rate across all tasks when the shared proprioception tokenizer is discarded and proprioception is integrated into each task tokenizer. This empirically validates the core design choice of a separate, shared $T_{prop}$ for promoting effective motor knowledge sharing and boosting multi-task performance.

#### 4.2. Evaluation on Policy Adaptation

TokenHSI's policy adaptation strategy proves highly efficient and robust, outperforming existing policy adaptation methods.

- **Skill Composition:**
 - **Robustness:** On challenging composite tasks like "Climb + Carry," where baselines (Scratch, CML, CML dual) show drastic performance degradation (e.g., Scratch dropping from 80%+ to 26.8%), TokenHSI maintains a high success rate of 99.2%. For easier tasks, it achieves near-optimal performance.
 - **Efficiency and Stability:** Learning curves demonstrate TokenHSI's superior efficiency and stability, converging faster and with less variance compared to baselines, especially on difficult tasks. This is attributed to the better generalization of its multi-task trained $T_{prop}$ and the attention mechanism enabling skill composition at a latent feature space, rather than just the action space (as in CML).
 - **Impact of Shared $T_{prop}$ (Adaptation Context):** Ablation shows a significant performance drop in difficult composite tasks if the base model was trained without $T_{prop}$, further reinforcing its necessity for robust adaptation.
- **Object Shape Variation:**
 - **Generalization to Irregular Objects:** TokenHSI successfully adapts the box-carrying skill to irregular objects like chairs and tables, surpassing both full fine-tuning ("Finetune") and the state-of-the-art policy adaptation method ("AdaptNet").
 - **Skill-Preserving Efficiency:** While full fine-tuning can sometimes be faster, TokenHSI offers efficient and *skill-preserving* adaptation, which is crucial for retaining previously learned behaviors. Its performance significantly exceeds AdaptNet under this constraint.
- **Terrain Shape Variation:**
 - **Adaptation to Complex Environments:** TokenHSI effectively generalizes locomotion (Follow) and carrying skills from flat ground to uneven terrain (stairs, obstacles). It outperforms "Scratch" and "AdaptNet" in success rate and efficiency for incorporating new environmental observations (height maps).
 - **Role of Adapters:** An ablation study shows that the zero-initialized adapter layers ($\xi_A$) in the action head are critical for improving performance during adaptation, validating their importance in the architectural design.
- **Long-horizon Task Completion:**
 - **Seamless Skill Transitions:** TokenHSI successfully tackles a complex, multi-stage task (following → carrying → climbing → sitting) in a cluttered 3D environment. It achieves fluent skill execution and transitions, demonstrating its ability to handle unseen transition states and obstacle avoidance, unlike sequentially executed unadapted skills.
 - **Quantitative Advantage:** Achieves a significantly higher completed sub-task count (3.79 ± 0.14) compared to training from scratch (0.82 ± 0.06) or iterative fine-tuning of multiple specialist policies (1.86 ± 0.02), while also showing superior learning efficiency.

#### 4.3. Extensibility (Supplementary Material)

- **Out-of-Domain Skill Insertion:** TokenHSI successfully integrates new, out-of-domain manipulation skills like "pushing down a large object" (100% success rate) and "two-hand box lifting" (80.6% success rate) into the pre-trained transformer policy, simply by adding new task tokenizers and adapter layers.
- **Further Skill Composition:** It further demonstrates the ability to combine these newly inserted skills with previously learned foundational skills (e.g., "lifting + sitting," "lifting + following"), showcasing remarkable extensibility.

In summary, TokenHSI consistently outperforms baselines in terms of both performance (success rate) and learning efficiency across various challenging HSI tasks. Its architectural design, particularly the shared proprioception tokenizer and the flexible transformer with lightweight adapters, enables robust multi-skill unification and highly efficient, skill-preserving adaptation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TokenHSI represents a significant advancement in the field of physically plausible human-scene interaction synthesis, offering a unified, adaptable, and extensible framework that addresses critical limitations of prior work. Its significance and potential impact span across multiple domains:

- **Revolutionizing Computer Animation:**
 - **Increased Realism and Diversity:** By enabling physics-based characters to perform a wide array of diverse and complex interactions in a physically plausible manner, TokenHSI can lead to more realistic, dynamic, and engaging virtual worlds for video games, film, and virtual reality experiences.
 - **Reduced Manual Effort:** The ability to learn and adapt multiple skills within a single controller significantly reduces the need for tedious manual animation or the development of separate, specialized controllers for each new interaction, streamlining animation pipelines.
 - **General-Purpose Characters:** It moves towards the vision of general-purpose virtual characters that can react dynamically and intelligently to their environment, offering unprecedented levels of interactive control.

- **Advancing Embodied AI and Robotics:**
 - **More Capable Agents:** TokenHSI lays foundational groundwork for developing more versatile and adaptable embodied AI agents and humanoid robots. The ability to seamlessly integrate various interaction skills, including dynamic manipulation, and adapt to novel geometries or terrains, is crucial for robots operating in complex, unstructured real-world environments.
 - **Efficient Skill Transfer:** The efficient policy adaptation mechanism, requiring only lightweight fine-tuning, is particularly valuable for robotics, where data collection and training are often expensive and time-consuming. It offers a promising path for transferring learned skills to new scenarios with minimal retraining.
 - **Benchmarking and Research Tool:** The framework provides a powerful tool for future research into generalizable motor control, complex task planning, and human-robot interaction in simulated environments.

- **Fundamental Contributions to Reinforcement Learning and Neural Architectures:**
 - **Unified Architectures:** The paper demonstrates the power of transformer networks and intelligent tokenization (especially the shared proprioception token with masking) for multi-task learning and robust generalization in complex control problems. This architectural insight could inspire similar designs in other RL domains.
 - **Effective Policy Adaptation:** The zero-initialized adapter layers offer a simple yet highly effective strategy for skill-preserving policy adaptation, an area of growing importance in continuous control and real-world deployment.

**Acknowledged Limitations and Future Directions:**

The authors candidly discuss limitations, which also hint at important future research avenues:

- **Reward Function Engineering:** The current approach still relies on hand-engineered reward functions, a common challenge in goal-oriented RL that involves tedious trial-and-error. Future work could explore methods to reduce this cost, such as inverse reinforcement learning from human demonstrations or leveraging knowledge from large language models and internet data.
- **Autonomous Long-Horizon Task Completion:** While TokenHSI excels at adapting skills for long-horizon tasks, the overall task completion is currently non-autonomous, requiring a Finite State Machine for task switching. Achieving truly autonomous, complex, long-term task completion in realistic environments without human guidance remains an open and ambitious problem.

In conclusion, TokenHSI marks a significant step towards creating unified, adaptable, and highly capable physically simulated characters. Its novel architectural design and robust empirical validation position it as a foundational work that will likely influence future developments in character animation, embodied AI, and generalizable motor control.
