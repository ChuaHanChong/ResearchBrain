# Reasoning as Representation: Rethinking Visual Reinforcement Learning in Image Quality Assessment

> [!summary] Summary
> This research analyzes how reinforcement learning (RL) enhances the generalization of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) in Image Quality Assessment (IQA) by compressing visual data into generalized text representations. The work introduces Reasoning-Aligned Cross-domain Training (RACT) for robust multi-dataset learning and Reasoning-Aligned Lightweight IQA (RALI), which achieves comparable performance to state-of-the-art reasoning MLLMs while reducing model parameters by over 95% and inference time/memory by over 95%.

## Problem

- Despite strong generalization in RL-based MLLMs for IQA, the underlying mechanisms for their cross-domain performance were not systematically understood.
- Current reasoning-based IQA models suffer from high inference energy consumption and latency due to stepwise reasoning and large LLM parameters, limiting real-time deployment.
- Traditional IQA datasets are small and diverse, making generalization challenging as high-dimensional visual representations often lead to overfitting.

## Method

- Systematically analyzed RL-based MLLMs (e.g., ==Q-Insight==) to understand how RL training facilitates the compression of visual information into concise, quality-correlated text representations.
- Proposed Reasoning-Aligned Cross-domain Training (RACT) to leverage quality reasoning text for label alignment and cross-domain supervised fine-tuning (SFT), enabling robust multi-dataset learning.
- Introduced Reasoning-Aligned Lightweight IQA (RALI), an efficient framework that uses contrastive learning to align images with quality reasoning text, followed by feature compression and a specialized scoring mechanism, eliminating the need for explicit reasoning or large LLMs during inference.

## Results

- RACT achieved the highest average PLCC/SRCC on out-of-domain IQA datasets when co-trained with multiple IQA datasets, outperforming other MLLM-based IQA methods.
- RALI delivered generalization performance comparable to state-of-the-art reasoning MLLMs like Q-Insight, while using less than **5%** of their parameters and over **95%** less inference time and memory.
- Ablation studies confirmed that each component of RALI (contrastive alignment, PCA reduction, bucketed k-means, seed augmentation, scoring definition) contributed positively to its overall IQA performance and efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RL training in MLLMs shifts dependency for IQA scoring from visual tokens to concise reasoning text, which acts as a compact (10x compression) and generalizable representation.
> - Textual representations effectively mitigate domain discrepancies observed in visual feature spaces across different IQA datasets, enhancing out-of-domain generalization.
> - The image-to-text reasoning process learned through RL is inherently highly consistent and generalizable across datasets, reducing overfitting risks even when reasoning capabilities are disabled during evaluation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Image Quality Assessment (IQA) is a fundamental task in computer vision, serving critical applications in natural scenarios such as photography selection and video platform monitoring, as well as in the training of generative algorithms. In the context of generative models, IQA functions as a core reward signal within the Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) framework, influencing the convergence efficiency and effectiveness of reinforcement learning strategies.

Historically, IQA methods have evolved from classical approaches, often categorized as full-reference (requiring a pristine reference image) or no-reference (assessing quality without a reference). No-reference methods have progressed from hand-crafted natural scene statistics to deep learning models that learn quality priors from data. More recently, the advent of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) has introduced new paradigms to IQA. Initial MLLM-based approaches, such as Q-Align and DeQA, leveraged supervised fine-tuning (SFT) to enable MLLMs to output direct quality scores or descriptive text.

A significant recent development has been the integration of visual reinforcement learning (RL) into IQA, exemplified by models like Q-Insight and VisualQuality-R1. These RL-based MLLMs demonstrate enhanced generalization capabilities in image quality prediction compared to their SFT-based predecessors, often by jointly producing quality reasoning text and scores. Despite their superior performance, these models present two main challenges: a lack of systematic analysis regarding the underlying mechanisms driving their generalization, and high inference energy consumption and latency due to their stepwise reasoning processes and large model parameters. The high computational cost restricts their deployment in real-time, online RL, or mobile environments.

This paper addresses these gaps by analyzing the generalization mechanisms of RL-based IQA models and proposing a more efficient alternative. It aims to clarify how reasoning contributes to generalization in IQA and whether the explicit reasoning process is essential for achieving such generalization. Existing CLIP-based IQA methods (e.g., CLIP-IQA) have attempted to leverage contrastive learning for IQA but faced limitations due to shallow alignment with general text rather than quality-specific descriptions, and overly simplistic text corpora. This work aims to overcome these limitations by aligning images with generalizable, quality-specific text representations learned through RL, but without requiring the explicit reasoning process during inference.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The research presented in this paper is primarily driven by two critical questions stemming from the recent advancements in RL-based MLLMs for IQA:
1. **Understanding the Source of Generalization:** The paper aims to systematically analyze and explain the principle behind the exceptional generalization capability observed in reasoning-based IQA models trained through reinforcement learning (e.g., Q-Insight). While these models perform well, the specific mechanisms and critical factors contributing to their cross-domain performance remain underexplored. This understanding is crucial because IQA datasets are typically small-scale and exhibit significant domain gaps, making generalization a persistent challenge. Traditional approaches using high-dimensional visual representations often lead to overfitting on specific datasets.
2. **Addressing Inference Efficiency:** A significant practical limitation of current reasoning-based IQA models is their high inference energy usage and latency. The stepwise reasoning process and the necessity of loading large language models (LLMs) during inference result in computational overheads that are orders of magnitude greater than earlier IQA counterparts. This restricts their deployment in scenarios requiring low latency, such as online reinforcement learning, mobile applications, and real-time processing. The paper seeks to determine if comparable generalization performance can be achieved without incurring these substantial computational costs, specifically questioning whether the explicit reasoning process itself is essential.

To address these objectives, the authors investigate whether the underlying image-to-text mapping achieved by RL-based MLLMs can be decoupled from the explicit reasoning process and implemented more efficiently. The motivation is to retain the benefits of strong generalization while significantly reducing the computational footprint, thereby enabling broader applicability of high-performance IQA solutions. This involves proposing new algorithms that directly leverage the generalized text representations without the need for multi-step reasoning or the deployment of an LLM during inference.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this paper involves a two-pronged approach: first, a detailed analysis of the reasoning mechanisms in RL-based MLLMs for IQA, followed by the proposition of two novel frameworks, RACT and RALI, to leverage these insights for improved performance and efficiency.

#### 3.1. Revisiting Reasoning-Based MLLMs in IQA

The paper uses Q-Insight (Li et al., 2025) as a case study to analyze how RL training impacts MLLMs for IQA.
- **Reasoning Mechanisms Analysis:**
 - **Conciseness and Correlation:** A comparison between Q-Insight (RL-fine-tuned) and Qwen VL (general-purpose MLLM) revealed that Q-Insight generates more concise and quality-correlated reasoning descriptions. T-SNE visualizations of CLIP features derived from reasoning outputs confirmed that Q-Insight's text representations showed stronger correlation with subjective quality scores.
 - **Attention Analysis:** An attention heatmap study during score token generation in Q-Insight indicated that 95% of attention weights were directed towards the previously generated reasoning text tokens, rather than visual tokens. This suggests a dependency shift from visual to concise textual representations for scoring.
- **Key to Generalization: Visual to Text Compression:**
 - **Compactness and Domain Bridging:** The paper argues that text is a more compact and generalizable representation for IQA. Visual representations can require over 1,000 tokens for an image, while text representations achieve similar descriptive capability with fewer than 100 tokens, a compression ratio of over 10 times.
 - **Domain Discrepancy Mitigation:** T-SNE visualizations of visual tokens and reasoning text tokens from different datasets (KonIQ and SPAQ) demonstrated that while visual features exhibited notable disparities between datasets, this domain gap was alleviated in the textual representational space. This suggests that textual representations can mitigate domain differences, enhancing generalizability.
 - **Generalizable Image-to-Text Compression:** An experiment was conducted by training the LLM components of Qwen VL with RL on different IQA datasets (KonIQ and KADID), then pairing them with a fixed visual encoder. The results showed minimal differences in performance on out-of-domain datasets, indicating that the reasoning process (image-to-text conversion) itself is highly consistent and generalizable across datasets, reducing overfitting risks.

#### 3.2. Reasoning-Aligned Cross-domain Training (RACT)

To further validate the efficacy of quality reasoning text as a generalizable IQA representation and address dataset distribution challenges, the RACT framework is proposed:
- **Single-domain RL Training:** Independent reinforcement learning training is performed on each IQA dataset to optimize an MLLM to produce both reasoning and scores.
- **Label Alignment:** The reasoning module of the RL-trained model is used to generate image quality reasoning text for each image, forming unified, cross-dataset image-text pairs.
- **Cross-domain SFT:** A supervised fine-tuning (SFT) step is then applied to the single-domain RL-trained model using these aligned image-text pairs. The primary goal is to adapt the visual encoder to varying image qualities and scenes, enabling effective conversion into visual tokens. Critically, only score information from a single dataset is introduced during this stage, as multi-domain scores were found to impair convergence due to annotator biases.

#### 3.3. Reasoning-Aligned Lightweight IQA (RALI)

To investigate whether explicit reasoning is essential and to address efficiency concerns, the RALI framework is introduced, designed to achieve comparable performance to RL-based MLLMs without multi-step reasoning or LLM inference:
- **Contrastive Alignment:**
 - An RL-based IQA model (Q-Insight) is used to generate image-text-score triplets {I, T, s} from its training set, with diverse reasoning texts obtained using multiple seeds for the same input image.
 - A pre-trained CLIP vision encoder is then fine-tuned using an image-text contrastive learning loss to align images with this quality reasoning text space. The text encoder remains frozen during this process.
- **Feature Compression:**
 - The fine-tuned vision encoder converts training images into high-dimensional embeddings (768-D).
 - Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is applied to reduce the dimensionality of these embeddings (e.g., from 768-D to 512-D), filtering out quality-irrelevant information.
 - **Bucketed K-means:** The score range is partitioned into *N* buckets. Within each bucket, *k*-means clustering is performed to obtain cluster centroids (basis vectors, $\mu_{nj}$) and their corresponding representative scores ($f_{nj}$), ensuring a dispersed score distribution and compact feature space (e.g., K=250 basis vectors).
- **Scoring Definition:**
 - For an input image, its embedding is computed using the aligned encoder and projected via PCA.
 - Cosine similarities are calculated between this projected embedding and the *K* basis vectors.
 - These similarities are normalized using a softmax function to obtain weights.
 - The final quality score is a weighted sum of the *K* basic scores ($f_i$).
 - The basis vectors and scores are further fine-tuned using image-score pairs to better align with human preferences. During inference, only these fixed, pre-computed basis vectors and scores need to be stored, drastically reducing computational overhead.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding the mechanisms of reasoning-based IQA models and the effectiveness of the proposed frameworks.

#### 4.1. Understanding Generalization in Reasoning-Based MLLMs

- **Dependency Shift:** Through reinforcement learning, reasoning-based MLLMs (e.g., Q-Insight) shift their primary dependency for image quality scoring from redundant visual tokens to concise, quality-relevant reasoning text tokens. The attention mechanism during score prediction primarily focuses on the generated text.
- **Visual-to-Text Compression:** RL training enables MLLMs to acquire a highly generalizable compression strategy, mapping high-dimensional visual information into compact text representations. This textual representation is significantly more compact (over 10x compression) and effectively mitigates domain discrepancies observed in visual feature spaces across different datasets.
- **Generalizable Reasoning Process:** The image-to-text reasoning process itself is found to be highly generalizable, meaning the reasoning logic learned across different datasets remains consistent. This contributes to the models' improved out-of-domain performance and reduces overfitting. Ablation studies confirmed that disabling the reasoning capability in GRPO-trained models leads to a significant performance drop, particularly on out-of-domain datasets.

#### 4.2. Performance of Reasoning-Aligned Cross-domain Training (RACT)

- **Improved Cross-Domain Generalization:** RACT, which leverages quality reasoning text for label alignment and cross-domain SFT, demonstrated superior out-of-domain performance in multi-dataset co-training scenarios. When co-trained on KonIQ, SPAQ, KADID, and PIPAL, RACT achieved the highest average PLCC/SRCC on out-of-domain datasets (LiveW, AGIQA, CSIQ, TID13) compared to other MLLM-based IQA methods, including Q-Align, DeQA, VisualQuality-R1, and Q-Insight (with naive mixed training).
- **Addressing Convergence Issues:** RACT successfully mitigates convergence problems faced by naive multi-dataset RL training (e.g., Q-Insight showed performance degradation with mixed training).
- **Impact of Scores and Training Modules:** Ablation studies showed that incorporating scores from multiple datasets into cross-domain SFT only benefits in-domain performance but does not improve out-of-domain generalization. This is attributed to annotator biases in numerical scores across datasets, whereas text retains objective quality. Furthermore, fine-tuning only the Visual Encoder during cross-domain SFT yielded comparable performance to joint training with the LLM, supporting the conclusion that cross-domain reasoning is well-aligned and the visual encoder primarily adapts to diverse image inputs.

#### 4.3. Performance and Efficiency of Reasoning-Aligned Lightweight IQA (RALI)

- **Comparable Generalization with High Efficiency:** RALI achieved generalization performance comparable to state-of-the-art reasoning-based MLLMs like Q-Insight in single-dataset training (on KonIQ). For example, RALI demonstrated competitive average PLCC/SRCC scores across various test datasets.
- **Significant Resource Reduction:** RALI drastically reduced computational resources. It utilized less than 5% of the model parameters of Q-Insight (0.3B vs. 7B) and required over 95% less inference time and memory. This makes it suitable for resource-constrained environments.
- **Outperformance of Non-MLLM Baselines:** RALI surpassed other non-MLLM deep-learning IQA methods, including CLIP-IQA+, by substantial margins in both PLCC and SRCC.
- **Ablation Studies Validate Components:** Ablation studies on RALI confirmed the importance of each proposed component: contrastive alignment with quality reasoning text, PCA reduction for noise filtering, bucketed k-means for a dispersed score distribution, seed augmentation for diverse text, and the final scoring definition mechanism. Each component contributed positively to the overall IQA performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of Image Quality Assessment (IQA) and broader vision-language understanding in several significant ways:

**Fundamental Understanding of Generalization:** The paper provides a systematic analysis that elucidates the core mechanism behind the strong generalization capabilities of reasoning-based MLLMs in IQA. By demonstrating that reinforcement learning enables these models to effectively compress redundant visual information into compact, cross-domain aligned textual representations, the work offers a critical insight into how complex visual tasks can be effectively generalized through an abstract, linguistic medium. This understanding is not confined to IQA but offers principles applicable to other multimodal reasoning tasks.

**Advancement in Cross-Domain IQA Training (RACT):** The proposed Reasoning-Aligned Cross-domain Training (RACT) framework offers a practical solution to a long-standing challenge in IQA: managing diverse data distributions across different datasets. By leveraging the generalizability of quality reasoning text for label alignment, RACT enables effective co-training on multiple IQA datasets, achieving state-of-the-art out-of-distribution performance. This can significantly improve the robustness and applicability of IQA models trained on varied real-world data.

**Efficient and High-Performance IQA (RALI):** The Reasoning-Aligned Lightweight IQA (RALI) framework represents a substantial practical breakthrough. It demonstrates that the high generalization performance of reasoning-based MLLMs can be achieved without the explicit, computationally expensive reasoning process or the need to deploy large language models during inference. RALI's ability to match the performance of reasoning MLLMs while requiring less than 5% of their parameters and over 95% reduction in inference time and memory overhead makes it highly impactful. This efficiency facilitates the deployment of advanced IQA capabilities in resource-constrained environments, such as mobile devices, edge computing, and real-time online systems, where large MLLMs are impractical.

**Broader Implications for Vision-Language Models:** The findings extend beyond IQA, providing actionable insights for the design and optimization of future vision-language models. The investigation into attention mechanisms, the exploration of compact textual representation spaces, and the strategies for mitigating cross-domain bias offer valuable guidance for enhancing generalization and efficiency in various multimodal tasks. Moreover, the RALI framework provides a general paradigm for converting complex reasoning-based evaluators into highly efficient, reasoning-free counterparts. This could streamline the integration of sophisticated reward models into post-training pipelines for generative models (e.g., video diffusion models), accelerating their development and deployment by reducing the computational burden of evaluation.

In summary, this research not only deepens the theoretical understanding of generalization in advanced IQA models but also delivers practical, efficient solutions that can accelerate the adoption of high-performance IQA in real-world applications and inform future research directions in multimodal AI.
