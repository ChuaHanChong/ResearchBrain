# Real-Time Whole-Body Teleoperation of a Humanoid Robot Using IMU-Based Motion Capture with Sim2Sim and Sim2Real Validation

> [!summary] Summary
> A real-time whole-body humanoid teleoperation system maps a Virdyn IMU motion-capture suit onto a Unitree G1 through a learning-free pipeline: functional joint mapping with geometric projection, soft joint-limit clipping, per-joint exponential-moving-average smoothing, and single-cycle upper/lower-body synchronization. The identical code passes MuJoCo sim2sim verification and then runs unmodified on hardware, reproducing walking, sitting, turning, bowing, and compound gestures with no perceptible latency.

## Problem

- Learning-based whole-body controllers need large demonstration datasets, reward engineering, and domain randomization before deployment, making them slow to port to a new humanoid platform.
- Prior IMU-suit work mostly served offline dataset collection and retargeting, rarely driving continuous real-time humanoid teleoperation deployed directly on physical hardware.
- Human and robot morphologies differ in link lengths and joint limits, while IMU pose noise and control latency destabilize naive one-to-one angle mapping.

## Method

- ==Kinematic mapping== module pairs human skeleton joints with closest functional Unitree G1 counterparts, using ==geometric projection== where structures mismatch (human hip complex versus the robot's three-DoF hip), plus ==soft joint-limit clipping== inside hardware stops.
- Per-joint ==exponential moving average== filter attenuates high-frequency IMU noise at control rate, its time constant traded off against responsiveness, chosen over heavier filters for low compute cost.
- ==Synchronization== stage fuses upper-body, torso, and lower-body commands within one retargeting step, with no buffering, so each sensor frame emits a robot command inside the same control cycle.

## Results

- MuJoCo sim2sim verification on the official Unitree G1 model reported **no** joint-limit violations, **no** self-collisions, and **no** abrupt joint-velocity discontinuities across all tested behaviors.
- The identical pipeline deployed on the physical G1 with **zero** modification, no domain adaptation, no parameter retuning, no retraining, covering **five** motion categories from walking to compound gesture-plus-locomotion.
- Balance held using only the G1's onboard low-level joint servos, without any added stabilization controller; latency evidence is qualitative only, reported as **no** perceptible operator-to-robot delay.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Kinematic retargeting is physics-agnostic, so the sim-to-real gap largely disappears; the transfer problem is dynamics-side, not geometry-side, when no learned policy is involved.
> - Running one code path in simulator and hardware turns sim2sim into a genuine pre-deployment safety check for joint limits, collisions, and velocity discontinuities.
> - Caution: smoothing buys stability with phase lag, and quasi-static demos plus qualitative latency claims say little about dynamic balance limits.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

The development of humanoid robots capable of operating effectively in unstructured human environments presents significant challenges, particularly in generating responsive, stable, and naturalistic full-body movements. Current approaches to this problem typically fall into three categories: pre-scripted motion playback, offline trajectory optimization, and learning-based whole-body controllers. While these methods have demonstrated capabilities in controlled settings, they often necessitate substantial tuning, extensive demonstration datasets, or lengthy training periods prior to deployment.

An alternative approach involves leveraging captured human motion, which inherently exhibits stability, expressiveness, and rich data. Retargeting this motion onto a humanoid robot in real time offers a direct and intuitive teleoperation paradigm, potentially reducing the overhead associated with model training. However, real-time humanoid teleoperation, particularly when utilizing wearable Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) suits, is subject to several complicating factors. These include:
1. **Kinematic discrepancies:** Differences in link lengths, joint structures, and degrees of freedom between human operators and robots.
2. **IMU noise:** High-frequency noise inherent in IMU-based pose estimation, which can lead to unstable or jerky robot movements.
3. **Joint limit compliance:** The necessity of ensuring that retargeted motions remain within the robot's physical joint limits to prevent damage or instability.
4. **Sim-to-real transfer gaps:** Challenges in porting control strategies developed in simulation environments directly to physical hardware without degradation in performance or stability.

The field of whole-body control for humanoids has seen advancements with hierarchical controllers that prioritize tasks while respecting joint limits, as well as recent neural whole-body controllers based on reinforcement learning. The latter have shown robustness on physical platforms but demand extensive simulation training and meticulous reward engineering.

Motion retargeting has been explored in animation and robotics to map human movements onto morphologically distinct robots. Some approaches involve learning retargeting policies from video demonstrations or utilizing trained controllers to map human keypoints onto humanoids. However, these often rely on learned components, requiring training. IMU-based motion capture offers markerless, room-scale-free body tracking suitable for various environments and has been used for data collection and offline retargeting, but its application in continuous real-time humanoid teleoperation with direct hardware deployment is less common.

Sim-to-real transfer remains an active research area, with common strategies including domain randomization and physics calibration. This paper contributes to addressing these challenges by proposing a system that specifically targets the real-time, learning-free, and direct sim-to-real transfer aspects of IMU-based teleoperation, diverging from methods heavily reliant on learned policies or extensive offline processing.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the increasing expectation for humanoid robots to perform complex tasks in human-centric environments, requiring coordinated and expressive full-body movements. Existing methods for generating such movements, including pre-scripted motions, offline trajectory optimization, and learning-based controllers, often entail significant engineering effort, such as extensive tuning, large datasets, or lengthy training procedures.

The authors propose that captured human motion offers a compelling alternative due to its inherent stability, expressiveness, and data richness, enabling direct and intuitive teleoperation. However, realizing real-time humanoid teleoperation from wearable IMU suits presents several technical hurdles. These include:
- Overcoming kinematic mismatches between human and robot morphologies.
- Mitigating the impact of accumulated noise from inertial sensors.
- Managing non-trivial control latency to ensure responsiveness.
- Bridging persistent simulation-to-real-world transfer gaps.

Given these challenges, the key objectives of this work are to develop and validate a complete real-time whole-body teleoperation system that:
1. **Maps human motion directly onto a Unitree G1 humanoid robot** using data from a Virdyn IMU-based full-body motion capture suit.
2. **Achieves continuous, imperceptibly-low-latency operation** through a custom motion-processing, kinematic retargeting, and control pipeline, specifically designed without any offline buffering or learning-based components.
3. **Addresses and mitigates the four identified challenges** (kinematic discrepancies, IMU noise, joint-limit-safe motion, and sim-to-real transfer instability).
4. **Undergoes systematic staged validation**, first in simulation (sim2sim) using the MuJoCo physics model of the Unitree G1, and then via direct deployment on the physical platform (sim2real) without modification.
5. **Demonstrates stable, synchronized reproduction** of a broad motion repertoire, including walking, standing, sitting, turning, bowing, and coordinated expressive full-body gestures.
6. **Establishes a practical and scalable framework** for whole-body humanoid teleoperation using commodity wearable motion capture hardware.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology involves an end-to-end real-time teleoperation pipeline designed to map human motion from an IMU suit to a humanoid robot, validated through a two-stage approach: simulation-to-simulation (sim2sim) and simulation-to-real (sim2real).

#### 3.1. Human Motion Capture

Human motion is acquired using a Virdyn full-body IMU motion capture suit. This suit is equipped with inertial sensors positioned on major body segments, enabling the estimation of segment orientations and relative joint angles in real time. The system operates independently of external cameras or optical markers, making it suitable for unconstrained environments. Prior to its integration with the robot control pipeline, the motion capture system was independently validated using the Virdyn reference visualization platform to confirm sensor calibration, full-body tracking consistency, and accurate reproduction of various upper and lower-body motions.

#### 3.2. Real-Time Retargeting and Control Pipeline

The core of the system is a custom motion-processing, kinematic retargeting, and control pipeline. This pipeline is engineered for continuous, low-latency operation without offline buffering or learning components. It addresses the morphological differences between humans and the Unitree G1 robot, including variations in link proportions, degrees of freedom, and joint range limits. The pipeline consists of four tightly coupled modules that execute synchronously within the robot's control loop:

1. **Kinematic Mapping:** This module establishes a mapping between human skeleton joints and their corresponding functional counterparts on the Unitree G1's kinematic tree. For structural differences where direct correspondence is not possible (e.g., the human hip complex versus the robot’s three-degrees-of-freedom hip joint), equivalent angles are computed through geometric projection. This projection aims to preserve the operator's intended motion while ensuring the resulting angles remain within the robot’s physical range.

2. **Joint Limit Enforcement:** Following kinematic mapping, every generated joint command is subjected to a clipping process. This ensures that the commands adhere to the robot’s hardware joint limits. Additionally, "soft limits," positioned slightly inside the hard mechanical stops, are applied. This measure serves to prevent actuator saturation and reduce wear on the robot, particularly during high-velocity or expressive movements.

3. **Real-Time Smoothing:** Raw IMU data inherently contains high-frequency noise. If unprocessed, this noise could manifest as rapid, high-amplitude joint oscillations, which are detrimental to stable robot locomotion. To mitigate this, a lightweight exponential moving average (EMA) filter is applied independently to each joint's command. The time constant of this filter is tuned to balance the trade-off between attenuating noise and maintaining motion responsiveness. This approach prioritizes computational efficiency over more complex filtering methods while still achieving adequate noise rejection for the required control rate.

4. **Synchronization:** The pipeline ensures that upper-body, lower-body, and torso motions are synchronized within a single retargeting step. This synchronization is crucial for maintaining the robot’s center-of-mass trajectory consistent with the operator’s posture. The entire pipeline operates within a tight control loop, processing each sensor frame to produce a corresponding robot command within the same control cycle, thereby avoiding buffering or batch processing to guarantee real-time performance.

A key design principle of this pipeline is its model-free nature, requiring no neural network weights, training data, or environment-specific parameters. This design choice contributes to its immediate portability across other humanoid platforms with comparable kinematics.

#### 3.3. Validation Strategy

The system underwent a systematic staged validation process:

1. **Simulation Verification (Sim2Sim):** The complete pipeline was first evaluated using the official Unitree G1 model within the MuJoCo physics simulator. Live joint estimates from the IMU suit were streamed to the retargeting module, and its output was directly applied to the simulated robot. This stage served to verify the correctness of the retargeting algorithm, identify and resolve pathological joint configurations, and confirm motion stability across a range of behaviors. It specifically validated that the algorithm produced physically plausible configurations without joint limit violations, self-collisions, or abrupt discontinuities in joint velocity. The successful completion of this stage provided preliminary evidence for the pipeline's potential for successful hardware transfer.

2. **Real Robot Deployment (Sim2Real):** Following successful sim2sim validation, the identical pipeline was deployed directly on the physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot. This transition was performed without any modifications to the motion processing or retargeting logic, including no additional domain adaptation, parameter retuning, or controller retraining. This direct transfer capability highlights the physics-agnostic nature of the kinematic retargeting design.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded specific findings regarding the performance and capabilities of the developed teleoperation system across both simulated and physical environments.

#### 4.1. Simulation Verification (Sim2Sim) Results

The initial validation phase in the MuJoCo physics simulator, utilizing the official Unitree G1 model, confirmed the fundamental integrity of the retargeting pipeline. During this stage, the system was observed to produce physically plausible robot configurations. Specific metrics of success included the absence of joint limit violations, no detected self-collisions, and the generation of smooth, continuous joint velocities. These results indicated that the kinematic retargeting algorithm could translate human motion intent into viable robot movements within a simulated environment, establishing a foundation for real-world deployment.

#### 4.2. Real Robot Deployment (Sim2Real) Results

Upon successful sim2sim verification, the identical teleoperation pipeline was deployed on the physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot. This deployment occurred without any modifications to the motion processing or retargeting logic, including the absence of domain adaptation, parameter retuning, or controller retraining.

The system demonstrated real-time reproduction of a diverse set of motion categories, reflecting the operator's actions:
- **Locomotion:** The robot successfully performed forward walking, backward walking, and stepping in place.
- **Postural Transitions:** The robot was able to execute transitions such as sitting down onto a chair and standing back up.
- **Orientation Changes:** The system facilitated in-place turning maneuvers and lateral weight shifts by the robot.
- **Expressive Gestures:** The robot replicated expressive movements, including bowing, waving, and various coordinated arm motions.
- **Compound Motions:** The system also handled more complex, compound motions, such as simultaneous arm gestures performed during locomotion.

A notable observation was the absence of any perceptible delay between the operator's physical motion and the robot's corresponding execution. This confirmed that the pipeline met the requirements for real-time performance. Qualitatively, the reproduced motions were consistently smooth and stable throughout all tested sequences. The robot maintained its balance throughout these operations without requiring any supplementary stabilization controllers beyond the Unitree G1’s onboard low-level joint servo. These results collectively demonstrate the effective and stable transfer of the teleoperation system from simulation to physical hardware.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work demonstrates the practical feasibility of IMU-based full-body teleoperation for humanoid robots using a purely kinematic, learning-free pipeline. The system exhibits stability, low latency, and a direct transfer capability from simulation to real hardware without modification. This combination of attributes addresses limitations often associated with learning-based approaches, which can be sensitive to training distributions and simulation fidelity.

#### 5.1. Advantages over Prior Approaches

The proposed system offers several advantages when compared to existing methods:
- **Reduced Training Overhead:** Unlike reinforcement learning-based whole-body controllers, it requires no training data, no simulation-to-real domain randomization, and no reward engineering, simplifying development and deployment.
- **True Real-Time Operation:** In contrast to offline retargeting pipelines, this system supports continuous, real-time operation with imperceptible latency.
- **Interpretability and Determinism:** The absence of learned components results in a fully interpretable and deterministic system behavior. This characteristic is advantageous in safety-critical deployment scenarios where understanding and predicting system responses are paramount.
- **Scalability and Accessibility:** The framework utilizes commodity wearable motion capture hardware, indicating a practical and scalable solution for human-robot interaction.

#### 5.2. Limitations and Future Work

While demonstrating robust performance, the current system has specific limitations:
- **Reliance on Onboard Stability:** The system depends on the Unitree G1’s onboard servo controllers for low-level stability. For highly dynamic motions, such as rapid direction changes or large-amplitude arm swings, the robot's balance could potentially be improved by integrating more advanced control strategies, such as an online whole-body momentum controller or a model-predictive footstep planner.
- **Filtering-Induced Lag:** The exponential moving average (EMA) filter, while effective for noise attenuation, introduces a small phase lag that is dependent on motion speed. Future improvements could explore adaptive Kalman filters to further reduce this effect while maintaining noise rejection.

Building on the current achievements, several future directions are identified:
- **Platform Adaptability:** Developing methods for automatic adaptation of retargeting parameters to accommodate other humanoid robot platforms, enhancing the system's universality.
- **Integration with Learning:** Exploring integration with imitation learning pipelines to leverage teleoperated demonstrations for bootstrapping neural whole-body controllers, potentially combining the benefits of direct control with advanced learned behaviors.
- **Enhanced Dexterity:** Extending the system to include dexterous hand manipulation, possibly through the use of finger-level IMU data or vision-based hand tracking, to enable more intricate interaction with objects.

This work establishes a practical framework that could significantly contribute to advancing the deployment of humanoid robots in real-world applications by simplifying control and enhancing natural interaction capabilities.
