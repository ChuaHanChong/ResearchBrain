# Reasoning-Driven Synthetic Data Generation and Evaluation

> [!summary] Summary
> The Simula framework, developed by researchers including those from EPFL and Google, introduces a reasoning-driven approach for generating and evaluating synthetic data without requiring seed examples. It leverages multi-modal models to construct hierarchical taxonomies and uses agentic refinement, consistently yielding superior or equivalent downstream model performance across various specialized domains.

## Problem

- Developing AI models for specialized, uncommon, or privacy-sensitive domains is hampered by the scarcity and inaccessibility of relevant, high-quality training data.
- Existing synthetic data methods face limitations in scalability, explainability, and granular control due to reliance on manual prompts, evolutionary algorithms, or substantial seed data.
- The field lacks consistent definitions for "good" synthetic data, effective mechanisms for practical deployment (scalability, explainability, control), and generalizable evaluation strategies.

## Method

- **Simula** utilizes large multi-modal models to create ==hierarchical taxonomies==, which map the conceptual space of a target domain by disentangling its "prime factors of variation" and refining them through a ==generator-critic process==.
- Synthetic data is generated via ==controlled taxonomic sampling== and ==agentic refinement== steps, including "complexification" for increasing complexity and a ==double-critic== mechanism to enhance output quality and correctness.
- The framework incorporates intrinsic evaluation methods, such as ==unsupervised calibrated Elo scores== for complexity and taxonomy-based assignment for measuring dataset coverage, enabling structured assessment of data properties.

## Results

- Simula's taxonomy generation approach achieved high completeness (**0.74-0.99**) and soundness (**0.34-1.00**), demonstrating its ability to systematically cover and expand conceptual spaces compared to 0-shot methods.
- The full Simula system consistently outperformed or matched other configurations across **five** diverse datasets and various data sizes, indicating its robust ability to improve downstream model accuracy.
- The framework generated synthetic datasets with higher taxonomic coverage and a broader range of complexity than real data, and its calibrated complexity scores accurately aligned with human ratings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-modal models, when combined with a generator-critic approach, can reliably produce high-quality, sound, and novel taxonomies, offering fine-grained control over the generated conceptual space.
> - A "double-critic" rejection sampling mechanism improves data quality and model accuracy, especially when targeting complex problems, by filtering samples deemed less suitable by the critic.
> - Optimizing intrinsic data properties like diversity (both global and local) and complexity simultaneously within a reasoning-driven system is crucial for achieving better downstream model performance, affecting data scaling laws.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in artificial intelligence have been significantly driven by the availability of extensive and diverse internet data, enabling the development of generalist models. However, the widespread application of AI necessitates specialization in novel, uncommon, and privacy-sensitive domains where relevant data is intrinsically scarce or inaccessible. Traditional methods of data acquisition, such as human annotation, are often associated with high costs, proneness to errors, and considerable time investment. This has led to an increasing interest in synthetic data as a scalable alternative.

Existing synthetic data generation techniques often rely on manual prompting, evolutionary algorithms, or substantial seed data from the target distribution. These approaches can present limitations in terms of scalability, explainability, and granular control over the generated data's characteristics. Furthermore, the field faces challenges in consistently defining "good" synthetic data, designing mechanisms that meet practical deployment requirements (e.g., scalability, explainability, control), and conducting generalizable evaluations that are not susceptible to benchmark overfitting. The reliance on real-world data also introduces structural, safety, and operational costs, including economic thresholds for participation, reactive approaches to safety, slow development cycles, and intractable issues related to societal biases and data ownership.

This research introduces Simula, a framework designed to address these challenges by providing a reasoning-driven, seedless, and agentic approach to synthetic data generation and evaluation. It positions itself within the landscape by focusing on transparency, fine-grained control, and scalability, aiming to overcome the limitations of prior methods that might be opaque or heavily dependent on pre-existing data. The work contributes to the evolving understanding of how to systematically generate and evaluate synthetic data to enable AI development in data-constrained or sensitive environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop and evaluate a novel framework, Simula, for generating high-quality synthetic data at scale, thereby mitigating the specialized data bottleneck that currently impedes the broader integration and specialization of advanced AI models. The authors identify several motivations and specific problems that Simula aims to address:

1. **Addressing Data Scarcity and Inaccessibility:** Many AI applications of interest, particularly those requiring specialized multi-modal models, encounter a fundamental lack of relevant training data. Manual annotation is presented as an unsustainable solution due to its expense, time-consuming nature, and susceptibility to errors. Simula is motivated by the need for a scalable, automated alternative.

2. **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Synthetic Data Methods:** Current synthetic data generation methods are often criticized for their reliance on manual prompts (limiting scalability), evolutionary algorithms (limiting explainability), or extensive seed data (limiting control and generalizability). The research seeks to introduce a "seedless, agentic approach" that offers greater scalability, explainability, and fine-grained control over dataset characteristics.

3. **Enhancing Explainability and Controllability:** A key motivation is to allow users to define desired dataset characteristics through an explainable and controllable process. This aims to enable precise resource allocation during data generation and provide transparency, in contrast to "black box" real-world data, which complicates bias mitigation and ownership issues.

4. **Improving Data Quality, Diversity, and Complexity:** The paper acknowledges the ambiguity surrounding what constitutes "good" synthetic data (often discussed in terms of quality, diversity, and complexity). Simula aims to provide mechanisms to systematically optimize these desiderata, moving beyond approaches that only optimize for a subset of requirements or equate complexity with "difficulty" without clear definition.

5. **Establishing Mechanism Design for Synthetic Data:** The research is motivated by the need to define clear mechanism criteria for generating effective data at scale. It posits that realistic deployment requires scalability, explainability (e.g., audit traces), and control (e.g., fine-grained resource allocation), which are often lacking in methods relying on elaborate custom prompts or stochastic algorithms. The work seeks to establish mechanism design as a distinct research axis.

6. **Developing Generalizable Evaluation Strategies:** Current evaluation approaches, often relying on benchmarks, are noted to be prone to issues such as incompleteness, incorrect labels, unbalance, under-annotation, and risks of data leakage or overfitting to benchmark-specific quirks. A motivation is to develop evaluation methods that assess both intrinsic properties of generated data and its effectiveness for downstream tasks, while promoting generalization.

By addressing these challenges, Simula aims to offer guidelines for synthetic data mechanism design, provide insights into scalable generation and evaluation, and unlock new opportunities for AI development and deployment in domains where data scarcity or privacy concerns are paramount.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Simula is introduced as a reasoning-driven framework for synthetic data generation and evaluation that operates through a three-stage pipeline, emphasizing transparency, fine-grained control, and scalability. The methodology constructs each data point "from first principles," aiming for inherent future-proofing as the reasoning capabilities of underlying models advance.

#### 3.1. Taxonomies to Capture Dataset Coverage

The initial stage focuses on mapping the conceptual space of the target domain. This begins by disentangling the target dataset into its "prime factors of variation" (e.g., "cat type," "story format," "intended audience"). A multi-modal model (M3) is then used to propose these factors based on user instructions and/or a sample of existing data. These factors are expanded breadth-first into hierarchical taxonomies, T_i, to a user-specified depth.

To mitigate potential coverage loss during taxonomy expansion, the process alternates between three steps:
1. **Proposal (Best-of-N):** An M3 is prompted multiple times (N times) to propose an initial set of child nodes for a given node, its ancestors, and siblings. This strategy is intended to increase the proposal distribution and cover edge cases.
2. **Critic Refinement:** A separate M3 acts as a critic, refining the initial proposals by adding, removing, merging, or editing nodes to enhance completeness, soundness, and specificity, leveraging the generator-critic gap.
3. **Planning:** Optionally, after generating all nodes for a specific level, an M3 generates a "plan" for the next level to ensure consistent granularity across independent node expansions.
This approach provides granular explainability and control over the conceptual space compared to random sampling.

#### 3.2. Controlled, Explainable, and Scalable Data Synthesis with Agentic Steps

Once taxonomies are established, synthetic datasets are generated through two phases: taxonomic sampling and agentic refinement.

- **Sampling Strategies to Control Global Diversity:** An M3 formulates a plan comprising sampling strategies. Each strategy defines which taxonomies are sampled together and with what weights, preventing illogical combinations (e.g., combining simple topics with age-appropriate formats). Nodes are sampled from selected taxonomies, which, along with original instructions, guide the M3 to construct "meta prompts."
- **Optimizing Local Diversity and Complexity:** For a given node-set, multiple meta prompts can be generated to increase local diversity and mitigate mode collapse. A user-defined fraction of these meta prompts undergoes "complexification," where the M3 is prompted to increase their complexity and the complexity of the generated outputs. For very large ratios of data points per node-set, Simula can sequentially generate more diverse or complex meta prompts by providing previous attempts as context, enabling reflection.
- **Enhancing Sample Quality with Critics:** The system incorporates agentic refinement steps to optimize output quality. It performs point-wise checks where an M3 "critiques" generated samples against specified semantic and syntactic requirements derived from the taxonomy nodes and meta prompts. For tasks requiring a defined notion of correctness, an additional "double-critic" step is employed. This step independently assesses both correctness and incorrectness to mitigate sycophancy bias. If a negative verdict is returned, the sample is either rejected or undergoes automated modification based on the explanation, followed by re-critique.

#### 3.3. Evaluating and Comparing Dataset Properties

Simula also offers approaches for evaluating and comparing properties of both synthetic and real datasets using agentic reasoning and taxonomies.

- **Reasoning about Complexity using Calibrated Attribute Scoring:** To assign complexity scores to individual data points in an unsupervised manner, a batch-wise evaluation method is used. Batches of data points, where each appears multiple times (K), are sampled. An M3 assigns complexity scores to each sample within the batch, using the dataset description. To reduce noise and calibrate outputs, pairwise comparisons within batches are performed, from which Elo scores are computed. These Elo scores enable complexity comparisons across different datasets and provide control over sample complexity.
- **Using Taxonomies to Curate Dataset Coverage:** Taxonomies are utilized to understand existing data. An M3 is queried to perform taxonomy assignments for each data point in a dataset, linking it to the most relevant node. This process creates a detailed map of the dataset's composition, allowing derivation of coverage metrics, such as "Level Ratio Coverage," which quantifies the proportion of unique nodes covered at each taxonomy level, highlighting representation gaps.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

The research validates Simula's core reasoning assumptions and efficacy through a series of experiments.
- **Core Reasoning Component Verification:** Experiments assess M3 capabilities in:
 - Generating high-quality taxonomies, comparing Simula's generator-critic approach against 0-shot expansion on six real-world taxonomies (both grounded and conceptual), evaluating completeness, soundness, and novelty.
 - Functioning as effective "critics" using a "double critic" on reference tasks with corrupted answers (controlled setting) and on the model's own generated outputs (empirical setting), measuring rejection rates and accuracy changes.
 - Distinguishing data complexity by aligning calibrated scores with human-provided labels (MATH dataset) and educational levels (Global MMLU) as proxies.
- **System Versions and Datasets:** An ablation study compares five system configurations: Baseline (top-level nodes, direct M3 generation), Local (top-level, meta-prompting, complexification), Global (full taxonomy depth, no meta-prompting), Local + Global, and Full System (Local + Global + Critic). Five datasets are used: CTI-MCQ, CTI-RCM (Cyber Threat Intelligence), LEXam (legal domain), GSM8k (math word problems), and a subset of Global MMLU (multilingual multiple-choice). Generated data undergoes de-duplication and decontamination.
- **Intrinsic Metrics:** Metrics include embedding diversity (dataset-wide and nearest-neighbors cosine distance) and taxonomic coverage (assignment-based Level Ratio Coverage), as well as complexity using calibrated Elo scores.
- **Downstream Metrics:** Gemma 3 4B is used as the student model and Gemini 2.5-Flash as the teacher. LoRA fine-tuning is performed for 10 iterations with different seeds, measuring mean accuracy and 95% confidence intervals. Data complexity's impact is isolated by splitting datasets into Low, High, and All Complexity subsets.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents detailed findings from the evaluation of Simula's core reasoning components, intrinsic data properties, and downstream model performance.

#### 4.1. Results for Core Reasoning Components

- **M3s Can Generate High-Quality Taxonomies:** Simula's generator-critic approach produced taxonomies that approximated expert-created ones. For conceptual taxonomies, generated nodes were almost entirely sound, and many contained novel expansions, leading to increased total coverage (Simula completeness ranged from 0.74 to 0.99, soundness from 0.34 to 1.00 across grounded and conceptual taxonomies, consistently outperforming 0-shot generation). This supported the approach of using taxonomies to approximate global coverage.
- **Double-Critic Rejection Sampling Enhances Quality:** In controlled settings (MATH dataset), the double-critic method demonstrated a consistent theoretical "lift" in accuracy. However, maintaining this lift required higher rejection rates as task complexity increased. In the empirical setting (filtering the model's own outputs), a lift over the baseline was still observed, but it was less effective and also demanded higher rejection rates for more complex problems to sustain accuracy gains.
- **Complexity Scores Align; Rejected Samples Perceived Harder:** The calibrated complexity scoring method yielded model-assigned Elo scores that aligned with human-assigned complexity ratings. Furthermore, rejected samples were systematically assigned higher Elo scores than accepted ones, indicating the critic system effectively filtered samples perceived as more complex by the model. This alignment and filtering capability were observed to be robust across multiple languages in the MMLU dataset.

#### 4.2. Intrinsic Metric Results

- **Intrinsic Diversity:**
 - **Embedding Diversity:** The "Global" diversification component was crucial for increasing dataset-wide embedding diversity. The "Local" component improved local diversity by increasing the variation among k-nearest neighbors. These components generally had an additive effect. While real data's embedding diversity could be higher or lower than synthetic variants, its taxonomic coverage was consistently lower.
 - **Taxonomic Coverage:** The full Simula system exhibited higher taxonomic coverage, especially at deeper levels, compared to real datasets. This suggests that real data often misses significant subsets of the target domain that Simula can systematically generate. Critiquing steps did not significantly affect diversity.
- **Intrinsic Complexity:** The full Simula system demonstrated the capacity to cover, and often exceed, the entire complexity range present in real data. The "Local" and "Global" components contributed additively to different types of complexity, indicating they address distinct aspects. Datasets exhibited varied complexity profiles, with LEXam showing a more concentrated profile.

#### 4.3. Downstream Results

- **Simultaneously Optimizing All Data Axes Never Hurts:** The full Simula system consistently emerged as the dominant strategy across all tested datasets and data sizes, yielding the best or equivalent performance. In contrast, the Baseline configuration scaled poorly, suggesting that data-scaling laws are influenced by data properties, not merely size.
- **Student-Teacher Gap Impacts Scaling Laws:** The performance of the student model at different data scales was observed to be influenced by the performance gap with the teacher model. For example, CTI-RCM performance saturated after closing approximately 83% of the student-teacher performance gap, while GSM8k performance continued to grow as the student model remained further from the teacher's performance.
- **Combining Global and Local Diversity is Critical:** Individual application of Local or Global diversification often yielded suboptimal returns. Local diversification plateaued early for CTI-MCQ and underperformed in small data regimes for CTI-RCM. Global diversification sometimes did not surpass the Baseline (LEXam) or scaled worse (GSM8k). Combining both components consistently improved performance across all datasets and sizes.
- **Critiquing Impact is Dataset Dependent:** Adding the critiquing step either maintained or improved downstream performance, never causing degradation. It showed a significant improvement for Global MMLU, despite a low rejection rate (3%). For LEXam, a high rejection rate (61%) due to weaker teacher performance did not negatively impact the fine-tuned model's accuracy.
- **Complexity is Crucial in Most Domains but Can Hurt with a Weak Teacher:** Data complexity was critical for performance in many domains. For GSM8k, high complexity data yielded a 10% accuracy gain over low complexity data at 64k samples, and CTI-MCQ's low complexity split showed negligible scaling. However, for LEXam, only the low complexity split showed improvement, suggesting that complex data can be detrimental when the underlying teacher model is weak.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Simula framework, through its reasoning-driven approach to synthetic data generation and evaluation, presents several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of artificial intelligence:

1. **Enabling AI in Data-Scarce and Sensitive Domains:** By offering a scalable, seedless method for generating specialized data, Simula directly addresses the bottleneck of data scarcity and inaccessibility. This can unlock new opportunities for developing and deploying AI in critical areas where human-annotated data is prohibitively expensive, time-consuming, or sensitive due to privacy concerns.

2. **Enhanced Control and Explainability in Data Generation:** Simula's emphasis on taxonomies and agentic steps provides fine-grained control over desired dataset characteristics. This transparency contrasts with the "black box" nature of real-world data, enabling a clearer understanding of potential biases, allowing for proactive mitigation, and facilitating audit trails for legal and operational requirements. This shift towards programmable, controllable data generation workflows can accelerate AI development cycles.

3. **Reframing Synthetic Data Mechanism Design:** The work advocates for "mechanism design" – how data is generated – as a distinct research axis, orthogonal to the traditional focus on what constitutes "good data." This perspective highlights the importance of practical requirements like scalability, explainability, and control, offering a structured approach to building flexible synthetic data systems tailored to diverse needs.

4. **Improved and Actionable Evaluation Metrics:** Simula introduces novel intrinsic evaluation metrics, such as node-level taxonomic coverage and reasoning-based calibrated complexity scores. These provide more structured, explainable, and actionable insights into data properties compared to coarse embedding-based metrics. This allows practitioners to diagnose data composition, identify gaps, and curate optimal corpora more effectively.

5. **Insights into Scaling Laws for Data Properties:** The empirical analysis demonstrates that the efficacy of data scaling is not solely a function of data size but also depends on intrinsic data properties like diversity and complexity. The finding that simultaneously optimizing these properties (via the full Simula system) consistently leads to superior downstream performance offers valuable guidance for training AI models.

6. **Future-Proofing and Generalizability:** The framework's reliance on the reasoning capabilities of underlying large language models implies that its generation capabilities are expected to improve naturally with advancements in these foundational models. While the current experiments focus on text-based data, the authors suggest the reasoning-driven system could extend to other modalities, indicating broader applicability.

7. **Addressing Societal Impact (Dual-Use and Mitigation):** The authors acknowledge the dual-use potential of a system with such fine-grained control, including the risk of generating nefarious content or reinforcing biases. However, they highlight Simula's integrated mitigation mechanisms, such as transparent generation processes, taxonomic coverage measurements for detecting imbalances, and calibrated attribute scoring for identifying shifts in sensitive attributes, thereby promoting responsible and ethical synthetic data generation.

In conclusion, Simula represents a step towards making synthetic data a more robust, controlled, and interpretable resource for AI development, particularly for specialized applications, while also offering a framework for understanding and mitigating the inherent challenges in this evolving field.
