# Failing Forward: Adaptive Failure-Informed Learning for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Adaptive Failure-Informed Learning (AFIL) enhances Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by integrating online-generated failure trajectories as adaptive negative guidance during action generation. This framework improves robotic task success rates and robustness across various manipulation tasks, particularly for diffusion and flow-based VLA policies.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models are brittle due to training primarily on success-only trajectories, leading to compounding errors and unrecoverable failures in out-of-distribution scenarios.
- Current methods for learning from failure often require handcrafted failure modes or real-time human intervention, limiting scalability and generalization.
- Diffusion- and flow-based VLA policies lack explicit mechanisms to actively avoid failure-prone actions during generation, as they are trained solely on successful demonstrations.

## Method

- Introduces **Adaptive Failure-Informed Learning (AFIL)**, an end-to-end framework that uses ==online-generated failure trajectories== to provide ==adaptive negative guidance== during VLA action generation.
- Employs a ==Dual Action Generator (DAG)== architecture with a shared VLM backbone and parallel generators for successful and failed action distributions.
- Implements an ==adaptive failure-informed sampling== mechanism, dynamically adjusting guidance strength based on the ==cosine similarity== between success and failure noise predictions, repelling from failure modes.

## Results

- AFIL consistently achieved the highest success rates on both short- and long-horizon in-domain tasks, demonstrating significant gains over static failure-informed guidance, particularly a **+12.0%** improvement on one long-horizon task.
- The framework outperformed all compared methods (e.g., Diffusion Policy, Octo, OpenVLA) across all four challenging LIBERO task suites, increasing average success rate from "π₀.₅"'s **96.9%** to **98.4%**.
- AFIL significantly enhanced robustness in out-of-domain tasks involving unseen objects and cluttered scenes, with improvements like **62.7%** success for "Lift Cylinder" compared to **14.7%** for the success-only baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically adapting the negative guidance strength, based on the cosine similarity between predicted success and failure actions, effectively steers the policy away from emerging failure modes without overly repelling it from valid actions.
> - Collecting failure data through online inference from a pre-trained policy captures a richer, more realistic distribution of errors than handcrafted taxonomies, improving generalizability.
> - The Dual Action Generator architecture, by sharing a VLM backbone, allows for efficient learning of both success and failure distributions with minimal additional computational overhead.
