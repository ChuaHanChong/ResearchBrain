# Domain Randomization via Entropy Maximization

> [!summary] Summary
> DORAEMON (DOmain RAndomization via Entropy MaximizatiON) automates dynamics randomization for sim-to-real RL by solving a constrained problem: maximize the entropy of the Beta-parameterized dynamics-parameter distribution subject to a target in-distribution success rate, with KL trust regions and importance-sampled success estimates reusing training rollouts. It reaches 66.6% sim2sim and 60% real success on a 7-DoF box-pushing task, beating AutoDR and LSDR.

## Problem

- Domain randomization outcomes hinge on hand-tuned sampling distributions: too narrow fails to generalize, too wide over-regularizes policies into conservative behavior that never reaches optimal performance.
- Adaptive randomization methods that infer parameters from real trajectories need real-world data collection, which is costly, unsafe, and unavailable before deployment on new hardware.
- Prior automatic schemes need a reference distribution plus Monte-Carlo re-evaluations, or bias half of training toward uniform boundaries, wasting environment interactions and restricting distributions to uniform.

## Method

- Constrained objective: maximize ==differential entropy== of the dynamics distribution subject to probability of success staying above threshold alpha, converting DR tuning into defining one binary success indicator.
- Decoupled iterative solver: ==SAC== trains a history-conditioned policy with a dynamics-privileged critic, while distribution updates use a ==KL trust region== and an ==importance-sampling estimator== recycling training episodes.
- Back-up optimization: when the importance-sampled success estimate violates alpha, a fallback problem searches within the trust region for maximum-success parameters, backtracking entropy of the uncorrelated ==Beta distributions==.

## Results

- On PandaPush, **DORAEMON** hits **66.57%** sim2sim and **60%** real success with **2.68cm** final box distance, versus **46.67%** for LSDR, **26.67%** AutoDR, **13.33%** no-randomization.
- Across **six** MuJoCo tasks (**2-13** randomized dimensions), it reaches higher or faster global success rate on the maximum-entropy uniform distribution than No-DR, Fixed-DR, LSDR and AutoDR over **ten** seeds.
- Ablations: alpha=**0.5** gave best Hopper generalization; return thresholds **1400/1600/1800** each tracked the **50%** success target; all **ten** Fixed-DR PandaPush seeds collapsed to **0.02%** sim success.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Entropy is a data-free proxy for robustness: widening the training distribution until success degrades gives generalization without any real-world data or reference distribution.
> - Gradual widening beats training directly on the widest distribution, since the curriculum effect persists even when both end at identical final entropy.
> - Success rate beats average return as a curriculum signal: medians ignore catastrophic scores from infeasible dynamics that would otherwise drag the distribution back.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a fundamental challenge in Reinforcement Learning (RL) for robotics: the **reality gap** (or sim-to-real gap). Training RL agents directly on real robots is prohibitively expensive, time-consuming, and often unsafe due to the high sample inefficiency of RL algorithms. Simulation offers a safe and cost-effective alternative, but discrepancies between the simulated and real environments (e.g., imperfect physics models, unknown material properties) prevent policies trained in simulation from directly transferring to the real world.

**Domain Randomization (DR)** is a widely adopted sim-to-real approach that tackles the reality gap by varying simulator parameters (e.g., friction, mass, visual textures) during training. The intuition is that if an agent learns to operate effectively across a wide range of randomized environments in simulation, it will be robust enough to generalize to the unknown dynamics of the real world. However, traditional DR methods suffer from a critical limitation: the **manual tuning problem**. Selecting an effective range and distribution of dynamics parameters is a tedious, expert-driven process. Excessive randomization can lead to overly conservative policies that fail to achieve optimal performance, while insufficient randomization results in policies that lack generalization.

This paper positions itself within the "automated DR" paradigm, specifically aiming for **zero-shot transfer** (no real-world data required for transfer). It critically reviews existing automated DR methods:
- **Adaptive Domain Randomization (ADR):** These methods infer simulator parameters from real-world data (e.g., Tiboni et al., 2023; Chebotar et al., 2019). While effective, they contradict the goal of zero-shot transfer by requiring real data.
- **Automated DR in absence of real data:**
 - **LSDR (Mozian et al., 2020):** Guides a training distribution to maximize average performance over a *fixed reference range*. This requires iterative policy testing on out-of-distribution dynamics, leading to poor data efficiency.
 - **AutoDR (Akkaya et al., 2019):** Gradually expands a *uniform* distribution by assessing policy performance at its boundaries. This approach is confined to uniform distributions, biases training data towards boundaries, and can be data-inefficient as it updates one dimension at a time.
 - Other approaches (e.g., Mehta et al., 2020) learn sampling strategies but often still rely on a reference DR distribution.

DORAEMON differentiates itself by:
1. **Directly maximizing the entropy of the training distribution:** This is a novel approach for automated DR, aiming to inherently increase the diversity of sampled dynamics parameters.
2. **Constraining entropy growth by policy success rate:** This prevents performance collapse from excessive randomization, ensuring the policy remains proficient.
3. **Not requiring real-world data or a predefined reference distribution:** This makes it truly zero-shot and simplifies the setup compared to LSDR.
4. **Data efficiency:** It updates the distribution using data naturally collected during training, unlike LSDR and AutoDR which may require additional Monte-Carlo rollouts or discard data.

The work also draws parallels with **curriculum learning (CL)**, where task difficulty is gradually increased. DORAEMON can be seen as inducing a curriculum over dynamics parameters, akin to self-paced CL methods. However, it specifically adapts CL principles to the unique requirements of DR (unknown real-world target distribution, latent dynamics parameters at test time), distinguishing it from traditional CL which assumes observable contexts and known target tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel, automated method for **Domain Randomization (DR) that facilitates robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies without requiring real-world data or extensive manual tuning.**

The core motivations underpinning this objective are:
1. **Overcoming the Reality Gap:** The persistent discrepancy between simulation and reality hinders the deployment of RL-trained robots in real-world scenarios. DR is a powerful tool, but its effectiveness is often bottlenecked by manual parameter tuning.
2. **Addressing Data Inefficiency and Safety Concerns of Real-World Training:** RL algorithms demand vast amounts of data. Collecting this data directly on physical robots is expensive, time-consuming, and potentially hazardous. Simulation offers a safe and scalable alternative, but policies trained solely in a static simulation often fail to transfer.
3. **Automating DR Distribution Selection:** Existing automated DR methods have limitations. Some require real-world data (Adaptive DR), while others (AutoDR, LSDR) are either data-inefficient, confined to specific distribution types (uniform), or rely on predefined reference ranges. The authors aim for a more general, efficient, and truly automated approach.
4. **Promoting Generalizability and Adaptivity:** The central hypothesis is that policies trained on a broader, more diverse range of dynamics parameters will be more robust and adaptable to unseen real-world conditions. However, this must be balanced with the need for policies to maintain high performance.
5. **Simplifying the Sim-to-Real Pipeline:** By automating the DR process, the proposed method seeks to reduce the expertise and manual effort required, making sim-to-real transfer more accessible and practical for robotic applications.

In essence, the authors aim to solve the long-standing problem of effectively and automatically shaping the randomized dynamics distributions in simulation to produce policies that generalize optimally to the real world, thus closing the reality gap in a data-efficient and fully simulated manner.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose **DOmain RAndomization via Entropy MaximizatiON (DORAEMON)**, a constrained optimization framework designed to automatically shape the dynamics parameter distribution during RL training.

**Core Idea:** DORAEMON operates on the intuition that better generalization is achieved with increased diversity of sampled dynamics parameters. It aims to maximize the entropy of the training distribution, effectively making the sampled environments as diverse as possible, while crucially ensuring the policy retains a high probability of success.

**Mathematical Formulation:**
The problem is formulated as:
`max_{θ∈Θ,ϕ∈Φ} H(ν_ϕ) s.t. G(θ, ϕ) ≥ α`
where:
- `H(ν_ϕ)` is the differential entropy of the dynamics parameter distribution `ν_ϕ`. Maximizing this encourages maximum diversity (approaching a uniform distribution over the valid parameter space).
- `G(θ, ϕ) = P[σ(τ) = 1|θ, ϕ]` is the probability of success of the policy `π_θ` when interacting with environments sampled from `ν_ϕ`.
- `σ(τ)` is a binary success indicator function (e.g., reaching a goal, achieving a certain return threshold).
- `α` is a user-defined desired in-distribution success rate, acting as a lower bound constraint.

**Algorithmic Implementation:**
DORAEMON uses a decoupled optimization approach, interleaving policy training and dynamics distribution updates.

1. **Policy Training:** A history-dependent policy `π_θ` is trained using any suitable RL algorithm (Soft Actor-Critic, SAC, in this work). The critic network is additionally conditioned on the true dynamics parameters (asymmetric actor-critic), which is a common trick in DR to mitigate non-stationarity and aid learning under partial observability without requiring these parameters at test time. The history allows the policy to implicitly infer latent dynamics.
2. **Dynamics Distribution Update:** After a policy update (or collecting K episodes), the dynamics distribution `ν_ϕ` is updated by solving:
 `max_{ϕ_i+1∈Φ} H(ν_{ϕ_i+1}) s.t. G(θ_i, ϕ_{i+1}) ≥ α, D_KL(ν_{ϕ_i+1} || ν_{ϕ_i}) ≤ ϵ`
 - A Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence constraint (`D_KL(ν_{ϕ_i+1} || ν_{ϕ_i}) ≤ ϵ`) is added to ensure gradual changes in the distribution (trust region), acting as a curriculum.
 - The success rate `G(θ_i, ϕ_{i+1})` is approximated using an **importance sampling (IS) estimator** based on trajectories `{(ξ_k, τ_k)}` collected during the *current* policy training step. This avoids additional Monte-Carlo rollouts, making the approach data-efficient.
3. **Backup Optimization Problem:** A critical component for stability. If the approximated success rate falls below `α` (indicating a violated constraint), a backup optimization is triggered:
 `ϕ_B_i = arg max_{ϕ'_i∈Φ} ˆG(θ_i, ϕ_i, ϕ'_i) s.t. D_KL(ν_{ϕ'_i} || ν_{ϕ_i}) ≤ ϵ`
 This finds a new, sufficiently close distribution `ν_{ϕ'_i}` that maximizes the (approximated) in-distribution success rate, effectively backtracking the entropy to a feasible region. This mechanism is crucial for recovering performance and maintaining the success constraint.

**Distribution Parameterization:**
The authors use uncorrelated univariate **Beta distributions** for `ν_ϕ(ξ)`. This is suitable because dynamics parameters are often inherently bounded (e.g., positive masses, friction coefficients), and Beta distributions naturally operate on a bounded support, making them well-suited for converging to uniform distributions within specified ranges. The framework, however, is general enough to accommodate other parametric distribution families (e.g., Gaussian, as explored in the appendix).

**Toy Problem Illustration:**
A simple frictionless inclined plane task (cart balancing against gravity with varying inclination angle `ω`) demonstrates DORAEMON's ability to find the maximum entropy distribution within the physically feasible range of `ω` where the policy can succeed, illustrating the trade-off between `α` and the final distribution's entropy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DORAEMON was empirically validated in both simulated (Sim-to-Sim) and real-world (Sim-to-Real) settings, demonstrating superior performance and generalization capabilities compared to existing baselines.

#### 4.1. Sim-to-Sim Transfer (MuJoCo Environments)

Experiments were conducted on six OpenAI Gym MuJoCo tasks (CartPole, SwingUpCartpole, Hopper, Walker2D, HalfCheetah, Swimmer), randomizing various dynamics parameters (e.g., link masses, friction, gravity). Success was defined by a lower bound on trajectory return.

- **Superior Generalization:** DORAEMON consistently achieved higher and/or faster convergence in **Global Success Rate** (success rate evaluated on the maximum-entropy uniform distribution `ν_max`) across all tasks compared to baselines (No-DR, Fixed-DR, LSDR, AutoDR). This directly validates its ability to train highly generalizable policies.
- **Effective Entropy Maximization:** DORAEMON successfully increased the entropy of its training distribution, gradually exposing the agent to more diverse dynamics, while maintaining the desired in-distribution success rate.
- **Curriculum Learning Effect:** DORAEMON significantly outperformed **Fixed-DR** (policies trained on a fixed maximum-entropy uniform distribution), highlighting the importance of the gradual curriculum-like widening of the training distribution rather than directly exposing the agent to maximal diversity from the start.
- **Baseline Limitations:**
 - **LSDR** converged to intermediate entropy values and showed higher variance, attributed to its M-projection objective and computational complexity with high-dimensional dynamics.
 - **AutoDR** increased entropy but with higher variance and generally lower global success rates, likely due to its data-inefficient approach of updating one dimension at a time and discarding data if performance thresholds were not met.
- **Visualization of Generalization:** For HalfCheetah, DORAEMON produced policies that solved the task over a visibly wider 2D slice of the dynamics space (torso mass and friction) compared to baselines.
- **Trade-off Analysis (`α`):** The hyperparameter `α` (desired in-distribution success rate) effectively controlled the balance between distribution entropy and policy success. Lower `α` (e.g., 0.5) allowed for higher entropy and better generalization, as the policy was encouraged to explore more challenging dynamics. DORAEMON consistently maintained the chosen `α` value, showcasing the robustness of the backup optimization.
- **Trade-off Analysis (Success Indicator `J_LB`):** DORAEMON's behavior was agnostic to the specific return threshold `J_LB` used to define success, successfully maintaining the desired `α` even for varied `J_LB` values. It efficiently traded performance (median return) for robustness to wider dynamics, as intended.

#### 4.2. Sim-to-Real Transfer (PandaPush Environment)

A novel 7-DoF robotic manipulation task, PandaPush, was designed to test zero-shot transferability under unknown object center-of-mass (CoM). 17 dynamics parameters were randomized, including box mass, surface friction, joint damping/friction, and box CoM.

- **Remarkable Zero-Shot Transfer:** DORAEMON achieved a **60% Sim2Real success rate** and a significantly lower average distance to target (2.68 ± 1.01 cm) in real-world deployments under unknown parameters. This was significantly better than all baselines.
- **Adaptive Behavior:** Visual evidence demonstrated that DORAEMON-trained policies exhibited impressive adaptive behavior; for instance, by subtly touching the box, the robot implicitly inferred the shifted CoM and adjusted its pushing strategy accordingly.
- **Baseline Performance in Sim-to-Real:**
 - **LSDR** achieved a 46.67% success rate but with higher variance and distance to target, reflecting its scalability issues.
 - **AutoDR** showed limited performance (26.67% success rate), which the authors attribute to its slower, data-inefficient distribution growth.
 - **Fixed-DR** failed entirely (0% success rate), underscoring the severe challenge of learning on excessively diverse dynamics without a curriculum.
 - **No-DR** had a low success rate (13.33%), demonstrating the reality gap without DR.

**Ablation Studies (Appendix B):**
A detailed ablation of DORAEMON from the perspective of self-paced curriculum learning (SPDL) methods highlighted the critical contributions of each component: history-based policies, asymmetric actor-critic, and particularly the backup optimization problem, which was vital for consistently maintaining the desired in-distribution success rate.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DORAEMON represents a significant advancement in the field of sim-to-real transfer for reinforcement learning, with several important implications:

- **Automation of a Critical Bottleneck:** It effectively automates the challenging and labor-intensive process of selecting and tuning dynamics randomization parameters. This dramatically reduces the need for expert knowledge and manual iteration, lowering the barrier to entry for developing RL-controlled robots.
- **Enhanced Generalizability and Adaptivity:** By systematically maximizing the diversity of training environments while maintaining task proficiency, DORAEMON produces policies that are inherently more robust and adaptive to unknown real-world dynamics. This is crucial for deploying RL agents in complex, unstructured environments.
- **True Zero-Shot Transfer:** The method eliminates the need for any real-world data during the sim-to-real transfer process, offering a truly zero-shot capability. This translates to faster development cycles, reduced costs, and safer experimentation.
- **Data Efficiency:** DORAEMON's use of importance sampling to approximate success rates from existing training data makes it more data-efficient than previous automated DR approaches that required additional policy evaluations.
- **Broader Applicability:** The method's flexibility in parameterizing distributions (e.g., Beta or Gaussian) and its general formulation suggest applicability across a wide range of robotics tasks and simulation environments.
- **Bridging RL and Curriculum Learning:** DORAEMON draws inspiration from and conceptually bridges self-paced curriculum learning with domain randomization, adapting principles of gradual task difficulty increase to the unique challenges of learning generalizable policies for unknown real-world dynamics.

**Potential Impact:**
The successful zero-shot transfer demonstrated on a complex robotic manipulation task (PandaPush) indicates that DORAEMON can enable more widespread and practical applications of RL in real-world robotics. This could accelerate the development of autonomous robots for manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, and other domains where robust and adaptive control is paramount. By making sim-to-real transfer more reliable and less resource-intensive, DORAEMON facilitates the transition of cutting-edge RL research from simulation to practical, impactful real-world deployments.

**Limitations and Future Work (as identified by authors):**
The authors acknowledge a few limitations and suggest future research directions:
1. **"Easy" Region Collapse:** DORAEMON might occasionally collapse to "easy" regions of the optimization landscape when backtracking, potentially leading to catastrophic forgetting of previously learned skills. Future work could investigate adding a KL constraint between the current and best-performing policy to mitigate this.
2. **Lack of Prior Knowledge Integration:** While DORAEMON removes the need for prior knowledge, if such knowledge exists, biasing the distribution growth around known real-world parameters could be beneficial, although this presents a harder optimization problem.
3. **Multimodal Distributions:** Leveraging multimodal distributions could allow isolating problematic regions in the dynamics space while maintaining generalizability over feasible dynamics, addressing scenarios where certain parameter ranges are inherently difficult or infeasible.

In conclusion, DORAEMON presents a principled and effective solution to a long-standing challenge in robot learning, pushing the boundaries of automated domain randomization and bringing the promise of zero-shot sim-to-real transfer closer to reality.
