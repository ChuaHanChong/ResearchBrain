# Opening Articulated Structures in the Real World

> [!summary] Summary
> A modular mobile manipulation system (MOSART) developed at the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign achieves a 61% success rate in opening 31 novel articulated objects across 13 diverse real-world environments. The system demonstrates that modular approaches can significantly outperform state-of-the-art end-to-end imitation learning methods for zero-shot generalization in complex physical interaction tasks.

## Problem

- Existing mobile manipulation systems frequently struggle to generalize to novel objects and unfamiliar environments, limiting their real-world utility.
- Much robotics research focuses on improving individual subproblems, potentially obscuring true system-level bottlenecks in practical deployment.
- A lack of large-scale empirical evidence for whether modular or end-to-end learned systems are more effective for generalization in robotics.

## Method

- Introduces **MOSART** (MOdular System for opening ARTiculated structures), a modular mobile manipulation pipeline for zero-shot generalization on a commodity robot (Stretch RE2).
- Employs an ==Articulation-parameter Prediction Module (APM)== using modified ==Mask R-CNN== and depth lifting for 2D/3D handle and articulation type estimation from RGB-D images.
- Integrates a motion plan generation module utilizing adapted whole-body trajectory optimization (==SeqIK==) and a robust execution module with ==contact-based correction== for 'last-centimeter' grasping.

## Results

- MOSART achieved a **61%** success rate in end-to-end opening of 31 novel cabinets and drawers across 13 previously unseen real-world environments.
- MOSART substantially outperformed a state-of-the-art end-to-end imitation learning system (RUM), achieving **13/20** successes compared to RUM's **6/20** in paired evaluations on novel environments.
- Perception errors were the largest source of failure (**59%**), and a contact-based correction strategy improved grasp success from **35% to 61%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modular systems, like MOSART, can outperform state-of-the-art end-to-end imitation learning methods in generalizing to novel real-world mobile manipulation tasks.
> - The primary bottleneck for end-to-end mobile manipulation in unstructured environments is robust perception, accounting for 59% of system failures, rather than precise control.
> - Articulation parameter estimation models developed in isolation often struggle with noisy, robot-centric viewpoints encountered in real-world deployment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of several critical areas within robotics and artificial intelligence, most notably mobile manipulation, generalization in robotics, and the ongoing debate between modular and end-to-end learning approaches.

**Mobile Manipulation:** The ability of robots to move freely and interact with objects in unstructured human environments remains a grand challenge. Current systems often struggle with tasks that require robust perception, complex motion planning, and precise interaction, especially when faced with novel objects or environments. This paper directly contributes to advancing the state-of-the-art in this domain by tackling the ubiquitous task of opening articulated structures.

**Generalization in Robotics:** A significant hurdle for practical robot deployment is their inability to generalize from seen training data to unseen objects, variations, or environments. While many research efforts focus on improving individual components (e.g., object detection, grasp planning), this paper argues that true generalization requires a holistic, system-level approach that integrates these components and evaluates them in diverse, real-world settings. The paper's "zero-shot" approach to novel objects in novel environments directly addresses this generalization gap.

**Modular vs. End-to-End Learning:** This paper makes a profound contribution to a fundamental debate in modern AI and robotics: whether to build systems with distinct, specialized modules (e.g., separate perception, planning, control) or to train a single, monolithic end-to-end learned model. Recent years have seen a surge in end-to-end learning, especially with large language models and vision-language models, often driven by the "Bitter Lesson" argument—that general-purpose methods leveraging massive data and compute tend to outperform hand-engineered systems. This paper directly challenges this notion in the context of mobile manipulation, presenting strong empirical evidence that a well-designed modular system (MOSART) can significantly outperform state-of-the-art end-to-end imitation learning methods (like RUM) trained on extensive datasets. This finding suggests that for tasks with complex, multi-stage physical interactions, explicit structure and modularity might still offer superior generalization and robustness, especially when real-world training data is inherently limited compared to data in domains like language or vision.

**Perception for Articulated Objects:** The work also engages with the sub-field of articulation parameter estimation. While significant progress has been made on models for predicting articulation parameters from images in isolation, this paper highlights a critical gap: these models often fail when faced with the noisy, robot-centric viewpoints of real-world deployment. This emphasizes the need for perception research to consider the full system context.

By performing extensive real-world testing and a direct comparison with contemporary end-to-end learning methods, this work shifts the focus from theoretical advancements in isolated components to pragmatic, system-level performance and bottlenecks, offering crucial insights for the future direction of generalizable mobile manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to advance the field of mobile manipulation by developing systems capable of **robust generalization**—specifically, the ability to competently operate on previously unseen objects in previously unseen environments. To rigorously test this capability, the authors choose the task of "end-to-end opening of articulated structures" (such as cabinets, drawers, and ovens) as their mobile manipulation testbed.

The key motivations driving this work are:

- **Addressing the Generalization Gap:** Existing mobile manipulation systems frequently struggle to adapt to novel objects and unfamiliar settings, limiting their utility in diverse real-world scenarios. The authors aim to overcome this fundamental challenge.
- **Emphasizing System-Level Research:** Much of the robotics research focuses on improving individual subproblems (e.g., perception, motion planning, control) in isolation. The authors argue that this approach can obscure the true bottlenecks in practical deployment. By focusing on building and studying a cohesive, end-to-end system, they seek to uncover hidden challenges and prioritize overall task success over marginal gains in isolated components.
- **Empirically Testing Modular vs. End-to-End Learning:** There is an ongoing and critical debate in robotics about the efficacy of modular systems versus end-to-end learned policies. The authors aim to provide concrete, large-scale empirical evidence from real-world experiments to inform this discussion, especially regarding generalization capabilities.
- **Identifying Real-World Bottlenecks:** By conducting extensive tests in diverse, previously unseen real-world environments, the authors intend to identify the primary failure modes and practical limitations of current mobile manipulation pipelines, thereby providing a "pragmatic roadmap" for future research and development.
- **Zero-Shot Performance:** The ambition is to achieve successful manipulation without any privileged information (e.g., ground truth articulation parameters, object models) and without requiring specific training or adaptation for each new object or environment. The robot starts with the object in view and must navigate, perceive, grasp, and open it.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose **MOSART (MOdular System for opening ARTiculated structures)**, a modular mobile manipulation system designed for zero-shot generalization. The system integrates state-of-the-art components for perception, planning, and adaptation, deployed on a Stretch RE2 robot, a commodity mobile manipulator.

The MOSART pipeline consists of three main modules:

1. **Articulation-parameter Prediction Module (APM):**
 - **Input:** RGB-D images captured by the robot's on-board camera.
 - **2D Prediction:** A modified Mask R-CNN model, trained on the ArtObjSim dataset (which contains 3500+ articulated objects in simulated scenes), is used to predict 2D quantities from RGB images. These include a 2D segmentation mask, articulation type (drawer, left-hinged cabinet, right-hinged cabinet), 2D handle pixel location, and handle orientation (horizontal/vertical).
 - **3D Lifting:** The 2D predictions are then lifted to 3D using the corresponding depth image. This involves fitting a plane to 3D points within the segmentation mask to estimate the surface normal, and looking up the 3D coordinate for the predicted 2D handle location. For cabinets, the rotation axis and radius are inferred by lifting the simplified 2D convex hull of the segmentation mask to 3D.
 - **Alternatives Explored:** The authors also experimented with using Detic, an open-vocabulary object detector, to replace Mask R-CNN for 2D predictions, demonstrating the modularity's benefit.

2. **Motion Plan Generation:**
 - **Waypoint Computation:** Based on the predicted articulation parameters (handle location, surface normal, radius), the module computes a sequence of end-effector waypoints. For drawers, this is a linear trajectory; for cabinets, it's a quarter-circle path.
 - **Whole-Body Planning (SeqIK):** The system leverages and extends SeqIK, a trajectory optimization framework. SeqIK takes an initial robot configuration (base position and arm joint angles) and a desired end-effector pose trajectory, then sequentially performs inverse kinematics calls to generate a whole-body motion plan (desired joint angle trajectory).
 - **Adaptation for Stretch RE2:** The original SeqIK was for a Franka Panda robot; here, it's adapted for the Stretch RE2's fewer degrees of freedom and extended to produce whole-body plans (allowing base rotation in addition to arm joints for full articulation).
 - **Navigation Targets:** Initial base positions for planning are determined by MPAO, a data-driven method that identifies optimal navigation targets for different articulation types.

3. **Execution Module:**
 - **Pre-Grasp Execution:** The robot navigates to the target location and executes the first robot configuration from the generated motion plan to achieve a pre-grasp pose.
 - **Contact-Based Correction:** To mitigate "last-centimeter" errors in grasping due to inaccuracies in state estimation, navigation, or calibration, a crucial adaptation strategy is employed. The gripper incrementally extends towards the object surface until physical contact is detected using arm and gripper effort signals. This refines the pre-grasp pose and updates the desired trajectory. For vertical handles, an additional vertical primitive is used before contact correction.
 - **Full Execution:** Once a firm grasp on the handle is secured, the robot proceeds to execute the remainder of the precomputed motion plan to open the articulated object.

**Evaluation Protocol:**
The most notable aspect of the methodology is the **large-scale real-world evaluation**.
- **Test Environment:** 13 distinct test sites across 10 different buildings (offices, classrooms, apartments, kitchenettes, lounges) were used.
- **Test Objects:** 31 distinct articulated objects (cabinets, drawers) were used, none of which were seen during system development.
- **Trials:** Over 100 end-to-end system tests were conducted. Each trial started with the robot placed so the object was in view. A trial was deemed successful if a drawer opened >24cm or a cabinet opened >60 degrees.
- **Comparative Analysis:** MOSART was extensively compared against:
 - **Robot Utility Models (RUM):** A state-of-the-art, large-scale end-to-end imitation learning system trained on over 1000 demonstrations. The authors adapted RUM to their zero-shot setting by providing articulation type and handle height from APM and using MOSART's navigation.
 - **Sim2Real Behavior Cloning:** Training an open-loop policy in simulation and attempting transfer.
 - **Real-World Behavior Cloning:** Training a closed-loop policy on limited real-world tele-operated demonstrations.
 - **Other Articulation Parameter Estimation Models:** Quantitative and qualitative comparisons with AO-Grasp and OPDMulti.
- **Ablation Studies:** To understand module contributions, particularly for the contact correction mechanism.
- **Robustness Testing:** Quantifying the system's tolerance to perception errors (e.g., perturbing radius estimates).
- **Generalization to Diverse Handles & Types:** Testing on different handle geometries and extending to new articulation types (toaster ovens).
- **Failure Mode Analysis:** Detailed categorization of observed failures to pinpoint bottlenecks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive real-world evaluation of MOSART yielded several surprising and critical insights into building generalizable mobile manipulation systems:

- **Overall End-to-End Success:** MOSART achieved a **61% success rate** across 31 novel cabinets and drawers in 13 previously unseen real-world environments. Most successful trials involved complete opening (drawers by 35cm, cupboards by 90 degrees).

- **Modular Systems Outperform End-to-End Learning for Generalization:** This is a major finding. In direct comparison, MOSART (modular) substantially **outperformed RUM** (a state-of-the-art imitation learning method trained on 1000+ demonstrations) with a success rate of **13/20 versus RUM's 6/20** on a paired evaluation in novel environments. MOSART was also faster. Furthermore, Sim2Real behavior cloning and even real-world behavior cloning policies (trained on specific objects) achieved 0% success on unseen objects, demonstrating their complete lack of generalization. This directly challenges the broad applicability of end-to-end learning and the "Bitter Lesson" in robotics, suggesting that data scale alone, at current collection levels, may not be sufficient for generalization in manipulation tasks.

- **Perception is the Primary Bottleneck, Not Precise Control:** Surprisingly, the most significant hurdle to task success was identified as robust perception, not the precise control of the end-effector. **59% of all system failures were attributed to perception errors.** These included failures to detect objects (e.g., meshed cabinets), incorrect handle orientation predictions, poor radius predictions, and mistaking keyholes for handles. This finding contradicts the common focus in prior work on control precision.

- **State-of-the-Art Perception Models Struggle on Robot Viewpoints:** Articulation parameter prediction models developed in isolation (e.g., OPDMulti, AO-Grasp) performed poorly when evaluated on noisy images from the robot's perspective in real-world, in-the-wild scenarios. In contrast, MOSART's APM, specifically developed to handle robot-centric viewpoints, achieved substantially better handle prediction accuracy (mean handle error of 2.11cm for APM vs. 23.36cm for AO-Grasp) and more accurate segmentation masks.

- **Modular Systems Generalize and are Extendable:** MOSART demonstrated strong generalization to diverse handle types (18/18 success on 6 novel handles across 3 objects). Additionally, the modular design facilitated easy extension to a new articulation type: horizontal-hinged toaster ovens. By swapping the Mask R-CNN with Detic-based APM (which could better detect rarer objects), MOSART achieved an 80% success rate on novel toaster ovens. This flexibility contrasts with imitation learning, which would require extensive new data collection for each new articulation type.

- **Execution is Robust to Articulation Parameter Errors (Once Grasped):** While precise perception for grasping was critical, the execution of the opening motion was surprisingly robust. Even with a 10cm error in the estimated radius, the robot could still articulate the cabinet significantly (e.g., 75 degrees open with 10cm overestimation, 55 degrees with 10cm underestimation), provided a firm initial grasp was achieved. This suggests that achieving a reliable grasp is more crucial than millimeter-level accuracy in all articulation parameters.

- **Last-Centimeter Grasping Remains Challenging:** Despite the robustness of the opening motion, securing the initial firm grasp proved challenging, accounting for **25% of all failures**. While the contact-based correction strategy significantly improved the success rate from 35% to 61%, it did not entirely eliminate these "last-centimeter" grasping errors.

- **Navigation Failures:** A smaller portion of failures (15%) was attributed to navigation errors, particularly on challenging terrains like thick carpets, which prevented the robot from reaching the precise pre-grasp pose.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers profound implications and a pragmatic roadmap for the development of generalizable mobile manipulation systems, challenging some prevailing assumptions in the field.

- **Re-evaluating Modular vs. End-to-End Learning:** The most significant impact is on the debate surrounding modular versus end-to-end learning in robotics. By demonstrating that a modular system like MOSART can significantly outperform large-scale end-to-end imitation learning (RUM, trained on over 1000 demonstrations) for real-world generalization, the paper provides compelling empirical counter-evidence to the idea that "scaling data" and end-to-end approaches will universally prevail (the "Bitter Lesson"). It suggests that, at least for complex physical interaction tasks where real-world data collection is inherently difficult to scale to truly massive levels, modularity can provide crucial structure, interpretability, and adaptability that current end-to-end methods lack. The authors propose that modularity can even be "pro-scaling," allowing individual modules (like perception) to leverage vast, pre-trained foundation models.

- **Redirection of Research Focus:** The finding that perception, not control, is the primary bottleneck fundamentally redirects attention for future research. Instead of primarily focusing on increasingly precise motion control or complex trajectory optimization, the paper emphasizes the urgent need for more robust, robot-centric perception modules that can accurately interpret diverse real-world objects and their properties from imperfect, noisy sensor data. This calls for new datasets that better capture the variability of real-world environments beyond controlled lab settings or idealized simulations.

- **Advocacy for System-Level Evaluation:** The comprehensive real-world testing highlights the critical importance of evaluating robotic systems end-to-end, rather than focusing on isolated components. Such system-level deployments expose "hidden challenges" and true bottlenecks that might be overlooked in theoretical analyses or simulation-only studies. This rigorous evaluation methodology sets a high bar for future robotics research aimed at real-world applicability.

- **Practical Guidance for Practitioners:** For researchers and engineers building real-world robotic systems, the paper offers concrete takeaways:
 - Prioritize robust perception.
 - Modular designs facilitate easier adaptation to new tasks and benefit directly from advancements in foundation models without costly retraining.
 - Simple, robust adaptation mechanisms like contact correction can significantly improve performance in the face of residual errors.
 - Last-centimeter grasping remains a persistent challenge requiring further investigation, possibly through closed-loop visual servoing or more sophisticated force feedback.

- **Accessibility of Mobile Manipulation:** By demonstrating complex manipulation capabilities on a commodity mobile manipulator (Stretch RE2), the work contributes to making advanced robotics more accessible and potentially applicable in a wider range of commercial and research settings, moving beyond highly specialized and expensive hardware.

In conclusion, "Opening Articulated Structures in the Real World" is a pivotal paper that provides a reality check on the current state of mobile manipulation. It not only delivers a highly capable system for a complex real-world task but also profoundly influences the ongoing methodological debate in robotics, making a strong case for the continued relevance and effectiveness of well-designed modular architectures for achieving real-world generalization.
