# Sim2real Image Translation Enables Viewpoint-Robust Policies from Fixed-Camera Datasets

> [!summary] Summary
> MANGO is an unpaired sim2real image translation method that turns randomized-viewpoint simulated demonstrations into realistic images for viewpoint-robust manipulation policies. It combines a segmentation-conditioned SegNCE loss, a modified PatchNCE scoring function that damps false negatives, and a patch-sampling plus per-patch-rotation regularized discriminator. Trained on only fixed-camera real data, it raises shifted-view success by over 40 points on some tabletop tasks.

## Problem

- Tabletop manipulation datasets use fixed third-person cameras, so imitation policies collapse in success rate when the deployment camera viewpoint shifts.
- Off-the-shelf unpaired translators like CycleGAN and CUT assume diverse target data; robot scenes are near-identical, so discriminators memorize background and collapse viewpoints.
- Diffusion novel-view augmentation is prohibitively costly, roughly 435 GPU-hours for one 180,000-image single-task dataset, dwarfing the few hours needed to train the policy.

## Method

- ==SegNCE loss==: an ==InfoNCE== variant over generator features using simulator ground-truth segmentation, with a uniform multi-positive target that clusters same-class features and preserves object boundaries.
- Modified ==PatchNCE== scoring: negatives whose cosine similarity exceeds threshold 0.9 get their score scaled by 0.5, suppressing false negatives from repeated textures more effectively than raising temperature.
- Highly-regularized ==PatchGAN== discriminator: randomly located image patches with per-patch random rotations, preventing memorization of static background and enabling style transfer onto unseen viewpoints.

## Results

- MANGO reaches the best randomized-view FID of **160.9**, beating CycleGAN at **359.9**, CUT at **373.9**, and its own ablations without SegNCE at **207.5** or without modified scoring at **184.1**.
- In sim2sim, MANGO-trained behavior-cloning policies win **5** of **6** tasks, for example Hammer **86.0%** versus VISTA **56.0%** and fixed-camera-only **18.0%** success.
- On real shifted cameras, stack cups rises from **1/30** to **13/30** and pick up coke from **5/30** to **17/30**, using **35M** parameters and under **0.2%** of VISTA's GPU-hours.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Contrastive translation losses assume diverse target domains; when target images are nearly identical, discriminator regularization and false-negative damping matter more than architecture choice.
> - Cheap low-fidelity simulation with ground-truth segmentation is enough supervision signal to preserve geometry, so visual realism engineering can be delegated to the translator.
> - Lightweight GANs can trade some quality for tractability: pretrained diffusion priors still generalize better on 3 of 4 real tasks despite thousands-fold higher compute.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-based policies for robot manipulation have demonstrated capabilities in recent years, particularly through imitation learning methods. The development of single-task architectures and teleoperation techniques has facilitated the creation of large robot datasets and multi-task Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. However, robot demonstration datasets are often characterized by scarcity, labor-intensive collection, and limited diversity when compared to web-scraped vision-language datasets. A significant limitation is the frequent reliance on fixed, third-person cameras for observation during data collection, which can lead to policies that are brittle to variations in camera viewpoint during deployment.

Addressing the "sim2real gap" is a long-standing challenge in robotics. Traditional approaches include enhancing simulator realism, which is resource-intensive, or employing domain randomization, which can sometimes trade performance for robustness. Image-to-image translation methods have emerged as an alternative, directly learning mappings between simulated and real visual domains. Prior work in this area includes using general-purpose unpaired image translation models like CycleGAN, or tailoring these methods with task-specific losses (e.g., action cycle-consistency, Q-function consistency, object detector consistency) or auxiliary objectives (e.g., reproducing segmentation). This paper distinguishes itself by proposing an image translation method that is agnostic to the downstream learning algorithm, aiming for broader applicability.

The issue of viewpoint invariance in robot policies is also an active area of research. Previous efforts have explored training policies on multi-view data, developing viewpoint-invariant visual encoders, adapting image encoders at test-time, leveraging 3D representations, or utilizing pre-trained models with 3D priors for novel view generation. However, some of these methods require extensive data, calibrated multi-camera setups, or incur significant computational costs, particularly those based on diffusion models for novel view synthesis. This work positions itself as an efficient, GAN-based image translation approach specifically designed to generate diverse, unseen real-world viewpoints for robot policy augmentation from limited fixed-camera real data, thereby addressing both the sim2real gap and viewpoint brittleness without requiring complex 3D representations or high computational resources at the augmentation stage.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from a observed limitation in current vision-based robot manipulation policies: their brittleness to distribution shifts, particularly variations in camera viewpoint. Robot demonstration data, while crucial for imitation learning, is typically scarce and often collected using fixed-position cameras. This practice, while convenient (e.g., for consistent visual observations or avoiding repeated calibration), results in datasets that lack the viewpoint diversity necessary for policies to generalize robustly to new camera positions during deployment. Experimental observations indicated that policies trained on fixed-camera datasets exhibit significantly reduced success rates when the camera viewpoint is shifted. The challenge is complex because a change in viewpoint alters the entire scene, requiring the model to implicitly infer the robot's pose relative to the new camera.

To mitigate this data scarcity and diversity problem, the authors propose leveraging simulation to generate robot demonstrations from a wide array of camera viewpoints. This approach offers a scalable solution to collect varied data. However, directly using simulated observations for training real-world policies introduces the visual sim2real gap. Standard image translation methods, when trained on a real-world dataset collected from a single fixed camera, tend to collapse and generate images consistent only with that fixed viewpoint, failing to preserve the diverse viewpoints present in the simulated input.

Another key objective was to develop a computationally efficient solution. While diffusion models have shown promise in image generation and novel viewpoint synthesis, their high computational requirements (e.g., hundreds of GPU-hours for dataset augmentation) make them impractical for augmenting large robot demonstration datasets, which can comprise hundreds of thousands of images. The authors specifically argue for a GAN-based model due to its speed and competitive performance for unpaired image-to-image translation, particularly when the generated image's style and content are tightly constrained by the input and target domain.

The core objectives of the MANGO (Multiview Augmentation with Novel Generated Observations) project are therefore:
1. **Develop a sim2real image translation method:** Create an unpaired image translation model capable of bridging the visual gap between a simple, low-fidelity simulation and the real world.
2. **Preserve viewpoint consistency:** Ensure the translation method can accurately translate simulated observations from diverse, unseen viewpoints into realistic real-world observations, even when trained on a real dataset from a single fixed camera.
3. **Enhance policy robustness:** Demonstrate that policies trained with MANGO-generated synthetic data exhibit increased robustness and higher success rates when deployed with shifted camera viewpoints.
4. **Ensure computational efficiency:** Design a method that is significantly faster than diffusion-based alternatives, making it practical for augmenting large robot demonstration datasets.
5. **Identify crucial architectural components:** Analyze the specific elements (e.g., novel loss functions, discriminator design) that are essential for successful viewpoint preservation during sim2real translation in the context of robotic datasets.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed approach, MANGO (Multiview Augmentation with Novel Generated Observations), is an unpaired image-to-image translation method designed to translate visual observations from a simulated domain (domain A) to a real-world domain (domain B). A key characteristic is that domain A (simulation) provides diverse camera viewpoints and ground-truth segmentations, while domain B (real-world) consists of a small dataset collected from a single, fixed camera. The method aims to generate realistic real-world images from diverse simulated viewpoints, even those not present in the real training data.

MANGO employs a Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) architecture, comprising a generator (G) and a discriminator (D), alongside novel loss formulations and regularization techniques.

**A. Style Loss (GAN Objective and Discriminator Regularization):**
The standard GAN loss (Equation 1) is utilized to enforce the target domain (real-world) style on the generated images. A critical aspect of MANGO's design is the highly-regularized discriminator. Standard discriminators in image translation GANs can overfit to specific, repetitive details common in fixed-camera robot datasets (e.g., background, tabletop). To counter this, MANGO employs a "PatchGAN" approach where the discriminator processes only local image patches. This is further enhanced by randomly sampling patch locations and applying per-patch random rotations. This regularization strategy aims to prevent the discriminator from memorizing global image elements, thereby enabling it to enforce the real-world style on images with diverse viewpoints not present in the real training dataset.

**B. Content Loss (Modified PatchNCE and Segmentation NCE Loss):**
To ensure accurate content preservation during translation, MANGO incorporates two distinct content losses:

1. **Modified PatchNCE Loss:** This loss is adapted from the InfoNCE loss used in methods like CUT. It encourages features from the same spatial location in the input simulated image and its translated real image to be similar.
 - **Original Challenge:** In robot datasets, numerous background elements or textures (e.g., tabletop) can appear highly similar across different image patches, especially in simplified simulated environments where these regions have identical pixel values. The standard InfoNCE loss (Equation 2), which treats all non-corresponding features as "negative" samples to be repelled, can inadvertently penalize these naturally similar patches as "false negatives."
 - **MANGO's Modification:** To mitigate this, a modified scoring function (Equation 5) is introduced. If the cosine similarity between a query feature and a "negative" sample feature exceeds a threshold (θ), its contribution to the loss is reduced by multiplying the score by a factor (α < 1). This adjustment makes the negative sampling more robust to inherent similarities in content within robot scenes. The authors empirically set α=0.5 and θ=0.9.

2. **Novel Segmentation NCE (SegNCE) Loss:** This is a new InfoNCE-based loss that leverages ground-truth segmentation maps available from the simulator (Domain A). It aims to preserve object boundaries and semantic consistency during translation, which is crucial for object-centric manipulation tasks.
 - **Mechanism:** For each feature generated from an input simulated image, the SegNCE loss (Equation 6) considers all features belonging to the same segmentation class as positive samples. This contrasts with the standard InfoNCE which typically has only one positive sample. The loss encourages features within the same semantic region to be similar, thereby ensuring that objects and their boundaries are maintained across the sim2real translation. This loss is computed only from input image generator features.

**C. Model Architecture and Training:**
- The generator G is a ResNet-based network with approximately 12 million parameters.
- The discriminator D is a three-layer Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) with approximately 11 million parameters.
- The projection heads H_l (used in the InfoNCE scoring function) are parameterized as two-layer MLPs with 700,000 parameters.
- The total generator loss (Equation 8) combines the modified PatchNCE loss (applied for sim-to-real translation and as an identity regularization for real-to-real), the novel SegNCE loss, and the GAN objective.
- The discriminator loss (Equation 9) is the GAN objective.
- For experiments, a "pick up coke" task dataset was used, comprising 8,098 simulated images with randomized camera viewpoints (D_A) and 3,094 real-world images from a fixed camera (D_B). Images were cropped and scaled to 256x256 pixels. Training typically required around 20 hours on an RTX 2080 Ti GPU.
- Evaluation involved three test datasets: Fixed View (sim/real pairs from D_B's viewpoint), Randomized View (sim/real pairs from diverse unseen viewpoints), and Wrist View (sim/real pairs from a wrist-mounted camera). Paired real images for evaluation were obtained using robot state and AprilTags for pose estimation, which are not required during MANGO's deployment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings from image translation benchmarks, simulated robot tasks, and real-world robot manipulation experiments.

**A. Image Translation with MANGO:**
- **Quantitative Performance (FID Scores):** MANGO achieved the lowest Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) score of 160.9 on the Randomized Camera testset (Table I), which specifically evaluates generalization to unseen viewpoints. This outperformed several baselines, including CUT (373.9), CycleGAN (359.9), and ablations of MANGO itself. For instance, removing the SegNCE loss increased FID to 207.5, and removing the modified scoring function for PatchNCE (˜ρl(·)) resulted in 184.1, indicating the contribution of these components. The highly-regularized patch discriminator was observed to have the largest impact on FID scores across all test sets.
- **Qualitative Observations:** Translated image examples (Figure 5) illustrate the visual quality and viewpoint preservation.
- **Dataset Diversity Analysis:** The authors propose that off-the-shelf image translation methods (like CUT, CycleGAN) struggle with robot datasets due to their limited diversity compared to general computer vision datasets. An analysis of average pairwise LPIPS (learned perceptual image patch similarity) (Table III) showed that the real-world "pick up coke" dataset had the lowest LPIPS score (0.155) among tested datasets, including common computer vision benchmarks and other image-to-image translation datasets, supporting the claim of lower diversity in robotics datasets.

**B. Simulation Experiments (Sim2Sim):**
- MANGO was evaluated on six simulated manipulation tasks from Robomimic and Mimicgen, using two visually distinct simulated environments (sim2sim setup). Behavioral cloning (BC) policies were trained with data augmented by MANGO and compared against baselines.
- MANGO-trained BC policies achieved the highest success rates on 5 out of 6 tasks (Table II), demonstrating its effectiveness in generating useful training data for viewpoint-robust policies. For example, in the "Hammer" task, MANGO achieved an 86% success rate compared to 56% for VISTA and 18% for policies trained with fixed-camera data only.
- This performance was achieved with competitive FID scores, often lower than other augmentation methods, indicating good visual fidelity.

**C. Real Robot Experiments:**
- MANGO was tested on four real-world robotic manipulation tasks: "pick up coke", "stack cups", "close laptop", and "stack blocks." A single MANGO model was trained for all tasks using 59,520 simulated observations and 35,294 fixed-camera real observations.
- **Policy Performance (Success Rates):** Policies were trained using Action Chunking Transformer (ACT) and evaluated on a fixed camera viewpoint and three shifted camera viewpoints.
 - MANGO-augmented policies significantly improved viewpoint robustness (Table IV). For instance, in the "stack cups" task, MANGO-augmented policies achieved 13/30 successes on shifted cameras, compared to 1/30 for policies trained without augmentation. In "stack blocks," MANGO achieved 11/30, whereas no augmentation yielded 1/30.
 - Policies trained with untranslated simulated data ("Sim" column) generally performed worse than MANGO, particularly on shifted cameras, underscoring the importance of the sim2real translation.
 - Domain randomization ("Sim DR") also showed improvements over no augmentation but was generally outperformed by MANGO on shifted views.
 - **Comparison with VISTA:** VISTA, a diffusion-based method utilizing a 4.5 billion parameter pretrained model, achieved competitive or slightly better performance on shifted cameras for 3 out of 4 tasks (e.g., 29/30 for "close laptop" vs. MANGO's 22/30). However, the paper emphasizes MANGO's computational efficiency: MANGO, including data generation and image translation, required less than 0.2% of the GPU-hours required by VISTA, making it more practical for large-scale robot dataset augmentation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents MANGO, a sim2real image translation method that addresses a critical challenge in robot manipulation: the brittleness of vision-based policies to camera viewpoint variations due to limited diversity in real-world training data. The primary significance of this work lies in its ability to generate diverse, viewpoint-robust training data for robot policies using only a small dataset of fixed-camera real-world observations and a simple simulator.

The potential impacts are substantial:
- **Enhanced Robot Policy Robustness:** By augmenting training data with MANGO-generated observations, robot manipulation policies demonstrate increased success rates when deployed with camera viewpoints shifted from the training distribution. This directly contributes to more reliable and generalizable robotic systems, reducing their sensitivity to environmental variations.
- **Reduced Data Collection Burden:** MANGO significantly lowers the barrier to creating diverse robot datasets. Instead of labor-intensive and expensive real-world data collection across numerous viewpoints, researchers and practitioners can leverage easily generated simulated data and a small, fixed-camera real dataset, streamlining the development process for new tasks and environments.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The GAN-based approach of MANGO, combined with its novel loss formulations, offers a computationally efficient alternative to resource-intensive diffusion models for data augmentation. This makes it practical for scaling up robot learning with datasets containing hundreds of thousands of images, where other methods might become a bottleneck.
- **Advancements in Sim2Real Transfer:** The introduction of the segmentation-conditioned InfoNCE loss (SegNCE) and the modified PatchNCE loss specifically address challenges inherent in sim2real translation for robot manipulation – namely, preserving content and object boundaries in low-diversity scenes with repetitive backgrounds. These contributions provide insights into designing image translation models tailored for robotics applications.
- **Broader Applicability:** MANGO's design, being agnostic to the downstream learning algorithm, suggests its potential for use across various imitation learning frameworks without requiring task-specific modifications to the translation model itself.

While limitations exist, such as the continued requirement for some real-world data and competitive performance with larger pretrained models in some cases, MANGO offers a practical and effective solution for enabling viewpoint-robust robot policies, thereby advancing the field of robot learning towards more adaptable and deployable autonomous systems. The identified crucial architectural components also suggest promising directions for integrating these specialized loss formulations into larger generative models.
