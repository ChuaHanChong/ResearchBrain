# TacDyn-WAM: Learning Implicit Tactile Dynamics in a Heterogeneous Visuo-Tactile World Action Model

> [!summary] Summary
> TacDyn-WAM is a heterogeneous visuo-tactile world action model that predicts implicit tactile dynamics instead of reconstructing future tactile images. A dedicated tactile expert forecasts future representations and their changes at multiple horizons in TacRep, a dynamics-aware tactile space, in one forward pass, while a read-only tactile memory supplies the current contact state. It reaches 81.5% on UniVTAC without large-scale pretraining.

## Problem

- Vision-tactile-language-action models encode the current tactile state but never predict how contact will evolve, leaving policies purely reactive to tactile feedback during contact-rich manipulation.
- Existing tactile world action models reuse video-generation pipelines that iteratively denoise future tactile VAE latents, slowing inference and tying forecasts to pixel patterns that become unstable under deployment drift.
- Latent alternatives that use frozen DINOv2 features were trained on static images and predict only one net change at a single horizon, missing frame-to-frame contact evolution and intermediate slips.

## Method

- **TacRep**: a tactile encoder initialized from ==V-JEPA 2.1== and trained by ==masked spatio-temporal prediction== on four-frame tactile clips, plus ==relational structure distillation== matching DINOv2 local patch cosine relations.
- **Implicit Tactile Dynamics Expert**: a 0.4B fourth transformer expert whose learnable future queries predict TacRep future representations and deltas at 5, 15 and 25 frames in one pass, with ==patch-adaptive loss reweighting==.
- **Tactile Understanding Memory** and staged training: AnyTouch2 tokens are compressed into read-only keys and values inside ==joint attention==, and four stages progressively unlock attention between tactile modules and the pretrained InternVLA-A1 experts.

## Results

- On UniVTAC it averages **81.5%** from per-task demonstrations alone, **25.7 points** above base InternVLA-A1 and only **1.6 to 3.0 points** behind N0 models pretrained on **30,000+ hours**.
- In ablations, a Cosmos VAE target space drops to **62.0%**, below the **67.9%** no-tactile-prediction variant, and DINOv2 reaches **74.0%**. Removing the tactile world model or memory costs **13.6 and 10.2 points**.
- On five real-robot tasks it reaches **71.0%**, versus **55.0%** for FTP-1 and **40.0%** for InternVLA-A1, and **85.0%** after pretraining on 6,000 trajectories. Inference takes **9.7 ms** per action versus **33.5 ms** for LingBot-VA.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The prediction target space matters more than simply adding prediction: forecasting tactile pixels through a reconstruction space can perform worse than having no tactile world model.
> - For drift-sensitive sensors, predict dynamics in a representation trained for temporal predictability rather than reconstructing observations; contact trends stay forecastable even when the sensor pixels shift.
> - Predicting both future state and change across several horizons captures intermediate contact events, and modality-specific experts outperform a shared VAE pipeline while keeping single-pass inference fast.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

TacDyn-WAM changes what a tactile world model is asked to predict. In insertion, grasping, and other contact-rich tasks, camera views may not reveal local geometry or force. Small contact changes can also substantially alter tactile pixels. The authors argue that the underlying contact trend may remain more predictable than those pixels. Their model therefore does not iteratively generate future touch images. Instead, it forecasts how contact evolves in a learned representation, and a separate tactile memory supplies the current contact state. On eight UniVTAC simulation tasks, TacDyn-WAM's mean success rate is **81.5%**, versus **55.8%** for its InternVLA-A1 base model.

### 2. Forecasting contact instead of pixels

The forecast lives in TacRep, a tactile target space learned from four-frame clips. During masked spatio-temporal prediction, the masked blocks span the clip. This encourages features to represent how the imprint moves, rather than only its static appearance. A frozen DINOv2 teacher supplies a spatial prior. TacRep matches local patch-to-patch relations rather than DINOv2's feature values.

A tactile dynamics expert encodes the tactile history as context. At 5, 15, and 25 frames, it predicts both a future representation and how that representation differs from the present. These predictions are made in one pass, without iterative denoising. The action expert can read the predicted futures. Separately, a read-only memory summarizes the current tactile state, so the policy has the current contact as well as the predicted contact.

TacRep context tokens and future-token queries feed a 0.4B Dynamics Predictor. Its future-token outputs feed future-representation and tactile-delta readouts.

### 3. What the comparisons show

In the headline benchmark, each of the eight tasks uses a separate policy and 100 randomized rollouts. InternVLA-A1 was re-evaluated under the same protocol. Most other baseline results in the table are published results. TacDyn-WAM is competitive, but it is not the top scorer. The paper reports **83.1%** for N₀-VTLA and **84.5%** for N₀-TWAM, both pretrained on large-scale visuo-tactile data. TacDyn-WAM's score comes from demonstrations only.

The paper's own ablation provides the controlled test of the representation. All variants share the base model, data, and per-stage hyperparameters. The full model averages 81.5%. Replacing TacRep with frozen DINOv2 gives 74.0%, and replacing it with the Cosmos VAE gives 62.0%. Removing the tactile world model gives 67.9%, and removing the tactile memory gives 71.3%. This supports TacRep's value within this pipeline and suggests that both pathways contribute. It does not prove that temporal sensitivity alone explains the gap. The gains also vary by task. On Insert HDMI, the full model scores 12% and the Cosmos variant scores 15%. The authors report little tactile variation in that task's demonstrations.

Success rates (%) for the full model, target-space substitutions (DINOv2, Cosmos), and pathway removals across eight tasks, with the average at right. The full model leads most columns, but not Insert HDMI (Cosmos 15%, full 12%).

### 4. Forecast the contact

The established contribution is a design in which the policy reads a learned forecast of how contact is changing, alongside a summary of the contact already made. In the tested settings, this design improves on its base model, and its components hold up under ablation. The main boundary is that TacRep and the tactile dynamics expert are trained for one sensor type. Cross-sensor transfer is left for future work.
