# Latent Chain-of-Thought for Visual Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> A new framework for Large Vision-Language Models, Latent Chain-of-Thought (LaCoT), reformulates visual reasoning as a posterior inference problem, using Generative Flow Networks for efficient training and Bayesian inference for optimal answer selection. The LaCoT-Qwen-7B model demonstrated a 6.6% improvement over its base model and surpassed existing methods by 10.6% on various visual reasoning benchmarks.

## Problem

- Current Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) using Chain-of-Thought (CoT) often struggle with generalization across diverse visual reasoning tasks and tend to produce limited, deterministic rationales.
- Existing reinforcement learning approaches for CoT generation depend on external, potentially biased reward models, leading to issues like reward hacking and constrained exploration.
- Traditional inference-time scaling methods (e.g., Best-of-N, Beam Search) for CoT are computationally expensive for long multimodal sequences and also rely on potentially biased critic models.

## Method

- ==LaCoT== reformulates CoT reasoning as a posterior inference problem over latent variables, leveraging ==Amortized Variational Inference (AVI)== with ==Generative Flow Networks (GFlowNets)==.
- A ==Reference-Guided GFlowNet Fine-tuning (RGFN)== algorithm is introduced for efficient training, utilizing a token-level marginal reward approximation and guiding exploration with reference rationales to improve efficiency and quality.
- A principled ==Bayesian Inference over N latent rationales (BiN)== strategy is proposed for optimal rationale and answer selection at inference time, which estimates marginal likelihood without an explicit critic model.

## Results

- The LaCoT-Qwen-7B model achieved state-of-the-art performance among open-source LVLMs, outperforming its base Qwen2.5-VL-7B by **6.6%** and GRPO by **10.6%** across seven visual reasoning benchmarks.
- LaCoT models generate rationales with higher diversity and log-likelihood compared to SFT baselines, contributing to better posterior coverage and a higher probability of sampling correct answers.
- The Bayesian Inference (BiN) method consistently outperformed Best-of-N (BoN) for optimal rationale and answer selection, demonstrating improved accuracy, generalizability, and a notable ability to mitigate hallucination issues with increased rationale candidates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Treating CoT reasoning as a probabilistic inference problem over latent variables allows for capturing uncertainty and diverse reasoning paths more effectively than deterministic or purely reward-maximizing approaches.
> - Combining GFlowNets with targeted approximations (token-level marginal reward) and guided exploration (RGFN) makes advanced probabilistic modeling computationally feasible for long multimodal reasoning sequences.
> - Bayesian inference over sampled latent rationales offers a statistically robust, scalable, and principled alternative to heuristic inference-time scaling methods, enhancing output reliability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical challenge in the rapidly evolving field of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs): enhancing their reasoning capabilities, interpretability, and reliability through Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. LVLMs, which integrate visual perception and natural language processing, are increasingly applied to complex tasks like visual question answering and commonsense reasoning. However, their ability to generalize across diverse reasoning tasks and provide transparent, step-by-step rationalizations remains a significant hurdle.

Current mainstream training paradigms for visual CoT, such as Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO, exemplified by DeepSeek-R1), primarily optimize next-token distributions or scalar rewards. While effective for in-distribution tasks, these methods suffer from several limitations:
- **Generalization Issues:** They often struggle to generalize to unseen reasoning tasks due to their inability to explicitly capture dependencies across reasoning trajectories.
- **Limited Exploration:** SFT tends to "parrot" reference traces, while PPO and GRPO are constrained by KL penalties, limiting their exploration of novel rationales. This leads to under-representation of the full diversity of reasoning distributions.
- **Reliance on Biased Reward Models:** PPO and GRPO heavily depend on an external "critic" or reward model, which can be biased and susceptible to "reward hacking" – achieving high scores without genuinely solving the problem.
- **Deterministic Sampling:** Many existing methods generate deterministic reasoning CoTs, lacking the capacity to represent the inherent uncertainty and diversity in reasoning paths.

The paper positions itself as a significant departure from these limitations by reformulating reasoning in LVLMs as a **posterior inference problem over latent variables**. This allows for the use of rich, expressive probabilistic models that can better capture uncertainty and hidden structures without direct supervision. Specifically, it leverages **Amortized Variational Inference (AVI)**, drawing parallels with Generative Flow Networks (GFlowNets), which are designed to sample complex structured objects proportionally to a predefined reward function. While prior GFlowNets-based approaches have shown promise in text reasoning, they have not fully addressed visual reasoning, particularly due to the long sequence lengths (e.g., ~1k tokens) inherent in multimodal tasks.

Furthermore, the work addresses the computational inefficiency of traditional "inference-time scaling" methods like Best-of-N (BoN) sampling and Beam Search. These methods, while improving performance by generating multiple candidate responses and selecting the best, incur significant computational overhead and still rely on potentially biased critic models. The proposed Bayesian inference strategy aims to offer a more scalable, probabilistically justified alternative.

In essence, this paper seeks to push the boundaries of LVLM reasoning by introducing a novel, principled, and scalable training and inference framework that enhances generalization, interpretability, and diversity in visual CoT generation, moving beyond the shortcomings of existing supervised and reinforcement learning approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel reasoning model, named **LaCoT (Latent Chain-of-Thought)**, that enables **amortized latent CoT sampling in LVLMs** and demonstrates strong **generalization across various visual reasoning tasks**. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Enhance Interpretability and Reliability of LVLMs:** CoT reasoning is crucial for making LVLM decisions understandable and trustworthy. The authors aim to improve this by generating explicit, step-by-step rationalizations that reflect more diverse and high-likelihood reasoning paths.
2. **Overcome Limitations of Existing Training Paradigms:**
 - **Poor Generalization:** Current methods (SFT, PPO, GRPO) often struggle to generalize to unseen reasoning tasks. LaCoT aims to address this by explicitly capturing dependencies across trajectories and encouraging diverse exploration.
 - **Reliance on Biased Reward Models:** PPO and GRPO depend heavily on external reward models, leading to potential "reward hacking" where models optimize for the reward metric rather than genuinely solving the problem. LaCoT seeks to mitigate this by formulating reasoning as a posterior inference problem, reducing dependence on biased scalar rewards.
 - **Limited Exploration and Diversity:** SFT tends to "parrot" training data, while PPO/GRPO are constrained by KL penalties, leading to policies with limited diversity in generated rationales. LaCoT aims to encourage diverse, high-likelihood latent CoT by leveraging diversity-seeking reinforcement learning and amortized variational inference.
 - **Computational Cost for Long CoT Sequences:** Directly applying AVI methods like GFlowNets to multimodal tasks with potentially thousands of tokens in CoT sequences is computationally expensive. The authors are motivated to make such methods practical.
3. **Develop a Scalable and Principled Inference Strategy:** Existing inference-time scaling methods (e.g., Best-of-N, Beam Search) are computationally costly and rely on biased critic models. The motivation here is to replace these with a more efficient, probabilistically justified Bayesian inference-scaling strategy that can efficiently rank optimal rationales and answers without an explicit critic model.

In summary, the core motivation is to move beyond the deterministic, reward-model-dependent, and generalization-limited nature of current LVLM reasoning, towards a more probabilistic, diverse, and robust framework that can truly unlock the potential of CoT in visual reasoning tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The LaCoT model tackles the challenges of visual reasoning by reformulating it as a **probabilistic inference problem** over latent variables, specifically the Chain-of-Thought (Z), given a question-answer pair (X, Y). The core methodology revolves around **Amortized Variational Inference (AVI)**, implemented through **Generative Flow Networks (GFlowNets)**, combined with novel strategies for efficient training and principled inference.

The methodology can be broken down into three key components:

1. **Formulating Reasoning as Variational Inference with GFlowNets:**
 - The goal is to approximate the intractable posterior distribution P(Z|X,Y) – the distribution of latent CoTs given visual input and answer.
 - GFlowNets are used to train an autoregressive policy model, qθ(Z|X), to generate trajectories (CoTs) such that their probability is proportional to their reward (unnormalized posterior probability). This encourages higher-likelihood rationales.
 - The standard GFlowNets objective (Sub-Trajectory Balance, SubTB) requires token-level rewards, which is computationally prohibitive for long CoT sequences.

2. **Efficient Training Algorithm (RGFN) with Token-level Marginal Reward Approximation:**
 - **Token-level Marginal Reward Approximation:** To address the computational cost of token-level rewards, the paper proposes a **linear interpolation strategy** for estimating intermediate rewards (Equation 4). Instead of computing the exact reward for every token, rewards are calculated sparsely after every λ steps, and intermediate rewards are interpolated. This approximation leverages the local smoothness of log-likelihood, making the training of GFlowNets on long sequences feasible.
 - **Reference-Guided GFlowNet Fine-tuning (RGFN):** To combat the "catastrophic forgetting" issue (where unconstrained exploration leads to meaningless, high-likelihood content) and improve exploration efficiency without relying on restrictive KL penalties, RGFN introduces a **reference-guided exploration mechanism**.
 - During training, multiple candidate latent rationales are sampled from the current policy model.
 - Each candidate is evaluated against a "reference rationale" (Z_ref, typically from a teacher model like GPT-4o).
 - Only candidates whose reward exceeds a dynamically adjusted threshold (based on an annealing coefficient δ_s) compared to the reference rationale are used for gradient updates (Equation 6, 7). This filters out low-quality trajectories, guiding exploration towards high-quality rationales and reducing gradient variance.

3. **Bayesian Inference over Latent Rationales (BiN) for Optimal Rationale-Solution Search:**
 - At inference time, traditional methods like Best-of-N (BoN) or Beam Search are computationally expensive and depend on biased critic models.
 - BiN proposes a principled, probabilistic approach:
 - **Sample N latent rationales:** From the trained policy model qθ(Z|X).
 - **Sample N corresponding answers:** Using a reasoning LVLM πΦ (which also serves as the reward model for training qθ) for each sampled rationale, πΦ(Y|XZ_i).
 - **Compute Joint Likelihood:** For each (Z_i, Y_i) pair: πΦ(Z_iY_i|X).
 - **Estimate Marginal Likelihood:** The marginal likelihood P(Y_i|X) is estimated by normalizing the joint likelihood over the sequence length |Z_iY_i| (Equation 9). This length normalization helps in fair comparison across rationales of different lengths.
 - **Select Optimal Answer:** The answer Y_i* with the highest estimated marginal likelihood is chosen as the final output.
 - This strategy aligns with Bayesian principles, approximating the marginal likelihood through sampling over latent rationales, and eliminates the need for an explicit critic model during inference.

**Implementation Details:**
- **Reward Model (πΦ):** A fine-tuned reasoning LVLM (Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B) is used. It's initially SFT-trained on a mixture of visual reasoning datasets (LLaVA-CoT, R1-Onevision) augmented with high-quality, structured CoT examples. A special "Analyzer" token is introduced to encourage selective reasoning.
- **Rationale Sampler (qθ):** Initialized with πΦ, then fine-tuned using LoRA (with r=64, alpha=128) and the RGFN objective on 3k visual reasoning samples. Teacher-generated CoTs serve as reference rationales (Z_ref).
- **Hyperparameters:** λ is set to 8 for reward approximation, controlling the trade-off between accuracy and efficiency.

Overall, the methodology combines advanced probabilistic modeling (GFlowNets for AVI) with practical innovations (token-level reward approximation, RGFN's guided exploration) and a principled inference strategy (BiN) to create a robust and scalable framework for latent CoT reasoning in LVLMs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of LaCoT demonstrates significant improvements in effectiveness, generalization, and interpretability across multiple visual reasoning benchmarks.

1. **State-of-the-Art Performance:**
 - LaCoT models (3B and 7B) achieve state-of-the-art results among open-source LVLMs on seven reasoning benchmarks: MathVista, MathVision, MathVerse, MMMU, MMMU-pro, MMVet, and MME (Table 1).
 - The **LaCoT-Qwen-7B** model shows a **6.6% improvement** over its base Qwen2.5-VL-7B model and **outperforms GRPO by 10.6%**.
 - The **LaCoT-Qwen-3B** model surpasses its base model by **13.9%** and even achieves better results than larger models like LLaVA-CoT-11B and LLaVA-OV-7B, highlighting the efficiency of the latent CoT learning approach.
 - Notably, LaCoT significantly boosts diagram comprehension and OCR robustness, with the 3B model showing a **14-point accuracy jump on MathVerse-Vision-only**.

2. **Enhanced Rationale Diversity and Likelihood:**
 - Qualitative and quantitative analysis (Figure 6) confirms that LaCoT samples rationales with **higher diversity** and **higher log-likelihood** compared to the SFT baseline. This increased diversity is crucial for covering the full target posterior distribution, leading to a higher probability of sampling correct answers.

3. **Superior Inference-time Scaling (BiN vs. BoN):**
 - The proposed Bayesian inference method (BiN) consistently **outperforms Best-of-N (BoN)** across various visual reasoning benchmarks (Table 2). This demonstrates the effectiveness of BiN as a more principled, efficient, and robust strategy for selecting optimal rationales and answers without relying on external, potentially biased reward models.

4. **Effectiveness of RGFN (Ablation Study):**
 - An ablation study (Table 3) on the Qwen2.5-VL-7B model shows that **RGFN significantly outperforms** zero-shot prompting, supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).
 - SFT offers only slight improvements, and GRPO yields poor performance, attributed to inadequate guidance from the external reward model and limited exploration. This validates RGFN's ability to avoid catastrophic forgetting, prevent reward hacking, and promote better generalization through reference-guided exploration and target distribution matching.

5. **Scalability and Robustness of BiN:**
 - Further study of BiN (Figure 7) reveals that test accuracy consistently **increases with a higher number of rationale candidates (N)** and a **higher temperature (T)** during sampling. This indicates that increasing N reduces Monte-Carlo variance, improves posterior coverage, and smooths length-normalization fluctuations, leading to more reliable answer rankings.
 - The authors also note that higher N can **effectively mitigate hallucination issues** in visual reasoning, as increasing N from 1 to 5 significantly improves accuracy on the MMMU dataset.
 - BiN's generalizability is further demonstrated (Table 4) by showing consistent performance improvements even when applied to base SFT-trained Qwen2.5-VL models across all benchmarks, indicating its utility as a universal inference-scaling method.

In summary, the results strongly support the effectiveness of LaCoT's novel training and inference mechanisms. The model not only achieves superior accuracy on complex visual reasoning tasks but also demonstrates improved interpretability through diverse, high-likelihood rationales and robust, principled answer selection.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Latent Chain-of-Thought for Visual Reasoning" paper introduces a highly significant contribution to the field of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs), with several profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Paradigm Shift in LVLM Reasoning Training:** By reformulating CoT reasoning as a posterior inference problem and leveraging Amortized Variational Inference (AVI) with Generative Flow Networks (GFlowNets), this work offers a principled and scalable alternative to traditional supervised and reinforcement learning paradigms. This approach inherently encourages diverse and high-likelihood reasoning trajectories, moving beyond the limitations of deterministic sampling and reward-maximization methods that often struggle with generalization and reward hacking.

2. **Enhanced Interpretability and Reliability:** The ability to sample diverse, high-likelihood latent CoTs directly addresses the critical need for more interpretable and reliable LVLMs. Users can gain better insights into the model's reasoning process, which is crucial for deploying these models in sensitive applications where trust and transparency are paramount. The qualitative results vividly illustrate LaCoT's capacity to generate more accurate and comprehensive reasoning chains compared to baselines.

3. **Improved Generalization Capabilities:** The Reference-Guided GFlowNet Fine-tuning (RGFN) algorithm, with its novel exploration strategy, effectively prevents catastrophic forgetting and promotes learning policies that generalize better across diverse and unseen visual reasoning tasks. This is evidenced by LaCoT's superior performance across a wide array of benchmarks, including those requiring fine-grained visual understanding (MathVista) and complex multi-discipline knowledge (MMMU).

4. **Computationally Efficient and Principled Inference:** The introduction of Bayesian Inference over N latent rationales (BiN) provides a scalable and probabilistically justified inference-time scaling strategy. It effectively replaces computationally costly and potentially biased heuristic methods like Best-of-N and Beam Search. By estimating marginal likelihood and selecting answers based on a statistically robust criterion, BiN improves both the efficiency of finding optimal solutions and the trustworthiness of the selection process. Its effectiveness even on SFT-trained models highlights its general applicability.

5. **Mitigation of Key AI Challenges:** The methodology directly addresses several inherent challenges in training and deploying advanced AI models:
 - **Reward Hacking:** By aligning marginal likelihood with reward, the approach inherently reduces susceptibility to reward hacking.
 - **Limited Diversity:** The diversity-seeking nature of GFlowNets, enhanced by RGFN, ensures broader posterior coverage and more varied rationales.
 - **Hallucination:** The study shows that increasing the number of sampled rationales (N) with BiN can significantly mitigate hallucination issues, leading to more accurate and reliable outputs.

6. **Potential for Future Research and Applications:** The authors highlight future directions, including the application of LaCoT for knowledge distillation and synthetic data generation. The principled nature of generating diverse and high-quality rationales could unlock new avenues for self-improvement in LVLMs and create more robust training datasets. The code availability at https://github.com/heliossun/LaCoT further promotes reproducibility and encourages the broader research community to build upon this foundational work.

In conclusion, "Latent Chain-of-Thought for Visual Reasoning" offers a significant step forward in making LVLMs more intelligent, transparent, and robust. Its novel probabilistic framework, efficient training algorithms, and principled inference strategies pave the way for a new generation of visual reasoning models capable of tackling increasingly complex real-world problems with greater reliability and interpretability.
