# Latent Chain-of-Thought for Visual Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> A new framework for Large Vision-Language Models, Latent Chain-of-Thought (LaCoT), reformulates visual reasoning as a posterior inference problem, using Generative Flow Networks for efficient training and Bayesian inference for optimal answer selection. The LaCoT-Qwen-7B model demonstrated a 6.6% improvement over its base model and surpassed existing methods by 10.6% on various visual reasoning benchmarks.

## Problem

- Current Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) using Chain-of-Thought (CoT) often struggle with generalization across diverse visual reasoning tasks and tend to produce limited, deterministic rationales.
- Existing reinforcement learning approaches for CoT generation depend on external, potentially biased reward models, leading to issues like reward hacking and constrained exploration.
- Traditional inference-time scaling methods (e.g., Best-of-N, Beam Search) for CoT are computationally expensive for long multimodal sequences and also rely on potentially biased critic models.

## Method

- ==LaCoT== reformulates CoT reasoning as a posterior inference problem over latent variables, leveraging ==Amortized Variational Inference (AVI)== with ==Generative Flow Networks (GFlowNets)==.
- A ==Reference-Guided GFlowNet Fine-tuning (RGFN)== algorithm is introduced for efficient training, utilizing a token-level marginal reward approximation and guiding exploration with reference rationales to improve efficiency and quality.
- A principled ==Bayesian Inference over N latent rationales (BiN)== strategy is proposed for optimal rationale and answer selection at inference time, which estimates marginal likelihood without an explicit critic model.

## Results

- The LaCoT-Qwen-7B model achieved state-of-the-art performance among open-source LVLMs, outperforming its base Qwen2.5-VL-7B by **6.6%** and GRPO by **10.6%** across seven visual reasoning benchmarks.
- LaCoT models generate rationales with higher diversity and log-likelihood compared to SFT baselines, contributing to better posterior coverage and a higher probability of sampling correct answers.
- The Bayesian Inference (BiN) method consistently outperformed Best-of-N (BoN) for optimal rationale and answer selection, demonstrating improved accuracy, generalizability, and a notable ability to mitigate hallucination issues with increased rationale candidates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Treating CoT reasoning as a probabilistic inference problem over latent variables allows for capturing uncertainty and diverse reasoning paths more effectively than deterministic or purely reward-maximizing approaches.
> - Combining GFlowNets with targeted approximations (token-level marginal reward) and guided exploration (RGFN) makes advanced probabilistic modeling computationally feasible for long multimodal reasoning sequences.
> - Bayesian inference over sampled latent rationales offers a statistically robust, scalable, and principled alternative to heuristic inference-time scaling methods, enhancing output reliability.
