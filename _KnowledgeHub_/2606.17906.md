# WAM-RL: World-Action Model Reinforcement Learning with Reconstruction Rewards and Online Video SFT

> [!summary] Summary
> WAM-RL integrates reinforcement learning into World-Action models, facilitating the joint optimization of their world and action components. This framework leverages online video self-supervised fine-tuning and reconstruction rewards to enhance robotic manipulation, boosting success rates up to 82% on LIBERO-Object and enabling robust recovery behaviors in complex tasks.

## Problem

- Existing World-Action (WA) models rely on supervised learning from expert demonstrations, which limits their ability to acquire skills beyond the training data distribution.
- Current WA models lack mechanisms for continuous adaptation and improvement through direct interaction with the environment, hindering robustness in novel or complex scenarios.
- Applying reinforcement learning to WA models can lead to instability and degraded performance due to distribution shifts in the latent representation of the tightly coupled world and action models.

## Method

- **WAM-RL** introduces a reinforcement learning framework that enables the joint online optimization of both the world model and the action model within the WA paradigm.
- The world model is refined through ==online video self-supervised fine-tuning== (SFT) on successful interaction trajectories, stabilized by a KL regularization term that constrains latent feature distribution shifts.
- The action model is optimized via policy gradient methods, guided by a dense, reconstruction-based reward signal derived from the similarity between imagined future observations and actual executed observations.

## Results

- WAM-RL achieved a success rate of **82%** on LIBERO-Object, improving upon the base model (**68%**) and an actor-only RL baseline (**78%**).
- On the challenging RLBench Water Plants task, WAM-RL increased the success rate from **19%** (Base) to **22%**, while the actor-only RL baseline showed no improvement (**18%**).
- Qualitative analysis demonstrated that the world model, after online SFT, predicted corrective actions and recovery behaviors (e.g., re-grasping) after initial failures, contributing to overall policy robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly optimizing both the world model and the action model is crucial for improving performance, especially in long-horizon tasks, where actor-only reinforcement learning proves insufficient.
> - Online video self-supervised fine-tuning allows the world model to adapt and predict realistic recovery behaviors following errors, which is critical for robust and adaptive robotic policies.
> - Pixel-level Mean Squared Error for reconstruction provides the most effective reward signal for actor optimization, indicating that rewards aligned with the world model's intrinsic visual prediction objective lead to better policy learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper operates within the field of robot policy learning, specifically at the intersection of World-Action (WA) models and reinforcement learning (RL). Historically, robot policy learning has evolved from reliance on static visual representations towards more dynamic, video-based generative modeling.

**Evolution from VLA to WA Models:** Recent advancements have seen a transition from Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to World-Action (WA) models. WA models, by jointly modeling future observations and actions through video-based generative techniques, aim to provide a stronger inductive bias for long-horizon decision-making compared to VLA models. They leverage predictive structure in visual dynamics to enable implicit planning, demonstrating generalization and data efficiency in both simulation and real-world settings. Examples of related work include Video Prediction Policy, Unified World Models, Genie Envisioner, Cosmos Policy, LingBot-VA, and DreamZero.

**Limitations of Current WA Models:** Despite their advantages, existing WA models primarily rely on supervised learning from expert trajectories. This reliance imposes two main limitations:
1. **Limited Skill Acquisition:** The learned policies are often confined to the distribution of the training data, restricting their ability to acquire fine-grained manipulation skills that go beyond the demonstrated examples.
2. **Lack of Continuous Improvement:** These models typically lack mechanisms for continuous adaptation and improvement through direct interaction with the environment, which is essential for robustness and error correction in novel or complex scenarios.

**Integration of Reinforcement Learning:** A natural approach to overcome these limitations is to incorporate reinforcement learning, enabling models to learn from experience and rewards. However, applying RL to WA models presents challenges. Unlike conventional RL for VLA models, which primarily optimize the action policy while keeping visual representations fixed, WA models involve two tightly coupled components: a world model (for future predictions) and an action model (for mapping predictions to actions). Naively applying RL can lead to distribution shifts in the latent representation of the world model, resulting in instability and degraded performance of the action model.

**Contribution of WAM-RL:** This work positions itself as the first to introduce reinforcement learning into the World-Action paradigm with joint optimization of both the world model and the action model. It aims to address the challenges of instability caused by latent space shifts, thereby enabling WA models to acquire fine-grained skills and continuously improve through online interaction, moving beyond the constraints of expert demonstrations. It differentiates itself from existing RL approaches for VLA models by focusing on the co-evolution of both the world model and the actor within the WA framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable World-Action (WA) models to acquire fine-grained manipulation skills beyond the distribution of expert demonstrations and to allow them to continuously improve through real-world interaction. This overarching goal is motivated by the inherent limitations of current WA models, which are predominantly trained via supervised learning on expert trajectories.

The specific key objectives include:

1. **Developing an RL framework for WA models:** The paper aims to introduce WAM-RL, a novel reinforcement learning framework specifically designed for WA models. This framework is intended to facilitate the joint optimization of both the world model and the action model.
2. **Enabling Co-evolution of World and Action Models:** A central objective is to allow the world model and the action model to improve in a coordinated manner through online interaction with the environment. This co-evolution is hypothesized to enhance fine-grained control and adaptability of the robotic policies.
3. **Ensuring Stable Training for Joint Optimization:** The tightly coupled nature of WA models means that optimizing one component without considering the other can lead to instability (e.g., latent space shifts). Therefore, a crucial objective is to design a stable training strategy that prevents such issues while still allowing both components to adapt.
4. **Investigating the Impact of Online Optimization:** The research seeks to systematically investigate the effects of applying reinforcement learning to the action model, as well as the online training of the world model within an RL setting. This includes comparing the performance of actor-only optimization versus joint world model and actor optimization, particularly on tasks of varying horizons.
5. **Providing Empirical Insights:** An objective is to empirically demonstrate the proposed framework's effectiveness and to offer insights into how online optimization of both the action head and the world model impacts overall performance, especially regarding the acquisition of recovery behaviors and robustness.

The motivation for these objectives stems directly from the observed shortcomings of existing WA models:
- **Data Distribution Constraint:** Policies learned solely from demonstrations are limited by the support of the training data, hindering their ability to perform actions or acquire skills outside this distribution.
- **Lack of Adaptability:** The inability to continuously learn and improve from interaction means current WA models cannot adapt to novel scenarios or correct errors during execution, which is crucial for real-world robotic applications.
- **Technical Challenges of RL Integration:** Directly applying RL to WA models is non-trivial due to the interdependence of the world model's latent space and the action model. Without careful design, RL can induce distribution shifts that destabilize the entire system. Addressing these technical challenges is a core motivation for the methodological contributions of WAM-RL.
- **Enhancing Long-Horizon Performance:** The authors identify that actor-only optimization might be insufficient for complex, long-horizon tasks due to accumulated prediction errors from a fixed world model. This motivates the need for a mechanism to improve the world model itself.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed WAM-RL framework addresses the limitations of existing WA models by enabling the joint optimization of a world model and an action model (actor) through online interaction. The methodology is structured around a two-part optimization scheme: online video self-supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with KL regularization for the world model, and reinforcement learning with reconstruction-based rewards for the action model.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

The framework builds upon flow matching for video generation and its extension, Flow-SDE, for reinforcement learning with flow-based policies.
- **Flow Matching for Video Generation:** This technique learns a continuous-time vector field to transform a simple base distribution (e.g., Gaussian noise) into a complex data distribution (e.g., video sequences). The model minimizes the difference between its predicted vector field and the ground-truth transport direction along an interpolation trajectory. This allows for generative modeling of future visual observations.
- **Flow-SDE for Reinforcement Learning:** To introduce stochasticity necessary for RL, Flow-SDE converts the deterministic flow matching ODE into a Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE). This SDE formulation induces a sequence of conditional transitions, allowing for tractable likelihood estimation of action sequences. This enables the application of standard policy gradient methods to optimize the flow-based action model, treating the denoising process as a stochastic trajectory in the latent space.

#### 3.2. Overall Framework Design

WAM-RL proposes that the core capability of WA models originates from the world model's ability to capture predictive structure and support implicit planning, while the actor functions as a translator of these latent predictions into actions. Therefore, improving WA models requires enhancing both the world model's predictive accuracy and the actor's translation fidelity. The framework coordinates these improvements through:
- **World Model Refinement:** Online video self-supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on successful trajectories collected during interaction.
- **Actor Optimization:** Reinforcement learning guided by a reconstruction-based dense reward signal.
- **Stabilization Mechanism:** A KL regularization term is incorporated to maintain stability in the world model's latent space during online updates.

#### 3.3. Online Video SFT with KL Regularization for the World Model

To improve the world model's ability to generate accurate and task-relevant future predictions, it undergoes online fine-tuning using successful trajectories from the RL process.
- **Video Modeling Objective:** The world model is trained to predict future observations using a standard video modeling loss (e.g., flow matching or reconstruction loss) on the collected trajectories.
- **Stability Challenge:** Naive fine-tuning of the world model alongside the actor can lead to severe instability. The actor relies on the world model's latent feature distribution, and shifts in this distribution due to online updates can render the actor ineffective.
- **KL Regularization:** To mitigate this, a KL regularization term is introduced. This term constrains the latent feature distribution of the updated world model to remain close to that of a pretrained (frozen) reference model.
 - **Latent Distribution Approximation:** Since the intermediate features of a DiT-based world model are deterministic, approximate Gaussian distributions are constructed over the latent features. The mean is the deterministic feature, and the covariance is estimated using an exponential moving average (EMA) for the current model and fixed from the pretrained model for the reference.
 - **KL Divergence:** The regularization loss calculates the KL divergence between these approximate Gaussian latent distributions, ensuring the updated model's latent space preserves geometry consistent with the actor's expectations.
- **Combined Objective:** The world model's total training objective is a sum of the video prediction loss and the KL regularization term, weighted by a hyperparameter (L_WM = L_video + λ_KL * L_KL).

#### 3.4. Action Model RL with Reconstruction-Based Reward

The action model (actor) is optimized to faithfully realize the world model's predictions in the real environment.
- **Reconstruction-Based Reward:** A dense reward signal is defined based on the similarity between the imagined future observations (predicted by the world model) and the actual observations obtained by executing the actor's actions in the environment.
 - `r_t = sim(imagined_future_observations, executed_future_observations)`
 - This reward encourages the actor to produce actions consistent with the world model's latent plan.
- **Similarity Functions:** Various similarity functions are explored for the reward calculation:
 - Pixel-level Mean Squared Error (MSE)
 - Optical flow consistency
 - DINOv2 feature similarity
 - V-JEPA2 feature similarity
 These options represent different levels of visual alignment, from low-level appearance to high-level semantic features.
- **Policy Gradient Optimization:** The actor is optimized using a standard policy gradient objective, where the advantage function is computed from these reconstruction-based rewards.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

- The method is implemented based on the Genie Envisioner-ACT architecture, with the world model as a DiT-based video generator and the actor consuming intermediate latent features.
- Training involved 8 NVIDIA A800 GPUs for 8 hours under a mixed online RL and video fine-tuning setting.
- **Datasets:** Evaluation was conducted on LIBERO (object-centric manipulation) and RLBench (multi-step robotic skills), specifically the Water Plants task.
- **Baselines:** Performance was compared against a pretrained WA model without RL (Base) and actor-only reinforcement learning using π_RL.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate the effectiveness of WAM-RL in improving World-Action models for robotic manipulation tasks. The findings highlight the importance of jointly optimizing both the world model and the action model.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Improvements

WAM-RL consistently improved performance across both benchmark tasks:
- **LIBERO-Object:** The success rate increased from 68% (Base) to 82% with WAM-RL. This represents a substantial improvement over the actor-only RL baseline (π_RL), which achieved 78%.
- **RLBench (Water Plants):** WAM-RL improved the success rate from 19% (Base) to 22%. In this more challenging, long-horizon task, the actor-only RL baseline (π_RL) failed to yield any improvement over the base model, remaining at 18%.
These results indicate that while actor-only optimization can offer some gains on shorter-horizon tasks (LIBERO), it is insufficient for complex, long-horizon scenarios where the world model's predictive accuracy becomes critical. Joint optimization, as in WAM-RL, proves more effective in these settings.

#### 4.2. World Model Adaptation and Recovery Behaviors

A significant qualitative finding was the impact of online video SFT on the world model's behavior. After online training, the updated world model generated more realistic predictions of recovery behaviors. For instance, in scenarios involving an unsuccessful grasp, the model anticipated corrective actions such as re-adjusting the gripper or re-attempting the grasp, rather than predicting immediate task failure. This adaptive prediction of recovery dynamics contributed to the actor executing more robust policies and achieving higher overall success rates.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study on Reconstruction Loss for Reward

The study investigated the effect of different similarity functions used to formulate the reconstruction-based reward for actor optimization.
- **Discriminability vs. Performance:** Optical flow-based similarity exhibited the strongest discriminability between successful and failed trajectories in the reward space (largest gap between success and failure rewards). However, this high discriminability did not translate to the best task performance.
- **Pixel MSE Performance:** Pixel-level Mean Squared Error (MSE) for reconstruction achieved the highest task success rate, despite showing comparatively weaker separation in the reward distribution between success and failure.
- **Hypothesis:** The authors hypothesize that pixel-level reconstruction is better aligned with the world model's intrinsic training objective (visual observation prediction). It provides a more consistent learning signal for the actor and imposes stronger penalties on out-of-distribution actions, which may regularize the policy and prevent unstable behaviors. In contrast, feature-based metrics (DINOv2, V-JEPA2) and optical flow showed lower performance in this setting. These results indicate a trade-off between reward signal discriminability and its alignment with the model's internal representations for effective policy optimization.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study on Video SFT

A qualitative comparison demonstrated the critical role of online video self-supervised fine-tuning (Video SFT) for the world model:
- **Without Video SFT:** After an initial mistake (e.g., an unsuccessful grasp), the model without video SFT failed to predict corrective actions. The predicted trajectory continued along an erroneous path, quickly leading to out-of-distribution behaviors, indicating a lack of understanding of failure dynamics and recovery strategies.
- **With Video SFT:** The model with video SFT exhibited different behavior. Following an initial failure, the predicted trajectory included corrective adjustments, such as re-positioning the gripper and re-attempting the grasp. This recovery behavior emerged within a single open-loop chunk, suggesting that the world model learned to internalize failure patterns and their corresponding corrective actions.
This ablation demonstrates that video SFT is essential for improving the world model's ability to capture task-relevant dynamics, including anticipating and correcting errors, which is crucial for robust long-horizon behavior.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "WAM-RL" introduces a notable advancement in robot policy learning by integrating reinforcement learning into the World-Action (WA) paradigm. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized in several points:

**1. Novel Integration of RL with World-Action Models:** WAM-RL is identified as the first framework to successfully incorporate reinforcement learning into the World-Action paradigm while performing joint optimization of both the world model and the action model. This addresses a critical gap, as prior work on WA models largely relied on supervised learning, and previous RL efforts for VLA models did not typically optimize the visual representation or world model components in a coupled manner.

**2. Methodological Contribution for Stable Joint Optimization:** The proposed stable training strategy, which combines online video self-supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for the world model with KL regularization and reconstruction-based reinforcement learning for the actor, is a significant methodological contribution. The KL regularization specifically targets the challenge of latent space shifts, which can destabilize tightly coupled generative models during online adaptation. This strategy enables the co-evolution of the world model and actor, fostering fine-grained control and adaptability without leading to performance degradation due to distribution shifts.

**3. Empirical Insight into Long-Horizon Task Requirements:** A key empirical insight provided by this research is that optimizing the action model alone is insufficient for achieving robust performance in long-horizon tasks. The findings highlight that the quality of the world model fundamentally limits the overall effectiveness of the system, particularly when dealing with accumulated prediction errors over extended sequences of actions. Jointly improving the world model, which allows it to capture and predict recovery behaviors, is demonstrated as critical for success in complex, multi-step scenarios. This shifts the conventional focus from solely policy optimization to the comprehensive improvement of the entire World-Action system.

**4. Enhanced Robustness and Adaptability:** The framework enables the world model to learn from online interaction, leading to predictions that include recovery behaviors (e.g., re-attempting a failed grasp). This capability directly translates into more robust policies that can anticipate and correct errors, thereby improving task success rates and making the robotic system more resilient to real-world uncertainties. This ability to adapt and recover from mistakes is crucial for deploying robots in dynamic and unstructured environments.

**5. Implications for Future Research:** The paper identifies several limitations and suggests future research directions:
 - **Scalable World Model Adaptation:** The current KL regularization, while necessary for stability, may constrain the extent to which the world model can adapt and expand its capabilities significantly beyond its pretrained distribution. Future work could explore more scalable and less restrictive adaptation strategies.
 - **Discriminative Reward Learning:** The reliance on pretrained representations or manually designed similarity metrics for reconstruction rewards showed limitations in discriminability. Future research could focus on developing more expressive and task-aware reward formulations that provide better contrast between successful and failed trajectories, potentially through learned reward functions.

**6. Broader Impact on Generalist Robot Policies:** By demonstrating a pathway for WA models to continuously improve and adapt through interaction, WAM-RL contributes to the development of more capable and generalist robot policies. The ability to learn fine-grained skills and recover from errors through online optimization positions this work as a step towards robotic systems that are more autonomous and effective in real-world applications, potentially impacting fields such as manufacturing, service robotics, and exploration.
