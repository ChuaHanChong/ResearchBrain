# SASI: Leveraging Sub-Action Semantics for Robust Early Action Recognition in Human-Robot Interaction

> [!summary] Summary
> The research introduces SASI, a dual-branch framework that integrates sub-action semantics with spatiotemporal kinematic features to improve skeleton-based action recognition. It achieves enhanced accuracy for both complete and early action recognition on partial sequences, facilitating more proactive human-robot interaction.

## Problem

- Conventional action recognition methods treat human actions as monolithic entities, failing to capture their complex, hierarchical structure and limiting interpretability.
- Existing systems struggle with early action recognition from incomplete or partial observations, leading to latency and errors in real-time Human-Robot Interaction (HRI).
- There is a lack of explicit modeling of hierarchical semantic structures at the sub-action level within GCN-based action recognition pipelines for HRI.

## Method

- A dual-branch architecture, **SASI** (==Sub-Action Semantics Integrated cross-modal fusion==), is developed to combine ==GCN-derived kinematic features== with ==sub-action semantic features==.
- A ==Sub-Action Semantics Branch== uses a pre-trained ==segmentation model== and a ==CLIP-style text encoder== to convert sub-action labels into rich ==semantic text embeddings==.
- A ==cross-modal fusion mechanism==, employing ==cross-attention==, integrates the kinematic and semantic features, trained with a multi-task objective including a ==semantic consistency loss==.

## Results

- SASI achieved a **59.65%** accuracy for complete action recognition (100% observation ratio) on the **BABEL dataset**, outperforming baseline skeleton-based methods with a ProtoGCN backbone.
- The framework demonstrated improved early action recognition, achieving **51.71%** accuracy at **25%** observation ratio and **56.97%** at **50%** observation ratio, using a ProtoGCN backbone.
- Ablation studies confirmed that semantic loss improved accuracy by **1.67%**, and text retrieval contributed a **6.28%** gain, while a hypothetical perfect segmentation could yield a **10.99%** improvement over the baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly leveraging the hierarchical semantic structure of human actions through sub-actions provides a more fine-grained and interpretable understanding of complex behaviors.
> - Robust early action recognition from incomplete sequences, driven by sub-action semantics, is crucial for enabling proactive and responsive human-robot interaction.
> - The quality of sub-action segmentation directly impacts overall action recognition performance, indicating that improvements in segmentation are key for further gains.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Human action understanding, particularly action recognition, is a foundational area within computer vision and robotics, with significant implications for applications such as human-robot collaboration and intelligent industrial systems. Over the past decade, skeleton-based action recognition, often utilizing Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) to model human joint relationships, has achieved notable advancements. Techniques like Spatial Temporal Graph Convolutional Networks (ST-GCN) and subsequent adaptive GCN architectures have demonstrated proficiency in capturing spatiotemporal dependencies within motion sequences.

However, the existing research landscape faces two primary challenges, particularly in the context of real-time Human-Robot Interaction (HRI): action complexity and early action recognition. Conventional methods often treat human actions as monolithic entities, extracting coarse spatiotemporal features that may not capture the fine-grained, hierarchical nuances inherent in complex human behaviors. This simplification limits interpretability and can hinder recognition performance. Furthermore, most action recognition systems are designed assuming the availability of complete action data, a condition rarely met in real-time HRI scenarios where systems must infer actions from partial observations to provide proactive feedback. This mismatch can lead to increased latency and recognition errors, affecting the fluidity and safety of human-robot collaboration.

Prior work has explored various avenues to address aspects of these challenges. Multi-modal approaches have integrated visual data (RGB, depth), environmental context, and gaze information to enrich action understanding. Efforts in early action prediction have utilized prior knowledge or attention mechanisms to focus on discriminative early motion frames. In HRI specifically, multi-modal methods, trajectory mapping, and intention prediction have been investigated to enable robots to anticipate and adapt to human behaviors. However, a recurring limitation across these approaches is the insufficient explicit modeling of the hierarchical semantic structure of human actions at a sub-action level. The paper posits that human actions are naturally decomposable into meaningful sub-actions, a characteristic that is under-utilized by current systems. This gap—the explicit integration of sub-action semantics for robust early action recognition within a GCN-based pipeline for real-time HRI—is the specific area that this research aims to address.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the limitations of conventional human action recognition methods in real-world Human-Robot Interaction (HRI) scenarios. These methods typically treat human actions as indivisible, continuous sequences, which often fails to capture the intrinsic complexity and hierarchical structure of human behaviors. This simplification leads to two key issues that the authors aim to address:

1. **Action Complexity:** Human actions are inherently complex and can be decomposed into smaller, semantically meaningful units, or "sub-actions." For instance, a "throw" action might consist of sub-actions like "stand," "holding," and "throw an object." Conventional approaches, by focusing solely on holistic actions and extracting only coarse spatiotemporal features, tend to overlook this rich semantic structure. This results in less interpretable representations and limits the potential for fine-grained understanding and recognition accuracy. The authors are motivated to leverage this hierarchical nature, drawing inspiration from datasets like FineGym and BABEL which highlight the decomposable nature of human actions.

2. **Early Action Recognition in Real-time HRI:** Many HRI tasks, especially those requiring quick and proactive responses, demand that robots recognize human actions as early as possible from incomplete or partially observed motion sequences. Existing recognition methods, predominantly designed for complete action data, introduce latency and are prone to errors when applied to partial sequences. This reactive approach hinders seamless collaboration and can lead to failed interactions. The ability to predict actions early, by understanding the progression of sub-actions (e.g., inferring "drinking" from "open the bottle cap"), is crucial for robots to shift from reactive to proactive behavior, thereby improving safety, efficiency, and naturalness in human-robot collaboration.

To overcome these challenges, the key objectives of this work are:

- **To develop a novel framework that integrates sub-action semantics with spatiotemporal features for robust skeleton-based action recognition.** This involves moving beyond holistic action classification to explicitly model the compositional nature of actions.
- **To enhance recognition accuracy and interpretability by explicitly modeling the hierarchical dependencies between holistic actions and their constituent sub-actions.** This aims to create more nuanced and contextually aware action representations.
- **To improve the robustness of action recognition systems when faced with incomplete motion sequences.** This is specifically targeted at enabling early action recognition, which is essential for proactive HRI.
- **To design a pluggable, bottom-up architecture (SASI) that can augment existing GCN backbones, facilitating the practical adoption of sub-action semantic understanding in current state-of-the-art systems.**
- **To demonstrate through experimentation that leveraging sub-action semantics can reduce response latency in collaborative tasks, enabling proactive feedback in HRI environments.**

By addressing these objectives, the research aims to bridge a critical gap in human action understanding, providing robotic systems with a more human-like capacity to infer intent and anticipate behavior from dynamic, incomplete observations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, named SASI (Sub-Action Semantics Integrated cross-modal fusion), is a dual-branch architecture designed to fuse sub-action semantic features with spatiotemporal kinematic features for enhanced skeleton-based action recognition. It is conceptualized as a bottom-up pluggable framework that can augment standard GCN backbones.

**Overall Architecture:**
SASI processes raw motion input through two parallel branches: an Action Kinematic Branch and a Sub-Action Semantics Branch. The outputs of these branches are then combined using a cross-modal fusion mechanism, and the entire network is trained with a multi-task learning objective that includes both action recognition loss and a semantic consistency loss.

**A. Action Kinematic Branch:**
This branch is responsible for extracting spatiotemporal features from raw human motion data. It utilizes a standard GCN-based network as its backbone.
- **Input:** Raw motion data, denoted as `x`.
- **Processing:** The data `x` is passed through a series of `N` GCN layers. Each layer `l` computes features `X^(l)` using the formula: `X^(l) = σ(AX^(l-1)W^(l))`, where `X^(0)` is the input `x`, `W^(l)` are the weights for layer `l`, and `A` is a learnable adjacency matrix representing human joint connections. This process extracts kinematic features from the skeleton data.
- **Output:** The final skeleton-based spatiotemporal features, `X^(N)`.

**B. Sub-Action Semantics Branch:**
This branch is designed to extract semantic information derived from sub-actions.
- **Action Segmentation:** A pre-trained action segmentation model (a modified version of FACT, adapted for skeleton-based features) is used to segment the raw motion data `x` into sub-action labels, denoted as `s`. The weights of this segmentation model are frozen during the training of the main SASI network to maintain its performance and stabilize the training process. The output includes sub-action labels without explicit action boundaries.
- **Textual Description Retrieval:** The segmented sub-action labels `s` are converted into their corresponding textual descriptions, `s_text`, using a pre-saved label map `T_s`. For example, a numerical class ID might be mapped to "walk straight forward."
- **Text Encoding:** The `s_text` descriptions are then tokenized into indices `t` using a `Tokenizer`. These tokens are passed through a text encoder, which follows the architecture design of CLIP. This encoder includes an embedding layer `E`, a transformer architecture, and a normalization layer. This process generates the sub-action label text features, `T_sub`.

**C. Cross-Modal Fusion:**
This module is responsible for effectively combining the kinematic features `X^(N)` from the GCN backbone and the sub-action text embeddings `T_sub` from the sub-action semantics branch.
- **Projection:** Both `X^(N)` and `T_sub` are projected into query (`Q`), key (`K`), and value (`V`) spaces. `Q` is derived from `X^(N)`. `K` and `V` are derived from `T_sub` after passing through a Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) with batch normalization, ReLU, and dropout layers. This MLP (MLP1) transforms `T_sub` to match the sequence length dimension of `X^(N)`.
- **Cross-Attention:** A cross-attention mechanism is applied using the formula: `softmax((QK^T) / sqrt(d_k))V`, where `d_k` is the dimension of `K` and `Q`.
- **Fusion and Classification:** The output of the cross-attention is combined with `X^(N)` via a residual connection and normalization to produce `X_fused`. This fused feature then undergoes average pooling and a linear layer to generate the final action class prediction, `ŷ`.

**D. Semantic-Aware Multi-Task Learning:**
To ensure semantic consistency and aid the text encoder, a multi-task learning approach is employed.
- **Holistic Action Text Feature:** A text feature `T_hol` for the ground truth holistic action class `y` is generated using the same text encoder pipeline as `T_sub`.
- **Semantic Loss:** A semantic loss, `L_semantic`, is computed between a transformed version of `T_sub` (via another MLP, MLP2, consisting of only linear layers and dropout to avoid affecting embedding ranges) and `T_hol` using cosine similarity: `L_semantic = 1 - (MLP2(T_sub) · T_hol) / (||MLP2(T_sub)|| ||T_hol||)`. This loss encourages semantic alignment between sub-actions and their holistic parent action.
- **Overall Loss:** The total loss `L` is a weighted sum of the semantic loss `L_semantic` and the standard cross-entropy loss `L_recog` (between `y` and `ŷ`): `L = λ1 * L_recog + λ2 * L_semantic`. The hyperparameters `λ1` and `λ2` balance the contribution of each loss.

**Dataset and Implementation:**
- **Dataset:** Experiments are conducted on the BABEL dataset, which provides skeleton-based motion sequences with frame-level sub-action annotations derived from the AMASS dataset.
- **Data Cleansing:** Redundant labels in BABEL are merged based on cosine similarity using a pre-trained Sentence-BERT model. Joint positions are interpolated to a fixed sample length, and sub-action label sequences are padded with "none" tokens to ensure consistent length.
- **Segmentation Model:** The FACT model, originally for video features, is adapted to work with skeleton-based features for pre-training. Its weights are frozen during SASI training.
- **Inference Speed:** SASI operates at approximately 29 Hz on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU, with an input sequence length of 500, without specific optimization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of SASI was conducted on the BABEL dataset, comparing its performance against state-of-the-art skeleton-based action recognition methods across various observation ratios (ORs).

**Overall Performance Comparison (Table I):**
SASI consistently demonstrated improved recognition accuracy compared to conventional skeleton-based methods.
- **Complete Action Recognition (100% OR):** When trained and evaluated on complete action sequences, SASI, particularly with a ProtoGCN backbone, achieved the highest accuracy of 59.65%, outperforming all other methods. This indicates its effectiveness in capturing the full scope of an action.
- **Early Action Recognition (Partial ORs):** SASI showed superior performance on partial action sequences. When trained separately on 25%, 50%, and 75% ORs, SASI with a ProtoGCN backbone achieved the highest accuracies (51.71%, 56.97%, and 58.08% respectively). This highlights SASI's capability for robust early recognition from incomplete observations, which is critical for proactive HRI.
- **Generalizability to Partial Actions:** Models trained on 100% OR were also evaluated on partial OR test sets. SASI consistently achieved higher accuracy in these scenarios (e.g., 53.92% for 50% OR with ProtoGCN), suggesting that the learned hierarchical relationships are transferable and effective even when the model has only seen complete actions during training.
- **Modularity:** SASI proved to be a modular component, enhancing the performance of different state-of-the-art GCN backbones (CTR-GCN, BlockGCN, ProtoGCN) across most observation ratios, despite an increase in parameter count. This indicates its broad applicability.

**Segmentation Accuracy (Table II):**
The pre-trained FACT segmentation model, a component of SASI, exhibited varying accuracy. When trained separately on different ORs, its action-level segmentation accuracy ranged from 39.88% (25% OR) to 49.02% (100% OR). When trained on 100% OR, its accuracy was 35.70% on 25% OR. This finding is significant as it suggests that the overall recognition performance of SASI is potentially bounded by the quality of the underlying segmentation.

**Ablation Studies:**
Comprehensive ablation studies were conducted to isolate the contributions of different SASI modules.
- **Effectiveness of Sub-Action Segmentation Enhancement (Table III):** To evaluate the impact of segmentation quality, synthetic segmentation errors were introduced. The results demonstrated a monotonic increase in recognition performance with improved segmentation accuracy. A hypothetical 100% segmentation accuracy led to a substantial 10.99% gain over the baseline (from 58.17% to 69.16%). This provides strong evidence that accurate sub-action segmentation is a significant factor in enhancing holistic action understanding and indicates a clear path for future performance improvements.
- **Effectiveness of Cross-Attention Fusion (Table IV and Fig. 4):** The cross-attention mechanism with a residual connection (59.10%) outperformed alternative fusion strategies, including element-wise addition, multiplication (with and without residual), and concatenation. Qualitative analysis of attention weights (Fig. 4) showed that SASI's attention mechanism dynamically focuses on key sub-action regions that are semantically relevant to the holistic action (e.g., "sit" for "sit," "play guitar" for "strum"), confirming its ability to model inter-modal dependencies effectively.
- **Effectiveness of Semantic Loss Function (Table V and Fig. 4):** The inclusion of the semantic loss function improved recognition accuracy by 1.67% (from 57.43% to 59.10%). This confirms its role in enforcing semantic consistency between sub-actions and holistic actions. Visualizations further supported this, showing that SASI with semantic loss paid more attention to crucial sub-actions, whereas without it, attention could be drawn to less relevant static or transition actions.
- **Effectiveness of Text Retrieval (Table V and Fig. 4):** Text retrieval, which converts sub-action labels into textual descriptions for the text encoder, contributed a 6.28% improvement in accuracy (from 52.82% to 59.10%). This demonstrates its critical role in leveraging rich semantic information embedded in textual labels. Without text retrieval, attention weights appeared simpler, suggesting a reduced capacity to capture complex semantic information during cross-modal fusion.

In summary, the main findings indicate that integrating sub-action semantics via a cross-modal fusion architecture significantly improves both complete and early action recognition performance. The effectiveness of this approach is strongly linked to the quality of sub-action segmentation, the design of the cross-attention mechanism, and the incorporation of a semantic consistency loss and text retrieval.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents SASI, a framework designed to enhance human action recognition by integrating sub-action semantics. The findings suggest several areas of significance and potential impact:

**Advancement in Human Action Understanding:** SASI addresses the long-standing challenge of action complexity by explicitly modeling the hierarchical structure of human actions. By decomposing complex actions into meaningful sub-actions and leveraging their semantics, the framework moves beyond monolithic action classification, offering a more fine-grained and interpretable understanding of human behavior. This capability enriches the representational power of action recognition systems.

**Improved Early Action Recognition:** A key contribution of this work is its demonstrated superior performance in early action recognition from incomplete motion sequences. This is particularly significant for real-time applications where prompt anticipation of human intent is crucial. By enabling accurate recognition even from partial observations, SASI reduces the latency associated with waiting for complete actions, thereby allowing robotic systems to react proactively rather than reactively.

**Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction (HRI):** The ability of SASI to perform robust early action recognition has a direct and substantial impact on HRI. Proactive robots can anticipate human actions, adapt their behaviors, and offer assistance more seamlessly and safely. This translates to more efficient collaborative tasks, improved human trust and comfort, and reduced risk of collisions or errors in shared workspaces. The framework facilitates a shift from robots merely observing to robots understanding and predicting, which is foundational for truly intelligent HRI.

**Modular and Generalizable Framework:** SASI is designed as a pluggable, bottom-up framework that can augment existing GCN backbones. This modularity means it can be readily integrated into current state-of-the-art skeleton-based recognition pipelines, enhancing their performance without requiring a complete overhaul. Its consistent improvement across different GCN architectures suggests broad applicability and potential for widespread adoption within the research community. The transferability of learned hierarchical relationships to partial observations further underscores its generalizability.

**Guidance for Future Research:** The ablation studies provide critical insights into the components contributing to SASI's performance. Notably, the strong correlation between sub-action segmentation quality and overall recognition accuracy highlights a clear avenue for future improvement. This observation suggests that advancements in segmentation models or the development of end-to-end joint training of segmentation and recognition modules could unlock further significant gains. Furthermore, the reliance on annotated sub-actions points towards future work in unsupervised discovery of action primitives, which would broaden the method's applicability by reducing annotation dependency.

In conclusion, SASI represents a step towards embedding a more sophisticated, semantic, and hierarchical understanding of human actions into robotic perception systems. By doing so, it contributes to the development of more intelligent, proactive, and effective human-robot collaborations in diverse real-world settings.
