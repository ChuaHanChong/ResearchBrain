# SASI: Leveraging Sub-Action Semantics for Robust Early Action Recognition in Human-Robot Interaction

> [!summary] Summary
> The research introduces SASI, a dual-branch framework that integrates sub-action semantics with spatiotemporal kinematic features to improve skeleton-based action recognition. It achieves enhanced accuracy for both complete and early action recognition on partial sequences, facilitating more proactive human-robot interaction.

## Problem

- Conventional action recognition methods treat human actions as monolithic entities, failing to capture their complex, hierarchical structure and limiting interpretability.
- Existing systems struggle with early action recognition from incomplete or partial observations, leading to latency and errors in real-time Human-Robot Interaction (HRI).
- There is a lack of explicit modeling of hierarchical semantic structures at the sub-action level within GCN-based action recognition pipelines for HRI.

## Method

- A dual-branch architecture, **SASI** (==Sub-Action Semantics Integrated cross-modal fusion==), is developed to combine ==GCN-derived kinematic features== with ==sub-action semantic features==.
- A ==Sub-Action Semantics Branch== uses a pre-trained ==segmentation model== and a ==CLIP-style text encoder== to convert sub-action labels into rich ==semantic text embeddings==.
- A ==cross-modal fusion mechanism==, employing ==cross-attention==, integrates the kinematic and semantic features, trained with a multi-task objective including a ==semantic consistency loss==.

## Results

- SASI achieved a **59.65%** accuracy for complete action recognition (100% observation ratio) on the **BABEL dataset**, outperforming baseline skeleton-based methods with a ProtoGCN backbone.
- The framework demonstrated improved early action recognition, achieving **51.71%** accuracy at **25%** observation ratio and **56.97%** at **50%** observation ratio, using a ProtoGCN backbone.
- Ablation studies confirmed that semantic loss improved accuracy by **1.67%**, and text retrieval contributed a **6.28%** gain, while a hypothetical perfect segmentation could yield a **10.99%** improvement over the baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly leveraging the hierarchical semantic structure of human actions through sub-actions provides a more fine-grained and interpretable understanding of complex behaviors.
> - Robust early action recognition from incomplete sequences, driven by sub-action semantics, is crucial for enabling proactive and responsive human-robot interaction.
> - The quality of sub-action segmentation directly impacts overall action recognition performance, indicating that improvements in segmentation are key for further gains.
