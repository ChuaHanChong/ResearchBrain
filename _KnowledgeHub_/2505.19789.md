# What Can RL Bring to VLA Generalization? An Empirical Study

> [!summary] Summary
> An empirical study systematically investigates how reinforcement learning fine-tuning impacts generalization in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models compared to supervised fine-tuning. It demonstrates that RL fine-tuning markedly improves VLA performance in semantic understanding and execution robustness, while maintaining visual robustness.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, primarily trained with supervised fine-tuning (SFT), exhibit poor generalization due to compounding errors and susceptibility to distribution shifts.
- SFT policies struggle to recover from minor deviations from expert trajectories, leading to performance degradation in novel environments and situations.
- A systematic understanding of specific generalization benefits offered by reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning for VLAs, in direct comparison to SFT, was previously lacking.

## Method

- Conducted a systematic empirical study to compare ==RL fine-tuning== against SFT for OpenVLA models on a representative pick-and-place task.
- Designed a comprehensive generalization benchmark to evaluate VLA performance across distinct dimensions: vision, semantics (language and objects), and execution robustness.
- Developed an efficient PPO-based reinforcement learning fine-tuning recipe, incorporating a shared actor-critic backbone, VLA warm-up, and minimal PPO epochs, for practical application.

## Results

- RL fine-tuning significantly improved overall VLA generalization, outperforming the strongest SFT baseline (SFT-16k) by **42.6%** on unseen objects and tables in out-of-distribution tasks.
- RL fine-tuning demonstrated superior performance over SFT in all semantic understanding and execution robustness tasks, while showing comparable performance in visual generalization tasks.
- Qualitative analysis revealed that RL agents learn to perform iterative adjustments and explore a broader action space, enabling successful recovery from errors where SFT agents failed to adapt.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) proved to be the most effective online RL algorithm for VLA fine-tuning among those tested (GRPO, DPO), given challenges like non-stationary robotic environments and sparse rewards.
> - Reinforcement learning enables VLA models to learn robust and adaptive behaviors that extend beyond expert demonstrations, allowing for recovery from perturbations and generalization to novel scenarios.
> - An optimized PPO fine-tuning recipe can achieve practical training efficiency, converging in approximately 42 hours on a single A100 GPU for OpenVLA.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper is situated at the forefront of research into embodied artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. VLAs represent an emerging class of foundation models designed to unify perception, language understanding, and robot control. Recent breakthroughs, exemplified by models like RT-1, RT-2, PaLM-E, and OpenVLA, demonstrate their immense potential for enabling robots to interpret complex natural language instructions and execute diverse manipulation tasks in real-world environments.

However, the prevailing training paradigm for these VLA models heavily relies on supervised fine-tuning (SFT) through behavioral cloning of expert demonstrations. While effective for in-distribution tasks, SFT inherently suffers from limitations, notably susceptibility to compounding errors and poor generalization when faced with even slight distribution shifts. This issue is well-documented in imitation learning, where minor deviations from expert trajectories can lead the policy into unfamiliar states from which it cannot recover, often resulting in performance degradation that scales quadratically with the task horizon.

In parallel, reinforcement learning (RL) has demonstrated transformative success in other large-scale foundation models, particularly Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs). Techniques like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) have enabled LLMs to exhibit enhanced reasoning capabilities, better alignment with human preferences, and superior out-of-distribution performance compared to their SFT-only counterparts. This success has spurred interest in applying similar RL fine-tuning strategies to VLAs, with initial efforts exploring offline RL, human feedback, or online algorithms like PPO, sometimes in multi-stage processes combined with imitation learning.

Despite these pioneering applications, a critical gap remains: a systematic and comprehensive understanding of *what specific generalization benefits* RL fine-tuning confers upon VLAs, especially in direct comparison to SFT baselines, and how these benefits manifest across different dimensions of robotic tasks. Existing works, while showcasing RL's utility, have often not prioritized a detailed breakdown of generalization capabilities. This paper precisely addresses this gap by offering an empirical, systematic study to dissect the generalization properties of RL-fine-tuned VLAs against SFT, thereby providing crucial insights into the strengths and weaknesses of each approach in the context of embodied AI. It aims to bridge the understanding gap between the successes of RL in LLMs/VLMs and its targeted application to the unique challenges of VLA generalization in robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this study stems from the observed limitations of supervised fine-tuning (SFT) in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, particularly concerning their generalization capabilities. While SFT has enabled impressive progress in embodied AI, its inherent reliance on expert demonstrations makes VLA models vulnerable to "compounding errors" and poor performance under distribution shifts—situations where the test environment or task differs from the training data. Reinforcement learning (RL), with its ability to optimize directly for task objectives through trial-and-error, offers a promising alternative to overcome these generalization hurdles by enabling policies to explore beyond expert trajectories and learn robust, corrective behaviors. However, a systematic understanding of RL's specific benefits for VLA generalization, especially in contrast to SFT, has been largely absent.

To address this critical knowledge gap, the paper sets forth several key objectives:

1. **Systematic Investigation of Generalization Benefits:** The primary objective is to empirically investigate the unique benefits that RL fine-tuning brings to VLA generalization compared to SFT. This involves a granular examination across diverse dimensions of task variability.
2. **Development of a Comprehensive Generalization Benchmark:** To facilitate this investigation, the authors aim to establish a rigorous and challenging benchmark tailored to evaluate VLA fine-tuning methods across multiple dimensions. This benchmark includes variations in visual appearance, semantic understanding (language and objects), and execution robustness (robot and object dynamics).
3. **Identification of Effective RL Algorithms for VLAs:** The study seeks to evaluate mainstream RL algorithms previously successful in large-scale models (like LLMs/VLMs), such as PPO, DPO, and GRPO, to determine which are most effective for VLA fine-tuning and to understand the challenges in adapting them to robotic tasks.
4. **Development of an Efficient RL Fine-tuning Recipe:** Based on the identified effective algorithm, another objective is to distill a simple and efficient recipe for PPO-based VLA fine-tuning, considering practical aspects like computational cost and training stability.
5. **Empirical Comparison and Analysis:** Ultimately, the goal is to provide extensive experimental results and deep analyses comparing the generalization performance of RL-fine-tuned VLAs with SFT, thereby identifying where RL excels and offering qualitative insights into the underlying reasons for its performance.

By achieving these objectives, the researchers aim to provide fundamental insights that will inform and accelerate the development of more generalizable and robust embodied agents capable of operating effectively in complex, unstructured real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopted a systematic empirical approach to compare RL fine-tuning and SFT for VLA generalization, centered around a representative robotic task and a state-of-the-art VLA model.

**Base Vision-Language-Action Model:**
The study utilizes **OpenVLA (Kim et al., 2024)** as its foundation model. OpenVLA is an open-source architecture that combines a fused visual encoder (SigLIP and DINOv2) with a Llama-2 7B language backbone. It processes a single RGB image and a natural language instruction at each timestep, mapping them to robot actions. Actions are discretized into 256 bins, similar to RT-2, and output as tokens by the language model. For efficiency, all fine-tuning methods (SFT and RL) employ **Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)** with a rank of 32 for linear layers and fully fine-tune the value head.

**Task Definition:**
The primary task for evaluation is **pick-and-place**, where the agent is instructed to place a specified object from a table into a receptacle. This task is chosen for its representativeness and its suitability for defining distinct dimensions of generalization. The environment is simulated using ManiSkill (Tao et al., 2024) with an 8-DoF WidowX-250S arm. A sparse reward structure is used: 0.1 for grasping, 0.1 for continuously holding the object, and 1.0 for successful placement. An extended "open articulated objects" task using a Franka Panda arm is explored in the appendix for additional dexterity evaluation.

**Generalization Benchmark Design:**
A comprehensive benchmark is introduced to probe VLA generalization across three key dimensions, each with multiple out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios (illustrated in Figure 1 and detailed in Section 5.1 and A.4):

1. **Vision:** Tests robustness to novel visual inputs.
 - **Unseen Table:** New background appearances for the table surface.
 - **Dynamic Texture (weak/strong):** Novel textures overlaid on foreground objects, receptacles, and robot arm with varying transparency.
 - **Dynamic Noise (weak/strong):** Novel textures overlaid on the entire image with varying transparency.

2. **Semantics (Language and Objects):** Challenges the model's understanding of objects and instructions.
 - **Unseen Objects:** Objects not encountered during training.
 - **Unseen Receptacles:** Novel receptacles for placement.
 - **Unseen Instruction Phrasings:** Different linguistic formulations of the "put O on R" instruction.
 - **Multi-Object (seen/unseen):** Presence of multiple objects, requiring selection of the correct one, with either seen or unseen objects.
 - **Distractive Receptacle:** An unused, novel receptacle present in the scene.
 - **Multi-Receptacle (unseen):** Multiple receptacles, requiring selection of the correct, unseen one.

3. **Execution (Action Robustness):** Probes the policy's ability to adapt to physical perturbations and novel robot states.
 - **Unseen Position (Object & Receptacle):** Randomized initial positions beyond the training distribution.
 - **Unseen Robot Init Pose:** Randomized initial joint configurations of the robot arm.
 - **Mid-Episode Object Reposition:** The target object is dynamically moved to a new position during the task.

**Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) Approach:**
SFT policies are trained via behavioral cloning on expert demonstration datasets. These datasets are collected using the MPLib motion planner (Guo et al., 2024). An action filtering technique is applied to remove idling actions, preventing the SFT model from getting stuck. The study investigates the effect of data scale on SFT performance, training models with varying numbers of trajectories (from hundreds to 64k) to establish a strong SFT baseline for comparison. Performance plateaus around 16k trajectories, which is then chosen as the baseline (SFT-16k).

**Reinforcement Learning (RL) Fine-tuning Approach:**
The authors evaluate three representative RL algorithms known for their use with large-scale models:
- **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)** (Schulman et al., 2017): An on-policy algorithm using clipped importance ratios and Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE). A variant disabling GAE (PPO-ORZ) is also tested.
- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)** (Shao etal., 2024): A method that estimates advantages from group-wise sample comparisons, avoiding explicit value estimation.
- **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)** (Rafailov et al., 2023): An offline method that optimizes policies using pairwise preference feedback over trajectories. Adapted to robotic tasks by inferring preferences from sparse reward signals.

**PPO Optimization Recipe for VLAs:**
Given PPO's superior performance in initial comparisons, the study distills an efficient PPO-based fine-tuning recipe:
- **Shared Actor-Critic Backbone:** The VLA policy serves as the actor, and a lightweight three-layer MLP critic is attached, sharing the entire Transformer backbone. The critic takes the hidden vector `h0` from the final Transformer block as input for value estimation, which is found to be the most efficient and stable design.
- **VLA Warm-up:** The official OpenVLA checkpoint is warmed up with 140 motion planner demonstration trajectories to improve initial performance and reduce convergence time.
- **Minimal PPO Epoch:** Experiments show that increasing the PPO epoch beyond one does not yield significant performance gains but linearly increases training time. Therefore, `epoch = 1` is used for optimal efficiency.

The overall methodology is characterized by its systematic comparison, comprehensive benchmark, and practical optimization of RL algorithms for VLA models, ensuring both rigorous evaluation and practical applicability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive empirical study yielded several key findings regarding the effectiveness of RL fine-tuning for VLA generalization, particularly in comparison to supervised fine-tuning (SFT).

#### 4.1. RL Algorithm Effectiveness for VLAs

The initial evaluation of different RL algorithms (PPO, GRPO, DPO) revealed that **PPO consistently outperformed both GRPO and DPO** on the pick-and-place task. GRPO struggled, possibly due to the non-stationary environment dynamics of robotic POMDPs destabilizing its advantage estimates, a contrast to its reported success in natural language generation tasks. DPO also showed limitations, which the authors hypothesize is due to the sparse reward structure making it difficult to infer meaningful trajectory preferences, as well as significant distribution shifts between offline datasets and interactive execution. This finding established PPO as the preferred online RL algorithm for VLA fine-tuning in this context.

#### 4.2. Efficient PPO Recipe

The study successfully developed an efficient PPO-based fine-tuning recipe for OpenVLA, characterized by:
- **Shared Actor-Critic Backbone:** A lightweight critic sharing the VLA's Transformer backbone, specifically using the `h0` hidden vector (first action-token position) as input, proved to be the most efficient and stable design. This approach saves VRAM and training time compared to separate backbones or different token inputs.
- **VLA Warm-up:** Warming up the OpenVLA model with a small set of demonstration trajectories (140) significantly accelerated convergence, reducing environment steps by approximately 50% to reach comparable asymptotic returns.
- **Minimal PPO Epoch:** The analysis showed that setting the PPO epoch hyper-parameter to 1 yielded the fastest training without sacrificing performance, indicating that a low update-to-data ratio is optimal for efficient VLA fine-tuning.

These design choices allowed the PPO fine-tuning to converge in approximately 42 hours on a single NVIDIA A100 GPU, demonstrating practical scalability.

#### 4.3. Effect of Data Scale on SFT Performance

Investigating SFT's scaling behavior with demonstration count, the authors found that OpenVLA's performance on both in-distribution (IND) and out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks (unseen objects/tables) plateaued around **16,000 expert trajectories**. This "SFT-16k" checkpoint was subsequently used as the robust supervised baseline for comparison with RL fine-tuning.

#### 4.4. Performance Comparison between RL and SFT

The core finding is that **RL fine-tuning significantly enhances generalization in semantic understanding and execution robustness over SFT, while maintaining comparable visual robustness.**
- **Overall Generalization:** RL fine-tuning overtook the strongest SFT checkpoint (SFT-16k) on OOD tasks after roughly 0.4 million environment steps. At convergence, RL performed comparably to SFT-16k in training settings but achieved a remarkable **42.6% better performance on unseen objects and tables**.
- **Vision Generalization:** In tasks involving unseen tables, dynamic textures, or dynamic noise, RL fine-tuning showed **comparable performance to SFT**. The authors hypothesize that both methods' visual robustness is primarily driven by the visual randomness introduced during initial training rather than the fine-tuning paradigm itself.
- **Semantics Generalization:** RL notably **outperformed SFT** across all semantic tasks, including unseen objects, receptacles, instruction phrasings, and multi-object/multi-receptacle scenarios. This gain is attributed to RL's trial-and-error learning, which enables it to acquire grasping skills less dependent on specific object types and to generalize better to novel language variations.
- **Execution Generalization:** RL demonstrated **significant superiority over SFT** in all execution tasks. This includes adapting to unseen object and receptacle positions, novel robot initial poses, and critically, **recovering from mid-episode object repositioning**. SFT struggled in these scenarios, likely because such dynamic perturbations are absent in static demonstration datasets, highlighting RL's ability to learn corrective and adaptive behaviors.

#### 4.5. Dissecting RL’s Contributions Qualitatively

Qualitative analysis through trajectory visualizations provided deeper insights. SFT agents often failed to recover from perturbations (e.g., dropping objects repeatedly under strong visual noise, stalling with unseen objects, or continuing along a path despite position errors). In contrast, RL agents demonstrated iterative adjustments, re-targeting, and successful task completion in challenging OOD scenarios. The visual evidence showed that RL trajectories spanned a broader workspace and a richer range of end-effector orientations compared to SFT, which clustered around motion-planner paths. This expanded exploratory behavior appears to be key to RL's superior generalization in execution tasks.

In summary, the study provides compelling empirical evidence that RL fine-tuning, particularly with the optimized PPO approach, is a powerful paradigm for boosting the generalization capabilities of VLA models, especially in aspects of semantic understanding and robust embodied execution that are critical for real-world robotic deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This study makes several significant contributions to the field of embodied AI and holds substantial potential for impact, both academically and practically.

**Academic Significance:**
- **Filling a Critical Knowledge Gap:** The paper directly addresses the previously underexplored area of systematic generalization benefits of RL fine-tuning for VLAs. By providing a comprehensive empirical comparison against SFT across diverse dimensions (vision, semantics, execution), it offers foundational insights into the strengths of RL in robotic learning.
- **Novel Benchmark:** The introduction of a rigorous and challenging benchmark for VLA generalization is a valuable contribution. This benchmark, with its detailed categorization of out-of-distribution scenarios, can serve as a standardized tool for future research, enabling more consistent and thorough evaluation of new VLA models and fine-tuning techniques.
- **Algorithmic Insights:** The comparative analysis of PPO, GRPO, and DPO for VLA fine-tuning provides crucial algorithmic insights. Identifying PPO as the most effective algorithm and articulating the reasons why others struggle (e.g., POMDP challenges for GRPO, sparse reward issues for DPO) guides future algorithmic development for robotics.
- **Efficient Recipe:** The distilled PPO fine-tuning recipe, incorporating shared actor-critic backbones, VLA warm-up, and minimal PPO epochs, offers a practical and computationally efficient methodology. This recipe can accelerate research by providing a strong baseline and best practices for researchers looking to apply RL to large VLA models.

**Practical Significance and Potential Impact:**
- **More Generalizable Embodied Agents:** The core finding—that RL significantly enhances semantic understanding and execution robustness—is critical for deploying robots in real-world environments. Real-world scenarios are inherently characterized by unforeseen variations, novel objects, and dynamic perturbations. RL's ability to handle these distribution shifts means robots can be more adaptable, reliable, and capable of recovering from errors, thus reducing human intervention.
- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy:** By enabling VLAs to learn more robust and generalizable policies, this work moves closer to truly autonomous embodied agents. Robots that can better understand diverse instructions, interact with unseen objects, and adapt to unexpected environmental changes will be able to perform a wider range of tasks in less structured settings.
- **Catalyst for Sim-to-Real Transfer:** While the current study is simulation-based, the enhanced generalization capabilities demonstrated by RL in simulation are a crucial prerequisite for successful sim-to-real transfer. If a policy generalizes poorly in simulation, it is unlikely to perform well in the real world. Thus, the findings pave the way for more effective real-world robot deployments, potentially reducing the extensive real-world data collection typically required. The preliminary real-world experiment with Franka Panda arm already shows RL's superior grasp and pick-and-place success rates over SFT in an OOD real-world setting.
- **Foundation for Complex Tasks:** The demonstrated improvements in generalization, especially in execution robustness, are foundational for tackling more complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks that involve sequential decision-making and error recovery.

**Broader Impacts and Ethical Considerations:**
The advancements in VLA generalization naturally bring broader societal implications. Increased robot autonomy could lead to benefits in assistive robotics, logistics, manufacturing, and hazardous environment exploration, making these technologies safer and more accessible. However, as noted in the "Broader Impacts" section, greater capability also necessitates careful consideration of potential risks. More generalizable robots could be misused in surveillance or privacy-sensitive applications. Deployment in safety-critical scenarios requires robust verification and stringent ethical guidelines to prevent unintended consequences. The authors' acknowledgment of these limitations and recommendation for responsible use are crucial, highlighting the ongoing need for interdisciplinary dialogue on the ethical development and deployment of advanced AI systems.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors transparently acknowledge several limitations, which also point to fruitful avenues for future research:
- **Data Source:** Reliance on motion-planner generated demonstrations for SFT might limit the full scope of variability present in human-collected data. Future work could explore the impact of diverse human demonstrations on both SFT and RL baselines.
- **Task Scope:** The evaluation is primarily confined to pick-and-place tasks (though an articulated object task is explored in the appendix). Scaling to broader, more complex, multi-task settings with longer horizons is an important next step.
- **Simulation-Only Evaluation:** While preliminary real-world experiments are included, the majority of the study is simulation-based. Integrating RL fine-tuning with advanced sim-to-real transfer techniques and rigorous validation on physical robots remains a crucial next step to fully realize the potential of these findings.

In conclusion, this research provides a comprehensive and insightful analysis of RL fine-tuning for VLA generalization, establishing PPO as a leading method and offering practical guidance for its efficient implementation. The findings significantly advance the understanding of how to build more robust and adaptable embodied AI agents, contributing substantially to the progression of robotics and artificial intelligence.
