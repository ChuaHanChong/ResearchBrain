# Efficient On-policy Visual-RL via Stochastic Decoupled Policy Gradient

> [!summary] Summary
> An on-policy visual reinforcement learning method called Stochastic Decoupled Policy Gradient (SDPG) enables efficient training of visuomotor policies on a single GPU by replacing analytical trajectory Jacobians with a stochastic smoothing gradient estimation. This approach allows for robust learning across diverse robotic tasks and achieves successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

## Problem

- Training visual reinforcement learning (RL) policies is computationally and memory intensive, especially for on-policy methods requiring many parallel environments.
- First-order RL methods with differentiable simulators suffer from gradient instability and strictly require full trajectory differentiability, limiting compatibility with common simulators and physical fidelity.
- Simulation-based distillation often leads to suboptimal performance in complex tasks due to information asymmetry and distributional shift, where the student policy encounters out-of-distribution states.

## Method

- **SDPG** replaces analytical trajectory Jacobians with a stochastic smoothing estimation using random perturbations, removing the need for full trajectory differentiability through dynamics.
- The policy improvement step is formulated as a gradient descent on a supervised objective, where the policy learns to map observations to stochastically improved action sequences.
- The method integrates practical components like an actor-critic framework with a low-dimensional state critic, short-horizon rollouts with auxiliary environments, and a dynamically learned exploration factor.

## Results

- SDPG achieved the highest rewards across visual MuJoCo benchmarks, matching state-based performance and outperforming DrQv2 and DreamerV3 in final reward and training speed.
- The method demonstrated memory usage comparable to off-policy approaches (e.g., 10-11 GB) and enabled end-to-end training within hours on a single NVIDIA RTX 4080 GPU.
- Policies trained with SDPG successfully performed zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a Unitree Go2 robot for complex terrain navigation using ego-centric depth camera inputs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Stochastic smoothing gradient estimation provides stable and robust updates, allowing on-policy visual RL to be trained efficiently on a single GPU without requiring full trajectory differentiation.
> - A unified 'guided search' perspective connects various policy gradient methods, which can simplify hyperparameter tuning by decoupling trajectory search from representation learning.
> - Reliable gradient estimates can be achieved with a small number of batched environments, significantly reducing memory consumption for on-policy methods and enabling single-GPU training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of visual reinforcement learning (RL) aims to enable control policies to learn directly from high-dimensional visual inputs, a critical capability for advanced robotics applications such as autonomous navigation and dexterous manipulation. However, training visual RL policies is significantly more resource-intensive than training policies from low-dimensional state information.

Current approaches in visual RL face distinct challenges:
- **Off-policy and Model-based Methods:** Algorithms like DrQV2, TD-MPC2, and DreamerV3 often require extensive sequential updates to learn effective visual representations and world models, leading to slow wall-clock training times.
- **On-policy Methods:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), a popular on-policy algorithm, relies on a large number of parallel environments with batch rendering to collect diverse experience. This necessity results in high memory consumption, making it challenging to scale without access to GPU clusters.
- **Simulation-based Distillation:** An alternative involves training a low-dimensional "teacher" policy in simulation and then having a "visual student" policy imitate it. While efficient in some cases, this approach frequently yields suboptimal performance. This suboptimality arises from information asymmetry, where the student lacks access to privileged state information available to the teacher, and distributional shift, where compounding imitation errors can lead to unrecoverable failures as the student policy diverges from the teacher's experienced states.
- **Differentiable Simulation and First-order RL:** More recent work combines differentiable simulators with first-order RL to train visual policies, aiming for lower-variance gradient estimates and continuous optimization. However, practical adoption of these methods is limited by several factors:
 - **Gradient Instability:** Backpropagating gradients through long-horizon dynamics can lead to severe instability, particularly in contact-rich manipulation tasks.
 - **Differentiability Requirement:** These methods often necessitate full trajectory differentiation, which can reduce physical fidelity when using soft contact models and restrict the use of non-differentiable reward components, which are sometimes crucial for successful sim-to-real transfer.
 - **Simulator Support:** Many widely used robotics simulators provide limited or no direct support for differentiable dynamics and sensor models, limiting flexibility and widespread applicability.

This paper introduces Stochastic Decoupled Policy Gradient (SDPG) as a method designed to address these practical limitations. SDPG positions itself as an efficient, on-policy visual RL algorithm that aims to overcome gradient instability and the strict requirement for full trajectory differentiation, thereby enhancing the accessibility and scalability of end-to-end visual RL on common hardware. It builds upon the concept of decoupled policy gradients but replaces analytical trajectory gradients with a stochastic estimation approach.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient, stable, and practical reinforcement learning method for training visuomotor control policies directly from visual inputs. The authors identify several specific goals:

1. **Enhance Training Efficiency:** To enable the training of diverse visuomotor control policies end-to-end within a few hours on a single GPU, reducing the computational and memory overhead associated with existing visual RL methods. This is motivated by the slow wall-clock training times of off-policy/model-based approaches and the high memory demands of on-policy methods.

2. **Improve Gradient Stability:** To address the issue of gradient instability encountered in first-order RL methods that backpropagate through long-horizon dynamics, especially in contact-rich robotic tasks. The motivation here is to enable more robust and consistent policy learning.

3. **Eliminate Full Trajectory Differentiability:** To remove the strict requirement for differentiating through entire trajectories. This is motivated by the need to:
 - Increase compatibility with a wider range of existing robotics simulators, many of which lack comprehensive differentiable dynamics and sensor models.
 - Allow the use of physically accurate, non-differentiable contact models and other non-differentiable reward components, which can be crucial for achieving high physical fidelity in simulation and bridging the sim-to-real gap.

4. **Achieve High Performance:** To develop a method that consistently outperforms baseline approaches in terms of training speed, memory usage, and the final achieved rewards on standard and novel benchmarks.

5. **Facilitate Future Research in Visual Robotics:** To contribute to the broader research community by introducing a new suite of realistic visual robotics benchmarks. These benchmarks are designed to cover challenging tasks such as dexterous manipulation and complex locomotion, providing diverse scenarios for evaluating and developing future visual RL algorithms.

6. **Demonstrate Sim-to-Real Transferability:** To validate the practical utility of the learned policies by demonstrating effective zero-shot transfer from simulation to physical robot hardware, a critical step for real-world robotic deployment.

In essence, the research is motivated by the practical bottlenecks and limitations of existing visual RL techniques, seeking a lightweight, robust, and accessible solution that can bridge the gap between complex visual inputs and efficient, high-performance robotic control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Stochastic Decoupled Policy Gradient (SDPG) method addresses the challenges of visual RL by introducing a novel approach to policy gradient estimation and integrating several practical design components.

**Core Idea: Stochastic Decoupled Policy Gradient (SDPG)**
SDPG's central innovation is the replacement of analytical trajectory Jacobians (∇_A J), which require full differentiability through dynamics, with a stochastic smoothing estimation.
1. **Gradient Estimation via Random Perturbations:** For a given action sequence `A` generated by the current policy `π_θ`, SDPG generates `M` random perturbations `E_j` (sampled from a Gaussian distribution `N(0, I)`). The corresponding returns `J(A + δE_j)` are then evaluated.
2. **Smoothed Trajectory Gradient:** The trajectory gradient, `∇J_smooth(A)`, is estimated as a weighted average:
 `∇J_smooth(A) ≈ (1/M) Σ_{j=1 to M} [(J(A + δE_j) - J(A))/σ * E_j]`,
 where `δ` is an exploration factor and `σ` is the standard deviation of returns. This estimator is mathematically shown to be proportional to the gradient of a smoothed surrogate objective, `∇_A J_δ(A)`.
3. **Benefits of Smoothing:** This stochastic estimation offers several advantages:
 - **Smoother Updates:** It yields more stable and smoother gradients compared to analytical methods, which can exhibit large spikes (as illustrated in Figure 3).
 - **No Differentiability Requirement:** It eliminates the need for full trajectory differentiation, allowing seamless integration with simulators that do not support differentiable dynamics.
 - **Reduced Computation:** It avoids backward passes through the simulator, effectively reducing computation time.

**Policy Update via Supervised Objective**
The policy improvement step in SDPG is formulated as a gradient descent step on a supervised objective, drawing from a proposition that links certain update rules to supervised learning.
1. **Supervised Loss:** The policy `θ` is updated by minimizing the loss:
 `L(θ, O, A) := E[||π(O | θ) - sg(A + ∇J_smooth(A))||^2_2]`,
 where `O` represents observations and `sg(·)` is the stop-gradient operator. This formulation avoids backpropagating through the rendering pipeline, consistent with the "decoupled policy gradient" idea.
2. **Connection to Classical Policy Gradient:** The authors establish that classical policy gradient (e.g., REINFORCE) can be seen as a special case of SDPG where only one perturbation (`M=1`) is sampled to estimate the gradient. This distinction is crucial for memory-intensive visual RL; SDPG achieves reliable gradient estimates with fewer nominal trajectories (`N`), thus requiring less batch rendering and memory.

**Unified View of Policy Gradient Methods**
The paper presents an alternative "guided search" perspective that unifies various policy-based methods.
1. **Guided Search Analogy:** The decoupled policy gradient is shown to be equivalent to minimizing a behavior cloning loss, `L_BC(θ, O, A_target)`, where `A_target` is an improved action sequence obtained from a single step of trajectory optimization (`A_target = A + α∇_A J(A)`).
2. **Categorization:** This perspective categorizes methods based on the number of trajectory search and behavior cloning iterations per rollout batch. Policy gradient methods (including SDPG) perform one step of trajectory optimization and one supervised update per batch, distinguishing them from traditional guided policy search algorithms that perform multiple iterations.
3. **Hyperparameter Tuning:** This unified view separates hyperparameters related to trajectory search from those for representation learning, which can aid in more efficient hyperparameter tuning.

**Practical Algorithm Design**
Several practical components are integrated to enhance SDPG's stability and performance:
1. **Actor-Critic Learning:** SDPG uses an actor-critic framework. The return estimation is bootstrapped with a value function `V_ϕ(s_H+1)` at the final step of a horizon `H`. The critic `V_ϕ` is trained by minimizing a supervised objective using a standard TD(λ) formulation (`L_ϕ`). The critic operates on low-dimensional privileged states, while the actor uses visual observations. Target networks are employed for critic stability.
2. **Short-horizon Rollout:** To improve throughput and reduce variance, long trajectories are divided into shorter segments. The system runs `N` nominal environments (with batch rendering) and `M` state-only auxiliary environments per nominal. Auxiliary environments are reset to match their corresponding nominal environments at the start of each segment or upon termination, saving computational overhead on rendering.
3. **Learning Exploration Factor:** The exploration factor `δ` (parameterized as `log δ`) is dynamically adapted during training using a supervised objective `L_{tilde_δ}` derived from the policy gradient objective. This ensures numerically stable updates.
4. **Entropy Terms:** To encourage exploration and prevent premature convergence:
 - **Entropy Regularization:** An entropy term is added to the actor's objective.
 - **Soft Critic:** Entropy is also incorporated into critic learning (similar to SAC), where the `k`-step return `J^k_t` is augmented with an entropy reward reflecting deviation from a desired exploration scale.
 - **Temperature Auto-tuning:** An automatic temperature tuning mechanism is used to control the strength of the entropy regularization.
5. **Action Bounds:** The action outputs are processed through a `tanh` function to respect control bounds. To prevent saturation effects from reducing exploration, the pre-activation input to `tanh` is clipped.
6. **Simulation Environment:** All experiments are conducted using the Genesis simulation platform, which provides fast physics and batch rendering capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings across standard benchmarks, a new ego-centric task suite, and sim-to-real experiments.

#### 4.1. Benchmarks (Visual MuJoCo)

SDPG was evaluated on visual MuJoCo benchmarks (Hopper, Walker, Ant, Humanoid), controlling robots from third-person RGB images. Comparisons were made against DrQv2 (off-policy), DreamerV3 (model-based), and Teacher-Student Distillation, all trained on a single NVIDIA RTX 4080 GPU.

- **Performance and Efficiency:**
 - **Highest Rewards:** SDPG consistently achieved the highest rewards across all tasks, matching the performance levels typically seen with state-based inputs.
 - **Wall-Clock Training Time:** SDPG demonstrated competitive training speeds, matching that of teacher-student distillation and being significantly faster than end-to-end visual RL methods like DrQv2 and DreamerV3.
 - **Memory Usage:** Despite being an on-policy method, SDPG exhibited memory usage comparable to off-policy and model-based approaches (e.g., 10-11 GB for SDPG vs. 48-50 GB estimated for PPO). This efficiency is attributed to its ability to achieve accurate gradient estimates with a small number of batched environments (e.g., 64 nominal environments compared to 4096 often required by PPO), enabling training on a single GPU.

- **Comparison with Teacher-Student Distillation:**
 - For moderately difficult tasks, distillation can achieve comparable performance. However, for more challenging problems, particularly humanoid tasks, SDPG showed greater robustness. Distillation-based policies were observed to plateau at suboptimal performance and fail in certain states, attributed to distributional shift where the student encounters states underrepresented in the teacher's training data. SDPG, by learning directly from its own experience, demonstrated more stable and continuous operation in these complex regimes.
 - The paper also noted difficulties in designing effective low-dimensional observations for teacher training (e.g., local height maps for the G1 hurdle task), whereas rich RGB observations naturally provide more comprehensive information.

- **Learning State vs. Vision Inputs:**
 - The guided search perspective facilitated independent tuning of trajectory search-related hyperparameters using faster-iterating low-dimensional state inputs. These hyperparameters were then transferred to visual settings by adding a CNN encoder.
 - The results showed consistent final rewards across both state and visual modalities with identical hyperparameters (apart from the CNN). However, visual inputs typically required 2-4 times more iterations to converge, indicating the overhead of visual representation learning.

#### 4.2. Ego-centric Suite

The authors introduced a new suite of challenging robotics tasks, including dexterous manipulation (e.g., Allegro hand reorienting a cube, ALOHA insertion) and challenging locomotion (e.g., G1 humanoid hurdling, Go2 quadruped on procedural terrain). These tasks featured egocentric images and proprioceptive measurements, mimicking real hardware sensor setups. The method successfully learned control policies for these diverse tasks, generating effective trajectories.

#### 4.3. Sim-to-Real Experiment

SDPG's practical applicability was validated through sim-to-real transfer experiments. A policy trained entirely in simulation for two hours, controlling a Unitree Go2 robot using an ego-centric RealSense depth camera, was successfully deployed on physical hardware. The robot demonstrated zero-shot transfer capability, navigating challenging terrains such as uneven surfaces, boxes, and stairs without further real-world training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Stochastic Decoupled Policy Gradient (SDPG) method presents several significant contributions to the field of visual reinforcement learning and robotics, with notable potential impacts:

1. **Increased Accessibility and Scalability of End-to-End Visual RL:** SDPG enables the training of complex visuomotor control policies end-to-end within hours on a single consumer-grade GPU (NVIDIA RTX 4080). This dramatically lowers the hardware barrier for researchers and practitioners, making advanced visual RL techniques more accessible and scalable to complex robotic settings that previously required GPU clusters or significantly longer training times.

2. **Enhanced Robustness and Performance:** The method consistently achieves high rewards on challenging tasks, matching state-based performance and outperforming other end-to-end visual RL methods in terms of final reward and training speed. Its robustness in situations where distillation methods falter (e.g., due to distributional shift in complex humanoid tasks) suggests it provides a more reliable approach for learning in diverse and potentially underspecified environments.

3. **Greater Flexibility in Simulator Usage:** By eliminating the requirement for full trajectory differentiation, SDPG allows seamless integration with a broader range of existing robotics simulators, including those that do not support differentiable dynamics. This flexibility also permits the use of physically accurate contact models and non-differentiable reward components, which can be crucial for bridging the sim-to-real gap and achieving high fidelity.

4. **Unified Theoretical Perspective:** The introduction of a unified "guided search" perspective for policy gradient methods provides a novel conceptual framework. This perspective can aid in understanding the relationships between different RL algorithms, guide hyperparameter tuning by explicitly separating trajectory search and representation learning, and potentially inform the design of future algorithms, for instance, by facilitating integration with high-capacity architectures like diffusion models.

5. **Support for Future Robotics Research:** The release of a new suite of realistic egocentric visual robotics benchmarks (covering dexterous manipulation and challenging locomotion) provides valuable resources for the research community. These benchmarks offer diverse and complex scenarios for the development, evaluation, and comparison of new visual RL algorithms.

6. **Practical Real-World Applicability:** The demonstrated zero-shot sim-to-real transfer capability on a physical Unitree Go2 robot for complex terrain navigation validates SDPG's potential for real-world robotic deployment. This is a critical step towards deploying learned visual policies outside of simulation.

In summary, SDPG offers a practical and efficient solution to several long-standing challenges in visual RL, making end-to-end learning from pixels more viable for a wider array of robotic applications and research endeavors.
