# Test-Time Prompt Tuning for Zero-Shot Generalization in Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Test-Time Prompt Tuning (TPT) introduces a method to adapt vision-language models like CLIP to individual test samples by optimizing only the text prompt embeddings, without requiring any training data or labels. It significantly enhances zero-shot generalization across natural distribution shifts and unseen categories, outperforming data-dependent prompt tuning methods on benchmarks like ImageNet-A by 3.6% average accuracy gain.

## Problem

- Existing prompt tuning methods (e.g., CoOp, CoCoOp) require labeled downstream training data, which compromises true zero-shot applicability and can limit generalization to out-of-distribution data.
- Hand-crafted prompts for Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are often suboptimal and necessitate extensive domain expertise, hindering the full potential of pre-trained foundation models.
- Test-time adaptation methods that fine-tune model parameters risk distorting the generalized features learned by large foundation models during their extensive pre-training.

## Method

- **TPT** optimizes only the ==text prompt embeddings== for a single test sample at inference time, using an ==unsupervised objective== tailored for the task (e.g., consistency for classification, cross-entropy for reasoning).
- For image classification, **TPT** generates multiple ==augmented views== of a test image and optimizes the prompt to minimize the ==entropy of the averaged prediction== probability distribution across these views.
- A ==confidence selection== module filters out low-confidence augmented views (those with high prediction entropy) before averaging, ensuring the optimization is guided by more reliable samples.

## Results

- TPT improves CLIP's zero-shot Top-1 accuracy by an average of **3.6%** on natural distribution shifts (ImageNet-A, -V2, -R, -Sketch), with up to **6.9%** gain on ImageNet-A (CLIP-RN50).
- It matches or surpasses data-dependent prompt tuning methods (CoOp, CoCoOp) on OOD benchmarks, notably outperforming them in cross-dataset generalization to truly unseen categories where other methods fail.
- TPT achieved state-of-the-art results on the Bongard-HOI benchmark for context-dependent visual reasoning, outperforming the previous best method, HOITrans, by an average of **4.1%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimizing only the prompt embedding, rather than model parameters, effectively extracts knowledge from the foundation model without distorting its pre-trained, generalized features.
> - A consistency objective across augmented views of a single test sample, coupled with confidence-based filtering, provides a robust unsupervised signal for test-time prompt adaptation.
> - Test-time adaptation of prompts can achieve competitive or superior performance compared to data-dependent prompt tuning methods, especially in scenarios with true zero-shot constraints or significant distribution shifts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper operates at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields in artificial intelligence: **Foundation Models**, **Vision-Language Models (VLMs)**, **Prompt Engineering/Tuning**, **Zero-Shot Generalization**, and **Test-Time Adaptation**.

**Foundation Models and Vision-Language Models:** The emergence of large-scale, pre-trained models like CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training) and ALIGN has revolutionized AI, demonstrating remarkable "zero-shot generalization" capabilities. These models, trained on vast quantities of diverse data, can perform novel tasks without explicit fine-tuning on task-specific data. This work builds directly upon CLIP, leveraging its rich pre-trained knowledge.

**Prompt Engineering and Tuning:** To harness the power of foundation models, particularly VLMs, "prompts" are crucial. Initially, these were hand-crafted text instructions (e.g., "a photo of a [category]"). While effective, hand-crafted prompts are suboptimal and require domain-specific heuristics. This led to the development of "prompt tuning" methods (e.g., CoOp, CoCoOp), which learn prompts from downstream *training data*. These methods improve performance over hand-crafted prompts but introduce a critical limitation: they still require labeled training data, thereby compromising true zero-shot application and often leading to reduced generalization to out-of-distribution (OOD) data. CoCoOp, for instance, attempts to address generalization but remains reliant on training data for input-dependent prompt generation.

**Zero-Shot Generalization and Distribution Shifts:** A major challenge for machine learning models is maintaining performance under "data distribution shifts," where test data differs from training data. CLIP has shown initial promise in this area. This paper directly addresses the need to *further enhance* CLIP's zero-shot generalization and robustness, especially when encountering natural distribution shifts or entirely new categories across datasets.

**Test-Time Adaptation/Optimization:** The idea of adapting models on-the-fly to test samples is gaining traction (e.g., TENT, MEMO, Test-Time Training). Existing test-time methods often involve:
 - **Batch-level adaptation:** Requiring multiple test samples (e.g., TENT's entropy minimization on batches).
 - **Parameter fine-tuning:** Optimizing parts of the model (e.g., Batch Normalization layers, feature extractors, or even the entire network, as in MEMO). A significant drawback of fine-tuning model parameters, especially for foundation models, is the risk of "distorting pre-trained features," leading to a loss of their broad generalization capabilities.

**Novel Contribution:** The key innovation of this work, Test-Time Prompt Tuning (TPT), differentiates itself by combining the strengths of prompt tuning with test-time adaptation in a novel way. Unlike prior prompt tuning methods, TPT operates *without any training data or annotations*, making it truly zero-shot. Crucially, it adapts prompts *for a single test sample*, which is distinct from batch-level test-time adaptation methods. Furthermore, by optimizing *only the prompt embeddings* and leaving the core VLM (CLIP) intact, TPT aims to extract knowledge more effectively without compromising the model's fundamental pre-trained generalization, addressing the "distortion" problem seen in parameter-tuning adaptation methods. This positions TPT as a significant step towards developing truly adaptive and robust foundation models for real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of Test-Time Prompt Tuning (TPT) are:

1. **Enhance Zero-Shot Generalization of Foundation Models:** The overarching goal is to maximize the utility and robustness of pre-trained vision-language foundation models, specifically CLIP, in truly zero-shot settings. This means improving their ability to perform well on new tasks, unseen categories, and under natural distribution shifts, without any task-specific training data.

2. **Overcome Limitations of Current Prompting Strategies:**
 - **Suboptimal Hand-Crafted Prompts:** While simple, manually designed prompts require significant domain expertise and are rarely optimal for a given task, limiting the full potential of VLMs.
 - **Data-Dependent Prompt Tuning (e.g., CoOp, CoCoOp):** These methods learn prompts from downstream *training data*. The authors argue that this approach:
 - **Reduces Zero-Shot Purity:** Requires labeled data, which can be expensive and is often unavailable in true zero-shot scenarios.
 - **Limits Generalization:** Prompts learned on specific training data may not generalize well to out-of-distribution data or entirely unseen domains, thereby hindering the foundation model's inherent robustness. This is a crucial point, as the benefits of foundation models often lie in their broad applicability.

3. **Develop a Truly Adaptive and Data-Independent Prompt Optimization Method:** The core motivation is to create a mechanism that can adapt the prompt to the specific context of *each individual test sample*, on-the-fly, without needing any prior training data or annotations. This would unlock the "context-tailored knowledge retrieval" from the foundation model for every single inference.

4. **Preserve Pre-trained Features:** Unlike methods that fine-tune model parameters (e.g., feature extractors or batch normalization layers), the authors explicitly aim to avoid "distorting" the rich, generalized features learned by the foundation model during its massive pre-training. They hypothesize that prompt tuning, by working *outside* the pre-trained model on the input context, is an ideal handle to achieve this preservation while still enhancing performance.

In essence, TPT seeks to bridge the gap between the broad knowledge of foundation models and the specific requirements of individual test instances, delivering superior zero-shot generalization without compromising the model's integrity or requiring additional labeled data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TPT's methodology centers on optimizing text prompts in real-time for a single test sample, employing an unsupervised objective. The approach is tailored for different downstream tasks.

**Core Principle: Test-Time Prompt Optimization for a Single Sample**
The fundamental idea of TPT is to tune the text prompt `p` based solely on the input test sample `X_test`, without relying on any labels or a dataset of training examples. This is formulated as `p* = arg min_p L(F, p, X_test)`, where `L` is a carefully constructed unsupervised loss function.

**Why Optimize Prompts?**
The authors argue that prompts are an ideal choice for test-time adaptation because they modify the *input context* to the pre-trained model rather than altering the model's internal parameters. This strategy aims to leverage the extensive knowledge within the foundation model (CLIP) more effectively while preserving its pre-trained features and broad zero-shot generalization capabilities, which might otherwise be compromised by fine-tuning model weights.

**TPT for Image Classification**
For image classification, TPT designs an unsupervised objective that promotes consistency in predictions:
1. **Data Augmentation:** Given a single test image `X_test`, `N` randomly augmented views (`A_1(X_test), ..., A_N(X_test)`) are generated using a family of random augmentations (e.g., random resized crops).
2. **Consistency Objective (Entropy Minimization):** The prompt `p` is optimized to minimize the marginal entropy of the *averaged prediction probability distribution* across these `N` augmented views. The idea is that a robust and well-adapted prompt should lead to consistent, high-confidence predictions across semantically similar (augmented) versions of the same input. The objective is:
 `p* = arg min_p - Σ_i p̃_p(y_i|X_test) log p̃_p(y_i|X_test)`
 where `p̃_p(y_i|X_test)` is the average probability of class `y_i` across all augmented views for the current prompt `p`.
3. **Confidence Selection:** To mitigate noise from potentially misleading or heavily distorted augmentations, TPT introduces a "confidence selection" module. Augmented views with high prediction entropy (i.e., low confidence) are filtered out before averaging. This is achieved by setting a threshold `τ` based on a `ρ`-percentile of the self-entropy values of all augmented views. Only views with entropy below `τ` are included in the average probability calculation, ensuring that the consistency objective is guided by more reliable augmented samples.
4. **Optimization Strategy:** The prompt `p` (specifically, its embedding in the text embedding space) is optimized for a single step using the AdamW optimizer. For image classification experiments, the authors used 63 augmented views plus the original image (total 64) and selected the top 10% most confident views.

**TPT for Context-Dependent Visual Reasoning (Bongard-HOI)**
For tasks like Bongard-HOI, where the correct prediction depends on a set of support examples rather than absolute labels:
1. **Input:** A test sample consists of two sets of support images (positive and negative examples of a human-object interaction concept) and a query image.
2. **Objective:** TPT learns an optimal prompt prefix `p` and binary class tokens `cls` = `{cls_1, cls_2}`. These are jointly optimized on the *support images* using a cross-entropy loss. The support images are treated with their binary (positive/negative) labels. This allows TPT to explicitly learn the visual concept demonstrated by the support set in the form of text prompts.
3. **Zero-Shot Definition:** Even though support images are used, the approach is still considered zero-shot because no training tasks from other concepts or annotations for the query image are used to update the prompt.
4. **Optimization Strategy:** Prompt prefix and class tokens are initialized randomly and optimized for 64 steps on the support images using AdamW.

The vision-language foundation model used throughout the paper is CLIP, specifically evaluated with both ResNet-50 (RN50) and ViT-B/16 backbones.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents compelling evidence for TPT's effectiveness across various generalization challenges:

**Robustness to Natural Distribution Shifts (ImageNet Variants):**
- **Significant Improvement:** TPT boosts the zero-shot Top-1 accuracy of CLIP by an average of 3.6% over a hand-crafted prompt baseline on ImageNet-A, ImageNet-V2, ImageNet-R, and ImageNet-Sketch. The most substantial gain was 6.9% on ImageNet-A for CLIP-RN50.
- **Superior to Data-Dependent Prompt Tuning:** TPT, *without using any training data*, achieves accuracy on par with or surpasses previous prompt tuning methods like CoOp and CoCoOp, which require 16-shot training data per category on ImageNet. For instance, on ImageNet-A (CLIP-RN50), TPT (26.67%) significantly outperforms CoOp (23.06%) and CoCoOp (23.32%).
- **Complementary Nature:** Applying TPT on top of prompts learned by CoOp or CoCoOp further improves performance (e.g., TPT + CoOp achieves 49.55% average OOD accuracy with CLIP-RN50, compared to 46.61% for CoOp alone). This indicates that TPT can enhance *any* pre-defined prompt.

**Cross-Dataset Generalization (10 Fine-Grained Datasets):**
- **Competitive Performance:** When generalizing from ImageNet to fine-grained datasets, TPT achieves on-par performance with ImageNet-trained CoCoOp, again without requiring any training data.
- **Robust to Unseen Categories:** In a more challenging setting where source and target datasets have no category overlap, data-dependent prompt tuning methods (CoOp, CoCoOp) *underperform* the zero-shot CLIP baseline. In stark contrast, TPT consistently shows positive accuracy improvements across all 10 datasets, demonstrating its superior ability to generalize to truly unseen categories and domains.

**Context-Dependent Visual Reasoning (Bongard-HOI Benchmark):**
- **State-of-the-Art Results:** TPT adapted for visual reasoning significantly outperforms previous methods, including the state-of-the-art HOITrans, by an average of 4.1% on the Bongard-HOI benchmark (66.59% for TPT vs. 62.46% for HOITrans with CLIP-RN50). This extends TPT's applicability beyond standard image classification.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Parameter Group Optimization:** Optimizing the text prompt yields the highest performance gain compared to optimizing other parameter groups of CLIP (entire model, text encoder, or visual encoder). Optimizing the visual encoder resulted in the worst performance, empirically supporting the hypothesis that fine-tuning image encoders can distort pre-trained features.
- **Confidence Selection:** This module significantly contributes to TPT's performance. It brings non-trivial improvements (e.g., +3.02% on ImageNet-A for CLIP-RN50) over TPT without it. An optimal cutoff percentile of ~10% for selecting high-confidence augmented views was found. The benefits of confidence selection were also shown to generalize to other entropy-based test-time optimization methods like MEMO.
- **Efficiency vs. Accuracy:**
 - **Number of Augmented Views:** Accuracy increases with the number of views, plateauing around N=64. Even with N=8, TPT still provides substantial gains, indicating adaptability for more efficient applications.
 - **Number of Optimization Steps:** A single optimization step captures most of the performance gains. Increasing to two steps provides only a marginal improvement, highlighting TPT's computational efficiency at test time.

**Qualitative Analysis:**
- TPT effectively makes the model's predictions more consistent across different augmented views of a test sample, leading to more confident and accurate final predictions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Test-Time Prompt Tuning (TPT) represents a significant advancement in the field of vision-language models and their practical application, with several key points of significance and potential impact:

1. **True Zero-Shot Generalization:** TPT fulfills the promise of true zero-shot generalization by enabling foundation models to adapt to individual test samples *without any task-specific training data or annotations*. This is a crucial distinction from previous prompt tuning methods that, despite being "prompt-based," still required labeled downstream training data, thus limiting their real-world zero-shot applicability.

2. **Enhanced Robustness to Distribution Shifts:** By demonstrating substantial improvements in accuracy on natural distribution shifts (e.g., ImageNet-A, -R, -Sketch) and superior cross-dataset generalization to unseen categories, TPT makes foundation models more robust and reliable in dynamic, open-world environments where data distributions are constantly evolving.

3. **Preservation of Foundation Model Knowledge:** A key insight of TPT is that by optimizing only the text prompt embeddings, it avoids altering the pre-trained weights of the vision-language model (CLIP). This is vital because fine-tuning model parameters can "distort" the rich, generalized features learned during massive pre-training, leading to a loss of out-of-distribution performance. TPT offers a parameter-efficient and non-destructive way to extract knowledge.

4. **Complementary and Versatile Approach:** TPT is not just an alternative but also a complementary technique. It can further boost the performance of prompts initially derived from other methods (like CoOp or CoCoOp), indicating its broad applicability and potential for integration into existing workflows. Its success on both image classification and complex context-dependent visual reasoning (Bongard-HOI) demonstrates its versatility.

5. **Efficiency at Inference:** While it involves a one-step backpropagation, the ablation studies show that TPT is remarkably efficient, with most gains achieved in a single optimization step and with a reasonable number of augmented views. This makes it practical for many real-time or near real-time inference scenarios.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge that TPT has some limitations:
- **Inference Time and Memory:** The one-step backpropagation and the generation of multiple augmented views increase computational cost and memory usage during inference compared to standard feedforward inference.
- **Task-Specific Objective Design:** While TPT provides a general framework, designing an effective test-time objective still requires careful consideration for the specific nature of each downstream task.

These limitations also open avenues for future research:
- **Application to Other Foundation Models:** Extending TPT to other vision-language models (e.g., BLIP, GLIP) or even large-scale language models (LLMs like BERT, GPT) could further enhance their zero-shot capabilities.
- **More Efficient Optimization:** Research into reducing the memory footprint and improving the computational efficiency of TPT at test time could make it even more broadly applicable.
- **Automated Objective Design:** Developing methods for automatically discovering optimal test-time objectives for diverse tasks would further democratize its use.

In summary, TPT represents a significant step towards unlocking the full zero-shot potential of foundation models, offering a robust, adaptive, and data-independent method that can profoundly impact the development of more generalized and reliable AI systems.
