# Learning a Shape-Conditioned Agent for Purely Tactile In-Hand Manipulation of Various Objects

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Technical University of Munich and DLR Institute of Robotics & Mechatronics developed a purely tactile, shape-conditioned agent capable of reorienting diverse objects in-hand to specified target orientations. The system, which uses Basis Point Sets (BPS) for shape representation and a learned recurrent state estimator, achieved zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and demonstrated comparable manipulation success rates on novel objects to state-of-the-art object-specific methods.

## Problem

- Existing robotic in-hand manipulation methods are often object-specific or rely heavily on continuous visual supervision, limiting generalization and robustness in complex environments.
- Purely tactile manipulation faces challenges in accurately estimating an object's absolute pose from touch alone, often restricting strategies to fixed axes or requiring human intervention.
- Goal-oriented manipulation of diverse objects without external visual sensors is difficult in environments with occlusions, poor lighting, or clutter.

## Method

- A single, ==shape-conditioned reinforcement learning== agent is developed, comprising a policy and a ==recurrent state estimator==, operating solely on tactile (joint torque and position) feedback.
- Object shape information is dynamically encoded using ==Basis Point Sets (BPS)== transformed by the estimated object pose, providing geometric context to the policy and state estimator.
- The policy and state estimator are concurrently trained using an ==Estimator-Coupled Reinforcement Learning (ECRL)== approach, where the policy learns based on the estimator's predicted state.

## Results

- The tactile agent achieved comparable success rates for reorienting novel, out-of-distribution objects to those reported by state-of-the-art object-specific manipulation methods.
- Successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer was demonstrated, with real-world robot performance on both training and unseen objects closely matching simulation results.
- Ablation studies showed that omitting shape information entirely from the agent led to a substantial performance drop, including a **30%** relative decrease in success rate and an **89%** increase in final angle error.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically transformed Basis Point Sets (BPS) serve as a highly effective shape representation for tactile manipulation, outperforming other encodings and facilitating generalization across diverse objects.
> - Purely tactile pose estimation and manipulation are notably more challenging for round objects compared to non-convex shapes, where tactile cues for absolute orientation are less distinct.
> - The learned manipulation strategies are critically dependent on accurate shape conditioning; incorrect object shape information substantially degrades the agent's performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic in-hand manipulation, particularly the reorientation of diverse objects, presents significant challenges. Existing methods often fall into two primary categories: those that are highly specific to a single object, requiring extensive retraining for new items, or those that rely on continuous visual supervision to track the object's state. While reinforcement learning (RL) has shown promise in this area, most prior work has focused on continuous rotation about a single axis or multi-axis manipulation for specific, predefined objects.

Recent advancements have enabled single policies to manipulate multiple, even novel, objects by leveraging visual information, typically in the form of depth images or point clouds. These vision-based approaches implicitly provide shape information but necessitate the use of external camera sensors, which can be a limiting factor in many real-world industrial or cluttered environments due to occlusions or lighting conditions.

Conversely, purely tactile manipulation, which relies solely on force and position feedback from the robot hand, is considered more challenging. Previous tactile work has demonstrated in-hand rotation of different objects around fixed axes but has often required human operators to judge object orientation and guide subsequent actions, rather than autonomously achieving desired goals. The difficulty of accurately estimating an object's absolute pose from tactile feedback alone often restricts the complexity of manipulation strategies.

This paper addresses a critical gap in the existing research by developing a method for purely tactile, goal-oriented in-hand reorientation of *diverse objects* using a *single, shape-conditioned agent*. This approach aims to emulate human-like dexterity and generalization capabilities without relying on external visual sensors or supporting surfaces, thus offering a more robust and adaptable solution for scenarios where visual feedback is unavailable or unreliable. By explicitly incorporating object shape information, which is often available in industrial contexts, the work explores how this data can facilitate learning and generalization in a purely tactile setting, moving beyond object-specific or vision-dependent paradigms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable a multi-fingered robot hand to autonomously reorient diverse objects to specified target orientations solely through tactile feedback. This involves developing a shape-conditioned agent capable of dexterous in-hand manipulation without relying on external visual sensors or supporting surfaces.

The key motivations and more specific objectives driving this work are:

- **Addressing the Gap in Dexterity:** Current robotic manipulation methods are often limited either to specific objects or require constant visual monitoring. This disparity with human capabilities, where individuals can manipulate various objects by feel alone, highlights a need for more generalized and sensor-independent robotic solutions. The authors aim to bridge this gap by enabling robots to reorient diverse objects purely through touch.
- **Leveraging Available Shape Information:** In many industrial applications, the 3D mesh of an object to be manipulated is known. The authors are motivated to investigate how this explicit shape information can be effectively integrated into learning-based manipulation approaches, particularly reinforcement learning, to enhance performance and generalization.
- **Developing Robust Shape Representation:** A crucial objective is to identify and validate a 3D shape representation that is well-suited for facilitating the learning process of reinforcement learning agents and promoting generalization to novel objects. The paper specifically explores the effectiveness of Basis Point Sets (BPS) in a dynamic context.
- **Extending Tactile Manipulation Capabilities:** Building upon previous work that focused on object-specific tactile manipulation, a core objective is to extend these methods to a single, shape-conditioned agent that can handle a wide variety of objects, including those not encountered during training. This moves beyond the limitations of policies tailored to individual objects.
- **Autonomous Goal-Oriented Reorientation:** Unlike prior tactile work that might require human intervention for judging object orientation or defining rotation axes, this research aims for fully autonomous, goal-conditioned reorientation. This necessitates the robot's ability to estimate the object's absolute pose from tactile feedback alone and execute deliberate manipulation strategies to achieve a desired target orientation.
- **Achieving Generalization and Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The authors intend to demonstrate that the learned shape-conditioned agents can achieve high success rates comparable to object-specific state-of-the-art methods, generalize effectively to novel objects outside their training distribution, and exhibit successful zero-shot transfer from simulation to a real-world robot system.

By pursuing these objectives, the research seeks to advance the capabilities of robotic in-hand manipulation towards greater autonomy, versatility, and robustness in challenging, sensor-limited environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method focuses on training a shape-conditioned agent to reorient objects in-hand using purely tactile feedback. This involves a reinforcement learning policy coupled with a learned state estimator.

**A. Problem Formulation**
The task is defined as reorienting an object from an initial grasp to a target orientation R_g in SO(3), without external supporting surfaces. The only sensor inputs are torque and position measurements from the robot hand's joints. The system requires the 3D mesh of the object in its canonical pose (M) and its initial orientation (R_0) relative to the hand frame.

**B. Representing Shapes for In-Hand Manipulation**
The agent is conditioned on a shape encoding S = S(x, R, M), which represents the object's geometry based on its canonical mesh (M) and its current 3D pose (x, R). The pose information is obtained either from a visual tracking system (for oracle experiments) or a learned tactile state estimator.
The chosen shape representation, **Basis Point Sets (BPS)**, is extended for dynamic objects:
- **Basis Point Sets (BPS):** A fixed set of N_b basis points {p_k} is defined. For each p_k, a vector v_k is computed pointing to the closest point on the object's surface. These vectors are concatenated to form the BPS encoding V.
- **Dynamic BPS:** The calculation of v_k is adapted for dynamic objects by computing the closest point on the *transformed* mesh (M, x, R), which accounts for the object's current position (x) and orientation (R). If a basis point lies inside the transformed mesh, its vector v_k is set to zero. This representation, V_t = B(x_t, R_t, M), provides information about the object's geometry relative to the fixed basis points. In experiments, N_b = 4^3 = 64 basis points are uniformly placed on a 3D grid.

**C. Shape-Conditioned Agent Architecture**
The agent consists of two main components: a policy (π) and a state estimator (f).
- **Policy (π):** The policy outputs N_dof = 12 target joint angles (q_d) for the hand.
 - **Inputs:**
 - Relative rotation to the goal (R_∆).
 - Shape representation (S = V_t), computed from the *estimated* object pose.
 - A stack of N_s = 6 joint measurements (q) and control errors (e = q_d - q) sampled over 0.1 seconds at 60 Hz.
 - Predicted uncertainties (σ) from the state estimator, if available.
 - **Output:** Target joint angles (q_d) at 10 Hz, which are low-pass filtered and sent to a high-fidelity impedance controller operating at 1000 Hz.

**D. Learning the State Estimator**
A recurrent state estimator (f) is trained to predict the object's state based on the history of joint measurements, incorporating shape information.
- **Recurrent State:** Composed of estimated object position (ˆx), estimated orientation (ˆR), and a latent state vector (l).
- **Prediction Mechanism:** At each timestep, the BPS feature vector (V_t-1) is computed from the *estimated* previous object pose (ˆx_t-1, ˆR_t-1) and mesh (M). This, along with the previous latent state (l_t-1) and current joint measurements (z_t), is fed into a learnable function f_φ which predicts the residual in object state (δ_t) for the next timestep.
- **Loss Function:** For supervised training, the loss function (L) is based on the negative log-likelihood of distances under a Normal distribution. It penalizes:
 - The difference in magnitudes between ground truth BPS vectors (v_k) and those computed from the estimated pose (d_v). This metric is chosen for its continuity with respect to pose changes and its invariance to object symmetries.
 - The angular difference between the absolute predicted and ground truth object rotations (d(ˆR, R)).
 - Uncertainties (σ_v, σ_R) for these distances are predicted by a feed-forward network (f_σ) from the latent state (l_t).
- **Differentiability:** The BPS calculation is integrated into the computation graph, allowing backpropagation through it during training.

**E. Reinforcement Learning Task**
The RL task rewards actions that reduce the angle to the goal orientation and penalizes unwanted object movements.
- **Reward Function:** r_t = λ_θ * min(θ_t-1 - θ_t, θ_clip) - λ_x * (||x_t - x_0|| - ||x_t-1 - x_0||) - λ_q * ||(q_t - ¯q_0)_4||_1.
 - Positive reward for decreasing the angle (θ) to the goal.
 - Penalties for deviations of object position (x_t) from its initial value (x_0) and joint angles (q_t) from a nominal initial configuration (¯q_0).
 - Clipping the rotation reward encourages slower, more controlled movements.
- **Episode Setup:** A new goal orientation (R_g) is uniformly sampled for each episode. Objects are initialized in the hand using a grasping heuristic.
- **Success Criteria:** An episode is successful if the final angle to the goal (θ_T) is less than 0.4 radians after 10 seconds. Dropping the object constitutes a failure.

**F. Training the Agent (Estimator-Coupled Reinforcement Learning - ECRL)**
- The policy (using Proximal Policy Optimization - PPO) and the state estimator are trained concurrently.
- During training, the policy receives the *predicted state* from the estimator, not ground truth. This encourages the policy to learn strategies that avoid object movements leading to unobservable or unpredictable states for the tactile estimator.
- **Simulation Environment:** GPU-accelerated Isaac Sim is used, with substantial domain randomization (object mass, friction, control parameters, measurement noise) to facilitate sim-to-real transfer.
- **Implementation Details:** PPO implementation based on, D2RL architecture for policy network. BPS calculations are performed by a custom, differentiable Warp Kernel for parallelization.
- Training takes approximately 60 hours for tactile agents on a single NVIDIA T4 GPU, utilizing 4096 parallel simulation instances across diverse objects.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings from extensive simulation experiments and real-world validations, comparing different shape representations and evaluating the performance and generalization capabilities of the purely tactile, shape-conditioned agents.

**A. Object Datasets**
The agents were trained on "GEOMETRIC 8" objects, which include various convex and non-convex shapes with randomized axis-independent scaling. Generalization was assessed using five "Out-of-Distribution" (OOD) objects, including the YCB apple.

**B. Comparing Shape Representations (Oracle Agents)**
In a simplified setting where the object pose was known to the policy (oracle agent, no state estimator needed), various shape encodings were compared:
- **VecBPS** (BPS vectors) and **BPS** (BPS vector magnitudes) consistently outperformed other representations. Their performance benefit increased with the complexity of the object dataset, from single objects to diverse geometric sets.
- **PointNet** encoding showed only moderate improvements over omitting shape information entirely.
- Providing the policy with only the object's pose (**PosRot**) did not improve performance over providing only the position (**Pos**), and sometimes increased variance. Adding the canonical mesh alongside the pose (**Mesh**) also did not yield improvement. This highlights the effectiveness of transforming the BPS representation according to the current pose.
- Position information (**Pos**) was beneficial, likely signaling when the object was close to dropping or sliding excessively.

**C. Benchmarking Purely Tactile Shape-Conditioned Agents**
For agents operating with the learned state estimator and relying purely on tactile feedback, using the VecBPS encoding:
- **Performance vs. Oracle:** Tactile agents generally had higher final angle errors compared to oracle agents.
- **Impact of Object Geometry:** A notable difference emerged for round objects (e.g., cylinder, 26-sided "sphere"). While oracle agents performed exceptionally well on these, tactile agents (especially with shape information) showed significantly lower success rates on round objects (almost 25% lower) compared to non-convex objects. This indicates that estimating the absolute pose of round objects purely from tactile feedback is particularly challenging, even with shape information. Conversely, shape information proved most beneficial for manipulating non-convex shapes in the tactile setting.
- **Generalization to OOD Objects:** The tactile agent demonstrated comparable success rates for the GEOMETRIC 8 training objects and the OOD objects, suggesting good generalization to novel objects with similar properties. The agent successfully manipulated the OOD apple, achieving success rates similar to state-of-the-art object-specific agents for this object, which typically requires deliberate strategies to avoid estimator drift.

**D. Real-World Experiments**
Zero-shot sim-to-real transfer was validated using the torque-controlled DLR-Hand II. For a selection of training and OOD objects, real-world success rates closely matched simulation results, confirming the robustness of the simulation setup and learned policies.

**E. Assessing the Limits of OOD Generalization**
Testing on highly complex OOD objects (with small features and high curvature) revealed a performance gap between the purely tactile agent and the oracle policy. While the oracle handled these well, the tactile agent's performance was lower. This is hypothesized to be due to increased sensitivity of the control strategy to small inaccuracies in the estimated object pose for complex geometries, potentially leading to permanent loss of observability.

**F. Sensitivity to Shape Conditioning**
Providing incorrect shape information (e.g., wrong scale or an entirely different mesh) significantly decreased the agent's performance. This finding confirms that the learned manipulation strategies are heavily dependent on accurate shape conditioning. Some objects showed correlation in performance when conditioned on similar incorrect meshes, but for complex OOD objects, precise shape conditioning was vital.

**G. Ablations**
Ablation studies on the tactile agent demonstrated:
- Omitting the estimated uncertainty (σ) as input to the policy led to a slight performance decrease.
- Stopping the gradient flow through the BPS calculation during estimator training also resulted in a small performance reduction.
- **Omitting shape information entirely from the system (both policy and estimator) caused a substantial drop in performance (30% relative decrease in success rate, 89% increase in final angle error).** This highlights the critical role of shape information for learning accurate tactile state estimation.
- Using only BPS distances (magnitudes) instead of vectors did not significantly impact performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in robotic in-hand manipulation, primarily by demonstrating for the first time autonomous, goal-oriented reorientation of diverse objects using a single, shape-conditioned agent, relying purely on tactile feedback. This capability is significant because it eliminates the dependency on external visual sensors, which are often impractical or unreliable in real-world scenarios due to occlusions, poor lighting, or cluttered environments.

The key significance and potential impact of this work include:

- **Enhanced Autonomy and Robustness:** By leveraging only tactile feedback, the robot system can operate in environments where visual information is unavailable or compromised. This increases the autonomy and robustness of manipulation tasks, extending the applicability of robot dexterity to a wider range of industrial and service robotics settings.
- **Validation of Shape Representation:** The study rigorously compares different 3D shape representations and establishes that Basis Point Sets (BPS), particularly when dynamically transformed by the object's estimated pose, are a highly effective encoding method for facilitating learning and generalization in dexterous, contact-rich manipulation tasks. This provides a valuable blueprint for future research in incorporating geometric priors into learning-based control.
- **Generalization to Novel Objects:** The demonstrated ability of a single agent to generalize manipulation strategies to novel objects, including complex out-of-distribution shapes like an apple, without prior exposure, is a crucial step towards truly versatile robotic systems. This reduces the need for extensive retraining for every new object, making deployment more efficient.
- **Comparable Performance to Object-Specific Methods:** Achieving success rates for multi-object manipulation that are on par with state-of-the-art results from agents specifically trained for single objects underscores the effectiveness and efficiency of the proposed shape-conditioned approach.
- **Successful Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The successful zero-shot transfer of policies trained entirely in simulation to a real-world robot hand without further fine-tuning validates the robustness of the training methodology, including extensive domain randomization, and demonstrates the practical applicability of the learned skills.
- **Foundation for Advanced Tactile Sensing:** The observed limitations with highly complex objects suggest a clear direction for future work. Integrating advanced tactile skin sensors could provide finer-grained contact information, potentially enabling more precise manipulation of objects with intricate features and higher curvature, thereby expanding the agent's capabilities even further.

Overall, this research contributes significantly to the field by pushing the boundaries of what is achievable with purely tactile robotic manipulation. It offers a robust, generalizable, and sensor-independent framework that could have a substantial impact on the development of more versatile and autonomous robots for diverse real-world applications.
