# Zero-Shot Robot Manipulation from Passive Human Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> This work learns robot manipulation purely from passive internet videos of humans. An image Transformer with a conditional-VAE head predicts stochastic 6D palm-pose trajectories of a human hand over a 2-second horizon from one scene image, trained on Epic-Kitchens egocentric clips. Predictions are transformed into robot end-effector poses and executed via inverse kinematics, achieving roughly 50% zero-shot success on drawers, doors, toasters and bowls.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning for manipulation needs online interaction, hand-designed rewards and environment resets, confining it to narrow tasks in structured laboratory settings.
- Behavior cloning depends on teleoperated or kinesthetic robot demonstrations that require predefined tasks and scale poorly to unseen objects and unstructured scenes.
- Video-imitation methods need aligned human-robot arm poses, special collection hardware, simulators for reward feedback, or per-task online fine-tuning in the real world.

## Method

- A stochastic hand-trajectory predictor - an encoder-decoder image ==Transformer== over scene features feeding a ==conditional-VAE== head - auto-regressively predicts palm delta poses from one image, minimizing reconstruction error plus a ==KL term==.
- Training pairs come from 2-second Epic-Kitchens clips labeled by off-the-shelf ==FrankMocap== hand tracking; a goal-conditioned variant adds the clip's final frame as a second encoder token.
- Deployment maps predicted camera-frame poses to robot world coordinates via calibrated intrinsics and extrinsics, then an ==inverse-kinematics== controller with loose error thresholds executes the seven-step trajectory.

## Results

- Unconditioned model averaged **50%** success across drawer, door, toaster, bowl and vegetable tasks over 20 trials each, versus **14%** when trained on only 30% of data.
- Goal-conditioned model averaged **37%** success across six goal categories, beating a RAFT3D scene-flow baseline at **12%** despite that baseline additionally consuming depth input.
- Over **70%** of 15 door trials produced a non-zero opening angle; goal conditioning reached specified drawer configurations **3-4 of 10** times versus **1-3 of 10** unconditioned.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Agent-agnostic action representations, here palm pose without finger articulation, transfer across embodiments better than pixel-level imitation requiring human-robot pose alignment.
> - Data diversity dominates: cutting training videos to 30% collapsed success from 50% to 14%, a far larger effect than architectural choices.
> - Zero-shot affordance prediction is coarse; 60% of failures came from trajectories physically incompatible with object kinematics, so contact-aware refinement remains necessary.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot manipulation has seen rapid advancements, primarily driven by data-driven approaches. However, a persistent challenge remains: enabling robots to perform a wide variety of tasks in diverse, unstructured environments without extensive, costly, and task-specific training data. This paper directly addresses this critical gap by proposing a method for **zero-shot robot manipulation from passive human videos**, differentiating itself from existing paradigms.

**Traditional Robot Learning Approaches and Their Limitations:**

1. **Deep Reinforcement Learning (RL):** While powerful for optimizing task-specific behaviors (e.g., Kalashnikov et al., 2021), RL suffers from significant **sample inefficiency**, requiring vast amounts of online interaction. It also necessitates careful **reward function design** and **environment resets**, typically limiting its application to structured lab settings or simulations. Generalization to novel tasks or environments remains a major hurdle.
2. **Imitation Learning (IL) / Behavior Cloning:** These methods learn directly from expert robot demonstrations (e.g., Pomerleau, 1989; Finn et al., 2017). While effective, collecting high-quality robot demonstration data via tele-operation or kinesthetic teaching is **labor-intensive, expensive, and difficult to scale** to diverse tasks and environments. Crucially, these approaches often require *task definitions* upfront and are restricted to specific robot embodiments.
3. **Learning from Human Videos (LFHV) – Direct Imitation:** A more recent trend involves leveraging human videos, which are abundant and diverse. However, many LFHV approaches still face limitations:
 - **Specialized Data Collection:** Some require humans to provide demonstrations through special hardware interfaces or in-lab settings, limiting scalability (e.g., Young et al., 2020b; Song et al., 2020).
 - **Alignment Challenges:** Direct imitation often demands near-perfect alignment between human and robot arm poses, making generalization difficult (e.g., Smith et al., 2019; Xiong et al., 2021).
 - **Online Fine-tuning/Simulation:** Others use human videos to train policies that still require online fine-tuning with reinforcement learning in simulations or the real world, which is costly (e.g., Shao et al., 2020; Schmeckpeper et al., 2020; Bahl et al., 2022).
 - **Dexterous Manipulation Focus:** Some advanced techniques like DexMV (Qin et al., 2021) focus on dextrous hand motions but still need in-lab human videos for training.

**This Work's Novel Contribution and Position:**

The core innovation of this paper lies in its **decoupling of action representation learning from robot embodiment and task-specific data collection**. Instead of trying to directly map pixels to robot actions or imitate a specific human video in real-time, the authors propose to:

1. **Extract Agent-Agnostic Action Representations:** By learning to predict plausible human hand trajectories (6D poses of the palm) from a vast, diverse dataset of *passive* human interaction videos (e.g., Epic-Kitchens), the model learns a general understanding of how objects can be manipulated. These videos are "passive" because they were not collected with robot learning in mind or with task-level segregation.
2. **Zero-Shot Deployment:** These learned hand trajectories are then transformed to the robot's end-effector space and executed by a simple Inverse Kinematics (IK) controller, *without any in-lab demonstrations, fine-tuning, or prior task definitions*.

This approach significantly advances the field by:
- **Addressing Data Scarcity:** Tapping into the immense, readily available resource of internet human videos, bypassing the need for expensive robot data collection.
- **Enhancing Generalization:** By learning fundamental interaction patterns from diverse human activities, the robot can generalize to unseen objects and novel manipulation tasks in an unstructured environment.
- **Establishing a Strong Baseline:** It seeks to define the limits and capabilities of learning robot manipulation *solely* from passive human observations, offering a foundational step towards truly generalist robotic agents.

The work builds upon prior developments in human-object interaction understanding, hand pose estimation, and activity forecasting, but critically bridges the "vision-robotics loop" by converting visual understanding into deployable robot actions in a zero-shot manner.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable robots to perform everyday manipulation tasks in unstructured settings by **learning solely from passive videos of humans**, without requiring any task-specific robot data, in-domain demonstrations, or fine-tuning. This ambitious goal is summarized by the paper's title: "Zero-Shot Robot Manipulation from Passive Human Videos."

The specific, interwoven goals driving this research are:

1. **Extract Agent-Agnostic Action Representations:** Develop a framework capable of extracting generalized, embodiment-independent action representations—specifically, plausible human hand trajectories—from diverse, arbitrary human interaction videos found on the internet.
2. **Zero-Shot Mapping and Deployment:** Design a method to map these extracted human hand trajectories to a robot's end-effector space and execute them successfully on a physical robot for manipulation tasks, entirely in a zero-shot manner (i.e., without any prior robot experience or fine-tuning in the target environment).
3. **Demonstrate Generalization:** Show that this approach can generalize to manipulate novel objects in unseen physical environments, establishing a strong baseline for what can be achieved with robot-agnostic human data.
4. **Explore Unconditioned and Goal-Conditioned Manipulation:** Investigate two modes of operation:
 - An **unconditioned model** to predict diverse plausible interactions (affordances) given a scene, useful for exploration.
 - A **goal-conditioned model** to predict trajectories that lead to a specific desired outcome, guided by a goal image.

The motivation behind pursuing these objectives stems from critical limitations in current robot learning paradigms:

- **Scaling Data Collection:** Current methods for robot learning (RL, IL) are hampered by the immense cost and difficulty of collecting diverse, high-quality robot interaction data across numerous tasks and environments. Human demonstrations, while valuable, are equally challenging to scale directly for robot policies.
- **Lack of Generalization:** Robots trained on specific tasks or in structured environments often struggle to generalize to novel objects, variations in scene configurations, or entirely new tasks. The vision is to create "generalist robots" capable of adapting to the complexities of real-world, unstructured settings.
- **Abundance of Human Data:** Humans effortlessly perform a vast array of manipulation tasks daily. Videos of these activities are readily available on the internet (e.g., YouTube, specialized datasets like Epic-Kitchens). This research is motivated by the desire to unlock this "readily available source of such large-scale data" for robot learning.
- **Simplified Deployment:** By enabling zero-shot learning, the framework aims to significantly simplify the deployment pipeline for robots, reducing the need for extensive human expertise or laborious programming for each new task or environment.
- **Understanding Fundamental Interactions:** By focusing on agent-agnostic hand trajectories, the work seeks to capture a more fundamental understanding of human-object interactions that transcends specific physical embodiments.

In essence, the authors aim to overcome the data bottleneck and generalization challenges in robot learning by creatively leveraging the vast repository of passive human video data, transforming observations of human intent into actionable robot commands with unprecedented efficiency and adaptability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework enables zero-shot robot manipulation by learning plausible human hand trajectories from diverse passive human videos and then mapping these trajectories to a robot's end-effector. The methodology is split into three main components: learning to predict future hand trajectories, transforming these predictions to the robot's frame, and evaluating two versions of the model.

#### 3.1. Learning to Predict Future Hand Trajectories

The core of the approach is a stochastic model, $p(a_{1:T} | o_1)$, which predicts a sequence of future human actions ($a_{1:T}$) given an initial image of a scene ($o_1$). The predictions focus on the 6D pose (position and orientation) of the human palm, ignoring fine-grained hand articulation, making it suitable for a robot arm with a simple gripper.

- **Delta Actions:** Instead of predicting absolute hand poses ($h_t$), the model predicts *delta actions* ($a_t = h_t \ominus h_{t-1}$), representing changes in hand pose between time steps. This minimizes prediction error and enhances temporal correlation, as the model is auto-regressive, taking previous actions as input for predicting the next.

- **Model Architecture (Figure 2):**
 - **Image Transformer:** A Transformer-based Encoder-Decoder architecture forms the backbone.
 - **Encoder:** Extracts features ($f_1$) from the initial image ($o_1$) and encodes them into a latent code ($z^e_1$). For the goal-conditioned model, it also takes an embedding of a goal image ($f_g$) with a positional embedding.
 - **Decoder:** Conditions on the latent code ($z^e_1$) and previously predicted delta actions ($a_{1:t-1}$) to output a feature ($z^d_t$) for the current time step's action prediction.
 - **CVAE (Conditional Variational Autoencoder) Prediction Head:** This head captures the multi-modality of plausible future actions.
 - During **training**, the CVAE takes the ground-truth delta action ($a_t$) and the Transformer decoder's feature ($z^d_t$) to output a reconstruction ($\hat{a}_t$) and parameters for a Gaussian distribution (mean $\mu_t$, std $\sigma_t$).
 - During **inference**, it samples from the prior of the CVAE (concatenated with $z^d_t$) to generate a predicted delta action ($\hat{a}_t$).
 - **Recovery of Absolute Actions:** Predicted delta actions are aggregated to recover absolute hand poses ($\hat{h}_t = \hat{h}_{t-1} \oplus \hat{a}_t$).

- **Training Loss:** The model is trained end-to-end using a loss function that combines an L2 reconstruction loss for the predicted delta actions and a KL divergence term for the CVAE's latent space, encouraging diverse yet plausible predictions. There is no intermediate supervision for the different stages.

- **Training Data Generation:**
 - **Dataset:** Egocentric videos from the **Epic-Kitchens** dataset are used. These videos capture people performing diverse household activities, particularly in kitchens. The model considers 2-second clips.
 - **Hand Pose Estimation:** For each frame in a clip, an off-the-shelf **FrankMocap** model (Rong et al., 2020) is used to obtain the 6D pose of the human palm (position and orientation) relative to the initial camera frame. This process provides the ground-truth hand trajectories ($h_{1:T}$).
 - **Goal-Conditioned Data:** For the goal-conditioned model, the last image of a video clip is used as the goal image ($f_g$).

#### 3.2. Mapping Trajectories to the Robot’s Frame

After the prediction model is trained on human videos, it is deployed directly on a physical robot in a zero-shot manner.

- **Camera Calibration:** The robot's workspace is equipped with a fixed, calibrated camera, providing its intrinsic and extrinsic parameters (I, [R, T]).
- **Coordinate Transformation:** The predicted human hand poses (initially in the camera frame) are transformed into the robot's world coordinate frame (located at the robot's base) using inverse projection and known camera-to-robot base transformations. The prediction horizon is fixed at $T=7$ time steps for experiments.
- **Robot Execution:** An Inverse Kinematics (IK) controller is used to command the robot's 7DOF Franka Emika Panda arm to move its two-finger Robotiq gripper to the desired position and orientation specified by each transformed pose in the sequence. The IK controller has error thresholds to prevent the robot from getting stuck on difficult poses.

#### 3.3. Model Versions

The framework is implemented and evaluated in two variations:

- **Unconditioned Model:** Given only an initial scene image, it predicts a distribution of plausible hand trajectories, implicitly discovering object affordances (e.g., opening a closed drawer).
- **Goal-Conditioned Model:** Given an initial scene image *and* a goal image, it predicts trajectories aimed at achieving the state depicted in the goal image (e.g., a fully open door).

#### 3.4. Experiment Design

The experiments aim to answer how well the unconditioned model generates diverse and plausible task outcomes, and how effectively the goal-conditioned model solves known tasks. They evaluate success rates for various manipulation tasks (opening/closing drawers, doors, toasters, moving bowls, moving veggies with a tool) using unseen objects in a lab setting.

- **Baselines:**
 - **Our method (less data):** A version of the proposed method trained on only 30% of the Epic-Kitchens data to assess the importance of data scale.
 - **3D Scene Flow Baseline:** This baseline uses RAFT3D (Teed & Deng, 2021) to predict scene flow between initial and goal images (requiring RGB-D input), guiding the robot's motion based on dominant flow. This serves as a strong visual-kinematic comparison.

By systematically predicting hand trajectories from diverse human videos and translating them to robot actions, this methodology offers a robust and data-efficient pathway towards generalist robot manipulation without the typical overheads of robot-specific data collection or task programming.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted with a 7DOF Franka Emika Panda arm and a Robotiq gripper in a lab setting, using a fixed IntelRealsense camera and various unseen everyday objects (drawers, doors, toaster oven, fruit bowls, chopping board with veggies). Each task involves manipulating these objects from an initial state to a desired outcome.

#### 4.1. Unconditioned Generation Results

The unconditioned model predicts plausible hand trajectories given an initial scene image, aiming to generate diverse and reasonable interactions.

- **Quantitative Evaluation (Table 1):** The model demonstrated an average success rate of **50%** across five distinct object manipulation categories (drawer, door, toaster, bowl, veggies).
 - Success rates varied from 45% (door) to 55% (bowl), indicating a consistent ability to perform plausible interactions. For objects like drawers, doors, and toasters, success meant changing the object's state (e.g., opening a closed door). For the bowl, it meant moving it without toppling, and for veggies, moving at least half the pieces.
- **Importance of Data:** A comparison with "Our method (less data)" (trained on only 30% of Epic-Kitchens) showed a significantly lower average success rate of **14%**. This highlights the crucial role of large-scale, diverse human video data for learning effective action representations.
- **Qualitative Observations (Figure 6):** The model was able to generate diverse plausible behaviors for different scenes, such as opening a door to varying degrees or moving a bowl in different directions, demonstrating its ability to capture the multi-modality of human-object interactions.
- **Fine-grained Analysis (Figure 5):** For the door opening task, over 70% of trials resulted in the door being opened to a non-zero angle, confirming the model's capacity to induce plausible state changes in objects.

#### 4.2. Goal-Conditioned Generation Results

The goal-conditioned model aims to generate trajectories that reach a specific goal configuration, provided as a goal image, from an initial scene image.

- **Quantitative Evaluation (Table 2):** The goal-conditioned model achieved an average success rate of **37%** across six goal-oriented tasks (opening/closing drawers, opening/closing doors, moving bowls, moving veggies).
 - Success rates ranged from 30% (open door) to 45% (move bowl). The slightly lower success rate compared to the unconditioned model is expected, as achieving a specific goal is inherently more challenging than merely performing a plausible interaction.
- **Baseline Comparison:** The goal-conditioned model significantly outperformed both baselines: "Our method (less data)" (11% average success) and the "3D Scene Flow" baseline (12% average success). This demonstrates the superiority of the proposed framework, particularly its ability to learn from passive human videos and generalize zero-shot, even against a baseline that uses more information (RGB-D for scene flow).
- **Goal Consistency (Table 3):** A finer analysis on a scene with stacked drawers showed that when conditioned on a goal, the model achieved the specified final configuration more frequently than the unconditioned model incidentally reaching that same configuration. This provides concrete evidence that the goal-conditioning mechanism effectively guides the robot towards desired outcomes.
- **Qualitative Observations (Figure 7):** Visualizations showed the robot successfully executing trajectories to achieve various goals, such as opening different drawers, opening/closing a door, and moving vegetables on a table, validating its ability to follow high-level visual instructions.

#### 4.3. Analysis of Failures

The paper also provides an insightful analysis of common failure modes for both models:

- **Unconditioned Model Failures:**
 - **No Contact (approximately 40% of failures):** The robot's end-effector failed to make physical contact with the object at the initial time-step, leading to no interaction.
 - **Infeasible Motion (approximately 60% of failures):** Contact was made, but the predicted trajectory was not physically feasible or did not align with the object's affordances (e.g., attempting to pull a door outwards when it should rotate on hinges, or pushing against a bowl in a way that causes it to topple).
- **Goal-Conditioned Model Failures (in addition to the above):**
 - **Plausible but Incorrect Goal (approximately 40% of goal-conditioned failures):** The robot performed a plausible interaction, but it did not correspond to the *specific* goal image provided. For example, if the goal was to open the top drawer, the robot might open the bottom drawer instead. This indicates a limitation in precisely understanding and achieving the nuanced specifics of a visual goal.

These results establish a strong baseline for zero-shot robot manipulation from passive human videos, demonstrating promising capabilities while also identifying clear areas for future improvement, particularly in robustness of contact and precise goal-conditioning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in the field of robot learning, particularly in addressing the long-standing challenges of data scarcity, generalization, and the high cost of robot training. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Pioneering Zero-Shot Generalization from Passive Human Videos:** The paper demonstrates, for the first time, a viable framework for enabling robots to perform manipulation tasks **zero-shot**—without any in-domain robot data or fine-tuning—by learning **solely from passive human videos**. This is a paradigm shift from traditional methods that require extensive robot-specific demonstrations or online interactions.
- **Unlocking a Vast Data Source:** By effectively leveraging the abundance of "passive" human videos available on the internet (like Epic-Kitchens), the work provides a pathway to bypass the laborious and expensive process of collecting specialized robot datasets. This dramatically expands the potential data pool for robot skill acquisition, making learning more scalable and accessible.
- **Learning Agent-Agnostic Action Representations:** The core idea of extracting "agent-agnostic action representations" in the form of human hand trajectories is crucial. It decouples the understanding of *what* a manipulation action entails from the specific *how* of a robot's embodiment. This abstraction allows for broad generalization across different robot platforms (with appropriate inverse kinematics) and diverse manipulation scenarios.
- **Establishing a Strong Baseline:** The reported success rates (around 50% for unconditioned, 37% for goal-conditioned tasks) on unseen objects in a zero-shot setting represent a surprisingly strong baseline. This sets a new benchmark for what can be achieved with passive observational learning and provides a foundation for future research to build upon.
- **Moving Towards Generalist Robots:** This work contributes directly to the vision of developing more generalist robots capable of operating effectively in unstructured, unknown environments without predefined tasks. By learning a broad repertoire of interaction skills from humans, robots can adapt to novel situations and objects more readily.
- **Simplifying Robot Deployment:** For practical robotics applications, this framework could significantly simplify the deployment process for new tasks and environments. Instead of reprogramming or extensively training a robot for every new manipulation skill, a robot could, in principle, acquire new capabilities by observing human activities. This reduces the "cold start" problem in robotics.
- **Understanding Affordances and Intent:** The unconditioned model's ability to predict diverse, plausible interactions offers insights into how robots can learn about object affordances (what actions are possible with an object). The goal-conditioned model pushes this further by demonstrating an initial capability to infer and execute human intent from visual goals.

While the current success rates indicate room for improvement, particularly for fine-grained manipulation and precise goal achievement, the authors acknowledge that incorporating environment-specific fine-tuning would likely boost performance. However, the true impact lies in what has been demonstrated *without* such fine-tuning. This research provides compelling evidence that valuable action information can be extracted from readily available human videos and effectively utilized for zero-shot robotic manipulation, paving the way for more autonomous, adaptable, and human-like robotic agents in the future.
