# InstructVLA: Vision-Language-Action Instruction Tuning from Understanding to Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> InstructVLA presents an end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that maintains vision-language reasoning capabilities while achieving strong robotic manipulation performance. It utilizes a two-stage training paradigm and a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) adaptation to enable dynamic switching between textual reasoning and action generation, yielding a 92% improvement over a fine-tuned OpenVLA on the SimplerEnv-Instruct benchmark and improved real-world performance.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models suffer from catastrophic forgetting, where fine-tuning on robotic data degrades their pre-trained vision-language reasoning capabilities.
- Large-scale real-world robotic datasets often lack diverse human instructions, limiting models to simple, templated commands and hindering their ability to handle free-form directives.
- Prior approaches struggle to seamlessly integrate high-level vision-language understanding with precise low-level action generation within a single, unified framework.

## Method

- A unified VLM architecture based on Eagle2-2B is introduced, capable of generating both textual outputs and ==latent action representations==, featuring learnable action queries and a ==Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) adaptation== for dynamic mode switching.
- A ==two-stage training paradigm== is employed: Stage 1 for 'Action Pretraining' aligns a VLM-driven action expert with latent actions, and Stage 2 for 'Vision-Language-Action Instruction Tuning' (VLA-IT) reactivates and aligns VLM reasoning with embodied tasks while freezing the action expert.
- A 650K-sample VLA-IT dataset is curated, annotating diverse hierarchical language instructions for embodied scene understanding and latent action planning, complemented by the ==SimplerEnv-Instruct benchmark== for evaluating zero-shot instruction following and situated reasoning.

## Results

- InstructVLA (Generalist, 2B parameters) outperforms larger VLMs on multimodal benchmarks and achieves a **92%** improvement over a fine-tuned OpenVLA on the SimplerEnv-Instruct benchmark (**46.0%** vs **23.9%** average success rate).
- In real-world zero-shot and few-shot experiments, InstructVLA shows superior performance (**41.7%** to **46.7%** improvement over OpenVLA on reasoning instructions) and robustness to out-of-distribution conditions.
- Ablation studies validate design choices: language motion data boosts success by **10.5%**, FiLM enhancement improves performance by **15.3%**, and internal 'test-time thinking' provides a **36.1%** performance gain on SimplerEnv-Instruct.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) adaptation within a two-stage training paradigm effectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting, preserving a VLM's general multimodal understanding while achieving strong manipulation performance.
> - Integrating textual descriptions of low-level actions ('language motion') during pre-training significantly boosts overall success rates, enabling a better association of visual cues with manipulation primitives.
> - Allowing the model to generate internal textual analyses ('test-time thinking') during manipulation yields substantial performance gains on complex reasoning tasks, demonstrating the value of integrated, internal reasoning over external LLM assistance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of versatile foundation models in computer vision (CV) and natural language processing (NLP) has led to significant advancements. Building on this progress, recent Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have emerged, aiming to adapt large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for generalizable robotic manipulation. The integration of multimodal reasoning, prominent in VLMs, remains an area with unexplored potential in VLA settings.

Existing VLA models often encounter limitations, including a trade-off between multimodal reasoning and precise action generation. They can specialize in task-specific manipulation data, which may restrict their broader applicability, or experience catastrophic forgetting of their pre-trained vision-language capabilities during adaptation for action tasks. Previous efforts to integrate VLM reasoning into action learning have primarily adopted two strategies:
1. **Unified auto-regressive modeling:** Approaches such as RT-2 and Magma co-train models on both vision-language and manipulation data. However, this method has been observed to overlook complex embodied reasoning and may suffer from a domain gap between general VLM corpora and embodied scenarios.
2. **Integrating embodied reasoning into manipulation datasets:** Methods like ECoT and Emma-X embed chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning into manipulation datasets. These approaches often rely on existing action-pretrained architectures and structured reasoning formats, which can limit expressiveness, lead to catastrophic forgetting of broader VLM abilities, and may not demonstrate general multimodal capabilities effectively.

This research aims to address these identified limitations by developing a VLA model that can simultaneously preserve the flexible reasoning of VLMs while achieving high-performance manipulation, thereby bridging the gap between intuitive human-robot interaction and efficient policy learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from a fundamental question within VLA-based manipulation: how can manipulation skills be acquired without eroding the VLM’s multimodal reasoning capabilities, and concurrently, how can such reasoning enhance manipulation performance?

The authors identify three main obstacles in current attempts to integrate VLM reasoning into action learning:
1. **Task Interference and Catastrophic Forgetting:** The process of training for action generation can lead to the degradation or loss of pre-existing multimodal understanding abilities from the VLM.
2. **Data Scarcity:** There is a limited availability of comprehensive manipulation datasets that feature rich multimodal supervision, hindering the development of robust and generalizable VLA models.
3. **Methodological Gaps:** Effective mechanisms and training paradigms are lacking to seamlessly translate the nuanced capabilities of multimodal reasoning into precise and responsive action generation for robots.

To address these challenges, the key objectives of this research are:
- **Develop InstructVLA:** To propose an end-to-end VLA model that extends pre-trained VLMs to achieve accurate action generation while maintaining strong multimodal understanding.
- **Introduce VLA-IT Training Paradigm:** To design a novel training strategy, Vision-Language-Action Instruction Tuning (VLA-IT), specifically tailored to integrate vision-language knowledge with action generation, treating language-conditioned action generation as an integral part of instruction following.
- **Curate a Large-Scale Dataset:** To support the VLA-IT paradigm, a new dataset consisting of 650K human-robot interactions, annotated with diverse instructions, scene captions, and question-answer pairs, is curated.
- **Establish a New Benchmark:** To thoroughly evaluate the generalization capabilities of VLA models, a new benchmark called SimplerEnv-Instruct, featuring 80 zero-shot manipulation tasks requiring closed-loop control and high-level instruction understanding, is introduced.
- **Investigate Multimodal Contribution:** To conduct extensive experiments to understand how multimodal capabilities contribute to and enhance manipulation performance.
- **Demonstrate Real-World Effectiveness:** To validate InstructVLA's performance in both simulated and real-world settings, demonstrating its potential for intuitive and steerable human-robot interaction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on InstructVLA, an end-to-end VLA model, and its associated Vision-Language-Action Instruction Tuning (VLA-IT) training paradigm.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture: InstructVLA

InstructVLA integrates multimodal reasoning with precise robotic control through a unified architecture designed to mitigate task interference and catastrophic forgetting.

- **Embodied VLM for Text and Latent Action Generation:** A single VLM, based on the Eagle2-2B backbone, is employed for both multimodal reasoning and language-steered latent action planning. It generates textual outputs to preserve strong language understanding and multimodal inference, and subsequently produces `N` learnable action queries (`Q`) to extract task-relevant latent actions (`C`) from the VLM's hidden states. Language output is supervised using a cross-entropy loss (`L_LM`).
- **MoE Adaptation to Harmonize Reasoning and Action:** A Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) design is implemented to enable dynamic switching between reasoning and execution modes. LoRA modules serve as experts within the LLM backbone, preserving pre-trained capabilities. A scalar head predicts gating coefficients (`λ_i`) to adaptively blend the outputs of these experts, allowing the model to alternate between textual reasoning and latent action prediction.
- **Flow Model as an Efficient Action Expert:** To decouple low-level control from high-level understanding, a flow-matching action expert is used. This expert generates actions from image observations, conditioned on VLM-derived intentions. It processes image features from a DINOv2 vision encoder, latent actions, noisy action embeddings, and optional proprioception. The architecture uses a simple transformer with block-wise causal attention, and the vision encoder is enhanced with feature-wise linear modulation (FiLM) to direct actions based on spatial and contextual input. Action learning is supervised using a flow matching objective (`L_FM`).
- **Inference Strategies:** InstructVLA employs strategies to improve inference speed:
 - **Decoding Strategies:** Textual responses are generated via greedy search until the first action query token appears, followed by parallel decoding of remaining action queries in a single VLM forward pass.
 - **Language Response and Latent Action Caching:** Textual outputs are cached across multiple action steps due to their temporal stability. InstructVLA also supports caching latent actions, reducing the frequency of VLM forward passes.

#### 3.2. Training Recipe

A two-stage training paradigm is adopted to ensure stable optimization and effective integration of vision, language, and action.

- **Stage 1: Action Pre-training (InstructorVLA-Expert):**
 - The model is pre-trained using heterogeneous manipulation data from datasets such as Bridge Data and RT-1.
 - It learns to predict both actions (via `L_FM`) and language motion (textual descriptions of low-level actions, via `L_LM`). The total loss is `L = L_LM + L_FM`.
 - During this stage, only the embedding of the latent action and the action LoRA adapter on the LLM backbone are tuned (650M parameters), preserving the VLM's multimodal reasoning capabilities.
- **Stage 2: Vision-Language-Action Instruction Tuning (VLA-IT) (InstructorVLA-Generalist):**
 - This stage extends visual instruction tuning, adapting the LLM backbone to handle complex manipulation instructions.
 - A language LoRA and a scalar head are added to form the MoE adaptation module. This module is the sole trainable component (220M parameters).
 - Training involves jointly optimizing on multimodal datasets, manipulation datasets, and the curated VLA-IT corpus. This enables the model to automatically switch between textual reasoning and action generation, leveraging vision-language understanding for action generation.
 - A 1:7 multimodal-to-action training ratio is used to maintain multimodal ability efficiently.

#### 3.3. VLA Dataset and Benchmark

The research introduces new data and an evaluation pipeline.

- **InstructVLA Tuning Dataset:** A dataset of 650K human-robot interactions is curated with diverse hierarchical language annotations from large-scale manipulation datasets (RT-1, Bridge Data). GPT-4o is utilized to annotate data with scene captions, question-answer pairs, command rewritings, and context creation, across three frames from each episode. These annotations are categorized for:
 - **Embodied Scene Understanding:** Scenario captioning for environment descriptions and question answering for scene understanding.
 - **Instruction Understanding and Latent Action Planning:** Command rewriting to introduce instructional diversity and context creation to generate implicit user goals for multi-step tasks.
- **SimplerEnv-Instruct Benchmark:** An 80-task benchmark is introduced on the SimplerEnv platform, specifically designed to evaluate zero-shot instruction-following and reasoning. It features novel objects and instructions that are out-of-distribution from training data and encompasses:
 - **Task Aggregation (50 tasks):** Assesses interpretation and execution of core tasks despite visual or linguistic variations (e.g., novel verbs, multilingual expressions, diverse object references, OOD objects).
 - **Situated Reasoning (30 tasks):** Evaluates reasoning over contextual cues or indirect instructions, and decomposition of commands into sub-goals (e.g., selecting a suitable tool based on a high-level goal like "clean the table").

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluate InstructVLA across multimodal understanding, robotic manipulation benchmarks, and real-world deployments.

#### 4.1. Multimodal Understanding

InstructVLA-Generalist, with a 1.5B parameter LLM backbone, demonstrates competitive performance on multimodal understanding benchmarks. It outperforms the co-trained baseline Magma (8B parameters) and is comparable to its base model Eagle2 and other VLMs like Bunny. The model also shows strong embodied understanding capabilities, as detailed in the supplementary material.

#### 4.2. Robotic Manipulation

- **In-domain SimplerEnv Tasks:** InstructVLA-Expert achieves a 33.3% improvement over SpatialVLA in closed-loop manipulation.
- **Generalization on SimplerEnv-Instruct:** InstructVLA-Generalist demonstrates a 31.7% relative improvement over OpenVLA fine-tuned and assisted by GPT-4o on the SimplerEnv-Instruct benchmark. While fine-tuning OpenVLA on multimodal and manipulation data partially restores its multimodal capabilities and improves task performance, it does not match InstructVLA's performance. GPT-4o, when used as an external instruction interpreter, aids OpenVLA but encounters challenges in accurate instruction rewriting for embodied tasks.
- **Comparison with Baselines:** Methods such as Magma, which co-train VLM abilities, preserve multimodal capabilities more effectively than fully fine-tuned OpenVLA but still do not achieve the performance of InstructVLA. ECoT, which relies on textual chain-of-thought over manipulation datasets, exhibits limited multimodal question-answering capabilities and generates manipulation-style CoT responses without demonstrating effective instruction-following.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiments

InstructVLA's capabilities were evaluated in real-world settings using a WidowX-250 Arm (zero-shot) and a Franka Research 3 robot (few-shot).
- On atomic tasks (in-domain objects and instructions), InstructVLA and baselines showed comparable performance.
- For reasoning tasks, including celebrity recognition, OCR, and tool-use inference, InstructVLA demonstrated a substantial performance advantage, outperforming OpenVLA by 41.7% in few-shot and 46.7% in zero-shot settings.
- On reasoning and math tasks, InstructVLA achieved a 2.5x improvement over π_0, which performed near random guessing. The model's ability to operate under extreme lighting conditions and perform semantic grounding in novel contexts was also observed.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies provided insights into architectural and training decisions:
- **Multimodal and Manipulation Co-training:** The proposed MoE design and two-stage training paradigm of InstructVLA proved more effective than full finetuning or autoregressive co-training methods for integrating multimodal and manipulation skills. Incorporating "language motion" supervision improved overall success rate by 9.3%, and enhanced expert perception (DINOv2-based ViT encoder with FiLM) was found to be crucial, improving performance by 15.3% when fully integrated and causing a 50% drop without the DINOv2 encoder.
- **Multimodal Ability Transfer to Manipulation:** Freezing the action expert during VLA instruction tuning yielded comparable performance to joint finetuning, indicating that finetuning only the VLM can adapt to complex manipulation tasks. Data scaling with VLA-IT annotations primarily benefited situated reasoning tasks. OpenVLA, when fine-tuned on VLA-IT, showed limited gains in situated reasoning due to catastrophic forgetting. Adding QA and captioning diversity to the VLA-IT dataset improved InstructVLA's generalization by 10.8%. Explicit textual reasoning ("Think" prompting) further enhanced manipulation performance by 36.1% and surpassed InstructVLA-expert aided by GPT-4o as an external interpreter.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents InstructVLA, a VLA model that integrates multimodal reasoning with precise action generation, addressing a critical challenge in robotic control. The work contributes to the field by demonstrating that it is possible to acquire robust manipulation skills without diminishing the extensive multimodal understanding capabilities of pre-trained VLMs.

The proposed Vision-Language-Action Instruction Tuning (VLA-IT) paradigm, coupled with a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) adaptation framework, offers an efficient method for jointly optimizing embodied reasoning and action generation. This approach mitigates catastrophic forgetting, a common issue in prior VLA models, and allows for effective leveraging of vision-language understanding to enhance manipulation.

The curation of the 650K-sample VLA-IT dataset and the introduction of the SimplerEnv-Instruct benchmark provide valuable resources for the research community, enabling more comprehensive evaluation of VLA models' instruction generalization capabilities, especially in zero-shot settings that demand high-level instruction understanding and closed-loop control.

InstructVLA's performance on both simulated and real-world tasks indicates its potential for broader applicability. Its ability to interpret diverse, high-level instructions and perform reasoning-guided manipulation could lead to more intuitive and adaptable human-robot interaction systems. The reported results suggest a pathway for developing more generalizable, interpretable, and interactive robots capable of operating effectively in complex, unstructured environments.
