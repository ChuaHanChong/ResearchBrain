# AIC MLLM: Autonomous Interactive Correction MLLM for Robust Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces an Autonomous Interactive Correction (AIC) MLLM framework that enables robots to self-correct low-level SE(3) pose predictions for manipulating articulated objects. It employs multimodal feedback to refine actions, achieving an average success rate of 0.75 on unseen test categories and improving real-world manipulation success from 37.5% to 65%.

## Problem

- Robotic systems frequently encounter failures during interaction with complex, varied real-world environments.
- Existing MLLM-based correction methods primarily address high-level task planning, overlooking low-level SE(3) pose prediction errors critical for atomic manipulation.
- Manipulating articulated objects demands high precision due to their complex kinematics, making them particularly susceptible to low-level pose inaccuracies.

## Method

- An **AIC MLLM** framework is proposed that predicts initial ==manipulation poses== and comprehends specific visual and textual feedback prompts for correction.
- Distinct prompt interfaces are designed to address ==positional errors== (highlighting unmovable parts with masks) and ==rotational errors== (providing joint type and axis direction for gripper adjustment).
- A ==Feedback Information Extraction (FIE)== module automatically detects failure causes and generates appropriate prompts, complemented by a ==Test Time Adaptation (TTA)== strategy for iterative model updates during inference.

## Results

- The AIC MLLM achieved an average success rate of **0.75** on unseen test categories in simulation, surpassing baseline methods and demonstrating strong generalization capabilities.
- Real-world experiments using a Franka Emika robot showed the framework improved manipulation success rates from **37.5%** without correction to **65%** with correction on diverse articulated objects.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the position and rotation correction modules, as well as the Test Time Adaptation (TTA) strategy, were individually critical for the observed performance gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLMs can be effectively trained to interpret specific multimodal feedback (visual masks and text descriptions) for precise, low-level SE(3) pose correction.
> - The combination of both positional and rotational error correction mechanisms is essential and complementary for robust manipulation of complex articulated objects.
> - Iterative self-correction, informed by failed interaction experiences, coupled with test-time adaptation, significantly enhances a robot's ability to generalize and adapt to new object instances and environments.
