# Visual hallucination detection in large vision-language models via evidential conflict

> [!summary] Summary
> This work targets visual hallucination in large vision-language models. It introduces PRE-HAL, a multiple-choice VQA benchmark covering perception and reasoning across instance, scene and relation semantics, and a Dempster-Shafer detection method that reads the decoder's final-layer features as evidence and scores the conflict between supporting and opposing mass functions. The conflict score beats five internal uncertainty baselines, improving AUROC by 4-10% on three LVLMs.

## Problem

- Existing visual hallucination benchmarks such as POPE, NOPE and R-Bench probe only perception of objects or relations, so hallucinations produced by higher-level reasoning go unmeasured.
- Monte Carlo dropout and ensembles are infeasible at LVLM scale, while verbal self-reported confidence fails when models misread prompt instructions or express confidence imprecisely.
- Softmax-based entropy and log-probability metrics inherit overconfidence and poor calibration, and external consistency checks need repeated generations or auxiliary models, making them costly.

## Method

- **PRE-HAL** benchmark: 10,000 human-verified multiple-choice VQA entries assembled from MMBench, MMVP, POPE, R-Bench plus hand-built out-of-distribution items, labelled across five perception/reasoning hallucination types over instances, scenes and relations.
- ==Evidence modelling==: final-layer FFN features are treated as evidence, each weight's positive and negative parts forming simple mass functions over a token and its complement, with parameters set by ==Least Commitment Principle== centering.
- ==Two-stage Dempster fusion==: same-focal-set masses merge by summing evidence weights, avoiding power-set cost; ==Dempster's rule== then yields the ==conflict== per token, and the sentence score is the maximum over generated tokens.

## Results

- PRE-HAL exposes relation reasoning as the weakest capability: hallucination rates of **49.44%**, **50.42%** and **19.92%** for LLaVA-v1.5, mPLUG-Owl2 and mPLUG-Owl3, with overall ECE scores of **0.35**, **0.19** and **0.48**.
- Total AUROC for the conflict score reaches **0.59**, **0.67** and **0.58** versus best internal baselines **0.55**, **0.57** and **0.51**, and scene perception hits **0.71**, **0.71** and **0.69**.
- At a fixed **0.08** false-positive rate it gives the best internal-method F1 on all three models (**25.55**, **28.68**, **19.66**); ablations show it leads internal metrics across temperatures **0.1-1.5** and **7B/13B/34B** sizes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Conflict between opposing feature-level evidence is a better hallucination signal than softmax entropy, because it sidesteps the overconfidence that miscalibrates probability-derived uncertainty.
> - Reasoning hallucinations far exceed perception ones, so benchmarks that test only object presence understate a model's true unreliability; lower hallucination rates can hide worse calibration.
> - Simple mass functions make Dempster-Shafer fusion tractable at vocabulary scale, but the method needs white-box access to weights and features, so API-only models are excluded.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) has enabled near-human-level capabilities in processing and understanding both textual and visual data, contributing to advancements in tasks such as image captioning, visual question answering (VQA), and text generation. Despite these advancements, LVLMs exhibit a critical reliability gap due to "hallucination," a pervasive issue common in generative models. Hallucinations involve models generating false, fabricated, or factually incorrect information, or producing outputs that conflict with input prompts, common sense, or physical laws. For LVLMs, visual hallucinations specifically refer to discrepancies between the factual content of visual inputs and the generated textual outputs, including descriptions of nonexistent objects, misrepresentations of spatial relationships, or incorrect interpretations of object attributes.

This challenge poses substantial risks in safety-critical Artificial Intelligence (AI) applications, such as intelligent medical diagnosis and autonomous driving. Research indicates that hallucinations may be an inherent characteristic of these models, rather than a defect easily rectified, implying a statistical lower bound on their occurrence. Consequently, developing methods to assess the trustworthiness of LVLM outputs and quantify prediction uncertainty is crucial.

Existing hallucination detection approaches typically fall into three categories: verbal elicitation, external consistency checks, and internal uncertainty estimation. Verbal methods, which prompt models to self-report confidence, often fail due to misinterpretation or imprecise expression. External methods, which evaluate output consistency across multiple generations using external validation models, face scalability issues due high computational costs and dependency on prior knowledge. Traditional internal methods, such as Monte Carlo dropout and ensemble techniques, are largely infeasible for large-scale models due to architectural incompatibilities and excessive resource demands. Current internal alternatives often rely on token-level probability aggregation (e.g., negative log-likelihood, entropy) or activation analysis. However, LVLMs, like their language-only counterparts, frequently suffer from overconfidence and poor calibration, especially after fine-tuning with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), which limits the effectiveness of probability-based metrics in detecting erroneous predictions.

Furthermore, existing visual-centric hallucination benchmarks primarily assess LVLMs from a perception perspective, overlooking hallucinations that arise from advanced reasoning capabilities. This gap necessitates a more comprehensive evaluation framework. This paper addresses these limitations by proposing a new, more comprehensive benchmark and an efficient, robust internal detection method based on evidential conflict, aiming to enhance the trustworthiness of LVLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

This research is driven by two primary objectives, each motivated by identified gaps and challenges in the field of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs):

**Objective 1: To develop a comprehensive evaluation benchmark for visual hallucination in LVLMs.**
- **Motivation:** Existing benchmarks for visual hallucination in LVLMs, such as POPE, NOPE, and R-Bench, predominantly focus on evaluating the models' perceptual capabilities (e.g., recognizing objects, scenes, or relationships). These benchmarks overlook hallucinations that stem from advanced reasoning processes. Visual hallucinations can arise not only from misperceiving elements in an image but also from erroneous logical inferences or complex decision-making based on visual input. The absence of a benchmark that systematically evaluates both perception- and reasoning-based hallucinations across various visual semantics (instances, scenes, relations) leads to an incomplete understanding of LVLM vulnerabilities. A more robust evaluation tool is needed to uncover the full spectrum of hallucination issues, particularly in complex reasoning tasks where current models may exhibit critical failures. The paper notes that inconsistencies in data formats and evaluation metrics across existing datasets further complicate extensive hallucination assessment.

**Objective 2: To propose an effective and scalable method for detecting visual hallucinations in LVLMs through uncertainty estimation.**
- **Motivation:** Hallucinations in LVLMs pose significant risks in safety-critical AI applications, making the accurate and efficient detection of these errors crucial. The inherent nature of hallucinations, which are not easily rectifiable, underscores the need for effective detection mechanisms rather than solely relying on prevention. Current hallucination detection methods, particularly internal uncertainty estimation techniques, face several limitations when applied to large-scale models:
 - **Computational Infeasibility:** Traditional uncertainty quantification methods like Monte Carlo dropout and ensemble methods are computationally intensive and often incompatible with the architecture or scale of modern LVLMs.
 - **Overconfidence and Poor Calibration:** LVLMs, like Large Language Models (LLMs), often exhibit overconfidence, especially after fine-tuning with human feedback. This overconfidence leads to poorly calibrated probability outputs, rendering probability-based uncertainty metrics (e.g., negative log-likelihood, entropy) unreliable for accurately reflecting a model's true confidence and detecting hallucinations.
 - **Scalability Challenges:** External methods, while sometimes effective, often incur substantial computational overhead as they may require multiple generations or external validation models, hindering their scalability for real-world applications.
The motivation for this objective is to develop an internal, efficient, and accurate method for uncertainty estimation that can circumvent the limitations of existing approaches by effectively capturing discrepancies in high-level model features during the inference phase, thereby enhancing the trustworthiness and reliability of LVLMs without requiring extensive computational resources or architectural modifications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research addresses the identified gaps through two main methodological contributions: the development of a new evaluation dataset and the proposal of a novel hallucination detection method.

#### 3.1. Perception-Reasoning Evaluation Hallucination (PRE-HAL) Dataset

The authors developed the **Perception-Reasoning Evaluation Hallucination (PRE-HAL)** dataset to provide a more comprehensive benchmark for evaluating visual hallucinations in LVLMs.

- **Data Composition:** PRE-HAL consists of Visual Question Answering (VQA) data formatted as multiple-choice questions. Each entry includes an image, a question, 2 to 4 options, the correct answer, and a specified hallucination type. The dataset is structured around two core capabilities:
 - **Perception:** Focuses on gathering sensory information, subdivided into:
 - **Instance Perception:** Identifying specific objects.
 - **Scene Perception:** Understanding the overall context or category of a scene.
 - **Relation Perception:** Recognizing relationships between objects.
 - **Reasoning:** Involves drawing conclusions from information, subdivided into:
 - **Instance Reasoning:** Inferring properties or roles of instances.
 - **Relation Reasoning:** Inferring abstract or causal relationships between entities.
 This categorization aims to provide a systematic approach for detecting fine-grained visual hallucinations. Questions are designed to be visually dependent, requiring reliance on the image input.
- **Data Collection:** Data entries were collected from various sources, including MMBench, MMVP, POPE, and R-Bench.
 - MMBench and MMVP data, which align with the VQA multiple-choice format, were directly incorporated.
 - POPE (for object hallucinations) and R-Bench (for relationship hallucinations), originally designed with yes/no answers, were converted into a multiple-choice question format.
 - To enhance completeness and explore hallucinations related to Out-of-Distribution (OOD) data, a manual construction process was undertaken. This involved selecting rare object nouns (e.g., biomedical terms from Therapeutics Data Commons) and combining common nouns to create uncommon word compounds (e.g., "sand monkey") to generate challenging questions. The MCOCO image dataset was used to create image-text pairs for these OOD questions.
 - All data entries, including their hallucination types, were manually annotated and verified by human evaluators.

#### 3.2. Visual Hallucination Detection via Evidential Conflict

The paper proposes a novel visual hallucination detection method based on quantifying "evidential conflict" within the high-level features of LVLMs during inference, leveraging Dempster-Shafer Theory (DST). The rationale is that uncertainties leading to visual hallucinations, whether from intra-modality (e.g., low-resolution images) or inter-modality (e.g., conflicting information between modalities) sources, ultimately manifest as discrepancies in the fused high-level features.

- **Next-Token Prediction in LVLMs:** The process begins with text and image inputs being processed by a text embedding layer and visual encoder, respectively. These embeddings are then combined by a modality fusion module into a unified fusion embedding. This fused representation feeds into the Large Language Model (LLM) decoder, which generates features. These features are then passed through a Feed-Forward Network (FFN) and a softmax layer to predict the next token. The token with the highest probability is selected and concatenated with the preceding text for subsequent inference steps until a stop condition is met. The final output probability distribution for the next token is derived from the FFN's output and bias, followed by a softmax normalization.
- **Evidence Modeling:** The core idea is to treat the high-level feature vector (Φ) from the FFN as evidence. Following an interpretation from prior work, the softmax layer calculations within an FFN can be viewed as a combination of "simple mass functions" based on Φ.
 - Each feature ϕ_j contributes support to either a singleton class {z_i} or its complement {z_i}, depending on the sign of an associated weight of evidence w_ij. These weights form an "evidence pool matrix" W, derived from the feature vector Φ and parametric matrices B and A (representing weights and biases).
 - The parameters B and A are estimated according to the Least Commitment Principle (LCP). This principle aims to avoid unnecessary assumptions by determining parameters that yield mass functions with minimal informational content. The estimation involves closed-form solutions that effectively perform row-wise centering on the original model weights and adjust the bias, reducing the overall commitment of the evidence.
- **Mass Function Fusion and Conflict Estimation:**
 - **Two-Stage Fusion:** To manage the computational complexity of combining evidence across power sets, a two-stage fusion process is employed:
 - **Stage 1 (Identical Focal Sets):** Simple mass functions with the same focal set (e.g., all supports for {z_i} from different features) are aggregated by summing their weights of evidence. This significantly reduces computational overhead. This yields aggregated simple mass functions m⁺_j(·) and m⁻_j(·) for each class {z_i} and its complement {z_i}, respectively.
 - **Stage 2 (Different Focal Sets):** These aggregated simple mass functions, which now have different focal sets, are combined using Dempster's rule. This results in an overall mass function m⁺(·) (for classes and the entire frame of discernment V) and m⁻(·) (for subsets of V).
 - **Conflict Estimation (κ):** The "conflict" (κ) between m⁺(·) and m⁻(·) is then computed as the final metric. This κ value quantifies the degree of inconsistency or disagreement among the evidence derived from the high-level features.
 - **Sentence-Level Conflict:** Since LVLMs generate responses token-by-token, each generated token corresponds to a κ value. To represent the conflict for an entire generated sentence, the maximum κ value (κ_max) among all tokens in the response is chosen as the overall hallucination detection metric.
 - The final combined mass function can be transformed into a probability mass function through plausibility transformation, demonstrating its equivalence to the standard softmax output in a principled way.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the nature of visual hallucinations in LVLMs and the efficacy of the proposed detection method.

#### 4.1. Comprehensive Visual Hallucination Evaluation on PRE-HAL

The evaluation of three state-of-the-art LVLMs (LLaVA-v1.5, mPLUG-Owl2, and mPLUG-Owl3) on the PRE-HAL dataset revealed the following:
- **Overall Hallucination Rates:** LLaVA-v1.5 exhibited a hallucination rate of 23.66%, mPLUG-Owl2 22.71%, and mPLUG-Owl3 14.38%.
- **Calibration (ECE Scores):** The Expected Calibration Error (ECE) scores were 0.35 for LLaVA-v1.5, 0.19 for mPLUG-Owl2, and 0.48 for mPLUG-Owl3. Notably, despite having the lowest hallucination rate, mPLUG-Owl3 exhibited the highest ECE score, suggesting increased overconfidence even in its correct predictions.
- **Vulnerabilities in Reasoning Tasks:** The evaluation identified that reasoning-based tasks, particularly "relation reasoning," posed greater challenges for LVLMs than perception-based tasks. Hallucination rates for relation reasoning reached 49.44% for LLaVA-v1.5, 50.42% for mPLUG-Owl2, and 19.92% for mPLUG-Owl3. Generally, reasoning-related hallucinations were significantly higher than perception-based ones. The incidence of scene, instance, and relation hallucinations also increased progressively, indicating increasing difficulty.

#### 4.2. Visual Hallucination Detection Performance

The proposed evidential conflict method (κ_max) was evaluated against several baseline uncertainty metrics, including predictive entropy (PE), length-normalized predictive entropy (LN-PE), sum of probabilities (PS), sum of logarithm of probabilities (LPS), response length (L), and external methods like Semantic Entropy (SE) and Self-Consistency (SC).

- **Fixed False-Positive Rate (FPR) Evaluation:** At a fixed FPR of 0.08, κ_max demonstrated strong performance:
 - On mPLUG-Owl2 and mPLUG-Owl3, it achieved the highest accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score among all internal methods. For mPLUG-Owl2, κ_max's metrics (Accuracy 76.67%, Precision 46.90%, Recall 20.65%, F1 28.68%) surpassed even the external methods SE and SC.
 - On LLaVA-v1.5, κ_max outperformed all baselines in accuracy, precision, and F1 score, except for recall, indicating its general effectiveness.
 - The length (L) of the model's response tokens also showed satisfactory results, consistent with prior findings that longer outputs correlate with increased hallucination probability.
- **AUROC and AUPR Scores:** For a more comprehensive evaluation, AUROC (Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic curve) and AUPR (Area Under the Precision-Recall curve) were used:
 - **Overall Improvement:** The evidential conflict method consistently outperformed the best internal baseline methods across all three LVLMs. It achieved average AUROC improvements of 4% for LLaVA-v1.5, 10% for mPLUG-Owl2, and 7% for mPLUG-Owl3.
 - **Robustness Across Hallucination Types:** κ_max demonstrated robust performance across all five fine-grained hallucination types within PRE-HAL. Specifically, it exhibited exceptional robustness in scene perception tasks, achieving AUROC scores of 0.71-0.73%, significantly outperforming entropy-based and probability-based baselines in this category.
 - **Comparison to External Methods:** While external methods like SC and SE showed strong performance in some metrics or hallucination types, the proposed internal method (κ_max) achieved competitive results without requiring multiple inferences or external model calls, highlighting its efficiency.
 - **Model Calibration Impact:** The performance of all detection methods, including κ_max, was generally superior on mPLUG-Owl2, which was noted for its better calibration compared to the other models.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies were conducted to assess the influence of temperature during generation and model size on detection performance.
- **Temperature:** κ_max consistently maintained the best performance across various temperature settings (0.1, 0.3, 0.7, 1.0, 1.5) on LLaVA-v1.5-7B, indicating stability of the evidence conflict metric to inference temperature variations.
- **Model Size:** κ_max consistently outperformed baselines across 7B, 13B, and 34B parameter models (using LLaVA-v1.5 for 7B/13B and LLaVA-v1.6 for 34B). The metric remained stable across these sizes, with a slight performance improvement observed at the 13B parameter setting.

#### 4.4. Investigation of LLM-Based Result Check

The study explored the use of ChatGPT 4o for automated assessment of LVLM output correctness.
- **ChatGPT 4o vs. Human Evaluation:** Comparisons between ChatGPT 4o's judgments and human volunteer assessments showed that the differences were not substantial enough to impact the overall experimental results. This suggests that ChatGPT 4o can serve as an efficient alternative to resource-intensive human assessment for multiple-choice question evaluation, within an acceptable deviation range.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of trustworthy AI, particularly concerning the reliability of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs), in several significant ways:

- **Enhanced Understanding of LVLM Vulnerabilities:** The introduction of the **PRE-HAL** dataset represents a significant step towards a more comprehensive evaluation of LVLMs. By systematically assessing both perception and reasoning capabilities across various visual semantics (instances, scenes, relations), the benchmark exposes critical vulnerabilities, especially in complex reasoning tasks where state-of-the-art LVLMs exhibit hallucination rates exceeding 49%. This deeper insight into where and why LVLMs hallucinate is crucial for targeted model improvements and development.
- **Computationally Efficient and Scalable Hallucination Detection:** The proposed Dempster-Shafer theory (DST)-based visual hallucination detection method, leveraging evidential conflict in high-level features, offers a practical solution. Unlike many existing methods that require multiple forward passes, architectural modifications, or additional training, this approach is an inference-phase solution that efficiently quantifies token-level uncertainties. Its demonstrated superiority over conventional uncertainty metrics (average AUROC improvements of 4% to 10%) validates its effectiveness. The method's robustness, particularly in scene perception tasks, highlights its ability to circumvent the calibration limitations of traditional probability-based approaches.
- **Improved Trustworthiness in Critical Applications:** By providing a reliable and efficient mechanism for detecting visual hallucinations, this work directly addresses a critical reliability gap that limits the deployment of LVLMs in safety-critical applications such as autonomous driving and medical diagnosis. Enhancing the trustworthiness of LVLM outputs is essential for their responsible integration into real-world systems.
- **Broader Applicability of Evidential Uncertainty:** The methodology, which calculates evidential uncertainty using intermediate features and Feed-Forward Network (FFN) parameters, has potential applicability beyond visual hallucination detection in VQA tasks. It can be adapted for detecting other types of prediction errors (e.g., misprediction in classification networks, semantic segmentation errors) and for identifying abnormal examples (e.g., adversarial inputs, out-of-distribution data) in various neural network architectures that incorporate FFNs. This plug-and-play nature, requiring no additional model training, makes it a versatile tool for uncertainty quantification in deep learning.
- **Validation of LLM-Based Evaluation Tools:** The empirical finding that LLM-based tools like ChatGPT 4o can serve as efficient alternatives for assessing model output correctness with acceptable deviation from human judgment suggests a pathway for more scalable and automated evaluation processes in AI research, reducing the resource intensity of benchmark creation and maintenance.

A limitation of the proposed detection method is its reliance on access to internal model parameters and intermediate features, meaning it is currently applicable only to open-source models and cannot be directly applied to black-box models accessible solely through APIs. However, for transparent models, it offers a cost-effective and robust solution for enhancing LVLM reliability.
