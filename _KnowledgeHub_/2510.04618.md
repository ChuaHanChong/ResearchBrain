# Agentic Context Engineering: Evolving Contexts for Self-Improving Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The Agentic Context Engineering (ACE) framework dynamically evolves and curates comprehensive 'playbook' contexts for large language models, allowing them to continuously improve performance. This enables smaller, open-source models to match or exceed proprietary LLM agent performance on benchmarks like AppWorld, simultaneously reducing adaptation latency by up to 91.5% and token cost by 83.6%.

## Problem

- Current prompt optimizers often prioritize concise instructions, leading to a "brevity bias" that discards valuable domain-specific heuristics and detailed failure modes essential for complex tasks.
- Iterative rewriting of contexts by LLMs frequently results in "context collapse," where detailed information is compressed into shorter, less informative summaries, causing performance degradation over time.
- Achieving scalable and self-improving LLM systems for complex tasks and autonomous agents requires efficient context adaptation without constant reliance on expensive human-labeled supervision.

## Method

- ACE employs a modular agentic architecture with a ==Generator== for task execution, a dedicated ==Reflector== for critically analyzing traces and distilling insights, and a ==Curator== for synthesizing lessons into compact context items.
- Context is represented as structured, itemized "bullets" (==delta context items==) that enable localized, incremental updates rather than monolithic rewrites, preserving past knowledge and reducing computational overhead.
- A =="grow-and-refine" mechanism== continuously appends new insights (growth) and periodically prunes redundancy using semantic embeddings (refinement) to maintain comprehensive yet relevant contexts.

## Results

- On the AppWorld benchmark, ACE improves ReAct's accuracy by an average of **17.0%** offline and **7.6%** online over Dynamic Cheatsheet, with a smaller open-source model matching or surpassing GPT-4.1 powered agents.
- ACE achieves an average performance gain of **8.6%** on financial reasoning tasks (FiNER and Formula), outperforming ICL, MIPROv2, and GEPA by an average of **10.9%** in offline adaptation.
- The framework dramatically reduces adaptation overhead, showing an **82.3%** reduction in latency and **75.1%** fewer rollouts compared to GEPA in offline settings, and a **91.5%** reduction in latency and **83.6%** reduction in token dollar cost compared to Dynamic Cheatsheet in online settings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLMs benefit significantly from comprehensive, detailed contexts, from which they can autonomously distill relevance, countering the common assumption that shorter prompts are always better.
> - Separating the generation, reflection, and curation processes through a specialized agentic architecture leads to higher-quality context evolution and prevents the information loss seen in monolithic context rewriting.
> - Efficient and robust self-improvement for LLMs can be achieved by leveraging natural execution feedback and environment signals, reducing the need for expensive ground-truth labels during adaptation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the evolving landscape of large language model (LLM) applications, particularly those focused on LLM agents and compound AI systems. A prominent trend in this area is the increasing reliance on "context adaptation" or "context engineering." This paradigm involves modifying the inputs to an LLM with clarified instructions, structured reasoning steps, or domain-specific evidence, rather than altering the model's weights through fine-tuning. Context adaptation offers advantages such as enhanced interpretability, rapid integration of new knowledge at runtime, and the ability to share adapted contexts across different models or modules within a system. Advances in long-context LLMs and efficient inference techniques, like KV cache reuse, are making context-based approaches increasingly viable for deployment.

Despite the benefits, existing context adaptation methods exhibit two primary limitations:
1. **Brevity Bias:** Many prompt optimization techniques prioritize concise summaries, which can inadvertently omit detailed domain-specific heuristics, tool-use guidelines, or common failure modes essential for complex tasks. This focus on brevity, while potentially useful for some validation metrics, may not capture the granular strategies required by agents and knowledge-intensive applications.
2. **Context Collapse:** Methods that rely on monolithic rewriting of contexts by an LLM often lead to a gradual or sudden degradation, where accumulated information is compressed into shorter, less informative summaries. This iterative erosion of detail can result in significant performance declines, particularly in domains demanding precise, detailed, and task-specific knowledge.

This research addresses these limitations by proposing a framework that views contexts not as static, concise summaries, but as dynamic, comprehensive "playbooks." This perspective aligns with a broader shift in LLM research towards leveraging extensive, detailed contexts, where LLMs are capable of distilling relevance from longer inputs autonomously. The paper positions its contribution as essential for building reliable, scalable, and self-improving AI systems that can effectively retain and evolve knowledge.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce Agentic Context Engineering (ACE), a framework designed to enhance context adaptation in LLM applications. The motivation stems from the identified shortcomings of current context adaptation methods, specifically brevity bias and context collapse, which hinder the performance and scalability of advanced LLM-based systems like autonomous agents and domain-specific reasoning tools.

The detailed motivations include:
- **Overcoming Brevity Bias:** Current prompt optimizers often prioritize conciseness, leading to the loss of critical, granular information such as domain-specific heuristics, detailed tool-use guidelines, and specific failure modes. This compression can be detrimental to tasks requiring deep, specialized knowledge. ACE aims to foster the accumulation of comprehensive, detailed contexts that function as rich "playbooks."
- **Preventing Context Collapse:** The iterative rewriting of contexts by LLMs often results in a loss of information over time, leading to shorter, less informative summaries and subsequent performance degradation. This is particularly problematic for long-running or complex tasks where accumulated knowledge is crucial. ACE seeks to implement a structured update mechanism that preserves detailed knowledge and prevents such collapse.
- **Enabling Scalable and Self-Improving LLM Systems:** The goal is to develop a framework that allows LLMs to continuously adapt and improve their performance through context modification, rather than costly weight updates. This adaptation should be efficient, scalable, and capable of operating with low overhead, supporting both offline optimization (e.g., system prompts) and online adaptation (e.g., agent memory).
- **Leveraging Execution Feedback for Self-Improvement:** A key motivation is to enable effective context adaptation without requiring explicit labeled supervision. Instead, the framework should leverage natural execution feedback and environment signals, which are readily available in agentic and interactive settings, to guide the self-improvement process. This reduces the dependency on manually curated datasets and makes the system more autonomous.
- **Supporting Knowledge-Intensive Applications:** For applications such as multi-turn agents, domain-specific programming, and complex financial or legal analysis, retaining and organizing detailed, task-specific knowledge is paramount. ACE is motivated by the need to provide LLMs with contexts that are rich enough to support these demanding applications reliably.

In essence, the paper aims to shift the paradigm of context adaptation from simple summarization to structured, incremental, and comprehensive knowledge accumulation, thereby unlocking new levels of performance and adaptability for LLM-powered systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ACE (Agentic Context Engineering) is presented as a framework for comprehensive context adaptation, applicable in both offline (e.g., system prompt optimization) and online (e.g., test-time memory adaptation) scenarios. The core methodological principle of ACE is to treat contexts as dynamic, evolving "playbooks" that continuously accumulate, refine, and organize strategies rather than being static or compressed summaries.

The ACE framework adopts an agentic architecture, inspired by Dynamic Cheatsheet, which distributes responsibilities across three specialized components:

1. **Generator:** This component is responsible for producing reasoning trajectories for new queries. It utilizes the current evolved context (the "playbook") to guide its decision-making, tool use, and interaction with the environment. The Generator's performance provides signals on the effectiveness of the current context.
2. **Reflector:** The Reflector analyzes the reasoning trajectories generated by the Generator, along with execution feedback, unit test reports, and ground truth (when available). Its function is to diagnose errors, identify successful strategies, and distill concrete insights. It can optionally refine these insights through multiple iterations to ensure high quality. The Reflector also assesses the helpfulness or harmfulness of existing context "bullets" used by the Generator.
3. **Curator:** This component synthesizes the lessons extracted by the Reflector into compact "delta entries." These deltas represent small, incremental updates to the context. The Curator's output is then integrated into the existing playbook by a lightweight, non-LLM logic.

ACE introduces three key innovations to address the limitations of prior methods:

1. **Dedicated Reflector for Insight Extraction:** By separating the complex tasks of evaluating execution traces and extracting actionable insights into a dedicated Reflector component, ACE aims to improve the quality of context updates. This modularity prevents overloading a single model with all responsibilities (generation, reflection, and curation) and allows for more focused and effective insight generation.
2. **Incremental Delta Updates:** A fundamental design principle of ACE is to represent context as a collection of structured, itemized "bullets," rather than a single monolithic prompt. Each bullet is a small unit of knowledge (e.g., a reusable strategy, domain concept, or common failure mode) with associated metadata (unique identifier, counters for helpful/harmful usage). Instead of regenerating contexts in full, ACE incrementally produces "delta contexts"—small sets of candidate bullets distilled by the Reflector and integrated by the Curator. This approach localizes updates to only relevant bullets, enables fine-grained retrieval, and ensures past knowledge is preserved, preventing context collapse. The deterministic merging of deltas by non-LLM logic reduces computational cost and latency.
3. **Grow-and-Refine Mechanism:** To maintain context compactness and relevance while accommodating growth, ACE incorporates a "grow-and-refine" mechanism. New bullet points are appended with unique identifiers, while existing ones are updated in place (e.g., incrementing usage counters). A de-duplication step, performed by comparing bullets via semantic embeddings, prunes redundant information. This refinement can be performed proactively after each delta or lazily when context window limits are approached, offering flexibility based on application needs.

The framework supports multi-epoch adaptation, where the same queries can be revisited to progressively strengthen the context. For evaluation, the same LLM (DeepSeek-V3.1-671B by default) is used for the Generator, Reflector, and Curator to isolate the benefits of context construction itself, preventing knowledge transfer from a potentially stronger reflection/curation model to a weaker generator.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ACE across various benchmarks and adaptation settings yielded several findings:

**Overall Performance:**
ACE consistently outperformed strong baselines in both agentic and domain-specific tasks. Across agent benchmarks, an average gain of 10.6% was observed, and on domain-specific benchmarks, an average gain of 8.6% was recorded. These improvements were demonstrated in both offline (system prompt optimization) and online (test-time memory adaptation) settings.

**Results on Agent Benchmark (AppWorld):**
- **Outperformance:** On the AppWorld benchmark, ACE consistently improved over baselines. In offline adaptation, ReAct + ACE surpassed ReAct + ICL by 12.3% and ReAct + GEPA by 11.9%. In online adaptation, ACE outperformed Dynamic Cheatsheet (DC) by an average of 7.6%.
- **Self-Supervision:** ACE demonstrated effectiveness in constructing contexts without requiring labeled supervision. By leveraging execution feedback and environmental signals, ReAct + ACE achieved an average improvement of 14.8% over the ReAct baseline, indicating its robustness in self-improving scenarios.
- **Leaderboard Standing:** On the AppWorld leaderboard (as of September 20, 2025), ReAct + ACE (59.4% average) matched the top-ranked production-level agent, IBM-CUGA (60.3%), which is powered by GPT-4.1. Notably, ACE achieved this using a smaller open-source model (DeepSeek-V3.1). With online adaptation, ReAct + ACE surpassed IBM-CUGA by 8.4% in Task Goal Completion (TGC) and 0.7% in Scenario Goal Completion (SGC) on the harder test-challenge split.

**Results on Domain-Specific Benchmarks (Finance, Medical, Text-to-SQL):**
- **Financial Analysis (FiNER and Formula):** ACE demonstrated substantial improvements. In offline adaptation with ground-truth labels, ACE surpassed ICL, MIPROv2, and GEPA by an average of 10.9%. In online adaptation, ACE exceeded DC by an average of 6.2%. The gains were particularly pronounced on Formula, with an 18.0% increase.
- **Feedback Dependency:** A observation was that without reliable ground-truth supervision or execution signals, the performance of both ACE and DC could degrade. This suggests that the quality of feedback critically influences the effectiveness of context adaptation in knowledge-intensive domains.
- **Generalizability:** Consistent gains were also observed on medical reasoning (DDXPlus, +15.0%) and text-to-SQL (BIRD-SQL, +5.1% average), indicating that the playbook-style context adaptation is transferable across various specialized domains.

**Generalization Across LLMs:**
ACE's performance gains were consistent when evaluated with different backbone LLMs, including GPT-OSS-120B, GPT-5.1, and Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct. This indicates that the framework is model-agnostic and its benefits are not limited to a specific LLM family.

**Ablation Study and Sensitivity Analysis:**
Ablation studies on AppWorld confirmed the contribution of ACE's design choices: the dedicated Reflector with iterative refinement, multi-epoch adaptation, and offline warmup each contributed to performance gains. Incremental delta updates were found to be critical for preventing context collapse and achieving high performance. Sensitivity analyses showed that ACE's performance is stable across a reasonable range of hyperparameter settings, such as reflection iterations, deduplication thresholds, and pruning triggers.

**Cost and Speed Analysis:**
ACE demonstrated advantages in reducing adaptation cost and latency:
- **Adaptation Latency:** On offline AppWorld adaptation, ACE achieved an 82.3% reduction in latency and a 75.1% reduction in rollouts compared to GEPA. For online FiNER adaptation, ACE showed a 91.5% reduction in latency and an 83.6% reduction in token dollar cost compared to DC.
- **Fine-Grained Cost:** During the adaptation stage, ACE substantially reduced input-token usage (80.8%) and output-token usage (83.6%) compared to GEPA, largely by avoiding GEPA’s prompt-validation loop and replacing full rewrites with localized delta updates.
- **KV Cache Reuse:** Although ACE generates longer contexts, the study on OpenAI API (GPT-5.1) indicated high cache reuse (91.8% of input tokens served from cache), leading to an 82.6% reduction in billed input-token cost during evaluation. This suggests that modern serving infrastructures can amortize the cost of longer contexts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research on Agentic Context Engineering (ACE) presents several implications for the development and deployment of LLM-based systems.

**Enhanced Scalability and Efficiency in Self-Improvement:** ACE offers a pathway for LLM systems to achieve continuous self-improvement in a scalable and efficient manner. By evolving contexts through incremental updates rather than costly weight adjustments, the framework reduces adaptation latency and computational overhead. This efficiency is critical for long-horizon tasks and for operating LLMs in dynamic environments where rapid adaptation is necessary. The findings suggest that scalable self-improvement can be achieved with both higher accuracy and lower resource expenditure.

**Bridging Performance Gaps:** The demonstration that ACE, when integrated with a smaller open-source model (DeepSeek-V3.1), can match or surpass the performance of a larger, production-level proprietary model (GPT-4.1) on a challenging benchmark like AppWorld, highlights its potential to democratize high-performance AI. This capability allows developers to build sophisticated LLM applications without exclusive reliance on the largest models, fostering innovation across a broader range of resources.

**Advancements in Online and Continual Learning:** ACE provides a flexible and efficient alternative to traditional model fine-tuning for online and continual learning paradigms. Adapting contexts is generally less resource-intensive than updating model weights, making it suitable for scenarios involving distribution shifts, limited training data, or real-time learning from new experiences. This positions ACE as a significant contribution to developing AI systems that can continuously learn and adapt in deployment.

**Support for Responsible AI and Interpretability:** The human-interpretable nature of contexts, combined with ACE's structured, itemized "playbook" approach, offers benefits for responsible AI. It facilitates selective unlearning, where specific pieces of information can be removed or modified due to privacy concerns, legal constraints, or the identification of outdated/incorrect knowledge. This capability enhances transparency and control over what an LLM "knows" and applies.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
Despite its contributions, ACE has identified limitations. Its effectiveness is contingent on the quality of the Reflector; if the Reflector fails to extract meaningful insights, the context may become noisy or detrimental. This dependency on reliable feedback signals (e.g., ground-truth labels, clear execution outcomes) means that in settings lacking such signals, the performance of context adaptation methods, including ACE, may degrade. Additionally, ACE is most beneficial for tasks requiring detailed domain knowledge, complex tool use, or environment-specific strategies. Tasks that primarily benefit from concise, high-level instructions (e.g., certain question-answering tasks) or fixed strategies might not fully leverage ACE's detailed context accumulation.

Future work could focus on enhancing the robustness of the Reflector to noisy feedback, exploring automated mechanisms for detecting and mitigating context pollution, and further investigating the optimal balance between context comprehensiveness and conciseness for various task types. The integration of ACE with advanced KV cache management systems also points to ongoing research in ML systems to make context-rich approaches increasingly practical.
