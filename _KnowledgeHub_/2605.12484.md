# Learning, Fast and Slow: Towards LLMs That Adapt Continually

> [!summary] Summary
> The Fast-Slow Training (FST) framework enables large language models (LLMs) to adapt continually by jointly optimizing persistent model parameters (slow weights) and mutable textual prompts (fast weights). This method achieves higher performance, greater data efficiency, and improved plasticity across reasoning tasks while reducing catastrophic forgetting, demonstrating superior continual learning capabilities.

## Problem

- Existing LLM adaptation methods (SFT, RL) encode all task-specific information into a single set of model weights, leading to catastrophic forgetting, reduced plasticity, and decreased out-of-distribution generalization.
- In-context learning via prompts offers rapid adaptation but often cannot match the peak performance of parameter-based updates.
- Traditional parameter-only updates are computationally expensive and data-intensive, struggling in continual learning scenarios where models need to acquire new skills sequentially without performance degradation.

## Method

- Introduces **Fast-Slow Training (FST)**, a framework that integrates ==slow weights== (model parameters) and ==fast weights== (optimized textual prompts) for joint optimization.
- Slow weights are updated using ==Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)== with a ==cispo== objective, targeting general, persistent behaviors.
- Fast weights are optimized through ==Generative Evolutionary Prompt Optimization (GEPA)==, a reflective evolutionary process that generates and refines natural language prompts for rapid, task-specific adjustments.

## Results

- Achieved peak performance of RL with substantially fewer training samples (e.g., **3.0x fewer** on CodeIO and HoVer-hard), demonstrating superior data efficiency without OOD generalization decline.
- Consistently reached a higher performance asymptote than RL alone (e.g., **+4.4 percentage points** on CodeIO, **+7.7 pp** on HoVer-hard), indicating enhanced maximum accuracy.
- Maintained greater plasticity and reduced catastrophic forgetting, enabling acquisition of multiple tasks sequentially in continual learning scenarios where RL-only methods struggled or stalled.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Distributing adaptation across both textual and parametric channels allows the system to converge to a higher overall performance ceiling and remain closer to the base model's capabilities.
> - The fast weight channel rapidly extracts and injects task structure early in training, providing effective gradient signals that accelerate the learning process for slow weights.
> - Separating transient task-specific knowledge into mutable textual contexts prevents over-specialization of core model parameters, preserving general reasoning behaviors and plasticity for future tasks.
