# Training Diffusion Models with Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Berkeley and MIT introduced Denoising Diffusion Policy Optimization (DDPO), a method for training diffusion models using reinforcement learning to directly optimize arbitrary downstream objectives. DDPO reformulates the denoising process as a multi-step Markov Decision Process, enabling improved performance over reward-weighted likelihood approaches and demonstrating strong generalization on objectives like image compressibility, aesthetic quality, and automated prompt alignment.

## Problem

- The conventional training objective of diffusion models (maximum likelihood) often does not directly align with desired downstream objectives, such as human aesthetic preferences or specific physical properties.
- Existing methods like Reward-Weighted Regression (RWR) implicitly view the diffusion process as a one-step decision, lacking strong theoretical justification for multi-step generation and showing less empirical effectiveness.
- Fine-tuning generative models for complex, subjective, or computationally defined goals typically requires extensive human annotation or manual prompt engineering.

## Method

- Researchers from UC Berkeley and MIT introduced **DDPO** (Denoising Diffusion Policy Optimization), a method for training diffusion models using ==reinforcement learning== to directly optimize arbitrary downstream objectives.
- **DDPO** reformulates the iterative denoising process of diffusion models as a ==multi-step Markov Decision Process (MDP)==, allowing for the application of ==policy gradient== algorithms like ==REINFORCE== and ==importance sampling==-based variants.
- The approach involves an alternating training loop where samples are generated from the current diffusion model, their rewards computed, and the model parameters updated based on these samples and their rewards.

## Results

- **DDPO** consistently and significantly outperformed **Reward-Weighted Regression (RWR)** variants across diverse objectives (compressibility, incompressibility, aesthetic quality), achieving higher reward scores for the same computational cost.
- The VLM-based automated reward function, combined with DDPO, successfully improved prompt-image alignment, making images generated for challenging prompts more faithful to the specified actions and subjects **without human labels**.
- Models fine-tuned with DDPO demonstrated strong generalization capabilities, effectively adapting to unseen prompts and novel compositions beyond their training set.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Treating the multi-step denoising process as a sequential decision-making problem (MDP) allows for principled application of policy gradient methods.
> - Direct policy gradient optimization (DDPO) is empirically more effective for aligning diffusion models with arbitrary objectives than reward-weighted likelihood approaches.
> - Vision-Language Models (VLMs) can provide automated "AI feedback" for prompt-image alignment, significantly reducing the need for human annotation in RL fine-tuning.
