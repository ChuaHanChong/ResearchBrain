# Training Diffusion Models with Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Berkeley and MIT introduced Denoising Diffusion Policy Optimization (DDPO), a method for training diffusion models using reinforcement learning to directly optimize arbitrary downstream objectives. DDPO reformulates the denoising process as a multi-step Markov Decision Process, enabling improved performance over reward-weighted likelihood approaches and demonstrating strong generalization on objectives like image compressibility, aesthetic quality, and automated prompt alignment.

## Problem

- The conventional training objective of diffusion models (maximum likelihood) often does not directly align with desired downstream objectives, such as human aesthetic preferences or specific physical properties.
- Existing methods like Reward-Weighted Regression (RWR) implicitly view the diffusion process as a one-step decision, lacking strong theoretical justification for multi-step generation and showing less empirical effectiveness.
- Fine-tuning generative models for complex, subjective, or computationally defined goals typically requires extensive human annotation or manual prompt engineering.

## Method

- Researchers from UC Berkeley and MIT introduced **DDPO** (Denoising Diffusion Policy Optimization), a method for training diffusion models using ==reinforcement learning== to directly optimize arbitrary downstream objectives.
- **DDPO** reformulates the iterative denoising process of diffusion models as a ==multi-step Markov Decision Process (MDP)==, allowing for the application of ==policy gradient== algorithms like ==REINFORCE== and ==importance sampling==-based variants.
- The approach involves an alternating training loop where samples are generated from the current diffusion model, their rewards computed, and the model parameters updated based on these samples and their rewards.

## Results

- **DDPO** consistently and significantly outperformed **Reward-Weighted Regression (RWR)** variants across diverse objectives (compressibility, incompressibility, aesthetic quality), achieving higher reward scores for the same computational cost.
- The VLM-based automated reward function, combined with DDPO, successfully improved prompt-image alignment, making images generated for challenging prompts more faithful to the specified actions and subjects **without human labels**.
- Models fine-tuned with DDPO demonstrated strong generalization capabilities, effectively adapting to unseen prompts and novel compositions beyond their training set.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Treating the multi-step denoising process as a sequential decision-making problem (MDP) allows for principled application of policy gradient methods.
> - Direct policy gradient optimization (DDPO) is empirically more effective for aligning diffusion models with arbitrary objectives than reward-weighted likelihood approaches.
> - Vision-Language Models (VLMs) can provide automated "AI feedback" for prompt-image alignment, significantly reducing the need for human annotation in RL fine-tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the crucial intersection of generative modeling, specifically diffusion models, and reinforcement learning. Diffusion probabilistic models, introduced by Sohl-Dickstein et al. (2015) and popularized by Ho et al. (2020), have rapidly become the dominant paradigm for high-fidelity generative tasks across continuous domains, including image, video, and 3D synthesis, as well as applications in drug design and robotics. Their conventional training relies on approximating a maximum likelihood objective, typically a variational lower bound (VLB) on the log-likelihood of the training data.

However, a critical observation motivating this work is that while diffusion models excel at data distribution matching, their training objective (log-likelihood) often does not directly align with desired *downstream objectives*. For instance, a high-likelihood image might not necessarily be aesthetically pleasing to a human, or a generated molecule might not be optimally effective as a drug. This misalignment is a well-recognized challenge, as modifications to diffusion objectives for stricter likelihood optimization sometimes lead to worse perceptual quality (Nichol & Dhariwal, 2021; Kingma et al., 2021).

The broader research landscape has seen significant success in aligning large language models (LLMs) with human preferences and complex instructions through Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) (Ouyang et al., 2022; Bai et al., 2022a,b). This paper extends the principles of RLHF and direct objective optimization to the domain of diffusion models, bridging a gap between powerful generative capabilities and fine-grained, task-specific control.

Prior work has explored connections between generation and sequential decision-making, such as Bachman & Precup (2015) applying RL to image generation. More recently, Fan & Lee (2023) introduced a policy gradient method for diffusion models, but their focus was primarily on improving data distribution matching using a GAN-like discriminator, rather than optimizing arbitrary downstream objectives. Lee et al. (2023) explored aligning text-to-image diffusion models with human preferences using reward-weighted likelihood maximization (RWR), a method that this paper directly compares against and significantly improves upon.

Crucially, this work is positioned as a direct advancement over RWR-style approaches by framing the denoising process as a *multi-step* Markov Decision Process (MDP), enabling the application of theoretically sound policy gradient methods. The paper also acknowledges concurrent work, DPOK (Fan et al., 2023), which also applies policy gradients to text-to-image models for human preferences. This paper differentiates itself by demonstrating broader applicability to diverse reward functions beyond a single preference model, training on multiple prompts simultaneously, and showcasing strong generalization capabilities. By directly comparing against and outperforming RWR and DPOK, this research establishes a new state-of-the-art in fine-tuning diffusion models with RL for custom objectives.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the fundamental mismatch between the conventional training objective of diffusion models—approximating maximum likelihood of training data—and the actual objectives desired by users in real-world applications. While likelihood is a powerful statistical measure, it often fails to capture nuanced, task-specific requirements such as human-perceived image quality, adherence to complex compositional instructions, or specific material properties like compressibility.

The key objectives of this paper are:

1. **Direct Optimization of Downstream Objectives:** To investigate and develop reinforcement learning methods capable of directly optimizing diffusion models for arbitrary, black-box downstream objectives, moving beyond simple data distribution matching.
2. **Framing Denoising as a Multi-step MDP:** To conceptually and formally recast the iterative denoising process inherent in diffusion models as a multi-step sequential decision-making problem, thereby enabling the principled application of policy gradient reinforcement learning algorithms.
3. **Developing Denoising Diffusion Policy Optimization (DDPO):** To introduce a novel class of policy gradient algorithms, termed DDPO, specifically designed for diffusion models. The aim is for DDPO to be more effective than existing reward-weighted likelihood approaches (e.g., Reward-Weighted Regression, RWR) by leveraging the sequential nature of denoising.
4. **Empirical Validation on Diverse Objectives:** To empirically demonstrate DDPO's effectiveness on large text-to-image diffusion models across a spectrum of challenging objectives, including:
- **Image Compressibility/Incompressibility:** Objectives difficult to specify through traditional prompting.
- **Aesthetic Quality:** Objectives derived from human feedback (RLHF), using pre-trained predictors.
- **Automated Prompt-Image Alignment:** A novel approach using Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to provide automated feedback, mimicking RL from AI Feedback (RLAIF) and reducing the need for extensive human annotation.
5. **Assessing Generalization:** To evaluate whether the fine-tuning procedure on a limited set of training prompts can generalize effectively to unseen prompts and novel compositions, akin to the generalization observed in instruction-tuned large language models.

In essence, the research aims to provide a flexible and powerful mechanism for users to *steer* generative diffusion models towards specific, often subjective or complex, goals that are difficult or impossible to achieve through mere prompt engineering or by relying solely on the inherent biases of the pre-training data distribution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodological innovation of this paper lies in its reformulation of the diffusion model's sampling process as a Markov Decision Process (MDP), enabling the direct application of policy gradient reinforcement learning.

The general problem is defined as maximizing a reward function `r(x_0, c)` for a generated sample `x_0` given a context `c`, denoted as `J_DDRL(θ)`. The paper explores two main approaches to achieve this:

1. **Reward-Weighted Regression (RWR):** This approach minimizes the standard denoising loss (`L_DDPM`) but weights each training sample (`x_0`) by its associated reward.
- **Mechanism:** Samples are generated from the current diffusion model, their rewards are computed, and the model is then re-trained on these samples with weights proportional to their rewards. Two weighting schemes are considered: an exponentiated reward function (`w_RWR`) and a sparse binary thresholding (`w_sparse`).
- **MDP Formulation (Implicit):** RWR implicitly views the diffusion process as a *one-step MDP* where the "action" is the final generated image `x_0`, and the reward is received immediately.
- **Limitation:** The `L_DDPM` objective is an approximation of the log-likelihood, meaning RWR applied to diffusion models lacks strong theoretical justification and is shown to be empirically less effective.

2. **Denoising Diffusion Policy Optimization (DDPO):** This is the paper's primary contribution, directly framing the iterative denoising process as a multi-step MDP.
- **MDP Formulation (Explicit):**
 - **State (`s_t`):** Composed of the context `c`, current timestep `t`, and the noisy image `x_t`.
 - **Action (`a_t`):** The denoised image `x_{t-1}` produced at that step.
 - **Policy (`π(a_t | s_t)`):** The reverse diffusion process `p_θ(x_{t-1} | x_t, c)`, which is typically an isotropic Gaussian parameterized by the diffusion model's neural network output.
 - **Reward (`R(s_t, a_t)`):** Only non-zero at the final timestep (`t=0`), where it equals the overall sample reward `r(x_0, c)`. All intermediate steps have a zero reward.
- **Advantage:** This multi-step formulation allows for the calculation of *exact log-likelihoods* of the actions (denoising steps) and their gradients with respect to the model parameters. This is crucial for applying direct policy gradient methods.
- **Policy Gradient Variants:**
 - **DDPO_SF (Score Function Estimator/REINFORCE):** Computes the gradient by weighting the sum of log-likelihood gradients of all actions in a trajectory by the final reward. This requires on-policy data, so only one gradient update per data collection round.
 - **DDPO_IS (Importance Sampling Estimator):** Employs importance sampling to correct for off-policy data, allowing multiple optimization steps using data collected from an older policy. Similar to Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), it uses a clipping mechanism to maintain a trust region around the old policy, though a very small clip range (1e-4) was found necessary.
- **Training Loop:** Both DDPO and RWR operate in an alternating fashion: collecting denoising trajectories (generating samples) and then updating model parameters based on the collected samples and their rewards.

**Reward Functions Employed for Text-to-Image Diffusion:** The authors designed three distinct types of reward functions to showcase DDPO's versatility:

1. **Compressibility and Incompressibility:**
- **Definition:** Based on the JPEG file size of the generated 512x512 image.
- **Purpose:** Tests objectives that are easy to compute but impossible to specify via text prompts.
2. **Aesthetic Quality:**
- **Definition:** Uses the LAION aesthetics predictor (Schuhmann, 2022), a linear model trained on 176,000 human image ratings.
- **Purpose:** Represents a reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) scenario.
3. **Automated Prompt Alignment with Vision-Language Models (VLMs):**
- **Definition:** Leverages LLaVA (Liu et al., 2023), a state-of-the-art VLM, to generate a textual description of the produced image. The reward is the BERTScore (Zhang et al., 2020) semantic similarity between the original text prompt and LLaVA's description.
- **Purpose:** This innovative approach provides an automated "AI feedback" mechanism, akin to RLAIF in LLMs (Bai et al., 2022b), significantly reducing reliance on human annotation for prompt-image alignment.

**Implementation Details:** Experiments were conducted using Stable Diffusion v1.4 (and v1.5 for comparison with DPOK), fine-tuning only the UNet weights while keeping text encoder and autoencoder frozen. Critical details include using CFG (Classifier-Free Guidance) training, where the guided epsilon-prediction is used during training to maintain guidance weight calibration, which was found essential for multi-round finetuning. Rewards were normalized on a per-context basis.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation provides compelling evidence for the efficacy and superiority of DDPO over alternative methods for fine-tuning diffusion models on arbitrary objectives.

1. **Algorithm Comparisons (DDPO vs. RWR):**
- **Qualitative Results:** DDPO demonstrated a remarkable ability to adapt the pretrained Stable Diffusion model to diverse, non-trivial objectives. For aesthetic quality, the model transitioned from producing naturalistic images to stylized artwork. For compressibility, it learned to simplify backgrounds and apply smoothing to foregrounds to minimize file size. Conversely, for incompressibility, it introduced high-frequency noise and sharp edges—artifacts difficult for JPEG compression. This showed that DDPO learns intelligent, complex strategies to optimize rewards.
- **Quantitative Results:** Across all tested tasks (compressibility, incompressibility, aesthetic quality), DDPO consistently and significantly outperformed both RWR variants (exponentiated and sparse weighting). The policy gradient variants achieved higher reward scores for the same number of reward queries (a proxy for computational cost). Specifically, DDPO_IS (importance sampling) slightly surpassed DDPO_SF (score function) due to its ability to perform multiple optimization steps per data collection round. This outcome strongly supports the paper's central hypothesis: framing denoising as a multi-step MDP and directly applying policy gradients is more effective than approximate likelihood-based methods.

2. **Automated Prompt Alignment with Vision-Language Models (VLMs):**
- **Effectiveness:** The VLM-based reward function, combined with DDPO, proved highly successful in improving prompt-image alignment without requiring human labels. Qualitatively, images generated for challenging prompts like "a dolphin riding a bike" became significantly more faithful to the specified actions and subjects over the course of training.
- **Emergent Style:** An interesting side effect was that images often adopted a cartoon-like or artistic style during fine-tuning for prompt alignment. The authors hypothesize this might be due to the prevalence of such depictions in the pretraining data for unusual compositions or a preference of the LLaVA VLM for recognizing content in simpler, stylized images.
- **Transfer Learning:** Critically, prompts that initially had a "zero success rate" from the pretrained model (e.g., "a dolphin riding a bike" which never produced a bike) were able to improve. This indicates that the learning transferred across prompts, allowing the model to learn generalizable "rules" for composition.

3. **Generalization:**
- The study demonstrated strong generalization capabilities of DDPO-finetuned models. Even when trained on a limited set of animal prompts (e.g., 45 common animals), the aesthetic quality model generalized effectively to unseen animals and even non-animal everyday objects. Similarly, the prompt-image alignment model generalized to novel subjects and activities not present in the training set (e.g., "a capybara washing dishes," "a duck taking an exam"). This generalization property mirrors observations in RLHF for large language models, suggesting a similar underlying mechanism for transferring learned capabilities to out-of-distribution inputs.

4. **Overoptimization (Identified Limitation):**
- The authors candidly acknowledge the challenge of "overoptimization" or "reward exploitation," a common issue in RL fine-tuning where the model achieves high reward but deviates too far from the original data distribution, becoming nonsensical or generating artifacts. Examples include the incompressibility objective leading to high-frequency noise that lacks semantic content, or VLM-based alignment leading to "typographic attacks" (generating text that fools the VLM rather than actual objects). The paper notes that current solutions, like KL-regularization, might be empirically equivalent to early stopping, highlighting this as an important area for future research.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in the control and fine-tuning of generative diffusion models, with broad implications across various applications.

1. **Enhanced Controllability and Alignment:** The most immediate impact is providing a powerful methodology to align diffusion models with specific, often complex, user-defined objectives that are difficult or impossible to achieve through prompt engineering or conventional likelihood-based training. This paradigm shift from "generating plausible data" to "generating data that achieves a goal" unlocks new levels of control over generative AI.

2. **Bridging RL and Generative Models:** The work effectively translates the success of Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and AI Feedback (RLAIF) from the domain of large language models to high-dimensional continuous generative models like diffusion models. This demonstrates the universality of RL principles for aligning complex AI systems with human values or task specifications.

3. **Reducing Annotation Burden:** The innovative use of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to provide automated feedback for prompt-image alignment is a crucial advancement. By enabling "RL from AI feedback," this method drastically reduces the need for expensive and time-consuming human labeling, accelerating the development and customization of aligned generative models. This has significant economic and practical implications for deploying custom AI solutions.

4. **Diverse Application Potential:**
- **Creative Industries:** Artists and designers could fine-tune models to generate images adhering to specific aesthetic criteria, styles, or even technical specifications (e.g., image file size constraints for web design).
- **Scientific Discovery:** In domains like drug and material design (explicitly mentioned in the introduction), where diffusion models are already being explored, DDPO could enable optimization for desired chemical properties, binding affinities, or material strengths, rather than just generating plausible molecular structures.
- **Robotics and Control:** Given the authors' background, DDPO could be applied to refine diffusion-based policies in robotics to optimize for specific performance metrics, safety constraints, or human preferences in robot behavior.
- **Educational Content:** The ability to improve prompt-image alignment could lead to the generation of more accurate and contextually relevant visual aids for educational materials, making complex concepts more understandable.

5. **Generalization for Robust Models:** The observed generalization capabilities, where models fine-tuned on a narrow distribution perform well on unseen prompts and novel compositions, are highly significant. This suggests that DDPO enables the models to learn underlying compositional rules or abstract properties, making the fine-tuning process more efficient and the resulting models more robust and versatile.

6. **Future Research Directions:** The paper openly discusses limitations, particularly the challenge of overoptimization, and highlights it as a critical area for future research. This transparency helps steer the field towards developing more robust alignment techniques, perhaps through refined regularization strategies or more sophisticated reward modeling.

In summary, this paper provides a robust, principled, and empirically validated framework for training diffusion models with reinforcement learning, significantly advancing the field of controllable generative AI. Its methodology paves the way for a new generation of generative models that are not only capable of creating high-quality content but also precisely aligned with diverse, complex, and user-specified objectives.
