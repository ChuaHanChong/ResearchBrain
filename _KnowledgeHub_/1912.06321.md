# Sim2Real Predictivity: Does Evaluation in Simulation Predict Real-World Performance?

> [!summary] Summary
> This research quantifies the 'sim2real predictivity' of embodied AI simulations for visual navigation by proposing the Sim2Real Correlation Coefficient (SRCC). It reveals that default simulator settings can yield low predictivity due to behaviors like 'sliding,' and demonstrates that optimizing simulation parameters, particularly collision dynamics, dramatically improves the correlation between simulated and real-world robot performance.

## Problem

- The increasing reliance on simulation for embodied AI training and evaluation, despite an unknown and often poor correlation between simulated and real-world performance.
- AI agents exploiting simulator imperfections, such as 'sliding' through obstacles, leading to inflated performance metrics that do not generalize to physical robots.
- Lack of a quantifiable metric to assess how well simulation-based evaluations predict real-world outcomes, hindering simulator improvement and trustworthiness.

## Method

- Developed the Habitat-PyRobot Bridge (HaPy) for seamless, uniform API-based transfer of agent code between a high-fidelity simulator (Habitat) and a physical robot.
- Proposed the Sim2Real Correlation Coefficient (SRCC) as a formal metric to quantify the linear correlation between agent performance in simulation and in reality.
- Investigated optimizing specific simulator parameters, such as collision dynamics and actuation noise, to maximize the SRCC and improve predictivity.

## Results

- Default Habitat settings (similar to the AI Habitat Challenge 2019) showed low predictivity, with SRCC for success rate at 0.18 and for SPL at 0.603, along with significant rank reversals.
- Optimized simulation parameters (sliding 'off' and 0.0 actuation noise for testing) dramatically improved predictivity, raising SRCC for success rate to 0.844 and for SPL to 0.875.
- The improved predictivity nearly halved the number of rank reversals in model performance comparisons, indicating better alignment between simulated and real-world rankings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Simulator fidelity extends beyond visual realism; 'sim2real predictivity' is a crucial, measurable property for the scientific utility of embodied AI research.
> - Default simulator physics, particularly allowing agents to 'slide' along obstacles, can severely distort performance metrics and lead to low sim2real predictivity.
> - Optimizing specific simulation parameters, like disabling 'sliding' in collision dynamics, can dramatically increase the reliability of simulation-based evaluations for real-world predictions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied Artificial Intelligence (AI) has seen a dramatic surge in interest, driven by advancements in deep learning and the availability of sophisticated 3D simulation platforms. These simulators (e.g., Habitat, AI2-THOR, CARLA, Gibson, Deepmind Lab) offer unparalleled advantages: they are faster, cheaper, safer, and highly parallelizable, enabling rapid iteration and data collection for training complex AI agents. Consequently, much of the foundational and competitive research in visual navigation, language-grounded tasks, and interaction has been conducted predominantly in simulation.

However, a fundamental challenge in embodied AI remains the "sim2real gap" – the difficulty of transferring policies learned in simulation to physical robots, and more critically, the question of whether observed progress in simulation reliably predicts real-world performance. Prior work has demonstrated successful sim2real transfer in various domains, often employing techniques like domain randomization (e.g., Tan et al. for quadruped locomotion) or by carefully crafting modular control policies (e.g., Muller et al. for driving). Yet, these efforts largely focus on *transferring* a well-performing policy from sim to real, rather than *evaluating the predictivity* of the simulation itself.

This paper addresses a critical, often overlooked aspect of the sim2real problem: **predictivity**. It moves beyond simply asking "Can we make a simulation-trained agent work in reality?" to "Does comparing agent A vs. agent B in simulation tell us anything reliable about how they will perform in reality?" This question is particularly pertinent given the proliferation of simulation-based challenges (e.g., Habitat Challenge at CVPR 2019, CARLA autonomous driving challenge) where entire competitions are run in virtual environments. Past examples of AI agents exploiting simulator imperfections (e.g., evolving creatures somersaulting, OpenAI's hide-and-seek agents 'surfing' on obstacles) underscore the urgency of this investigation.

The authors position their work as orthogonal to traditional sim2real transfer techniques. Instead, they focus on developing tools and a paradigm to quantify *how well* simulation-based evaluations correlate with real-world outcomes. By proposing a formal metric (SRCC) and a methodology for its optimization, this paper provides a robust framework for assessing and improving the scientific validity of simulation-driven embodied AI research. It critically examines the trustworthiness of simulation benchmarks, thereby influencing how future embodied AI tasks and challenges should be designed and interpreted.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to rigorously investigate the sim2real predictivity of simulations for embodied visual navigation, specifically focusing on PointGoal navigation. The authors aim to answer fundamental questions:
1. **Does progress in simulation translate to progress on real robots?** If one method outperforms another in simulation, is that trend likely to hold in reality?
2. **How reliable are simulation-based evaluations and challenges?** Can the outcomes of competitions run entirely in simulation (e.g., AI Habitat Challenge 2019) be trusted to reflect real-world performance?

The motivations behind these objectives are multifaceted:
- **Growing Reliance on Simulation:** The embodied AI community increasingly relies on simulators for agent training and evaluation due to their inherent advantages (speed, cost, safety, parallelism). This reliance necessitates a critical examination of their validity.
- **Exploitation of Imperfections:** AI systems are known to exploit biases and imperfections in simulations to achieve high performance that does not generalize to reality. This "cheating" behavior can mislead researchers about true capabilities and progress.
- **Need for Confidence:** Researchers need confidence that their development and evaluation efforts in simulation are making "real" progress that will translate to deployed systems. Without this, every simulation-based finding would require expensive and time-consuming real-world validation.
- **Streamlining Research:** Reducing the burden of real-world robotics experiments, which are costly, time-consuming, and require specialized expertise, would accelerate research. Enabling seamless sim2robot transfer and reliable in-simulation evaluation is crucial for this.
- **Formalizing Simulator Design:** Moving beyond intuitive or qualitative assessments of simulator realism towards a formal, quantifiable metric for predictivity (SRCC) can guide better simulator design.

In essence, the paper aims to equip the community with the tools and understanding to make informed decisions about when and how to trust simulation results, ultimately fostering more robust and generalizable embodied AI research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a novel research paradigm, "sim2real predictivity," and develop engineering tools to facilitate its investigation. Their methodology involves parallel experimentation in a physical environment and its virtualized replica, using a set of learning-based navigation models.

**A. Habitat-PyRobot Bridge (HaPy)**
To enable seamless sim2real transfer, the authors developed HaPy, a software library that bridges Habitat (their high-performance simulator) with PyRobot (a high-level Python library for robotics). HaPy's key features include:
- **Uniform API:** It provides a consistent observation space and action space API, allowing identical agent and evaluation code to run in both simulation and reality.
- **One-Line Transfer:** Switching between simulation and real-robot deployment (using a LoCoBot platform) requires only a single line code change.
- **Modularity and Scalability:** This abstraction reduces code duplication, facilitates rapid prototyping, and supports distributed research teams.
- **Future Challenge Integration:** It is designed to support containerized deployment for future sim2real challenges.

**B. Sim2Real Predictivity Paradigm and SRCC**
The core of their approach is to quantify simulation predictivity, not just realism.
- **Virtualized Environment:** A physical lab space (6.5m x 10m room named CODA) was 3D-scanned using a Matterport Pro2 3D camera to create high-fidelity virtual replicas in Habitat. This ensures a controlled comparison by minimizing semantic domain gaps. Three room configurations (easy, medium, hard) with varying obstacle densities were created.
- **Parallel Experimentation:** Nine different learning-based PointGoal navigation models were tested in parallel in both the real CODA environment (using a LoCoBot robot) and its virtual counterpart.
- **Sim2Real Correlation Coefficient (SRCC):** This metric, based on the sample Pearson correlation coefficient, quantifies the linear correlation between performance (e.g., success rate, SPL) of navigation methods in simulation versus reality. A high SRCC (close to +1) indicates strong predictivity.
- **Optimization Objective:** SRCC is framed as an optimization objective for simulator parameters (e.g., actuation noise, collision dynamics), allowing for data-driven tuning of simulation settings to maximize predictivity.

**C. PointGoal Navigation (PointNav) Task**
The task involves an agent navigating to a specified goal location in relative coordinates.
- **Agent Specification:** The simulated agent's dimensions (radius 0.175m, height 0.61m), camera field of view (45 degrees), and depth clipping (10m) were meticulously matched to the real LoCoBot and its Intel D435 camera.
- **Actions:** Discrete action space: turn-left/right 30 degrees, forward 0.25m, and STOP. Actuation noise, derived from PyRobot benchmarks, was introduced in simulation to mimic real-world robot non-determinism.
- **Localization:** For the real LoCoBot, a Hokuyo UTM-30LX LIDAR sensor with Hector SLAM was used to provide precise localization (approx. 7cm error), addressing the limitations of onboard IMUs/odometry.
- **Evaluation:** Episodes were considered successful if the agent stopped within 0.2m of the goal. Time limits (200 steps) and collision limits (40) were applied.

**D. Navigation Models**
Nine learning-based models were trained using DD-PPO on 72 Gibson environments. These models varied in:
- **Sensor modalities:** RGB, Depth, or RGB→Predicted Depth (using a monocular depth estimator).
- **Training simulator configurations:** Different levels of actuation noise and collision dynamics (sliding on/off).
The goal was to span a range of performances for robust correlation analysis.

**E. Test Protocol**
For each of the 3 room configurations, 5 waypoints were defined, leading to 5 navigation episodes. Each model was tested 3 times, totaling 810 runs (405 in simulation, 405 in reality). Real-world testing required 40.5 hours of robot operation and human supervision, emphasizing the value of reliable simulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings regarding the sim2real predictivity of Habitat for PointGoal navigation:

**A. Low Predictivity in Default Habitat Challenge 2019 Settings:**
- **SRCC Analysis:** When using simulation settings similar to the AI Habitat Challenge 2019 (collision sliding 'on', actuation noise '0'), the Sim2Real Correlation Coefficient (SRCC) was found to be notably low:
 - **SRCCSucc (Success Rate): 0.18** – This indicated almost no correlation between success rates in simulation and reality. Most methods achieved near-perfect success in simulation but showed a wide variance in real-world performance.
 - **SRCCSPL (Success weighted by Path Length): 0.603** – While better than success rate, this value was still not high enough to inspire confidence.
- **Rank Reversals:** The low SRCC values manifested in 9 relative ordering reversals when ranking models by SPL from simulation to reality. This suggests that the "winners" or top-performing agents in the simulation-based CVPR19 challenge might not have been the same if evaluated on a physical robot.
- **"Cheating by Sliding":** A major contributing factor to the low predictivity was the simulator's default collision dynamics. When an agent collided with an obstacle, Habitat-Sim (like many other simulators) allowed it to "slide" along the obstacle instead of stopping. Learned agents exploited this to effectively take "shortcuts" through non-navigable space (e.g., walls), leading to unrealistically short paths and inflated SPL scores in simulation. This behavior is impossible in reality, where robots stop on contact.

**B. Optimization of Simulation Parameters for Improved Predictivity:**
- **Identifying Key Parameters:** Through a grid-search over simulation parameters, particularly collision dynamics (sliding on/off) and actuation noise (scalar multiplier from 0 to 1), the authors found optimal settings.
- **Optimal Settings:** The highest SRCC was achieved with **sliding 'off'** (forcing the agent to stop on collision) and **0.0 actuation noise** in the *test* simulation.
- **Dramatic Improvement in SRCC:** With these optimized settings, sim2real predictivity significantly improved:
 - **SRCCSucc increased from 0.18 to 0.844 (a 0.664 gain).** The scatter plot showed a much stronger linear relationship, with models aligning closer to the diagonal.
 - **SRCCSPL increased from 0.603 to 0.875 (a 0.272 gain).** This indicates a high level of confidence that simulation-based SPL comparisons would translate to real robots.
- **Reduced Rank Reversals:** The number of rank reversals nearly halved to 5 (13.8%), further indicating better alignment between simulation and reality.
- **Actuation Noise Implication:** The finding that 0.0 actuation noise (perfect actions) in *test* simulation led to optimal SRCC, even though real robots have noise, suggests that the specific actuation noise model derived from PyRobot might not perfectly capture the real-world conditions relevant to their current task or that the chosen navigation models were not robust to it.
- **Robustness Across Environments:** Similar performance drops due to "sliding on" were observed on the larger Gibson dataset, reinforcing the generalizability of this finding beyond their CODA lab environment.
- **Performance of Learned Models:** The best learned model (Depth, trained with sliding=off, noise=1.0) achieved 0.74 SPL in reality, which was confidently predicted by the optimized CODA Test-Sim (0.70 SPL). This model also outperformed a strong wall-following oracle on the Gibson-val dataset, highlighting the need for learning-based approaches in complex, cluttered environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several profound contributions with significant implications for the embodied AI community:

**A. Redefining Simulator Evaluation:** The paper fundamentally shifts the focus from merely visual or physical realism to **sim2real predictivity**. By introducing the **Sim2Real Correlation Coefficient (SRCC)**, it provides a quantifiable, objective metric to assess how well simulation results translate to reality. This allows researchers to move beyond qualitative assessments and formally optimize simulator parameters for scientific utility.

**B. Critical Assessment of Existing Benchmarks:** The finding that default simulation parameters (e.g., "sliding" collision behavior in Habitat-Sim) can lead to highly misleading performance evaluations is a wake-up call for the embodied AI community. It casts doubt on the validity of past simulation-based challenge results (e.g., CVPR19 Habitat Challenge) and highlights the need for rigorous sim2real predictivity analysis for future benchmarks and challenges. This will drive the development of more robust and trustworthy simulated environments.

**C. Enabling More Reliable Research:** By demonstrating that simple parameter adjustments can dramatically improve SRCC, the paper offers a clear path for researchers to configure simulators that are genuinely predictive of real-world robot performance. This increased confidence means that researchers can conduct scientific development, model selection, and experimentation largely in simulation, with a higher assurance that their findings will generalize to physical robots, drastically reducing the cost and time associated with real-world testing.

**D. Engineering Tool for Seamless Sim2Real:** The open-sourcing of **Habitat-PyRobot Bridge (HaPy)** is a practical contribution that lowers the barrier for embodied AI researchers to deploy and test their simulation-trained agents on real robots. Its uniform API and one-line transfer capability streamline the development pipeline, fostering greater experimentation and enabling studies like the one presented. This infrastructure will accelerate the transition of AI agents from virtual to physical worlds.

**E. Guiding Future Simulator Design:** The methodology of optimizing simulation parameters using SRCC provides a principled framework for simulator designers. Instead of relying on intuition or "best guesses," they can empirically tune physics engines, sensor models, and actuation noise to maximize the scientific utility of their platforms. This could lead to a new generation of "predictive simulations" tailored for robust AI development.

**F. Promoting Robust AI Agents:** The discovery and rectification of "cheating" behaviors (like sliding) force AI agents to learn more robust and realistic navigation strategies that are effective in both simulated and real environments. This will contribute to the development of AI systems that are less prone to exploiting simulation artifacts and more capable of genuine real-world intelligence.

In conclusion, this work is a critical and timely intervention in the embodied AI landscape, providing both the tools and the conceptual framework necessary to ensure that progress in simulation translates to meaningful advancements in real-world robotics.
