# Sim2Real Predictivity: Does Evaluation in Simulation Predict Real-World Performance?

> [!summary] Summary
> This research quantifies the 'sim2real predictivity' of embodied AI simulations for visual navigation by proposing the Sim2Real Correlation Coefficient (SRCC). It reveals that default simulator settings can yield low predictivity due to behaviors like 'sliding,' and demonstrates that optimizing simulation parameters, particularly collision dynamics, dramatically improves the correlation between simulated and real-world robot performance.

## Problem

- The increasing reliance on simulation for embodied AI training and evaluation, despite an unknown and often poor correlation between simulated and real-world performance.
- AI agents exploiting simulator imperfections, such as 'sliding' through obstacles, leading to inflated performance metrics that do not generalize to physical robots.
- Lack of a quantifiable metric to assess how well simulation-based evaluations predict real-world outcomes, hindering simulator improvement and trustworthiness.

## Method

- Developed the Habitat-PyRobot Bridge (HaPy) for seamless, uniform API-based transfer of agent code between a high-fidelity simulator (Habitat) and a physical robot.
- Proposed the Sim2Real Correlation Coefficient (SRCC) as a formal metric to quantify the linear correlation between agent performance in simulation and in reality.
- Investigated optimizing specific simulator parameters, such as collision dynamics and actuation noise, to maximize the SRCC and improve predictivity.

## Results

- Default Habitat settings (similar to the AI Habitat Challenge 2019) showed low predictivity, with SRCC for success rate at 0.18 and for SPL at 0.603, along with significant rank reversals.
- Optimized simulation parameters (sliding 'off' and 0.0 actuation noise for testing) dramatically improved predictivity, raising SRCC for success rate to 0.844 and for SPL to 0.875.
- The improved predictivity nearly halved the number of rank reversals in model performance comparisons, indicating better alignment between simulated and real-world rankings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Simulator fidelity extends beyond visual realism; 'sim2real predictivity' is a crucial, measurable property for the scientific utility of embodied AI research.
> - Default simulator physics, particularly allowing agents to 'slide' along obstacles, can severely distort performance metrics and lead to low sim2real predictivity.
> - Optimizing specific simulation parameters, like disabling 'sliding' in collision dynamics, can dramatically increase the reliability of simulation-based evaluations for real-world predictions.
