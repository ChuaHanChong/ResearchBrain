# VIScore: Diagnosing Planning-Relevant Quality in Latent World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> VIScore is a diagnostic metric for latent world models that multiplies three bounded terms: veracity (open-loop rollout error against the task's success tolerance), influence (action empowerment capped at a sufficiency threshold), and sobriety (fraction of expert anchors where planner search beats the expert action). It explains planning success with pooled Spearman correlation of 0.75-0.91 across held-out checkpoints, training methods, and datasets.

## Problem

- Existing latent world-model diagnostics such as straightness and physical-state probing score only the encoder, leaving the predictor and planner that actually execute planning unmeasured.
- Empowerment captures action-conditioned capacity, but its raw nat scale means different planning quality on different tasks, so its pooled cross-task correlation collapses.
- Self-supervised regularization gains are assumed to transfer to planning, while single-seed success evaluation and in-domain short-horizon benchmarks obscure why planning actually succeeds or fails.

## Method

- Controlled ==SIGReg==-to-==VISReg== swap inside LeWorldModel gives **VIS-WM**: VISReg factorizes the isotropic-Gaussian prior into separately weighted center, scale, and shape terms over a ==ViT-Tiny== encoder and autoregressive action-conditioned Transformer predictor.
- **VIScore** multiplies ==veracity== (erf of tolerance-to-rollout-error ratio), ==influence== (latent ==empowerment== clipped at a shared threshold tau=82 nats), and ==sobriety== (one minus the planner-beats-expert rate).
- Validation protocol uses run-level disjoint checkpoint pools, ==cluster bootstrap resampling== over training runs, ==leave-one-task-out isotonic calibration== against a constant-median reference, and five sampling-based planners.

## Results

- On held-out checkpoints from **33** runs disjoint from development, VIScore reaches **+0.91** pooled Spearman and **7.0** calibration error versus empowerment's **+0.49** and **15.6**, reference **18.2**.
- Transfer holds: **+0.75** pooled on **four** held-out world-modeling methods (**8.3** vs **11.3** reference) and **+0.87** on the unseen MAZE task, where uncapped empowerment calibrates better at **9.2**.
- VIS-WM matches LeWM under **three** fresh evaluation seeds (**92**/**82**/**92** on PushT/Reacher/Two-Room), while VISReg at batch **512** lifts OOD PushObj success to **82.0** against SIGReg's **79.1**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-supervised advantages do not automatically transfer to planning: loss-component reweighting that helps long-tailed SSL collapsed Reacher success to 12, whereas closer distribution matching improved OOD.
> - Action-conditioned capacity is a requirement to satisfy rather than maximize; saturating it into a sufficiency condition is what makes one numerical scale comparable across tasks.
> - Single-seed planning evaluation is overconfident, so report multiple fresh seeds, and treat any latent metric as a diagnostic rather than a success predictor.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research operates within the field of latent world models, particularly those leveraging Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) for data-efficient and lightweight training. Latent world models aim to learn compact representations of the environment and predict future states based on actions, enabling planning. Current world model evaluation often focuses on prediction losses or representation-level summaries (e.g., encoded information, trajectory geometry, action-conditioned capacity). However, there remains a notable gap in understanding how specific latent space properties directly correlate with the success or failure of planning algorithms that operate on these latent representations. Previous work, such as LeWorldModel (LeWM), employs regularization techniques like SIGReg to maintain stable latent spaces. This paper builds upon such foundations by investigating the direct impact of regularization properties on planning performance and by developing a more comprehensive diagnostic tool that assesses the entire planning pipeline, from encoder to predictor and planner. Existing diagnostic metrics (e.g., straightness, physical-state probing, empowerment) are noted for their limitations in analyzing the full system, motivating the development of a more integrated approach.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to establish a clearer connection between the quality of latent spaces in world models and the empirical success rate of planning tasks. This overarching goal is pursued through two main lines of inquiry:

**Motivation from Regularization Intervention:**
The first motivation stems from a controlled study comparing two regularization loss functions, SIGReg (used in LeWM) and VISReg. Both aim to regularize the latent space towards an isotropic Gaussian distribution, which is theoretically considered stable and information-maximized for planning. However, VISReg offers greater flexibility, allowing independent weighting of center, scale, and shape constraints, and exhibits different behavior concerning batch size approximation. The researchers were motivated to investigate whether the self-supervised learning (SSL) advantages reported for VISReg (e.g., stronger corrective gradients near collapse, better performance on long-tailed or low-rank data) translate into improved planning performance. Specifically, they sought to understand which properties of regularization loss functions are truly consequential for planning success, particularly in both in-domain and out-of-domain (OOD) scenarios. The observed disconnect between latent space properties and planning success, as noted in previous work on LeWM, underscored the necessity of this direct investigation.

**Motivation for a Comprehensive Diagnostic Metric:**
The second, and central, motivation is the development of a diagnostic metric that can effectively explain why planning succeeds or fails. Existing metrics for evaluating latent world models (such as straightness, physical-state probing, and empowerment) tend to focus on isolated components—primarily the encoded latent features. These metrics do not jointly assess the entire system comprising the vision encoder, action encoder, predictor, and the planner itself, nor do they account for task-specific success tolerances. The absence of a unified metric that covers these critical components limits the ability of researchers to diagnose specific failure modes (e.g., unreachable imagined dynamics, insufficient action-conditioned capacity, or exploitable predictor hallucinations). The paper aims to fill this void by proposing a metric that integrates these aspects, thereby providing a more robust and interpretable tool for analyzing world model performance and guiding their design. The ultimate goal is to offer insights that can assist in future world model development and diagnosis by systematically identifying strengths and weaknesses across the planning pipeline.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a two-pronged approach: first, a controlled study on regularization effects, and second, the development and rigorous evaluation of a novel diagnostic metric called VIScore.

**Part 1: Regularization Study (SIGReg vs. VISReg)**

- **VISReg Definition:** VISReg (Variance-Invariance-Sketching Regularization) is introduced as an alternative to SIGReg. Both target an isotropic Gaussian prior, but VISReg factorizes the constraint into three independently weighted loss components: $L_{center}$, $L_{scale}$, and $L_{shape}$. These terms regulate the mean, variance, and shape (distribution quantiles) of the latent embeddings, respectively. The overall regularization loss is $L_{reg} = \lambda_{center}L_{center} + \lambda_{scale}L_{scale} + \lambda_{shape}L_{shape}$, added to the prediction loss $L_{pred}$ to form the total training objective $L_{VISReg} = L_{pred} + \lambda L_{reg}$.
- **Experimental Settings:**
 - **Architecture:** The LeWorldModel (LeWM) architecture is adopted, featuring a ViT-Tiny encoder and an autoregressive, action-conditioned Transformer predictor, totaling approximately 15 million trainable parameters.
 - **Training:** Models were trained for 10 epochs using AdamW optimizer, with a linear warmup followed by cosine decay. A batch size of 128 (default) and a learning rate of $10^{-4}$ were used. The VISReg weight $\lambda$ was set to 4.5. Three training seeds were used for each configuration to account for variability.
 - **Datasets:** Four offline pixel-control benchmarks were utilized: PushT, Reacher, OGBench-Cube, and Two-Room. Observations were rendered at 224x224 pixels.
 - **Planning Evaluation:** Model Predictive Control (MPC) was employed with the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) to optimize action sequences. Goals were set `d` environment steps ahead on an expert trajectory. A default horizon of $d=25$ was used, with longer horizons of $d \in \{50, 75\}$ also tested. The interaction budget was set to $T=2d$. Both single evaluation seed results and an average over three fresh evaluation seeds were reported to assess statistical significance.
- **Specific Experiments:**
 - **Short- and Long-Horizon Planning:** Direct comparison of VIS-WM and LeWM performance on standard planning tasks.
 - **OOD Generalization:** Creation of PushObj datasets with six unseen object shapes (L, Z, plus, I, small t, square). Models were trained only on the original 'T' shape and evaluated on the variants to test out-of-domain planning. Batch size ablation studies were conducted for VISReg.
 - **Loss-Component Flexibility:** An ablation study on the Reacher dataset was performed by reweighting the $L_{center}$, $L_{scale}$, and $L_{shape}$ components of VISReg to investigate if the flexibility beneficial in SSL also aids planning.

**Part 2: VIScore Development and Evaluation**

- **VIScore Definition:** VIScore is proposed as a product of three bounded diagnostic factors: Veracity, Influence, and Sobriety, such that `VIScore = Veracity * Influence * Sobriety`.
 - **Veracity:** Measures the accuracy of an H-step open-loop rollout relative to the task's success tolerance ($d_{tol}$). It is calculated as `erf(d_tol / (2 * sqrt(2) * sigma_roll))`, where $\sigma_{roll}$ is the RMS terminal error of the rollout. This makes the measure insensitive to arbitrary latent scale and accounts for task-specific precision requirements.
 - **Influence:** Quantifies the action-conditioned capacity, specifically how many H-step futures actions can steer to, remaining distinguishable above the predictor's noise floor. It is a reformulated latent empowerment, calculated as `min(m_emp / tau, 1)`, where $m_{emp}$ is the mutual information $I(\delta; z_{t+H})$ derived from a local Gaussian channel model around expert actions. A shared sufficiency threshold $\tau = 82$ nats (chosen via run-level two-fold cross-validation on the development pool) is used to cap empowerment, aligning the scale across tasks.
 - **Sobriety:** Assesses whether the planner exploits predictor errors by finding "better" imagined paths than the expert's actual trajectory. It is defined as `(1 - p_hat)`, where $p_{hat}$ is the fraction of expert anchors on which a small search (mini-CEM) within the learned predictor discovers a lower cost than the expert's executed action.
- **Experimental Settings:**
 - **Checkpoint Pools:** Evaluation was performed on several pools:
 - **Development Pool:** Spanning training progress from the runs in Section 3 (n=137 checkpoints from 14 runs). Used for choosing constants like $\tau$ and calibration maps.
 - **Held-out Checkpoints Pool:** Draws from non-development runs (n=103 checkpoints from 33 runs), labelled with three fresh evaluation seed sets.
 - **Held-out World-Modeling Methods Pool:** Checkpoints from four different world-modeling methods (Qantara, RC-aux, INTACT, DINO-WM variant, n=23 from 23 runs), re-evaluated with the paper's planner and seeds.
 - **Held-out Dataset Pool:** Checkpoints from an unseen MAZE task (n=20 from 2 runs).
 - All splits were done at the run level to ensure no constant was tuned on a sibling epoch of a test checkpoint.
 - **Baselines:** VIScore was compared against existing diagnostics: straightness, physical-state probe, and raw uncapped empowerment.
 - **Validation Criteria:** Spearman correlation was used to assess rank consistency (within-task and pooled across tasks). Calibration error (using isotonic regression from metric value to success rate) was employed to measure absolute predictability, compared against a constant predictor reference. The OGBench-Cube task was excluded from pooled and calibration errors due to its success being largely determined by a discrete gripping outcome not captured by the continuous VIScore factors.
 - **Transfer Settings:** The applicability of VIScore was further tested across different searching-based planners: CEM, MPPI, iCEM, predictive sampling, multi-start AdamW, and single-start AdamW, using a fixed 36-checkpoint subset.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

**Regularization Study (SIGReg vs. VISReg):**

- **Competitive Planning Performance:** VIS-WorldModel (VIS-WM) demonstrated competitive short-horizon planning success rates, achieving 98% on PushT, 77% on OGBench-Cube, and 97% on Two-Room under single-seed evaluation. However, multi-seed evaluation revealed similar success rates to LeWM (e.g., 92% for PushT and 92% for Two-Room for VIS-WM vs. 91% for PushT and 90% for Two-Room for LeWM), suggesting that single-seed evaluation can lead to overconfidence regarding improvements. For long-horizon planning, VIS-WM also achieved competitive results, outperforming several dedicated hierarchical designs in single-seed evaluation and showing similar performance to LeWM under multi-seed evaluation.
- **OOD Generalization and Distribution Matching:** In the OOD PushObj task, VISReg with larger batch sizes (e.g., B=512) achieved higher success rates (82.0% for d=25) compared to SIGReg at B=128 (79.1%), and VISReg at B=128 (77.4%). This indicates that a more accurate approximation of the target isotropic Gaussian distribution, enabled by larger batch sizes for VISReg, positively impacts OOD planning performance, supporting the theoretical importance of an ideal latent distribution for unknown tasks.
- **Loss-Component Flexibility:** Reweighting the center, scale, and shape components of VISReg, which is beneficial for self-supervised learning on low-quality datasets, did not translate into improved planning performance on the Reacher dataset. This suggests that advantages from SSL do not automatically transfer to latent planning contexts.

**VIScore Evaluation:**

- **Strong and Consistent Association with Planning Success:**
 - VIScore consistently exhibited high Spearman correlation with planning success rates across various evaluation pools. On the development pool, it achieved a pooled correlation of +0.88 with a calibration error of 10.2 points. On held-out checkpoints, these improved to +0.91 pooled correlation and 7.0 calibration error.
 - Compared to baselines, raw empowerment showed competitive within-task correlations (e.g., +0.82 on PushT, +0.86 on Reacher in development pool) but its pooled correlation collapsed to +0.40 (development pool) and +0.49 (held-out checkpoints), with higher calibration errors (23.1 and 15.6, respectively), indicating a lack of cross-task comparability. Straightness and physical-state probe showed lower and inconsistent correlations.
- **Superior Calibration:** VIScore was the only metric whose calibration error remained below the constant predictor reference across all testing scenarios (development, held-out checkpoints, held-out methods, held-out dataset). This indicates its ability to provide more accurate absolute predictions of success likelihood across different models and tasks.
- **Generalization to Unseen Methods and Tasks:**
 - On a pool of held-out world-modeling methods, VIScore maintained the strongest association (+0.75 pooled correlation) and lowest calibration error (8.3 points) among all diagnostics, outperforming the constant predictor (11.3 points).
 - On the unseen MAZE task, VIScore again ranked best with a +0.87 pooled correlation and a calibration error of 11.5 points, significantly better than the constant reference (41.7 points). Raw empowerment was also well-calibrated on MAZE (9.2 points) because the task operated below its influence cap.
- **Complementary Factors:** The three components of VIScore (veracity, influence, sobriety) were found to be complementary. No single factor consistently dominated across all pools, as their individual strengths varied depending on the specific failure modes prevalent in each scenario. The product (VIScore) provided a robust summary that maintained strong performance universally.
- **Applicability Across Search-Based Planners:** VIScore demonstrated positive within-task associations (median +0.67) across a range of searching-based planners (CEM, MPPI, iCEM, predictive sampling, multi-start AdamW, single-start AdamW). This suggests its utility is not limited to a specific planning algorithm. While cross-task comparability sometimes weakened for planners with task-selective effectiveness (e.g., predictive sampling, single-start gradient descent), the diagnostic generally held up.
- **Limitations:** The paper notes that VIScore is a diagnostic, not a deterministic predictor of success, and does not account for discrete environmental outcomes (e.g., gripping success in OGBench-Cube). It is also not designed for amortized planners, as its components measure properties specific to predictor-based world models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of latent world models by addressing critical challenges in diagnosing planning performance and guiding model development.

- **Enhanced Diagnostic Capabilities:** The introduction of VIScore provides a comprehensive and interpretable diagnostic tool that measures planning-relevant quality across the entire world model pipeline—encoder, predictor, and planner. By quantifying veracity, influence, and sobriety, VIScore offers granular insights into specific failure modes, which was previously lacking in existing metrics that focused on isolated components. This capability can enable researchers to identify bottlenecks and weaknesses more precisely in world model designs.
- **Improved Model Design Guidance:** The findings from the regularization study highlight that while certain regularization properties (like better distribution matching with larger batch sizes for VISReg) can improve out-of-domain planning, advantages observed in self-supervised learning (e.g., loss component flexibility) do not automatically transfer to planning contexts. This provides concrete guidance for future world model architects, indicating that regularization strategies need to be specifically tailored or validated for planning objectives rather than solely relying on SSL benefits.
- **More Robust Evaluation Protocols:** The paper empirically demonstrates the significant bias introduced by single-seed evaluations and advocates for multi-seed evaluations to ensure statistical significance of reported improvements. Furthermore, it reinforces the necessity of including out-of-domain (OOD) and long-horizon tasks in benchmarking protocols, as current benchmarks often originate from the same environment as training and can lead to saturation. Adopting these recommendations could lead to more reliable and generalizable world model comparisons.
- **Foundational for Future Research:** By systematically validating VIScore against planning success across diverse models, tasks, and planners, the research establishes a robust foundation for future studies. It also explicitly identifies several open problems, such as the need for diagnostic toolkits for IDM/amortized planning, approaches for multi-task planning, more complex training datasets, and challenges in training without action labels. Addressing these gaps, potentially informed by VIScore's diagnostic capabilities, could drive significant advancements in the field.
- **Accelerated Development:** By providing a reliable and efficient diagnostic (computationally much cheaper than full planning evaluations), VIScore can potentially accelerate the iterative design and development cycle of world models, allowing researchers to quickly assess changes and pinpoint areas for improvement without extensive and costly planning evaluations.
