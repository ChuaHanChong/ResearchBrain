# PixWorld: Unifying 3D Scene Generation and Reconstruction in Pixel Space

> [!summary] Summary
> PixWorld introduces a unified pixel-space diffusion framework that directly optimizes a 3D Gaussian Splatting representation for both 3D scene generation and reconstruction. This method leverages a two-stream Diffusion Transformer and a geometry perception loss, achieving state-of-the-art performance in novel-view synthesis and scene generation quality with improved geometric faithfulness across multiple benchmarks.

## Problem

- 3D scene reconstruction and generation are traditionally addressed by separate models, limiting versatility and leading to distinct paradigms.
- Prior unified approaches often rely on latent-space diffusion, which introduces information loss from autoencoder encoding and results in indirect optimization of the 3D representation.
- Standard 2D photometric and perceptual losses used in 3D scene modeling are insufficient for guaranteeing geometrically faithful 3D structures, often leading to ambiguities like depth inaccuracies and floaters.

## Method

- Developed **PixWorld**, a unified ==pixel-space diffusion== framework that directly supervises the fidelity of a ==3D Gaussian Splatting== representation through multi-view rendered images.
- Introduced a ==two-stream Diffusion Transformer== that processes both clean (reconstruction) and noisy (generation) multi-view inputs to predict a pixel-aligned 3D Gaussian scene representation.
- Incorporated a novel ==geometry perception loss==, utilizing a frozen, pretrained 3D foundation model as a structural critic, to provide explicit 3D structural guidance during training.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance in both 3D scene reconstruction (**26.21 dB** PSNR on 4-view RealEstate10K) and single/two-image 3D scene generation (**18.88 dB** PSNR on RealEstate10K single-image), with improved camera control (AUC@5) over all baselines.
- Ranked first on the WorldScore benchmark with an overall average score of **71.04**, leading in Camera Control (**91.08**), 3D Consistency (**91.39**), and Photometric Consistency (**93.84**).
- Ablation studies confirmed the geometry perception loss is critical, with its removal causing a **1.13 dB** drop in PSNR and a **0.080** drop in AUC@5, underscoring its role in cross-view consistency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Operating diffusion directly in pixel space, rather than compressed latent space, for 3D scenes eliminates information loss from autoencoders and enables more direct optimization of the underlying 3D representation.
> - A two-stream Diffusion Transformer architecture can effectively unify 3D scene reconstruction and generation by adaptively handling both clean and noisy input views.
> - Explicit 3D geometric supervision via a geometry perception loss is crucial for establishing and maintaining cross-view consistency and pose fidelity, outperforming traditional 2D losses alone.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "PixWorld" addresses the interconnected yet traditionally distinct fields of 3D scene reconstruction and 3D scene generation within computer vision.

**3D Scene Reconstruction:** This area focuses on recovering a 3D representation of a scene from observed data, typically one or more images. Historically, methods relied on multi-view stereo and structure-from-motion pipelines. More recently, neural radiance fields (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) have advanced per-scene optimization for high-fidelity novel view synthesis. The current state-of-the-art in reconstruction includes feed-forward methods that directly map sparse or dense multi-view inputs to a 3D representation, avoiding expensive per-scene fitting. These methods are deterministic, producing a 3D model faithful to the input observations but typically lacking the ability to synthesize unobserved content.

**3D Scene Generation:** This field aims to synthesize plausible 3D scenes from limited or imagined conditions, such as text prompts or a few input images. Initial approaches leveraged pretrained 2D generative models and score distillation techniques to optimize 3D representations like NeRF or 3DGS. However, these often suffered from cross-view inconsistencies and required per-scene optimization, limiting scalability. A subsequent direction involved synthesizing multi-view images or videos with 2D diffusion models, followed by 3D reconstruction. More recent advancements have shifted towards operating in compressed latent spaces. These "latent diffusion" models typically use pretrained image or video autoencoders (VAEs or RAEs) to encode images into latents, perform diffusion in this latent space, and then decode the generated latents into 3D representations. This approach reduces computational costs but introduces an information bottleneck and an indirect objective.

**Unification Attempts:** The development of 3D reconstruction and generation as separate research lines has led to models specialized for one task. Recent efforts have attempted to unify these tasks within a single framework. Notably, some latent-space generation pipelines have been extended to also handle reconstruction. However, these unified latent-space approaches face limitations, including defining the diffusion objective on intermediate latent features rather than directly on the 3D representation, potential information loss due to latent encoding, and the requirement for pretraining a VAE or RAE.

**PixWorld's Contribution to Unification:** PixWorld positions itself by directly addressing the limitations of latent-space unification. It proposes a pixel-space diffusion paradigm that eliminates the intermediate latent stage, thereby avoiding information loss and the need for pretrained autoencoders. By supervising diffusion directly on rendered images, PixWorld aims to align the optimization signal directly with the fidelity of the 3D scene, creating a unified model that performs both 3D reconstruction and generation more effectively and directly in the pixel domain. This work extends the recent trend of pixel-space diffusion in 2D image synthesis to the 3D scene generation and reconstruction domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of the PixWorld research are to:

1. **Unify 3D scene generation and reconstruction within a single model:** The authors aim to overcome the traditional separation of these two tasks, which are often tackled by distinct paradigms (pixel-based regression for reconstruction, latent diffusion for generation).
2. **Reformulate unification under a pixel-space diffusion paradigm:** This involves moving away from latent-space diffusion, which has become common in generative models, and instead applying diffusion directly in the pixel space of multi-view renderings.
3. **Optimize the underlying 3D representation directly and end-to-end:** The goal is to ensure that the training objective directly supervises the fidelity of the 3D scene representation, rather than indirectly through intermediate latent features.
4. **Introduce a geometry perception loss for structural supervision:** Beyond conventional 2D photometric and perceptual losses, the authors seek to incorporate a mechanism that provides explicit 3D structural guidance during training, ensuring geometric faithfulness.

These objectives are driven by several motivations, primarily stemming from the perceived drawbacks of prior latent-space approaches to 3D scene generation and unification:

- **Information Loss from Latent Encoding:** Previous methods that unify 3D generation and reconstruction often rely on a pretrained Variational Autoencoder (VAE) or Representation Autoencoder (RAE). Encoding images into a compressed latent space inevitably introduces some degree of information loss, which can compromise the fidelity of the generated or reconstructed 3D scenes, especially for reconstruction tasks where high fidelity is critical.
- **Indirect Optimization of 3D Representation:** In latent diffusion, the diffusion objective is typically defined on the intermediate latent features. This means the 3D output is not directly optimized by the diffusion signal, leading to a decoupling between the generative process and the final 3D scene fidelity. This indirect supervision can make it challenging to achieve optimal 3D structural consistency and visual quality.
- **Computational Cost and Complexity of Pretraining Autoencoders:** VAEs or RAEs require significant additional pretraining, adding to the overall computational cost and complexity of the system. Eliminating this intermediate component can streamline the training process.
- **Lack of Explicit 3D Geometric Awareness in 2D Losses:** Standard photometric (e.g., MSE) and perceptual (e.g., LPIPS) losses operate at the 2D image level. While they enforce visual consistency, they may not adequately constrain the underlying 3D geometry. This can lead to ambiguities such as depth inaccuracies, "floaters," or reliance on view-dependent textures, especially when dealing with noisy or extrapolated views. The motivation is to counteract these ambiguities with a more direct form of 3D structural supervision.

By addressing these issues, PixWorld aims to develop a more efficient, direct, and geometrically robust framework for unified 3D scene modeling.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PixWorld employs a unified pixel-space diffusion framework to jointly perform 3D scene reconstruction and generation. The methodology centers on a two-stream Diffusion Transformer (DiT) that operates directly on rendered multi-view images and incorporates a novel geometry perception loss.

**Unified Pixel-Space Diffusion Paradigm:**
Unlike latent diffusion models that operate on compressed latent representations, PixWorld adopts a pixel-space diffusion approach. In this paradigm, the diffusion process and its objective are defined directly on RGB image pixels. For a clean image $x$, a noisy version $x_t$ is created by linear interpolation with Gaussian noise $\epsilon$. A denoiser $f_\theta$ predicts the clean image $\hat{x}$ from $x_t$, $t$, and conditional information $c$. The training objective is a flow-matching (FM) loss that measures the difference between the predicted velocity field $\hat{v}$ (derived from $\hat{x}$) and the ground-truth velocity $v$ (derived from $x$). This direct pixel-space supervision ensures that the diffusion objective is aligned with the fidelity of the 3D scene as rendered, bypassing the information loss and indirect optimization associated with latent spaces.

**Task Formulation:**
PixWorld unifies reconstruction and generation by strategically partitioning a set of $N$ posed multi-view input images $I = \{I_n\}_{n=1}^N$ with corresponding camera parameters $T = \{T_n\}_{n=1}^N$. The views are divided into a "clean" subset $\Omega_c$ and a "noisy" subset $\Omega_n$.
- If $\Omega_n$ is empty, the task is pure multi-view 3D reconstruction from clean observations.
- If $\Omega_n$ contains views, PixWorld performs generation by predicting these noisy views conditioned on the clean ones.
For each input view $n$, the model receives $\tilde{I}_n^t$, which is the original image $I_n$ for clean views ($n \in \Omega_c$) or a noisy version $t I_n + (1-t)\epsilon_n$ for noisy views ($n \in \Omega_n$), where $t$ is the shared timestep for noisy views. An optional text prompt $y$ can also condition the generation.

**Two-stream Diffusion Transformer:**
The core of PixWorld is a two-stream Diffusion Transformer, denoted $f_\theta$. This transformer takes the mixed input $\tilde{I}_t$, camera parameters $T$, and optional text prompt $y$. It processes these inputs to predict multi-view depth maps $\hat{D} = \{\hat{D}_n\}_{n=1}^N$ and a pixel-aligned 3D Gaussian scene representation $\hat{G}$.
- **Architecture:** It is a 24-layer DiT with a hidden width of 1024, 16 attention heads, SwiGLU feed-forward layers, and RMSNorm.
- **Two Streams:** Clean and noisy views are embedded by separate input projections and then processed by distinct but shared transformer blocks. The noisy stream is conditioned on the sampled timestep $t$, while the clean stream receives a time embedding corresponding to $t=1$ (fully denoised).
- **Attention Mechanism:** The per-stream Query (Q), Key (K), and Value (V) tensors are concatenated along the token axis, allowing a single full attention computation over all clean and noisy tokens. This enables cross-stream interaction in every layer.
- **Conditioning:** Camera parameters are injected via PRoPE (Positional Encoding for Relative Poses). Text conditions are fused through cross-attention, which is shared across both streams.

**3D Gaussian Decoding and Rendering:**
From the shared features produced by the DiT, the network predicts:
1. **Depth Maps:** A depth map $\hat{D}_n$ for each view.
2. **Gaussian Attributes:** Parameters (e.g., color, opacity, scale, rotation) for the pixel-aligned 3D Gaussian representation $\hat{G}$.
Instead of directly regressing 3D Gaussian centers, each pixel is unprojected into world space using its predicted depth $\hat{d}_n^p$ and the camera parameters $T_n$, yielding the Gaussian center $\mu_n^p$. The aggregated scene-level representation $\hat{G}$ is then rendered back to images $\bar{I} = \{\bar{I}_n\}_{n=1}^N$ using a differentiable renderer $R$.

**Loss Functions:**
The model is trained with a composite objective: $L = L_{render} + \lambda_{depth}L_{depth} + \lambda_{geo}L_{geo}$.

1. **FM Rendering and Depth Objectives ($L_{render}$, $L_{depth}$):**
 - **$L_{render}$:** This loss combines reconstruction for clean views and flow-matching for noisy views.
 - For clean views ($n \in \Omega_c$), a direct Mean Squared Error (MSE) term $\parallel \bar{I}_n - I_n \parallel^2_2$ supervises the rendered image $\bar{I}_n$ against the ground-truth observation $I_n$. These views act as noise-free anchors.
 - For noisy views ($n \in \Omega_n$), the flow-matching term $\parallel \bar{v}_n - v_n \parallel^2_2$ is applied, where $\bar{v}_n$ is the rendered velocity and $v_n$ is the ground-truth velocity. This is equivalent to $\frac{\parallel \bar{I}_n - I_n \parallel^2_2}{(1-t)^2}$ in image space.
 - A perceptual loss $L_{lpips}$ (LPIPS) is added to improve visual quality, activated only when $t$ is above a threshold ($t > t_{th}=0.3$), as it is unreliable for very noisy inputs.
 - Additionally, 4-8 novel views are sampled per iteration, and their renderings are supervised against ground-truth images using MSE and LPIPS to regularize the 3DGS.
 - **$L_{depth}$:** This supervises the predicted depth maps $\hat{D}_n$ against pseudo-depth labels $D_n^\star$ (obtained from DA3) using a Huber loss $\rho(\log \hat{D}_n - \log D_n^\star)$.

2. **Geometry Perception Loss ($L_{geo}$):**
 - **Motivation:** Standard 2D losses are insufficient to guarantee geometrically faithful 3D structure. Depth ambiguities and appearance-geometry decoupling can occur, especially for noisy views.
 - **Mechanism:** A frozen, pretrained 3D foundation model $\Psi$ (e.g., $\pi_3$) is used as a structural critic. It takes multi-view images $I$ and camera parameters $T$ to extract geometry-aware feature maps $H$. $L_{geo}$ minimizes the average per-location cosine distance between the features extracted from the rendered views $\bar{H} = \Psi(\bar{I}, T)$ and the ground-truth views $H^\star = \Psi(I, T)$.
 - **Functionality:** Because $\Psi$ processes all views and cameras jointly, its features encode cross-view 3D structure. This loss encourages the rendered scene to share the same underlying geometry as the ground truth. It is activated only when $t > t_{th}$ and gradients are stopped on the reference branch $H^\star$.

**Training Details:**
PixWorld has approximately 1.04 billion parameters. It is trained from scratch on a mixture of RealEstate10K and DL3DV-10K datasets (∼67K scenes), augmented with 10M single images from the BLIP-3o corpus to enhance the 2D appearance prior. Views are randomly partitioned into clean and noisy subsets, biased towards small $\Omega_c$ to emphasize conditioned generation. The model uses AdamW optimizer, with a linearly decayed learning rate and EMA.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

PixWorld's performance was evaluated across various 3D reconstruction and generation protocols using RealEstate10K, DL3DV-10K, and WorldScore datasets.

**3D Scene Reconstruction (Table 1):**
- **Performance:** PixWorld demonstrates competitive performance in novel-view synthesis for 3D reconstruction. It consistently achieved the best PSNR and LPIPS scores across all tested settings (4-view and 8-view inputs on both RealEstate10K and DL3DV-10K). For SSIM, it achieved the best score on RealEstate10K and was second only to DepthSplat on DL3DV-10K.
- **Comparison:** Compared to strong baselines like YoNoSplat (which leverages ground-truth camera poses), PixWorld consistently improved PSNR and lowered LPIPS across all scenarios. For example, on 4-view RealEstate10K, PixWorld showed a PSNR of 26.21 dB and LPIPS of 0.138, outperforming YoNoSplat's 25.86 dB and 0.143, respectively.
- **Conclusion:** These results indicate that the pixel-space formulation and the integration of geometry-aware supervision contribute to enhanced cross-view consistency and accurate 3D reconstruction.

**3D Scene Generation (Tables 2, 3, 4):**

**Single-Image 3D Scene Generation (Table 2):**
- **Performance:** PixWorld outperformed all compared baselines (LVSM, GF, Gen3C, FlashWorld, Gen3R) across every evaluation metric on both RealEstate10K and DL3DV-10K datasets. This includes Novel View Synthesis (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS), Generation Quality (I2V Subject, I2V Background, Image Quality, Aesthetic Quality), and Camera Control (AUC@30, AUC@15, AUC@5).
- **Specific Gains:** PixWorld achieved a PSNR of 18.88 dB on RealEstate10K, a 1.06 dB improvement over the next best (LVSM at 17.82 dB). Similarly, on DL3DV-10K, its PSNR of 16.50 dB surpassed Gen3R's 15.75 dB. The improvement in camera control was particularly notable, with AUC@5 rising significantly (e.g., from 0.546 to 0.614 on RealEstate10K), suggesting superior geometric faithfulness.
- **Conclusion:** The pixel-space diffusion with differentiable rendering facilitates the generation of geometrically accurate trajectories from single-image inputs.

**Two-Image 3D Scene Generation (Table 3):**
- **Performance:** PixWorld maintained its lead in the two-image conditioning setting, outperforming or matching baselines across perceptual, generation, and pose metrics. It achieved the best LPIPS (0.210 on RealEstate10K, 0.340 on DL3DV-10K) and AUC@5 (0.649 on RealEstate10K, 0.534 on DL3DV-10K).
- **Comparison:** While LVSM showed competitive raw PSNR/SSIM, PixWorld's performance across the broader set of metrics, particularly those assessing geometric and camera control, demonstrated its overall superiority.
- **Conclusion:** PixWorld demonstrates robust performance even with increased conditioning, showcasing its ability to generate high-quality and geometrically consistent scenes.

**WorldScore Benchmark (Table 4):**
- **Performance:** On the WorldScore benchmark, PixWorld achieved the best overall average score (71.04). It ranked first in Camera Control (91.08), 3D Consistency (91.39), and Photometric Consistency (93.84).
- **Conclusion:** These results further corroborate PixWorld's effectiveness in generating 3D consistent and controllable scenes across diverse metrics.

**Ablation Study (Table 5):**
- **Impact of Geometry Perception Loss:** An ablation study on RealEstate10K showed that removing the geometry perception loss significantly degraded performance across all three metric groups. PSNR dropped by 1.13 dB, SSIM by 0.105, and AUC@5 by 0.080 (approximately 12.5% relative drop), while VBench scores showed minor shifts.
- **Conclusion:** This indicates that the geometry perception loss is crucial for establishing and maintaining cross-view consistency and pose fidelity. It provides the necessary 3D structural signal that 2D photometric and perceptual losses alone cannot supply, leading to sharper later-view renderings and more accurate pose control.

**Inference Speed (Table 9):**
- **Comparison:** PixWorld generates a scene in 15 seconds, which is comparable to highly optimized methods like FlashWorld (10 seconds) but much faster than Gen3C (791 seconds) and Gen3R (882 seconds), while using a comparable number of function evaluations (NFE=100) to Gen3R.
- **Note:** The comparison is noted as not strictly "apples-to-apples" due to differences in output resolution and approach (PixWorld reconstructs a 3D representation from key frames, while video diffusion baselines denoise long frame sequences). The authors suggest that distillation and quantization could further accelerate PixWorld.

Overall, the findings indicate that PixWorld, through its pixel-space diffusion paradigm and geometry-aware supervision, establishes a unified framework that either matches or surpasses state-of-the-art methods in both 3D scene reconstruction and generation, particularly in maintaining 3D structural consistency and camera control.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PixWorld research contributes to the field of computer vision by presenting a unified framework for 3D scene generation and reconstruction, addressing several limitations inherent in prior approaches.

**Scientific Significance:**

1. **Unified Paradigm for 3D Scene Modeling:** PixWorld demonstrates the viability and advantages of a single model that jointly handles both 3D reconstruction (recovering scenes from real-world captures) and 3D generation (synthesizing plausible scenes). This unification simplifies the modeling landscape and could lead to more efficient development and deployment of 3D content creation tools.
2. **Advancement of Pixel-Space Diffusion for 3D:** By moving diffusion directly into pixel space for 3D scenes, the work extends the success of pixel-space diffusion in 2D image synthesis. This paradigm shift eliminates the information loss associated with latent autoencoders (VAEs/RAEs) and the need for their pretraining, which were significant drawbacks in latent-space 3D methods.
3. **Direct Optimization of 3D Representations:** The methodology allows the diffusion objective to directly supervise the underlying 3D Gaussian Splatting representation through differentiable rendering. This aligns the training signal with the true fidelity of the 3D scene, rather than an intermediate latent space, leading to more accurate and consistent 3D outputs.
4. **Novel Geometry-Aware Supervision:** The introduction of a geometry perception loss, leveraging features from a pretrained 3D foundation model (e.g., $\pi_3$), is a significant contribution. This loss provides explicit 3D structural supervision that goes beyond 2D photometric and perceptual losses, mitigating issues like geometric inconsistencies and depth ambiguities. The ablation study confirms its critical role in enhancing cross-view consistency and pose fidelity.

**Potential Impact:**

1. **Enhanced 3D Content Creation:** The ability to generate high-fidelity, geometrically consistent 3D scenes from limited inputs (e.g., text, single image) within a single model could significantly streamline workflows in industries like gaming, film production, and virtual/augmented reality (VR/AR). It facilitates rapid prototyping and populating digital environments.
2. **Robotics and Embodied AI:** Accurate 3D scene reconstruction and the generation of diverse 3D environments are crucial for training and deploying robotic systems and embodied AI agents. PixWorld's capabilities could lead to more realistic simulations for robot learning, better environment understanding for navigation, and more robust visual perception.
3. **Reduced Computational Overhead:** By removing the requirement for pretrained VAEs/RAEs, PixWorld offers a more end-to-end and potentially less resource-intensive training pipeline for unified 3D tasks, although further inference acceleration (e.g., through distillation) is acknowledged as future work.
4. **Foundation for Future 3D Models:** The success of pixel-space diffusion in unifying 3D tasks could establish a new promising paradigm for scalable and unified 3D scene modeling. It opens avenues for future research into higher-resolution outputs, more diverse scene types, and further optimization techniques.

In conclusion, PixWorld represents a notable step towards a more cohesive and efficient approach to 3D scene understanding and creation, addressing fundamental challenges in the field and offering broad potential benefits across various applications.
