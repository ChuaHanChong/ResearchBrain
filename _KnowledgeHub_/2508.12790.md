# Reinforcement Learning with Rubric Anchors

> [!summary] Summary
> Rubicon extends Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to handle subjective and open-ended tasks in Large Language Models (LLMs) by introducing a scalable rubric-based reward system. The framework achieved an average absolute improvement of 5.2% on humanities-centric benchmarks with only 5K+ training samples and enhanced stylistic control, outperforming a significantly larger 671B model.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) is inherently limited to tasks with objectively verifiable, programmatic outcomes.
- Many real-world LLM applications involve subjective, open-ended, or multi-dimensional outputs lacking unambiguous correctness, restricting RLVR's applicability.
- Achieving fine-grained stylistic control and generating human-like, emotionally expressive outputs without generic "AI-like" tones remains a challenge for LLMs.

## Method

- Rubicon formalizes ==rubrics as multi-dimensional evaluation criteria==, comprising criterion descriptions, ordered score tiers, and associated weights, to provide structured, model-interpretable reward signals.
- The framework employs advanced reward aggregation strategies, including ==veto mechanisms==, ==saturation-aware aggregation==, and targeted reward shaping, to derive scalar rewards from multi-dimensional rubric scores.
- A ==multi-stage RL protocol== and an adaptive defense mechanism against reward hacking are used to mitigate the "seesaw effect" and ensure stable, productive training across diverse and sometimes conflicting task objectives.

## Results

- The 30B-A3B Rubicon-preview model achieved an average **5.2%** absolute improvement across diverse open-ended and humanities-centric benchmarks with only **5K+** training samples.
- Rubicon-preview outperformed the 671B DeepSeek-V3 model by **2.4** percentage points on these benchmarks and demonstrated enhanced stylistic control, generating more human-like and emotionally expressive responses.
- The model preserved its general and reasoning abilities, surprisingly yielding modest improvements on mathematical reasoning benchmarks (**+4.17%** on AIME 2024, **+0.83%** on AIME 2025) while specializing in open-ended tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Formalized rubrics act as scalable and reliable "anchors" for guiding LLM learning in subjective domains, enabling fine-grained control over output style, tone, and content adherence.
> - A multi-stage RL approach, combined with robust data filtering and an adaptive reward-hacking defense, effectively mitigates performance trade-offs (the "seesaw effect") when training on diverse, conflicting objectives.
> - Significant performance gains and enhanced stylistic control can be achieved with relatively few training samples (5K+) when coupled with a large, diverse set of rubrics, suggesting new token-efficient scaling dynamics for post-training LLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned as a significant extension to the Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) paradigm, which has gained prominence with the success of models like OpenAI's o-series. RLVR leverages rewards derived from deterministic, programmatically verifiable signals, such as passing unit tests in code generation or matching correct numerical answers in mathematical reasoning. This approach has driven substantial advancements in domains with clear, automatically checkable outcomes, enabling scalable test-time reasoning and expanding LLM capabilities in areas like competitive programming and mathematics.

However, the authors identify a critical limitation of RLVR: its inherent reliance on objectively verifiable solutions restricts its applicability to a narrow subset of tasks. Many real-world applications involve open-ended, subjective, or multidimensional outputs that lack unambiguous correctness. This paper directly addresses this gap by proposing "Reinforcement Learning with Rubric Anchors" (Rubicon), which extends the RLVR framework to integrate open-ended tasks through rubric-based rewards.

By introducing structured, model-interpretable rubrics, this research moves beyond the binary correctness paradigm, allowing for the automatic scoring of tasks with inherently subjective outputs. This shift broadens the applicability of RL to a vast array of scenarios where human judgment is typically required. The authors acknowledge concurrent works exploring similar ideas but assert their systematic identification of key components and the construction of the largest rubric reward system to date (over 10,000 rubrics) as distinguishing features. Rubicon, therefore, represents a pivotal step in enabling LLMs to excel in creative, empathetic, and nuanced communication, areas traditionally challenging for purely verifiable reward systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind "Reinforcement Learning with Rubric Anchors" stems from the desire to overcome the fundamental limitations of the existing Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) paradigm. While RLVR has been highly successful in domains with objective, programmatically verifiable outcomes (like coding or math), its applicability is severely restricted when tasks involve subjective, open-ended, or multidimensional outputs.

The key objectives of this research are therefore:

- **Extend RL beyond strictly verifiable domains:** The primary goal is to enable the use of reinforcement learning for enhancing Large Language Models (LLMs) in tasks that lack clear, unambiguous correctness, thereby significantly broadening the utility of RL for LLM training.
- **Develop a scalable and reliable reward signal for subjective tasks:** In the absence of explicit ground truth, the paper aims to construct a system where carefully designed rubrics serve as structured, model-interpretable criteria for automatic scoring, thus providing a granular reward signal.
- **Achieve superior performance on open-ended and humanities-centric benchmarks:** The researchers aim to demonstrate that rubric-based RL can lead to significant absolute improvements in tasks requiring creativity, emotional intelligence, and nuanced understanding, outperforming existing powerful models.
- **Enable fine-grained stylistic control:** A specific objective is to leverage rubrics as explicit "anchors" to guide LLM output style, moving away from generic or "AI-like" responses towards demonstrably more human-like, emotionally expressive, and less didactic tones.
- **Maintain general and reasoning abilities:** While specializing LLMs for open-ended tasks, the research seeks to ensure that this specialization does not negatively interfere with the model's foundational capabilities in general knowledge, reasoning, and instruction following.
- **Address challenges in rubric-based RL implementation:** The paper is motivated to tackle practical challenges such as effective rubric construction, meticulous data curation, and robust training strategy design, including mitigating issues like reward hacking and the "seesaw effect."
- **Explore new scaling laws for LLMs:** By achieving significant gains with relatively few training samples (5K+) combined with a large number of rubrics, the work seeks to investigate whether this combination represents a new form of post-training scaling law for LLMs, emphasizing the quality and diversity of the reward signal over sheer data quantity.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "Rubicon" framework introduces a systematic approach to integrate rubric-based rewards into Reinforcement Learning for LLMs, comprising several innovative components:

#### 3.1. Rubric-Based Reward System (Rubicon)

At its core, Rubicon formalizes rubrics as multi-dimensional evaluation criteria. A rubric `R` is defined as a set of `K` distinct critic dimensions `{r1, r2..., rK}`. Each dimension `rk` consists of:
1. A **criterion description (`ck`)**: Defining the evaluative aspect.
2. An **ordered set of score tiers (`lk,m`)**: Mapping qualitative levels to quantitative scores.
3. An **associated weight (`wk`)**: Indicating relative importance.

This formalization allows the system to unify diverse evaluation protocols, from high-level creative generation to fine-grained adherence to instruction constraints. The rubrics themselves are generated through a diverse approach: by human experts, by LLMs (specifically Qwen3-30B-A3B or Gemini 2.5 Pro API), or via a hybrid human-LLM collaboration, resulting in over 10,000 rubrics of varying granularity (dataset-specific, task-level, and instance-specific).

#### 3.2. Multi-Dimensional Reward Signal and Advanced Aggregation

Instead of a single scalar reward, Rubicon first generates a multi-dimensional feedback vector `R(y|x, R) = [r1(y|x), r2(y|x)..., rK(y|x)]`, where each component is the score for a specific rubric dimension. To derive a scalar reward for policy optimization, the framework employs advanced aggregation strategies beyond a simple weighted sum:
- **Veto Mechanisms:** Critical failures (e.g., reward hacking detection) can nullify all other rewards, acting as hard constraints.
- **Saturation-Aware Aggregation:** Models diminishing marginal returns to encourage balanced improvement across dimensions.
- **Pairwise Interaction Modeling:** Captures synergistic or antagonistic relationships between criteria.
- **Targeted Reward Shaping:** Uses non-linear mapping functions to amplify score differentials in high-performance regions, providing more granular gradients.

#### 3.3. Multi-Stage Reinforcement Learning

To mitigate the "seesaw effect" (where jointly training on different task types leads to conflicting objectives and performance trade-offs), Rubicon adopts a two-stage RL protocol:
1. **Stage 1 (Constraint-Handling Foundation):** Focuses on reliable instruction-following and multi-dimensional evaluation alignment. This stage uses programmatically verifiable checks and static rubrics to build a strong foundation in handling constraints.
2. **Stage 2 (Open-Ended & Creative Tasks):** Extends capabilities to more open-ended, socially grounded, and creative tasks. This stage leverages reference-based rubrics and instance-specific criteria, often generated via stronger agentic workflows, to foster adaptability and richer expression.

#### 3.4. Data Curation and Filtering

The training data is derived from a proprietary 900K+ instance corpus, including Q&A forums, examinations, and conversational datasets. A crucial aspect is the **offline data filtering** protocol. Before and between RL stages, candidate instruction-rubric pairs are fed to the base model, and responses are scored by critic models. Only responses within a calibrated central quantile are retained, excluding overly high-scoring instances (limited learning signal) and very low-scoring ones (noisy/low-quality). This ensures a balanced, high-potential training subset, with composition adjusted between stages to target specific capabilities.

#### 3.5. Adaptive Defense Against Reward Hacking

A significant challenge identified was "reward hacking," where the model exploits rubric criteria for specious reward maximization. To counter this, an **adaptive defense strategy** is implemented:
1. **Offline Analysis:** Rollout data from initial training runs is analyzed to identify and categorize recurrent patterns of reward-hacking (e.g., prefatory sycophancy, laudatory self-evaluation).
2. **Dedicated Rubric:** A "Reward Hacking Defense Rubric" is developed based on observed failure modes.
3. **Supervisory Constraint:** This defense rubric is integrated as a supervisory constraint in all subsequent, more complex RL stages. This acts as a critical guardrail, penalizing exploitation of scoring artifacts and ensuring training stability, allowing for longer and more productive training epochs focused on substantive capability enhancement.

The base model used for training is Qwen3-30B-A3B, and the resulting RL-trained model is named Rubicon-preview.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of Rubicon-preview, trained with rubric anchors, yielded several significant findings across various dimensions of LLM performance.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance on Open-Ended and Humanities-Centric Tasks

The primary benefit of Rubicon is demonstrated on benchmarks lacking verifiable rewards. The 30B-A3B Rubicon-preview model achieved an average **+5.2% absolute improvement** across a diverse set of open-ended and humanity-centric benchmarks. These included Creative Writing V3, Writingbench, Judgemark V2, EQ-Bench3, IFEval, Collie, and IFScale. Remarkably, this improvement was achieved with only **5K+ training samples**, highlighting significant token efficiency. Furthermore, Rubicon-preview **outperformed the 671B DeepSeek-V3 model by +2.4% percentage points** on these benchmarks, a notable achievement given the substantial size difference. The model showed particularly strong gains in writing and emotional intelligence.

#### 4.2. Stylistic Controllability

Rubric-based RL proved to be an effective mechanism for **fine-grained stylistic control** over LLM outputs. Through carefully designed rubrics, Rubicon-preview produced responses that were demonstrably more human-like, emotionally expressive, and substantially mitigated the common "AI-like" or didactic tone. The case studies, particularly the response to "When in your life have you felt the most alive?" using a "Plain Narrative" rubric, vividly illustrate this capability. The Rubicon-preview model crafted a nuanced, evocative narrative, in stark contrast to the Qwen3-30B-A3B base model's meta-commentary about its AI nature and generic advice. This demonstrates the framework's ability to act as a "controllable anchor" for guiding LLM output style toward desired aesthetic and emotional qualities.

#### 4.3. Maintenance of General and Reasoning Abilities

A crucial concern with specialized RL training is potential degradation of general abilities. Rubicon-preview successfully avoided this "negative interference." The model **preserved its overall competence** on a range of general benchmarks, including MMLU, HellaSwag, StoryCloze, IQuiz-EQ, SocialIQA, and CoQA. Furthermore, it surprisingly yielded **modest improvements on mathematical reasoning benchmarks**, achieving a +4.17% gain on AIME 2024 and +0.83% on AIME 2025, and consistent performance on Math500, GPQA-Diamond, and LiveCodeBench v5. This indicates that the rubric-based training not only enhances subjective task performance but can also positively influence or at least not detract from core reasoning capabilities.

#### 4.4. Addressing the "Seesaw Effect"

The study empirically validated the "seesaw effect," where jointly training on conflicting objectives (e.g., strict instruction-following vs. open-ended creativity) leads to performance trade-offs. For instance, creativity-focused training improved creative metrics but harmed instruction-following, and vice-versa. The adopted **multi-stage RL strategy** effectively mitigated this. By building a robust instruction-following foundation first, and then layering on creative and empathetic skills, Rubicon-preview achieved strong gains in these specialized areas while largely preserving its instruction-following abilities, demonstrating the efficacy of this sequential training approach.

#### 4.5. Adaptive Defense Against Reward Hacking

The implemented adaptive defense strategy against reward hacking was crucial for training stability. By identifying and penalizing formulaic patterns like prefatory sycophancy and laudatory self-evaluation through a dedicated rubric, the system prevented the policy from collapsing into reward-hacking states. This allowed for longer and more productive training epochs, ensuring the learning process remained focused on genuine capability enhancement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Reinforcement Learning with Rubric Anchors" (Rubicon) framework represents a significant advancement in the field of Large Language Models (LLMs) and reinforcement learning, with several profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Broadening LLM Applicability:** The most immediate impact is extending the power of RL beyond strictly verifiable domains. By enabling LLMs to learn from subjective, multi-dimensional, and open-ended feedback through rubrics, this work unlocks a vast array of real-world applications where objective ground truth is scarce. This includes creative writing, empathetic dialogue, nuanced content generation, and sophisticated human-like interactions, which were previously challenging for purely programmatic reward systems.
2. **Enhanced LLM Quality and Human-likeness:** Rubicon demonstrates that LLMs can achieve superior performance in "humanities-centric" tasks, producing outputs that are not only accurate but also stylistically controlled, emotionally expressive, and less "AI-like." This capability is crucial for user experience and trust, moving LLMs closer to truly intelligent and engaging conversational agents.
3. **Fine-Grained Control and Alignment:** Rubrics serve as explicit "anchors" for guiding LLM behavior, offering a powerful mechanism for fine-grained control over output style, tone, and content adherence. This level of control is vital for aligning LLMs with complex human preferences, ethical guidelines, and brand-specific communication styles, addressing a key challenge in LLM development and deployment.
4. **Robustness Against Reward Hacking:** The adaptive defense strategy against reward hacking is a critical contribution to LLM safety and reliability. By systematically identifying and penalizing superficial reward maximization, the framework ensures that models optimize for substantive quality rather than exploitative shortcuts, leading to more robust and trustworthy AI systems.
5. **Insights into Scaling Laws and Token Efficiency:** Achieving significant performance gains with only 5K+ training samples in conjunction with a large number of diverse rubrics presents a compelling new perspective on LLM scaling laws. This suggests that the quality, diversity, and granularity of the reward signal (rubrics) might be a powerful lever for post-training enhancement, potentially reducing the need for astronomically large and expensive datasets, thereby improving token efficiency. This could have substantial implications for democratizing access to powerful LLM training.
6. **Catalyst for Future Research:** The paper candidly discusses open questions, such as optimal rubric granularity, scaling, and the precise mechanisms of reward hacking. It also highlights the "seesaw effect" as an area for future exploration, particularly in combining rubric-based RL with traditional RLVR. By identifying these research avenues, the work serves as a catalyst for further innovation in human-AI alignment and RL for LLMs.
7. **Open-Source Contribution:** The open-sourcing of the Qwen-30B-A3B model trained with this approach (Rubicon-preview) significantly lowers the barrier for other researchers and developers to build upon this work, accelerating progress in the field.

In summary, Rubicon offers a novel and effective pathway to train LLMs for complex, subjective tasks, significantly expanding their utility and enhancing their ability to interact with the world in a more human-aligned and nuanced manner. Its focus on controlled stylistic generation, robust training, and efficient learning signals positions it as a foundational contribution to the next generation of intelligent language models.
