# RoboHiMan: A Hierarchical Evaluation Paradigm for Compositional Generalization in Long-Horizon Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboHiMan is a hierarchical evaluation paradigm plus HiMan-Bench, a manipulation benchmark of atomic and compositional tasks under diverse perturbations with a progressive multi-level training dataset, and three evaluation modes (vanilla, decoupled, coupled) that isolate planning versus execution failures. State-of-the-art VLA models compose skills poorly, exposing gaps prior benchmarks miss.

## Problem

- Prior long-horizon benchmarks train and evaluate on full tasks as fixed skill permutations, without systematically testing flexible composition of atomic skills.
- Existing work like DeCoBench studies skill composition but lacks analysis of how environmental perturbations affect compositionality across stages.
- Colosseum probes perturbation robustness only at the atomic-task level, so success there gives no evidence about multi-stage compositional robustness.

## Method

- **HiMan-Bench**: 114 atomic and 144 compositional PyRep/CoppeliaSim tasks categorized into atomic, atomic-perturbation, compositional, compositional-perturbation groups across 12 perturbation factors.
- ==Progressive multi-level training dataset (L1-L4)== that incrementally adds atomic-perturbation, compositional, and compositional-perturbation demonstrations to probe data-scaling effects.
- ==Three-mode hierarchical evaluation paradigm== (vanilla non-hierarchical, decoupled planner/policy-separate, coupled end-to-end) using rule-based and VLM-based planners to disentangle planning from execution failure.

## Results

- Vanilla policies without a planner stay near-zero success on compositional and perturbed-compositional tasks even as training data scales, versus large gains for rule-based-planner variants.
- In the coupled setting, the VLM planner (RVT2-VLM) drops up to **0.382** success rate versus the rule-based planner (RVT2-RP) at L4 on compositional-perturbation tasks, exposing planner-execution compounding.
- Real-robot tests show pi-0.5 vanilla achieves only **17.5%** (**10.0%** under perturbation) on compositional tasks, while adding a rule-based planner raises this to **47.5%** (**27.5%** under perturbation).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Scaling training data alone cannot induce compositional generalization; explicit planning structure is necessary for reliable multi-skill chaining.
> - Robustness to perturbations and compositionality are separate axes of difficulty that must both be measured, since strong atomic-task performance hides compositional fragility.
> - Hierarchical systems need tighter feedback between planner and policy, since independently-good components still compound errors catastrophically end-to-end.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot manipulation is actively striving to enable robots to perform complex, long-horizon tasks in unstructured environments. A central challenge in this pursuit is **compositional generalization**: the ability to combine a set of learned atomic skills (e.g., "open drawer," "pick up object") into novel sequences to achieve new, more intricate goals (e.g., "open drawer, then place object inside"). Compounding this, real-world applications demand robustness against diverse environmental **perturbations** such as changes in lighting, object appearance, size, or distractor objects.

Existing benchmarks in long-horizon robotic manipulation, such as RLBench, CALVIN, and Libero-Long, have significantly advanced the field by providing platforms for training and evaluating models on complex tasks. However, the authors argue that these benchmarks largely focus on **task completion** without adequately disentangling or systematically evaluating the flexible **composition of atomic skills**. More recent benchmarks like DeCoBench and VLA-Bench have started to emphasize language-conditioned task decomposition and planning, acknowledging the need for skill composition. Yet, even these often overlook the impact of real-world perturbations on compositional abilities. Colosseum specifically examines robustness under perturbations but primarily at the level of atomic skills, not multi-stage compositional tasks. Critically, prior benchmarks also lack mechanisms to **disentangle the sources of failure**, making it difficult to determine whether poor performance stems from a flawed high-level planner, an ineffective low-level policy, or sensitivity to environmental variations.

RoboHiMan directly addresses these critical gaps. It introduces a comprehensive evaluation paradigm that is unique in its explicit focus on:
1. **Compositional generalization** under diverse **environmental perturbations**.
2. Providing a **hierarchical framework** to systematically diagnose failures, distinguishing between planning and execution issues.
3. Offering a **progressive multi-level training dataset** to analyze how data diversity and scale affect generalization.

By doing so, RoboHiMan positions itself as a crucial next step in robotic evaluation, moving beyond simple task success metrics to provide deeper insights into the underlying capabilities and limitations of state-of-the-art robot learning models, especially hierarchical and VLA architectures. This work is highly relevant to the ongoing research in foundation models for robotics, visual-language-action models, and generalizable robot learning, aiming to bridge the gap between controlled lab settings and the complexities of real-world deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of RoboHiMan is to systematically investigate the core challenge of **compositional generalization in long-horizon manipulation tasks in the presence of diverse perturbations**. The motivation stems from several limitations observed in the current research landscape:

1. **Limited Generalization of Existing Models:** End-to-end Visual-Language-Action (VLA) models often struggle to generalize beyond their training distribution, particularly when faced with novel compositions of skills or varied environmental conditions. Hierarchical approaches show promise but still fall short under complex perturbations, indicating limited capability in skill composition and robustness.
2. **Inadequate Benchmarking for Compositional Robustness:** Current benchmarks primarily emphasize task completion in long-horizon settings. They offer insufficient insight into how models compose skills flexibly, how robust these compositions are to real-world perturbations (e.g., changes in object appearance, lighting, or distractors), and how planning and execution modules interact.
3. **Lack of Diagnostic Tools for Failure Analysis:** A significant problem is the inability to disentangle the root causes of failure. When a robot fails a complex task, it's unclear whether the planner generated a poor sequence of subgoals, the low-level policy failed to execute a given subgoal, or environmental perturbations exacerbated either issue. This lack of diagnostic clarity hinders targeted research and development.

To address these motivations, RoboHiMan poses a central research question: **"How well can models trained solely on atomic skills generalize to long-horizon compositional tasks in the presence of various perturbations?"** The overall goal is to establish a rigorous framework for evaluating and understanding the true capabilities and bottlenecks of robotic systems in achieving generalized, robust long-horizon manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoboHiMan proposes a hierarchical evaluation paradigm built upon two core contributions: **HiMan-Bench** and a novel **Hierarchical Evaluation Paradigm**.

#### 3.1. HiMan-Bench: A Novel Benchmark

HiMan-Bench is designed for compositional generalization in robot manipulation, extending existing paradigms to include both complex skill composition and diverse environmental perturbations.
- **Task Design:** It builds upon RLBench, DeCoBench, and Colosseum, featuring 114 atomic tasks and 144 compositional tasks. Tasks are categorized into four types:
 - **Atomic (A):** Basic skills (e.g., "open drawer").
 - **Atomic-Perturbation (AP):** Basic skills under various environmental changes.
 - **Compositional (C):** Sequences of atomic skills (e.g., "open drawer then place object inside").
 - **Compositional-Perturbation (CP):** Sequences of atomic skills under environmental changes.
- **Perturbation Factors:** 12 factors are adopted from Colosseum, including manipulation object color/texture/size, receiver object color/texture/size, light color, table color/texture, distractor objects, background texture, and camera pose. These factors introduce variations in appearance, geometry, and viewpoint, making tasks more realistic and challenging.
- **Multi-Level Training Dataset Design (L1-L4):** HiMan-Bench introduces a progressive training data design to analyze the impact of data scale and diversity on generalization.
 - **L1 (Minimal Atomic):** 20 demonstrations per Atomic (A) task. This is the most challenging setting for generalization.
 - **L2 (Atomic with Perturbations):** L1 + 1 demonstration per Atomic-Perturbation (AP) task. Aims to improve robustness for atomic skills.
 - **L3 (Compositional):** L2 + 5 demonstrations per Compositional (C) task (for a subset of C tasks). Introduces direct exposure to multi-step compositional processes.
 - **L4 (Compositional with Perturbations):** L3 + 1 demonstration per Compositional-Perturbation (CP) task (for a subset of C tasks). Exposes the model to perturbed compositional scenarios.

#### 3.2. Hierarchical Evaluation Paradigm

This paradigm is designed to systematically evaluate model capabilities and diagnose failures by disentangling planning and execution. It involves a high-level planner and a low-level policy.
- **High-Level Planner:** Takes natural language instruction and visual observation as input, and outputs a subtask description. The Qwen2.5-VL VLM is used as the backbone, fine-tuned on HiMan-Bench data.
- **Low-Level Policy:** Translates a subtask description into a sequence of robot actions. Four state-of-the-art VLA models are used: RVT-2, 3D Diffuser Actor, π0, and π0.5. These models vary in vision modalities (RGB, RGB-D, multi-view), language encoders (CLIP, PaliGemma-3B), and action representations (next keypoint, EEF pose, trajectory, joint).

The evaluation is conducted across three modes:
1. **Vanilla Mode (Non-hierarchical, Online):** The low-level policy directly executes the entire long-horizon task from the original instruction without any high-level planner. This serves as a non-hierarchical baseline to assess end-to-end capability.
2. **Decoupled Mode (Hierarchical, Planner and Policy Evaluated Separately):**
 - **Rule-based Planner (Online):** A heuristic planner schedules subtasks based on annotated robot state changes. This provides a strong baseline for the low-level policy by offering "ideal" planning, allowing for isolated evaluation of execution capabilities.
 - **VLM-based Planner (Offline):** The VLM acts as a planner, predicting the next subtask at fixed intervals. Its accuracy is measured in a VQA-style evaluation, assessing its scene understanding and task decomposition abilities.
3. **Coupled Mode (Hierarchical, Online):** The full hierarchical system is deployed online, where the VLM-based planner dynamically generates subtask descriptions based on gripper state transitions, which are then executed by the low-level policy. This evaluates the end-to-end integration of planning and execution in a realistic setting.

**Evaluation Metrics:** Online evaluations report average success rates for A, AP, C, and CP tasks. Offline evaluation for the VLM-based planner measures subtask prediction accuracy.

**Real-World Validation:** A small set of atomic and compositional tasks with distractors, positional variations, and human interventions are performed on a Mobile ALOHA robot to confirm the simulation-based findings in a physical environment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted using RoboHiMan reveal several critical insights into compositional generalization in long-horizon manipulation:

1. **Skill Composition Without Planning (Q1):** Models operating in the "Vanilla" (non-hierarchical) mode, without an explicit planner, show near-zero success rates on both compositional (C) and perturbed compositional (CP) tasks. While marginal gains in robustness are observed with richer data, they fundamentally fail to compose atomic skills into coherent long-horizon behaviors. This strongly indicates that **explicit planning is essential** for robust skill composition in complex long-horizon tasks.

2. **Scaling Effects of Training Data (Q2):**
 - Increasing atomic-skill data (L1 to L2) significantly improves performance on atomic tasks (A&AP) and also provides a prerequisite for compositional tasks.
 - Adding compositional data (L3 and L4) further enhances performance on compositional tasks (C&CP) for hierarchical systems. However, the overall success rate for compositional tasks remains remarkably low across all models, even with the most comprehensive training data (L4). This highlights the **inherent difficulty of compositional generalization**, where simply scaling data does not fundamentally solve the problem.

3. **Sensitivity to Perturbations (Q3):**
 - Skill composition is highly sensitive to perturbations, particularly changes in object color (MO Color, RO Color), texture (MO Texture, RO Texture), and the "All" perturbations setting.
 - Models trained only on atomic data (L1, L2) exhibit limited adaptability to these perturbations. In contrast, introducing even a small amount of **perturbed compositional data (L3, L4) significantly improves the model’s robustness** in performing compositional tasks, outperforming models only exposed to perturbed atomic data.
 - Architectural inductive biases play a role, with RVT-2 and 3D Diffuser-Actor consistently outperforming π0 and π0.5 across all training scales, suggesting benefits from their 3D representations or keyframe selection mechanisms.

4. **Generalization to Unseen Compositions (Q4):** Current VLA models demonstrate **clear limitations in generalizing to unseen compositional tasks**. Even with partial exposure to compositional skills during L4 training, their success rates on novel compositions remain extremely low. While some models show moderate performance on "seen" compositional tasks, they almost completely fail on "unseen" ones, indicating a lack of true compositional generalization rather than mere memorization.

5. **Bottlenecks in Hierarchical Architectures (Q5):** The study identifies three primary bottlenecks:
 - **High-level Planner Errors:** The VLM-based planner, even when trained with compositional data, exhibits limited generalization to unseen compositional skills offline.
 - **Low-level Policy Execution Failures:** The policies can struggle to reliably execute subtasks, especially under perturbations.
 - **Error Accumulation:** In online coupled evaluation, planning errors from the VLM-based planner and execution failures from the low-level policy compound over long horizons. This leads to a **sharp degradation in overall performance** (e.g., RVT2-VLM shows significant drops compared to RVT2-RP, which uses an "ideal" rule-based planner). This suggests current hierarchical systems lack robust feedback mechanisms to handle and recover from errors at either level.

6. **Real-World Validation (Q6):** Real-world experiments on the Mobile ALOHA robot confirm the challenges observed in simulation. End-to-end execution without a planner ("Vanilla") achieved only 17.5% success on compositional tasks, dropping to 10.0% under perturbations. In contrast, pairing the same low-level policy with an "idealized" rule-based planner substantially improved success rates (47.5% and 27.5% respectively). This validates that real-world long-horizon manipulation faces similar compositional generalization and robustness issues, and that **hierarchical architectures hold clear potential benefits** when planning is reliable.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboHiMan makes highly significant contributions to the field of robotic manipulation by addressing critical gaps in current evaluation methodologies and offering profound insights into the capabilities and limitations of modern robot learning architectures.

Its primary significance lies in providing a **diagnostic and analytical framework** that goes beyond mere task success rates. By systematically disentangling the roles of planning and execution, and by explicitly incorporating diverse environmental perturbations and progressive training data, RoboHiMan enables researchers to:
1. **Pinpoint Failure Sources:** Clearly identify whether a system's failure stems from an ineffective high-level plan, poor low-level execution, or a lack of robustness to environmental changes. This diagnostic capability is invaluable for targeted algorithm development and improvement.
2. **Advance Generalizable Robot Learning:** The benchmark rigorously tests compositional generalization, forcing models to truly combine learned skills rather than just executing pre-programmed sequences. This is a crucial step toward building general-purpose robotic agents.
3. **Promote Robustness in Real-World Settings:** By emphasizing perturbations, RoboHiMan pushes the research community to develop models that are not only capable but also reliable in the face of real-world variability, which is essential for deployment.
4. **Guide Architectural Design:** The findings highlight that while hierarchical architectures are promising, their brittleness due to error accumulation demands stronger feedback mechanisms and more robust coordination between high-level planners and low-level policies. This will likely drive innovation in designing more resilient and adaptable hierarchical systems.

The potential impact of RoboHiMan is substantial. It provides:
- **A challenging, open-source benchmark (HiMan-Bench):** This will serve as a standard platform for comparing and advancing new robot learning algorithms, particularly those focused on long-horizon, compositional, and robust manipulation.
- **A structured evaluation paradigm:** This offers a blueprint for how to systematically assess robot capabilities, fostering more rigorous and insightful research.
- **Clear research directions:** The paper explicitly calls for future work in robust skill composition mechanisms, feedback-rich hierarchical architectures, perturbation-aware training recipes, scalable compositional datasets, and novel evaluation metrics (beyond success rates, to include error recovery and skill reusability).

Ultimately, RoboHiMan is poised to accelerate progress towards the long-term goal of building truly generalizable robotic agents capable of performing complex, reliable, and adaptable manipulation tasks in diverse and unpredictable real-world environments.
