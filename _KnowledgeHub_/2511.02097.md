# A Step Toward World Models: A Survey on Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tongji University provided a comprehensive survey on world models for robotic manipulation, categorizing existing paradigms, architectural designs, and functional roles while identifying key challenges and outlining future research directions. The work clarifies ambiguities in the field and integrates modern AI models like LLMs and VLMs into the world modeling framework.

## Problem

- Ambiguity in the definition, scope, architectures, and essential capabilities of 'world models' in robotic manipulation.
- Fragmented understanding of diverse research efforts, hindering cumulative progress in developing generalizable and practical world models.
- The need for autonomous agents to move beyond reactive control and develop an internal understanding of world dynamics for effective operation in complex environments.

## Method

- Conducted a systematic and analytical literature review of approaches exhibiting core world model capabilities in robotic manipulation, regardless of explicit labeling.
- Developed a comprehensive multi-dimensional taxonomy, categorizing world models by paradigms (Implicit, Latent Dynamics, Video Generation), architectural design, observation/representation, and functional roles.
- Proposed a normative framework outlining 13 essential core components and capabilities that a truly generalizable world model should possess.

## Results

- Categorized world models into three primary paradigms: Implicit World Modeling, Latent Dynamics Modeling, and Video Generation, detailing their respective strengths and limitations.
- Identified and thoroughly analyzed key challenges facing world model research, such as data limitations, long-horizon reasoning, spatiotemporal consistency, generalization, and physics-informed learning.
- Outlined a framework of 13 core capabilities, including Comprehensive Multimodal Perception, Imagination, Physics Awareness, and Generalization Ability, serving as aspirational goals for future world model development.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modern AI paradigms, including Large Language Models (LLMs), Vision-Language Models (VLMs), and Vision-Language-Action (VLAs), implicitly or explicitly contribute to world modeling for robotic tasks.
> - World models serve dual critical functions in robotics: providing decision support for planning and action prediction, and facilitating training by generating data or acting as learned simulators.
> - Effective world models for robotic manipulation increasingly require structured, task-relevant representations (e.g., object-centric) and hierarchical architectures to address complexity and long-horizon reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The concept of "world models" has a long history in artificial intelligence, dating back to the 1960s with figures like Marvin Minsky. These models represent an agent's internal understanding of its environment, enabling prediction, planning, and reasoning beyond mere reactive control. In recent years, with the advent of deep learning and large-scale data, interest in world models has surged, particularly in robotics where agents operate in complex, dynamic, and uncertain physical environments.

This survey positions itself critically within this burgeoning landscape by addressing a key challenge: the ambiguity surrounding the definition, scope, architectures, and essential capabilities of world models. The authors note that while numerous methods exhibiting world-model-like characteristics exist (e.g., video generation models, latent dynamics models, vision-language-action models), they are not always explicitly labeled or consistently framed as such. This leads to a fragmented understanding of the field.

The paper distinguishes itself from existing surveys which often focus on specific aspects like video generation, 3D/4D world modeling, dynamics learning, or physics cognition. Instead, Zhang et al. adopt a "holistic, problem-centered perspective." Their aim is to provide a comprehensive conceptual and architectural taxonomy, analyze functional roles, distill core capabilities, and identify pressing challenges and future directions for building *generalizable and practical* world models specifically tailored for robotic manipulation.

A significant contribution to the broader landscape is the survey's embrace of modern AI paradigms—Large Language Models (LLMs), Vision-Language Models (VLMs), and Vision-Language-Action (VLAs)—as implicit or explicit contributors to world modeling. By framing these powerful models through the lens of world modeling, the authors highlight a convergence in AI research, where the objective of constructing internal representations of world dynamics drives the integration of perception, reasoning, and action in intelligent systems. This perspective is crucial for understanding how the impressive capabilities of foundation models can be harnessed and adapted for embodied intelligence and robotic control, moving beyond purely language- or vision-centric tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this survey stems from the increasing demand for autonomous agents to operate effectively in complex, dynamic, and uncertain real-world environments. To achieve tasks like manipulation, navigation, and decision-making, robots must move beyond simple reactive control or imitation; they require an internal understanding of the world's underlying mechanisms and dynamics. This necessity drives the development of "world models" as internal representations that encode environmental states, capture dynamics, and support prediction, planning, and reasoning.

However, despite growing interest and diverse research efforts, the field of world models is characterized by significant ambiguities. The definition, scope, architectures, and essential capabilities of what constitutes a "world model" remain unsettled. Researchers often employ different terminologies and focus on specific aspects, leading to a fragmented understanding and hindering cumulative progress.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of this survey are:

1. **Clarify the Conceptual Landscape:** To provide a comprehensive review that goes beyond rigid definitions, examining approaches in robotic manipulation that exhibit core world model capabilities, regardless of explicit labeling. This involves addressing fundamental questions such as "What is the world model and its conceptual, architectural, and functional boundaries?"
2. **Systematize Existing Knowledge:** To analyze the roles of these approaches across perception, prediction, and control, identifying key challenges and solutions presented in the literature.
3. **Distill Core Components and Capabilities:** To identify and articulate the essential components, capabilities, and functions that a fully realized, generalizable world model should possess. This aims to provide a clear framework for future development.
4. **Outline Future Directions:** To identify major challenges in current world model research, such as data limitations, long-horizon reasoning, and generalization, and to propose promising avenues for future investigation towards building practical and adaptive robotic agents.
5. **Revisit Modern AI through World Modeling Lens:** To understand how contemporary large-scale AI models (LLMs, VLMs, video generation models) inherently capture or contribute to aspects of world understanding, thereby blurring traditional boundaries and fostering interdisciplinary insights.

In essence, the authors aim to consolidate and clarify the diverse research efforts in world models for robotic manipulation, providing a foundational reference point and a roadmap for future progress in the field.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This paper is a comprehensive **survey**, and its methodology centers on a structured and analytical review of the existing literature. Unlike experimental research that proposes new models or algorithms, this work systematically synthesizes prior contributions to provide clarity and establish a conceptual framework.

The authors' approach is characterized by several key aspects:

1. **Broad Scope and Inclusive Definition:** A core methodological choice is to move "beyond prescribing a fixed definition and limiting our scope to methods explicitly labeled as world models." Instead, they examine any approach in robotic manipulation that "exhibits the core capabilities of world models." This inclusive perspective allows for a more holistic understanding, integrating diverse techniques that contribute to world understanding, such as video generation models or certain vision-language models, even if their creators did not explicitly brand them as "world models."

2. **Systematic Categorization and Taxonomy:** The paper employs a rigorous categorization scheme to organize the vast literature. This involves analyzing world models across multiple dimensions:
- **Paradigms:** Differentiating between Implicit World Modeling (e.g., LLMs, VLMs, VLAs), Latent Dynamics Modeling (e.g., RSSM, JEPA, MuZero), and Video Generation paradigms.
- **Architectural Design:** Distinguishing between Flat and Hierarchical architectures, discussing their respective advantages and limitations.
- **World Observation and Representation:** Examining how the "world" is perceived and represented, considering aspects like dimensionality (2D, 3D, 4D), observation viewpoint (egocentric, exocentric), and representation type (scene-centric, object-centric, flow-centric).
- **Task Scope:** Classifying models based on whether they support single-task objectives or aim for multi-task generalization, including discussions on "foundation world models."
- **Functions:** Analyzing the primary roles of world models in robotics: "Decision Support" (e.g., action prediction, planning) and "Training Facilitation" (e.g., data generation, learned simulators).

3. **Identification of Key Techniques and Challenges:** The survey dedicates significant sections to detailing the enabling techniques and persistent challenges faced by researchers in this field. These include, but are not limited to, data limitations (scarcity, heterogeneity, missing labels), perception and representation issues (instruction understanding, raw pixel vs. abstraction, spatiotemporal awareness), long-horizon reasoning, spatiotemporal consistency, generalization, physics-informed learning, and memory mechanisms. For each challenge, the authors discuss proposed solutions and the limitations of current approaches.

4. **Framework Development for Core Capabilities:** A crucial part of the methodology is to synthesize the insights from the comprehensive review into a normative framework. This involves distilling a set of "Core Components and Capabilities" that a truly effective and generalizable world model should possess. This framework, outlined in Section VI, serves as a conceptual blueprint and a set of aspirational goals for future research.

5. **Discussion of Datasets:** The survey also includes a dedicated section on datasets, acknowledging their critical role in training and evaluating world models for robotic manipulation. It highlights the diversity of data sources, from general robotic and human manipulation datasets to specialized egocentric, 3D, and 4D datasets, and notes the trend toward combining multiple sources for large-scale pretraining.

By systematically dissecting and synthesizing existing work through these analytical lenses, the authors aim to move beyond a mere compilation of papers towards a deeper, problem-centered understanding of world models and their role in advancing robotic intelligence.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey by Zhang et al. yields several important findings and presents a consolidated view of the state of world models in robotic manipulation:

1. **Ambiguity and Diversity Define the Current Landscape:** A central finding is the continued ambiguity in defining "world models." The paper demonstrates that the field encompasses a wide array of conceptual, architectural, and functional interpretations. This is reflected in the diverse approaches, ranging from implicit models that directly map perception to action, to explicit models that build latent representations or generate raw visual predictions.

2. **Categorization of World Model Paradigms:** The survey successfully categorizes world models into three main paradigms:
- **Implicit World Modeling:** Models like LLMs, VLMs, and VLAs that leverage powerful reasoning and next-token prediction capabilities to infer actions without explicit environmental dynamics. They excel in semantic grounding and generalization but may struggle with long-horizon prediction.
- **Latent Dynamics Modeling:** Approaches like Recurrent State-Space Models (RSSMs) and Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) that encode high-dimensional observations into compact latent states and predict their temporal evolution. These are efficient for planning and imagination but face challenges in maintaining interpretability and preserving task-relevant features.
- **Video Generation:** Models that directly produce visually realistic and temporally coherent future scenes. They offer detailed predictions but often at higher computational cost and may prioritize visual fidelity over accurate dynamics or causal understanding.

3. **Architectural Trends:** The review highlights a shift from simpler "flat architectures" to more sophisticated "hierarchical architectures." Flat models are limited in handling multi-scale dynamics and long-horizon tasks, whereas hierarchical models (e.g., two-system architectures inspired by human cognition) aim to separate immediate reactions from long-term planning, albeit with increased complexity.

4. **Importance of World Observation and Representation:** Effective world models necessitate careful choices regarding data dimensionality (2D, 3D, 4D), observation viewpoint (egocentric, exocentric), and representation type (scene-centric, object-centric, flow-centric). The trend shows a move towards higher dimensionality and more structured, task-relevant representations (e.g., object-centric) to capture richer geometric and dynamic information.

5. **Dual Functions of World Models:** The paper identifies two complementary functions:
- **Decision Support:** Enabling action prediction, planning, and visual foresight for autonomous agents. This includes implicitly planning with LLMs/VLMs, latent-space planning, and vision-based planning with diverse goal conditions (vision-conditioned, language-conditioned).
- **Training Facilitation:** Acting as "data engines" to generate synthetic trajectories for imitation or reinforcement learning, and serving as "evaluation modules" by providing internal reward estimation or predictive feedback, thereby reducing reliance on costly real-world interactions and addressing the sim-to-real gap.

6. **Comprehensive Identification of Key Challenges:** The survey provides a detailed breakdown of persistent challenges:
- **Data Limitations:** Scarcity of labeled data, heterogeneity across datasets, and missing action labels. Solutions include leveraging pre-trained models, auxiliary data, synthetic data, and self-supervised learning for action inference.
- **Perception and Representation:** Ambiguity in instruction understanding, balancing raw pixel modeling with conceptual abstraction, filtering task-irrelevant information, and enhancing spatiotemporal awareness.
- **Long-horizon Reasoning and Spatiotemporal Consistency:** The inherent difficulty in maintaining coherence and accuracy over extended temporal sequences, addressed through closed-loop learning, subgoals, hierarchical structures, and memory mechanisms.
- **Generalization:** Adapting to novel environments, objects, and tasks, tackled via data scaling, pre-trained models, instruction decomposition, and learning invariant representations.
- **Physics-informed Learning:** The current struggle to generate physically consistent predictions, requiring explicit incorporation of physical priors and rethinking model design for interpretability.
- **Other challenges:** Video fidelity, sim-to-real gap, fine-grained interaction modeling, multi-agent operations, and error propagation.

7. **Proposed Core Components and Capabilities:** A significant "result" is the framework of 13 essential capabilities for future world models (Section VI): Comprehensive Multimodal Perception, Interactivity, Imagination, Long-horizon Reasoning, Spatiotemporal Reasoning, Counterfactual Reasoning, Abstract Reasoning, High-fidelity Prediction, Physics Awareness, Generalization Ability, Causality, Memory, and Collaboration Ability. This framework encapsulates the aspirational goals for the field.

In summary, the paper synthesizes a fragmented field into a coherent overview, highlighting both the impressive progress and the substantial hurdles remaining on the path to fully realized, general-purpose world models for robotic manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The survey "A Step Toward World Models: A Survey on Robotic Manipulation" by Zhang et al. carries substantial significance and holds the potential for broad impact across both academic research and practical applications in robotics and artificial intelligence.

1. **Clarifying a Complex and Evolving Field:** One of the most immediate impacts is its role in clarifying the often ambiguous and rapidly evolving landscape of world models. By systematically categorizing diverse approaches, architectures, and functionalities, the survey provides a much-needed conceptual anchor for researchers. It helps to consolidate understanding, reconcile differing terminologies, and identify commonalities across various strands of research, thereby fostering more coherent discourse and collaborative efforts.

2. **Guiding Future Research Directions:** The comprehensive identification of "Key Techniques and Notable Challenges" (Section V) and the articulation of "Future Research Directions" (Section VIII-B) serve as a critical roadmap for the field. It highlights under-explored areas such as causality, fairness, security, and resource-constrained deployment, directing researchers toward high-impact problems. The proposed framework of 13 "Core Components and Capabilities" (Section VI) provides an aspirational blueprint for what a "fully realized" world model should entail, motivating the development of more holistic and robust systems.

3. **Accelerating Robotic Autonomy and Intelligence:** Advancements in world models are directly proportional to enhancements in robotic autonomy. By enabling robots to predict future outcomes, plan complex behaviors internally, and learn from imagined interactions, world models reduce the reliance on costly and potentially unsafe real-world trial-and-error. This directly contributes to more intelligent, adaptable, and efficient robotic systems capable of operating in unstructured and dynamic environments, such as manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, and domestic assistance. The discussion on sim-to-real gaps and how world models can act as learned simulators is particularly impactful for practical robot deployment.

4. **Fostering Interdisciplinary Integration:** The survey's explicit treatment of modern AI models—LLMs, VLMs, and video generation models—as integral to world modeling encourages deeper interdisciplinary integration. It showcases how the powerful representational and generative capabilities of these foundation models, originally developed for language or vision tasks, can be leveraged and adapted for embodied AI. This perspective can inspire new hybrid architectures that combine the semantic understanding of large language models with the dynamic prediction of world models, pushing the boundaries of what integrated AI systems can achieve.

5. **Highlighting Ethical and Practical Considerations:** The inclusion of "Fairness and Security" as future research directions is particularly significant. As world models become central to autonomous decision-making, their potential for bias, vulnerability to adversarial attacks, and impact on real-world safety become critical concerns. By addressing these early, the survey advocates for responsible AI development in robotics, ensuring that powerful models are deployed ethically and securely. Similarly, the emphasis on "Resource-Constrained Deployment" addresses a major practical barrier, pushing for the development of lightweight and efficient models suitable for real-world robotic hardware.

6. **Benchmarking and Evaluation Standards:** The call for "Evaluation Protocols" underscores a crucial need in the field. Fragmented and task-specific evaluation methods hinder objective progress comparison. The push for standardized benchmarks for visual fidelity, policy success, causal consistency, physical plausibility, and generalization will significantly impact how research is conducted and validated, fostering healthy competition and clearer progress metrics.

In conclusion, this survey is not merely a summary of existing work; it is a forward-looking analysis that provides structure, clarifies ambiguities, and outlines a comprehensive research agenda for the development of truly intelligent, generalizable, and ethically robust world models for robotic manipulation. Its impact will be felt in guiding new research, inspiring novel architectural designs, and ultimately contributing to the realization of more capable and autonomous robotic agents.
