# A Step Toward World Models: A Survey on Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tongji University provided a comprehensive survey on world models for robotic manipulation, categorizing existing paradigms, architectural designs, and functional roles while identifying key challenges and outlining future research directions. The work clarifies ambiguities in the field and integrates modern AI models like LLMs and VLMs into the world modeling framework.

## Problem

- Ambiguity in the definition, scope, architectures, and essential capabilities of 'world models' in robotic manipulation.
- Fragmented understanding of diverse research efforts, hindering cumulative progress in developing generalizable and practical world models.
- The need for autonomous agents to move beyond reactive control and develop an internal understanding of world dynamics for effective operation in complex environments.

## Method

- Conducted a systematic and analytical literature review of approaches exhibiting core world model capabilities in robotic manipulation, regardless of explicit labeling.
- Developed a comprehensive multi-dimensional taxonomy, categorizing world models by paradigms (Implicit, Latent Dynamics, Video Generation), architectural design, observation/representation, and functional roles.
- Proposed a normative framework outlining 13 essential core components and capabilities that a truly generalizable world model should possess.

## Results

- Categorized world models into three primary paradigms: Implicit World Modeling, Latent Dynamics Modeling, and Video Generation, detailing their respective strengths and limitations.
- Identified and thoroughly analyzed key challenges facing world model research, such as data limitations, long-horizon reasoning, spatiotemporal consistency, generalization, and physics-informed learning.
- Outlined a framework of 13 core capabilities, including Comprehensive Multimodal Perception, Imagination, Physics Awareness, and Generalization Ability, serving as aspirational goals for future world model development.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modern AI paradigms, including Large Language Models (LLMs), Vision-Language Models (VLMs), and Vision-Language-Action (VLAs), implicitly or explicitly contribute to world modeling for robotic tasks.
> - World models serve dual critical functions in robotics: providing decision support for planning and action prediction, and facilitating training by generating data or acting as learned simulators.
> - Effective world models for robotic manipulation increasingly require structured, task-relevant representations (e.g., object-centric) and hierarchical architectures to address complexity and long-horizon reasoning.
