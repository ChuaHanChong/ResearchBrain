# Neurosymbolic Embodied Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> A neurosymbolic embodied agent factors long-horizon household tasks into VLM-driven task-directed visual exploration that builds a grounded symbolic state, followed by PDDL-constrained decoding plus Monte Carlo tree search that guarantees plans are executable by construction, reaching over 90% success on VirtualHome and ALFWorld with 4B-27B models.

## Problem

- Direct VLM policies generate fluent action sequences but cannot guarantee executability, since a single wrongly grounded object or premature action invalidates the remaining trajectory.
- Prior LLM+PDDL systems treat PDDL as a generated artifact or downstream solver input rather than exposing grounded operator applicability during inference itself.
- Existing embodied systems entangle perception, state tracking, and action semantics inside one autoregressive loop, letting perceptual and planning errors compound uncontrollably.

## Method

- A ==VLM-driven exploration harness== (Phase I) actively selects inspection targets and grounded interactions to build a task-relevant symbolic initial state and instance bindings, admitting facts only under visual or interaction evidence.
- ==State-dependent constrained decoding== restricts token generation to only those extending ==PDDL==-applicable actions, making every generated plan executable by construction under the transition model.
- ==Token-level Monte Carlo tree search== combines a policy-prior upper-confidence bound over the constrained model distribution with a domain-independent planning heuristic to select goal-reaching plans among valid candidates.

## Results

- The agent achieves **94.5-99.5%** success on VirtualHome and **90.3-97.8%** on ALFWorld across Qwen3.5 4B-27B, with the 4B model beating the 27B direct visual policy by **32.0** and **63.4** percentage points respectively.
- Ablations show constraints and search are complementary: on ALFWorld constrained-greedy alone reaches **32.3%** and unconstrained MCTS alone reaches **29.2%**, while combining both reaches **95.5%**.
- The method uses up to **4.0x** fewer generated tokens than extended thinking, **1.5x** fewer than unconstrained MCTS, and **5.6x** fewer model-visible images than direct interactive policies at matched model scale.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit symbolic structure can substitute for considerable model scale, since a small constrained model outperforms a much larger unconstrained one on long-horizon executable planning.
> - Search and hard applicability constraints are complementary, not redundant, and their combined nonlinear gain grows with problem difficulty (denser instance and receptacle semantics).
> - Factoring perception from planning localizes residual failure to state acquisition, isolating where future visual-recognition improvements would transfer directly to overall performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

This work operates at the intersection of embodied artificial intelligence, vision-language models (VLMs), symbolic planning, and neurosymbolic AI. The broader research landscape for embodied agents often grapples with the challenge of translating high-level task goals into executable, low-level actions within dynamic, partially observable environments.

Vision-language models have demonstrated capabilities in recognizing objects, leveraging commonsense knowledge, and generating plausible action sequences from natural language goals. However, as noted in the introduction, directly applying these models to long-horizon embodied tasks presents significant limitations. The primary issue is that VLM-generated plans, while plausible, frequently lack guaranteed executability. They can violate environment dynamics, act on incorrectly grounded entities, or make choices that render subsequent actions invalid. These errors tend to accumulate over lengthy interactions, and increasing model scale or linguistic fluency does not inherently prevent such fundamental planning failures.

Existing approaches to mitigate these issues include employing affordance models, dynamic replanning mechanisms, or integrating task-and-motion planning (TAMP). While these methods address aspects of the problem, the paper identifies a persistent need for a mechanism that robustly binds visually acquired state information to formal action semantics during the plan generation process itself.

The proposed neurosymbolic approach connects to several streams of research:
- **Symbolic Planning and LLM Planners:** It leverages the strengths of classical symbolic planning, particularly PDDL (Planning Domain Definition Language), known for its precise semantics and ability to ensure action applicability. It contrasts with approaches where Large Language Models (LLMs) are used to generate PDDL domains or plans, which often suffer from semantic inconsistencies despite syntactic plausibility.
- **Constrained Decoding:** The work extends recent advancements in constrained decoding, where formal specifications are used to mask invalid tokens during language model generation. Previous work largely focused on syntactic or text-only semantic control (e.g., SEM-CTRL). This paper applies state-dependent constraints to visually grounded planning, where the underlying state is not given but must be actively perceived.
- **Perception and Planning:** It follows a long-standing design pattern in robotics that separates environment modeling (e.g., SLAM) from goal-directed control. This involves using semantic knowledge to guide information acquisition, while grounded observations determine the precise state representation. The paper builds on observations that VLMs perform better as PDDL formalizers than as end-to-end long-horizon planners, with visual extraction of object relations being a key bottleneck.

By explicitly factoring perception and planning into two connected phases and enforcing symbolic constraints, this research aims to provide a robust solution to the problem of generating executable plans for embodied agents, thereby addressing a fundamental limitation in the current capabilities of VLM-driven systems.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from a critical challenge in embodied artificial intelligence: Vision-Language Models (VLMs), despite their ability to generate plausible action sequences and leverage commonsense knowledge, do not guarantee the executability of these plans in real or simulated environments. These plans often fail due to violations of environment dynamics or actions taken on incorrectly grounded entities. Such errors compound over long-horizon tasks, leading to unreliable agent behavior. The authors highlight that neither increasing model scale nor improving linguistic fluency inherently resolves this issue, as the core problem lies in the tight coupling of perception and planning within a single autoregressive loop. An agent must simultaneously:
1. Identify task-relevant objects from egocentric observations.
2. Determine their spatial relations and states.
3. Maintain these facts across different viewpoints.
4. Select applicable actions.
5. Reason about the delayed effects of these actions.

Attempting to resolve all these aspects within a single model architecture leads to intertwined perceptual and planning errors. For example, a fluent plan might reference an unobserved object, or a correct observation could still be followed by an inapplicable action. While execution feedback can identify errors post-hoc, it does not prevent the model from repeating invalid choices or deviating from the state its own actions induced.

Given this context, the key objectives of this research are:

1. **Develop a neurosymbolic architecture** that factors the long-horizon embodied planning problem into two explicitly connected phases: visual state acquisition and constrained symbolic planning. This separation aims to disentangle perceptual, grounding, and planning errors, allowing for more reliable operation.
2. **Implement a task-directed visual exploration phase** (Phase I) where a VLM, guided by an exploration harness, actively acquires only the goal-relevant objects, relations, and instance bindings from egocentric observations and grounded interactions. This avoids exhaustive scene reconstruction and focuses perception on task-critical information.
3. **Construct a grounded symbolic initial state** as a narrow interface between the exploration and planning phases. This interface provides a formal, explicit representation of the environment relevant to the task, ensuring that the planning phase operates on a clear and consistent understanding of the scene.
4. **Design a constrained symbolic planning phase** (Phase II) that leverages a PDDL transition model to enforce action applicability at the token level during language model decoding. This mechanism aims to ensure that every generated plan is executable by construction under the formal transition model.
5. **Integrate Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS)** within the constrained planning phase. MCTS, guided by the language model's procedural prior and a domain-independent planning heuristic, evaluates executable continuations, optimizing for long-horizon goal reachability. This combines the semantic preferences of the language model with explicit state transitions and deliberative search.
6. **Empirically evaluate the end-to-end performance** of the proposed agent across diverse embodied household benchmarks (VirtualHome and ALFWorld), comparing it against direct visual policies, unconstrained reasoning models, and embodied-specialized baselines. The evaluation also seeks to quantify inference and observation costs and attribute residual failures to their causal stages.

Ultimately, the motivation is to create embodied agents that can reliably execute complex tasks by providing a robust mechanism for binding visually acquired state to formal action semantics, thereby making plan executability an inherent property rather than an inferred behavior.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The proposed neurosymbolic embodied agent operates via a two-phase architecture designed to factor long-horizon household tasks into distinct components: task-directed visual exploration and constrained symbolic planning.

**Problem Setting:**
The task is modeled as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP), where the agent receives egocentric RGB observations ($I_t$) while the full environment state ($s_t$) remains latent. The objective is to produce a plan $\pi = (a_1, \ldots, a_n)$ whose actions are sequentially applicable and whose final state satisfies a goal formula G. The method reduces the POMDP to a deterministic classical planning problem by acquiring a point estimate of the task-relevant state in Phase I and solving for a deterministic open-loop plan in Phase II.

**Overall Architecture:**
The agent’s architecture separates visual state acquisition from valid, goal-directed deliberation. This is achieved through a grounded initial-state interface that connects Phase I (Exploration) and Phase II (Exploitation).

**Phase I: Exploration (Task-Directed Visual Grounding)**
This phase aims to acquire only the objects, relations, and instance bindings required by the task goal, avoiding exhaustive scene reconstruction.

- **Components:**
 - **Vision-Language Model (VLM) ($f_\phi$):** Utilized to interpret egocentric images and propose facts.
 - **Deterministic Exploration Harness (H):** A controller that tracks inspection history, grounds skills, and enforces environment interaction constraints.
- **Process:**
 1. **Skill Generation:** The harness ($H$) constructs a set of candidate high-level exploration skills ($K_t$) such as `GOTO(x)`, `OPEN(x)`, `CLOSE(x)`, `LOOK`, or `DONE`. These skills are determined by the goal, established facts ($F_t$), and interaction record ($R_t$), omitting targets already inspected or not yet grounded. `LOOK` involves acquiring additional viewpoints and possibly grounded interactions to test object-receptacle hypotheses.
 2. **VLM Proposal and Selection:** The VLM ($f_\phi$) receives the goal, current image ($I_t$), established facts ($F_t$), interaction record ($R_t$), and candidate skills ($K_t$). It proposes a set of facts ($P_t$) from the current image (e.g., `VISIBLE(o)`, `ON(o, r)`, `INSIDE(o, r)`) and selects an exploration skill ($k_t \in K_t$).
 3. **Environment Interaction and Evidence Update:** The selected skill ($k_t$) is executed in the environment, yielding a new image ($I_{t+1}$) and an event ($e_t$) (success or concise error). A deterministic evidence-update function ($U$) then updates the established facts ($F_t$), interaction record ($R_t$), and symbol-to-instance binding ($\beta_t$). Crucially, relational facts are only committed if supported by multi-viewpoint localization or successful grounded interaction, making this not a general hallucination detector but a mechanism for evidence-based grounding.
 4. **Termination:** Exploration ends when `Ready(F_τ, G)` (all needed objects and relations for the planning problem are grounded), the VLM selects `DONE`, or the interaction budget is exhausted.
- **Output:** The phase returns a grounded initial state (`bs_0 = F_τ ∪ F_str`, where $F_{str}$ are fixed domain facts) and the final symbol-to-instance binding ($\beta_τ$). Neither contains privileged scene state.

**Phase II: Exploitation (Constrained Symbolic Planning)**
This phase combines the grounded initial state from Phase I with a PDDL transition model to formulate and solve a classical planning task.

- **Components:**
 - **PDDL Transition Model (D):** Defines grounded operators with preconditions, add effects, and delete effects. This model is used to determine applicable actions ($A_D(s)$) and successor states ($T_D(s, a)$).
 - **Language Model (LM):** Provides a procedural prior over plans.
 - **Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS):** Evaluates long-horizon consequences of plan prefixes.
- **Process:**
 1. **State-Dependent Constrained Decoding:** This is a core mechanism ensuring executability. At every token decoding step, the language model's distribution is restricted to tokens that can extend an applicable PDDL action. If `L_D(s)` is the set of applicable action strings at state `s`, `C_D(s, u)` defines the allowed next tokens for a partial action prefix `u`. The LM's probability `p_θ(v | u, c_Π)` is re-normalized to `p_{θ,C}(v | s, u, c_Π)`, effectively assigning zero probability to invalid tokens.
 2. **Validity Guarantee:** By construction, any complete plan generated under `p_{θ,C}` is applicable under the transition model `D`. This guarantee transfers to the simulator if `D` is sound and `β_τ` from Phase I is accurate.
 3. **Constrained Language-Guided Search (MCTS):** To ensure goal reachability (beyond mere applicability), token-level MCTS is employed.
 - **Node Representation:** Each node `n = (s, u)` contains the current symbolic state `s` and partial action prefix `u`.
 - **Selection:** The selection process in MCTS is restricted to `C_D(s, u)` and uses a Policy-Prior Upper-Confidence Bound (PUCB) (`Q(n, v) + U(n, v)`). This combines the backed-up search value (`Q`) with the constrained language model's prior (`p_{θ,C}`), guiding the search towards plausible and executable continuations.
 - **Evaluation:** Completed action sequences (plans $\pi$) are evaluated based on whether they reach the goal (`R_G` if `s_π |= G`) or by a heuristic estimating remaining symbolic distance (`-h(s_π, G)`) and a penalty for plan length (`-λ|π|`). Standard domain-independent planning heuristics are used for `h`.
 - **Termination:** Search stops upon finding a goal state or exhausting a predefined budget, returning the highest-valued valid candidate.

**Benchmarks:**
The method is evaluated on two distinct household simulators:
- **VirtualHome:** Focuses on multi-object rearrangement, containment, and state reasoning across 200 problems.
- **ALFWorld:** Emphasizes egocentric search, instance-level grounding, closed receptacles, and irreversible state transformations across 134 unseen episodes.
Crucially, scene graphs and admissible action lists are not exposed to the agents.

**Models and Baselines:**
The primary models are the Qwen3.5 family (4B, 9B, 27B parameters). Baselines include direct interactive VLM policies (Qwen, Gemini-3-Flash), embodied VLM policies (MiMo-Embodied-7B, Embodied-R1.5-8B, RoboBrain2.0-32B), and ablations of Phase II (unconstrained greedy, unconstrained thinking, constrained greedy, unconstrained MCTS).

**Metrics:**
Task success rate is the primary metric. Secondary metrics include generated tokens, model-visible images, and a causal failure attribution analysis.

### 4. Main findings and results

The research yielded several key findings regarding the efficacy, efficiency, and robustness of the proposed neurosymbolic embodied agent.

#### 4.1. Overall Task Success

The neurosymbolic agent achieved high task success rates across both benchmarks: 94.5–99.5% in VirtualHome and 90.3–97.8% in ALFWorld. These results represent a substantial improvement over direct visual interaction and unconstrained chain-of-thought reasoning baselines, consistently outperforming them across all model scales. For instance, the 4B agent, the smallest model tested, surpassed the 27B direct VLM by 32.0 percentage points in VirtualHome and an even larger 63.4 points in ALFWorld. It also exceeded unconstrained thinking with the 27B model by 35.5 and 73.4 points, respectively. This indicates that the explicit structure and formal constraints implemented in the neurosymbolic approach effectively substitute for considerable model scale or free-form reasoning capacity.

Among the direct-policy baselines, Gemini-3-Flash achieved the highest success (91.0% in VirtualHome and 50.8% in ALFWorld), yet still lagged behind the neurosymbolic agent, particularly in the more challenging ALFWorld environment. Specialized embodied VLM baselines (MiMo-Embodied-7B, Embodied-R1.5-8B, RoboBrain2.0-32B) demonstrated limited zero-shot transfer, achieving very low success rates, which underscores the difficulty of maintaining an executable, stateful policy over many interaction turns using solely pre-trained embodied priors.

#### 4.2. Inference and Environment Cost

The increased reliability of the neurosymbolic agent did not come at the expense of higher inference or environment interaction costs.
- **Generated Tokens:** The constrained MCTS variant used up to 1.5 times fewer generated tokens than unconstrained MCTS and 4.0 times fewer than extended thinking. This efficiency is attributed to constraints pruning inapplicable branches early, focusing search on executable alternatives.
- **Model-Visible Images:** The agent required significantly fewer model-visible images—up to 5.5 times fewer than direct interaction (typically 4-6 images per task compared to 13-34). This reduction stems from Phase I amortizing perception across the search tree by acquiring a compact, task-relevant representation once, after which Phase II plans without further image-conditioned model calls. This also implies fewer unnecessary exploratory motions in a physical deployment context.

#### 4.3. Failure Analysis

A crucial finding was the distinct distribution of residual failures.
- The neurosymbolic agent's failures were predominantly concentrated in Phase I (exploration/representation). Approximately 2.3% of VirtualHome tasks and 4.5% of ALFWorld tasks failed due to insufficient or incorrect state acquisition (e.g., missed goal objects, erroneous object-receptacle bindings).
- Only a small fraction (1.0% in ALFWorld) of tasks failed during plan generation or execution *after* a usable state was acquired. This indicates that once the symbolic state is accurate, the constrained planning mechanism is highly reliable. The qualitative analysis suggested that visual recognition errors, particularly for small or low-contrast objects, were a primary cause of Phase I failures.
- In contrast, direct Qwen policies failed at different boundaries: 64.2% of VirtualHome tasks failed to reach the goal, while in ALFWorld, 39.8% failed due to grounding issues and 48.8% terminated without satisfying the goal. This highlights their struggle with both action applicability and long-horizon goal completion.

#### 4.4. Constrained Planning Ablations

Ablation studies using the Qwen3.5-27B model revealed the complementary nature of search and constraints:
- **VirtualHome:** Constrained greedy decoding alone significantly improved success from 61.0% to 98.5%, demonstrating the strong impact of applicability constraints. Unconstrained MCTS also achieved 99.0% success in VirtualHome.
- **ALFWorld:** This environment proved more discriminating. Constraints alone yielded only 32.3% success, and unconstrained MCTS achieved 29.2%. However, their *combination* achieved 95.5%. This non-linear gain in ALFWorld, characterized by longer plans and more complex instance/receptacle bindings, strongly supports the neurosymbolic design: symbolic semantics (constraints) ensure feasibility, while model-guided search (MCTS) provides goal-directed deliberation over valid plans.

In summary, the neurosymbolic agent demonstrated robust performance, significantly outperforming unconstrained VLM policies while being more resource-efficient. Its success largely stems from ensuring plan executability by construction and localizing failures primarily to the visual state acquisition process.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The research on "Neurosymbolic Embodied Agents" presents several significant contributions and has a notable potential impact on the field of embodied artificial intelligence and beyond.

**Advancement in Embodied AI Reliability:** The most direct significance is its contribution to addressing the critical reliability gap in VLM-generated plans for embodied agents. By guaranteeing plan executability by construction under a formal transition model, the work moves beyond "plausible" plans to "executable" plans, a fundamental requirement for autonomous agents operating in physical or simulated environments. This is crucial for real-world applications where errors cannot be tolerated.

**Validation of Neurosymbolic AI Paradigm:** The paper provides strong empirical evidence for the effectiveness of a neurosymbolic approach. It effectively combines the strengths of neural networks (VLMs for visual perception and commonsense priors, LMs for procedural knowledge) with symbolic AI (PDDL for formal action semantics and state transitions, MCTS for deliberative search). This integration demonstrates that explicit symbolic reasoning, when coupled intelligently with neural components, can yield more robust and interpretable agent behavior than purely end-to-end neural systems for complex tasks.

**Enhanced Efficiency:** The findings highlight that explicit structure and formal constraints can significantly reduce computational and interaction costs. The agent used substantially fewer generated tokens and, more importantly, required far fewer model-visible images compared to direct interactive policies. This efficiency, particularly in terms of visual queries, is vital for resource-constrained applications (e.g., robotics with limited battery or processing power) and for accelerating development and testing in simulators by reducing simulation time.

**Clearer Failure Attribution:** By factoring the problem, the research enables more precise causal attribution of failures. Identifying that residual errors largely concentrate in the visual state acquisition (Phase I) rather than plan generation (Phase II) provides clear directions for future research. This allows for targeted improvements in visual recognition, object grounding, and active viewpoint selection, knowing that advancements in these areas are likely to directly translate into overall task success.

**Implications for Robotics and Automation:** The method has direct implications for robotics, particularly in household and service robotics where agents need to perform long-horizon, multi-step tasks in dynamic, partially known environments. The guarantee of plan executability is essential for safety and efficiency in such domains. The structured approach could simplify the development of complex robotic behaviors by providing a more stable planning foundation.

**Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a robust baseline and a clear methodology for future exploration. It encourages further research into improving visual state acquisition components (e.g., better active perception strategies, more robust object recognition under occlusion), refining symbolic domain learning, and exploring how more complex human-like planning heuristics might be integrated within the constrained MCTS framework. It also opens avenues for applying similar neurosymbolic factorization to other challenging embodied AI problems.

In summary, this research advances the state of the art in embodied agents by demonstrating a practical and efficient method for generating executable plans. Its impact extends to making embodied AI more reliable, interpretable, and resource-efficient, thereby accelerating its progression towards real-world deployment.
