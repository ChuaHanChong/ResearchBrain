# LLMs Meet Multimodal Generation and Editing: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey systematically reviews how Large Language Models (LLMs) are integrated into multimodal generation and editing across images, videos, 3D content, and audio. It categorizes existing approaches, details the diverse roles LLMs play in these processes, and outlines critical safety considerations and future research directions.

## Problem

- Prior surveys on Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) largely concentrated on multimodal understanding, neglecting the rapidly evolving areas of generation and editing.
- The explosive growth of LLMs created a need for a systematic framework to understand their application in generating and modifying diverse non-textual content.
- Researchers lacked a unified overview of how LLMs enhance multimodal content creation across modalities like images, videos, 3D, and audio.

## Method

- The authors conducted a systematic review, categorizing methods into pre-LLM (==CLIP/T5==-based) and ==LLM==-based approaches for multimodal generation and editing across various modalities.
- They detailed the diverse roles LLMs assume in these generative pipelines, such as layout planners, prompt synthesizers, backbones, or ==tool-augmented orchestrators== for external tools.
- The survey also examined key technical components, relevant datasets, and dedicated sections to ==tool-augmented multimodal agents== and generative AI safety.

## Results

- The survey documented a clear paradigm shift from CLIP/T5-based methods to LLM-dominant approaches, which enable more controllable and interactive multimodal content creation.
- It elucidated how LLMs fulfill diverse, specialized roles (e.g., planners, synthesizers, backbones) that enhance generation and editing across images, videos, 3D, and audio.
- The research highlighted the critical emergence of LLM-orchestrated multimodal agents and underscored the increasing importance of generative AI safety concerns, including bias, copyright, and fabricated content.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Language Models (LLMs) are fundamentally shifting multimodal generation and editing from basic conditioning to advanced prompt understanding, reasoning, and interactive capabilities.
> - LLMs adopt diverse and specialized roles across modalities, acting as layout planners for images, temporal prompt generators for videos, semantic guides for 3D content, and coordinated backbones for audio.
> - The rise of LLMs as orchestrators for tool-augmented multimodal agents represents a new paradigm for complex, interactive content creation, further extending their utility beyond text.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Artificial Intelligence for Generative Content (AIGC) has seen explosive growth, particularly with the advent of Large Language Models (LLMs). This survey positions itself at the cutting edge of this landscape by focusing specifically on the intersection of LLMs with *multimodal generation and editing*.

Prior to this work, existing surveys on Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) predominantly concentrated on *multimodal understanding*. The authors explicitly identify this as a gap, stating their unique contribution: to "elaborate on multimodal generation and editing across various domains, comprising image, video, 3D, and audio." This differentiation is crucial, as the capabilities of LLMs for generating new content (rather than just interpreting existing content) in non-textual modalities represent a significant leap towards more flexible and human-like AI systems.

The survey contextualizes its work by tracing the historical progression of generative models:
- **Early Monomodal Generation:** Initially, research focused on generating single modalities (e.g., text generation with GPT, image generation with GANs/Diffusion Models).
- **CLIP/T5-based Open-Domain Generation:** The emergence of models like CLIP and T5 enabled open-domain text-guided generation for images (e.g., DALL-E, LDM), videos (e.g., VideoCrafter), and 3D (e.g., CLIP-NeRF) by aligning text and other modalities. This marked a shift from narrow-domain generation to more versatile, text-conditioned synthesis.
- **LLM-Enhanced Multimodal Generation:** The most recent paradigm, and the focus of this survey, is the integration of powerful LLMs to *enhance* the generation process. This goes beyond simple conditioning, allowing for more complex prompt understanding, interactive editing, planning, and unified multimodal systems. The introduction of models like OpenAI's Sora, despite its limitations in multimodal perception and multi-modal output, serves as a recent example highlighting the potential for AI to simulate real-world scenes, which further motivates the need for comprehensive multimodal generation capabilities.

By systematically reviewing LLM-augmented generative capabilities across diverse modalities, the paper aims to provide a "systematic and insightful overview," thus promoting further development in AIGC and, ultimately, contributing to the realization of "world models" – AI systems capable of perceiving and interacting with the world using multimodal information. This work effectively bridges the advancements in core LLM technology with the needs and challenges of robust multimodal content creation, marking a pivotal moment in AI research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this survey stems from the remarkable advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) and the subsequent "growing interest in combining LLMs with multimodal learning." The authors specifically pinpoint a critical gap in existing literature: while previous surveys on Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) largely concentrated on multimodal *understanding*, there was a distinct lack of a comprehensive overview dedicated to multimodal *generation and editing*.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of this survey are:
1. **Systematic Review of LLM-Augmented Multimodal Generation and Editing:** To present the first systematic review of how LLMs are applied to the generation and editing of multiple modalities, including images, videos, 3D content, and audio. This involves categorizing existing approaches and highlighting milestone works.
2. **Comparative Analysis of Generative Techniques:** To discuss the evolution of generative techniques by offering a clear comparative analysis between the pre-LLM era (primarily CLIP/T5-based methods) and the post-LLM era, thereby illustrating the progression and refinement brought about by LLMs.
3. **Summarize Various Roles of LLMs:** To exhaustively investigate and summarize the diverse roles that LLMs play within the generation or editing pipeline for each specific modality. These roles can range from evaluators and labelers to instruction processors, planners, providers of semantic guidance, or even serving as central backbone architectures.
4. **Investigate Technical Components and Datasets:** To delve into the critical technical components underpinning these LLM-based multimodal generation methods and to summarize the multimodal datasets that are utilized in these studies.
5. **Explore Tool-Augmented Multimodal Agents:** To specifically investigate tool-augmented multimodal agents, which leverage LLMs to orchestrate existing generative models for advanced human-computer interaction, enabling the LLM to comprehend and generate non-textual content.
6. **Discuss Generative AI Safety:** To address the crucial and emerging field of generative AI safety, examining challenges related to toxic/biased content, copyright, and fabricated content, and discussing advancements in mitigation strategies.
7. **Identify Emerging Applications and Future Prospects:** To discuss current emerging applications of LLM-augmented multimodal generation and to explore potential future directions, with a particular emphasis on advancing Artificial Intelligence for Generative Content (AIGC) and contributing to the development of robust "world models."

In essence, the authors are motivated by the transformative potential of LLMs to create and modify diverse content, aiming to provide a foundational resource for researchers navigating this rapidly expanding and complex interdisciplinary field.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopt a systematic and structured approach to survey the rapidly evolving landscape of LLM-augmented multimodal generation and editing. Their methodology can be broken down into several key components:

1. **Scope Definition:** The survey clearly defines its scope, focusing on the generation and editing of specific modalities: images, videos, 3D content, and audio (including sounds, music, and speech). It explicitly excludes pure text generation and unconditional generation, concentrating on *language-based* generation and editing driven by LLMs. This focused scope allows for in-depth analysis of the designated areas.

2. **Categorization of Methods:** A central aspect of their approach is the categorization of methods. For each modality, the authors broadly divide approaches into:
 - **CLIP/T5-based methods:** Representing the "pre-LLM era" where multimodal generation was primarily guided by large-scale contrastive pre-trained models like CLIP or powerful language encoders like T5.
 - **LLM-based methods:** Highlighting the advancements brought by integrating Large Language Models. This comparative structure effectively illustrates the unique contributions and improvements facilitated by LLMs.

3. **Analysis of LLM Roles:** The survey deeply investigates the "various roles of LLMs" within multimodal generation pipelines. These roles are broadly summarized as: evaluator, labeller, instruction processor, planner, semantic guidance provider, or as backbone architectures. For each modality, the authors elaborate on how LLMs manifest in these specific functions (e.g., for image generation, LLMs act as layout planners, prompt synthesizers, or quality evaluators; for video, they serve as backbones, planners, or temporal prompt generators).

4. **Identification of Technical Components:** Beyond high-level roles, the authors dissect the "critical technical components" behind these methods. This includes discussing different types of generative models (GANs, VAEs, Flow-based models, Diffusion Models, Autoregressive Models), multimodal alignment models (CLIP, CLAP, ImageBind), and the architectural specifics of LLMs and Multimodal LLMs (MLLMs), such as modality-specific encoders, input/output projectors, and training paradigms.

5. **Dataset Review:** A comprehensive review of the multimodal datasets pertinent to each modality (image-language, video-language, audio-language, 3D-language) is provided. This highlights the data-driven nature of these advancements and the resources available for further research.

6. **Specialized Sections:** The survey dedicates separate sections to crucial emerging topics:
 - **Tool-Augmented Multimodal Agents:** This section explores how LLMs act as intelligent controllers, orchestrating various external generative models (e.g., image generation tools, video editing tools) to achieve complex, interactive multimodal tasks. Methods are further categorized into "training-free" and "instruction-tuning" approaches.
 - **Generative AI Safety:** This addresses the ethical and security implications, discussing attacks (optimization-based, prompt-level, data poisoning) and defense strategies (detection, alignment, watermarking), along with relevant safety datasets.
 - **Applications and Future Prospects:** This section provides a forward-looking perspective, outlining current real-world applications and future research directions (e.g., high-resolution generation, long-term sequence generation, fine-grained control, unified training, efficient strategies, ethical safety), culminating in a vision for "world models."

By systematically breaking down the field, categorizing approaches, analyzing technical details, and addressing both current applications and future challenges, the authors provide a structured and insightful overview that aims to advance the development of AIGC.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey consolidates a wealth of research, presenting several main findings and key advancements in LLM-augmented multimodal generation and editing:

1. **Shift from CLIP/T5 to LLM Dominance:** The survey clearly illustrates a paradigm shift from earlier CLIP/T5-based multimodal generation (which primarily focused on aligning modalities for open-domain generation) to LLM-based approaches. LLMs offer superior prompt understanding, reasoning capabilities, and interactive potential, pushing the boundaries beyond simple text conditioning.

2. **Diverse Roles of LLMs Across Modalities:**
 - **Image Generation and Editing:** LLMs are instrumental in creating unified interfaces for simultaneous understanding and generation. They excel as **layout planners** (generating bounding boxes, scene graphs), **prompt synthesizers and refiners** (expanding user prompts into detailed descriptions), and **quality evaluators** (providing feedback for iterative refinement). This enables more controllable, interactive, and high-fidelity image synthesis and editing.
 - **Video Generation and Editing:** LLMs serve as **unified backbones** for joint multimodal generation, as **video layout planners** (sequentially generating object bounding boxes or dynamic scene graphs for temporal consistency), and as **temporal prompt generators** (decomposing high-level narratives into frame-by-frame descriptions for coherent long-term video sequences). For editing, LLMs assist in generating synthetic training data for instruction-tuned video editing models.
 - **3D Generation and Editing:** LLMs act as a **bridge between users and 3D assets**, enabling more intuitive interaction. They can translate natural language instructions into **Blender code** or directly guide the generation of 3D representations (e.g., human motion parameters). They assist in understanding and iteratively refining 3D content, moving beyond the semantic-level guidance of CLIP.
 - **Audio Generation, Understanding, and Editing:** LLMs are increasingly serving as **coordinated backbones** for multimodal audio systems (general sounds, music, speech), **conditioners** for specific tasks, **labelers** for augmenting audio datasets with detailed captions, and **agents** for interactive generation and editing. LLM-inspired architectures also lead to advancements in discrete audio token modeling for high-quality audio synthesis.

3. **Emergence of Tool-Augmented Multimodal Agents:** A significant finding is the rise of LLMs as central *controllers* or *orchestrators* for diverse external multimodal tools. These agents (e.g., HuggingGPT, Visual ChatGPT, ControlLLM) perform task planning, model selection, and response generation, enabling LLMs to execute complex tasks involving various generative models across image, video, and audio modalities. These agents are categorized into "training-free" (relying on prompt engineering) and "instruction-tuning" (fine-tuning LLMs on instruction datasets) methods.

4. **Generative AI Safety as a Critical Concern:** The survey highlights that advancements in generative AI necessitate a strong focus on safety. Key issues include mitigating biased/toxic content, safeguarding copyright (via data attribution and watermarking), and addressing fabricated content (Deepfakes). Research on adversarial attacks (optimization-based, prompt-level, data poisoning) and defensive mechanisms (detection, alignment algorithms like PPO/DPO) is crucial. Datasets like SafetyBench and GOAT-Bench are being developed to evaluate these safety aspects.

5. **Practical Applications and Future Trajectory:** The widespread adoption of LLM-augmented multimodal generation is evident in commercial tools (Midjourney, DALL-E 3, Sora, Pika, Suno AI, Luma AI). These applications are transforming creative industries, from digital art and film production to music composition and 3D design. The survey concludes that these advancements are collectively pushing towards the realization of highly capable "world models" that can seamlessly perceive, understand, and interact with the physical world across all modalities.

In summary, the main findings demonstrate that LLMs are not just enhancing, but fundamentally reshaping multimodal generation and editing, offering unprecedented levels of control, interactivity, and realism, while also bringing to light critical safety considerations that require ongoing research.
