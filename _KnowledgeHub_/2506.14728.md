# AgentDistill: Training-Free Agent Distillation with Generalizable MCP Boxes

> [!summary] Summary
> AgentDistill presents a training-free framework for distilling advanced problem-solving capabilities from large language model (LLM) agents into smaller, more efficient student LLMs. It achieves this by having a teacher agent generate reusable Model–Context–Protocols (MCPs) which are then consolidated into an 'MCP-Box' that student agents directly leverage for tool-use, leading to substantial performance gains and enabling them to perform comparably to larger, more expensive systems.

## Problem

- Large language models (LLMs) used in agents incur substantial inference costs, limiting their widespread and continuous deployment.
- Existing agent distillation methods often require computationally expensive fine-tuning on full teacher trajectories and struggle to generalize to novel environments or tasks.
- Current distillation approaches frequently overlook inherent capability differences between teacher and student models, hindering effective knowledge transfer for adaptive problem-solving.

## Method

- A training-free agent distillation framework called ==AgentDistill== is introduced, centered on ==Model–Context–Protocols (MCPs)== generated by a teacher agent.
- The teacher agent identifies subtasks requiring tool-use and generates structured, self-contained MCPs from successful reasoning trajectories.
- These collected MCPs are then abstracted, clustered by functionality, and consolidated into a compact, parameterized =='MCP-Box'== by a high-capacity instruction-tuned LLM, which student agents directly utilize during inference.

## Results

- Student agents demonstrated high MCP-Box calling rates (**24.3%** to **100%**) across benchmarks, confirming broad applicability and reuse of distilled MCPs without additional training.
- AgentDistill led to substantial performance improvements for student agents, including gains of **+7.0%** on PathVQA for GPT-3.5-turbo and **+48.4%** on Game of 24 for GPT-3.5-turbo.
- Distilled student agents achieved performance comparable to or sometimes surpassing teacher agents and strong retrieval-based baselines like OctoTools with GPT-4o on tasks like PathVQA.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Teacher-generated Model–Context–Protocols (MCPs) can effectively encapsulate generalizable and reusable tool-use logic from complex tasks.
> - Distilling knowledge into structured, parameterized MCPs and packaging them into an 'MCP-Box' allows for a training-free transfer of sophisticated capabilities.
> - Small language models can achieve performance comparable to larger LLMs by leveraging a pre-validated, pre-packaged library of functional tools, without needing to learn tool implementations or elaborate reasoning chains through fine-tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned at the intersection of two critical and rapidly evolving fields: **knowledge distillation** and **LLM-based agents**.

**Knowledge Distillation (KD)**, traditionally, involves transferring knowledge from a larger, more complex "teacher" model to a smaller, more efficient "student" model. Early methods focused on aligning output logits (Hinton et al., 2015), while subsequent work extended to matching hidden features, attention patterns, and architecturally aware objectives. A significant recent advancement is **Chain-of-Thought Distillation (CoTD)**, which aims to teach smaller models to replicate the step-by-step reasoning processes (rationales) of larger LLMs, often by fine-tuning on teacher-generated reasoning chains. **In-Context Learning Distillation (ICLD)**, another variant, focuses on internalizing few-shot reasoning capabilities.

The rise of **LLM-based agents** marks a paradigm shift beyond simple text generation. These agents integrate LLMs with planning, memory, and tool-use capabilities, allowing them to interact with environments and solve complex, multi-step tasks. Distilling these agents is a nascent but critical challenge. Prior work in **agent distillation** has primarily explored:
- **Trajectory Distillation:** Training student agents to imitate full reasoning-action trajectories (e.g., Structured Agent Distillation (SAD)) or step-by-step tool usage from teacher agents. While effective for specific executions, these methods are computationally expensive and struggle with generalization to novel environments because students passively replicate fixed sequences.
- **Structure Distillation:** Guiding students with abstracted teacher strategies, such as subgoal sequences or interaction graphs (e.g., MAGDi, Sub-goal Distillation). These are more efficient but can overlook critical details related to model capabilities or tool usage nuances.
- **Action Policy Distillation:** Transferring language-based reasoning into lightweight, non-linguistic controllers for direct action execution.

"AGENTDISTILL" differentiates itself significantly from these existing approaches by introducing a **training-free** framework for agent distillation. Instead of fine-tuning students on trajectories or abstract structures, it leverages the teacher agent's ability to autonomously generate **Model–Context–Protocols (MCPs)**. MCPs, introduced by Anthropic, are a standardized, open protocol for providing context to LLMs, often encapsulating self-contained, reusable tool-use logic.

This work builds upon the concept of MCPs as a foundational element for secure and generalizable agent systems, as noted in related works like MCP Landscape, MCIP, and Alita. By focusing on distilling these modular MCPs into a "MCP-Box" that is *directly integrated* into student agents at inference time, AgentDistill bypasses the costly fine-tuning process inherent in most distillation methods. This unique "training-free" and "direct reuse" approach addresses the limitations of high computational cost and poor generalization prevalent in existing agent distillation techniques. It bridges the gap between the sophisticated problem-solving of large, expensive LLM agents and the efficiency requirements of small, deployable models, offering a scalable solution for building intelligent agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of AgentDistill is to develop a novel, efficient, and scalable framework for distilling the capabilities of large language model (LLM)-based teacher agents into smaller, more cost-effective student agents. The overarching motivation stems from the limitations of current LLM-based agents and existing distillation techniques:

1. **High Computational Cost of Large LLMs:** While large LLMs (like GPT-4o, Claude-Sonnet-4) are powerful, their inference costs are substantial, limiting their widespread and continuous deployment, especially in real-world applications requiring frequent interactions.
2. **Limitations of Existing Agent Distillation Methods:**
 - **Costly Training:** Most methods, particularly trajectory-level distillation, rely on replaying full teacher trajectories or imitating step-by-step tool usage, which involves extensive data collection and computationally intensive fine-tuning of student models. This negates some of the cost benefits of using smaller models.
 - **Poor Generalization:** Students trained on fixed trajectories often struggle to dynamically plan and act in novel environments or adapt to unseen tasks, as they learn to passively replicate rather than understand and apply underlying problem-solving logic.
 - **Overlooking Model Differences:** Structured distillation methods, while more efficient, may not account for the inherent capability differences or knowledge boundaries between teacher and student models.
3. **Need for Dynamic and Adaptive Agents:** Real-world scenarios demand agents that can robustly handle diverse problems, dynamically plan, and effectively utilize tools. Current distillation methods often fall short in endowing student agents with this level of adaptability.

To address these challenges, AgentDistill proposes to:
- **Achieve Training-Free Distillation:** Eliminate the need for costly fine-tuning or gradient updates for the student agent, making the distillation process highly cost-efficient and deployable.
- **Enable Scalable Knowledge Transfer:** Leverage the teacher agent's capacity to generate self-contained, reusable, and generalizable Model–Context–Protocols (MCPs) that encapsulate sophisticated problem-solving skills.
- **Enhance Student Agent Generalization:** By directly integrating these distilled MCPs (as "MCP boxes"), empower student agents with smaller LLMs to inherit advanced, transferable problem-solving skills and generalize their capabilities across diverse domains and novel problems with minimal supervision.
- **Bridge the Capability Gap:** Allow student agents to achieve performance comparable to or even surpassing advanced systems backed by stronger LLMs (e.g., OctoTools with GPT-4o), thereby democratizing access to complex agentic behaviors.

In essence, the motivation is to make advanced agentic capabilities more accessible and practical by drastically reducing the training and inference overhead, enabling small language models to perform complex tasks through efficient, direct knowledge transfer in a modular and generalizable fashion.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AgentDistill introduces a novel, training-free framework for agent distillation, centered around the concept of Model–Context–Protocols (MCPs). The methodology involves three main phases: MCP Creation by the teacher agent, MCP-Box Construction, and Student Inference with the MCP-Box.

#### 3.1. Agent Structure

The framework utilizes distinct teacher and student agent architectures:

- **Teacher Agent:** This agent employs powerful large language models (LLMs) like Claude-Sonnet-4 and GPT-4o. It comprises:
 - **Manager Agent:** The central coordinator that decomposes tasks, identifies the need for external tools, and aggregates results.
 - **Basic Image Captioner:** Converts visual inputs into text descriptions, allowing text-only LLMs to process image information.
 - **MCP Creation Module:** This is the core component for distillation. It has four sections:
 - **MCP Brainstorming Section:** Generates initial conceptual plans for MCPs.
 - **Open-Source Searching Section:** Identifies relevant open-source resources.
 - **Script Generation Section:** Synthesizes plans and resources into executable scripts.
 - **Virtual Environment Execution Section:** Validates and executes these scripts in a controlled environment to ensure correctness and applicability.

- **Student Agent:** This agent uses compact, cost-effective small language models (sLMs) such as GPT-3.5-turbo, Qwen3-8B, and LLaMA3.1-8B. Its structure is streamlined:
 - **Manager Agent:** Coordinates task decomposition, tool utilization, and result aggregation.
 - **Basic Image Captioner:** Similar to the teacher, processes visual inputs.
 - **Crucially, it lacks the MCP Creation Module.** Instead, it directly utilizes the pre-constructed MCP-Box distilled from the teacher.

#### 3.2. MCP Creation (Teacher Phase)

When a teacher agent successfully solves an input `x_i` from the supervision dataset `D`, it generates a reasoning trajectory `τ_i`. During this process, the teacher (specifically the MCP Creation Module) identifies distinct subtasks that require tool-use and generates structured, self-contained MCPs (`MCP_i,j`). Only MCPs generated from *successful* teacher trajectories (`π_T(x_i) = y_i`) are collected into a temporary pool `L`, provided they are syntactically correct and executable. This pool `L` represents a rich but potentially noisy set of teacher-demonstrated tool-use strategies.

#### 3.3. MCP-Box Construction

The collected MCPs from `L` are then processed by a high-capacity instruction-tuned LLM (e.g., Claude-Sonnet-4) to form the compact and structured `MCP-Box (B)`. This process involves three key steps:

1. **Abstraction:** Each raw `MCP_i,j` is rewritten into a concise, task-agnostic, and parameterized format (`^MCP_i,j`). The LLM removes example-specific phrases while preserving generalizable tool-use strategies. It also identifies and configures up to three critical parameters for each MCP, enhancing its reusability.
2. **Clustering:** All abstracted `^MCP_i,j` are grouped by functionality using a code-level clustering prompt. The LLM assigns them to clusters (e.g., "image utils", "numeric analysis") based on shared application semantics.
3. **Consolidation:** Within each functional cluster `C_k`, the LLM consolidates all tool implementations into a single, general-purpose version (`MCP_final_k`). This involves parameter unification, proper validation, and documentation, resulting in a production-ready, FastMCP-compatible Python file.

The final `MCP-Box (B)` is a collection of these consolidated, parameterized MCPs, each with its functional label.

#### 3.4. Student Inference with the MCP-Box

During inference, the entire `MCP-Box (B)` is directly mounted into the student agent's tool interface, leveraging the SmolAgents framework. This means:
- **No Retrieval or Re-ranking:** Unlike some tool-use systems, the student agent doesn't need to dynamically retrieve or re-rank tools; they are all readily available.
- **Frozen Policy:** The student agent operates under a frozen policy, meaning it receives no gradient updates (`∇_θ π_S = 0`). All tool-use competence is derived from the pre-constructed MCP-Box.
- **High-Level Planning:** The student LLM still performs high-level planning – deciding *whether* to invoke a tool, *which* MCP to select from the box, and *how* to fill in its arguments (through text generation or function call templates). The execution environment provides the return value, updating the context.

This design ensures the student agent remains lightweight and inference-time-efficient, while effectively transferring complex tool-related task-solving capabilities from the teacher agent to the student agent through a modular, reusable library.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on biomedical (PathVQA, SLAKE) and mathematical (Game of 24) benchmarks consistently demonstrate the effectiveness of AgentDistill.

**Generalizability and Usage Frequency of Distilled MCPs (Table 1):**
- The teacher agent successfully generates a manageable number of unique MCPs per dataset (e.g., 9 for PathVQA, 13 for SLAKE, 1 for Game of 24, although the "Number of Distilled MCPs" in Table 1 might refer to distinct final consolidated MCPs rather than raw ones).
- Student agents show a high "MCP-Box Calling Rate" (percentage of test cases where at least one MCP was invoked), ranging from 24.3% (LLaMA3.1-8B on PathVQA) to 100% (all models on Game of 24, Qwen3-8B on SLAKE). This high usage rate confirms that the distilled MCPs are broadly applicable and consistently reused by student agents across diverse inputs, demonstrating their reusability and generalizability without additional training.

**Consistent Performance Improvements for Student Agents (Table 2):**
- **Substantial Gains:** AgentDistill leads to significant performance improvements across all tested student agents (GPT-3.5-turbo, Qwen3-8B, LLaMA3.1-8B) and datasets.
 - **PathVQA:** Improvements ranged from +2.3% (Qwen3-8B) to +7.0% (GPT-3.5-turbo).
 - **SLAKE:** More pronounced gains, with LLaMA3.1-8B seeing a +10.0% increase, GPT-3.5-turbo +7.3%, and Qwen3-8B +6.7%.
 - **Game of 24:** Exceptional improvements for models with weaker initial performance, such as GPT-3.5-turbo (+48.4%) and LLaMA3.1-8B (+42.3%). Qwen3-8B, which already performed well, still gained +7.0%.
- These results highlight that the MCP-Box effectively enhances the task-solving ability of small language models, particularly in symbolic reasoning tasks where structured arithmetic operations (like Game of 24) benefit significantly from the constrained and correct tool-calling space provided by the MCPs.

**Narrowing the Gap with Teacher Agents and Strong Baselines (Table 3 & Figure 2):**
- **Comparable to Teacher:** After distillation, the average performance of student agents often approaches or even surpasses the teacher agent (Claude 4 + GPT-4o). For example, on PathVQA, student agents averaged 52.7% accuracy, slightly outperforming the teacher (52%). On SLAKE, they reached 65.1%, close to the teacher's 66%.
- **Outperforming Retrieval-Based Systems:** Student agents equipped with the MCP-Box frequently outperform or match strong retrieval-based baselines like OctoTools (GPT-4o) and OctoTools with pre-defined tools (sLMs). On PathVQA, distilled students (52.7%) outperformed both OctoTools (GPT-4o at 49%) and Agents with Pre-defined Tools (sLMs at 51.3%). On SLAKE, they were slightly below the teacher but above both OctoTools baselines. On Game of 24, while the teacher (99%) significantly outclassed others, the distilled students (75.5% average) substantially improved over their pre-distillation state and OctoTools baselines, indicating that the MCP-Box provides a robust mechanism for task-specific performance.
- **Overall:** Figure 2 visually reinforces this, showing student agents after distillation achieving performance comparable to OctoTools (GPT-4o) and approaching the teacher agent, far surpassing their pre-distillation capabilities.

**Case Study: Brain MRI Analysis (Figure 5):**
- The case study illustrates how AgentDistill takes narrow, task-specific MCPs generated by the teacher (e.g., "detect bright areas," "analyze left hemisphere") and consolidates them into a single, generalizable, parameterized MCP template.
- This consolidated MCP can then support diverse configurations (e.g., different brain regions, analysis modes, thresholds) without requiring new code or retraining, showcasing the framework's ability to create highly reusable and adaptable tools from ad-hoc teacher traces.

In summary, AgentDistill successfully provides a training-free mechanism for small LLMs to acquire sophisticated tool-use capabilities, leading to substantial performance gains and enabling them to perform comparably to much larger and more expensive LLM-based systems, even outperforming some retrieval-based baselines. The core reason for its effectiveness lies in constraining the student's tool-calling space to a set of pre-validated, functional, and parameterized MCPs, which reduces generation complexity while maintaining the student's high-level planning capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AgentDistill represents a significant advancement in the field of LLM agent research, offering several profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Democratization of Advanced Agent Capabilities:** By enabling smaller, more cost-efficient language models to inherit sophisticated problem-solving skills without expensive fine-tuning, AgentDistill makes advanced agentic behaviors more accessible. This can lower the barrier to entry for developing and deploying intelligent agents in resource-constrained environments or for applications where high inference costs are prohibitive. It allows individuals and smaller organizations to leverage the power of agents that were previously exclusive to large-scale LLMs.

2. **Enhanced Efficiency and Scalability:** The "training-free" nature of AgentDistill is its most striking contribution. Eliminating gradient updates and extensive trajectory replay drastically reduces computational overhead, development time, and energy consumption associated with agent distillation. This makes the knowledge transfer process highly efficient and scalable, allowing for rapid deployment and iteration of agent systems across diverse tasks and domains.

3. **Improved Generalization and Adaptability:** The core mechanism of distilling Model–Context–Protocols (MCPs) into generalizable and reusable "MCP-Boxes" is crucial for adaptability. Unlike methods that merely imitate fixed trajectories, AgentDistill allows student agents to access a curated library of parameterized tool-use strategies. This enables students to dynamically select and configure tools for novel problems, significantly enhancing their generalization capabilities to unseen scenarios and different configurations, as demonstrated by the consistent improvements across diverse benchmarks.

4. **Modular and Interpretable Agent Design:** MCPs are structured, self-contained modules. By distilling these protocols, AgentDistill promotes a modular approach to agent design. This modularity can lead to more interpretable agent behaviors, as the logic encapsulated within each MCP is explicit and verifiable. This is vital for debugging, safety, and understanding how agents arrive at their solutions.

5. **Foundation for Future Agent Development:** The concept of directly injecting formalized task-solving protocols into agents can become a cornerstone for future agent development. It suggests a paradigm where expert knowledge (from teacher agents or human experts) can be encoded into reusable components that augment the capabilities of smaller, more nimble LLMs, rather than solely relying on end-to-end learning. This could pave the way for hybrid AI systems that combine the reasoning prowess of LLMs with specialized, pre-defined functional modules.

6. **Impact on Specific Domains:** The strong performance on biomedical (PathVQA, SLAKE) and mathematical (Game of 24) benchmarks highlights its immediate applicability. In medicine, it could lead to more affordable and deployable diagnostic or research assistants. In mathematics, it could power intelligent tutors or problem-solvers. The framework's ability to abstract and consolidate tool use for different domains is particularly impactful for creating domain-specific agents without requiring extensive bespoke development for each new task.

In conclusion, AgentDistill pushes the boundaries of agent distillation by offering a truly training-free, highly efficient, and generalizable solution. Its emphasis on structured protocol transfer rather than behavioral imitation addresses key challenges in building practical and scalable intelligent agents, potentially accelerating the real-world adoption of LLM-based agent technology across various industries and research fields.
