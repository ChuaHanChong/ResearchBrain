# Multi-modal Interactive Control of Robotic Arm based on Offline Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Socratic Models-ChatGLM is a modular robotic-arm control pipeline that swaps the online ChatGPT of prior Socratic-Models/Code-as-Policies work for a locally deployed ChatGLM2-6B. ViLD does open-vocabulary detection, ChatGLM2-6B writes pick-and-place code policies from the object list plus user text, CLIPort executes them on a simulated UR5, reaching roughly 100% task completion and 80-90% performance scores.

## Problem

- Popular hosted language models charge continuously per API call, so repeated code-generation experiments for robotic manipulation become costly and usage-capped for researchers.
- Cloud-hosted models demand a stable network connection and secret keys, limiting freedom and flexibility for cross-device, embodied robotic interaction deployments.
- Prior Socratic-Models and code-generation control pipelines assume an online proprietary model, leaving open whether a small offline model can drive long-horizon manipulation.

## Method

- Offline deployment step: ==ChatGLM2-6B==, a 6.2-billion-parameter bilingual GLM runnable at ==INT4== quantization on 6GB cards, served through ==FastChat== plus ==Autogen== as OpenAI-compatible local APIs.
- **Socratic Models-ChatGLM** pipeline chains three pretrained models: ==ViLD== open-vocabulary detection for the object list, ChatGLM2-6B for hierarchical code-policy generation, ==CLIPort== pick-place heatmaps for execution.
- Prompt-based grasping template conditions the model on scene object lists plus user text, emitting pseudocode calls like robot.pick_and_place(block, bowl) under a 50-token generation cap.

## Results

- Three single-instruction multi-step tasks (sort blocks, move to corners, move to middle) all reached **100%** completion in **2min42s** to **3min16s**, scoring **88%**, **88%**, and **85%**.
- Continuous multi-task sequences of **2-4** chained instructions succeeded, with the color-matching task scoring **92%** and the follow-on corner-placement subtask **86%** at **100%** completion.
- Reported scores deduct for observed failure modes: ViLD confusing pink and yellow blocks, uniformly stacking blocks in one corner, and redundantly re-moving an already-centered block.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A quantized 6-billion-parameter local model suffices for template-constrained code-policy generation, suggesting proprietary-scale models are not required for structured manipulation prompting.
> - Modular chaining of frozen perception, language, and policy models keeps the language component swappable, so upgrading or localizing one stage needs no retraining elsewhere.
> - Errors concentrate in the perception stage and in under-specified instructions, so scored evaluation without baselines or repeated trials should be read as qualitative evidence only.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) into human-robot interaction and robotic manipulation represents a significant area of contemporary research. LLMs have demonstrated capabilities in natural speech conversation, text UI interfaces, and code policy generation, fostering the development of multi-modal AI paradigms for robotics. Existing approaches, such as those leveraging models like ChatGPT and Gemini, typically rely on online, proprietary LLM services. These online services often incur continuous payment requirements and necessitate stable network connectivity, which can limit their flexibility and freedom for deployment in diverse robotic interaction applications, particularly in cross-device or resource-constrained environments.

Previous works, such as "Socratic Models" and "Code as Policies," have explored using LLMs for zero-shot multimodal reasoning and generating programs for embodied control. "Socratic Models" utilize structured user dialogue as prompting between multiple pre-trained models for joint predictions in multi-modal robotic tasks. "Code as Policies" demonstrated that LLMs could generate hierarchical planning code to control robots based on natural language instructions.

This research addresses the limitations of reliance on online LLMs by proposing an approach that leverages an *offline, locally-deployed* open-source LLM. By deploying an LLM on local servers, this work aims to provide a more cost-effective and flexible alternative to existing methods, making LLM-driven robotic control more accessible and stable for continuous experimentation and deployment without recurring costs or network dependencies. The proposed "Socratic Models-ChatGLM" builds upon the conceptual framework of "Socratic Models" but differentiates itself by replacing the online LLM component with a self-deployed, open-source model, thereby contributing to the development of autonomous, locally-managed embodied AI systems for complex robotic tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and implement a multi-modal interactive control algorithm for robotic arms using locally deployed, open-source Large Language Models. This objective is driven by several key motivations:

- **Addressing Limitations of Online LLMs:** The most prominent motivation is to overcome the inherent limitations associated with popular online LLMs (e.g., ChatGPT, Gemini). These models typically require continuous payment for service usage and depend on stable internet connectivity. This reliance imposes financial burdens, restricts the freedom and flexibility for extensive experimentation, and limits deployment in environments lacking consistent network access or requiring privacy for sensitive robotic operations.
- **Enhancing Accessibility and Cost-Effectiveness:** By deploying an open-source LLM (ChatGLM2-6B) on local GPU servers, the research aims to significantly reduce the cost of continuous AI service usage. This approach eliminates the need for purchasing online API keys, thereby allowing for unlimited model calling for code generation and extensive experimentation without financial constraints. This enhances the accessibility of advanced LLM capabilities for robotics research and development.
- **Achieving Stable and Flexible Deployment:** Local deployment provides a more stable and controlled environment for LLM operation, removing dependency on external network conditions. This increases the reliability of the AI intelligent conversation system for robotic control, allowing for cross-device robotic interaction applications where network stability or access might be a concern.
- **Developing Embodied AI with Open-Source Models:** The work aims to demonstrate the feasibility and efficacy of designing and implementing creative embodied AI algorithms using open-sourced LLMs. This contributes to the broader goal of fostering an open-source ecosystem for advanced AI in robotics.
- **Enabling Complex Robotic Manipulation Tasks:** The motivation extends to demonstrating that an offline LLM-based system can effectively address complicated multi-modal text-image integrated multi-step and long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks. The goal is to show that such a system can generate appropriate hierarchical planning code and execute it to complete various interactive tasks in dynamic environments.
- **Adapting and Improving Existing Frameworks:** Inspired by the "Socratic Models" framework, the research aims to adapt this concept by integrating a locally-deployed LLM. This modification seeks to retain the multi-modal reasoning capabilities while addressing the practical deployment challenges of online-only solutions.

In essence, the core motivation is to democratize advanced LLM capabilities for robotic control by offering a stable, cost-effective, and flexible offline alternative, thereby enabling more robust and unconstrained research and application development in embodied AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology involves two primary steps: (1) Offline Large Language Model Deployment and (2) Development and Implementation of the "Socratic Models-ChatGLM" algorithm for robotic control.

**A. Offline Large Language Model Deployment**
The first step focuses on establishing a local, offline AI intelligent conversation system.
- **Model Selection:** The ChatGLM2-6B large language model was selected for deployment. This model is an open-source, Chinese and English bilingual chat LLM based on the General Language Model (GLM) architecture, possessing 6.2 billion parameters.
- **Hardware and Quantization:** A key aspect of its selection is its compatibility with consumer-grade graphics cards, requiring a minimum of 6GB video memory when utilizing INT4 quantization technology. This enables local deployment on accessible hardware.
- **Deployment Process:**
 1. The ChatGLM2-6B model and associated code were downloaded from platforms such as Github and Huggingface.
 2. Initial testing of user communication effect was performed by running `web_demo.py` and `cli_demo.py` Python scripts.
 3. Once the model's functionality was confirmed, OpenAI-compatible LLM APIs were deployed on local GPU servers. This was achieved using the Autogen framework and FastChat platform. This deployment enables users to directly call offline ChatGLM2-6B APIs from local terminal ports for tasks like dialogue and text prompt completion, without requiring specific API keys or internet connection. This establishes a local API service mimicking the functionality of online OpenAI services.

**B. Socratic Models-ChatGLM Algorithm**
After successful offline LLM deployment, the "Socratic Models-ChatGLM" algorithm was established using the UR5 robotic arm within PyBullet multi-modal interactive scenario environments. This algorithm integrates visual perception, language processing, and policy execution.

- **Modular Framework:** The "Socratic Models-ChatGLM" is a modular framework comprising three core sections, adapting the structure of original Socratic Models:
 1. **Large Language Model for Text Demand Interaction:** This component utilizes the locally-deployed ChatGLM2-6B for generating hierarchical planning code based on user text input and scenario descriptions.
 2. **Object Detection and Visual Reasoning Method:** The open-vocabulary ViLD (Vision and Language knowledge Distillation) model is employed for visual inference. ViLD detects objects on the table and returns a list of detected objects (e.g., "yellow block," "pink bowl"). This visual information is then transmitted to the LLM.
 3. **Code Policy Execution Model:** The CLIPort model is used for executing the generated step plans. CLIPort, which utilizes pick-place heatmaps, translates the robotic action code policies into physical movements of the UR5 robotic arm.

- **Workflow:** The overall pipeline for "Socratic Models-ChatGLM" operates as follows:
 1. **User Input:** Users provide interactive text commands (e.g., "Pick the gray block and place it on the pink bowl") describing the desired task in a multi-modal environment (e.g., 4 blocks, 3 bowls).
 2. **Visual Perception:** The ViLD object detection model performs visual inference on the current environment, identifying and listing detected objects.
 3. **Code Generation:** The locally-deployed ChatGLM2-6B, accessed via its OpenAI-compatible APIs (FastChat/Autogen), receives the user's text instruction, the scenario description from ViLD, and predefined robotic arm grasping code prompts (as illustrated in Fig. 2 of the paper). Based on this combined input, ChatGLM2-6B generates a matching hierarchical planning code policy. The generated code can be in natural language or pseudocode templates (e.g., `robot.pick and place("gray block", "pink bowl")`). The `max_tokens` parameter for ChatGLM2-6B is set to 50, which may lead to partial code generation followed by supplementary text.
 4. **Policy Execution:** The generated code policy is then directly passed to the CLIPort model. CLIPort processes this step plan (e.g., "Pick the gray block and place it on the pink bowl") and executes the corresponding actions with the UR5 robotic arm.
 5. **Continuous Operation:** For continuous multi-task scenarios, this cycle can repeat, with ViLD providing updated visual reasoning for the current state after each action, enabling the LLM to generate subsequent code policies.

This modular design allows for structured interaction between various pre-trained models, with the locally-deployed ChatGLM2-6B serving as the central intelligence for language-conditioned code generation, driving the robotic arm to complete multi-modal interactive tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted extensive experiments using the PyBullet platform with diverse multi-modal scenarios involving various colors and numbers of blocks and bowls. The performance of "Socratic Models-ChatGLM" was evaluated on two types of manipulation tasks: (a) Single instruction multi-step tasks and (b) Continuous combination multiple tasks. Performance scores were based on control code policy accuracy, task completion time, absence of errors/redundant actions, and consistency between input demand and final execution.

**A. Single Instruction Multi-step Tasks**
Three distinct single instruction multi-step tasks were tested: "Sort the blocks in the way you think it best fits," "Move the blocks to the corners," and "Move the blocks in the middle."

- **Task A: "Sort the blocks in the way you think it best fits" (4 bowls, 4 blocks)**
 - **Outcome:** The ChatGLM2-6B model successfully generated a code policy that resulted in placing pink, brown, and blue blocks into the pink bowl, and the yellow block into the yellow bowl.
 - **Performance:** Completed in 2 minutes 42 seconds with a 100% task completion rate and an 88% performance score. The 12% deduction in performance was attributed to minor recognition errors by the ViLD visual model regarding pink and yellow blocks.
- **Task B: "Move the blocks to the corners" (4 bowls, 4 blocks)**
 - **Outcome:** ChatGLM2-6B generated code enabling the UR5 robotic arm to place purple, brown, green, and blue blocks in the upper left corner of the desktop.
 - **Performance:** Completed in 3 minutes 16 seconds with a 100% task completion rate and an 88% performance score. The performance reduction was due to a lack of flexibility, as the robot placed all blocks uniformly in one corner instead of distributing them to different corners as might be implicitly intended by "corners."
- **Task C: "Move the blocks in the middle"**
 - **Outcome:** The ChatGLM2-6B generated code instructed the UR5 robotic arm to place the purple block and yellow block in the middle of the table.
 - **Performance:** Completed in 3 minutes 11 seconds with a 100% task completion rate and an 85% performance score. The lower score was attributed to redundant actions, specifically moving a yellow block that was already in the middle back to a left bowl during execution.

**Overall for Single Instruction Multi-step Tasks:** The "Socratic Models–ChatGLM" algorithm consistently achieved task completion rates of 100% and performance scores ranging from 85% to 88%. The system demonstrated the ability to intelligently and quickly generate multi-step code policies for novel scenarios and execute them efficiently. The findings suggest that performance could be further improved by optimizing LLM code prompts or parameters like `max_tokens` and `temperature`.

**B. Continuous Combination Multiple Tasks**
Experiments also demonstrated the system's capability to handle continuous multi-task scenarios involving 2, 3, or 4 consecutive missions.

- **Task A (3 consecutive tasks):** "Pick the gray block and place it on the pink bowl," "Move the yellow colored block to the bottom side," "Put all the blocks to the top right corner."
 - **Outcome:** The system successfully completed all three sub-tasks sequentially. Task A-2 was performed in 1 minute 23 seconds with an 82% performance score. Task A-3 achieved 100% completion and an 86% performance score. This demonstrated the system's ability to execute code strategies efficiently for multi-step long-horizon tasks.
- **Task B (2 consecutive tasks):** "Group the pink objects together," "Match the blocks with similar-colored bowls."
 - **Outcome:** The system generated accurate code policies and completed the missions with a 100% completion rate and a 92% performance score.
- **Task C (2 consecutive tasks):** "Move the blocks to the bottom side," "Sort all the blocks into their matching color bowls."
 - **Outcome:** This task highlighted the system's ability to handle more flexible manipulation tasks, benefiting from ViLD's detection performance.
- **Task D (4 consecutive long-horizon tasks):** "Move the gray block left about 3cm," "Place the yellow block bottom down about 4cm," "Stack the blocks on the pink bowl as possible," "Pick the cyan block to the up right corner."
 - **Outcome:** Task D-1 and D-2 demonstrated that the offline LLM could achieve performance comparable to online models (like OpenAI GPT-4 in "Code as Policies") for precise block movements at specified distances and orientations. The system also successfully completed block stacking and object placing.

**Overall for Continuous Combination Multiple Tasks:** The "Socratic Models-ChatGLM" demonstrated strong capabilities in handling long-horizon, complex tasks. The flexibility and unlimited reusability offered by the locally deployed ChatGLM allowed for extensive experimentation and revealed potential for addressing intricate multi-modal text-image multi-step tasks, outperforming traditional Socratic Models in this aspect due to the deployment strategy. Performance scores for these tasks ranged up to 92%.

In summary, the locally-deployed ChatGLM2-6B, integrated within the "Socratic Models-ChatGLM" framework, consistently generated effective robot code action policies, leading to successful execution of diverse single-instruction multi-step and complex continuous multi-modal long-horizon manipulation tasks. The system achieved performance scores of 80%-90% and approximately 100% task completion rates, validating the approach of using offline LLMs for embodied interactions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of human-robot interaction and embodied AI, particularly through its focus on utilizing offline Large Language Models (LLMs) for robotic control. The key significance and potential impacts are:

- **Increased Accessibility and Cost-Effectiveness:** By successfully deploying an open-source LLM like ChatGLM2-6B on local GPU servers with OpenAI-compatible APIs, the research provides a cost-effective alternative to proprietary online LLMs. This eliminates continuous payment for API usage, making advanced LLM capabilities more accessible to a broader range of researchers, developers, and educational institutions, particularly those with limited budgets or privacy concerns. This democratizes access to sophisticated AI for robotics.
- **Enhanced Stability and Flexibility in Deployment:** The offline nature of the deployed LLM removes dependencies on stable internet connectivity and external server availability. This allows for greater flexibility in deploying robotic systems in diverse environments, including remote locations, industrial settings with restricted network access, or cross-device applications where consistent online access cannot be guaranteed. The local deployment ensures a more stable and controlled operational environment for the AI agent.
- **Promotion of Open-Source Embodied AI Development:** The methodology demonstrates a viable pathway for designing and implementing embodied AI algorithms using open-source tools and models. This fosters the growth of an open-source ecosystem in robotics, enabling greater transparency, reproducibility, and collaborative development of advanced robotic systems.
- **Robustness in Multi-modal Robotic Manipulation:** The "Socratic Models-ChatGLM" framework effectively integrates visual perception (ViLD), language understanding (ChatGLM2-6B), and robotic policy execution (CLIPort). This integration enables the system to intelligently interpret complex natural language commands, visually reason about dynamic environments, and generate appropriate multi-step code policies for a wide array of manipulation tasks, including object sorting, placement, color matching, and stacking. The demonstrated ability to handle continuous, long-horizon tasks is particularly noteworthy.
- **Foundation for Future Research and Applications:** The work lays a foundation for future research into locally-controlled, intelligent robotic systems. Potential impacts include:
 - **Development of privacy-preserving robots:** Operations can be conducted entirely on-device without data being sent to external servers.
 - **Edge AI applications:** Facilitating the deployment of intelligent robots in environments with limited bandwidth or high latency.
 - **Personalized robotics:** Enabling users to train and customize LLM-driven robot behaviors locally without external costs.
 - **Addressing complex, real-world tasks:** The demonstrated ability to handle multi-step, long-horizon tasks suggests potential for application in manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, and assistive robotics, where complex sequential actions are common.

In conclusion, this research significantly advances the practicality and accessibility of LLM-driven robotic control by offering a robust, locally deployable, and cost-efficient solution, thereby broadening the scope for future innovation in embodied AI.
