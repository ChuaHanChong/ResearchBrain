# Downscaling Intelligence: Exploring Perception and Reasoning Bottlenecks in Small Multimodal Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Stanford University systematically investigate how reducing large language model capacity affects small multimodal models, pinpointing perception and reasoning as key bottlenecks. They introduce EXTRACT+THINK, a two-stage framework that achieves up to 19.5% higher performance on the MMStar benchmark while utilizing 73% fewer visual training samples compared to existing smaller models.

## Problem

- Large multimodal models (MLLMs) are computationally demanding, restricting their deployment in resource-limited settings like edge devices.
- A systematic understanding of how MLLM capabilities, specifically perception and reasoning, degrade when the underlying large language model (LLM) capacity is reduced is lacking.
- Inconsistent findings in previous studies on the impact of LLM size on perceptual abilities necessitate a principled investigation into these effects.

## Method

- Conducted a systematic empirical study by downscaling Qwen3 series LLMs in multimodal models and evaluating performance across a diverse set of visual tasks.
- Utilized a decoupled framework, adapting the ==Prism method==, to independently analyze the impact of LLM downscaling on perception and reasoning modules.
- Developed ==EXTRACT+THINK==, a two-stage framework employing =="Visual Extraction Tuning"== to enhance perception and "Chain-of-Thought reasoning" to improve the LLM's reasoning capabilities.

## Results

- The EXTRACT+THINK framework achieved state-of-the-art efficiency, with its smaller variant surpassing LLaVA-OneVision-0.5B by up to **19.5%** on MMStar while using **73%** fewer visual training samples.
- Visual extraction tuning significantly enhanced perceptual performance, yielding up to **+5.2** accuracy for the 0.6B perception module on in-domain data and **+4.6** on the MMStar benchmark compared to a captioning baseline.
- Chain-of-Thought reasoning effectively boosted the reasoning module's capabilities, leading to substantial improvements in out-of-domain performance across various LLM scales.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Performance degradation from LLM downscaling is most pronounced in visually demanding tasks, suggesting that core vision-centric capabilities are disproportionately affected.
> - The perception module's capacity, alongside the reasoning module's, represents a critical bottleneck in smaller multimodal models, challenging prior assumptions about perception's robustness to LLM scale.
> - Training the perception module with visual extraction tuning, which emphasizes fine-grained, question-relevant detail extraction, significantly improves performance and data efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) has led to significant advancements in visual understanding and reasoning. Much of the existing research in this area has focused on scaling up these models, investigating how increasing parameters and training data enhances their capabilities. However, a parallel and pressing demand exists for smaller, more efficient models suitable for resource-constrained environments and on-device applications. While several compact architectures have been developed to address this need, the specific consequences of downscaling intelligence in these models remain largely underexplored.

Prior work on small MLLMs, such as Moondream, Phi-3-Vision, SmolVLM, MiniCPM, and compact variants of models like Gemma, DeepSeek-VL, Qwen-VL, LLaVA-OneVision, and InternVL, has demonstrated general capabilities. However, these studies have not consistently identified their failure modes, particularly concerning visual capabilities and perception. Some studies suggest that the size of the large language model (LLM) backbone has minimal effect on perception, while others indicate that perception-heavy tasks, such as Optical Character Recognition (OCR) and Chart Visual Question Answering (VQA), are highly sensitive to model size. These discrepancies highlight a gap in the understanding of how LLM downscaling affects visual capabilities and the underlying mechanisms of performance degradation.

Furthermore, existing research on MLLM failures often focuses on larger, state-of-the-art models, revealing shortcomings in perceptual and visual reasoning tasks. For instance, studies have identified difficulties with fundamental perceptual tasks, visual spatial planning, and abstract reasoning puzzles. These failures are frequently attributed to inadequate utilization of visual information from the vision encoder or insufficient exposure to relevant visual training data. However, these analyses typically do not specifically investigate the impact of *LLM downscaling* on these failure modes.

This paper addresses these gaps by systematically investigating the impact of reducing LLM size within multimodal systems. It aims to provide a principled characterization of downscaling effects, differentiate between the degradation of perceptual and reasoning abilities, and propose targeted solutions specifically for small, efficient MLLMs. By doing so, it contributes to a more nuanced understanding of the trade-offs involved in creating compact, high-performing multimodal models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the widespread practical demand for smaller, more efficient MLLMs that can operate effectively in resource-constrained environments, such as on-device applications. Despite the advancements driven by scaling up MLLMs, the implications of reducing the capacity of their underlying LLM components—referred to as "downscaling intelligence"—are not well understood. Specifically, it is unclear which multimodal capabilities degrade most significantly when an MLLM is built upon a smaller LLM backbone, and the underlying reasons for such degradation.

The research pursued three key objectives:
1. **Understand Practical Limitations**: The initial objective was to systematically investigate how reducing the size of the language model backbone impacts the overall multimodal performance across a diverse range of visual instruction tuning tasks. This aimed to characterize the practical limitations faced by small multimodal models when acting as general visual assistants.
2. **Uncover Mechanisms Behind Failures**: Following the initial observation of performance drops, a critical objective was to pinpoint the specific mechanisms responsible for these failures. The authors sought to determine whether the observed decline in visual capabilities under LLM downscaling was primarily due to a degradation in visual reasoning abilities, or if it also reflected a more fundamental impairment in the model's ability to interpret and extract visual information (perception). This involved a decoupled analysis of perception and reasoning.
3. **Develop Targeted Solutions**: Based on the identified bottlenecks, the final objective was to develop effective solutions to improve the performance of small multimodal models. This focused on alleviating the discovered perception and reasoning limitations, ultimately aiming to propose a new, efficient framework for high-performing generalist small MLLMs.

A central hypothesis emerged during the investigation: LLM downscaling disproportionately affects visual capabilities rather than abilities inherited directly from the LLM. Furthermore, the authors hypothesized that the perception bottleneck observed in small MLLMs arises from the heterogeneity of how perceptual skills are learned under the visual instruction tuning paradigm. They posited that requiring a model to acquire diverse skills for interpreting instructions and extracting relevant visual information across varied tasks is challenging for smaller models, aligning with the "Quantization Model" of neural scaling laws. This hypothesis guided the development of targeted methodological advancements.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research was structured into three main parts: an exploration of LLM downscaling, a decoupled analysis of perception and reasoning, and the development of a two-stage solution.

**Part 1: LLM Downscaling Exploration**
To systematically investigate the effects of LLM downscaling, the authors constructed multimodal models using well-established architectural components:
- **Architecture**: Qwen3 series LLMs (8B, 4B, 1.7B, and 0.6B parameters) served as the language backbone. SigLIP was used as the pre-trained vision encoder, connected to the LLM token space via a 2-layer MLP projector. Visual processing utilized the Higher AnyRes with Bilinear Interpolation scheme.
- **Data**: A comprehensive set of visual instruction tuning datasets was employed, comprising 574K single-image tasks (e.g., OCR-VQA, VQAv2, ImageNet, ScienceQA, Grounding) and 309K multi-image tasks (e.g., Spot the Difference, Multi-Image-VQA, Perceptual Similarity). BLIP558K was used for connector pretraining.
- **Training Recipe**: The connector was first pre-trained for language-image alignment. Subsequently, visual instruction tuning was performed, fine-tuning all parameters on single-image data, followed by a combination of multi-image data and randomly sampled single-image examples.
- **Analysis**: The impact of LLM downscaling (from 8B to 0.6B) on performance was measured across all tasks. An additional analysis correlated the magnitude of performance drop-off under LLM downscaling with the task's intrinsic reliance on visual information, quantified by the performance difference with and without visual input (using the 8B LLM).

**Part 2: Decoupled Perception / Reasoning Analysis**
To disentangle the effects of LLM downscaling on perception and reasoning, the Prism framework was adopted:
- **Setup**: A two-stage pipeline was implemented. In Stage 1, a multimodal "perception module" (the VLM from Part 1, with varying LLM sizes) received an image and a question-specific instruction to extract visually relevant information. In Stage 2, an "reasoning module" (a Qwen3 LLM, with varying sizes) processed this extracted textual description to generate the final answer.
- **Controlled Prompting**: To ensure consistency, question-specific instructions for the perception module were generated offline using a single model (Qwen3-8B), preventing variations in instruction generation from influencing the analysis.
- **Evaluation**: Evaluation utilized a multiple-choice format of the in-domain datasets (excluding purely perceptual tasks like Grounding and ImageNet) derived from AutoConverter. The out-of-domain MMStar benchmark, which explicitly assesses both perceptual and reasoning abilities, was also used.
- **Analysis**: The LLM size in the perception module and the reasoning module were independently downscaled (while holding the other module at 8B) to quantify their respective performance degradations.

**Part 3: Solutions (EXTRACT+THINK)**
Addressing the identified bottlenecks, the authors proposed a two-stage approach:
- **Visual Extraction Tuning**:
 - **Motivation**: To unify perceptual skills and enable extraction of question-relevant visual details, moving beyond generic captioning.
 - **Data Generation Pipeline**: For each visual instruction tuning example (Q/A pair), a model (Qwen3VL-8B) first converted the pair into a declarative statement. This statement was then incorporated into a prompt that instructed the model to describe fine-grained visual details, emphasizing relevance to the declarative statement. The VLM then generated this "visual extraction" response.
 - **Training**: This pipeline was applied to 382K training samples from in-domain tasks. The perception module, initially trained as a captioner (on ALLaVA-4V), was then post-trained using this visual extraction data. A "trained from scratch" variant was also developed.
- **Step-by-Step Visual Reasoning (THINK)**:
 - **Approach**: The Qwen3 reasoning module's "thinking mode" was activated to enable Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning.
 - **Prompting**: The reasoning module was instructed to reason step-by-step.
 - **Efficiency**: Techniques like NOWAIT were employed to suppress self-reflection tokens, and a thinking budget was limited to 4096 tokens to manage inference efficiency.
- **EXTRACT+THINK Framework**: The final approach combined a perception module trained with visual extraction tuning and a reasoning module enhanced with CoT. Configurations with differing LLM sizes for perception and reasoning (e.g., 0.6B perception / 1.7B reasoning, and 1.7B perception / 4B reasoning) were evaluated.
- **Comparison**: EXTRACT+THINK was benchmarked against end-to-end MLLMs (LLaVA-OneVision, InternVL2.5, SmolVLM) and other decoupled methods (PrismCaptioner, baseline decoupled setup, CAPTION+THINK). Evaluation metrics included in-domain and out-of-domain (MMStar) accuracy, along with an analysis of inference latency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. LLM Downscaling Exploration

- **Disproportionate Impact on Vision-Centric Tasks**: The analysis revealed that LLM downscaling disproportionately affected tasks reliant on visual processing, rather than those primarily leveraging the base LLM's inherent linguistic abilities. For instance, tasks like Grounding exhibited a 48% decrease in performance, and Perceptual Similarity (NIGHTS) a 38% decrease, when downscaling from an 8B to a 0.6B LLM. In contrast, tasks such as ScienceQA (knowledge-based) or GQA (general abilities) showed more modest declines.
- **Correlation with Visual Reliance**: A strong, approximately linear relationship was observed between a task's sensitivity to LLM downscaling and its reliance on visual information. As the performance drop due to downscaling increased, so did the task's dependence on visual input for accurate resolution. This suggested that processes related to visual understanding and/or reasoning are significantly impaired in smaller LLM-backed multimodal models.

#### 4.2. Decoupled Perception / Reasoning Analysis

- **Reasoning Degradation**: As anticipated, downscaling the reasoning module's LLM size had a considerable negative impact on task performance, confirming visual reasoning as a critical bottleneck for small multimodal models.
- **Significant Perceptual Degradation**: A notable finding was that downscaling the perception module's LLM size also resulted in substantial performance drops. For instance, downscaling the perception module from 8B to 0.6B led to an average accuracy drop of 0.15 for in-domain data and 0.07 for out-of-domain data (MMStar). Critically, for tasks targeting visual reasoning (e.g., Instance Reasoning, Logical Reasoning), the impact of downscaling the *perception* module was often comparable to, or even exceeded, that of downscaling the *reasoning* module. This indicated that fundamental visual perception is a central bottleneck, not solely a prerequisite for successful reasoning.
- **Perception Bottleneck Hypothesis**: The authors hypothesized that this perceptual decline, despite fixed visual representations, stems from the heterogeneity of how perception is learned during visual instruction tuning. Smaller models struggle to acquire the diverse skills needed to extract relevant visual information across varied task instructions, consistent with the "Quantization Model" of neural scaling.

#### 4.3. EXTRACT+THINK Solutions

- **Captioning as a Baseline**: Initially, post-training the perception module as a captioner mitigated downscaling effects and even surpassed end-to-end baselines at smaller scales. However, this approach lacked question-relevant extraction and domain-specific understanding.
- **Effectiveness of Visual Extraction Tuning**: The proposed visual extraction tuning paradigm, which explicitly trains the model to extract instruction-relevant visual details, yielded substantial performance improvements over the captioning baseline. For a 0.6B perception LLM, in-domain performance improved by 5.2 points and MMStar performance by 3.6 points. This demonstrated visual extraction tuning as an effective and efficient solution for the perception bottleneck.
- **Benefits of Step-by-Step Reasoning (CoT)**: Incorporating Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning via "thinking mode" in the reasoning module significantly enhanced out-of-domain performance across all LLM sizes. For in-domain tasks, CoT provided notable gains at intermediate model scales (4B and 1.7B), bringing their results closer to those of larger models without requiring additional visual data supervision.
- **EXTRACT+THINK Performance**: The combined EXTRACT+THINK approach, leveraging visual extraction tuning for perception and CoT for reasoning, demonstrated strong performance and efficiency:
 - A smaller EXTRACT+THINK variant (0.6B perception LLM, 1.7B reasoning LLM) surpassed the largest PrismCaptioner model (7.0B perception, 70B reasoning) on both in-domain and out-of-domain tasks, using a perception module 12x smaller and a reasoning module 41x smaller.
 - This variant also outperformed LLaVA-OneVision-0.5B by 12.9% on in-domain data and 19.5% on MMStar, utilizing 73% fewer visual training samples.
 - A configuration trained from scratch under the visual extraction tuning paradigm (EXTRACT+THINK†, 0.4M visual samples) improved over LLaVA-OneVision-0.5B by 9.7% in-domain using 95% fewer visual samples, highlighting the data efficiency of the approach.
- **Inference Latency Trade-off**: While the two-stage design incurred higher inference latency due to longer text generation, using vLLM improved throughput. The performance gains of EXTRACT+THINK were attributed more to the visual extraction tuning than merely the two-stage structure or increased token budget.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work provides a systematic and principled characterization of the effects of LLM downscaling on multimodal task performance, addressing a critical gap in the understanding of small MLLMs. By identifying that visually demanding tasks are disproportionately impacted, and through a decoupled analysis, revealing that both foundational perception and downstream reasoning abilities are central bottlenecks, the research offers a more nuanced understanding of the limitations of small multimodal models. This challenges previous assumptions that perception might be less sensitive to LLM scale than reasoning, redirecting focus for future compact model development.

The introduction of **visual extraction tuning** represents a methodological advancement. This training paradigm, which explicitly trains models to extract instruction-relevant visual details consistently across tasks, offers an effective and data-efficient solution for alleviating the perception bottleneck in small MLLMs. Coupled with **step-by-step reasoning (THINK)**, the **EXTRACT+THINK** framework establishes a new standard for efficiency and performance in compact multimodal systems.

The potential impact of this research is multifaceted:
- **Advancing Small MLLM Development**: By providing a clear understanding of the challenges and offering an effective framework, this work lays the groundwork for the development of more robust, high-performing generalist small multimodal models. These models are crucial for real-world applications in resource-constrained environments, such as mobile devices, edge computing, and embedded systems, where large, computationally intensive models are impractical.
- **Guiding Future Research**: The paper opens several avenues for future research. On the analytical front, further studies can explore downscaling across a broader spectrum of model sizes, compare the downscaling behavior of visual representations versus language models, and investigate the interplay of data size with downscaling effects. Methodologically, further investigation into the visual extraction tuning paradigm, including its comparison with visual instruction tuning and its effectiveness with larger language models, could lead to further advancements.
- **Informing Model Design**: The findings underscore the importance of addressing both perceptual and reasoning limitations when designing compact multimodal architectures. The two-stage, decoupled approach, enhanced by visual extraction tuning and CoT reasoning, provides a blueprint for creating models that are both parameter- and data-efficient without sacrificing significant performance. This could lead to a new generation of intelligent agents capable of sophisticated visual understanding and reasoning even with limited computational resources.
