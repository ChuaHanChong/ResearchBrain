# Downscaling Intelligence: Exploring Perception and Reasoning Bottlenecks in Small Multimodal Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Stanford University systematically investigate how reducing large language model capacity affects small multimodal models, pinpointing perception and reasoning as key bottlenecks. They introduce EXTRACT+THINK, a two-stage framework that achieves up to 19.5% higher performance on the MMStar benchmark while utilizing 73% fewer visual training samples compared to existing smaller models.

## Problem

- Large multimodal models (MLLMs) are computationally demanding, restricting their deployment in resource-limited settings like edge devices.
- A systematic understanding of how MLLM capabilities, specifically perception and reasoning, degrade when the underlying large language model (LLM) capacity is reduced is lacking.
- Inconsistent findings in previous studies on the impact of LLM size on perceptual abilities necessitate a principled investigation into these effects.

## Method

- Conducted a systematic empirical study by downscaling Qwen3 series LLMs in multimodal models and evaluating performance across a diverse set of visual tasks.
- Utilized a decoupled framework, adapting the ==Prism method==, to independently analyze the impact of LLM downscaling on perception and reasoning modules.
- Developed ==EXTRACT+THINK==, a two-stage framework employing =="Visual Extraction Tuning"== to enhance perception and "Chain-of-Thought reasoning" to improve the LLM's reasoning capabilities.

## Results

- The EXTRACT+THINK framework achieved state-of-the-art efficiency, with its smaller variant surpassing LLaVA-OneVision-0.5B by up to **19.5%** on MMStar while using **73%** fewer visual training samples.
- Visual extraction tuning significantly enhanced perceptual performance, yielding up to **+5.2** accuracy for the 0.6B perception module on in-domain data and **+4.6** on the MMStar benchmark compared to a captioning baseline.
- Chain-of-Thought reasoning effectively boosted the reasoning module's capabilities, leading to substantial improvements in out-of-domain performance across various LLM scales.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Performance degradation from LLM downscaling is most pronounced in visually demanding tasks, suggesting that core vision-centric capabilities are disproportionately affected.
> - The perception module's capacity, alongside the reasoning module's, represents a critical bottleneck in smaller multimodal models, challenging prior assumptions about perception's robustness to LLM scale.
> - Training the perception module with visual extraction tuning, which emphasizes fine-grained, question-relevant detail extraction, significantly improves performance and data efficiency.
