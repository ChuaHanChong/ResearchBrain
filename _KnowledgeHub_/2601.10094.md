# V-Zero: Self-Improving Multimodal Reasoning with Zero Annotation

> [!summary] Summary
> V-Zero, a framework developed at Zhejiang University, enables Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to self-improve their reasoning capabilities using only unlabeled images and internal feedback. The approach enhances base models by an average of 2.0 points and notably outperforms fully supervised baselines on various multimodal benchmarks.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art Vision-Language Models (VLMs) heavily rely on extensive, costly, and labor-intensive human-annotated datasets, particularly for complex Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning tasks.
- The domain-specific nature of human-annotated datasets limits the generalization capabilities of VLMs to novel scenarios.
- Existing VLM self-improvement methods still necessitate external expert models or curated edited images for data construction.

## Method

- ==V-Zero== introduces a ==co-evolutionary loop== where a Questioner and a Solver, both initialized from the same VLM, iteratively improve each other using only unlabeled images.
- The Questioner is trained using a ==Dual-Track Reasoning Reward== that incentivizes the generation of challenging questions which highlight a divergence between an intuitive guess and a reasoned answer from the Solver.
- The Solver is optimized via RLVR on a curated dataset, employing ==Difficulty-Guided Data Sampling== to focus on questions that are challenging yet solvable (confidence between **0.3** and **0.8**).

## Results

- V-Zero consistently improved the average score of the Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct base model by **+2.0 points** (from **49.9** to **51.9**) across diverse multimodal benchmarks over two iterations.
- The framework notably outperformed a Supervised GRPO baseline (**51.9** vs. **50.8** average score) that utilized the same image dataset but with full human annotations, demonstrating the efficacy of self-generated curricula.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical impact of the Dual-Track Reasoning Reward and Difficulty-Guided Data Sampling, with their removal leading to significant performance drops (e.g., General Avg. **-2.1** without difficulty sampling).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-improvement for Vision-Language Models is achievable with zero human annotation, significantly overcoming the traditional data bottleneck in VLM development.
> - A dynamically self-curated curriculum, where a continuously improving Questioner generates progressively challenging problems, can be more effective than static, human-designed training data.
> - Training on abstract visual reasoning tasks, such as geometric figures, leads to strong generalization and robust improvements across broader vision-centric benchmarks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has been a significant area of research, leading to models capable of sophisticated image understanding and complex reasoning. However, a pervasive challenge in this field is the **heavy reliance on vast, human-annotated datasets**. These datasets, particularly those involving fine-grained reasoning steps like Chain-of-Thought (CoT), are incredibly costly, labor-intensive, and often domain-specific, limiting the generalization capabilities of the resulting models.

In parallel, the field of Large Language Models (LLMs) has witnessed a paradigm shift with the emergence of **self-improvement methods**. Techniques like self-play, role specialization, and internal feedback loops have enabled LLMs to autonomously generate training data and learn signals, thereby enhancing their capabilities without constant human guidance. Examples include SPC (Chen et al., 2025), SPIRAL (Liu et al., 2025a), Absolute Zero (Zhao et al., 2025a), and R-Zero (Huang et al., 2025a), which demonstrate models learning from their own interactions or by solving self-generated problems.

V-Zero directly addresses a critical gap at the intersection of these two trends: **extending self-improvement to VLMs in a truly annotation-free manner**. While some prior work, such as Vision-Zero (Wang et al., 2025a), has adapted self-play to VLMs, it still required external expert models or curated edited images for data construction. V-Zero distinguishes itself by proposing a framework that operates *exclusively on raw, unlabeled images*, removing any form of human annotation or reliance on external expert models. This represents a significant step towards autonomous VLM development and vastly reduces the human effort traditionally required.

Furthermore, the paper situates itself against other advancements in multimodal reasoning, which typically focus on either exploring better reasoning trajectories (e.g., MM-Verify, GenPRM, Mulberry) or improving training signals by converting verifiable constraints into explicit rewards (e.g., Vision-R1, R1-Onevision, R1-VL). While effective, these methods largely depend on pre-existing, often large-scale, human-annotated data. V-Zero, by contrast, proposes a fundamental shift in the training paradigm, demonstrating that superior performance can be achieved *without* such annotation, thereby offering a highly scalable and cost-effective alternative for developing more generalized and robust multimodal AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the V-Zero project is to **develop a general post-training framework that enables Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to self-improve their reasoning capabilities exclusively using unlabeled images, completely bypassing the need for human annotation.**

The motivation behind this ambitious goal is multifaceted:

1. **Overcome Annotation Bottleneck:** The current state-of-the-art VLMs are heavily reliant on extensive human-annotated datasets, especially for complex reasoning tasks that require fine-grained Chain-of-Thought (CoT) annotations. Acquiring such high-quality data is prohibitively costly, time-consuming, and resource-intensive, making it a significant bottleneck for VLM development and deployment.
2. **Enhance Generalization:** Human-annotated datasets are often tailored to specific tasks or domains, which can limit the model's ability to generalize to novel scenarios or broader applications. A self-improvement framework could potentially generate more diverse and challenging training signals, leading to more robust and generalized VLMs.
3. **Leverage Success of Self-Improvement in LLMs:** Inspired by the remarkable success of self-improvement and self-play paradigms in Large Language Models (LLMs), the authors are motivated to explore whether similar autonomous learning principles can be effectively applied to the multimodal domain.
4. **Address Limitations of Existing VLM Self-Improvement:** Previous attempts at VLM self-improvement (e.g., Vision-Zero by Wang et al., 2025a) still required external expert models for data generation or relied on carefully designed game-based settings that entailed significant human design effort and data preparation. V-Zero aims to push the boundaries further by operating directly on raw, unlabeled images, thus achieving a truly "zero-human-involvement" training scheme.
5. **Advance Autonomous AI Development:** Ultimately, the project seeks to pave the way for more autonomous and scalable AI systems, where models can continuously learn and evolve their capabilities from readily available, unlabeled data, reducing dependence on human supervision throughout their lifecycle.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

V-Zero introduces a novel, general post-training framework that orchestrates a **co-evolutionary loop** between two distinct roles: a **Questioner** ($Q_\theta$) and a **Solver** ($S_\theta$). Both roles are initialized from the same base VLM and iteratively trained using **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**, relying solely on internally generated reward signals from interactions with unlabeled images.

#### 3.1. Overview of the Co-evolutionary Loop

The framework operates in cycles:
1. The Questioner generates challenging questions based on unlabeled images.
2. The Solver attempts to answer these questions.
3. Feedback from the Solver's performance is used to update the Questioner, encouraging it to generate even better questions.
4. The Solver is then updated based on the questions generated by the improved Questioner, learning from its own "pseudo-labels."
This dynamic process drives mutual enhancement of both components.

#### 3.2. Questioner ($Q_\theta$) Training

The Questioner's role is to perceive an input image ($I$) and generate a high-quality, multiple-choice question ($q$), a concise visual description ($d$), and an "intuitive" reference answer ($a_{fast}$). The multiple-choice format is chosen for its structured output, facilitating automated evaluation.

The core innovation for Questioner training is the **Dual-Track Reasoning Reward ($r_d$)**, designed to encourage the generation of challenging questions. This reward contrasts two distinct answering modes from the Solver:
- **Intuitive Track ($a_{fast}$):** The direct answer generated by the Questioner itself, representing its initial "guess."
- **Reasoning Track ($\hat{a}$):** A pseudo-label derived from the Solver ($S_\theta$) using Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. The Solver samples $m$ responses, and a majority vote determines $\hat{a}$, with confidence $c$ being the proportion of votes matching $\hat{a}$.

The reward $r_d(q)$ is adaptively calculated:
- **Consistency Case ($\hat{a} = a_{fast}$):** If intuition and reasoning align, the question is likely straightforward. The reward is $\min(c, 1-c)$. This "uncertainty reward" peaks at $c=0.5$, encouraging the Questioner to find questions at the Solver's decision boundary—neither too simple nor overly ambiguous.
- **Divergence Case ($\hat{a} \neq a_{fast}$):** If reasoned answer corrects the intuitive guess, the question is valuable. The reward is $0.5 \cdot c$. This incentivizes challenging questions where initial intuition is misleading but deeper reasoning leads to a confident correct answer.
This design inherently prevents reward hacking; nonsensical questions yield low confidence and thus low reward, while trivial questions lead to convergence but a low uncertainty reward.

Finally, strict formatting constraints (e.g., question and answer tags, four MCQ options) are enforced. The Questioner's final reward ($r_Q$) is $r_d(q)$ if the format is valid, and -1 otherwise.

#### 3.3. Solver ($S_\theta$) Training

The Solver's objective is to accurately answer the questions generated by $Q_\theta$. This phase involves **Difficulty-Guided Data Sampling** and optimization via **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)**.

1. **Difficulty-Guided Data Sampling:**
 - For a set of unlabeled images, $Q_\theta$ generates questions ($q_i$).
 - $S_\theta$ samples $m$ candidate answers, derives a pseudo-label ($\hat{a}_i$) via majority voting, and assigns a difficulty score ($s_i$) based on the vote proportion.
 - A training dataset ($D$) is curated by selecting only pairs ($q_i, \hat{a}_i$) where $0.3 \leq s_i \leq 0.8$. This crucial filtering step ensures the Solver learns from data that is challenging yet solvable, avoiding overly ambiguous or trivially easy examples.

2. **Binary Accuracy Reward and GRPO Optimization:**
 - For each ($q_i, \hat{a}_i$) in the curated dataset, $S_\theta$ samples a new group of candidate answers.
 - A binary reward ($r_j$) is assigned: 1 if the candidate answer matches $\hat{a}_i$, 0 otherwise.
 - These rewards are then normalized (z-score) to compute group relative advantage ($\hat{A}_{i,j}$).
 - The Solver's policy is updated by minimizing the GRPO loss, which combines a clipped surrogate objective with a KL-divergence penalty, similar to PPO.

The implementation builds upon the `verl` framework, leveraging `vLLM` for efficient inference during training rollout. The chosen backbones are Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B-Instruct, trained on 4 NVIDIA A800 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

V-Zero demonstrates significant advancements in VLM self-improvement, presenting compelling evidence for its efficacy without human annotation.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Improvements

- **Consistent Gains:** V-Zero consistently enhances the performance of the base models across diverse benchmarks. For Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, the average score improved by +2.0 points (from 49.9 to 51.9) over two iterations.
- **Specific Task Improvements:** Significant gains were observed in general vision-centric tasks (MMMU: +3.9 points) and visual mathematical reasoning (MathVerse: +3.0 points), validating the framework's broad effectiveness.
- **Model Capacity Impact:** The smaller Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct also showed improvements (average +0.7 points), peaking earlier in Iteration 1. This suggests that while effective, the self-improvement process's magnitude and stability are influenced by the base model's intrinsic capacity and robustness to self-generated noise.

#### 4.2. Generalization Across Tasks

- Despite being trained primarily on geometric figures aimed at improving mathematical reasoning, V-Zero demonstrated remarkable **generalization capabilities**. The Solver achieved an average improvement of +1.7 points on mathematical reasoning benchmarks.
- Crucially, it also yielded even larger improvements (+2.6 points average) on **general vision-centric datasets**. This indicates that the rigorous visual perception and reasoning honed through complex geometric problems effectively transfer to broader domains, strengthening fundamental visual understanding rather than merely overfitting to specific training data types.

#### 4.3. Comparison with Supervised Baselines

- **Outperforming Supervised GRPO:** V-Zero (7B, 51.9 avg.) remarkably **outperformed the Supervised GRPO baseline** (50.8 avg.), which was trained on the same image dataset but with *full human annotations*. This is a critical finding, suggesting that the diverse, dynamically generated questions by the Questioner can offer better exploration of the solution space than a static, human-curated dataset.
- **Highly Competitive with OpenVLThinker:** V-Zero remained highly competitive with OpenVLThinker-7B, a strong baseline post-trained using both supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and GRPO on human-annotated data. While OpenVLThinker had a slight edge in some mathematical reasoning benchmarks (MathVista, MathVision), V-Zero achieved superior performance on LogicVista and demonstrated a more balanced profile across general vision-centric tasks.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of V-Zero's core components:
- **Impact of Improving Questioner:** Freezing the Questioner at its initial state led to a catastrophic performance drop, even below the Base Model's general average. This unequivocally validates that the dynamic co-evolution and continuous improvement of the Questioner are essential for the framework's success.
- **Dual-Track Reasoning Reward:** Replacing the dual-track reward with a single uncertainty reward resulted in a notable performance decline (Math Avg. -0.8, General Avg. -0.6). This confirms that explicitly rewarding questions that expose a divergence between intuition and reasoning successfully drives the Questioner to generate more challenging, reasoning-intensive questions.
- **Difficulty-Guided Data Sampling:** Removing the data filtering mechanism (0.3 $\leq s \leq$ 0.8) for Solver training caused a significant performance drop (General Avg. -2.1, Math Avg. -1.1). This highlights the importance of curating a high-quality, 'challenging yet solvable' curriculum from self-generated data, preventing contamination by overly simple or noisy samples.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Analysis of Generated Questions

- **Format Validity:** The Base Model struggled with question formatting (64.9% valid), but V-Zero rapidly improved this to near-perfect rates (99.1% in Iter 1, 99.8% in Iter 2), demonstrating the effectiveness of format constraints and the Questioner's learning.
- **Question Difficulty:** The average difficulty score, evaluated by Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct, increased from 0.52 (Base) to 0.60 (Iter 2). This quantitative evidence, supported by qualitative examples (e.g., evolving from simple area calculation to complex ratio problems), confirms that the Questioner learns to generate progressively harder questions, creating a self-curated curriculum of increasing challenge for the Solver.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

V-Zero represents a significant advancement in the field of multimodal AI, with several far-reaching implications and potential impacts:

1. **Breakthrough in Zero-Annotation Learning:** The most profound significance of V-Zero is its demonstration of **truly annotation-free self-improvement for VLMs**. By operating exclusively on unlabeled images and generating internal feedback loops, V-Zero effectively eliminates the most substantial bottleneck in VLM development: the enormous cost, time, and human effort associated with creating high-quality, reasoning-intensive datasets. This could democratize access to advanced VLM capabilities, enabling smaller research groups or resource-constrained organizations to develop and fine-tune powerful multimodal models.

2. **Enabling Autonomous VLM Development:** V-Zero paves the way for more autonomous and continuous VLM development. Models equipped with such a framework could theoretically learn and refine their understanding and reasoning skills indefinitely by simply observing and interacting with ever-increasing volumes of raw visual data available online or in real-world environments, without human intervention. This moves closer to the vision of self-improving artificial general intelligence (AGI).

3. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The observed generalization of improvements from specific geometric reasoning tasks to broader vision-centric tasks is highly impactful. It suggests that V-Zero fosters the acquisition of fundamental visual perception and reasoning abilities rather than task-specific brittle skills. Models trained with V-Zero may be inherently more robust and adaptable to novel, unseen scenarios, a critical step towards deploying reliable AI in diverse real-world applications.

4. **Novel Curriculum Learning Strategy:** The dual-track reasoning reward and difficulty-guided data sampling mechanisms introduce a sophisticated, self-organizing curriculum. The Questioner learns to generate questions that strategically target the Solver's capabilities, pushing it beyond its current comfort zone. This dynamic, self-curated curriculum is potentially more effective and adaptive than static, human-designed curricula.

5. **Scalability to Unstructured Data:** The ability to learn from raw, unlabeled images unlocks immense scalability. The internet and countless real-world visual data sources provide an inexhaustible supply of visual information. V-Zero offers a pathway to leverage this vast, unstructured data for VLM training and refinement, potentially leading to models with unprecedented breadth and depth of understanding.

6. **Inspiration for Future Research:** V-Zero provides a strong foundation for future research in several directions. It encourages further exploration of sophisticated internal feedback mechanisms, alternative self-play architectures for multimodal systems, and methods to mitigate optimization fluctuations and capacity constraints in smaller models. Its success also invites investigation into applying similar zero-annotation principles to other challenging domains within multimodal AI, such as video understanding or embodied AI.

In conclusion, V-Zero is a pivotal contribution that challenges conventional wisdom regarding data annotation in multimodal learning. By demonstrating superior performance through purely self-supervised means, it opens up exciting possibilities for the future of AI development, promising a path towards more autonomous, scalable, and generalized intelligent systems.
