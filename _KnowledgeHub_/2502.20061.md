# HiFAR: Multi-Stage Curriculum Learning for High-Dynamics Humanoid Fall Recovery

> [!summary] Summary
> The HiFAR framework, developed by Tsinghua University and Booster Robotics, enables humanoid robots to autonomously recover from diverse fall scenarios using a multi-stage curriculum learning approach. This method achieved a 100% real-world success rate for supine and prone falls on the Booster T1 robot, with average recovery times of 2.711 seconds and 2.875 seconds respectively, demonstrating robustness to external disturbances, varying loads, and complex environments.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots lack robust, autonomous fall recovery capabilities, leading to potential damage, operational downtime, and limited practical deployment in dynamic environments.
- Reinforcement Learning (RL) approaches for fall recovery often struggle with high-dimensional dynamics, sparse rewards, and the sim-to-real gap, particularly in contact-rich and diverse fall scenarios.
- Traditional methods like Finite State Machines and optimization-based controllers offer limited adaptability and struggle with the computational complexity of multi-contact situations inherent in falls.

## Method

- HiFAR employs a multi-stage curriculum learning framework, progressively training the robot from low-dimensional (2D) to high-dimensional (3D) fall recovery tasks.
- Key State Initialization (KSI) uses predefined critical states to stabilize the learning process and accelerate policy convergence, complemented by a comprehensive, weighted reward function.
- The framework integrates extensive domain randomization in simulation, a mirror loss term for symmetric learning, and network expansion for transferring knowledge between stages to ensure robust sim-to-real transfer to the Booster T1 humanoid robot.

## Results

- Achieved a 100% success rate for supine and prone falls on the Booster T1 humanoid robot, with average recovery times of 2.711 ± 0.319 seconds and 2.875 ± 0.474 seconds respectively.
- Demonstrated high robustness in simulation, recovering from 150-200N push forces, an 80% increase in torso mass, and friction coefficients down to 0.4, with successful recovery on the physical robot even with a 5kg load or strong disturbances.
- Successfully generalized to diverse real-world fall scenarios (e.g., lateral, crossed-leg, seated) and complex environments (e.g., slopes, obstacles, outdoor settings) on the Booster T1 robot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Progressively increasing task dimensionality and complexity through a multi-stage curriculum learning effectively addresses the challenges of sparse rewards and high-dimensional dynamics in complex robotic tasks like fall recovery.
> - Key State Initialization significantly stabilizes the RL training process and improves policy convergence by providing consistent, critical starting points for contact-rich recovery behaviors.
> - A combination of comprehensive reward shaping, extensive domain randomization, and targeted network architecture choices enables successful sim-to-real transfer of complex, high-dynamic behaviors to physical humanoid robots.
