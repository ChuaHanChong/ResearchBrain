# WALL-WM: Carving World Action Modeling at the Event Joints

> [!summary] Summary
> The X Square Robot Team introduces WALL-WM, a World Action Model that resolves the granularity mismatch in embodied foundation models by shifting the learning unit from fixed-length action chunks to semantically coherent action events. This approach leads to enhanced embodied video generation quality and superior task progress in real-robot manipulation, reasoning, and generalization tasks compared to existing baselines.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies and World Action Models (WAMs) use fixed-length action chunks, creating a granularity mismatch between high-level language, continuous visual scene dynamics, and control-level actions.
- This chunk-centric formulation can underutilize or overwrite valuable visual-semantic priors from large multimodal models, limiting compositional abilities and long-horizon generalization in robotic systems.
- Traditional autoregressive latent reasoning for language-guided manipulation is often inefficient, hindering the effective integration of high-level semantic instructions.

## Method

- **WALL-WM** redefines the atomic unit of learning from fixed-length action chunks to semantically coherent action events, aligning with the natural structure of human behaviors.
- It employs a layer-coupled video-action denoiser architecture, combining a multi-view video tower (derived from a text-to-video model) with an action ==transformer==, augmented by language-guided reasoning using "Staircase ==Latent== CoT Decoding" for efficient instruction processing.
- The model leverages a diverse, carefully processed data ecosystem featuring event-centric hierarchical captioning, cluster-balanced sampling, and recovery data via contact-rich initializations to enable scalable and robust training.

## Results

- WALL-WM consistently outperformed Wan-series baselines in embodied video generation, demonstrating improvements in Motion Quality (0.771 vs 0.683), Semantic Alignment (0.886 vs 0.805), and Interaction Quality (0.434 vs 0.226).
- On real-robot experiments, WALL-WM achieved the highest average Task Progress across diverse manipulation (75.86), reasoning manipulation (71.60), and generalization (53.75) benchmarks, surpassing baselines like DreamZero and LingBot-VA.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the combination of cross-view interaction and event-conditioned execution substantially improved Task Progress for reasoning tasks (from 32.6 to 71.6) and generalization tasks (from 22.0 to 53.75).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Shifting the learning unit to semantically coherent action events effectively preserves pretrained visual-semantic priors, significantly enhancing action compositionality and long-horizon generalization in embodied systems.
> - A layer-coupled video-action denoiser, combined with multi-view adaptations and precise temporal alignment, facilitates the learning of robust physical priors and 3D-aware, view-consistent world representations.
> - The Staircase Latent CoT Decoding mechanism amortizes latent reasoning costs, providing an efficient method for integrating complex language instructions into continuous action generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses an identified limitation within the field of embodied foundation models, specifically Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies and World Action Models (WAMs). Recent advancements in embodied foundation models have increasingly leveraged large-scale priors from multimodal understanding models and video foundation models. Existing WAMs typically initialize from these models and then optimize fixed-length action chunks conditioned on observations and instructions.

The authors identify a "fundamental granularity mismatch" in this chunk-centric formulation. Language describes semantic goals and events, vision perceives continuous scene dynamics, and actions operate at control-level timescales. Forcing these modalities into a fixed-length prediction window can lead to short-horizon correlation fitting, potentially underutilizing or even overwriting pretrained visual-semantic priors. This can weaken compositional abilities and long-horizon generalization in robotic systems.

WALL-WM aims to mitigate this mismatch by shifting the atomic unit of learning from fixed-length chunks to semantically coherent action events. This approach is positioned as a "prior-preserving lift" from large-scale video models to executable world-action models, seeking to inherit and maintain the semantic and temporal structure learned from videos while acquiring the controllability and contact sensitivity necessary for embodied action. It contrasts with reactive observation-to-action mappings common in many VLAs and builds upon the concept of generative world models that predict future states for planning and control. The work also incorporates latent reasoning techniques, but distinguishes its "Staircase Decoding" approach from traditional autoregressive or purely latent chain-of-thought methods by amortizing reasoning costs through staggered layer depths.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of WALL-WM is to develop a World Action Model that resolves the granularity mismatch inherent in current chunk-centric video-action learning paradigms for embodied foundation models. The authors motivate this by detailing several specific challenges and goals:

- **Addressing Granularity Mismatch:** The core motivation is the incongruity between how language, vision, and action naturally operate and the fixed-length action chunks typically used in VLA training. Language expresses high-level semantic goals and events, vision observes continuous scene dynamics, and actions require fine-grained control. Fixed chunks, determined by an external clock, often cut through natural semantic boundaries of embodied behaviors. This formulation is argued to hinder effective transfer of visual-semantic priors from large-scale pretraining.
- **Preserving Pretrained Visual-Semantic Priors:** The paper posits that current chunk-centric VLA training can underuse or even overwrite valuable visual-semantic priors derived from large multimodal or video foundation models. This can lead to models learning "chunk-specific action shortcuts" that limit their ability to compose actions and generalize over longer horizons. WALL-WM aims for a training regime that actively preserves and leverages these priors.
- **Enabling Scalable Learning over Diverse Behaviors:** A goal is to enable scalable learning for general-purpose WAMs across a wide range of behaviors, scenes, and task structures. This necessitates a data ecosystem and training methodology capable of handling such diversity.
- **Improving Compositionality and Long-Horizon Generalization:** By aligning learning with semantically coherent action events, the authors hypothesize that the model can learn more meaningful and composable action primitives, thereby improving its ability to generalize to novel and long-horizon tasks.
- **Supporting Comprehensive World Modeling:** The work aims to integrate future observation modeling with action prediction explicitly. This involves developing a model that can not only predict actions but also how the physical world will evolve under those actions, making it a true "world-action model."
- **Establishing Design Principles for Alignment:** The paper outlines three design principles for its "alignment unit" (the action-grounded semantic event):
 - **Geometry Preservation:** Connecting language, video, and action without flattening their distinct structures into a single embedding space.
 - **Prior Preservation:** Maintaining compatibility with the caption-to-video structure inherited from video foundation models.
 - **Executable Causality:** Providing a prediction target with clear temporal support, where duration adapts to the task rather than being fixed.
- **Facilitating Flexible Inference Modes:** To cater to different deployment needs, the objective includes supporting two complementary inference modes from a single event-pretrained backbone: an "event mode" for variable-length, event-space rollouts, and a "unified mode" for conventional fixed-length chunk inference that incorporates event-structured latent reasoning.
- **Developing Scale-Up Infrastructure:** A practical objective is to develop a robust infrastructure, including a data ecosystem, balanced sampling, and optimized pretraining, to enable the scalable training of general-purpose WAMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WALL-WM instantiates its event-centric world action modeling through a multi-stage, prior-aligned training and inference recipe.

#### 3.1. Architectural Design

The core of WALL-WM is a layer-coupled video-action denoiser. It consists of a video tower, inherited from a Wan Series text-to-video model, and a randomly initialized action transformer.
- **Multi-View Visual World Events Modeling (Video Tower):**
 - The video tower extends a single-view Diffusion Transformer (DiT) to multi-view and multi-embodiment video generation.
 - **Multi-View Adaptation:** An additional cross-view branch in each DiT block performs self-attention over concatenated spatial tokens from multiple cameras, initialized from within-view self-attention. A zero-initialized projector ensures a gradual learning of cross-view exchange.
 - **Camera RoPE:** Rotary Position Embeddings are extended with a view axis, providing each camera a learnable, calibration-free identity, enabling operation across diverse camera setups.
 - **Cross-View Geometric Masking:** At training time, a sight-cone attention mask restricts cross-view attention to geometrically plausible regions (intersecting viewing frustums). A tube patch masking mechanism selectively masks spatial windows in one view across all frames, forcing the network to recover information from other views. These are dropped at inference.
 - **Video Flow-Matching Objective:** The video tower is trained with a Wan-style v-prediction flow-matching loss (L_V) on event latents, optionally applying a weighted loss for tube-masked regions.
- **Event-Centric Action Dynamics Modeling (Action Tower):**
 - An action DiT, with similar depth to the video tower, processes proprioceptive state and noisy actions.
 - **Layer-Wise Coupling:** At each layer, action tokens cross-attend to multi-view video features from the matched video block. This coupling is one-directional (video to action).
 - **Video-Action Temporal Alignment:** Learnable positional embeddings (E_τ for within-window relative time and E_abs for absolute window index) align video and action streams.
 - **Event-centric window:** Used for pretraining, where captions localize segments.
 - **Observation-centered window:** For unified deployment, supporting historical context and future prediction.
 - **Video-Action Timestep Mapping:** For large-scale training, an "asymmetric 1-to-N_d" mapping is used, where all action denoising steps read cross-attention from a single, fixed-noise-level video forward (s*).
 - **Action Objective:** The action tower is optimized under action flow matching (L_A), typically using v-prediction. Optional x-prediction and a Type-II DCT auxiliary loss can be used for contact-heavy data.
- **Language-Guided Reasoning:**
 - A Qwen3.5-9B backbone is used to produce hidden states for instruction conditioning.
 - **Staircase Latent CoT Decoding:** To overcome the inefficiency of autoregressive latent reasoning, WALL-WM models reasoning as a sequence of K_c continuous latent states. A Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) structure, coupled to the frozen VLM backbone, partitions the transformer at a relay depth (N_r). Lower layers compute shared visual-language grounding, while upper layers generate reasoning states in parallel.
 - **Frozen Latent-to-Text Reconstruction Supervision:** Latent reasoning states are projected into a soft prefix for a frozen lightweight language model (Qwen3.5-0.8B), which reconstructs a textual Chain-of-Thought (CoT) trace (L_CoT). Only the staircase branch and prefix projector are optimized.

#### 3.2. Training Data

The WALL-WM data ecosystem comprises diverse sources and careful processing:
- **Data Composition:** Includes general internet video (e.g., OpenVID), egocentric human video (e.g., Ego4D), robot-free UMI-style recordings (e.g., XRZero-G0), and heterogeneous teleoperation/open robot data. Human-intervention and failure-recovery data are centrally used.
- **Deployment-Aligned Temporal Synchronization and Data Postprocessing:** Video and action streams are synchronized by estimating and correcting frame-to-frame offsets based on motion signals. A cleaning pass removes corrupted or misaligned episodes.
- **Event-Centric Hierarchical Captioning:** Every episode is annotated with a four-level hierarchy (Task, Subtask, Action, Segment), plus an optional Human layer. Spans are temporally grounded on atomic manipulation actions, crucial for surfacing corrective behaviors.
- **Cluster-Balanced Sampling:** Offline vision-language (VL) and action clustering categorize instruction-scene and trajectory modes. During training, batches are sampled to balance these clusters, addressing long-tail distributions.
- **Recovery Data via Contact-Rich Random Initialization:** Pretraining is augmented by sampling robot initializations around nominal contact poses of critical events (e.g., grasping, insertion), generating recovery rollouts to enhance coverage of contact-rich action spaces.

#### 3.3. Training and Inference Recipe

The training follows sequential stages:
1. **Event-Centric Video-Action Pretraining:** First, the video DiT is trained on event latents and T5-encoded event captions. Then, the action tower is trained with the video DiT frozen, using the asymmetric 1-to-N_d mapping.
2. **VLM Text-Conditioner Pretraining:** The project-out head and auxiliary heads of the Qwen3.5-9B VLM are trained to predict next-event descriptions and remaining time, while aligning its conditioning features to the T5 space. The VLM backbone remains frozen.
3. **Staircase Distillation:** The Staircase latent CoT module and its prefix projector are optimized using the latent-to-text reconstruction objective, with the rest of the model frozen.
4. **Optional Next-Chunk Adaptation:** The event-pretrained backbone is fine-tuned for fixed-length chunk prediction on observation-centered windows, conditioned by global instructions.
- **Inference Modes:**
 - **Event Mode:** The VLM or an agent proposes the next-event description, and WALL-WM executes a variable-length video-action segment.
 - **Unified Mode:** Fixed H_a-step chunks are rolled out, conditioned by current observations, history, and a global instruction. Context can come from a gradient-continuous source, atomic-instruction source, or the VLM-CoT source via Staircase decoding.

#### 3.4. Infrastructure

- **Muon Optimizer (DMuon):** A distributed version of the Muon optimizer is implemented for hybrid parallelism, with pipeline scheduling and kernel optimizations to reduce overhead in sharded training.
- **Kernel Library:** A customized kernel library using TVM FFI fuses common operator patterns to reduce global memory access and improve GPU utilization.
- **Fine-Grained Overlapping:** Scheduling optimizations hide communication overhead of view attention in sequence parallelism.
- **Multi-Event Sequence Packing:** Multiple events are packed into single long sequences at the dataloader level, using attention masks to block cross-event leakage, ensuring full batch utilization.

#### 3.5. Model Compression

- **Distillation:** Distribution-Matching Distillation (DMD) trains a few-step student generator to align its output with a multi-step teacher. A joint distillation objective retains the action-prediction loss to prevent action head drift.
- **FP8 Quantization:** Post-training quantization to 8-bit floating-point precision is applied to dominant matrix multiplications. Weights are pre-packed, and activation quantization is fused into preceding operators for efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of WALL-WM focuses on embodied video generation and real-robot performance, with comparisons against existing foundation models and ablations.

#### 4.1. Embodied Video Generation Evaluation

- **Protocol:** Evaluation was conducted on an Embodied Video Generation benchmark from a generalized embodied data mixture, including held-out in-distribution and out-of-distribution tasks, using the WorldArena protocol.
- **Metrics:** Assessed Visual Quality (Image Quality, Aesthetic Quality), Motion Quality (Dynamic Degree, Flow Score, Motion Smoothness), Semantic Consistency (Subject Consist., Background Consist., Semantic Alignment), and Physical Plausibility (Interaction Quality, Perspective, Instruction Following, Trajectory Acc.). Also evaluated 3D awareness using Probed Feature Point Error, Depth Error, and AUC@5/30, and multi-view consistency via cross-view correspondence error.
- **Results:**
 - WALL-WM consistently outperformed Wan-series baselines (Wan2.1-1.3B, Wan2.2-5B) on embodied-relevant dimensions such as Motion Quality (e.g., Motion Smoothness improved from 0.683 to 0.771 compared to Wan2.2), Semantic Consistency (e.g., Semantic Alignment improved from 0.805 to 0.886), and Physical Plausibility (e.g., Interaction Quality improved from 0.226 to 0.434, Trajectory Acc. from 0.223 to 0.234). Visual quality remained competitive.
 - Qualitative results indicated that WALL-WM produced more coherent and goal-consistent rollouts compared to generic video models that exhibited semantic drift or unrealistic dynamics.
 - On 3D awareness, WALL-WM achieved competitive results against models like DINOv2, V-JEPA, CogVideoX, Aether, Open-Sora2.0, and WAN2.1-14B, with a Probed Feature Point Error of 0.271 and Depth Error of 0.132, alongside strong AUC scores. It demonstrated jointly low probe and correspondence errors, indicating 3D-aware and view-consistent representations.
 - The model exhibited stronger text generalization, attributed to multi-level event captions and prompt augmentation, allowing better adherence to paraphrased or compositional instructions.

#### 4.2. Real-Robot Evaluation

- **Setup:** Experiments were performed on high-performance tabletop bimanual robot arms from an internal deployment platform, using synchronized multi-view observations and language instructions.
- **Metric:** Task Progress (0–100 score for partial completion), which provides a continuous measure beyond binary success rate.
- **Baselines:** $\pi_{0.5}$, DreamZero, LingBot-VA, and WALL-WM-U-Scratch (a from-scratch unified baseline without event-centric pretraining).
- **Diverse Manipulation:**
 - WALL-WM (event mode) achieved the highest average Task Progress (75.86), outperforming WALL-WM-U-Scratch (63.00), $\pi_{0.5}$ (55.64), DreamZero (39.97), and LingBot-VA (29.71).
 - Gains were particularly noted on tasks requiring complete object-state transitions, suggesting a reusable physical prior for grasp, transfer, alignment, and release phases.
- **Reasoning Manipulation:**
 - WALL-WM (event mode) obtained the strongest average Task Progress (71.60), compared to WALL-WM-U-Scratch (59.50), $\pi_{0.5}$ (56.40), DreamZero (32.70), and LingBot-VA (31.60).
 - This indicates improved instruction grounding for tasks requiring category grounding, relational matching, and ordered execution, supported by the language-guided reasoning module converting instructions into next-event descriptions.
- **Dexterous Manipulation:**
 - WALL-WM (event mode) achieved the highest average Task Progress (32.00), closely followed by WALL-WM-U-Scratch (31.25), both outperforming $\pi_{0.5}$ (15.00), LingBot-VA (24.00), and DreamZero (25.00).
 - Despite these relative gains, overall absolute scores remained low, highlighting the inherent challenge of fine contact control and precise insertion in real-robot settings.
- **Generalization:**
 - WALL-WM (event mode) obtained the highest average Task Progress (53.75), substantially outperforming DreamZero (28.50), $\pi_{0.5}$ (24.00), and WALL-WM-U-Scratch (18.50).
 - This suggests improved robustness under instruction switching and in cluttered shared scene contexts, by leveraging pretrained event knowledge.
- **Ablation on Event and View Modeling:**
 - An ablation comparing event-mode WALL-WM (with View-Interaction Self-Attention, VI-SA) against a pretrained unified baseline without VI-SA demonstrated significant improvements.
 - On Reasoning Manipulation, average Task Progress increased from 32.6 to 71.6. On Generalization, it increased from 22.0 to 53.75. This supports the combined benefit of cross-view interaction and event-conditioned execution for robust reasoning and generalization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

WALL-WM presents a methodology for scaling up general-purpose World Action Models by addressing what the authors identify as a fundamental granularity mismatch in embodied learning. Its significance and potential impact stem from several aspects:

- **Foundation for General-Purpose WAMs:** By shifting the learning unit from fixed-length action chunks to semantically coherent action events, WALL-WM provides a framework for building more robust and generalizable embodied foundation models. This event-grounded approach is proposed as a "prior-preserving scale-up methodology," enabling models to better leverage vast amounts of pretraining data from video foundation models without sacrificing critical semantic and temporal structures.
- **Enhanced Generalization and Compositionality:** The empirical results demonstrate that WALL-WM achieves broad generalization across varied language instructions, scenes, and tasks. Its performance improvements in real-robot evaluations across diverse manipulation, reasoning, and generalization benchmarks suggest that event-centric pretraining contributes to more capable and composable robotic skills, particularly for complex tasks involving sequential steps, semantic reasoning, and dynamic environments.
- **Improved Physical Plausibility and 3D Awareness:** The work contributes to developing WAMs that learn stronger physical priors. The embodied video generation evaluations indicate enhanced motion quality, semantic consistency, and physical plausibility in generated videos, along with competitive 3D awareness and multi-view consistency. This implies that the model develops a more accurate internal representation of world dynamics, which is critical for robust control.
- **Flexible Deployment with Two Inference Modes:** The design supports two complementary inference modes (event mode and unified mode) from a single backbone. This offers flexibility for different deployment scenarios, allowing variable-length, event-driven execution for open-ended tasks and conventional fixed-horizon control, while retaining the benefits of event-centric pretraining.
- **Practical Scaling Infrastructure:** The development of sophisticated infrastructure, including the DMuon optimizer, a custom kernel library, fine-grained overlapping, and multi-event sequence packing, along with model compression techniques like distillation and FP8 quantization, makes large-scale training and real-time deployment of complex WAMs more feasible. This addresses practical challenges in scaling embodied AI research.
- **Shift in Evaluation Paradigm:** The use of "Task Progress" as a dense, continuous metric for real-robot evaluation is significant. This approach aims to provide finer-grained feedback than binary success rates, which can be noisy and limit resolution for large-scale pretraining. This could influence future evaluation protocols in embodied AI.
- **Facilitating Agentic Intelligence:** The proposal to use event-centric instruction generalization as an interface to broader agentic intelligence suggests a potential path for integrating high-level reasoning from general agents with robust low-level embodied control, rather than forcing the manipulation model to handle all reasoning aspects itself.

The work acknowledges challenges such as the inherent difficulty of dexterous manipulation, the resource-intensive nature of comparisons against other large-scale systems, and the evaluation advantages of platform alignment. Future work directions, such as self-supervised pretraining of event boundaries and refining autoregressive units to event-level latent primitives, indicate a continuous effort to push the boundaries of general-purpose WAMs.
