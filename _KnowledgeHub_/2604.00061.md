# Advancing Multi-Robot Networks via MLLM-Driven Sensing, Communication, and Computation: A Comprehensive Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces the Robot-to-Everything (R2X) paradigm, a unified framework for multi-robot networks that integrates MLLM-driven sensing, communication, and computation. Through four experimental demonstrations, the R2X orchestrator shows improvements in task completion time, communication reliability, and overall success rates in complex robotic operations.

## Problem

- Existing multi-robot coordination methods lack a holistic framework integrating communication, MLLM-based AI, and joint optimization of sensing and computation.
- Transmitting extensive multimodal sensor data from multiple robots poses communication bottlenecks, particularly for centralized MLLM processing.
- Robots struggle with open-vocabulary perception and executing language-grounded actions in dynamic, unseen environments using only on-device processing.

## Method

- Proposes the ==Robot-to-Everything (R2X) paradigm== that unifies MLLM-driven intelligence with integrated sensing, communication, and computation.
- Leverages MLLMs to translate high-level language goals into dynamic, ==task-oriented resource orchestration== across heterogeneous robot teams.
- Implements ==hierarchical computation==, ==semantic communication==, and adaptive sensing (e.g., compression) to optimize system-level KPIs.

## Results

- Orchestrated R2X configurations significantly reduced task completion times in warehouse navigation, e.g., from **209.0s** to **108.0s** for long paths.
- An MLLM-driven proactive communication controller achieved **102.68%** throughput gains and **40.89%** latency reductions compared to reactive baselines in mobile environments.
- Real-world robot prototypes demonstrated **100%** follow-me task success with mean Command-to-Action latency of **19.60 ms** and **90-100%** trash sorting success using edge-assisted MLLMs for open-vocabulary grounding.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLMs can effectively translate high-level natural language intents into concrete, system-level resource allocation decisions for multi-robot tasks.
> - Semantic communication, by extracting and transmitting only task-relevant features, drastically reduces bandwidth requirements and latency in multi-robot networks.
> - Jointly optimizing sensing, communication, and computation, rather than treating them in isolation, is critical for achieving robust and efficient multi-robot coordination.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses the challenges of coordinating multiple robots in complex, real-world environments, particularly in the context of emerging multimodal large language models (MLLMs). It positions itself within the broader landscape of networked robotic systems, drawing comparisons with existing paradigms such as Machine-to-Machine (M2M) communications, Vehicle-to-Everything (V2X) communications, Ultra-Reliable Low-Latency Communication (URLLC), and traditional multi-robot collaboration frameworks.

The authors observe that while these existing paradigms offer solutions for specific aspects (e.g., generic device connectivity in M2M, traffic safety in V2X, low-latency links in URLLC, or distributed learning in robot collaboration), none of them provide a holistic framework that simultaneously integrates communication-layer design, MLLM-based AI synergy, task-centric communication requirements, and joint sensing, computation, and communication optimization. For instance, traditional robot collaboration often lacks considerations for physical and network layer communication issues. V2X and M2M generally do not incorporate advanced multi-robot AI or goal-driven collaboration. URLLC, while focused on low latency, is not intrinsically coupled with AI-based decision-making or multi-robot sensor fusion.

The paper introduces the **Robot-to-Everything (R2X) paradigm** as a unified framework designed to bridge these gaps. R2X aims to encompass the full stack, from physical-layer wireless protocols to MLLM-based semantic reasoning, enabling intent-to-resource orchestration for multi-robot systems. This work surveys the state-of-the-art in these integrated areas and provides empirical demonstrations to substantiate the R2X concept, distinguishing it from prior, more specialized approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to present a comprehensive survey and tutorial on the communication systems and architectural frameworks necessary for generative AI-empowered R2X networks in robotics. The authors aim to guide the robotics research community and industry practitioners in developing end-to-end solutions that integrate sensing, communication, and computation for real-time and large-scale multi-robot operations.

The motivation for this comprehensive approach stems from several key challenges and emerging capabilities:

- **Emergence of MLLM-powered Robots:** The advent of advanced humanoid robots and autonomous systems, increasingly powered by MLLMs, enables sophisticated interpretation of real-time sensing data and execution of complex tasks. These robots can process not only their own sensor data but also information from other networked agents (e.g., drones, autonomous vehicles).
- **Demands for Holistic Coordination:** While single robots demonstrate local autonomy, realistic industrial and rescue missions necessitate coordinated actions among multiple agents. This requires sharing and joint interpretation of large volumes of sensor information.
- **Communication Bottlenecks:** Transmitting comprehensive sensor data from numerous robots, each with bandwidth and latency constraints, can overwhelm communication networks. This challenge is amplified when a system-level orchestrator or cloud-based MLLM needs to fuse multimodal inputs for holistic decisions (e.g., route planning, anomaly detection).
- **Intent-to-Resource Orchestration:** Multi-robot missions are often initiated by high-level natural language instructions (e.g., "Search for the yellow bin"). A fundamental motivation is to develop a capability for "intent-to-resource" mapping, where understanding the specific goal via MLLMs allows the system to selectively activate relevant sensing modalities, dynamically allocate communication bandwidth, and determine optimal computation placement. This transforms the problem into jointly optimizing sensing, wireless communication, and computation to maximize task success under resource constraints.
- **Open-Vocabulary Perception and Language-Grounded Action:** Many real-world deployments require robots to recognize previously unseen objects or operate beyond their immediate field-of-view, capabilities difficult to achieve with closed-set, on-device perception alone. Integrating MLLMs and collective sensing via communication can address these limitations.

Ultimately, the work is motivated by the need to develop a unified framework that can achieve holistic awareness and responsiveness for autonomous robot teams in complex environments by uniting advanced sensors, flexible network design, and MLLM-based intelligence, with a focus on measurable system-level metrics like payload size, end-to-end latency, reliability, and task success.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper adopts a survey approach to synthesize existing knowledge and propose a unified R2X framework, which is then validated through four end-to-end experimental demonstrations.

**Core R2X Framework:**
The R2X paradigm is defined as an integrated orchestrator for communication, sensing, and computing, specifically tailored for LLM-based collaborative robots. It emphasizes:
- **MLLM-Driven Intelligence:** MLLMs are central, acting as both perception/reasoning engines and intent interfaces. They translate high-level language goals (e.g., urgency, safety) into system-level decisions for sensing, communication, and computation. The MLLM architecture typically includes modality encoders, modality connectors, and an LLM core. Training involves pre-training (aligning modalities, world knowledge), instruction tuning (understanding user instructions), and alignment tuning (refining responses to human preferences).
- **Task-Oriented Collaboration:** The coordination of sensing, communication, and computation is task-dependent. Different tasks impose varied requirements on latency, reliability, semantic abstraction, and resource utilization. The orchestrator dynamically adapts these aspects based on task objectives.

**Integrated Sensing:**
The approach considers sensing not as isolated hardware but as cooperative, multimodal perception resources.
- **Modalities:** It reviews diverse sensors (cameras, LiDAR, radar, IMUs, microphones, ultrasonic, tactile sensors) and their roles in providing complementary information.
- **Feature Extraction and Compression:** To reduce bandwidth, raw multimodal data is processed to extract compact, meaningful semantic features or representations. Techniques like vector quantization (VQ) or deep learning-based compression are used to significantly reduce payload size while preserving task-relevant details.

**Integrated Communication:**
R2X defines a taxonomy of robot communications: Robot-to-Robot (R2R), Robot-to-Human (R2H), Robot-to-Infrastructure (R2I), Robot-to-Vehicle (R2V), and Robot-to-Device (R2D).
- **Differentiation from V2X/M2M:** R2X uniquely handles diverse mobility and unstructured environments, requiring flexible network requirements (URLLC for control, high throughput for data), wider sensor integration, stringent energy/size constraints, and varied control timescales.
- **Semantic Communication:** It advocates for semantic and task-oriented communication policies, where bandwidth, latency, and reliability are dynamically aligned with robot priorities and semantic importance, rather than optimizing link throughput in isolation.

**Integrated Computation:**
The framework employs a hierarchical computation strategy:
- **On-Device (Fully Distributed):** For simple reflex actions and real-time responsiveness, limited by hardware and energy.
- **Edge Computing (Hybrid):** For balancing real-time processing with higher model complexity, offloading heavier inference tasks to nearby servers.
- **Cloud Computing (Fully Centralized):** For large-scale inference and coordination, leveraging abundant resources, suitable for tasks tolerating slight delays.
- **Dynamic Resource Management:** This involves dynamic code offloading, caching strategies, and DNN model partitioning to optimize energy, latency, and computational load.

**Experimental Demonstrations:**
The paper validates the R2X concept through four end-to-end demonstrations, progressively increasing in realism and complexity:

1. **Digital-Twin Demo I (Warehouse Navigation):**
 - **Environment:** Ray-tracing digital twin of an indoor warehouse with two mobile robots and dynamic human workers.
 - **Orchestration:** LLM-based orchestrator configures sensing (raw RGB-D vs. semantic features), communication (reactive vs. predictive link adaptation), and computation (local stop-and-go vs. centralized global replanning).
 - **Methodology:** The LLM acts as a configuration layer, translating natural-language intent and system context into schema-valid JSON for deterministic solvers (path planner, wireless resource configurator). Semantic encoding compresses data, and ray-tracing predicts path-gain for proactive uplink power control.

2. **Mobility Demo II (MLLM-Driven Proactive MCS Control):**
 - **Environment:** Urban mobility simulation using Sionna ray-tracing for channel modeling.
 - **Orchestration:** Centralized Orchestrator MLLM predicts short-horizon channel variations from compact semantic features (RGBD, geometry, motion) and delayed channel feedback.
 - **Methodology:** The MLLM proactively selects modulation-and-coding schemes (MCS) to maintain reliability (target BLER ≤ 0.1) and maximize effective rate, mitigating issues from stale feedback.

3. **Real-Hardware Demo III (FollowMe Robot with Semantic-Sensing Switch):**
 - **Environment:** Physical mobile robot following a human in an indoor corridor over WiFi.
 - **Orchestration:** The orchestrator dynamically switches between JPEG-compressed image streaming and Vector Quantization (VQ)-based compact codeword-index transmission, and configures ROS2/DDS QoS (reliable vs. best-effort).
 - **Methodology:** Switching decisions are based on measured WiFi conditions (RSSI, RTT, loss) to maintain stable tracking with low Command-to-Action (CTA) tail latency.

4. **Real-Hardware Demo IV (Open-Vocabulary Trash Sorting):**
 - **Environment:** Mobile manipulation platform performing trash sorting in a room-to-corridor scenario, with a robot camera and a fixed corridor camera.
 - **Orchestration:** Edge-hosted MLLM performs open-vocabulary visual grounding from text instructions for target items and bins.
 - **Methodology:** Multi-view sensing (robot ego camera + corridor camera) is uplinked to the edge. The MLLM generates bounding boxes, which are then used for grasping and SLAM-based navigation to the correct bin. A semantic-sensing switch (YOLO vs. MLLM) determines the perception strategy based on whether objects are predefined.

Across all demos, the methodology emphasizes measuring system-level KPIs (task completion time, latency, reliability, success ratio) to evaluate the benefits of R2X-style orchestration over purely on-device or reactive baselines.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The four experimental demonstrations provided empirical validation for the R2X paradigm, highlighting the benefits of integrated sensing, communication, and computation under MLLM guidance.

#### 4.1. Digital-Twin Demo I: Warehouse Navigation

- **Finding:** The fully orchestrated R2X configuration (LORC-SC-P), combining semantic visual compression, predictive link-quality awareness, and centralized multi-agent replanning, consistently achieved the lowest task completion times across various navigation scenarios (short, medium, long paths, and long paths with obstacles).
- **Quantitative Results:** LORC-SC-P reduced task completion time from a baseline of 72.0 seconds (Stop-and-Go) to 66.0 seconds for short paths, and from 209.0 seconds to 108.0 seconds for long paths, and 209.5 seconds to 117.0 seconds for long paths with obstacles.
- **Mechanism:** Semantic sensing (reducing uplink payload by ~1024:1) enabled faster offloading, while predictive link adaptation (maintaining target SNR) mitigated communication-induced halts, allowing continuous motion and timely server-side replanning. The end-to-end sensing-to-command round-trip time (RTT) for LORC-SC-P was approximately 1.028 seconds, fitting within the allowed grid traversal time.

#### 4.2. Mobility Demo II: MLLM-Driven Proactive MCS Control

- **Finding:** The MLLM-driven proactive MCS controller demonstrated enhanced temporal stability and improved performance across SNR regimes compared to reactive baselines, particularly under delayed feedback in dynamic urban mobility scenarios.
- **Quantitative Results:** The proposed method achieved significant throughput gains (e.g., 102.68% over baseline t-20) and latency reductions (e.g., 40.89% over baseline t-20) under varying feedback delays. The Block Error Rate (BLER) distribution remained consistently below the target of 0.1, indicating improved reliability.
- **Mechanism:** The centralized Orchestrator MLLM, fusing multimodal semantic features (RGBD cues, geometry, motion embeddings) and delayed channel feedback, accurately predicted near-future channel variations. This allowed for proactive MCS selection that balanced rate and reliability, suppressing retransmissions and latency spikes inherent in reactive approaches.

#### 4.3. Real-Hardware Demo III: FollowMe Robot with a Practical Semantic-Sensing Switch

- **Finding:** A real-hardware FollowMe robot prototype, leveraging an orchestrator that adaptively switched between JPEG image compression and VQ-based semantic feature encoding based on WiFi conditions, achieved stable tracking and low control latency.
- **Quantitative Results:** The orchestrated system achieved a 100% FollowMe success ratio, outperforming fixed JPEG-only or VQ-only baselines (which had 75% success). It maintained a real-time Command-to-Action (CTA) mean latency of 19.60 ms, with a 95th percentile CTA of 32.62 ms, significantly better than JPEG-only (Q=95) at 118.75 ms mean and 514.77 ms 95th percentile. The User Tracking Failure Rate (UTFR) was reduced to 8.47% for the orchestrated system, compared to 63.73% for high-quality JPEG and 25.37% for VQ-only.
- **Mechanism:** The orchestrator dynamically adjusted sensing representation and ROS2/DDS QoS based on real-time RSSI measurements. This semantic-sensing switch reduced uplink payload size and stabilized communication, directly improving CTA tail latency and tracking robustness in a real-world, interference-prone WiFi environment.

#### 4.4. Real-Hardware Demo IV: Open-Vocabulary Trash Sorting via Edge-Assisted MLLM Grounding

- **Finding:** Edge-assisted MLLM grounding, combined with multi-view sensing, enabled open-vocabulary object recognition and discovery of targets outside the robot’s field-of-view (out-of-FOV), leading to significantly higher task success and reduced completion times for a trash sorting task.
- **Quantitative Results:** The proposed edge MLLM approach achieved item detection success rates of 90–95% and bin detection success rates of 90–100%, with an end-to-end task completion time of 55 ± 5 seconds and a success ratio of 90–100%. This was substantially better than Baseline A (on-device closed-set + exploration) which had 0% success and Baseline B (closed-set + retraining) with 40–50% success and 70 ± 15 seconds task time.
- **Mechanism:** The edge-hosted MLLM could ground arbitrary text queries for unseen objects and utilize a fixed corridor camera for out-of-FOV bin detection. This reduced the need for extensive retraining for new object classes and minimized time-consuming blind exploration, improving overall task efficiency and reliability. Semantic compression was utilized for efficient uplink of visual data to the edge MLLM.

#### 4.5. Cross-Demo Synthesis

The cumulative results across the demonstrations indicate that R2X is an effective policy layer for jointly optimizing sensing, communication, and computation. Key observations include:
- Semantic sensing is particularly valuable when closed-loop stability is bottlenecked by packet loss, jitter, or uplink contention.
- Prediction is beneficial when feedback is stale, such as in mobility or dynamic environments, to mitigate reactive policy oscillations.
- Compute placement (on-robot vs. edge) should align with task metrics, with communication-aware sensing being crucial to ensure offloading benefits outweigh increased latency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a significant contribution to the field of robotics and communication networks by proposing and empirically validating the R2X paradigm. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Unified Framework:** The R2X paradigm offers a comprehensive and unified framework that integrates MLLM-driven intelligence with sensing, communication, and computation. This addresses a critical gap in existing research, which often treats these components in isolation or focuses on more specialized communication scenarios (e.g., M2M, V2X, URLLC) without explicit MLLM synergy or task-centric orchestration.
- **Enabling Complex Multi-Robot Missions:** By providing a structured approach to translate high-level language intentions into system-level resource management decisions, R2X can enable more effective coordination, shared perception, and intelligent decision-making for fleets of autonomous robots. This is crucial for expanding robotic capabilities beyond local autonomy to complex collaborative tasks.
- **Bridging AI and Communications:** The paper highlights the indispensable role of communication for MLLM-powered robots, especially in multi-agent scenarios where vast streams of multimodal data must be shared and processed. It emphasizes semantic communication and feature vector encoding, demonstrating how intelligent data compression can drastically reduce bandwidth requirements without compromising task-relevant fidelity, thereby making MLLM-driven robotics more practical for wireless environments.
- **Practical Demonstrations and Measurable Outcomes:** The inclusion of four end-to-end demonstrations, ranging from digital-twin simulations to real-hardware prototypes, provides tangible evidence of R2X's efficacy. By focusing on system-level metrics such as task completion time, end-to-end latency, reliability (BLER, UTFR), and success ratios, the work offers a practical blueprint for evaluating future robotic systems, moving beyond isolated algorithmic gains to overall mission performance.
- **Industry Relevance and Application Potential:** The demonstrated use cases, such as warehouse navigation, proactive link adaptation under mobility, real-time human following, and open-vocabulary trash sorting, are directly applicable to various industries. This includes logistics, manufacturing, precision agriculture, public safety, and rescue operations, where advanced multi-robot collaboration is becoming increasingly vital.
- **Future Research Roadmap:** The paper concludes by outlining actionable research questions (RQs) across critical areas, including advanced communication protocols, edge computing and distributed AI, adaptive strategies, data compression, security and robustness, standardization, latency reduction, scalability, and MLLM optimization. This provides a clear and comprehensive roadmap for future investigations, emphasizing measurable, task-facing KPIs for validation.
- **Emphasis on Robustness and Safety:** The explicit discussion of security, adversarial robustness, fault tolerance, and fallback behaviors under orchestration failures underscores a crucial aspect for real-world deployment. This focus helps guide the development of safe and reliable MLLM-driven R2X systems.

In conclusion, this survey significantly advances the understanding of how sensing, communication, and computation must be synergistically integrated and orchestrated, guided by MLLM-based intelligence, to realize the full potential of multi-robot networks in dynamic and complex real-world environments.
