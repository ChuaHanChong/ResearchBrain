# Video Generation Models in Robotics -- Applications, Research Challenges, Future Directions

> [!summary] Summary
> A comprehensive survey analyzes video generation models as embodied world models in robotics, detailing their applications in areas like imitation learning and visual planning, while also identifying critical research challenges such as physical consistency and uncertainty quantification. It concludes by proposing future directions for their trustworthy integration into safety-critical robotic systems.

## Problem

- Physics-based simulators for robotics are often limited by simplifying assumptions, expensive asset curation, and a 'sim-to-real gap' for complex phenomena.
- Learned Markovian state-based world models struggle to capture intricate physical interaction processes and fine-grained spatial-temporal dependencies in the real world.
- Language-only abstractions (LLMs/VLMs) lack the expressiveness needed to accurately model the spatial and temporal dynamics essential for physical robot-environment interactions.

## Method

- The paper provides a systematic review of ==generative video models==, particularly ==diffusion== and ==flow-matching== models, positioning them as ==embodied world models== for robotics.
- It categorizes their applications across ==imitation learning==, ==reinforcement learning==, ==policy evaluation==, and ==visual planning==.
- The survey identifies key research challenges hindering their adoption and proposes future directions to address these limitations for trustworthy integration into robotics.

## Results

- The survey identified **4** key application areas for video models in robotics: data generation/action prediction in imitation learning, dynamics/rewards modeling in reinforcement learning, scalable policy evaluation, and visual planning.
- **10** major research challenges were systematically enumerated, encompassing issues like hallucinations, physics violations, uncertainty quantification, long video generation, and prohibitive computational costs.
- Specific future research directions were proposed to address these challenges, including integrating physics priors, improving instruction following, and developing robotics-centric evaluation benchmarks for more trustworthy and efficient robotic systems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Video generation models offer superior high-fidelity, physically consistent spatiotemporal predictions compared to traditional simulators and language models for embodied world modeling.
> - These models provide broad utility in robotics, acting as cost-effective data generators, dynamics/reward models, scalable policy evaluators, and visual planners.
> - Significant challenges persist, including hallucinations, violations of physics, poor instruction following, lack of uncertainty quantification, and high computational costs, which impede reliable real-world deployment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This survey paper positions itself at the critical intersection of generative AI, particularly video generation models, and robotics. The broader research landscape has seen a significant shift towards "world models" – computational representations that predict the future states of an environment given an agent's actions. Traditionally, robotics has relied on two primary types of world models:
1. **Physics-based simulators:** These are powerful but often limited by restrictive simplifying assumptions, expensive asset curation, and difficulty in accurately simulating complex phenomena like deformable bodies, leading to a "sim-to-real gap."
2. **Learned Markovian state-based world models:** These models learn dynamics from data but often operate in a latent space or predict high-level scene dynamics, struggling with the "intricate interaction processes inherent in the physical world" and "fine-grained interaction tasks."
3. **Language-only abstractions:** Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have shown remarkable capabilities in reasoning and task planning but lack the "expressiveness required to efficiently capture the intricate interaction processes inherent in the physical world" and "accurately model the spatial and temporal dependencies."

Against this backdrop, recent breakthroughs in generative modeling, specifically diffusion and flow-matching models, have enabled "high-fidelity controllable video synthesis." These video generation models are emerging as powerful *embodied world models* that can capture the "spatiotemporal evolution of real-world environments" with "photorealistic, physically consistent" predictions.

This survey distinguishes itself from existing literature by offering a unique focus. While prior surveys have broadly covered video generation models (e.g., diffusion models, controllable video generation) or general world models (encompassing RNNs, transformers, language models, scene reconstruction), they often lack an extensive discussion of the *specific applications, associated challenges, and future directions of generative video models exclusively within the domain of robotics, especially robot manipulation*. This paper explicitly fills that gap, aiming to provide a "comprehensive review of generative video models as world models in robotics," unlike other surveys that might touch upon autonomous driving but not fine-grained manipulation. It bridges the gap between the rapid advancements in video generation and the practical needs and challenges of robotic systems, offering a focused roadmap for researchers in this burgeoning interdisciplinary field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind this survey paper are clearly articulated in the abstract and introduction:

1. **Review Video Models as Embodied World Models in Robotics:** The core objective is to provide a comprehensive overview of how video generation models are being utilized as "embodied world models" in robotics. This includes detailing their capabilities to generate high-fidelity, physically consistent spatiotemporal predictions of robot-environment interactions.
2. **Highlight Key Applications in Robotics:** The authors aim to systematically categorize and discuss the prominent applications of video models across various sub-fields of robotics, specifically:
- Cost-effective data generation and action prediction in imitation learning.
- Dynamics and rewards modeling in reinforcement learning.
- Scalable and reliable policy evaluation.
- Visual planning.
3. **Identify Research Challenges:** A crucial motivation is to pinpoint the significant limitations and open research challenges that currently hinder the "trustworthy integration" and broader adoption of video models in robotics. These challenges include issues like hallucinations (violations of physics, unsafe content), poor instruction following, fundamental limitations (data curation, training, and inference costs), and the need for uncertainty quantification and safe robot interaction.
4. **Propose Future Directions:** Beyond identifying problems, the survey seeks to "present potential future directions" to address these challenges. By outlining a clear research agenda, the authors aim to "motivate research and ultimately facilitate broader applications, especially in safety-critical settings."

In essence, the motivation stems from the recognition of video models' impressive capabilities in overcoming long-standing challenges in traditional physics-based simulators and language-only abstractions for world modeling. The paper serves as a guide for the robotics community to leverage these advancements while being acutely aware of the existing hurdles that need to be overcome for robust, safe, and efficient real-world deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a survey paper, the methodology revolves around a structured and comprehensive review and synthesis of existing literature. The authors adopt a layered approach, first establishing foundational concepts before delving into applications, evaluation, challenges, and future directions. This structure is explicitly outlined in Section 2 and Figure 2 of the paper.

The core components of their approach include:

1. **Background Establishment (Section 2):**
- **Learned World Models:** They begin by contextualizing video models within the broader landscape of learned world models, tracing their evolution from Markovian state-based representations to high-fidelity video-based representations. They discuss the limitations of traditional physics-based simulators and earlier learned models.
- **Video Model Architectures:** They provide an in-depth review of the dominant video generation paradigms:
 - **Diffusion/Flow-Matching Models:** This constitutes a significant portion, covering mathematical formulation, conditioning mechanisms (classifier-based, classifier-free guidance), model architectures (U-Nets, Transformers/DiTs), and supported conditioning modalities (text-to-video, image-to-video, motion/trajectory-guided generation). They highlight these as "state-of-the-art" for high-fidelity synthesis.
 - **Video Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (V-JEPA) Models:** They introduce JEPAs as an alternative, focusing on learning effective latent representations for future prediction and understanding, contrasting them with diffusion models' emphasis on photorealism.
2. **Categorization of Robotics Applications (Section 3):** The authors systematically organize existing research into four key application areas where video models serve as "embodied world models":
- Cost-effective data generation and action prediction in imitation learning.
- Dynamics and rewards modeling in reinforcement learning.
- Scalable policy evaluation.
- Visual planning.
 For each application, they describe how video models are employed, providing specific examples of recent works and discussing different approaches (e.g., modular vs. end-to-end action estimation).
3. **Evaluation Framework (Section 4):** They discuss how video models are assessed, covering:
- **Metrics:** A review of both frame-level (PSNR, SSIM, CLIP similarity, FID, LPIPS) and spatiotemporal (FVD, KVD, FVMD, optical flow, VLM-based consistency) metrics.
- **Benchmarks:** A summary of broad benchmarks (WorldModelBench, EvalCrafter, EWMBench, VBench, PAI-Bench, T2V-CompBench, WorldSimBench) and those specifically focused on physical commonsense (Physics-IQ, PhyGenBench, VideoPhy, VP²).
4. **Identification of Open Challenges and Future Directions (Section 5):** This section is highly critical, systematically enumerating ten major challenges. For each challenge, they provide context on its current state and then propose concrete "Future Directions" for research, often drawing from interdisciplinary fields or novel architectural ideas.

The paper synthesizes a vast amount of literature, citing hundreds of recent works, demonstrating a thorough understanding of the current state of the art. The use of figures (Figure 1 for overview, Figure 2 for organization, Figure 3 for diffusion architectures, Figure 4 for embodied world models, Figure 5 for data generation, Figure 6 for RL modeling) helps to visually organize complex information and illustrate key concepts.

### 4. Main Findings and Results (Key Insights)

As a survey, the "main findings and results" are not experimental outcomes but rather the synthesized insights and conclusions drawn from the comprehensive review of the literature. The key insights provided by the authors are:

1. **Video Models as High-Fidelity Embodied World Models:** Video generation models have emerged as superior world models compared to traditional physics-based simulators and language-only abstractions. They offer photorealistic, physically consistent spatiotemporal predictions, capturing fine-grained interactions crucial for robotics. This capability addresses the limitations of physics engines (e.g., deformable bodies, sim-to-real gap) and language models (lack of expressiveness for intricate physical interactions).
2. **Broad Utility Across Robotics Sub-fields:** The survey systematically demonstrates that video models are not niche tools but have wide-ranging applications across critical areas of robotics:
- **Imitation Learning:** They serve as cost-effective data generators for expert demonstrations, circumventing expensive human supervision, and as policy backbones for joint video and action prediction.
- **Reinforcement Learning:** They provide high-accuracy dynamics and rich reward signals, crucial for training policies and overcoming challenges in system identification and reward engineering.
- **Policy Evaluation:** They enable scalable, reproducible, and trustworthy evaluation of robot policies by simulating rollouts in diverse environments, offering valuable sanity checks and insights into policy robustness and safety without real-world hardware costs.
- **Visual Planning:** They facilitate generating feasible robot trajectories by synthesizing visual plans directly from language instructions or optimizing action sequences based on video predictions.
3. **Two Main World Model Categories:** Video world models can be broadly classified into implicit (scene representation encoded within the model, generating only videos) and explicit (creating concrete 3D scene representations like point clouds or Gaussian splatting from generated videos).
4. **Diffusion/Flow-Matching Dominance:** State-of-the-art video generation is largely driven by diffusion and flow-matching models, utilizing architectures like U-Nets and especially Diffusion Transformers (DiTs), and supporting diverse conditioning modalities (text, image, motion/trajectory).
5. **Significant Persistent Challenges:** Despite their capabilities, video models face critical limitations that impede their trustworthy integration into robotics:
- **Hallucinations and Physics Violations:** A fundamental lack of physical understanding, leading to unrealistic videos (e.g., object appearance/disappearance, inconsistent physics laws).
- **Poor Instruction Following:** Difficulty in accurately translating user prompts (text, image, motion) into desired video content, especially for long durations or precise camera control.
- **Lack of Uncertainty Quantification:** Inability to express confidence in predictions, which is crucial for safety-critical robotics.
- **Evaluation Deficiencies:** A lack of unified, robotics-centric metrics and benchmarks for assessing physical consistency and predictive accuracy.
- **Safety Concerns:** Propensity to generate unsafe content (illegal, unethical, violent) and the need for robust mechanisms for safe robot-environment interaction.
- **Action Estimation Inaccuracy:** Limitations in latent action models and inverse-dynamics models for accurately extracting robot actions from generated videos.
- **Long Video Generation:** Current models are limited to short video clips (seconds), insufficient for many long-horizon robotic tasks.
- **High Data Curation Costs:** The need for high-quality, diverse, and densely annotated video data (including failure cases) is expensive, despite efforts to leverage internet-scale data.
- **Prohibitive Training and Inference Costs:** Significant computational resources required for training and slow inference speeds hinder real-time applications and accessibility.

These findings collectively highlight the immense potential of video models in robotics but also underscore the necessity of targeted research to overcome their current shortcomings, particularly concerning reliability, safety, and efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey holds significant importance for the robotics and AI communities, offering a foundational and forward-looking perspective on the role of video generation models. Its potential impact is multifaceted:

1. **Roadmap for Future Research:** By meticulously identifying and categorizing open challenges, the paper provides a clear roadmap for future research directions. This structured identification of problems like "Hallucinations and Violations of Physics," "Uncertainty Quantification," "Safe Robot Interaction," and "Long Video Generation" will guide researchers toward critical areas that need innovation. It suggests specific avenues, such as integrating physics priors, developing intrinsic methods for task understanding, or designing efficient memory mechanisms for long videos.
2. **Accelerating Adoption in Robotics:** By clearly articulating the benefits (cost-effective data, scalable evaluation, robust planning) and limitations of video models in robotics, the survey informs practitioners and researchers, fostering a more realistic and effective integration of these powerful tools. It serves as a strong advocate for leveraging video models to address long-standing bottlenecks in robot learning, such as data scarcity and the sim-to-real gap.
3. **Promoting Trustworthy AI in Robotics:** A core theme of the paper is the "trustworthy integration" of video models. The emphasis on safety (content generation, robot interaction), reliability (uncertainty quantification, physical consistency), and instruction following directly addresses key concerns for deploying AI systems in safety-critical real-world robotic applications. This focus on trustworthiness is crucial for public acceptance and regulatory compliance.
4. **Catalyzing Interdisciplinary Collaboration:** The challenges outlined, such as the need for better physical understanding or improved instruction following, often require insights from diverse fields—physics, cognitive science, natural language processing, and computer graphics. The survey implicitly encourages interdisciplinary collaboration to tackle these complex problems.
5. **Benchmarking and Evaluation Standards:** By reviewing existing metrics and benchmarks and highlighting their shortcomings, the paper calls for the development of "robotics-centric benchmarks and multi-dimensional metrics for quantitative, efficient, and task-relevant video model evaluation." This will be critical for objectively measuring progress and comparing different video models in their practical utility for robotics.
6. **Addressing Computational Efficiency:** The discussion on training and inference costs, along with proposed solutions like spatial/temporal compression and shortcut models, points towards a future where these powerful models can become more accessible and deployable in real-time robotic systems, which is essential for closed-loop control and planning.

In conclusion, "Video Generation Models in Robotics: Applications, Research Challenges, Future Directions" is a timely and essential survey that not only synthesizes the current state of the art but also proactively charts a course for the future. It underscores the transformative potential of video models to act as embodied world models, fundamentally reshaping how robots learn, plan, and operate, while also serving as a critical reminder of the significant scientific and engineering hurdles that must be overcome for this vision to be fully realized.
