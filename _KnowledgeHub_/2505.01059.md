# Model Tensor Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> Model Tensor Planning (MTP), a sampling-based Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework, enhances control trajectory exploration using tensor sampling and spline interpolation. MTP demonstrates improved task success and lower cumulative costs across various challenging robotic tasks compared to baselines, achieving real-time performance through vectorized JAX implementation.

## Problem

- Many sampling-based MPC methods suffer from local minima and mode collapse due to locally greedy search strategies in complex, high-dimensional control landscapes.
- The curse of dimensionality in MPC leads to exponentially increasing sample requirements for global exploration with longer planning horizons and control dimensions.
- Existing sampling methods struggle to generate globally diverse, high-entropy control trajectories while ensuring real-time applicability and smooth, dynamically feasible control inputs.

## Method

- A tensor sampling strategy over randomized multipartite graphs generates high-entropy control waypoints, reformulating control sampling as a scalable tensor operation.
- Spline-based interpolation (B-spline or Akima-spline) transforms sampled waypoints into smooth and dynamically feasible continuous control trajectories.
- A β-mixing strategy balances globally exploratory tensor samples with locally exploitative samples, integrated with a modified ==Cross-Entropy Method== (CEM) update and a fully vectorized JAX implementation for real-time control.

## Results

- MTP variants (MTP-Bspline, MTP-Akima) consistently achieved lower cumulative costs and higher task success compared to standard MPC (MPPI, PS) and evolutionary strategies in tasks like PushT, Cube-In-Hand, and Navigation.
- MTP-Akima demonstrated the highest entropy curve and lowest cost convergence in navigation tasks, effectively bypassing large local minima, and enabled successful humanoid standup (G1-Standup) due to aggressive yet smooth interpolation.
- Computational benchmarks confirm that MTP maintains real-time planning rates comparable to baselines after JIT compilation, validating its practical feasibility for GPU-accelerated robotic control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reforming control sampling as a tensor operation over randomized multipartite graphs provides theoretical guarantees for asymptotic path coverage and maximum entropy in the control trajectory space.
> - The β-mixing mechanism effectively balances global exploration from tensor sampling with local exploitation from CEM-like updates, proving crucial for navigating complex, multimodal control landscapes.
> - Full vectorization using JAX and MuJoCo XLA enables the entire sampling, rollout, and update pipeline to operate in real-time, making sophisticated exploration practically feasible on modern hardware.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Sampling-based Model Predictive Control (MPC) is an established framework for controlling nonlinear and contact-rich systems due to its model-agnostic nature and inherent parallelism. However, a limitation of many existing sampling-based MPC methods, including those based on local perturbation strategies or the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM), is their susceptibility to local minima and mode collapse. These methods typically refine nominal trajectories or current best samples, leading to locally greedy search behavior. This issue is exacerbated by the curse of dimensionality, where the number of samples required for global exploration grows exponentially with the planning horizon and control dimension, posing a bottleneck for computational resources. While evolutionary strategies (ES) have been applied to enhance exploration in MPC, the paper indicates that they may not consistently explore multimodal control landscapes, sometimes resulting in inconsistent performance.

This work addresses the challenge of achieving globally diverse and high-entropy control trajectory generation within sampling-based MPC. It aims to develop a framework that can systematically explore the control space more effectively than existing methods, thereby mitigating issues of local minima and mode collapse, without compromising real-time performance. The research contributes to the broader trend of developing computationally efficient and robust control strategies for complex robotic systems, particularly by leveraging vectorized computation on modern hardware, as seen in recent advancements in both sampling-based MPC vectorization (e.g., MPPI, PS, DIAL-MPC, STORM) and general motion planning vectorization (e.g., RRT-based methods, trajectory optimization). Model Tensor Planning (MTP) positions itself as a unified sampling-based control framework that integrates global exploration with local refinement through a tensorized computational pipeline.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce Model Tensor Planning (MTP), a sampling-based Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework designed to enhance global exploration in control trajectory generation while maintaining real-time applicability.

The specific objectives and motivations supporting this aim are detailed as follows:

- **Objective 1: Introduce High-Entropy Control Trajectory Generation.**
 - **Motivation:** Existing sampling-based MPC methods often suffer from poor exploration, as their locally greedy sampling schemes (e.g., perturbing a nominal trajectory or refining current best samples) can lead to suboptimal solutions or mode collapse in complex, high-dimensional, and contact-rich environments. The research aims to overcome this by introducing a mechanism that generates control trajectories with higher entropy, facilitating a broader search of the control space.

- **Objective 2: Ensure Smooth and Globally Diverse Control Candidates.**
 - **Motivation:** Raw, unstructured sampling can lead to jerky or dynamically infeasible control inputs. Smoothness and continuity are crucial for realistic robot execution and system stability. The work proposes to achieve this by sampling over randomized multipartite graphs and interpolating control trajectories using splines (B-splines and Akima splines). These interpolation methods are intended to provide continuous and flexible control inputs that simplify parameterization and adhere to dynamic constraints.

- **Objective 3: Balance Exploration and Exploitation.**
 - **Motivation:** While global exploration is necessary to avoid local minima, purely explorative sampling might be inefficient or unstable for fine-grained control refinement. Conversely, purely exploitative methods can miss better global solutions. The research aims to address this by proposing a β-mixing strategy. This mechanism is designed to blend globally exploratory samples (generated by tensor sampling) with locally exploitative samples (derived from the modified Cross-Entropy Method update), thereby balancing the need for discovering new solutions with refining existing promising ones.

- **Objective 4: Provide Theoretical Justification.**
 - **Motivation:** A robust sampling strategy benefits from theoretical underpinnings regarding its coverage properties. The work aims to theoretically demonstrate that MTP’s sampling scheme can achieve asymptotic path coverage and maximum entropy in the control trajectory space in the limit of infinite tensor depth and width. This theoretical analysis provides a basis for the method's ability to approximate the full space of feasible control paths.

- **Objective 5: Enable Real-time Control with Modern Hardware.**
 - **Motivation:** For practical robotic applications, control algorithms must operate in real-time. This necessitates efficient computation, especially in high-dimensional settings. The work aims to design MTP to be fully vectorized using JAX and compatible with MuJoCo XLA, supporting Just-in-time (JIT) compilation and batched rollouts. This design choice is intended to facilitate high-throughput simulation and integration with online domain randomization, crucial for robust control performance in varied conditions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Model Tensor Planning (MTP) is a sampling-based MPC framework designed to improve control trajectory exploration. The methodology consists of a structured tensor sampling strategy, spline-based control trajectory interpolation, a mechanism to blend exploratory and exploitative samples, and a modified CEM update rule, all implemented for vectorized computation.

#### 3.1. Tensor Sampling

MTP reformulates control sampling as a tensor operation over randomized multipartite graphs.
- **Definition of Random Multipartite Graph:** A graph G(M, N) is constructed with M layers (L_i). Each layer L_i contains N control-waypoints, which are sampled uniformly from the control space U. Edges are defined as pairwise connections between waypoints of consecutive layers (from L_i to L_i+1), forming a complete M-partite directed graph. The control-waypoints are represented as a tensor Z ∈ R^(M × N × n).
- **Path Generation:** To generate a batch of B control paths, MTP subsamples with replacement (Algorithm 1) from the set of all combinatorial paths within G(M, N). This process generates control waypoints C ∈ R^(B × M × n), which are then interpolated into full control trajectories. The sampling with replacement is noted for its computational efficiency (O(MN)) and scalability with vectorized operations.

#### 3.2. Control Path Interpolation

MTP employs spline-based interpolation to generate smooth and dynamically feasible control trajectories from the sampled waypoints.
- **Linear Interpolation:** A baseline approach that generates trajectories by linearly connecting equidistant points between layers. While simple, it can introduce discontinuities. It is used primarily for theoretical analysis.
- **B-spline Parameterization:** This method expresses control trajectories u(t) as a linear combination of K control points z_i, weighted by B-spline basis functions B_i,p(t). The B-spline functions are defined by a series of time knots and a polynomial degree 'p'. B-splines ensure smoothness and keep the trajectory within the convex hull of the control points, resulting in conservative behavior.
- **Akima-spline Parameterization:** This is a piecewise cubic interpolation method that ensures C1 smoothness. Given a set of control points, it constructs a cubic polynomial for each interval between points, determined by conditions of smoothness and interpolation. Akima splines are noted for tracking control-waypoints smoothly without the oscillations or overshooting that can occur with other methods.

#### 3.3. Path Coverage Guarantee

The research provides a theoretical analysis of the tensor sampling method with linear interpolation.
- **Theorem 1 (Asymptotic Path Coverage):** It is shown that for any uniformly continuous path with bounded variation (u ∈ F), a path g in G(M, N) can approximate u arbitrarily well as the number of layers M and samples per layer N approach infinity. This implies that the support of the path distribution generated by G(M, N) converges to the set of all feasible paths F, and the sampling scheme can achieve maximum entropy in the control trajectory space in the limit.

#### 3.4. Algorithm Integration (Algorithm 2)

MTP integrates tensor sampling with a modified Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) update rule and a mixing strategy.
- **β-mixing Mechanism:** A simple mixing rate β ∈ is introduced to balance global exploration and local exploitation. For each planning step, a proportion ⌊βB⌋ of samples (U_G) are generated using tensor sampling (high-entropy, globally exploratory), while the remaining (B - ⌊βB⌋ - 1) samples (U_Local) are drawn from a Gaussian distribution based on the current nominal control (μ, σ) (local, exploitative). The current nominal control μ is also included as a candidate.
- **Batch Rollout and Cost Evaluation:** The combined batch of control trajectories U is rolled out through the dynamics model (f(x, u)) using a vectorized simulator, and a cumulative cost J(τ, U) is computed for each trajectory.
- **Modified CEM Update:** Instead of a simple mean and variance update, MTP employs a softmax weighting on the elite set of trajectories (those with the lowest costs). This weighting scheme, similar to MPPI, computes new mean (μ') and variance (σ') parameters, which are then smoothed with an exponential factor α. A minimum variance (σ_m) is enforced for stability.
- **Control Application:** The first control input of the best-evaluated candidate trajectory is applied to the system.

#### 3.5. Vectorized Implementation

The entire MTP framework is designed for efficient, real-time control.
- **JAX and MuJoCo XLA:** The implementation leverages JAX's Just-in-time (JIT) compilation and vectorized mapping (vmap) capabilities, compatible with MuJoCo XLA. This allows for high-throughput batch sampling, rollout evaluation, and online domain randomization, where cost evaluation can be averaged over multiple randomized model instances. Fixed tensor shapes are used across subroutines for optimization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across various challenging robotic tasks aimed to evaluate MTP's performance against standard MPC and evolutionary strategy baselines, and to analyze the effectiveness of its design components.

#### 4.1. Performance Comparison Against Baselines

- **General Performance:** MTP variants (MTP-Bspline and MTP-Akima) generally demonstrated improved performance in task success and control robustness compared to standard MPC methods (MPPI, PS) and evolutionary strategies (OpenAI-ES, DE).
- **Exploration in Challenging Tasks:**
 - **PushT and Cube-In-Hand:** MTP variants consistently achieved lower cumulative costs, indicating superior performance. Standard MPC methods (MPPI, PS) frequently encountered mode collapse due to insufficient exploration. Evolutionary algorithms (OpenAI-ES, DE) showed better exploration than MPPI/PS in PushT but sometimes fell short of MTP in Cube-In-Hand due to noisy rollouts or difficulty maintaining intricate control.
 - **Crane:** MTP variants maintained stable control trajectories and smooth transitions even with significant modeling errors and underactuation, outperforming baselines. The conservative nature of B-spline interpolation was noted as beneficial here.
 - **Navigation:** MTP-Akima exhibited the highest entropy curve and the lowest cost convergence, effectively finding optimal paths that bypassed large local minima where other baselines struggled (Figure 3). MTP-Bspline showed moderate entropy, still outperforming evolutionary strategies in this specific task.
 - **G1-Standup:** MTP-Akima enabled effective humanoid standup, attributed to its aggressive yet smooth trajectory interpolation, which facilitated efficient exploration and rapid convergence. Evolutionary algorithms struggled more with the high dimensionality of this task.
 - **G1-Walk:** MTP variants showed marginally higher performance and better control stability for extended periods compared to standard MPC baselines, before succumbing to stability challenges inherent in long-horizon locomotion.
- **Simple Task Performance:** In tasks with simpler dynamics and less sensitivity to sampling distribution, such as Walker (without modeling error), MTP and classical MPPI/PS performed similarly.

#### 4.2. Design Ablation

- **Impact of Mixing Rate (β) and Number of Elites (E):** Experiments on PushT and G1-Walk (Figure 5) revealed that MTP's performance is sensitive to β and E.
 - **Mixing Rate (β):** Moderate values of β generally led to significantly lower costs in PushT, while the absence of tensor sampling (β=0, equivalent to PS/MPPI) yielded poor performance. This supported the effectiveness of the β-mixing strategy.
 - **Number of Elites (E):** The choice of E was crucial in tasks like G1-Walk. Using a single elite (E=1, akin to PS) resulted in unstable behavior, whereas MPPI-like updates (using more elites with softmax weighting) combined with tensor sampling improved performance. Pure tensor sampling (β=1) with a fixed rollout budget sometimes degraded performance in G1-Walk, as global samples became too sparse for fine-grained control.
- **Softmax Weighting:** Ablations demonstrated that applying softmax weighting to elite candidates resulted in smoother and lower-variance control updates over time, which supported effective mixing of global and local samples (Figure 9).

#### 4.3. Sensitivity Ablation

- **Sensitivity to β (Figure 6):**
 - **PushT:** Showed minimal sensitivity to β, implying that the task's lack of significant failure modes allowed consistent success regardless of exploration-exploitation balance.
 - **Cube-In-Hand:** Demonstrated high sensitivity; larger β values often caused instability (cube falling), indicating that lower β values were optimal for this task's precision requirements.
 - **G1-Standup:** A smaller β helped stabilize control, leading to more consistent stand-up performance.
- **B-spline Degree (p):** Ablations on B-spline degrees (p=2 to p=5) showed minimal performance differences across tasks (Figure 7), suggesting that lower degrees could be chosen for computational efficiency.
- **Graph Parameters (M, N):** Sweeping M (number of layers) and N (waypoints per layer) on the Navigation task (Figure 8) revealed an optimal range for M. Increasing M initially improved performance by adding path complexity but degraded performance beyond a certain point due to sample sparsity (fixed B). Increasing N consistently improved performance but with higher computational cost.

#### 4.4. Computational Efficiency

- The JAX-based, JIT-compiled implementation demonstrated real-time planning capabilities. While initial JIT compilation incurred a significant one-time cost, per-step planning rates after compilation were similar across MTP, MPPI, PS, and evolutionary baselines for comparable batch sizes (Tables 5, 6, 7, 8). This confirms MTP's practical feasibility for real-time control in GPU-accelerated environments like MuJoCo XLA.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Model Tensor Planning (MTP) contributes to the field of robotic control by addressing a critical limitation in sampling-based Model Predictive Control (MPC): the balance between exploration and exploitation, particularly the generation of high-entropy, globally diverse control trajectories.

**Enhanced Exploration for Complex Robotics:** MTP's core contribution is its structured tensor sampling mechanism, which provides a theoretically justified method for achieving asymptotic path coverage and maximum entropy in the control trajectory space. This capability enables MTP to systematically explore complex, multimodal control landscapes more effectively than conventional locally-greedy MPC methods or even some evolutionary strategies. This can lead to more robust and potentially globally optimal solutions in high-dimensional, contact-rich robotic tasks such as dexterous manipulation, humanoid locomotion, and underactuated system control.

**Practical Real-time Applicability:** MTP is designed with real-world deployment in mind. Its full vectorization using JAX and compatibility with MuJoCo XLA, along with support for Just-in-time (JIT) compilation and batched rollouts, facilitates high-throughput computation. This ensures that the framework can operate in real-time, even when incorporating online domain randomization, which is crucial for robustness in uncertain environments. The computational benchmarks confirm its efficiency compared to other state-of-the-art methods.

**Balanced Control and Adaptability:** The proposed β-mixing strategy offers a flexible mechanism to combine globally exploratory samples with locally exploitative refinements. This allows for tailoring the algorithm's behavior to specific task requirements, promoting stability and precision when needed (e.g., lower β values in G1-Standup) while still enabling significant exploration to escape local minima (e.g., higher β values in Navigation). The use of B-spline and Akima-spline interpolations ensures that the generated control trajectories are smooth, continuous, and dynamically feasible, which is essential for actual robot execution and preventing wear or instability.

**Broader Impact on Autonomous Systems:** MTP's ability to facilitate robust exploration could benefit a wide range of autonomous decision-making systems beyond robotics, particularly those operating in underexplored or uncertain high-dimensional environments. This could include applications in autonomous vehicles, manufacturing, or complex logistics where discovering novel, effective control sequences is paramount.

**Foundations for Future Research and Digital Twin Integration:** The framework establishes a foundation for future research directions, such as integrating task-specific priors or learning-based proposal distributions to further improve sample efficiency. It also highlights the potential for seamless integration with "digital twin" architectures—where real-time state estimation and high-fidelity simulators are tightly coupled—to enable robust and scalable autonomous control in hardware. However, the authors acknowledge the engineering challenges in ensuring low-latency communication and safety-aware control filtering for practical deployment.
