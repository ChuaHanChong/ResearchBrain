# Model Tensor Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> Model Tensor Planning (MTP), a sampling-based Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework, enhances control trajectory exploration using tensor sampling and spline interpolation. MTP demonstrates improved task success and lower cumulative costs across various challenging robotic tasks compared to baselines, achieving real-time performance through vectorized JAX implementation.

## Problem

- Many sampling-based MPC methods suffer from local minima and mode collapse due to locally greedy search strategies in complex, high-dimensional control landscapes.
- The curse of dimensionality in MPC leads to exponentially increasing sample requirements for global exploration with longer planning horizons and control dimensions.
- Existing sampling methods struggle to generate globally diverse, high-entropy control trajectories while ensuring real-time applicability and smooth, dynamically feasible control inputs.

## Method

- A tensor sampling strategy over randomized multipartite graphs generates high-entropy control waypoints, reformulating control sampling as a scalable tensor operation.
- Spline-based interpolation (B-spline or Akima-spline) transforms sampled waypoints into smooth and dynamically feasible continuous control trajectories.
- A β-mixing strategy balances globally exploratory tensor samples with locally exploitative samples, integrated with a modified ==Cross-Entropy Method== (CEM) update and a fully vectorized JAX implementation for real-time control.

## Results

- MTP variants (MTP-Bspline, MTP-Akima) consistently achieved lower cumulative costs and higher task success compared to standard MPC (MPPI, PS) and evolutionary strategies in tasks like PushT, Cube-In-Hand, and Navigation.
- MTP-Akima demonstrated the highest entropy curve and lowest cost convergence in navigation tasks, effectively bypassing large local minima, and enabled successful humanoid standup (G1-Standup) due to aggressive yet smooth interpolation.
- Computational benchmarks confirm that MTP maintains real-time planning rates comparable to baselines after JIT compilation, validating its practical feasibility for GPU-accelerated robotic control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reforming control sampling as a tensor operation over randomized multipartite graphs provides theoretical guarantees for asymptotic path coverage and maximum entropy in the control trajectory space.
> - The β-mixing mechanism effectively balances global exploration from tensor sampling with local exploitation from CEM-like updates, proving crucial for navigating complex, multimodal control landscapes.
> - Full vectorization using JAX and MuJoCo XLA enables the entire sampling, rollout, and update pipeline to operate in real-time, making sophisticated exploration practically feasible on modern hardware.
