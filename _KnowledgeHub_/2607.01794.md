# Lightweight Safe Reinforcement Learning for End-to-End UAV Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed a lightweight, end-to-end safe reinforcement learning framework for autonomous UAV navigation using sparse LiDAR in dense environments. The system integrates specialized convolutional networks for perception, a Lagrangian-based Safe PPO algorithm, and curriculum learning to achieve robust collision avoidance and high success rates on resource-constrained platforms, even during high-speed flight.

## Problem

- Many learning-based UAV navigation methods are computationally intensive, limiting deployment on resource-constrained lightweight UAVs.
- Sparse multi-line LiDAR data often fails to explicitly encode collision risk, leading to unstable policy training and reduced generalization in cluttered environments.
- Traditional RL frameworks lack explicit safety mechanisms, potentially causing unsafe exploration and risky behaviors during high-speed navigation or in dense obstacle fields.

## Method

- A lightweight perception network utilizes ==asymmetric== and ==depthwise separable convolutions== to extract collision-risk-aware features from sparse LiDAR data.
- A ==Lagrangian-based== **Safe Proximal Policy Optimization (Safe PPO)** algorithm is employed, incorporating a ==cost critic== and an adaptively updated ==Lagrange multiplier== for explicit safety constraints.
- A ==curriculum learning== strategy with feature reuse and policy reset improves training stability and enables policy generalization across environments with increasing obstacle densities.

## Results

- The full framework achieved a success rate of **0.94531** in environments with **300 obstacles**, substantially outperforming a vanilla PPO baseline (**0.60938**) and other DRL methods.
- The proposed model features a lightweight design with **143k parameters**, significantly fewer than comparable methods like SAC (**694k**) and TD3 (**693k**), reducing computational overhead.
- The learned policy maintained high success rates (around or above **0.94**) across a wide range of flight velocities (**2-11 m/s**) and obstacle densities, demonstrating robust performance under dynamic constraints.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Lightweight convolutional architectures can effectively process sparse LiDAR data into collision-risk-aware features, enabling efficient onboard perception for resource-constrained UAVs.
> - Explicit safety constraints, integrated via a Lagrangian relaxation in Safe PPO, are crucial for stable training and generating inherently safe navigation policies, outperforming implicit safety mechanisms.
> - Curriculum learning with feature transfer and policy reset significantly enhances the stability and generalization ability of reinforcement learning agents in complex, progressively denser environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous navigation for Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) is a critical area of research with applications spanning inspection, environmental monitoring, logistics, and emergency response. Existing approaches for UAV navigation broadly fall into two categories: model-based planning methods and learning-based approaches.

Classical model-based planning algorithms (e.g., A*, RRT) generate collision-free trajectories based on known or reconstructed environment maps. While effective in structured environments, these methods often face limitations in complex, unstructured scenarios with dense obstacles and incomplete perception due to high computational costs and reduced robustness requiring frequent replanning.

Learning-based approaches, particularly Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL), have emerged as an alternative, enabling UAVs to learn navigation policies directly through interaction with the environment. Algorithms such as DQN, DDPG, TD3, SAC, and PPO have been applied to various UAV tasks, leveraging sensory inputs like LiDAR for end-to-end obstacle avoidance. LiDAR-based perception is favored due to its robustness to illumination changes and accurate geometric sensing.

Despite advancements in DRL for UAV navigation, several challenges persist, particularly under conditions of sparse perception and high-speed flight.
1. **Computational Burden:** Many learning-based methods rely on dense sensory inputs or large neural networks, which can be computationally intensive, limiting their deployment on resource-constrained onboard platforms typical of lightweight UAVs. This also increases training complexity.
2. **Sparse Perception Issues:** Lightweight UAVs often use sparse multi-line LiDAR, which has limited vertical resolution. Raw distance measurements from such sensors may not explicitly encode collision risk effectively, making policy training unstable and reducing generalization ability in cluttered environments where identifying navigable gaps and modeling obstacle continuity is difficult.
3. **Lack of Explicit Safety Mechanisms:** Most traditional RL frameworks prioritize maximizing expected rewards without explicitly incorporating safety constraints. This can lead to unsafe exploration during training, unstable learning, and the generation of risky behaviors during deployment, especially in high-speed scenarios or dense obstacle fields. While some safe RL approaches exist, they often enforce safety by projecting policy outputs onto a safe action set, which can introduce instability.

This work positions itself within the safe reinforcement learning paradigm for UAVs, specifically addressing the gap in lightweight, robust, and explicitly safe navigation solutions for sparse LiDAR environments. It aims to develop an integrated framework that overcomes the computational, perceptual, and safety limitations inherent in many existing DRL methods for autonomous UAV navigation in complex, unstructured settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to address the challenges of autonomous UAV navigation in dense environments characterized by sparse perception and dynamic constraints, particularly concerning computational efficiency, training stability, and flight safety.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in the current state of UAV navigation research:

1. **Overcoming Computational and Deployment Constraints:** Many existing learning-based UAV navigation methods utilize dense sensory inputs (e.g., high-resolution 3D LiDAR, depth cameras) or large, high-capacity neural networks. While these can provide rich environmental information, they impose a substantial computational burden, making them unsuitable for deployment on resource-constrained onboard platforms common in lightweight UAVs. This work aims to develop a lightweight solution that is efficient enough for real-time onboard execution.
2. **Improving Robustness with Sparse Perception:** Sparse LiDAR observations, common in lightweight UAVs, often fail to explicitly highlight high-risk regions in cluttered environments. This limited information can lead to unstable policy training and reduced generalization capabilities. A key objective is to design a perception representation that can effectively utilize sparse LiDAR data to encode collision-risk-aware features, thereby improving the agent's understanding of its surroundings.
3. **Ensuring Explicit Safety Guarantees:** Most traditional reinforcement learning frameworks are designed to maximize expected rewards, often overlooking explicit safety constraints. This oversight can result in unsafe exploration, unstable training processes, and the generation of risky flight behaviors, especially crucial during high-speed navigation where reaction times are minimal. The research aims to integrate explicit safety mechanisms into the learning process to prevent collisions and maintain stable, safe flight trajectories. This moves beyond implicit safety via reward shaping or unstable action projection methods.
4. **Enhancing Training Stability and Generalization:** The complexity of dense environments, coupled with sparse perception and the need for safety, often leads to training instabilities and difficulties in generalizing learned policies to varied conditions. The research seeks to combine advanced learning strategies, such as curriculum learning, with robust RL algorithms to improve training stability and ensure the learned policy can generalize effectively across different obstacle densities and flight speeds.

In summary, the core objective is to propose a safety-constrained, perception-control integrated framework for UAV navigation that is lightweight, capable of effectively processing sparse LiDAR observations into collision-risk-aware features, and ensures inherently safe behaviors through a robust learning algorithm and training strategy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology integrates a lightweight perception network, a unified state representation, a hierarchical control framework, a Lagrangian-based Safe PPO algorithm, and a curriculum learning strategy. This aims to enable efficient and safe end-to-end collision avoidance for UAVs in complex environments.

**A. Overall Framework**
The UAV obstacle avoidance navigation problem is formulated as a Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP). The framework employs a shared latent representation derived from sparse LiDAR observations and UAV states, feeding into an Actor, a Critic, and a Cost Critic. The Actor outputs bounded velocity commands via a Beta policy, while the Critics estimate reward and safety cost. Policy updates are performed using a Lagrangian-based objective that balances task performance and safety.

**B. Convolutional Feature Extraction from LiDAR Data**
A lightweight convolutional encoder is used to extract geometric features from sparse LiDAR observations.
- **Architecture:** The network employs asymmetric kernels in early convolutional layers to model horizontal angular resolution and vertical beam coupling. Progressive downsampling expands the receptive field.
- **Depthwise Separable Convolutions (DSConv):** The final two layers utilize DSConv, which decomposes standard convolutions into channel-wise spatial filtering and pointwise feature fusion. This significantly reduces parameter count and computational complexity, suitable for resource-constrained platforms, while retaining the ability to model local geometric structures from sparse data.
- **Proximity-based Representation:** Raw LiDAR distances are converted into proximity values that emphasize nearby obstacles, enhancing feature contrast and helping the network capture local structures. These values are normalized to and arranged as an angle-elevation grid.
- **Output:** The encoder maps convolutional features into a 128-dimensional embedding vector, followed by Layer Normalization for stability.

**C. State Space Definition**
The observation space combines proprioceptive (UAV motion) and exteroceptive (environment perception) information.
- **Proprioceptive State (s_t):** Defined in the drone’s body frame to enhance robustness to global heading variations. It includes the normalized direction vector to the goal (d_B_t), attitude quaternion in the world frame (q_W_t), linear velocity (v_B_t), and angular velocity (ω_B_t) in the body frame.
- **Exteroceptive Perception (X_t):** Provided by a sparse multi-beam LiDAR, represented as a 2D tensor of size R^(1x36x4), discretizing the horizontal direction into 36 angular bins and the vertical into four laser beams. This uses the proximity-based representation.
- **Shared Feature Encoder (f_enc):** The LiDAR observation X_t is processed by the convolutional encoder, and the ego-state s_t is linearly embedded. These are concatenated and mapped by a multilayer perceptron into a unified latent representation (h_t), which serves as input for the Actor, Critic, and Safety Value Network to reduce redundancy and stabilize training.

**D. Reward Function Design**
The overall reward function (r_t) encourages efficient, smooth, and safe navigation by maximizing discounted cumulative return. It comprises:
- **Velocity Tracking Reward (r_track):** Encourages movement towards the goal, defined by the dot product of current linear velocity and goal direction, clipped by a maximum velocity threshold.
- **LiDAR-based Safety Reward (r_safety):** A logarithmic obstacle potential reward based on LiDAR proximity, encouraging the UAV to maintain a safe distance from obstacles. It averages the logarithm of clipped scan distances.
- **Height Constraint Reward (r_height):** A quadratic penalty for flying outside a predefined safe altitude interval (z_min, z_max).
- **Action Smoothness Reward (r_smooth):** A penalty on velocity variation to reduce aggressive maneuvers.
- **Sparse Event Reward (r_event):** Discrete rewards (+100 for goal reached), or penalties (-50 for collision, -20 for abnormal state/boundary exit) at episode termination.

**E. Safe Reinforcement Learning Algorithm Design**
A Lagrangian relaxation-based Safe Proximal Policy Optimization (Safe PPO) algorithm is employed for decision-making.
- **Action Modeling and Actor Network:** A hierarchical control architecture is used, where the RL policy generates high-level body-frame velocity commands (a_t ∈ R^3), which are then tracked by a geometric controller. The actor models the policy using a Beta distribution to output continuous, bounded actions, preventing distortion from Gaussian action clipping. The output distribution parameters (α_t, β_t) are derived from the latent feature h_t.
- **Critic Network:** Estimates the state value function V(s) to provide a baseline for Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE). It outputs a scalar state value from the shared latent representation h_t. Orthogonal initialization is applied to its output layer.
- **Safety Value Network (Cost Network):** Introduces a safety cost function C_total = I_collision + w · C_margin. I_collision is a hard constraint for collision/severe boundary violation. C_margin is a linear penalty activated when the minimum obstacle distance (d_min) falls below a safe threshold (d_safe), providing differentiable gradients. The objective is to maximize reward J_R(π) subject to an expected average cost constraint J_C(π) ≤ d_limit.
- **Lagrangian Relaxation:** A multiplier λ converts the constrained problem into a dual optimization problem: min_λ max_π L(π, λ) = J_R(π) - λ(J_C(π) - d_limit).
- **Cost Critic:** A safety value network V_C_φ(s_t) operates in parallel with the reward value network, estimating expected cumulative cost. Its training objective minimizes the squared error between its estimate and the cost return.
- **Safe PPO Objective:** A joint advantage function A_total(s,a) = A_GAE_R(s,a) - λ A_GAE_C(s,a) is defined. The clipped policy objective L_policy(θ) utilizes this joint advantage, suppressing high-risk actions.
- **Adaptive Update of Lagrange Multiplier:** The multiplier λ is updated via dual gradient ascent: λ_k+1 = ReLU(λ_k + η_λ (C_k - d_limit)). This increases constraint strength during safety violations and relaxes it when safety improves. A linear learning rate decay is also applied.

**F. Curriculum Learning via Feature Reuse and Policy Reset**
A feature-transfer-based curriculum learning strategy is adopted to mitigate training instability in increasing obstacle densities.
- **Staged Transfer:** The model is first pretrained in a low-density environment.
- **Weight Transfer:** The pretrained model's weights, specifically those of the shared feature encoder, are preserved and transferred to higher-density environments. This reuses learned environmental representation capabilities.
- **Policy Reset:** The output layer of the Actor network is orthogonally reinitialized during transfer. This prevents overfitting to the previous environment and restores high-entropy exploration, allowing the agent to adapt its policy efficiently to new, more complex scenarios within the already learned feature space.

**G. Simulation Environment and Task Setup**
Experiments are conducted in a high-fidelity 3D forest-like environment on NVIDIA Isaac Sim with OmniDrones. The 40m x 40m environment features cylindrical obstacles (100, 200, or 300) with randomized widths (0.4-1.0m) and heights (1.0-5.0m) to create multi-scale occlusions. The UAV is equipped with a sparse multi-beam LiDAR (1x36x4 tensor, 4.0m range). The task involves navigating from a random start to a target, avoiding collisions, and maintaining altitude.

**H. Evaluation Metrics**
Performance is evaluated using:
- **Success Rate (SR):** Proportion of successful episodes without collision.
- **Collision Rate (CR):** Proportion of episodes terminated by collision.
- **Average Return (AR):** Mean cumulative reward per episode.
- **Average Episode Length (AEL):** Average number of steps per episode.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation demonstrates the effectiveness of the proposed framework across varying obstacle densities and flight speeds, showing improvements over existing reinforcement learning baselines.

**A. Module Contribution under Increasing Complexity**
Module-wise evaluations in environments with 100, 200, and 300 obstacles demonstrated the cumulative impact of the proposed components:
- **Vanilla PPO:** Showed a decrease in Success Rate (SR) from 0.83594 (100 obstacles) to 0.60938 (300 obstacles), indicating limited capability in cluttered environments with sparse LiDAR.
- **PPO + DSC (Depthwise Separable Convolutions):** Introducing the lightweight convolutional encoder improved SR across all scenarios, with more pronounced gains in the 300 obstacles setting (SR up to 0.66406 from 0.60938). This suggests that the feature extraction module enhances the capture of local geometric information from sparse LiDAR.
- **PPO + DSC + Safe RL:** Incorporating explicit safety constraints further improved SR significantly, reaching 0.96875 (100 obstacles), 0.90625 (200 obstacles), and 0.86719 (300 obstacles). This highlights the stabilizing effect of safety mechanisms in guiding the policy towards safer behaviors.
- **Ours (Full Model with Curriculum Learning):** Integrating curriculum learning resulted in additional performance gains, particularly in the denser 200 and 300 obstacles scenarios, achieving SRs of 0.95313 and 0.94531, respectively. This indicates that staged training effectively mitigates performance degradation in increasing environmental complexity.

These results suggest that the perception module, safety mechanism, and curriculum learning collectively enhance navigation robustness and reliability.

**B. Robust Navigation under Increasing Obstacle Density**
Comparison with representative RL baselines (PPO, SAC, TD3, PPO+GRU, PPO+LSTM) showed:
- **Superior Performance:** The proposed "Ours" method consistently achieved the highest success rates (0.96875 at 100 obstacles, 0.95313 at 200, 0.94531 at 300) across all obstacle densities, demonstrating stronger robustness and stability in cluttered scenarios compared to baselines which exhibited noticeable degradation with increased density.
- **Recurrent Variants:** While "Ours+GRU" and "Ours+LSTM" showed marginal improvements in some cases, these were associated with significantly higher computational costs (2.4x and 2.5x longer training times, respectively) without proportional performance benefits. SAC and TD3 also required approximately 2.7x more training time.
- **Average Return:** The proposed method also achieved higher average returns across all scenarios, indicating stronger overall performance in balancing task efficiency and safety constraints.

**C. Model Complexity and Efficiency**
An analysis of network parameters revealed:
- **Lightweight Design:** The proposed model has 143k parameters in its main network, substantially fewer than SAC (694k), TD3 (693k), and recurrent PPO variants (over 1 million, with target networks adding further complexity).
- **Computational Efficiency:** This lightweight design reduces computational overhead and accelerates convergence, making it more suitable for resource-constrained onboard deployment. The study concluded that the non-recurrent version of their model (Ours) was sufficient for robust navigation under sparse LiDAR, given its performance and efficiency.

**D. High-Speed Flight under Constraints**
Evaluation of navigation success rate across maximum flight velocities (2–11 m/s) and obstacle densities (100, 200, 300) revealed:
- **High Performance across Velocities:** The policy maintained high success rates (around or above 0.94) across a wide velocity range.
- **Optimal Velocity:** Performance peaked near 6 m/s in the 100 obstacles environment (SR ~0.99), indicating a balance between maneuverability and control stability.
- **Degradation at Higher Speeds:** As velocity increased beyond this optimal range, the SR gradually decreased, with degradation becoming more pronounced in denser environments (e.g., in the 300 obstacles scenario, a noticeable drop occurred above 8 m/s). This reflects the increased difficulty of collision avoidance with reduced reaction time.
- **Robustness:** Despite degradation at very high speeds in dense environments, the proposed method maintained competitive performance, demonstrating robustness and stable generalization across varying dynamic constraints.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presents a significant contribution to the field of autonomous UAV navigation, particularly for resource-constrained platforms operating in complex, unstructured environments with sparse sensory data.

**Key Contributions:**
- **Addressing Lightweight Deployment:** By developing a lightweight perception network utilizing asymmetric and depthwise separable convolutions, the work directly addresses the computational burden that often limits the onboard deployment of learning-based navigation systems on UAVs. This enhances the practical applicability of DRL for real-world UAV systems.
- **Enhancing Safety Guarantees:** The integration of a Lagrangian-based Safe PPO algorithm provides explicit safety constraints within the learning framework. This moves beyond implicit reward shaping, leading to more stable training, reduced risky behaviors, and higher success rates in collision avoidance, particularly critical for operations in dense environments and at high speeds. This contributes to building trust and reliability in autonomous systems.
- **Robustness to Sparse Perception:** The method effectively extracts collision-risk-aware features from sparse LiDAR observations using its specialized convolutional encoder and proximity-based representation. This is a crucial step towards enabling robust navigation for UAVs equipped with more affordable and lighter LiDAR sensors.
- **Improved Training Efficiency and Generalization:** The strategic use of curriculum learning, specifically feature reuse and policy reset, significantly improves training stability and allows the learned policy to generalize across varying environmental complexities (obstacle densities). This makes the training process more sample-efficient and the resulting policies more adaptable.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Wider Adoption of Autonomous UAVs:** The lightweight and safe nature of the proposed framework could facilitate the broader adoption of autonomous UAVs in applications such as industrial inspection, environmental monitoring, and search and rescue in complex, previously challenging environments like dense forests or urban canyons.
- **Increased Safety in Operations:** Explicit safety mechanisms contribute to a safer operational envelope for UAVs, reducing the risk of accidents and damage, which is vital for both regulatory compliance and public acceptance.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The integrated perception-control architecture and the effectiveness of combining specialized convolutions with safe reinforcement learning and curriculum strategies provide a foundation for future research in several directions. This includes exploring adaptation to more diverse and dynamic environments, improving robustness to various sensing noises and failures, and potentially extending to multi-UAV coordination under safety constraints.
- **Hardware-Software Co-design:** The emphasis on a lightweight network design can further motivate hardware-software co-design efforts to optimize computational resources for onboard AI inference in autonomous systems.

Overall, this work moves towards bridging the gap between theoretical advancements in reinforcement learning and the practical requirements for reliable and safe autonomous UAV deployment in challenging real-world scenarios.
