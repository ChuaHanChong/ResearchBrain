# HyCodePolicy: Hybrid Language Controllers for Multimodal Monitoring and Decision in Embodied Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> HyCodePolicy is a closed-loop code-as-policy framework for robot manipulation. It decomposes instructions into subgoals, synthesizes geometry-grounded programs, and runs them in simulation. A VLM watches key checkpoints while symbolic logs are fused with its diagnoses to localize failures and drive targeted code repair. Average success rises from 47.4% to 63.9% on RoboTwin 1.0 and from 62.1% to 71.3% on Bi2Code.

## Problem

- Code-as-policy methods commit to one-shot program generation and depend entirely on that single attempt being correct, despite perception noise, execution errors, and dynamic environments in real robotic tasks.
- Existing systems lack mechanisms to adaptively monitor execution, detect and diagnose failures, and repair behavior in a closed loop, so they need extensive human debugging and recovery.
- Symbolic execution logs alone cannot attribute failures caused by subtle visual or semantic inconsistencies, and self-refinement methods are often tested only under idealized conditions without perceptual grounding.

## Method

- ==Hierarchical subgoal decomposition== plus ==program synthesis== conditioned on an API list, exemplars, and ==Geometric Operation Primitives== (point primitives for grasp/place/util sites, axis primitives for approach/orientation) yields physically executable code.
- Simulated execution runs ten trials with structured ==symbolic logs==, while a VLM agent captures observations at ==observation hooks== inserted only after operations causing visible state changes, then judges subgoal success and infers causes.
- ==Hybrid failure attribution== fuses VLM diagnoses with symbolic logs, analyzing only the most diagnostically salient trial, and drives ==grammar-constrained iterative code repair==; **Bi2Code** is the re-engineered dual-arm interface with structured prompts, logging, and observation hooks.

## Results

- On RoboTwin 1.0, average success rate rises from **47.4%** (Code as Policies) and **60.4%** (CodeAct) to **63.9%** with HyCodePolicy. On Bi2Code it rises from **62.1%** and **66.7%** to **71.3%**.
- Convergence is faster: iterations to exceed **50%** success fall to **1.76** on Bi2Code versus **2.42** on RoboTwin 1.0. Bi2Code also cuts generated code from **1236.6** to **569.4** tokens and raises AST similarity from **23.72%** to **44.78%**.
- Multimodal feedback helps most on perception-heavy tasks, e.g. Beat Block Hammer **48%** to **56%** and Place Container Plate **79%** to **91%**. Zero-shot on all 50 tasks averages **43.34%**, with **0%** on press, scan, shake, pull, and pour.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Treat generated programs as falsifiable, evolvable hypotheses: execute, monitor, diagnose, and repair them in a loop rather than trusting one-shot synthesis.
> - Symbolic logs suffice for deterministic, low-ambiguity tasks, but visual diagnosis is needed to explain why failures occur in spatially subtle cases where traces are silent.
> - Modular, interpretable APIs with built-in logging and observation hooks make failures localizable and repairable; missing action primitives, not the feedback loop, cap performance on articulated, non-rigid, and temporal tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "HyCodePolicy" addresses a challenge within the field of language-grounded robotic manipulation, specifically concerning the robustness and adaptability of policies generated by large language models (LLMs) and multi-modal large language models (MLLMs). Recent advancements in these models have enabled robots to interpret high-level natural language instructions and translate them into executable plans. However, a persistent challenge in this paradigm is effectively bridging the semantic expressiveness of natural language with precise, structured, and physically grounded representations necessary for reliable robot execution in uncertain real-world environments.

Prior research has made strides in synthesizing robot actions from language through methods such as direct LLM-generated plans and "code-as-policy" approaches. These methods commonly rely on one-shot generation of a complete behavior plan. However, real-world robotic tasks are characterized by inherent uncertainties stemming from perception noise, execution errors, and dynamic environments. Existing systems often lack robust mechanisms to adaptively monitor task execution, detect and diagnose failures, and subsequently repair the robot's behavior in a closed loop. This limitation leads to reduced robustness, efficiency, and real-world reliability, frequently necessitating extensive human intervention for debugging and recovery.

"HyCodePolicy" positions itself as a framework that unifies code synthesis, geometric grounding, and multi-modal monitoring within a closed-loop programming cycle. It aims to evolve beyond treating generated code as a static output by considering each program as an evolving hypothesis that can be actively validated, evaluated, and corrected through perceptual cues and symbolic reasoning. This approach extends existing work by integrating structured program synthesis with both symbolic and perceptual feedback for closed-loop planning and self-correction, departing from prior MLLM-guided failure attribution methods that often rely on introspection or static logic. The paper highlights its contribution as enabling dynamic feedback to drive targeted repair, thereby enhancing interpretable, robust, and adaptive behavior in real-world tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for HyCodePolicy stems from a critical gap identified in current language-conditioned robot manipulation systems. While multi-modal large language models (MLLMs) offer powerful perceptual grounding for generating code policies, most existing systems do not possess effective mechanisms to adaptively monitor policy execution and iteratively repair them in response to encountered failures. This deficiency results in policies that are not sufficiently robust or efficient for real-world deployment, frequently requiring manual intervention when errors occur.

To address this, the key objectives of the HyCodePolicy framework are:

1. **To establish a closed-loop control framework:** The system aims to seamlessly integrate language-conditioned program synthesis with adaptive multi-modal monitoring and iterative repair. This is intended to improve the robustness and self-correction capabilities of robot policies, moving away from a one-shot generation paradigm.
2. **To facilitate geometry-aware program synthesis:** The framework seeks to decompose natural language instructions into hierarchical sub-goals and generate an initial program that is explicitly grounded in object-centric geometric primitives. This ensures that the synthesized code is not only logically coherent but also physically executable in the robotic environment, reducing initial execution failures.
3. **To implement a robust multi-modal monitoring system:** A key objective is to enable a Vision-Language Model (VLM) to monitor program execution at designated checkpoints. This VLM is designed to identify and localize failures, as well as infer their underlying causes, providing rich perceptual feedback that goes beyond simple error messages.
4. **To develop a hybrid feedback mechanism for causal failure attribution:** The system aims to integrate structured execution logs, which capture program-level events, with the VLM-derived perceptual feedback. This hybrid approach is designed to pinpoint the root causes of failures, enabling more accurate diagnosis and targeted code repairs with minimal human intervention.
5. **To enhance the robustness and sample efficiency of robot manipulation policies:** Ultimately, the framework seeks to demonstrate that this closed-loop, self-correcting program synthesis approach significantly improves the reliability and efficiency of robotic task execution across diverse manipulation scenarios.
6. **To offer a scalable strategy for autonomous decision-making:** By enabling policies to evolve and improve autonomously through multimodal self-assessment and revision, the work aims to provide a scalable method for acquiring long-horizon tasks under uncertainty, embodying a form of policy evolvability.

The overall motivation is to transform robot code from a static execution medium into a dynamic, evolving hypothesis that can be actively validated, monitored, diagnosed, and repaired, thereby reducing reliance on extensive human debugging and promoting greater autonomy in embodied agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HyCodePolicy introduces a closed-loop architecture that reframes program generation as an iterative process of hypothesis validation and refinement. This methodology is structured into four integrated phases:

#### 3.1. Grounding High-Level Intent in Code

This phase focuses on translating natural language instructions into executable robot programs with geometric awareness.
- **Hierarchical Subgoal Decomposition:** Given a natural language instruction, an LLM decomposes it into a sequence of *N* semantically coherent subgoals. Each subgoal represents a high-level behavioral unit that constrains subsequent program synthesis.
- **Structured Program Synthesis:** Code generation is performed through structured prompting. This process is conditioned on a general API list, exemplar function calls, and the generated subgoal constraints. This frames code synthesis as a constrained prediction task to ensure functional and syntactic validity.
- **Geometric Operation Primitives:** To ensure physical executability, the system integrates geometric reasoning through a library of primitives. These abstract physical constraints into two categories:
 - **Point Operation Primitives (P):** Define spatial targets like `p_grasp` (stable grasp point), `p_place` (placement support point), and `p_util` (interaction site).
 - **Axis Operation Primitives (A):** Capture directional alignment such as `a_grasp` (approach axis for grasping), `a_place` (orientation axis for placing), and `a_util` (motion axis for functional tasks).
 These primitives guide the synthesis of subgoal-conditioned code that respects geometric feasibility, aiming to reduce execution-time failures.

#### 3.2. Simulate Execution & Multimodal Monitoring

After code synthesis, the system executes and monitors the generated program.
- **Program Execution and Symbolic Logging:** The initial program is executed in a simulated robotic environment for validation. Each program undergoes ten independent trials to account for stochasticity. Structured symbolic logs are generated, recording success/failure outcomes and diagnostic error messages (e.g., unreachable grasp configurations, invalid function calls). These logs provide low-level feedback on program integrity but are limited in diagnosing subtle visual or semantic inconsistencies.
- **Concurrent Multimodal Observation:** A Vision-Language Model (VLM) agent concurrently monitors the execution. Observation points are strategically inserted into the program by identifying operations that cause visible state changes. This ensures that visual context (RGB-D images, timestamps, step identifiers) is captured at critical state transitions, aligning visual evidence with symbolic execution steps. Observations are also captured at the beginning and end of the execution.

#### 3.3. Hybrid Feedback and Failure Attribution

This phase combines symbolic and perceptual information to diagnose failures.
- **VLM-based Perceptual Verification:** The VLM agent analyzes the sequence of collected visual observations to verify subgoal completion. For any detected failure, the VLM identifies the precise point of deviation within the subgoal's execution and infers a high-level causal hypothesis (e.g., "logic error," "API misuse," "execution failure") based on the perceptual context.
- **Fusing Symbolic and Perceptual Feedback:** The VLM's perceptual diagnostics are fused with the symbolic execution logs. Symbolic traces check procedural integrity, while visual diagnostics localize failures in space and time and characterize their nature (e.g., incorrect grasp angle, missing object alignment). This hybrid diagnosis generates a structured signal that informs the program revision process, moving from mere failure detection to causal understanding.

#### 3.4. Closed-Loop Autonomy via Adaptive Monitoring and Iterative Code Evolution

This phase closes the loop, enabling self-correction.
- **Adaptive Monitoring via Selective Observation and Log-Guided Re-inspection:**
 - **Selective Observation Insertion:** Observation hooks are selectively inserted into the generated code only for operations likely to induce visually observable changes, reducing monitoring overhead.
 - **Selective Trial Analysis:** Across ten stochastic executions, the system aggregates symbolic logs to identify the single most diagnostically salient failure (e.g., severe failure modes, divergent symbolic traces). Visual inspection is then triggered exclusively for this informative instance, ensuring efficient use of perceptual resources. This mechanism acts as an adaptive attention mechanism.
- **Closed-Loop Repair and Policy Evolution:** Based on the fused symbolic and visual diagnostics, a targeted repair cycle is initiated. Faulty operations are localized, and the code-generation agent proposes structured edits (e.g., logic rewrites, API substitutions, geometric parameter retuning). These repairs are constrained by a symbolic grammar and subgoal template to maintain compatibility and executability. The revised program is then re-executed, re-monitored, and re-diagnosed, forming a feedback-driven refinement pipeline. This iterative process allows policies to evolve autonomously, becoming more robust over time.

The experimental setup for HyCodePolicy utilized a re-engineered interface called Bi2Code (built on RoboTwin2.0), which offers dual-arm API support, decomposable and structured prompts, standardized symbolic logging, and embedded observation hooks for multimodal tracing. This interface was designed to optimize the feedback loop and facilitate the effective deployment of HyCodePolicy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated HyCodePolicy against baseline methods (Code as Policies, CodeAct) across 10 robotic manipulation tasks on two platforms: RoboTwin 1.0 and the newly developed Bi2Code interface. Metrics included Average Success Rate (ASR), Top-5 ASR, and Mean Code Revision Iterations (CR-Iter).

#### 4.1. Efficiency and Quality of Bi2Code

The Bi2Code interface, when compared to RoboTwin 1.0 in a one-shot generation setting (Code as Policies), demonstrated architectural improvements:
- **Code Conciseness:** Bi2Code yielded significantly shorter programs (569.4 vs. 1236.6 tokens) and reduced prompt lengths.
- **Code Quality:** It achieved higher structural similarity to human-written code, as indicated by increased AST similarity (+21.06%), CodeBERT similarity (+1.08%), and Unixcoder alignment (+5.97%).
- **Functionality:** Bi2Code enabled dual-arm parallelism through a unified API, a capability absent in RoboTwin 1.0. These improvements were attributed to structured prompting and geometric API modularization, facilitating subsequent feedback-based refinement.

#### 4.2. Impact of Feedback and Multimodal Repair

A hierarchical ablation analysis systematically assessed the impact of different feedback modalities:
- **Overall Performance Improvement (Table 1):**
 - **RoboTwin 1.0:** Moving from "Code as Policies" (47.4% ASR) to "CodeAct" (symbolic feedback) improved ASR to 60.4%. "HyCodePolicy" (symbolic + VLM feedback) further increased ASR to 63.9%.
 - **Bi2Code:** The improvements were more pronounced with Bi2Code. "Code as Policies" achieved 62.1% ASR, "CodeAct" reached 66.7%, and "HyCodePolicy" achieved 71.3% ASR.
- **Convergence Efficiency:** HyCodePolicy significantly reduced the mean code revision iterations (CR-Iter) required to exceed 50% success. With Bi2Code, HyCodePolicy converged in 1.76 iterations, compared to 2.42 iterations under RoboTwin 1.0. This indicates faster and more efficient policy refinement.
- **Success Rate Distribution (Figure 4):** The distribution of task success rates showed that HyCodePolicy in Bi2Code resulted in more concentrated and higher success rates (above 80%), indicating improved consistency and worst-case performance compared to other configurations.

#### 4.3. Role of Multimodal Feedback in Planning

Analysis of per-task success rates (Table 3) revealed where multimodal feedback was most impactful:
- **Tasks Benefiting Most:** Tasks requiring accurate spatial reasoning, precise object alignment, and nuanced perception (e.g., "stack blocks three," "place empty cup," "handover block") showed substantial gains under HyCodePolicy. This suggests that the VLM's perceptual introspection provided crucial "why it failed" signals that symbolic logs alone could not capture.
- **Tasks with Minimal Impact:** Tasks relying on deterministic logic (e.g., "pick dual bottles easy") showed similar performance across all variants, indicating that symbolic feedback was sufficient in low-ambiguity domains.
- **Platform Differences:** The paper notes that direct performance comparisons between RoboTwin 1.0 and Bi2Code in Table 3 are not straightforward due to their distinct motion planning backends (deterministic vs. stochastic Curobo planner in Bi2Code).

#### 4.4. Generalization Across Diverse Tasks

HyCodePolicy was evaluated on the full 50-task suite supported by Bi2Code to assess its generalization capabilities:
- **Strong Zero-Shot Generalization:** The framework demonstrated strong performance in tasks involving structured placement, stacking, and planar manipulation (e.g., "place mouse pad," "stack blocks two," "adjust bottle"). This suggests that the framework's abstractions, compositional prompts, and perceptual repair mechanisms generalize across a range of tasks.
- **Identified Limitations (Table 4, Figure 5):**
 - **Challenging Skills:** The system exhibited limitations in tasks requiring non-rigid object handling ("place bread basket"), articulated motion ("open microwave"), or complex temporal sequencing ("press stapler," "scan object"). Tasks involving skills like "press," "scan," "shake," "pull," and "pour" achieved 0% success due to the absence of these specialized skills in the current API, and the system's inability to simulate them effectively through parameter adjustments or action combinations.
 - **API/World Model Dependence:** These shortcomings were attributed to limitations in the action API, world modeling, and trajectory-level reasoning, highlighting areas for future extension, particularly in expanding the API to support more specialized manipulation skills.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

HyCodePolicy represents a contribution to the field of robotic manipulation by addressing the challenge of robust, self-correcting policy generation from natural language instructions. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Robustness and Reliability:** By integrating a closed-loop framework for program synthesis, multi-modal monitoring, and iterative repair, HyCodePolicy significantly enhances the robustness of robot manipulation policies. This adaptive capability allows robots to detect, diagnose, and recover from failures autonomously, which is crucial for deployment in dynamic and uncertain real-world environments.
- **Increased Sample Efficiency:** The framework's ability to refine policies through targeted repairs and faster convergence (reduced CR-Iter) contributes to improved sample efficiency. This means that functional robotic behaviors can be acquired and optimized with fewer trials and less human oversight, leading to more efficient development and deployment cycles.
- **Reduced Human Intervention:** By enabling self-correcting program synthesis, HyCodePolicy minimizes the need for extensive human debugging and recovery efforts. This automation of the debugging process makes robotic systems more autonomous and scalable, reducing the operational burden on human operators.
- **Causally Grounded Failure Attribution:** The novel hybrid feedback mechanism, which fuses symbolic execution logs with Vision-Language Model (VLM)-based perceptual observations, allows for precise and causally grounded failure attribution. Understanding *why* a policy failed, rather than merely *that* it failed, is critical for generating effective and targeted repairs, leading to more interpretable and stable policies.
- **Bridging Language and Physical Execution:** The approach effectively bridges the semantic expressiveness of natural language instructions with the precise, structured, and physically grounded representations required for reliable robot execution. The integration of geometric primitives into code synthesis ensures that generated policies are physically feasible from the outset.
- **Scalable Strategy for Autonomous Decision-Making:** HyCodePolicy offers a scalable strategy for incorporating multi-modal reasoning into autonomous decision-making pipelines. The concept of "policy evolvability," where programs improve autonomously through iterative self-assessment, is a step towards lifelong learning and adaptability in robotic agents.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The identified limitations, particularly in articulated object manipulation, fine-grained parameter adjustments, deformable object dynamics, and temporal reasoning, provide clear directions for future research. Addressing these areas could lead to more versatile and capable robotic systems. The open-sourcing of the code also facilitates further research and development in this domain.

In essence, HyCodePolicy moves beyond static code generation to create dynamic, evolving robot policies that can learn from their failures, paving the way for more autonomous, interpretable, and resilient embodied agents.
