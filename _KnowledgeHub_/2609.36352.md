# StructRL: Online Structured Reinforcement Learning for Long-Horizon Vision-Language-Action Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> StructRL is an online RL framework for post-training VLA models on long-horizon manipulation. Its main idea is to replace sparse terminal-only reward with rewards for subtasks the simulator can verify. An LLM breaks each task into dependency-ordered subtasks. Rewards are gated on prerequisites and scaled by completion pace, then optimized with PPO. It lifts GR00T-N1.5 on RoboCasa365 from 41.5% to 49.1% success.

## Problem

- Most online VLA RL methods reward only full task success, so the signal becomes vanishingly sparse as horizon grows and successful rollouts become rare.
- Terminal rewards cannot represent partial progress: a rollout failing at the last step earns the same return as one failing at the very beginning.
- Learned progress reward models give scalar estimates that ignore prerequisite structure, need large-model inference during rollouts, and can credit locally useful but invalid out-of-order events.

## Method

- ==LLM subtask decomposition== with Verifiability and Progress checks keeps only subtasks having ==binary simulator-state completion predicates== that indicate real progress. It organizes them into ==ordered dependency groups== (prerequisite sets).
- ==Structure-aware reward gating== pays a subtask's intermediate reward only once, and only after all prerequisites are complete, so out-of-order completions earn no credit.
- ==Dynamic reward pacing== scales each subtask reward by beta/(1+T/T_d), where T is chunks since the last rewarded completion and T_d the demonstration-derived reference duration. Rewards are ==chunk-level== for ==PPO==.

## Results

- On RoboCasa365 with GR00T-N1.5, overall SR reaches **49.1%** vs **41.5%** for SimpleVLA-RL and **38.6%** for SFT. On LIBERO-Long it reaches **96.6%** vs **92.4%**. pi0.5 gains **+3.9** and **+2.2** points.
- In the ablation, subtask rewards give most of the gain (**41.3%** to **47.4%** on RoboCasa365), pacing adds **0.5** and gating adds **1.3**. The longest tasks improve **10.3** points overall.
- It beats the learned Robometer progress reward by **2.4** to **3.2** points on LIBERO-Long. Decomposition density peaks at **50.4%** with about **5** subtasks and drops to **37.6%** at **52**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - When the simulator exposes state, verifiable dependency-aware event rewards can beat learned visual progress models and need no reward-model inference during rollouts.
> - More dense reward is not always better: excessively fine decompositions add misaligned early signals and inflate total shaped reward, ending below terminal-only training.
> - Credit granularity matters: per-chunk PPO advantages exploit intermediate rewards better than GRPO's rollout-summed returns. Shaped rewards can still induce idling after collecting partial credit.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

Consider a single household instruction: put leftovers into a bowl and carry the bowl to the refrigerator. Long-horizon robot tasks like this ask a vision-language-action (VLA) policy to complete a sequence of dependent manipulations from one instruction. A policy trained on demonstrations can drift into poorly covered states after a small mistake.

Ordinary online reinforcement learning (RL) can encounter those states. When it rewards only final success, however, it gives no credit for meaningful partial completion. StructRL replaces that all-or-nothing signal with simulator-checkable task milestones. It credits them only when their prerequisites are met and pays more for completions that arrive sooner relative to demonstrations.

In a paired rollout, both policies begin from the same scene. StructRL completes intermediate object placements and advances to putting the bowl in the refrigerator, while SFT does not reach that final stage.

StoreLeftoversInBowl in RoboCasa365, from the same starting scene: GR00T-N1.5 after SFT (top) and after StructRL (bottom). Playback is 6×, and the task has a 1,700-step horizon. The panel scores both rollouts with the StructRL reward after the fact and labels completed subtasks.

### 2. Making intermediate credit meaningful

The first step is to decide which events count as progress. An LLM decomposes the instruction into candidate subtasks. It retains events that have a binary environment-state completion test and that also indicate progress toward the goal. "Approach Box" is excluded because it has no reliable binary completion criterion. "Open Gripper" is excluded because it does not itself mark meaningful progress.

The retained steps are then arranged into ordered dependency groups. Steps within a group may happen in either order, but later groups must wait until earlier ones are complete. For example, after a lunch box is opened, the chicken and apple additions can occur in either order. Closing the box is eligible for reward only after both additions.

A detected, eligible subtask is rewarded once. A detected step whose prerequisites are unmet receives no intermediate credit. The reward is larger when the step follows the previous rewarded step sooner relative to its demonstration-derived reference duration. Final task success still receives a terminal bonus. The decomposition is produced before RL training, grounded to binary simulator predicates, and then reused, so no LLM is needed during rollout collection.

Overview of StructRL. A "Pack Lunch" command yields candidate subtasks that pass through verifiability and progress checks, which reject "Approach Box" and "Open Gripper." The VLA sends action chunks to the environment. Reward gating decides eligibility, reward pacing sets magnitude, and chunk-level rewards feed a PPO update.

### 3. What the comparisons isolate

The headline comparison uses RoboCasa365 Composite-Seen, which has 16 tasks with 100 episodes per task. Success rate (SR) is the mean of task-level success rates. Online methods start from the same SFT checkpoints and receive the same number of environment interactions. Under these conditions, GR00T-N1.5's overall SR is 49.1% with StructRL. The strongest evaluated online RL baseline, SimpleVLA-RL, reaches 41.5%.

SimpleVLA-RL uses a binary outcome reward, success or failure, for each rollout. A component ablation with PPO and GR00T-N1.5 tests which parts of StructRL's reward account for the gain over that kind of signal. It starts from terminal-only reward, then adds fixed subtask rewards, dynamic pacing, and prerequisite gating. On RoboCasa365, adding fixed subtask rewards raises overall SR from 41.3% to 47.4%. The authors report further improvement with pacing and gating, and a similar overall pattern on LIBERO-Long.

Reward-component ablation on RoboCasa365 and LIBERO-Long, showing SR by horizon bucket and overall; values are means over three evaluation runs. In RoboCasa365 overall, SR rises from 41.3% with terminal-only reward to 47.4% with subtask reward, followed by smaller gains from pacing and gating. In the 1,000–1,400-step bucket, the subtask-reward arm scores 61.2%, compared with 61.0% for complete StructRL.

More intermediate reward is not automatically better. A decomposition-density sweep relaxed the normal filtering criteria to admit fine motion checkpoints. In that sweep, performance peaked at moderate density and declined at the densest setting.

### 4. Closing

StructRL lets training distinguish a nearly finished rollout from an early failure. It credits intermediate steps that the environment can verify, gates those credits by task order, and paces them against demonstration timing. The evaluations are in simulation, and the main completion signals use simulator-state predicates. Shaped rewards also produced some rollouts that idled until timeout after collecting intermediate rewards.
