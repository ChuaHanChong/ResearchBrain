# HumanoidVLM: Vision-Language-Guided Impedance Control for Contact-Rich Humanoid Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Skolkovo Institute of Science and Technology developed HumanoidVLM, a framework integrating vision-language models with retrieval-augmented generation, enabling the Unitree G1 humanoid robot to autonomously adapt its end-effector impedance and grasping based on visual scene understanding. The system achieved 93% retrieval accuracy for appropriate control parameters and demonstrated stable, compliant manipulation across various contact-rich tasks.

## Problem

- Traditional robotic control systems use fixed impedance gains and gripper configurations, limiting adaptability in dynamic human environments and contact-rich manipulation tasks.
- A significant gap exists between the high-level semantic understanding provided by Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and the low-level physical control parameters that govern robot-environment interaction.
- Humanoid robots require adaptive, task-appropriate physical interaction behaviors (e.g., stiffness, damping, grasping) to ensure safety and stability in unstructured human-centric environments.

## Method

- The **HumanoidVLM** framework employs a Molmo-7B-O VLM to process ego-centric RGB images and infer high-level manipulation tasks, converting visual output into an embedded semantic label.
- A ==Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)== module utilizes the VLM's semantic output in a two-stage FAISS-based similarity search to retrieve task-appropriate stiffness, damping, and gripper angle from custom, empirically-derived databases.
- A task-space Cartesian impedance controller then dynamically applies these retrieved parameters to the Unitree G1 humanoid's end-effectors, generating compliant trajectories and managing gripper configurations for stable interaction.

## Results

- The VLM–RAG pipeline demonstrated high accuracy, correctly retrieving impedance and gripper parameters in 13 out of 14 (**93%**) real-world test scenarios.
- During task execution, z-axis tracking errors consistently remained small (typically 1–**3.5** cm maximum absolute error), with computed virtual force magnitudes scaling appropriately with the selected impedance gains.
- The system successfully modulated compliance for diverse tasks, applying softer settings for delicate interactions (e.g., egg placement, surface following) and stiffer gains for tasks requiring pressure (e.g., pushing a massage ball), demonstrating adaptive and stable interaction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision-language models can effectively bridge the gap between high-level semantic perception and low-level continuous physical control parameters for humanoid manipulation.
> - A retrieval-augmented generation approach, using experimentally validated impedance and gripper parameters stored in databases, allows for adaptive, context-aware physical interaction without complex online learning.
> - By selecting specific impedance and gripper settings based on visual task inference, humanoids can achieve task-appropriate compliance, enhancing safety and stability in contact-rich scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research addresses challenges in enabling humanoid robots to operate effectively in unstructured human environments, where tasks demand both semantic understanding and compliant physical interaction. Traditional robotic control systems frequently rely on fixed, manually tuned impedance gains and gripper configurations, which restrict a robot's adaptability to variations in scene geometry, object characteristics, or task requirements. This limitation is particularly evident in contact-rich manipulation tasks, where dynamic adjustment of stiffness, damping, and grasping parameters is crucial for stable and safe execution.

Concurrently, Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have advanced significantly, demonstrating capabilities in open-world perception, contextual reasoning, and grounded language understanding. These models hold promise for high-level task inference in robotics. However, a notable gap exists between the semantic understanding provided by VLMs and the low-level control parameters that govern a robot's physical interaction with its environment. Existing VLM-driven systems often operate at the policy or pose level, typically relying on fixed impedance gains, which restricts their ability to autonomously translate complex visual semantics into adaptable, task-appropriate physical behaviors.

This work positions itself to bridge this gap by integrating VLM-based semantic perception with adaptive, low-level physical control for humanoid robots. Previous related efforts include:
- **Foundation Models and Semantic Manipulation:** Some systems like OmniVIC and ImpedanceGPT have combined VLMs with variable impedance control via retrieval. However, OmniVIC primarily targets industrial arms, neglecting humanoid embodiment, shared workspaces, or human-robot interaction (HRI) safety considerations. ImpedanceGPT focuses on drone swarms, not humanoid manipulation. Other vision-language-action (VLA) systems, such as PG-VLM, SayCan, RT-2, Bi-VLA, and SwarmVLM, demonstrate generalization in manipulation but operate on fixed impedance settings, lacking continuous modulation. Broader surveys on foundation models for manipulation acknowledge semantic reasoning but often do not specifically address continuous impedance modulation in humanoid contexts. Work on foundation models for collaborative assembly emphasizes contextual reasoning in shared autonomy, yet compliant control remains largely unaddressed.
- **Impedance and Variable Impedance Control:** The foundational concept of impedance control, introduced by Hogan, provides a framework for regulating robot-environment interaction through stiffness and damping. Research into variable impedance strategies, including learning-based adaptation, has been documented. In the context of human-robot collaboration, the importance of compliance modulation for safety and ergonomics has been highlighted, building on earlier studies of tactile and impedance-based interaction in humanoid systems.

HumanoidVLM contributes by specifically addressing the unique requirements of humanoid robots. It links semantic scene understanding from VLMs directly to the retrieval and selection of specific, adaptive Cartesian impedance and gripper parameters. This approach aims to enable a humanoid robot to autonomously determine task-appropriate compliance settings, thereby enhancing its adaptability, safety, and stability in contact-rich manipulation tasks within human-centric environments. The work extends the application of variable impedance control by integrating it with semantic-guided parameter selection, explicitly considering humanoid embodiment and HRI-oriented safety by providing context-dependent compliant behaviors.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable a humanoid robot to autonomously adapt its end-effector impedance and grasping behavior based on visual scene understanding and retrieval-based reasoning. Specifically, the system aims to allow the Unitree G1 humanoid to select appropriate Cartesian impedance parameters (stiffness and damping) and gripper configurations directly from an ego-centric RGB image.

The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from several limitations in current robotic systems and the increasing demands placed on humanoid robots:

- **Lack of Adaptability in Unstructured Environments:** Humanoid robots are increasingly deployed in complex, unstructured human environments. Tasks in these environments, such as placing objects, applying force to surfaces, grasping diverse items, or manipulating tools, necessitate a nuanced and adaptive approach to physical interaction. Conventional control pipelines, which rely on fixed, hand-tuned impedance gains and pre-set gripper configurations, severely limit a robot's capacity to adapt to dynamic changes in the scene, object properties, or task intent.
- **Disconnection Between Semantic Understanding and Low-Level Control:** While Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have demonstrated strong capabilities in open-world perception and contextual reasoning, they remain largely disconnected from the low-level control parameters that dictate how a robot physically interacts with its environment. This creates a critical gap where high-level semantic understanding cannot be directly translated into actionable, task-appropriate physical behaviors.
- **Necessity for Task-Appropriate Contact Behavior:** For contact-rich manipulation tasks, the selection of correct stiffness, damping, and grasping configurations is essential for ensuring safe, stable, and effective execution. Without autonomous adaptation, robots are either overly rigid, potentially damaging objects or environments, or too compliant, leading to instability and task failure.
- **Towards Interpretable and Adaptive Humanoid Manipulation:** The development of a framework that links semantic perception to retrieval-based control offers an interpretable pathway toward truly adaptive humanoid manipulation. Such a system would allow robots to understand the context of a task visually and then automatically adjust their physical interaction parameters accordingly, moving beyond rote execution to context-aware behavior.
- **Addressing Humanoid-Specific Challenges:** The Unitree G1, as a humanoid platform, presents specific challenges related to its embodiment, the need for safe interaction in shared workspaces, and the requirement for robust manipulation capabilities. The research is motivated to address these by providing a mechanism for the robot to autonomously determine appropriate compliance settings for a given scene.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The HumanoidVLM framework is designed as a vision-language-driven retrieval system. It enables the Unitree G1 humanoid robot to adapt its end-effector impedance and grasping behavior based on visual scene understanding and retrieval-based reasoning.

**Overall System Architecture:**
The system receives an ego-centric RGB image from the robot's head camera. This image is processed by a Vision-Language Model (VLM) which infers the high-level manipulation task. The semantic task label generated by the VLM is then used by a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) module to retrieve task-appropriate control parameters from custom databases. These parameters are subsequently used by a task-space Cartesian impedance controller for compliant manipulation.

**VLM–RAG System:**
1. **Vision-Language Model (VLM):**
 - **Model Used:** The Molmo-7B-O BnB 4-bit model is employed to process the ego-centric image and identify task-relevant objects and scene context.
 - **Task Inference:** The VLM infers the manipulation task through a series of structured visual queries, specifically sequential yes/no questions.
 - **Embedding:** The visual output from the VLM is converted into a multimodal representation and embedded into a shared vector space using the all-MiniLM-L6-v2 sentence-transformer. This embedding facilitates semantically meaningful retrieval.
2. **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) Module:**
 - **Input:** The embedded semantic task label from the VLM.
 - **Retrieval Mechanism:** The RAG module performs a FAISS-based similarity search to retrieve parameters from two custom databases.
 - **Custom Databases:**
 - **Cartesian Impedance Database:** This database stores experimentally validated stiffness coefficients (𝐾_𝑥, 𝐾_𝑦, 𝐾_𝑧) and damping coefficients (𝐷_𝑥, 𝐷_𝑦, 𝐷_𝑧) for the end-effector. These parameters dictate how the robot's end-effector regulates contact forces and compliance along Cartesian axes. The entries were derived from real-world experiments on the G1 humanoid, where multiple settings were tested per task to identify the most stable and compliant configurations.
 - **Gripper-Angle Database:** This database contains optimal closing angles (𝛾_𝑎) for the robot's grippers, tailored for manipulating specific object types (e.g., rigid, soft, deformable, fragile). These angles were determined through repeated empirical trials to ensure secure yet compliant grasp strategies.
 - **Two-Stage Retrieval Process:** To enhance precision and disambiguate similar tasks, the retrieval operates in two stages:
 1. The VLM's inferred task label is used as an initial FAISS query to retrieve the impedance scenario.
 2. The retrieved impedance scenario description is then concatenated with the VLM task label to form a second query, used to retrieve the appropriate gripper configuration.
 - **Output:** The RAG system outputs the corresponding stiffness (K), damping (D), and gripper angle (𝛾) parameters for the inferred task.

**Robot Control System:**
1. **Parameter Transmission:** The retrieved control parameters (K, D, and 𝛾) are transmitted to the Unitree G1's onboard computer.
2. **End-effector Impedance Control:**
 - **Controller Type:** A task-space Cartesian impedance controller is employed. This controller is designed to generate compliant end-effector trajectories.
 - **Model:** Due to the absence of wrist force-torque sensing and reaction wrench feedback in the G1's grippers, the system adopts a homogeneous Cartesian mass-spring-damper model in task space. Each end-effector is regulated as a 6-Degrees-of-Freedom (DoF) point tracking a desired pose trajectory, which serves as a virtual reference.
 - **Parameters:** The translational impedance parameters K and D are dynamically selected by the VLM-RAG system. The virtual mass M and rotational impedance are kept fixed to maintain stability.
 - **Dynamics:** For each arm 'a', the translational error (e_a) is defined as the difference between the desired reference position (x_a,ref) and the current end-effector position (x_a). The impedance dynamics follow a second-order ordinary differential equation: M_a * ë_a + D_a * ė_a + K_a * e_a = 0.
 - **Virtual Force:** The interaction response is captured by a virtual force (F_virt_a = K_a * e_a + D_a * ė_a), which acts as a quantitative proxy for physical contact forces.
 - **Execution:** Desired virtual poses are converted into joint targets using inverse kinematics and then executed by the robot's built-in position controllers.
3. **Gripper Configuration:**
 - **Control:** Each hand uses a single-DoF gripper. The controller receives a discrete action (open or close) corresponding to a predefined joint angle target retrieved from the gripper database, complementing the impedance parameters.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Robot Hardware:** Experiments were conducted on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot equipped with two 1-DoF grippers and an Intel RealSense RGB-D camera mounted on the robot's head for ego-centric perception.
- **Computational Hardware:** The task-space impedance controller ran on the robot's onboard PC at 50 Hz. The VLM–RAG pipeline was executed on an external workstation equipped with an RTX 4090 GPU and an Intel i9-13900K CPU to ensure real-time inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of HumanoidVLM focused on the correctness of parameter retrieval and the stability and compliance of the robot's interaction behavior in real-world manipulation tasks.

**Experimental Setup for Evaluation:**
Experiments primarily involved tabletop manipulation tasks, with a focus on normal-direction (z-axis) interaction. Five representative task groups were evaluated:
1. **Following an irregular surface:** Maintaining smooth right-hand contact.
2. **Applying controlled z-direction forces:** While holding a massage ball.
3. **Bimanual object placement:** Placing a sauce bottle (left arm) and an egg (right arm).
4. **Tool interaction:** Using a fork to poke a fruit or vegetable.
5. **Grasping and lifting:** Various tabletop objects.
Optimal impedance and gripper parameters for the custom databases were empirically determined through multiple trials for each scenario.

**VLM–RAG Retrieval Evaluation:**
- **Test Scenarios:** 14 ego-centric test images were collected, representing variations of the nine tasks stored in the database. These images included variations in camera viewpoint, object placement, and arm pose to assess robustness against changes beyond simple memorization.
- **Retrieval Correctness Criteria:** A retrieval was deemed correct if the system successfully (1) classified the task via sequential visual yes/no queries, (2) retrieved the correct impedance entry based on the VLM output, and (3) retrieved the correct gripper configuration conditioned on both the VLM task label and the impedance scenario.
- **Retrieval Accuracy:** The VLM–RAG pipeline achieved a retrieval accuracy of 93%, successfully retrieving the correct impedance and gripper parameters in 13 out of 14 tested scenarios.
- **Failure Analysis:** The single retrieval failure occurred in a scenario where the primary object was partially occluded. This suggests a limitation of the current vision-based task inference under occluded conditions.

**Control Evaluation Results:**
To assess the stability and boundedness of the interaction behavior, the system analyzed z-axis tracking errors and virtual force magnitudes during task execution. Performance metrics included the selected normal-direction stiffness (𝐾_𝑧), damping (𝐷_𝑧), mean and maximum absolute position error in 𝑧 (|𝑒_𝑧|), and maximum virtual normal force magnitude (|𝐹_virt_𝑧|).

- **Overall Performance:** Across all evaluated scenarios, 𝑧-axis tracking errors consistently remained small, typically within the range of 1–3.5 cm (maximum absolute error). The computed virtual force magnitudes showed consistent scaling with the selected impedance gains, indicating effective modulation of contact intensity. This confirmed that the retrieved parameters enabled stable and task-appropriate interaction behavior.

- **Task-Specific Observations:**
 - **Surface Following:** For following an irregular surface, soft stiffness settings (𝐾_𝑧 = 3.0 N/m, 𝐷_𝑧 = 2.0 Ns/m) facilitated compliant tracking of the curved geometry. This resulted in low virtual forces (maximum 0.103 arbitrary units) and small position errors (mean 0.016 m, maximum 0.024 m), with the 𝑧-error closely following the surface profile.
 - **Apply Pressure:** When applying pressure to a massage ball, higher stiffness and damping values (𝐾_𝑧 = 5.0 N/m, 𝐷_𝑧 = 3.0 Ns/m) were selected. This led to larger virtual forces (maximum 0.334 arbitrary units) while maintaining comparable tracking accuracy (mean 0.017 m, maximum 0.035 m), demonstrating effective modulation of contact intensity.
 - **Dual-Object Placement:** In the bimanual placement task, asymmetric stiffness values were applied to account for object fragility. The arm handling the egg (right arm) used softer settings (𝐾_𝑧 = 2.0 N/m, 𝐷_𝑧 = 1.0 Ns/m), resulting in a mean 𝑧-error of 0.013 m and a maximum virtual force of 0.089 arbitrary units. The arm handling the sauce bottle (left arm) used stiffer gains (𝐾_𝑧 = 6.0 N/m, 𝐷_𝑧 = 1.5 Ns/m), leading to a mean 𝑧-error of 0.009 m and a maximum virtual force of 0.176 arbitrary units. This demonstrated the system's ability to match compliance to object-specific needs.
 - **Tool Interaction:** For tool interaction (poking a fruit), moderate stiffness and damping (𝐾_𝑧 = 2.0 N/m, 𝐷_𝑧 = 1.5 Ns/m) supported controlled poking motions. This resulted in low tracking errors (mean 0.006 m, maximum 0.016 m) and transient force peaks appropriate for brief contact events.
 - **Grasp and Lift:** In grasp-and-lift tasks, intermediate stiffness settings (𝐾_𝑧 = 4.0 N/m, 𝐷_𝑧 = 1.5 Ns/m) ensured stable lifting without applying excessive pressure. Tracking errors remained below 2.5 cm (mean 0.013 m, maximum 0.022 m).

- **Consistency and Virtual Force Proxy:** The controller consistently exhibited behavior where lower stiffness enabled compliant contact, while higher stiffness facilitated more forceful interaction. The observed clear correlation between selected impedance parameters and the resulting virtual force responses suggests that these virtual forces can serve as effective proxies for real contact forces, despite the absence of direct force sensing.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HumanoidVLM framework presents a notable contribution to the field of humanoid robotics by establishing a functional link between high-level semantic perception and low-level physical control.

**Key Significance:**

- **Proof of Feasibility:** The results demonstrate the feasibility of integrating vision-language models with retrieval-based control to achieve adaptive humanoid manipulation. This establishes a precedent for developing more intelligent and responsive robotic systems.
- **Enhanced Humanoid Adaptability:** By enabling the Unitree G1 humanoid to autonomously select task-appropriate Cartesian impedance and gripper configurations from visual input, the work overcomes limitations of conventional, fixed-parameter control. This allows the robot to adapt its physical interaction behavior to diverse objects and dynamic task requirements in unstructured environments.
- **Bridging AI and Robotics Control:** The framework successfully bridges a gap between advanced artificial intelligence components (VLMs for semantic understanding) and fundamental robotics control (impedance control). This integration enables robots to translate high-level cognitive understanding into precise and compliant physical actions.
- **Improved Safety and Stability:** The consistent achievement of small z-axis tracking errors and virtual force magnitudes that scale appropriately with selected impedance gains indicate improved stability and control during contact-rich tasks. This capability is crucial for enhancing the safety of human-robot interaction in shared workspaces.
- **Interpretability in Adaptive Control:** The use of a retrieval-augmented system, where specific, experimentally validated parameters are retrieved based on explicit semantic task labels, offers an interpretable pathway for understanding how the robot adapts its behavior. This interpretability can be beneficial for debugging, verification, and human trust.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Advancement in Human-Robot Interaction (HRI):** The ability of humanoids to autonomously adjust their compliance and grasping strategies based on context can lead to more natural, safer, and efficient interactions with humans. This is particularly relevant for collaborative tasks and scenarios where robots need to handle delicate objects or interact physically with people.
- **Broader Applications for Humanoid Robots:** The enhanced adaptability and robust compliant control could expand the range of applications for humanoid robots in various domains, including domestic assistance, industrial tasks requiring delicate manipulation, healthcare, and logistics.
- **Foundation for Future Intelligent Control Systems:** This work lays a foundation for future research in several directions. The concept of using small, human-validated databases linked to semantic perception can be scaled and refined.
 - **Enhanced Rotational Impedance:** Incorporating task-dependent rotational impedance would enable more sophisticated orientation-sensitive behaviors, crucial for complex manipulations.
 - **Continuous Parameter Mapping:** Moving from discrete database retrieval to a learned continuous mapping could allow for interpolation of control parameters for unseen tasks and object types, significantly improving generalization capabilities.
 - **Closed-Loop Adaptation with Feedback:** Integrating external force or visuotactile feedback would enable closed-loop impedance adaptation, further enhancing robustness, safety, and responsiveness to unexpected interactions.

While the current evaluation scale and task diversity are limited, indicating a proof of feasibility, the HumanoidVLM framework demonstrates a significant step toward developing humanoids capable of intelligent, adaptive, and compliant manipulation.
