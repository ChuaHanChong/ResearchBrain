# HumanoidVLM: Vision-Language-Guided Impedance Control for Contact-Rich Humanoid Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Skolkovo Institute of Science and Technology developed HumanoidVLM, a framework integrating vision-language models with retrieval-augmented generation, enabling the Unitree G1 humanoid robot to autonomously adapt its end-effector impedance and grasping based on visual scene understanding. The system achieved 93% retrieval accuracy for appropriate control parameters and demonstrated stable, compliant manipulation across various contact-rich tasks.

## Problem

- Traditional robotic control systems use fixed impedance gains and gripper configurations, limiting adaptability in dynamic human environments and contact-rich manipulation tasks.
- A significant gap exists between the high-level semantic understanding provided by Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and the low-level physical control parameters that govern robot-environment interaction.
- Humanoid robots require adaptive, task-appropriate physical interaction behaviors (e.g., stiffness, damping, grasping) to ensure safety and stability in unstructured human-centric environments.

## Method

- The **HumanoidVLM** framework employs a Molmo-7B-O VLM to process ego-centric RGB images and infer high-level manipulation tasks, converting visual output into an embedded semantic label.
- A ==Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)== module utilizes the VLM's semantic output in a two-stage FAISS-based similarity search to retrieve task-appropriate stiffness, damping, and gripper angle from custom, empirically-derived databases.
- A task-space Cartesian impedance controller then dynamically applies these retrieved parameters to the Unitree G1 humanoid's end-effectors, generating compliant trajectories and managing gripper configurations for stable interaction.

## Results

- The VLM–RAG pipeline demonstrated high accuracy, correctly retrieving impedance and gripper parameters in 13 out of 14 (**93%**) real-world test scenarios.
- During task execution, z-axis tracking errors consistently remained small (typically 1–**3.5** cm maximum absolute error), with computed virtual force magnitudes scaling appropriately with the selected impedance gains.
- The system successfully modulated compliance for diverse tasks, applying softer settings for delicate interactions (e.g., egg placement, surface following) and stiffer gains for tasks requiring pressure (e.g., pushing a massage ball), demonstrating adaptive and stable interaction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision-language models can effectively bridge the gap between high-level semantic perception and low-level continuous physical control parameters for humanoid manipulation.
> - A retrieval-augmented generation approach, using experimentally validated impedance and gripper parameters stored in databases, allows for adaptive, context-aware physical interaction without complex online learning.
> - By selecting specific impedance and gripper settings based on visual task inference, humanoids can achieve task-appropriate compliance, enhancing safety and stability in contact-rich scenarios.
