# Safe Learning for Contact-Rich Robot Tasks: A Survey from Classical Learning-Based Methods to Safe Foundation Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from IIT and Purdue University provide the first comprehensive, safety-centric survey on learning-based methods for contact-rich robot tasks, developing a novel taxonomy to categorize approaches from classical control to robotic foundation models. The work systematically outlines how safety is formulated and enforced across different learning phases, task types, sensing modalities, and abstraction levels, while identifying critical challenges for future research.

## Problem

- Robots increasingly need to perform complex, contact-rich tasks (e.g., assembly, HRI) in unstructured, dynamic environments.
- These tasks inherently pose high risks of damage to robots, environments, or humans due to complex, discontinuous dynamics and inherent uncertainties.
- Existing literature lacks a systematic, safety-centric framework for learning-based methods in contact-rich robotics, especially concerning emerging robotic foundation models.

## Method

- Conducted the first comprehensive, safety-centric survey specifically for learning-based methods in contact-rich robotics.
- Proposed a novel, multi-dimensional taxonomy to classify existing approaches by learning phase (safe exploration vs. execution), safety integration level, sensing modalities, and enforcement spaces.
- Systematically analyzed the role and implications of robotic foundation models (Vision-Language Models/Vision-Language-Action Models) for safety in contact-rich tasks.

## Results

- Established a comprehensive taxonomy for safe learning methods, distinguishing between safe exploration and safe execution strategies, rated by guarantee strength and adaptability.
- Characterized the unique safety considerations and challenges for four categories of contact-rich tasks: assembly/insertion, surface interaction, object manipulation, and physical human-robot interaction.
- Identified significant open challenges in the field, including data scarcity, fragility of safety guarantees, limited generalization, safe exploration under physical risk, and safety issues specific to large robotic foundation models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Safety for contact-rich tasks necessitates deep integration throughout the entire robot learning and execution lifecycle, moving beyond general safe learning concepts.
> - A detailed taxonomy encompassing classical control, learning, and foundation models reveals fundamental trade-offs between safety guarantee strength, adaptability, and computational efficiency.
> - Robotic foundation models offer potential for high-level, language-based safety specification but also introduce unique challenges like misinterpretation, hallucination, and overconfident execution of unsafe plans.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotics is increasingly moving from structured industrial settings to unstructured, dynamic environments, demanding sophisticated interaction capabilities. Contact-rich tasks, such as assembly, insertion, polishing, and human-robot physical collaboration, are at the forefront of this shift. However, these tasks are inherently challenging due to complex, discontinuous dynamics, inherent uncertainties, and the high risk of damage to the robot, environment, or humans. While traditional model-based control struggles with these complexities, learning-based methods offer adaptability and generalization through data-driven approaches.

This survey meticulously positions itself within this evolving landscape by addressing a critical, yet often fragmented, aspect: **safety in learning-based contact-rich robotic tasks.** The authors highlight that existing surveys tend to cover safe learning and reinforcement learning (RL) generally, often neglecting the nuances of physical interaction, or focus on manipulation strategies without deeply integrating learning-based safety frameworks. Furthermore, while recent efforts touch upon physical risk in the context of data-driven and foundation model-based robotics, they often lack specificity for contact-rich manipulation.

The unique contribution of this work is its **safety-centric focus** at the intersection of safe learning and contact-rich robotics. It systematically reviews how safety principles are formulated, enforced, and evaluated across both classical learning methods and the rapidly emerging field of robotic foundation models, particularly Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Vision-Language-Action Models (VLAs). By providing a structured overview, identifying unique challenges posed by physical contact, and explicitly addressing the implications of foundation models, the survey aims to bridge the current gaps and provide timely guidance for developing safe, capable, and trustworthy autonomous systems that can effectively operate in the real world. This emphasis on VLM/VLA integration for safety, from language-level constraint specification to multimodal grounding, is particularly forward-looking and distinguishes it from prior literature.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation for this survey stems from the urgent need for principled and comprehensive safe learning as robots increasingly undertake complex, contact-rich tasks in uncertain environments. The inherent risks of physical interaction—ranging from robot damage to human injury—demand that safety be paramount during both the learning and deployment phases.

The key objectives of this survey are:

- **Present a Safety-Centric Survey:** To offer the first comprehensive survey that places safety at the core of learning for contact-rich robotic tasks, contrasting with existing literature that often treats safety as a secondary consideration or in a more general context.
- **Categorize and Analyze Existing Approaches:** To systematically classify and analyze a wide range of learning-based methods based on a novel taxonomy. This includes distinguishing between approaches for *safe exploration* (minimizing risk during learning) and *safe execution* (ensuring robustness and constraint satisfaction during deployment).
- **Contextualize within Contact-Rich Tasks:** To specifically detail how safety constraints are formulated and enforced in the unique context of contact-rich tasks (e.g., assembly, surface interaction, physical HRI), mapping methods to operational challenges like compliance, collision avoidance, and force control.
- **Highlight the Role of Robotic Foundation Models:** To emphasize how emerging VLM/VLA-based methods interact with safe learning principles. This includes discussing both the new opportunities (e.g., language-level safety specification) and the amplified risks and evaluation challenges introduced by these powerful, yet complex, models.
- **Identify Gaps, Challenges, and Future Directions:** To synthesize current limitations and outline promising research avenues. This involves addressing issues like sim-to-real transfer, the scarcity of standardized benchmarks, provably safe generalization, and the integration of safety across system levels, particularly for foundation-model-enabled robots.

In essence, the survey is motivated by the critical need to accelerate the development of reliable, safety-aligned robots capable of performing contact-rich tasks by providing a structured understanding of the state-of-the-art and charting a clear path forward for future research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

To achieve its objectives, the authors employed a multi-step, systematic methodology for conducting their literature review:

- **Extensive Literature Investigation:** The authors performed a broad search across major academic databases, including IEEE Xplore, ACM Digital Library, SpringerLink, and Google Scholar. The search strings were carefully designed to capture relevant articles by combining keywords such as "(robot OR robotic) AND (safe OR safety OR stable) AND (contact-rich OR multi-contact OR contact-intensive OR physical interaction OR environment interaction OR contact interaction OR contact force) AND (intitle: "learning" OR intitle: "learn")." This comprehensive string aimed to ensure that papers directly addressing safe learning in contact-rich robotics were identified.
- **Inclusion Criteria:** The review focused on both peer-reviewed and pre-print articles published specifically between 2018 and 2025. This timeframe emphasizes recent advancements, acknowledging the rapid evolution of learning-based methods in robotics. The selection prioritized papers that explicitly addressed or discussed safety within learning-based approaches for contact-rich tasks, considering both theoretical contributions and empirical studies with experimental validation. The authors also explicitly stated the exclusion of pure imitation learning methods, given their focus on *online* safe learning where dynamic interaction and uncertainty are critical.
- **Detailed Analysis and Taxonomy Development:** Each selected paper was thoroughly analyzed across several dimensions: its methodological contributions, the type and strength of safety guarantees offered, the experimental setups (including simulation and real-world validation), and identified limitations. Based on this in-depth analysis, the authors developed a **safety-centric taxonomy**. This taxonomy categorizes existing approaches by:
 - **Learning Phase:** Safe Exploration (before contact) versus Safe Execution (during contact).
 - **Level of Safety Integration:** High-level planning, low-level control, or end-to-end approaches, including hybrid methods.
 - **Sensing and Policy Modalities:** Proprioceptive state, force/torque, vision, language, tactile, and multimodal fusion.
 - **Safety Enforcement Spaces:** Task space, joint space, dual space, and policy spaces.
 - **Task Characteristics:** Grouping contact-rich tasks into categories such as assembly/insertion, surface interaction, object manipulation, and physical human-robot interaction.
 - **Data Acquisition and Evaluation:** Examining simulation environments, benchmarks, and safety evaluation metrics.
- **Multi-View Categorization:** The authors explicitly structured their review to offer a "multi-view categorization," arguing that this approach reveals common patterns and gaps not apparent from any single taxonomy. This helps connect classical control-theoretic techniques with modern learning-based and foundation-model-based approaches under a unified safety lens.
- **Future Directions:** The methodology culminated in identifying open research questions, critical challenges, and promising future directions, particularly emphasizing the integration of safe contact-rich learning with large robotic foundation models.

This rigorous and structured approach ensures that the survey provides a comprehensive, timely, and well-organized synthesis of the current state of safe learning in contact-rich robotics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey presents a multifaceted analysis of safe learning for contact-rich robot tasks, revealing several key findings and organizing the field through a novel taxonomy:

#### 4.1. Categorization of Safe Learning Methods

The authors classify safe learning into three main phases/approaches:
- **Safe Exploration (before contact):** Focuses on minimizing risk during the learning phase itself. Methods include reachability-based trajectory safeguards (RTS), Model Predictive Shielding (MPS), Control Barrier Function (CBF)-guided MPC, Implicit Safe Set Algorithms, and Advantage-Based Intervention. A key trade-off identified is between guarantee strength (e.g., hard invariance) and adaptability/computational efficiency, often summarized using a hexagonal rating system.
- **Safe Execution (post contact stage):** Emphasizes maintaining safety and performance during actual robot interaction. Compliant controllers (impedance, admittance) are widely adopted, with learning-based methods used to adapt their parameters (stiffness, damping) online. This includes RL for parameter tuning, and the integration of VLAs with safe constraints and tactile feedback to translate semantic rules into physical constraints.
- **Provable Safety Methods:** Reviews foundational control theory tools (Lyapunov-based methods, CBFs, Contraction Metrics, Reachability Analysis) that offer mathematical guarantees. These methods, originally model-based, are increasingly integrated with learning. Challenges include finding suitable functions for complex nonlinear systems and the curse of dimensionality for reachability.

#### 4.2. Taxonomy of Contact-Rich Tasks

The survey categorizes contact-rich tasks into four main types, highlighting their unique challenges:
- **Assembly and Insertion:** Requires high precision, avoids damage, and handles sensing difficulties. Includes variants like multi-peg, multi-stage, deformable holes, and disassembly.
- **Surface Interaction:** Requires continuous, purposeful contact, consistent force, and desired trajectory following. Sub-categorized into surface altering (grinding), non-altering (wiping), and particle interaction (stirring), with special attention to deformable surfaces and human-involving tasks like massaging.
- **Object Manipulation:** Involves dynamic, long-term contact with objects. Divided into prehensile (grasping) and non-prehensile (pushing/sliding), and further by object nature (constrained, articulated, deformable).
- **Physical Human-Robot Interaction (pHRI):** Critical due to human safety and uncertainty. Involves passive human roles (robot assistance) and active robot roles (massaging, rehabilitation), emphasizing not only physical limits but also perceived safety (predictability, trust).

#### 4.3. Role of Sensing and Policy Modalities

The paper details how different sensor inputs contribute to safe learning:
- **Proprioceptive State:** Used for conservative motion generation, collision-aware planning, and geometric reasoning, often augmented by explicit safety constraints or value functions.
- **Force/Torque (F/T):** Primary interface for real-time enforcement of force/impedance limits, slip detection, and compliant correction. Often coupled with variable impedance control and structured safety features like Lyapunov or CBF indicators.
- **Vision:** Provides non-contact global information for anticipating collisions, localizing task features, and supplying high-level context. Used for pre-contact safety planning and monitoring interaction, especially when fused with haptic sensing.
- **Language Instructions:** Enables human users to specify tasks, constraints, and preferences at a high level, which VLMs/VLAs can ground into actionable, safety-aware plans and policies.
- **Tactile Sensing:** Offers high-resolution local contact information (pressure distribution, slip detection) crucial for nuanced, gentle interaction and detecting unsafe contact patterns.
- **Multimodal Fusion:** Combining modalities (e.g., vision-haptic) significantly enhances precision and robustness, especially under occlusions and complex contact.

#### 4.4. Data Acquisition and Simulation

The scarcity of high-fidelity, safety-critical contact data (especially failure modes) is a major challenge.
- **Simulation-Based Data:** Offers a safe, scalable way to generate large datasets using physics engines (MuJoCo, Isaac Gym). Domain randomization and procedural generation improve sim-to-real transfer, but a "reality gap" persists for complex contact phenomena.
- **Real-World Data:** Remains essential for capturing real-world complexities but is time-consuming, expensive, and risky. Teleoperation and integrated safety constraints are used.
- **Hybrid and Self-Supervised Approaches:** Combine simulation pre-training with real-world fine-tuning and self-supervision to bridge the reality gap.
- **Simulation Environments and Benchmarks:** Highlights general simulators (Gazebo, PyBullet, Isaac Sim) and GPU engines for parallel RL. Standardized benchmarks (RoboSuite, ManiSkill, Safety Gymnasium) are crucial for comparing safety, generalization, and robustness.

#### 4.5. Safety Evaluation Metrics and Abstraction Levels

- **Metrics:** Beyond task success and efficiency, safety is measured by constraint violations, physical metrics (force, acceleration, velocity), control stability (convergence, energy flow), and human safety (physical, perceived safety). Trade-offs between these objectives are frequently analyzed.
- **Abstraction Levels:** Safety is integrated at High-Level Planning (symbolic reasoning, task graphs, LLM/VLM planning), Low-Level Control (adaptive impedance, CBFs, robust control), and End-to-End Learning (safe RL, VLA models). Hybrid methods combine these levels for robustness and flexibility.

#### 4.6. Safety Enforcement Spaces

Safety constraints are enforced across:
- **Task Space:** Regulates end-effector motion and contact forces, often using compliant control (impedance/admittance) and workspace bounds (CBFs, safety filters).
- **Joint Space:** Prioritizes robot integrity by constraining joint angles, velocities, and torques to prevent self-collisions and actuator saturation, often via joint-level CBFs or Lyapunov stability.
- **Dual-Space:** Hybrid architectures that enforce safety concurrently in both task and joint spaces to avoid blind spots and ensure comprehensive protection.
- **Policy Spaces:** Integrates safety directly into the learning objective (CMDPs, safety critics) or planning horizon, often in latent spaces for high-dimensional inputs.

#### 4.7. Key Challenges

The survey identifies critical open problems:
- **Fragile Safety and Stability Guarantees:** Lack of formal guarantees for learning-based controllers in hard contact.
- **Data Scarcity and Biased Supervision:** Difficulty in obtaining high-quality, safety-relevant contact and failure data.
- **Limited Robustness, Generalization, and Uncertainty Awareness:** Policies often overfit and fail under distribution shifts, with weak uncertainty estimation.
- **Safe Exploration Under Physical Risk:** Naive exploration causes damage; existing methods can be conservative.
- **Safe Execution and Performance Trade-offs:** Balancing responsiveness, efficiency, and physical safety margins.
- **Benchmark and Dataset Limitations:** Lack of comprehensive benchmarks for deformable objects, multistage tasks, and human-robot collaboration with fine-grained safety annotations.
- **Safety Risks Specific to VLMs and VLAs:** Misinterpretation, hallucination, and overconfident execution of unsafe plans.
- **Human-Robot-Collaboration Safety:** Neglect of soft constraints (comfort, predictability) and transparency in learned controllers.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey represents a significant contribution to the field of robotics by providing the first dedicated, safety-centric review of learning methods for contact-rich tasks. Its comprehensive scope and multi-dimensional taxonomy offer a much-needed structured understanding of a complex and rapidly evolving area.

The potential impact of this work is substantial:

- **Unifying and Structuring the Field:** By systematically categorizing diverse approaches (classical learning, control theory, foundation models) under a unified safety lens, the survey helps researchers navigate the vast literature, understand interconnections, and identify synergistic opportunities. This clarity can accelerate research and development.
- **Guiding Future Research:** The explicit identification of key challenges and promising future directions provides a clear roadmap for researchers. This includes calls for standardized safe-contact benchmarks, data-efficient and safety-aware learning frameworks, bridging the sim-to-real safety gap, developing multimodal semantic grounding, and integrating human-centric and lifecycle-aware safety.
- **Promoting Robust and Reliable Robot Deployment:** By emphasizing formal safety guarantees and addressing the unique risks of physical interaction, the survey underscores the importance of developing robots that are not only capable but also reliably safe. This is crucial for expanding robotic applications into real-world, human-centric environments (e.g., healthcare, household, advanced manufacturing).
- **Informing the Development of Foundation Models for Robotics:** The survey's particular emphasis on VLMs and VLAs highlights their transformative potential while simultaneously addressing their inherent safety challenges. It advocates for physically-aware foundation models, safety-aligned prompting, and robust semantic-to-physical constraint grounding, which will be vital for the responsible development and deployment of generalist robotic AI.
- **Fostering Interdisciplinary Collaboration:** The breadth of topics covered, from control theory to machine learning, and from hardware design to human factors, inherently promotes interdisciplinary collaboration, which is essential for tackling the complex problem of safe human-robot interaction in contact-rich scenarios.

In conclusion, this survey acts as a critical compass for the robotics community, pointing towards the essential research required to realize a future where intelligent robots can safely and effectively perform intricate physical tasks, even in close collaboration with humans, by leveraging the full potential of learning-based methods and emerging foundation models.
