# Large Reward Models: Generalizable Online Robot Reward Generation with Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Large Reward Models (LRMs) specialize foundation Vision-Language Models to produce dense, frame-level reward signals for robotic reinforcement learning. This framework improves initial policy success rates on long-horizon manipulation tasks in a zero-shot manner and supports real-world policy self-improvement.

## Problem

- Robotic Reinforcement Learning (RL) is bottlenecked by the difficulty of designing accurate, dense, and generalizable reward functions.
- Existing VLM-based reward generation methods suffer from sparse or delayed feedback, or high computational latency for dense, frame-level signals.
- Initial policies trained via Imitation Learning (IL) often plateau, requiring continuous refinement through RL which lacks suitable reward signals.

## Method

- Adapting foundation Vision-Language Models (VLMs) into **Large Reward Models (LRMs)** to generate dense, ==frame-level reward signals==.
- Designing a ==tri-faceted reward structure==, including ==temporal contrastive==, ==absolute progress==, and ==task completion== signals, interpreted from current visual observations.
- Training **LRMs** on a diverse, multi-domain dataset of unlabeled robot, human-object interaction, and simulated videos to achieve ==zero-shot generalization==.

## Results

- The LRM for temporal contrastive reward improved correlation metrics by **15.3%**, and the absolute progress reward LRM reduced MAE by **20.0%** compared to a VLM baseline.
- LRM-driven policy refinement improved success rates on ManiSkill3, with task completion reward achieving **60.93%**, surpassing the **56.88%** SFT baseline and existing reward models.
- Real-world pick-and-place task success rate increased from **38.3%** to **51.7%** with LRM-driven RL finetuning, demonstrating practical self-improvement.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reward supervision can be extracted from unlabeled video trajectories by leveraging their inherent temporal monotonicity, enabling scalable reward model training.
> - A tri-faceted reward design, combining temporal contrast, absolute progress, and task completion, offers comprehensive and granular feedback for online policy refinement.
> - Directly integrating specialized Vision-Language Models as online reward engines provides effective real-time guidance, narrowing the performance gap between IL and privileged RL.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of generalist robot policies has seen advancements through large-scale pre-training using diverse robotic datasets, primarily via Imitation Learning (IL). However, such policies often encounter performance limitations in tasks requiring high precision or long-horizon coordination. Reinforcement Learning (RL) offers a framework for continuous policy refinement, but its effectiveness is constrained by the challenge of designing accurate, dense, and generalizable reward functions. Traditional methods for reward engineering often involve labor-intensive human labeling or brittle, task-specific hand-coded objectives.

Recent research has explored the utility of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) as automated reward generators, leveraging their extensive pre-trained knowledge of the physical world. These VLM-based approaches generally fall into two categories:
1. **Episode-level or sequence-based evaluation:** Models like RoboReward assign holistic progress scores to entire video trajectories post-hoc. While capable of facilitating policy refinement, this sparse and delayed signal may lack the temporal resolution required for real-time guidance. RoboMeter provides dense per-frame rewards but generates these by considering all frames from the trajectory's start, leading to increasing computational load and inference latency due to expanding visual context, which can pose practical constraints for high-frequency online refinement.
2. **Interactive VLM feedback:** Methods such as RL-VLM-F utilize VLMs to provide preferences over pairs of agent observations, which are then used to learn a proxy reward function. While demonstrating potential, these approaches suggest further opportunities to directly harness VLMs for instant, multi-dimensional online feedback.

This work positions itself to bridge the gap identified in existing VLM-based reward generation. It aims to develop a framework that scales foundation VLMs into dense, frame-level reward generators that can provide instant, multi-dimensional feedback, thereby addressing the limitations of sparse signals and high computational latency in prior VLM-based methods. The goal is to enable robust closed-loop optimization of robotic behaviors by directly leveraging VLM capabilities for signals such as frame-level relative progress and definitive completion markers. The paper connects to broader trends in real-robot reinforcement learning, learned reward models for robotics, process-level supervision, and autonomous self-improvement frameworks that leverage agentic reasoning and self-correction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the recognized bottleneck in robotic Reinforcement Learning (RL): the difficulty of designing generalizable reward functions that provide both high temporal resolution and semantic reasoning capabilities. While Imitation Learning (IL) can establish base policies, these often plateau in performance for complex, high-precision, or long-horizon tasks. RL offers a path to surpass these plateaus, but its success fundamentally depends on the availability of accurate, dense, and informative reward signals, which are arduous to obtain through traditional manual engineering or brittle, task-specific coding.

The paper identifies specific limitations in existing VLM-based approaches for automated reward generation:
- **Sparsity and Latency of Episode-level Rewards:** Current methods that evaluate entire trajectories (e.g., RoboReward) provide sparse and delayed signals, lacking the temporal resolution needed for active, real-time guidance of robotic policies.
- **Computational Burden of Trajectory-based Dense Rewards:** Approaches that generate dense per-frame rewards based on an expanding visual context from the start of a trajectory (e.g., RoboMeter) suffer from significantly increased sequential computational load and non-negligible inference latency, making them impractical for high-frequency online refinement.
- **Indirect VLM Utilization:** While interactive methods (e.g., RL-VLM-F) use VLMs to learn proxy reward functions from preferences, there is an opportunity to directly leverage the scaling capabilities of VLMs to generate instant, multi-dimensional online feedback.

To address these limitations, the key objectives of this work are:
1. **Develop a framework for online policy refinement:** Introduce Large Reward Models (LRMs) that adapt foundation Vision-Language Models into dense, frame-level reward generators.
2. **Formulate a multifaceted reward signal:** Design a tri-faceted reward structure comprising process, completion, and temporal contrastive rewards derived from current visual observations, aiming to interpret robot states from distinct cognitive perspectives.
3. **Ensure broad zero-shot generalization:** Train LRMs on a large-scale, multi-source dataset encompassing real-world robot trajectories, human-object interactions, and diverse simulated environments to facilitate generalization to unseen tasks and environments.
4. **Enable closed-loop policy refinement:** Utilize LRM-generated rewards to guide an initial policy, typically trained via Imitation Learning (IL), to correct sub-optimal behaviors in a closed-loop manner.
5. **Demonstrate efficiency and efficacy:** Evaluate the framework on challenging long-horizon manipulation benchmarks in a purely zero-shot manner, aiming to show significant improvement in success rates of initial IL policies with high sample efficiency and without manual reward engineering.

The overall motivation is to overcome the reward engineering bottleneck in robot learning by providing a robust, scalable, and generalizable method for generating high-quality, dense reward signals directly from visual observations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, Large Reward Models (LRMs), aims to refine a robotic policy π_ϕ by maximizing the expected return, where the reward signal *r* is directly mapped from visual observations *I* and a task description *d*. This mapping is achieved by specializing foundation VLMs into LRMs across three functional modalities.

**A. Extracting Reward Signals from Multi-domain Unlabeled Videos**
To enable scalable and generalizable reward generation without manual intervention, supervision is extracted directly from unlabeled video trajectories. The temporal monotonicity inherent in these episodes is leveraged to map video timelines to a discrete progress scale, thereby transforming raw video frames into a structured dataset *D* = {(I_t, d, p_t)}.
- **Multi-domain Dataset Composition:** The dataset is curated from 24 diverse sources to ensure zero-shot generalization.
 - **Real-Robot Corpora:** Open X-Embodiment dataset provides diverse robotic embodiments and camera perspectives, supporting embodiment-agnostic reward learning.
 - **Human-Object Interaction:** HOI4D and EgoDex datasets contribute high-resolution dexterity data, bridging coarse robot movements with fine-grained success criteria.
 - **Simulated Benchmarks:** LIBERO and RoboCasa data address the Real-to-Sim (R2S) domain gap, training LRMs on structured task hierarchies and diverse rendering/physics engines.
- **Temporal Progression Sampling:** For each episode, 11 keyframes are extracted based on normalized temporal progress *p* ∈ {0.0, 0.1..., 1.0}. Each keyframe is paired with its original task description *d*.

**B. Tri-faceted Reward Formulation**
A tri-faceted reward structure is designed to bridge high-level semantic instructions with instant feedback for online RL, decomposing task evaluation into three complementary reward formats:
- **Temporal Contrastive Reward (r_cont):** Provides a relative directional gradient by comparing the current state (I_t) against a previous observation (I_{t-Δt}) in relation to the task goal (d). It outputs a discrete numerical reward: +1.0 if I_t is closer to the goal, -1.0 if I_{t-Δt} is closer, and 0.0 if ambiguous (Equation 2). This formulation aims to mitigate calibration issues of absolute scoring.
- **Absolute Progress Reward (r_prog):** Offers continuous spatial-temporal grounding as a numerical regression task. Given I_t, an initial state I_0 (as a visual anchor), and d, r_prog represents a normalized completion score ranging from 0.0 to 1.0 in 0.1 increments (Equation 3). This provides granular estimation of progress.
- **Task Completion Reward (r_comp):** Provides a definitive binary assessment. Given I_t and d, it indicates whether the semantic requirements of the goal have been fully satisfied (1 for met, 0 otherwise) (Equation 4), anchoring successful states.

**C. Training LRMs for Reward Generation**
The Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct model is specialized into LRMs using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) on the structured dataset *D*. The visual input *I* varies based on the reward modality.
- **Contrastive Discrimination Model:** Trained as a preference judge using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) (Equation 5). It incorporates a Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning step to ensure rewards (r_cont ∈ {+1, -1, 0}) are derived from verifiable physical interactions.
- **Progress Estimation Model:** Trained via Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) to map observations I to r_prog ∈ {0.0..., 1.0} (Equation 6). A "reasoning-first" approach compels the model to articulate physical cues before outputting the reward, ensuring numerical consistency.
- **Completion Judgment Model:** Optimized via SFT to directly predict the binary success signal r_comp ∈ {0, 1} (Equation 7), preserving its ability to verify goal satisfaction.

**D. Online Policy Refinement via LRM-Generated Rewards**
The trained (frozen) LRMs are used as an online reward engine to refine a base policy π_ϕ, initialized from π_SFT, transitioning it from passive imitation to active, reward-driven control.
- **Online Interaction and Reward Integration:** The policy interacts with the environment, sampling actions based on I_t and d. An Interval-Hold strategy queries LRMs every K environment steps for a chosen reward modality (r_m = LRM_m(I, d)). The resulting reward r_t = w_m * r_m (Equation 8) is cached and held for K steps, providing a continuous supervisory stream.
- **Policy Optimization and Refinement:** The Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) framework is used to update policy parameters ϕ (Equation 9). Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) computes the advantage A_t (Equation 10) using the LRM-generated reward r_t and a critic network V_ψ(I_t). This process aims to bridge the gap from imitation to high-precision manipulation by grounding policy updates in the LRM's generalized physical priors.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the framework focused on both the intrinsic quality of LRM-generated rewards and their utility in robotic reinforcement learning.

**A. Evaluating Large Reward Models (LRMs)**
The specialized LRMs were compared against the zero-shot Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct baseline on held-out evaluation sets.
- **Contrastive Discrimination Model:** For temporal contrastive reward (r_cont), the LRM showed a 15.3% improvement in both Kendall’s τ and Spearman’s ρ correlation metrics over the baseline. This indicates enhanced capability for consistent temporal ordering, crucial for stable directional gradients.
- **Progress Estimation Model:** For absolute progress reward (r_prog), the LRM demonstrated substantial improvements in evaluative precision. The Mean Absolute Error (MAE) dropped by 20.0%, and RMSE decreased by 19.3%. Accuracy at ±0.2 tolerance improved by 8.6 percentage points, suggesting more precise reward intensities and correction of systematic estimation biases. The LRM consistently outperformed the baseline across all tolerance thresholds.
- **Task Completion Model:** For task completion reward (r_comp), the LRM achieved 69.38% accuracy, a marginal increase over the zero-shot baseline's 69.23%. This suggests the foundation Qwen3-VL model already possesses a robust innate capability for semantic goal recognition in zero-shot settings.

**B. Policy Refinement on the ManiSkill3 Benchmark**
Starting from a π_SFT baseline, 30 RL iterations were conducted for each reward modality on ManiSkill3, with comparisons to current SOTA progress reward models and an Env Reward upper bound.
- **Closed-Loop Performance Evaluation:** All policies refined with LRM-generated rewards showed performance gains over the π_SFT baseline (56.88%). The Task Completion Reward (r_comp) achieved the highest success rate at 60.93%, followed by Temporal Contrastive Reward (r_cont) at 60.31% and Absolute Progress Reward (r_prog) at 60.00%. The Env Reward, utilizing privileged simulator states, achieved 66.87%. The LRM-driven refinement reduced the gap between the IL baseline and the privileged optimum.
- **Comparison with SOTA Progress Reward Models:** The LRM (r_prog) achieved a superior success rate of 60.00% when compared to RoboReward-8B (59.06%) and Robometer-4B (56.56%), under a standardized reward query interval of K=10 steps.
- **Open-Loop Reward Quality Analysis:** An open-loop analysis of 80 trajectories demonstrated co-improvement between LRM evaluative quality and policy performance.
 - For absolute rewards (r_comp and r_prog), ROC-AUC scores for r_comp improved from 0.660 to 0.795, and for r_prog from 0.874 to 0.950 after RL training. Per-trajectory Pearson correlation and pairwise ranking accuracy also improved, confirming that LRM-generated rewards provided training signals aligned with true task progress.
 - For the temporal contrastive reward (r_cont), step-level reliability metrics also improved, with correct direction identification in 85.5% of steps and monotonicity of cumulative rewards on successful trajectories increasing from 0.535 to 0.559 after RL training.

**C. Real-World Policy Self-Improvement**
Real-world experiments on a pick-and-place task utilized r_comp as an autonomous sparse reward classifier for trajectory filtering and iterative policy refinement.
- The task involved grasping a toy giraffe and placing it into a target bowl.
- The success rate improved from 38.3% for the SFT baseline to 51.7% with LRM-driven RL finetuning.
- Qualitative analysis showed the refined model corrected the SFT baseline's failure to place the giraffe inside the bowl, demonstrating the LRM's ability to resolve execution errors during deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Large Reward Models (LRMs) as a framework that scales foundation vision-language models into effective online reward generators, addressing a critical bottleneck in robotic reinforcement learning: the manual and often brittle engineering of reward functions.

The significance and potential impact are multifaceted:
- **Automated Reward Generation:** The work demonstrates a pathway to eliminate the need for manual reward engineering. By adapting VLMs, LRMs can autonomously generate dense, frame-level reward signals, making reinforcement learning more accessible and scalable for complex robotic tasks.
- **Enhanced Policy Performance and Sample Efficiency:** The empirical results show that LRM-generated rewards significantly improve the success rate of initial Imitation Learning (IL) policies within a limited number of RL iterations (30 iterations), indicating notable sample efficiency. This suggests that LRMs can facilitate efficient online refinement, pushing policies beyond IL performance plateaus.
- **Generalizability and Zero-shot Capabilities:** By training on a large, multi-domain dataset encompassing real-robot corpora, human-object interactions, and simulated environments, the LRMs demonstrate zero-shot generalization to unseen environments like ManiSkill3. This broad applicability is crucial for deploying robotic systems in diverse, unstructured real-world settings.
- **Multifaceted and Fine-grained Feedback:** The proposed tri-faceted reward formulation (Temporal Contrastive, Absolute Progress, and Task Completion Rewards) provides a rich and diverse set of feedback signals. These signals allow for fine-grained control and decision-making by offering relative progress gradients, continuous progress estimation, and definitive terminal state anchoring.
- **Bridging the Performance Gap:** The LRM-driven refinement effectively narrows the performance gap between policies initialized via imitation learning and those trained with privileged ground-truth environment rewards, all without relying on access to simulator-specific information. This indicates the LRMs' ability to provide physically grounded supervisory streams from vision-only input.
- **Real-World Applicability:** The successful demonstration of real-world policy self-improvement, where the Task Completion Reward acts as an autonomous supervisor for trajectory filtering, highlights the practical utility of LRMs for robust, real-world manipulation by enabling policies to self-correct and adapt to deployment conditions.
- **Emergent Synchronization:** The observation of emergent synchronization between RL-driven behaviors and LRM-perceived value, where improving policies generate trajectories with clearer semantic markers and physical transitions, suggests that LRMs can guide behaviors towards being more interpretable and aligned with human understanding of task progression.

In conclusion, this research presents a scalable and generalizable approach for autonomous robotic mastery in complex, vision-language environments, offering a foundational step towards more capable and adaptive robot learning systems.
