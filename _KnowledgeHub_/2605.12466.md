# Solve the Loop: Attractor Models for Language and Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Attractor Models propose an architecture that frames latent refinement as a fixed-point problem, enabling stable and memory-efficient iterative computation in language models and complex reasoning tasks. This approach demonstrates performance gains over traditional Transformers and prior recurrent models, while also observing that models internalize iterative refinement into their initial predictions.

## Problem

- Traditional Transformer architectures rely on fixed feed-forward computation for token generation, lacking the ability for iterative refinement of latent representations.
- Prior recurrent architectures attempting iterative refinement face issues such as unstable training, memory scaling linearly with recurrence depth, and high sequential computational costs.
- Some small recursive models exhibit a 'less is more' phenomenon, where increased model size can paradoxically lead to performance degradation or collapse on certain reasoning tasks.

## Method

- Attractor Models integrate a backbone Transformer for generating an initial output embedding proposal and a separate, smaller attractor module for refining this proposal to an equilibrium state.
- The model directly solves for a fixed point in the tied output embedding space using a root-finding algorithm (Anderson acceleration), initialized with the backbone's meaningful prediction.
- Training leverages implicit differentiation to ensure constant memory footprint for the recurrent block and enables adaptive refinement steps, improving efficiency and stability.

## Results

- Attractor Models achieved Pareto improvements in large-scale language modeling, outperforming parameter-matched Transformers (e.g., 140M model reduced validation perplexity by 14.8% and Lambada perplexity by 46.6%).
- Training required 25-31% fewer FLOPs than stable looped language models, with memory consumption in the recurrent block remaining constant irrespective of refinement steps.
- Tiny Attractor Models (27M parameters) attained high accuracy on hard reasoning tasks (Sudoku-Extreme: 91.4%, Maze-Hard: 93.1%), outperforming frontier LLMs and overcoming the scaling limitations of previous recursive models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Formulating latent refinement as a fixed-point problem addresses stability, memory, and computational efficiency challenges inherent in unrolled recurrent architectures.
> - The model exhibits a novel phenomenon called 'equilibrium internalization,' where the backbone learns to produce initial predictions already close to the final fixed point, reducing the need for explicit iterative refinement during inference.
> - Implicit differentiation for training biases the recurrent map towards contractive dynamics, preventing optimization from entering unstable fixed-point regimes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The modern landscape of language modeling has been significantly shaped by Transformer architectures, which predominantly rely on fixed feed-forward computation for token generation. Despite their success, these models raise a fundamental question regarding the optimal computational paradigm: whether predictions should result from a single computational pass or benefit from iterative refinement of latent representations.

A growing body of research indicates that such iterative refinement, often termed "latent thinking" or "architectural recurrence," can enhance model capabilities, particularly in complex reasoning tasks. This includes approaches like Chain-of-Thought reasoning, which explicitly uses discrete tokens for intermediate steps, and various recurrent architectural designs. Examples of these recurrent architectures encompass Universal Transformers, looped Transformers, recurrent-depth Transformers, looped language models, and continuous Chain-of-Thought methods. These architectures aim to leverage weight sharing to emulate increased depth, facilitate iterative or algorithmic processes, mitigate context-length costs associated with token-level reasoning, and improve generalization.

However, the integration of recurrence as a stable architectural component has historically presented challenges. Common issues include unstable training dynamics, memory requirements that scale linearly with the number of recurrent steps, and significant sequential computational costs. Training often necessitates techniques such as backpropagation through time, along with stabilization mechanisms, yet latent-thinking models frequently remain fragile and difficult to optimize. For instance, some recurrent language models demand considerably more computational resources than comparable feed-forward models, experiencing memory limitations at greater recurrence depths. Conversely, specialized, smaller recursive models have sometimes demonstrated a "less is more" phenomenon, where increased model size can paradoxically lead to performance degradation or collapse.

This work addresses these established challenges by proposing Attractor Models. This new architectural family seeks to provide a stable, memory-efficient, and computationally advantageous approach to iterative refinement. It departs from previous recurrent architectures, such as Looped Language Models (e.g., Parcae, Huggin, Ouro) that unroll for a fixed or adaptively sampled number of steps, and from Deep Equilibrium Models (DEQs) which solve for a hidden-state equilibrium typically initialized from an uninformative state. Attractor Models aim to offer a more robust and scalable solution for incorporating iterative latent refinement into deep learning architectures, particularly within the context of language modeling and complex reasoning tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind developing Attractor Models stems from the observed limitations of existing language models, specifically the predominantly feed-forward nature of Transformers and the inherent difficulties in effectively implementing architectural recurrence.

#### 2.1. Motivation

1. **Refinement Capability for Language Models:** Traditional Transformers produce tokens via a single, fixed feed-forward computation. However, the capacity to iteratively refine a latent prediction before committing to an output is posited to be beneficial, especially for complex language understanding and generation.
2. **Limitations of Existing Refinement Methods:**
 - **Chain-of-Thought Reasoning:** While demonstrating the power of refinement, this method routes computation through discrete token channels, necessitating that "thinking" processes be explicitly written out, which may be inefficient for internal representation refinement.
 - **Challenges in Recurrent Architectures:** Prior attempts at architectural recurrence, including various looped Transformers and recurrent language models, have faced significant practical hurdles. These include unstable training, high memory consumption (scaling linearly with recurrence depth), substantial sequential computation during both training and inference, and the fragility of optimization. The selection of a fixed recurrence depth at training time often leads to a train-test mismatch if the depth is altered during inference, potentially degrading performance.
 - **"Less is More" Phenomenon in Tiny Recursive Models:** On certain reasoning tasks, small recursive models have shown that increasing model size can negatively impact or even completely undermine performance, hindering their scalability.
3. **Observation of Fixed-Point Dynamics:** Recent mechanistic analyses of looped language models suggest that, for a majority of tokens, the recurrent trajectory often converges to a fixed point. This observation motivates a direct approach to modeling this fixed-point behavior rather than approximating it through finite unrolling.

#### 2.2. Key Objectives

Based on these motivations, the authors established the following specific objectives for Attractor Models:
1. **Stable Training:** Develop an architecture for iterative refinement that exhibits stable training characteristics, overcoming the fragility observed in many recurrent networks.
2. **Constant Memory Footprint:** Ensure that the memory required during training remains constant with respect to the number of refinement steps, contrasting with the linear memory growth of explicit unrolling.
3. **Cost-Efficiency in Training:** Achieve substantially lower training costs compared to models that rely on explicit unrolling of recurrent blocks.
4. **Inference Efficiency:** Design the architecture to be efficient during inference, with adaptive refinement capabilities.
5. **Broad Performance Across Scales:** Demonstrate strong performance across two distinct regimes: large-scale language model pretraining and complex reasoning tasks utilizing small models.
6. **Novel Phenomenon: Equilibrium Internalization:** Observe and characterize a novel phenomenon where the model learns to integrate the iterative refinement process into its initial output embedding, effectively rendering the explicit solver less critical during inference.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Attractor Models are designed to address the challenges of recurrent computation by reframing latent refinement as a fixed-point problem within the output embedding space. This approach integrates two distinct modules: a backbone for initial proposal generation and an attractor module for refinement to an equilibrium state.

**Architectural Design:**
The model architecture consists of three core components:
1. **Input Embedding:** Input tokens $x$ are first mapped to input embeddings $\tilde{x} = E(x)$, where $E$ denotes a tied embedding/unembedding matrix.
2. **Backbone Module ($T_{\theta_b}$):** This module, typically implemented as a high-capacity causal Transformer, processes the input embeddings $\tilde{x}$ to produce an initial "guess" output embedding, $\tilde{y}_0 = T_{\theta_b}(\tilde{x})$. A crucial aspect is that $\tilde{y}_0$ serves as a semantically meaningful and coherent initial prediction, distinguishing it from uninformative initializations (e.g., zero or Gaussian noise) commonly used in other recurrent or equilibrium models.
3. **Attractor Module ($T_{\theta_a}$):** This is a separate, generally smaller, weight-tied recurrent network responsible for refining the initial proposal $\tilde{y}_0$. The refinement process is defined by the iteration: $\tilde{y}_{t+1} = T_{\theta_a}(\tilde{y}_t, \tilde{y}_0)$.
 - **Persistent Injection:** The initial guess $\tilde{y}_0$ is consistently provided at every refinement step. This persistent injection is crucial for maintaining the dependence of the attractor on the initial proposal and preventing the fixed point from becoming independent of the input.
 - **Fixed-Point Solution:** Instead of performing a fixed number of recurrent steps, the Attractor Module directly solves for the fixed point (equilibrium) $\tilde{y}^\star$, where $\tilde{y}^\star = T_{\theta_a}(\tilde{y}^\star, \tilde{y}_0)$. This is expressed as a root-finding problem: $A_{\theta_a}(\tilde{y}^\star, \tilde{y}_0) := T_{\theta_a}(\tilde{y}^\star, \tilde{y}_0) - \tilde{y}^\star = 0$. The solution $\tilde{y}^\star$ is obtained using a root-finding algorithm, specifically Anderson acceleration, initialized with the backbone proposal $\tilde{y}_0$.
 - **Adaptive Convergence:** The solver terminates when the normalized residual norm, $\lVert A_{\theta_a}(\tilde{y}_t, \tilde{y}_0)\rVert^2 / \lVert\tilde{y}_t\rVert^2$, falls below a predefined tolerance $\epsilon$, or after a maximum number of steps $T_{\text{max}}$. This allows the number of refinement steps to be chosen adaptively based on convergence, rather than being a fixed architectural parameter.
4. **Output Decoding:** The approximate equilibrium embedding $\tilde{y}^\star$ is decoded into next-token probabilities using the tied unembedding matrix $E^\top$.

**Training and Inference:**
1. **Training Objective:** The model is trained using the standard next-token prediction cross-entropy loss applied to the fixed-point output $\tilde{y}^\star$.
2. **Backward Pass (Implicit Differentiation):** To train the model parameters $\theta = (\theta_a, \theta_b, E)$, gradients are computed through the fixed-point equation using the implicit function theorem. This approach ensures that training memory in the attractor block remains constant with respect to the number of forward iterations, as it avoids backpropagating through every solver step.
 - **One-Step Approximation:** For computational efficiency, a one-step approximation $u \approx v$ (where $v = \partial L / \partial \tilde{y}^\star$) is employed in the backward pass. This approximation avoids the computationally intensive linear solve for $u = (I - J_{\tilde{y}}^\top)^{-1}v$ where $J_{\tilde{y}}$ is the Jacobian of $T_{\theta_a}$ with respect to its first argument at equilibrium.
 - **Stability Inducement:** The implicit gradient formulation inherently biases the recurrent map towards contractive dynamics. The term $(I - J_{\tilde{y}}^\top)^{-1}$ becomes ill-conditioned if the dynamics are non-contractive, creating a barrier that prevents the optimization from moving into unstable fixed-point regimes.
3. **Inference:** During inference, the process mirrors training: the backbone generates $\tilde{y}_0$, the attractor solver computes $\tilde{y}^\star$, and $E^\top$ decodes it. The solver tolerance $\epsilon$ and $T_{\text{max}}$ are inference-time hyperparameters, allowing for flexible adjustment of computation budget without retraining.

**Distinction from Deep Equilibrium Models (DEQs):**
Attractor Models differ from traditional DEQs in several key aspects:
- **Equilibrium Space:** Attractor Models operate directly in the tied output-embedding space, whereas DEQs typically solve for a hidden-state equilibrium.
- **Initialization:** Attractor Models warm-start the solver with a meaningful proposal $\tilde{y}_0$ from the backbone, unlike DEQs which often initialize from uninformative states (e.g., zero).
- **Architectural Flexibility:** Attractor Models allow for an arbitrary depth backbone Transformer, with the refinement block operating on its prediction, which has been shown to mitigate some scaling issues observed in DEQs.

**Specific Approach for Reasoning Tasks:**
For tasks like Sudoku-Extreme and Maze-Hard, the approach adapts the protocol of Tiny Recursive Models (TRM). Instead of a separate Transformer backbone, deep supervision provides the initialization for the solver at each step, with a learned embedding at step zero. The core idea of fixed-point refinement and implicit differentiation remains central. For these small-data, sensitive regimes, the phantom-gradient scheme is used for the backward pass instead of the one-step approximation, as it provides a more robust training signal.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research demonstrates the efficacy of Attractor Models across both large-scale language modeling and hard reasoning tasks with small models, highlighting improvements in performance, efficiency, and stability, along with a novel learning phenomenon.

#### 4.1. Large-Scale Language Modeling Performance

- **Pareto Improvement:** Attractor Models consistently outperform parameter-matched standard Transformers and the stable looped language model Parcae across 140M, 370M, and 770M parameter scales. This performance gain is observed in lower perplexity (validation and out-of-distribution on Lambada) and higher downstream accuracy (CORE and CORE-Extended benchmarks).
 - For instance, a 140M Attractor Model achieved a 14.8% reduction in validation perplexity and a 46.6% reduction in Lambada perplexity compared to a 140M Transformer baseline. Downstream accuracy improved by up to 12.2% on CORE and 14.0% on CORE-Extended.
 - A 770M Attractor Model notably achieved performance comparable to a 1.3B Transformer that was trained on twice as many tokens, demonstrating a significant improvement in efficiency and quality scaling.

#### 4.2. Training Efficiency

- **Reduced Training FLOPs:** Attractor Models utilized 25-31% less training FLOPs compared to Parcae. This efficiency gain is attributed to the adaptive convergence of the fixed-point solver, which often terminates before the maximum iteration limit ($T_{\text{max}}$), and the use of the one-step implicit-gradient approximation in the backward pass.
- **Constant Training Memory:** A key advantage is that training memory requirements for Attractor Models remain nearly constant regardless of the number of solver iterations. This is in contrast to standard looped language models like Parcae, where memory usage scales linearly with the number of loops due to the necessity of storing intermediate activations for backpropagation.

#### 4.3. Hard Reasoning Tasks with Tiny Models

- **Superior Accuracy and Scaling:** On challenging reasoning benchmarks, Sudoku-Extreme and Maze-Hard, Attractor Models with only 27M parameters and approximately 1000 training examples achieved 91.4% and 93.1% accuracy, respectively.
- **Overcoming "Less is More" Limitation:** Unlike Tiny Recursive Models (TRM), which showed performance degradation or collapse (0% accuracy for 27M TRM) when scaled up, Attractor Models demonstrated improved performance with increased parameter count (from 7M to 27M).
- **Outperforming Frontier LLMs:** Standard Transformers and several proprietary frontier large language models (e.g., DeepSeek R1, Claude, o3-mini) entirely failed on these tasks, achieving 0% accuracy.

#### 4.4. Equilibrium Internalization and Test-Time Behavior

- **Novel Phenomenon:** Attractor Models exhibited "equilibrium internalization," where the backbone module learns to produce an initial output embedding ($\tilde{y}_0$) that is already very close to the eventual fixed point ($\tilde{y}^\star$).
- **Minimal Inference-Time Refinement:** Consequently, the models reached their peak performance at a very low number of inference iterations (e.g., $T=1$, and for the 770M model, even $T=0$ by decoding $\tilde{y}_0$ directly). This implies that the iterative refinement process, while crucial during training to shape the learned representations, becomes largely internalized into the backbone's initial prediction at inference time.
- **Stable Convergence during Training:** During training, Attractor Models rapidly converged to the minimum iteration count required by the solver and maintained stability, unlike a DEQ baseline which showed an increasing number of iterations as training progressed.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Comparison to DEQ:** The design choice of placing the fixed point directly in the tied output-embedding space and initializing the solver from the backbone proposal $\tilde{y}_0$ was critical. This configuration yielded significantly lower perplexity and fewer average iterations compared to conventional DEQ formulations or DEQ with tied unembedding.
- **Proposal Injection Mechanism:** Additive persistent injection of the backbone proposal $\tilde{y}_0$ at every step of the attractor module ($T_{\theta_a}(\tilde{y}_t, \tilde{y}_0)$) proved most effective, leading to better quality and faster convergence than initial-only injection or concatenation.
- **Backward Pass Approximation:** The one-step implicit-gradient approximation provided a favorable trade-off between computational cost and performance. It achieved nearly the same quality as full implicit differentiation while drastically reducing peak training memory (4.8x less) and step time (2.7x faster).
- **Solver Initialization:** Initializing the attractor solver with the meaningful backbone proposal $\tilde{y}_0$ significantly outperformed uninformative initializations (zero or Gaussian noise) in terms of perplexity, convergence speed, and downstream accuracy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Attractor Models represent a significant development in the field of deep learning, particularly for architectures employing iterative refinement and recurrence. The work addresses long-standing challenges associated with these models, offering a robust and efficient alternative.

**Key Contributions and Impact:**
1. **Overcoming Recurrent Architecture Limitations:** The primary significance lies in solving critical issues faced by recurrent and looped architectures, namely unstable training, linear memory scaling with depth, and high sequential computational costs. By formulating latent refinement as a fixed-point problem solvable via implicit differentiation, Attractor Models achieve stable training, constant memory footprint in the recurrent block, and adaptive iteration control. This makes iterative refinement a more practical and scalable architectural component for neural networks.
2. **Enhanced Language Model Performance:** The demonstration of Pareto improvements in large-scale language modeling, where Attractor Models outperform parameter-matched Transformers and stable looped baselines with less computational cost, indicates a new frontier for developing high-performing and efficient language models. The finding that a 770M Attractor Model can surpass a 1.3B Transformer trained on double the data suggests substantial gains in model efficiency and potentially a more favorable scaling paradigm.
3. **Breakthrough in Reasoning with Small Models:** The ability of Attractor Models to achieve high accuracy on complex reasoning tasks (Sudoku-Extreme, Maze-Hard) with modest parameter counts (27M) is particularly impactful. This performance, coupled with its robust scaling behavior where other specialized recursive models collapse and frontier LLMs fail entirely, indicates a potential for developing highly efficient and specialized reasoning agents without the immense computational burden of very large models. This could open avenues for deploying powerful reasoning capabilities in resource-constrained environments.
4. **Discovery of Equilibrium Internalization:** The identification of "equilibrium internalization" is a novel and noteworthy phenomenon. It suggests that Attractor Models not only learn to perform iterative refinement but also internalize the outcome of this process into their initial, feed-forward predictions. This implies that the iterative computation, while crucial for training the model to find the correct fixed point, can be largely omitted during inference without significant performance degradation. This mechanism effectively transforms an inherently sequential computation into an efficient, almost "free," component at test time, addressing a major bottleneck of recurrent designs.
5. **Flexible and Adaptive Computation:** The adaptive nature of the fixed-point solver, where the number of refinement steps is determined by convergence criteria rather than a fixed parameter, provides significant flexibility. This allows for dynamic adjustment of computational resources at inference time without requiring model retraining, which is a practical advantage for deployment scenarios with varying latency or accuracy requirements.
6. **Future Research Directions:** This work lays the groundwork for further exploration of equilibrium-based architectures. Future research could delve deeper into the mechanisms of equilibrium internalization, investigate its generalizability across different tasks and model modalities, and further refine the implicit differentiation techniques for even greater efficiency and stability. The application of Attractor Models to other domains benefiting from iterative refinement (e.g., computer vision, control systems) also represents a promising direction.

In summary, Attractor Models offer a novel and effective paradigm for incorporating iterative refinement into neural networks, resolving long-standing issues in recurrent architectures and demonstrating significant performance improvements across diverse tasks, while also uncovering a unique aspect of how models learn and optimize iterative processes.
