# 3D HAMSTER: Bridging Planning and Control in Hierarchical Vision Language Action Models through 3D Trajectory Guidance

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from KAIST, POSTECH, Holiday Robotics, and KRAFTON AI present 3D HAMSTER, a hierarchical Vision-Language-Action framework where a large VLM directly generates metrically reliable 3D end-effector trajectories to guide a 3D low-level policy. This method significantly enhances manipulation robustness and generalization across various distribution shifts in both simulated and real-world robotic tasks.

## Problem

- Current hierarchical VLA models suffer from representational misalignment, where 2D VLM planners provide ambiguous guidance to 3D-native low-level controllers, leading to geometrically distorted paths (the 'graffiti effect').
- 2D planning outputs inherently lack precise depth information, making it difficult for 3D controllers to execute fine-grained manipulation tasks accurately or robustly under varying viewpoints.
- The latent 3D spatial understanding within large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) remains largely unexploited by existing robotic planning approaches, which typically confine VLM outputs to 2D representations.

## Method

- Developed a ==3D Trajectory Planner== using an augmented Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct VLM with a dedicated ==depth encoder== and a ==dense depth reconstruction loss== to directly generate metrically reliable 3D end-effector trajectories (u, v, d).
- Implemented a ==trajectory-conditioned 3D low-level policy== (**3DFA**) that unprojects the predicted 3D trajectories into world coordinates, fuses them with the scene point cloud, and generates motor actions.
- Employed a ==two-stage training strategy==: first aligning depth representations while freezing the VLM backbone, then fine-tuning for 3D trajectory prediction using ==LoRA== and a diverse set of 3D capability and VLM preservation datasets.

## Results

- On the DroidSpatial-Bench, 3D HAMSTER achieved **66.2%** end-position accuracy (δ=5 cm), outperforming RoboBrain-2.5-8B (**58.1%**) and proprietary VLMs, with ablations confirming the value of the depth encoder and dense depth loss.
- In the Colosseum simulation benchmark, it achieved the highest average success rate of **44.8%**, surpassing 2D-guided HAMSTER (**38.8%**) and unguided 3DFA (**36.6%**), showing notable improvements under appearance-altering shifts like lighting (**+15.6%**).
- Demonstrated superior real-world transfer with higher average success rates across tasks like button pressing (**80%**), pouring (**68%**), and pick-and-place (**62%**), particularly benefiting tasks requiring depth precision (e.g., pouring, **+23%** over 2D guidance).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly providing 3D metric trajectories from the VLM planner to a 3D controller creates a unified metric space, effectively resolving the geometric ambiguity and distortions inherent in 2D-to-3D lifting.
> - Augmenting VLMs with explicit depth encoding and dense depth reconstruction loss allows them to leverage and regularize their internal representations for generating accurate, metrically consistent 3D plans.
> - Aligning the planning and execution domains in 3D significantly improves robotic manipulation robustness and generalization, particularly in tasks demanding precise spatial control and when operating under various appearance or spatial shifts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot manipulation faces challenges in integrating high-level semantic reasoning with low-level motor control. Early approaches involved end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) architectures that directly mapped visual observations and language instructions to continuous actions. These monolithic models, however, have demonstrated limitations due to the scarcity of large-scale robot demonstration data, which often results in reduced generalization capabilities and vulnerability to out-of-distribution (OOD) visual shifts.

To address these limitations, hierarchical VLA frameworks have emerged as an alternative. These frameworks explicitly decouple semantic reasoning, often handled by a Vision-Language Model (VLM) acting as a high-level planner, from low-level motor control. The VLM planner typically generates intermediate representations, such as 2D keypoints or 2D end-effector trajectories on a camera image, which then guide a downstream low-level controller to produce motor commands. This decoupling allows the planner to be trained on abundant non-robot data, thereby preserving the VLM's broad generalizability.

However, a fundamental representational misalignment exists within many current hierarchical VLA systems. While VLM-based planners often reason in 2D pixel coordinates, state-of-the-art low-level policies for fine-grained manipulation increasingly favor 3D-native architectures that operate on point clouds. These 3D controllers have demonstrated superior spatial precision and robustness to viewpoint changes compared to their 2D counterparts. When a 2D planner is paired with a 3D controller, the 2D waypoints must be projected into 3D space by sampling depth from the scene surface. Since the 2D planner provides no explicit depth information, the resulting 3D trajectory becomes dependent on the arbitrary geometry underneath each waypoint, leading to geometrically distorted paths. This phenomenon, termed the "graffiti effect," can hinder the controller's ability to distinguish the intended path from the scene geometry, potentially causing brittle execution.

Recent advancements in VLMs have shown capabilities in acquiring 3D spatial knowledge through large-scale geometry-rich pre-training, including 3D bounding box prediction and joint 3D scene reconstruction. This suggests a latent 3D understanding within VLMs that has not been fully leveraged by robotic planning methods, which have largely confined VLM planners to 2D outputs. Concurrent work, such as RoboTracer and RoboBrain 2.5, has begun to explore depth-aware keypoint sequences in (u,v,d) form, indicating a move towards 3D-aware planning. However, these efforts often focus on standalone motion planning without fully integrating predicted 3D trajectories with a downstream 3D low-level policy for closed-loop manipulation.

This work, 3D HAMSTER, positions itself by directly addressing this representational gap. It aims to bridge 3D-aware planning with 3D-native execution by having the VLM planner directly output metrically reliable 3D trajectories, thereby establishing a shared metric space for robust manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to resolve the representational misalignment inherent in current hierarchical Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, where high-level planners operate in 2D image space while low-level controllers operate in 3D metric space using point clouds. This misalignment presents a significant obstacle to achieving robust and generalizable robotic manipulation.

The motivation stems from several key observations:
1. **Limitations of 2D Planning:** Existing hierarchical VLA frameworks, such as HAMSTER, utilize Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to predict 2D end-effector trajectories. While VLMs exhibit strong semantic understanding, their 2D outputs inherently lack depth information. When these 2D plans are fed to a 3D low-level policy, they must be "lifted" into 3D by assigning depth values from the underlying scene surface. This process results in geometrically distorted trajectories, often adhering unnaturally to scene surfaces (the "graffiti effect"), rather than tracing a free path through 3D space. Such distortions make it challenging for the 3D controller to accurately interpret the intended motion, leading to reduced precision and robustness.
2. **Advancements in 3D Control Policies:** Contemporary low-level manipulation policies, particularly those based on point clouds (e.g., 3DFA, DP3), consistently outperform 2D alternatives in terms of spatial precision and resilience to viewpoint changes. The performance gains of these 3D-native controllers highlight the desirability of providing them with guidance that is naturally aligned with their operational space.
3. **Untapped VLM 3D Capabilities:** Recent VLMs have demonstrated an emergent understanding of 3D spatial concepts, evidenced by capabilities such as 3D bounding box prediction. This suggests that VLMs possess a foundational capacity for 3D reasoning that has not been fully exploited for robotic trajectory planning.
4. **Need for Metrically Reliable Guidance:** For precise and robust manipulation, especially in tasks requiring fine-grained contact or specific spatial relationships (e.g., pouring liquids, placing objects accurately), the robot requires metrically reliable guidance that is consistent across different viewpoints and visual conditions. 2D guidance, with its inherent depth ambiguity, cannot provide this level of reliability.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of 3D HAMSTER are:
- To develop a hierarchical VLA framework that enables the high-level planner to directly output metrically reliable 3D end-effector trajectories in (u, v, d) form, where (u, v) are image-plane coordinates and d is metric depth.
- To augment a VLM backbone with a dedicated depth encoder and a dense depth reconstruction objective. This aims to enable the VLM to effectively process depth information and regularize its internal representations to preserve faithful scene geometry, thereby predicting accurate metric depth for trajectory waypoints.
- To integrate these predicted 3D trajectories seamlessly into a pointcloud-based low-level policy, ensuring a unified metric interface from planning through execution.
- To demonstrate that aligning planning and execution in 3D metric space yields improved manipulation robustness and generalization under various distribution shifts, including appearance-altering shifts, unseen language, and novel spatial and visual conditions, compared to 2D-guided and unguided baselines.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

3D HAMSTER is a hierarchical VLA framework designed to produce motor actions for manipulation tasks based on RGB-D camera observations and natural language instructions. It achieves this by decoupling the process into a 3D trajectory planner and a trajectory-conditioned 3D low-level policy, trained in a two-stage strategy.

**Problem Formulation:** The system receives an RGB image (I), a depth map (D), and a task instruction (l). The 3D trajectory planner outputs an end-effector trajectory τ = {(u_t, v_t, d_t)} for T waypoints. This trajectory is then used by the low-level policy π_low(P, τ) to generate a chunk of T_a actions {a_t} from the current scene point cloud (P).

**Overall Framework:**
The framework consists of:
1. **3D Trajectory Planner:** Takes an RGB image, depth map, and language instruction to autoregressively generate a 3D end-effector trajectory (u, v, d).
2. **Trajectory-conditioned 3D Low-level Policy:** Receives the predicted 3D trajectory, unprojects it into world coordinates, appends it to the scene point cloud, and predicts motor actions.

**A. 3D Trajectory Planner**
The planner is built upon the Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct VLM backbone. To enable metrically reliable 3D trajectory prediction, it incorporates three main components:

1. **Training Data:** A curated mixture of eight data sources is used, categorized into:
 - **3D Capability Data (RGB + Depth):** This includes end-effector trajectories from real (DROID) and simulated (RLBench, InternData-M1) robot demonstrations, with supervision for 2D-only, 3D-only, and 2D-to-3D chain-of-thought trajectories. Additionally, RefSpatial, a spatial reasoning dataset, is augmented with generated depth maps and depth-aware tasks to provide explicit 3D spatial understanding. Ground-truth depth is used for RLBench and DROID, while MoGe-2 generates metric depth for InternData-M1 and RefSpatial.
 - **Preservation Data (RGB only):** To prevent the fine-tuning process from eroding the VLM's existing vision-language capabilities, this category includes datasets like RoboPoint, PixMo (for point-based referring), LVIS (for bounding-box detection), and Honey-1M (for general VQA). These are processed without the depth pathway.

2. **Depth Encoder:** A dedicated depth pathway is introduced to provide explicit geometric cues. The RGB image is processed by the pretrained visual encoder, while the depth map is processed by a separately initialized depth encoder. The resulting RGB and depth tokens are projected into the LLM embedding space via dedicated projectors, fused element-wise, and then passed to the LLM backbone along with the tokenized instruction.

3. **Dense Depth Reconstruction Loss:** To ensure that the LLM's internal representations maintain metrically faithful depth information, a dense depth reconstruction loss (L_depth) is applied. The depth tokens (z_D) are routed through a lightweight decoder to reconstruct the full depth map (D̂), supervised by an ℓ1 loss: L_depth = ∥D − D̂∥_1. This loss regularizes the model's representations, complementing the sparse gradient signal from the standard autoregressive language modeling loss (L_LM) used for trajectory prediction. The total training loss is L = L_LM + λ L_depth, with λ = 0.1.

**Two-stage Training Strategy:**
- **Stage 1 (Depth Alignment):** The RGB encoder, depth encoder, and LLM backbone are frozen. Only the depth projector and depth decoder are trained. This step aims to align the depth representation with the pretrained visual-language space while preserving existing VLM capabilities.
- **Stage 2 (Task Fine-tuning):** Both the RGB and depth encoders remain frozen to preserve their learned representations. LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) is applied to the LLM backbone, and the projectors and decoder are fully trained for 3D trajectory prediction.

**B. Trajectory-conditioned 3D Low-level Policy**
The low-level policy uses 3DFA, a pointcloud-based policy that predicts actions via rectified flow matching.

1. **Trajectory Unprojection:** The planner's predicted 3D trajectory τ = {(u_t, v_t, d_t)} is first unprojected into world coordinates ˆτ = {(X_t, Y_t, Z_t)} using known camera intrinsic (K) and extrinsic (R, t_cam) parameters.

2. **Trajectory-Scene Fusion:** The world-frame trajectory ˆτ is appended to the current scene point cloud P. This creates a unified 3D representation where guidance and visual observations coexist. Each waypoint in ˆτ is color-coded by its temporal position. Learnable modality embeddings (e_traj, e_scene) are added to point features to allow the policy to distinguish between trajectory points and scene points. During point cloud subsampling, trajectory points are prioritized to ensure the guidance signal is always preserved.

3. **Action Prediction:** The policy then learns a velocity field v_θ and gripper open/close actions (g_θ) using a rectified flow matching formulation. The training objective (L_policy) involves supervising the velocity field and a BCE loss for gripper actions. During inference, the policy denoises from Gaussian noise to predict a chunk of T_a actions. The shared 3D metric space between the planner's output and the controller's input eliminates the need for implicit 2D-to-3D lifting.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments addressed three research questions related to 3D trajectory prediction, simulated manipulation robustness, and real-world transfer.

**RQ1: 3D Trajectory Prediction Accuracy on DroidSpatial-Bench**
- **Comparison with Baselines:** Proprietary VLMs (Sonnet-4.6, GPT-5.2, Gemini-3.0-Pro) showed limited accuracy for 3D metric trajectory prediction, with Gemini-3.0-Pro achieving 16.2% on the 5 cm "Both" metric. The open-source RoboBrain-2.5-8B provided a stronger baseline at 39.2% (5 cm "Both") and 60.1% (10 cm "Both") using RGB input. 3D HAMSTER, however, surpassed RoboBrain-2.5-8B across all metrics, showing particular improvement in end-position accuracy (66.2% vs. 58.1% at δ=5 cm; 82.4% vs. 74.3% at δ=10 cm).
- **Ablation Study:** The incremental addition of components to a Qwen3-VL-8B base model demonstrated their contributions:
 - Targeted 3D trajectory dataset supervision provided a substantial boost (from 0.7% to 27.7% at 5 cm "Both").
 - The dedicated depth encoder yielded further significant gains (from 27.7% to 42.6% at 5 cm "Both").
 - The dense depth reconstruction loss improved end-point accuracy (from 75.0% to 82.4% at 10 cm "End"), suggesting it helps maintain depth fidelity over the trajectory.
- **Qualitative Analysis:** Visualizations (Fig. 4) indicated that baseline models often produced trajectories that appeared plausible from one viewpoint but exhibited significant depth errors from another. In contrast, 3D HAMSTER maintained metric consistency across different viewpoints and demonstrated better generalization to unseen real-world scenes.

**RQ2: Robustness Under Systematic Distribution Shifts on Colosseum Simulation Benchmark**
- **Overall Performance:** 3D HAMSTER achieved the highest average success rate (44.8%), surpassing 2D-guided HAMSTER (38.8%) and the unguided 3DFA baseline (36.6%).
- **Impact of 2D vs. 3D Guidance:** 2D guidance (HAMSTER) sometimes led to a degradation in unperturbed performance compared to no guidance (49.5% vs. 53.8%), suggesting that projecting 2D plans introduces geometric ambiguity. Conversely, 3D guidance improved unperturbed performance to 62.9%, affirming alignment with the 3D action space.
- **Generalization to Perturbations:** 3D guidance showed the largest improvements over 2D guidance under appearance-altering shifts such as lighting (+15.6%), manipulated object (MO, +11.3%), and background texture (BG Tex., +8.7%) perturbations. This indicates a benefit from specifying paths in metric coordinates independent of visual appearance, even when object geometry itself changes. Under the "All Var." condition (all perturbations combined), both 2D and 3D guided methods converged to 7.2%, outperforming the unguided baseline (0.8%) but suggesting a limit to VLM grounding under severe visual corruption.

**RQ3: Real-World Transfer Across Generalization Axes**
- **Consistent Improvement:** 3D HAMSTER demonstrated the highest average success rates across three real-world task families: button pressing (80%), pouring (68%), and pick-and-place (62%). It consistently outperformed 2D-guided HAMSTER (60%, 45%, 46%) and the monolithic π_0.5 (74%, 41%, 40%).
- **Out-of-Distribution Performance:** While π_0.5 performed well in-distribution, its performance degraded significantly under distribution shifts (e.g., 15% on pouring and pick-and-place under spatial variation). 3D HAMSTER maintained higher performance, particularly under visual shifts (e.g., button pressing: 100% vs. 80% for 2D; pouring: 65% vs. 35% for 2D) and spatial shifts (e.g., button pressing: 50% vs. 20% for 2D; pouring: 65% vs. 40% for 2D).
- **Tasks Requiring Depth Precision:** The largest overall gain for 3D HAMSTER was observed in pouring tasks (68% vs. 45% for 2D), which demand precise height control for cup tilting, a requirement directly encoded by 3D waypoints. Under compounding shifts ("multiple" generalization axis), 3D guidance degraded gracefully, outperforming or matching all baselines.
- **Qualitative Real-World Execution:** Real-world rollouts (Fig. 5) visually confirmed the "graffiti effect" in HAMSTER's 2D trajectories, leading to task failures. In contrast, 3D HAMSTER's 3D trajectories remained metrically consistent and clearly separated from the scene surface, enabling successful execution by the low-level policy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "3D HAMSTER" addresses a fundamental challenge in robotic manipulation: the misalignment between high-level planning and low-level control representations within hierarchical Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. By enabling the VLM planner to directly output metrically reliable 3D trajectories, this research offers a significant contribution towards more robust and generalizable robotic systems.

The significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Bridging the 2D-3D Representational Gap:** The core contribution is the establishment of a unified 3D metric space for both planning and execution. This directly resolves the "graffiti effect" and inherent geometric distortions that arise when 2D plans are unreliably lifted to 3D for execution by 3D-native controllers. By providing guidance that is natively aligned with the controller's operational space, 3D HAMSTER facilitates a more seamless and unambiguous communication between the planner's intent and the robot's actions.

2. **Enhancing Robustness and Generalization:** The empirical results demonstrate that 3D trajectory guidance consistently outperforms 2D alternatives and unguided baselines in both simulated and real-world environments. This is particularly evident under various distribution shifts, including changes in object appearance, lighting, textures, and spatial configurations. The ability of 3D HAMSTER to maintain performance under these perturbations suggests an increased resilience to real-world variability, which is critical for deployment in unstructured environments. The benefits are most pronounced in tasks requiring precise depth control, such as pouring, highlighting the practical advantages of metric 3D planning.

3. **Advancing VLM Capabilities for Robotics:** The methodology employed to train the 3D trajectory planner, combining a dedicated depth encoder, a dense depth reconstruction loss, and a carefully curated data mixture, provides a blueprint for effectively channeling and augmenting the implicit 3D spatial knowledge of large VLMs for robotic applications. This demonstrates that VLMs can be adapted beyond their typical 2D outputs to generate high-fidelity, metric 3D plans, expanding their utility in embodied AI.

4. **Practical Implications for Robot Manipulation:** More reliable planning and execution translate directly to increased success rates for complex manipulation tasks. This work has the potential to reduce the brittleness of robot behaviors, make robots more adaptable to novel situations, and decrease the need for extensive task-specific data collection and engineering. For applications requiring precise interaction with objects in 3D space, such as manufacturing, logistics, or assistive robotics, the improvements in robustness and generalization could lead to more deployable and versatile robotic solutions.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** The identified limitations, such as the reliance on explicit RGB-D sensors, single-view planning, and focus on tabletop tasks, suggest clear directions for future work. Integrating monocular depth estimation, incorporating multi-view fusion for cluttered scenes, and extending to more complex scenarios like mobile manipulation or bimanual coordination could further enhance the generality and capabilities of 3D-aware hierarchical VLA models.

In conclusion, 3D HAMSTER represents a significant step forward in hierarchical VLA frameworks by directly addressing the representational mismatch between vision-language planning and 3D robotic control. Its proposed architecture and training methodology provide a robust solution for generating metrically reliable 3D trajectories, contributing to more capable and adaptable robotic manipulation systems.
