# Collision-Free Humanoid Traversal in Cluttered Indoor Scenes

> [!summary] Summary
> A new method, Humanoid Potential Field (HumanoidPF), combined with a hybrid scene generation strategy, enables humanoid robots to perform collision-free traversal in highly cluttered indoor scenes. This approach allows a Unitree G1 robot to navigate complex obstacles like hurdles and low-hanging objects with high success rates in real-world environments.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots face difficulty in navigating complex indoor environments characterized by full-spatial obstacle layouts and intricate geometries.
- Reinforcement learning for collision avoidance is hampered by sparse rewards and the need for policies to implicitly infer complex kinematic reasoning from raw sensor data.
- Developing generalizable traversal skills for humanoids is challenging due to the lack of sufficiently diverse and complex training scenarios, particularly for "extreme" obstacle layouts.

## Method

- ==Proposes Humanoid Potential Field (HumanoidPF)== as a novel perceptual representation to provide explicit, anticipatory, and dense guidance for whole-body collision avoidance.
- Implements a hybrid scene generation method, combining realistic 3D indoor scenes with procedurally generated "extreme" obstacles, for scalable training of a generalist policy.
- Develops the Click-and-Traverse (CAT) system for real-world deployment, integrating LiDAR-inertial SLAM for real-time environment mapping and intuitive teleoperation.

## Results

- The proposed method achieved superior traversal performance in simulation, demonstrating consistently higher success rates and lower distance errors across various cluttered indoor scene types compared to existing baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed HumanoidPF's critical role in both observation and reward, showing significant performance drops when either component was removed.
- The learned policies successfully transferred to a real Unitree G1 humanoid robot, exhibiting robust collision-free traversal in challenging indoor environments and outperforming other perceptual representations in sim-to-real scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly encoding humanoid-obstacle relationships through HumanoidPF provides effective anticipatory and dense guidance, accelerating reinforcement learning for whole-body collision avoidance.
> - A hybrid scene generation approach, combining realistic 3D environments with procedurally generated "extreme" obstacles, is critical for training policies that generalize robustly to diverse and unseen cluttered scenes.
> - Representing perception with HumanoidPF inherently smooths out real-world sensor noise, acting as a low-pass filter and significantly enhancing sim-to-real transfer for complex humanoid locomotion.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical challenge in the field of legged robotics: enabling humanoid robots to autonomously and safely navigate highly cluttered and geometrically complex indoor environments. While significant strides have been made in legged locomotion for both quadrupeds and humanoids, existing research often falls short in several key areas that this work aims to address.

Historically, legged robots have demonstrated impressive parkour capabilities and navigation over challenging terrains. Quadruped robots, for instance, can traverse complex obstacles, climb stairs, and navigate confined spaces. Humanoids have also shown advanced locomotion skills, such as stepping on stairs, balance beams, and stepping stones. However, a common limitation across many of these existing works, as highlighted in Table I of the paper, is their focus on environments with either **partial spatial layouts** (e.g., only ground obstacles, or only overhead obstacles, but not a combination) or **simple geometries** (e.g., rectangular blocks). Very few studies have systematically tackled scenarios where ground, lateral, and overhead obstacles coexist with intricate, realistic geometries—a ubiquitous characteristic of real-world indoor settings. For example, while `Gallant` considered different obstacle layouts in isolation, it did not address their joint presence.

Furthermore, the existing approaches for collision avoidance in reinforcement learning (RL) often suffer from two major drawbacks:
1. **Sparse and Delayed Supervision:** Policies typically receive penalty signals only upon actual collisions, leading to inefficient trial-and-error exploration and slow learning. There is a clear need for anticipatory and dense guidance.
2. **Implicit Kinematic Reasoning:** Conventional perceptual representations often expose the RL policy to raw, high-dimensional environmental measurements. This forces the policy to implicitly infer complex kinematic reasoning for collision avoidance from these low-level inputs, making learning difficult and less generalizable.

This paper directly tackles these limitations by introducing **Humanoid Potential Field (HumanoidPF)**, a novel representation designed to explicitly encode humanoid–obstacle relationships. This approach positions the work as a significant advancement beyond traditional Artificial Potential Fields (APF), which typically abstract robots as a single rigid body and are insufficient for the whole-body control demands of humanoids. By providing a continuous, differentiable gradient field that guides collision-free motion and acts as an informative perceptual observation, HumanoidPF offers anticipatory and dense supervision, thus making RL-based traversal skill learning significantly more tractable.

Moreover, the research contributes to the crucial area of **sim-to-real transfer**. The continuous field formulation of HumanoidPF naturally acts as a low-pass perceptual filter, smoothing out perception artifacts and enabling robust transfer from simulation to real-world robots—a perennial challenge in robotics. The development of a **hybrid scene generation method** further pushes the boundaries of training scalability and generalization by systematically exposing robots to diverse and challenging cluttered configurations, which are often underrepresented in existing datasets.

In summary, this work pushes the frontier of humanoid robot capabilities by addressing the complex, real-world problem of navigating highly cluttered indoor environments with full-spatial obstacle layouts and intricate geometries. It does so by proposing a novel perceptual representation and a robust training methodology, directly improving the efficiency of RL and the reliability of sim-to-real transfer, thereby paving the way for more practical domestic humanoid applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable collision-free humanoid traversal in challenging and cluttered indoor scenes. This encompasses a range of complex maneuvers, such as hurdling over floor objects, crouching under low-hanging obstacles, and squeezing through narrow passages, often requiring simultaneous consideration of these constraints. The overarching goal is to achieve this with a single, generalist policy that can adapt to diverse spatial layouts and obstacle geometries.

The core motivations behind this objective stem from several critical challenges identified in the existing literature:

1. **The Need for Generalizable Traversal Skills:** Current humanoid locomotion solutions are frequently limited to simplified environments or specific types of obstacles. For a domestic humanoid robot to be truly useful in real-world homes, it must possess generalizable skills to navigate dynamic and cluttered spaces, which often feature a mix of ground, lateral, and overhead impediments with realistic, intricate geometries. The paper explicitly points out that "existing works are often limited in their ability to handle traversal in cluttered indoor scenes (full-spatial obstacle layouts and intricate, realistic geometries)."

2. **Lack of Effective Humanoid-Obstacle Relationship Representation:** A fundamental barrier to learning robust collision avoidance is the absence of an efficient way to represent the dynamic relationship between the humanoid's complex body and its surrounding obstacles. Existing methods often rely on:
 - **Sparse and Delayed Collision Signals:** Reinforcement learning agents typically receive feedback only when a collision occurs, leading to inefficient learning through trial and error. This motivates the need for "anticipatory and dense guidance."
 - **Raw, High-Dimensional Perceptual Inputs:** Policies are often fed raw sensor data, forcing them to implicitly infer kinematic reasoning for traversal decisions. This is computationally expensive and hinders generalization, motivating a representation that allows the policy to "reason directly over traversal decisions."

3. **Challenges in Scalable Training for Diverse Scenarios:** Real-world cluttered environments present an enormous variety of obstacle configurations. Training a robot to handle all these scenarios requires a vast and diverse dataset, especially for reinforcement learning. However, typical indoor scene datasets lack the "highly challenging obstacle layouts" that are crucial for developing robust, generalizable skills, particularly in "near-collision and emergency scenarios." This necessitates a method to systematically expand the space of training scenarios.

4. **Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Successfully deploying learned policies from simulation to the real world is a notorious challenge due to the "sim-to-real gap." This gap arises from discrepancies in physics, sensor noise, and environmental details between simulated and real environments. The motivation here is to find a perceptual representation that inherently mitigates this gap.

To address these motivations, the paper proposes HumanoidPF as a novel representation, a hybrid scene generation strategy for scalable training, and a practical teleoperation system named "Click-and-Traverse" (CAT) to validate real-world applicability. These components collectively aim to advance the state-of-the-art in humanoid locomotion towards practical, real-world domestic robot applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is structured into three main components: Humanoid Potential Field (HumanoidPF) for whole-body traversal learning, a scalable training pipeline utilizing hybrid scene generation, and a real-world deployment system called Click-and-Traverse (CAT). The overall pipeline is depicted in Figure 2.

**A. HumanoidPF for Whole-Body Traversal Learning**
The core innovation lies in HumanoidPF, a reformulation of the classical Artificial Potential Field (APF) specifically designed for learning-based whole-body humanoid traversal.

1. **HumanoidPF Construction:**
 - **Attractive Field ($U_{att}$):** Models the goal position 'g' as an attractive pole. Crucially, it uses **geodesic distance** ($\|x-g\|_{geo}$) rather than Euclidean distance. This ensures that the attractive force inherently accounts for obstacles, guiding the robot along collision-free paths.
 - **Repulsive Field ($U_{rep}$):** Prevents collisions by modeling obstacles 'O' as repulsive surfaces. It is based on the signed distance $d(x)$ to the nearest obstacle, with an influence range $d_0$.
 - **Combined Potential and Gradient Field (F):** The total potential $U(x)$ is the sum of $U_{att}(x)$ and $U_{rep}(x)$. The guidance field 'F' is the negative gradient of this combined potential ($F = -\nabla U$), indicating collision-free motion directions.
 - **Priority-Weighting:** To overcome the "multi-modal dilemma" (where symmetrical obstacles might cause conflicting forces or local minima for a multi-jointed robot), a priority-weighting scheme is introduced. This assigns higher priority ($w_0=1$) to the root body part (e.g., pelvis) for global guidance and a dynamic collision-urgency weight ($w_1$) to all body parts. $w_1$ increases as a body part approaches an obstacle or moves faster towards it, prioritizing collision avoidance for more critical or immediate threats. The final HumanoidPF vector for each body part is $F_H = w_0 w_1 F / ||F||$, ensuring coordinated and context-aware whole-body control.

2. **Traversal Skill Learning with HumanoidPF:**
 - **HumanoidPF for Policy Observation ($OBS_{Field}$):** Instead of raw sensor data, the policy receives a compact, task-relevant observation by sampling $F_H$ at K=13 key body parts. Each sampled vector $F_H(x_k)$ provides explicit directional guidance for that body part to avoid obstacles and move towards the goal. This allows the policy to reason directly about traversal decisions. A key benefit is its function as a **low-pass perceptual filter**, smoothing out isolated noise and artifacts from real-world perception, thereby promoting robust sim-to-real transfer.
 - **HumanoidPF for Policy Reward ($R_{Field}$):** HumanoidPF is used to generate an anticipatory and dense reward signal. It defines a distribution of preferred motion directions at each time step using the von Mises-Fisher (vMF) distribution, with its mean $\mu(x)$ derived from the normalized $F_H(x)$ and its concentration $\kappa(x)$ scaled by the magnitude of $F_H(x)$. Body parts with higher priority (larger $||F_H(x)||$ receive a stronger alignment enforcement. The reward function encourages the robot's actual body part motion directions to align with this vMF distribution, fostering safe and dexterous collision avoidance. This reward formulation is designed for cross-scene generalization without manual tuning.

**B. Scalable Training in Diverse and Challenging Scenes**
To enable a generalist policy capable of handling diverse and complex indoor scenes, the authors propose a multi-faceted training approach.

1. **Hybrid Scene Generation:** Addresses the sparsity of challenging obstacle layouts in existing datasets.
 - **Crops of Realistic 3D Indoor Scenes:** Leverages the 3D-FRONT dataset for structurally realistic environments and furniture. Specific 5m x 5m blocks are cropped and filtered to focus on traversable areas.
 - **Procedurally Generated Obstacles:** Supplements the realistic scenes with synthesized "extreme" obstacles. These are boxes with varying positions, dimensions, and orientations, designed to create full-spatial constraints (ground, lateral, overhead simultaneously) and narrow passages. Random rotations and 2D Perlin noise are applied to enhance geometric realism, and morphological operations mitigate artifacts. A "layout-agnostic difficulty factor" allows for curriculum learning.

2. **Specialist-to-Generalist Training:**
 - **Large-scale Specialist Training:** Multiple specialist policies are trained in parallel using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) across a vast array of diverse scenes (139 cropped 3D-FRONT and 216 procedurally generated scenes). The HumanoidPF-derived reward facilitates this scalable training.
 - **Generalist Policy Distillation:** A single generalist policy is then distilled from these multiple specialists using DAgger, enabling it to acquire robust generalization capabilities across varying scenarios.
 - **Robustness Enhancement:** Both specialist and generalist policies are trained with sensor noise and force perturbations, along with a curriculum of progressively increasing scene difficulty, to ensure stability and robustness in real-world conditions.

**C. Real-World Deployment: Click-and-Traverse (CAT)**
For practical real-world application, the methodology culminates in a teleoperation system called Click-and-Traverse (CAT).
- **Perception and Mapping:** Integrates a LiDAR-inertial SLAM pipeline (Fast-LIO2) and OctoMap to maintain an up-to-date 3D environment map. This map is used to construct the HumanoidPF in real-time (10 Hz).
- **User Interface:** Users can command the humanoid by simply clicking a target location on a grid map. The system then autonomously computes and executes the collision-free traversal. This intuitive interface removes the need for complex manual control.

This comprehensive methodology, combining a novel perceptual representation, scalable training with diverse environments, and a practical deployment system, provides a robust solution for collision-free humanoid traversal in cluttered indoor scenes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted in both simulation (MuJoCo) and the real world (Unitree G1 humanoid robot) validate the effectiveness of the proposed HumanoidPF and hybrid scene generation method. The findings address three key questions:

**Q1: Can HumanoidPF improve the performance of traversal in cluttered indoor scenes compared to existing methods?**
The results, summarized in Table II, unequivocally demonstrate the superior performance of the proposed method (Ours) across eight distinct types of cluttered indoor scenes in simulation. These scene types cover a broad range of challenging obstacle configurations, including "Hurdle-Crouch," "Side-Hurdle-Crouch," "Crouch-Pass," and "Multi-Hurdle."
- **Overall Superiority:** "Ours" consistently achieved the highest Success Rate (SR, %) and lowest Distance Error (DE, m) compared to baselines such as ASTraversal and Humanoid Parkour. For instance, in the "Hurdle-Crouch" scenario, "Ours" achieved a 93.9% SR and 0.08m DE, significantly outperforming ASTraversal (28.1% SR, 1.11m DE) and Humanoid Parkour (33.3% SR, 1.16m DE).
- **Ablation Studies for HumanoidPF Components:** The ablation studies highlight the critical contributions of both HumanoidPF for observation ($OBS_{Field}$) and HumanoidPF for reward ($R_{Field}$):
 - **Ours w/o $OBS_{Field}$:** Replacing HumanoidPF observation with multi-layer elevation maps (similar to ASTraversal) led to a notable drop in performance, especially in highly complex scenarios (e.g., "Side-Hurdle-Crouch" SR 53.7% vs. 86.6% for "Ours"). This confirms that $OBS_{Field}$ provides crucial, task-relevant information.
 - **Ours w/o $R_{Field}$:** Removing the HumanoidPF-guided reward and using a basic collision-penalty reward resulted in a severe performance degradation. For example, in "Side-Pass," the SR dropped from 97.3% to 28.0%. This underscores that the anticipatory and dense guidance provided by $R_{Field}$ is essential for efficient and robust RL-based skill learning, particularly for coordinated whole-body avoidance.
- **Robustness and Stability:** The low variance observed in "Ours" over multiple trials indicates the high robustness and stability of the learned policy.

**Q2: Can our hybrid scene generation method help the policy generalize to unseen and challenging scenes?**
Table III presents results validating the effectiveness of the hybrid scene generation method for zero-shot generalization to unseen, artist-designed indoor scenes categorized as "easy" and "hard."
- **Significant Generalization Improvement:** Training solely on 3D-FRONT scenes ("Base") yielded a very low success rate on hard unseen scenes (1.2% SR). Progressively incorporating procedurally generated obstacles, especially those with high difficulty ("Base + Syn-Full-Hard"), dramatically improved generalization to hard unseen scenes, achieving a 66.7% SR.
- **Importance of "Extreme" Obstacles:** The findings show that while expanding obstacle diversity (e.g., "Base + Syn-Partial-Easy") helps, the key breakthrough in generalization comes from training with scenes that feature novel complexities, tighter constraints, and full-spatial layouts. This "extreme" obstacle generation pushes the policy to develop superior capabilities.

**Q3: Can the HumanoidPF for observation $OBS_{Field}$ help the sim-to-real transfer?**
Table IV summarizes the real-world transfer performance of the policy using different perceptual representations on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot in various challenging indoor scenarios (crouching, hurdling, side-pass, combined hurdle-crouch).
- **Superior Sim-to-Real Transfer with HumanoidPF:** HumanoidPF as a perceptual representation consistently outperformed traditional methods like Voxel Grids and Multi-layer Elevation Maps. For instance, in "Hurdle-Pass," "Ours" achieved a 5/5 success rate, while Voxel Grids and Elevation Maps only managed 3/5.
- **Robustness to Real-World Noise:** The results support the claim that HumanoidPF, acting as a low-pass perceptual filter, effectively smooths out real-world sensor noise and fine-grained geometric artifacts. This makes the policy less sensitive to discrepancies between simulation and reality, leading to more consistent and reliable collision-free traversal behaviors in the physical world.
- **Qualitative Validation:** Figures 1 and 4 provide compelling qualitative examples of the humanoid robot demonstrating collision-free traversal in both simulation and real-world cluttered environments, performing maneuvers like detouring, crouching, squeezing, and hurdling, further reinforcing the quantitative findings.

In conclusion, the main findings strongly support that HumanoidPF significantly enhances RL-based traversal skill learning, the hybrid scene generation method is crucial for policy generalization, and HumanoidPF for observation plays a vital role in robust sim-to-real transfer, paving the way for practical humanoid robot applications.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in the field of humanoid robotics, particularly in enabling autonomous navigation within complex, unstructured indoor environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized in several key areas:

1. **Advancing Humanoid Autonomy in Cluttered Spaces:** This paper presents, to the best of the authors' knowledge, the first systematic study of collision-free humanoid traversal in *cluttered indoor scenes* encompassing *full-spatial obstacle layouts* and *intricate, realistic geometries*. This pushes beyond the limitations of previous works that often addressed only partial obstacle layouts or simplified geometries. By demonstrating robust traversal skills in such challenging environments, the work directly contributes to the realization of highly autonomous humanoid robots for domestic and service applications.

2. **Novel and Effective Perceptual Representation (HumanoidPF):** The introduction of HumanoidPF is a major methodological contribution. By explicitly encoding humanoid-obstacle relationships as collision-free motion directions, it offers a more informative and task-relevant representation than raw sensor data.
 - **Facilitates RL:** HumanoidPF provides anticipatory and dense guidance, overcoming the problem of sparse and delayed rewards in traditional collision avoidance, thereby significantly accelerating and improving RL-based skill learning.
 - **Enhances Generalization:** Its abstraction allows the policy to reason directly about traversal decisions, promoting better generalization across diverse obstacle configurations.
 - **Mitigates Sim-to-Real Gap:** The inherent "low-pass perceptual filter" property of HumanoidPF, which smooths out noisy real-world sensor data, is a crucial innovation for robust sim-to-real transfer. This reduces the extensive effort typically required to bridge the simulation-reality divide.

3. **Scalable and Robust Training Methodology:** The proposed hybrid scene generation strategy is critical for developing generalizable policies. By combining realistic 3D indoor scenes with procedurally synthesized "extreme" obstacles, the method systematically exposes the robot to a wide range of challenging scenarios that are often underrepresented in existing datasets. This curriculum learning approach ensures the policy acquires robust skills for near-collision and emergency situations, which are vital for real-world safety and reliability.

4. **Practical Real-World Applicability:** The successful transfer of the learned policy to a real Unitree G1 humanoid robot, integrated into a user-friendly "Click-and-Traverse" (CAT) teleoperation system, demonstrates immediate practical utility. This system simplifies commanding humanoid robots in complex environments, moving away from labor-intensive control methods and closer to intuitive, high-level task specification. This showcases a clear path toward deploying humanoid robots in actual homes and workplaces.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Domestic Robotics:** The research lays a foundational groundwork for future generations of domestic humanoid robots that can seamlessly and safely navigate cluttered homes, perform chores, and assist humans without damaging themselves or the environment.
- **Robotics Research:** HumanoidPF could serve as a new paradigm for collision avoidance and motion planning in complex multi-body robotic systems, inspiring further research into similarly effective representations that bridge perception and control.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The demonstrated robustness of HumanoidPF in sim-to-real transfer could influence the design of future perception systems and training methodologies across various robotic applications, reducing development cycles and costs.
- **Human-Robot Interaction:** The intuitive Click-and-Traverse system improves the accessibility and usability of humanoid robots, potentially accelerating their adoption and integration into human-centric environments.

While the authors acknowledge limitations, such as the current lack of contact-rich interactions (e.g., leaning or stepping on obstacles) and the challenge of generalizing to entirely unseen, highly unstructured, and extremely cluttered scenes, these limitations clearly define promising avenues for future research. Overall, this work represents a substantial contribution towards realizing the vision of agile, intelligent, and safe humanoid robots operating autonomously in complex human environments.
