# A Comprehensive Survey on World Models for Embodied AI

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Nankai University and a collaborative network introduce a unified three-axis taxonomy for world models in Embodied AI, classifying them by functionality, temporal modeling, and spatial representation. This comprehensive survey systematically organizes diverse approaches, data resources, and evaluation metrics, providing a structured understanding and identifying critical future research directions.

## Problem

- The rapid proliferation of diverse world model variants in Embodied AI leads to inconsistent terminology and a fragmented understanding across different research communities.
- A lack of a unified taxonomy hinders systematic comparison, classification, and clear communication within the field.
- The heterogeneous nature of data resources and evaluation metrics across sub-domains (robotics, autonomous driving, video generation) impedes standardized assessment and reproducibility.

## Method

- Proposed a novel ==three-axis taxonomy== to categorize world models based on ==Functionality== (Decision-Coupled vs. General-Purpose), ==Temporal Modeling== (Sequential Simulation vs. Global Difference), and ==Spatial Representation== (Latent Vector, Token Sequence, Latent Grid, Decomposed Rendering).
- Formalized the problem setting of world models within ==Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs)== and detailed their learning objectives via the ==Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO)==.
- Systematized data resources and ==evaluation metrics== into comprehensive categories, including ==simulation platforms==, interactive benchmarks, offline datasets, real-world robot platforms, and multi-level performance metrics.

## Results

- Quantitative comparisons demonstrated improved visual fidelity (FID) and temporal consistency (FVD) in recent diffusion-based models for video generation, particularly in autonomous driving benchmarks like nuScenes.
- Models utilizing explicit 3D geometric priors (e.g., BEV, voxel grids, 3D Gaussian Splatting) achieved superior performance in 4D occupancy forecasting (mIoU) and robotic manipulation tasks (Success Rate).
- World models increasingly enable sample-efficient learning in control tasks (e.g., DMC), while fundamental challenges such as developing unified datasets, physically consistent metrics, and achieving long-horizon temporal consistency persist.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The proposed three-axis taxonomy effectively organizes hundreds of world model variants, providing a cohesive framework to understand their design choices and trade-offs.
> - An evolutionary trend from compact global latent vector representations (e.g., RSSMs) towards token feature sequences (e.g., Transformers with LLMs) and explicit 3D rendering representations (e.g., NeRF, 3D Gaussian Splatting) is observed.
> - There is a growing convergence between large-scale generative models and embodied AI, enabling general-purpose simulation and pre-training for diverse downstream applications.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied Artificial Intelligence (AI) endeavors to develop agents capable of perceiving complex, multimodal environments, acting within them, and anticipating the effects of their actions on future world states. A central component in achieving this capability is the "world model," which functions as an internal simulator. These models capture environment dynamics to facilitate both forward and counterfactual rollouts, thereby supporting perception, prediction, and decision-making processes.

The concept of internal world models has roots in cognitive science, where they are posited to not only predict future events but also shape perception and guide action. Early AI research integrated world models into model-based reinforcement learning (RL) to enhance sample efficiency and planning. The seminal work by Ha and Schmidhuber popularized the term "world model" and influenced subsequent developments such as the Dreamer series, which demonstrated imagination-based policy optimization.

More recently, advancements in large-scale generative modeling and multimodal learning have expanded the scope of world models beyond policy learning to include general-purpose environment simulators capable of high-fidelity future prediction, exemplified by models like Sora and V-JEPA 2. This expansion has diversified their functional roles, temporal modeling strategies, and spatial representations. However, this rapid growth has also introduced inconsistencies in terminology and taxonomy across various sub-communities within AI research.

Previous surveys on world models have generally adopted either a function-oriented approach (e.g., categorizing by understanding and prediction capabilities) or an application-driven approach (e.g., focusing on autonomous driving). This current work distinguishes itself by addressing the lack of a unified taxonomy within embodied AI. It proposes a three-axis framework—functionality, temporal modeling, and spatial representation—and applies this framework uniformly across diverse domains including robotics, autonomous driving, and general video settings. This approach aims to provide a standardized, comprehensive overview that integrates disparate research threads, systematizes evaluation, and identifies overarching challenges and future directions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this survey is to provide a detailed and unified understanding of world models within the field of embodied AI. The authors identify a critical need for such a comprehensive analysis due to the rapid evolution and diversification of world model research, which has led to fragmented terminology and inconsistent classification across different research communities.

The specific objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Addressing Terminological and Taxonomical Inconsistency:** The expansion of world models into various functional roles, temporal modeling strategies, and spatial representations has created a heterogeneous landscape. Existing surveys tend to be either function-specific or application-specific. This survey is motivated by the necessity to reconcile these disparate views by proposing a unified, three-axis taxonomy. This framework aims to provide a consistent language and structure for organizing current and future research, thereby overcoming the inconsistencies prevalent in the field.
- **Formalizing Problem Settings and Learning Objectives:** To establish a clear foundation, the survey aims to formalize the mathematical problem settings and learning objectives common to world models. This includes defining how observations are compressed into latent states, how dynamics models predict state evolution under actions, and how policies or planners utilize these imagined rollouts.
- **Systematizing Data Resources and Evaluation Metrics:** The development and assessment of world models rely on diverse datasets and metrics across robotics, autonomous driving, and general video. A key objective is to systematize these resources, covering aspects such as pixel prediction quality, state-level understanding, and task performance. This systematization is intended to provide a common ground for evaluating and comparing models across different domains and tasks.
- **Quantitative Comparison of State-of-the-Art Models:** Beyond qualitative classification, the survey aims to offer a quantitative comparison of representative state-of-the-art models. This objective involves analyzing their performance on standardized benchmarks to highlight their strengths, limitations, and the trade-offs inherent in different design choices.
- **Identifying Open Challenges and Future Directions:** The survey seeks to distill key open challenges that hinder the progress of world models in embodied AI. These include issues such as the scarcity of unified datasets, the need for evaluation metrics that prioritize physical consistency over pixel fidelity, the trade-off between model performance and computational efficiency for real-time control, and the fundamental difficulty of achieving long-horizon temporal consistency while mitigating error accumulation. By identifying these challenges, the authors aim to guide future research toward impactful areas.
- **Creating an Actionable Knowledge Map:** Ultimately, the motivation is to offer a panoramic and actionable knowledge map for researchers. By providing a structured overview, quantitative comparisons, and insights into challenges, the survey intends to facilitate informed decision-making in the design, development, and evaluation of world models for embodied AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this survey is structured around a proposed three-axis taxonomy designed to provide a unified framework for categorizing and analyzing world models in embodied AI. This framework is systematically applied across various domains to organize the existing literature, followed by a comprehensive review of data resources, evaluation metrics, and performance comparisons.

**A. Three-Axis Taxonomy**
The core of the methodology involves classifying world models along three independent dimensions:

1. **Functionality:** This axis differentiates models based on their primary purpose:
 - **Decision-Coupled:** These models are explicitly designed for specific control objectives. They typically learn dynamics tightly aligned with a particular task, leading to improved sample efficiency and closed-loop performance for that task. However, their generalization capabilities beyond the training distribution may be limited.
 - **General-Purpose:** These models act as task-agnostic simulators, prioritizing broad coverage of visual and physical phenomena. Their aim is to facilitate transfer across various tasks and domains. While offering broader applicability, their pretraining objectives may not always align optimally with specific downstream control tasks.

2. **Temporal Modeling:** This dimension categorizes how models handle the evolution of states over time:
 - **Sequential Simulation and Inference:** These models operate autoregressively, predicting future states one step at a time. This approach allows for fine-grained control and naturally supports closed-loop planning. However, it is susceptible to error accumulation over long prediction horizons, and computational costs scale linearly with rollout length.
 - **Global Difference Prediction:** This paradigm involves directly estimating future states in parallel, amortizing computation across time. This can reduce latency for long-horizon imagination but may weaken closed-loop interactivity and tend to smooth over fine-grained local dynamics, making it less suitable for tasks requiring precise step-wise control.

3. **Spatial Representation:** This axis outlines the strategies used to encode the spatial aspects of world states, each presenting distinct trade-offs in coherence, fidelity, and efficiency:
 - **Global Latent Vector:** Encodes the entire world state into a compact vector, enabling efficient real-time rollouts but sacrificing fine-grained spatial detail.
 - **Token Feature Sequence:** Discretizes world states into token sequences, modeling token-token dependencies via attention mechanisms. This supports fine-grained multimodal modeling but often requires extensive data, large models, and high inference costs.
 - **Spatial Latent Grid:** Injects spatial priors by encoding scenes into Bird's-Eye View (BEV) or voxel grids. This preserves local topology, supports multi-view fusion, and facilitates map-like planning, but incurs high memory costs, limited resolution, and reduced adaptability to unstructured environments.
 - **Decomposed Rendering Representation:** Decomposes scenes into renderable primitives (e.g., 3D Gaussian Splatting, Neural Radiance Fields) and reconstructs observations via differentiable rendering. This approach achieves geometry-consistent, high-fidelity, and object-level controllable views but typically involves expensive training and limitations in handling rapid dynamics or topology changes.

**B. Literature Systematization and Analysis**
The authors apply this three-axis taxonomy to systematically review and classify a broad range of representative works across robotics, autonomous driving, and general-purpose domains. Tables I and II categorize these works, detailing their publication, taxonomy labels, core characteristics, datasets used, platforms, input modalities, and whether they were validated on physical robots.

**C. Data Resources and Metrics Overview**
The survey dedicates a section to systematizing data resources and evaluation metrics. Data resources are categorized into:
- **Simulation Platforms** (e.g., MuJoCo, CARLA, Habitat, NVIDIA Isaac).
- **Interactive Benchmarks** (e.g., DeepMind Control, Atari, Meta-World, RLBench, nuPlan, LIBERO).
- **Offline Datasets** (e.g., RT-1, Open X-Embodiment, nuScenes, Waymo, Occ3D, Something-Something v2, OpenDV, VideoMix22M, VisionMix163M).
- **Real-world Robot Platforms** (e.g., Franka Emika, Unitree Go1/G1).

Evaluation metrics are organized into three levels of abstraction:
- **Pixel Prediction Quality:** Measures image fidelity and temporal consistency (e.g., FID, FVD, SSIM, PSNR, LPIPS, VBench).
- **State-level Understanding:** Assesses capture of objects, layouts, and semantics (e.g., mIoU, mAP, Displacement Error, Chamfer Distance).
- **Task Performance:** Evaluates goal achievement under real-world constraints (e.g., Success Rate, Sample Efficiency, Reward, Collision).

**D. Quantitative Performance Comparison**
The methodology includes a quantitative comparison of state-of-the-art models across various benchmarks. This section presents results for:
- Pixel generation on nuScenes (using FID and FVD).
- 4D Occupancy Forecasting on Occ3D-nuScenes (using mIoU).
- Control tasks on DeepMind Control (using Episode Return).
- Manipulation tasks on RLBench (using Success Rate).
- Open-loop planning on nuScenes (using L2 error and Collision Rate).

**E. Identification of Challenges and Trends**
Finally, the survey synthesizes the identified limitations and promising directions in data and evaluation, computational efficiency, and core modeling strategies. This involves discussing future research avenues to overcome current bottlenecks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey's analysis of world models across the proposed three-axis taxonomy reveals distinct design trade-offs and performance trends, alongside a comprehensive overview of existing resources and persistent challenges.

**A. Taxonomy-Driven Insights**
The application of the three-axis taxonomy clarifies the landscape of world models:
- **Functionality:** Decision-coupled models (e.g., early Dreamer variants like PlaNet, DreamerV2/V3) generally demonstrate high sample efficiency and strong closed-loop performance within their specific task domains. However, they exhibit limited generalization beyond the training distribution. Conversely, general-purpose models (e.g., Sora, V-JEPA 2, WorldDreamer) prioritize broad coverage of visual and physical phenomena, aiming for transferability across tasks and domains. Their task-agnostic pretraining, however, can lead to suboptimal behavior in specific control tasks unless fine-tuned.
- **Temporal Modeling:** Sequential simulation and inference approaches (e.g., RSSM-based models like PlaNet, Dreamer, TransDreamer) offer fine-grained control and are suitable for closed-loop planning. A notable drawback is their susceptibility to error accumulation over long prediction horizons, with computational costs scaling linearly. In contrast, global difference prediction models (e.g., Sora, V-JEPA variants, MaskGWM) directly estimate future states in parallel, amortizing computation and reducing latency for long rollouts. This approach, however, may exhibit smoother dynamics, which can weaken fine-grained interactivity and step-wise control.
- **Spatial Representation:** Different spatial representations offer varying balances of detail, efficiency, and coherence:
 - **Global Latent Vector** (e.g., PlaNet, Dreamer series): Provides computational efficiency for real-time rollouts but sacrifices fine-grained spatial detail.
 - **Token Feature Sequence** (e.g., TWM, IRIS, WorldDreamer, V-JEPA 2): Enables fine-grained multimodal modeling and causal inference by discretizing states into tokens. This approach often requires large datasets, models, and significant inference costs.
 - **Spatial Latent Grid** (e.g., DriveWorld, OccLLaMA, EnerVerse): Incorporates spatial priors by encoding scenes into grids (BEV or voxel), preserving local topology and supporting multi-view fusion. However, these models can incur large memory costs and limited resolution.
 - **Decomposed Rendering Representation** (e.g., ManiGaussian, DreMa, GAF): Decomposes scenes into renderable primitives (e.g., 3DGS, NeRFs), enabling geometry-consistent, high-fidelity, and object-level controllable views. Training and dynamic handling remain computationally expensive.

**B. Data Resources and Metrics**
The survey identifies a diverse but fragmented ecosystem of data resources and evaluation metrics. Data resources span various domains, from physics-based simulators (MuJoCo, CARLA) and interactive benchmarks (DMC, RLBench) to large-scale offline datasets (nuScenes, Waymo, RT-1, OXE). Recent efforts, such as OpenDV, VideoMix22M, and VisionMix163M, highlight a trend toward larger, more diverse datasets for pretraining. However, a unified, multimodal, cross-domain benchmark remains absent.

Evaluation metrics exist at three abstraction levels:
- **Pixel Prediction Quality:** Metrics like FID and FVD assess visual realism and temporal consistency.
- **State-level Understanding:** Metrics like mIoU, mAP, Displacement Error, and Chamfer Distance evaluate structural and semantic understanding.
- **Task Performance:** Metrics such as Success Rate, Sample Efficiency, Reward, and Collision directly measure an agent's ability to achieve goals. A key finding is that many metrics prioritize pixel fidelity over physical consistency or causal accuracy.

**C. Quantitative Performance Overview**
The quantitative comparisons reveal several performance trends:
- **Pixel Generation on nuScenes:** Diffusion-based models like DrivePhysica and MiLA demonstrate advancements in visual fidelity (lower FID) and temporal coherence (lower FVD), respectively, setting new state-of-the-art performance.
- **4D Occupancy Forecasting on Occ3D-nuScenes:** Methods leveraging ground-truth occupancy inputs and auxiliary supervision, particularly those with ego trajectory information (e.g., COME-O with GT ego), achieve superior mIoU scores, especially for longer horizons. Camera-based methods are showing improved competitiveness.
- **Control on DeepMind Control (DMC):** Recent world models (e.g., Dreamer variants, MWM, HRSSM) demonstrate enhanced data efficiency, achieving strong episode returns with significantly fewer training steps compared to earlier models.
- **Manipulation on RLBench:** Studies show that integrating multimodal inputs (depth, language, proprioception) and employing advanced backbones (e.g., 3DGS, DiT) improve success rates in robotic manipulation tasks. VidMan, using an Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) with diffusion, achieves high average success rates across a broad set of tasks.
- **Planning on nuScenes:** Open-loop planning evaluations highlight a trade-off between L2 trajectory error and collision rate. Approaches with extensive auxiliary supervision (e.g., UniAD+DriveWorld) achieve low L2 errors, while others like SSR attain competitive L2 and low collision rates without additional supervision. Camera-based methods are increasingly performing on par with or surpassing models using privileged occupancy data.

**D. Identified Challenges and Future Directions**
The survey distills three main categories of challenges:
- **Data & Evaluation:** The lack of unified, large-scale, multimodal, cross-domain datasets, especially for multi-agent interaction and safety-critical events, is a significant bottleneck. Current metrics predominantly focus on pixel fidelity, often failing to assess physical consistency, causal reasoning, and long-horizon error accumulation.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The high inference costs and memory footprints of advanced models (Transformers, diffusion) pose a challenge for deployment on real-time, resource-constrained robotic platforms.
- **Modeling Strategies:** Balancing the trade-offs between recurrent simulation (prone to error accumulation) and global prediction (computationally intensive, weaker closed-loop interactivity) remains difficult. Similarly, developing efficient spatial representations that explicitly encode geometry and physics while remaining tractable for control is an ongoing challenge. Visually plausible but physically incorrect predictions from generative models highlight the need for robust causal structures.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This comprehensive survey holds significant value for the field of embodied AI, offering a structured overview and critical analysis of world models. Its potential impact spans several key areas:

**A. Clarification and Standardization of Research Landscape**
The primary significance lies in the introduction of a unified, three-axis taxonomy (functionality, temporal modeling, and spatial representation). This framework clarifies the diverse and rapidly expanding landscape of world models, addressing inconsistencies in terminology and classification that have emerged across different sub-communities. By systematically mapping existing methods onto this framework, the survey provides a standardized lens through which researchers can categorize, compare, and understand the trade-offs inherent in various world model designs. This standardization can foster clearer communication and reduce fragmentation within the field.

**B. Guidance for Future Research and Development**
The survey serves as an actionable knowledge map for guiding future research. By meticulously outlining key objectives, methodologies, and quantitative performance benchmarks, it offers researchers a comprehensive overview of the current state-of-the-art. More importantly, the explicit identification and detailed discussion of open challenges (data scarcity, inadequate evaluation metrics, computational efficiency, and modeling long-horizon dynamics/causal structures) highlight critical areas requiring concerted effort. This strategic foresight can direct research toward addressing fundamental limitations, promoting the development of more robust, efficient, and generalizable world models.

**C. Promotion of Robust Evaluation Practices**
By systematizing data resources and evaluation metrics across different abstraction levels (pixel quality, state-level understanding, task performance), the survey advocates for more rigorous and comprehensive evaluation practices. It highlights the current over-reliance on pixel-fidelity metrics and underscores the need for new evaluation protocols that emphasize physical consistency, causal reasoning, and effective decision-making in embodied agents. This push towards more physically grounded metrics can ensure that models are judged not just on visual realism, but on their ability to support reliable and safe real-world interactions.

**D. Advancing Embodied AI Capabilities**
The insights derived from the performance comparisons and identified challenges directly contribute to advancing the capabilities of embodied AI agents. Understanding the trade-offs between different architectural choices (e.g., recurrent vs. global prediction, various spatial representations) can inform the design of more effective world models tailored for specific applications or designed for broader generalizability. The discussion on bridging the gap between large-scale generative video models and practical embodied control suggests pathways for leveraging powerful foundation models as reliable predictive infrastructure for downstream planning and policy learning.

**E. Facilitating Cross-Domain Transfer and Integration**
By uniformly analyzing world models across robotics, autonomous driving, and general video, the survey implicitly promotes the transfer of knowledge and techniques between these domains. This cross-domain perspective can inspire hybrid architectures and pretraining strategies that exploit shared principles of dynamics and scene representation, ultimately leading to more generalist embodied AI systems. The emphasis on multi-agent interaction and safety-critical events also highlights crucial directions for developing world models capable of operating in complex, dynamic, and social environments.

In conclusion, this survey provides a valuable and timely contribution to the embodied AI community. Its structured analysis and forward-looking perspective are poised to clarify the field, foster systematic research, and accelerate the development of world models that can robustly empower intelligent agents to perceive, predict, and act effectively in the physical world.
