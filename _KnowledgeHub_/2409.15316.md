# Towards Social AI: A Survey on Understanding Social Interactions

> [!summary] Summary
> A survey organizing machine-learning research on social interaction understanding around three verbal, non-verbal, and multimodal cue branches, arguing that multimodal alignment, multi-party dynamics, and belief awareness are the three core capabilities machines still lack for seamless social intelligence.

## Problem

- Prior surveys cover narrow subdomains like gaze, dialogue systems, or facial expression separately, without a unified view spanning verbal and non-verbal social understanding.
- Existing position papers on social AI infrastructure and agents give high-level discussion but lack a detailed, comprehensive review of the technical literature.
- Machines still cannot seamlessly interpret social interactions despite progress in narrow NLP and vision tasks, leaving a gap between specialized performance and holistic social intelligence.

## Method

- Proposes a ==three-capability framework==, ==multimodal social cue understanding==, ==multi-party dynamics modeling==, and ==belief awareness==, as the organizing lens for evaluating social AI progress.
- Builds a ==taxonomy== of social understanding research classified by cue type, verbal (dialogue act, dialogue emotion, common sense reasoning), non-verbal (gesture, gaze, facial expression), and multimodal (emotion, conversation dynamics, social situation).
- Compiles three comparative dataset tables spanning verbal, non-verbal, and multimodal social cue benchmarks, annotated with data type, label type, size, and interaction setting.

## Results

- Synthesizes coverage across **nine** research areas showing verbal-cue work like dialogue act and emotion analysis dominates, while multi-party and belief-aware tasks remain comparatively sparse in the literature.
- Tabulates roughly **40** datasets across the three cue categories, revealing most non-verbal datasets are lab-based and isolated rather than continuous or in-the-wild multi-party recordings.
- Identifies persistent benchmark limitations, e.g., Social-IQ's multiple-choice format enables shortcut solutions, and Ego4D cannot observe the camera wearer's own facial cues.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Holistic social intelligence requires integrating capabilities across modalities rather than treating verbal, non-verbal, and multimodal understanding as isolated research silos.
> - Person-level and temporal alignment between modalities, not just global multimodal fusion, is necessary since social meaning is often tied to specific individuals in a scene.
> - Belief and cultural awareness are consistently underrepresented across benchmarks, signaling that future datasets must annotate mental states and relationships, not just observable behavior.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This survey paper is positioned at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields within Artificial Intelligence, primarily **Social AI**, **Affective Computing**, **Human-Computer Interaction (HCI)**, **Computer Vision**, and **Natural Language Processing (NLP)**. The broader research landscape acknowledges that while AI has achieved impressive feats in specialized tasks (e.g., game playing, image recognition, language generation), its ability to genuinely understand and participate in complex human social interactions remains a significant, open challenge.

The paper argues that developing machines with "social intelligence" is foundational for a wide array of next-generation AI applications. This includes creating more effective embodied agents and virtual assistants that can collaborate naturally with humans, building more intuitive chatbots, and enhancing recommendation systems to better grasp user needs and build rapport. The authors highlight a critical gap: existing AI systems often lack the nuanced understanding of social cues, multi-party dynamics, and underlying human beliefs that are intuitive for humans.

This survey differentiates itself from previous literature by offering a **holistic and comprehensive overview** of machine learning studies for social understanding. The authors explicitly state that earlier surveys tended to focus on narrow subdomains (e.g., only gaze analysis, specific dialogue systems, or facial expression recognition) without fully integrating the broader social context. By synthesizing research across verbal, non-verbal, and multimodal modalities, this paper aims to provide a unified perspective, serving as a much-needed comprehensive resource for researchers looking to advance socially intelligent AI. It frames *understanding* social interactions as a prerequisite for the *creation* of socially intelligent agents, thereby setting a foundational agenda for the field. It also implicitly connects to fields like computational social science and cognitive science, drawing on human psychology to define the necessary capabilities for machines.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this survey stems from the recognition that despite significant advancements in specialized AI tasks, machines still fall short in seamlessly understanding and navigating complex human social interactions. Humans effortlessly interpret subtle social cues, understand intentions, emotions, and situational contexts, enabling effective relationship building. This remarkable human capability, termed "social intelligence," is largely absent in current AI systems, posing a major impediment to creating truly effective and natural human-AI collaboration.

Against this backdrop, the paper sets forth several key objectives:

1. **Identify Core Capabilities:** The foremost objective is to pinpoint the fundamental capabilities machines require to effectively understand social interactions. Based on analyzing complex human interactions (such as social deduction games), the authors propose three crucial pillars:
 - **Understanding Multimodal Social Cues:** The ability to interpret and integrate information from various channels, including verbal utterances (language) and non-verbal behaviors (facial expressions, body language, gaze).
 - **Understanding Multi-party Dynamics:** The capacity to model and reason about interactions involving multiple participants, including turn-taking, inter-personal relationships, and group behaviors.
 - **Understanding Beliefs:** The recognition of the complex mental models humans possess, including individual and shared beliefs (e.g., personalities, social norms, cultural contexts), which profoundly influence interactions.

2. **Systematically Review Existing Works:** To assess the current state of the art, the survey aims to classify and review existing machine learning research in social understanding. This review is structured according to the identified modalities of social cues: verbal, non-verbal, and multimodal.

3. **Clarify Development Trajectory and Illuminate Future Paths:** By meticulously reviewing the scope and limitations of current approaches and benchmarks within each category, the survey intends to clarify the progress made, identify existing gaps, and suggest promising future research directions. This includes highlighting the need for better integration across modalities, more sophisticated multi-party modeling, and deeper incorporation of belief systems.

4. **Stimulate Further Research:** Ultimately, the authors hope this comprehensive survey will "spur further research interest and insights" into the vital yet challenging domain of social understanding, thereby accelerating the development of more socially intelligent AI. The emphasis is on providing a foundational understanding that can guide the creation of more effective and human-centric AI applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a survey paper, the methodology primarily involves a structured and critical review of the existing literature in machine learning for social understanding. The authors do not present new empirical data or develop novel models; instead, they synthesize knowledge, identify trends, and highlight limitations and future directions.

The core of their approach is built around a **taxonomy-driven literature review**, guided by the three key capabilities they identify for social understanding: multimodal understanding, multi-party modeling, and belief awareness. The paper organizes existing research into three main branches based on the type of social cues:

1. **Understanding Verbal Cues:**
 - **Dialogue Act Analysis:** Examines techniques for classifying the communicative function or intended action of utterances (e.g., request, assertion). It reviews early neural network approaches (CNNs, RNNs), hierarchical models, and recent transformer-based methods, noting challenges in broad social context and multi-party complexity.
 - **Dialogue Emotion Analysis:** Covers methods for recognizing and classifying emotions or sentiments expressed in conversations. It discusses models that integrate context from surrounding utterances, speaker states, and preceding emotions (e.g., LSTM, GCNs, transformer-based approaches), highlighting issues with fine-grained emotions and cultural variation.
 - **Common Sense Reasoning:** Explores the machine's ability to draw logical conclusions based on world knowledge, crucial for resolving linguistic ambiguity and understanding social context. It reviews progress from embedding models to large language models (LLMs) and the integration of external knowledge bases, noting challenges with data sparsity and human-level reasoning.

2. **Understanding Non-verbal Cues:**
 - **Gesture and Body Language Analysis:** Reviews techniques for interpreting social meaning from hand movements and body postures. It distinguishes between isolated and continuous gesture recognition, surveying approaches from traditional computer vision to deep learning (CNNs, 3D CNNs, Transformers), while pointing out limitations in communicative social gestures and cultural variations.
 - **Gaze and Attention Analysis:** Focuses on detecting gaze targets and understanding multi-person social gaze dynamics. It covers models for gaze target detection (e.g., 2-stream neural networks, Transformer-based architectures) and higher-level shared attention or mutual gaze detection, emphasizing the limited work on joint modeling with other modalities.
 - **Facial Expression Analysis:** Discusses advancements in interpreting emotions from facial movements. It distinguishes between static and dynamic facial expression recognition, reviewing deep learning models (CNNs, Vision Transformers) and highlights critical issues such as bias (gender, race, culture) and privacy concerns.

3. **Understanding Multimodal Cues:**
 - **Multimodal Emotion Analysis:** Explores approaches that integrate verbal, visual, and acoustic cues for comprehensive emotion recognition. It surveys fusion techniques (e.g., LSTM-based, GCN-based models) and discusses limitations related to narrow emotion categories and the need for individual-specific features.
 - **Conversation Dynamics Analysis:** Reviews methods for modeling fine-grained interactions in multi-party conversations, such as identifying speaking targets and referential relationships. It includes works on multimodal gaze following, active speaker localization, and modeling spatial relations of participants, noting the lack of higher-level semantic reasoning.
 - **Social Situation Analysis:** Addresses the holistic interpretation of social scenarios, encompassing social relationship reasoning and social question answering. It covers single-modal and multimodal approaches for inferring relationships and answering questions about social contexts, pointing out biases in Q&A benchmarks and the need for cultural context.

For each sub-area, the authors provide a "Social Background" to explain the human importance of the cue, then detail the "Recognition/Classification" techniques, and conclude with a "Discussion" of current limitations and open challenges. The survey also includes comprehensive tables of relevant datasets for each category, providing details on data types, label types, data size, and remarks. This structured approach allows for a systematic and critical evaluation of the state of the art, building a strong foundation for their proposed future directions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper, as a comprehensive survey, does not present new experimental results in the traditional sense. Instead, its "findings" are the synthesis and critical analysis of the existing research landscape, highlighting both progress and persistent challenges. The main findings can be summarized as follows:

1. **Significant Progress in Modality-Specific Understanding:** The survey acknowledges that AI has made considerable strides in understanding individual social cues within specific modalities. For instance:
 - In **verbal cues**, advanced neural networks and large language models (LLMs) have improved dialogue act classification, emotion recognition in conversations, and commonsense reasoning.
 - In **non-verbal cues**, deep learning models (CNNs, 3D CNNs, Transformers) have enabled increasingly accurate gesture recognition, gaze target detection, and facial expression analysis.

2. **Identification of Three Core Capabilities:** The paper firmly establishes that achieving truly comprehensive social understanding in AI necessitates progress across three interconnected capabilities:
 - **Multimodal Understanding:** The ability to integrate and interpret information from verbal (language), non-verbal visual (gestures, gaze, facial expressions), and non-verbal acoustic (tone, prosody) cues holistically.
 - **Multi-party Modeling:** The capacity to analyze and reason about interactions involving more than two participants, capturing complex group dynamics, inter-personal relationships, and interaction history.
 - **Belief Awareness:** The imperative for AI to model and understand the underlying individual and shared beliefs (e.g., intentions, desires, cultural norms, social roles) that drive human behavior in social contexts.

3. **Pervasive Limitations and Challenges:** Across all reviewed areas, the survey consistently identifies several recurring limitations that prevent current AI from achieving human-level social intelligence:
 - **Inadequate Multimodal Integration:** Many existing approaches rely on simple fusion methods or fixed weighting schemes for different modalities, failing to adaptively integrate cues based on context. Person-level alignment of multimodal data in multi-party scenes remains a significant open problem. The potential of general-purpose multimodal foundation models for social understanding is largely unexplored.
 - **Simplistic Multi-party Dynamics Modeling:** Most research focuses on dyadic interactions or struggles to capture the complexity of one-to-many dynamics, emergent group-level behaviors, and the influence of long-term interaction history. Benchmarks often lack the necessary granularity for these complex scenarios.
 - **Limited Belief Awareness:** Current models often overlook the profound impact of cultural background, specific social relationships, and individual mental states (intentions, desires) on social interactions. Explicitly incorporating this "theory of mind" into AI remains a major challenge.
 - **Dataset Gaps and Biases:** A critical bottleneck is the lack of sufficiently comprehensive, diverse, and unbiased datasets. Many datasets:
 - Lack fine-grained, nuanced annotations for emotions or social states.
 - Are confined to controlled lab settings or specific, less generalizable scenarios (e.g., games).
 - Fail to represent the full spectrum of real-world multi-party interactions, cultural variations, or long-range dependencies.
 - Are susceptible to annotation biases (e.g., gender, race, cultural, or question-answer biases).

4. **The Need for Holistic Integration:** The overarching finding is that isolated advancements in specific modalities are insufficient. True social AI requires a deep, interconnected understanding that integrates multimodal cues, models complex multi-party dynamics, and incorporates explicit awareness of human beliefs. The components listed in the taxonomy are not isolated but "key building blocks and capabilities that need to be connected and integrated."

In essence, the paper concludes that while significant components of social understanding are being built, the true challenge lies in assembling these components into a coherent, adaptive, and human-like social intelligence, guided by a robust conceptual framework that accounts for multimodal input, multi-party interactions, and mental states.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance and potential impact of "Towards Social AI: A Survey on Understanding Social Interactions" are substantial, primarily due to its comprehensive scope and its clear articulation of the path forward for socially intelligent AI.

1. **Provides a Unified Roadmap for Social AI Research:** The most significant contribution of this survey is its synthesis of fragmented research across computer vision, natural language processing, and affective computing into a coherent conceptual framework. By identifying and emphasizing the three core capabilities—multimodal understanding, multi-party modeling, and belief awareness—it offers a much-needed roadmap for future research. This structured view helps researchers prioritize efforts, identify interdependencies, and build upon a shared understanding of the field's grand challenges. It moves beyond merely listing advancements to defining the foundational pillars required for true social intelligence in machines.

2. **Catalyst for Interdisciplinary Collaboration:** By bringing together a diverse group of authors from leading institutions and fields, the paper itself exemplifies the interdisciplinary nature required for social AI. It highlights the necessity of combining expertise from various AI sub-disciplines to tackle the multifaceted problem of social understanding, thus encouraging broader collaboration across previously siloed research areas.

3. **Informs Dataset Development and Benchmarking:** A recurrent theme in the survey is the limitations of current datasets in capturing the complexity, diversity, and nuances of real-world social interactions. By meticulously detailing these shortcomings across modalities and tasks, the paper provides invaluable guidance for the creation of next-generation benchmarks that are more comprehensive, fine-grained, culturally aware, and robust against biases. This will be crucial for training and evaluating more sophisticated social AI models.

4. **Foundation for Human-Centric AI Applications:** The ultimate impact of advancing social understanding in AI lies in its potential to revolutionize human-AI interaction. AI systems that can genuinely understand social cues, dynamics, and beliefs will be far more effective, empathetic, and trustworthy. This can lead to:
 - **More natural human-robot and human-agent collaboration:** Embodied agents and virtual assistants could adapt to human emotions, intentions, and group dynamics more seamlessly.
 - **Improved conversational AI:** Chatbots and dialogue systems could engage in more contextually appropriate and emotionally intelligent conversations.
 - **Enhanced assistive technologies:** AI could provide better support in domains like education, mental health counseling (as suggested by the HOPE dataset), or elder care by interpreting subtle human signals.
 - **More personalized systems:** Recommendation systems and other adaptive AI could better understand user needs and preferences by interpreting social signals beyond explicit input.

5. **Raises Awareness of Ethical Considerations:** While not explicitly a focus on ethics, the discussions around biases in facial expression recognition and the need for cultural awareness implicitly highlight the ethical imperatives in developing social AI. By identifying these issues, the survey encourages responsible research practices and the development of AI systems that are fair, inclusive, and respect privacy.

In summary, this survey is not just a review; it's a strategic document that defines the boundaries of current knowledge, articulates the fundamental missing pieces, and offers a compelling vision for a future where AI can navigate the richness of human social interactions with a level of understanding that approaches human capability. This foundational work is critical for steering the field towards creating AI that is truly intelligent in a social context, ultimately leading to more beneficial and integrated human-AI ecosystems.
