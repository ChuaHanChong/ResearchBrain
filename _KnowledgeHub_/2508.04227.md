# Continual Learning for VLMs: A Survey and Taxonomy Beyond Forgetting

> [!summary] Summary
> A systematic review and taxonomy characterize the unique challenges and methodologies for enabling Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to learn continually from non-stationary data streams without forgetting. This work identifies specific VLM-CL failure modes and categorizes existing solutions within an emerging research field.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) suffer catastrophic forgetting when continually learning from non-stationary data, hindering their real-world deployment.
- Existing continual learning (CL) and multimodal CL (MMCL) approaches do not adequately address the unique architectural and knowledge preservation challenges of VLMs.
- Continual updates to VLMs lead to cross-modal feature drift, interference in shared modules, and erosion of zero-shot generalization capabilities.

## Method

- A ==systematic literature review== was conducted to diagnose the specific challenges of ==continual learning for Vision-Language Models (VLM-CL)==.
- A novel, ==challenge-driven taxonomy== was developed to categorize existing VLM-CL methodologies based on their primary ==problem-solving mechanisms==.
- The work analyzes current ==evaluation protocols==, datasets, and metrics to identify gaps and limitations in benchmarking VLM-CL.

## Results

- A comprehensive taxonomy was established, classifying VLM-CL methods into Multi-Modal Replay, Cross-Modal Regularization, and Parameter-Efficient Adaptation strategies.
- The review revealed that current evaluation benchmarks for VLM-CL are fragmented, with a significant reliance on repurposed unimodal datasets that often fail to capture VLM-specific forgetting.
- The paper outlines critical open problems and future research directions for VLM-CL, including the need for scalable continual pre-training and improved theoretical understanding.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Continual learning in VLMs faces unique failure modes, including cross-modal feature drift, shared module interference, and erosion of zero-shot generalization abilities.
> - Existing VLM-CL solutions can be systematically categorized into Multi-Modal Replay, Cross-Modal Regularization, and Parameter-Efficient Adaptation strategies.
> - Current VLM-CL evaluation benchmarks are fragmented and often repurposed, limiting their ability to fully diagnose VLM-specific continual learning challenges.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and the more recent Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have significantly advanced artificial intelligence by demonstrating strong cross-modal alignment and zero-shot generalization capabilities. These models typically operate under a "pre-train and transfer" paradigm, where they are trained on vast, static datasets to create robust multimodal embedding spaces.

However, real-world applications such as embodied AI, scientific robots, or personalized assistants often involve data streams that are non-stationary and evolving. This necessitates that models continually acquire new knowledge without "catastrophic forgetting" of previously learned information. The field of continual learning (CL) addresses this challenge in unimodal contexts through techniques like regularization, rehearsal, and dynamic network expansion.

More recently, multimodal continual learning (MMCL) has emerged, but early studies have primarily focused on smaller-scale models and datasets, often assuming homogeneous architectures across tasks. These assumptions diverge from the reality of modern foundation models, which feature frozen vision encoders, intricate cross-modal projectors, and extensive LLM backbones optimized for zero-shot generalization.

This survey positions itself as the first comprehensive, diagnostic review specifically addressing continual learning for *foundation-level* predictive VLMs and generative MLLMs. It aims to bridge the gap between traditional CL research and the distinct vulnerabilities of these large, pre-trained multimodal systems, which are not adequately captured by existing MMCL surveys. The work highlights that forgetting in these advanced models extends beyond simple factual amnesia to encompass systemic collapses in cross-modal alignment, feature spaces, and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research is to address the fundamental question: "How can VLMs acquire new knowledge over time without forgetting prior capabilities?" The authors identify that simply fine-tuning these models on new tasks inevitably leads to catastrophic forgetting, a problem amplified by their complex multimodal nature.

The paper delineates three core challenges unique to continual learning in VLMs and MLLMs, distinguishing them from unimodal CL:

- **Cross-Modal Feature Drift:** Pre-training aligns visual and textual features within a shared semantic space. During sequential updates, particularly under domain shifts or with asymmetric modality inputs, this delicate alignment can become distorted. This drift, exacerbated by the inherent modality gap between vision and language, can topologically warp the joint embedding space, causing previously learned concept pairs to decouple and thereby destroying cross-modal retrieval capabilities.
- **Shared Module Interference:** Many modern VLM architectures, especially fusion-based ones, rely on deep cross-modal projectors and shared LLM backbones to integrate visual and linguistic information. Sequential updates to these shared modules, without proper isolation, can lead to destructive gradient crosstalk. In parameter-efficient settings, this often results in a "misaligned co-drift" between routing mechanisms and expert modules, disrupting the model's multimodal interaction logic.
- **Zero-Shot Capability Erosion & Reasoning Collapse:** A defining characteristic of foundation models is their broad open-vocabulary generalization. Continual fine-tuning on narrow, task-specific datasets can compress this wide manifold, leading to the erosion of zero-shot capabilities. More critically for generative MLLMs, this erosion manifests as a micro-level collapse of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) logic, where models lose fundamental spatial and compositional reasoning skills even before exhibiting surface-level factual forgetting. Generative MLLMs specifically exhibit an "alignment tax," where forgetting involves a systemic breakdown of deep reasoning rather than just factual amnesia.

Given these unaddressed micro-level failure mechanisms in foundation models, the primary objectives of this survey are:

1. To explicitly identify and analyze the three fundamental failure modes specific to VLM/MLLM continual learning.
2. To propose a solution-oriented taxonomy that classifies state-of-the-art methods into distinct paradigms based on how they mitigate these pathologies.
3. To systematically review the evolution of evaluation protocols, highlighting the shift towards dual-track MLLM benchmarks (Domain vs. Ability CL) and micro-diagnostic CoT evaluations.
4. To discuss open challenges and outline a roadmap for future research, emphasizing areas like compositional zero-shot generalization, embodied AI with sensor fusion, and autonomous agentic ecosystems.

The survey predominantly focuses on the Continual Fine-tuning (CFT) paradigm, acknowledging Continual Pre-training (CPT) as a critical future direction for lifelong multimodal AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a survey paper, the methodology involved a systematic review and synthesis of existing literature on continual learning for Vision-Language Models. The authors approached this by:

1. **Deconstructing Failure Modes:** They began by systematically analyzing the unique challenges faced by VLMs and MLLMs in continual learning, moving beyond traditional unimodal catastrophic forgetting. This involved identifying and illustrating Cross-Modal Feature Drift, Shared Module Interference, and Zero-Shot Capability Erosion as primary pathologies.
2. **Developing a Challenge-Driven Taxonomy:** Based on the identified failure modes, the authors propose a taxonomy to categorize existing continual learning methods. This taxonomy organizes approaches into three main paradigms, each addressing specific aspects of VLM-CL:
 - **Multi-Modal Replay Strategies (MMRE):** These methods mitigate forgetting and feature drift by re-visiting past knowledge. They are further classified into:
 - *Explicit Replay:* Directly storing and reusing a small subset of real data from previous tasks.
 - *Implicit Replay:* Simulating past data distributions using generative models or pseudo-samples to reduce storage and privacy concerns.
 - **Cross-Modal Regularization (CREG):** These strategies maintain alignment by imposing constraints directly on the model's loss function or update process. Key sub-approaches include:
 - *Knowledge Distillation:* Using a "teacher" model (pre-trained or from a previous task) to guide the learning of the current model.
 - *Alignment Maintenance:* Directly constraining the structure of the cross-modal feature space (e.g., similarity matrices, graph structures).
 - *Guided Regularization:* Using one modality to inform and constrain the learning of another.
 - **Parameter-Efficient Adaptation (PEA):** These methods address shared module interference by freezing the main pre-trained model and only updating a small, task-specific subset of parameters. PEA is further sub-divided into:
 - *Prompt-based Methods:* Injecting learnable prompt vectors at the input level while keeping the entire PTM frozen.
 - *Adapter-based Methods:* Inserting small, trainable neural modules into a frozen backbone.
 - *Low-Rank Decomposition Methods (e.g., LoRA):* Learning low-rank updates to original weight matrices to balance stability and plasticity.
 - *MoE-based Methods:* Employing a router to dynamically select from a pool of "expert" modules (which can be adapters or LoRA modules).
3. **Reviewing Evaluation Protocols:** The survey systematically examines the evolution of evaluation metrics and benchmarks. This includes traditional CL metrics (e.g., average accuracy, forgetting ratio, backward transfer) adapted for multimodal contexts, as well as VLM-specific metrics (e.g., zero-shot transfer, zero-shot degradation, Recall@K for retrieval). It categorizes benchmarks into repurposed unimodal, adapted multimodal, and native VLM-CL benchmarks, discussing their suitability for diagnosing VLM-specific challenges.
4. **Analyzing Performance and Identifying Gaps:** The authors synthesize performance trends of state-of-the-art methods across tasks like image classification, multimodal retrieval, and Visual Question Answering (VQA), also considering parameter and memory efficiency. This analysis informs the identification of current limitations and future research directions.

The survey primarily focuses on the **Continual Fine-tuning (CFT)** paradigm, where a fixed, pre-trained VLM backbone is adapted to a sequence of downstream tasks, often using Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) techniques. The **Continual Pre-training (CPT)** paradigm, aiming for truly lifelong multimodal AI, is recognized as an emerging area with only exploratory works.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The core findings of this survey are presented through its diagnostic analysis of VLM-CL challenges, its proposed taxonomy of solutions, and its review of evaluation methodologies and performance.

#### 4.1. Identification of Core Challenges

The survey establishes that VLM-CL faces distinct challenges beyond unimodal catastrophic forgetting:
- **Cross-Modal Feature Drift:** This is a fundamental issue where the pre-trained alignment between visual and textual embeddings deteriorates during continual updates, particularly under domain shifts. This desynchronization compromises tasks reliant on multimodal linking, such as retrieval.
- **Shared Module Interference:** In fusion-based architectures, shared components (e.g., cross-attention layers, LLM backbones) are susceptible to destructive overwriting during sequential learning, leading to performance degradation on older tasks.
- **Zero-Shot Capability Erosion & Reasoning Collapse:** Continual fine-tuning, by specializing representations for new tasks, can degrade the broad open-vocabulary generalization of foundation models. For generative MLLMs, this is particularly severe, manifesting as a collapse of deep Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning.

#### 4.2. Proposed Taxonomy of VLM-CL Methods

The survey categorizes existing methods into three main paradigms, often used in hybrid forms:
- **Multi-Modal Replay Strategies (MMRE):**
 - **Explicit Replay** (e.g., VQACL, MSPT) directly reuses past data, effectively tackling feature drift and preserving generalization.
 - **Implicit Replay** (e.g., IncCLIP, SGP, GIFT) generates pseudo-samples or hard negatives, mitigating storage and privacy concerns. These methods are direct and effective but face inherent storage and computational overhead, with a risk of overfitting if only downstream data is replayed.
- **Cross-Modal Regularization (CREG):**
 - **Knowledge Distillation** (e.g., DualTeacher, SCD) uses a teacher model to guide the student, stabilizing modality relationships.
 - **Alignment Maintenance** (e.g., ZSCL, DKR, Mod-X) directly constrains the structure of the cross-modal feature space or modality gap, preventing alignment degeneration.
 - **Guided Regularization** (e.g., RAPF, CPE-CLIP) uses one modality to inform the learning of another. These methods offer data-efficient alignment maintenance but require careful tuning and a nuanced understanding of VLM mechanisms to balance stability and plasticity.
- **Parameter-Efficient Adaptation (PEA):** These strategies are crucial for VLM-CL due to the large scale of foundation models. They freeze the main model and update a small subset of parameters, structurally isolating updates and alleviating shared module interference and zero-shot erosion.
 - **Prompt-based Methods** (e.g., S-liPrompt, LPI) inject learnable prompt vectors to steer model behavior, preserving zero-shot capabilities and offering lightweight designs, but are sensitive to modal alignment.
 - **Adapter-based Methods** (e.g., CLAP4CLIP, ATLAS) insert small, trainable modules, providing clear parameter isolation, though the number of adapters can grow with tasks.
 - **Low-Rank Decomposition Methods** (e.g., CoDyRA, DIKI) learn low-rank updates to weight matrices, balancing stability and plasticity efficiently but requiring careful rank selection.
 - **MoE-based Methods** (e.g., MoE-Adapters, HiDe-LLaVA) use routers to dynamically select expert modules, supporting dynamic parameter allocation but often relying on task identity and potentially incurring higher computational overhead. PEA methods are a cornerstone, maximizing learning while minimizing parameter modification.

#### 4.3. Evolution of Evaluation Protocols

The survey highlights a shift in evaluation:
- **Core Metrics:** Beyond traditional accuracy, forgetting, and transfer metrics (Average Accuracy, Last Accuracy, Forgetting Ratio, Backward Transfer, Zero-shot Transfer), VLM-specific metrics like Zero-Shot Degradation are critical. Retrieval tasks use Recall@K and mAP, decomposed into I2T and T2I, while VQA uses Answer Precision and F1 scores.
- **Specialized Benchmarks:** The field has progressed from repurposing unimodal datasets (e.g., CIFAR-100, ImageNet) to adapted multimodal benchmarks (e.g., MDL-VQA, P9D) and increasingly to native VLM-CL benchmarks (e.g., VQACL, COCO-CL, TiC, CoIN, UCIT, MLLM-CL) that explicitly address cross-modal forgetting and compositional reasoning decay. Most current evaluation relies on class-incremental protocols.

#### 4.4. Performance Analysis on Key Tasks

- **Image Classification:** SOTA methods (e.g., MoE-Adapter, Dual-RAIL, DPeCLIP, GIFT) on benchmarks like MTIL demonstrate improvements in transfer, average, and last accuracy, with parameter-efficient techniques bolstering zero-shot accuracy.
- **Multimodal Retrieval:** On benchmarks like Flickr30K and MS-COCO, methods like DKR and Mod-X show strong performance by actively preserving the relational structure of the multimodal embedding space, significantly outperforming traditional CL methods.
- **Visual Question Answering (VQA):** Benchmarks like VQACL (with standard and novel-composition testing) and newer instruction-tuning benchmarks (CoIN, UCIT, MLLM-CL) are used to evaluate reasoning capabilities. QUAD demonstrates strong results, combining pseudo-label memory and attention distillation.
- **Efficiency:** PEFT methods (e.g., CoDyRA) significantly reduce trainable parameters compared to full fine-tuning, demonstrating efficiency and practicality for real-world deployment, especially when compared to the memory costs of replay-based methods.

Overall, the survey finds that no single strategy is a complete solution; hybrid methods combining PEA with replay or regularization are increasingly common to balance stability, plasticity, and efficiency. The majority of research operates under the Continual Fine-tuning (CFT) paradigm, leveraging powerful pre-trained models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey holds significant implications for the future development of robust and adaptive artificial intelligence systems, particularly within the domain of multimodal learning.

**Key Significance:**

- **First Diagnostic Review:** This work provides the first comprehensive and diagnostic review specifically tailored to continual learning in *foundation-level* Vision-Language Models and Multimodal Large Language Models. By explicitly identifying and analyzing the three unique failure modes—cross-modal feature drift, shared module interference, and zero-shot capability erosion (including reasoning collapse)—it moves beyond a simple enumeration of existing works to offer a problem-centric understanding.
- **Challenge-Driven Taxonomy:** The proposed taxonomy, categorizing methods based on how they address these specific challenges, offers a structured framework for understanding the field. This organization helps researchers to identify the strengths and weaknesses of different approaches and to design more targeted solutions.
- **Systematization of Evaluation:** The systematic review of evaluation protocols, metrics, and benchmarks provides a clearer understanding of how progress in VLM-CL should be measured. It highlights the crucial need for dual-track benchmarks and micro-diagnostic CoT evaluations to capture the true complexities of multimodal forgetting and generalization.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** By delineating current gaps and suggesting promising directions, the survey acts as a foundational guide. It clarifies where research efforts should be concentrated to overcome limitations and build genuinely lifelong learning multimodal AI.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Improved Model Robustness:** A deeper understanding of VLM-CL challenges and solutions can lead to the development of more robust multimodal AI systems that can adapt to evolving data environments without suffering from catastrophic forgetting or degradation of core capabilities. This is critical for real-world applications where data streams are inherently non-stationary.
- **Enhanced Multimodal Generalization:** By focusing on preserving zero-shot capabilities and preventing reasoning collapse, the research can lead to VLMs and MLLMs that maintain their broad generalization power even after sequential learning, allowing them to tackle novel, unseen tasks effectively.
- **Advancement of Embodied AI and Agentic Systems:** The insights from this survey are particularly relevant for embodied agents and autonomous systems that must continually learn from diverse sensor inputs and interactive experiences. Mitigating cross-modal drift and ensuring robust reasoning will be paramount for such systems to operate effectively in dynamic environments.
- **Guidance for Model Development:** The taxonomy and performance analysis provide practical guidance for practitioners and researchers in selecting appropriate CL strategies for their VLM-based applications, considering trade-offs between memory, computation, stability, and plasticity.
- **New Research Avenues:** The outlined future work directions, such as unified benchmarking, advancing Continual Pre-training (CPT), developing CL for generative and interactive tasks, and pursuing a deeper theoretical understanding (e.g., modality gap modeling), are likely to inspire significant new research, pushing the boundaries of lifelong multimodal learning.

In conclusion, this survey serves as a critical diagnostic tool and a roadmap for the burgeoning field of continual learning for vision-language models, aiming to enable multimodal AI that can truly learn and adapt over its lifetime.
