# BEiT: BERT Pre-Training of Image Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> BEIT adapts BERT's masked language modeling for Vision Transformers by predicting discrete "visual tokens" of masked image patches using a pre-trained discrete VAE. This self-supervised pre-training method achieves competitive or superior performance on ImageNet classification and ADE20K semantic segmentation, with BEIT-L matching ViT-L's 88.4% top-1 ImageNet accuracy using 214 times fewer labels than the JFT-3B dataset.

## Problem

- Vision Transformers (ViTs) require massive amounts of labeled data for effective pre-training, limiting their practical application.
- Directly applying BERT-style masked prediction to images is challenging due to the continuous nature of pixels and the lack of a natural discrete visual vocabulary.
- Pixel-level reconstruction for masked image patches is inefficient, focusing on low-level details rather than learning high-level semantic features.

## Method

- Introduces ==Masked Image Modeling (MIM)== where a Vision Transformer predicts original content of masked image patches.
- Leverages a pre-trained ==discrete Variational Autoencoder (dVAE)== to discretize image content into =="visual tokens"== which serve as the prediction targets.
- Employs ==blockwise masking== of image patches to encourage learning of larger, semantic dependencies, replacing masked patches with a learnable "[M]" embedding.

## Results

- Achieves **83.2%** top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K classification, matching or exceeding state-of-the-art self-supervised methods.
- For semantic segmentation on ADE20K, BEIT reaches **45.6%** mIoU, surpassing supervised pre-training and DINO models.
- Demonstrates exceptional label efficiency, with BEIT-L pre-trained on ImageNet-22K achieving **88.4%** top-1 accuracy, comparable to models trained on datasets **214 times** larger.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - BERT-style masked prediction can be effectively applied to computer vision by first discretizing image content into a learned "visual vocabulary."
> - Predicting discrete visual tokens for masked regions is crucial for learning semantic representations, significantly outperforming direct pixel-level regression.
> - Blockwise masking on image patches, rather than random masking, fosters learning of higher-level semantic dependencies important for downstream tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of BEIT is situated at the intersection of two rapidly advancing fields: **Transformer architectures** and **self-supervised learning (SSL)**, specifically applied to **computer vision**.

The **Transformer architecture**, introduced in 2017 for natural language processing (NLP), revolutionized the field with its attention mechanism, enabling models to capture long-range dependencies effectively. Its success led to the development of powerful pre-trained language models like BERT (Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers) and GPT (Generative Pre-trained Transformer).

Subsequently, researchers adapted the Transformer for computer vision, most notably with the **Vision Transformer (ViT)** in 2020. ViT demonstrated that a pure Transformer, when fed sequences of image patches, could achieve competitive performance with state-of-the-art convolutional neural networks (CNNs) on image recognition tasks. However, a significant observation was that ViTs are **data-hungry**, requiring vast amounts of labeled data for pre-training to perform well, often more so than CNNs. This limitation prompted a surge of research into **self-supervised pre-training** for vision Transformers, aiming to leverage large unlabeled image datasets.

Before BEIT, several strands of self-supervised methods emerged for Vision Transformers:
- **Contrastive Learning:** Methods like MoCo v3 and DINO adapt successful contrastive learning paradigms from CNNs to ViTs. These approaches learn representations by pulling "positive" pairs (different augmented views of the same image) closer in embedding space while pushing "negative" pairs (different images) apart. DINO, in particular, introduced self-distillation for ViTs without explicit negative pairs.
- **Generative Pre-training:** Early works like iGPT (Image GPT) explored generative pre-training for images by predicting pixels in an autoregressive manner or using a BERT-like masked prediction on quantized image tokens obtained via clustering. While iGPT demonstrated the potential, its performance was often limited compared to supervised or contrastive methods, and it faced challenges with high-frequency details.
- **Ad-hoc Pretext Tasks:** Other methods, inherited from early SSL for CNNs, involve pretext tasks like predicting image rotation or relative patch positions.

**BEIT's unique contribution** to this landscape is its direct and effective adaptation of the **BERT-style masked language modeling (MLM)** paradigm to computer vision, a challenge that previous attempts (including a preliminary exploration in the original ViT paper) found difficult to tackle efficiently. The primary obstacles identified were the lack of a natural "vocabulary" for image patches and the inefficiency of pixel-level regression as a prediction target. BEIT overcomes these by:
1. **Introducing "visual tokens":** Analogous to discrete words or subword units in NLP, BEIT creates a discrete visual vocabulary using a pre-trained discrete Variational Autoencoder (dVAE). This effectively quantizes continuous image information into discrete, high-level abstractions.
2. **Masked Image Modeling (MIM):** Instead of predicting raw pixels, BEIT predicts these discrete visual tokens for masked image patches. This approach moves beyond low-level pixel details, focusing the model's learning capacity on higher-level semantic understanding and long-range dependencies, akin to how BERT learns contextual relationships between words.

By successfully porting the highly effective BERT pre-training strategy to vision, BEIT provides a strong alternative to contrastive learning and traditional supervised pre-training. It highlights the potential for "unifying" pre-training approaches across modalities, paving the way for more general-purpose foundation models. Its method directly addresses the "data-hungry" problem of ViTs, making them more accessible and effective even with fewer labeled examples for downstream tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the BEIT research is to **develop a novel and effective self-supervised pre-training framework for Vision Transformers (ViTs)** that addresses their known data-hungry nature and facilitates strong performance on downstream computer vision tasks without relying on vast amounts of labeled data for initial pre-training.

The core motivations behind BEIT stem directly from the remarkable success of **BERT (Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers)** in natural language processing (NLP). BERT's masked language modeling (MLM) objective, where it learns to predict masked words based on their context, proved incredibly powerful for learning rich, bidirectional representations. The authors sought to translate this success to the visual domain, believing that a similar "denoising auto-encoding" idea could be highly beneficial for Vision Transformers.

However, the authors identified **two significant challenges** in directly applying BERT-style pre-training to image data:
1. **Lack of a Pre-existing Vocabulary:** Unlike NLP, where words or subword units (like BPE tokens) form a natural discrete vocabulary, image patches do not have an inherent, pre-defined vocabulary. This makes it difficult to use a simple softmax classifier to predict masked content over a finite set of possibilities.
2. **Inefficiency of Pixel-level Regression:** A straightforward alternative would be to treat the task as a regression problem, predicting the raw pixel values of masked patches. The authors, however, point out that such pixel-level recovery tends to "waste modeling capability on pre-training short-range dependencies and high-frequency details." This implies that forcing the model to reconstruct exact pixel values might hinder its ability to learn high-level semantic features and global context, which are crucial for many downstream tasks.

Therefore, the key motivations can be summarized as:
- **Leverage BERT's Success:** Adapt the powerful masked prediction paradigm of BERT for learning visual representations.
- **Overcome Vision-Specific Challenges:** Develop a method that handles the continuous nature of image data and the lack of a natural visual vocabulary.
- **Improve ViT Data Efficiency:** Provide an effective self-supervised pre-training mechanism to mitigate the need for massive labeled datasets, making ViTs more practical and broadly applicable.
- **Learn Semantic Representations:** Encourage the model to learn meaningful, high-level semantic features rather than just low-level pixel correlations, thus improving its generalization capabilities on diverse tasks like classification and segmentation.
- **Theoretical Grounding:** Provide a theoretical justification for the proposed approach, viewing it through the lens of variational autoencoders.

By addressing these motivations, BEIT aims to advance self-supervised learning for Vision Transformers, making them more robust, efficient, and capable of learning rich visual knowledge from unlabeled data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

BEIT introduces **Masked Image Modeling (MIM)**, a self-supervised pre-training task for Vision Transformers. The core idea is to train a Transformer to recover masked portions of an image by predicting discrete "visual tokens" corresponding to those masks, rather than raw pixels.

#### 3.1. Image Representations

BEIT operates on two distinct views of an input image `x`:
- **Image Patches:** The standard input representation for Vision Transformers (ViT). A 2D image is split into a grid of non-overlapping patches (e.g., 16x16 pixels). These patches are flattened, linearly projected, and augmented with position embeddings before being fed into the Transformer encoder. These patches serve as the **input** to the BEIT Transformer.
- **Visual Tokens:** These are discrete, abstract representations of the image content. They are obtained by an "image tokenizer," which is a **pre-trained discrete Variational Autoencoder (dVAE)**. The dVAE's tokenizer maps image pixels to discrete tokens from a learned vocabulary (e.g., 8192 tokens). The dVAE's decoder then reconstructs the image from these tokens. Importantly, the image tokenizer is learned in a separate, initial stage using reconstruction loss and Gumbel-softmax relaxation, and then kept fixed during BEIT's pre-training. These visual tokens serve as the **prediction targets** for the masked patches. The number of visual tokens for an image matches the number of image patches (e.g., 14x14 grid for a 224x224 image with 16x16 patches).

#### 3.2. Backbone Network: Image Transformer

The backbone of BEIT is a standard **Vision Transformer (ViT)** architecture (specifically, ViT-Base in the main experiments), similar to the original ViT paper. This choice allows for fair comparison with existing methods and leverages the proven capacity of Transformers for global context modeling. The Transformer takes linearly projected image patch embeddings (along with a special `[S]` token and positional embeddings) as input and processes them through multiple self-attention and feed-forward layers.

#### 3.3. Pre-Training BEIT: Masked Image Modeling (MIM)

The MIM task involves the following steps:
1. **Masking:** A proportion of image patches (e.g., 40%) are randomly selected and masked.
 - **Blockwise Masking:** Crucially, BEIT employs a "blockwise masking" strategy (Algorithm 1) where contiguous blocks of patches are masked, rather than individual random patches. This encourages the model to learn dependencies over larger, more semantic regions, rather than just local pixel correlations.
2. **Corrupted Input:** The masked image patches are replaced with a special learnable `[M]` (mask) embedding. This corrupted sequence of patches is then fed into the ViT backbone.
3. **Prediction Objective:** For each *masked position*, the BEIT Transformer outputs an encoded representation. A softmax classifier (the "Masked Image Modeling Head") is then applied to this representation to predict the *original visual token* that corresponds to the masked patch. The pre-training objective is to maximize the log-likelihood of correctly predicting these visual tokens. This is a classification task over the discrete visual vocabulary, analogous to predicting words in BERT.

#### 3.4. From the Perspective of Variational Autoencoder

The authors provide a theoretical interpretation of BEIT pre-training as a two-stage variational autoencoder (VAE) training process:
- **Stage 1 (Image Tokenizer):** The dVAE learns to reconstruct the original image `x` from its discrete visual tokens `z`, essentially defining `q_phi(z|x)` (tokenizer) and `p_psi(x|z)` (decoder). This minimizes the reconstruction loss.
- **Stage 2 (BEIT MIM):** With `q_phi` and `p_psi` fixed, BEIT learns the "prior" `p_theta(z|x_masked)`, which recovers the visual tokens `z` based on the masked image `x_masked`. This is precisely the MIM objective. This perspective shows that BEIT learns to model the distribution of visual tokens given a corrupted image, rather than directly reconstructing pixels.

#### 3.5. Pre-Training Setup

- **Dataset:** ImageNet-1K (1.2M unlabeled images).
- **Resolution:** 224x224 input, split into 14x14 patches (16x16 pixels each).
- **Training Schedule:** 500k steps (800 epochs), batch size 2048, Adam optimizer, cosine learning rate decay with warmup, stochastic depth, and specific initialization scaling for Transformer layers. This setup requires significant computational resources (e.g., 16 Nvidia Tesla V100 GPUs for five days).

#### 3.6. Fine-Tuning on Downstream Tasks

After self-supervised pre-training, BEIT can be fine-tuned for specific downstream tasks by appending task-specific layers:
- **Image Classification:** A simple linear classifier is added on top of the average-pooled final hidden states of the image patches.
- **Semantic Segmentation:** BEIT acts as an encoder, and deconvolution layers are added as a decoder (following architectures like SETR-PUP) to produce pixel-level segmentation maps.
- **Intermediate Fine-tuning:** A common practice from NLP, where the self-supervised pre-trained model is first fine-tuned on a large *labeled* intermediate dataset (e.g., ImageNet-1K) before being fine-tuned on the final, potentially smaller, target dataset.

This methodology elegantly bridges the gap between successful NLP pre-training paradigms and the unique challenges of computer vision, enabling effective self-supervised learning for data-hungry Vision Transformers.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

BEIT demonstrates strong empirical performance across various computer vision benchmarks, significantly advancing the state-of-the-art in self-supervised pre-training for Vision Transformers.

#### 4.1. Image Classification (ImageNet-1K)

- **Outperforms From-Scratch Training:** BEIT-B consistently and significantly outperforms ViT models trained from scratch on ImageNet-1K. For instance, BEIT-B achieves 83.2% top-1 accuracy at 224x224 resolution, compared to DeiT-B's 81.8% (trained from scratch).
- **Competitive with State-of-the-Art Self-Supervised Methods:** BEIT-B matches or outperforms leading self-supervised methods like MoCo v3-B (83.2%) and DINO-B (82.8%) on ImageNet-1K fine-tuning, demonstrating its effectiveness within the same pre-training data constraints.
- **Scalability:** When scaled to larger models (BEIT-L), the performance gain over base models is more substantial for BEIT (e.g., BEIT-L 85.2% vs BEIT-B 83.2%, a 2.0% gain) compared to supervised pre-training on ImageNet-22K (e.g., ViT-L 85.2% vs ViT-B 84.0%, a 1.2% gain). This suggests that BEIT's self-supervised pre-training is particularly beneficial for larger models, potentially alleviating the acute data hunger they exhibit when supervised pre-training data is insufficient.
- **Higher Resolution Fine-tuning:** Fine-tuning at 384x384 resolution further improves performance (e.g., BEIT-B to 84.6%, BEIT-L to 86.3%), demonstrating the adaptability of the pre-trained features. Notably, BEIT-B at 384x384 (pretrained on ImageNet-1K) outperforms ViT-B at 384x384 pretrained on the much larger ImageNet-22K (84.6% vs 84.0%).
- **Convergence Speed and Stability:** Fine-tuning BEIT converges much faster and more stably than training a ViT from scratch, reducing training costs on downstream tasks.
- **Efficiency in Large-Scale Comparison (Appendix B):** A particularly impactful result is that BEIT-L, after self-supervised pre-training and intermediate fine-tuning on ImageNet-22K (14M labeled images), achieves 88.4% top-1 accuracy, closely matching or even exceeding ViT-L trained on Google's JFT-3B (3 billion labeled images), which has 214 times more labels. With an in-house 70M dataset, BEIT-L reaches 89.5% top-1 accuracy, setting new state-of-the-art results for large-size vision transformers. This highlights BEIT's exceptional label efficiency.

#### 4.2. Semantic Segmentation (ADE20K)

- BEIT achieves 45.6% mIoU on ADE20K, outperforming models pretrained with supervised learning on ImageNet (45.3%) and DINO (44.1%).
- Intermediate fine-tuning on ImageNet-1K further boosts BEIT's performance on ADE20K to 47.7% mIoU, demonstrating its complementarity with supervised pre-training. In Appendix B, BEIT-L with in-house 70M data achieves 58.4% multi-scale mIoU, setting a new SOTA.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Blockwise Masking:** Essential for performance, especially on semantic segmentation. Random masking yields lower results.
- **Visual Tokens vs. Raw Pixels:** Predicting discrete visual tokens is *critical*. Replacing this with direct pixel-level regression of masked patches leads to significantly worse performance (81.04% ImageNet accuracy vs. BEIT's 82.86%), even falling below models trained from scratch in some cases. This validates the motivation of avoiding low-level detail reconstruction.
- **Masking Ratio:** Recovering 100% of visual tokens (i.e., no masking) hurts downstream task performance compared to the 40% masking ratio.
- **Pre-training Duration:** Longer pre-training (800 epochs vs. 300 epochs) consistently improves performance.

#### 4.4. Analysis of Self-Attention Map

- The self-attention maps within BEIT demonstrate that the model, *without any human annotation*, learns to distinguish semantic regions and object boundaries. This emergent property is a strong indicator that the self-supervised pre-training effectively captures meaningful visual semantics, which directly benefits downstream tasks.

#### 4.5. Multi-Task Pre-training (Appendix E)

- Combining BEIT's MIM with DINO's self-distillation (multi-task pre-training) can further improve semantic segmentation (46.85% mIoU) while maintaining ImageNet classification performance.
- BEIT pre-training is significantly more efficient (4.2x faster throughput) than DINO alone, due to its memory efficiency and simpler architecture (DINO uses two Transformer copies and multi-crop augmentation).

#### 4.6. CIFAR-100 Classification (Appendix F)

- BEIT achieves 90.1% top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-100, a smaller dataset, drastically outperforming ViT trained from scratch (48.5%). This further underscores its ability to alleviate the data-hungry issue and transfer knowledge effectively.

In summary, BEIT successfully demonstrates that a BERT-like masked prediction approach, when adapted appropriately with discrete visual tokens and blockwise masking, is a highly effective self-supervised pre-training strategy for Vision Transformers. It yields competitive or superior performance to existing methods, shows remarkable scalability, and significantly reduces the dependency on large labeled datasets.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

BEIT represents a significant step forward in self-supervised learning for computer vision, particularly in the context of the rapidly evolving field of Vision Transformers. Its significance and potential impact can be elaborated as follows:

- **Bridging NLP and CV Pre-training Paradigms:** The most prominent contribution of BEIT is its successful and effective adaptation of the highly successful BERT-style masked language modeling (MLM) to the visual domain. Previous attempts to directly apply MLM to images faced challenges. BEIT's innovation of using a pre-trained discrete VAE to generate "visual tokens" as prediction targets, coupled with blockwise masking, successfully overcomes these hurdles. This demonstrates a powerful convergence of successful pre-training strategies across modalities, suggesting a path towards more unified and generalizable foundation models.

- **Addressing the "Data Hunger" of Vision Transformers:** ViTs have shown impressive capabilities but are notoriously data-hungry, requiring massive labeled datasets (like JFT-3B) for pre-training to outperform CNNs. BEIT significantly mitigates this issue by providing a powerful self-supervised pre-training method that leverages unlabeled data. The results showing BEIT-L pre-trained on ImageNet-22K achieving performance comparable to ViT-L trained on JFT-3B (214x more labels) underscore its exceptional label efficiency. This makes ViTs more accessible and practical for a wider range of applications where vast labeled datasets are unavailable.

- **Learning Semantically Rich Representations without Supervision:** The analysis of self-attention maps revealing that BEIT learns to distinguish semantic regions and object boundaries without any explicit human annotation is highly significant. This "emergent property" indicates that the masked image modeling task encourages the model to build a deep understanding of visual semantics and structures, rather than just superficial pixel correlations. Such semantically rich representations are crucial for robust performance across diverse downstream tasks and contribute to better generalization.

- **Efficiency and Scalability of Pre-training:** BEIT demonstrates superior pre-training throughput and memory efficiency compared to some leading contrastive self-supervised methods like DINO. This efficiency makes scaling up BEIT to even larger models and datasets more feasible, pushing the boundaries of what's achievable with self-supervised vision models. The demonstrated gains from scaling BEIT from base to large models further highlight its strong scalability potential.

- **Foundation for Future Multimodal AI:** The conceptual alignment with BERT paves the way for truly unified multimodal pre-training. The authors explicitly state this as a future direction: "multimodal pre-training in a more unified way, using the similar objectives and the shared architecture for texts and images." If vision and language models can learn powerful representations using analogous masked prediction objectives and shared architectures, it could accelerate the development of sophisticated AI systems capable of understanding and generating across different modalities seamlessly.

- **Reduced Development Costs and Environmental Impact:** By reducing the need for extensive manual labeling, BEIT lowers the human effort and cost associated with developing high-performing vision models. Furthermore, by enabling more effective learning from unlabeled data and potentially leading to more efficient pre-training, it could contribute to reducing the computational resources and environmental impact associated with training increasingly large models.

In conclusion, BEIT offers a compelling and effective paradigm for self-supervised learning in computer vision. By successfully adapting the BERT-style pre-training, it not only addresses critical challenges related to data efficiency and semantic understanding in Vision Transformers but also opens exciting avenues for more generalized, efficient, and unified AI systems across different data modalities.
