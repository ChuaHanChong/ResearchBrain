# TriSplat: Simulation-Ready Feed-Forward 3D Scene Reconstruction

> [!summary] Summary
> TriSplat introduces a feed-forward network that reconstructs 3D scenes from sparse, unposed images directly into oriented triangle meshes, which are immediately usable in simulation environments. This approach achieves superior geometric and rendering quality, outperforming existing methods in accuracy and being orders of magnitude faster by eliminating post-hoc mesh extraction.

## Problem

- Existing feed-forward 3D reconstruction models produce implicit scene representations (e.g., Gaussians) that require computationally expensive and often lossy post-hoc conversion to explicit triangle meshes for simulation and robotics.
- Achieving high geometric fidelity and well-defined surfaces in feed-forward reconstruction from sparse, unposed images remains challenging, as implicit representations can lead to blurring or missing structures when meshed.
- Training with explicit triangle primitives is unstable due to their sensitivity to orientation errors, making it difficult to achieve accurate surface definition without per-scene optimization.

## Method

- **TriSplat** proposes a feed-forward network that directly outputs oriented triangle primitives by jointly predicting dense local 3D point maps, per-pixel triangle attributes, and camera poses from sparse, unposed input images.
- It anchors triangle orientations to geometry normals derived from the predicted point map, further refined by a U-Net and stabilized with a time-varying monocular normal estimator bootstrap.
- The method incorporates progressive surface sharpening through opacity and blur scheduling, which gradually transitions from soft to sharp triangle primitives to enhance training stability.

## Results

- TriSplat demonstrated superior geometric accuracy (e.g., Chamfer Distance 0.190, F1 0.622 on RE10K, 6 views) and mesh-rendering quality (**24.69** dB PSNR on RE10K) compared to Gaussian feed-forward baselines.
- It achieved significantly faster end-to-end reconstruction times, being **33x** to **249x** quicker than Gaussian baselines by eliminating the post-hoc mesh extraction step (e.g., 0.57s for 6 views on DL3DV).
- The directly exported meshes were successfully loaded and functioned in Unity and NVIDIA Isaac Sim, enabling real-time physics simulations and robotic interactions without manual cleanup or conversion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly outputting triangle primitives intrinsically solves the compatibility issue with simulation environments, enabling immediate use without post-hoc mesh extraction, fulfilling the 'feed-forward' promise.
> - Anchoring triangle orientation to local geometry derived from dense point maps, and using a monocular normal bootstrap, is crucial for achieving high geometric fidelity and stable learning of explicit surface elements.
> - Progressive surface sharpening by scheduling opacity and blur is essential for robustly training with hard-edged triangle primitives, leading to sharper and more accurate reconstructions.
