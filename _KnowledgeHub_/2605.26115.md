# TriSplat: Simulation-Ready Feed-Forward 3D Scene Reconstruction

> [!summary] Summary
> TriSplat introduces a feed-forward network that reconstructs 3D scenes from sparse, unposed images directly into oriented triangle meshes, which are immediately usable in simulation environments. This approach achieves superior geometric and rendering quality, outperforming existing methods in accuracy and being orders of magnitude faster by eliminating post-hoc mesh extraction.

## Problem

- Existing feed-forward 3D reconstruction models produce implicit scene representations (e.g., Gaussians) that require computationally expensive and often lossy post-hoc conversion to explicit triangle meshes for simulation and robotics.
- Achieving high geometric fidelity and well-defined surfaces in feed-forward reconstruction from sparse, unposed images remains challenging, as implicit representations can lead to blurring or missing structures when meshed.
- Training with explicit triangle primitives is unstable due to their sensitivity to orientation errors, making it difficult to achieve accurate surface definition without per-scene optimization.

## Method

- **TriSplat** proposes a feed-forward network that directly outputs oriented triangle primitives by jointly predicting dense local 3D point maps, per-pixel triangle attributes, and camera poses from sparse, unposed input images.
- It anchors triangle orientations to geometry normals derived from the predicted point map, further refined by a U-Net and stabilized with a time-varying monocular normal estimator bootstrap.
- The method incorporates progressive surface sharpening through opacity and blur scheduling, which gradually transitions from soft to sharp triangle primitives to enhance training stability.

## Results

- TriSplat demonstrated superior geometric accuracy (e.g., Chamfer Distance 0.190, F1 0.622 on RE10K, 6 views) and mesh-rendering quality (**24.69** dB PSNR on RE10K) compared to Gaussian feed-forward baselines.
- It achieved significantly faster end-to-end reconstruction times, being **33x** to **249x** quicker than Gaussian baselines by eliminating the post-hoc mesh extraction step (e.g., 0.57s for 6 views on DL3DV).
- The directly exported meshes were successfully loaded and functioned in Unity and NVIDIA Isaac Sim, enabling real-time physics simulations and robotic interactions without manual cleanup or conversion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly outputting triangle primitives intrinsically solves the compatibility issue with simulation environments, enabling immediate use without post-hoc mesh extraction, fulfilling the 'feed-forward' promise.
> - Anchoring triangle orientation to local geometry derived from dense point maps, and using a monocular normal bootstrap, is crucial for achieving high geometric fidelity and stable learning of explicit surface elements.
> - Progressive surface sharpening by scheduling opacity and blur is essential for robustly training with hard-edged triangle primitives, leading to sharper and more accurate reconstructions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 3D scene reconstruction from images is a foundational area in computer vision, with increasing importance for applications in robotics, augmented reality, and embodied perception. These applications often require reconstructed scenes to support collision checking, contact-rich planning, and physics simulation, which typically rely on explicit triangle meshes as input for engines such as NVIDIA Isaac Sim, Unity, and Unreal.

Traditional multi-view reconstruction pipelines, both classical and learned, can produce meshes but are often characterized by multi-stage optimization, sensitivity to camera calibration, and degradation in performance when input views are sparse or camera poses are unknown.

Recent advancements have focused on feed-forward models that predict geometry and rendering primitives directly from images, sidestepping per-scene optimization. A prominent class within this category is Gaussian splatting (e.g., 3DGS), which excels at efficient, high-quality novel-view synthesis. A parallel line of research has developed pose-free models that jointly learn camera estimation and 3D reconstruction. However, a significant limitation of most existing feed-forward methods, including those based on Gaussian primitives or point maps, is that they represent surfaces implicitly. Extracting a usable mesh from these implicit representations typically requires computationally intensive post-hoc steps, such as TSDF (Truncated Signed Distance Function) fusion or Poisson reconstruction. This requirement effectively breaks the "feed-forward" promise, introducing delays and potential loss of geometric fidelity. While some geometry-aware Gaussian variants have been introduced to encourage stronger geometric structure, they still rely on auxiliary extraction methods or per-scene optimization to recover explicit meshes. Separately, methods that directly generate meshes (e.g., InstantMesh) have primarily focused on object-level reconstruction from controlled viewpoints, rather than complex, unposed, scene-level inputs.

TriSplat addresses this gap by introducing a feed-forward, sparse-view, and pose-free 3D reconstruction model that inherently represents scenes using oriented triangle primitives. By doing so, it directly outputs meshes that are immediately "simulation-ready" without any intermediate conversion or post-processing steps. This positions TriSplat as a bridge between the efficiency and generalization of feed-forward models and the explicit surface requirements of simulation environments, extending the concept of triangle-based differentiable rendering (seen in optimization-based approaches) to the feed-forward domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a feed-forward network capable of reconstructing 3D scenes from sparse, unposed images, where the output is directly usable as a triangle mesh for downstream simulation, physics reasoning, and embodied interaction.

This objective is motivated by several critical shortcomings in the existing 3D reconstruction landscape:
- **Bridging the Gap to Simulation Environments:** Physics engines, collision detectors, and standard rendering pipelines fundamentally operate on explicit triangle meshes. Current feed-forward reconstruction methods, particularly those based on Gaussian splatting or point clouds, produce implicit scene representations. Converting these to meshes is a required but costly and often lossy post-processing step that negates the efficiency benefits of a feed-forward approach. The authors identify this as breaking the "feed-forward promise." TriSplat aims to eliminate this conversion by making the rendering primitive itself an explicit surface element.
- **Improving Geometric Fidelity and Usability:** Implicit surface representations derived from Gaussians or point maps often lack sharp, well-defined boundaries and can suffer from view-inconsistent depth or "floaters" when post-processed into meshes. This reduces the fidelity required for precise physical interactions. TriSplat seeks to provide more geometry-faithful reconstructions by anchoring triangle orientation to predicted local geometry, offering a strong prior for improved surface accuracy.
- **Addressing Challenges of Sparse and Unposed Inputs:** Real-world scenarios frequently involve sparse image observations and unknown camera parameters (pose-free settings). While existing feed-forward models have made progress in these challenging conditions, the problem of directly producing high-quality, simulation-ready meshes in such settings remains largely unresolved.
- **Stabilizing Triangle-Based Reconstruction:** The authors observe that triangle primitives are significantly more sensitive to orientation errors than Gaussian splats, as misoriented triangles create noticeable hard-edged artifacts. This necessitates a robust approach to defining and refining triangle orientations, rather than treating them as unconstrained latent variables.

To achieve its objective, TriSplat is designed based on three core observations:
1. The rendering primitive itself must be a surface element (triangles satisfy this for direct mesh export).
2. Triangle orientation should be anchored to predicted local geometry, providing a strong prior for surface fidelity.
3. Given the sensitivity of triangles to orientation errors, explicit normal bootstrapping and validity-aware training are essential for stable learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TriSplat operates as a feed-forward network that takes a sparse set of `V` unposed images as input and, in a single forward pass, reconstructs a scene as a collection of oriented triangle primitives. It jointly predicts dense local 3D point maps, per-pixel triangle attributes, camera poses, and optionally camera intrinsics.

The methodology can be broken down into several key components:

- **Network Architecture:**
 - **Encoder:** Employs a DINOv2 ViT-L/14 backbone, augmented with 2D rotary position embeddings.
 - **Decoder:** A custom transformer decoder that alternates between intra-view self-attention for local reasoning and cross-view joint attention for multi-view correspondence aggregation. It integrates two-dimensional rotary position embeddings and per-pixel ray-direction embeddings for spatial and geometric conditioning.
 - **Parallel Heads:** Three specialized heads convert the decoded features:
 - **Point Head:** Predicts a dense local 3D point map `P` in each camera's coordinate frame, outputting three unconstrained scalars (u, v, z') where depth `z` is derived as `exp(z')`.
 - **Camera Head:** Predicts an SE(3) camera-to-world pose per view by pooling decoder tokens and regressing translation and rotation (via SVD orthogonalization). Poses are relative to the first view, and scheduled sampling is used during training.
 - **Primitive Head:** Predicts per-pixel attributes for each triangle, including a density logit, three scale logits, a quaternion for orientation, spherical-harmonic (SH) appearance coefficients, and a blur parameter. This head receives patch-embedded RGB image features directly.

- **Triangle Primitive Construction:**
 - Triangle centers `c` are defined by the predicted point maps and camera poses in world space.
 - Each triangle is instantiated from a canonical equilateral template.
 - Raw scale logits are mapped to world-space sizes considering predicted depth and pixel footprint.
 - The `k`-th vertex of a triangle is computed as `v_k = R_c R_n (T_k ⊙ s) + c`, where `R_c` is the camera-to-world rotation, `R_n` is the tangent-frame rotation (derived from normals), `T_k` is the template vertex, and `s` is the scale vector.
 - A differentiable triangle rasterizer renders the constructed triangles, producing RGB images, depth maps, and surface normals.

- **Anchoring Triangle Orientation to Geometry (Core Contribution):**
 - **Geometry Normals:** Initial surface normals (`n_geo`) are derived from finite differences on the dense 3D point map `P`. These are normalized and their orientation is aligned towards the camera. A Boolean validity mask `m` handles border pixels and degenerate cases. The point map can be optionally detached to de-couple normal refinement from point prediction and smoothed to suppress high-frequency noise. An orientation-aware box filter further refines the field.
 - **Learned Refinement:** A lightweight U-Net refines the geometry normals by incorporating appearance and depth cues. It takes concatenated raw and smoothed geometry normals, downsampled RGB image, predicted depth map, and the validity mask as input. Its output layer is zero-initialized to start as an identity mapping, ensuring stability during early training. The refined normal is `n_ref = normalize(n_sm + f_θ(n_geo, n_sm, I_v, D_v, m))`.
 - **Mono-Normal Bootstrap:** To stabilize training when point maps are inaccurate, a time-varying coefficient `α(t)` blends the refined model normal (`n_ref`) with normals from a pretrained monocular normal estimator (`n_tch`, specifically Omnidata DPT). This schedule has three phases: takeover (teacher-dominant), blending (cosine decay), and release (model-dominant). This direct blending into the forward-pass representation is crucial.
 - **Tangent Frame Construction:** The blended normal `n_fwd` is converted into an orthonormal tangent frame `[t, b, n_fwd]`. The tangent `t` is derived by projecting the point-map derivative onto the plane perpendicular to `n_fwd`, and the bitangent `b` is then computed. This 3x3 rotation matrix serves as `R_n` in the triangle construction.

- **Progressive Surface Sharpening:** This curriculum mitigates the instability of training with hard-edged triangles by gradually transitioning from soft to sharp primitives.
 - **Opacity Scheduling:** The predicted density `p` is mapped to opacity `o` using a non-linear function with a time-varying exponent `e(t)` (linearly increasing) and a temperature factor `τ(t)` (linearly increasing). This progressively binarizes the opacity field, pushing intermediate densities towards 0 or 1.
 - **Blur Scheduling:** Each triangle has a scalar blur parameter `σ` that modulates alpha falloff at its edges. This blur parameter decays linearly over time (`β(t)` decreases), starting with large blur for broad, forgiving footprints and gradually tightening into well-defined surface elements.

- **Training Objectives and Mesh Extraction:**
 - **Loss Function:** The model is trained end-to-end with a combined loss: `L = λ_photo L_photo + λ_cam L_cam + λ_normal L_normal`.
 - `L_photo`: Combines pixel-wise mean-squared error (MSE) and perceptual LPIPS loss between rendered and ground-truth images.
 - `L_cam`: A pairwise relative pose loss (Huber for translation, angular for rotation), providing dense, globally invariant supervision.
 - `L_normal`: A cosine similarity loss aligning the refined normal `n_ref` with the monocular teacher normal `n_tch` at valid pixels.
 - **Optimization:** AdamW optimizer with a linear warm-up, gradient clipping, and scheduled sampling for camera poses.
 - **Mesh Extraction:** This is a distinct advantage. After a forward pass, low-opacity triangles are discarded, winding order is corrected, and nearby duplicate vertices are merged using quantized position hashing. No auxiliary reconstruction methods (TSDF, marching cubes) are required, as the rendering primitives themselves form the mesh.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

TriSplat's performance was evaluated along four primary axes: surface geometry quality, mesh-rendering quality, depth and normal accuracy, and runtime efficiency, with comprehensive ablation studies.

- **Surface Reconstruction and Mesh Rendering:**
 - **Geometric Quality (Tables 1, 3):** TriSplat consistently outperformed all Gaussian feed-forward baselines in surface accuracy metrics (Chamfer Distance, Precision, Recall, F1 score) on both RealEstate10K (RE10K) and DL3DV datasets, across varying numbers of input views (6, 12, 24). For example, on RE10K (6 views), TriSplat achieved a Chamfer Distance of 0.190 and an F1 score of 0.622, significantly improving over the strongest Gaussian baseline (YoNoSplat, CD 0.267, F1 0.443). The improvement was particularly notable in Recall, indicating better coverage of ground-truth surfaces and thin structures compared to TSDF-fused meshes from baselines. Even geometry-aware Gaussian variants (MeshSplat, SurfelSplat) did not match TriSplat's geometric fidelity.
 - **Mesh Rendering Quality (Tables 2, 3):** When the exported meshes were rendered using a standard triangle rasterizer, TriSplat achieved the best novel-view synthesis quality. On RE10K, TriSplat reached 24.69 dB PSNR, a 2.75 dB improvement over the strongest Gaussian baseline. This advantage is attributed to TriSplat's native triangle representation, which ensures minimal information loss during mesh export, unlike Gaussian baselines that suffer substantial quality degradation due to the TSDF fusion process. Qualitative results (Figs. 3, 4, 5, 6) demonstrated that TriSplat produced sharper edges, finer details, and more complete silhouettes, whereas TSDF-fused Gaussian meshes often showed blurring, missing structures, and geometric inaccuracies.

- **Depth and Normal Quality:**
 - **Zero-Shot ScanNet Evaluation (Table 4, Fig. 7):** Trained on RE10K and evaluated zero-shot on ScanNet, TriSplat achieved superior depth and normal accuracy. It yielded the best AbsRel (0.188) and AbsDiff (0.341) for depth. For normals, it significantly outperformed baselines with a mean angular error of 27.9° and a `<30°` accuracy of 71.7%, compared to 54.1° and 41.0% respectively for the best baseline. This highlights the effectiveness of the geometry-anchored normal pipeline and the mono-normal bootstrap schedule in optimizing orientation quality. Qualitative comparisons showed TriSplat generating smoother, geometrically coherent normals aligned with surface boundaries.

- **Efficiency:**
 - **End-to-End Time-to-Mesh (Fig. 8):** TriSplat demonstrated significantly faster end-to-end reconstruction times. On DL3DV, it generated a usable mesh in 0.57 s (6 views), 0.62 s (12 views), and 1.23 s (24 views). In contrast, the fastest Gaussian baseline (AnySplat) took 18.7 s (6 views) and 33.0 s (24 views), while the slowest (DepthSplat) reached 306 s for 24 views. TriSplat was 33x faster than the fastest Gaussian baseline and up to 249x faster than the slowest, attributing this structural advantage to the elimination of the post-hoc mesh-extraction step.

- **Simulation-Ready Demonstration:**
 - **Practical Utility (Fig. 9):** Directly exported TriSplat meshes were successfully loaded into Unity and NVIDIA Isaac Sim, enabling demonstrations of rigid-body simulation, collision detection, robot navigation, and robotic grasping without any manual cleanup or format conversion. This contrasts with Gaussian baselines, which would require TSDF fusion and further mesh cleaning.

- **Ablation Study (Table 5):**
 - Each proposed component (normal anchoring, mono-normal bootstrap, normal refinement, progressive sharpening) was shown to contribute positively to the overall performance. Removing any single component led to a degradation in both surface geometry and mesh-rendering quality metrics. The mono-normal bootstrap had the largest impact on surface quality (F1 -0.065), while normal refinement showed the largest drop in rendering quality (PSNR -1.58 dB).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The TriSplat research introduces a notable advancement in feed-forward 3D scene reconstruction, with significant implications across several domains:

- **Direct Simulation-Readiness:** The most profound impact of TriSplat is its ability to directly output simulation-ready triangle meshes from sparse, unposed images in a single forward pass. This fundamentally alters the workflow for applications in robotics, virtual reality, augmented reality, and game development. By eliminating the need for time-consuming, computationally expensive, and often lossy post-hoc mesh extraction processes (like TSDF fusion), TriSplat offers a streamlined pipeline. This can accelerate the deployment of reconstructed scenes for tasks such as robot path planning, collision avoidance, and realistic physics interactions, truly fulfilling the "feed-forward" promise.
- **Enhanced Geometric Fidelity:** TriSplat's native triangle representation, coupled with its geometry-anchored orientation pipeline, results in significantly more accurate and well-defined surface geometry compared to methods relying on implicit surfaces. This improved fidelity is crucial for tasks requiring precise contact detection, fine-grained object manipulation, or realistic rendering of environmental details.
- **Increased Efficiency and Scalability:** The elimination of the post-hoc mesh extraction step translates into a substantial reduction in end-to-end reconstruction time. TriSplat's inference speed, demonstrated to be orders of magnitude faster than Gaussian-based methods when considering mesh output, makes it a more viable solution for dynamic environments, large-scale scene reconstruction, or real-time applications where rapid scene updates are necessary.
- **Robustness in Challenging Conditions:** The model's effectiveness in sparse-view and pose-free settings, combined with its strong generalization capabilities (demonstrated by zero-shot performance on ScanNet), broadens the applicability of feed-forward 3D reconstruction to unconstrained real-world data capture scenarios.
- **Conceptual Shift in Representation:** TriSplat advocates for a representational shift where "simulation readiness" is an intrinsic property of the chosen primitive (triangles) rather than an outcome of a subsequent conversion process. This conceptual framework could inspire future research into other inherently explicit representations for various downstream tasks.

**Limitations and Future Work (as acknowledged by the authors):**
The directly exported meshes are described as a "non-manifold triangle soup," which is suitable for many rendering and physics applications but not for all. Applications requiring watertight meshes, such as finite-element analysis, would still necessitate further processing. Additionally, the triangle density is currently tied to the input image resolution. Future work could explore topology-aware mesh export and adaptive tessellation techniques to address these limitations, potentially enabling watertight mesh generation and more efficient representation irrespective of input resolution.
