# SpaceR: Reinforcing MLLMs in Video Spatial Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> SpaceR introduces a framework for improving Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) in video spatial reasoning, achieving state-of-the-art performance on benchmarks such as VSI-Bench by surpassing GPT-4o by 11.6% accuracy. This advancement is facilitated by a novel, large-scale dataset, SpaceR-151k, and a training approach called Spatially-Guided Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (SG-RLVR), which includes a map imagination mechanism.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with inferring and reconstructing underlying 3D spatial layouts and relationships from sequences of 2D video frames.
- There is a scarcity of high-quality, verifiable training datasets specifically designed to foster and evaluate spatial reasoning in MLLMs.
- Current training strategies, primarily Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), are often insufficient for cultivating deep, abstract reasoning capabilities in MLLMs.

## Method

- Created ==SpaceR-151k==, a novel large-scale dataset (151,310 samples) tailored for video spatial reasoning, including 91k verifiable spatial reasoning QA pairs derived from 3D scene data and 60k general video understanding samples.
- Developed ==Spatially-Guided Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (SG-RLVR)==, which extends the GRPO algorithm with specialized mechanisms and reward functions for spatial reasoning.
- Introduced a ==map imagination mechanism== that guides the MLLM to explicitly generate a cognitive map of object distributions and positions, and a `Map Reward` to quantitatively evaluate its accuracy, encouraging spatial thinking.

## Results

- SpaceR achieves state-of-the-art performance on spatial reasoning benchmarks, scoring **45.6%** accuracy on VSI-Bench and surpassing GPT-4o by **11.6%** accuracy.
- The SG-RLVR training paradigm consistently improves performance across all evaluated spatial reasoning and general video understanding benchmarks (VSI-Bench, STI-Bench, SPAR-Bench, Video-MME, TempCompass, LongVideoBench), demonstrating superior generalizability compared to SFT.
- Ablation studies confirm the critical contribution of the SpaceR-151k dataset, particularly its spatial reasoning component, and the map imagination mechanism for enhancing spatial reasoning and overall video understanding.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (SG-RLVR) significantly outperforms Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) in fostering generalizable reasoning capabilities in MLLMs, especially for complex tasks like spatial understanding.
> - Explicitly guiding MLLMs to generate cognitive maps during their reasoning process, coupled with a reward for map accuracy, effectively enhances their ability to infer and represent 3D spatial layouts from video.
> - The creation of a large-scale, high-quality dataset specifically designed for spatial reasoning, derived from 3D scene reconstructions, is crucial for training MLLMs in this advanced cognitive ability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has witnessed rapid advancements, significantly enhancing performance on conventional video understanding tasks such as video captioning, video question answering, and temporal grounding. These tasks primarily involve recalling or summarizing explicit video content. However, this paper identifies a critical frontier: **video spatial reasoning**.

Video spatial reasoning is a higher-level cognitive ability that goes beyond mere content recall. It requires MLLMs to infer and reconstruct the underlying 3D spatial layouts and relationships from sequences of observed video frames. This capability is fundamental for developing more sophisticated AI systems, particularly for building robust "world models" (e.g.,) and enabling intelligent "embodied agents" (e.g.,) that can navigate and interact with the physical world.

The paper highlights two major limitations hindering MLLMs from excelling in spatial reasoning:
1. **Absence of High-Quality Datasets:** There is a scarcity of training data specifically designed to foster and evaluate spatial reasoning in MLLMs. Existing datasets primarily focus on general video understanding, not the nuanced spatial relationships.
2. **Lack of Effective Training Strategies:** Most current MLLMs rely heavily on Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), which, while effective for content understanding, is often insufficient for cultivating deep, abstract reasoning capabilities.

In parallel, **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (RLVR)** has emerged as a powerful paradigm for enhancing reasoning in both Large Language Models (LLMs) and MLLMs. Previous works like DeepSeek-R1 and Video-R1 have successfully applied RLVR, particularly using algorithms like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), to unlock reasoning in text and general video understanding tasks. SpaceR builds directly upon this promising trend, extending the RLVR paradigm to specifically address the unmet challenge of video spatial reasoning.

Therefore, SpaceR positions itself at the intersection of advanced MLLM development, spatial reasoning, and reinforcement learning, aiming to bridge the gap between MLLMs' current capabilities and the complex demands of understanding 3D space from 2D video sequences. It contributes by providing both the necessary data infrastructure and a tailored training methodology.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **significantly improve Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) in video spatial reasoning**. This overarching goal is motivated by the recognized struggle of existing MLLMs with inferring 3D spatial structures from video, a limitation stemming from two key factors:

1. **Data Scarcity for Spatial Reasoning:** The authors observe a critical "absence of high-quality datasets specifically designed for this task." Traditional video understanding datasets do not adequately capture the intricacies of spatial relationships, object sizes, relative positions, and appearance orders necessary for true spatial intelligence.
2. **Limitations of Current Training Paradigms:** The reliance of most MLLMs on Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) is deemed "insufficient for fostering deep reasoning capabilities." SFT often leads to memorization rather than the development of robust, generalizable reasoning.

Inspired by the demonstrated success of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (RLVR) in enhancing reasoning abilities in LLMs and MLLMs for other tasks, the authors propose to leverage this paradigm for video spatial reasoning.

To achieve their objective, the SpaceR framework sets forth two specific goals:
- **Dataset Creation:** To address the data gap, create a novel, high-quality, large-scale dataset, `SpaceR-151k`, specifically tailored for video spatial reasoning, while also preserving general multimodal understanding. This dataset aims to feature diverse spatial reasoning scenarios with verifiable answers.
- **Training Strategy Development:** To overcome the limitations of SFT, propose an effective reinforcement learning approach, `Spatially-Guided RLVR (SG-RLVR)`, that extends existing RL algorithms with mechanisms specifically designed to foster spatial reasoning, such as encouraging the model to explicitly infer spatial layouts during its thought process.

In essence, the motivation is to elevate MLLMs from merely describing video content to genuinely understanding and reasoning about the spatial world depicted within videos, which is a crucial step towards more human-like AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SpaceR framework introduces two core innovations to achieve its objectives: the `SpaceR-151k` dataset and the `Spatially-Guided Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (SG-RLVR)` training approach.

#### 3.1. SpaceR-151k Dataset Construction

Addressing the critical data scarcity, `SpaceR-151k` is a large-scale dataset comprising 151,310 samples, meticulously designed to train MLLMs in both spatial reasoning and general video understanding. It consists of two main parts:

- **SR-91k (Spatial Reasoning QA Pairs):**
 - This component contains 91,315 spatial reasoning question-answering pairs, curated primarily from ScanNet, a 3D indoor scene reconstruction dataset.
 - **Data Collection:** ScanNet provides 3D models and semantic annotations. The authors parse this into a unified meta-information format including object categories, appearance indices, bounding boxes, and other attributes. RGB frames are sampled at 24FPS to form video clips. Crucially, a `10x10 map` is constructed for each video, summarizing object distributions, with object coordinates projected onto a 2D map in dictionary format for structured spatial reference.
 - **QA Generation:** QA pairs are automatically generated leveraging the parsed meta-information. They are categorized into:
 - **Multi-choice QA:** Relative Distance, Relative Direction (various levels of complexity), Appearance Order.
 - **Numerical QA:** Object/Room Size, Absolute Distance, Counting.
 - Examples include: "What is the direct distance between the door and the radiator?", "If I am standing by the jacket and facing the radiator, is the window to my front-left, front-right, back-left, or back-right?".
 - **Data Filtering:** Rigorous filtering steps are applied to ensure quality, including limiting QA pairs per video for scene diversity, shuffling multi-choice answers to mitigate position bias, adjusting numerical value distributions, removing noisy objects (e.g., walls, floors), and excluding videos that overlap with the VSI-Bench evaluation set to ensure fair benchmarking.
- **General Multimodal Understanding Data (60k samples):**
 - To maintain general video comprehension capabilities, 60,000 diverse QA instances are uniformly sampled from the `Video-R1-260k` dataset. This subset includes multi-choice, numerical, OCR, free-form, and regression QA types, all with verifiable answers.

#### 3.2. Spatially-Guided Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (SG-RLVR)

SG-RLVR builds upon the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm and introduces novel mechanisms tailored for spatial reasoning.

- **Verifiable Reward Functions:**
 - **Format Reward (R_format):** Binary reward ensuring the model's response adheres to a predefined structure, specifically by wrapping reasoning within `<think>...</think>` tags and the final answer within `<answer>...</answer>` tags.
 - **Multi-choice Reward (R_mc):** Binary reward for exact matches with ground truth in multi-choice questions.
 - **Numerical Reward (R_num):** Computes relative accuracy across varying confidence thresholds to assess numerical values.
 - **Additional Rewards (from Video-R1-260k):** OCR reward (based on Word Error Rate), Free-form reward (average of ROUGE scores), and Regression reward (relative distance for numerical values). These maintain general understanding.

- **Map Imagination Mechanism:** This is a key innovation for spatial reasoning:
 - The model is guided to **explicitly generate a cognitive map** within specialized tags (`<map>...</map>`) that identifies object distributions and their positions within the scene. This encourages the model to "think in space."
 - **Map Reward (R_map):** A novel reward function quantitatively evaluates the quality of the generated map. It calculates the relative accuracy between predicted object coordinates and ground truth object coordinates (based on Euclidean distance relative to map size) and averages this across all objects. This incentivizes the model to accurately infer spatial layouts.
 - **Length-based Reward (R_l):** To regulate reasoning depth, a reward is applied if the output length falls within a predefined range `[l_min, l_max]`, but only if the primary task answer is correct. This balances thorough reasoning with avoiding unnecessary verbosity.
 - **Augmented Reward (R_m):** The final reward for policy optimization is `R_format + R_task + R_map + R_l` (if R_task = 1), or `R_format + R_task + R_l` (otherwise). This shaping ensures that models receive additional positive reinforcement when they correctly answer and demonstrate proper spatial understanding through map generation.

- **Policy Optimization:** The GRPO algorithm's clipped surrogate objective is used for policy updates, incorporating an advantage term `A_i` (representing the relative quality of responses within a group) and a KL-divergence term to prevent the optimized policy from deviating too far from the reference policy. This framework encourages the adoption of a more spatially aware reasoning policy.

The base model used for training is `Qwen-2.5-VL-7B-Instruct`, a powerful open-source MLLM. The training involves generating multiple response candidates and optimizing the policy based on the designed rewards. During evaluation for spatial reasoning, SpaceR is prompted to explicitly perform step-by-step reasoning, including map generation, while for general video understanding, it generates answers directly.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted on a diverse set of spatial reasoning and video understanding benchmarks demonstrate the superior performance and generalizability of the SpaceR framework.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

- **State-of-the-Art in Spatial Reasoning:** SpaceR consistently achieves state-of-the-art performance on challenging spatial reasoning benchmarks: VSI-Bench, STI-Bench, and SPAR-Bench.
- **Outperforming Proprietary Models:** Notably, SpaceR achieves 45.6% accuracy on VSI-Bench, **surpassing the advanced proprietary model GPT-4o by 11.6% accuracy**. It also performs on par with Gemini-2.0-Flash on VSI-Bench, highlighting its exceptional spatial reasoning capabilities.
- **Generalizability in Video Understanding:** Beyond spatial reasoning, SpaceR maintains competitive and often superior performance on general video understanding benchmarks: Video-MME, TempCompass, and LongVideoBench. This indicates that the spatial reasoning enhancements do not compromise, but rather contribute to, broader video comprehension.

#### 4.2. SG-RLVR vs. SFT

A direct comparison reveals the superior effectiveness of SG-RLVR over traditional Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):
- While SFT provides localized performance improvements (e.g., on VSI-Bench), it often leads to performance degradation on other benchmarks, demonstrating **limited generalizability**.
- In contrast, SG-RLVR consistently improves performance across *all* evaluated spatial reasoning and video understanding benchmarks, showcasing **better generalizability**. This supports the authors' claim that "SG-RLVR generalizes, while SFT memorizes," establishing SG-RLVR as a more effective training paradigm for deep reasoning.

#### 4.3. Impact of Data Sampling

The study explored a data sampling strategy on SR-91k, filtering out "too easy" (all correct) and "too difficult" (all wrong) samples to focus on those with higher learning utility. Retraining SpaceR on this filtered dataset resulted in **consistent performance gains across nearly all benchmarks**. This indicates that targeted data resampling significantly enhances training efficiency and model generalization by concentrating on more informative samples.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

Ablation studies confirm the critical contribution of each component within SpaceR SG-RLVR:
- **W/o Map Imagination:** Removing the map imagination mechanism led to decreased performance on spatial reasoning benchmarks, **validating the advantage of explicitly guiding MLLMs to understand spatial information**.
- **W/o General Data:** Excluding the 60k general multimodal understanding data resulted in lower performance on video understanding benchmarks, **proving the effectiveness of including diverse general training data**.
- **W/o SR Data:** Training without the SR-91k spatial reasoning data resulted in significantly lower performance on spatial reasoning benchmarks, **emphasizing the indispensable role of the dedicated SR-91k dataset**.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Analysis

Qualitative examples from VSI-Bench vividly illustrate SpaceR's superior reasoning. For instance, in an object relative distance task, SpaceR accurately identifies the closest object by generating a structured cognitive map and logical reasoning steps, unlike the base model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) which provides an incorrect, assumption-based answer. Similarly, in an object counting task, SpaceR correctly counts partially occluded objects by reasoning across multiple frames and utilizing its map imagination, while the base model underestimates. These cases underscore SpaceR's enhanced spatial awareness and reasoning depth.

#### 4.6. Impact of Thinking and Model Size

- **Impact of Thinking:** SpaceR SG-RLVR consistently gains performance on spatial reasoning benchmarks when explicitly utilizing its "think" mode during inference. Conversely, base models not trained with such mechanisms show performance drops when forced to "think," indicating they lack genuine reasoning capabilities. For general video understanding tasks, the "think" mode sometimes introduces slight drops for SpaceR, suggesting that unnecessary or inaccurate reasoning might introduce noise, prompting future work on adaptive reasoning.
- **Impact of Model Size:** The SG-RLVR framework demonstrates scalability, proving effective for both 3B (SpaceR-Tiny) and 7B (SpaceR) parameter models. Both sizes show consistent improvements over their SFT counterparts, confirming the general applicability of the training paradigm.

#### 4.7. Impact of Data Scale

Training SpaceR on progressively larger subsets of SpaceR-151k revealed strong data efficiency. Notable performance improvements on spatial reasoning benchmarks were observed even with small subsets (5k–15k). A substantial performance jump, especially for VSI-Bench (surpassing 45% accuracy), occurred when training on the full 151k dataset, confirming that the method benefits significantly from larger, high-quality training sets while also showing strong generalization from limited supervision.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SpaceR framework makes several significant contributions to the field of multimodal AI, particularly in pushing the boundaries of MLLMs' cognitive abilities.

1. **Addressing a Critical Gap in Spatial Reasoning:** The paper successfully tackles the long-standing challenge of video spatial reasoning, which has remained a weak point for MLLMs despite their advancements in general video understanding. This is crucial for developing more sophisticated and intelligent AI systems that can genuinely understand and interact with the physical world.

2. **Introducing a High-Quality, Verifiable Dataset:** The `SpaceR-151k` dataset, particularly its `SR-91k` component, fills a critical void in the research landscape. By providing a large-scale, meticulously curated dataset with diverse spatial reasoning QA pairs and verifiable answers derived from 3D scene reconstructions, it establishes a new benchmark and a valuable resource for future research in this domain. Its integration with general multimodal understanding data ensures that models trained on it retain broad video comprehension.

3. **Proposing a Novel Spatially-Guided RL Framework:** The `SG-RLVR` framework, with its innovative `map imagination mechanism` and tailored `verifiable reward functions`, represents a significant methodological advancement. By explicitly encouraging models to infer and represent spatial layouts during their reasoning process and rewarding the accuracy of these cognitive maps, SpaceR effectively fosters deeper spatial understanding, moving beyond superficial pattern matching.

4. **Achieving State-of-the-Art Performance:** The empirical results are compelling, with SpaceR demonstrating significant performance gains on spatial reasoning benchmarks, even outperforming advanced proprietary models like GPT-4o. This validates the effectiveness of both the `SpaceR-151k` dataset and the `SG-RLVR` training paradigm. The strong generalizability observed across various video understanding tasks further underscores the robustness of the approach.

5. **Paving the Way for Reasoning-Aware MLLMs:** The paper provides strong evidence that reinforcement learning, particularly with task-specific guidance and verifiable rewards, is more effective than traditional SFT for developing complex reasoning abilities in MLLMs. The qualitative analyses clearly show how structured thinking processes, facilitated by map imagination, lead to more accurate and interpretable spatial reasoning.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Foundation for Embodied AI:** Enhanced spatial reasoning is a cornerstone for creating more capable embodied agents (e.g., robots, virtual assistants) that can navigate, manipulate objects, and understand environments with human-like proficiency.
- **Improved World Models:** The ability to reconstruct and reason about 3D spatial layouts from video is a key step towards building more accurate and comprehensive "world models" for AI, which are essential for prediction, planning, and decision-making.
- **Advancing Multimodal AI Research:** SpaceR serves as a solid foundation and inspiration for future research into reasoning-aware training paradigms for MLLMs, encouraging the development of models that can not only perceive but also deeply understand and reason about complex multimodal information.
- **Practical Applications:** Improved spatial reasoning could lead to more robust video analysis tools for surveillance, smart homes, augmented reality, and virtual reality applications, where understanding relative positions, sizes, and movements of objects is critical.

Despite its breakthroughs, the paper acknowledges a limitation regarding adaptively controlling the depth of reasoning, suggesting that unnecessary reasoning can sometimes hinder efficiency or even performance for simpler tasks. This highlights a valuable future research direction: developing mechanisms for MLLMs to decide *when* and *how deeply* to engage in reasoning, further optimizing their performance and interpretability.
