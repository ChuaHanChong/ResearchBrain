# EgoTac: In-the-wild Tactile Prediction from Egocentric Vision

> [!summary] Summary
> EgoTac predicts dense per-vertex tactile force and binary contact over both hands directly from egocentric RGB video, using a DINOv2 encoder, temporal fusion, and an AdaLN tactile decoder trained jointly on force and contact labels unified onto a shared MANO mesh across 5.7M image-tactile pairs from nine datasets. It achieves sub-0.06N force error in-domain and beats prior contact estimators out-of-domain, enabling zero-shot tactile labeling of unconstrained human videos.

## Problem

- Large-scale egocentric human video datasets capture pixels, hand poses, and object trajectories but omit tactile signals, since touch requires contact-interface sensors unlike passively recorded vision.
- Prior vision-to-tactile methods are confined to controlled lab setups, localized regions like fingertips, or rely on analytical mesh-distance contact heuristics rather than continuous force.
- Existing tactile-labeled HOI datasets are limited in scale, restricted to specific surfaces, or lack real-world scene diversity, preventing generalizable in-the-wild tactile prediction.

## Method

- **EgoTac-SC**: a self-collected 3.9M-frame dataset using flexible tactile gloves with 264 force sensors, hand-tracking gloves, and a head-mounted camera across 70+ tasks in 24 environments.
- Unified ==MANO-aligned representation== mapping sparse glove tactile vectors and mesh-proximity contact labels from eight prior HOI datasets onto shared 778-vertex per-hand force and contact fields.
- **EgoTac**'s ==AdaLN-conditioned encoder-decoder== architecture with a ==DINOv2== vision encoder, cross-attention temporal fusion token, and joint force-regression plus contact-classification heads trained with a four-term loss including active-region and consistency terms.

## Results

- In-domain evaluation on EgoTac-SC achieves active-region MAE of **0.056N** and temporal Pearson correlation of **0.506**, with contact AUROC above **0.97** and F1 above **0.70** across HOT3D, H2O, and ARCTIC splits.
- On out-of-domain OAKINK2 and FPHA contact benchmarks, EgoTac outperforms BSTRO, DECO, and HACO, reaching AUROC **0.844** and F1 **0.435** on OAKINK2 versus HACO's 0.772 and 0.400.
- Data scaling from one to six training sources raises OAKINK2 F1 from 0.120 to **0.435**, and scaling volume from 10% to 100% further improves force-cosine similarity above **0.7** and rise/fall F1 above **0.4** on OpenTouch.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Contact-only supervision alone is insufficient for realistic force estimation, showing contact logits do not directly encode force magnitude and require explicit force-supervised data.
> - Unifying heterogeneous tactile and contact-only datasets onto a shared mesh topology lets a single model exploit both real force measurements and cheaper geometric contact proxies jointly.
> - Both data source diversity and raw volume drive steady out-of-domain generalization gains, suggesting vision-to-touch transfer is fundamentally a data-scaling problem rather than an architecture one.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous manipulation in robotics and physical intelligence research increasingly relies on large-scale human interaction data for learning. Egocentric human video datasets, such as Ego4D and EPIC-KITCHENS, offer a scalable source of diverse manipulation experiences. However, a significant limitation of these datasets is the absence of tactile information. While visual observations, hand poses, and object trajectories are typically captured, the measurement of tactile signals at scale presents challenges related to sensor placement, calibration, spatial coverage, durability, and wearability, contrasting with the passive and remote nature of visual data collection.

Prior research in vision-based tactile prediction has explored several avenues. Early work utilizing optical-tactile sensors like GelSight focused on texture recognition and geometric cues, often overlooking complex force dynamics. Contact-centric methods have localized interaction areas using thermal imaging or mesh-distance heuristics but generally do not provide continuous force values. Simulation-based approaches have used physics engines for supervision, but these often encounter a sim-to-real gap. Recent efforts to predict real-world forces have primarily been confined to controlled laboratory environments or specialized for localized regions, such as fingertips.

This work addresses the challenge of recovering tactile information from existing or easily collectible human videos. By proposing a method to infer dense hand tactile states, including continuous force and binary contact, EgoTac aims to augment vast egocentric human video corpora with physical supervision, thereby bypassing the difficulty and cost of direct tactile data collection. This approach positions EgoTac as a development towards enabling tactile-aware robot learning from a scalable and ubiquitous data source.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the disparity between the recognized importance of touch for dexterous manipulation and the practical difficulties of collecting large-scale tactile data. Robots require the ability to perceive and regulate physical contact—including where, how much pressure, and when to release—to perform everyday skills. While human video datasets are abundant and scalable, providing a rich source of manipulation experience, they typically lack the crucial tactile variables necessary for learning such nuanced physical intelligence. The direct collection of high-quality, large-scale tactile data remains challenging due to limitations of current tactile sensors in terms of spatial coverage, durability, and ease of integration into wearable systems for "in-the-wild" scenarios.

This gap motivates the central question of the research: can tactile signals be inferred purely from vision, specifically from egocentric human videos? If successful, this inference capability would allow existing visual datasets to be augmented with dense hand tactile states, providing scalable physical supervision for robot learning without requiring tactile sensors during data collection.

Based on this motivation, the key objectives of the EgoTac research are:

- **To develop a generalizable model for vision-to-dense tactile prediction:** The goal is to create a model, EgoTac, capable of predicting rich tactile information (both continuous force and binary contact) directly from egocentric human videos recorded in unconstrained, real-world environments.
- **To construct a unified, large-scale visual-tactile dataset:** To support the training and evaluation of such a model, the project aims to create EgoTac-Dataset, a comprehensive corpus that combines newly collected real-force data with existing hand-object interaction datasets. This dataset features a shared, standardized representation of tactile information mapped onto the MANO hand model.
- **To enable effective learning from heterogeneous data sources:** The model should be capable of learning from a diverse dataset that includes both samples with continuous force measurements and those with only binary contact labels, overcoming the challenges posed by differing annotation types and collection methods.
- **To demonstrate robust generalization and transferability:** The research aims to evaluate EgoTac's performance across various scenarios, including accurate in-domain prediction, strong out-of-domain generalization to unseen contact benchmarks, preservation of temporal force dynamics on different tactile data, and zero-shot prediction capabilities on unconstrained real-world videos.
- **To analyze the impact of data scale and diversity:** The study seeks to investigate how increasing the volume and diversity of training data affects EgoTac's prediction performance, thereby providing insights into the scalability of the proposed approach.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The EgoTac framework is designed to predict dense tactile information from egocentric RGB video. The methodology involves the construction of a unified dataset, a specific model architecture, and a joint training strategy.

#### 3.1. Unified Dataset for Visual-Tactile Learning

The EgoTac-Dataset serves as the foundation for training. It unifies data from nine sources, totaling over 5.7 million image-tactile pairs. This corpus includes a newly self-collected dataset, EgoTac-SC, and eight pre-existing hand-object interaction (HOI) datasets.

- **Unified Data Format:** All annotations are standardized to a shared MANO hand representation. For each time step, tactile information for both hands (2V, where V=778 vertices per MANO hand) is represented as `F` for continuous tactile intensity values (force) and `C` for binary contact labels. This format allows for joint training across data sources with different annotation granularities.

- **Custom Tactile Data Collection (EgoTac-SC):** This component of the dataset was specifically collected to provide high-resolution force signals during natural interactions.
 - **Hardware:** Participants wore custom flexible tactile gloves equipped with 264 force sensors, alongside a commercial hand-tracking glove for 3D hand pose capture. A head-mounted egocentric camera recorded visual context at 30 FPS.
 - **Collection Procedure:** Participants performed over 70 contact-rich manipulation tasks (e.g., folding clothes, opening doors) in 24 diverse environments.
 - **Post-processing:** Raw tactile readings were filtered and mapped to MANO vertices. Force values were linearly interpolated and distributed to dense MANO vertices using predefined region patches and bilinear sensor weights. Binary contact labels were then derived by thresholding the mapped tactile force (e.g., > 0.05 N).

- **Integration of Existing HOI Datasets:** To enhance scale and coverage, eight prior egocentric HOI datasets were integrated:
 - **OpenTouch:** Provided real tactile measurements, primarily for single-hand interactions, which were converted to the MANO-aligned tactile vector format.
 - **Contact-only Datasets:** HOT3D, TACO, HOI4D, H2O, ARCTIC, OAKINK2, and FPHA. For these datasets, which provide hand and object meshes but not direct force, vertex-level contact labels were derived geometrically. A MANO vertex was marked as contact if its minimum distance to the object mesh fell below a threshold (1 cm). This produced binary contact targets compatible with the unified MANO topology.

#### 3.2. EgoTac Model Architecture

EgoTac follows an encoder-decoder architecture designed for joint prediction of dense continuous tactile values and binary contact labels from temporal egocentric RGB video inputs.

- **Vision Encoder:** Each egocentric RGB frame within an observation horizon `T` is encoded into spatial-semantic vision tokens using a pretrained DINOv2 ViT backbone.
- **Temporal Fusion Module:** A learnable temporal token aggregates global context over time by attending to the sequence of vision tokens via cross-attention. This results in a global context vector `z`.
- **Tactile Decoder:** This module performs direct prediction in a single forward pass, conditioned on the context vector `z`. It consists of Transformer layers modulated by Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN). A set of learnable tactile query tokens are iteratively updated through these layers, producing tactile estimations for each prediction step `K` across the MANO hand topology. The AdaLN mechanism dynamically scales and shifts layer normalization parameters based on `z`, allowing visual context to guide predictions efficiently.

#### 3.3. Force-Contact Joint Training

The framework employs a joint training scheme to leverage heterogeneous supervision (continuous force and discrete contact).

- **Problem Formulation:** Given an egocentric RGB clip `I` over an observation horizon `T`, the model predicts continuous tactile force fields (`ˆF`) and discrete contact states (`ˆC`) over a prediction horizon `K` (typically K=1), both anchored on the 2V MANO hand mesh vertices. `ˆF` represents scalar normal pressure, and `ˆC` represents per-vertex contact probability logits.
- **Task-Specific Heads:** Two heads are attached to the shared decoder: a tactile regression head for continuous force values and a contact classification head for per-vertex contact logits.
- **Mixed-Dataset Learning:** Force-supervised samples (from EgoTac-SC and OpenTouch) update both heads (contact labels derived from thresholding continuous force). Contact-only samples (from other HOI datasets) update only the contact classification branch. Force-valid (`M_F`) and contact-valid (`M_C`) masks are used to handle missing annotations.
- **Loss Function:** The total objective `L` is a weighted sum of four components:
 - `L_F`: Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss on normalized tactile values, applied only where continuous force supervision is available.
 - `L_C`: Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss for contact classification, with positive class reweighting, applied where contact labels are available.
 - `L_A`: Active-region loss (Smooth-L1 penalty) on active interacting vertices (ground-truth force > 0.05 N), encouraging accurate modeling of contact areas.
 - `L_cons`: Consistency loss (BCE) enforcing alignment between implicit contact probabilities derived from the force branch and predictions from the contact branch.
- **Implementation Details:** The model is optimized end-to-end using AdamW on 4 A10 GPUs with a batch size of 128. A cosine-decayed learning rate schedule is used, and the vision encoder is fine-tuned with a reduced learning rate.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of EgoTac was structured around three key research questions: in-domain performance, out-of-domain generalization, and data scaling effects.

#### 4.1. In-Domain Performance and Ablations

- **Tactile-based Evaluation (EgoTac-SC):**
 - EgoTac demonstrated an average active-region force error (MAE_act) below 0.06 N and a global MAE of 0.004.
 - Temporal Pearson correlation, measuring the consistency of predicted force trajectories with ground truth, reached 0.506.
 - **Ablation Studies:**
 - Removing temporal visual context reduced temporal Pearson correlation to 0.463, indicating its importance for consistent predictions.
 - Removing the active-region loss increased MAE_act significantly to 0.097 N, highlighting its role in enhancing force fidelity in interacting areas.
 - Training with contact-only supervision (without direct force supervision) resulted in a substantially higher MAE_act of 0.108 N and lower temporal Pearson of 0.240, confirming that binary contact labels alone are insufficient for accurate continuous force estimation.

- **Contact-based Evaluation (HOI test splits):**
 - EgoTac maintained strong contact prediction performance across in-domain HOI datasets (HOT3D, H2O, ARCTIC, TACO, HOI4D), achieving AUROC values above 0.95 and F1 scores generally above 0.70. This indicates that the joint force prediction objective did not compromise contact localization accuracy.

#### 4.2. Out-of-Domain Generalization

- **Contact-based Benchmarks (OAKINK2, FPHA):**
 - EgoTac consistently outperformed state-of-the-art RGB-based 3D contact estimators (BSTRO, DECO, HACO) on unseen datasets. For instance, on OAKINK2, EgoTac achieved an AUROC of 0.844 and F1 of 0.435, surpassing HACO (AUROC 0.772, F1 0.400).
 - Qualitative analysis indicated that EgoTac produced cleaner separation between contact and non-contact states compared to HACO, which tended to over-predict contact regions.

- **Force-sensitive Benchmark (OpenTouch):**
 - On the OpenTouch dataset, which uses a different tactile sensing system, EgoTac demonstrated its ability to predict continuous force dynamics. After normalization, EgoTac achieved a force-cosine similarity above 0.7 and rise/fall F1 scores above 0.4.
 - Predicted temporal force traces were observed to track ground-truth trends effectively, suggesting reliable prediction of relative tactile intensity and temporal structure despite sensor and domain shifts.

- **Qualitative Results on Diverse HOI Scenes:**
 - Zero-shot tactile predictions on unconstrained datasets (EgoDex, EPIC-KITCHENS, Ego4D, EgoPAT3D) showed that EgoTac generated spatially coherent tactile patterns and smooth temporal transitions. The model demonstrated sensitivity to object-specific geometry and identified active manipulation hands and fine-grained contact changes over long-horizon episodes. Attention maps further confirmed that EgoTac focused on hand-object interaction regions relevant for tactile prediction.

#### 4.3. Effects of Data Scaling

- **Data Diversity:** Increasing the number of diverse data sources used for training (from 1 to 6 datasets) consistently improved out-of-domain contact estimation performance on OAKINK2. For example, using 6 datasets resulted in an F1 score of 0.435, compared to 0.120 with only 1 dataset.
- **Data Volume:** Scaling the total training data volume from 10% to 100% led to steady improvements across multiple out-of-domain metrics. This enhancement was observed in contact AUROC, IoU, and F1 on OAKINK2, and significantly boosted force-cosine similarity and rise/fall F1 scores on the OpenTouch benchmark.
- These consistent gains indicate that EgoTac benefits from both the diversity and volume of training data, suggesting further performance improvements are possible with more extensive tactile-supervised egocentric data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EgoTac represents a step toward addressing a fundamental challenge in robot learning and physical intelligence: the scarcity of large-scale, high-fidelity tactile data. The work's significance lies in demonstrating the feasibility of inferring rich, dense tactile information directly from readily available egocentric human videos, which typically lack such data.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Bridging the Data Gap:** By unifying a new self-collected real-force dataset (EgoTac-SC) with eight existing hand-object interaction datasets into a standardized MANO-aligned representation, EgoTac creates a substantial corpus of over 5.7 million visual-tactile samples. This unified dataset is foundational for learning complex vision-to-tactile mappings.
- **Generalizable Tactile Prediction:** EgoTac introduces a scalable architecture and a mixed force-contact objective that effectively learns from this heterogeneous supervision. This enables the model to predict both continuous force and binary contact across both hands from in-the-wild egocentric RGB videos, a capability not previously demonstrated with this level of generalization.
- **Robust Performance and Transferability:** The comprehensive empirical evaluation highlights EgoTac's accurate in-domain predictions, strong out-of-domain generalization, and impressive zero-shot transfer capabilities to unconstrained real-world scenes. The ability to maintain temporal force dynamics across different sensing systems and to outperform previous state-of-the-art contact estimators indicates the model's robustness.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

The ability to extract tactile priors from ubiquitous human video corpora holds several potential impacts:
- **Tactile-Aware Robot Learning:** EgoTac provides a scalable pathway to augment existing visual datasets with tactile supervision. This can enable robots to learn dexterous manipulation skills that are critically dependent on touch, such as precise force regulation and contact localization, without the prohibitive cost and difficulty of direct tactile data collection on robots. This could lead to more physically intelligent and adaptive robotic systems.
- **Enhanced Physical Intelligence:** By providing a detailed understanding of hand-object interactions beyond mere kinematics, EgoTac can contribute to developing more sophisticated models of human physical intelligence.
- **Applications in AR/VR and Assistive Systems:** The framework can be applied to enrich virtual and augmented reality experiences by providing more realistic hand-object interaction feedback or to improve assistive technologies by better interpreting user intentions through estimated tactile states.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The unified dataset and mixed-supervision framework provide a valuable resource and benchmark for future research in cross-modal learning, particularly in addressing challenges like visual-domain gaps (e.g., between gloved and bare hands), occlusion reasoning, and motion blur in tactile prediction.
