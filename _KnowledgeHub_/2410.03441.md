# CLoSD: Closing the Loop between Simulation and Diffusion for multi-task character control

> [!summary] Summary
> The CLOSD system introduces a method for controlling virtual characters by closing the loop between motion planning and physical execution. It combines an auto-regressive diffusion model for real-time motion planning with a physics-based reinforcement learning controller, enabling characters to perform diverse, physically plausible actions in response to text commands and interact realistically with environments.

## Problem

- Generating diverse, physically plausible virtual character movements from text commands remains challenging, requiring a balance between creative generation and physical realism.
- Motion diffusion models excel at generating diverse movements but often lack physical plausibility and struggle to adapt to dynamic environments.
- Physics-based control methods ensure physical realism but typically struggle with scalability to diverse motions and tasks.

## Method

- CLOSD (Closing the Loop between Simulation and Diffusion) integrates a ==Diffusion Planner (DiP)== for creative motion planning with a physics-based Tracking Controller for plan execution.
- The system operates in a closed loop, where the DiP generates kinematic plans, the Tracking Controller executes them in a physics simulator, and the executed motion is fed back to the DiP as a motion prefix.
- A state machine coordinates transitions between high-level tasks, allowing for complex multi-task sequences like moving to a target, striking an object, and sitting down.

## Results

- CLOSD demonstrated superior performance in goal reaching, target striking, and object interaction tasks (e.g., sitting on furniture) compared to open-loop baselines and other text-to-motion controllers.
- Closed-loop fine-tuning was crucial for enabling the system to succeed in complex interaction scenarios, as shown by ablation studies.
- The system showed competitive performance on standard text-to-motion benchmarks like HumanML3D, indicating that physical constraints did not significantly compromise motion diversity or text alignment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating motion diffusion models with physics-based reinforcement learning in a closed loop enables both diverse motion generation and physical plausibility.
> - A crucial innovation is the joint fine-tuning of the diffusion planner and the tracking controller in a closed-loop system, which significantly improves performance on complex interaction tasks.
> - Using an auto-regressive diffusion planner that generates short-term motion plans in real-time allows for continuous adaptation to physical execution and environmental changes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Creating virtual characters that can perform diverse, physically plausible movements in response to text commands is a significant challenge in computer animation, gaming, and robotics. The difficulty lies in balancing the creative generation of diverse motions with physical realism and real-time interactivity. CLOSD (Closing the Loop between Simulation and Diffusion) represents a novel approach to this challenge by integrating two complementary technologies: motion diffusion models and physics-based reinforcement learning.

Traditional approaches have typically focused on either motion synthesis (which can generate diverse movements but often lacks physical realism) or physics-based control (which ensures physical plausibility but struggles with diversity and scalability). CLOSD bridges this gap by using a diffusion model to generate creative motion plans that are then executed by a physics-based controller, with the executed motion feeding back into the planning process in a closed loop.

### 2. Research Context

Character animation and control have evolved significantly over the years, from keyframe animation to data-driven approaches and, more recently, physics-based control and generative AI models.

Most closely related to CLOSD are two specific approaches:

1. **Motion Diffusion Models (MDMs)**: These are generative models that have shown remarkable ability to create diverse human-like motions from text descriptions. Recent examples include MDM, MoFusion, A-MDM, and CAMDM. While these models excel at generating creative motions, they often struggle with physical plausibility and cannot adapt to dynamic environments.

2. **Physics-based Controllers with Reinforcement Learning**: Methods like PHC (Perpetual Humanoid Control) use RL to train characters that can interact realistically with environments. While physically plausible, these methods often struggle with scalability to diverse motions and tasks.

CLOSD aims to combine the strengths of both approaches by using a diffusion model for planning and a physics-based controller for execution, all while maintaining a closed feedback loop between them.

### 3. The CLOSD Method

The core idea of CLOSD is to create a tight integration between motion planning and execution through a closed-loop architecture. This approach enables the system to continuously adapt its motion plans based on the actual physical execution, leading to more robust and realistic character control.

The CLOSD system consists of three primary components:

1. A Diffusion Planner (DiP) that generates kinematic motion plans
2. A Tracking Controller that executes these plans in a physics simulator
3. A feedback mechanism that updates the motion planning based on actual execution

The interaction begins with a text prompt (e.g., "A person is sitting down on the sofa") and a target location. The DiP generates a motion plan, which the Tracking Controller attempts to execute in the physics simulator. The resulting motion is then fed back to the DiP as a motion prefix, closing the loop and allowing for continuous adaptation.

### 4. Diffusion Planner

The Diffusion Planner (DiP) is an auto-regressive diffusion model that generates short-term motion plans in real-time. It is conditioned on:

1. A text prompt describing the desired motion
2. A target location for a specific body joint (e.g., the pelvis for sitting or the hand for punching)
3. A motion prefix representing the character's recent movements

The DiP architecture uses a transformer decoder backbone with DistilBERT for text encoding. The model is trained using a denoising diffusion process with a geometric target loss that encourages accurate target reaching.

The mathematical formulation of the diffusion process can be described as:

$$q(x_t|x_0) = \mathcal{N}(x_t; \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}x_0, (1-\bar{\alpha}_t)I)$$

where $x_0$ represents the clean motion data, $x_t$ is the noised motion at diffusion step $t$, and $\bar{\alpha}_t$ controls the noise schedule.

During inference, the DiP generates motion continuations in an auto-regressive manner, producing 1-2 seconds of motion at each step. Critically, the DiP runs at approximately 3,500 frames per second, enabling real-time interaction.

### 5. Tracking Controller

The Tracking Controller is responsible for executing the motion plans generated by DiP in a physics simulator. It is a reinforcement learning policy based on the Perpetual Humanoid Control (PHC) framework.

The controller takes as input:
- The current state of the character in the physics simulation
- The target motion from the DiP

It then outputs joint torques that drive the simulated character to follow the target motion as closely as possible while maintaining physical plausibility.

The RL policy is trained with a reward function that balances tracking accuracy with physical stability:

$$r = w_p r_p + w_v r_v + w_e r_e + w_c r_c$$

where $r_p$, $r_v$, $r_e$, and $r_c$ represent rewards for pose matching, velocity matching, energy efficiency, and contact consistency respectively, weighted by their corresponding coefficients.

A key innovation in CLOSD is the fine-tuning of this tracking controller in a closed loop with the DiP, which significantly improves its ability to handle object interactions and physically challenging motions.

### 6. State Machine for Task Coordination

For more complex multi-task sequences, CLOSD employs a simple state machine to coordinate transitions between different tasks.

The state machine defines transitions between high-level tasks such as:
- Goal reaching (moving to a target location)
- Target striking (hitting an object)
- Sitting down on furniture
- Getting up from furniture

Each state transition is triggered by specific completion signals, such as the pelvis reaching the sitting area or the hand successfully striking a target. This simple yet effective approach allows for the sequencing of complex behaviors without the need for manual scripting.

### 7. Training and Fine-tuning

The CLOSD system is trained in three stages:

1. **DiP Training**: The diffusion planner is trained on motion capture data, including the HumanML3D dataset and additional data for object interactions. It is trained with conditioning on text, target locations, and motion prefixes.

2. **Initial Controller Training**: The tracking controller is initially trained to follow reference motions from the motion capture database.

3. **Closed-Loop Fine-tuning**: The key innovation is the joint fine-tuning of the tracking controller in a closed loop with DiP. During this phase, the controller learns to handle the sometimes physically implausible motions generated by DiP, while DiP learns to generate motions that are more executable by the controller.

The fine-tuning process is crucial for tasks involving object interaction, as shown in the ablation studies. Without fine-tuning, the system struggles with complex tasks like sitting on furniture.

### 8. Results and Evaluation

CLOSD was evaluated on several tasks:

1. **Goal reaching**: Moving to a target location with specified movements
2. **Target striking**: Hitting objects with specified strikes (e.g., punches, kicks)
3. **Sitting down**: Sitting on furniture with different styles
4. **Getting up**: Rising from seated positions

The system demonstrated superior performance compared to existing methods across all tasks. In particular:

- CLOSD achieved higher success rates in goal reaching and target striking compared to text-to-motion controllers
- The method demonstrated more realistic object interactions, particularly for sitting tasks, compared to both open-loop approaches and other controllers
- Fine-tuning in a closed loop was shown to be crucial for tasks involving physical interaction

The method also showed competitive performance on standard text-to-motion benchmarks like HumanML3D, demonstrating that the physical constraints did not significantly compromise motion diversity or text alignment.

Ablation studies revealed:
- Open-loop baselines (where DiP plans motion offline) struggled with interaction tasks
- Closed-loop without fine-tuning showed improved performance but still failed in many interaction scenarios
- Full CLOSD with fine-tuning achieved the highest success rates across all tasks

### 9. Applications and Impact

CLOSD has potential applications in several domains:

1. **Video Games and Virtual Reality**: The method could enable more interactive and responsive non-player characters that can perform diverse actions in response to player commands.

2. **Animation and Content Creation**: The text-based interface makes character animation more accessible to non-experts, potentially streamlining animation workflows.

3. **Robotics**: The approach could inform physical robot control by demonstrating how planned motions can be adapted based on actual execution feedback.

4. **Virtual Assistants and Digital Humans**: CLOSD could enable more natural and physically plausible movements for virtual humans in interactive applications.

The key innovation of combining diffusion models with physics-based control in a closed loop represents a step forward in character animation, potentially influencing the broader field of intelligent physical systems.

### 10. Conclusion

CLOSD introduces a novel approach to character control by closing the loop between motion planning and physical execution. By combining the complementary strengths of diffusion models (for creative and diverse motion generation) and reinforcement learning (for physical plausibility), the system achieves both realistic physics and diverse motion capabilities.

The closed-loop approach enables continuous adaptation, allowing the character to maintain balance and achieve goals even when faced with physical constraints. Fine-tuning in this closed loop proves crucial for complex interaction tasks, demonstrating the importance of joint optimization of planners and controllers.

CLOSD represents an important step toward more interactive, physically plausible, and diverse character control, with potential applications across gaming, animation, robotics, and virtual reality. The work suggests that closing the loop between high-level motion planning and low-level execution is a promising direction for creating more capable embodied agents.
