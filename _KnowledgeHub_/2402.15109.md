# Remaining-data-free Machine Unlearning by Suppressing Sample Contribution

> [!summary] Summary
> MU-Mis introduces a machine unlearning method that efficiently removes data influence from deep neural networks without requiring access to remaining training data. It minimizes the input sensitivity gap between target and irrelevant class logits for forgotten samples, outperforming previous remaining-data-free methods and rivaling data-dependent state-of-the-art approaches while being up to 30 times faster for large models.

## Problem

- Deep neural networks often memorize training data, leading to privacy risks and making compliance with "right to be forgotten" regulations computationally prohibitive due to expensive retraining.
- Existing approximate machine unlearning (MU) methods suffer from limitations like computational expense, catastrophic unlearning, or critically, requiring access to remaining training data, which is often unavailable or introduces additional privacy concerns.
- Current remaining-data-free MU methods generally exhibit a significant performance gap compared to state-of-the-art methods that utilize remaining data.

## Method

- Proposes ==MU-Mis (Machine Unlearning by Minimizing Input Sensitivity)==, a remaining-data-free method that reconceptualizes unlearning as withdrawing a sample's contribution to the learning process.
- The method directly suppresses the learned model's ==input sensitivity== for forgotten data by minimizing a novel loss function that reduces the ==magnitude gap between target and irrelevant class logit sensitivities==.
- MU-Mis fine-tunes the pre-trained model using only the ==forgetting data==, employing a pragmatic, remaining-data-free stopping guideline and freezing batch normalization layers to preserve utility on remaining data.

## Results

- MU-Mis significantly outperforms all **four** existing remaining-data-free unlearning methods and is the first remaining-data-free method to rival or surpass data-dependent state-of-the-art approaches, achieving the lowest Average Gap to the retrained model on datasets like CIFAR-100 and PinsFaceRecognition.
- The method achieves strong privacy protection with Membership Inference Attack success rates near **0%** and provides substantial efficiency gains, reducing unlearning time by up to **30x** for large models like ViT on Tiny ImageNet.
- MU-Mis demonstrates superior resilience and stability in sequential unlearning tasks, maintaining low utility and resilience gaps while avoiding issues like performance recovery, knowledge residue, and utility breakdown observed in other state-of-the-art methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A sample's contribution to the dynamic learning process in deep neural networks reflects in the learned model's static *sensitivity to its input*, which can be precisely quantified and targeted for unlearning.
> - During training, models significantly amplify the input sensitivity of the target class logit and suppress that of irrelevant class logits for training data, creating a discernible "magnitude gap" encoding the sample's contribution.
> - Unlearning can be effectively achieved by minimizing this sensitivity magnitude gap for the forgetting data, thereby reversing the impact of their original contribution without requiring access to any remaining training data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of machine unlearning (MU) addresses the challenge of removing the influence of specific training data from a pre-trained model. This area of research has gained relevance due to growing concerns regarding data privacy, epitomized by regulations like the "right to be forgotten," and evidence that deep neural networks (DNNs) can store identifiable training information, making them susceptible to privacy attacks.

Ideally, machine unlearning would replicate the state of a model retrained from scratch without the forgotten data. However, retraining from scratch is computationally intensive and often impractical for large-scale DNNs. Consequently, approximate MU methods have emerged, focusing on efficiently modifying a pre-trained model to achieve a similar outcome.

A central challenge in MU is precisely quantifying and disentangling the contribution of individual training samples to the overall learning process. Many existing approximate MU methods circumvent this difficulty by employing heuristic strategies, such as random relabeling of forgotten data or knowledge distillation from "useless" teacher models. While these heuristics aim to erase information, they often introduce limitations:
- They can lead to significant degradation of the model's utility on the remaining, non-forgotten data, a phenomenon sometimes termed "catastrophe unlearning" or "over-forgetting."
- To counteract this utility degradation, these methods frequently require access to and re-optimization with the remaining training data, which reduces their efficiency.
- In practical scenarios, access to remaining data may be restricted due to privacy regulations or the sheer volume and distribution of data.

This research aims to advance machine unlearning by proposing a more principled approach that directly addresses the challenge of identifying and suppressing sample contributions within the pre-trained model itself. By doing so, it seeks to improve both model utility preservation and unlearning efficiency, particularly by eliminating the dependency on remaining data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an effective and efficient machine unlearning (MU) mechanism that can remove the influence of specified training samples (referred to as forgetting data, $D_f$) from a pre-trained model. The goal is for the unlearned model to closely approximate a model that was originally trained without the $D_f$, thereby satisfying the "right to be forgotten" principle.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in existing MU approaches:

- **Addressing the "Right to be Forgotten":** Deep neural networks are known to retain specific details of their training data, which poses privacy risks and conflicts with legal mandates for data deletion. While retraining from scratch offers an ideal solution, its computational cost renders it impractical for modern large-scale models. This necessitates efficient approximate unlearning methods.
- **The Challenge of Quantifying Sample Contribution:** The core distinction between a pre-trained model and a model retrained without $D_f$ lies in the contribution of $D_f$. In the pre-trained model, $D_f$ contributed to parameter updates; in the retrained model, it did not. Identifying and withdrawing this contribution is complex because the learning process is dynamic, and isolating individual sample influences is challenging. Prior work often struggled with directly backtracking training trajectories or accurately estimating influence functions in deep learning contexts.
- **Limitations of Heuristic-Based Unlearning:** Current approximate MU methods frequently rely on heuristics like confusing the model with random labels or distilling knowledge from an ineffective "teacher" model. These approaches, while functional, typically incur several drawbacks:
 - **Utility Degradation:** Introducing confusion can severely impair the model's performance on the remaining data ($D_r$), leading to a state of "catastrophe unlearning" where the model forgets too much or learns incorrect information.
 - **Dependency on Remaining Data:** To mitigate utility loss, most heuristic methods require re-optimization using the remaining data. This adds significant computational overhead and undermines the efficiency gains intended by unlearning.
 - **Practical Inaccessibility of Remaining Data:** In many real-world scenarios, the remaining data may not be accessible for re-training due to privacy regulations, data distribution across different entities, or simply their massive scale, making data-dependent unlearning impractical.

This research is motivated by the need to move beyond these heuristic approaches. It seeks to establish a more principled foundation for unlearning by explicitly characterizing how a sample's contribution manifests within a pre-trained model. The central hypothesis is that a sample's contribution to the learning process is reflected in the learned model's increased sensitivity to that particular input. By identifying and directly suppressing this contribution, the aim is to achieve effective unlearning without degrading the utility of the remaining data and, crucially, without requiring access to them.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this research involves a theoretical derivation and empirical validation of how sample contributions manifest in a pre-trained model's input sensitivity, followed by the development of a novel unlearning method based on this insight.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Analysis of Sample Contribution and Input Sensitivity

The paper conceptualizes the machine learning process as a mapping, $A_D$, which takes a training dataset $D$ and outputs a learned function $f(x; w_p)$. Machine unlearning is framed as the withdrawal of a sample's contribution, which is linked to the derivative of this training mapping with respect to a specific training sample $x_i$, denoted as $\partial A_D / \partial x_i$. This derivative essentially quantifies how the learned function $f$ would change if the training sample $x_i$ were to vary.

Since a closed-form expression for $\partial A_D / \partial x_i$ is typically unavailable for deep learning models, the research seeks a surrogate. Through an analysis of gradient descent (GD) updates in both parameter space and function space, the paper derives that the contribution of a training sample $x_i$ to the learning process can be approximately reflected by the learned model's sensitivity to its own input $x$, specifically by $\partial f(x; w_p) / \partial x$ evaluated at $x_i$. This is interpreted as a "self-influence" or "memorization" of the sample.

The derivative of the learned function $f(x; w_p)$ with respect to its input $x$ is decomposed into a "Contribution Term" (related to the sample's self-influence via its own gradient updates) and a "Residual Term" (related to influences from other samples and initial model state). The paper posits, with supporting arguments and a toy MLP example in the appendix, that the residual term is comparatively smaller, implying that the model's sensitivity to a training sample predominantly reflects that sample's contribution. Empirical validation comparing input sensitivity ($||\nabla_x f||_F$) of training data before and after training shows a significant increase (orders of magnitude), supporting the idea that training enhances a model's sensitivity to its training inputs.

#### 3.2. Input Sensitivity of Target and Irrelevant Class Logits

To refine the understanding of sample contribution, the analysis extends to individual logits of the output function $f(x)$. For a given sample $x$, $f_c(x)$ represents the logit for its correct (target) class, and $f_{c'}(x)$ for an irrelevant class. Empirical investigation reveals that in a well-trained model, the Frobenius norm of the input gradient for the target logit ($||\nabla_x f_c||_F$) becomes substantially larger than that for irrelevant logits ($||\nabla_x f_{c'}||_F$). This amplified sensitivity gap between target and irrelevant logits is proposed as a distinct signature of a sample's contribution to its correct classification during training. A connection to the implicit density modeling aspect of discriminative classifiers is also provided, where logits are un-normalized log-densities, and their input gradients are log-gradients of class-conditional densities.

#### 3.3. Empirical Validation for Unlearning Scenarios

To ensure the sensitivity gap is a reliable objective for unlearning, the paper empirically examines how this gap changes for forgetting data when comparing a pre-trained model to a retrained model (which completely excludes the forgetting data). The observations indicate a consistent trend: in the retrained model, the target logit sensitivity ($||\nabla_x f_c||_F$) to forgetting data is lower, the irrelevant logit sensitivity ($||\nabla_x f_{c'}||_F$) is higher, and consequently, the magnitude of the sensitivity gap ($||\nabla_x f_c||_F - ||\nabla_x f_{c'}||_F$) is consistently smaller than in the pre-trained model. This trend confirms that reducing this sensitivity gap aligns with the desired behavior of an unlearned model.

#### 3.4. Proposed Method: MU-Mis (Machine Unlearning by Minimizing Input Sensitivity)

Based on these theoretical and empirical insights, the authors propose MU-Mis.
- **Loss Function:** The core of MU-Mis is an unlearning loss function designed to directly suppress the identified sample contribution:
 $$L(D_f; w) = \frac{1}{N_f} \sum_{x_f \in D_f} \left( \| \nabla_x f_c(x_f, w) \|_F^2 - \| \nabla_x f_{c'}(x_f, w) \|_F^2 \right)$$
 This loss aims to minimize the squared Frobenius norm of the input sensitivity of the target logit while simultaneously maximizing the squared Frobenius norm of the input sensitivity of a randomly selected irrelevant logit for each forgetting sample $x_f$. This directly reduces the sensitivity disparity that characterizes a learned sample.
- **Optimization:** The pre-trained model parameters ($w_p$) are fine-tuned by minimizing this loss using an optimizer (SGD without momentum for ResNet-18, Adam for ViT). To avoid unintended degradation of the remaining data's performance, batch normalization layers are frozen during this fine-tuning. Only the forgetting data $D_f$ are used during this process, making MU-Mis a remaining-data-free method.
- **Stopping Guideline:** A data-driven stopping criterion is established. Empirical analysis shows that as the MU-Mis loss decreases, forgetting accuracy drops, and the accuracy on retained/test data initially declines slightly before recovering as the irrelevant-class logit sensitivity ($||\nabla_x f_{c'}||_F$) approaches its initial level. The optimization halts when this sensitivity recovers sufficiently, specifically when $||\nabla_x f_{c'}(D_f, w_t)||_F > \epsilon$ (where $\epsilon$ is the minimal observed sensitivity) AND $||\nabla_x f_{c'}(D_f, w_t)||_F / ||\nabla_x f_{c'}(D_f, w_p)||_F > \delta$ (a predefined threshold ratio).
- **Reweighted Loss (Appendix):** A dynamic reweighting scheme for the two terms in the loss is explored, where weights $\alpha_c$ and $\alpha_{c'}$ are adjusted based on whether target class sensitivity is greater than irrelevant class sensitivity, controlled by a parameter $\kappa$. This aims to enhance optimization efficiency by prioritizing terms based on their relative magnitudes during different stages of unlearning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducts comprehensive experiments across various unlearning tasks, datasets, and model architectures, evaluating MU-Mis against both remaining-data-dependent and remaining-data-free baselines.

#### 4.1. Unlearning Utility

- **General Performance:** MU-Mis effectively unlearns forgetting data (demonstrating low Forgetting Accuracy, FA) while largely preserving model utility on remaining (RA) and test data (TA).
- **Comparison with Baselines:**
 - **Remaining-Data-Free Methods:** MU-Mis consistently demonstrates higher performance compared to existing remaining-data-free methods (e.g., NG, RL, JiT, SCAR) across all evaluated scenarios (full-class, sub-class, random-subset unlearning). Many existing remaining-data-free methods exhibit notable utility degradation.
 - **Remaining-Data-Dependent Methods:** MU-Mis achieves performance comparable to state-of-the-art remaining-data-dependent methods. In full-class unlearning (CIFAR-100, PinsFaceRecognition) and sub-class unlearning (CIFAR-20 'Sea' and 'Lamp'), MU-Mis often records the smallest average gap (Avg. Gap) to the retrained model, indicating a closer approximation of the target unlearned state.
- **Challenging Scenarios (Random Subset Unlearning):** In the random-subset unlearning task, which is considered highly challenging due to high entanglement between forgetting and remaining data, MU-Mis shows improvement over other remaining-data-free methods. While its model utility performance in this specific scenario is not on par with leading remaining-data-dependent methods like RUM+SalUn, MU-Mis exhibits a lower Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence to the retrained model on the forget set, suggesting a more faithful removal of data influence at the output distribution level.

#### 4.2. Privacy Guarantee

The privacy of the unlearned model is assessed using two membership inference attack (MIA) methods: MIA-Entropy and MIA-SCRUB.
- MIA-Entropy scores for MU-Mis are consistently low (often 0%), indicating that the forgotten data no longer retain significant membership information that could be exploited by such an attack.
- MIA-SCRUB scores are observed to be close to that of the retrained model across tasks, indicating successful non-membership of forgotten data. These results collectively suggest that MU-Mis effectively protects the privacy of forgotten data.

#### 4.3. Computational Efficiency

MU-Mis demonstrates advantages in computational efficiency.
- For unlearning a full class from Tiny ImageNet, MU-Mis is reported to be up to 30 times faster than SalUn, while maintaining comparable utility.
- This efficiency advantage is more pronounced in larger models. For instance, in full-class unlearning on Tiny ImageNet with a ViT backbone, MU-Mis reduces unlearning time from over an hour to approximately 3 minutes, performing comparably to competitive methods in utility and privacy.

#### 4.4. Resilience to Sequential Unlearning

The study investigates the resilience of MU-Mis to sequential unlearning requests, simulating scenarios where multiple unlearning requests are made iteratively.
- MU-Mis exhibits stable and effective performance throughout sequential full-class and sub-class unlearning tasks. It maintains a small average gap to the retrained model in both utility and resilience metrics (e.g., Forgotten Class Training Accuracy - FGTA, Forgotten Class Validation Accuracy - FGVA).
- Other state-of-the-art methods frequently show deficiencies, such as "performance recovery" (knowledge residue on forgotten classes) or "utility breakdown" (significant degradation of retained data performance) during sequential unlearning.
- MU-Mis consistently achieves the lowest KL divergence from the retrained model's output distributions in sequential unlearning, suggesting a high degree of output indistinguishability.

#### 4.5. Supplementary Analyses (Appendix)

- **Ablation Study:** Confirms that both components of the MU-Mis loss—minimizing target logit sensitivity and maximizing irrelevant logit sensitivity—are crucial for achieving effective unlearning and preserving model utility.
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** Demonstrates that MU-Mis exhibits robustness to variations in the stopping threshold ratio ($\delta$), simplifying its application.
- **Attention Maps:** Visualizations using Grad-CAM show that MU-Mis effectively shifts the model's attention away from the salient features of forgotten data while preserving attention on retained data.
- **Orthogonality of Input Sensitivity Gradients:** Empirical analysis suggests that gradients of input sensitivity with respect to parameters exhibit greater orthogonality between inter-class samples compared to standard loss or logit gradients. This property is proposed as a factor contributing to MU-Mis's ability to preserve utility by reducing interference between forgotten and retained data.
- **Sensitivity Gaps and Memorization/Influence:** Higher memorized samples are found to exhibit smaller sensitivity gaps, while more influential samples show higher sensitivity gaps. Unlearning highly memorized samples leads to a more significant utility drop.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents MU-Mis, a remaining-data-free machine unlearning method that exhibits effective performance and addresses several practical limitations of existing approaches. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enabling Remaining-Data-Free Unlearning:** A primary impact is the development of an unlearning method that does not require access to the remaining training data. This is a critical advancement for real-world deployment, particularly in contexts governed by strict privacy regulations (e.g., GDPR's "right to be forgotten"), distributed data architectures, or scenarios where data volume makes reloading retained data impractical or computationally prohibitive. MU-Mis bridges a notable performance gap between data-dependent and data-free methods.
- **Enhanced Efficiency and Scalability:** By operating without the remaining data, MU-Mis significantly reduces the computational and storage overhead associated with utility recovery. This leads to substantial reductions in unlearning time, especially for large models and datasets. For instance, the reported speed-up of up to 30x over some baselines and the ability to unlearn large ViT models in minutes contribute to making machine unlearning a more timely and scalable solution for dynamic AI systems.
- **Principled Approach to Sample Contribution:** The work offers a theoretically and empirically grounded method for quantifying and suppressing the influence of specific training samples based on their input sensitivity. This approach moves beyond heuristic-driven strategies, potentially leading to more robust, reliable, and predictable unlearning outcomes.
- **Improved Resilience in Dynamic Environments:** The method's demonstrated stability and effectiveness in sequential unlearning scenarios address a known challenge for many MU techniques. This resilience is important for real-world applications where unlearning requests are not isolated events but occur iteratively over time, necessitating methods that can maintain performance without cumulative degradation.
- **Advancing Research into Fundamental Unlearning Mechanisms:** By linking sample contribution to input sensitivity, the research provides a new perspective on the core mechanics of how models retain and forget information. The empirical correlations observed between sensitivity gaps and memorization/influence levels open new avenues for fundamental research into model privacy, generalization, and interpretability. This could guide the development of even more sophisticated unlearning techniques, especially for complex cases like random-subset unlearning, by focusing on more fine-grained aspects of sample influence.

In summary, MU-Mis represents a notable step towards making machine unlearning more practical, efficient, and broadly applicable in an era of increasing data privacy demands and large-scale AI deployments.
