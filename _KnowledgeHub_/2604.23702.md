# QuietWalk: Physics-Informed Reinforcement Learning for Ground Reaction Force-Aware Humanoid Locomotion Under Diverse Footwear

> [!summary] Summary
> QuietWalk presents a physics-informed reinforcement learning framework for humanoid locomotion, utilizing an inverse-dynamics-constrained neural network to estimate ground reaction forces from proprioceptive signals. This approach achieved a 7.17 dB reduction in mean noise level on average across various surfaces while enabling robust and stable walking across diverse footwear and outdoor terrains.

## Problem

- Humanoid locomotion generates significant foot-ground impact transients, leading to acoustic noise, accelerated hardware wear, and potential damage to surfaces.
- Existing low-noise locomotion methods often rely on indirect kinematic proxies, which can compromise gait agility and stability, creating a trade-off with quietness.
- Reliably estimating ground reaction forces (GRFs) for direct impact control is challenging due to the fragility and noise of physical force sensors and the lack of physical consistency or real-time applicability in current estimation methods.
- Humanoid robots lack robust adaptation to diverse footwear, which significantly alters contact dynamics and hinders policy generalization across different end-effector configurations.

## Method

- **QuietWalk** develops an ==inverse-dynamics-constrained Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN)== that accurately estimates per-foot vertical ==ground reaction forces (GRFs)== using only proprioceptive signals, removing the dependency on external force sensors.
- Integrated the frozen, physics-consistent ==GRF predictor== directly into the ==reinforcement learning reward function==, enabling direct penalization of predicted impact forces for quiet locomotion.
- Designed a ==footwear asset modeling== and parameterization pipeline and employed a ==curriculum learning== strategy to train a single policy robust to diverse footwear (e.g., high heels) and surface conditions.

## Results

- The GRF predictor achieved high accuracy, with RMSEs of **14.49 N** (left foot) and **14.00 N** (right foot) and **R² values of 0.9887 and 0.9899**, respectively, on a held-out dataset.
- The Quiet RL policy reduced the mean noise level by **7.17 dBA** and peak noise level by **4.98 dBA** on average across four indoor surfaces, compared to a standard RL policy, approaching an engineering baseline controller.
- The policy demonstrated robust and stable locomotion across **four diverse footwear types** (barefoot, skate shoes, athletic sneakers, high heels) and various outdoor terrains (e.g., grass, gravel, cobblestone) without significant posture divergence or slipping.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Enforcing inverse-dynamics consistency within a neural network is critical for robust and accurate GRF prediction, reducing errors by 82-86% and significantly improving physical plausibility compared to purely supervised methods.
> - Directly penalizing predicted ground reaction forces via a physics-informed reward function effectively reduces both mean and peak noise levels (e.g., 7.17 dBA MNL reduction) without compromising gait stability or locomotion performance.
> - Footwear properties strongly interact with surface materials, causing up to a 20 dB difference in noise levels between optimal and adverse conditions, demonstrating the necessity of cross-footwear generalization for practical humanoid deployment.
