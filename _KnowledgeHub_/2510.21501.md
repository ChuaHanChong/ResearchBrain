# GranViT: A Fine-Grained Vision Model With Autoregressive Perception For MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University and Huawei Inc. developed GranViT, a fine-grained vision model for MLLMs, by constructing the Gran-29M dataset and proposing a two-stage pretraining-adaptation framework. GranViT achieves state-of-the-art performance in fine-grained recognition and OCR understanding, demonstrating enhanced regional perception and strong transferability to larger language models.

## Problem

- Existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) primarily rely on global image representations, leading to a neglect of fine-grained regional analysis.
- A significant scarcity of large-scale, high-quality datasets explicitly annotated with region-level details (e.g., bounding boxes for objects or text) for training vision encoders.
- The absence of a dedicated pre-training paradigm designed to instill fine-grained perception capabilities into vision encoders while maintaining effective alignment with LLMs.

## Method

- Introduced ==Gran-29M==, a large-scale dataset comprising **29.51 million** natural and OCR images with over **183 million** high-quality, region-level bounding box and caption annotations, formatted into question-answer pairs.
- Developed a ==two-stage pretraining-adaptation framework==: Stage 1 focuses on pretraining the vision encoder for ==Bbox2Caption regression== and localized self-distillation, while Stage 2 adapts the LLM for Caption2Bbox regression with the frozen vision encoder.
- Incorporated a ==localized self-distillation mechanism== during pre-training to impose explicit localization constraints on the vision encoder, ensuring features are aligned with ground truth localized regions.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art average top-1 scores of **80.78** on fine-grained recognition benchmarks (**2.83 points** relative improvement) and **55.97** on OCR understanding tasks (**2.64 points** relative improvement), outperforming previous leading models.
- Demonstrated strong transferability, with the GranViT vision encoder effectively integrating with larger LLMs (Qwen2.5-3B/7B) to maintain superior performance on fine-grained tasks and achieve competitive results on multimodal VQA benchmarks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that each component of the proposed framework, including the Gran-29M dataset, Bbox2Caption and Caption2Bbox tasks, and localized self-distillation, progressively contributes to significant performance gains in fine-grained and OCR capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit region-level supervision via Bbox2Caption tasks, coupled with self-distillation, is crucial for compelling the vision encoder to learn generic and precise fine-grained visual features.
> - A decoupled two-stage training approach efficiently optimizes both the vision encoder's fine-grained feature extraction and the LLM's capability to accurately localize and utilize these features.
> - The creation of a vast, high-quality dataset with multi-granularity annotations (Gran-29M) is fundamental for overcoming data scarcity and enabling the training of robust fine-grained perception in MLLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has witnessed explosive growth, integrating visual information with the powerful language understanding and reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs). The current research landscape for MLLMs primarily relies on pre-trained vision encoders (e.g., Vision Transformers or ViTs) to extract visual features, which are then projected into the LLM's semantic space. Models like CLIP, SigLIP, InternViT, AIMv2, and SAILViT exemplify this trend, focusing on aligning global image representations with textual semantics, often through contrastive learning or autoregressive modeling.

However, a critical limitation in this burgeoning field, which GranViT directly addresses, is the **over-emphasis on global image representations at the expense of fine-grained regional analysis**. Existing vision encoders, while excellent at capturing overall scene understanding, often "overlook fine-grained regional analysis" and are "limited in fine-grained perception." This deficiency stems from two main challenges: a **scarcity of high-quality, large-scale datasets with fine-grained annotations** (e.g., bounding boxes for specific objects or text regions paired with descriptions) and the **lack of a dedicated pre-training paradigm** designed to instill fine-grained perception capabilities into vision encoders while maintaining alignment with LLMs.

GranViT positions itself as a crucial advancement in this landscape by tackling these two fundamental issues. It recognizes that while global understanding is important, many real-world vision-language tasks—such as precisely answering questions about specific objects, accurately performing visual grounding (locating an object based on a description), or effectively understanding text within images (OCR)—demand a much deeper, localized visual understanding.

Compared to its predecessors and contemporaries:
- **Contrastive Learning Models (e.g., CLIP, SigLIP):** These models primarily learn strong image-text alignment at a global level, but their ability to pinpoint and describe specific regions is often an emergent property rather than a core trained capability.
- **Autoregressive Models (e.g., AIMv2, InternViT, SAILViT):** While improving alignment by decoding visual features into text, they still largely operate on overall image features and can "sacrifice the discrimination ability of visual features" for specific regions. SAILViT, for instance, focuses on multi-stage pre-training and infusing world knowledge but does not fundamentally alter the fine-grained visual feature extraction. SigLIP2 introduces autoregressive and self-distillation losses but still primarily operates on global visual representations.
- **MLLMs with Resolution Handling (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL, Seed-VL1.5, Kimi-VL):** Some newer MLLMs handle higher-resolution inputs or train vision encoders from scratch. However, simply providing high resolution doesn't guarantee fine-grained *semantic* understanding without dedicated training. GranViT's approach is complementary, ensuring that even with high-resolution inputs, the vision encoder can extract meaningful fine-grained features.
- **Post-training Strategies (e.g., SFT, RL):** While these enhance task-specific capabilities, they typically build upon the capabilities of the pre-trained vision encoder. GranViT aims to improve this foundational component itself.

By introducing a large-scale, fine-grained annotated dataset (Gran-29M) and a novel pretraining-adaptation framework that explicitly trains for region-level understanding, GranViT aims to elevate the capabilities of the vision encoder from a "global observer" to a "detailed perceiver." This shift is vital for building MLLMs that are not only intelligent in general reasoning but also precise and reliable in tasks demanding meticulous visual scrutiny, thereby pushing the boundaries of what MLLMs can achieve in complex, detail-oriented real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **integrate fine-grained localization capabilities into the vision encoder component of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs)**. The authors observe a significant gap in existing MLLMs: while they excel at general vision-language tasks by processing global image representations, they "overlook fine-grained regional analysis" and struggle with tasks demanding precise understanding of specific objects, regions, or text within an image.

This central objective is driven by two main challenges and motivations:

1. **Addressing Data Scarcity for Fine-Grained Annotations:**
 - **Motivation:** The lack of large-scale, high-quality datasets explicitly annotated with region-level details (like bounding boxes and corresponding descriptive captions) has been a bottleneck for training vision encoders capable of fine-grained perception. Existing datasets often provide image-level captions, which are insufficient for localized understanding.
 - **Objective:** To construct **Gran-29M**, a novel and expansive dataset comprising 29 million natural and OCR images with over 180 million high-quality, region-level annotations (bounding boxes and fine-grained captions). This dataset is designed to provide the necessary granular supervision for training fine-grained vision models at scale.

2. **Developing a Dedicated Fine-Grained Pre-training Paradigm:**
 - **Motivation:** Even with abundant fine-grained data, a standard pre-training approach (like global image-text contrastive learning) is insufficient to effectively train a vision encoder for fine-grained regional analysis. There's a need for a specialized framework that can both extract localized visual features and semantically align them with LLMs.
 - **Objective:** To propose a **novel pretraining-adaptation framework** along with a **self-distillation mechanism** that specifically enhances the vision encoder's ability to extract fine-grained features and allows the LLM to effectively utilize these features for localization. This framework aims to achieve:
 - **Enhanced localized visual representation:** Through "bounding-box-to-caption (Bbox2Caption) regression" in the pre-training phase, directly linking visual regions to textual descriptions.
 - **Improved vision feature utilization and localization for the LLM:** Through "caption-to-bounding-box (Caption2Bbox) regression" in the adaptation phase, enabling the LLM to accurately ground textual queries to visual regions.
 - **Strengthened regional reasoning capability:** Via a "self-distillation mechanism" that imposes explicit localization constraints on the vision encoder.
 - **Strong transferability:** To varying LLM architectures, ensuring the trained vision encoder can be broadly adopted.

Ultimately, the overarching motivation is to empower MLLMs to move beyond superficial global understanding to a more profound, detailed, and reliable comprehension of visual content, thereby enabling them to perform robustly on complex vision-language tasks such as visual question answering, reasoning, visual grounding, and particularly, OCR understanding, where precise local details are paramount.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GranViT introduces a comprehensive methodology centered around a novel large-scale dataset and a two-stage pretraining-adaptation framework designed to imbue vision encoders with fine-grained perception capabilities.

#### 3.1. Gran-29M: Fine-Grained Annotated Dataset Construction

The foundation of GranViT's fine-grained capabilities is the meticulously constructed **Gran-29M dataset**, comprising 29.51 million images with 183.55 million region-level annotations.

- **Data Sources:**
 - **Natural Images:** Aggregated from existing large-scale datasets including UMG-41M (which itself combines CC3M, IN21k, SBU, CC12M, YFCC15M, VisualGenome), LAION, and FLICKR30k. This ensures diversity in content and scale.
 - **OCR Images:** A diverse collection from 30 publicly available OCR datasets, covering plain text, charts and tables, receipt images, and rich text documents. This broad coverage is critical for robust OCR understanding.

- **Data Annotation Workflow:**
 - **Natural Images:** Utilizes existing bounding box (bbox) coordinates from UMG-41M. For LAION and FLICKR30k, bounding boxes are detected using **ViTDet** (a Vision Transformer-based object detector). For all natural images, global and local captions are regenerated or generated using **Qwen2.5-VL-7B** to ensure high quality and semantic richness.
 - **OCR Images:** Employs **PaddleOCR** for simultaneous detection of bounding boxes and extraction of textual content, focusing specifically on local text regions due to the often vague nature of global descriptions for OCR documents.

- **Filtering Criteria:** A rigorous filtering process is applied to ensure annotation quality. This includes requirements for image resolution (shorter side > 448 pixels), aspect ratio of both images and bboxes (between 1/3 and 3), minimum bbox area (100 square pixels), and a minimum of one bbox per image.

- **Data Reformatting:** All global and local captions, along with bounding box coordinates, are reformatted into a standard **question-answer (QA) pair structure**. This includes:
 - **Global Captioning:** "Describe in detail what is shown in the image..."
 - **Bbox2Caption:** "Describe the content contained within the normalized bounding box coordinates [bbox coordinates] in no more than 10 words."
 - **Caption2Bbox:** "Please provide the bounding box coordinate of the region this sentence describes: [local captions]."
 Absolute bbox coordinates are converted to relative coordinates for generalization.

#### 3.2. Fine-Grained Pretraining-Adaptation Framework with Autoregressive Perception

GranViT proposes a novel two-stage framework to train the vision encoder for fine-grained feature extraction and the LLM for effective utilization of these features.

- **Stage 1: Pretraining Vision Encoder and Projector (LLM Frozen)**
 - **Objective:** To compel the vision encoder to learn generic, fine-grained visual features.
 - **Tasks:**
 - **Global Image Captioning:** Maintained throughout to preserve global perception capabilities, similar to existing MLLMs.
 - **Bounding-box-to-Caption (Bbox2Caption) Regression:** The core task for fine-grained learning. The MLLM (with a frozen lightweight LLM, Qwen2.5-VL-1.5B) is prompted with bbox coordinates and asked to generate a localized caption. This directs supervision directly to the visual features extracted from the specified region, enhancing their fine-grained characteristics.
 - **Localized Self-Distillation:** An innovative addition to provide *explicit* localization constraints. A frozen "teacher" vision encoder (initialized from the student and updated via EMA) processes cropped image regions (`x_crop`). The student vision encoder's features (`x'`) are then subjected to an MSE loss against the teacher's features (`x_crop'`) via ROIAlign. This mechanism ensures the student's features are explicitly aligned with localized ground truth.
 - **Loss Function:** `L = L_caption + λL_distill`, where `L_caption` is the autoregressive cross-entropy loss for text generation, and `L_distill` is the MSE loss for self-distillation.

- **Stage 2: Adaptation and Transfer (Vision Encoder Frozen, Projector and LLM Tuned)**
 - **Objective:** To enhance the LLM's capability to localize fine-grained visual features and ensure the transferability of the pre-trained vision encoder to larger LLMs.
 - **Tasks:**
 - **Global Image Captioning:** Continued from Stage 1.
 - **Caption-to-Bounding-box (Caption2Bbox) Regression:** The MLLM is given a textual description and required to output the corresponding bbox coordinates. This directly trains the LLM to ground text queries in the fine-grained visual features provided by the *frozen* Stage 1 vision encoder.
 - **Rationale for Freezing Vision Encoder:** The ablation studies confirm that the vision encoder is sufficiently trained in Stage 1, and further tuning in Stage 2 yields only marginal benefits at a significantly increased computational cost. This design decision optimizes training efficiency and promotes transferability.

#### 3.3. Experimental Settings

- **Framework:** LLaVA-Next for pre-training and SFT.
- **Vision Encoder Initialization:** SigLIP2 (default).
- **LLM Initialization:** Qwen2.5-VL-1.5B (Stage 1), then adapted to Qwen2.5-3B/7B (Stage 2).
- **Image Processing:** Resized to 512x512, with an optional image tiling strategy for higher resolutions.
- **Optimizer:** AdamW, learning rate 10^-5.
- **Computational Resources:** 128 Ascend 910B NPUs.

This systematic approach, combining a bespoke dataset with a specialized, multi-stage training paradigm and explicit localization supervision, allows GranViT to simultaneously enhance the vision encoder's fine-grained feature extraction ability and the LLM's capacity to utilize these features for precise visual grounding, leading to a more robust and versatile MLLM.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

GranViT demonstrates significant advancements in fine-grained visual perception for MLLMs, validated through extensive experimentation and comparisons against leading vision encoders on a wide array of benchmarks.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Performance Across Benchmarks

- **Fine-Grained Recognition:** GranViT achieves an average top-1 score of **80.78**, significantly surpassing the second-best model (SAILViT, 77.95) by **2.83 points** on benchmarks such as RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg, BLINK*, and MMVP. This highlights its superior ability to localize and understand specific regions within images.
- **OCR Understanding:** On OCR-related tasks (OCRBench, DocVQA, ChartQA, InfoVQA, TextVQA), GranViT obtains an average top-1 score of **55.97**, outperforming the second-best model (SAILViT, 53.33) by **2.64 points**. This demonstrates its strong capability in extracting and comprehending text from diverse visual documents, a task heavily reliant on fine-grained perception.
- **Multimodal VQA:** GranViT performs comparably to state-of-the-art models like SAILViT, with only a marginal difference (0.3 points) in average scores on MMBench, MMStar, HallusionBench, GQA, and SEEDBench.
- **Multimodal Reasoning:** GranViT shows competitive performance, though with a slight acknowledged trade-off (0.4 points lower than SigLIP2) compared to models explicitly optimized for reasoning. The authors explain this by GranViT's prioritization of fine-grained tasks over extensive reasoning capability training, noting that reasoning can be further improved with specialized data.

#### 4.2. Strong Transferability to Larger LLMs

GranViT showcases remarkable transferability, a crucial attribute for practical MLLM deployment. When adapted to larger LLMs (Qwen2.5-3B and Qwen2.5-7B), GranViT consistently delivers outstanding performance:
- It maintains its lead on fine-grained and OCR tasks.
- It achieves comparable or even state-of-the-art results on some VQA tasks (e.g., HallusionBench and SEEDBench) even though its initial pre-training in Stage 1 used a lightweight LLM. This confirms that GranViT's vision encoder learns robust, generalizable fine-grained features that can be effectively leveraged by more powerful LLMs.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies Validating Methodological Components

- **Effectiveness of Training Paradigm:** The ablation studies clearly demonstrate the incremental performance gains attributed to each component of the proposed framework:
 - **Stage 1 Pretraining (Bbox2Caption):** Substantial improvements (2.2 points in fine-grained, 1.2 points in OCR) in initial capabilities.
 - **Self-Distillation:** Further enhances fine-grained and OCR evaluations.
 - **Stage 2 Adaptation (Caption2Bbox):** Yields additional gains (1.0 points in fine-grained, 0.7 points in OCR) by improving the LLM's localization capacity.
 The analysis in Fig. 4(a) explicitly shows that Bbox2Caption excels in Stage 1 (vision encoder focus), while Caption2Bbox significantly improves in Stage 2 (LLM focus), validating the two-stage separation.
- **Different Vision Encoder Initializations:** GranViT's pre-training paradigm consistently improves performance regardless of the initial vision encoder model (InternViT, AIMv2, SAILViT). This indicates the robustness and general applicability of the proposed fine-grained training approach.
- **Frozen Vision Encoder in Stage 2:** The decision to freeze the vision encoder in Stage 2 is validated, as tuning it further yields only marginal performance gains at a much higher computational cost.
- **Impact of Self-Distillation Coefficients (Appendix):** Ablation on `λ` and `α` parameters for self-distillation confirms that `λ=1` and `α=0.9` are optimal for fine-grained performance, showing gradual improvements as `λ` increases and `α` decreases.

#### 4.4. Visualization of Attention Maps

Figure 1(b) provides compelling visual evidence of GranViT's superior fine-grained feature extraction. While other models like AIMv2 and SAILViT exhibit global attention, and SigLIP2 shows some local focus but with redundant features, GranViT effectively concentrates its attention on relevant local regions, demonstrating its ability to "simultaneously consider local regions and exclude interference from redundant features."

#### 4.5. Scaling Laws and Continuous Pretraining (Appendix)

- **Scaling Laws:** The appendix shows that as the data scale for Bbox2Caption and Caption2Bbox tasks increases, both fine-grained and OCR performance improve, indicating the scalability of the GranViT approach.
- **Compatibility with Continuous Pretraining:** GranViT's paradigm is compatible with subsequent continuous pretraining using SFT data (following a Stage 3 approach), leading to further performance gains over a baseline SigLIP2. This highlights its potential for building even more specialized and capable MLLMs.

In summary, GranViT presents compelling evidence of achieving state-of-the-art fine-grained perception and OCR understanding in MLLMs, driven by its novel dataset and specialized training framework. Its strong transferability and validated design choices underscore its significant contribution to the field.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of GranViT marks a significant advancement in the field of Multimodal Large Language Models, with profound implications for both research and practical applications. Its core contribution lies in effectively addressing the long-standing limitation of existing MLLMs: the lack of robust fine-grained visual perception.

#### 5.1. Bridging the Fine-Grained Perception Gap

GranViT fundamentally shifts the paradigm of vision encoding for MLLMs. By moving beyond global image representations to truly fine-grained regional analysis, it tackles a critical bottleneck that has hindered MLLMs from achieving human-level understanding in detail-intensive tasks. This is not just an incremental improvement but a foundational enhancement that makes MLLMs more reliable and accurate when precise visual information is required.

#### 5.2. Enabling Advanced Real-World Applications

The enhanced fine-grained capabilities of GranViT open doors for a multitude of advanced applications:

- **Superior OCR and Document Understanding:** GranViT's state-of-the-art performance in OCR understanding means MLLMs can now parse and reason over complex documents, receipts, charts, and infographics with unprecedented accuracy. This has immense value for business process automation, data extraction, and intelligent information retrieval from unstructured documents.
- **Precise Visual Grounding and Object Interaction:** The ability to accurately locate specific objects or regions based on textual descriptions is crucial for interactive AI systems, robotics, and augmented reality. GranViT can power more intuitive visual search, instruction following, and human-AI collaboration where spatial precision is paramount.
- **Reduced Hallucinations and Improved Trustworthiness:** A common challenge for MLLMs is "hallucination," where models generate factually incorrect but plausible text. By providing a richer and more precise understanding of visual details, GranViT can significantly mitigate these issues, leading to more trustworthy and grounded responses.
- **Enhanced Visual Question Answering and Reasoning:** For complex VQA and reasoning tasks that depend on understanding intricate visual relationships, counting objects, or identifying specific attributes, GranViT provides the necessary granular visual input, leading to more accurate and nuanced answers. This can benefit applications in education, scientific research, and expert systems.
- **Medical Imaging and Scientific Analysis:** In domains like medical imaging or scientific data visualization, where minute details are critical for diagnosis or analysis, GranViT could potentially contribute to more accurate interpretation and automated assistance.

#### 5.3. Contribution to Open Science and Research

The creation and release (or intention to release, given the arXiv nature) of **Gran-29M** is a significant contribution to the research community. This large-scale, high-quality dataset with multi-granularity annotations for both natural and OCR images provides an invaluable resource for future research in fine-grained multimodal learning. It sets a new standard for data curation in this domain and will undoubtedly accelerate further advancements.

#### 5.4. Methodological Innovation

The proposed two-stage pretraining-adaptation framework, coupled with the novel Bbox2Caption and Caption2Bbox tasks, and the localized self-distillation mechanism, represents a robust and effective methodology for integrating fine-grained perception. This architectural and training innovation provides a blueprint for future MLLM development, particularly for scenarios where detailed visual scrutiny is essential. The demonstrated transferability to various LLM sizes also ensures broad applicability.

#### 5.5. Industrial Relevance and Scalability

The collaboration with Huawei Inc. underscores the industrial relevance and scalability of GranViT. Large-scale datasets and computational resources are often prerequisites for such foundational model development. GranViT's design, which includes efficient training paradigms (e.g., freezing the vision encoder in Stage 2) and demonstrated performance scaling, suggests its readiness for deployment in real-world products and services.

In conclusion, GranViT provides a critical piece of the puzzle for building truly intelligent MLLMs. By empowering these models with fine-grained visual perception, it paves the way for a new generation of AI systems that are not only powerful in language and general vision but also precise, reliable, and capable of understanding the world's visual intricacies at a deeper, more actionable level.
