# Temporal-Distance JEPA: Plan-Aware Representation Learning for Latent World Model Predictive Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Temporal-Distance-JEPA introduces a method to derive a directed temporal progress cost from reward-free offline demonstrations for latent world model predictive control. It consistently matches or exceeds baseline planning performance on various robotic tasks, achieving 100% success on Two-Room and 82.2% on OGBench-Cube.

## Problem

- Latent world models, particularly JEPA-style, typically rely on short-horizon latent prediction, leading to a 'train-plan gap' when applied to multi-step model predictive control (MPC).
- Offline demonstration logs often lack dense reward signals, making it difficult for standard predictive dynamics to provide a robust progress signal for planning.
- Using latent Euclidean distance as a planning cost, a byproduct of representation learning, is often misaligned with the true 'steps-to-goal' or directed reachability needed for effective MPC.

## Method

- **Temporal-Distance-JEPA** (**TD-JEPA**) extends the ==LeWM backbone== by incorporating a directed ==Metric-Residual Network (MRN) head== to parameterize a ==temporal cost function==, d_ψ(z_s, z_g).
- The directed cost is calibrated using ==reward-free temporal labels== (trajectory step differences) from offline demonstrations, coupled with a ==hinge loss for cross-trajectory negative pairs== to prevent collapsed costs.
- A ==horizon-aligned rollout consistency loss== is added to minimize compounding errors in multi-step predictions, and the learned temporal cost is either directly deployed for planning or used to shape representations for geometric planning, depending on the task.

## Results

- Temporal-Distance-JEPA matched or surpassed LeWM and RC-aux baselines across all tested environments, achieving **100.0%** success on Two-Room (using d_ψ) and **82.2%** on OGBench-Cube (using latent l2 on temporally-shaped representations).
- Ablation studies confirmed that the directed temporal cost head, cross-trajectory negative sampling, and horizon-aligned rollout consistency each significantly contribute to the overall performance gains.
- Analysis revealed that deploying the pure directed temporal cost (d_ψ) was most effective for topological tasks, while latent Euclidean distance on the temporally-trained checkpoint consistently outperformed d_ψ for contact-rich manipulation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly mining and training a directed temporal cost from reward-free offline demonstrations can effectively bridge the 'train–plan gap' in JEPA world models for latent MPC.
> - Temporal supervision offers a dual benefit: it can act as a direct planning cost for topological tasks (e.g., navigation) and as a representation shaping signal that enhances geometric planning for contact-rich tasks (e.g., manipulation).
> - The optimal plan-time cost is task-dependent; directed temporal costs are more effective for tasks requiring clear progression, while latent Euclidean distance on temporally-shaped representations is superior for fine-grained contact interactions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the field of latent world models, specifically focusing on their application to model predictive control (MPC) from offline demonstration logs. World models aim to develop an agent's ability to anticipate future states of an environment without direct interaction, by learning the environment's underlying dynamics. In the context of planning, a world model encodes observations, simulates future states resulting from candidate action sequences, and subsequently ranks these imagined futures to identify the optimal path toward a goal.

A significant challenge arises when the only available data consists of offline demonstration trajectories, which provide observation-action sequences but lack dense reward signals. In this scenario, standard predictive dynamics alone do not inherently provide a robust progress signal that can be reliably used by a planner.

Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) have emerged as a foundational approach for this setting. Unlike models that reconstruct pixels, JEPAs learn an encoder to map observations into a latent representation space and a predictor to forecast future embeddings based on context and actions. This framework posits that useful world knowledge is embedded within the predictive structure of these representations, allowing the model to internalize dynamics without requiring a generative decoder. LeWM (Latent World Model) is an instantiation of this JEPA recipe for latent control, employing an action-conditioned encoder-predictor for next-latent prediction, regularizing representations with SIGReg to prevent collapse, and utilizing CEM (Cross-Entropy Method) for action sequence search at test time. Typically, LeWM and similar JEPA planners infer progress toward a goal using the Euclidean distance between latent embeddings.

However, a structural discrepancy exists between the learning objective of JEPA-style models and the requirements of latent MPC. JEPA training primarily optimizes short-horizon latent prediction. Conversely, MPC necessitates a multi-step ranking of potential futures based on their estimated progress toward a goal. Relying on latent Euclidean distance as a planning cost, while convenient, is a byproduct of representation learning rather than an explicitly trained progress signal derived from the demonstration logs. This geometric measure may correlate with progress in some scenarios, but it is not specifically designed to reflect "steps-to-goal" or directed reachability, which are critical for effective planning.

Prior efforts to address this train-plan mismatch in planning-aware JEPA variants have included value shaping using offline reinforcement learning (RL), temporal straightening of latent paths, horizon-conditioned reachability auxiliaries, and the creation of progression subspaces. These approaches either introduce auxiliary value estimators beyond the raw demonstration logs, modify latent geometry without defining an explicit plan cost, or treat temporal structure as a supplementary signal rather than a primary ranking mechanism for MPC. This background highlights a persistent gap in how progress is learned and subsequently utilized for multi-step planning within reward-free, offline world models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address the structural gap between the representation learning of JEPA world models and the requirements of latent model predictive control (MPC). Specifically, the authors aim to investigate how reward-free demonstration trajectories can be leveraged to extract temporal progress structure that improves planning performance for JEPA world-model planners. A secondary objective is to determine when this discovered temporal structure should be directly deployed as the planning cost, as opposed to being used to shape the latent representations for conventional geometric planning.

The motivation stems from the observation that while JEPA-style models like LeWM excel at learning predictive dynamics in a latent space, their inherent training objective focuses on short-horizon prediction. When applied to MPC, these models typically default to using Euclidean distance in the latent space as a proxy for goal progress. The authors contend that this inherited geometric ranking is often misaligned with the true "steps-to-goal" or directed reachability required for effective multi-step planning. As illustrated by the "temporal gap" analysis on Push-T, LeWM's latent Euclidean distance exhibits a Spearman correlation of 0.65 with true temporal separation, whereas a cost directly mined from the logs can achieve a correlation of 0.95. This discrepancy highlights the potential for improved planning by explicitly incorporating temporal progression.

The research is motivated by the hypothesis that demonstration logs, even without explicit reward signals, contain implicit information about temporal order and task progression. By mining this information, it should be possible to create a planning cost that more accurately reflects the directed nature of goal-reaching. The work seeks to move beyond relying solely on geometric properties that emerge as a byproduct of self-supervised representation learning and instead explicitly train a goal-conditioned progress cost. This would allow for a more direct alignment between the training signal and the plan-time objective, thereby narrowing the "train–plan gap."

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Temporal-Distance-JEPA, builds upon the existing LeWM encoder-predictor backbone and SIGReg regularization. It introduces mechanisms to mine a directed temporal cost from offline demonstration logs and integrates this cost into the planning process. The methodology consists of several key components:

**LeWM Backbone Prediction Loss:**
Temporal-Distance-JEPA retains the core LeWM backbone, which includes an encoder `Enc_θ` that maps observations `o_t` to latent states `z_t`, and an action-conditioned predictor `Pred_φ` that forecasts the next latent state `ẑ_{t+1}` from `z_t` and action `a_t`. The standard next-latent prediction loss `L_pred = ||ẑ_{t+1} - sg(Enc_θ(o_{t+1}))||^2` (where `sg` denotes stop-gradient) is maintained. SIGReg regularization is also preserved to prevent representation collapse.

**Two Roles of Temporal Supervision: Cost Class vs. Cost Calibration:**
The method distinguishes between the *form* of the cost and its *calibration*.
1. **Directed Cost Head (Geometry Class):** To capture the asymmetric nature of goal-reaching (i.e., the cost from A to B may differ from B to A), Temporal-Distance-JEPA employs a metric-residual network (MRN) parameterization for the directed cost `d_ψ(z_s, z_g)`. This head maps each latent `z` to symmetric `φ_sym(z)` and asymmetric `φ_asym(z)` features. The cost is defined as:
 `d_ψ(z_s, z_g) = ||φ_sym(z_s) - φ_sym(z_g)||^2 + max_k ReLU(φ_asym,k(z_s) - φ_asym,k(z_g))`
 This formulation ensures the cost is non-negative and inherently supports directionality, unlike symmetric Euclidean distance.
2. **Temporal-Distance Supervision (Calibration Signal):** The numeric values for this directed cost are calibrated using reward-free temporal labels extracted from offline demonstrations. For any pair of observations `o_i` and `o_j` along the same trajectory where `i < j`, the temporal gap `τ(i, j) = j - i` serves as a proxy for "steps-to-subgoal." The `L_td` loss minimizes:
 `L_td = E_{i<j} [ρ(d_ψ(z_i, z_j) - τ(i, j))] + E_{i, g'~N} [ReLU(m - d_ψ(z_i, z_{g'}))]`
 Here, `ρ` is the Smooth L1 loss, `N` represents cross-trajectory negative pairs (where `z_{g'}` is a latent from a different trajectory at the same time index), and `m = η(T-1)` is a margin. The first term encourages `d_ψ` to regress to the temporal gap for positive pairs, while the second term uses a hinge loss to penalize spuriously low costs for off-trajectory negative pairs.

**Train–Plan Coupling:**
A key aspect is that `d_ψ` is directly optimized during training and then used as the default planning cost in MPC. The optimal action sequence `a*_{1:H}` is chosen by minimizing `d_ψ(ẑ_H(a_{1:H}), z_g)`, where `ẑ_H` is the H-step open-loop rollout of the learned predictor. This direct coupling ensures that the learned cost is directly relevant to the planner's objective.

**Latent MPC with CEM and iCEM:**
At test time, the system uses Model Predictive Control (MPC) with either the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) or improved CEM (iCEM) to find action sequences. Both algorithms involve sampling candidate action sequences, performing H-step latent rollouts using the `Pred_φ` predictor, and then scoring these rollouts with the trained energy `d_ψ(ẑ_H, z_g)` (or latent `l2` distance in specific cases). CEM iteratively refines a distribution over action sequences by selecting elites, while iCEM incorporates enhancements like temporally colored noise, elite reuse, and momentum-smoothed updates for improved sample efficiency.

**Horizon-Aligned Rollout Consistency:**
To further align the model with the multi-step planning horizon (`H`), a rollout consistency loss `L_roll` is added:
`L_roll = (1/H) * Σ_{h=1}^H ||ẑ_{t+h} - sg(z_{t+h})||^2`
This teacher-forcing loss encourages the predictor to accurately forecast multiple steps into the future, mitigating compounding errors in the same open-loop rollout operator used by the planner.

**Full Objective:**
The complete objective function for Temporal-Distance-JEPA is a weighted sum of these components:
`L = L_pred + λ_roll L_roll + λ_td L_td + λ_sig SIGReg(z)`
Default weights are `λ_roll=0.5`, `λ_td=1.0`, `λ_sig=0.09`.

**Cost Selection at Plan Time:**
A critical design choice in Temporal-Distance-JEPA is the context-dependent selection of the plan-time cost.
- For tasks where progress is primarily topological (e.g., navigation, reaching), the directed temporal cost `d_ψ` is deployed as the planning objective.
- For tasks dominated by contact geometry (e.g., manipulation), the same temporally trained checkpoint is planned using latent Euclidean distance (`l2`). This indicates that temporal supervision in these cases acts as a representation shaping signal rather than a direct cost.

This co-design of cost form and deployment, based on task structure, is integral to the methodology.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of Temporal-Distance-JEPA was conducted on four environments: Two-Room (navigation), Reacher (reaching), Push-T (contact-rich manipulation), and OGBench-Cube (contact-rich manipulation). The findings address three main questions: planning performance, contributions of individual components, and appropriate deployment of the mined temporal cost.

#### 4.1. Planning Performance

Under a locked evaluation protocol (fixed episode manifests and planner budgets), Temporal-Distance-JEPA consistently matched or exceeded the performance of LeWM and the concurrent RC-aux baseline across all tested environments:
- **Two-Room:** Temporal-Distance-JEPA achieved 100.0% success (±0.0), surpassing LeWM's 97.4% (±1.3) and RC-aux's 98.6% (±1.0). For this environment, Temporal-Distance-JEPA deployed its mined `d_ψ` cost.
- **Reacher:** Temporal-Distance-JEPA achieved 97.0% success (±2.4), slightly outperforming LeWM's 96.0% (±1.9) and RC-aux's 96.8% (±1.4). `d_ψ` was deployed here as well.
- **Push-T:** Temporal-Distance-JEPA achieved 86.0% success (±4.2), exceeding LeWM's 83.6% (±3.2) and RC-aux's 81.4% (±1.9). For Push-T, planning was conducted using latent `l2` distance on the temporally trained checkpoint.
- **OGBench-Cube:** Temporal-Distance-JEPA achieved 82.2% success (±2.9), showing a substantial improvement of 14.2 percentage points over LeWM's 68.0% (±2.8) and slightly surpassing RC-aux's 81.6% (±2.8). `l2` distance was also used for planning in this environment.

These results indicate that temporal supervision is beneficial in both its roles: as a direct planning cost for topological tasks and as a representation shaping signal for geometric planning in contact-rich tasks. When compared to a broader range of published baselines (though not under matched protocols), Temporal-Distance-JEPA demonstrated competitive or superior performance on several tasks.

#### 4.2. Contribution of Cost Components (Ablations)

Ablation studies on Push-T (using three independent seed runs) demonstrated the contribution of each proposed component:
- **Symmetric Euclidean TD head:** Replacing the directed MRN head with a symmetric Euclidean distance head, while retaining temporal labels, resulted in the largest drop in success across all planning settings. This suggests that the directed cost form is critical, not just the temporal calibration.
- **Without cross-trajectory hinge:** Removing the negative sampling (cross-trajectory hinge) led to a notable decrease in performance, indicating its role in discouraging spuriously low off-trajectory costs.
- **Without rollout consistency:** Removing the horizon-matched `L_roll` loss also reduced performance, highlighting the importance of explicitly minimizing compounding errors over the planner's rollout horizon.
Each ablation negatively impacted the planner's performance, confirming that the directed head, cross-trajectory negatives, and rollout consistency collectively contribute to the observed gains.

#### 4.3. Task-Dependent Deployment of Mined Temporal Cost

An analysis varying the plan-time objective (pure `d_ψ`, pure `l2`, or a blend `α=0.10`) while keeping the trained checkpoint and other parameters fixed revealed two distinct regimes:
- **Navigation and Reaching (Topology-dominated):** For Two-Room and Reacher, deploying pure `d_ψ` matched or exceeded `l2` planning. On Two-Room, `d_ψ` achieved 100% success compared to `l2`'s 99.0%. On Reacher, `d_ψ` yielded 97.0% versus `l2`'s 95.0%. This indicates that for tasks where directed progression is key, the explicit temporal cost is more effective.
- **Contact-Rich Manipulation:** For Push-T and OGBench-Cube, `l2` planning consistently outperformed pure `d_ψ`. On Push-T, `l2` achieved 86.0% success compared to `d_ψ`'s 69.0%. On OGB-Cube, `l2` scored 82.2% versus `d_ψ`'s 77.0%. A small blend with `l2` (α=0.10) partially closed this gap but did not eliminate it. This suggests that while temporal training improves the latent space, fine-grained contact interactions still benefit more from the local geometric properties preserved by `l2` distance.

#### 4.4. Analysis of Pure Temporal Cost Limitations on Push-T

Further investigation into Push-T failures with pure `d_ψ` revealed that most failures occurred post-contact (14.3 failures post-contact vs. 1.3 pre-contact out of 50 episodes), whereas `l2` planning significantly reduced post-contact failures (6.0 post-contact failures, 0.0 pre-contact). This suggests that `d_ψ`, despite its strong correlation with temporal separation (Spearman `ρ = 0.914` for `d_ψ` vs. `0.785` for `l2`), struggles with the fine-grained pose and angle control required after contact, where local geometric fidelity (captured by `l2`) is more crucial than a global progress ranking. Proximity-gated strategies that switch costs near contact did not fully recover the performance of pure `l2` planning, indicating that geometric scoring is important throughout the CEM search process for manipulation tasks.

#### 4.5. Robustness and Calibration

The learned `d_ψ` cost was shown to rise monotonically with demonstrated step separation on held-out pairs, confirming that the head learns the intended temporal ordering. The method's robustness was also tested against different planner aggregation strategies on Reacher, showing that the choice of aggregating terminal and trajectory-mean costs affects absolute success rates but Temporal-Distance-JEPA remains competitive under the primary protocol.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Temporal-Distance-JEPA introduces a method for enhancing latent world model predictive control by explicitly leveraging temporal ordering information from reward-free offline demonstration logs. The research contributes to the field by providing a clear diagnosis of the "train–plan gap" in existing JEPA world models, where predictive dynamics learning does not automatically yield an optimal planning cost.

The key significance lies in its dual approach to utilizing temporal supervision. First, by mining a directed temporal cost (`d_ψ`) that more accurately reflects goal progress and reachability, the method provides a planning objective that is better aligned with the nature of goal-conditioned tasks, particularly those dominated by topological considerations like navigation and reaching. This explicit training of a plan-time cost, rather than relying on emergent latent geometry, represents a methodological advance.

Second, the study demonstrates that even when the directed temporal cost is not directly deployed for planning (as in contact-rich manipulation tasks), the temporal supervision significantly shapes the latent representation. This improved representation, when combined with conventional geometric planning (`l2` distance), leads to enhanced performance. This finding highlights that temporal training is not merely about finding a new cost function but also about enriching the latent space with meaningful progress information.

The co-design principle, where the choice of plan-time cost (`d_ψ` vs. `l2`) is determined by the task structure (topology-dominated vs. contact-rich), is a practical and impactful insight for future research and application. It suggests that a one-size-fits-all planning cost may not be optimal and encourages careful consideration of the task's inherent challenges.

The potential impact of Temporal-Distance-JEPA includes:
- **Improved Efficiency in Offline RL:** By deriving effective planning costs from reward-free demonstrations, the method could reduce the reliance on costly reward engineering or online interaction in various domains.
- **Enhanced World Model Capabilities:** The approach suggests a pathway for world models to learn not just *what* happens next, but also *how* to measure progress towards a goal, making them more directly "plan-aware."
- **Foundational for Autonomous Systems:** Better latent world models that can reason about multi-step goal progress are crucial for developing more capable autonomous agents in complex, unstructured environments.
- **Guidance for Future Research:** The detailed ablations and analysis of `d_ψ`'s limitations in contact-rich scenarios provide valuable insights, suggesting that combining progress signals with geometric fidelity remains an important area for continued research in robotics and control.

Overall, Temporal-Distance-JEPA represents a step towards bridging the gap between representation learning and practical planning in latent world models, particularly under the constraints of offline, reward-free data.
