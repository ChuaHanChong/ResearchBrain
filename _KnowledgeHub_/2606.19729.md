# VOiLA: Vectorized Online Planning with Learned Diffusion Model for POMDP Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> VOiLA introduces a framework for online planning under uncertainty, integrating learned task-agnostic POMDP models with a vectorized online planner. The approach achieves significant data efficiency and superior generalization to unseen environments, successfully transferring models trained purely in simulation to a physical quadruped robot for real-world tasks.

## Problem

- Developing accurate, hand-crafted POMDP models for complex, stochastic real-world robotic systems is often impractical.
- Many reinforcement learning (RL) methods suffer from data inefficiency and limited generalization to novel environments or tasks.
- Expressive generative models like diffusion models are too computationally intensive for real-time online planning, and traditional online POMDP solvers often lack the scalability to leverage modern parallel computing architectures for real-time performance in complex tasks.

## Method

- **VOiLA** learns task-agnostic POMDP transition, observation, and likelihood models from offline simulated data using conditional diffusion models.
- Computational efficiency is addressed by distilling the trained diffusion models into compact, feedforward generators for fast sampling during online planning.
- These learned and distilled models are integrated into a massively parallel, GPU-accelerated online POMDP planner (VOPP), enhanced with vectorized progressive widening for continuous observation spaces.

## Results

- VOiLA demonstrated superior data efficiency (50K transitions vs. 2.6M-32M for baselines on FloorPositioning, 750K for Recurrent SAC on LeggedNavigation) and enhanced generalization, maintaining **72-80%** success on unseen LeggedNavigation environments vs. Recurrent SAC's drop to **10-12%**.
- Distilled transition and observation samplers achieved approximately **5.9** × 10^4 samples per second, providing up to 283 times higher throughput than original diffusion models.
- VOiLA enabled successful sim-to-real transfer, with a physical quadruped successfully completing a target-finding task in 10 out of 10 real-world runs using simulation-trained models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning reusable, task-agnostic POMDP models from data improves data efficiency and generalization over task-specific policies, and combining these learned models with a principled, massively parallel online planner enables robust decision-making in high-dimensional, partially observable environments.
> - Distilling expressive diffusion models into compact generators is essential for achieving the computational throughput required for real-time online planning.
> - POMDP models learned entirely from simulated data can be sufficiently robust for direct sim-to-real transfer to physical robots without additional real-world training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous robots operating in real-world environments frequently encounter uncertainty stemming from imprecise actuators, noisy sensors, perception errors, and incomplete knowledge. The Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) framework offers a principled approach for reasoning and planning under such conditions. However, the application of POMDPs in practice is constrained by several factors.

Historically, POMDP solutions have evolved along two primary directions. The first involves approximate POMDP solvers, often executed online to avoid extensive offline computation. These methods, including POMCP, DESPOT, ABT, and more recently, the Vectorized Online POMDP Planner (VOPP), require pre-existing, accurate POMDP models (transition, observation, and reward functions). Obtaining faithful POMDP models for complex real-world systems can be challenging.

The second direction encompasses reinforcement learning (RL) based approaches, such as Recurrent model-free Reinforcement Learning (e.g., Recurrent SAC) and Model-based Reinforcement Learning (e.g., MuZero, Dreamer, Visual Tree Search). These methods often circumvent the need for explicit, hand-crafted models by learning policies or dynamics directly from data. However, they typically demand substantial amounts of data, considerable training time, and often result in policies or models that are highly task-specific, limiting their generalization to new environments or tasks. Some integrated planning-and-learning methods attempt to combine search trees with learned components or value functions, but these approaches have historically been single-threaded and not designed to leverage modern parallel computing architectures.

VOiLA positions itself within this landscape by integrating learning and planning to address the limitations of both pure planning and pure learning paradigms. It aims to reduce the reliance on hand-crafted models while simultaneously improving data efficiency and generalization compared to many RL methods. Specifically, VOiLA learns *task-agnostic* POMDP models (transition, observation, and likelihood functions) using modern deep learning techniques (conditional diffusion models and distillation) and integrates these models with a *massively parallel online POMDP planner (VOPP)* designed for GPU acceleration. This approach seeks to provide a scalable, data-efficient, and generalizable framework for online POMDP planning that can operate in complex, partially observable environments and facilitate sim-to-real transfer.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop VOiLA, a framework for online planning under uncertainty that learns task-agnostic POMDP models and integrates them with a vectorized online POMDP planner. This addresses several key challenges and motivations in autonomous robotics:

- **Difficulty of Obtaining Faithful POMDP Models:** In complex real-world scenarios, it is often impractical or impossible to hand-craft accurate transition and observation models that capture all the stochasticity and high-dimensional aspects of robot dynamics and sensory inputs. VOiLA aims to automate the acquisition of these models through learning from data.

- **Data Inefficiency and Limited Generalization of Reinforcement Learning:** Many reinforcement learning methods, particularly model-free approaches, require vast amounts of interaction data and tend to learn task-specific policies that struggle to generalize to novel environment configurations or tasks. VOiLA seeks to improve data efficiency by learning reusable models and enhance generalization by decoupling model learning from task-specific policy optimization.

- **Computational Cost of Expressive Generative Models:** While deep generative models, such as diffusion models, are capable of capturing complex, multimodal distributions inherent in robot dynamics and high-dimensional observations (e.g., images), their iterative sampling processes are computationally intensive. This makes their direct use prohibitive for online planning, which requires thousands of fast forward simulations. A motivation is to reduce this computational burden while retaining expressiveness.

- **Scalability of Online POMDP Planning:** Traditional online POMDP solvers were often designed for serial CPU computation. To effectively plan in real-time for complex robotic tasks, a planner must exploit modern hardware capabilities, especially GPU parallelization. VOiLA is motivated to integrate learned models with a planner (VOPP) specifically designed for massive vectorization and GPU acceleration.

- **Enabling Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Collecting large amounts of training data in the real world is costly and time-consuming. A significant motivation is to demonstrate that POMDP models learned purely from simulated data can be directly transferred and effectively used for planning on physical robots without requiring additional real-world training, thereby facilitating the sim-to-real paradigm.

In summary, VOiLA is motivated by the need for a unified framework that combines the representational power of learned generative models with the robustness and decision-making capabilities of principled online POMDP planning, all while striving for data efficiency, generalization, computational scalability, and direct transferability to real-world robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VOiLA is structured as a two-component framework: an offline POMDP model learning stage and an online belief-space planning stage that utilizes these learned models.

#### 3.1. Offline Data Generation

The first step involves generating a dataset of interaction trajectories. This is done entirely offline using high-fidelity robot simulators (e.g., Isaac Sim). A task-independent data collection policy, specifically a uniform random policy, is executed in the simulated environments. This ensures the collected data captures general environment dynamics and observation structures rather than task-specific behaviors. The dataset `D` comprises sequences of states, actions, and observations `{(s, a, o)}`.

#### 3.2. POMDP Model Learning Approach

VOiLA learns three distinct POMDP model components from the offline dataset:

- **Transition Sampler (f_T):** This model learns to approximate the conditional probability distribution `P(s'|s, a)`, generating a next state `s'` given a current state `s` and action `a`. The learning process is two-staged:
 1. **Diffusion Model Training:** An expressive conditional denoising diffusion model is initially trained on `(s, a, s')` tuples. This model uses a v-prediction objective to model the complex, potentially multimodal, stochastic dynamics by learning to reverse a diffusion process that adds noise to the next state.
 2. **Model Distillation:** Because diffusion models are computationally expensive for iterative sampling, the trained diffusion model is distilled into a compact feedforward generator `f_T(s, a, ω_T)`. This generator directly maps `(s, a)` and a latent noise variable `ω_T` to a next-state sample `s'`. The distillation process minimizes an L2 loss between the samples generated by the full ODE-based diffusion sampler and the compact `f_T`. This significantly reduces sampling cost.

- **Observation Sampler (f_Z):** Analogous to the transition sampler, `f_Z` learns to approximate `P(o|s', a)`, sampling an observation `o` given `s'` and `a`.
 - For low-dimensional observations, the same two-stage diffusion and distillation procedure (training a conditional diffusion model, then distilling it into a feedforward `f_Z(s', a, ω_Z)`) is applied directly in the observation space.
 - For high-dimensional observations (e.g., images), a conditional observation autoencoder (encoder `E` and decoder `D`) is first trained to map raw observations into a lower-dimensional latent observation space `Z`. The autoencoder is trained using a reconstruction objective. Once trained, the encoder `E` is used to transform observations into their latent representations. The diffusion model and subsequent distillation for `f_Z` are then performed within this latent space.

- **Likelihood Model (ℓ):** This model approximates the unnormalized observation density `Z(s', a, o)`. Learning normalized densities is challenging, but particle-based belief updates only require relative particle weights. Therefore, `ℓ` is trained contrastively using an InfoNCE-style objective. This objective encourages the model to assign higher output values to matching `(s', a, o)` tuples from the dataset compared to mismatched (negative) tuples within a mini-batch. The exponentiated outputs `exp(ℓ(s', a, o)/λ)` are then used as relative particle weights during belief updates, with `λ` being a temperature parameter.

#### 3.3. Online Belief-Space Planning with VOiLA

The learned models are integrated into a modified version of the Vectorized Online POMDP Planner (VOPP).
- **Vectorized Forward Search:** VOPP’s original hand-crafted generative model for forward search is replaced by the learned transition sampler `f_T` and observation sampler `f_Z`. These samplers efficiently generate successor states and corresponding observations for thousands of parallel simulations on the GPU.
- **Progressive Widening for Continuous Observations:** VOPP is extended with a fully vectorized implementation of Progressive Widening (PW) to handle continuous observation spaces. PW dynamically limits the number of observation branches in the belief tree. When a widening criterion is met, a new observation branch is created using a newly sampled observation; otherwise, an existing branch is reused.
- **Particle-based Belief Updates:** Beliefs are represented as sets of weighted particles. After executing an action and perceiving an observation in the environment, the belief is updated using a Sequential Importance Resampling (SIR) particle filter. The learned transition sampler `f_T` is used to propose new particles, and the learned likelihood model `ℓ` is used to compute their importance weights based on the actual observed data before resampling. All these operations are designed for GPU-accelerated tensor computations to maintain VOPP’s parallel efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of VOiLA was conducted on three distinct problems: FloorPositioning (a simple POMDP benchmark), LeggedNavigation (quadruped locomotion in uncertain environments with image observations), and TargetFinding (a sim-to-real object search task for a physical quadruped). The performance was compared against Recurrent Soft Actor Critic (Recurrent SAC) and Visual Tree Search (VTS).

- **Data Efficiency:** VOiLA demonstrated significant data efficiency compared to baselines.
 - For FloorPositioning, VOiLA used 50K transitions, while Recurrent SAC required 2.6M and VTS required 32M transitions.
 - For LeggedNavigation, VOiLA used 50K transitions, whereas Recurrent SAC used 750K transitions. This suggests that learning reusable task-agnostic models is more data-efficient than learning task-specific policies or complex, coupled models.

- **Performance on Training Environments:**
 - On FloorPositioning, VOiLA achieved a 100% success rate, comparable to Recurrent SAC (which had a slightly higher average discounted reward) and superior to VTS (91% success).
 - On LeggedNavigation (environments 1 and 2, which were used for training), VOiLA achieved success rates of 92.0% for both, which was comparable to Recurrent SAC (92.0% and 80.0%, respectively) and substantially better than VTS (32.5% and 36.0%).

- **Generalization to Unseen Environments:** A key finding was VOiLA's superior generalization capability.
 - In LeggedNavigation, training was conducted only on environments 1 and 2. When tested on unseen environments 3, 4, and 5, Recurrent SAC's success rate dropped significantly (e.g., to 12% in env 3 and 10% in env 5).
 - In contrast, VOiLA maintained success rates between 72% and 80% across all unseen environments, consistently achieving the highest average discounted return. This indicates that VOiLA's learned transition, observation, and likelihood models capture reusable aspects of environment dynamics, whereas Recurrent SAC's policy exhibited overfitting to the training environments.

- **Computational Efficiency of Learned Models:** The distillation strategy proved effective in reducing the computational cost of sampling from generative models. For LeggedNavigation, the distilled transition and observation samplers achieved approximately 5.9 × 10^4 samples per second, which is up to 283 times higher throughput compared to the original diffusion models (2.1 × 10^2 and 7.2 × 10^2 samples per second, respectively). This efficiency is critical for online planning.

- **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** VOiLA demonstrated successful sim-to-real transfer on the TargetFinding problem. Models trained exclusively in simulation were deployed on a physical Unitree Go2 quadruped without any additional real-world training data. The robot successfully completed the task in 10 out of 10 real-world test runs, across different target locations and manually varied obstacle positions. This suggests the robustness and transferability of the learned POMDP models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VOiLA framework presents several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of robotic sequential decision-making under uncertainty:

- **Reduced Reliance on Hand-Crafted Models:** VOiLA addresses a persistent challenge in POMDP applications by providing a method to learn complex, stochastic transition and observation models directly from data. This circumvents the laborious and often inaccurate process of hand-crafting models for real-world robotic systems, potentially expanding the applicability of principled POMDP planning to more intricate domains.

- **Enhanced Data Efficiency and Generalization:** By learning task-agnostic POMDP models and decoupling model learning from task-specific policy optimization, VOiLA demonstrates significantly improved data efficiency compared to many model-free or end-to-end learning methods. Its ability to generalize to unseen environment configurations is crucial for developing robust and adaptable autonomous systems that can operate in varied and unpredictable real-world settings.

- **Scalable Online Planning with Expressive Models:** The integration of distilled, efficient generative models (diffusion models) with a massively parallel online POMDP planner (VOPP) represents a step towards making belief-space planning feasible for high-dimensional, complex problems in real-time. This combination leverages modern GPU architectures to handle the computational demands of extensive forward simulations required for online planning.

- **Facilitating Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The successful direct transfer of simulation-trained models to a physical robot without further real-world training is a substantial impact. This finding suggests a pathway for developing and testing complex robotic behaviors predominantly in simulation, significantly reducing the cost, time, and safety concerns associated with real-world data collection and training. It validates the potential for learned POMDP models to be sufficiently robust for physical deployment.

- **Bridging Learning and Planning:** VOiLA contributes to the growing body of research that seeks to combine the strengths of data-driven learning with the theoretical guarantees and decision-making rigor of planning. It provides a framework where expressive learned models enhance the capabilities of a principled online planner, offering a robust approach to sequential decision-making in partially observable environments. This direction could inspire further research into model learning specifically tailored for efficient integration with scalable planning algorithms.
