# SocialNav-SUB: Benchmarking VLMs for Scene Understanding in Social Robot Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The University of Texas at Austin, in collaboration with the Army Research Laboratory and Sony AI, introduced SocialNav-SUB, a Visual Question Answering benchmark for evaluating Vision-Language Models in real-world social robot navigation. The benchmark revealed that current VLMs struggle with spatial and spatiotemporal reasoning but show comparative strength in social reasoning, though a gap remains compared to human and rule-based baselines.

## Problem

- Robot navigation in human-populated environments requires robust scene understanding, including interpreting human intentions and adhering to social norms, which existing methods often cannot fully address in dynamic settings.
- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) show promise for complex tasks but lack systematic evaluation of their specific capabilities in spatial, spatiotemporal, and social reasoning crucial for social robot navigation.
- Previous VLM assessments in robotics have been partial, often limited to controlled scenarios or lacking temporal components, providing an incomplete understanding of their efficacy in realistic, dynamic environments.

## Method

- Developed **SocialNav-SUB**, a ==Visual Question Answering (VQA)== benchmark utilizing 60 challenging, human-populated scenarios from the SCAND dataset, featuring moderate-to-high crowd densities and dynamic motion.
- Augmented raw RGB image sequences with object-centric annotations, including tracked 3D human poses and color-coded positions on front-view and ==Bird's-Eye View== images, to provide explicit spatial information to VLMs.
- Designed multiple-choice questions across spatial, spatiotemporal, and social reasoning categories, establishing ground-truth through an IRB-approved human-subject study with multiple annotators per question, and evaluated state-of-the-art VLMs against human and rule-based baselines.

## Results

- OpenAI o4-mini achieved the highest overall performance among evaluated VLMs, yet all models demonstrated a substantial gap when compared to Human Oracle and Rule-Based baselines.
- VLMs performed less effectively in spatial and spatiotemporal reasoning, while showing comparatively closer performance to the Average Human baseline in social reasoning tasks.
- Ablation studies revealed that removing Chain-of-Thought reasoning significantly reduced social reasoning performance, and the impact of Bird's-Eye View prompts varied by model, with GPT-4o experiencing a notable decrease without them.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art VLMs exhibit a notable performance gap in accurate spatial and spatiotemporal reasoning compared to humans and rule-based systems within complex social navigation scenes.
> - VLMs demonstrate relatively stronger performance in social reasoning tasks, suggesting an implicit capacity for interpreting social cues, but still require improvement to reach human-level understanding.
> - Chain-of-Thought prompting is particularly beneficial for improving VLM performance in social reasoning tasks, indicating its role in facilitating complex multi-step inference pathways.
> - Improved lower-level spatial and spatiotemporal understanding is critical for enhancing VLMs' higher-level social reasoning capabilities, as shown by experiments providing ground-truth context.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robot navigation in dynamic, human-populated environments necessitates socially compliant decision-making, which in turn relies on robust scene understanding. The broader research landscape in robotics has seen a growing interest in leveraging advanced artificial intelligence models, particularly Vision-Language Models (VLMs), for complex tasks. VLMs have demonstrated capabilities in areas such as object recognition, common-sense reasoning, and contextual understanding, aligning with the nuanced requirements of social robot navigation.

Despite these promising capabilities, the application of VLMs to the specific challenges of social robot navigation presents several underexplored aspects. Prior work, such as VLM-Social-Nav, has indicated the potential of VLMs for social robot navigation; however, these evaluations have often been limited to controlled scenarios, providing only preliminary insights. Moreover, research has identified limitations in state-of-the-art VLMs regarding robust spatial reasoning, which is a fundamental requirement for interpreting complex navigation scenes or proposing socially compliant actions.

The existing body of literature lacks a systematic evaluation of VLMs across the critical dimensions necessary for effective social robot navigation: spatial reasoning, spatiotemporal reasoning, and the interpretation of human intentions. Previous evaluations have typically offered partial assessments, often focusing on static environments or lacking temporal components, thus providing an incomplete understanding of VLM efficacy in realistic, dynamic scenarios.

This work addresses these limitations by introducing a comprehensive benchmark, SocialNav-SUB. By rigorously testing VLM capabilities across the identified critical dimensions within real-world social navigation scenarios, the research aims to bridge the gap between the theoretical capabilities of VLMs and the practical demands of autonomous social navigation. This contribution is positioned to guide the development of VLMs tailored specifically to meet the scene understanding requirements of social robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent challenges of social robot navigation. For robots to operate effectively and safely in human-populated environments, they must interpret human intentions, adhere to social norms, and reason about spatial and temporal interactions. Existing learning-based and conventional navigation methods often prove insufficient in handling the complexity and nuance of dynamic, real-world social navigation scenarios due to their reliance on small datasets or validation in controlled settings with limited human interaction.

The advent of large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has opened new avenues for addressing these challenges. VLMs, trained on extensive and diverse multimodal datasets, exhibit strong capabilities in contextual understanding, commonsense reasoning, and chain-of-thought processing. It is hypothesized that these models may implicitly encode an understanding of human behavior and social norms due to their exposure to a wide array of real-world data.

However, a critical gap exists in understanding whether current VLMs can robustly perform the specific types of scene understanding necessary for social robot navigation. Specifically, the authors identify three critical dimensions that remain largely underexplored and unevaluated in this context:
1. **Spatial Reasoning:** The ability to accurately perceive and describe spatial relations among agents in a single frame. This includes understanding positions relative to the robot and to other objects or goals.
2. **Spatiotemporal Reasoning:** The capability to interpret the motion and evolution of spatial relationships over time. This involves understanding how agents, including the robot, move and how distances and positions change.
3. **Interpretation of Complex Human Intentions (Social Reasoning):** The capacity to infer social interactions, predict the impact of robot actions on humans, and understand human responses, which is crucial for socially compliant behavior.

Current VLM evaluations in robotics have offered only partial assessments of these capabilities, often in controlled settings or without a temporal component, providing an incomplete picture of their efficacy in realistic, dynamic social navigation scenarios. This lack of systematic evaluation impedes the development of VLMs specifically designed for this domain.

The key objective of this paper is to introduce the **Social Navigation Scene Understanding Benchmark (SocialNav-SUB)**. This benchmark is designed as a Visual Question Answering (VQA) framework to systematically evaluate state-of-the-art VLMs on their ability to perform spatial, spatiotemporal, and social reasoning in real-world social robot navigation scenarios. By establishing this benchmark, the researchers aim to:
- Provide a human-labeled VQA dataset to serve as ground-truth for evaluating VLM performance.
- Systematically identify performance gaps between state-of-the-art VLMs and human as well as rule-based baselines.
- Inform and guide future research and development of VLMs, highlighting specific areas where improvements are needed for practical application in social robot navigation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on the development and application of SocialNav-SUB, a Visual Question Answering (VQA) benchmark, to evaluate Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for scene understanding in social robot navigation contexts.

#### 3.1. Data Source and Scenario Selection

The benchmark utilizes social navigation scenarios extracted from the SCAND dataset. SCAND is a dataset comprising demonstrations of socially compliant robot navigation in diverse human-populated environments. For SocialNav-SUB, 60 challenging scenarios were selected. These scenarios feature moderate to high crowd densities (averaging 6.65 humans per scene with a standard deviation of 2.80), close pedestrian proximity, and dynamically changing human motion. The selected scenarios encompass various environment types, including outdoor walkways, narrow doorways/corridors, sidewalks, and street crossings, ensuring a diverse and representative set of real-world complexities. Each scenario consists of a 2.5-second image sequence sampled at 4 Hz.

#### 3.2. Rich and Object-Centric Visual Representations

To enhance VLM performance and provide clear visual references, the raw RGB image sequences from the robot's front-view camera are augmented with object-centric annotations. The process involves:
1. **Human Tracking and 3D Pose Estimation:** The PHALP algorithm is employed to track pedestrians and estimate their 3D poses relative to the camera frame using monocular video input.
2. **Global Pose Transformation:** Robot odometry data from SCAND is used to transform the relative human poses at future timesteps into global poses, referenced to the robot's initial pose.
3. **Kalman Smoothing:** Kalman smoothing is applied to the estimated human poses to reduce noise and improve trajectory consistency.
4. **Projection and Annotation:** Camera intrinsics and extrinsics from SCAND are used to project the 3D pedestrian coordinates onto both front-view and Bird's-Eye View (BEV) images. Pedestrian positions in both views are then annotated with numbered, color-coded circles.
This augmentation aims to provide VLMs with explicit spatial and object-level information, addressing their known limitations in fine-grained spatial reasoning. The same augmented visual inputs are provided to human annotators for consistency.

#### 3.3. Diverse Scene Understanding Questions

A set of multiple-choice questions is designed to comprehensively evaluate VLMs across three categories of scene understanding pertinent to social robot navigation:
1. **Spatial Reasoning:** Questions focus on static spatial relationships within a single frame, such as a person's initial or ending position relative to the robot, or whether a person obstructs the robot's goal path at the end of the video.
2. **Spatiotemporal Reasoning:** Questions address dynamic aspects and changes over time, including the robot's moving direction, the change in a person's distance relative to the robot, or whether a person's path obstructs the robot's goal path *during* the video.
3. **Social Reasoning:** Questions delve into interpreting interactions and intentions, such as whether the robot's or a person's movement is affected by the other, and the high-level relational actions (e.g., avoiding, yielding to) taken by either party. These questions also cover projected future interactions.

#### 3.4. Robust Human Baseline from Human-Subject Study

To establish ground-truth and robust baselines, a human-subject study was conducted using an IRB-approved protocol. Participants were recruited via Prolific. For each scenario, at least five human participants provided responses to the designed questions. This multi-annotator approach accounts for the subjective nature of some questions, particularly those related to social reasoning.

#### 3.5. Evaluation Metrics

Two metrics are defined to quantify the agreement between VLM outputs and human judgments:
1. **Probability of Agreement (PA):** Measures the fraction of times an evaluated answer (from a VLM or another human) exactly matches a human's response. It is calculated as the average agreement across all questions and all human annotators.
2. **Consensus-Weighted Probability of Agreement (CWPA):** This metric weighs agreement based on human consensus. Questions where humans largely agree (high consensus) impose a greater penalty for VLM disagreement, while questions with more human disagreement incur a lower penalty. This accounts for the inherent subjectivity in some tasks.

#### 3.6. Baselines

In addition to the evaluated VLMs, three baselines are established:
- **Human Oracle Baseline:** Represents an "expert-level" human performance by selecting the most common answer among human responses for each question.
- **Average Human Baseline:** Calculates the average PA of one human's responses against all other human responses, providing an indicator of typical human performance.
- **Rule-Based Baseline:** A non-VLM baseline that uses hand-crafted rules based on pedestrian position data to generate answers, providing a comparison to traditional algorithmic approaches.

#### 3.7. Experiment Process

VLMs are provided with survey prompts, front-view images, and BEV representations, mirroring the input given to human participants. Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning is employed as a prompting technique, where the VLM's previous answers are provided as context for subsequent questions, simulating human sequential thought processes. VLM responses are then quantitatively compared against the human and rule-based baselines using PA and CWPA.

#### 3.8. Evaluated VLM Models

The study evaluates state-of-the-art VLMs from three categories:
- Closed-source, general-purpose VLMs: GPT-4o and Gemini 2.0.
- Reasoning VLMs (fine-tuned for enhanced vision-language reasoning): OpenAI o4-mini and Gemini 2.5.
- Open-source, deployable VLMs: LLaVa-Next-Video.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical results reveal insights into the capabilities and limitations of state-of-the-art Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for scene understanding in social robot navigation.

#### 4.1. Overall VLM Performance

Across all evaluated models, OpenAI o4-mini achieved the highest overall performance. However, all VLMs demonstrated a considerable performance gap when compared to the Human Oracle and the Rule-Based baselines. While some VLM performance levels approached or were comparable to the Average Human baseline, the findings indicate that current state-of-the-art VLMs are not yet fully proficient in addressing the complexities of scene understanding for social robot navigation.

#### 4.2. Performance Across Reasoning Categories

The evaluation metrics (PA and CWPA) were analyzed across the three reasoning categories:
- **Spatial Reasoning:** VLMs consistently exhibited lower agreement with human and rule-based baselines in spatial reasoning tasks. The human oracle's consensus on spatial relationships significantly surpassed that of the best-performing VLMs, suggesting that these models currently struggle with accurate interpretation of static spatial configurations.
- **Spatiotemporal Reasoning:** Similar to spatial reasoning, VLMs showed greater difficulty in spatiotemporal tasks, which involve understanding dynamic changes over time. Their performance in capturing motion and temporal evolution of scenes lagged behind the baselines.
- **Social Reasoning:** In contrast to the other categories, VLMs performed relatively closer to the Human Oracle baseline and, in some instances, slightly outperformed the Average Human baseline in social reasoning tasks. This indicates a comparative strength in interpreting social cues and interactions, though a noticeable performance gap still exists compared to the human oracle and rule-based systems.

#### 4.3. Failure Case Analysis

Qualitative and quantitative analysis of failure cases revealed that VLMs frequently failed on questions where human consensus was high, particularly in scenarios with high crowd densities. Specific examples highlighted failures in recognizing precise object positions, interpreting changes in distance over time, and inferring appropriate social actions (e.g., whether to avoid a distant person). Quantitative summaries indicated that certain environments, such as blind corners, often correlated with higher VLM failure rates. Furthermore, specific social actions, such as "yielding to" or "overtaking," were frequently misidentified by VLMs when those actions were predicted, demonstrating a lack of nuanced social understanding in complex interaction types.

#### 4.4. Ablation Experiments on Querying Strategies

Ablation studies were conducted to assess the impact of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning and Bird's-Eye View (BEV) visual prompts on VLM performance:
- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning:** Removing CoT reasoning did not substantially impact spatial or spatiotemporal reasoning performance. However, it led to a notable decrease in social reasoning performance across all evaluated models. This suggests that CoT provides a structured inference pathway that is particularly beneficial for the multi-step reasoning often required in complex social tasks.
- **BEV Visual Prompts:** The impact of BEV prompts varied among models. While LLaVa-Next-Video and Gemini 2.0 showed minimal changes in performance without BEV, GPT-4o experienced a notable decrease across all reasoning capabilities. This indicates that the effectiveness of BEV representations is model-dependent and that prompt design remains an area requiring further investigation.

#### 4.5. Impact of Spatial/Spatiotemporal Reasoning on Social Reasoning

An additional experiment demonstrated that providing VLMs with ground-truth answers for spatial and spatiotemporal questions (as CoT context) significantly improved their performance on subsequent social reasoning questions. This finding suggests that a limiting factor for current VLMs' social reasoning capabilities is their underlying performance in lower-level spatial and spatiotemporal understanding. It implies that advancements in basic spatial reasoning could lead to substantial gains in higher-level social interpretation.

#### 4.6. Waypoint Selection Experiments

Preliminary experiments on waypoint selection, detailed in the appendix, reinforced the practical value of accurate scene understanding. Providing additional social scene context, particularly when derived from human oracle responses, improved the alignment of VLM-selected waypoints with those chosen by human operators. This indicates that enhanced scene understanding directly contributes to more socially appropriate navigation actions.

In summary, the evaluation indicates that while certain VLMs show encouraging agreement with human responses, particularly in social reasoning, a significant gap persists in achieving human-level or even rule-based performance for comprehensive scene understanding in social robot navigation, particularly in spatial and spatiotemporal aspects.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces the Social Navigation Scene Understanding Benchmark (SocialNav-SUB), which is a significant contribution to the field of robotics and artificial intelligence. Its primary significance lies in providing the first dedicated Visual Question Answering (VQA) benchmark for systematically evaluating Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in complex, real-world social robot navigation scenarios.

#### 5.1. Addressing a Critical Gap

SocialNav-SUB directly addresses the previously identified gap in evaluating VLM capabilities across the three critical dimensions of scene understanding for social robot navigation: spatial reasoning, spatiotemporal reasoning, and social reasoning. By focusing on challenging, crowded, and dynamic environments from the SCAND dataset, the benchmark moves beyond limited, controlled settings, providing a more realistic assessment of VLM performance.

#### 5.2. Robust and Quantifiable Evaluation

The benchmark establishes robust human-subject studies to collect ground-truth labels for VQA tasks, acknowledging the subjective nature of social interactions. The introduction of specific metrics, Probability of Agreement (PA) and Consensus-Weighted Probability of Agreement (CWPA), allows for quantifiable evaluation that directly reflects agreement with human judgments. This robust evaluation framework provides a clear standard for comparing VLM performance against human-level understanding and rule-based baselines.

#### 5.3. Identification of Strengths and Weaknesses

The empirical results systematically highlight the current strengths and weaknesses of state-of-the-art VLMs. While models show comparative promise in social reasoning, their underperformance in spatial and spatiotemporal reasoning, especially when compared to human oracle and rule-based baselines, identifies critical areas for improvement. This fine-grained understanding is crucial for targeted research efforts.

#### 5.4. Guiding Future Research and Development

SocialNav-SUB sets a clear research agenda for the advancement of foundation models in social robot navigation. It enables researchers to:
- **Systematically compare and benchmark models:** Providing a common framework for model evaluation and iterative improvement.
- **Refine prompting strategies:** The ablation studies on Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning and Bird's-Eye View (BEV) prompts demonstrate the benchmark's utility in understanding how different input modalities and reasoning techniques impact VLM performance, guiding the design of more effective VLM interfaces.
- **Develop new methods:** The finding that improved spatial and spatiotemporal reasoning directly enhances social reasoning suggests specific avenues for future VLM development, potentially through fine-tuning on specialized spatial reasoning data or the integration of dedicated perception modules.
- **Explore hybrid approaches:** The results encourage the exploration of hybrid systems where VLMs handle high-level social reasoning while other specialized modules address their current weaknesses, such as precise spatial perception.

#### 5.5. Impact on Robotic Systems

The ultimate impact of SocialNav-SUB is to facilitate the development of more socially aware, reliable, and deployable robotic systems. By systematically evaluating and driving improvements in VLMs' ability to understand complex social scenes, this work contributes to building robots that can navigate human environments more safely and compliantly, enhancing human-robot interaction in real-world applications. The commitment to open-sourcing the benchmark further ensures its accessibility and utility for the broader research community.
