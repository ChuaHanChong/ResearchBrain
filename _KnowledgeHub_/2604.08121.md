# Uni-ViGU: Towards Unified Video Generation and Understanding via A Diffusion-Based Video Generator

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Academy of AI for Science and Fudan University developed Uni-ViGU, a diffusion-based framework that unifies video generation and understanding by extending a pretrained video generator as its core. This framework utilizes a unified flow method and a modality-driven Mixture-of-Experts architecture to simultaneously produce high-quality videos and richly detailed, visually faithful textual captions.

## Problem

- The significant computational cost of diffusion-based video generation makes it challenging to scale understanding-centric Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for high-quality video generation.
- Existing MLLM-diffusion integrations often feature decoupled training, which limits the direct benefit of generation objectives for visual understanding.
- There is an architectural mismatch between continuous video latents, suitable for diffusion models, and discrete text tokens, which traditionally hinders their unified processing.

## Method

- **Uni-ViGU** extends a pretrained video generator (==WAN2.1==) by integrating a ==unified flow method== that handles both continuous video and discrete text within a single generative process.
- It implements a ==modality-driven Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)== architecture, sharing ==attention layers== for cross-modal interaction and employing separate ==Feed-Forward Networks (FFNs)== for video and text processing.
- A ==two-stage bidirectional training== mechanism is used: =='Knowledge Recall'== to learn video-to-prompt mapping and =='Capability Refinement'== to develop fine-grained video understanding from detailed captions.

## Results

- The framework demonstrates the simultaneous generation of both high-quality videos and corresponding detailed textual captions from noise.
- Generated captions are significantly more descriptive and faithful to the visual content than the initial conditioning prompts used for video generation alone.
- The process enables mutual refinement, where progressively denoised text guides video generation and emerging video latents steer text generation, leading to coherent multimodal outputs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generation-centric models, specifically powerful video generators, offer a scalable and principled foundation for unified multimodal intelligence due to their inherently rich visual priors.
> - The knowledge acquired by a text-to-video generator can be effectively repurposed and inverted for robust video understanding, minimizing the need for extensive new training.
> - Decoupling modality-specific knowledge into separate FFNs while sharing attention layers allows for efficient cross-modal interaction and disentanglement of domain-specific generation patterns.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "Uni-ViGU" addresses the field of unified multimodal models, specifically focusing on the integration of visual understanding and generation capabilities within a single framework. Historically, research in this domain has explored various architectural paradigms to achieve such unification.

Early approaches often formulated image generation as an autoregressive sequence prediction problem within Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). However, these methods frequently faced limitations in visual fidelity. Subsequent advancements integrated MLLMs with diffusion frameworks, freezing pretrained MLLMs and using learnable query tokens as interfaces for diffusion-based image or video generation. While this improved generation quality, the decoupled training often prevented generation objectives from directly benefiting visual understanding. More recent work introduced dual-tower unified frameworks, employing separate MLLMs for understanding and generation, coupled via cross-attention, to achieve tighter integration.

A persistent challenge identified by the authors is the substantial computational cost disparity between visual generation and understanding, particularly for video. Diffusion-based generation, especially for video, requires numerous iterative denoising steps and considerable token consumption, leading to high training and inference costs. This computational imbalance makes it difficult to scale understanding-centric MLLMs to support high-quality visual generation, especially for videos.

Uni-ViGU proposes an inversion of this conventional paradigm. Instead of extending understanding-centric MLLMs to support generation, it posits that generation-centric models, specifically video generators, could serve as a more scalable and principled architectural foundation for unified multimodal intelligence. This perspective is motivated by the observation that visual generation dominates computational costs and, from a developmental standpoint, visual perception often precedes linguistic articulation. The paper argues that strong generative visual priors, already embedded in large video generators, might offer a more natural starting point for unification.

This work builds upon existing diffusion-based language modeling, which extends flow matching to discrete data, and draws inspiration from prior efforts to unify text and image generation via diffusion. However, a key distinction is Uni-ViGU's focus on reusing pretrained generation knowledge from diffusion-based *video* models, rather than training generation from scratch, aiming to achieve unified video understanding and generation with reduced computational overhead.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of Uni-ViGU is to develop a unified framework that integrates video generation and video understanding by extending a video generator as its foundational architecture. This objective is driven by several key motivations and aims to address specific challenges in multimodal AI:

1. **Addressing Computational Cost Imbalance:** The core motivation stems from the recognition that visual generation, particularly for video using diffusion models, incurs significantly higher computational costs than visual understanding. Generating even a short video can require processing millions of tokens across numerous denoising steps, making it computationally prohibitive to extend understanding-centric MLLMs to support high-quality video generation. Uni-ViGU seeks to overcome this by inverting the paradigm, building on a robust video generator rather than augmenting an MLLM.

2. **Leveraging Strong Generative Visual Priors:** The authors propose that generation-centric models, which inherently learn comprehensive visual priors, may offer a more natural and scalable foundation for unified multimodal intelligence. They draw an analogy to human cognitive development, where visual perception precedes linguistic expression. By starting with a video generator, Uni-ViGU aims to capitalize on the rich spatiotemporal knowledge already encoded in these models from extensive pretraining.

3. **Endowing Video Generators with Language Comprehension:** A specific objective is to enable a video generator, traditionally designed for text-to-video synthesis, to also perform language reasoning and comprehension. This involves allowing the model to generate coherent text descriptions from video input.

4. **Unifying Continuous and Discrete Modalities:** A significant technical challenge is the architectural mismatch between continuous video latents (suited for diffusion) and discrete text tokens (traditionally handled by autoregressive prediction). The objective is to bridge this gap by developing a unified generative process that can handle both continuous video flow matching and discrete text flow matching within a single framework.

5. **Repurposing Generative Knowledge for Understanding:** The framework aims to demonstrate that the knowledge embedded in video generators, which learns to map text to video, can be effectively repurposed for video understanding (the reverse process). This objective seeks to reduce the complexity of learning video understanding by leveraging existing cross-modal correspondences.

6. **Developing Fine-Grained Multimodal Alignment:** While video generators learn coarse text-to-video mappings, understanding tasks often require fine-grained semantics. Uni-ViGU aims to establish a more discriminative shared representation by training the model to generate detailed video captions, thereby enforcing deeper semantic grounding.

In summary, the motivation is to create a scalable and principled unified multimodal model by re-imagining the foundational architecture, moving from understanding-first to generation-first, to more effectively handle the computational demands of video and repurpose learned generative knowledge for comprehensive understanding.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Uni-ViGU proposes a diffusion-based framework that extends a video generator to unify video generation and understanding. Its methodology comprises a unified flow method, a modality-driven Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture, and a bidirectional training mechanism.

**Foundation Model (WAN2.1):**
Uni-ViGU builds upon WAN2.1, a text-to-video generator based on a standard latent diffusion paradigm. WAN2.1 synthesizes videos by iteratively denoising Gaussian noise in a latent space, compressed by a Variational Autoencoder (VAE). The model learns a diffusion process through flow matching, predicting a velocity field to transport noise to data. Its architecture includes a causal VAE, a pretrained text encoder (umT5), and a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) as the diffusion backbone. DiT blocks consist of self-attention (for spatial/temporal dependencies), cross-attention (for text conditioning), and Feed-Forward Network (FFN) layers.

**Unified Text and Video Generation via Uni-Flow:**
To bridge the architectural mismatch between continuous video and discrete text, Uni-ViGU introduces a uni-flow process:
- **Continuous Flow Matching for Video:** For video, the process remains standard continuous flow matching. A video `x` is encoded into a latent `z_v,1` by the VAE. A noisy latent `z_v,τ` is constructed via linear interpolation between `z_v,0` (Gaussian noise) and `z_v,1`. The model learns to predict the velocity `u_v = z_v,1 - z_v,0`.
- **Discrete Flow Matching for Text:** For text, discrete tokens `y` are mapped to a continuous embedding space `z_t,1` via a learnable embedding matrix `E`. Flow matching is then applied in this embedding space, constructing `z_t,τ` between Gaussian noise `z_t,0` and `z_t,1`. The model predicts the velocity `u_t = z_t,1 - z_t,0`. At inference, `z_t,1` is decoded back to discrete tokens by finding the closest embedding in `E`.
- **Unified Flow Matching:** The two modalities are unified by modeling a joint video-text distribution. Both `z_v,τ` and `z_t,τ` are constructed, but crucially, their time steps `τ_v` and `τ_t` are sampled *independently* from U(0,1). A unified model `f_θ` conditioned on a text prompt `c` jointly predicts both velocity fields (`ˆv_v`, `ˆv_t`). The combined objective `L_UFM` is a weighted sum of the mean squared errors for both velocity predictions, with `λ_t` normalized by the ratio of video to text token counts to balance contributions. This unified framework supports both video understanding (clean video, noisy text) and video generation (noisy video, clean text) by setting appropriate `τ` values.

**Modality-Driven Mixture-of-Experts Architecture:**
Uni-ViGU adapts the DiT architecture by hypothesizing that cross-modal alignment resides in attention layers, while modality-specific generation patterns are encoded in FFNs.
- **Shared Attention:** Self-attention and cross-attention layers operate over the concatenation of video and text tokens (`[h_v; h_t]`), enabling bidirectional cross-modal interaction.
- **Modality-Specific FFNs:** The outputs from the shared attention layers are then routed to separate FFN branches: `FFN_v` for video tokens and `FFN_t` for text tokens. This deterministic routing based on modality disentangles domain-specific knowledge.
- **Initialization Strategy:** To facilitate knowledge transfer, the `FFN_v` layers retain the pretrained weights from the original WAN2.1 video generator, preserving strong generative priors. The `FFN_t` layers are newly initialized for text generation. This design maximizes knowledge reuse in attention parameters while incurring minimal parameter overhead for the text expert.

**Bidirectional Training Mechanism:**
The training proceeds in two stages to effectively reuse knowledge and develop new capabilities:
- **Stage 1: Knowledge Recall:**
 - **Goal:** Learn video-to-prompt mapping, leveraging existing text-to-video correspondences.
 - **Setup:** The model is initialized with a pretrained video generator. The target text `z_t,1` is set identical to the conditioning prompt `c`.
 - **Mechanism:** To prevent trivial copying of `c`, condition dropout is applied (dropping `c` with probability `p`). This forces the model to reconstruct `c` from the co-noised video latent `z_v,τ_v`, compelling it to utilize pretrained text-to-video correspondences in reverse.
 - **Loss Weighting:** `λ_v = 1.0` for video and `λ_t = |z_v|/|z_t|` for text, to balance supervision between video (many tokens, well-pretrained) and text (fewer tokens, novel task).
- **Stage 2: Capability Refinement:**
 - **Goal:** Develop fine-grained video understanding.
 - **Setup:** The target text is replaced with detailed video captions, which are richer and more semantically precise than the brief conditioning prompts.
 - **Mechanism:** While still conditioned on `c`, the text generation branch must now attend to the video latent `z_v,τ_v` through the shared self-attention to recover fine-grained visual details not present in `c`. This promotes genuine video comprehension and deeply aligned multimodal representations.

**Inference:**
- **Video Understanding:** The video latent `z_v,τ_v` is set to the clean video representation (`z_v,1`), and the text flow is integrated from noise (`z_t,0`) to predict `z_t,1`, which is then decoded to text.
- **Video Generation:** The text latent `z_t,τ_t` is set to the embedded prompt (`z_t,1`), and the video flow is integrated from noise (`z_v,0`) to predict `z_v,1`, which is then decoded to video pixels.
- **Joint Video-Text Generation:** Both modalities are initialized from noise (`z_v,0`, `z_t,0`) and denoised simultaneously. The two streams are coupled through shared self-attention, allowing partially denoised text to guide video and emerging video to steer text, leading to mutually refined, coherent video-text pairs.

**Implementation Details:**
- **Dataset:** Two stages use 10K video-prompt pairs (Stage 1) and 10K video-prompt-detailed caption triples (Stage 2).
- **Data Curation:** Conditioning prompts are used to synthesize videos with state-of-the-art generators. LLMs then generate detailed captions from these video-prompt pairs. Token length constraints are enforced (prompts: 0-128 tokens, detailed captions: 128-256 tokens) to ensure the detailed caption is not trivially inferable from the prompt.
- **Training:** Model initialized from pretrained WAN2.1. Stage 1: 40K steps (LR: 2e-4). Stage 2: 60K steps (LR: 5e-5). Adam optimizer used. Total training cost: 16 H800 GPUs over one week.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental section of the paper primarily focuses on demonstrating the capability of Uni-ViGU in joint video-text generation.

**Joint Video-Text Generation:**
The main finding presented is the model's ability to simultaneously generate high-quality videos and corresponding detailed captions from Gaussian noise. As described in the methodology, during this process, the two modalities are designed to co-evolve through the shared attention module. The progressively denoised text latents provide increasingly precise semantic guidance for video generation, while the emerging video latents supply visual context that steers text generation.

The qualitative results, as illustrated in Figure 3, demonstrate instances where Uni-ViGU produces videos coupled with detailed captions. The authors state that these generated captions are "substantially more descriptive and faithful to the visual content than the original conditioning prompts" that might typically be used for video generation alone. For example, a brief prompt like "A tranquil tableau of a coastal house, with a waiter standing on the porch, holding a tray of glasses filled with refreshing beverages..." leads to a video and a much more elaborate caption describing the waiter's attire, the number of glasses, the type of beverage, the movement of seagulls, and the environmental details. Similar observations are made for scenes involving a shower set inside a shoebox with an apple, and a nurse in a courtyard.

The paper does not provide quantitative metrics for video generation (e.g., FID, FVD) or video understanding (e.g., CIDEr, BLEU, ROUGE) tasks. The "competitive performance" mentioned in the abstract is not quantified or compared against specific baselines within the "Experiments" section for either individual task or for joint generation using established metrics. The results presented are qualitative examples illustrating the framework's capacity for integrated multimodal output.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Uni-ViGU framework presents a generation-centric paradigm for achieving unified video generation and understanding, which has several potential significances and impacts:

1. **Addressing Computational Bottlenecks:** By proposing an inversion of the conventional approach (extending understanding-centric MLLMs), Uni-ViGU offers a potential solution to the computational imbalance where video generation is significantly more expensive than understanding. Starting from a pretrained, efficient video generator could lead to more scalable unified models, particularly as video data and desired generation quality increase.

2. **Efficient Knowledge Transfer:** The methodology emphasizes leveraging existing knowledge. By repurposing the rich visual-semantic correspondences learned during text-to-video pretraining for video understanding, the framework suggests a more efficient path for developing multimodal capabilities. This bidirectional utilization of generative priors could reduce the need for extensive training from scratch for understanding tasks.

3. **Principled Unification of Modalities:** The introduction of a unified flow method that simultaneously handles continuous video and discrete text within a single generative process addresses a fundamental architectural mismatch. This coherent approach could lead to more deeply aligned multimodal representations and simplify the design of future unified models.

4. **Architectural Efficiency:** The modality-driven Mixture-of-Experts architecture, which shares attention layers for cross-modal interaction while separating FFNs for modality-specific processing, is designed to maximize knowledge reuse with minimal additional parameters. This could contribute to building more efficient and compact unified models.

5. **Enhanced Multimodal Interaction:** The bidirectional training mechanism and the joint generation process, where modalities co-evolve and mutually refine each other, demonstrate a strong interaction between video and text. This deep coupling could lead to more coherent and contextually rich multimodal outputs, where text descriptions are highly faithful to visual content, and generated videos accurately reflect detailed textual prompts.

6. **Path Towards General-Purpose Visual Intelligence:** The authors position Uni-ViGU as a "scalable path toward unified multimodal intelligence." This generation-centric perspective could inspire further research into foundational models that prioritize visual generative capabilities as the cornerstone for broader visual intelligence, including various understanding, editing, and interactive tasks.

In summary, Uni-ViGU's significance lies in its proposal of an alternative, generation-first architectural and training paradigm that could offer a more scalable and principled solution for unified video-text processing, potentially influencing the design of future multimodal AI systems.
