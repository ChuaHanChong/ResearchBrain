# Active-Perceptive Motion Generation for Mobile Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The ActPerMoMa framework enables mobile manipulators to perform robust grasping in unknown, cluttered environments by intelligently integrating active perception with grasp executability, improving success rates in complex scenes and for challenging grasps.

## Problem

- Mobile manipulators struggle to extract sufficient task-relevant visual information from unknown and cluttered real-world environments using onboard sensors.
- Existing active perception methods, often focused on scene reconstruction (Next-Best-View), do not adequately consider the extensive movement capabilities and associated costs of mobile manipulators.
- Grasp detection systems frequently propose high-quality grasps that are not physically reachable or feasible for a mobile manipulator from its current base pose, leading to task failures.

## Method

- A holistic Active-Perceptive Motion Generation for Mobile Manipulation (ActPerMoMa) pipeline that operates in a receding horizon fashion, continuously planning and executing motions.
- A path-wise utility function that combines visual Information Gain (IG) for scene understanding and grasp executability (using pre-computed reachability maps) for task success.
- Generation of numerous candidate robot paths, with the optimal path selected by maximizing the combined utility function while considering movement costs and a momentum term to prevent oscillations.

## Results

- ActPerMoMa achieved the highest success rate (92.6%) and lowest abort rate in simulated complex scenes, outperforming baselines which had lower success rates (e.g., Naive 86.8%).
- In scenarios with only "Hard Grasps" available, ActPerMoMa significantly improved success rate (61.8%) and reduced abort rate (29.6%) compared to baselines (e.g., Naive SR 43.8%, AR 49.2%), demonstrating the importance of integrating grasp executability.
- The framework successfully transferred to a real TIAGo++ robot, achieving an 80% success rate and 20% abort rate over 10 real-world experiments in a cluttered room environment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimizing over entire robot paths, rather than single viewpoints, is crucial for efficient active perception and motion generation in mobile manipulation.
> - Explicitly balancing visual information gain (exploration) with task-specific objectives like grasp executability (exploitation) significantly improves mobile manipulation success, especially for challenging grasps or in complex environments.
> - A receding-horizon control strategy enables reactive and adaptive robot motion planning, allowing continuous adjustment to newly observed scene information.
