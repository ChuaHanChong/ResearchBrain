# Active-Perceptive Motion Generation for Mobile Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The ActPerMoMa framework enables mobile manipulators to perform robust grasping in unknown, cluttered environments by intelligently integrating active perception with grasp executability, improving success rates in complex scenes and for challenging grasps.

## Problem

- Mobile manipulators struggle to extract sufficient task-relevant visual information from unknown and cluttered real-world environments using onboard sensors.
- Existing active perception methods, often focused on scene reconstruction (Next-Best-View), do not adequately consider the extensive movement capabilities and associated costs of mobile manipulators.
- Grasp detection systems frequently propose high-quality grasps that are not physically reachable or feasible for a mobile manipulator from its current base pose, leading to task failures.

## Method

- A holistic Active-Perceptive Motion Generation for Mobile Manipulation (ActPerMoMa) pipeline that operates in a receding horizon fashion, continuously planning and executing motions.
- A path-wise utility function that combines visual Information Gain (IG) for scene understanding and grasp executability (using pre-computed reachability maps) for task success.
- Generation of numerous candidate robot paths, with the optimal path selected by maximizing the combined utility function while considering movement costs and a momentum term to prevent oscillations.

## Results

- ActPerMoMa achieved the highest success rate (92.6%) and lowest abort rate in simulated complex scenes, outperforming baselines which had lower success rates (e.g., Naive 86.8%).
- In scenarios with only "Hard Grasps" available, ActPerMoMa significantly improved success rate (61.8%) and reduced abort rate (29.6%) compared to baselines (e.g., Naive SR 43.8%, AR 49.2%), demonstrating the importance of integrating grasp executability.
- The framework successfully transferred to a real TIAGo++ robot, achieving an 80% success rate and 20% abort rate over 10 real-world experiments in a cluttered room environment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimizing over entire robot paths, rather than single viewpoints, is crucial for efficient active perception and motion generation in mobile manipulation.
> - Explicitly balancing visual information gain (exploration) with task-specific objectives like grasp executability (exploitation) significantly improves mobile manipulation success, especially for challenging grasps or in complex environments.
> - A receding-horizon control strategy enables reactive and adaptive robot motion planning, allowing continuous adjustment to newly observed scene information.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is rapidly advancing towards developing embodied agents capable of operating autonomously in unstructured, real-world environments such as households. A crucial component of this vision is **Mobile Manipulation (MoMa)**, which combines the benefits of robot mobility (enlarged operational space) and dexterity (interaction with the environment). However, equipping MoMa systems with onboard sensors, such as cameras, presents significant challenges in extracting task-relevant visual information, especially in cluttered or partially observed scenes. This paper addresses a core problem within MoMa: how to effectively perceive the environment to facilitate complex manipulation tasks like grasping.

This work specifically builds upon and extends the concept of **active perception**. Active perception, first formalized by Bajcsy and Aloimonos, refers to an agent's ability to intelligently choose "what to perceive, and determines how, when and where to achieve that perception." For mobile robots, the primary objective of active perception has traditionally been scene reconstruction, often relying on **Next-Best-View (NBV)** strategies. These methods typically involve selecting viewpoints that maximize information gain (IG) by minimizing uncertainty or exploring unobserved regions. While effective for reconstruction, NBV approaches can lead to suboptimal or wasteful motions for mobile manipulators due to their large movement range, as they don't explicitly consider the cost of reaching views or information gained during transit. Previous research, such as and, has begun to consider IG over paths and receding horizon planning to address these limitations.

Furthermore, this paper integrates active perception with the specific task of **grasping**. For static manipulators, active grasping often involves optimizing viewpoints to improve grasp pose estimation or quality. Methods like Breyer et al. focus on volumetric information gain around the target object to find stable grasps. However, applying these methods directly to MoMa is problematic because they generally do not account for the extensive movement capabilities of mobile bases, the associated costs, or the crucial factor of **grasp reachability and feasibility** from various robot base poses. A high-quality grasp detected from a certain viewpoint might be impossible or highly inefficient for a mobile manipulator to execute from its current base position.

"Active-Perceptive Motion Generation for Mobile Manipulation (ActPerMoMa)" directly addresses these critical gaps. It moves beyond pure reconstruction-focused active perception and static-manipulator active grasping by developing a holistic framework for MoMa that explicitly trades off visual information gain for scene understanding with task-oriented objectives like grasp success and, critically, grasp executability from various mobile base positions. By planning over paths in a receding horizon fashion, it ensures reactivity to new information while considering the entire trajectory, making it more efficient and robust for complex, unknown environments compared to single-viewpoint or non-active approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable mobile manipulators to perform manipulation tasks, specifically grasping, in unknown and cluttered environments by generating motions that are both visually informative and conducive to task success. The authors envision a future where embodied agents can operate autonomously in everyday settings like households, necessitating robust solutions for perception and interaction in unstructured reality.

The key motivations driving this work stem from the inherent challenges of mobile manipulation:
- **Information Extraction in Unstructured Environments:** Even with onboard sensors, perceiving task-relevant information in complex, cluttered scenes remains difficult. Robots need to actively gather visual data to understand their surroundings.
- **Balancing Exploration and Exploitation:** A mobile manipulator must simultaneously explore its environment to build a sufficient scene model (exploration) and move effectively to execute a task (exploitation). A purely exploratory approach might lead to inefficient movements, while pure exploitation without sufficient scene understanding could lead to failures.
- **Limitations of Existing Active Perception:** Traditional active perception methods, particularly NBV, are often optimized for scene reconstruction and do not fully consider the kinematics and motion costs of mobile manipulators, potentially leading to wasteful or impractical trajectories.
- **Grasp Executability in Mobile Manipulation:** High-quality grasps detected by vision systems are not always physically reachable or feasible for a mobile manipulator from its current base pose. Motion planning for mobile grasping must explicitly account for the robot's reachability and potential collisions during grasp execution.

To address these motivations, the authors propose ActPerMoMa with the following key objectives:
1. **Novel Formulation for Active Perceptive MoMa:** To generate robot paths toward objects of interest without prior scene knowledge, integrating principles of active perception and mobile manipulation coordination.
2. **Path-Wise Utility Calculation:** To balance exploration (maximizing visual information gain for scene reconstruction) and exploitation (maximizing task-specific objectives like grasp reachability and grasp success) by evaluating utilities over entire robot paths rather than just individual viewpoints.
3. **Reactive and Adaptive Control:** To employ a receding-horizon control approach that allows the robot to continuously adapt its motion based on newly observed scene information, thereby ensuring reactivity and robustness in dynamic environments.
4. **Efficient Mobile Grasping:** To resolve the mobile grasping problem efficiently by planning motions that gather enough visual information to infer collisions and good grasps, while critically accounting for grasp executability from the robot's base.

By achieving these objectives, ActPerMoMa aims to enable more intelligent, efficient, and robust mobile manipulation in real-world, cluttered settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, **Active-Perceptive Motion Generation for Mobile Manipulation (ActPerMoMa)**, is a holistic pipeline designed for mobile manipulators to perform grasping tasks in unknown, cluttered scenes. It operates on the principle of continuous planning and execution in a receding horizon fashion.

**Robot Model and Scene Representation:**
The system uses a dual-armed TIAGo++ MoMa robot equipped with a holonomic base and a head-mounted RGBD camera. While the robot has 20 Degrees of Freedom (DoF), the robot's state is simplified to its mobile base pose (SE(2)), camera pose (SE(3)), and end-effector pose (SE(3)) for planning simplicity. The robot begins with only approximate knowledge of the target object's location. As the robot perceives the scene, it continuously builds a **3D Truncated Signed Distance Function (TSDF)**, a volumetric representation of the environment. This TSDF is crucial for two main purposes: planning collision-free motions for the robot's base and arm, and detecting 6DoF grasps. For grasp detection, the system uses a **VGN (Volumetric Grasping Network)**, which predicts grasp poses and quality scores for 3D voxels within the TSDF. To ensure robustness, grasps are considered "stable" only if they maintain a high-quality score over several consecutive timesteps.

**Core Design Principles for Motion Generation:**
ActPerMoMa adheres to four key principles:
1. **Exploration-Exploitation Trade-off:** The robot must balance gathering more information about the scene (exploration) with acting on existing information to complete the task (exploitation).
2. **Adaptive and Reactive Control:** Given the continuous information flow, the motion planning must be reactive and update costs dynamically.
3. **Balanced Utilities:** Motion planning must consider movement cost, visual Information Gain (IG), and the likelihood of grasp success.
4. **Grasp Executability:** The feasibility and reachability of detected grasps from various base locations are paramount for successful task completion.

**Pipeline Overview (Fig. 2):**
At each timestep `t`, the robot integrates RGBD data into the TSDF. It then samples numerous feasible candidate robot paths (`T`) towards the approximate target object location. Each path `τ` consists of a sequence of base and camera poses. For every candidate path `τ_j`, the system computes a path-wise utility function `J_τ` that combines two main objectives: visual Information Gain (`J_IG`) and grasp executability (`J_exec`). The optimal path `τ*` is chosen by maximizing this combined utility. The robot then executes the first waypoint of `τ*` and repeats the process, ensuring continuous adaptation to new sensory data. Once the robot is close enough to the target object and `J_exec` is above a threshold, the highest utility grasp is executed.

**Detailed Components:**

- **Candidate Goals & Paths Generation:**
 - The system samples `N_b` candidate base poses near the approximate target location, ensuring they are collision-free according to the current TSDF.
 - For each base goal, feasible SE(3) camera poses are jointly sampled, ensuring the camera is oriented towards the target area.
 - `M` candidate paths (`T`) are generated from the robot's current pose to these base goals using an optimal path planner (e.g., A* on discretized grids). Each path includes both base and camera poses, providing a sequence of viewpoints.

- **Receding-Horizon Control:**
 - At every timestep, the optimal path `τ*` is selected by maximizing `J_IG(o_TSDF, τ) + J_exec(G, τ)`.
 - Only the first waypoint of `τ*` is executed using a low-level controller for base and camera velocities.
 - This re-computation at each step makes the robot highly reactive to new observations. Grasp execution is initiated when the robot is within a certain distance of the target, and `J_exec` is sufficiently high.

- **Information Gain (IG) Computation (`J_IG`):**
 - The perception objective focuses on gaining information about the target object.
 - It uses a "rear-side voxel" IG formulation. For each candidate camera view `p_cam` along a path, rays are cast from a virtual camera. The IG is computed by counting voxels within the target object's approximate bounding box that are on the "rear side" of already observed TSDF voxels, indicating new information.
 - Crucially, `J_IG` accumulates IG over *all* camera poses in a path, not just a single NBV. It's weighted by the inverse square of the distance to the camera pose along the path (`dist(p_cam)^2`), prioritizing information gained sooner.

- **Reachability Utility & Grasp Selection (`J_exec`):**
 - This component facilitates the transition from exploration to exploitation.
 - A pre-computed **reachability map** (`R`) is used. This map indicates the manipulability and likelihood of an end-effector reaching any SE(3) pose from a given base pose.
 - For each detected grasp `g` (from VGN) and each candidate base goal `p_goal_base` (from the sampled paths), `R` is queried to determine the grasp's reachability.
 - The highest reachability across all stable, detected grasps provides `J_exec` for that path. For dual-armed robots, the maximum reachability from either arm is used.
 - `J_exec` is weighted by the inverse of the path length (`len(τ)`) to favor shorter paths to reachable grasping locations. This utility is also used for final grasp selection.

- **Additional Hyperparameters:**
 - Weighting factors `w_IG` and `w_exec` are used to balance the IG and grasp execution objectives.
 - A grasp quality threshold (`q_th`) and stability window (`n_stab`) are applied for VGN output.
 - A **momentum term** is introduced to penalize oscillatory motions, encouraging the robot to commit to a direction unless a significantly better path is found.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of ActPerMoMa was rigorously evaluated through extensive simulated experiments and a real-world demonstration.

**Experimental Setup:**
Experiments were conducted using a dual-armed TIAGo++ MoMa robot in Isaac Sim. Two main scenarios were designed:
- **Simple Scenes:** A table in free space with 4 randomly spawned YCB objects.
- **Complex Scenes:** 6 YCB objects to create more clutter, with an additional random obstacle to obstruct the robot's path.
Performance was measured using Success Rate (SR), Abort Rate (AR), Grasp Failure Rate (GFR), total distance covered (`d_total`), and total number of views visited (`v_total`). Each experiment was run for 500 episodes.

**Ablations and Hyperparameter Study (Tables I & II):**
This study justified the design choices and the balance between exploration and exploitation.
- **Path-Length Scaling:** The `ActPerMoMa-no-weights` ablation, which removed path-length related scaling from utilities, performed very poorly (e.g., 42.4% SR in simple scenes vs. 95.4% for full ActPerMoMa). This highlights the crucial importance of considering movement costs and temporal proximity of information gain/reachability along a path.
- **Information Gain Weight (`w_IG`):** A lower weight for IG (0.2) generally yielded better SR and reduced `d_total`/`v_total` across both simple and complex scenes. This suggests that once sufficient information for grasp detection is gathered, the system should prioritize exploitation of task-specific objectives (grasp executability) over continued extensive exploration.
- **Momentum Term:** While less impactful in simple scenes, the momentum term significantly improved performance in complex scenes (e.g., 90.6% SR with momentum 700 vs. 83.2% without), reducing `AR` and `d_total`. This validates its role in mitigating oscillatory behaviors in cluttered environments.
- **Grasp Quality Threshold (`q_th`) & Stability Window (`n_stab`):** The method was relatively robust to variations in these parameters within reasonable ranges, indicating that the VGN's base performance combined with the active perception pipeline effectively finds viable grasps. However, excessively large stability windows were detrimental for mobile grasping.
- **The Crucial Role of Grasp Executability (`J_exec`):**
 - In *normal* scenarios (where VGN could find easy top-down grasps), `ActPerMoMa-IG-only` (without `J_exec`) sometimes performed *slightly better* or comparably in terms of SR and `d_total`/`v_total`. The authors explain this by noting that VGN, trained on tabletop environments, often favors easier-to-execute top-down grasps. If such grasps are abundant, prioritizing IG alone might be sufficient.
 - **However, the "Hard Grasps" scenario fundamentally changed this.** When VGN was restricted to generating only side grasps (at 45 degrees), ActPerMoMa (full with `J_exec`) showed a significant performance improvement (e.g., 61.8% SR vs. 56.2% for `IG-only` in complex scenes, and critically, a much lower Abort Rate: 29.6% vs. 36.2%). This is the strongest empirical evidence that integrating grasp executability planning is vital when physically challenging grasps are required or when the environment limits easy access.

**Comparison with Baselines (Table III):**
- **Naive & Random Baselines:** Surprisingly, these simple non-active perception baselines performed comparably to, or even slightly better than, the active grasping approach by Breyer et al. in *simple scenes*. The "Naive" approach (simply moving close and grasping) achieved a high 95.2% SR. This suggests that for scenarios with minimal clutter and easily reachable objects, complex active perception might not be necessary.
- **ActPerMoMa vs. Baselines in Complex Scenes:** In complex scenes, ActPerMoMa demonstrated clear superiority, achieving the highest SR (92.6% vs. Naive 86.8%, Random 90.4%, Breyer 90.0%) and the lowest Abort Rate. This indicates its robustness in challenging environments where obstacles and clutter necessitate more intelligent path planning and perception.
- **ActPerMoMa's Strongest Advantage: Hard Grasps:** The comparison in the "Hard Grasps" scenario further cemented ActPerMoMa's value. It significantly outperformed all baselines in Success Rate (61.8% vs. 43.8% Naive, 24.6% Random, 47.2% Breyer) and substantially reduced the Abort Rate (29.6% vs. 49.2%, 68.4%, 48.8%, 48.8%). This confirms that ActPerMoMa's integrated approach of balancing IG with grasp executability is crucial for successful manipulation in physically constrained or challenging scenarios.

**Real Robot Demonstration:**
ActPerMoMa was successfully transferred to a real TIAGo++ robot in a cluttered room environment (Fig. 1). The system achieved an 80% success rate and 20% abort rate over 10 experiments, with no grasp failures once a grasp was initiated. This positive real-world performance, despite the inherent sim-to-real gap, highlights the practical applicability and promise of the proposed method.

**Limitations:**
The authors acknowledge limitations, including:
- **Oscillatory Motions/Deadlocks:** Reactive planning can sometimes lead to the robot getting stuck in oscillations between similar utility goals, though the momentum term helps mitigate this.
- **Limited Volumetric Information:** In highly occluded scenes, insufficient volumetric information can hinder IG computation and cause "zigzag" paths, although ActPerMoMa's path planning over views generally improves upon NBV-only methods.
- **Real-World Practical Challenges:** Camera frame rate and RGBD integration can limit control frequency (down to 5 Hz), and imperfect localization can affect continuous stable grasp detection.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant step forward in the field of mobile manipulation, particularly for tasks in unstructured and unknown environments. The ActPerMoMa framework introduces a novel and comprehensive approach that intelligently integrates active perception with task-specific objectives for mobile grasping.

The key contributions and their significance are:
1. **Holistic Path-Wise Optimization:** Unlike previous active perception methods that often focus on single next-best-views, ActPerMoMa optimizes over entire robot paths, accounting for the information gained throughout the trajectory and the cost of movement. This leads to more efficient and less wasteful motions for mobile manipulators with their larger operational envelopes.
2. **Balanced Exploration and Exploitation:** The core strength lies in its explicit trade-off between maximizing visual information gain (exploration for scene understanding) and maximizing grasp executability (exploitation for task success). This dual objective function, weighted by factors and path length, ensures that the robot not only sees enough but also positions itself optimally for action.
3. **Robustness in Challenging Scenarios:** The empirical results, particularly in complex scenes and for "hard grasps," convincingly demonstrate that integrating grasp reachability planning is critical. In scenarios where easy grasps are not readily available or the environment is cluttered, ActPerMoMa significantly outperforms baselines that rely solely on information gain or simpler heuristics. This makes the method more applicable to real-world complexities.
4. **Reactive and Adaptive Control:** The receding-horizon control strategy ensures that the robot continuously adapts its plan based on newly acquired sensory data, providing a high degree of reactivity to dynamic or partially observed environments.
5. **Practical Demonstrability:** The successful real-world deployment on a TIAGo++ robot, despite common sim-to-real gaps, underscores the practical viability and potential for real-world applications of ActPerMoMa.

The potential impact of this work is substantial. By enabling mobile manipulators to intelligently perceive and interact with unknown, cluttered environments, ActPerMoMa contributes directly to the development of more autonomous and capable robotic systems for applications in logistics, manufacturing, service robotics (e.g., household assistance), and even hazardous environments. The methodology could be extended to other mobile manipulation tasks beyond grasping, such as object placement, assembly, or dexterous interaction, by modifying the task-specific utility function. The identified limitations also provide clear directions for future research, such as leveraging deep learning for direct information gain prediction or reinforcement learning to handle complex POMDPs and inherent oscillations more robustly. This paper paves the way for a new generation of mobile manipulators that are not just mobile or dexterous, but truly "active-perceptive" in their interaction with the world.
