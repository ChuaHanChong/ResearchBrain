# Learning to Walk in Costume: Adversarial Motion Priors for Aesthetically Constrained Humanoids

> [!summary] Summary
> A research collaboration led by UCLA developed a locomotion system for the aesthetically-driven Cosmo humanoid robot, using Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP) and a robust sim-to-real transfer pipeline. This enabled stable and natural walking on hardware despite the robot's disproportionately large head and physical movement constraints.

## Problem

- Entertainment humanoids often feature non-standard morphologies, disproportionate mass distributions, and restricted movement ranges due to aesthetic shells.
- The Cosmo robot specifically has a disproportionately large head (16% of total mass), leading to a high and shifted center of mass, making it inherently top-heavy and challenging to balance.
- Aesthetic design choices limit onboard vision and restrict the robot's joint ranges of motion, complicating control and requiring specialized solutions beyond conventional methods.

## Method

- ==Employed Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP)== to train a policy that imitates human motion capture data, promoting natural, human-like movement, with a discriminator distinguishing between robot-generated and reference motions.
- Utilized a comprehensive simulation environment (Isaac Sim for analysis, Isaac Gym for parallel training) to develop policies, integrating human and robot-specific motion references.
- Implemented a robust sim-to-real transfer pipeline using extensive domain randomization (physics, external perturbations, sensor noise) and a tailored PD controller for hardware deployment.

## Results

- Achieved stable standing, robust push-recovery (regaining stability within 2 seconds), and natural, human-like walking on the physical Cosmo robot, reproducing coordinated shoulder swings and subtle head oscillations.
- Successfully managed Cosmo's top-heavy mass distribution while keeping joint torques within safe operating limits (±20 Nm) during locomotion, demonstrating respect for hardware limitations.
- Ablation studies confirmed that specific reward components (e.g., stumble prevention) and motion references (e.g., standing pose) were essential for preventing hazardous motions and enabling smooth, safe locomotion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - AMP can effectively adapt to and induce compensatory behaviors for eccentric robot morphologies, such as Cosmo's top-heavy design, while maintaining human-like motion styles.
> - Specialized safety rewards (e.g., foot stumble prevention) and diverse motion references (standing, model-based, expressive human-like gaits) are crucial for hardware protection and achieving both stability and expressiveness.
> - Cosmo's extreme mass distribution results in significantly higher Center of Mass (CoM) deviation (5x more than a biomechanically optimized robot), confirming the substantial inherent instability challenge.
