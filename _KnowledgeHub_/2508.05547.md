# Adapting Vision-Language Models Without Labels: A Comprehensive Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey provides a comprehensive, structured overview of unsupervised adaptation methods for Vision-Language Models (VLMs), introducing a novel taxonomy based on the availability of unlabeled visual data. It consolidates diverse approaches across four paradigms: Data-Free Transfer, Unsupervised Domain Transfer, Episodic Test-Time Adaptation, and Online Test-Time Adaptation, detailing their methodologies, applications, and future research challenges.

## Problem

- Pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often perform suboptimally when applied to specific downstream tasks or environments due to inherent distribution shifts.
- Traditional supervised fine-tuning methods for VLM adaptation incur high annotation costs and exhibit performance degradation under distribution shifts between training and test data.
- A unified, task-oriented survey specifically dedicated to unsupervised VLM adaptation, particularly considering the practical availability of unlabeled visual data, was lacking in the literature.

## Method

- The paper proposes a novel taxonomy that categorizes ==unsupervised VLM adaptation== methods into four distinct paradigms: ==Data-Free Transfer==, ==Unsupervised Domain Transfer==, ==Episodic Test-Time Adaptation==, and ==Online Test-Time Adaptation==.
- It systematically analyzes the fundamental methodologies, ==adaptation strategies==, and ==algorithmic designs== employed by various approaches within each of the proposed paradigms.
- The survey reviews representative benchmarks and diverse application scenarios where unsupervised VLM adaptation techniques are utilized, providing a practical overview for practitioners and researchers.

## Results

- A novel and unified taxonomy for unsupervised VLM adaptation was established, delineating Data-Free Transfer, Unsupervised Domain Transfer, Episodic Test-Time Adaptation, and Online Test-Time Adaptation.
- The survey presented a detailed analysis of core methodologies and specific strategies within each adaptation paradigm, summarizing approaches like pseudo-labeling, entropy minimization, and generative feedback utilization.
- It provided a comprehensive review of application scenarios (e.g., object classification, semantic segmentation, medical diagnosis) and their corresponding benchmark datasets, identifying critical open challenges and future research directions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unsupervised VLM adaptation methods can be effectively organized into a structured taxonomy, offering a clearer understanding of distinct approaches based on data availability constraints.
> - Diverse strategies, including text augmentation, self-training, entropy optimization, and memory mechanisms, are employed across adaptation paradigms to enhance VLM performance without requiring labels.
> - Key future research directions include developing theoretical analyses, addressing open-world scenarios, improving adversarial robustness, ensuring privacy, and exploring efficient inference for VLMs beyond current architectures and for MLLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language Models (VLMs), such as CLIP, ALIGN, Flamingo, and LLaVA, have revolutionized cross-modal reasoning by learning joint image-text representations from massive datasets. These models exhibit impressive zero-shot performance and generalization across various tasks, leading to their successful application in diverse domains like autonomous driving, robotics, and medical diagnosis. However, a fundamental challenge remains: while powerful, pre-trained VLMs often perform suboptimally when directly applied to specific downstream tasks or environments due to inherent distribution shifts and the inability of pre-training to capture the full diversity of real-world scenarios.

Earlier efforts to address this challenge primarily relied on supervised fine-tuning, which, despite its effectiveness, incurs high annotation costs and suffers performance degradation under distribution shifts between training and test data. This limitation has spurred a growing interest in unsupervised adaptation techniques, often termed zero-shot inference, test-time methods, or unsupervised tuning. These approaches aim to improve VLM performance in downstream tasks without requiring costly labeled data, making them more practical for real-world deployment.

Despite the rapid growth and increasing number of unsupervised VLM adaptation methods, the authors identify a significant gap in the existing literature: a unified, task-oriented survey dedicated specifically to this area. Previous surveys have either focused predominantly on unimodal model transfer, discussed VLM fine-tuning at a coarse-grained level, or analyzed VLM generalization from a parameter-space perspective. None, to the authors' knowledge, provide a comprehensive and structured overview specifically addressing unsupervised adaptation of VLMs, particularly considering the practical factor of unlabeled visual data availability.

This survey positions itself as a critical contribution to bridge this gap. By introducing a novel taxonomy centered on the availability and nature of unlabeled visual data, it offers a principled framework for understanding the diverse landscape of unsupervised VLM adaptation. This structured approach not only synthesizes the current state-of-the-art but also guides practitioners in selecting appropriate techniques and facilitates fair comparisons across future research. The work thus serves as a foundational resource for navigating and advancing the field, moving beyond mere empirical development to a more systematic understanding and strategic deployment of unsupervised VLM adaptation techniques.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research paper is to present a **comprehensive and structured overview of the rapidly advancing field of unsupervised adaptation methods for Vision-Language Models (VLMs).** The authors identify a notable gap in existing literature, where a unified, task-oriented survey dedicated to unsupervised VLM adaptation, especially considering the practical availability of unlabeled visual data, is lacking.

To address this, the paper sets forth several specific objectives:

1. **Propose a Novel Taxonomy:** Introduce a new classification framework for existing unsupervised VLM adaptation approaches based on the availability and nature of unlabeled visual data. This taxonomy aims to categorize methods into four distinct paradigms: Data-Free Transfer, Unsupervised Domain Transfer, Episodic Test-Time Adaptation, and Online Test-Time Adaptation.
2. **Analyze Core Methodologies and Strategies:** Within each proposed paradigm, systematically analyze the fundamental methodologies, adaptation strategies, and algorithmic designs employed by various approaches.
3. **Review Representative Benchmarks and Applications:** Explore the diverse application scenarios where unsupervised VLM adaptation is utilized, along with the corresponding benchmark datasets used for evaluation.
4. **Highlight Open Challenges and Future Directions:** Identify the current limitations, emerging trends, and key scientific questions that could inspire and guide future research in the field.

The motivation behind this work stems from the inherent limitations of pre-trained VLMs and traditional adaptation techniques:

- **Suboptimal Performance on Downstream Tasks:** Despite their powerful generalization capabilities, pre-trained VLMs often perform poorly when directly applied to specific downstream scenarios or new environments without adaptation, mainly due to distribution shifts.
- **High Annotation Costs of Supervised Fine-tuning:** Early adaptation methods relied heavily on supervised fine-tuning, which is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming due to the need for large amounts of labeled data.
- **Performance Degradation Under Distribution Shifts:** Supervised methods often struggle when the distribution of test data differs significantly from the training data, a common occurrence in real-world deployments.
- **Lack of a Unified Understanding:** The proliferation of unsupervised adaptation methods has created a fragmented research landscape, making it difficult for researchers and practitioners to grasp the field systematically, compare methods fairly, and identify the most suitable techniques for specific real-world constraints.

By addressing these motivations, the authors aim to provide a valuable resource that not only consolidates current knowledge but also facilitates strategic research and practical deployment of VLMs in label-scarce or data-sensitive environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a survey paper, the methodology focuses on the systematic collection, categorization, and analysis of existing literature on unsupervised Vision-Language Model (VLM) adaptation. The core of the paper's approach is the development and application of a novel taxonomy to structure the diverse landscape of research in this area.

The methodological steps and approach can be summarized as follows:

1. **Literature Review and Problem Identification:** The authors begin by reviewing the state-of-the-art in VLMs, their generalization capabilities, and the challenges associated with their direct application to downstream tasks. They identify the transition from supervised fine-tuning to unsupervised adaptation as a key trend, highlighting the practical limitations of labeled data and the prevalence of distribution shifts. Crucially, they identify the lack of a unified, task-oriented survey in this specific domain as a significant gap.

2. **Proposed Taxonomy based on Unlabeled Visual Data Availability:** The central methodological contribution is the introduction of a new taxonomy. This taxonomy categorizes existing unsupervised VLM adaptation methods based on **the availability and nature of unlabeled visual data** during the adaptation process. This criterion is chosen because it is deemed a "practically critical factor in real-world deployment." The four paradigms defined are:
 - **Data-Free Transfer (no data):** The most constrained setting, where no visual data from the downstream task is available, relying solely on textual class names.
 - **Unsupervised Domain Transfer (abundant data):** Assumes access to a large, static collection of unlabeled target data, allowing for offline adaptation.
 - **Episodic Test-Time Adaptation (batch data):** Adapts the model to a small batch of test instances during the inference phase.
 - **Online Test-Time Adaptation (streaming data):** Continuously adapts the model to a sequential stream of incoming test data points in real-time.

3. **Systematic Analysis within Each Paradigm:** For each of the four paradigms, the authors conduct a detailed analysis of existing approaches. This involves:
 - **Identifying Core Methodologies:** Extracting the fundamental techniques and principles underpinning the methods (e.g., self-training, entropy optimization, pseudo-labeling, memory mechanisms).
 - **Categorizing Adaptation Strategies:** Further subdividing the methodologies into specific strategies (e.g., Text Augmentation, Image Utilization, Network Modification for Data-Free Transfer; Self-Training, Entropy Optimization, External Resource Utilization for Unsupervised Domain Transfer).
 - **Reviewing Representative Works:** Citing and briefly explaining key papers that exemplify each strategy, providing a comprehensive overview of the technical landscape.

4. **Review of Related Research Topics:** Before delving into the core taxonomy, the survey provides contextual background on several related areas to ensure a comprehensive understanding. These include:
 - **Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** A brief overview of their architecture and training.
 - **Zero-Shot Learning (ZSL):** Its foundational role in unsupervised VLM adaptation.
 - **Supervised Fine-Tuning of VLMs:** Highlighting its limitations and contrasting it with the survey's focus.
 - **Source-Free Domain Adaptation (SFDA):** Its relevance to unsupervised VLM adaptation when source data is inaccessible.
 - **Traditional Test-Time Adaptation (TTA):** How its mechanisms are repurposed for VLMs.

5. **Application Scenarios and Benchmarks:** The survey dedicates a section to reviewing the diverse application scenarios where these unsupervised techniques are employed. For each application (e.g., Object Classification, Semantic Segmentation, Out-of-Distribution Detection), relevant and widely used benchmark datasets are listed, providing a practical guide for evaluation.

6. **Identification of Research Challenges and Future Directions:** Based on the comprehensive review, the authors synthesize emerging trends and critical gaps in the field. This includes outlining theoretical analyses, open-world scenarios, adversarial robustness, privacy considerations, efficient inference, exploring VLMs beyond CLIP, extending to MLLMs, new downstream tasks, and critically, addressing failure modes and negative transfer.

By following this structured, taxonomy-driven approach, the survey aims to establish a systematic understanding of unsupervised VLM adaptation, guide future research, and aid practitioners in selecting suitable techniques for real-world deployment. The actively maintained GitHub repository further enhances the utility and dynamism of this methodological approach.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The main "findings and results" of this survey paper are not experimental outcomes but rather a comprehensive, structured synthesis of the research landscape concerning unsupervised adaptation of Vision-Language Models (VLMs). The key contributions and results presented by the authors are:

1. **A Novel, Unified Taxonomy for Unsupervised VLM Adaptation:** The most significant finding is the proposal of a novel and crucial taxonomy that categorizes existing unsupervised VLM adaptation methods into four distinct paradigms. This categorization is based on the **availability and nature of unlabeled visual data** during the adaptation process, a practically critical factor often overlooked.
 - **Data-Free Transfer:** Adapting VLMs with *no visual data* from the downstream task, relying solely on textual class names. This is the most constrained setting.
 - **Unsupervised Domain Transfer:** Adapting VLMs with *abundant unlabeled data* from the downstream task, typically in an offline setting.
 - **Episodic Test-Time Adaptation:** Adapting VLMs to *a batch of unlabeled test instances* at inference time.
 - **Online Test-Time Adaptation:** Continuously adapting VLMs to a *streaming sequence of unlabeled test data* points in real-time.

2. **Detailed Analysis of Core Methodologies and Strategies within Each Paradigm:** For each of the four paradigms, the survey systematically dissects the underlying techniques:
 - **Data-Free Transfer:** Strategies include **Text Augmentation** (leveraging LLMs to enrich class descriptions, generate subclass names, or construct hierarchical structures), **Image Utilization** (retrieving or synthesizing visual data from external datasets/generative models), and **Network Modification** (architectural enhancements for dense prediction tasks like segmentation).
 - **Unsupervised Domain Transfer:** Key strategies are **Self-Training** (generating and refining pseudo-labels, often with consistency regularization or curriculum learning), **Entropy Optimization** (minimizing sample-level entropy for confident predictions while maximizing category-level entropy to avoid mode collapse), and **External Resource Utilization** (incorporating LLMs, MLLMs, or knowledge distillation from stronger models).
 - **Episodic Test-Time Adaptation:** Approaches include **Entropy Minimization** (tuning prompts or parameters to reduce prediction uncertainty on test batches, often with data augmentation), **Feedback Signal** (leveraging generative feedback from diffusion models or CLIP-like models), **Distribution Alignment** (aligning test sample distributions with source characteristics or refining representations), and **Self-Supervised Learning** (e.g., contrastive prompt tuning).
 - **Online Test-Time Adaptation:** Methods utilize **Pseudo-Labeling** (assigning and refining pseudo-labels to streaming data, mitigating noise), **Memory Mechanisms** (storing and retrieving feature representations and pseudo-labels from historical data for progressive refinement), and **Distribution Modeling** (using probabilistic frameworks like Gaussian estimations to refine predictions).

3. **Comprehensive Review of Application Scenarios and Benchmarks:** The survey provides an extensive overview of how unsupervised VLM adaptation is applied across various tasks:
 - **Object Classification:** Evaluating fine-grained generalization and robustness to distribution shifts using benchmarks like ImageNet variants, Caltech101, OxfordPets, and domain adaptation datasets (Office-Home, DomainNet).
 - **Semantic Segmentation:** Assessing general and fine-grained object segmentation on datasets such as PASCAL VOC, COCO Stuff, ADE20K, and Cityscapes.
 - **Visual Reasoning:** Employing datasets like Bongard-HOI for context-dependent reasoning.
 - **Out-of-Distribution Detection:** Categorizing detection into far, near, and fine-grained OOD, using combinations of datasets like CIFAR-100, iNaturalist, and ImageNet-O.
 - **Text-Image Retrieval and Image Captioning:** Using datasets like MS-COCO, Flickr30K, and Nocaps.
 - **Beyond Vanilla Object Images:** Specialized applications in **Medical Image Diagnosis** (chest X-rays, diabetic retinopathy, brain tumor detection) and **Video-based Tasks** (action recognition, temporal action localization using HMDB-51, Kinetics-600, ActivityNet).

4. **Identification of Key Research Challenges and Future Directions:** The survey concludes by outlining critical open problems and promising avenues for future research, synthesizing gaps discovered during the analysis. These include the need for theoretical analysis, robust methods for open-world scenarios, adversarial robustness, privacy considerations, efficient inference, exploring VLMs beyond CLIP, extending to MLLMs, investigating new downstream tasks, and crucially, systematically documenting failure modes and negative transfer.

In essence, the survey successfully delivers a much-needed structured perspective on unsupervised VLM adaptation, consolidating disparate research efforts into a coherent framework, detailing the technical landscape, and charting a clear path for future innovation and development.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This comprehensive survey on "Adapting Vision-Language Models Without Labels" holds significant implications for both academic research and practical applications of Vision-Language Models (VLMs). Its potential impact spans several key areas:

1. **Structuring and Unifying a Fragmented Field:** The most immediate and profound impact is the introduction of a novel taxonomy that systematically categorizes unsupervised VLM adaptation methods. By classifying approaches based on the availability and nature of unlabeled visual data (Data-Free, Unsupervised Domain, Episodic TTA, Online TTA), the survey brings much-needed order to a rapidly growing and often fragmented research area. This unified framework helps researchers to better understand the distinct assumptions and challenges associated with different adaptation scenarios, fostering clearer communication and comparison within the community.

2. **Guiding Future Research:** By meticulously outlining existing methodologies, strategies, and benchmarks, the survey serves as a critical reference point for future investigations. The dedicated section on "Research Challenges and Future Directions" is particularly impactful, as it identifies crucial open problems such as theoretical analysis, open-world scenarios, adversarial robustness, privacy, and efficient inference. This clear articulation of gaps and promising avenues will undoubtedly inspire new research questions, stimulate innovative solutions, and direct efforts towards high-impact areas. For instance, the call to investigate VLMs beyond CLIP and extend to Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) highlights emerging frontiers.

3. **Facilitating Fair Comparisons and Benchmarking:** The proposed taxonomy and detailed review of benchmarks allow for more principled comparisons between different adaptation methods. Researchers can now more accurately assess whether methods are truly comparable based on their data availability assumptions, leading to more rigorous evaluation and a better understanding of each technique's strengths and weaknesses. This will help prevent "illusion of progress" where methods are compared across incompatible settings.

4. **Informing Practical Deployment:** For practitioners, the survey provides a valuable guide for selecting appropriate VLM adaptation techniques based on real-world constraints. The taxonomy directly addresses a practical concern: the availability of unlabeled visual data, which varies significantly across different deployment scenarios (e.g., sensitive medical imaging, streaming sensor data, or completely data-free environments). This practical guidance will enable more effective and efficient deployment of powerful VLMs in various applications, particularly in resource-constrained or data-scarce settings.

5. **Addressing Critical Limitations of VLMs:** The focus on unsupervised adaptation directly addresses the high cost of data annotation and the vulnerability of VLMs to distribution shifts when deployed in novel environments. By providing a comprehensive overview of label-free adaptation strategies, the survey contributes to making VLMs more robust, adaptable, and accessible for a wider range of real-world applications where obtaining labeled data is impractical or impossible.

6. **Highlighting Ethical and Practical Concerns:** The inclusion of "Privacy Considerations," "Adversarial Robustness," and "Failure Mode and Negative Transfer" as future directions underscores the growing importance of trustworthiness and ethical considerations in AI deployment. By flagging these issues, the survey encourages the community to develop more robust, secure, and transparent VLM adaptation methods, particularly for sensitive domains like healthcare and autonomous driving.

In conclusion, "Adapting Vision-Language Models Without Labels: A Comprehensive Survey" is a timely and essential contribution to the field of multimodal AI. It not only consolidates existing knowledge into a coherent structure but also lays a clear roadmap for future research, offering both academic insights and practical guidance for advancing the reliable and widespread deployment of Vision-Language Models.
