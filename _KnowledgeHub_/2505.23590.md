# Jigsaw-R1: A Study of Rule-based Visual Reinforcement Learning with Jigsaw Puzzles

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from KU Leuven and collaborators explored rule-based reinforcement learning (RL) for multimodal large language models (MLLMs) using jigsaw puzzles as a structured experimental framework. They found that MLLMs robustly generalize to various visual downstream tasks after training on jigsaw puzzles, with complex reasoning patterns being pre-existing and amplified rather than emerging, and demonstrated RL's superior generalization compared to Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT).

## Problem

- Extending successful rule-based RL paradigms from textual domains to complex multimodal MLLMs for perception-heavy tasks.
- Investigating whether explicit, lengthy reasoning is beneficial or detrimental in perception-heavy visual tasks, unlike reasoning-intensive textual tasks.
- Lack of structured, algorithmically verifiable visual benchmarks with inherent ground truth for generating rule-based rewards without expensive human annotation.

## Method

- Adapted ==jigsaw puzzles as a structured experimental framework==, leveraging their inherent ground truth, adjustable difficulty (m x n grid), and demand for spatial reasoning.
- Implemented a ==two-component rule-based reward system== (accuracy and format) to guide MLLM training using an on-policy RL algorithm (GRPO).
- Compared =="thinking" (explicit reasoning chain) and "non-thinking" (direct answer) prompting strategies== to analyze the role of explicit reasoning.

## Results

- MLLMs rapidly learn and generalize from simple **2x1** jigsaw puzzles to more complex **3x1** configurations after training, despite initial poor performance.
- Models trained on jigsaw puzzles demonstrate improved performance across various downstream visual tasks (CV-Bench, MMVP, SAT, Super-CLEVR), indicating robust transfer capabilities.
- Open-source MLLMs generally perform better with direct answers ("non-thinking") in perception tasks, and an SFT "cold start" can be detrimental to subsequent RL optimization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complex reasoning patterns in MLLMs are pre-existing and activated or amplified by structured tasks and increased difficulty, rather than suddenly emerging (the "aha moment" is not observed).
> - Rule-based RL training offers superior generalization to diverse downstream visual tasks compared to Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for MLLMs.
> - The effectiveness of generalization depends on specific puzzle configurations, with 'pair' jigsaw puzzles often yielding better transfer and larger puzzle sizes improving generalization for non-thinking models.
