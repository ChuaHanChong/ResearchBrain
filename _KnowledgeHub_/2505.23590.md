# Jigsaw-R1: A Study of Rule-based Visual Reinforcement Learning with Jigsaw Puzzles

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from KU Leuven and collaborators explored rule-based reinforcement learning (RL) for multimodal large language models (MLLMs) using jigsaw puzzles as a structured experimental framework. They found that MLLMs robustly generalize to various visual downstream tasks after training on jigsaw puzzles, with complex reasoning patterns being pre-existing and amplified rather than emerging, and demonstrated RL's superior generalization compared to Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT).

## Problem

- Extending successful rule-based RL paradigms from textual domains to complex multimodal MLLMs for perception-heavy tasks.
- Investigating whether explicit, lengthy reasoning is beneficial or detrimental in perception-heavy visual tasks, unlike reasoning-intensive textual tasks.
- Lack of structured, algorithmically verifiable visual benchmarks with inherent ground truth for generating rule-based rewards without expensive human annotation.

## Method

- Adapted ==jigsaw puzzles as a structured experimental framework==, leveraging their inherent ground truth, adjustable difficulty (m x n grid), and demand for spatial reasoning.
- Implemented a ==two-component rule-based reward system== (accuracy and format) to guide MLLM training using an on-policy RL algorithm (GRPO).
- Compared =="thinking" (explicit reasoning chain) and "non-thinking" (direct answer) prompting strategies== to analyze the role of explicit reasoning.

## Results

- MLLMs rapidly learn and generalize from simple **2x1** jigsaw puzzles to more complex **3x1** configurations after training, despite initial poor performance.
- Models trained on jigsaw puzzles demonstrate improved performance across various downstream visual tasks (CV-Bench, MMVP, SAT, Super-CLEVR), indicating robust transfer capabilities.
- Open-source MLLMs generally perform better with direct answers ("non-thinking") in perception tasks, and an SFT "cold start" can be detrimental to subsequent RL optimization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complex reasoning patterns in MLLMs are pre-existing and activated or amplified by structured tasks and increased difficulty, rather than suddenly emerging (the "aha moment" is not observed).
> - Rule-based RL training offers superior generalization to diverse downstream visual tasks compared to Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for MLLMs.
> - The effectiveness of generalization depends on specific puzzle configurations, with 'pair' jigsaw puzzles often yielding better transfer and larger puzzle sizes improving generalization for non-thinking models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper makes a significant contribution at the intersection of multimodal large language models (MLLMs), reinforcement learning (RL), and computer vision pretext tasks. It directly addresses the evolving challenges of extending powerful language model capabilities to handle visual information and complex reasoning.

The foundation of this work is built upon the success of rule-based RL in *textual* domains, notably exemplified by **DeepSeek-R1** (Guo et al., 2025). DeepSeek-R1 demonstrated that simple rule-based RL can enhance LLM performance, mitigate reward hacking, and induce robust generalization across diverse tasks like mathematics and coding. The current paper aims to explore whether these advantages translate to the more complex *multimodal* setting, where MLLMs must integrate and reason over both text and vision. This is a critical but largely underexplored area, as multimodal environments introduce unique difficulties and potential deviations from text-only findings (Gandhi et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025e).

A key aspect of this research is the re-introduction of **jigsaw puzzles** as a pretext task. Jigsaw puzzles have a long history in computer vision as a self-supervised learning mechanism for Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Vision Transformers (ViTs) (Doersch et al., 2015; Noroozi & Favaro, 2016; Carlucci et al., 2019; Chen et al., 2023). By training models to reassemble shuffled image patches, rich visual feature representations can be learned and transferred to downstream tasks. This paper pioneers the adoption of jigsaw puzzles as pretext tasks in the era of LLMs, leveraging their inherent properties (algorithmic ground truth, adjustable difficulty, demand for spatial reasoning) for rule-based visual RL. This bridges a gap between classic computer vision techniques and modern MLLM training paradigms.

The paper also directly engages with ongoing debates in the RL for LLMs community, particularly concerning the **"aha moment"**—the phenomenon of emergent, complex reasoning patterns and increased completion lengths observed in models like DeepSeek-R1. While some studies in textual or multimodal mathematical domains report this emergence (Guo et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025a; Meng et al., 2025), others, especially in perception-heavy tasks, question its presence or utility, sometimes finding concise answers more effective than lengthy reasoning (Jiang et al., 2025; Lai et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025c). This study contributes empirical evidence specifically for visual perception tasks.

Furthermore, the work aligns with the growing trend of **rule-based RL without labeled datasets**. Just as textual logic puzzles like Knights and Knaves (Xie et al., 2025b) or Sudoku (Chen et al., 2025b) provide algorithmically generated ground truth, jigsaw puzzles offer a similar advantage for visual domains, reducing the dependence on expensive human annotation. This approach is vital for scaling AI capabilities efficiently.

Finally, the paper critically compares **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with RL** in terms of generalization, an important practical consideration for MLLM development. The findings on SFT's limitations and the potential detriment of SFT "cold starts" for RL optimization provide valuable guidance for practitioners. Overall, Jigsaw-R1 pushes the boundaries of MLLM capabilities in visual reasoning, offering a robust experimental framework and key insights into how MLLMs learn and generalize in multimodal environments using rule-based RL.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to conduct a **comprehensive study of rule-based reinforcement learning within the multimodal domain**, specifically focusing on perception-heavy tasks. The authors explicitly acknowledge that applying rule-based RL to MLLMs presents unique challenges and potential deviations from findings in text-only contexts, particularly for tasks heavily reliant on visual perception.

The core motivation behind this study stems from several observations and unmet needs in the field:

1. **Bridging the Gap from Text to Multimodality:** While rule-based RL (e.g., DeepSeek-R1) has demonstrated remarkable success in enhancing LLM reasoning in textual domains, its application and effectiveness in multimodal settings, where models must integrate visual and textual information, remain largely underexplored. The authors aim to fill this knowledge gap.
2. **Addressing Unique Challenges in Perception-Heavy Tasks:** Unlike reasoning-intensive tasks (e.g., mathematics, coding) that benefit from extended, step-by-step reasoning, perception-heavy tasks (e.g., spatial reasoning) often permit concise answers directly from visual understanding. The authors hypothesize that an explicit, lengthy thinking process might even be detrimental in such domains, motivating an investigation into the role of reasoning.
3. **The Need for Structured, Verifiable Visual Benchmarks:** Traditional MLLM benchmarks often lack inherent ground truth for reasoning processes, making rule-based reward generation challenging and often requiring expensive human annotation. The authors sought a task that naturally provides ground truth for rule-based rewards across various visual domains.

To address these motivations, the authors chose **jigsaw puzzles** as their experimental framework, motivated by three key characteristics:

- **Inherent Ground Truth:** Jigsaw puzzles naturally provide a verifiable solution, enabling direct generation of rule-based rewards without human annotation.
- **Adjustable Difficulty:** The complexity of jigsaw puzzles can be easily scaled by varying the number of pieces (m x n grid), allowing for a structured experimental framework to study learning and generalization across different difficulty levels.
- **Demands Complex Decision-Making:** Solving jigsaw puzzles requires an interplay of step-by-step reasoning and visual perception (e.g., iteratively placing pieces considering local and global coherence), making it a rich analog for the complex decision-making processes MLLMs might employ.

Based on this framework, the research sets out to answer five specific key research questions:

1. **How do contemporary MLLMs perform on jigsaw puzzles** initially and after fine-tuning, and do they generalize to unseen configurations?
2. **Can MLLMs trained on jigsaw puzzles develop generalizable abilities** applicable to other visual downstream tasks, and what task configurations impact this generalization?
3. **Is an explicit thinking process beneficial for rule-based visual RL** in perception-heavy tasks like jigsaw puzzles, given that it might be detrimental?
4. **Does the "aha moment" (emergence of complex reasoning patterns) still emerge** in MLLMs trained on jigsaw puzzles, or do these patterns manifest differently?
5. **How does Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) compare with RL** in terms of generalization, and what is the impact of an SFT cold start phase on subsequent RL optimization?

These objectives collectively aim to deepen the understanding of how MLLMs can acquire robust visual reasoning capabilities through rule-based RL, offering insights into effective training strategies and the nature of emergent intelligence in multimodal contexts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in "Jigsaw-R1" is meticulously designed to systematically study rule-based visual RL using jigsaw puzzles. It encompasses a structured task formulation, a custom rule-based reward system, and comprehensive experimental setups involving various MLLMs and datasets.

#### 3.1. Task Design: Jigsaw Puzzles for MLLMs

The core of the methodology revolves around adapting jigsaw puzzles for MLLMs:

- **Jigsaw Image Generation:** An input image is divided into an *m x n* grid of patches, which are then randomly shuffled to create the jigsaw image. Difficulty is directly controlled by *m* and *n*. Patches are assigned sequential position indices (1 to *mn*).
- **Question Types:**
 - **Full:** MLLMs must identify the original position index for each shuffled patch, effectively reconstructing the image. This is a complex task with *mn!* permutations.
 - **Pair:** Two patches are randomly selected, and the MLLM must determine their relative positions in the original image from a set of 2 (for 1x*n* or *m*x1 grids) or 8 possible directions. This is structured as a multiple-choice question.
 - **Box (Appendix A.2):** This variant evaluates both spatial reasoning and visual grounding. The model needs to identify the bounding box coordinates for a patch that originally belonged to a specific target region, even after shuffling.
- **Prompting Strategies:** Two distinct approaches are explored to investigate the role of explicit reasoning:
 - **Thinking:** MLLMs are instructed to first output their reasoning process within `<think>` and `</think>` tags, followed by the final answer in `<answer>` and `</answer>` tags.
 - **Non-thinking:** MLLMs are prompted to provide the final answer directly, without intermediate reasoning steps.

#### 3.2. Rule-based Rewards

A two-component reward system is central to the rule-based RL approach:

- **Accuracy Reward:**
 - **Full:** Calculated as the proportion of correctly identified position indices (fractional, 0 to 1).
 - **Pair/Image Rotation:** Binary (1 for correct choice, 0 otherwise).
 - **Box:** Intersection over Union (IoU) between predicted and ground truth bounding boxes.
- **Format Reward:** A binary reward (0.5 or 0) given if the model's output adheres to the prescribed format (e.g., correct grid for full questions, single letter for pair questions, and correct tag usage/sequence for thinking prompts). An ablation study in Appendix B confirms the robustness of the chosen weights.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setups

- **Reinforcement Learning Algorithm:** GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) is used, an on-policy RL algorithm, with specific hyperparameters for KL coefficient, clipping value, and training steps (1,000 for thinking, 2,000 for non-thinking due to computational cost).
- **Datasets:**
 - **Training Data (for Jigsaw and Image Rotation):** COCO (Lin et al., 2014) train2014 split, leveraging its diverse image collection. Ground truth permutations are randomly generated.
 - **Downstream Task Evaluation Datasets:** To assess generalization, models are evaluated on four benchmarks:
 - **CV-Bench** (Tong et al., 2024a): Spatial relationship, object counting (2D understanding), depth order, relative distance (3D understanding).
 - **MMVP** (Tong et al., 2024b): 9 fundamental visual patterns (orientation, perspective, structural characteristics).
 - **SAT** (Ray et al., 2024): Synthetic dataset with indoor scenes, categorized into CV-Bench task types.
 - **Super-CLEVR** (Li et al., 2023): Synthetic dataset for counting problems.
- **Models Evaluated:** A mix of proprietary and open-source MLLMs are used:
 - **Proprietary:** GPT-4.1, GPT-4.1-mini (OpenAI), Claude 3.5 Haiku (Anthropic).
 - **Open-Source:** Qwen2-VL-2B-Base, Qwen2.5-VL-72B/7B/3B (Wang et al., 2024b; Bai et al., 2025a), InternVL2.5-2B (Chen et al., 2024).
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** For comparison, SFT data is prepared: for thinking, rejection sampling is used to keep only instances where reasoning leads to the correct answer; for non-thinking, models are fine-tuned directly on ground-truth answers. This ensures a fair comparison with RL.

This comprehensive and systematic approach allows the authors to rigorously investigate the defined research questions, covering model performance, generalization, the role of explicit reasoning, the nature of reasoning emergence, and the comparative efficacy of RL versus SFT.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings, addressing each of the posed research questions:

#### 4.1. Research Question #1: How Do MLLMs Perform on Jigsaw Puzzles?

- **Initial Struggle, Efficient Learning:** MLLMs, including powerful proprietary ones, perform near random guessing on the simplest jigsaw puzzles (e.g., 2x1) *without* task-specific training. However, they demonstrate rapid and efficient learning through fine-tuning, achieving near-perfect accuracy (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-3B converges quickly).
- **Generalization to Complexity:** Models trained solely on simple 2x1 puzzles successfully generalize their learned abilities to more complex, unseen configurations (e.g., 3x1), indicating robust transfer capabilities within the jigsaw domain.

#### 4.2. Research Question #2: Can MLLMs Trained to Solve Jigsaw Puzzles Develop Generalizable Abilities Applicable to Other Visual Tasks?

- **Robust Generalization to Downstream Tasks:** Training MLLMs on jigsaw puzzles generally leads to improved performance on a variety of downstream visual tasks (CV-Bench, MMVP, SAT, Super-CLEVR). Notably, models trained on COCO jigsaw puzzles generalized effectively to synthetic datasets like SAT and Super-CLEVR.
- **Impact of Puzzle Configuration:** The effectiveness of generalization is highly dependent on specific task configurations:
 - **Puzzle Size:** Larger, more challenging puzzle sizes or a curriculum learning approach (mixing sizes) during training lead to better generalization for non-thinking models.
 - **Question Type:** "Pair" jigsaw puzzles result in superior generalization compared to "full" jigsaw puzzles, possibly due to their analogy to downstream tasks (multi-choice, explicit spatial relationships).
 - **Training Dataset:** Aligning the training dataset with the target domain (e.g., training on SAT for SAT tasks) significantly enhances performance, and leveraging label-free jigsaw puzzles for training on test sets can further boost gains.

#### 4.3. Research Question #3: Is an Explicit Thinking Process Still Beneficial When Employing Rule-based Visual RL to Solve Jigsaw Puzzles?

- **Learning and Generalization with or Without Reasoning:** MLLMs can learn from jigsaw puzzles and generalize to downstream tasks whether they generate an explicit reasoning chain ("thinking") or provide direct answers ("non-thinking").
- **Open-Source Models Favor Direct Answering:** Open-source MLLMs typically achieve stronger performance when directly answering questions. Models trained to think, even after fine-tuning, show weaker generalization on downstream tasks compared to direct-answering models that haven't been fine-tuned.
- **Neglect of Reasoning Process:** Models, even when trained for step-by-step reasoning, often ignore the thinking process. InternVL2.5-2B, for instance, significantly reduces completion length by directly embedding the answer in its "thinking." Qwen models may present reasoning steps that are inconsistent with their final, correct answers (as verified by GPT-4.1).

#### 4.4. Research Question #4: Does the Aha Moment Still Emerge in MLLMs Trained on Jigsaw Puzzles?

- **Pre-existing Complex Reasoning Patterns:** The "aha moment" (sudden emergence of complex reasoning) is *not* observed. Instead, complex reasoning patterns (verification, backtracking, subgoal setting, backward chaining, as defined by Gandhi et al., 2025) are found to be *pre-existing* within MLLMs (even instruction-tuned models and base models like Qwen2-VL-2B-Base) and are readily activated by tasks with inherent reasoning structures like jigsaw puzzles.
- **Evolution, Not Emergence:** The frequency of these reasoning patterns demonstrably increases throughout the training process and in response to greater task difficulty (e.g., larger jigsaw puzzles). This suggests an evolution or amplification of latent capabilities rather than a sudden emergence.

#### 4.5. Research Question #5: How Does Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) Compare with RL in Terms of Generalization?

- **RL Superior for Generalization:** RL consistently exhibits more effective generalization compared to SFT, whether SFT is applied to reasoning chains or direct answers.
- **SFT Cold Start Can Be Detrimental:** Initiating training with an SFT "cold start" phase, though sometimes used to help models learn specific output formats, can diminish the effectiveness of subsequent RL optimization. For this task, the models rapidly learned output formats without an SFT cold start.

#### 4.6. Image Rotation (Appendix C)

- A preliminary study on image rotation as an alternative pretext task shows similar trends in learning and generalization, though jigsaw puzzles yield more effective generalization overall. This suggests that combining multiple visual pretext tasks could be beneficial.

These findings collectively provide a nuanced understanding of rule-based visual RL, highlighting its potential to enhance MLLM capabilities for perception-heavy tasks and offering practical guidelines for training methodologies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Jigsaw-R1" paper contributes significantly to the field of multimodal AI, particularly in understanding and advancing the capabilities of MLLMs through reinforcement learning. Its findings carry substantial implications for both theoretical research and practical applications.

#### 5.1. Advancing Multimodal Reinforcement Learning

This work represents one of the first comprehensive studies of rule-based RL in a multimodal context, specifically for perception-heavy tasks. By extending the successful paradigm of DeepSeek-R1 from text to vision, it lays crucial groundwork for developing more robust and generalizable MLLMs. It demonstrates that rule-based RL is a powerful mechanism for MLLMs to acquire complex visual reasoning skills, moving beyond simple classification or object detection to structured problem-solving.

#### 5.2. Re-evaluating the "Aha Moment" and Reasoning Emergence

Perhaps one of the most significant theoretical impacts is the challenge to the "aha moment" narrative in perception-heavy tasks. The finding that complex reasoning patterns are *pre-existing* and *activated* rather than abruptly *emerging* suggests a shift in focus for AI research. Instead of merely observing emergence, the community can now investigate how to better *elicit, amplify, and refine* these latent reasoning capabilities within MLLMs. This implies that base MLLMs may possess more inherent reasoning potential than previously thought, which can be unlocked and strengthened through appropriate training paradigms like rule-based RL with structured tasks.

#### 5.3. Utility of Algorithmic Pretext Tasks for MLLMs

The successful re-introduction of jigsaw puzzles (and, to a lesser extent, image rotation) as effective pretext tasks for MLLMs highlights their immense practical value. These tasks offer **algorithmic ground truth**, eliminating the need for expensive and labor-intensive human annotation, which is a major bottleneck in scaling AI models. This opens avenues for developing truly self-supervised or autonomously trained MLLMs that can learn complex visual and spatial reasoning skills from vast amounts of unlabeled data, significantly reducing development costs and time. The "label-free" nature makes them ideal for test-time training or for generating synthetic training data.

#### 5.4. Optimized Training Strategies for MLLMs

The comparative analysis between RL and SFT offers critical guidance for MLLM development. The clear demonstration of RL's superior generalization ability, coupled with the observation that an SFT "cold start" can hinder subsequent RL optimization, provides actionable insights for designing more effective MLLM training pipelines. Furthermore, the findings on optimal puzzle configurations (e.g., "pair" questions, larger sizes, domain-aligned datasets) provide a roadmap for tailoring pretext tasks to maximize transfer learning to specific downstream applications. The insight that direct answering is often more effective for open-source models in perception tasks challenges the assumption that explicit reasoning is always beneficial.

#### 5.5. Foundations for Future Research and Applications

The paper explicitly outlines several promising directions for future work, which themselves indicate the broader impact potential:
- **Skill Alignment:** Emphasizes the need for data-centric ablation studies to determine how to best integrate jigsaw puzzles with other pretext tasks for broader skill development, moving towards a more holistic MLLM training.
- **Addressing Data Contamination:** Raises awareness about a persistent challenge in MLLM evaluation, underscoring the importance of robust evaluation protocols.
- **Integration with Advanced Models:** Calls for exploring this framework with cutting-edge visual reasoning models (OpenAI o3/o4-mini) and multimodal generative models, paving the way for truly autonomous learning environments where models can generate their own inputs and learn from experience (Silver & Sutton, 2025).
- **Cross-Modal Transfer:** Suggests extending the approach to pretext tasks in other modalities (video, audio, point clouds), fostering generalized multimodal intelligence.

In essence, "Jigsaw-R1" provides a valuable "piece of jigsaw" to the collective understanding of rule-based visual RL. It not only demonstrates a practical method for enhancing MLLM visual reasoning but also contributes to fundamental discussions about learning, generalization, and the nature of intelligence in advanced AI systems, setting the stage for future advancements in more capable and autonomous MLLMs.
