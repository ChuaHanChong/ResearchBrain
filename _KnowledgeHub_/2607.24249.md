# SILICA: Repurposing Diffusion Priors for Joint Glass Segmentation and Depth Estimation

> [!summary] Summary
> The research introduces SILICA, a method that adapts pre-trained text-to-image diffusion models for joint glass segmentation and depth estimation from a single image. It achieved improved performance over existing baselines in transparent surface perception and enabled robust metric depth recovery, facilitating safer robotic navigation in glass-rich environments.

## Problem

- Standard depth sensors fail on transparent surfaces, leading to corrupted 3D maps and navigation hazards for autonomous systems.
- Existing learning-based glass perception models lack generalization due to the scarcity of real-world datasets with paired RGB and metric depth annotations for transparent surfaces.
- Foundational monocular depth estimators often predict the depth of background objects seen through glass, not the glass surface itself, and current datasets have a domain disparity between real-world segmentation and synthetic depth data.

## Method

- A unified pipeline, **SILICA**, repurposes a pre-trained text-to-image ==latent diffusion model== (Stable Diffusion's U-Net) for ==deterministic, single-step joint regression== of glass segmentation masks and ==affine-invariant depth maps==.
- A ==CLIP-conditioned cross-attention== mechanism robustly routes task-specific information within the shared U-Net, allowing effective learning from combined real-world segmentation and synthetic depth data.
- Metric depth is recovered by aligning the predicted affine-invariant depth with noisy raw sensor depth on non-glass background regions, then fusing this aligned depth within the predicted glass areas.

## Results

- SILICA achieved state-of-the-art performance in glass segmentation, consistently outperforming baselines (e.g., SAM3) with an mIoU of **77.83** on unseen datasets and **91.60** on GDD.
- The model demonstrated superior monocular depth estimation on glass-aware datasets (e.g., Mirage 18k, GW-Depth), improving AbsRel by up to **20%** compared to foundation models like Lotus-D and Depth Anything V2.
- Real-world deployment on an autonomous wheelchair validated its practicality, achieving **5 Hz** inference and enabling safe navigation and improved 3D mapping in glass-structured environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pre-trained text-to-image diffusion models contain rich spatial and semantic priors that can be effectively repurposed for specialized perception tasks like glass segmentation and depth estimation through deterministic, single-step latent regression.
> - The CLIP-conditioned multimodal pathway of diffusion models can serve as a robust task-routing mechanism, allowing a single U-Net to jointly learn from diverse data modalities and domains (real-world segmentation, synthetic depth) without task interference.
> - Accurate glass segmentation is critical for recovering metric depth on transparent surfaces by enabling the explicit filtering of unreliable raw sensor data before aligning affine-invariant predictions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The perception of transparent structures, such as glass, presents a persistent challenge in robotic perception and computer vision. Standard depth sensors, including LiDAR, RGB-D cameras, and stereo systems, typically fail on these surfaces due to phenomena like specular reflection, refraction, and visual ambiguity, leading to corrupted 3D maps and potential navigation hazards for autonomous systems. While specialized hardware solutions, such as acoustic sensors, exist, they often produce sparse outputs and have extensive calibration dependencies, limiting their practical utility.

Learning-based monocular depth estimation has emerged as a software-centric alternative. Early approaches often focused on 2D boundary segmentation of transparent objects, but these methods lack the explicit 3D geometric context necessary for robotic navigation. More recently, "glass-aware" monocular depth estimators have been developed; however, these models often exhibit limited generalization capabilities to unfamiliar environments due to a scarcity of real-world datasets providing paired RGB and metric depth annotations for transparent surfaces. Foundational monocular depth estimators, while demonstrating advancements in general scene understanding, also struggle with transparent structures, frequently predicting the depth of background objects rather than the glass surface itself when prominent features are visible behind it.

Simultaneously, the field of generative models has seen rapid progress, particularly with text-to-image latent diffusion models (LDMs) like Stable Diffusion. These models, trained on internet-scale data, capture extensive spatial priors and complex structural hierarchies. Recent works such as Marigold and GeoWizard have begun to repurpose these diffusion priors for monocular depth estimation, achieving notable zero-shot generalization with resource-efficient fine-tuning. However, these methods often discard the multimodal conditioning pathway (e.g., text prompts) inherent in the original diffusion model architecture.

SILICA positions itself within this landscape by addressing the generalization limitations of existing glass-aware depth estimation methods. It proposes to leverage the rich, pre-trained priors of text-to-image diffusion models, specifically by re-engaging and exploiting their CLIP-conditioned multimodal pathway. This approach aims to bridge the data domain gap – using real-world segmentation data with synthetic depth data – to enable robust, generalizable joint glass segmentation and depth estimation without requiring paired real-world glass depth annotations, a significant bottleneck for previous supervised methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent limitations of current perception systems in accurately processing transparent surfaces, which is critical for safe and effective robotic operation in human environments.

The specific motivations are:
- **Sensor Failure on Transparent Surfaces:** Standard depth sensors fundamentally fail on transparent materials, producing inaccurate or missing depth data. This leads to unreliable 3D scene reconstruction, which is detrimental for tasks like robotic mapping, localization, and navigation, posing collision risks to autonomous systems.
- **Limitations of Specialized Hardware:** While acoustic sensors can detect glass, their sparse output and calibration requirements make them an unsuitable primary solution for dense 3D perception.
- **Lack of Generalization in Learning-Based Methods:** Existing learning-based monocular depth estimators, including those specifically designed for glass, exhibit poor generalization. This is largely attributed to the severe scarcity of diverse, real-world datasets containing ground-truth metric depth annotations for transparent surfaces, which restricts these models to specific, familiar domains.
- **Inadequacy of Foundational Models:** Even advanced foundational monocular depth estimators tend to fail when encountering transparent structures, often perceiving and estimating the depth of objects visible *through* the glass, rather than the physical surface of the glass itself.
- **Domain Disparity in Datasets:** A significant challenge in joint perception of transparent objects is the disparity between available datasets: real-world imagery often provides reliable 2D segmentation masks, while dense, artifact-free 3D depth supervision for glass typically necessitates synthetic data.
- **Hypothesized Mutual Benefit of Joint Learning:** The authors hypothesize that resolving the foreground-background ambiguities inherent to transparent surfaces requires jointly learning glass segmentation and monocular depth estimation. An accurate glass mask could guide depth estimation, and geometric depth context could aid in isolating objects in front of the glass from the background.

Given these motivations, the key objectives of the SILICA work are:
- **Develop a Unified Perception Pipeline:** Create a single, efficient framework that jointly predicts both glass segmentation masks and glass-aware, affine-invariant depth maps from a single input image.
- **Leverage Diffusion Model Priors:** Exploit the extensive spatial and semantic priors embedded within pre-trained text-to-image diffusion models to enable generalizable perception of transparent surfaces.
- **Eliminate Real-world Paired Depth Annotations:** Design a training strategy that bypasses the need for difficult-to-acquire paired real-world glass depth annotations by effectively combining real-world segmentation data with synthetic depth data.
- **Achieve Zero-Shot Generalization:** Enable the model to perform robustly in diverse, unseen environments without requiring specific fine-tuning for new scenarios.
- **Recover Accurate Metric Depth:** Utilize the predicted glass segmentation mask to explicitly filter inaccurate depth readings from standard sensors on transparent surfaces, and then align the model's affine-invariant depth predictions to recover accurate metric depth for downstream robotic tasks like 3D mapping and collision avoidance.
- **Introduce a Novel Dataset:** Create and release a new dataset, Mirage 18k, specifically designed to support the training and evaluation of transparent surface perception tasks, including both segmentation and depth evaluation on glass.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SILICA proposes a unified pipeline that leverages the priors of text-to-image diffusion models for joint glass segmentation and monocular depth estimation. The methodology involves adapting a latent diffusion model for deterministic, single-step inference, an innovative task-routing mechanism using CLIP conditioning, and a post-processing step to recover metric depth.

**A. Latent Regression**
The core of SILICA's approach is to repurpose a text-to-image Latent Diffusion Model (specifically Stable Diffusion's U-Net) for deterministic regression.
- **Prediction Strategy:** Instead of the common ε-prediction, SILICA employs x0-prediction, where the model directly estimates the clean target sample (z_y). This choice is motivated by prior work indicating x0-prediction's stability and avoidance of output variance magnification at high noise levels.
- **Deterministic Inference:** To meet robotics' latency and consistency requirements, the traditional multi-step, stochastic denoising process is collapsed into a single, deterministic step. Gaussian noise addition to the input latent (z_y_t) is removed. Instead, the image latent (z_x) is directly fed into the U-Net at a fixed timestep t=T, learning to predict the target latent (z_y). This ensures consistent outputs irrespective of random seeds and improves inference efficiency. The loss function is defined as L_T = ||z_y - f_θ(z_x, t)||².

**B. Joint Modeling of Glass Segmentation and Depth Estimation**
- **Shared VAE and Decoder:** A preliminary sanity check verified that Stable Diffusion's frozen Variational Autoencoder (VAE) Encoder (E) and Decoder (D), originally trained on RGB images, can effectively compress and reconstruct binary glass segmentation masks. This was achieved by normalizing and replicating single-channel ground-truth masks across three channels to simulate RGB input. This validation allowed the use of a single, shared VAE and Decoder for both depth and segmentation tasks, avoiding the need for domain-specific components.
- **Addressing Domain Disparity:** The training data consists of real-world imagery with segmentation masks and synthetic imagery with depth annotations. To handle this multi-modal, multi-domain mixture and facilitate mutual information exchange, a robust task-routing mechanism within the shared U-Net is crucial.
- **CLIP-Conditioned Cross-Attention:** Initial attempts using a naive positional encoder for task switching (a one-dimensional vector added to time embeddings) were found to be unstable and led to task interference. SILICA instead leverages the pre-trained CLIP text encoder (τ_θ) of the Stable Diffusion U-Net. Task-specific text prompts ("predict glass segmentation" and "predict depth map") are encoded by CLIP into text embeddings (c_s and c_d). These embeddings are then injected directly into the U-Net's cross-attention layers. This approach exploits the model's inherent text-image alignment learned during pre-training, providing a robust global conditioning mechanism for routing cross-modal information without interference. The final joint fine-tuning loss function is L_T = ||z_s - f_θ(z_x_s, t, s_s, c_s)||² + ||z_d - f_θ(z_x_d, t, s_d, c_d)||².

**C. Inference and Recovering Metric Depth to Glass**
- **Inference:** During inference, an input RGB image is encoded to its latent representation (z_x) by the frozen Encoder E. This latent is then passed through the shared U-Net simultaneously with both task-specific CLIP text embeddings (c_s and c_d). This allows the U-Net to predict both the glass segmentation latent (ˆz_s) and the glass-aware depth latent (ˆz_d) in a single forward pass. The predicted latents are then decoded back to pixel space by the frozen Decoder D, averaged across channels, and the segmentation output is thresholded to yield a binary glass mask.
- **Metric Depth Recovery:** To obtain accurate metric depth, the predicted affine-invariant depth map (ˆd) is aligned with noisy raw sensor depth (D_raw) from standard depth sensors.
 1. D_raw is converted to raw disparity (D'_raw = 1/D_raw).
 2. The predicted segmentation mask (ˆs) is used to create a background mask (M_bg) that explicitly *excludes* glass regions, ensuring only reliable non-glass depth points are considered.
 3. A linear least-squares optimization is performed over the valid background pixels to compute a global scale (s) and shift (t) that align ˆd with D'_raw (Eq. 4: arg min_s,t Σ_{i∈M_bg} (s ⋅ ˆd_i + t - D'_raw,i)²).
 4. This affine transformation (s ⋅ ˆd + t) is applied to the entire predicted disparity map, which is then inverted to recover the aligned metric depth (D_aligned = 1/(s ⋅ ˆd + t)).
 5. Finally, a fused, glass-aware depth map is constructed by retaining the reliable raw sensor depth for background areas and injecting the aligned metric predictions within the predicted glass regions. This strategy resolves sensor failure artifacts on glass.

**D. Datasets**
- **Mirage 18k Dataset:** A novel dataset introduced by the authors, comprising 18,353 manually annotated RGB images with binary glass segmentation masks across 38 unique indoor scenes. A subset of 2,406 samples also includes ground-truth metric depth for transparent surfaces, used exclusively for zero-shot depth and segmentation evaluation. The dataset includes diverse glass types (indoor panes, frosted doors, windows, clear doors) and negative samples (no glass). Ground-truth depth for glass was acquired by modeling panes as 3D planar surfaces, using temporary markers for corner annotation, median depth extraction, and least-squares optimization in inverse depth space.
- **Training Datasets:**
 - *Glass Segmentation:* An aggregate of Mirage 18k training split and existing datasets (3DRef, Trans10k, GDD, GSD-S, GW-Depth), totaling 24,811 samples.
 - *Monocular Depth Estimation:* 24,811 randomly sampled images from Hypersim, a large-scale synthetic dataset, to match the size of the segmentation corpus for balanced multi-task learning.
- **Evaluation Datasets:**
 - *Glass Segmentation:* Official test splits of GDD, 3DRef, Trans10k, Mirage 18k, and an internal, unseen "shopping mall" dataset for out-of-distribution generalization.
 - *Monocular Depth Estimation:* Mirage 18k, GW-Depth (domain-specific glass datasets), and NYUv2 (general indoor depth benchmark).

**E. Evaluation Protocols & Metrics**
- **Glass Segmentation:** Mean Intersection-over-Union (mIoU) using TorchMetrics. Specific comparison protocols were established for various baselines (TransLab, EBLNet, GDNet, GlassSemNet, SAM3) to ensure fair comparison.
- **Monocular Depth Estimation:** Standard affine-invariant depth estimation protocol, aligning predictions with ground truth using least-squares fitting over all valid pixels. Metrics include Absolute Mean Relative Error (AbsRel), and threshold accuracies δ1 (max(a_i/d_i, d_i/a_i) < 1.25) and δ2 (max(a_i/d_i, d_i/a_i) < 1.25²).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that SILICA achieves notable performance in joint glass segmentation and glass-aware depth estimation, particularly exhibiting zero-shot transfer capabilities.

**A. Quantitative Results**
- **Glass Segmentation:** SILICA consistently outperformed established domain-specific baselines and the foundational segmentation model SAM3 across all evaluated public datasets (GDD, 3DRef, Trans10k, Mirage 18k) and an unseen internal shopping mall dataset. For instance, on the challenging unseen Mall dataset, SILICA achieved an mIoU of 77.83, surpassing SAM3 (61.03) and other baselines. On GDD, it recorded 91.60 mIoU, outperforming GlassSemNet (90.80).
- **Monocular Depth Estimation:** The joint prediction pipeline demonstrated superior performance compared to the domain-specific GW-Depth model across all evaluation datasets (NYUv2, GW-Depth, Mirage 18k). On datasets with prominent transparent structures (GW-Depth, Mirage 18k), SILICA achieved improvements of up to 20% in specific metrics compared to state-of-the-art discriminative monocular depth foundation models like Lotus-D and Depth Anything V2. For example, on Mirage 18k, SILICA achieved an AbsRel of 18.04, compared to 19.24 for Lotus-D and 25.78 for Depth Anything V2, alongside higher δ1 and δ2 accuracies.
- **Impact of Filtering Invalid Sensor Points:** An evaluation on the Mirage 18k depth test-only set highlighted the critical role of segmentation in metric depth recovery. Failing to segment and filter invalid sensor points on transparent surfaces before metric alignment resulted in a catastrophic error spike, with AbsRel increasing by over 85% compared to SILICA's strategy of filtering.

**B. Qualitative Results**
Qualitative comparisons across challenging environments (e.g., crowded shopping mall with large glass panes, half-opened glass doors, extreme close-up windows with minimal context) further supported the quantitative findings.
- **Complex Scenes:** SILICA successfully segmented glass and predicted accurate depth even in crowded scenes, ignoring dynamic obstacles, where baselines like TransLab and SAM3 struggled with background saliency or incorrect segmentation. In depth estimation, SILICA accurately predicted glass depth, while DepthAnythingv2 often captured background objects.
- **Half-Opened Doors:** SILICA produced accurate segmentation masks for half-open glass doors, a scenario where TransLab failed to predict the open door and SAM3 missed significant parts of the door structure. For depth, SILICA provided accurate surface depth, contrasting with Lotus-D and DepthAnythingV2, which exhibited significant errors or missed the transparent surface.
- **Minimal Context:** In extreme close-up window scenarios with limited visual cues, SILICA precisely isolated the true glass boundary and resolved correct glass depth, while baselines predicted incorrect boundaries or entirely missed the transparent surface, outputting background depth.

**C. Real-world Deployment**
The SILICA pipeline was integrated into an autonomous wheelchair. It achieved an inference rate of approximately 5 Hz on an NVIDIA RTX 3060 GPU. The corrected glass depth points were used to build accurate 3D maps with RTAB-Map, generating 2D occupancy grids where glass areas were included as obstacles. This enabled the wheelchair to safely plan paths and navigate in glass-structured environments. The model demonstrated rare false positives in glass-free scenes, contributing to reliable robotic operation, and also implicitly improved overall 3D mapping quality by providing stabilized depth points on glass surfaces.

**D. Ablation Studies**
- **CLIP-Conditioning:** Ablation experiments confirmed the importance of CLIP-based conditioning. Without it (using a naive positional encoder), the model suffered from severe task interference, leading to corrupted depth predictions (e.g., glass predicted abnormally close) and segmentation failures (e.g., misclassifying background furniture as foreground). CLIP-conditioned cross-attention enabled stable mutual information exchange and robust foreground-background disambiguation.
- **Eliminating Glass Depth Annotations:** Retraining the model from scratch on a filtered version of Hypersim that excluded scenes with significant glass content (>10% glass pixels) resulted in only marginal differences in depth metrics. This finding supports the core hypothesis that explicit glass depth annotations are not strictly necessary, as the CLIP-conditioned joint training framework teaches the network the correct spatial hierarchy for transparent surface perception.
- **Mutual Benefit of Joint Training:** Ablating SILICA against single-task baselines highlighted their interdependence. A segmentation-only model experienced a ~5.0% mIoU drop and increased false positives. Conversely, a depth-only model suffered an over 80% error spike on glass regions due to the absence of the predicted segmentation mask, which is crucial for filtering corrupted raw sensor data during metric alignment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SILICA research contributes to the field of robotic perception and computer vision by addressing a long-standing challenge with transparent surfaces. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Robotic Autonomy and Safety:** By enabling accurate glass segmentation and glass-aware depth estimation, SILICA directly contributes to safer and more robust autonomous navigation for robots, particularly in indoor environments prevalent with glass structures (e.g., offices, malls, homes). This reduces the risk of collisions and enables more reliable 3D mapping.
- **Overcoming Data Scarcity:** The methodology effectively bypasses the critical bottleneck of scarce real-world paired glass depth annotations. By leveraging the rich priors of text-to-image diffusion models and employing a novel CLIP-conditioned joint training strategy, the approach reduces reliance on expensive and difficult-to-acquire datasets.
- **Superior Generalization Capabilities:** The demonstrated zero-shot transfer performance across diverse and unseen environments represents a notable advancement, indicating that the model learns robust features for transparent surface perception that generalize beyond specific training domains.
- **Methodological Innovation:** The research introduces an effective way to repurpose and fully utilize the multimodal conditioning pathway of pre-trained text-to-image diffusion models for complex, multi-task perception problems. This demonstrates a novel paradigm for knowledge transfer from large generative models to specialized perception tasks.
- **Practicality for Real-world Deployment:** The single-step deterministic latent regression approach ensures efficient inference rates (5 Hz on moderate GPU hardware), making SILICA suitable for real-time robotic applications. Its ability to implicitly improve 3D mapping quality by providing stabilized depth points on glass surfaces further enhances its practical value.
- **Contribution of a New Dataset:** The introduction of the Mirage 18k dataset provides a valuable resource for the research community, facilitating further development and evaluation of methods for transparent surface perception, particularly for joint segmentation and depth estimation.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The findings regarding the non-necessity of explicit glass depth annotations and the critical role of CLIP conditioning for bridging sim-to-real domain gaps offer insights that could inform the design of future generalizable perception systems for challenging visual phenomena.
