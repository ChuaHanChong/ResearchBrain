# HOMIE: Humanoid Loco-Manipulation with Isomorphic Exoskeleton Cockpit

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Shanghai AI Laboratory and The Chinese University of Hong Kong developed HOMIE, a semi-autonomous teleoperation system for humanoids that integrates robust locomotion with precise manipulation. The system utilizes a low-cost isomorphic exoskeleton cockpit and an RL framework, demonstrating enhanced real-time control accuracy and efficiency while effectively generating high-quality data for training autonomous loco-manipulation policies.

## Problem

- Existing humanoid robotics research typically separates agile locomotion (often RL-trained) from precise manipulation (teleoperation), limiting the ability of robots to perform complex, coordinated whole-body tasks.
- Traditional teleoperation systems for robot arms and hands often suffer from inaccuracies, latency, and high costs due to reliance on inverse kinematics, vision-based pose estimation, or expensive duplicate hardware.
- Many whole-body control methods for humanoids lack the ability to dynamically adjust the robot's height, severely restricting the operational workspace for interaction with objects at varying elevations.

## Method

- A semi-autonomous teleoperation system is developed, allowing a human operator to control upper-body and hand movements directly via an isomorphic exoskeleton cockpit, while an RL-trained policy manages robust whole-body locomotion.
- A low-cost, 3D-printed isomorphic exoskeleton and motion-sensing gloves are designed to directly map human joint angles to robot joints, bypassing the need for complex inverse kinematics or visual pose estimation.
- A novel reinforcement learning framework is introduced, featuring an upper-body pose curriculum, a targeted height-tracking reward, and symmetry utilization to train locomotion policies that maintain balance under dynamic upper-body movements and precisely adjust torso height.

## Results

- The RL framework demonstrated faster convergence and lower tracking errors for linear velocity, angular velocity, and height due to the upper-body pose curriculum and height-tracking reward.
- The HOMIE hardware achieved superior output frequencies to the robot's arm (263 Hz) and hand (293 Hz) compared to vision-based (e.g., AnyTeleop: 125/111 Hz) and VR-based (e.g., OpenTelevision: 60/60 Hz) systems, while costing approximately $500.
- The system enabled real-world performance of complex loco-manipulation tasks and showed task completion times nearly half of a comparable VR-based teleoperation system; autonomous policies trained on collected data achieved **73.3%** and **80.0%** success rates for 'Squat Pick' and 'Pick & Place' tasks, respectively.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct joint angle mapping via isomorphic hardware provides superior accuracy and speed for humanoid teleoperation compared to inverse kinematics or vision-based methods.
> - Training a locomotion policy with an upper-body pose curriculum and a specific height-tracking reward enables robust balance and dynamic squatting during continuous upper-body movements.
> - An affordable, high-fidelity teleoperation system can function as an efficient 'data flywheel' for collecting expert demonstrations, significantly accelerating the training of autonomous humanoid policies through imitation learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics is rapidly advancing, with a long-standing goal of enabling robots to perform complex tasks in human environments. A critical challenge in this domain is "generalizable humanoid loco-manipulation," which requires robots to exhibit both agile locomotion (walking, squatting, balancing) and precise, dexterous object manipulation, often simultaneously. The authors highlight a significant "dichotomy" in existing research that HOMIE aims to bridge.

On one hand, **reinforcement learning (RL)-trained locomotion policies** (e.g.,) have made remarkable strides, allowing humanoids to exhibit impressive athletic capabilities like walking over uneven terrain or recovering from pushes. However, these policies often lack effective real-time interfaces for human operators to precisely control the robot's upper body and hands for intricate manipulation tasks while the robot is moving. This limits their practical utility in scenarios requiring human intervention or fine-tuned control.

On the other hand, **teleoperation systems** (e.g.,) have focused on enabling human operators to control robots remotely. Traditional teleoperation systems for manipulation often fall into several categories, each with limitations:
- **Identical Robotic Arms (e.g., Mobile-ALOHA):** While offering high accuracy and speed due to direct joint matching, these systems are prohibitively expensive as they require a duplicate robotic arm. Moreover, they typically don't address dexterous hand control or locomotion.
- **Vision-Based Systems (e.g., AnyTeleop, Telekinesis):** These use cameras and computer vision techniques to estimate human operator poses, which are then retargeted to the robot using inverse kinematics (IK). Limitations include inaccuracies, slow inference speeds, and susceptibility to occlusions, which hinder rapid and precise control.
- **VR/AR Devices (e.g., OpenTelevision, Open Teach):** Similar to vision-based methods, these often rely on pose estimation and IK, suffering from similar accuracy and speed issues, especially in tangential directions.
- **MoCap Systems (e.g., DexCap):** Offer high accuracy and frequency but are extremely expensive and require specialized environments, making them impractical for general use or widespread data collection.
- **Exoskeleton-Based Systems (e.g., ACE, GELLO, AirExo):** While promising for their direct control and lower cost than identical robots, many still rely on IK for mapping or are limited to controlling grippers rather than dexterous hands.

Furthermore, **whole-body control (WBC) methods** for humanoids, whether model-based (e.g.,) or RL-based (e.g.,), often rely on motion priors derived from costly MoCap data. They also frequently lack the ability to control the robot's height, severely limiting the operational workspace for interacting with objects at different elevations. Moreover, locomotion commands often rely on body movement data or joysticks, which can be impractical for large-scale environments or when hands are occupied.

HOMIE directly addresses this fragmented landscape by presenting a unified, semi-autonomous teleoperation system that seamlessly integrates robust locomotion with precise manipulation. It innovates by:
1. **Marrying RL and Teleoperation:** It allows an RL policy to manage dynamic whole-body locomotion while an operator provides real-time, precise upper-body and hand commands.
2. **Overcoming IK/Pose Estimation Issues:** By designing isomorphic exoskeleton arms, it bypasses the need for error-prone inverse kinematics and visual pose estimation, directly mapping human joint angles to the robot for superior accuracy and speed.
3. **Enhancing Locomotion for Manipulation:** Its RL framework incorporates novel techniques (upper-body pose curriculum, height-tracking reward) that enable the robot to maintain balance and adapt its base dynamically to arbitrary upper-body movements and target heights, significantly expanding the robot's usable workspace during manipulation.
4. **Cost-Effectiveness and Accessibility:** The hardware system's affordability ($500) makes advanced humanoid teleoperation accessible to a much broader research community, fostering greater data collection and experimentation.
5. **Enabling Data Flywheel:** By providing an efficient means of collecting high-quality human demonstrations, HOMIE facilitates the training of autonomous policies through imitation learning, moving beyond the reliance on motion priors.

In essence, HOMIE pushes the boundaries of humanoid robotics by offering a practical, affordable, and highly effective solution to the long-standing problem of generalizable loco-manipulation, positioning itself as a significant advancement towards deploying humanoids in real-world, labor-intensive tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the HOMIE project is to enable **generalizable humanoid loco-manipulation**, a crucial step toward integrating humanoid robots into daily life and automating labor-intensive tasks. This ambitious goal necessitates robots possessing both strong athletic capabilities and precise, contact-rich object manipulation skills, coordinated across the entire body.

The authors identify several specific motivations and challenges in the current research landscape that HOMIE aims to address:

- **Bridging the Locomotion-Manipulation Gap:** Existing solutions are fragmented. RL-trained locomotion policies excel at environmental adaptation but lack real-time, precise teleoperation interfaces. Conversely, most teleoperation systems focus on upper-body control without considering how locomotion impacts the robot's operational workspace. This creates a "lose-lose situation" where robots either sacrifice dexterous manipulation during movement or compromise their workspace when manipulating. HOMIE's core motivation is to eliminate this fragmentation by enabling coordinated whole-body control.

- **Enabling Robust and Generalizable Whole-Body Control:** Current methods often rely on motion priors derived from expensive and difficult-to-obtain MoCap data, hindering scalability and adaptation to new poses. HOMIE seeks to eliminate this dependency, making the training pipeline more efficient and the resulting policies more generalizable.

- **Expanding Operational Workspace:** Many whole-body control methods lack the ability to control a robot's body height. This severely restricts the robot's capacity to handle objects at varying elevations, limiting its practical utility. A key objective of HOMIE is to enable precise height tracking and dynamic squatting to significantly expand the robot's operational reach.

- **Improving Teleoperation Accuracy and Speed:** Mainstream teleoperation systems often rely on inverse kinematics (IK) or vision-based pose estimation, which introduce inaccuracies and latency. HOMIE is motivated to deliver faster and more precise arm and hand control by using an isomorphic exoskeleton that bypasses IK and motion-sensing gloves with direct joint mapping.

- **Cost-Effectiveness and Accessibility:** High-fidelity teleoperation systems, especially those using MoCap or identical robot arms, are extremely expensive, limiting their adoption and the scope of research. HOMIE aims to provide an affordable solution (around $500) that is accessible to a wider research community, thereby fostering more widespread experimentation and data collection.

- **Facilitating Data-Driven Learning (Data Flywheel):** Teleoperation is a promising technique for collecting high-quality expert demonstrations. HOMIE's unified and efficient cockpit is designed to establish a "data flywheel," where collected real-world data can be effectively used to train autonomous policies through imitation learning, accelerating the development of generalizable robotic skills.

In summary, HOMIE's key objectives are to develop a semi-autonomous teleoperation system that allows a single operator to intuitively and precisely control a humanoid robot's full body, enabling robust loco-manipulation across diverse tasks and environments. This is driven by the motivation to overcome the limitations of existing approaches in terms of control integration, accuracy, cost, and data collection efficiency, ultimately pushing humanoid robots closer to real-world deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HOMIE's methodology combines a novel reinforcement learning (RL) training framework for whole-body control with a meticulously designed, low-cost hardware teleoperation system.

**A. System Overview**
The HOMIE system operates as a semi-autonomous teleoperation platform. An operator in a unified cockpit receives a first-person view (FPV) from the robot via Wi-Fi. The operator provides:
- **Locomotion Commands:** Via a foot pedal, specifying desired forward/backward speed (`v_x,t`), turning speed (`ω_yaw,t`), and target torso height (`h_t`). These commands are fed into a low-level RL policy, `π_loco`, which controls the robot's lower body.
- **Upper-Body Commands:** Via isomorphic exoskeleton arms and motion-sensing gloves, providing direct joint angles (`q_upper`) for the robot's upper body. These angles are directly set on the robot, bypassing inverse kinematics (IK).

The upper and lower body controls work in coordination. The system is designed to collect demonstrations during teleoperation, which can then be used to train an autonomous policy (`π_auto`) that can take over control, establishing a "data flywheel."

**B. Humanoid Whole-body Control (RL-based Training Framework for `π_loco`)**
The core of HOMIE's autonomous locomotion capabilities lies in its RL-based training framework, designed to enable robust walking and squatting under continuously changing upper-body poses. The framework is implemented in Isaac Gym for zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and built upon existing RL works.

1. **Training Settings:**
 - **Observations (`O_t`):** Include current and past (up to `t-5`) commands (`C_t`), body angular velocity (`ω_t`), gravity projection (`g_t`), joint angles (`q_t`), joint velocities (`˙q_t`), and previous action (`a_t-1`).
 - **Actions (`a_t`):** Correspond one-to-one with the lower-body joints. Torques (`τ_t,i`) are calculated using a proportional-derivative (PD) controller based on the policy's actions, stiffness (`Kp_i`), damping (`Kd_i`), and default joint positions (`q_0,t,i`).
 - **PPO:** Proximal Policy Optimization is used as the RL algorithm.

2. **Upper-body Pose Curriculum:**
 - To ensure `π_loco` can handle dynamic and arbitrary upper-body poses without losing balance, a curriculum learning approach is employed.
 - The sampling range for upper-body joint angles is gradually increased using an "upper action ratio" (`ρ_a`), starting at 0 and reaching 1.
 - A specific probability distribution `p(ρ'_a|ρ_a)` (Eq. 2 & 3) is used to sample `ρ'_a`, ensuring a smoother transition and consistent sampling across the range. This avoids "gaps" in the sampled pose space that could arise from a simpler uniform sampling approach.
 - Target upper-body poses are resampled every 1 second and uniformly interpolated to simulate continuous, smooth changes during teleoperation.

3. **Height Tracking Reward (`r_knee`):**
 - A novel reward function is designed to enable the robot to squat precisely to a target height (`h_t`).
 - `r_knee` (Eq. 4) penalizes deviations between the robot's actual height (`h_r,t`) and `h_t`, specifically encouraging knee flexion when `h_r,t < h_t` and extension when `h_r,t > h_t`. This provides direct guidance for squat motion.
 - During training, one-third of environments focus on squatting, while two-thirds focus on standing/walking, allowing the policy to learn to smoothly transition between these behaviors.

4. **Symmetry Utilization:**
 - Inspired by, this technique enhances data efficiency and promotes symmetrical policy learning.
 - **Symmetrical Data Augmentation:** For every collected transition, a mirrored version (flipped across the robot's x-z plane) is generated and added to the rollout storage. This includes flipping joint positions, velocities, actions, and turning velocities.
 - **Symmetry Loss:** Additional losses (`L_actor_sym`, `L_critic_sym` in Eq. 5 & 6) are added to the network optimization process, enforcing symmetry in the actor and critic networks themselves. This ensures the learned policy's left-right performance is balanced.

**C. Hardware System Design**
HOMIE's hardware system is designed for single-operator full-body control at low cost.

1. **Isomorphic Exoskeleton Arms:**
 - **Design:** 3D-printed, 7-DoF arms (per side), matching the kinematics of target humanoids (Unitree G1, Fourier GR-1).
 - **Control:** Each joint is equipped with a DYNAMIXEL XL330-M288-T servo. These servos provide precise absolute joint angle readings (0.09° accuracy) and can directly map to the robot's joint positions using a simple kinematic equivalence (`q_t = ±k_t(p_t + n_tπ/2) + τ_t`). This direct mapping eliminates the need for complex IK calculations, leading to faster and more accurate control.
 - **Cost:** Primary expense of the system, but still affordable.

2. **Motion-sensing Gloves:**
 - **Design:** Low-cost, capable of capturing up to 15 DoF for finger angles (e.g., three sensors per finger, capturing pitch of fingertip and pad, and yaw of pad). Based on the Nepyone glove project.
 - **Sensing:** Utilizes Hall effect sensors and small neodymium magnets. Joint rotation changes the magnetic field, which is sensed and mapped to finger joint angles.
 - **Modularity:** Gloves connect directly to the exoskeleton and are detachable, allowing reusability with different isomorphic systems or robots.
 - **Custom Microcontroller:** Features exposed ports for easy reassigning and modifying mapping relationships.

3. **Foot Pedal:**
 - **Design:** Replaces traditional remote controllers for locomotion commands. Consists of three small pedals and two mode-switching buttons.
 - **Functionality:** High-precision rotary potentiometers map pedal pressure to electrical signals for linear velocity, yaw velocity, and height adjustment commands. Foot-operated momentary switches toggle movement directions (forward/backward, left/right).
 - **Advantage:** Frees the operator's hands for complex manipulation tasks, enabling simultaneous loco-manipulation.

The combined hardware cost is approximately $500, making it significantly more affordable than most existing high-fidelity teleoperation solutions. Communication between the cockpit and the robot is via Wi-Fi, using a compact data packet (128 bytes) for low latency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents extensive findings validating the effectiveness of both the RL training framework and the hardware teleoperation system.

**A. Humanoid Whole-body Control (RL Training Framework)**
Ablation studies were conducted on the Unitree G1 robot, evaluating the impact of the proposed RL techniques on tracking errors (linear velocity, angular velocity, height), symmetry loss, and living time (stability).

- **Upper-body Pose Curriculum:**
 - The proposed curriculum ("ours") significantly outperforms variants without the curriculum ("w/o cur") or with a simpler curriculum ("rand"). "Ours" achieved faster convergence and smaller errors in linear velocity, angular velocity, and height tracking.
 - This confirms that the gradual and smooth progression of upper-body pose diversity effectively trains the policy to maintain balance under continuous and varying upper-body movements.

- **Height Tracking Reward (`r_knee`):**
 - The inclusion of `r_knee` in "ours" leads to faster convergence and lower height error compared to omitting it ("w/o knee") or simply scaling up a general height reward ("hei").
 - This validates that the specific guidance provided by `r_knee` for knee flexion/extension is crucial for enabling precise squatting capabilities and effectively expanding the robot's operational workspace.

- **Symmetry Utilization:**
 - Combining symmetrical data augmentation and symmetry loss ("ours") yields slightly better overall tracking accuracy and significantly lower symmetry loss compared to using only augmentation ("w/ aug"), only loss ("w/ sym"), or neither ("none").
 - This demonstrates that symmetry utilization improves training efficiency and enforces a balanced, symmetrical policy, which indirectly benefits locomotion tasks.

- **Generality Across Different Robots:**
 - The RL framework successfully trained the Fourier GR-1, a robot with significantly different physical parameters (taller, heavier, different hand weight ratio) than the Unitree G1, with minimal changes to reward scales or training pipeline.
 - Evaluation metrics showed robust performance for both robots, confirming the generality of the proposed RL training framework across heterogeneous humanoid models.

**B. Teleoperation Hardware Performance**
The hardware system demonstrates superior performance in terms of cost, acquisition metrics, and output frequency compared to existing systems.

- **Cost-Effectiveness:** The total hardware cost is remarkably low at approximately $500, a fraction of MoCap systems ($4k) or systems using identical robot arms ($32k).
- **High Acquisition Frequency and Accuracy:** The exoskeleton, gloves, and pedal all boast high acquisition frequencies (0.26 kHz to 0.5 kHz) and high accuracy (12-bit resolution for angle readings), enabling precise input capture.
- **Superior Output Frequency to Robot:** Due to direct joint mapping (joint-matching scheme) that bypasses IK and pose estimation, HOMIE achieves significantly higher output frequencies to the robot's arm (263 Hz) and hand (293 Hz). This vastly outperforms vision-based (e.g., AnyTeleop: 125/111 Hz) and VR-based (e.g., OpenTelevision: 60/60 Hz) systems, which are limited by processing overhead for estimation. Crucially, this high performance is achieved without requiring intensive GPU or SoC hardware.
- **Dexterous Hand Compatibility:** The motion-sensing gloves successfully controlled various dexterous hands (LEAP, Shadow, Inspire, Schunk) with different DoF counts in simulation, showcasing their versatility and adaptability.

**C. Teleoperation System Performance (Real-World & Simulation)**
The integrated HOMIE system was rigorously tested in both real-world and simulated environments.

- **Real-World Loco-Manipulation:**
 - The system successfully enabled the Unitree G1 to perform a wide range of complex loco-manipulation tasks: squatting to grasp/place objects, apple handover between two robots, box transfer, pushing a 60kg person, opening an oven door, dual-hand object transfer (tomato), retrieving objects from the ground, and lifting/placing heavy items.
 - The robot maintained balance under vigorous upper-body motions, demonstrating the robustness of the `π_loco` policy.
 - Communication via Wi-Fi had an acceptable latency of 16 ms, allowing for effective real-time control.

- **Efficiency Comparison:**
 - In a direct comparison with a VR-based teleoperation system (OpenTelevision) on four desktop manipulation tasks (Pick & Place, Scan Barcode, Hand Over, Open Oven), HOMIE achieved task completion times nearly **half** of the VR method.
 - This efficiency gain was particularly pronounced in tasks requiring precise positioning and orientation, where VR-based pose estimation struggles.

- **User Study:**
 - A user study with five novice testers (varying heights, weights, genders) demonstrated the system's intuitive usability. All novices quickly approached expert-level performance in a "Hand Over" task after minimal practice. This highlights the system's ease of learning and adaptability to diverse operator body types.

- **Simulation Migration:**
 - The trained policies for Unitree G1 and Fourier GR-1 were successfully transferred from Isaac Gym to a more realistic Isaac Sim/IsaacLab environment (GRUtopia). This demonstrates the framework's ability to control robots in diverse simulated contexts, enabling cost-effective and wide-ranging scenario testing.

**D. Autonomous Policy (Imitation Learning)**
The quality of data collected via HOMIE for training autonomous policies was validated.

- **Data Collection:** 50 episodes of high-quality RGB images, robot states, and precise commands were collected for "Squat Pick" and "Pick & Place" tasks.
- **Autonomous Performance:** An autonomous policy (`π_auto`) trained using the collected data and a Seer (autoregressive transformer) model achieved impressive success rates in real-world deployment: 73.3% for "Squat Pick" and 80.0% for "Pick & Place."
- This result unequivocally proves that HOMIE effectively serves as a "data flywheel," enabling the rapid and effective development of complex, autonomous humanoid loco-manipulation skills from expert demonstrations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

HOMIE represents a significant leap forward in humanoid robotics, offering a comprehensive solution that addresses several long-standing challenges in simultaneous locomotion and manipulation. Its potential impact is multi-faceted:

- **Enabling True Humanoid Utility:** By seamlessly integrating robust locomotion with precise, dexterous manipulation under human control, HOMIE brings humanoid robots closer to real-world applicability in unstructured environments. This is crucial for their deployment in labor-intensive tasks in industries, homes, and dangerous settings where both mobility and fine motor skills are required.

- **Accelerating Research and Development:** The system's affordability (total cost of ~$500) democratizes access to high-fidelity humanoid teleoperation. This significantly lowers the barrier to entry for researchers, universities, and potentially even hobbyists, fostering broader experimentation, innovation, and comparative studies in humanoid control and learning.

- **Powerful Data Generation Tool (Data Flywheel):** One of the most impactful contributions is HOMIE's proven ability to act as an efficient and accurate data collection tool for imitation learning. The demonstration that collected data can train autonomous policies with high success rates validates the concept of a "data flywheel." This capability is critical for scaling up the development of autonomous humanoid skills, moving beyond costly and limited motion capture datasets. It allows researchers to quickly generate task-specific, expert-level demonstrations, which are essential for data-driven robot learning.

- **Overcoming Technical Bottlenecks:** By directly mapping isomorphic exoskeleton joint angles, HOMIE bypasses the inherent inaccuracies and latency associated with traditional methods like inverse kinematics and vision-based pose estimation. This leads to significantly faster and more precise control, which is paramount for real-time interaction and dexterous manipulation.

- **Robust and Generalizable Control Policies:** The novel RL training framework, incorporating an upper-body pose curriculum, height-tracking reward, and symmetry utilization, yields highly robust and generalizable locomotion policies. This means humanoids can maintain balance and perform tasks despite dynamic upper-body movements and target varying heights, significantly expanding their functional workspace. The generality demonstrated across different robot models (Unitree G1, Fourier GR-1) is particularly promising for broader application.

- **Open-Source Contribution:** The commitment to making the system fully open-source (demos and code available) is a strong positive. This will allow the wider robotics community to build upon, extend, and integrate HOMIE's components into their own research, accelerating collective progress in the field.

While acknowledging limitations such as terrain traversal challenges, lack of force feedback, and thumb DoF alignment, HOMIE sets a new standard for accessible, high-performance humanoid teleoperation. It lays foundational groundwork for future human-robot collaboration and the development of truly autonomous, capable humanoids that can seamlessly integrate into diverse human-centric environments.
