# SCRIBES: Web-Scale Script-Based Semi-Structured Data Extraction with Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> SCRIBES, a framework from Stanford University and Meta, leverages a Large Language Model (LLM) within a Reinforcement Learning (RL) setup to generate reusable extraction scripts for semi-structured web content. This method significantly reduces computational costs for web-scale extraction by applying a few scripts to many similar pages, achieving competitive extraction accuracy and boosting downstream question answering performance.

## Problem

- Current LLM-based information extraction is computationally expensive for web-scale applications, requiring per-page inference.
- Traditional extraction methods are brittle and lack generalization across diverse webpage layouts, even within the same website.
- Generating high-quality, generalizable extraction scripts for LLMs faces challenges due to the difficulty and cost of direct human annotation.

## Method

- **SCRIBES** generates reusable ==extraction scripts== using an LLM within a Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework, specifically Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).
- It employs structure-preserving HTML deduplication to manage context window limitations and efficiently represent webpage content.
- The framework incorporates unlabeled web data from CommonCrawl, using LLM-based synthetic annotations and focusing training on "failure cases" to improve robustness and coverage.

## Results

- SCRIBES models improved macro-averaged F1 score (FLM1F^1_{LM}FLM1) by over **13%** compared to agentic baselines, with the Q-**32B** model achieving performance comparable to a **120B** model.
- Demonstrated web-scale efficiency by extracting 2.78 million triples from 113,129 webpages while requiring direct LLM script generation for only **4.1%** of pages, indicating substantial GPU resource savings.
- Incorporating CommonCrawl data, particularly through a staged training approach focusing on failure cases, improved FLM1F^1_{LM}FLM1 by approximately **2%** for Q-**14B** and **5%** for Q-**32B** models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Rewarding script performance averaged across a group of similar pages (cross-scores) is crucial for generating generalizable extraction scripts, outperforming self-score-only rewards.
> - Targeted training on "failure cases" identified from large unlabeled datasets (CommonCrawl) effectively enhances model robustness and performance despite noisy synthetic supervision.
> - HTML deduplication significantly reduces token usage (by 85.1% on average) while preserving essential structural information, making lengthy web content amenable to LLM processing.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The extraction of structured information from semi-structured web content, such as HTML tables, lists, and infoboxes, is a longstanding challenge in information extraction (IE). This content constitutes a substantial portion of factual data on the web, yet its varied formatting complicates automated usage. The current research landscape for this task can be broadly categorized into two main approaches:

**Traditional Information Extraction (IE) Methods:** These methods, including wrapper induction, graph mining, layout-based techniques, and early Deep Neural Networks, have been developed over decades. While effective in specific scenarios, they generally exhibit limitations in terms of generalization. They tend to be brittle, struggling to adapt to structural changes or generalize across diverse domains and schemas, often requiring extensive manual effort or clean annotations for specific site structures.

**Large Language Model (LLM)-based Methods:** More recently, LLMs have been applied to information extraction, offering improved parsing capabilities for individual pages and supporting Knowledge Graph construction. These methods can produce high-quality outputs by leveraging the LLMs' advanced natural language understanding and generation capabilities. However, a significant drawback of existing LLM-based approaches is their resource intensiveness. They typically require invoking an LLM for each page to be processed, which becomes computationally expensive and impractical for web-scale applications. Furthermore, many current LLM methods treat each webpage independently, thereby failing to exploit the structural regularities often present across pages within the same website or group.

**Reinforcement Learning (RL) Without Annotations:** A parallel trend in AI research involves developing RL frameworks that can operate with minimal or no explicit human annotations, leveraging internal signals or consensus-based rewards. This area explores how models can learn and refine their behavior using less direct forms of supervision, a concept SCRIBES draws upon for its training methodology.

SCRIBES positions itself as an approach designed to bridge the gap between the generalization capabilities of LLMs and the efficiency required for web-scale extraction. It aims to overcome the resource limitations of per-page LLM inference by generating reusable extraction scripts, while also addressing the brittleness of traditional methods by employing LLMs and an RL framework that promotes script generalization. By exploiting layout similarities across webpages within the same domain, SCRIBES seeks to enable both effective and efficient knowledge extraction from semi-structured web content.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel framework, SCRIBES (SCRIpt-Based Semi-Structured Content Extraction at Web-Scale), that can effectively and efficiently extract knowledge from semi-structured content on the web at a large scale. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations:

**1. Addressing Resource Intensiveness of LLM-based Extraction:** Current LLM-based approaches, while capable of producing high-quality extractions, are computationally expensive. Performing LLM inference for every single webpage makes them impractical for web-scale applications due to the substantial computational resources and time required. The motivation here is to devise a method that significantly reduces the per-page computational cost without compromising extraction quality.

**2. Enhancing Generalization of Extraction Methods:** Traditional information extraction methods are often brittle and lack the ability to generalize across different webpage layouts or structural variations, even within the same website. While LLMs offer better generalization for individual pages, they often struggle to generate extraction *scripts* that are robust and reusable across structurally similar but not identical pages. A key motivation is to improve the generalizability of extraction mechanisms, moving beyond single-page processing to solutions applicable to groups of similar pages.

**3. Overcoming Challenges in Script Annotation for LLM Fine-tuning:** Training LLMs to generate high-quality, generalizable extraction scripts requires extensive annotated data. However, creating such annotations is reported to be difficult even for expert human labelers. This difficulty in data creation motivates the development of a training paradigm that reduces reliance on direct, high-quality human annotations for scripts.

**4. Leveraging Inherent Web Structure for Efficiency and Generalization:** Many webpages within the same website or domain often share highly similar underlying layouts and structures, despite variations in content. This observation serves as a core motivation to exploit these structural regularities. By generating a single, reusable extraction script for a group of structurally similar pages, rather than processing each page individually, the framework aims to achieve significant efficiency gains and improve script generalization.

**5. Utilizing Reinforcement Learning for Script Quality and Robustness:** Given the difficulty of direct annotation for script generation, the research is motivated to employ a Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework. This approach allows the model to learn and refine its script-generation capabilities by evaluating the performance of its generated scripts on multiple pages within a group. The feedback mechanism, particularly rewarding scripts that generalize well across similar pages, is intended to implicitly encourage the learning of robust and reusable extraction logic.

**6. Augmenting Training with Unlabeled Web Data:** The scarcity of large, high-quality annotated datasets for semi-structured content extraction limits the scope and robustness of trained models. To address this, a motivation is to develop a method for leveraging vast amounts of unlabeled data from sources like CommonCrawl. This involves creating synthetic annotations and strategically incorporating them into the RL training process to expand coverage and further improve model performance, particularly on "failure cases" where the model struggles.

In summary, the key objectives are to develop a scalable, resource-efficient, and generalizable method for semi-structured data extraction by generating reusable scripts. This is achieved through an RL framework that exploits layout similarities within webpages and is further enhanced by iterative training on strategically selected synthetic data from the broader web.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SCRIBES addresses the problem of web-scale semi-structured data extraction by generating reusable extraction scripts, leveraging an LLM within a Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework. The approach is designed to be both effective and efficient, exploiting layout similarities across webpages and utilizing synthetic data for training.

#### 3.1. Problem Definition

The task is defined as **knowledge extraction**, where a group of structurally similar webpages ($G = \{p_1, \dots, p_n\}$) is parsed to generate a list of (subject, predicate, object) triples. The goal is to train a model, LM, that given any webpage $p \in G$, predicts an extraction script $\hat{y}_p = \text{LM}(p)$. This script should, when applied to all pages in $G$, produce triples close to the ground truth triples $\{y^*_p \mid p \in G\}$.

#### 3.2. HTML Deduplication (Dedup)

To mitigate the challenge of lengthy HTML content exceeding LLM context windows, SCRIBES employs a structure-preserving HTML deduplication algorithm. This method collapses repeated HTML blocks into a compact representation (e.g., "n more ... elements") and removes certain irrelevant tags (e.g., `<script>`, `<style>`) and attributes (except a whitelist). This process significantly reduces the token length of the HTML input while retaining crucial structural information. For instance, it reduces token usage from over 114k to under 17k on average, which is an 85.1% reduction.

#### 3.3. Reinforcement Learning Setup

SCRIBES utilizes an RL framework with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to train models, motivated by the difficulty of annotating extraction scripts directly.

**Reward Calculation:** The system defines a score $r(p \to q) = S(\hat{y}_p(q), y^*_q)$ to measure the quality of a script $\hat{y}_p$ (generated from page $p$) when executed on page $q$.
- **Scoring Function (S):** During training, $S$ is set to the fuzzy F1 score ($F^1_{fuzzy}$). This is computed by a bipartite matching algorithm that aligns predicted and gold triples based on character-level Levenshtein distance similarity, maximizing their pairwise fuzzy matching score. A greedy heuristic is used for faster approximation during training.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** For final evaluation, both fuzzy F1 ($F^1_{fuzzy}$) and an LLM-as-a-judge F1 ($F^1_{LM}$) are used. The LLM-as-a-judge (Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct) evaluates the semantic equivalence of matched triple pairs.

**Reward Signal from Labeled Data:**
For training on annotated data, SCRIBES uses Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a specific reward function for each training sample $p$:
$r_{SCRIBES}(p) = \frac{1}{|G(p)|} \sum_{q \in G(p)} r(p \to q)$
This reward function averages the scores obtained when the script generated for page $p$ is applied to all pages $q$ within its group $G(p)$. This includes the "self-score" (applying the script to $p$ itself) and "cross-scores" (applying it to other pages in the group). The design, where cross-scores form the majority of the reward, explicitly encourages the model to generate scripts that generalize across variations within the group.

**Reward Signal from Unlabeled Data in the Wild (CommonCrawl):**
To address the limitations of small annotated datasets and achieve broader coverage, SCRIBES incorporates unlabeled webpages from CommonCrawl (CC) through an iterative approach:
1. **Data Collection Pipeline:**
 - A sample of CC data is filtered to remove explicit content and retain English-language pages.
 - Webpages are grouped by domain using URL prefixes. Groups with at least $n=30$ pages are retained.
 - An LLM-based classifier (Prompt 10) identifies pages containing semi-structured content. Only groups where at least $m=90\%$ of pages are classified as semi-structured are kept.
 - From these groups, one webpage is sampled as the training example, and up to $k=13$ in-group webpages are selected for reward calculation.
2. **Synthetic Annotations:** Since no human annotations exist for CC data, LLM-based direct extraction (Prompt 11) is used as a proxy for gold annotations to compute rewards.
3. **Failure-Case Learning:** To mitigate the impact of noisy synthetic rewards, training on CC data begins from a checkpoint pre-trained on annotated data. The model then specifically refines its capabilities by focusing on "failure cases" from the CC data – webpages where its initially predicted scripts fail to produce any triples. This targeted training aims to improve performance without being degraded by the imperfections of synthetic supervision.

#### 3.4. Dataset

- **Annotated Dataset:** SemiBench (Sun et al., 2025) is used, comprising 268 webpages from 56 groups, each with more than one webpage. The dataset is split into 34 training groups (192 webpages) and 22 test groups (76 webpages). After context window filtering, 141 training and 65 test webpages remain. Evaluation metrics are reported on the test set, ensuring generalization to unseen website groups.
- **In-the-wild Webpages:** The CC pipeline yields 1,898 groups (from an initial 19,566 groups meeting size criteria), each used for generating synthetic training data.

#### 3.5. Training Setup and Baselines

- **Training:** Qwen2.5-Instruct family models are trained. Hyperparameters are minimally tuned for stability. Full-parameter fine-tuning is performed using FSDP. Models are trained on the annotated set for 50 epochs and on CommonCrawl data for 1 epoch, collecting 8 rollouts per update for GRPO.
- **Baselines:** A range of strong baselines is implemented using both closed-source (GPT-4o) and open-source LLMs (Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-Instruct, gpt-oss). All baselines use the Dedup HTML processing.
 - `agentic-n-iter`: A ReAct-style iterative refinement approach where the model retries script generation if the previous attempt fails or yields empty output.
 - `n-shot`: In-context learning examples (HTML and gold extraction results) are provided to the model.
 - `flatten`: HTML is directly flattened to plain text, eliminating structural information, and used as input for direct triple extraction without script generation. This baseline has no generalizability requirement.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluate SCRIBES's performance across various metrics, focusing on script quality, resource efficiency, and downstream impact.

#### 4.1. RQ1: SCRIBES Framework Improves Semi-Structured Data Extraction

SCRIBES-trained models consistently demonstrated improved capabilities in extracting semi-structured data compared to agentic baselines. The best Q-14B and Q-32B SCRIBES models exhibited a significant improvement of over 13% in macro-averaged F1 score ($F^1_{LM}$) when compared to their respective few-shot agentic base models. The best Q-32B SCRIBES model achieved performance comparable to the more powerful few-shot agentic GO-120B model.
A key aspect of SCRIBES's performance is its ability to generate generalizable scripts. While some baselines might perform adequately on the "Example" webpage used for script generation, SCRIBES showed stronger performance on "Holdout" webpages, confirming its generalization capacity. For instance, the Q-32B (+CC) model achieved an $F^1_{LM}$ of 34.6% on examples and 32.4% on holdout pages, demonstrating consistent performance across similar pages.

#### 4.2. RQ2: Resource-Efficient, Web-Scale Extraction

SCRIBES demonstrated its applicability for resource-efficient, web-scale extraction. In an evaluation on a subset of CommonCrawl data (113,129 webpages) not used for training, the model extracted 2,788,760 triples. Notably, only 4,661 webpages (approximately 4.1%) required direct model predictions to generate a script; the vast majority of triples were generated automatically by applying these few model-produced scripts to structurally similar pages.
The efficiency gain is quantified by the token speedup. Processing a webpage with deduplicated HTML requires approximately 8,879 tokens, whereas using flattened HTML requires 2,399 tokens, resulting in a token ratio ($\rho$) of about 3.7. SCRIBES becomes more efficient than per-page LLM inference when a website contains at least 4 structurally similar pages, with the speedup growing linearly with the number of similar pages ($k$). This indicates a substantial reduction in GPU resource usage for web-scale operations.

#### 4.3. RQ3: Impact of SCRIBES Reward Design on Generalization

An ablation study focused on the reward design confirmed the importance of SCRIBES's Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework. When a Q-14B model was trained using only "self-score" (rewarding only performance on the specific training example, Eq. 3) instead of the group-averaged reward (Eq. 1), its performance on holdout webpages significantly declined by 7.2% in $F^1_{LM}$. Although the self-score model performed slightly better on the example webpage itself (+1.2%), its inability to generalize led to a worse overall performance (-4.2%). This finding supports the effectiveness of SCRIBES's reward function in instilling the capability to produce generalizable scripts.

#### 4.4. RQ4: Impact of CommonCrawl Data

Incorporating synthetic training data derived from CommonCrawl further improved the performance of SCRIBES-trained models. Additional training on CC data yielded gains of approximately 2% in $F^1_{LM}$ for Q-14B and 5% for Q-32B overall.
Further ablation studies clarified the strategy for using noisy CC data. Direct training solely on CC data or mixing CC data with annotated data at a 1:1 ratio did not lead to performance improvements. Instead, a staged approach—first training the model with gold rewards from annotated data to establish strong prior knowledge, and then continuing training on CC data—proved effective. This suggests that exposing the model to more diverse inputs with noisy rewards, after an initial strong foundation, can preserve and even improve performance.

#### 4.5. RQ5: Effect of Selecting Failure-Case Subset in CommonCrawl Training

The strategy of concentrating CC training on webpages where the model's predicted scripts initially failed to produce any results proved beneficial. Training on this "Failure-Case CC" subset generally yielded stronger performance gains compared to training on the "All CC" dataset. Specifically, for the Q-32B model, training on Failure-Case CC resulted in a 3.5% higher $F^1_{LM}$ compared to training on All CC, while for Q-14B, performance remained comparable across both settings. This indicates that focusing on difficult examples identified as failure cases helps to improve model robustness.

#### 4.6. Error Analysis

An error analysis of the best-performing Q-32B model revealed that performance inversely correlates with the structural complexity of webpages. Models performed best on "Horizontal Tables," followed by "Attribute-Value Pairs," and performed worst on "Free-Form" pages, where semi-structured content is more distributed and less uniform. This suggests that webpages with more numerous or complex structures present greater challenges for the model.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SCRIBES framework introduces several contributions and holds implications for various applications in web information extraction and related fields.

### 6. Enhanced Efficiency and Scalability for Web-Scale Extraction

SCRIBES offers a solution to the resource-intensiveness of current LLM-based extraction methods. By generating reusable extraction scripts rather than performing per-page LLM inference, it drastically reduces computational costs. The demonstrated linear speedup as a function of structurally similar pages within a site indicates that SCRIBES can enable scalable web data extraction that was previously impractical with LLM-only approaches. This efficiency allows for processing significantly larger volumes of web data with reduced GPU resource usage.

### 7. Improved Generalization of Extraction Logic

The reinforcement learning framework, particularly the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) reward design, explicitly trains models to generate scripts that generalize across structurally similar webpages. This addresses a major limitation of traditional, brittle wrapper-induction methods and improves upon LLM-based methods that treat pages in isolation. The ability to produce robust, reusable scripts is fundamental for handling template variations and evolving web content within the same domain.

### 8. Advancements in Training with Scarce Labeled Data

SCRIBES's iterative approach to leveraging unlabeled CommonCrawl data, combined with LLM-based synthetic annotations and targeted training on "failure cases," provides a method for extending model capabilities beyond limited annotated datasets. This reduces reliance on expensive human annotations for complex script generation tasks, offering a pathway to build more comprehensive and robust extraction models.

### 9. Boost to Downstream Question Answering (QA) Systems

The improved quality of extracted structured triples directly translates into benefits for downstream applications. The research demonstrates that incorporating SCRIBES-extracted triples boosts question answering accuracy across a range of LLMs, including a notable improvement of over 4% for GPT-4o on QA tasks requiring structured reasoning over HTML. This impact suggests that SCRIBES can augment the capabilities of LLMs by providing structured knowledge, complementing their textual understanding.

### 10. Enabler for Multi-page, Complex QA

The efficient extraction of structured triples across multiple similar webpages facilitates complex queries that require aggregation or ranking of information from several sources. Traditional RAG solutions struggle with such multi-page reasoning without resource-intensive, page-by-page Knowledge Graph construction. SCRIBES provides a mechanism for building a structured view of a website, opening avenues for more sophisticated information retrieval and reasoning.

### 11. Potential for Enriching LLM Pretraining Data

Current open-source LLM pretraining corpora often filter out semi-structured content, leading to a potential gap in LLMs' understanding of such data. SCRIBES offers an efficient and resource-effective means to extract and incorporate structured information from semi-structured web content into pretraining datasets. This could lead to future LLMs with an enhanced ability to process and reason over diverse web data formats.

In conclusion, SCRIBES represents a step towards making web-scale structured data extraction both effective and economically feasible, providing a valuable framework and tool for the research community and for practical applications requiring knowledge from the vast semi-structured web.
