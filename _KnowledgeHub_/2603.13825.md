# Building Explicit World Model for Zero-Shot Open-World Object Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Delft University of Technology developed an explicit world model framework enabling robots to perform zero-shot, open-world object manipulation based on natural language instructions, without requiring task-specific demonstrations. The system constructs physically-grounded digital twins from partial observations, samples manipulation strategies in a physics simulator, and evaluates outcomes using a vision-language model, achieving successful manipulation in 6 out of 9 diverse real-world tasks.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for open-world manipulation often rely on extensive, costly robot action demonstrations, limiting their generalization to novel objects or tasks.
- Implicit, 2D-based world models lack explicit 3D structure and physical consistency, hindering accurate reasoning about geometry, contact, and dynamics crucial for robust physical manipulation.
- Generalizing to novel, previously unseen objects and tasks in unstructured environments (open-world settings) remains a significant challenge for robotic manipulation systems.

## Method

- A multi-stage framework integrates ==open-set segmentation== (**GPT-4o**, **Grounded-SAM**) and ==grasp pose prediction== (**AnyGrasp**) to prepare for object interaction.
- A ==digital twin construction== module generates 3D meshes (**Hunyuan 3D 2.0**) and employs a robust ==two-stage pose alignment== (**DINOv2** for coarse alignment, RANSAC/ICP for fine alignment) to accurately fit them to real-world observations, also predicting material properties via **GPT-4o**.
- Manipulation strategies are sampled within a physics-enabled simulator (**NVIDIA Isaac Sim**) using the constructed digital twin, and their outcomes are evaluated against natural language instructions by a VLM (**GPT-4o**) to select the optimal action for real-robot execution.

## Results

- The two-stage mesh alignment method achieved substantially higher success rates (e.g., **90.91%** for a 'mug' freely placed, **88.89%** for a 'mug' grasped) compared to direct alignment (e.g., **27.27%** and **11.11%** respectively), while maintaining comparable geometric accuracy (RMSE typically 0.003-0.007).
- The system successfully performed **6 out of 9** zero-shot open-world manipulation tasks on a real robot with at least **75%** success, including 'Put the yellow cube into the white box' (**91.7%**) and 'Put the banana into the basket' (**83.3%**).
- Analysis revealed that **11.9%** of failures occurred during the VLM-based 'sampling result checker' due to visual ambiguity, and **16.0%** occurred during 'real robot execution' due to the sim-to-real gap, physics engine limitations, and hardware precision issues.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A two-stage mesh alignment pipeline, combining appearance-based coarse alignment with geometric fine alignment, significantly improves the robustness of 6-DoF digital twin reconstruction from partial observations.
> - Leveraging large vision-language models for semantic segmentation, material property inference, and outcome evaluation allows for flexible, zero-shot task understanding and strategy assessment without explicit task-specific training.
> - The 'imagine-then-act' paradigm, enabled by a high-fidelity explicit world model in a physics simulator, allows for exploring and validating diverse manipulation strategies, reducing the need for real-world trial-and-error and improving safety.
