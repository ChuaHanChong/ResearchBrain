# Building Explicit World Model for Zero-Shot Open-World Object Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Delft University of Technology developed an explicit world model framework enabling robots to perform zero-shot, open-world object manipulation based on natural language instructions, without requiring task-specific demonstrations. The system constructs physically-grounded digital twins from partial observations, samples manipulation strategies in a physics simulator, and evaluates outcomes using a vision-language model, achieving successful manipulation in 6 out of 9 diverse real-world tasks.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for open-world manipulation often rely on extensive, costly robot action demonstrations, limiting their generalization to novel objects or tasks.
- Implicit, 2D-based world models lack explicit 3D structure and physical consistency, hindering accurate reasoning about geometry, contact, and dynamics crucial for robust physical manipulation.
- Generalizing to novel, previously unseen objects and tasks in unstructured environments (open-world settings) remains a significant challenge for robotic manipulation systems.

## Method

- A multi-stage framework integrates ==open-set segmentation== (**GPT-4o**, **Grounded-SAM**) and ==grasp pose prediction== (**AnyGrasp**) to prepare for object interaction.
- A ==digital twin construction== module generates 3D meshes (**Hunyuan 3D 2.0**) and employs a robust ==two-stage pose alignment== (**DINOv2** for coarse alignment, RANSAC/ICP for fine alignment) to accurately fit them to real-world observations, also predicting material properties via **GPT-4o**.
- Manipulation strategies are sampled within a physics-enabled simulator (**NVIDIA Isaac Sim**) using the constructed digital twin, and their outcomes are evaluated against natural language instructions by a VLM (**GPT-4o**) to select the optimal action for real-robot execution.

## Results

- The two-stage mesh alignment method achieved substantially higher success rates (e.g., **90.91%** for a 'mug' freely placed, **88.89%** for a 'mug' grasped) compared to direct alignment (e.g., **27.27%** and **11.11%** respectively), while maintaining comparable geometric accuracy (RMSE typically 0.003-0.007).
- The system successfully performed **6 out of 9** zero-shot open-world manipulation tasks on a real robot with at least **75%** success, including 'Put the yellow cube into the white box' (**91.7%**) and 'Put the banana into the basket' (**83.3%**).
- Analysis revealed that **11.9%** of failures occurred during the VLM-based 'sampling result checker' due to visual ambiguity, and **16.0%** occurred during 'real robot execution' due to the sim-to-real gap, physics engine limitations, and hardware precision issues.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A two-stage mesh alignment pipeline, combining appearance-based coarse alignment with geometric fine alignment, significantly improves the robustness of 6-DoF digital twin reconstruction from partial observations.
> - Leveraging large vision-language models for semantic segmentation, material property inference, and outcome evaluation allows for flexible, zero-shot task understanding and strategy assessment without explicit task-specific training.
> - The 'imagine-then-act' paradigm, enabled by a high-fidelity explicit world model in a physics simulator, allows for exploring and validating diverse manipulation strategies, reducing the need for real-world trial-and-error and improving safety.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is progressing towards enabling autonomous agents to perform complex manipulation tasks in unstructured, dynamic environments, commonly referred to as open-world manipulation. This domain requires robots to interpret high-level natural language commands, perceive and interact physically with novel, previously unseen objects.

Current research in open-world manipulation primarily explores two main paradigms: Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and world models. VLA models, leveraging large-scale vision-language backbones, have shown capabilities in directly predicting robot actions. However, these models often depend on extensive and costly robot action demonstrations for training. This dependency can limit their generalization capabilities, particularly when encountering out-of-distribution objects or tasks. Recent efforts, such as the Open X-Embodiment dataset, aim to scale VLA training data, but collecting diverse, high-quality action trajectories remains an ongoing challenge.

An alternative approach involves world models, which learn to predict the consequences of actions rather than directly imitating them. These models can be broadly categorized into implicit dynamics modeling and explicit world construction. Implicit, often 2D image- or video-based, world models can generate visually plausible predictions but typically lack explicit 3D structure and physical consistency. This limitation hinders their ability to accurately reason about geometry, contact, and dynamics, which are critical for robust physical manipulation.

Explicit world models, in contrast, aim to represent the environment using geometrically and physically meaningful digital twin assets. Prior work in explicit world models has either focused on specialized problem settings, such as articulated object manipulation towards specific joint configurations, or continued to rely on task-specific demonstrations to train policies. For instance, some frameworks reconstruct simulation-ready digital twins from observations but still use these for training imitation learning policies with demonstrations.

This research contributes to the explicit world model paradigm by addressing a key limitation: the need for zero-shot generalization. The paper proposes a framework that constructs a physically grounded digital twin from partial 2D observations and employs this twin for strategy exploration and evaluation in a physics-enabled simulator, all without requiring task-specific demonstrations or training. This approach distinguishes itself from previous explicit world model methods by targeting a wider range of open-set manipulation tasks and eliminating the reliance on demonstrations for novel tasks, thus aiming for improved zero-shot generalization to both novel objects and novel tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an explicit world-model-based framework that enables zero-shot open-world object manipulation. This objective is driven by several motivations rooted in the limitations of existing approaches:

1. **Overcoming Data Dependency in VLA Models:** Current VLA models, while effective in some scenarios, necessitate large volumes of robot action demonstrations. Collecting this data is resource-intensive and can constrain the models' ability to generalize to new tasks or objects not encountered during training. The research aims to move beyond this imitation learning paradigm by enabling robots to reason about actions without prior demonstrations for specific tasks.

2. **Addressing Generalization Gaps:** Both VLA models and many traditional robot learning methods often struggle with out-of-distribution generalization. When presented with novel objects, environments, or task instructions, their performance can degrade significantly. The motivation here is to develop a system that can infer task goals from natural language, perceive previously unseen objects, and generate appropriate physical interactions in a zero-shot manner, meaning without any task-specific training or demonstrations.

3. **Enhancing Physical and 3D Understanding in World Models:** While some world models, particularly image- or video-based 2D variants, generate visually compelling predictions, they frequently lack explicit 3D structure and physical consistency. This deficiency limits their utility for tasks requiring precise physical interaction, contact reasoning, and understanding of real-world dynamics. The authors are motivated to build an *explicit* world model that represents the environment with geometrically accurate and physically meaningful digital twin assets, enabling more reliable simulation and planning.

4. **Enabling Open-Set Object Handling and Digital Twin Reconstruction from Partial Observations:** In open-world settings, target objects are not predefined. A significant challenge is accurately reconstructing a digital twin of an object from inherently partial 2D observations, which can be further obscured by occlusions (e.g., from a gripper). The research is motivated to integrate modern generative and visual foundation models with classical point-cloud registration techniques to construct digital twins that are both geometrically accurate and semantically consistent from such limited inputs.

5. **Efficient Exploration and Evaluation of Manipulation Strategies:** Instead of directly predicting actions, the proposed framework aims to explore and evaluate diverse interaction strategies within a physics-enabled simulator. This allows for testing hypothetical futures and making informed decisions without immediate real-world execution. The motivation is to leverage the capabilities of physics engines for reliable outcome prediction and large vision-language models (VLMs) for evaluating strategy consistency with high-level task prompts.

In summary, the key objectives are to develop a zero-shot manipulation framework that:
- Constructs dynamic, physically grounded digital twins of open-set objects from onboard observations.
- Accurately aligns these digital twins with real-world objects in scale and pose for simulation.
- Samples and evaluates manipulation strategies in a physics simulator using VLM-based outcome assessment.
- Operates without any task-specific training or demonstrations, achieving generalization to novel objects and tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework integrates several modules to achieve zero-shot open-world object manipulation. Given an RGB-D image of the scene and a natural language task instruction, the system generates and executes a manipulation plan. The core idea is to construct an explicit world model, sample candidate actions, simulate their outcomes, and evaluate these outcomes using a VLM. The framework focuses on rigid objects.

The system comprises four main stages:

**A. Open-set Segmentation and Grasping**
1. **Object Segmentation:** GPT-4o and Grounded-SAM are employed to identify and segment objects relevant to the natural language task prompt. GPT-4o queries the RGB image with a question to categorize objects as directly manipulated or other interactive objects. Grounded-SAM then generates accurate 2D segmentation masks for these identified objects.
2. **Grasp Pose Prediction:** AnyGrasp is used to predict a dense set of grasp pose candidates from the input RGB-D image. The top 1000 predictions, ranked by confidence, are retained.
3. **Instance-Level Grasp Selection:** The 2D masks from open-set segmentation are used to filter grasp candidates, retaining only those spatially proximal to the surface of the target object's partial 3D point cloud (obtained by back-projecting the masked RGB-D image).
4. **Grasp Result Checking:** After attempting a grasp, GPT-4o is queried with an observation from a bottom camera to verify grasping success. If grasping fails, the system attempts to re-identify and grasp the object.

**B. Digital Twin Construction**
This module aims to create accurate 3D meshes of target objects, align them with real-world observations, and predict their material properties for simulation.
1. **Mesh Generation:** Hunyuan 3D 2.0 generates a textured 3D mesh from the masked RGB image. Since these meshes are in unit scale and canonical pose, alignment with the real world is necessary.
2. **Two-Stage Pose Alignment:** A coarse-to-fine pipeline is proposed to align the generated mesh with the real-world observation.
 - **Coarse Alignment:** A set of hypothesized 6-DoF object poses are sampled. For each pose, the textured 3D mesh is rendered to produce RGB and depth images. DINOv2 is used as a feature extractor to compute cosine similarities between the rendered RGB image features and the real-world observation features. The hypothesized pose with the highest similarity is selected as the initial coarse pose estimate.
 - **Fine Alignment:** Using the coarse pose, a depth image of the generated mesh is rendered and back-projected to obtain a partial point cloud corresponding to the observed view. This converts the alignment into a partial-to-partial problem. Scale estimation is performed by comparing 3D bounding box dimensions of the rendered mesh's partial point cloud and the real object's partial point cloud. Finally, RANSAC and ICP (Iterative Closest Point) are applied for precise refinement of the transformation.
3. **Material Property Prediction:** GPT-4o is utilized to infer the material properties of the object from its masked image, which are then assigned in the simulation environment to enhance physical realism.

**C. Manipulation Strategy Sampling**
Once the digital twin is constructed, it is integrated into NVIDIA Isaac Sim, a physics-enabled simulation environment, for strategy sampling and evaluation.
1. **Interaction Area Segmentation:** To reduce the search space, GPT-4o and Grounded-SAM segment the most probable interaction region based on the task prompt. The centroid of the back-projected 3D point cloud of this region serves as a reference for constraining the translation component of the grasped object's pose.
2. **Strategy Sampling and Simulation:** Within the constrained interaction area, various rotation angles for the grasped object are sampled to generate diverse 6-DoF pose hypotheses. For each sampled strategy, the scene is simulated in Isaac Sim with the non-manipulated objects at their real-world poses and the grasped object at the sampled pose. An inverse kinematics solver determines the Kinova arm's joint values.
3. **Result Checking and Selection:** An RGB image is rendered from a fixed viewpoint for each simulated outcome. GPT-4o is then queried with this rendered image and the natural language task instruction to assess whether the outcome satisfies the instruction. This provides binary labels (success/failure) for each candidate pose.
4. **Success Probability Estimation:** A Gaussian Process (GP) classifier is trained on the VLM-provided labels to output calibrated success probabilities for each candidate strategy. The strategy with the highest predicted success likelihood is selected for real-robot execution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were divided into two main parts: digital twin alignment accuracy and real-world manipulation tasks. A 7-DoF Kinova Gen3 Lite robotic arm was used, equipped with two Intel RealSense D405 stereo cameras. NVIDIA RTX 2070 and RTX 3090 GPUs were used for simulation and computer vision models, respectively.

**A. Digital Twin Alignment Accuracy**
The effectiveness of the proposed two-stage mesh alignment pipeline was compared against a direct alignment baseline (RANSAC and ICP applied without a coarse initial guess). Ground-truth object poses were not available, so evaluation relied on alignment success rate and Root Mean Square Error (RMSE).
- **Results:** The two-stage alignment method demonstrated a substantial improvement in alignment success rate compared to direct alignment. For objects freely placed, the proposed method achieved success rates ranging from 40% to 100%, while direct alignment ranged from 20% to 100%. Specifically, for a 'mug', the proposed method achieved 90.91% success compared to 27.27% for direct alignment. For objects grasped in the gripper, the improvement was more pronounced, with the proposed method achieving 66.67% to 100% success, whereas direct alignment ranged from 11.11% to 100%. For instance, a 'mug' grasped in the gripper showed 88.89% success for the proposed method versus 11.11% for direct alignment. Both methods maintained comparable geometric accuracy as measured by RMSE, typically in the range of 0.003-0.007.
- **Analysis:** The increased success rate is attributed to the initial coarse alignment stage, which uses appearance similarity to provide a robust starting point, effectively mitigating the common issue of RANSAC/ICP converging to local minima or incorrect correspondences when dealing with partial point clouds and scale differences.

**B. Real Robot Task Performance**
Nine distinct real robot manipulation tasks were performed, totaling 96 attempts. These tasks were designed to test the system's ability to understand semantic instructions, spatial relations, and operate on previously unseen objects without demonstrations.
- **Overall Success Rates:** Six out of nine tasks achieved a success rate of at least 75%. These included tasks like "Put the yellow cube into the white box" (91.7%) and "Put the banana into the basket" (83.3%). The remaining three tasks, notably "Put the blue cup upside on the wooden box" and "Put the blue cup upside down on the wooden box," performed less reliably, both achieving 33.3% success.
- **Stage-wise Success/Failure Analysis (Sankey Diagram):**
 - **Grasping:** High success rate (93/96 trials).
 - **Mesh Alignment:** High success rate (93/93 successful grasps).
 - **Interaction Area Segmentation:** High success rate (93/93 successful alignments).
 - **Simulation Strategy Sampling:** Very high success rate (92/93 trials led to successful sampling).
 - **Sampling Result Checker:** This stage accounted for a significant portion of failures (11 failures out of 92 simulations).
 - **Real Robot Execution:** This stage also showed a notable number of failures (13 failures out of 81 successful checks).
- **Failure Analysis:**
 - **Result Checker Failures:** Predominantly occurred in tasks requiring subtle visual distinctions, such as discerning a cup standing upside versus upside down. The visual information rendered for the LLM was sometimes ambiguous, leading to incorrect judgments.
 - **Real Robot Execution Failures (Sim-to-Real Gap):** Despite successful simulation, real robot execution failures indicate a non-trivial sim-to-real gap. Contributing factors include:
 - **Physics Engine Limitations:** Isaac Sim's physics engine, while realistic, could not perfectly replicate real-world contact dynamics. Material property estimation helped but was limited, especially for objects with hollow or deformable structures (e.g., plastic cubes), leading to inconsistencies.
 - **Robot Hardware and Control:** Precision limitations and latency in the robot hardware introduced minor pose and timing errors, particularly affecting tasks with tight spatial constraints.
 - **Sensor Noise and Calibration:** Errors in camera extrinsics and depth measurements contributed to small misalignments between the reconstructed digital twin and the actual environment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents an explicit world model-based framework for open-world object manipulation that aims to achieve zero-shot generalization. The work demonstrates several significant contributions and has potential impacts on the field of robotics:

1. **Advancement in Zero-Shot Generalization:** The framework's ability to perform manipulation tasks on novel, open-set objects based on natural language instructions, without requiring any task-specific demonstrations or training, represents a step towards more autonomous and versatile robotic systems. This contrasts with many existing methods that are bottlenecked by data collection or limited to specific object categories or tasks.

2. **Robust Digital Twin Construction:** The proposed two-stage mesh alignment pipeline (coarse alignment via visual feature similarity and fine alignment via RANSAC/ICP) addresses a critical challenge in creating accurate digital twins from partial and occluded real-world observations. This enhanced robustness in object pose and scale estimation directly contributes to the fidelity of the simulation environment, which is foundational for reliable physics-based planning.

3. **Integration of Foundation Models and Classical Robotics:** The work effectively integrates modern generative (Hunyuan 3D 2.0), visual (Grounded-SAM, DINOv2), and vision-language foundation models (GPT-4o) with classical robotics techniques like point cloud registration, grasp pose prediction (AnyGrasp), and physics simulation. This hybrid approach leverages the semantic understanding and generation capabilities of large models while grounding them in the physical realities modeled by classical methods.

4. **Enabled Dynamic Reasoning and Strategy Evaluation in Simulation:** By constructing a physically grounded digital twin, the system can explore and evaluate diverse manipulation strategies within a physics-enabled simulator. This 'imagine-then-act' paradigm allows robots to predict action outcomes and select the most promising sequence without risky or time-consuming real-world trial and error, improving safety and efficiency. The VLM-based evaluation of simulated outcomes against natural language instructions provides a flexible and semantic understanding of task success.

5. **Addressing the Sim-to-Real Gap:** While acknowledging existing limitations, the methodology actively works to bridge the sim-to-real gap by estimating material properties for more accurate physics simulation and through its two-stage alignment process. Demonstrating successful transfer from simulation to real-world execution for a variety of tasks validates the potential of explicit world models for practical robotic applications.

**Potential Impact:**
The development of such a zero-shot, explicit world model-based framework could lead to:
- **More adaptable robots:** Robots capable of quickly learning and performing new tasks in dynamic, unpredictable environments with minimal human intervention.
- **Reduced data collection costs:** Shifting away from heavy reliance on costly robot action demonstrations could accelerate research and deployment of robotic systems.
- **Enhanced human-robot interaction:** Robots that can interpret complex, high-level natural language instructions and adapt to unforeseen circumstances.
- **Broader application domains:** This approach could unlock robotic manipulation in various sectors, including manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, and service industries, where object variety and task diversity are high.

The research also highlights areas for future development, such as improving the computational efficiency of the simulation-based strategy sampling process to facilitate real-time deployment, and further refining the robustness of VLM-based result checking in ambiguous visual scenarios. These considerations point towards continued efforts to refine and optimize explicit world models for broader real-world applicability.
