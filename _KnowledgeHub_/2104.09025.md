# The MIT Humanoid Robot: Design, Motion Planning, and Control For Acrobatic Behaviors

> [!summary] Summary
> This research details an integrated system for dynamic, acrobatic humanoid robot behaviors, combining a novel hardware design, an actuation-aware motion planner, and a hierarchical landing controller. Simulations demonstrated successful execution of complex maneuvers like backflips and front flips, ensuring planned trajectories respected detailed actuator and battery limitations.

## Problem

- Enabling highly dynamic and acrobatic behaviors in humanoid robots faces challenges due to limitations in existing hardware designs (e.g., high impedance hydraulics, limited force control in compliant actuators).
- Generating feasible motion plans for complex, high-degree-of-freedom acrobatic maneuvers is difficult, with existing trajectory optimization methods often computationally expensive or failing to account for critical actuator limitations.
- Achieving stable and robust landings after high-impact aerial maneuvers requires precise control and effective energy dissipation, which is challenging with simplified models or inconsistent control frameworks.

## Method

- Developed the ==MIT Humanoid== robot, designed with torque-dense electric motors and backdrivable proprioceptive actuators, extending principles from the MIT Cheetah series to a humanoid platform.
- Introduced an Actuation-Aware Kino-Dynamic (AAKD) planner that explicitly integrates detailed, empirically validated actuator and battery dynamic limits into trajectory optimization.
- Implemented a hierarchical landing control framework combining Model-Predictive Control (MPC) for long-horizon planning with Whole-Body Impulse Control (WBIC) for high-bandwidth, dynamically consistent feedback.

## Results

- The MIT Humanoid robot successfully demonstrated a range of acrobatic motions in a high-fidelity simulation environment, including backflips, front flips, and 180° spinning jumps, using accurate actuator and battery models.
- The AAKD planner generated trajectories for acrobatic maneuvers that consistently adhered to all specified actuator limits, unlike standard kino-dynamic planning, with an acceptable **28.2%** increase in solve time.
- The hierarchical MPC + WBIC landing controller achieved stable recovery from high-impact landings, effectively managing body orientation and angular momentum, with WBIC enabling emergent arm motions for stabilization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Proprioceptive actuator design, previously successful in quadrupeds, can be effectively adapted for humanoid robots to enable high-impulse, dynamic behaviors while managing impacts.
> - Explicitly incorporating actuator torque, speed, and power limits, including battery voltage droop, directly into kino-dynamic motion planning is essential for generating physically executable acrobatic trajectories.
> - A hierarchical control architecture that carefully prioritizes tasks, such as body orientation over centroidal momentum, allows for robust post-impact stabilization and can lead to emergent stabilizing motions.
