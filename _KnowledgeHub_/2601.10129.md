# LaViT: Aligning Latent Visual Thoughts for Multi-modal Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from City University of Hong Kong developed LaViT, a framework that aligns latent visual thoughts from a large teacher model into a smaller student Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM), addressing a "Perception Gap" in multimodal distillation. LaViT-3B surpassed larger 7B models and proprietary models like GPT-4o on various complex visual reasoning benchmarks, achieving 78.23% on Relative Depth and 32.0% on IQ-Test.

## Problem

- Existing latent reasoning methods in MLLMs primarily rely on externally provided visual supervision, neglecting the intrinsic, dynamic visual attention mechanisms a model employs during complex reasoning.
- Standard knowledge distillation for MLLMs often fails to transfer the teacher's visual attention trajectories to the student, creating a "Perception Gap" where students mimic text but lack genuine visual grounding.
- Student models, when merely mimicking textual outputs or static embeddings, frequently rely on linguistic priors rather than genuinely observing relevant visual evidence, leading to unreliable responses.

## Method

- ==LaViT== introduces =="White-box Trajectory Distillation"== to autoregressively reconstruct the teacher's dynamic visual gaze (attention trajectories) and contextualized visual thoughts (semantics) using continuous latent tokens.
- A =="Curriculum Sensory Gating"== mechanism dynamically modulates the direct visual perception path with a time-dependent attention bias, compelling the student to internalize latent visual reasoning and preventing shortcut learning.
- A ==dual-stream distillation scheme== employs Semantic Reconstruction Loss, Trajectory Alignment Loss, and Next-Token Prediction Loss to jointly train the student in capturing both visual concepts to encode and where to attend during reasoning.

## Results

- LaViT-3B achieved substantial performance gains over its Qwen2.5-VL-3B baseline (e.g., **+16.94%** on Relative Depth, **+15.67%** on Relative Reflectance) and surpassed larger 7B models on multiple benchmarks.
- The model demonstrated superior complex visual reasoning, scoring **78.23%** on Relative Depth and **32.0%** on IQ-Test, outperforming proprietary models like GPT-4o on these perception-intensive benchmarks.
- LaViT reduced the information entropy of the student's attention distribution and achieved more stable attention patterns (lower Coefficient of Variation) than the teacher, attributed to Top-K sparsification filtering attentional noise.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Focused visual attention is causally linked to correct multimodal reasoning, and a lack of it leads to hallucinations, as evidenced by higher reasoning accuracy correlating with increased attention mass in ground-truth regions.
> - Standard knowledge distillation fails to transfer visual attention patterns from teacher to student, confirming that textual mimicry does not guarantee visual understanding or grounding.
> - Explicitly aligning both visual semantics and attention trajectories via latent tokens can effectively bridge the "Perception Gap," enabling student models to learn both 'what to see' and 'where to look'.
> - Curriculum Sensory Gating is a robust method to prevent shortcut learning, ensuring student models genuinely internalize latent visual reasoning capabilities rather than bypassing them.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has witnessed rapid advancements, evolving from models that primarily "think about images" (i.e., captioning visual inputs and then reasoning in the language space) to those that "think with images" by more tightly integrating visual evidence into the reasoning process. This shift emphasizes the importance of grounded visual perception for complex tasks.

Within this landscape, a complementary direction known as "latent reasoning" has emerged. This paradigm compresses intermediate reasoning steps into continuous hidden states, offering improved computational efficiency and flexibility compared to explicit, discrete Chain-of-Thought (CoT) token sequences. Current MLLMs employing latent reasoning typically align specialized latent tokens with visual embeddings derived from *explicit* auxiliary supervision, such as helper images or annotated bounding boxes. However, a significant gap remains: these methods often overlook the *intrinsic visual attention dynamics* that guide a model's reasoning process.

This paper identifies a critical "Perception Gap" in existing multimodal distillation methods. Knowledge distillation, a technique to transfer capabilities from a high-capacity teacher model to a more compact student, has been applied to MLLMs. However, most existing approaches focus on aligning final textual outputs or static hidden state representations. The authors empirically demonstrate that merely mimicking textual outputs or static embeddings does not guarantee that the student model inherits the teacher's underlying visual reasoning process, specifically its visual attention patterns. Students may learn "what to say" without learning "where to look," relying on linguistic priors rather than genuine visual grounding.

LaViT directly addresses this Perception Gap. It represents a significant step towards enabling MLLMs to perform more genuinely *grounded* visual reasoning. By focusing on aligning *latent visual thoughts*—which encompass both the semantics of visual concepts and the trajectories of visual attention—LaViT pushes the boundary of multimodal reasoning beyond mere textual or static embedding alignment. It integrates insights from latent reasoning and knowledge distillation to create a more robust and visually aware student model, making it particularly relevant for tasks requiring fine-grained perception and complex spatial or relational understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation behind LaViT stems from a critical observation regarding the limitations of current multimodal latent reasoning and distillation techniques. The authors identify a "Perception Gap" through empirical analysis, which serves as the core impetus for their work.

**Motivation:**
1. **Limited Intrinsic Visual Understanding:** Existing latent reasoning methods in MLLMs primarily rely on externally provided visual supervision (e.g., auxiliary images, bounding box annotations). This approach neglects the intrinsic, dynamic visual attention mechanisms that a model employs during complex reasoning. The internal "thought process" of *how* a model visually grounds its reasoning remains largely unexplored and untransferred.
2. **Decoupling of Textual and Visual Alignment in Distillation:** The authors empirically show that standard knowledge distillation, which focuses on aligning textual outputs or static hidden states, often fails to transfer the teacher's visual attention trajectories to the student. They demonstrate that even when student models reproduce teacher outputs accurately, their visual attention can diverge significantly, especially for tasks requiring fine-grained visual grounding. This leads to a situation where student models "guess" based on language priors rather than genuinely "observing" relevant visual evidence. This "Perception Gap" highlights that textual mimicry does not guarantee visual understanding.
3. **Need for Grounded Perception:** The research posits that correct multimodal reasoning is causally linked to focused visual attention. Models that fail to attend to relevant visual regions are prone to generating hallucinated or unreliable responses. Therefore, enabling models to "look at the right place" is a prerequisite for robust visual reasoning.

### 3. Key Objectives

1. **Bridge the Perception Gap:** Develop a framework that explicitly aligns the teacher's internal visual semantics and attention trajectories with the student's latent representations, thereby enabling the student to learn both *what visual concepts to encode* and *where to attend* during reasoning.
2. **Enhance Visual Grounding:** Improve the student model's ability to precisely locate and leverage relevant visual information, reducing reliance on language priors and mitigating issues like "CLIP-blindness."
3. **Achieve Parameter-Efficient Reasoning:** Distill the complex reasoning capabilities of a large teacher model into a compact student model, allowing the smaller model to rival or surpass the performance of larger open-source and proprietary MLLMs on challenging visual reasoning tasks.
4. **Prevent Shortcut Learning:** Design a training mechanism that compels the student model to genuinely internalize latent visual reasoning rather than bypassing it by directly accessing raw visual features.

### 4. Methodology and Approach

LaViT introduces a novel framework built upon "White-box Trajectory Distillation" and "Curriculum Sensory Gating" to address the Perception Gap. The core idea is to compel a compact student MLLM to autoregressively reconstruct the teacher's internal visual semantics and attention trajectories using continuous latent tokens.

#### 4.1. Teacher-Student Setup and Data Construction

- **Teacher Model:** Qwen2.5-VL-32B, a large and capable MLLM, serves as the source of "latent visual thoughts."
- **Student Model:** Qwen2.5-VL-3B, a compact variant, is the target for distillation.
- **LaViT-SFT-15K Dataset:** A specialized dataset of 15,000 tuples `⟨I, Q, A, A_traj, V_sem⟩` is constructed. This dataset is rigorously filtered to ensure high quality:
 - **Correctness:** Only samples where the teacher model provides the correct answer are retained.
 - **Difficulty:** Instances solvable by a text-only model are removed to focus on visual reasoning.
 - **Alignment:** Samples where the teacher's aggregated attention mass (S_focus) is below 20% in ground-truth bounding box regions are discarded, ensuring strong visual grounding.
- **White-box Signals Extraction:** Two types of intrinsic teacher signals are extracted:
 - **Dynamic Visual Gaze (A_traj):** Aggregated cross-attention weights across all layers and heads for each token in the generated textual sequence, normalized and then sparsified using Top-K (k=8) selection. This captures the teacher's "where to look" trajectory.
 - **Contextualized Visual Thoughts (V_sem):** High-dimensional feature vectors (5120-dim) from the teacher's *last transformer layer*. These representations reflect how the teacher interprets visual content specifically in response to the given query, effectively capturing "what visual concepts to encode."

#### 4.2. White-box Trajectory Distillation

The student model is trained in a "Latent Teacher Forcing" manner, meaning it autoregressively generates a sequence of *K* continuous latent tokens (`V = {<v-trace_1>..., <v-trace_k>}`) *before* producing the textual response (`X_ans`). These latent tokens act as "Visual Information Containers" that must capture the distilled visual thoughts.

#### 4.3. Curriculum Sensory Gating

To prevent the student from "shortcut learning" (i.e., bypassing the latent tokens by directly attending to image patches), LaViT introduces **Curriculum Sensory Gating**. This mechanism dynamically modulates the direct visual perception path via a time-dependent scalar `γ(t) ∈ [ϵ, 1]`, implemented as an attention bias in the Transformer.
- **Phase 1: Sensory Warm-up (t < T_w):** `γ(t)` gradually increases from `ϵ` (e.g., 1e-6) to 1 following a cosine curve. Initially, this creates a strict "Latent Bottleneck," forcing the model to compress necessary visual information into `V`. This phase establishes a strong dependency on latent visual reasoning.
- **Phase 2: Fully Observable (t ≥ T_w):** `γ(t)` is set to 1, effectively removing the attention bias. The direct visual path functions as a "Residual Perception connection," allowing access to fine-grained pixel details. This configuration matches standard inference topology, ensuring zero distribution shift.

#### 4.4. Optimization Objectives

A dual-stream distillation scheme is employed with explicit gradient flow controls:
1. **Semantic Reconstruction Loss (L_concept):** Measures the cosine similarity between the student's latent hidden states (specifically, the last latent token's hidden state) and the teacher's `V_sem`. This compels `V` to reconstruct the teacher's high-quality visual semantics.
 `L_concept = 1 - CosSim(ϕ_mlp(h_z), V_sem)`
2. **Trajectory Alignment Loss (L_traj):** Uses KL Divergence to align the student's attention map (derived from `V`) with the teacher's sparsified `A_traj`. This ensures the student learns *where to look*.
 `L_traj = D_KL(A_traj || A_student)`
3. **Next-Token Prediction Loss (L_ntp):** The standard cross-entropy loss for language modeling, driving the textual response generation. Crucially, its gradient flow with respect to direct visual features is modulated by `γ(t)`, channeling gradients through `V` during Phase 1.

#### 4.5. Joint Training Dynamics

LaViT uses a streamlined joint training paradigm with a fixed distillation weight (`λ = 0.3`). The total loss is `L_total = L_ntp + λ * (L_concept + L_traj)`. This fixed weighting, combined with the curriculum gating, robustly regulates learning without complex hyperparameter scheduling.

### 5. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents compelling empirical evidence and detailed analyses to support the effectiveness of LaViT.

#### 5.1. Empirical Analysis of Perception Gap

- **Visual Attention Dictates Reasoning Bounds (RQ1):** The authors show a monotonic increase in reasoning accuracy as the "Visual Focusing Score" (S_focus – attention mass within ground-truth bounding boxes) increases. Correct samples exhibit significantly higher S_focus (15.89%) than incorrect ones (11.84%), demonstrating that focused visual attention is a necessary condition for successful reasoning and prevents hallucinations.
- **Perception Gap between Teachers and Students (RQ2):** Under standard Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), while the student model's textual representations align well with the teacher's (stable Cosine Distance of hidden states), their visual attention trajectories diverge significantly, especially for "Attribute" tokens requiring fine-grained visual grounding (KL Divergence increases). This confirms that textual mimicry does not guarantee visual understanding; students often rely on language priors.

#### 5.2. Main Results on Benchmarks

LaViT-3B consistently outperforms baselines across a diverse set of multimodal benchmarks:
- **Cross-Scale Superiority and Efficiency:**
 - LaViT-3B achieves substantial gains over its Qwen2.5-VL-3B baseline (e.g., +16.94% on Relative Depth, +15.67% on Relative Reflectance).
 - Remarkably, it surpasses the larger Qwen2.5-VL-7B on five out of seven benchmarks, and even outperforms other state-of-the-art 7B models like LVR-7B and R1-OneVision-7B on various tasks.
 - This demonstrates that LaViT's latent visual thinking approach is highly parameter-efficient.
- **Advantage in Complex Visual Reasoning:**
 - LaViT-3B excels on perception-intensive BLINK benchmarks, achieving 78.23% on Relative Depth and 32.0% on IQ-Test.
 - It surpasses proprietary models like GPT-4o (64.52% on Relative Depth, 30.0% on IQ-Test) and other reasoning-enhanced baselines, validating its superior capability in capturing structural visual semantics.
- **Fine-Grained Perception and Robustness:**
 - LaViT mitigates "CLIP-blindness" with a 67.33% score on MMVP, significantly outperforming models like DMLR (61.33%) and PAPO (50.0%).
 - Its improved performance on MMStar (54.07% vs. 50.2% baseline) confirms that gains stem from genuine visual understanding rather than language-based hallucinations.

#### 5.3. In-depth Analysis of Attention Dynamics

- **Sharpening Visual Focus:** LaViT effectively reduces the "Information Entropy" of the student's attention distribution (mean H=4.686) compared to the baseline (4.870), approaching the teacher's focused state (4.284). This indicates a transition from diffuse to focused observation, concentrating on task-relevant regions.
- **Achieving Superior Stability via Sparsification (Denoising Effect):** Surprisingly, LaViT-3B achieves a *more stable* attention pattern (Coefficient of Variation, CV=0.102) than the teacher Qwen2.5-VL-32B (CV=0.392). This is attributed to Top-K sparsification of the teacher's attention during distillation, which filters out low-confidence "attentional noise," resulting in a student that is more decisive and consistent.

#### 5.4. Ablation Studies

- **Impact of Alignment Components:** Removing either the Trajectory Alignment Loss (`L_traj`) or the Semantic Reconstruction Loss (`L_concept`) leads to significant performance drops across all evaluated tasks, confirming their essential roles. Masking latent tokens during inference also causes a marked decline, proving the student's genuine reliance on the generated visual thoughts.
- **Effectiveness of Curriculum Sensory Gating:** The "Curriculum Sensory Gating" module is crucial; its removal severely degrades performance, validating that the progressive gating strategy is essential for preventing shortcut learning.
- **Impact of Training Strategy:** A "Single Stage Training" baseline (where `γ(t)=1` always) consistently underperforms the full LaViT model, highlighting the importance of progressively exposing visual information to build robust reasoning capabilities.
- **Number of Latent Tokens (K):** An ablation study found that K=4 latent tokens achieved the best balance between visual grounding and reasoning capabilities, suggesting that a compact set is sufficient without introducing redundancy or noise.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

LaViT represents a significant advancement in the development of Multimodal Large Language Models, with several key implications:

1. **Bridging the "Perception Gap":** The most significant contribution of LaViT is its explicit identification and effective solution for the "Perception Gap" in MLLM distillation. By moving beyond textual or static embedding alignment, LaViT provides a principled way to transfer genuine visual understanding, ensuring student models not only know *what to say* but also *where to look* and *what to see*. This fundamental insight can guide future research in multimodal learning and distillation.

2. **Enhanced Visual Grounding and Reasoning:** LaViT-enabled MLLMs demonstrate significantly improved visual grounding, fine-grained perception, and complex reasoning abilities. This translates to more reliable performance on tasks requiring intricate visual analysis, such as relative depth estimation, object attribute recognition, and IQ-style visual tests. Such capabilities are crucial for real-world applications where precise visual interpretation is paramount.

3. **Parameter Efficiency and Accessibility:** Achieving state-of-the-art or competitive performance with a compact 3B model, even surpassing larger 7B models and proprietary behemoths like GPT-4o on specific benchmarks, underscores LaViT's exceptional parameter efficiency. This has a profound impact on making advanced MLLMs more accessible for deployment in resource-constrained environments, reducing computational costs for training and inference, and accelerating research for smaller labs.

4. **Robustness and Reliability:** By fostering genuine visual understanding over reliance on language priors, LaViT helps mitigate common MLLM issues like "CLIP-blindness" and hallucinations. This increased robustness makes the models more trustworthy and dependable, especially in sensitive domains.

5. **Methodological Innovations:**
 - **White-box Trajectory Distillation:** This novel approach of distilling dynamic attention trajectories and contextualized visual semantics from a teacher provides a powerful mechanism for transferring internal cognitive processes.
 - **Curriculum Sensory Gating:** This elegant, time-dependent mechanism is a robust solution for preventing shortcut learning during distillation, ensuring that student models genuinely internalize latent reasoning capabilities rather than finding superficial bypasses. This technique could be widely adopted in other knowledge distillation settings.
 - **Denoising Effect:** The unexpected finding that the student model achieves more stable attention patterns than the teacher (due to Top-K sparsification) offers valuable insights into how robust and essential visual cues can be extracted and transferred, potentially leading to models that are not just smaller but also *more* reliable in their focus.

In summary, LaViT pushes the boundaries of MLLM capabilities by addressing a core challenge in visual grounding and knowledge transfer. Its innovative methodology and impressive empirical results lay a strong foundation for developing more intelligent, efficient, and reliable multimodal AI systems.
