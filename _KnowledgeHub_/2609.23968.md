# Opt2VLA: Force-Aware Vision-Language-Action for Contact-Rich Humanoid Whole-Body Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Opt2VLA is a force-aware vision-language-action framework for contact-rich humanoid whole-body manipulation. A single multi-task policy, fine-tuned from GR00T-N1.7, jointly predicts end-effector waypoints and continuous contact-force references, which are executed by RL whole-body controllers trained with trajectory-optimization torque supervision. Training data comes from controller rollouts rather than teleoperation. Language-specified force levels are honored in 82.2% of closed-loop episodes and transfer to a 48 kg Digit humanoid.

## Problem

- Humanoid VLA systems represent actions as end-effector poses or joint targets, so whole-body controllers track motion only and never receive a desired interaction force.
- Geometrically identical motions need different force regimes depending on object mass, context or safety, and visual observations grow unreliable once contact and occlusion begin.
- Prior contact-aware VLAs use force or torque only as feedback describing ongoing interaction, and teleoperating contact-rich humanoid demonstrations with consistent force labels is costly.

## Method

- **Opt2VLA** policy: GR00T-N1.7 fine-tuned with a dedicated ==torque-encoder token== and 10-step state/torque history, predicting hand and foot waypoints plus normal contact-force references, with an auxiliary ==future-torque head==.
- **FCT controller**: asymmetric ==actor-critic RL== policy conditioned on noisy motion and force references, rewarded for contact-force tracking and for matching TO joint torques as training-only privileged supervision.
- Whole-body ==trajectory optimization== in Crocoddyl/Pinocchio under prescribed contact wrenches yields feasible motion, torque and force references; controller rollouts labeled gently/firmly/strongly form the VLA fine-tuning dataset.

## Results

- TO torque supervision cut average force-tracking error versus force-conditioning alone: wiping **1.7 N** vs **2.4 N**, shelf pushing **4.2 N** vs **11.2 N**, box pickup **0.9 N** vs **2.3 N**.
- Closed-loop joint success over **90** episodes reached **82.2%** for Opt2VLA versus **40.0%** for the kinematic-input baseline; a torque-encoder token gave **74.4%** against **27.8%** for state concatenation.
- On the **48 kg** Digit humanoid, FCT tracked **5-20 N** references with MAE **7.0/3.9/1.7 N** and R-squared **0.98/0.70/0.80** for wiping, pushing and pickup over **30** trials each.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Exposing desired contact force as an explicit command beside motion goals gives language a handle on interaction strength that purely geometric action spaces cannot express.
> - How torque feedback enters matters more than its presence: a dedicated torque encoder beat concatenating torque into the native state encoder by a wide margin.
> - Physics-based trajectory optimization can replace teleoperation as a supervision source, and a frozen vision-language backbone beat full fine-tuning on the hardest contact task.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

Moving a humanoid hand along the same path does not determine whether the robot should barely touch, press firmly, or grip tightly. Excess force can destabilize or damage an interaction; insufficient force can cause slipping, failed insertion, or loss of contact. Opt2VLA addresses this by letting a single policy issue two linked instructions—where to move and how strongly to interact—then letting a whole-body controller coordinate the joints. The same framework changes the commanded force regime across box pickup, shelf-box pushing, and surface wiping, both in simulation and on Digit hardware.

Recorded simulation rollouts showing gentle and strong variants of table wiping, box pickup, and shelf-box pressing. Blue/dashed overlays represent issued force; amber/solid overlays represent measured force. Illustrative props and scene dressing are not policy observations.

### 2. Make force a command

The key interface change is that a single VLA policy predicts both geometric 3D motion goals and continuous contact-force references; task-specific force-conditioned whole-body controllers then realize those commands. Offline DDP-based whole-body trajectory optimization (TO) enforces floating-base dynamics, contact feasibility, friction, joint limits, and actuator limits, producing feasible motion and privileged joint-torque references. The force-conditioned controller with torque supervision (FCT) actor receives deployable proprioception, measured upper-body joint torques, geometric end-effector targets, and target contact force; its joint-position offsets pass through a PD loop. Target contact force is available during both training and deployment, while TO torque references are training-only guidance.

Training data pairs RGB, proprioception, measured joint torques, issued end-effector targets, and continuous force labels with language such as "Wipe the table gently," "Push the box into the shelf firmly," and "Pick up the box strongly." The VLA is initialized from GR00T-N1.7 and predicts temporally aligned position waypoints for both hands and feet plus normal contact-force sequences for the active manipulation contact.

Panel (a) shows TO receiving motion goals, contact wrench, and contact schedule and producing force-aware reference trajectories. Panel (b) shows FCT training; TO torque references guide the critic but are not available at deployment. Panels (c–e) show data collection, VLA fine-tuning, and deployment through FCT controllers.

### 3. Force tracking and end-to-end planning

Three low-level variants isolate the effect of force conditioning and torque supervision. Motion-only (MO) tracks geometric targets without force inputs; FC adds target force and a force-tracking reward; FCT adds TO-derived reference joint-torque supervision during training. In surface wiping, MO produces similar force distributions across target levels; FC separates regimes; FCT separates regimes with more concentrated distributions. The FCT mean absolute force-tracking error is lower than FC for all three tasks: wiping 2.4 to 1.7 N, shelf pushing 11.2 to 4.2 N, and pickup 2.3 to 0.9 N. Shelf pushing retains a large ±5.5 N standard deviation for FCT, and the advantage is uneven at 20 N: FCT is still better on average, but its 20 N error is 7.0 ± 6.7 N.

Rows show FC and FCT; columns show increasing target force. Axes are end-effector position in meters; color encodes measured normal force in newtons. Force levels change with the requested target for FC and FCT. Table II supplies exact MAE values.

Joint success in the nine-variant closed-loop simulation requires task completion, a commanded mean within the prompt's force band (gentle N, firm (5, 16] N, strong (12, 22] N), and measured-versus-commanded mean-force agreement within 5 N, evaluated over 90 episodes. The default configuration C7 reaches 90.0% task-plus-force-intent and 82.2% overall joint success, compared with 53.3% task-plus-intent and 40.0% joint success for the motion–force baseline C0. Among planner variants, a dedicated torque-history encoder (C6) gives 74.4% joint success; adding an auxiliary future-torque objective raises C7 to 82.2%; full-backbone fine-tuning (C8) falls to 75.6% overall, with 50.0% on shelf pushing.

On hardware, 45 end-to-end trials use one VLA checkpoint; issued means move through intended gentle, firm, and strong regimes, while wiping shows the largest command-to-measurement gap. Across 30 RL-only hardware trials per task, FCT's MAE is 1.7 N for pickup, 3.9 N for shelf pushing, and 7.0 N for wiping; the authors explicitly note that absolute accuracy varies across tasks.

### 4. Autonomy includes contact

Success is not only reaching the object or surface, but regulating the physical interaction requested in language. Opt2VLA demonstrates that an explicit motion–force interface, physically grounded controller training, and torque-aware VLA conditioning can connect that request to whole-body execution—while remaining imperfect and task-dependent.
