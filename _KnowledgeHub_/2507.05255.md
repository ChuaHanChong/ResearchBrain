# Open Vision Reasoner: Transferring Linguistic Cognitive Behavior for Visual Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed Open-Vision-Reasoner (OVR), an MLLM that transfers linguistic cognitive behaviors to the visual domain, achieving state-of-the-art results on visual reasoning benchmarks such as 51.8% on MathVision and 54.6% on MathVerse for 7B models. This approach clarifies how cognitive patterns emerge and scale in multimodal contexts, using a two-stage training process.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) often lack the advanced reasoning capabilities (e.g., backtracking, self-verification) seen in Large Language Models (LLMs).
- Previous MLLM reinforcement learning efforts predominantly used RLHF, which is susceptible to 'reward hacking,' unlike the more robust Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (RLVR) paradigm.
- A systematic understanding of how linguistic cognitive behaviors from LLMs transfer to MLLMs for complex visual reasoning tasks was missing.

## Method

- A two-stage training paradigm was developed using Qwen2.5-VL-7B, starting with a ==linguistic cold start== fine-tuning on over 2 million language-only reasoning examples.
- The linguistic cold start was followed by large-scale multimodal Reinforcement Learning (RL) using the ==Open-Reasoner-Zero framework== on over 0.3 million mixed-modality examples.
- A minimalist, ==rule-based binary reward function== was employed during RL, providing a clear and unhackable signal for correct answers, aligning with the RLVR paradigm.

## Results

- OVR-7B achieved state-of-the-art visual reasoning among 7B models, scoring **51.8%** on MathVision, **54.6%** on MathVerse, and **33.5%** on DynaMath, demonstrating successful cross-modal transfer.
- The model exhibited enhanced language reasoning, outperforming other 7B open-source models by over **10%** on challenging math reasoning tasks like AIME 2024 (**63.5%**) and AIME 2025 (**52.1%**).
- Linguistic cold-start initially impaired visual perception but long-term multimodal RL effectively recovered and enhanced perceptual performance, while RL alone showed limitations for pure perceptual tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual-specific cognitive behaviors, such as visual reflection, emerge remarkably early in the linguistic cold-start phase due to 'mental imagery' encoded in linguistic patterns.
> - The linguistic cold-start phase broadly familiarizes the model with diverse cognitive behaviors, while large-scale multimodal RL critically discerns and strategically amplifies only the most effective ones for generalization.
> - The transfer of cognitive behaviors is strategic, with behaviors like backtracking showing significant growth in transfer rate (from 2.5% to 17.3%) due to their higher utility in complex visual reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical and evolving challenge in the field of artificial intelligence: equipping Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) with advanced reasoning capabilities akin to those observed in Large Language Models (LLMs). The broader landscape is characterized by:

- **Emergence of Reasoning in LLMs via RLVR:** Recent successes with LLMs like OpenAI's o1 and DeepSeek-R1 have demonstrated that Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (RLVR)—where correctness is objectively determined—can unlock powerful reasoning patterns (cognitive behaviors like backtracking, subgoal decomposition, self-verification). This stands in contrast to Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), which relies on learned reward models that can be susceptible to "reward hacking."
- **Transition to MLLMs:** MLLMs are a rapidly developing area, extending LLMs' linguistic prowess to visual understanding. Initial MLLM efforts often focused on tasks like image captioning and visual question answering, primarily using Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) or RLHF. However, transferring complex *reasoning* capabilities, especially those enabled by RLVR in LLMs, to MLLMs for visual tasks remains a significant challenge.
- **Limitations of Previous MLLM RL Efforts:** The paper highlights that early multimodal RL paradoxically adopted RLHF, relying on learned reward models. While recent efforts (e.g., Perception-R1, R1-OneVision, ReVisual-R1) have begun exploring rule-based rewards in multimodal settings and incorporating language-only cold starts, they haven't systematically answered the foundational question of *how linguistic cognitive behaviors transfer* to MLLMs for advanced visual reasoning.
- **Cognitive Behaviors as a Foundation for Reasoning:** The paper builds on the premise that robust reasoning in LLMs is tied to the acquisition of specific "cognitive behaviors" (e.g., backtracking, verification, subgoal setting, backward chaining). The key novelty here is investigating how these *linguistic* patterns can be translated and activated in a *multimodal* context to enhance visual reasoning.
- **Leveraging Existing Foundation Models:** The work is built upon Qwen2.5-VL-7B, a powerful open-source MLLM, positioning the research within the context of enhancing and understanding the capabilities of existing strong foundation models rather than building one from scratch.

By systematically investigating the transfer of linguistic cognitive behaviors to MLLMs using a large-scale RLVR paradigm, this work directly addresses a gap in the understanding of how advanced reasoning emerges in multimodal contexts. It seeks to bridge the success of RLVR in LLMs with the demands of visual reasoning, moving beyond superficial task performance to a deeper, behavior-aligned intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are multi-faceted, stemming from a fundamental question about intelligence transfer:

- **To Investigate Linguistic Cognitive Behavior Transfer:** The overarching goal is to understand "How can linguistic cognitive behaviors transfer to MLLMs for advanced visual reasoning?" This foundational question motivates the entire study, aiming to systematically analyze the emergence and scaling of such behaviors in the multimodal domain.
- **To Develop an Effective Two-Stage Training Paradigm:** The authors aim to construct a robust training methodology that first instills linguistic cognitive patterns and then effectively activates them for visual reasoning. This paradigm involves a large-scale linguistic "cold start" fine-tuning followed by extensive multimodal reinforcement learning.
- **To Create a State-of-the-Art Open-Source Multimodal Reasoner:** A concrete objective is to develop a highly capable MLLM, named Open-Vision-Reasoner (OVR), which demonstrates superior performance on both linguistic and multimodal reasoning benchmarks. This involves establishing the largest open-source RL practice on Qwen2.5-VL-7B to date.
- **To Conduct an In-depth Analysis of Visual Cognitive Behaviors:** Beyond achieving high performance, the research aims to trace the transfer and evolution of cognitive patterns throughout the training process. This includes identifying when and how these behaviors emerge and scale, and which types of behaviors are strategically favored.
- **To Understand the Impact on Visual Perception:** A secondary but important objective is to examine how the proposed training paradigm affects a foundational capability of MLLMs: visual perception, including aspects like perceptual degradation and recovery.

The motivation for this work is rooted in the success of RLVR in LLMs, which revealed that verifiable rewards lead to the emergence of robust cognitive behaviors. The authors hypothesize that the multimodal domain, with its "verifiable visual facts," is uniquely suited for a similar paradigm. By porting this principle to MLLMs, they aim to unlock more advanced and human-like visual reasoning capabilities, moving beyond basic perception to intricate problem-solving.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the Open-Vision-Reasoner (OVR) methodology is a novel two-stage training paradigm built upon the Qwen2.5-VL-7B foundation model, explicitly designed to foster and transfer cognitive behaviors from linguistic to visual domains.

#### 3.1. Base Model and Scale

The research utilizes **Qwen2.5-VL-7B** as the base multimodal Large Language Model. This is a powerful, open-source model, making the research reproducible and accessible to the broader community. The scale of the training is notable, described as "nearly 1,000 steps" for multimodal reinforcement learning, "surpassing all previous open-source efforts in scale" for this model.

#### 3.2. Two-Stage Training Pipeline

- **Stage 1: Linguistic Cold Start:**
 - **Objective:** To establish core cognitive behaviors (e.g., backtracking, subgoal decomposition) within a purely linguistic setting, preparing the LLM module for cross-modal generalization.
 - **Process:** The LLM module of Qwen2.5-VL-7B is supervised fine-tuned (SFT) on a massive dataset of over **2 million language-only reasoning examples**. These examples are distilled from high-quality sources like DeepSeek-R1 and other public and synthesized datasets covering mathematics, science, and general logic.
 - **Implementation:** Fine-tuning runs for 5 epochs with a large batch size (640), long sequence length (64k), and a learning rate of 2 × 10<sup>-4</sup>. This aggressive strategy is deemed crucial for imbuing the model with new cognitive paradigms.

- **Stage 2: Multimodal Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
 - **Objective:** To promote reasoning generalization and align the previously learned cognitive patterns with visual contexts, enabling effective cross-modal transfer.
 - **Process:** Reinforcement learning is applied under the **Open-Reasoner-Zero framework**, leveraging over **0.3 million mixed-modality examples**. This is described as the largest open-source RL practice on Qwen2.5-VL-7B.
 - **RL Algorithm:** A lightweight **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)** algorithm with **Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE)** is adopted.
 - **Policy and Value Networks:** Both are initialized from the cold-start model. The policy and critic (value) networks do not share weights.
 - **Reward Function:** A minimalist, **rule-based binary reward** is used. The model's predicted answer (encapsulated within `\boxed{}`) is compared against the reference answer. An exact match yields a reward of 1, otherwise 0. This approach aims for a "clear, scalable and unhackable reward signal," consistent with the RLVR paradigm.
 - **Training Dynamics:** The RL phase proceeds for 900 iterations, employing a curriculum for sequence length (starting at 24k, increasing to 32k, then 48k). Strict on-policy updates are enforced for the policy, while the critic performs multiple optimization steps.

#### 3.3. Dataset Construction

The authors carefully curate datasets for both stages:

- **Data Collection:** A broad range of prompt-answer pairs is collected for language (AIME, MATH, Numina-Math, Tulu3 MATH, OpenR1-Math-220k, programmatic logical problems) and multimodal scenarios (Geometry3k, GeoQA, Geos, IconQA, Pixmo, ChartQA, PuzzleVQA, AlgoPuzzleVQA, STEM datasets, AtomThink, in-house math).
- **Data Curation:** A multi-step process refines data quality:
 1. **Automated Filtering:** A pre-trained model filters out samples with high training loss (indicating noise or complexity). Rule-based and model-assisted methods remove undesirable patterns.
 2. **Reweighting:** To balance coverage, categories are reweighted, down-weighting overrepresented ones and emphasizing rare but valuable instances.
 3. **RL-Specific Filtering:** For the RL phase, problems incompatible with binary reward functions (e.g., proof-style) and overly trivial or infeasible samples are excluded to ensure stable learning and unhackability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields significant performance achievements and three fundamental insights into cognitive behavior transfer.

#### 4.1. Superior Performance on Reasoning Benchmarks

- **Enhanced Language Reasoning and General Capabilities:** OVR-7B demonstrates exceptional performance on diverse language benchmarks, surpassing other 7B open-source models by an average of over 10% on challenging math reasoning tasks like AIME 2024 (63.5%) and AIME 2025 (52.1%). It also achieves 95.3% on MATH500. For general reasoning, it gains +4.6% on MMLU and +10.4% on MMLU-Pro over parameter-matched competitors (Table 2). This validates the effectiveness of the curated linguistic cold-start data.
- **Superior Visual Reasoning Abilities:** OVR sets a new benchmark for 7B models in visual reasoning, being the first Qwen2.5-VL-7B-based model to surpass 50% on MathVision (51.8%). It also achieves state-of-the-art results among 7B models on MathVerse (54.6%) and DynaMath (33.5%), alongside significant gains on MMMU-Pro (+7.2%). This indicates successful cross-modal transfer of reasoning capabilities from language training to multimodal tasks (Table 3, Figure 1).

#### 4.2. Insights into Multimodal Cognitive Behavior Transfer

The analysis of training dynamics and cognitive behavior emergence reveals three key findings:

1. **Early Emergence of Behavior due to Linguistic Mental Imagery:** Visual-specific cognitive behaviors, such as visual reflection, emerge remarkably early in the cold-start phase. This is attributed to the linguistic patterns in the cold-start data encoding "mental imagery." DeepSeek-R1 responses, even in purely linguistic problems, showed signs of constructing internal visualizations (e.g., "let me visualize..."). Once this linguistic scaffolding was introduced into the MLLM, these mental images rapidly grounded themselves in actual visual input, facilitating rapid generalization.
2. **Cold Start Learns Broadly, Large-scale RL Discerns Critically:** The cold-start phase broadly memorizes diverse visual cognitive behaviors. However, their prevalence might initially be suppressed before being significantly amplified during multimodal RL. This suggests a division of labor: cold start indiscriminately ingests patterns, while RL acts as a "strategic filter" that discerns and scales up only the most effective and pivotal behaviors, leading to superior generalization.
3. **Strategic Transfer Favoring High-Utility Behaviors:** The transfer of cognitive behaviors from linguistic to visual modalities is strategic. For instance, backtracking shows consistent growth in transfer rate (from 2.5% to 17.3%), while verification exhibits near-zero transfer. This indicates that the RL phase prioritizes behaviors that are more fundamental to complex visual reasoning or that have stronger linguistic precursors (like backtracking's link to "mental imagination"), making their amplification a higher priority.

#### 4.3. Visual Perception Analysis

- **Cold Start Impairs Perception, RL Enhances:** The linguistic cold-start phase can initially lead to perceptual degradation and increased hallucinations, likely due to token distribution shifts from large-scale linguistic data. However, long-term multimodal RL effectively mitigates these issues, recovering and enhancing perceptual performance on benchmarks like MMBench and BLINK (Table 4). This highlights RL's ability to discern and reinforce perceptual capabilities critical for multimodal tasks.
- **Unscalability of RL for Pure Perception:** When RL focuses solely on discriminative perceptual tasks (e.g., OCR, counting), a divergence is observed: rewards increase, but average response length (a proxy for reasoning complexity) remains stagnant (Figure 6). This suggests a fundamental limitation in current RL paradigms for pure perceptual tasks, indicating the absence of certain core visual cognitive behaviors that would drive deeper reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Open Vision Reasoner (OVR) paper makes several significant contributions and has substantial potential impact on the development of multimodal AI:

- **Pioneering Cognitive Behavior Transfer in MLLMs:** The work offers a pioneering systematic investigation into how linguistic cognitive behaviors—previously observed primarily in LLMs—can be transferred and manifested in MLLMs for visual reasoning. This deepens the understanding of visual intelligence scaffolded by linguistic reasoning, moving beyond empirical performance gains to a more mechanistic understanding.
- **State-of-the-Art Open-Source Model:** The release of OVR, its data, and training dynamics provides a new, high-performing open-source baseline for multimodal reasoning. Achieving state-of-the-art results among 7B models on challenging benchmarks like MathVision, MathVerse, and MATH500, and performing competitively with much larger commercial models, makes it a valuable resource for future research and development.
- **Novel Two-Stage Training Paradigm:** The proposed linguistic cold-start followed by large-scale multimodal RL framework offers a robust and effective strategy for training MLLMs that can acquire and leverage complex cognitive patterns. This paradigm, demonstrating how aggressive SFT sets the foundation and extensive RL then discerns and amplifies useful behaviors, could inform future MLLM training methodologies.
- **Deep Insights into RL and Cognitive Emergence:** The in-depth analysis of training dynamics and cognitive behavior emergence (early emergence, broad learning vs. critical discernment, strategic transfer) provides critical insights for the MLLM community. Understanding *when* and *how* these behaviors arise and are refined helps researchers design more effective training regimes and diagnostic tools.
- **Catalyst for Behavior-Aligned Multimodal Agents:** By demonstrating the transfer and evolution of human-like cognitive behaviors (visual reflection, divide-and-conquer, verification, goal-driven visual tracing), the work serves as a significant step towards creating "cognitively aligned multimodal agents." Such agents would not only provide correct answers but also reason in a transparent, interpretable, and human-like manner.
- **Identified Limitations and Future Directions:** The honest discussion of perceptual degradation during cold start and the unscalability of current RL for pure perceptual tasks highlights important areas for future research. This includes integrating linguistic data into model pretraining, introducing more multimodal supervision during cold start, and exploring advanced RL frameworks like multi-turn or agentic RL with visual manipulation and imagination. This candid identification of challenges is crucial for steering the field towards more robust and universally capable MLLMs.

In essence, Open-Vision-Reasoner pushes the boundaries of multimodal reasoning by meticulously connecting the success of verifiable reward RL in LLMs to the multimodal domain. It provides not only a powerful new model but also a deeper understanding of the underlying cognitive mechanisms, paving the way for more intelligent and adaptable AI systems.
