# MonoArt: Progressive Structural Reasoning for Monocular Articulated 3D Reconstruction

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at S-Lab, Nanyang Technological University developed MonoArt, an end-to-end framework for reconstructing articulated 3D objects from a single image by progressively reasoning about geometry, part structure, and motion. It achieves improved geometric and kinematic accuracy on PartNet-Mobility and demonstrates robust generalization to real-world objects.

## Problem

- Reconstructing articulated 3D objects from a single image is challenging due to limited visual information, necessitating joint inference of geometry, part structure, and kinematic relationships.
- Existing methods struggle with the entanglement of motion cues and object structure, leading to unstable articulation parameter regression.
- Many approaches rely on multi-view data, external motion templates, or complex retrieval/generative techniques, limiting their scalability and generalization.

## Method

- **MonoArt** introduces a unified, end-to-end framework for monocular articulated 3D reconstruction, employing a ==progressive structural reasoning== approach.
- The framework transforms visual observations through geometry-aware, part-aware, and motion-aware representations using components like a ==TRELLIS-based 3D Generator== and ==Part-Aware Semantic Reasoner==.
- A ==Dual-Query Motion Decoder== jointly models movable part identities and spatial motion anchors, with a ==Kinematic Estimator== predicting articulation parameters and the kinematic tree structure.

## Results

- MonoArt achieved superior geometric quality (e.g., **Chamfer Distance 0.77 vs. 1.26**) and kinematic accuracy (e.g., **Type Accuracy 88.26% vs. 77.12%**) on the 7-category PartNet-Mobility benchmark.
- The framework demonstrated strong generalization to 100 in-the-wild images, with user study participants rating generated articulations highest for both geometric quality (**4.63/5**) and kinematic plausibility (**4.37/5**).
- It maintained a competitive inference speed of **20.5 seconds** per instance on a single NVIDIA A6000 GPU, comparable to or faster than most baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Disentangling geometry recovery, part-aware encoding, motion decoding, and kinematic regression within a progressive framework leads to more stable and interpretable articulation inference.
> - The dual-query formulation, which refines both content and position queries, effectively addresses the challenge of jointly identifying movable parts and their spatial motion anchors.
> - A four-phase training strategy, including specific warm-up and joint optimization stages, allows for robust learning of complex 3D structural priors from single-image input.
