# MonoArt: Progressive Structural Reasoning for Monocular Articulated 3D Reconstruction

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at S-Lab, Nanyang Technological University developed MonoArt, an end-to-end framework for reconstructing articulated 3D objects from a single image by progressively reasoning about geometry, part structure, and motion. It achieves improved geometric and kinematic accuracy on PartNet-Mobility and demonstrates robust generalization to real-world objects.

## Problem

- Reconstructing articulated 3D objects from a single image is challenging due to limited visual information, necessitating joint inference of geometry, part structure, and kinematic relationships.
- Existing methods struggle with the entanglement of motion cues and object structure, leading to unstable articulation parameter regression.
- Many approaches rely on multi-view data, external motion templates, or complex retrieval/generative techniques, limiting their scalability and generalization.

## Method

- **MonoArt** introduces a unified, end-to-end framework for monocular articulated 3D reconstruction, employing a ==progressive structural reasoning== approach.
- The framework transforms visual observations through geometry-aware, part-aware, and motion-aware representations using components like a ==TRELLIS-based 3D Generator== and ==Part-Aware Semantic Reasoner==.
- A ==Dual-Query Motion Decoder== jointly models movable part identities and spatial motion anchors, with a ==Kinematic Estimator== predicting articulation parameters and the kinematic tree structure.

## Results

- MonoArt achieved superior geometric quality (e.g., **Chamfer Distance 0.77 vs. 1.26**) and kinematic accuracy (e.g., **Type Accuracy **88.26%** vs. 77.12%**) on the 7-category PartNet-Mobility benchmark.
- The framework demonstrated strong generalization to 100 in-the-wild images, with user study participants rating generated articulations highest for both geometric quality (**4.63/5**) and kinematic plausibility (**4.37/5**).
- It maintained a competitive inference speed of **20.5 seconds** per instance on a single NVIDIA A6000 GPU, comparable to or faster than most baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Disentangling geometry recovery, part-aware encoding, motion decoding, and kinematic regression within a progressive framework leads to more stable and interpretable articulation inference.
> - The dual-query formulation, which refines both content and position queries, effectively addresses the challenge of jointly identifying movable parts and their spatial motion anchors.
> - A four-phase training strategy, including specific warm-up and joint optimization stages, allows for robust learning of complex 3D structural priors from single-image input.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The reconstruction of articulated 3D objects from visual observations represents a significant challenge within computer vision and graphics. Articulated objects, such as furniture and appliances, are prevalent in everyday environments, and the ability to generate high-fidelity, interactable 3D assets at scale has implications for fields like robotics and scene synthesis. Unlike rigid object reconstruction, articulated reconstruction necessitates an understanding of both the structural composition of an object and the kinematic relationships between its constituent parts.

Existing approaches to articulated 3D object modeling generally fall into several categories. Early methods often relied on multiple images, typically captured from videos or sequential frames, to observe an object across various articulation states. While these multi-view approaches, such as PARIS, DTA, and ArticulatedGS, can achieve high reconstruction accuracy, they are constrained by the requirement for multiple motion states, which may not always be readily available.

To address the data acquisition challenges of multi-view methods, subsequent research focused on single-image inputs. Some approaches, including SINGAPO, NAP, and Articulate-Anything, employ retrieval-based strategies, assembling objects from pre-built asset libraries. However, these methods can introduce issues such as texture misalignment and geometric inaccuracies, and their generalization may be limited by the scope of the underlying asset database. More recent efforts have explored generative techniques, such as generating auxiliary videos, leveraging vision-language models, or utilizing predefined motion directions. While these methods demonstrate progress in single-image articulation modeling, they often incur high computational costs, depend on handcrafted priors, or exhibit limitations in generalization due to a potential lack of intrinsic 3D understanding. These dependencies highlight a difficulty in directly inferring part composition and spatial relationships from a single static image.

MonoArt is positioned within this landscape as a unified, end-to-end framework designed to overcome the aforementioned limitations. It proposes a "progressive structural reasoning" approach to directly infer object geometry, part structure, and motion parameters from a single image without requiring multi-view supervision, external motion templates, or multi-stage pipelines. By embedding 3D structural priors and disentangling the reasoning process, MonoArt aims to achieve more stable, interpretable, accurate, and efficient articulated 3D reconstruction, thereby contributing to the scalability and practical applicability of 3D asset generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and efficient method for reconstructing articulated 3D objects from a single input image. This task is inherently challenging due to the limited visual information available, which necessitates jointly inferring the object's geometry, its constituent part structure, and the kinematic parameters governing their motion.

The core motivation stems from the perceived limitations of existing approaches to monocular articulated 3D reconstruction:

1. **Entanglement of Motion Cues and Object Structure**: The authors identify a key difficulty in the "entanglement" between motion cues and object structure. This entanglement makes direct regression of articulation parameters from image features unstable and unreliable. A method capable of disentangling these aspects is therefore sought.
2. **Dependency on Multi-view Data**: Many accurate methods rely on multiple images or video sequences capturing an object in different articulated states. This multi-view data is often difficult to acquire in practice, limiting the scalability and applicability of such approaches. The research aims to eliminate this dependency by focusing solely on single-image input.
3. **Limitations of Retrieval-Based Methods**: Approaches that reconstruct objects by retrieving and assembling parts from pre-existing asset libraries frequently suffer from texture misalignment and geometric inaccuracies. These methods are also constrained by the availability and quality of the asset library. The authors aim to move beyond retrieval-based assembly to a generative approach.
4. **Complexity and Computational Cost of Generative Methods**: Some recent generative methods, particularly those involving auxiliary video generation, are computationally expensive and complex, which can hinder their practical deployment. The research is motivated to achieve efficiency alongside accuracy.
5. **Reliance on Handcrafted Priors or External Cues**: Methods that leverage vision-language models or rely on predefined motion directions often depend on handcrafted priors or external cues. This dependency can limit their generalization capabilities, particularly to novel object categories or articulation patterns not covered by the priors. The authors aim for a solution grounded in intrinsic 3D understanding derived directly from the visual input.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of MonoArt are:

- To introduce a unified, end-to-end framework for monocular articulated 3D reconstruction.
- To implement a "progressive structural reasoning" mechanism that transforms visual observations into canonical geometry, structured part representations, and motion-aware embeddings within a single architecture.
- To enable stable and interpretable articulation inference without relying on external motion templates, multi-view supervision, or multi-stage pipelines.
- To demonstrate competitive performance in both reconstruction accuracy (geometric and kinematic) and inference speed against existing methods on standard benchmarks.
- To show the framework's generalization capabilities in practical applications, including robotic manipulation and articulated scene reconstruction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MonoArt is presented as a unified, end-to-end framework designed for monocular articulated 3D reconstruction. Its core principle is "progressive structural reasoning," which involves transforming visual observations through geometry-aware, part-aware, and motion-aware representations. The framework comprises four main components: the TRELLIS-based 3D Generator, Part-Aware Semantic Reasoner, Dual-Query Motion Decoder, and Kinematic Estimator.

#### 3.1. TRELLIS-based 3D Generator

The process begins with a single RGB input image $I$. The TRELLIS-based 3D Generator, utilizing a frozen TRELLIS backbone, reconstructs a canonical 3D object geometry. This component outputs an explicit 3D mesh $O$, which serves as the foundational geometry, and a structured sparse voxel latent representation $Z \in \mathbb{R}^{N_z \times N_z \times N_z \times d_1}$. The latent $Z$ encodes spatial features within a voxel grid, with active voxels storing $d_1$-dimensional features and empty regions remaining inactive.

#### 3.2. Part-Aware Semantic Reasoner

The objective of this component is to derive part-aware point features $H$ from the canonical geometry $O$ and the sparse voxel latent $Z$. This aims to enable downstream modules to reason about object parts and their potential motions.

- **Tri-linear Interpolation**: The method samples $M$ points $\{p_m\}_{m=1}^M$ from the surface of the reconstructed mesh $O$. For each point $p_m \in \mathbb{R}^3$, point-aligned features $f_m \in \mathbb{R}^{d_1}$ are obtained by tri-linear interpolation over the neighboring voxels in the sparse voxel latent $Z$. This process converts the discrete voxel features into continuous surface-aligned features, denoted as $F_{geo} = \{f_m\}_{m=1}^M \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times d_1}$.
- **Triplane Projection**: To integrate global spatial context while preserving geometric structure, the geometry-aligned point features $F_{geo}$ are projected onto three orthogonal planes (XY, YZ, ZX). Each surface point $p_m$ is orthographically projected onto these planes, and its feature $f_m$ is accumulated to corresponding 2D grid locations via bilinear interpolation. This results in three feature maps, $F_{tri} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times N_t \times N_t \times d_1}$, where $N_t$ is the resolution of the triplanes.
- **Part Contrast Transformer**: The triplane features $F_{tri}$ are flattened into a token sequence and processed by a multi-layer self-attention Transformer. This step is designed to capture global interactions across the planes, yielding refined tokens $T'$. These tokens are then reshaped back into refined triplane feature maps $F'_{tri}$. For each surface point $p_m$, its 3D coordinate is projected onto the three planes, and the refined triplane feature maps are queried via a tri-plane sampling operation. The sampled features are concatenated and processed by a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) to produce updated point embeddings $h_m$, forming the part-aware feature set $H = \{h_m\}_{m=1}^M \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times d_2}$. These embeddings are supervised using a triplet loss with 3D part annotations to enforce discriminative part-level feature separation.

#### 3.3. Dual-Query Motion Decoder

This component is designed to jointly model the identity of movable parts and their spatial motion anchors, addressing the challenge of articulated reasoning. It employs a dual-query formulation, consisting of content queries ($Q_c \in \mathbb{R}^{N_q \times d_2}$) to encode part semantics and position queries ($Q_p \in \mathbb{R}^{N_q \times 3}$) to represent spatial motion anchors, where $N_q$ is the number of dual queries. These queries are iteratively refined through $L$ stacked refinement blocks.

- **Dual-query Initialization**: Initial content queries $Q_c^0$ and position queries $Q_p^0$ are generated from aggregated global object context. This context is derived by globally pooling the part-aware point embeddings $H$ and geometry-aligned features $F_{geo}$, followed by concatenation and projection. An auxiliary object-category prediction $S_o$ is also generated.
- **Refinement Block**: Each of the $L$ refinement layers iteratively updates the dual queries. Within a layer, self-attention models interactions between different candidate parts, and cross-attention allows the queries to attend to the visual features $H$ to retrieve relevant image evidence. Position queries are updated residually ($Q_p^l = Q_p^{l-1} + \Delta_p^l$) for progressive refinement of spatial anchors. Content queries are similarly updated ($Q_c^l = Q_c^{l-1} + \Delta_c^l$), with $\Delta_c^l$ capturing semantic refinements. Per-query part class logits $S_p^l$ are predicted from refined content queries and used to retrieve semantic prototypes from frozen CLIP text embeddings, which are then fused into the content branch.
- **Query Confidence Estimation**: To handle an over-specified number of queries, a confidence score $c_i \in$ is predicted for each query based on its refined content embedding $Q_c^L$. This score indicates the reliability of the part hypothesis. During training, confidence supervision is derived from the IoU between predicted and ground-truth part masks after Hungarian matching. At inference, queries with confidence scores below a threshold are discarded.

#### 3.4. Kinematic Estimator

The Kinematic Estimator receives the refined dual queries ($Q_p^L, Q_c^L$), part logits $S_p^L$, and point embeddings $H$ to predict articulation parameters and the kinematic tree structure.

- **Articulate Parameter Regressor**:
 - **Part Masks ($m_m$)**: Soft assignments of surface points to queries are obtained by computing the affinity between content queries $Q_c^L$ and point features $H$ ($m_m = Q_c^L H^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{N_q \times M}$).
 - **Joint Parameters**: Per-query representations, integrating $Q_p^L$, $Q_c^L$, and part-level features $H$, are fed into lightweight MLP heads to predict physically interpretable joint parameters:
 - Joint type ($m_t \in \mathbb{R}^{N_q \times N_t}$): Categorical prediction (e.g., fixed, revolute, prismatic, continuous).
 - Joint axis ($m_a \in \mathbb{R}^{N_q \times 3}$): Unit direction vector of the joint axis.
 - Joint pivot ($m_o \in \mathbb{R}^{N_q \times 3}$): Pivot point for motion, predicted via a residual formulation from $Q_p^L$ (i.e., $m_o = Q_p^L + \Delta_o$).
 - Joint limits ($m_l \in \mathbb{R}^{N_q \times 2}$): Parameterized by a center and a symmetric span for motion ranges.
- **Kinematic Tree Predictor**:
 - **Pairwise Affinity**: Semantic attachment scores ($S_{i,j}$) between ordered pairs of parts (i, j) are computed using part category distributions ($s_i, s_j$) and a learnable compatibility matrix $C \in \mathbb{R}^{N_c \times N_c}$ ($S_{i,j} = s_i^\top C s_j$).
 - **Parent Assignment**: For each part, the parent with the highest attachment probability is selected, with a learnable root node as a candidate. A single-root, cycle-free constraint is enforced during tree construction to ensure a valid kinematic hierarchy.

#### 3.5. Training Procedure

MonoArt employs a four-phase training strategy:
1. **Stage I**: Warm-up the Part-Aware Semantic Reasoner for 100 epochs using only triplet loss, freezing the TRELLIS backbone and downstream modules.
2. **Stage II**: Freeze the semantic reasoner and train the dual-query initialization branch for 20 epochs with object-category supervision.
3. **Stage III**: Jointly optimize the Part-Aware Semantic Reasoner, Dual-Query Motion Decoder, and Articulation Parameter Regressor for 100 epochs using a combined loss (triplet, mask, confidence, motion losses), with the motion loss weight linearly increased during the first 40 epochs.
4. **Stage IV**: Freeze all preceding modules and train the Kinematic Tree Predictor for 30 epochs using structure loss.
Loss functions include triplet loss for part features, focal and Dice losses for masks, Quality Focal Loss for confidence, cross-entropy for joint type and object category, unsigned cosine similarity for axis direction, L1 loss for motion origin and limits, and cross-entropy for parent prediction.

#### 3.6. Evaluation Protocol

The method is evaluated on the PartNet-Mobility dataset, using both a 7-category subset and the full 46-category set. Metrics include:
- **Geometric reconstruction**: Chamfer Distance (CD), F-Score, PSNR, and CLIP similarity, computed across six sampled articulation states and averaged over 10 random viewpoints.
- **Kinematic prediction**: Type Accuracy, Axis Direction Error (angular deviation), and Pivot Distance Error (distance from predicted pivot to ground-truth axis), after bipartite matching of predicted and ground-truth parts.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of MonoArt on the PartNet-Mobility benchmark demonstrates its performance in both geometric reconstruction and kinematic prediction, alongside its generalization capabilities and computational efficiency.

#### 4.1. PartNet-Mobility Benchmark Performance

MonoArt was quantitatively compared against URDFormer, SINGAPO, Articulate-Anything (ArtAny), PhysXGen, and PhysXAnything (PhysXAny).

- **Partial 7 Categories**:
 - **Geometric Quality**: MonoArt achieved a Chamfer Distance (CD) of 0.77 (scaled by 10$^{-2}$), an F-score of 0.728, a PSNR of 17.55, and a CLIP similarity of 0.926. These metrics indicate an improvement over existing methods. For instance, SINGAPO, the next strongest baseline, reported a CD of 1.26, F-score of 0.572, PSNR of 15.22, and CLIP of 0.870.
 - **Kinematic Accuracy**: MonoArt demonstrated a Type Accuracy of 88.26%, an Axis Direction Error of 0.209 radians, and a Pivot Distance Error of 0.085. These values reflect higher accuracy in kinematic predictions compared to SINGAPO's 77.12% Type Accuracy, 0.493 rad Axis Error, and 0.201 Pivot Error.
- **Full 46 Categories**:
 - **Geometric Quality**: MonoArt achieved a CD of 1.25, an F-score of 0.670, a PSNR of 18.55, and a CLIP similarity of 0.907. These results generally indicate superior geometric reconstruction compared to ArtAny (CD 2.07, F-score 0.514) and PhysXAny (CD 1.88, F-score 0.531).
 - **Kinematic Accuracy**: MonoArt achieved a Type Accuracy of 67.47% and a Pivot Distance Error of 0.108. While PhysXAny reported a lower Axis Direction Error (0.289 rad compared to MonoArt's 0.423 rad), MonoArt's Type Accuracy and Pivot Error values were generally favorable. The pivot error reduction was noted as over 40% compared to PhysXAny.

Qualitative results (Figure 3) further supported these findings, illustrating that MonoArt generated more accurate geometry and joint predictions, leading to more plausible articulated motions across diverse objects.

#### 4.2. Real-World Generalization

To assess real-world applicability, MonoArt was tested on approximately 100 in-the-wild images of everyday articulated objects. The framework produced coherent geometry and plausible articulation across various real-world appearances, despite primarily being trained on synthetic data. A user study with 20 participants rated MonoArt's generated articulations highest for both geometric quality (4.63/5) and kinematic plausibility (4.37/5), outperforming other baselines.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies were conducted on the 46-category PartNet-Mobility dataset to evaluate the contribution of key architectural components:

- **Part-Aware Semantic Reasoner (PASR)**: The removal of PASR significantly degraded both geometric and kinematic performance, particularly joint type accuracy and pivot prediction, underscoring its importance for motion-aware part reasoning. Triplet supervision for PASR consistently yielded the best performance across metrics, indicating its effectiveness in fostering discriminative and motion-consistent part representations compared to cross-entropy supervision or no loss.
- **Dual-Query Motion Decoder (DQMD)**: Dual-Query Initialization (DQI) contributed to performance, as random initialization of queries led to degradation. Residual updates to both position and content query branches were observed to be optimal, highlighting the benefit of joint refinement. The number of refinement layers was also investigated, with L=6 providing the best balance, as deeper models (L=9) showed performance degradation.
- **Kinematic Estimator**: The use of a residual formulation for joint origin prediction ($m_o = Q_p^L + \Delta_o$) and the incorporation of point embeddings $H$ for parameter regression were found to be critical for achieving optimal results. Removing these elements led to considerable performance drops.

#### 4.4. Runtime

MonoArt demonstrated competitive inference speed, requiring 20.5 seconds per instance on a single NVIDIA A6000 GPU. This is comparable to, or faster than, most baselines (e.g., Articulate-Anything at 229.9s, PhysXAnything at 256.8s, PhysXGen at 31.6s, URDFormer at 34.1s). While SINGAPO reported a slightly faster time of 19.6s, the majority of MonoArt's inference time (18.2s) was attributed to the initial TRELLIS-based 3D reconstruction, with the articulation reasoning and post-processing steps contributing a marginal overhead.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MonoArt represents a notable development in monocular articulated 3D reconstruction, addressing several challenges inherent in the field. Its significance and potential impact can be discussed through its technical contributions, performance, and practical applications.

#### 5.1. Technical Contributions and Performance

The core contribution of MonoArt lies in its unified, end-to-end framework based on "progressive structural reasoning." This approach distinguishes itself by disentangling geometry recovery, part-aware encoding, motion decoding, and kinematic regression. By building structured representations from visual observations in a progressive manner, the method reduces the instability often associated with directly regressing articulation parameters from limited input. This design obviates the need for multi-view supervision, external motion templates, retrieval-based libraries, or complex auxiliary video generation pipelines, which often introduce scalability and efficiency challenges in other methods.

The reported quantitative and qualitative results indicate that MonoArt achieves competitive performance across both geometric reconstruction and kinematic prediction metrics on the PartNet-Mobility dataset. This includes improvements in reconstruction accuracy and robust estimation of joint types, axes, and pivots. The framework also demonstrates inference speeds that are competitive with, or faster than, many existing approaches, which contributes to its practical viability for applications requiring efficient processing.

#### 5.2. Generalization and Practical Applications

Beyond its performance on benchmark datasets, MonoArt exhibits generalization capabilities that extend its potential impact to real-world scenarios. The framework was shown to produce coherent geometry and plausible articulation from in-the-wild images, suggesting a degree of robustness to variations in appearance and context not explicitly covered by synthetic training data.

This ability to generate physically meaningful articulated models from a single image has direct implications for several downstream applications:

- **Robotic Manipulation**: By inferring articulation axes, joint types, and motion limits, MonoArt provides structured kinematic priors directly usable for robotic control and interaction planning. The demonstration of importing MonoArt reconstructions into a simulation environment like IsaacSim for contact-rich tasks (e.g., grasping and opening by a robot arm) suggests a practical "real-to-sim" pipeline. This could facilitate the generation of simulation-ready assets, reducing manual modeling effort and accelerating the development and testing of robotic policies.
- **Articulated Scene Reconstruction**: MonoArt can augment existing static scene reconstruction methods, such as SAM3D, with object-level articulation recovery. By processing masked object instances from a scene to infer their kinematic parameters and then re-placing them, the framework can convert rigid scene reconstructions into functionally operable environments. This capability could lead to more interactive and dynamic virtual scenes, useful for embodied AI research, virtual reality, and interactive content creation.

#### 5.3. Limitations

The authors acknowledge certain limitations. MonoArt may encounter difficulties with very small parts attached to larger objects (e.g., tiny buttons). This is attributed to the uniform point sampling strategy, which can lead to sparse coverage and less distinctive features for small components, potentially hindering reliable segmentation and parameterization. Furthermore, the framework's reliance on learned structural priors over part-whole relationships might limit its generalization to objects with highly novel topologies or uncommon articulation patterns not adequately represented in the training data. In such unseen configurations, the predicted motion parameters could exhibit reduced accuracy, even if the general part segmentation remains reasonable.

#### 5.4. Overall Impact

Overall, MonoArt contributes to advancing the state of 3D computer vision by offering a more robust and efficient method for creating high-fidelity, interactable 3D articulated assets from minimal input. Its progressive structural reasoning approach provides a pathway toward more stable and interpretable kinematic inference, which is a foundational requirement for numerous applications in robotics, virtual environments, and 3D content generation.
