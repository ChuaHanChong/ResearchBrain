# ViCrit: A Verifiable Reinforcement Learning Proxy Task for Visual Perception in VLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> A novel reinforcement learning proxy task, ViCrit, enhances Vision-Language Models' fine-grained visual perception by training them to identify subtle hallucinations in captions. This method, developed by researchers from the University of Maryland, Microsoft, and University of Michigan, effectively reduces visual hallucinations and improves general VLM performance across diverse benchmarks, while ViCrit-Bench serves as a new diagnostic tool for evaluating these capabilities.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) frequently suffer from visual hallucinations and often lack truly fine-grained visual perception, relying on shallow object recognition.
- The success of Reinforcement Learning (RL) in LLMs (e.g., for math or code) has not extended to VLM visual perception due to a lack of visual tasks that are both challenging and automatically verifiable.
- Existing VLM training, such as supervised fine-tuning on captioning data, often leads to superficial understanding rather than deep, transferable perceptual strategies.

## Method

- Introduces ==ViCrit==, a reinforcement learning proxy task where a VLM must identify a single, subtly injected visual hallucination within an almost-correct, long image caption.
- Employs an ==exact string match reward== for the identified hallucinated span, providing a precise and automatically verifiable signal crucial for scalable RL training.
- Develops ==ViCrit-Bench==, a human-curated diagnostic benchmark featuring fine-grained hallucinations across diverse image domains (natural, document, scene-text, abstract) to systematically evaluate VLM perception.

## Results

- ViCrit RL training significantly reduces visual hallucinations, with ViCrit-RL-72B improving CHAIRs/CHAIRi from **28.0/5.1** (baseline) to **21.0/3.9** and MMHal from **3.74** to **3.91**.
- Models trained with ViCrit show consistent average accuracy gains of **2.4%** (7B) and **3.4%** (72B) across eight diverse general VLM benchmarks, including strong generalization to out-of-domain tasks like visual math and document analysis.
- ViCrit-Bench is a challenging diagnostic for current VLMs, with top proprietary models achieving less than **50%** accuracy, and it strongly predicts overall VLM capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reframing fine-grained visual perception into a "hallucination criticism" task allows for scalable reinforcement learning, bridging the gap between perceptual difficulty and verifiable reward signals.
> - The ViCrit task design encourages VLMs to internalize transferable "how to perceive" strategies, prompting them to cross-check textual claims against visual evidence rather than relying on superficial object memorization.
> - Performance on the ViCrit-Bench diagnostic benchmark shows a strong positive linear correlation (r = 0.96) with overall VLM performance across diverse general reasoning tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) have been significantly propelled by Reinforcement Learning (RL) paradigms, particularly when tasks are both *challenging* and *automatically verifiable*. Examples include mathematical reasoning, where numerical answers provide clear reward signals, and code generation, which can be verified through execution in a sandboxed environment. This success story has, however, not fully extended to the domain of visual perception in Vision-Language Models (VLMs).

The broader research landscape for VLMs currently grapples with several key challenges:
- **Visual Hallucinations:** VLMs often generate plausible-sounding but factually incorrect descriptions of images, a phenomenon known as hallucination. This directly impacts their reliability and trustworthiness.
- **Fine-Grained Perception:** Many VLMs struggle with truly fine-grained visual understanding, often relying on shallow object recognition rather than deep contextual and relational comprehension.
- **Lack of Verifiable Visual Tasks for RL:** Unlike text or code, visual descriptions, especially long, detailed image captions, are notoriously difficult to grade automatically and unambiguously. This scarcity of "challenging yet easily verifiable" vision-centric tasks has impeded the widespread application of RL for improving VLM visual perception. Existing multi-hop visual question answering (VQA) benchmarks often probe only fragments of a scene, and exhaustive image captioning yields paragraph-length outputs that are hard to verify.

This paper directly addresses this critical gap. It identifies that the core impediment is not RL itself, but the *task design*. Supervised fine-tuning on captioning data, while improving descriptive abilities, often leads to "superficial object memorization" rather than a deep, transferable understanding of visual perception. The authors argue that traditional captioning objectives lack the precise reward signals needed for RL to compel models to "interrogate every visual detail" and "decide where to look next."

`ViCrit` innovates by reframing the problem of fine-grained visual perception into an RL-friendly format. Instead of asking a VLM to *generate* a correct caption (which is hard to grade), it asks the VLM to *critique* an almost-correct caption by identifying a synthetically injected hallucination. This formulation preserves the perceptual difficulty—a model must understand the entire scene to spot a subtle error—but collapses the output into a "few words that can be matched exactly," providing the necessary verifiable reward signal for scalable RL training.

By focusing on "hallucination criticism," `ViCrit` aligns with a growing trend in AI research towards more robust and reliable models. It contributes to the field by:
1. **Enabling Scalable RL for Visual Perception:** By creating a truly verifiable task, `ViCrit` unlocks the power of RL for fine-tuning VLMs specifically for improved visual understanding.
2. **Fostering Deeper Perceptual Strategies:** The task design incentivizes models to internalize cross-checking mechanisms, moving beyond memorization to genuine "how to perceive" strategies.
3. **Providing a Diagnostic Framework:** `ViCrit-Bench` offers a systematic way to evaluate fine-grained visual perception across diverse image types and hallucination categories, serving as a valuable tool for future VLM development.

This work therefore pushes the boundaries of VLM capabilities by leveraging a proven training paradigm (RL) in a novel way, with direct implications for reducing hallucinations and enhancing the overall robustness and generalizability of multimodal AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind the `ViCrit` research stems from the disparity between the success of Reinforcement Learning (RL) in fine-tuning Large Language Models (LLMs) for tasks like math reasoning and code generation, and its limited impact on improving fine-grained visual perception in Vision-Language Models (VLMs). The core impediment identified by the authors is the absence of vision-centric tasks that are simultaneously *perceptually challenging* and *unambiguously verifiable* for automated reward computation.

Based on this, the key objectives of the paper are:

1. **To introduce `ViCrit` (Visual Caption Hallucination Critic):** This is a novel Reinforcement Learning (RL) proxy task designed to train VLMs. The primary goal is to create a task that is both challenging enough to demand fine-grained visual perception across an entire image and simple enough to yield a precise, binary, and automatically verifiable reward signal. This addresses the bottleneck of scalable RL training for visual tasks.
2. **To demonstrate the effectiveness of `ViCrit` RL training:** The authors aim to show that fine-tuning VLMs using the `ViCrit` task significantly enhances their performance. This includes:
 - **Reducing visual hallucinations:** Quantifying improvements on existing hallucination benchmarks.
 - **Improving general VLM performance:** Showing gains across a broad suite of diverse vision-language benchmarks.
 - **Ensuring cross-domain generalization:** Proving that the learned perceptual strategies transfer effectively beyond the natural-image domain of the training data to more abstract reasoning tasks like visual math and document analysis. This aims to show that models learn to "perceive" rather than "barely memorize seen objects."
3. **To develop `ViCrit-Bench`:** This objective focuses on creating a high-quality, fine-grained, and challenging diagnostic benchmark. `ViCrit-Bench` is designed to systematically probe VLMs' perception errors across various image domains (natural, document, scene-text heavy, abstract) and fine-grained hallucination types (object, attribute, count, spatial, scene text, etc.). The goal is for this benchmark to not only evaluate hallucination detection but also serve as a strong indicator of overall VLM performance and perceptual capabilities.

In essence, the motivation is to "turn visual perception into an RL problem" that encourages models to deeply "interrogate every visual detail" and "cross-check textual claims against visual evidence," thereby moving beyond superficial understanding to internalize robust and generalizable visual perception strategies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper introduces a novel framework, `ViCrit`, which comprises a reinforcement learning (RL) proxy task and a corresponding diagnostic benchmark, `ViCrit-Bench`. The methodology is meticulously designed to address the challenges of verifiable fine-grained visual perception in VLMs.

#### 3.1. `ViCrit` RL Training Task

The core innovation lies in reshaping the objective of visual perception from generating a perfect, hard-to-grade paragraph-length caption to identifying a specific, easily verifiable hallucination.

- **Task Description:**
 - **Input:** An image ($I$) paired with a *minimally modified* caption ($\tilde{C}$). This $\tilde{C}$ is derived from an exhaustive, human-annotated caption ($C$) (average 196 words from PixMo-Cap dataset).
 - **Hallucination Injection:** GPT-4 is prompted to select a single object description ($o$) within $C$ and subtly perturb it into a visual hallucination ($\tilde{o}$). The perturbation is designed to be visually similar, semantically plausible, and unambiguous. Examples include object substitution, attribute flips (e.g., color, material), scene-text errors, relation swaps (e.g., spatial relations), count errors, and condition changes.
 - **Model Goal:** Given $I$ and $\tilde{C}$, the VLM must pinpoint the *exact* tokens that constitute the incorrect span ($\tilde{o}$).
 - **Reward Mechanism:** A binary, exact string match reward. If the model's predicted span ($\hat{\tilde{o}}$) precisely matches the ground-truth hallucinated span ($\tilde{o}$), it receives a positive reward (+1); otherwise, it receives 0. This "exact string match" is crucial for deterministic and scalable RL training. A slight relaxation allows for additional copied words from the original caption, as long as the core hallucinated span is correct.

- **Task Difficulty Discussion:** The authors argue that detecting a single subtle error in a long, detailed caption requires the VLM to parse and understand the *entire* visual scene, demanding the same level of fine-grained perception as perfect caption generation. However, by collapsing the output to a few words, it becomes easily verifiable, bridging the gap between perceptual difficulty and evaluation simplicity.

- **Data Generation:**
 - Started with 384K image-caption pairs from PixMo-Cap (Deitke et al., 2024).
 - Used GPT-4 with a small set of manually crafted in-context examples to generate 875K pairs of images and modified captions, each containing one synthetic hallucination.

- **Model Training Details:**
 - **Base VLM:** Qwen2.5-VL (7B-Instruct and 72B-Instruct) were chosen for their strong baseline performance. All model parameters are fine-tuned.
 - **RL Algorithm:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) (Shao et al., 2024) was used.
 - **Reward Function:** The final reward ($r_i$) for sample $i$ is a weighted sum: $r_i = 0.9 * r_{answer} + 0.1 * r_{format}$.
 - `r_answer`: +1 for exact string match of $\tilde{o}$ with $\hat{\tilde{o}}$, 0 otherwise.
 - `r_format`: +1 if the model adheres to a specific prompt format (e.g., using `<think>...</think>` for chain-of-thought and `\boxed{}` for final answers), 0 otherwise.

#### 3.2. `ViCrit-Bench` Diagnostic Benchmark

To evaluate the fine-grained perception capabilities precisely, the authors developed `ViCrit-Bench`, a human-curated and systematically balanced benchmark.

- **Image Domains:** Images are categorized into four types to probe diverse visual perception challenges:
 1. **Natural Images:** Everyday photos (landscapes, objects, people).
 2. **Documents:** Structured content (tables, charts, diagrams, text screenshots).
 3. **Scene-Text-Heavy Images:** Images with embedded text (signs, memes).
 4. **Abstract Images:** Non-real-world compositions (geometric shapes, symbols, illustrations).
 - *Note:* Training data (PixMo-Cap) is dominated by natural images, making performance on domains 2, 3, and 4 crucial for generalization.

- **Hallucination Categories:** Eight distinct fine-grained hallucination types are defined:
 1. **Count:** Incorrect quantity.
 2. **Material:** Incorrect material composition.
 3. **Spatial:** Misrepresented spatial configuration/relations.
 4. **Color:** Inconsistent color attributes.
 5. **Object:** Incorrect object classification/substitution.
 6. **Condition:** Incorrect physical state/action/expression.
 7. **Shape:** Inaccurate geometric structure/contour.
 8. **Text:** Incorrect detection/interpretation of embedded text.

- **Annotation Pipeline:** This involves a rigorous three-stage process:
 1. **Image Selection and Caption Sanitization:** Initial classification of PixMo-Cap images using OpenAI-o3, followed by manual filtering to ensure domain adherence and balance. Human annotators also reviewed and corrected original PixMo-Cap captions for accuracy.
 2. **Hallucination Injection:** Dedicated human annotators manually injected a single hallucination per image by replacing a noun phrase with a semantically plausible but misleading alternative. Each image category was capped at 20 examples per hallucination type, though some types might have fewer if naturally rare.
 3. **Cross-validation:** A final round of review by two independent annotators ensured the correctness and clarity of all injected hallucinations.

- **Statistics:** The final `ViCrit-Bench` contains 607 image-caption pairs, each with one fine-grained hallucination. The distribution of hallucination types is relatively balanced (around 13% each), except for "Material" (7.9%), which is less common in certain image types (e.g., abstract).

- **Metric and Evaluation:** Models are given the image and the corrupted caption with a specific prompt template (Appendix A.2) and asked to locate the hallucinated span as an open-ended QA task. The evaluation metric is **exact string match accuracy** between the model's prediction and the ground-truth hallucination.

This comprehensive methodology ensures that both the training task and the evaluation benchmark are robust, well-defined, and tailored to truly push the boundaries of fine-grained visual perception in VLMs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted in the paper provide compelling evidence for the effectiveness of `ViCrit` RL training and the utility of `ViCrit-Bench` as a diagnostic tool.

#### 4.1. Effectiveness of `ViCrit` RL Training

The authors fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct using the `ViCrit` RL task, creating `ViCrit-RL-7B` and `ViCrit-RL-72B`. These models were compared against their base models and other state-of-the-art open-source and proprietary VLMs across hallucination mitigation and broad generalization benchmarks.

- **Finding 1: RL with the `ViCrit` task significantly reduces visual hallucination.**
 - On hallucination benchmarks (CHAIRs, CHAIRi, MMHal), `ViCrit-RL` models consistently outperformed baseline Qwen2.5-VL models and other SOTA open-source VLMs.
 - For the 7B scale, `ViCrit-RL-7B` reduced CHAIRs/CHAIRi (lower is better) from 28.0/5.1 to 25.2/4.5, and increased MMHal (higher is better) from 3.74 to 3.77.
 - For the 72B scale, the improvements were even more pronounced: `ViCrit-RL-72B` achieved 21.0/3.9 on CHAIRs/CHAIRi and 3.91 on MMHal, outperforming all compared SOTA VLMs across these metrics. This demonstrates `ViCrit`'s direct effectiveness in curbing hallucination.

- **Finding 2: RL with the `ViCrit` task improves VLM performance in general.**
 - Beyond hallucination reduction, `ViCrit-RL` models showed consistent gains across eight diverse general VLM benchmarks, including mathematical reasoning (MathVista, MathVision, MathVerse), general knowledge (MMMU, MMStar, MM-Vet), visual understanding (VLMsAreBlind), and chart reasoning (Charxiv).
 - Average accuracy improvements were 2.4% for the 7B model and 3.4% for the 72B model.
 - **Crucially, these improvements generalized well to domains underrepresented in the PixMo-Cap training data (which is mostly natural images).** For example, the 72B model showed significant gains on MathVision (+4.9%), VLMsAreBlind (+4.5%), and Charxiv (+3.9%), despite abstract, math, and document images accounting for a small fraction (7% and 10% respectively) of the training data. This suggests that `ViCrit` training encourages the model to learn a transferable "how to look" strategy, prompting it to verify textual claims against visual evidence, rather than merely memorizing object lists.

#### 4.2. `ViCrit-Bench` Results

- **`ViCrit-Bench` is markedly challenging for current VLMs.** Even top proprietary models like OpenAI-o3 (47.7%) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (45.2%) achieved less than 50% accuracy, while the best open-source baseline (Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct) scored 42.4%.
- **RL Training Improves Performance on `ViCrit-Bench`.** `ViCrit-RL-7B` and `ViCrit-RL-72B` achieved improved accuracies of 35.6% and 43.0% respectively, with notable gains in "Document Image" and "Abstract Image" domains where baseline models struggled.
- **Challenging Hallucination Types:** Spatial hallucination proved to be the most challenging (best model achieved only 28.4%), while object and material hallucinations, though seemingly "easier," still presented substantial difficulty.
- **Data Imbalance and Model Scale Observations:** The authors note a drop in accuracy for Spatial and Text tasks after RL training, attributing this to data imbalance in the *training set* for these specific types. Additionally, the gains on `ViCrit-Bench` for the 72B model were "relatively marginal" compared to the 7B model, leading to the hypothesis that the current `ViCrit` training dataset might not be sufficiently challenging for already strong 72B models, suggesting a need for more complex data at higher scales.

- **Finding 3: `ViCrit-Bench` results strongly foreshadow overall VLM performance.**
 - A strong positive linear correlation (r = 0.96) was observed between models' performance on `ViCrit-Bench` (Overall accuracy) and their average performance across the eight general VLM tasks.
 - Models consistently ranked in the same order across both `ViCrit-Bench` and the general VLM suite (e.g., `ViCrit-RL-7B` topping 7B class, `ViCrit-RL-72B` topping 72B class). This confirms `ViCrit-Bench`'s efficacy as a reliable diagnostic tool and a strong proxy for overall VLM capability, implying that models better at "cross-checking textual claims against visual evidence" also perform better on broader reasoning tasks.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

Qualitative examples demonstrate that `ViCrit-RL-72B` exhibits sharper visual perception and more accurate reasoning compared to the baseline. It accurately identifies subtle object attributes, quantities, and spatial relationships, leading to correct answers in complex visual math or puzzle-solving scenarios, where the baseline often fails due to limited perceptual fidelity. This provides tangible evidence that the RL training indeed refines the model's low-level perception and internal representation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The `ViCrit` framework represents a significant advancement in the training and evaluation of Vision-Language Models, with broad implications for the field of multimodal AI.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Bridging the RL-VLM Gap:** The most critical contribution is overcoming the long-standing challenge of applying scalable reinforcement learning to improve fine-grained visual perception in VLMs. By devising `ViCrit` as a task that is both perceptually demanding and unambiguously verifiable, the authors unlock the potential of RL for visual domains, similar to its success in LLM reasoning.
- **Enhancing Fine-Grained Visual Perception:** The results definitively show that `ViCrit` RL training leads to substantial improvements in VLMs' ability to perceive and understand subtle visual details, moving beyond superficial object recognition. This is evidenced by significant reductions in visual hallucinations and consistent gains across a diverse range of benchmarks.
- **Promoting Generalizable Perceptual Strategies:** The observed improvements on out-of-domain tasks (e.g., abstract images, visual math, charts) are particularly significant. This indicates that `ViCrit` encourages models to learn fundamental, transferable perceptual strategies ("how to look" and "how to cross-check") rather than mere memorization of seen objects or datasets. This is crucial for building truly intelligent and adaptable multimodal agents.
- **Establishing a Robust Diagnostic Benchmark:** `ViCrit-Bench` is a valuable contribution as a carefully curated, fine-grained, and challenging evaluation tool. Its strong correlation with overall VLM performance validates its utility as a reliable indicator of a model's underlying visual comprehension capabilities. This will aid researchers in systematically identifying and addressing perceptual weaknesses in future VLM architectures.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **More Reliable and Trustworthy VLMs:** By directly addressing the problem of visual hallucinations, `ViCrit` can lead to the development of VLMs that are less prone to generating factually incorrect information. This is vital for critical applications in domains like healthcare, autonomous driving, and legal analysis, where hallucinated content can have severe consequences.
- **Advancement in Multimodal Reasoning:** The demonstrated improvements in visual math and abstract reasoning tasks suggest that `ViCrit` contributes to building VLMs with more robust multimodal reasoning capabilities. Models that can accurately perceive fine details are better equipped to solve complex problems requiring deep visual understanding and logical inference.
- **Foundation for Future Multimodal RL Research:** `ViCrit` provides a concrete example and a proven methodology for applying RL in multimodal contexts. This can inspire further research into novel RL objectives, reward functions, and training paradigms for other challenging visual-language tasks, paving the way for more sophisticated end-to-end trained multimodal agents that can interact with the world more intelligently.
- **Improved Human-AI Interaction:** As VLMs become more integrated into daily life through applications like personal assistants, visual search, and content generation, their ability to accurately perceive and describe the visual world is paramount. Models trained with `ViCrit` could provide more accurate and contextually aware responses, leading to more natural and effective human-AI interactions.

In conclusion, "ViCrit: A Verifiable Reinforcement Learning Proxy Task for Visual Perception in VLMs" is a timely and impactful paper that addresses a fundamental limitation in VLM training. By enabling VLMs to learn robust perceptual strategies through verifiable RL, it lays a strong foundation for more reliable, intelligent, and generalizable multimodal AI systems.
