# ViCrit: A Verifiable Reinforcement Learning Proxy Task for Visual Perception in VLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> A novel reinforcement learning proxy task, ViCrit, enhances Vision-Language Models' fine-grained visual perception by training them to identify subtle hallucinations in captions. This method, developed by researchers from the University of Maryland, Microsoft, and University of Michigan, effectively reduces visual hallucinations and improves general VLM performance across diverse benchmarks, while ViCrit-Bench serves as a new diagnostic tool for evaluating these capabilities.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) frequently suffer from visual hallucinations and often lack truly fine-grained visual perception, relying on shallow object recognition.
- The success of Reinforcement Learning (RL) in LLMs (e.g., for math or code) has not extended to VLM visual perception due to a lack of visual tasks that are both challenging and automatically verifiable.
- Existing VLM training, such as supervised fine-tuning on captioning data, often leads to superficial understanding rather than deep, transferable perceptual strategies.

## Method

- Introduces ==ViCrit==, a reinforcement learning proxy task where a VLM must identify a single, subtly injected visual hallucination within an almost-correct, long image caption.
- Employs an ==exact string match reward== for the identified hallucinated span, providing a precise and automatically verifiable signal crucial for scalable RL training.
- Develops ==ViCrit-Bench==, a human-curated diagnostic benchmark featuring fine-grained hallucinations across diverse image domains (natural, document, scene-text, abstract) to systematically evaluate VLM perception.

## Results

- ViCrit RL training significantly reduces visual hallucinations, with ViCrit-RL-72B improving CHAIRs/CHAIRi from **28.0/5.1** (baseline) to **21.0/3.9** and MMHal from **3.74** to **3.91**.
- Models trained with ViCrit show consistent average accuracy gains of **2.4%** (7B) and **3.4%** (72B) across eight diverse general VLM benchmarks, including strong generalization to out-of-domain tasks like visual math and document analysis.
- ViCrit-Bench is a challenging diagnostic for current VLMs, with top proprietary models achieving less than **50%** accuracy, and it strongly predicts overall VLM capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reframing fine-grained visual perception into a "hallucination criticism" task allows for scalable reinforcement learning, bridging the gap between perceptual difficulty and verifiable reward signals.
> - The ViCrit task design encourages VLMs to internalize transferable "how to perceive" strategies, prompting them to cross-check textual claims against visual evidence rather than relying on superficial object memorization.
> - Performance on the ViCrit-Bench diagnostic benchmark shows a strong positive linear correlation (r = 0.96) with overall VLM performance across diverse general reasoning tasks.
