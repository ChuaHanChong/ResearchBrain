# DenoiseRL: Bootstrapping Reasoning Models to Recover from Noisy Prefixes

> [!summary] Summary
> The DenoiseRL framework enhances large language model reasoning by training models to recover from noisy reasoning prefixes generated by weaker models, thus circumventing the need for stronger teacher models or extensive data curation. This approach led to consistent performance gains on mathematical and general reasoning benchmarks, with DenoiseRL-DAPO improving average scores for Qwen3-8B-Base from 42.8% to 44.8%.

## Problem

- Advanced reinforcement learning methods for large language model (LLM) reasoning are often constrained by the availability of stronger teacher models or the high cost of curating difficult datasets.
- Standard RL training for LLMs does not explicitly teach models how to recover from erroneous intermediate reasoning steps, leaving robust self-correction capabilities underdeveloped.
- On-policy RL can face an exploration bottleneck, limiting the diversity of learning signals and potentially hindering further capability improvements once a policy is reasonably performant.

## Method

- **DenoiseRL** introduces a framework where a policy is trained to "denoise" and correctly complete reasoning trajectories starting from incorrect partial solutions (noisy prefixes) sampled from a weak model.
- The approach augments standard RL training by incorporating both "main rollouts" (solving from scratch) and "denoise rollouts" (recovering from noisy prefixes) into a joint objective.
- During denoise rollouts, gradients are only applied to the model's generated continuation, with off-policy noisy prefix tokens masked from the PPO loss to ensure training stability.

## Results

- DenoiseRL consistently improved reasoning performance, with DenoiseRL-DAPO on Qwen3-8B-Base achieving an average score of **44.8%**, up from its **42.8%** baseline across mathematical and general reasoning benchmarks.
- Experiments showed an optimal balance with a prefix ratio of 
ho=0.2 and 4 denoise rollouts (K=4K=4K=4) per problem, yielding the highest average gain (+**16.3%** over the base model for Qwen3-4B-Base).
- Ablation studies confirmed that enabling gradient flow through off-policy prefix tokens leads to training instability and performance collapse, while an unfairly large output budget for recovery also reduced performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly training models to recover from mistakes (denoising noisy prefixes) fosters a stronger self-corrective capability, enhancing overall reasoning robustness.
> - Leveraging readily available weak model failures as structured perturbations provides a scalable, low-cost mechanism for generating diverse and informative learning signals, thereby overcoming exploration limitations.
> - Optimal performance hinges on carefully tuning the intensity of noise (prefix ratio) and the proportion of denoise rollouts, as excessive noise or over-emphasis on recovery can hinder overall learning efficiency.
