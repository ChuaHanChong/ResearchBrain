# iGibson 2.0: Object-Centric Simulation for Robot Learning of Everyday Household Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> iGibson 2.0 expands the scope of embodied AI simulation by integrating extended physical states and a logical predicate system, allowing robots to learn complex everyday household tasks like cooking and cleaning. It achieves this with an improved simulation performance and a VR interface for collecting human demonstrations.

## Problem

- Current embodied AI simulators primarily focus on kinodynamic interactions, limiting the study of complex everyday household tasks.
- Many household tasks require simulating non-kinodynamic object properties (e.g., temperature, wetness, cleanliness, functional states) which existing simulators lack or handle poorly.
- Collecting high-quality human demonstrations for complex, multi-modal tasks is a significant bottleneck in robot learning.

## Method

- **iGibson 2.0** introduces five new ==object-centric physical states==: temperature, wetness, cleanliness, toggled, and sliced, which are continuously simulated and visually manifest.
- Develops a ==logical predicate system== with ==discriminative functions== (physical to logical) and ==generative functions== (logical to physical) to bridge semantic and physical representations.
- Integrates an immersive ==Virtual Reality (VR) interface== with ==assistive grasping== and deterministic logging for efficient human demonstration collection.

## Results

- Enabled RL on six novel household tasks with extended states (soaking a towel, cooking meat), achieving **100%** success on several via "sticky mitten" grasping, improving further with diverse object/pose variation (**87%** soak towel).
- Collected VR demonstrations for bimanual tasks could train imitation learning policies to achieve partial success (**46%** when starting 3s from completion), validating the VR interface for data collection.
- Achieved a **25%** increase in average simulation performance (**125 steps/second**) compared to iGibson 1.0, despite added complexity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Simulating continuous, extended physical states is crucial for unlocking a broader range of realistic, everyday household tasks for robot learning.
> - A bidirectional mapping between continuous physical states and high-level logical predicates simplifies task specification, scene generation, and high-level reasoning.
> - Immersive VR interfaces can effectively collect human demonstrations for complex bimanual manipulation, which is essential for imitation learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied AI and robot learning has witnessed a surge in the development of simulation environments. Prior to iGibson 2.0, prominent simulators such as Habitat AI, AI2Thor, ThreeDWorld, Sapien, RoboSuite, and the original iGibson (1.0) primarily focused on kinodynamic simulation – modeling motion and physical contact between rigid bodies. These environments have been instrumental in advancing research in tasks like navigation, pick-and-place, and object rearrangement.

However, the authors identify a critical limitation: this focus has inadvertently skewed research towards problems that fit within the capabilities of kinodynamic simulators. Many everyday household tasks involve object properties beyond just pose and contact. Activities like cooking, cleaning, or watering plants require simulating changes in an object's temperature, wetness, cleanliness, or functional state (e.g., toggled, sliced). While some simulators have attempted to address these aspects (e.g., realistic fluid/flexible material simulation in Nvidia FleX or SoftGym, or object-centric representations with discrete symbolic states in AI2Thor and VirtualHome), they often fall short in scalability, realism, or the ability to handle continuous state changes.

iGibson 2.0 positions itself as a crucial bridge over this gap. Instead of merely allowing discrete state transitions (like "raw" to "cooked"), it introduces a *continuous* simulation of underlying physical processes (e.g., heat transfer for temperature, droplet systems for wetness). This allows for more realistic and nuanced task execution where an agent must control the specific values of extended states. Furthermore, it differentiates itself from highly accurate but computationally expensive fluid/soft body simulators by offering a "simple but effective" object-centric mechanism that prioritizes practical utility for robot learning in complex, interactive scenes. The inclusion of a VR interface also addresses the broader challenge of efficiently collecting high-quality human demonstrations for complex, multi-modal tasks, which is a common bottleneck in robot learning. In essence, iGibson 2.0 aims to expand the definition of "simulatable" tasks in embodied AI, thereby unlocking new and more complex real-world problems for research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind iGibson 2.0 stems from the recognition that current embodied AI simulation environments restrict the complexity and diversity of tasks that can be studied. While existing simulators excel at kinodynamic interactions (e.g., moving objects), they largely neglect the rich array of non-kinodynamic properties that are integral to everyday household activities. Tasks such as cooking (changing temperature), cleaning (modifying cleanliness levels), or watering plants (altering wetness) cannot be realistically simulated or thoroughly investigated in environments limited to rigid body dynamics. This deficiency creates a semantic gap between the capabilities of AI agents in simulation and the demands of real-world scenarios.

Given this motivation, the key objectives of iGibson 2.0 are multifaceted:

1. **Introduce Extended Physical States:** To enable a wider range of household tasks, the environment must accurately simulate object properties beyond just pose. This includes temperature, wetness level, cleanliness level (dustiness and stains), and functional states such as toggled (on/off) and sliced.
2. **Bridge Physical and Logical Representations:** To facilitate high-level reasoning and task specification, the simulator needs a robust system that maps continuous physical states to semantically meaningful logical predicates (e.g., `Cooked`, `Soaked`, `Stained`). Conversely, it aims to provide generative functions that can sample valid physical states based on these logical descriptions, simplifying scene initialization and task instance generation.
3. **Enhance Scene Realism and Diversity:** By leveraging the generative predicate system, iGibson 2.0 seeks to create more densely populated and ecologically valid scenes. This involves automatically placing a multitude of small objects in semantically appropriate locations, which is often a laborious manual process in other simulators.
4. **Facilitate Human Demonstration Collection:** To accelerate robot learning, especially for complex or bimanual tasks, the project aims to integrate an intuitive and immersive Virtual Reality (VR) interface. This interface is designed to allow humans to easily provide demonstrations, which can then be used for imitation learning.
5. **Enable Novel Robot Learning Research:** Ultimately, iGibson 2.0 strives to open up new avenues for research and development in embodied AI by providing the tools necessary to tackle complex, multi-modal, and multi-stage household tasks that were previously difficult or impossible to address.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

iGibson 2.0 introduces three core innovations to achieve its objectives: extended object states, a logical predicate system with generative capabilities, and a virtual reality (VR) interface for human demonstrations.

#### 3.1. Extended Physical States for Everyday Household Tasks

The simulator extends its object-centric representation to include five new physical states, beyond kinodynamics:
- **Temperature (T):** Objects can be annotated as heat sources or sinks, elevating or decreasing the temperature of nearby or contained objects. The temperature change is continuous, and a `MaxTemperature` history dictates persistent visual changes (e.g., cooked or burnt appearance, even if cooled).
- **Wetness Level (w):** A droplet system approximates liquid simulation. Droplets are created by sources (faucets), absorbed by soakable objects, and can be contained in receptacles. Wetness level tracks the number of absorbed droplets, visually altering the object's appearance.
- **Cleanliness (d/s):** Distinguishes between dustiness (d) and stain level (s). Dust is cleaned by dry tools, stains by wet tools. Cleaning involves physical contact with the appropriate tool, reducing the particle count and the cleanliness level. Objects can be initialized with visible dust/stain particles.
- **Toggled State (TS):** A binary functional state (on/off) for objects like stoves or microwaves. Toggling affects appearance and can activate other processes (e.g., heating). It's changed by contacting a specific `TogglingLink`.
- **Sliced State (SS):** An irreversible state where an object, when contacted with sufficient force by a `SlicingTool` at its `SlicingLink`, is replaced by two halves. The halves inherit the parent object's extended states.

These states are object-centric (a single value per object) and influence the object's visual appearance, allowing visually-guided agents to infer latent states. Object categories in the WordNet hierarchy are semantically annotated to determine which extended states are relevant.

#### 3.2. Logical Representation of Physical States

To bridge the gap between continuous physical states and high-level semantics, iGibson 2.0 defines a system of logical predicates:
- **Discriminative Functions:** These predicates map current simulated physical states (kinematic and extended) to semantically meaningful logical states (e.g., `Cooked(o)`, `Soaked(o)`, `InsideOf(o1, o2)`). `Cooked(o)` is `True` if `MaxTemperature` is within a cooked range, `Soaked(o)` if `WetnessLevel` exceeds a threshold.
- **Generative System:** This is a novel feature allowing the simulator to sample valid *physical* states that satisfy a given *logical* state.
 - For extended states (e.g., `Frozen`), it samples appropriate values for the underlying physical property (e.g., a temperature below freezing).
 - For particle-based states (`Dusty`, `Stained`), it samples particle locations on the object surface.
 - For kinematic predicates (`OnTopOf`, `InsideOf`), it employs a sophisticated pose sampling algorithm using ray-casting and analytical methods. This ensures sampled poses are stable, free of inter-object penetration, and semantically consistent (e.g., a book `OnTopOf` a table is actually supported by it).
This generative capability is used to create a new dataset of 15 highly populated iGibson scenes by applying semantic-logic rules to place hundreds of small objects in meaningful configurations, significantly boosting realism and task diversity.

#### 3.3. Virtual Reality Interface

iGibson 2.0 includes a novel VR interface compatible with major commercial headsets (HTC Vive, Oculus Rift S/Quest) to immerse humans in the simulated environment and collect demonstrations.
- **Immersive Control:** Humans control a virtual avatar (head, two hands, optional body) via headset and hand controllers. Head motion is directly mapped for comfort, while hands and body use constraint-based physics simulation for realistic interaction.
- **Assistive Grasping (AG):** To mitigate the difficulty of precise grasping in VR, an AG mechanism assists users. When a trigger is pressed past 50% and an object is in contact with the hand/palm, a temporary fixed joint is created. This constraint breaks if violated, ensuring realism for heavy or complex manipulations.
- **Navigation:** Human locomotion is combined with touchpad-based translation for navigating larger scenes.
- **Haptic Feedback:** Vibrations in controllers provide tactile feedback for collisions.
- **Logging and Replay:** All simulation states and VR actions are deterministically logged, enabling detailed analysis, replay, and use for imitation learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of iGibson 2.0 focused on demonstrating its new functionalities by enabling robot learning on six novel household tasks, which were previously difficult or impossible in other environments.

#### 4.1. Novel Task Capabilities

The paper defined six tasks to showcase the extended states and interactions:
1. **Grasping Book:** Demonstrates kinematic logical states.
2. **Soaking Towel:** Tests the liquid/droplet system and wetness level.
3. **Cleaning Stained Shelf:** Evaluates the cleanliness level update.
4. **Cooking Meat:** Highlights the temperature system.
5. **Slicing Fruit:** Showcases the sliced state and tool interaction.
6. **Bimanual Pick and Place:** A bimanual task involving a heavy object, difficult for single-arm manipulation.

#### 4.2. Reinforcement Learning (RL) Experiments

- **Bimanual Humanoid & Fetch (with "sticky mitten" grasping):** Using the Soft-Actor Critic (SAC) algorithm with a simplified "sticky mitten" grasping mechanism (fixed constraint on contact), agents achieved 100% success rates for Grasping Book, Soaking Towel, Cleaning Stained Shelf, and Cooking Meat. This strongly validates iGibson 2.0's ability to simulate and enable learning on tasks involving its new extended states.
 - For Slicing Fruit, success rates were lower (15% for humanoid, 0% for Fetch), indicating the task's high precision requirement, which remains a challenge for current RL approaches.
 - Bimanual Pick and Place for the humanoid showed 0% success, highlighting the persistent difficulty of coordinating bimanual actions with standard RL.
- **Fetch (without "sticky mitten" grasping):** When the sticky mitten simplification was removed, requiring the Fetch robot to rely on realistic gripper friction, performance significantly dropped (25% for Grasping Book and Soaking Towel, 0% for others). This result underscores that robust grasping for diverse objects remains a substantial challenge in realistic robotic manipulation.
- **Generalization Study (Soaking Towel):** An ablation study on `Soaking Towel` revealed the importance of training diversity. Policies trained with no variations achieved 19% success, those with different poses 79%, and those with different objects and poses 87%. This confirms that iGibson 2.0's generative system, which facilitates creating diverse task instances, is crucial for developing robust and generalizable policies.

#### 4.3. Imitation Learning (IL) Experiments with VR Demonstrations

- For the Bimanual Pick and Place task, 30 human demonstrations were collected using the VR interface and used to train an imitation learning policy (behavioral cloning).
- The policy failed to complete the full, long-horizon task due to covariate shift (diverging from the demonstration distribution).
- However, when the policy was initialized a few seconds before task completion from a successful human demonstration, it achieved 19% success (starting 6s away) and 46% success (starting 3s away). This indicates the potential of the VR interface to generate valuable demonstrations, especially for the later stages of complex bimanual tasks, even if long-horizon IL remains challenging.

#### 4.4. Performance Benchmark

Despite the significant addition of new physical states and complexities, iGibson 2.0 demonstrated a 25% *increase* in average simulation performance (125 steps/second vs. 100 steps/second in iGibson 1.0). This was achieved through optimizations such as improved object sleeping mechanisms and lazy updates in the renderer, ensuring the simulator remains computationally efficient for large-scale research.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

iGibson 2.0 represents a significant advancement in simulation environments for embodied AI and robotics, promising a substantial impact on the field.

**Expanded Research Scope:** The most profound significance lies in its ability to simulate a diverse and realistic range of everyday household tasks that extend beyond mere kinodynamic interactions. By introducing continuous simulation of object temperature, wetness, cleanliness, and functional states, iGibson 2.0 opens doors to novel research in robot learning for tasks like cooking, cleaning, and bimanual manipulation – areas previously underserved by existing simulators. This will allow researchers to tackle more complex, multi-modal, and human-relevant problems.

**Bridging the Semantic Gap:** The logical predicate system is a powerful contribution. Its discriminative functions provide a clear mapping from low-level physical states to high-level semantic concepts, which is vital for symbolic planning and task understanding in AI. Crucially, the generative functions allow researchers to specify task initializations symbolically (e.g., "a cooked fish on a stained plate") and have the simulator automatically generate valid physical instances. This significantly streamlines scene creation and task generation, enabling researchers to efficiently create diverse training curricula and test conditions.

**Empowering Data Collection:** The integrated Virtual Reality (VR) interface is a critical enabler for imitation learning. It provides an intuitive and immersive platform for humans to demonstrate complex manipulation skills, particularly bimanual tasks, which are notoriously difficult to program or demonstrate with traditional interfaces. The ability to log and replay these demonstrations deterministically will accelerate the development of robust robot policies, directly addressing a major bottleneck in robot learning.

**Fostering Community Advancement:** As an open-source platform with a publicly available dataset, iGibson 2.0 is poised to become a widely adopted tool within the embodied AI and robotics communities. Its comprehensive features and demonstrated capabilities are likely to inspire new benchmarks, facilitate comparative studies, and accelerate progress by providing a standardized, rich, and interactive environment.

**Limitations and Future Directions:** The authors candidly acknowledge limitations, such as the current lack of scalable soft body/flexible material simulation and accurate human behavior modeling. Addressing these in future iterations would further expand the environment's capabilities to tasks like laundry folding or sophisticated human-robot interaction. Nevertheless, iGibson 2.0's current innovations lay a robust foundation, and the demonstrated potential for sim-to-real transfer implies a direct path for learned policies to impact real-world robotic applications.

In conclusion, iGibson 2.0 is a timely and impactful contribution that pushes the boundaries of what is simulatable for embodied AI, enabling research into the more nuanced and complex aspects of robotic intelligence required for real-world household environments.
