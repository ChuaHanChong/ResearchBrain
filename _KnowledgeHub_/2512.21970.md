# StereoVLA: Enhancing Vision-Language-Action Models with Stereo Vision

> [!summary] Summary
> StereoVLA enhances Vision-Language-Action models for robotics by integrating stereo vision, leveraging a Geometric-and-Semantic (GeoSem) vision encoder and co-training objectives. The system achieved a 33.4% absolute gain in real-world manipulation task success rates and demonstrated improved robustness to varying camera viewpoints compared to existing baselines.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, relying on RGB encoders, often lack fine-grained spatial awareness and robustness to varying viewpoints, hindering precise manipulation tasks.
- Current sensory augmentations like wrist cameras, depth sensors, or multiple third-person cameras present limitations such as occlusions, noise, deployment complexity, and do not explicitly provide or effectively integrate stereo depth cues.
- Simply adapting existing VLA models with stereo inputs or using RGB-centric encoders does not sufficiently improve embodied manipulation due to a lack of explicit geometric cue extraction and the scarcity of large-scale real-world stereo robot data.

## Method

- **StereoVLA** introduces a novel architecture that processes stereo RGB image pairs through a specialized ==GeoSem Vision Encoder==, integrates these cues with language instructions via a pre-trained LLM backbone, and generates actions.
- The GeoSem Vision Encoder fuses dense geometric features from the ==filtered cost volume== of a stereo depth foundation model (FoundationStereo) with semantic features from general vision models (SigLIP, DINOv2).
- Two synergistic co-training tasks are implemented: ==Interaction-Region Depth Estimation==, which focuses spatial reasoning on task-relevant areas, and ==Camera Parameter Estimation==, which implicitly aligns perception and action coordinate systems for enhanced viewpoint robustness.
- A large-scale synthetic stereo dataset (5 million trajectories) is curated, alongside high-fidelity ray-traced rendering and extensive domain randomization, to facilitate effective learning and mitigate the sim-to-real gap for geometric understanding.

## Results

- StereoVLA achieved a **33.4%** absolute gain in success rate across diverse real-world manipulation tasks, including challenging small, transparent, and bar-shaped objects, where baselines often exhibited **0%** success.
- The model demonstrated superior robustness to camera pose variations, maintaining high success rates across small, medium, large, and 'moving' camera randomization ranges, outperforming other multi-camera or RGBD configurations.
- Ablation studies confirmed that each proposed component, especially the GeoSem Vision Encoder and the co-training tasks (Interaction-Region Depth Estimation and Camera Parameter Estimation), individually contributed to significant performance gains and robustness.
- StereoVLA showed strong zero-shot generalization on the LIBERO-MV-R benchmark, achieving substantially higher success rates across various viewpoint angles compared to fine-tuned baselines, which exhibited performance degradation with increased viewpoint shifts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly encoding both precise geometric information from stereo disparities and rich semantic features is crucial for improving VLA model performance in fine-grained robotic manipulation tasks.
> - Co-training objectives, specifically Interaction-Region Depth Estimation focused on interaction areas and Camera Parameter Estimation, significantly enhance a VLA model's spatial reasoning and robustness to varying camera viewpoints.
> - Leveraging intermediate feature representations (e.g., filtered cost volume) from a stereo depth foundation model offers a more efficient and geometrically rich source of information than full disparity maps for real-time VLA applications.
> - Large-scale synthetic stereo datasets, combined with robust domain randomization, can effectively overcome real-world data scarcity and facilitate successful sim-to-real transfer for geometric understanding in embodied AI.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models constitute a growing area within robotics, enabling robots to perform generalist manipulation tasks by leveraging pretrained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and large-scale robot datasets. While existing VLA models such as OpenVLA have demonstrated robust semantic reasoning and skill acquisition from demonstrations, they often exhibit limitations in fine-grained spatial awareness and viewpoint robustness. This limitation is primarily attributed to their reliance on pretrained RGB vision encoders, which are designed to prioritize semantic alignment over explicit geometric representation.

Previous research has explored various sensor modalities to augment single-view RGB inputs, including wrist-mounted cameras, depth sensors, and additional third-person cameras. However, each of these approaches presents inherent challenges:
- **Wrist-mounted cameras:** Offer limited fields of view, are prone to occlusion, and increase the risk of collision due to added hardware.
- **Depth sensors:** Provide noisy estimates, particularly for transparent, specular, or light-absorbing objects, and can be sensitive to external lighting conditions.
- **Extra third-person cameras:** Introduce deployment complexity for mobile robots and humanoid platforms, and their increased view diversity can sometimes hinder viewpoint robustness.

Furthermore, these existing setups typically do not explicitly provide stereoscopic perception or integrate learning methods specifically designed to leverage such cues for embodied manipulation. While some prior work has incorporated limited stereo data or focused on stereo-based depth foundation models, the effective learning and integration of stereo representations into VLA models for enhanced manipulation capabilities remains an underexplored area.

This work positions StereoVLA as a contribution to the VLA landscape by addressing the identified need for joint encoding of semantic and geometric information. It aims to integrate robust geometric cues from stereo vision into VLA models, proposing a systematic approach to improve spatial awareness and viewpoint robustness, thus bridging a perceived gap in current VLA capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models with improved fine-grained spatial awareness and robustness to varying viewpoints, thereby enabling more precise robotic manipulation.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in existing VLA models and their sensory inputs:

1. **Limited Spatial Awareness and Viewpoint Robustness:** Current VLA models predominantly rely on pretrained RGB vision encoders. These encoders are primarily optimized for semantic understanding and lack explicit mechanisms to extract and represent detailed geometric information. Consequently, robots powered by these models often struggle with precise interactions, such as manipulating small or transparent objects, and exhibit degraded performance when the camera perspective changes significantly.

2. **Inefficiency of Existing Sensory Augmentations:**
 - **Wrist-mounted cameras:** While providing egocentric views, they suffer from limited scope, frequent occlusions, and increase the mechanical complexity and collision risk of the robotic arm.
 - **Depth sensors:** Although capable of providing geometric information, their reliability is compromised by challenging material properties (e.g., transparency, specularity) and adverse lighting conditions, leading to inaccurate or noisy depth estimates.
 - **Multiple third-person cameras:** While offering complementary views, they complicate hardware deployment, especially for mobile or humanoid platforms, and the unaligned views can make coherent spatial reasoning challenging. Crucially, these setups do not explicitly provide stereoscopic depth cues that humans use for fine-grained spatial perception.

3. **Challenges with Direct Stereo Integration:** The authors observed that simply adapting existing RGB encoders within state-of-the-art VLA models to accept stereo inputs does not sufficiently improve embodied manipulation. This is attributed to two main factors: (a) the inherent lack of explicit geometric cue extraction in RGB-centric encoders, which prioritize semantic over geometric representation, and (b) the significant labor involved in collecting large-scale real-world robot data with synchronized stereo inputs.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of this research are:

- To introduce **StereoVLA**, a novel VLA framework that integrates rich spatial and geometric cues derived from stereo RGB pairs into the action generation process.
- To design a **Geometric-and-Semantic (GeoSem) vision encoder** capable of jointly extracting both precise geometric information from stereo disparities and rich semantic features from pixel observations.
- To overcome data scarcity by curating a **large-scale synthetic stereo dataset** that supports effective learning and sim-to-real transfer.
- To improve viewpoint robustness by implementing **two synergistic co-training objectives**: Interaction-Region Depth Estimation, to focus spatial reasoning on task-relevant areas, and Camera Parameter Estimation, to implicitly align perception and action coordinate systems.
- To empirically demonstrate that StereoVLA achieves substantial improvements in success rates and robustness across diverse, precision-demanding real-world manipulation tasks, including those with significant camera pose variations, when compared to baselines employing various input modalities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

StereoVLA is structured around a novel architecture that integrates stereo vision with a dual-objective co-training strategy. The overall system processes stereo image pairs through a specialized vision encoder, combines these visual cues with language instructions via a large language model backbone, and generates actions.

**A. Overall Architecture:**
The StereoVLA architecture comprises three main components:
1. **GeoSem Vision Encoder:** Takes a stereo RGB image pair (left and right views) and produces visual tokens encoding both geometric and semantic information.
2. **Vision-Language Backbone:** The visual tokens, along with language tokens (instructions), are fed into a pre-trained large language model, InternLM-1.8B, for multimodal reasoning.
3. **Action Expert:** Leverages intermediate vision-language features to predict action chunks, specifically delta end-effector poses, using a flow-matching technique.
In parallel, two co-training tasks – Interaction-Region Depth Estimation and Camera Parameter Estimation – are integrated to enhance spatial reasoning and viewpoint robustness during training.

**B. GeoSem Vision Encoder:**
The GeoSem Vision Encoder is designed to extract a unified representation from stereo images:
- **Geometric Feature Extraction:** Leverages **FoundationStereo**, a depth estimation foundation model. Instead of predicting a full disparity map, StereoVLA extracts features from an intermediate stage for efficiency.
 - FoundationStereo initially computes monocular features for each stereo image. These features are then processed in two parallel paths: one forming a 4D cost volume ($V_c$) and another generating a correlation volume ($V_{corr}$). The cost volume is further refined by an attention-based hybrid cost filtering module to yield a filtered cost volume ($V'_c$).
 - The paper selects the **filtered cost volume ($V'_c$)** as the primary source for geometric features. This choice is based on its density, rich geometric information, and the inclusion of long-range correlations, while avoiding the computational overhead of FoundationStereo's iterative disparity refinement or the limited information of the correlation volume.
- **Semantic Feature Extraction:** To complement the geometric features, semantic information is extracted from the monocular left view using **SigLIP and DINOv2**, following the approach in PrismaticVLM.
 - SigLIP, trained with a vision-language contrastive objective, captures high-level semantics.
 - DINOv2, trained with self-supervised discriminative objectives, extracts fine-grained visual details.
 - This extraction is applied only to the left view due to the redundancy of semantic information between stereo pairs, thus conserving computational resources.
- **Feature Fusion:** The spatially dense geometric features from FoundationStereo and the semantic features from SigLIP/DINOv2 are then fused.
 - The FoundationStereo features are spatially pooled to match the resolution (14-pixel stride) of the semantic features.
 - The final hybrid representation is obtained by **concatenating these feature maps along the channel dimension**. This channel-wise concatenation is chosen over token sequence concatenation to prevent an increase in the number of visual tokens, which would lead to higher computational overhead during training and inference.

**C. Co-training Tasks for Viewpoint Robustness:**
To improve the model's robustness to varying camera perspectives, the perception-action mapping is conceptualized as two sub-problems: camera-frame spatial perception and robot-frame action generation. Two auxiliary tasks are co-trained with action prediction:
- **1. Interaction-Region Depth Estimation:**
 - **Objective:** The model is trained to predict metric depth for specific (x,y) coordinates within the image.
 - **Task-Aware Sampling:** Instead of uniformly sampling points across the entire image (which might include irrelevant background), sampling is focused on an "interaction region." This region is dynamically defined by the 2D bounding boxes of the gripper and the target object. This encourages the model to learn precise spatial relationships critical for manipulation.
 - **Implementation:** Depth prediction is framed as a text generation task, where the model generates a string representing the depth value in response to a prompt. This is supervised using a cross-entropy loss ($L_{depth}$).
- **2. Camera Parameter Estimation:**
 - **Objective:** The model is tasked with inferring the camera's extrinsic (transformation from camera to robot frame, $T_{robot}^{camera}$) and intrinsic ($K$) parameters.
 - **Motivation:** This task encourages the model to learn camera-aware representations that account for camera pose variations, implicitly aligning perceptual information with the robot's action space.
 - **Implementation:** Similar to depth estimation, this is cast as a text generation task. The model generates a string representation of the ground-truth camera parameters (translation, Euler rotation, intrinsic parameters) in response to a prompt, supervised by a cross-entropy loss ($L_{camera}$).

**D. Dataset Curation and Sim-to-Real Transfer:**
- **Large-scale Synthetic Data:** A synthetic dataset comprising 5 million stereo trajectories was curated. This dataset includes both table-top Franka robot and humanoid robot data to ensure versatility.
- **Sim-to-Real Gap Mitigation:**
 - **Visual Domain Alignment:** Pretrained vision encoders are kept frozen, and co-training on GRIT (for 2D box prediction) helps align features to real imagery. High-fidelity ray-traced rendering with extensive domain randomization (lighting, textures, camera parameters) is employed. The authors note that stereo vision is inherently more robust for geometric sim-to-real transfer than depth sensors, as simulators struggle to replicate sensor-specific depth artifacts.
 - **Physical Dynamics Modeling:** A quasi-static assumption is adopted, utilizing simplified positional control and binary gripper states. Domain randomization of friction in simulation further enhances robustness.

**E. Training Details:**
- **Progressive Action Generation:** The model first predicts the 2D bounding box of the target object and the next keyframe pose of the gripper, which guide the subsequent action chunk prediction. Bounding box prediction is performed only on the left stereo image.
- **Total Loss Function:** The overall training loss combines the flow-matching loss for action chunk prediction ($L_{action}$) with cross-entropy losses for depth estimation ($L_{depth}$), camera parameter estimation ($L_{camera}$), bounding box prediction ($L_{bbox}$), and keyframe pose prediction ($L_{pose}$).
 - $L = L_{action} + L_{depth} + L_{camera} + L_{bbox} + L_{pose}$
- **Loss Balancing:** Samples from these five tasks are balanced with a ratio of 1:2:2:2:1.
- **Resources:** The model is trained for 300,000 steps on 32 NVIDIA H800 GPUs, using a batch size of 384 and a learning rate of $1.6 \times 10^{-4}$.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of StereoVLA involved comprehensive real-world experiments, comparisons with various camera settings, ablation studies on key components, and zero-shot evaluations on a challenging benchmark.

**A. Real-world Experiments (Section IV.A):**
- **Task Suite:** Evaluation was conducted across six categories: Common, Distractor, Environment, Transparent Object, Small Object (1-2 cm), and Bar-shaped Object tasks.
- **Metrics:** Success rates were measured over 15 trials per task set, with stringent criteria: single gripper closure/opening per trial, disabled "gripper sticking" heuristics, and full task completion for success.
- **Overall Performance:** StereoVLA consistently outperformed all baselines across every task. It achieved a 33.4% absolute gain in success rate in real-world experiments. For example, in the small-size object grasping task, StereoVLA achieved a 33% success rate, whereas all baselines failed (0%).
- **Depth-based Baselines:** SpatialVLA variants (using monocular, sensor, or stereo-estimated depth) showed performance correlating with depth quality. Sensor depth struggled with transparent objects. While stereo-derived depth (via FoundationStereo) improved quality, its 70ms inference overhead made it unsuitable for real-time control.
- **Qualitative Analysis:** Baselines often demonstrated premature gripper closure, indicating a lack of precise spatial perception. StereoVLA’s design, particularly its ability to leverage subtle stereo disparities, was credited for its superior performance. GraspVLA-S, a stereo-adapted baseline without StereoVLA's specific designs, performed adequately on simpler tasks but struggled with complex ones, suggesting that merely providing stereo input is insufficient without a tailored architecture.

**B. Comparison of Camera Settings (Section IV.B, Table I):**
- **Configurations:** StereoVLA (stereo RGBs) was compared against SpatialVLA-ED (single-view RGBD), π0.5 (front + wrist RGBs), and GraspVLA (front + side RGBs).
- **Robustness to Viewpoint Variation:** Models were evaluated under small, medium, large, and 'moving' camera randomization ranges.
- **Results:** StereoVLA demonstrated the highest success rates across all randomization ranges, indicating strong robustness to camera pose variations. GraspVLA (front + side) showed a significant performance drop with larger randomizations, highlighting challenges in integrating spatially disparate views. π0.5 (front + wrist) exhibited better resilience than GraspVLA in larger variations due to egocentric views, but suffered from lower baseline performance and premature gripper closures. RGBD-based models consistently underperformed, especially with large randomization.
- **Conclusion:** The stereo configuration, as implemented in StereoVLA, was identified as a promising alternative for balancing task performance, robustness to camera pose variations, and deployment simplicity.

**C. Ablation of Key Design Components (Section IV.C, Table IV):**
- A component-wise ablation study was conducted starting from GraspVLA-S (stereo-only baseline).
- **Findings:** The stereo-only baseline showed moderate performance. Adding the **GeoSem Vision Encoder** resulted in substantial performance gains. Further incorporating **Interaction-Region Depth Estimation (DE)** provided additional improvements. The **Camera Parameter Estimation (CPE)** task yielded the most significant gains in robustness, particularly under large and 'moving' camera pose shifts. Each proposed component contributed positively to the overall performance.

**D. Design Choices of GeoSem Vision Encoder (Section IV.D, Table II, Figure 6):**
- **Stereo Feature Selection:** Using the filtered cost volume ($V'_c$) from FoundationStereo yielded the highest success rate (77%) compared to the raw cost volume ($V_c$, 69%) or correlation volume ($V_{corr}$, 54%), validating its selection for rich geometric information.
- **Semantic Feature Inclusion:** Incorporating semantic features alongside geometric ones consistently improved success rates, preventing erroneous object grasping.
- **Feature Fusion Strategy:** Channel-wise concatenation of features outperformed sequence concatenation, offering both efficiency and higher success rates.
- **FoundationStereo vs. VGGT:** FoundationStereo demonstrated significantly higher depth estimation precision (AbsRel error of 0.0275) on the dataset compared to VGGT (0.4121), indicating its suitability for stereo-based manipulation.

**E. Effectiveness of Co-training Tasks (Section IV.E, Figure 7, Table IV):**
- **Interaction-Region Depth Estimation:** Explicitly supervising depth prediction within the interaction region led to the highest performance. Uniform depth sampling across the entire image negatively impacted early training.
- **Camera Parameter Estimation:** This task substantially improved viewpoint robustness, particularly for large and moving camera pose shifts, and was hypothesized to synergize with viewpoint randomization during training.

**F. Fine-Tuning on a Humanoid Platform (Section IV.F, Table III):**
- **Results:** Pre-training on general manipulation tasks significantly improved fine-tuning performance on humanoid tasks (Cleaning and Laundry).
- **Observation:** The "Laundry" task (opening a washing machine) exhibited a lower success rate than "Cleaning" (removing rubbish), which was attributed to a distributional shift from the table-top-centric pre-training data.

**G. Inference Speed (Section IV.G, Table V):**
- Extracting the filtered cost volume ($V'_c$) from FoundationStereo took 14ms, a significant reduction from the 68ms required for a full depth prediction.
- The total vision encoder inference time was 27ms. The overall inference time, including language (81ms) and action (52ms) processing, was 168ms on an NVIDIA H800 GPU.

**H. Zero-Shot Evaluation on LIBERO-MV-R (Section VII, Table VI, Figure 8):**
- **Benchmark:** LIBERO-MV-R, an enhanced version of LIBERO-Object with camera viewpoint shifts (0°, 45°, 90°, 135°) and object position perturbations, was used for zero-shot evaluation. Success criteria were relaxed to category-level completion with category-aware instructions.
- **Results:** StereoVLA achieved substantially higher zero-shot success rates across all viewpoint angles compared to fine-tuned baselines (OpenVLA-LIBERO and π0.5-LIBERO).
- **Baseline Analysis:** Baseline models showed fragile performance, with significant collapse as viewpoint angle increased, suggesting overfitting to specific training viewpoints and memorization of absolute object positions.
- **StereoVLA Robustness:** StereoVLA maintained robust performance, attributed to its GeoSem feature extractor and co-training tasks, enabling accurate 3D target localization irrespective of camera perspective or object perturbations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research on StereoVLA presents a methodological contribution to the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, with several implications for robotic manipulation and future research:

- **Enhanced Spatial Perception for VLAs:** StereoVLA demonstrates that incorporating explicit geometric cues from stereo vision can substantially improve the fine-grained spatial awareness of VLA models. This addresses a critical limitation of previous systems that primarily relied on RGB encoders optimized for semantic understanding.
- **Improved Manipulation Precision and Robustness:** The observed performance gains in real-world tasks, particularly for small, transparent, and bar-shaped objects, indicate that StereoVLA can facilitate more precise and robust robotic manipulation. Its consistent outperformance across tasks involving distractors and environmental variations suggests enhanced generalizability.
- **Increased Viewpoint Robustness:** The ability of StereoVLA to maintain high success rates under wide-ranging camera pose variations, including near-hemispheric views and moving cameras, is a significant step towards deploying robots in dynamic and less constrained environments where camera perspectives are highly variable. This is crucial for mobile robots and humanoids.
- **Practical Guidance for Sensor Selection:** The comparative evaluation of different camera setups provides empirical evidence that stereo RGB configuration can offer a favorable balance between task performance, robustness to viewpoint changes, and deployment simplicity, informing future hardware choices in robotics.
- **Effective Sim-to-Real Transfer for Geometry:** The successful leveraging of large-scale synthetic stereo data, coupled with domain randomization and the observation that stereo is robust for geometric sim-to-real transfer, provides a scalable approach to training VLA models without extensive real-world data collection.
- **Modular Architectural Contributions:** The GeoSem Vision Encoder, which combines geometric features from a specialized stereo foundation model with semantic features from general vision models, and the two co-training tasks (Interaction-Region Depth Estimation and Camera Parameter Estimation) offer modular strategies for improving geometric understanding and viewpoint invariance within multimodal learning systems.
- **Broader Generalization:** The demonstrated zero-shot generalization capabilities on the LIBERO-MV-R benchmark and the adaptability to a humanoid platform suggest that the principles of StereoVLA could extend to a wider range of robotic morphologies and complex, multi-stage tasks.

Future work directions identified include exploring higher input resolutions to further improve performance on very small objects and integrating mechanisms to capture long-horizon temporal dependencies, which would expand the model's capabilities for complex, sequential tasks.
