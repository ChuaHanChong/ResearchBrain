# RoboTwin: Dual-Arm Robot Benchmark with Generative Digital Twins (early version)

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboTwin introduces a benchmark for dual-arm robots by combining real-world teleoperated data with synthetic data generated from generative digital twins. This approach, which leverages large language models for automatically creating expert trajectories, yielded significant improvements in task success rates, such as raising 'Empty Cup Place' success from 10% to 96% when trained with more expert data.

## Problem

- Severe scarcity of specialized, high-quality training data for complex dual-arm robotic tasks, tool manipulation, and human-robot interaction.
- Traditional real-world data collection methods (teleoperation) are labor-intensive, costly, and difficult to scale for diverse scenarios.
- Existing simulation-based data generation often lacks real-world fidelity or relies on hand-designed heuristics, leading to a significant sim-to-real gap.

## Method

- Introduces **RoboTwin**, a benchmark dataset for dual-arm robots that integrates real-world teleoperated data with high-fidelity synthetic data.
- Develops an efficient real-to-simulation pipeline using ==AI-Generated Content (AIGC)== to construct 3D object models and scenes from a single 2D image.
- Leverages Large Language Models (LLMs) like GPT4-V and GPT4 to automatically generate expert-level training data, including precise pose sequences and trajectory planning scripts.

## Results

- The 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3) showed improved success rates across six dual-arm tasks when trained with more expert data generated by RoboTwin.
- For the 'Empty Cup Place' task, success rate increased from **10%** with 10 demonstrations to **96%** with 50 demonstrations.
- The 'Block Handover' task achieved a **98%** success rate with 50 demonstrations, up from **50%** with 10 demonstrations, validating data quality for interaction tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A novel real-to-simulation pipeline can efficiently create detailed 3D digital twins from a single 2D RGB image, overcoming the high cost of traditional methods.
> - Large Language Models (LLMs) can effectively automate the generation of high-quality, expert-level training data, including task-specific pose sequences and control scripts.
> - Increasing the amount of high-quality expert demonstration data directly correlates with higher success rates for visuomotor policies on complex dual-arm manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The advancement of robotics, particularly in areas requiring complex dexterity, tool use, and human-robot interaction, is critically dependent on the availability of high-quality training data. However, as the authors highlight, a significant bottleneck is the scarcity of specialized and diverse datasets for dual-arm robotic systems. This paper directly addresses this fundamental challenge.

Existing approaches to collecting robotic training data generally fall into two categories:
- **Real-world teleoperation:** Methods like BridgeData, RT-1, BC-Z, MimicGen, and RoboCasa involve human operators guiding robots to perform tasks. While this yields high-fidelity real-world data, it is inherently labor-intensive, time-consuming, and difficult to scale, especially for complex dual-arm coordination and tool manipulation. Moreover, scaling to diverse environments and objects remains a challenge, as these systems often rely on predefined scenes or limited human demonstrations.
- **Algorithmic trajectory generation in simulation:** Benchmarks like ManiSkill2 and RLBench leverage simulation environments to generate data. While efficient and scalable, these often rely on privileged information, hand-designed heuristics, or pre-configured virtual environments, which may not accurately reflect the complexities and variability of the real world. The fidelity of "expert" data generated this way can also be limited, often failing to mimic real machine operations.

RoboTwin carves out a unique niche by proposing a **hybrid data generation strategy** that bridges the gap between these two approaches. It aims to combine the realism of teleoperated data with the scalability and control of synthetic data, while overcoming the limitations of previous methods.
- **Addressing the "real-to-sim" gap:** Unlike traditional simulation datasets, RoboTwin's digital twin generation process is explicitly designed to create virtual replicas *from real-world observations* (a single 2D image). This dramatically reduces the effort and cost associated with manually constructing detailed simulated environments and objects, allowing for a more accurate and dynamic transfer of real-world scenarios into simulation.
- **Leveraging Generative AI for scalability:** A key innovation is the use of Large Language Models (LLMs) for automatically generating expert-level training data. This moves beyond manual programming or rigid algorithmic generators, allowing for the synthesis of task-specific pose sequences and control scripts. This LLM-driven approach offers unprecedented scalability and adaptability, enabling the creation of diverse scenarios and variations that would be prohibitively expensive to collect manually or design through traditional simulation methods.
- **Focus on dual-arm and complex interactions:** Many existing benchmarks focus on single-arm manipulation or simpler tasks. RoboTwin specifically targets dual-arm coordination, tool use, and human-robot interaction, which are crucial for more sophisticated real-world applications but notoriously difficult to model and generate data for.
- **Enabling advanced learning algorithms:** The benchmark is designed to support various robot manipulation learning methods, including Behavioral Cloning, Offline Reinforcement Learning, and Diffusion Policies (like the 3D Diffusion Policy used in their experiments). By providing a rich, diverse, and scalable dataset, RoboTwin can significantly enhance the training and evaluation of these advanced visuomotor policies, pushing the boundaries of what robots can learn and achieve.

In essence, RoboTwin advances the field by offering a practical, cost-effective, and scalable solution for generating the specialized training data necessary to develop more dexterous, versatile, and intelligent dual-arm robotic systems that can operate effectively in complex real-world environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching motivation behind RoboTwin stems from a critical bottleneck in the advancement of robotics: the severe scarcity of high-quality, specialized training data. This shortage particularly impacts the development of sophisticated capabilities such as dual-arm coordination, intricate tool manipulation, and nuanced human-robot interaction—skills vital for robots to operate effectively in diverse real-world environments like manufacturing, healthcare, and homes. Traditional data collection methods are either too costly, too labor-intensive, or lack the necessary fidelity and diversity to train robust robotic systems for these complex tasks.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the RoboTwin project are clearly defined:

1. **Develop the RoboTwin Benchmark Dataset:** To create a comprehensive dataset specifically designed for dual-arm robotic scenarios. This benchmark is unique in its integration of both real-world teleoperated data (collected using the COBOT Magic platform) and high-fidelity synthetic data generated from corresponding digital twins. The goal is to provide rich annotations and diverse examples to ensure robust training and evaluation for complex tasks involving tool usage and human-robot interaction.

2. **Establish an Efficient Real-to-Simulation Pipeline:** To overcome the limitations of traditional, expensive digital twin creation methods. The objective is to innovate a cost-effective and convenient pipeline that can transform a single 2D RGB image from the real world into a detailed 3D model of target objects and corresponding scenes. This aims to enable rapid and accurate replication of real-world scenarios in a simulated environment, thereby facilitating scalable data generation.

3. **Utilize Large Language Models (LLMs) for Automatic Expert-Level Data Generation:** To significantly reduce the manual effort and expertise required for generating high-quality training data. The objective is to leverage the reasoning capabilities of LLMs (such as GPT4-V and GPT4) to automatically generate expert-level training data, including task-specific pose sequences oriented toward functionality and code for activating trajectory planning tools. This aims to scale data generation while maintaining high quality and relevance.

Collectively, these objectives are designed to address the fundamental problem of robotic training data scarcity. By achieving these goals, the authors aim to accelerate the development of more capable, versatile, and adaptable robotic systems, pushing the boundaries of what robots can learn and perform in a wide range of real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The RoboTwin project employs a multi-faceted approach, combining real-world data collection, innovative generative AI for digital twin creation, and large language model-driven synthetic data generation.

#### 3.1. Real-World Data Collection

- **Platform:** The authors utilized the **COBOT Magic platform** developed by AgileX Robotics. This open-source system is robust and equipped for advanced manipulation tasks.
- **Hardware Setup:** The platform features four AgileX Arms for dual-arm manipulation and four Intel Realsense D-435 RGBD cameras. These cameras are strategically placed: one high on the stand for a broad view, two on the robot wrists for close-up manipulation views, and an optional one on the lower part of the stand.
- **Data Acquisition:** The front, left, and right cameras capture data simultaneously at a frequency of 30Hz. Each frame includes RGB and depth images (640x480 pixels) from each camera, along with the precise poses of the robot arms' joints and end-effectors (for both master and slave configurations, covering left and right arms).
- **Data Standards:** All data storage and formatting adhere to the unified standards established by the ARIO Data Alliance, ensuring consistency and interoperability.
- **Task Design:** The dataset comprises 17 diverse tasks, specifically highlighting dual-arm capabilities, tool usage (9 tasks), and human-robot interaction (5 tasks). Six tasks are explicitly dual-arm. For each task, 30 trajectories were collected. To enhance detail for model learning, tasks were broken down into multiple stages, with slower data collection for key, precise sub-trajectories.

#### 3.2. Generative Digital Twin System (Real-to-Simulation Transfer)

- **Challenge Addressed:** Traditional methods for creating high-fidelity digital twins are often expensive, requiring specialized sensors and extensive manual modeling. RoboTwin circumvents this by using an AI-Generated Content (AIGC) approach.
- **Core Technology:** The system leverages **Deemos’s Rodin platform**, an AI-powered 3D digital asset generation model, to construct 3D models from a single 2D RGB image.
- **Process:**
 1. **3D Model Generation:** A single 2D real-world image of a target object is processed to generate a detailed 3D mesh and texture, including geometry, surface normals, wireframes, and textures. This ensures visual realism and compatibility with physics engines for simulation.
 2. **Functional Axis Assignment:** Once the 3D model is created, specific coordinate axes are assigned to the functional parts of the objects. For instance, for a hammer, one axis aligns with the hammerhead (identifying the functional part), and another indicates the approach direction for grasping.
 3. **Automated Grasp Pose Calculation:** This strategic axis alignment is crucial for automating the computation of grasp poses. Grasp poses are calculated perpendicular to the surface normal of the functional part, along the designated approach direction axis, minimizing manual intervention for correct tool usage and manipulation.

#### 3.3. Expert Data Generation with Large Language Models (LLMs)

- **Leveraging LLM Capabilities:** The project integrates LLMs to automate the generation of high-quality, expert-level training data, enriching the dataset with scenario-specific examples.
- **GPT4-V for Pose Sequences:** GPT4-V, a multi-modal LLM, is utilized for its reasoning capabilities. It analyzes task requirements and generates code to calculate the relationships between key poses and the functional coordinate axes of objects. This allows for the automated generation of precise, task-specific pose sequences.
- **GPT4 for Trajectory Planning:** GPT4 is employed to generate scripts that invoke trajectory planning tools. This automates the programming efforts required for robotic movements, streamlining the deployment of robotic systems and providing a scalable method for generating diverse and accurate trajectories in simulation.

#### 3.4. Benchmark Design and Experimentation

- **Benchmark Structure:** RoboTwin provides a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating dual-arm robots. It includes an API for generating expert data across infinitely variable scenarios (e.g., different object placements) and an offline dataset of pre-generated expert data for training and benchmarking algorithms.
- **Experimental Validation:** To test the correctness and effectiveness of the benchmark's expert data and the COBOT Magic platform settings, the authors utilized the **3D Diffusion Policy (DP3)**. They conducted experiments on six tasks from the benchmark, training the policy with varying amounts of expert data (10, 20, and 50 sets of demonstrations) to assess the impact on success rates.

This integrated methodology allows RoboTwin to generate a rich, scalable, and high-fidelity dataset that bridges the real-world and simulation domains, addressing key challenges in robotic data scarcity and accelerating the development of advanced dual-arm manipulation capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental section of the paper focuses on validating two key aspects: the rationality of the COBOT Magic platform settings and the effectiveness of the automatically generated expert data. The authors used the 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3) as their chosen learning strategy to test six representative tasks from the RoboTwin benchmark. For each task, the policy was trained with three different quantities of expert demonstration data: 10, 20, and 50 sets. The primary metric for evaluation was the task success rate.

The results, summarized in Table 1, consistently demonstrate a strong positive correlation between the number of expert demonstrations provided and the success rate of the trained 3D Diffusion Policy. This is a crucial finding, as it validates the quality and utility of the generated expert data.

Here are the specific performance improvements observed across the six tasks:

- **Block Hammer Beat:** The success rate improved from a modest 24% with 10 demonstrations to a respectable 80% with 50 demonstrations. This indicates that while the task is challenging, increased data significantly enhances performance.
- **Empty Cup Place:** This task showed a dramatic improvement, soaring from only 10% success with 10 demonstrations to an impressive 96% with 50 demonstrations. This highlights the effectiveness of the expert data in mastering precise placement tasks.
- **Dual-Bottles Pick:** Similar to the "Empty Cup Place," this task started at 10% success with minimal data but climbed to 74% with 50 demonstrations, indicating the data's utility for complex multi-object manipulation.
- **Block Sweep:** Performance steadily improved from 28% (10 demonstrations) to 86% (50 demonstrations), showcasing the data's ability to train policies for continuous manipulation actions.
- **Apple Cabinet Storage:** This task showed a more moderate but still significant improvement, from 30% to 64%, suggesting it might be inherently more complex or require even more data to reach very high success rates.
- **Block Handover:** This task achieved the most significant improvement, reaching an almost perfect 98% success rate with 50 demonstrations, up from 50% with 10 demonstrations. This result strongly validates the effectiveness of the generated data for precise human-robot or robot-robot interaction scenarios.

**Overall Conclusion from Findings:**

The experimental results robustly confirm two main points:
1. **Effectiveness of Automatically Generated Expert Data:** The marked increase in success rates across diverse tasks with more training data clearly demonstrates that the data generated through RoboTwin's AIGC and LLM pipeline is high-quality and effective for training visuomotor policies. It successfully captures the expert behaviors necessary for complex dual-arm manipulation, tool use, and interaction tasks.
2. **Rationality of COBOT Magic Platform Settings:** The successful training and high performance achieved on tasks performed on the COBOT Magic platform implicitly validate its suitability as a robust and reliable real-world system for both data collection and policy deployment. The ability of the policies trained on RoboTwin data to achieve high success rates on this platform confirms the practical applicability of the benchmark.

In summary, the findings provide compelling evidence that RoboTwin effectively addresses the data scarcity problem, offering a scalable solution for generating high-fidelity, expert-level robotic training data that significantly boosts learning performance for complex dual-arm tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboTwin presents a significant advancement in the field of robotics, addressing a fundamental bottleneck that has long impeded the development of more advanced and autonomous robotic systems: the scarcity of specialized, high-quality training data. Its significance and potential impact are multi-fold:

1. **Bridging the Real-World to Simulation Gap:** One of the most impactful contributions is the innovative "real-to-simulation pipeline." By enabling the creation of detailed 3D digital twins from just a single 2D RGB image, RoboTwin dramatically reduces the cost and effort traditionally associated with developing high-fidelity simulated environments. This allows researchers to rapidly replicate real-world scenarios in a virtual space, facilitating extensive testing and data generation that would be impractical or impossible with physical robots alone. This capability is crucial for closing the sim-to-real gap, allowing policies trained in simulation to transfer more effectively to physical robots.

2. **Scalable and Cost-Effective Data Generation:** The integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) for automatically generating expert-level training data is a paradigm shift. Unlike labor-intensive human teleoperation or rigid, hand-coded simulation algorithms, LLMs can synthesize diverse, task-specific pose sequences and control scripts at scale. This vastly accelerates the data collection process, making high-quality data readily available for a wide array of robotic tasks without requiring constant human intervention or highly specialized programming expertise. This scalability is vital for the development of generalist robots capable of performing a multitude of tasks.

3. **Advancing Dual-Arm Dexterity and Tool Use:** RoboTwin specifically focuses on dual-arm coordination and tool manipulation, areas that are inherently complex and require nuanced control. By providing a benchmark and data generation capabilities for these challenging scenarios, the work directly contributes to pushing the boundaries of robotic dexterity. Robots that can skillfully use tools and coordinate multiple arms will be able to perform a much wider range of sophisticated tasks in unstructured environments.

4. **Enabling Robust Human-Robot Interaction:** The inclusion of human-robot interaction tasks in the benchmark is crucial. As robots become more ubiquitous in human environments (e.g., healthcare, homes, collaborative manufacturing), their ability to safely and effectively interact with people is paramount. RoboTwin provides the necessary data and testing framework to develop and evaluate policies for such interactions, fostering safer and more intuitive human-robot collaboration.

5. **Accelerating Robotic Learning Research:** By providing a comprehensive benchmark dataset with both real and high-fidelity synthetic data, RoboTwin will serve as a critical resource for the robotics research community. It enables researchers to more easily compare different robotic learning algorithms (e.g., Behavioral Cloning, Offline Reinforcement Learning, Diffusion Policies) on a standardized, challenging set of tasks. This can accelerate the discovery and refinement of novel learning architectures and control strategies, leading to faster progress in the field.

6. **Potential for Broader Applications:** The capabilities fostered by RoboTwin have immense potential in diverse real-world applications. In manufacturing, it could enable more agile and versatile assembly lines. In healthcare, it could facilitate more sophisticated surgical robots or automated patient care. In logistics and service industries, it could lead to more efficient handling of complex objects and collaborative tasks. Ultimately, by empowering robots with greater dexterity, versatility, and autonomy through robust training data, RoboTwin paves the way for more capable and adaptive robotic systems that can tackle challenges currently beyond their reach.
