# RoboTwin: Dual-Arm Robot Benchmark with Generative Digital Twins (early version)

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboTwin introduces a benchmark for dual-arm robots by combining real-world teleoperated data with synthetic data generated from generative digital twins. This approach, which leverages large language models for automatically creating expert trajectories, yielded significant improvements in task success rates, such as raising 'Empty Cup Place' success from 10% to 96% when trained with more expert data.

## Problem

- Severe scarcity of specialized, high-quality training data for complex dual-arm robotic tasks, tool manipulation, and human-robot interaction.
- Traditional real-world data collection methods (teleoperation) are labor-intensive, costly, and difficult to scale for diverse scenarios.
- Existing simulation-based data generation often lacks real-world fidelity or relies on hand-designed heuristics, leading to a significant sim-to-real gap.

## Method

- Introduces **RoboTwin**, a benchmark dataset for dual-arm robots that integrates real-world teleoperated data with high-fidelity synthetic data.
- Develops an efficient real-to-simulation pipeline using ==AI-Generated Content (AIGC)== to construct 3D object models and scenes from a single 2D image.
- Leverages Large Language Models (LLMs) like GPT4-V and GPT4 to automatically generate expert-level training data, including precise pose sequences and trajectory planning scripts.

## Results

- The 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3) showed improved success rates across six dual-arm tasks when trained with more expert data generated by RoboTwin.
- For the 'Empty Cup Place' task, success rate increased from **10%** with 10 demonstrations to **96%** with 50 demonstrations.
- The 'Block Handover' task achieved a **98%** success rate with 50 demonstrations, up from **50%** with 10 demonstrations, validating data quality for interaction tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A novel real-to-simulation pipeline can efficiently create detailed 3D digital twins from a single 2D RGB image, overcoming the high cost of traditional methods.
> - Large Language Models (LLMs) can effectively automate the generation of high-quality, expert-level training data, including task-specific pose sequences and control scripts.
> - Increasing the amount of high-quality expert demonstration data directly correlates with higher success rates for visuomotor policies on complex dual-arm manipulation tasks.
