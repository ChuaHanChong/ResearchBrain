# Nonlinear Predictive Control of the Continuum and Hybrid Dynamics of a Suspended Deformable Cable for Aerial Pick and Place

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Sapienza University of Rome and the University of Twente developed a hybrid nonlinear predictive control framework for UAVs carrying flexible, extensible cables. The system effectively manages both continuous cable deformation and discrete payload interactions, enabling real-time pick-and-place and obstacle avoidance with a reduced-order model that captures over 99% of cable dynamics using just two modes.

## Problem

- Controlling UAVs with flexible cable-suspended payloads is challenging due to highly nonlinear continuum cable dynamics.
- Systems exhibit complex hybrid behaviors involving discrete events like payload attachment and detachment, which existing models often simplify.
- Prior approaches either use simplified cable models that lack fidelity for distributed deformation or complex models that are too computationally intensive for real-time control.

## Method

- A high-fidelity hybrid Partial Differential Equation (PDE) model was developed to represent the cable as a flexible continuum, explicitly capturing payload attachment/detachment events.
- A Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (POD)-based Reduced-Order Model (ROM) was derived from the PDE, preserving dominant deformation modes and ensuring modal coherence during hybrid transitions.
- A Nonlinear ==Model Predictive Control== (NMPC) scheme integrates the ROM to achieve real-time UAV and cable-payload trajectory tracking, actively suppressing cable oscillations.

## Results

- The POD-based ROM reduced integration time by orders of magnitude compared to the full FDM model and allowed for significantly larger stable integration step sizes, with the first two modes capturing over **99%** of cable energy.
- The NMPC framework successfully performs real-time pick-and-place tasks with RMS cable tip position errors around **0.17**-**0.23** m and navigates obstacle avoidance scenarios without collisions.
- HiLQR-RTI achieved average MPC solution times of **16.4** ms, suitable for real-time operation at a 25 ms sampling interval, though the full HiLQR solver was required for robustness in complex obstacle avoidance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - POD effectively reduces the computational complexity of continuum cable models by orders of magnitude while accurately retaining dominant dynamics (e.g., >99% energy with two modes), making them suitable for real-time NMPC.
> - Integrating hybrid modeling with NMPC allows for robust control of systems with both continuous deformation and discrete interaction events, outperforming baseline ROMs in accuracy, especially with payloads.
> - The choice of NMPC solver (e.g., full HiLQR vs. HiLQR-RTI) involves a trade-off between computational speed and robustness, becoming crucial in complex, non-convex scenarios like obstacle avoidance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Aerial manipulation utilizing cable-suspended payloads presents a set of advantages over rigid manipulators, including enhanced safety, compliance for confined environments, and the potential to leverage passive cable dynamics for efficient maneuvers. However, these systems introduce challenges due to the highly nonlinear dynamics of continuum cables and the hybrid behaviors arising from payload interactions (attachment/detachment).

Existing research in this area can be broadly categorized by the fidelity of the cable model employed:
- **Rigid pendulum models:** These simplify the payload as a point mass or rigid body, facilitating swing attenuation and cooperative transport. Their limitation lies in neglecting cable slackness and distributed deformation, thereby restricting their use to taut-cable scenarios.
- **Hybrid slack/taut models:** These account for transitions between free-fall and tensioned states, enabling controllers to manage re-tensioning events. However, the underlying dynamics typically remain simplified, not capturing the full continuum behavior of the cable.
- **Reduced-order continuum models:** Approaches such as Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (POD) have been applied to reduce the dimensionality of soft robot and cable models, allowing for faster, yet representative, models. Previous work has utilized POD for cable approximation in Model Predictive Control (MPC) but often overlooked payload interactions.

The current work addresses a gap in the literature by integrating high-fidelity continuum cable modeling, model order reduction, and hybrid predictive control within a unified framework. This integration aims to provide a system that simultaneously offers accuracy in representing cable deformation, manages hybrid payload interactions, and is tractable for real-time control, thereby extending the applicability of aerial manipulation with flexible cables beyond the limitations of prior, often isolated, approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a hybrid control framework for unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) that are equipped with flexible, extensible cables and are designed to perform dynamic payload manipulation tasks. The motivation stems from the inherent advantages of flexible cables—such as enhanced safety, compliance in cluttered environments, and energy efficiency through passive dynamics—counterbalanced by the complexities of their nonlinear continuum dynamics and hybrid interactions with payloads.

Specifically, the paper identifies the following key contributions and objectives:
- **Development of a Hybrid Partial Differential Equation (PDE) Model:** The first objective is to construct a high-fidelity hybrid model that accurately represents the cable as a flexible continuum. This model is designed to explicitly capture discrete events such as payload attachment and detachment, which fundamentally alter the system's boundary conditions and dynamics. This addresses the need for a comprehensive model that can account for both continuous deformation and discrete operational modes.
- **Creation of a Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (POD)-Based Reduced-Order Model (ROM):** A subsequent objective is to derive a reduced-order model from the high-fidelity PDE representation. This ROM is specifically adapted to preserve the dominant deformation modes of the cable and ensure modal coherence across hybrid transitions (e.g., between free-tip and slung-payload configurations). The motivation for this is to significantly reduce computational complexity, making the model suitable for real-time control applications without sacrificing essential dynamic accuracy.
- **Formulation of a Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) Scheme:** The third objective is to integrate the developed ROM into a nonlinear model predictive control scheme. This controller is intended to achieve real-time trajectory tracking for the UAV and its cable-payload system, while actively suppressing undesired cable oscillations. The NMPC aims to provide robustness and accuracy throughout complex manipulation tasks, including those involving hybrid transitions.

The overarching motivation is to overcome the limitations of existing approaches that typically trade off modeling fidelity for computational tractability or simplify hybrid dynamics. By integrating these components, the framework seeks to enable real-time, dynamics-aware control of UAVs with suspended flexible cables, thereby expanding the range and complexity of tasks these aerial systems can perform.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research combines high-fidelity continuum modeling, model order reduction, and a hybrid nonlinear model predictive control scheme.

**A. Hybrid Model of Extensible Strings**
The cable is modeled as a perfectly flexible, extensible string of length L. Its configuration is described by r(s, t) in a 3D world frame. The internal contact force n(s) is related to string deformation through a constitutive equation involving Young's modulus (E) and cross-sectional area (A). The conservation of linear momentum yields a partial differential equation (PDE) for the string's motion.

To facilitate numerical solution and interaction with discrete elements, the PDEs are discretized using a Finite-Difference Method (FDM). The continuous domain is approximated as N+1 nodes, transforming the PDE into a system of 3(N+1) second-order ordinary differential equations. Spatial derivatives are approximated using a central differencing scheme.

- **Forces:** Each cable element is subject to gravitational and aerodynamic drag forces.
- **Boundary Conditions:**
 - **Proximal End (s=0):** Attached to the UAV, modeled as a point mass (m_b) subject to control force (f) and gravity. The interaction with the cable contact force (n_0) determines the UAV's acceleration.
 - **Distal End (s=L):** The behavior is hybrid:
 - **Free-Tip (No Payload):** The strain at the tip is null (n_N=0). This is enforced by introducing a "ghost node" (r_{N+1}) to approximate the derivative.
 - **Slung-Payload (Payload Attached):** A payload (m_p) is attached, and its dynamics, including gravity and drag, govern the boundary condition (n_N).
- **Hybrid Transitions:** These are instantaneous inelastic collisions.
 - **Attachment:** When the payload attaches, its velocity is matched to the distal node. The impact enforces conservation of linear momentum between the payload and an effective tip mass lump, resulting in a velocity reset at node N.
 - **Detachment:** The release of the payload leaves the continuous state unchanged, and the cable and payload evolve independently.

**B. POD-Based Model Order Reduction**
To reduce computational complexity, Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (POD) is applied to the FDM model. The approach involves:
1. **Reference Configuration:** A straight line segment connecting the two cable extremities (r_0 and r_N) defines a reference configuration, $\bar{r}_i$.
2. **Fluctuation Field:** The actual node positions are decomposed into this reference configuration plus a fluctuation term, $\tilde{r}_i = r_i - \bar{r}_i$. By construction, the fluctuation field has homogeneous boundary conditions ($\tilde{r}_0 = \tilde{r}_N = 0$).
3. **Snapshot Collection:** Snapshots of the fluctuating field $\tilde{r}_i$ at sampled spatial points and time instants are collected into a tensor.
4. **SVD and Mode Extraction:** Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) is performed on the snapshot tensor to extract spatial modes ($\phi_m$). These modes are orthonormal and their singular values indicate their energy contribution. The fluctuation field is then approximated as a linear combination of a finite number (R) of the most significant modes.
5. **State Space Reduction:** This projection reduces the original FDM state space (6(N+1) dimensions) to a much smaller ROM state space (6(R+2) dimensions), comprising the boundary positions/velocities and R modal coefficients/their derivatives.
6. **Modal Coherence:** Two separate POD bases ($\Phi_0$ for free-tip, $\Phi_1$ for slung-payload) are precomputed. When a hybrid transition occurs, the modal coordinates are transformed between these bases using a pseudo-inverse mapping to maintain coherence.

**C. Hybrid Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC)**
A receding-horizon NMPC scheme is formulated using the developed ROM as its internal model.
1. **System State:** The controller operates with a discrete state q (payload attached/detached) and a continuous state vector z, which aggregates boundary positions, velocities, modal coefficients, and their derivatives. The control input v is the UAV acceleration.
2. **Hybrid Automaton:** The internal model is a ROM-based hybrid automaton, where a transition function T encapsulates both the continuous reduced dynamics and the logic for mode transitions, including the velocity reset at the tip for attachment events.
3. **Optimization Problem:** The NMPC minimizes a cost function over a finite prediction horizon H. The objective penalizes tracking errors with respect to reference state ($\hat{z}$) and input ($\hat{v}$) trajectories.
4. **Cost Function:** The running and terminal costs are defined by evaluating the error in the full FDM space. This is achieved by mapping the reduced states (z, $\hat{z}$) back to the FDM space using linear projection matrices (P_q, P_$\hat{q}$) constructed from the current POD bases. This ensures consistency across mode switches. Weighting matrices (S, W) are used to tune error penalties. Soft state constraints can be included via smooth barrier terms.
5. **Solver:** Hybrid iLQR (iterative Linear Quadratic Regulator) is adopted as the solver due to its efficiency and ability to handle discrete transitions. A simplified HiLQR-RTI (real-time iteration) variant, performing a single rollout per iteration, is also considered for faster computation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents numerical experiments to validate the proposed hybrid model and control framework.

**A. ROM Training and Evaluation**
- **Training:** The POD basis was extracted from a 10-second simulation where the quadrotor followed an isotropic sinusoidal trajectory, designed to excite dominant cable modes. Snapshots were collected with a spatial decimation factor of 10 (M=10) and 50 temporal snapshots. Separate POD bases were computed for free-tip and slung-payload configurations.
- **Energy Distribution:** Analysis of singular values (Fig. 5) showed that the first two modes captured over 99% of the energy in both free-tip and slung-payload cases, with the first mode alone exceeding 95%. Mode shapes resembled distorted sinusoids (Fig. 6).
- **Computational Performance (Fig. 7):**
 - **Wall Time:** Both proposed and baseline ROMs drastically reduced integration time compared to the full FDM model. The proposed ROM was consistently faster due to fewer projection operations, growing almost linearly with the number of retained modes (R).
 - **Max Stable Step Size:** ROMs allowed integration step sizes more than an order of magnitude larger than the FDM model, significantly enhancing computational efficiency.
- **Approximation Accuracy (Fig. 8):** The accuracy of the ROMs relative to the FDM ground truth was measured by RMS functional errors for position ($\epsilon_{p}^{rms}$) and velocity ($\epsilon_{v}^{rms}$).
 - **Free-Tip (q=0):** Both ROMs showed comparable accuracy, with satisfactory performance from R=2.
 - **Slung-Payload (q=1):** The proposed ROM significantly outperformed the baseline, which was not specifically designed for payload dynamics.

**B. Real-Time Manipulation**
- **Scenario:** A pick-and-place task where the aerial system approached a distant point, attached/detached a payload, and followed a custom path. A ROM of order R=1 was used for the internal model.
- **Performance (Fig. 9a, 9b, Tab. II):**
 - The controller successfully manipulated the cable and payload along the reference trajectories with limited tracking errors at the cable tip (e.g., RMS position errors around 0.17-0.23 m and RMS velocity errors around 0.37-0.5 m/s).
 - **Solver Comparison:** HiLQR-RTI reduced the average MPC solution time to 16.4 ms (compared to 32.3 ms for full HiLQR), demonstrating its suitability for real-time operation (sampling interval 25 ms). The worst-case execution time of RTI remained below the sampling interval, preventing overruns. HiLQR-RTI generally exhibited slightly lower RMS tracking errors in these scenarios.

**C. Planned Obstacle Avoidance**
- **Scenario:** A more challenging pick-and-place task involving traversing a narrow window (0.5 m aperture), retrieving/dropping a payload, and returning, while avoiding collision with the window edges (Fig. 9c). A custom segmented planner with log-barrier homotropy was used to generate a collision-free reference trajectory. The ROM order was R=2.
- **Performance (Tab. III):**
 - The proposed strategy, with the full HiLQR solver, enabled the UAV to accomplish the task without collisions, successfully navigating through the constrained environment and managing hybrid transitions.
 - **Solver Robustness:** In this complex, highly non-convex scenario, the HiLQR-RTI variant failed to generate feasible solutions, indicating a compromise in robustness due to its simplifications. The full HiLQR, despite its higher computational cost (average wall time 42.8 ms), successfully completed the maneuver, highlighting the need for a more robust solver and custom planning for such complex tasks.
 - Whole-cable RMS tracking errors in this scenario were 0.153 m for position and 0.941 m/s for velocity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for aerial manipulation that integrates continuum cable dynamics, model order reduction, and hybrid predictive control, addressing a notable gap in existing approaches. The significance of this work lies in its potential to enable more complex and robust aerial manipulation tasks with flexible cables, which can offer advantages in safety, compliance, and energy efficiency over rigid manipulators.

The **hybrid PDE-based continuum model** provides a high-fidelity representation of the cable, capturing both its distributed deformation and the discrete events of payload attachment and detachment. This comprehensive modeling contributes to a more accurate understanding and prediction of the system's behavior, especially crucial for dynamic tasks.

The **POD-based reduced-order model (ROM)** is a key enabler for real-time control. By significantly reducing the state space (from 6(N+1) to 6(R+2) dimensions) while retaining the dominant deformation modes, the ROM achieves substantial computational savings. The method's ability to maintain modal coherence across hybrid transitions and its superior performance over a baseline ROM, particularly when a payload is attached, underscore its effectiveness and practical utility. This allows for models that are both accurate enough for critical dynamics and tractable for online computation, overcoming a fundamental trade-off in controlling flexible systems.

The **nonlinear hybrid Model Predictive Control (NMPC) scheme** leverages the ROM to achieve precise trajectory tracking and active suppression of cable oscillations. Its formulation, which maps reduced states back to the FDM space for cost evaluation, ensures consistency across hybrid mode switches. The demonstrated success in real-time manipulation tasks, including pick-and-place operations and even challenging obstacle avoidance scenarios, highlights the framework's capability to manage complex dynamic and hybrid behaviors. The evaluation of different iLQR solver variants also provides practical insights into the trade-offs between computational speed and numerical robustness for specific task complexities.

The **robustness of the framework** is demonstrated by its closed-loop performance despite the significant model-controller mismatch between the high-fidelity FDM ground truth and the coarse ROM used by the controller. This suggests that the reduction strategy adequately captures essential dynamics for control purposes.

The potential impact of this research extends to various applications requiring agile and safe aerial manipulation in complex or constrained environments. This includes industrial inspection, construction, logistics, and environmental monitoring, where flexible cables could offer versatile interaction capabilities. Future developments, such as addressing uncertainties, internal damping, self-collision, and experimental validation, are anticipated to further enhance the framework's robustness and expand its practical applicability.
