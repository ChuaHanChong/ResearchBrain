# Robot Drummer: Learning Rhythmic Skills for Humanoid Drumming

> [!summary] Summary
> This research enables a full humanoid robot to perform complex, expressive drumming by interpreting standard musical scores. The work introduces a reinforcement learning framework that translates MIDI data into a Rhythmic Contact Chain and optimizes control policies using a dense, unified reward function, achieving high-precision drumming with emergent human-like strategies.

## Problem

- Humanoid robot control largely focuses on goal-driven tasks (e.g., manipulation, locomotion) and struggles with expressive, process-driven tasks like music performance, which require precise timing and multi-limb coordination over long durations.
- Current motion imitation-based learning methods for humanoids are limited to short-horizon tasks and struggle to discover novel, temporally precise contact sequences needed for continuous musical expression.
- Robotic musicianship often employs simplified platforms or control paradigms, failing to fully leverage the complex kinematics and dynamic capabilities of a full humanoid body for intricate musical performances.

## Method

- The '**Robot Drummer**' system formulates humanoid drumming as the sequential fulfillment of timed contacts, converting MIDI scores into a '==Rhythmic Contact Chain==' (RCC) representing explicit temporal contact events.
- It employs a reinforcement learning framework using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with temporal decomposition, partitioning the RCC into shorter segments to improve sample efficiency for long-horizon songs.
- The system utilizes a dense, unified reward function that provides positive feedback for correct drum impacts and penalties for wrong strikes, missed strikes, and sticks being far from target drums.

## Results

- Specialist policies achieved high F1 scores (often above **0.9**) for drumming various rock, metal, and jazz tracks, demonstrating high-precision performance across diverse repertoires.
- Rhythmic irregularity (nPVI) and the number of distinct drums used were the primary factors negatively correlating with performance, while tempo and rhythmic diversity had minimal impact.
- Training a single generalist policy on multiple songs led to a noticeable performance degradation compared to specialist policies, indicating challenges with negative transfer for diverse rhythmic skills.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A dense, unified contact reward function, incorporating penalties for wrong and missed strikes alongside rewards for correct ones, is crucial for learning robust rhythmic accuracy.
> - Explicit one-hot encoded 'contact goals' (a lookahead sequence of target drums) in the observation space are essential for the policy to anticipate and plan future drum strikes effectively.
> - Reinforcement learning can enable humanoid robots to learn complex, human-like drumming strategies such as cross-over strikes, fast rolls, and dynamic stick assignments, without explicit programming.
