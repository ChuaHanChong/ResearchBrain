# Probabilistic Adaptation of Text-to-Video Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Video Adapter is a probabilistic method that enables efficient adaptation of large text-to-video diffusion models to specialized domains. It guides a small, task-specific video model's generation using the score function of a large pretrained model as a prior, achieving high-quality, specialized video outputs with significantly fewer trainable parameters than full finetuning.

## Problem

- Adapting large text-to-video foundation models to specialized domains is computationally intensive, requiring substantial hardware resources.
- The proprietary nature of many state-of-the-art text-to-video models restricts access to their weights, making direct finetuning impossible for external users.
- General internet-trained models often struggle to capture the specific visual statistics and dynamics of niche domains like egocentric video or robotics, leading to domain shift issues.

## Method

- The **Video Adapter** framework probabilistically combines a large, pretrained text-to-video ==diffusion== model (as a general prior) with a smaller, domain-specific video diffusion model.
- It leverages the equivalence between ==Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs)== and ==Energy-Based Models (EBMs)== to sample from a ==product distribution== by summing the score functions of both models during generation.
- A ==low-temperature sampling== strategy with a modified ==Classifier-Free Guidance== is applied to enhance the sharpness and quality of the generated videos, balancing general realism with specialized characteristics.

## Results

- Achieved high-quality, specialized video generation across various domains (e.g., robotics, egocentric, animation) using small adapter models, some with only **1.25% (0.07B)** of the pretrained model's **(5.6B)** parameters.
- Demonstrated controllable video stylization, generating videos in diverse artistic styles (e.g., 'Digital Art', 'Scifi') while maintaining core animated or genre-specific qualities.
- Quantitatively improved Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) and Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) on Bridge Data (**FVD 148.1**, **FID 29.5**) and Ego4D (**FVD 52.5**, **IS 3.53**), outperforming standalone small models and direct finetuning for the same computational budget.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The denoising function of a diffusion model can be interpreted as an estimator of an EBM's score function, allowing for the probabilistic composition of multiple diffusion models by summing their score functions.
> - Parameter-efficient adaptation for text-to-video models can be achieved by training only a small, domain-specific model and guiding its generation with the score function of a large, fixed pretrained model.
> - Combining general knowledge from a large model with domain-specific learning from a small model via a product distribution yields superior results in specialized tasks than either model alone or direct finetuning under equivalent budgets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge within the rapidly evolving field of generative artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on text-to-video synthesis. Recent advancements have led to the development of large text-to-video models, often with billions of parameters, capable of generating high-fidelity videos from arbitrary text descriptions after training on internet-scale datasets. These models, similar to their text-to-image counterparts, have demonstrated capabilities in creating realistic and semantically consistent visual content.

However, the paper identifies a significant bottleneck in applying these foundation models to specialized domains or tasks, such as generating animated videos, egocentric human motion sequences, or videos of robotic operations. Direct adaptation of these large models through finetuning presents several difficulties:
- **Computational Expense:** Finetuning models with billions of parameters is computationally intensive and requires substantial hardware resources, often inaccessible to smaller academic or industrial institutions.
- **Access Restrictions:** The weights of many state-of-the-art text-to-video models are often proprietary and not publicly available, limiting the feasibility of direct finetuning to commercial API calls, which remain costly.
- **Domain Shift:** While large models capture broad video characteristics, their pretraining data may not adequately represent the specific visual statistics or dynamics of specialized domains (e.g., the unique perspectives in egocentric video or the constrained movements in robotics).

The authors draw inspiration from the progression of adaptation techniques in large language models (LLMs). As LLMs became more capable and expensive, the field moved from full finetuning to more parameter-efficient methods like few-shot in-context learning, prompting, prefix tuning, and LoRA. These methods allow adaptation without modifying the bulk of the pretrained model's weights. The paper posits that a similar paradigm shift is required for text-to-video models, as existing text-to-image adaptation techniques (e.g., ControlNet, textual inversion, DreamBooth) may not directly translate to the increased complexity and scale of video generation.

Furthermore, this work relates to the broader area of compositional generative models, where multiple generative models are probabilistically combined to achieve specific generation goals. By framing the problem as combining a broad pretrained prior with a task-specific component, the paper contributes to methodologies for leveraging general knowledge in specialized generative tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop an efficient and effective method for adapting large, pretrained text-to-video diffusion models to diverse downstream domains and tasks without requiring expensive finetuning of the entire pretrained model.

The motivation for this objective stems from several challenges and observations in the current landscape of text-to-video generation:

1. **Addressing Computational and Accessibility Barriers:** Large text-to-video models are computationally demanding, making it impractical for many researchers and smaller organizations to finetune them for specific applications. The proprietary nature of many such models further restricts access to their internal weights, rendering finetuning impossible for external users. The research aims to democratize access to advanced text-to-video capabilities by proposing an adaptation method that circumvents these issues.

2. **Bridging the Gap Between General and Specialized Video Generation:** While pretrained models excel at generating high-fidelity, general-purpose videos, they often struggle with the specific styles, motions, or characteristics of niche domains like animation, egocentric video, or robotics. For example, a general model might not capture the specific motion dynamics of a robot arm or the unique camera perspective of egocentric footage. The motivation is to enable generation that is both high-fidelity (from the pretrained model) and specialized (from domain-specific data).

3. **Leveraging Existing Knowledge Efficiently:** The large pretrained models contain a vast repository of knowledge about video properties, such as temporal consistency, object permanence, and general semantics, acquired from internet-scale data. The researchers are motivated by the idea that this "broad knowledge" should be preserved and leveraged, rather than discarded or re-learned, when adapting to new tasks. The challenge is to integrate this knowledge with new domain-specific information without incurring the cost of full model finetuning.

4. **Inspiration from Language Model Adaptation:** The success of parameter-efficient adaptation techniques in large language models, which allow adaptation to new tasks with small, trainable components or prompt engineering, serves as a strong motivation. The authors aim to explore if a similar principle of using a small, controllable component can be effectively applied to the more complex domain of text-to-video generation.

To achieve these objectives, the paper proposes **Video Adapter**, a probabilistic approach designed to guide the generation of a task-specific small video model using the score function of a large pretrained video diffusion model as a prior. This framework seeks to synthesize videos that adhere to both the general realism of the pretrained model and the distinct characteristics of the target domain.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of Video Adapter lies in probabilistically combining the knowledge of a large, pretrained text-to-video diffusion model with a smaller, task-specific video model. This is achieved by leveraging the equivalence between Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs) and Energy-Based Models (EBMs).

**Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs):**
The paper first reviews DDPMs as generative models that create videos through an iterative denoising process. Given a video $\tau$ and a sequence of noise levels, a denoising model $\epsilon_{\theta}$ is trained to predict the noise added to a corrupted video at each step. Video generation then proceeds by iteratively applying this learned denoising function to a pure Gaussian noise sample, gradually transforming it into a clean video.

**Energy-Based Models (EBMs) View of DDPMs:**
A key conceptual bridge is established by connecting DDPMs to EBMs. The denoising function $\epsilon_{\theta}(\tau_t, t)$ of a diffusion model is shown to be an estimator of the score function, which is the gradient of the log-probability of an underlying, unnormalized EBM distribution $p_{\theta}(\tau) \propto e^{-E_{\theta}(\tau)}$. Consequently, the sampling procedure in a diffusion model is analogous to Langevin sampling on an EBM. This equivalence is critical because it implies that the product of two diffusion model distributions, $p_1(\tau)p_2(\tau)$, can be sampled by summing their respective score functions during the Langevin sampling process: $\tau_{t-1} = \alpha_t(\tau_t - \gamma(\epsilon_1(\tau_t, t) + \epsilon_2(\tau_t, t)) + \xi)$.

**Probabilistic Adaptation with Video Adapter:**
1. **Pretrained Model as a Prior:** The large text-to-video model, pretrained on extensive internet data, is considered a powerful prior $p_{\text{pretrained}}(\tau|\text{text})$. This prior encapsulates general video properties such as temporal consistency, object permanence, and semantic coherence.
2. **Domain-Specific Small Model:** A separate, much smaller video diffusion model, $p_{\theta}(\tau|\text{text})$, is trained specifically on the limited, domain-specific dataset $D_{\text{Adapt}}$. This small model learns the unique characteristics and styles of the target domain (e.g., robotics, animation). Due to its size and limited training data, this small model alone might generate unrealistic or lower-quality videos.
3. **Product Distribution:** Video Adapter constructs a product distribution $p_{\text{product}}(\tau|\text{text}) \propto p_{\text{pretrained}}(\tau|\text{text}) p_{\theta}(\tau|\text{text})$. This distribution aims to combine the broad knowledge of the pretrained model with the specific style of the small model. By multiplying the probabilities, the product distribution effectively downweights videos that are unlikely under either model, thereby leveraging the pretrained model to improve the realism and quality of the small model's specialized outputs.
4. **Implementation via Score Function Composition:** Based on the EBM interpretation, sampling from this product distribution involves summing the score functions of both models. During the iterative denoising process, the combined denoising prediction becomes $\epsilon(\tau_t, t|\text{text}) = \epsilon_{\theta}(\tau_t, t|\text{text}) + \gamma \epsilon_{\text{pretrained}}(\tau_t, t|\text{text})$. Here, $\gamma$ is an inverse temperature scalar that controls the influence or strength of the pretrained prior.

**Low Temperature Sampling with Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG):**
To generate sharper and higher-quality videos, diffusion models commonly employ Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG), which implicitly upweights the text-conditional probability. The paper adapts CFG for its compositional approach. Instead of directly using a CFG-enabled pretrained prior (which might have too few high-probability modes), Video Adapter first forms the product distribution $p_{\text{product}}(\tau|\text{text})$. Then, it uses the density ratio of this *modified text-conditioned distribution* with the *unconditional video density* $p_{\theta}(\tau)$ (learned by the small model) to construct an implicit classifier. This leads to a modified denoising function for sampling:
$\tilde{\epsilon}_{\theta}(\tau, t|\text{text}) = \epsilon_{\theta}(\tau, t) + \alpha(\epsilon_{\theta}(\tau, t|\text{text}) + \gamma \epsilon_{\text{pretrained}}(\tau, t|\text{text}) - \epsilon_{\theta}(\tau, t))$, where $\alpha$ is the CFG guidance scale. Algorithm 1 in the paper outlines this complete sampling procedure.

**Experimental Setup:**
The framework was evaluated using a 5.6-billion-parameter base model pretrained on generic internet videos as the large prior. Task-specific small models were significantly downscaled, ranging from 1.25% to 50% of the pretrained model's parameters. These small models were trained on datasets such as Ego4D (egocentric videos), Bridge Data (robotic videos), and custom datasets for animation/sci-fi stylization. Conditioning was either on the first frame of a video or on Sobel edges. A T5-XXL model (4.6B parameters) was used for text embeddings across all models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate the effectiveness of Video Adapter across diverse video generation tasks, highlighting its efficiency, quality, and adaptability.

#### 4.1. Efficient Parameter Usage

Video Adapter successfully incorporated the broad knowledge of a large pretrained model into a task-specific small model using significantly fewer parameters. In some cases, the small model comprised only 1.25% (0.07B parameters) of the pretrained model's parameters (5.6B parameters). The approach requires training only the small model, while the large model serves as an inference-time prior, without any finetuning.

#### 4.2. Controllable Video Synthesis (Stylization)

- **Stylizing Video Generation:** Video Adapter demonstrated the ability to adapt the styles of videos. For instance, a small model trained on an "animation" style could generate videos in diverse alternative styles (e.g., "Digital Art," "Outdoor Video," "Storybook Illustration") while consistently maintaining the core animated quality (Figure 4). Similarly, a "Scifi" style model could be adapted to generate videos in other genre-specific styles (e.g., "Arcade Style," "Science Fiction," "Blue Sunny Day") (Figure 5).
- **Instance-Specific Stylization:** The method could maintain the unique style of a particular artist (e.g., "Case Closed" animation) while allowing changes to elements like the background (Figure 3). This indicates fine-grained control over stylized video generation.
- **Analysis of Adaptation Weight:** An ablation study (Figure 6) revealed that Video Adapter maintained the adapter's style more accurately as the weight on the pretrained prior was increased. In contrast, directly interpolating classifier-free scores between the pretrained and adapter models often resulted in incoherent images or caused the generation to collapse to the style of the teacher model.

#### 4.3. High-Quality Efficient Video Modeling

- **Datasets:** Experiments were conducted on specialized datasets: Bridge Data (robotic manipulation videos, 7.2k pairs) and Ego4D (egocentric human activity videos, 97k pairs), which are out-of-distribution for general internet-trained models.
- **Quantitative Performance (Table 1):**
 - **Bridge Data:** A small model (0.07B parameters, 1.25% of pretrained) using Video Adapter achieved better Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) and Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) metrics (FVD 177.4, FID 37.6) than the pretrained model alone (FVD 350.1, FID 42.6) and even the standalone small model (FVD 186.8, FID 38.8). With a slightly larger small model (0.14B parameters), Video Adapter further improved performance to FVD 148.1 and FID 29.5.
 - **Ego4D Data:** For the more complex Ego4D dataset, the smallest model alone (0.07B parameters) performed worse than the pretrained model. However, Video Adapter significantly improved its performance (FVD 156.3, IS 2.82) compared to the standalone small model (FVD 228.3, IS 2.28) and even outperformed the pretrained model in terms of Inception Score. With a larger small model (2.8B parameters), Video Adapter achieved the best metrics (FVD 52.5, IS 3.53).
- **Qualitative Performance (Figure 7, Figure 8):**
 - **Bridge Data:** The pretrained model generated high-quality but often task-irrelevant videos (e.g., lacking robot arms or specific actions). The standalone small model produced low-quality videos with unrealistic robot movements. Video Adapter successfully generated high-quality videos that accurately depicted the described robotic tasks with realistic motions.
 - **Ego4D Data:** The pretrained model generated high-quality but static videos, lacking the characteristic egocentric motion. The standalone small model captured egocentric motion but produced low-quality results. Video Adapter combined these strengths, generating high-quality videos that effectively captured egocentric movements.
- **Ablation (Table 9):** Further analysis on Bridge Data showed that Video Adapter improved the generative modeling performance (FVD, FID) of the underlying small model, whereas directly mixing classifier-free scores (CFG Mix) deteriorated performance.

#### 4.4. Sim-to-Real Video Augmentation

- **Application:** Video Adapter was applied to the challenge of sim-to-real transfer in robotics. Small edge-to-real and edge-to-sim models were trained on the LanguageTable dataset.
- **Results (Figure 10):** The method successfully generated paired simulated and real robotic videos that completed tasks based on language prompts. Moreover, it enabled the generation of real robotic videos with various data augmentation styles (e.g., "colorful," "dark"). This capability is posited to be valuable for training robust robotic policies via techniques like domain randomization, improving generalization from simulation to the real world.

#### 4.5. Comparison to Finetuning (Appendix C, Table 3)

A computational budget comparison demonstrated that Video Adapter achieved superior FVD and FID on Bridge data compared to directly finetuning the 5.6B pretrained model for an equivalent amount of TPU time. This underscores the computational efficiency benefits of the proposed method.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Video Adapter holds several significant implications for the field of text-to-video generation and its practical applications:

1. **Increased Accessibility and Democratization of Research:** By proposing an adaptation method that circumvents the need for finetuning multi-billion parameter models, Video Adapter significantly lowers the computational and resource barriers to entry. This makes advanced text-to-video research and development more accessible to a broader range of academic institutions, small industrial labs, and individual researchers who may lack access to vast computational resources or proprietary model weights. This accessibility can foster innovation and accelerate progress in the field.

2. **Enhanced Efficiency and Resource Utilization:** The approach leverages a small, task-specific model (as little as 1.25% of the pretrained model's parameters) while effectively utilizing the pre-existing, broad knowledge of a large foundation model. This efficient use of parameters avoids the massive computational cost and time associated with training or finetuning very large models for every new domain or task. The demonstrated quantitative improvements over direct finetuning under equivalent computational budgets highlight this efficiency.

3. **High-Quality and Specialized Video Generation:** Video Adapter enables the generation of videos that simultaneously benefit from the high fidelity, temporal consistency, and semantic understanding of a large pretrained model, as well as the specialized characteristics (style, dynamics, egocentric perspective, robotic movements) learned by a smaller, domain-specific model. This capability can unlock new applications where both realism and specificity are crucial.

4. **Novel Applications Across Domains:**
 - **Stylization and Content Creation:** The ability to adapt models to specific animation styles or genres (e.g., sci-fi, digital art) provides powerful tools for content creators, enabling rapid prototyping and generation of stylized video assets.
 - **Robotics:** The application to sim-to-real transfer demonstrates a potential impact on robotic learning. By generating realistic robotic videos from simulation and augmenting real-world robotic videos with various styles, Video Adapter could improve the generalization of policies from simulated environments to physical robots, addressing a key challenge in reinforcement learning.
 - **Egocentric Vision:** The generation of high-quality egocentric videos has implications for augmented reality, wearable computing, and human-computer interaction research, allowing for more realistic data synthesis and scenario planning.

5. **Conceptual Framework for Knowledge Transfer:** The probabilistic composition of score functions from different diffusion models provides a distinct and effective mechanism for transferring and integrating knowledge. This method, inspired by adaptation techniques in large language models, offers an alternative to traditional finetuning and simple model interpolation, advancing the understanding of how to efficiently leverage pretrained generative models.

**Limitations (Acknowledged by the Authors):**
While offering significant advantages, the authors acknowledge certain limitations:
- **Not Entirely Training-Free:** Video Adapter still requires training a small domain-specific model, distinguishing it from purely training-free methods like in-context learning or prompt engineering.
- **API Compatibility:** The method requires access to the score function of the pretrained model, which is not a standard output for all existing text-to-image or text-to-video APIs. This could pose a barrier for commercial applications reliant solely on black-box APIs.

Despite these limitations, Video Adapter presents a robust framework for efficient and high-quality adaptation of large text-to-video models, broadly impacting the accessibility and applicability of advanced generative AI.
