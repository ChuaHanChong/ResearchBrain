# TrackVLA++: Unleashing Reasoning and Memory Capabilities in VLA Models for Embodied Visual Tracking

> [!summary] Summary
> TrackVLA++ advances embodied visual tracking by integrating an efficient spatial reasoning mechanism and a robust, confidence-gated long-term memory into Vision-Language-Action models. It achieves state-of-the-art performance on multiple simulation benchmarks and demonstrates improved real-world tracking robustness against occlusions and distractors.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for Embodied Visual Tracking (EVT) often lack explicit spatial reasoning and robust long-horizon memory for target identification.
- Current models struggle with maintaining target identity during severe occlusions and differentiating the target from visually similar distractors in complex environments.
- Traditional Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning methods in embodied AI generate computationally intensive intermediate representations, hindering real-time performance for dynamic tasks like EVT.

## Method

![[2510.07134-method.png]]
*The TrackVLA++ architecture integrates visual tokens from multi-camera inputs, language instructions, and two key innovations: Polar-CoT reasoning tokens and Target Identification Memory (TIM). The system generates action tokens that are decoded into tracking trajectories.*

- Introduces the ==Polar Chain==-of-Thought (Polar-CoT) mechanism, an agent-centric, lightweight spatial reasoning approach that discretizes the robot's field-of-view into angle-distance sectors and encodes them as compact vocabulary tokens.
- Develops the Target Identification Memory (TIM) module, a confidence-gated system that maintains a stable target identity representation over long horizons, freezing its state during occlusions and updating robustly upon confident re-detections.
- Integrates these modules into an end-to-end VLA framework built on a Large Language Model (Qwen2-7B), processing multi-view visual history, language instructions, and reasoning tokens to predict continuous tracking trajectories.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art success rates on the EVT-Bench dataset, with a notable improvement in Distracted Tracking (DT) to **74.0%** SR (four views), surpassing NavFoM's **62.0%** while using less training data.
- Demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization to unseen high-fidelity environments (Gym-UnrealCV) and improved human recognition accuracy on SYNTH-PEDES to **87.5%**, outperforming strong VLM and VLA baselines.
- Exhibited robust real-world performance on a Unitree GO2 quadruped robot, improving success rates by **7%** to **17%** across challenging scenarios (Obstacle, Winding Path, Distractor) compared to its predecessor, TrackVLA.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Efficient, agent-centric spatial reasoning (Polar-CoT) can be integrated into VLA models without significant computational overhead, enabling robust relative target position inference and signaling occlusions with an `` token.
> - A confidence-gated memory update mechanism (TIM) is crucial for maintaining long-horizon target identity, preventing memory corruption during uncertainty and enabling successful re-identification after occlusions.
> - Combining these specialized reasoning and memory components allows a VLA model to achieve superior performance with significantly less training data compared to models relying on sheer data scale.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied Visual Tracking (EVT) stands as a foundational and intensely challenging task within the field of embodied AI. It underpins numerous practical applications, from companion robots and guidance systems to service assistants, all requiring an agent to continuously perceive and follow a moving target within dynamic physical environments. The broader research landscape of EVT has seen a significant evolution, moving from decoupled systems to more integrated, end-to-end paradigms.

Initially, EVT approaches typically involved a decoupled pipeline: sophisticated Visual Foundation Models (VFMs) like Segment Anything (SAM) or Grounding DINO handled visual perception and target identification, while reinforcement learning or other policy learning techniques were employed for trajectory planning and action generation. While effective, this decoupled nature often led to information loss and error propagation between modules.

More recently, the field has gravitated towards end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which leverage the powerful generalization capabilities of pre-trained Visual-Language Models (VLMs) and Large Language Models (LLMs). This new paradigm allows for language-guided tracking in complex and unstructured scenes, offering greater flexibility and human-like interaction. Pioneering works like TrackVLA and LOVON have exemplified this trend. TrackVLA introduced a unified VLA framework that processes visual-language inputs to generate tracking trajectories through an anchor-based diffusion policy, showcasing strong sim-to-real generalization and real-time performance. LOVON, in contrast, adopted a hierarchical approach, using an LLM for high-level planning to decompose instructions into sub-tasks, which are then executed by a low-level motion model.

Despite these advancements, a critical limitation in current state-of-the-art VLA models for EVT is the lack of explicit spatial reasoning and robust temporal memory. This deficiency becomes particularly apparent in challenging scenarios involving severe occlusions (where the target temporarily disappears) or the presence of visually similar distractors (leading to target ambiguity). These models often struggle to maintain target identity over long horizons or to accurately infer relative target positions when visual cues are ambiguous or absent.

Furthermore, the integration of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, popularized in LLMs for step-by-step problem-solving, has recently been explored in embodied AI, particularly for robotic manipulation and navigation. However, existing CoT mechanisms in robotics often generate computationally intensive intermediate representations, such as verbose textual plans, object bounding boxes, or subgoal images. While effective for manipulation tasks, these approaches introduce significant inference overhead, rendering them impractical for the highly dynamic and real-time demands of EVT.

TrackVLA++ directly addresses these identified gaps in the research landscape. It aims to overcome the limitations of its predecessors (like TrackVLA) by enhancing the VLA framework with explicit, yet *efficient*, spatial reasoning and *robust* long-horizon memory. By refining the CoT paradigm into a compact, agent-centric design and introducing a confidence-aware memory update mechanism, TrackVLA++ advances the state of embodied visual tracking, making it more reliable and intelligent in cluttered and dynamic real-world environments. This work positions itself as a significant step towards more truly autonomous and adaptable embodied AI agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of TrackVLA++ is to significantly enhance the capabilities of Embodied Visual Tracking (EVT) models, moving beyond the current limitations of state-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) approaches. The authors are motivated by the need for more robust, intelligent, and real-world applicable embodied agents capable of reliably tracking moving targets in complex, dynamic, and unstructured environments.

Specifically, the key objectives and motivations are:

1. **Address Limitations in Existing VLA Models for EVT:** Current VLA models, including pioneering works like TrackVLA and LOVON, often lack explicit spatial reasoning abilities and effective mechanisms for long-horizon target identification. This leads to performance degradation and failures in critical scenarios.
2. **Overcome Challenges of Occlusion:** A major hurdle in EVT is maintaining target identity when the target is temporarily occluded or disappears from the agent's field of view. Existing models struggle to "remember" the target and confidently re-identify it upon reappearance. TrackVLA++ aims to provide a robust solution for target persistence during prolonged occlusions.
3. **Mitigate Distractor Interference:** In crowded or complex scenes, visually similar distractors can easily confuse tracking agents, causing them to lose the original target. The motivation is to enable the agent to accurately differentiate the target from distractors and maintain focus on the intended individual.
4. **Incorporate Explicit Spatial Reasoning Efficiently:** While Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning has proven valuable for complex tasks, its application in embodied AI, particularly for dynamic robotics, often involves computationally expensive intermediate representations (e.g., bounding boxes, textual plans). A key objective is to introduce an *efficient* spatial reasoning mechanism that provides crucial information (like relative target position) without incurring significant inference overhead, making it suitable for real-time EVT.
5. **Develop Robust Long-Horizon Memory:** The goal is to create a memory system that can reliably preserve the target's visual identity over extended periods, adaptively updating when new, confident observations are available, and intelligently "freezing" when the target is uncertain or absent. This ensures spatiotemporal consistency throughout long tracking episodes.
6. **Enhance Generalization and Real-World Robustness:** Ultimately, the motivation is to develop a VLA model that not only performs well on simulation benchmarks but also exhibits strong zero-shot generalization capabilities and robust performance in diverse, unstructured real-world scenarios, thereby bridging the sim-to-real gap more effectively.
7. **Improve Data Efficiency:** Despite some baselines being trained on massive datasets (e.g., NavFoM on 10 million trajectories), TrackVLA++ aims to achieve superior performance with significantly less data, highlighting the effectiveness of its architectural innovations over sheer data scale.

By achieving these objectives, TrackVLA++ seeks to push the boundaries of embodied visual tracking, paving the way for more reliable, intelligent, and autonomous robots in everyday applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TrackVLA++ is presented as a novel end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model designed to enhance embodied visual tracking by integrating explicit spatial reasoning and robust long-horizon memory. The model builds upon the foundation of NavFoM, a navigation foundation model, and introduces two core architectural improvements: the Polar Chain-of-Thought (Polar-CoT) mechanism and the Target Identification Memory (TIM) module.

**Architectural Overview:**
The system takes a video stream from multiple cameras and a language instruction describing the target. It processes these inputs to predict a continuous tracking trajectory.
1. **Observation Encoding:** A dual-encoder architecture (SigLIP and DINOv2) extracts visual features from historical and current RGB observations. To manage computational load for long-horizon inputs, a grid pooling strategy is applied, generating a multi-resolution representation: high-resolution features for current details and low-resolution features for coarse historical context.
2. **Memory Management:** A dual-memory approach is used. Short-term memory utilizes a sliding window of `k=32` frames (similar to TrackVLA). Long-term memory is handled by the fixed-size Target Identification Memory (TIM). Both short-term visual sequence features and long-horizon TIM features are projected into the LLM's latent space using a 2-layer MLP projector.
3. **Language Model Core:** The backbone of TrackVLA++ is a large language model (LLM), specifically Qwen2-7B. This LLM processes the concatenated visual, memory, language, and reasoning tokens to predict the final action token.

**Core Contributions – The Two Key Modules:**

A. **Polar Chain-of-Thought (Polar-CoT) Mechanism:**
 - **Agent-centric Reasoning:** Unlike traditional CoT methods that might predict object bounding boxes, which can be computationally intensive and ambiguous in multi-camera setups, Polar-CoT adopts a lightweight, agent-centric design based on polar coordinates.
 - **Discretized Spatial Representation:** The agent's perceivable annular field-of-view (between 0.6m and 5.0m) is discretized into a structured grid of sectors. Each sector represents a unique combination of relative angle (θ) and distance (d) to the target.
 - **Compact Reasoning Token:** This discrete angle-distance combination is encoded as a single, compact vocabulary token. An additional `<invalid>` token is introduced to explicitly signal when the target is occluded or outside the agent's FoV, enhancing robustness.
 - **Process:** Projected visual embeddings, long-term memory embeddings, and language tokens are fed into the LLM, which then generates this reasoning token (E_CoT).

B. **Target Identification Memory (TIM) Module:**
 - **Confidence-Gated Update:** TIM is designed to maintain a stable target identity representation over long horizons, particularly during occlusions or in the presence of distractors. It employs a confidence-aware gating mechanism for memory updates.
 - **Weighted Average Update:** The TIM state M_TIM at timestep T is updated from its previous state M_TIM_T-1 via a weighted average with a new candidate feature (f_T-1). This candidate feature is the visual embedding extracted from the region predicted by the Polar-CoT at the previous timestep.
 - **Confidence Score (C_T-1):** The weight of the update (w_T) is modulated by a confidence score, C_T-1, which quantifies the certainty of the Polar-CoT prediction. This score is derived from the normalized entropy of the Polar-CoT token's logits (1 - H(softmax(P))/log K). A high confidence (close to 1) allows integration of new features; low confidence preserves the previous state.
 - **Robustness to Occlusion:** Crucially, if Polar-CoT predicts an `<invalid>` token, its confidence C_t is forced to zero. This effectively freezes the TIM, preserving the last reliable target representation until the target is confidently re-detected, preventing memory corruption by spurious observations during occlusions.
 - **Initialization:** TIM is initialized to a null state and adopts the first confidently detected target feature as its initial state.

**Action Prediction:**
After the Polar-CoT generates the reasoning token and the TIM is updated, these, along with the visual and language tokens, form the comprehensive input sequence for the LLM. The LLM then predicts an action token (E_pred), which is subsequently decoded by an MLP-based action head into a sequence of waypoints (W_T) defining the tracking trajectory.

**Training Objective:**
The model is trained with a combined loss function:
- **Trajectory Planning Loss (L_traj):** Mean Squared Error (MSE) between predicted waypoints and ground truth waypoints.
- **Reasoning Loss (L_reason):** Log-likelihood of the Polar-CoT token, conditioning on the combined inputs.
- **Vanilla Text Prediction Loss (L_text):** Included to align with standard VLM training practices for open-world recognition.
These losses are weighted to balance their contributions.

**Dataset Construction:**
A large-scale dataset (1 million samples) was collected from the EVT-Bench training split in the Habitat 3.0 simulator. Each sample included multi-view RGB history, target description, expert trajectory, and crucially, Polar-CoT annotations (relative angle, distance, and an `<invalid>` flag for occluded/distant targets). Randomization of camera parameters and views was applied to enhance generalization. To boost open-world recognition, the model was co-trained with an additional 1 million QA samples (person identification, image/video QA) in a 1:1 ratio.

**Deployment:**
TrackVLA++ was deployed on a Unitree GO2 quadruped robot equipped with four SG3S11AFxK cameras, demonstrating its practical applicability in real-world scenarios. The video stream is processed on a remote server, with generated Polar-CoT tokens and trajectories sent back to the robot.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

TrackVLA++ demonstrates significant advancements in embodied visual tracking, establishing new state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance across multiple benchmarks and exhibiting robust generalization in real-world scenarios.

**A. State-of-the-Art Performance on Simulation Benchmarks:**

- **EVT-Bench:** TrackVLA++ achieved SOTA results across all three sub-tasks: Single-Target Tracking (STT), Distracted Tracking (DT), and Ambiguity Tracking (AT), in both egocentric (single-view) and multi-camera (four-view) settings (Table I).
 - **Distracted Tracking (DT) Gains:** This is the most challenging category due to similar-looking distractors. TrackVLA++ improved the Success Rate (SR) to 74.0% with four views, a substantial leap from the previous leading method NavFoM (62.0%). For single-view, it reached 66.5% SR, surpassing NavFoM's 61.4%. This highlights its enhanced robustness against distractors.
 - **Efficiency:** Despite NavFoM being trained on 10 million trajectories, TrackVLA++ achieved superior performance with only 2 million total training samples, underscoring its data efficiency.
- **Gym-UnrealCV (Zero-shot Performance):** TrackVLA++ showed remarkable zero-shot generalization to unseen, high-fidelity environments (Table II).
 - Achieved perfect scores (Episode Length (EL) = 500, Success Rate (SR) = 1.00) in "Single Target" and "Unseen Objects" tasks, matching TrackVLA.
 - Crucially, in the "Distractor" task, it outperformed TrackVLA with higher SR and longer EL, further validating its ability to handle visually similar decoys in novel environments.

**B. Superior Visual Recognition Ability:**

- On a zero-shot human recognition task using the SYNTH-PEDES dataset, TrackVLA++ achieved a SOTA accuracy of 87.5% (Table III). This surpassed strong VLM and VLA baselines, including SoM + GPT-4o (82.4%), TrackVLA (80.7%), and NavFoM (84.0%), confirming its fine-grained recognition capabilities.
- The Polar-CoT module specifically contributed to a notable improvement in recognition accuracy (87.5% with Polar-CoT vs. 83.0% without), demonstrating its effectiveness in enhancing the model's perception.

**C. Computational Efficiency:**

- TrackVLA++ maintained an inference speed of 4.8 FPS (Table III), which is comparable to NavFoM (5.1 FPS) and significantly faster (approximately 48x) than GPT-based baselines like SoM + GPT-4o (0.1 FPS). This balance of accuracy and speed is crucial for real-time embodied control.
- While the Polar-CoT module introduced a slight reduction in speed (4.8 FPS vs. 5.2 FPS without), the corresponding gain in recognition accuracy justifies this trade-off.

**D. Robust Real-World Performance:**

- Evaluated in three challenging real-world scenarios (Obstacle, Winding Path, and Distractor) with a Unitree GO2 quadruped robot (Fig. 5), TrackVLA++ substantially outperformed its predecessor, TrackVLA.
- It showed improvements of 14% (Obstacle), 7% (Winding Path), and 17% (Distractor) in success rate. These results highlight TrackVLA++'s enhanced robustness to temporary occlusions, complex target movements, and interference from other humans in dynamic real-world environments.

**E. Ablation Study:**

- An ablation study on the EVT-Bench DT split (four views) confirmed the individual contributions of the proposed modules (Table IV).
 - The **Polar-CoT** module improved the Success Rate (SR) by 6.0%.
 - The **Target Identification Memory (TIM)** module (with 4 tokens) contributed an additional 2.8% to the SR, showcasing the complementary benefits of both components.
- Interestingly, increasing the number of TIM tokens from 4 to 16 yielded no significant performance improvement, suggesting that a concise representation (4 tokens) is sufficient for robust tracking, optimizing computational overhead.

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate that TrackVLA++ addresses the core limitations of prior EVT models by integrating efficient spatial reasoning and robust target memory, leading to state-of-the-art performance across diverse benchmarks and challenging real-world applications.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TrackVLA++ represents a significant advancement in the field of Embodied Visual Tracking (EVT) and, more broadly, in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for embodied AI. Its significance and potential impact stem from its ability to address critical shortcomings in existing approaches, paving the way for more capable, autonomous, and reliable robotic systems.

1. **Elevating Embodied AI Capabilities:** By explicitly integrating spatial reasoning via Polar-CoT and long-horizon target memory via TIM, TrackVLA++ moves beyond purely reactive tracking. This enables robots to exhibit a more sophisticated understanding of their environment and targets, allowing for proactive decision-making and robust behavior in complex, dynamic, and unstructured real-world scenarios. This is crucial for bridging the gap between simulated performance and practical deployment.

2. **Enhanced Robustness for Real-World Applications:** The ability of TrackVLA++ to handle severe occlusions and visually similar distractors is a game-changer for practical EVT applications. Robots deployed in human environments (e.g., hospitals, homes, public spaces) constantly face these challenges. Companion robots, guidance systems, and service assistants can now maintain their task fidelity even when targets temporarily disappear behind obstacles or blend into crowds, greatly increasing their utility and trustworthiness.

3. **Efficient and Scalable Reasoning:** The introduction of Polar-CoT offers a novel and efficient approach to Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning for dynamic embodied tasks. By providing compact, agent-centric spatial priors rather than computationally heavy intermediate representations, TrackVLA++ demonstrates that sophisticated reasoning can be integrated into VLA models without sacrificing real-time inference speed. This efficiency is critical for robotic systems that require immediate responses, making advanced reasoning practical for time-sensitive applications.

4. **Reliable Long-Term Target Identification:** The confidence-gated Target Identification Memory (TIM) module is a key innovation for persistent tracking. By intelligently preserving the last confident target representation during periods of uncertainty or occlusion, TIM prevents memory corruption and enables successful target re-identification. This ensures spatiotemporal consistency over long tracking episodes, a fundamental requirement for sustained human-robot interaction or operational tasks.

5. **Strong Generalization and Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The demonstrated zero-shot generalization capabilities on unseen simulation environments and superior performance in real-world experiments are highly significant. This suggests that the learned reasoning and memory mechanisms are generalizable across different visual domains and physical realities, reducing the extensive fine-tuning typically required for real-world deployment and accelerating the development cycle for new robotic applications.

6. **Inspiration for Future VLA Model Development:** TrackVLA++ highlights the importance of incorporating explicit reasoning and memory architectures within VLA frameworks. Its success will likely inspire further research into developing specialized, efficient modules that extend the core capabilities of large foundation models, moving beyond monolithic architectures to more modular and interpretable designs. This could lead to a new generation of VLA models that are not only powerful but also more robust, efficient, and better suited for the nuances of embodied intelligence.

In conclusion, TrackVLA++ represents a pivotal step towards building truly intelligent and reliable embodied AI agents. By addressing fundamental limitations in spatial reasoning and long-term memory, it enhances the robustness, efficiency, and real-world applicability of embodied visual tracking, with profound implications for the future of robotics and human-robot collaboration.
