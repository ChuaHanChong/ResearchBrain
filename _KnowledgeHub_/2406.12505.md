# Demonstrating Agile Flight from Pixels without State Estimation

> [!summary] Summary
> This work by the Robotics and Perception Group (RPG) at the University of Zurich demonstrates agile quadrotor flight purely from a first-person-view video stream, directly mapping raw pixel observations to low-level control commands without explicit state estimation. Policies trained entirely in simulation achieved zero-shot transfer to the real world, navigating through a sequence of gates at speeds up to 40 km/h with 100% success rate on a Figure-8 track.

## Problem

- Achieving robust and agile autonomous drone flight historically relied heavily on explicit state estimation (e.g., VIO/SLAM), which requires powerful onboard computing and complex sensor fusion.
- Traditional state estimation pipelines are susceptible to failures in challenging perception environments, like aggressive maneuvers causing motion blur, leading to crashes.
- Human drone pilots achieve exceptional agility by relying solely on FPV video streams, bypassing internal state estimates, a capability autonomous systems lacked.

## Method

- Training an end-to-end vision-based policy using ==reinforcement learning== (PPO) to directly map 84x84 pixel-level gate segmentation masks to low-level collective thrust and body rate commands.
- Employing an asymmetric actor-critic architecture where the critic has access to privileged simulation state information during training to guide the actor, which only sees visual input.
- Exclusively training policies in simulation with efficient visual observation generation (inner gate edges) and robust domain randomization to enable zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- Demonstrated the first vision-based quadrotor system capable of autonomous navigation through gates at speeds up to 40 km/h and 2g accelerations, directly from pixels to low-level controls, without explicit state estimation.
- Achieved zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, with policies trained solely in simulation performing successfully on real hardware without fine-tuning.
- Achieved a **100%** success rate over multiple runs on a Figure-8 track in real-world flights, with performance comparable to state-based policies in terms of lap times and agility.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Agile quadrotor flight directly from pixels without explicit state estimation is achievable, mirroring human pilot capabilities.
> - The asymmetric actor-critic architecture is indispensable for successful vision-based reinforcement learning in high-dimensional observation spaces for agile robotics.
> - Careful simulation design, including simplified visual abstractions and domain randomization, enables effective zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for complex aerial tasks.
