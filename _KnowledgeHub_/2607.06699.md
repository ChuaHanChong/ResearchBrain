# RoboSnap: One-Shot Real-to-Sim Scene Generation for Generalizable Robot Learning and Evaluation

> [!summary] Summary
> ROBOSNAP automatically creates interactive, physically plausible simulation scenes from a single RGB image, reducing object instability by up to 73% compared to prior methods. This framework enhances robot learning by generating effective synthetic data, boosting real-world policy success rates by up to 13.4 percentage points, and offers a reliable evaluation harness with a 0.887 sim-real correlation.

## Problem

- High cost and labor intensity associated with acquiring diverse real-world training data and conducting reproducible policy evaluations for robotics.
- Inefficiency in creating interactive simulation scenes that are both physically plausible and visually faithful replicas of specific real-world deployment environments.
- Existing scene generation methods either focus on creating novel environments or require extensive multi-view data capture and manual refinement, limiting their scalability and reusability for robot learning.

## Method

- **ROBOSNAP** is a three-stage pipeline (==Layered Scene Reconstruction==, ==Simulation-ready Refinement==, Robot Data Generation and Evaluation) that reconstructs a comprehensive simulation environment from a single RGB image.
- Reconstructs an interactive physical layer (objects as textured meshes via VLM, SAM3, SAM3D, ICP) and a surrounding visual context layer (==Gaussian splats== via generative world model), both aligned to a canonical world frame.
- Refines initial object poses through an alternating ==Signed Distance Field (SDF)–physics optimization== procedure, guided by a VLM-inferred physical scene graph, to ensure physical stability and resolve inconsistencies.

## Results

- Significantly improved simulation stability vs. RoLA: reduced the fraction of falling objects to **0.1026** (from **0.3810**) and mean object displacement to **0.0474m** (from **0.2713m**) after **300** physics steps, and replayed **100%** of tested DROID trajectories without collisions (vs. **40%** for RoLA).
- Synthetic data generated in ROBOSNAP scenes improved real-world policy success rates by up to **13.4** percentage points (e.g., for π0, from **29.3% to 42.7%**) and enhanced robustness to real-world perturbations by reducing average performance degradation from **13% to 8%**.
- Achieved a high sim-real correlation (Pearson **r=0.887**, **MMRV=0.0066**) for policy evaluation, indicating the reconstructed scenes reliably reflect real-world performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A single RGB image can provide sufficient information to reconstruct a layered, interactive simulation scene, significantly reducing the data capture burden compared to multi-view approaches.
> - Explicit physical refinement, combining SDF-based geometric constraints with physics simulation, is critical for transforming raw 3D reconstructions into stable and robot-interactable environments.
> - Faithfully reconstructed simulation scenes can serve as both a potent source for synthetic data augmentation to improve real-world policy performance and a reliable proxy for evaluating real-world robot policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The field of robotics research is increasingly focused on developing generalizable robot policies, often leveraging large-scale datasets and foundation models. A significant challenge in this area is the high cost and labor intensity associated with acquiring diverse real-world training data and conducting reproducible policy evaluations. Simulation environments offer a complementary path by providing scalable data synthesis, scene augmentation, and repeatable assessment. However, a bottleneck exists in efficiently creating interactive simulation scenes that are both physically plausible and visually faithful replicas of specific real-world deployment environments.

Existing approaches to scene generation and reconstruction exhibit several limitations in addressing this bottleneck:
- **Procedural and generative scene synthesis methods** primarily focus on creating novel and diverse simulation environments (e.g.,) rather than reconstructing specific "in-the-wild" real-world scenes. While valuable for diversity, they do not provide digital twins of existing real settings.
- **Reconstruction-based real-to-sim methods** aim for better scene alignment but typically require extensive multi-view data capture, manual refinement, or significant human intervention (e.g.,). These methods can be cumbersome and less lightweight for broad application.
- **Recent single-image systems** attempt to reduce the data capture burden (e.g.,) but often produce outputs tailored for narrower endpoints, such as asset retrieval, static reconstructions, or synthesis of demonstrations. These systems often do not yield persistent, editable, and reusable simulation worlds that can be interacted with from novel viewpoints, and their effectiveness in comprehensive robot learning and evaluation workflows remains less explored.

ROBOSNAP addresses these limitations by introducing a method for creating simulation-ready environments from a single RGB image. This work aims to bridge the gap between real-world observations and interactive simulation by focusing on reconstructing specific real scenes into reusable digital infrastructure for robot learning and evaluation, thereby enabling more scalable data generation and more reliable policy assessment.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary motivation behind ROBOSNAP is to address the bottlenecks in acquiring large-scale training data and conducting reproducible evaluations for modern robot foundation models. While real-world datasets provide valuable physical demonstrations, their acquisition is costly, labor-intensive, and hardware-bound. Simulation offers a path to scalability, but current methods struggle to create interactive simulation scenes that accurately replicate specific real-world environments with both physical plausibility and visual fidelity.

The key objectives of this research are:

1. **Develop a one-shot real-to-sim framework**: The core objective is to reconstruct a comprehensive, physically plausible, and visually faithful simulation-ready environment from a single RGB image. This aims to significantly reduce the data capture burden and manual effort typically required for scene digitization.
2. **Ensure physical stability and visual faithfulness**: The reconstructed scenes must be robust enough for robot interaction (e.g., objects should not float or interpenetrate) and should accurately reflect the visual appearance of the original real-world scene under various viewpoints. This fidelity is critical for the utility of the simulation in downstream robotics tasks.
3. **Support generalizable robot learning workflows**: The authors aim to demonstrate that the generated simulation scenes can effectively support several critical aspects of robot learning:
 - **Trajectory replay**: The scenes should accurately represent the geometric and contact properties necessary to reliably replay existing real-world robot trajectories.
 - **Task-specific data generation**: The ability to synthesize large volumes of task-specific demonstration data within these simulated environments is targeted to improve real-world policy performance through fine-tuning and augmentation.
 - **Policy evaluation with meaningful sim-real correlation**: The reconstructed scenes are intended to serve as a reliable evaluation harness, where policy performance in simulation closely correlates with performance in the real world. This would enable more efficient and repeatable benchmarking.
4. **Contribute a real-to-sim companion dataset**: To further advance research in this area, the project aims to release DROID-Sim, a dataset derived from existing real-world robot manipulation data (DROID scenes) that converts recorded trajectories and images into reusable simulation environments.

By addressing these objectives, ROBOSNAP seeks to transform real-world scene observations into persistent, editable, and reusable simulation infrastructure, thereby accelerating the development and validation of generalist robot policies.

### 3. Methodology and approach

ROBOSNAP implements a three-stage pipeline: Layered Scene Reconstruction, Simulation-ready Refinement, and Robot Data Generation and Evaluation.

#### 3.1. Layered Scene Reconstruction from a Single Image

Given a single RGB image, ROBOSNAP first reconstructs an initial layered scene. This scene comprises an interactive physical layer and a surrounding visual context layer, both registered to a canonical world frame (W) where the dominant support platform's centroid is at the origin and gravity acts along the negative z-axis.

- **Interactive Physical Layer**:
 - A Vision-Language Model (VLM) is employed to parse the interaction area and identify object names, including the support platform.
 - Instance masks for each identified object are extracted using SAM 3.
 - SAM 3D reconstructs each object as a textured mesh with an initial pose and scale.
 - Camera geometry, confidence, and a dense point map are predicted using VGGT.
 - High-confidence foreground points from the VGGT map corresponding to each object mask are used to refine the initial SAM 3D pose through mask-guided, coarse-to-fine ICP (Iterative Closest Point) between sampled mesh surface points and the foreground point cloud.
- **Canonical Alignment and Robot Base**:
 - The dominant support platform is identified from the VGGT point map, and a plane is fitted to its support points using RANSAC followed by least-squares refinement.
 - The scene is aligned to the canonical world frame W by transforming the plane normal to align with the z-axis and placing the platform centroid at the origin.
 - Object poses are transformed into this world frame. For calibrated datasets, the robot base is placed using its known camera-frame base pose; otherwise, it is initialized from the support platform geometry and a specified robot-facing direction.
- **Visual Context Layer**:
 - The non-interactable surrounding context is reconstructed separately.
 - After foreground masking, missing background regions are inpainted using a VLM-guided prompt.
 - The completed image is fed into a generative world model to produce a Gaussian splat scene.
 - This Gaussian splat scene is aligned to the camera frame using sparse correspondences and ICP, then transformed into the canonical world frame.
- **Articulated Objects**:
 - For objects identified as articulated, their reconstructed mesh is decomposed into semantic parts using a point-based part segmentation model.
 - The resulting part meshes are then attached to category-level kinematic parameters retrieved from an articulated-object dataset.

#### 3.2. Simulation-ready Refinement

Initial, independently estimated per-object poses often result in physical inconsistencies such as floating objects, interpenetrations, or unstable contacts. To address this, ROBOSNAP refines object poses using an alternating SDF–physics optimization procedure guided by a physical scene graph.

- **Scene Graph Extraction**:
 - Pairwise physical relations (Support and Contact) are inferred using a VLM with Set-of-Mark prompting on instance masks and captions.
 - Objects that only support others are designated as fixed roots (kinematic in simulation).
- **Alternating SDF–physics Optimization**:
 - The refinement optimizes residual SE(3) updates for non-root objects, alternating between two phases:
 - **SDF Phase**: This phase uses precomputed Signed Distance Field (SDF) grids and surface samples for each object. It minimizes a combined loss function comprising:
 - *Penetration loss*: Penalizes interpenetration between objects.
 - *Support loss*: Attracts supported objects towards their supporters' zero-level SDF while penalizing penetration.
 - *Contact loss*: A symmetric penalty that prevents both separation and penetration for objects in contact.
 - *Regularization loss*: An L2 penalty on the translation and rotation residuals to prevent excessive drift from initial estimates.
 - **Physics Phase**: Meshes are decomposed into V-HACD collision hulls and simulated in a physics engine (e.g., SAPIEN). Root objects are treated as kinematic, while other objects are dynamic, allowing them to settle under gravity. The settled poses from the physics simulation are then used as the starting point for the next SDF optimization phase.
 - This alternation continues for a predetermined number of rounds or until the maximum object displacement falls below a convergence threshold, yielding the final simulation-ready scene.

#### 3.3. Robot Data Generation and Evaluation

The refined scene supports downstream robot learning applications.

- **Layered Rendering**: For any query camera, the physical layer (foreground objects) is rendered by Isaac Sim, and the visual layer (background Gaussian splats) is rendered from the corresponding camera pose. These two layers are depth-composited based on their alpha and depth values to form the final image.
- **Trajectory-based Data Generation**: The simulation-ready scene is instantiated within an Isaac Sim-based data engine. Task specifications, robot embodiment, and query cameras are defined. Grasp candidates (e.g., AnyGrasp) and skill modules generate 6D end-effector waypoints, which are then converted into collision-aware, dense joint-space actions by a motion planning system (e.g., cuRobo). This process generates task-specific synthetic demonstrations.
- **Policy Evaluation**: Actions from a policy can be executed within the simulated scene. For sim-real evaluation, Pearson correlation (r) is used to quantify agreement in success rates between real and simulated environments, and Mean Maximum Rank Violation (MMRV) is used to measure rank-order inconsistency. A higher r and lower MMRV indicate better sim-real alignment.

#### 3.4. DROID-Sim Dataset Construction

The methodology was applied to 564 scenes from the DROID dataset to create DROID-Sim. This involved automated processing from a single RGB frame (typically the first frame from an exterior camera) to VLM-based object captioning, multi-model segmentation (Grounding DINO, Florence-2, SAM 3), 3D asset generation (SAM3D), pose estimation and refinement, and Gaussian splatting for backgrounds. This automated pipeline generally takes 12 to 25 minutes per scene.

### 4. Main findings and results

The research validated ROBOSNAP through experiments addressing five key questions related to visual realism, simulation stability, trajectory replay, data generation, robustness, and policy evaluation.

**Q1: Visual realism and simulation readiness**
- **Visual Alignment**: Compared to RoLA, ROBOSNAP demonstrated comparable pixel fidelity (PSNR) and perceptual similarity (LPIPS) but showed improved structural similarity (SSIM: 0.4907 for ROBOSNAP vs. 0.4521 for RoLA), local geometry preservation (SIFT-MR: 0.1226 vs. 0.0664), color distribution (RGB-EMD: 11.4795 vs. 28.5806), and texture statistics (Gabor-L1: 0.000741 vs. 0.001817). These metrics suggest better preservation of the original scene's structure, color, and texture.
- **Simulation Stability**: After 300 Isaac Sim steps, ROBOSNAP significantly reduced instability metrics compared to baselines (SAM 3D, SAM 3D + FoundationPose, RoLA, and ROBOSNAP without refinement). The fraction of falling objects decreased to 0.1026 (from 0.3810 for RoLA), collisions to 0.0256 (from 0.3226), mean translation MSE to 0.0022 (from 0.0736), mean displacement to 0.0474m (from 0.2713m), and mean quaternion MSE to 0.0178 (from 0.1093). These results indicate that the refinement process produces physically stable scenes suitable for robot interaction.

**Q2: Trajectory replay**
- On a subset of 5 DROID scenes, ROBOSNAP successfully replayed all 5 original DROID trajectories without interpenetration or collisions. In contrast, RoLA achieved successful replay in 2 out of 5 scenes. This indicates that ROBOSNAP's scene reconstruction accurately preserves the necessary geometry and contact structure to reproduce real robot actions.

**Q3: Data generation and policy fine-tuning**
- Experiments across 10 real-world tasks using π0.5 and π0 policies were conducted with varying proportions of real, ROBOSNAP-generated, and simulation-augmented demonstrations.
- The data mixture termed "Ratio 2" (60% real, 20% ROBOSNAP-generated, 20% simulation-augmented) yielded the highest average real-world success rates. For π0.5, success improved from a real-only baseline of 32.7% to 41.7%. For π0, success improved from 29.3% to 42.7%.
- Even with "Ratio 3" (0% real, 50% ROBOSNAP-generated, 50% simulation-augmented), non-zero real-world success rates were observed (17.3% for π0.5 and 15.0% for π0), demonstrating the utility of synthetic data from ROBOSNAP without any real demonstrations. These findings suggest that ROBOSNAP scenes can be a source of effective task-specific data for real-world policy fine-tuning.

**Q4: Robustness under perturbation**
- Policies fine-tuned with ROBOSNAP-generated data (Ratio 2) exhibited improved robustness to six types of real-world perturbations (object pose, background objects, lighting, table texture, camera pose, robot initial state).
- The average degradation in success rate across all perturbations was reduced from 13% for real-only trained policies to 8% for policies co-trained with real and ROBOSNAP-generated data.
- Significant gains in robustness were observed specifically for camera-pose and robot-initial-state shifts, indicating that synthetic data can help policies maintain performance under certain real-world distribution shifts.

**Q5: Generative evaluation harness**
- The sim-real correlation between policies fine-tuned on real data and evaluated in both real and ROBOSNAP-generated simulation scenes was assessed.
- A Pearson correlation coefficient (r) of 0.887 and a Mean Maximum Rank Violation (MMRV) of 0.0066 were reported. This high correlation and low rank violation suggest that ROBOSNAP scenes capture sufficient sim-real dynamics and task-relevant characteristics to serve as a reliable simulation proxy for evaluating real-world manipulation policies.
- Behavior-space diagnostics, including end-effector displacement trajectories and per-joint increment distributions, also indicated similar low-level control behaviors between real and simulated rollouts, further supporting the use of ROBOSNAP scenes for evaluation.

**DROID-Sim Dataset**
- The work also introduces DROID-Sim, a real-to-sim companion dataset constructed from 564 real-world DROID scenes. This dataset extends existing real-world robot data to reusable simulation environments.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The ROBOSNAP framework represents an advancement in the capability to convert real-world observations into interactive, simulation-ready environments. Its significance and potential impact span several areas of robotics research and development:

- **Automation and Scalability in Scene Generation**: By enabling the reconstruction of a physically plausible and visually faithful scene from a single RGB image, ROBOSNAP automates a previously labor-intensive and costly process. This automation can significantly scale up the creation of diverse simulation environments tailored to specific real-world setups, which is critical for training large-scale robot foundation models and for developing generalist robot policies.
- **Enhanced Data Generation for Robot Learning**: The capability to generate task-specific synthetic data directly from reconstructed real scenes offers a valuable method for data augmentation. This is demonstrated by the improved real-world performance and robustness of policies fine-tuned with ROBOSNAP-generated data, even in the absence of additional real demonstrations. This approach can help address the data scarcity problem in robotics, enabling more robust and generalizable policy learning.
- **Reproducible and Efficient Policy Evaluation**: The high sim-real correlation achieved by ROBOSNAP scenes for policy evaluation suggests their utility as a generative evaluation harness. This allows for repeatable and scalable testing of robot policies in a virtual environment that reliably reflects real-world performance, reducing the reliance on physical hardware and its associated costs and time constraints. This can accelerate the development cycle of robot policies.
- **Foundation for Real-to-Sim Research**: The introduction of DROID-Sim as a real-to-sim companion dataset provides a standardized resource for the research community. By linking real-world robot trajectories and images to reusable simulation environments, it facilitates systematic investigation into real-to-sim transfer, domain randomization, and other aspects of robot learning infrastructure.
- **Shift in Perspective on Scene Reconstruction**: The work proposes a conceptual shift, viewing real-world scene reconstruction not merely as the creation of a visual digital twin, but as the establishment of reusable infrastructure for embodied AI training and evaluation. This perspective emphasizes the functional and interactive aspects of reconstructed scenes over purely visual fidelity.
- **Future Research Directions**: While addressing significant challenges, ROBOSNAP also highlights areas for future development, such as improving robustness to extreme input quality (occlusion, lighting), extending support to deformable or fluid materials, and integrating dedicated pipelines for automatic physical parameter estimation. These limitations suggest clear avenues for subsequent research to build upon the foundational work presented.

Overall, ROBOSNAP contributes to bridging the gap between real and simulated environments, which is a critical enabler for the advancement of generalizable and robust robot intelligence.
