# Robotic Compliant Object Prying Using Diffusion Policy Guided by Vision and Force Observations

> [!summary] Summary
> A robotic system for compliant object prying utilizes a diffusion policy guided by vision and force observations, integrated through a novel cross-attention architecture and force data augmentation. This approach demonstrated a 96% success rate in prying tasks, including zero-shot transfer to unseen battery types and configurations.

## Problem

- Automating robotic disassembly for product recycling, particularly for components combining compliant and rigid parts, presents challenges due to unknown assembly states and high precision requirements.
- Traditional offline programming and vision-only imitation learning methods struggle with real-world variability, tight tolerances, and the critical need for precise force control in contact-rich manipulation tasks.
- Effectively integrating low-dimensional contact force information with high-dimensional image data within diffusion policies is difficult, as force signals can be diluted or underutilized due to dimensionality disparity.

## Method

- A diffusion policy framework is developed that conditions action prediction on a joint feature embedding from synchronized vision and force data, along with the robot's end-effector state.
- A novel cross-attention architecture is employed, where projected force features query encoded image features, to learn relational information between heterogeneous sensor modalities and prevent force signal dilution.
- Force data augmentation techniques, including random scaling and Gaussian noise, are applied during training to improve the policy's generalizability and robustness to varying force requirements and out-of-distribution objects.

## Results

- The proposed Diffusion Policy with Cross-Attention (DP-CA) achieved an overall success rate of **96%** in compliant object prying tasks across diverse scenarios, encompassing both seen and unseen objects.
- DP-CA demonstrated a **57%** improvement over the vision-only baseline (**39%** success rate) and outperformed naive force integration methods, exhibiting strong zero-shot transfer capabilities to unseen battery types.
- The model maintained high success rates in edge-case scenarios, achieving **95%** for batteries in series and **90%** for batteries with varying colors, while robot-applied force magnitudes and trends closely matched human demonstrations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating force data at each time step allows the robot to maintain necessary contact and traction, thereby significantly enhancing robustness in complex, contact-rich manipulation tasks.
> - Learning relational features between image and force through cross-attention improves the diffusion policy's generalization capacity to unseen object sizes, configurations, and battery types.
> - The enhanced relational understanding from cross-attention enables the model to make more informed decisions regarding critical mode transitions (e.g., when to initiate prying), preventing common failure modes seen in vision-only baselines.
