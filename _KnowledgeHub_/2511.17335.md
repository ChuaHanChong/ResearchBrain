# Robot Confirmation Generation and Action Planning Using Long-context Q-Former Integrated with Multimodal LLM

> [!summary] Summary
> Extends AVBLIP's Q-former-based robot action confirmation and micro-step action planning with a long-context Q-former that fuses current-clip and surrounding-clip embeddings, plus text conditioning that prepends speech subtitles or VideoLLaMA3 descriptions directly to the LLM decoder. On YouCook2, combining both yields up to 17.9% relative METEOR gain over baseline.

## Problem

- Prior multimodal-LLM robot action planning (AVBLIP) processes single video clips independently, ignoring left/right dependencies across the 5-10 clips forming one long-horizon task.
- Feeding text embeddings only through the Q-former's shared cross-attention space over-abstracts language, losing concrete keywords like exact ingredient or utensil names.
- Existing action-replanning work relies on human correction after errors rather than reducing upstream confirmation-generation errors caused by missing context or lost textual detail.

## Method

- ==Long-context Q-former==: a second ==Q-former== module encodes surrounding clips (configurable window, e.g. -2,-1,0,+1,+2 or full video) alongside the original current-clip Q-former, combined via a jointly trained transformer encoder.
- ==Text conditioning==: prepends tokenized text (speech subtitles and/or VideoLLaMA3-generated descriptions) directly to Q-former token embeddings before the ==frozen LLM decoder==, bypassing the Q-former's abstraction bottleneck.
- Combined architecture jointly trains both long-context Q-formers, the ==fusion transformer==, and text-conditioned prompts end-to-end with a frozen OPT-2.7B decoder for micro-step action sequence and confirmation sentence generation.

## Results

- Long-context Q-former alone raises action-description METEOR from **0.159** to **0.170** with a two-clip window on each side, with diminishing returns beyond a 2-clip radius.
- Text conditioning gives the largest single-component gain: VideoLLaMA3+subtitle conditioning lifts action-sequence BLEU-2 from **0.370** to **0.424** and METEOR from **0.260** to **0.290**.
- Combining long-context and text conditioning is additive, reaching BLEU-2 **0.432** and METEOR **0.291** for sequences (**16.7%**/**11.9%** relative gains) and BLEU-2 **0.270**/METEOR **0.183** for descriptions (**17.9%**/**15.1%** relative gains), at modest extra cost (**8.2h** vs **5.5h** training, **5.8s** vs **4.1s** inference).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Confirmation-sentence accuracy is a major bottleneck for downstream action-planning quality, so improving language generation fidelity pays off across the whole pipeline.
> - Q-former bottlenecks can silently discard precise lexical detail even when multimodal features are informative; conditioning the decoder directly on raw text sidesteps this without fine-tuning the LLM.
> - Text-only conditioning cannot replace multimodal features entirely; ablations show video/audio/image features remain necessary even when rich subtitles or VLM-generated descriptions are available.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

Human-robot interaction (HRI) is a developing field focused on enabling robots to effectively collaborate with humans, particularly in shared tasks within dynamic environments. This objective necessitates robots possessing a comprehensive understanding of human actions and their interactions with the surroundings. Achieving this goal requires advancements across multiple research domains, including speech understanding, audio event detection, object and action recognition in computer vision, physical sensing and manipulation for robot control, and natural language generation for human interaction.

Early research in robotic manipulation actions explored high-level instruction storage and analysis, utilizing techniques like contrastive learning for reinforcement learning agents and the extraction of cost functions from task videos for imitation learning. The field has progressed to training perception modules on extensive manipulation datasets and leveraging vision and language models to generate action sequences from human instruction videos.

The advent of Large Language Models (LLMs) has provided a significant catalyst for bridging the gap between raw sensory information and abstract understanding in robot action planning. LLMs have been applied to various robotic tasks, such as open-vocabulary task performance (e.g., CLIPort, SayCan), generating situated robot task plans (e.g., PROGPROMPT), partially observable task planning (e.g., LLM-POP), and open-world task planning (e.g., COWP). Some studies have investigated direct prediction of end-effector poses or re-prompting strategies to enhance plan executability.

Multimodal scene understanding has advanced significantly with technologies like audio-visual transformers, applied to robot action planning using human action videos. However, limitations in multimodal reasoning due to data sparsity in fusion methods led to the adoption of approaches like BLIP2, which narrow down the semantic space. The integration of a Querying Transformer (Q-former) has emerged as an efficient method for embedding various features into the semantic space of LLMs, preserving information effectively while managing computational demands.

Despite these advancements, automatic action planning is subject to errors arising from discrepancies between training environments and unforeseen circumstances. To mitigate the execution of incorrect actions, research has explored neural action replanning based on human error correction via natural language dialogue. Current state-of-the-art methods in this area, particularly those leveraging multimodal LLMs like AVBLIP for robot action confirmation and action step generation, primarily focus on processing information from individual, short video clips. This clip-level processing overlooks the interdependencies of actions within a longer, overarching task video, meaning that valuable long-context information is often not utilized. This paper aims to address this specific limitation by integrating long-context understanding and refining how textual information is leveraged within the multimodal LLM framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance human-robot interaction (HRI) through natural language dialogue by improving the robot's ability to generate action confirmations and detailed action steps. This enhancement is predicated on robust multimodal scene understanding.

The motivation for this work arises from two principal limitations observed in existing state-of-the-art approaches:
1. **Limited Contextual Understanding:** Current methods typically generate robot action steps and corresponding action confirmations based on analysis of a single, short video clip. While a task may be composed of multiple micro-steps, the actions within a long-horizon task are often interdependent and sequentially linked across an entire video. Relying solely on single-clip processing neglects this crucial long-context information, potentially leading to inaccurate or inefficient action planning. The paper aims to enable robots to leverage the broader temporal context, encompassing preceding and succeeding actions, to form more coherent and accurate plans.
2. **Information Abstraction in Multimodal Fusion:** In existing Q-former-based multimodal LLM architectures, textual information (e.g., speech subtitles) is typically fed into the multimodal Q-former alongside visual and audio features. This process, while integrating modalities, can inadvertently lead to a loss of precise semantic details from the original language. The abstraction inherent in embedding text into a multimodal semantic space may obscure concrete keywords or specific instructions crucial for generating fine-grained action sequences and accurate confirmations. The motivation here is to preserve the richness of language information by allowing it to directly influence the LLM decoder.

To address these motivations, the paper sets forth several specific objectives:
- To propose a **long-context Q-former** architecture that can effectively incorporate both left (past) and right (future) contextual dependencies from full videos into the multimodal understanding process.
- To introduce a **text-conditioning approach** that feeds raw text embeddings directly into the LLM decoder. This aims to mitigate the loss of high-abstraction information that can occur when text is processed solely through the Q-former.
- To empirically demonstrate the effectiveness of these proposed approaches in improving the accuracy of robot confirmation sentence generation and robot action sequence generation within the cooking domain.
- To specifically examine and quantify the contribution of an external multimodal LLM, VideoLLaMA3, in providing rich video descriptions for the text-conditioning mechanism.

By addressing these objectives, the research seeks to move towards more intelligent, context-aware, and human-friendly robot assistants capable of engaging in more effective and reliable dialogue for collaborative tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology proposed in this paper extends the AVBLIP framework, which is an adaptation of BLIP-2 designed to handle multimodal features for robot action planning. The core of AVBLIP involves a Q-former to bridge multimodal inputs with an LLM decoder. The paper introduces two main innovations: a long-context Q-former and a text-conditioning approach.

**Core AVBLIP Framework:**
The base model, AVBLIP, comprises:
- **Multimodal Encoders:** Unlike BLIP-2, which uses a frozen image encoder, AVBLIP utilizes frozen audio-visual encoders to extract features from video, image, and audio inputs. Specifically, Omnivore is used for video and image features (concatenated and projected), CLIP for image features, and Audio Spectrogram Transformer (AST) for audio features. Text features, when available (e.g., from subtitles), are extracted using Glove word embeddings.
- **Q-former:** A Querying Transformer acts as an interface between the multimodal encoders and the LLM. It is trained to extract a fixed number of learnable query embeddings from variable-length multimodal feature sequences. The Q-former's self-attention layers are shared between a multimodal transformer (interacting with audio-visual encoders via cross-attention) and a text transformer (acting as an encoder/decoder).
- **LLM Decoder:** A frozen pre-trained LLM (OPT-2.7B in this study) is connected to the Q-former via a feed-forward layer. The Q-former's output token embeddings are projected into the LLM's embedding space, enabling the LLM to generate a sequence of micro-step actions for a single-arm robot and natural language action descriptions (confirmation sentences).

**Training Process:**
The training of AVBLIP is conducted in two stages:
1. **Vision-Language Representation Learning:** The Q-former is trained with frozen multimodal encoders to learn robust multimodal representations.
2. **Vision-to-Language Generative Learning:** The Q-former is connected to the frozen LLM Decoder. The entire system is then trained using a cross-entropy loss function to generate target action sequences and confirmation sentences from the multimodal features of video clips.

**Proposed Innovations:**

1. **Long-Context Q-former:**
 - **Architecture:** This innovation extends the AVBLIP architecture by incorporating two Q-former modules. One Q-former processes multimodal features from the *current* video clip (as in the baseline AVBLIP). The second Q-former processes features from *surrounding* video clips, which can include both preceding and succeeding clips, to capture long-range temporal dependencies.
 - **Integration:** The token embeddings generated by both the current-clip Q-former and the context-clip Q-former are then combined using a transformer encoder. This combined representation is subsequently fed to the LLM decoder.
 - **Training:** The two Q-formers and the transformer encoder are jointly trained from scratch, following the two-stage training paradigm of the original AVBLIP framework.

2. **Text Conditioning:**
 - **Mechanism:** This approach aims to preserve the granular semantic information in text. Instead of solely routing text features through the Q-former, the text information is directly prepended to the Q-former's token embeddings. This combined input is then fed into the LLM decoder. The text is tokenized and embedded by the same LLM used for decoding, bypassing the potential abstraction loss in the multimodal Q-former.
 - **Sources of Text:** Two main sources of text information are utilized for conditioning:
 - Speech subtitles: Automatically annotated subtitles from YouTube videos.
 - Video descriptions from VideoLLaMA3: Detailed video descriptions generated by a state-of-the-art multimodal LLM (VideoLLaMA3 7B model) using a generic prompt ("Describe the cooking video in detail.").
 - The paper also explores concatenating both speech subtitles and VideoLLaMA3 descriptions for enriched text conditioning.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Dataset:** YouCook2 dataset, a collection of cooking action video clips annotated with human instructions in natural language. The human instructions were translated into robot micro-step action sequences suitable for single-arm robots. The validation set was used for evaluation, with cross-validation employed for model selection and performance measurement.
- **Feature Dimensions:** Audio (768D), Visual (1024D), Text (300D).
- **Q-former Configuration:** Initialized with BERTbase weights (cross-attention layers randomly initialized), 768 hidden dimensions, 32 queries. For the long-context Q-former, 32 queries were used for each module.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** BLEU-2 and METEOR scores, standard metrics for natural language generation quality, were computed between generated and ground-truth sequences.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed long-context Q-former and text-conditioning approaches in enhancing robot action confirmation and planning.

**A. Quality of Action Sequences and Descriptions with Long-Context Q-former (Table III):**
- **Baseline Performance:** The baseline AVBLIP model, trained without contextual information, achieved BLEU-2 scores of 0.370 for action sequences and 0.229 for action descriptions, and METEOR scores of 0.260 and 0.159, respectively.
- **Left-Context Expansion:** Introducing previous clips significantly improved performance. For instance, using the previous two clips and the current clip ("-2,-1,0") resulted in BLEU-2 scores of 0.376 for action sequences and 0.237 for descriptions. Expanding to the full left context ("*,0") showed comparable, slightly higher METEOR scores.
- **Left and Right Context Expansion:** Incorporating both past and future context yielded further improvements. The configuration including two previous, the current, and two following clips ("-2,-1,0,+1,+2") achieved the highest scores for action sequences (BLEU-2: 0.381, METEOR: 0.269) and descriptions (BLEU-2: 0.242, METEOR: 0.170). The full context ("*,0,*") showed similar performance.
- **Computational Cost:** The long-context Q-former with full-video context increased training time (8.2 hours vs. 5.5 hours for baseline) and average inference time (5.8 seconds vs. 4.1 seconds), but these increases were considered manageable.

**B. Quality of Action Sequences and Descriptions with Text Conditioning (Table IV):**
- **Speech Subtitle Conditioning:** Directly feeding speech subtitles to the LLM decoder substantially improved all metrics compared to the baseline. BLEU-2 for action sequences rose to 0.411 (a 11.1% increase over baseline) and for descriptions to 0.257.
- **VideoLLaMA3 Description Conditioning:** Using video descriptions generated by VideoLLaMA3 also improved performance, with BLEU-2 scores of 0.401 for action sequences and 0.255 for descriptions, indicating its efficacy comparable to subtitles.
- **Combined Text Conditioning:** Concatenating VideoLLaMA3 descriptions and speech subtitles ("VideoLLaMA3+subtitle") yielded further gains, achieving the best text-conditioned results (BLEU-2: 0.424 for action sequences, 0.260 for descriptions). This suggests that richer and more precise text input as context is beneficial.
- **Ground-Truth Descriptions:** Utilizing ground-truth descriptions as conditioning text established an upper bound, achieving the highest BLEU-2 score of 0.499 for action sequences, demonstrating the potential when perfect textual information is available.
- **Generated Descriptions:** Using generated action descriptions as conditioning slightly degraded action sequence quality compared to VideoLLaMA3+subtitle, likely due to their brevity and potential loss of information.

**C. Combined Approach Performance (Table V):**
- The combination of the long-context Q-former and text conditioning (VideoLLaMA3+subtitle) demonstrated additive performance gains. The model using five-clip context ("-2,-1,0,+1,+2") with VideoLLaMA3+subtitle conditioning achieved the highest scores: BLEU-2 0.432 and METEOR 0.291 for action sequences, and BLEU-2 0.270 and METEOR 0.183 for action descriptions.
- These results represent relative gains from the baseline of 16.7% and 11.9% for action sequence BLEU-2 and METEOR, respectively, and 17.9% and 15.1% for action description BLEU-2 and METEOR, respectively.

**D. Analysis and Discussion (Table II & VI):**
- **Qualitative Examples (Table II):** Case studies illustrated that the proposed method corrected object-level errors present in the baseline's output. For example, "spring roll" was corrected to "fish" with VideoLLaMA3 support, and "potato" to "cabbage" using long-context information and VideoLLaMA3.
- **Impact of Text Information (Table VI):**
 - Removing subtitles from the multimodal *features* (input to Q-former) did not significantly impact baseline performance, suggesting that the Q-former, in its standard configuration, did not fully leverage subtitle features effectively.
 - However, providing subtitles as direct *text conditioning* to the LLM decoder significantly improved sequence quality.
 - When base multimodal features were removed, and only text (subtitles or VideoLLaMA3 descriptions) was used, a substantial degradation in quality occurred. This indicates that multimodal features remain essential for the task, even with rich textual input. The paper also notes that subtitle quality can be inconsistent, further reinforcing the need for robust multimodal understanding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces advancements in multimodal understanding and language generation for human-robot interaction, with several significant implications:

- **Enhanced Human-Robot Collaboration:** The ability of robots to generate more accurate action confirmations and detailed micro-step action plans, informed by both broad video context and precise textual cues, can significantly improve the efficiency and reliability of human-robot collaboration. This reduces ambiguity and potential for errors, fostering more seamless interaction in shared tasks.

- **Improved Robot Safety and Autonomy:** By enabling robots to confirm planned actions with humans before execution, and to leverage human corrections through natural language, the system contributes to enhanced operational safety. This interactive replanning capability allows for human oversight and intervention, preventing potentially incorrect or undesirable robot actions in dynamic environments.

- **Context-Aware Robot Planning for Long-Horizon Tasks:** The introduction of the long-context Q-former is a critical step towards equipping robots with the capacity to understand and plan for complex, long-horizon tasks, which often involve sequential and interdependent actions. Moving beyond single-clip analysis to full-video context enables robots to infer broader task goals and the logical flow of actions, making them more adaptable to real-world scenarios.

- **Effective Integration of Multimodal LLMs:** The paper demonstrates an effective strategy for integrating powerful Large Language Models with rich multimodal sensory data. By carefully managing information flow—using the Q-former for multimodal abstraction and direct text conditioning for preserving linguistic precision—the research optimizes the leverage of LLMs for specialized robotic tasks, without extensive fine-tuning of the LLM itself.

- **Practical Applicability in Diverse Domains:** While evaluated in the cooking domain, the proposed methodology is potentially transferable to other human-demonstrated tasks requiring sequential actions and precise object manipulation, such as assembly, maintenance, or laboratory procedures. The use of external LLMs like VideoLLaMA3 for generating descriptive text also broadens the applicability to scenarios where explicit subtitles might be unavailable or of limited quality.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work establishes a robust baseline and methodology for future research. It highlights avenues such as multitask training for action steps and descriptions, exploring alternative multimodal fusion strategies, evaluating the approach in different task domains, and ultimately, real-world deployment on physical robots or in advanced simulators. These directions promise to further bridge the gap between human instruction and reliable robot execution.
