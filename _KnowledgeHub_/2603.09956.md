# Kinodynamic Motion Retargeting for Humanoid Locomotion via Multi-Contact Whole-Body Trajectory Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> The Kinodynamic Motion Retargeting (KDMR) framework creates dynamically feasible and physically consistent reference trajectories for humanoid robots by integrating multi-contact whole-body trajectory optimization with human ground reaction force data. This approach generates smoother motions that adhere to physical constraints, leading to accelerated policy convergence and improved sample efficiency in downstream imitation learning tasks.

## Problem

- Traditional kinematic motion retargeting methods for humanoids often produce physically inconsistent artifacts like foot sliding, ground penetration, and dynamically infeasible transitions due to morphological differences.
- These physical inconsistencies in reference motions degrade the performance, sample efficiency, and stability of downstream imitation learning policies.
- Existing approaches struggle to accurately replicate complex human multi-contact gaits, such as the heel-to-toe roll, with proper dynamic feasibility for robots.

## Method

- A comprehensive pipeline that jointly optimizes kinematics and whole-body dynamics, formulated as a multi-contact trajectory optimization problem, to ensure dynamically feasible and smooth robot motions.
- An approach to estimate distinct heel-toe contact patterns and dynamically rescale ground reaction forces (GRF) from human biomechanics data, enabling the replication of natural human-like contact sequences.
- A two-stage optimization process: initial kinematic retargeting followed by dynamics-based refinement, explicitly enforcing robot dynamics, multi-contact holonomic constraints, and friction cone limits.

## Results

- KDMR produced significantly smoother robot trajectories and effectively eliminated physical artifacts like foot floating and ground penetration, strictly adhering to desired contact windows.
- The method successfully recovered both the timing of foot contact events and the magnitude of required ground reaction forces, demonstrating improved dynamic fidelity compared to mass-rescaled human data.
- Policies trained on KDMR-generated references exhibited faster convergence and superior sample efficiency in downstream imitation learning tasks, achieving higher mean episode rewards earlier than baseline methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly incorporating rigid-body dynamics and contact complementarity constraints during motion retargeting is essential for generating physically plausible and stable humanoid locomotion trajectories.
> - Leveraging biomechanics-inspired analysis of human ground reaction forces (GRF) allows for robust estimation and decomposition of complex heel-toe contact events, crucial for realistic robot gaits.
> - Providing dynamically consistent reference motions significantly reduces the learning burden on reinforcement learning agents, leading to more efficient and robust policy acquisition in imitation learning.
