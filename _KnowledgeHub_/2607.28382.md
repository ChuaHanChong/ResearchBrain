# SemAnCorr: Semantic Anchored Correspondence for Zero-Shot Manipulation Skill Transfer

> [!summary] Summary
> The SemAnCorr framework introduces a training-free method to establish dense, vertex-level correspondence between geometrically diverse 3D objects for zero-shot manipulation skill transfer. It combines semantic anchor selection with functional map propagation, achieving 90.8% semantic accuracy and a geometric coherence score of 0.40 on the PartNet-Mobility benchmark, and demonstrates improved success rates for complex real-world manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Difficulty in transferring manipulation skills across object instances that share functional similarities but exhibit significant geometric differences.
- Existing methods often fail to capture the full functional intent of manipulation skills, which includes precise local poses and orientations critical for articulated object interaction.
- Dense 3D feature matching frequently produces spatially incoherent correspondences, preventing the recovery of consistent local geometric frames necessary for reliable skill execution.

## Method

- **SemAnCorr** performs ==3D semantic acquisition and clustering== from multi-view observations using a pre-trained vision model (SigLip2Vision) to partition object surfaces into meaningful regions.
- A ==joint pose-correspondence optimization== process selects a small set of high-confidence, semantically and geometrically consistent anchor pairs between source and target objects.
- These semantic anchors constrain a ==functional map==, which then propagates a smooth, dense vertex-level correspondence across the entire object surface, ensuring geometric coherence.

## Results

- SemAnCorr achieved an average semantic accuracy of **90.8%** and a geometric coherence score (GCS) of **0.40** on the PartNet-Mobility benchmark, outperforming baselines like D3Fields (**84.6%** semantic accuracy, **0.16** GCS).
- In real-world manipulation tasks, SemAnCorr significantly improved success rates for complex actions, such as kettle pouring (**7/10** success rate vs. **3/10** for D3Fields) and water bottle twisting (**7/10** vs. **1/10**).
- The semantic anchor confidence score demonstrated utility as a lightweight signal for estimating generalization compatibility between object pairs, including cross-category transfers.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reliable zero-shot manipulation skill transfer necessitates correspondences that simultaneously maintain semantic consistency (where to interact) and geometric coherence (how to execute the interaction).
> - Combining semantic anchoring with functional map propagation effectively bridges the gap between high-level semantic understanding and the low-level geometric precision required for robot manipulation.
> - Each component of SemAnCorr—semantic clustering, anchor selection, and functional map refinement—plays a complementary role in achieving robust, dense surface correspondences that preserve both semantic accuracy and geometric smoothness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses the fundamental challenge of transferring manipulation skills across object instances that share functional similarities but exhibit significant geometric differences. The broader research landscape in robot learning and computer vision has explored various approaches to this problem, each with specific strengths and limitations that SemAnCorr aims to overcome.

**Challenges in Skill Transfer:**
Existing approaches for skill transfer often rely on identifying semantically corresponding interaction regions or affordances. While these methods can locate approximate contact points, they frequently fail to capture the comprehensive functional intent of manipulation skills, which includes not only *where* to interact but also *how* (e.g., specific poses, sequences, and relationships to articulated structures). This limitation underscores the necessity for correspondences that are both semantically consistent and geometrically coherent to recover local geometric frames essential for reliable skill execution.

**Dense 3D Correspondence Methods:**
The field of dense 3D correspondence has seen developments from classical to deep learning-based methods.
- **Classical Functional Maps (e.g., FM-WKS):** These methods typically use intrinsic geometric descriptors and operate purely on shape geometry. While they establish smooth surface correspondences, they are limited to objects with similar intrinsic geometries and often fail across semantically diverse objects.
- **Deep Functional Maps:** These approaches learn optimized feature extractors to improve correspondence but generally require category-specific training data, limiting their generalization capabilities.
- **Recent Hybrid Methods (e.g., DenseMatcher):** These combine advanced visual features (like SD-DINO) with learned refinement networks and functional maps, demonstrating improved cross-category correspondence. SemAnCorr shares the functional map backbone with such methods but diverges by replacing learned feature refinement with a training-free semantic anchor selection process, which involves per-object-pair optimization.

**Semantic Correspondence for Manipulation:**
Recent advancements leverage 2D foundation models to establish semantic correspondence for manipulation tasks, often in a zero-shot context.
- **2D-based Methods (e.g., DIFT, SD-DINO, Robo-ABC, RAM, UAD, Adapt by Analogy):** These approaches extract correspondences from diffusion or vision-language model features. While effective for identifying semantic regions in 2D, they often neglect crucial 3D geometric information such as interaction poses and orientations, which are critical for articulated object manipulation.

**3D Feature Fields for Manipulation Generalization:**
Lifting visual features into 3D has shown promise for manipulation generalization.
- **3D Descriptor Fields (e.g., D3Fields, O3Afford):** These methods build dynamic 3D descriptor fields by lifting features from multi-view RGB-D observations. They excel at capturing fine-grained local semantics. However, their reliance on point-wise nearest-neighbor matching in feature space can lead to spatially incoherent correspondences across distant surface regions, which compromises the recovery of consistent local geometric frames. SemAnCorr addresses this by building upon 3D descriptor fields and integrating functional map propagation to enforce global surface smoothness.

**Affordance Learning and Tool-Use Manipulation:**
Research in affordance learning for tool use has focused on actionability prediction and part-centric representations.
- **Category-Specific Affordance Learning (e.g., Where2act, 3D AffordanceNet, GapartNet, AdaAfford):** These methods typically require category-specific training and often do not directly address the transfer of demonstrated tool-use skills to novel object instances outside their training distribution.
- **Keypoint and Retrieval-Based Methods (e.g., KETO, GIFT, task-oriented grasping, robust grasp transfer, retrieve-align pipelines):** While effective for tasks like grasping, these methods often rely on sparse keypoints or coarse alignment, which are insufficient for recovering the dense local geometric frames required for complex manipulation skills.

SemAnCorr distinguishes itself by providing a dense surface correspondence that aims to transfer complete functional signatures without task-specific training. By combining semantic anchor selection with functional map propagation, it seeks to simultaneously achieve semantic consistency (where to interact) and geometric coherence (how to execute the interaction), a balance that previous methods have often struggled to achieve.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method for establishing dense, vertex-level correspondence between object instances that enables zero-shot manipulation skill transfer. The motivation stems from several critical limitations in existing robotic manipulation systems:

1. **Generalization Across Geometric Variation:** A robot should ideally be able to reuse a manipulation skill learned on one object instance (e.g., a specific mug) on a new instance that serves the same function (e.g., a different mug with a different handle shape), even if their geometries are substantially different. Current methods often struggle with this generalization, especially when geometric disparities are significant.

2. **Capturing Functional Intent:** While many approaches can identify semantically corresponding interaction regions or affordances, they frequently fail to capture the full functional intent of manipulation skills. This intent involves not just *where* a robot should interact with an object, but also *how* (e.g., the precise local pose and orientation), *in what sequence*, and with what *relationship to the object's articulated structure*. This detailed functional understanding is crucial for executing complex manipulation tasks.

3. **Limitations of Dense Visual Descriptors and 3D Feature Fields:** Recent advancements have leveraged dense visual descriptors and 3D feature fields to represent objects. However, approaches that rely on nearest-neighbor feature matching in 3D often produce spatially incoherent correspondences. Such incoherence means that points that are locally adjacent on one object might map to widely separated or fragmented regions on another, preventing the recovery of consistent local geometric frames necessary for reliable skill transfer.

To address these motivations, the research sets forth the following key objectives:

- **Develop a Dense Correspondence Framework:** The core objective is to propose SemAnCorr, a training-free framework that establishes dense correspondence across object instances. This framework is designed to overcome the limitations of prior methods by ensuring the resulting correspondences are both semantically consistent and geometrically coherent.
- **Integrate Semantic Anchoring and Functional Maps:** The framework's design specifically aims to select semantically consistent anchor regions through a joint pose-correspondence optimization process. These anchors then act as constraints that are propagated smoothly over the object surface using functional maps, thereby embedding both semantic meaning and geometric smoothness into the correspondence.
- **Enable Zero-Shot Skill Transfer:** A key practical objective is to use these high-quality correspondences to enable zero-shot transfer of object-centric manipulation skills. This implies that a skill demonstrated once on a source object can be directly applied to a novel, previously unseen target object without any additional training or demonstrations for the target.
- **Establish a Comprehensive Evaluation Benchmark:** The paper aims to construct a dense correspondence benchmark using PartNet-Mobility to rigorously evaluate method performance. This benchmark is designed to assess two critical aspects: semantic accuracy (evaluating "where to interact") and geometric coherence (evaluating "how to execute the interaction").
- **Validate Real-World Applicability:** The research further aims to demonstrate that improvements in correspondence quality, as measured on the benchmark, directly translate into more reliable performance for real-world manipulation tasks. This validation involves deploying SemAnCorr in a robotic setup to transfer a single demonstration to various unseen objects.

In summary, the motivation is to bridge the gap between high-level semantic understanding and low-level geometric precision required for generalizable robotic manipulation, fostering a robot's ability to intuitively adapt skills to a diverse array of real-world objects.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, Semantic Anchored Correspondence (SemAnCorr), is a training-free framework designed to establish dense, vertex-level correspondences that are both semantically consistent and geometrically coherent. The approach proceeds in three main stages: 3D Semantic Acquisition and Clustering, Joint Alignment and Anchor Selection, and Semantic Anchored Functional Map propagation.

**A. 3D Semantic Acquisition and Clustering**
This stage aims to equip each 3D object mesh with rich semantic features and then partition its surface into semantically meaningful regions.

1. **Multi-view Observation Generation:** Given a triangular surface mesh M, a set of N virtual cameras is defined to generate RGB images {I_i} from distinct viewpoints using a differentiable renderer.
2. **Semantic Feature Extraction:**
 - For each rendered image I_i, per-patch semantic embeddings E_i are extracted using the pretrained SigLip2Vision model. To obtain rich representations, the last four hidden states of the model are averaged.
 - Each 2D patch embedding is then lifted to a 3D surface location using camera parameters and depth information from the rendering.
 - These lifted 3D patch embeddings are aggregated across all views. Each point p_j on the mesh is endowed with a semantic embedding s_j, computed as the mean of all patch embeddings whose lifted 3D location is nearest to p_j. This process results in a semantic point cloud P_s, where each point (p_j, s_j) has a 3D position and a corresponding semantic embedding.
3. **Semantic Clustering:**
 - The dimensionality of the semantic embeddings is reduced using Principal Component Analysis (PCA).
 - For each point, a joint feature vector is formed by concatenating the reduced semantic embedding with its normalized 3D position.
 - K-means clustering is applied in this joint feature space to obtain an initial partition of K clusters.
 - To ensure contiguous and coherent parts, these initial clusters are refined: spatially disconnected regions within a cluster are split into separate parts, and small fragments are merged into their nearest neighboring cluster.
 - This yields a set of K semantically meaningful parts {C_c}, each characterized by an average embedding ¯s_c.

**B. Joint Alignment and Anchor Selection**
This stage identifies a small set of high-confidence, semantically and geometrically consistent anchor pairs between two object meshes M^(1) and M^(2), which will serve as constraints for correspondence.

1. **Relative Cosine Similarity:**
 - To focus on part-discriminative signals rather than object-level category signals, the mean embedding (μ^(i)) of all clusters on each object M^(i) is subtracted from each cluster's average embedding ¯s^(i)_c, yielding ˜s^(i)_c.
 - A relative similarity matrix S is then computed, where S_cc' is the cosine similarity between ˜s^(1)_c and ˜s^(2)_c'.
2. **Bilateral Margin Anchor Selection:**
 - Candidate anchor pairs (c, c') are ranked using a bilateral margin confidence score. This score measures how strongly cluster c on M^(1) prefers c' on M^(2) over other options, and symmetrically, how much c' prefers c.
 - Specifically, m_row(c, c') quantifies c's preference for c' over other clusters on M^(2), and m_col(c, c') quantifies c's preference for c over other clusters on M^(1). The confidence conf(c, c') is the minimum of these two margins, ensuring mutual exclusivity.
 - The top α highest-confidence pairs are greedily selected, subject to a bijectivity constraint (each cluster can be part of only one anchor pair).
3. **Joint Pose-Correspondence Optimization:**
 - Initial anchor pairs based purely on semantic similarity may be geometrically inconsistent. To refine this, a rigid alignment (R, t) between the two objects and a soft cluster correspondence are jointly optimized.
 - Both objects are first normalized to the unit sphere to factor out global scale.
 - A joint score for each candidate pair (c, c') is defined as a weighted sum of geometric compatibility (geo) and relative semantic similarity (S_cc'). The geometric compatibility geo(c, c'; R, t) is computed as an exponential of the negative symmetric Chamfer distance between the transformed point cloud of cluster c and point cloud of cluster c'.
 - A loss function L(R, t) is formulated based on these joint scores, where soft assignment weights w_cc' (computed via softmax over scores for c') implicitly couple the pose optimization with the correspondence.
 - The loss is minimized via gradient descent with multiple initializations. A cosine learning rate schedule ensures that early iterations prioritize semantic similarity for rough pose alignment, while later iterations emphasize geometric compatibility for refinement.
 - The joint scores from the best optimization result are then used for the final anchor selection based on bilateral margin confidence, yielding a refined set of α anchors that are consistent in both semantics and geometry.

**C. Semantic Anchored Functional Map**
This final stage uses the selected semantic anchors to constrain a functional map, which then propagates a smooth, dense vertex-level correspondence across the entire object surface.

1. **Functional Map Basics:** A functional map C is a compact linear operator that describes how functions defined on one surface transform to another. It operates in a low-dimensional spectral basis derived from the Laplace-Beltrami eigenfunctions of each mesh, implicitly enforcing a smoothness prior. A dense pointwise map is subsequently recovered from C.
2. **Anchor Region Correspondence:** For each selected anchor pair (c, c'), vertex correspondences within these regions are established. This involves pre-registering the meshes using the optimal global alignment (R\*, t\*), normalizing the anchor regions (centering and scaling), and then performing a nearest-neighbor search to find vertex-to-vertex correspondences within these normalized anchor spaces, resulting in (V_anc_2, V_anc_1) pairs.
3. **Functional Map Initialization and Refinement:**
 - An initial functional map C ∈ R^(k×k) is fitted from a sparse set of n_kp keypoints subsampled within each anchor cluster. This prevents over-constraining the spectral basis. The fitting minimizes the Frobenius norm of the difference between transformed spectral embeddings of corresponding keypoints.
 - An initial dense pointwise map is recovered by matching each M^(2) vertex to its nearest neighbor in the spectral embedding space (Φ_2 C ≈ Φ_1).
 - C is then refined using a geometry-constrained variant of the ZoomOut algorithm. This iterative process progressively increases the basis dimension k and alternates between updating the functional map and the pointwise correspondence. At each step, the search for correspondences is restricted to k_nb spatial neighbors of the current match to maintain local smoothness, and interior anchor correspondences are re-pinned after every update to prevent drift.
 - This iterative refinement ensures the final dense correspondence is semantically grounded within the anchor regions and geometrically smooth across the entire surface.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluations aimed to assess SemAnCorr's ability to preserve semantic consistency and geometric coherence, and whether this translates to reliable zero-shot manipulation skill transfer.

**A. Dataset and Implementation:**
- **Dataset:** PartNet-Mobility, comprising over 100 articulated objects from seven categories (Scissors, Pliers, Eyeglasses, Knife, Suitcase, Bottle, Kettle).
- **Hyperparameters:** Fixed K=6 semantic clusters, α=3 semantic anchors, and k=30 Laplace-Beltrami basis functions across all experiments.
- **Performance:** Average computation time for dense correspondence for an object pair was approximately 6 seconds on an RTX 4090 GPU.

**B. Evaluation Metrics:**
- **Semantic Accuracy (sAcc):** Measures part-level semantic preservation. It is the fraction of target vertices whose ground-truth semantic label matches the label of its corresponding source vertex.
- **Geometric Coherency:** Assessed by two properties:
 - **Continuity (Cont):** The fraction of target mesh edges whose endpoints map to points on the source mesh that are within a predefined tolerance (τ = 2 * mean edge length of M2). This indicates preservation of local neighborhoods.
 - **Coverage (Cov):** The fraction of source vertices that receive at least one correspondence from the target mesh. This ensures global structure preservation and avoids collapsing many points onto a small region.
 - **Geometric Coherence Score (GCS):** The harmonic mean of Continuity and Coverage. GCS is designed as a single manipulation-oriented measure, emphasizing the weaker component to reflect the necessity of both properties for local interaction frame recovery.

**C. Baselines:**
- **FM-WKS:** A classical functional map method based on intrinsic geometric descriptors, representing geometry-only approaches.
- **Robo-ABC:** A 2D pixel-level semantic correspondence method using pretrained visual features, representing 2D-to-3D lifting approaches.
- **D3Fields:** Constructs per-vertex 3D descriptors by lifting DINOv2 features from multi-view renders, performing nearest-neighbor matching in feature space. This isolates the contribution of functional map propagation over point-wise matching.
- **DenseMatcher:** Combines SD-DINO features with a learned DiffusionNet refiner and functional maps, representing learned feature refinement approaches.

**D. Inner-Category Generalization:**
- **Semantic Accuracy:** SemAnCorr achieved an average semantic accuracy of 90.8% across all 7 categories, outperforming all baselines. This was a 6.2% improvement over D3Fields (84.6%), which was the next highest. Qualitatively, SemAnCorr visualizations showed smoother transitions in transferred color gradients compared to baselines.
- **Geometric Coherence Score:** SemAnCorr demonstrated the highest average GCS of 0.40 ± 0.12, which was more than double that of the next best method (D3Fields, 0.16 ± 0.09). While FM-WKS had high continuity, its coverage was low, suggesting it collapsed correspondences. D3Fields, despite its higher semantic accuracy, exhibited fragmented local mappings, leading to a lower GCS. SemAnCorr was shown to achieve a balance of high continuity and meaningful surface coverage simultaneously.

**E. Cross-Category Generalization:**
- **Semantic Accuracy:** SemAnCorr achieved the highest semantic accuracy on cross-category pairs (e.g., Scissors→Pliers: 89.7%, Kettle→Bottle: 87.8%). This indicates that the semantic anchor selection mechanism effectively generalizes beyond strict category boundaries to functionally related objects.
- **Anchor Confidence as Compatibility Signal:** The average semantic anchor confidence (from Section IV-B) was found to be highest for within-category pairs and functionally related categories (e.g., containers, hand tools), and lower for functionally dissimilar pairs. This suggests that the anchor confidence score can serve as a lightweight signal for estimating generalization compatibility between objects.

**F. Ablation Study:**
- **Removal of Semantic Clustering:** Substantially degraded geometric coherence (GCS), implying that clustering effectively suppresses noise in the lifted features.
- **Removal of Anchor Selection:** Caused the largest drop in semantic accuracy, confirming the critical role of semantic anchors in guiding accurate correspondence.
- **Removal of Functional Map Propagation:** Reduced both semantic accuracy and geometric performance, highlighting its necessity for combining semantic consistency with geometric smoothness.
- **Conclusion:** The study affirmed that the semantic consistency and geometric coherence achieved by SemAnCorr arise from the complementary contributions of its proposed components.

**G. Real-World Manipulation Skill Transfer:**
- **Tasks:** Five manipulation tasks were performed, ranging from simple to complex: banana peeling, pen opening, kettle pouring, water bottle twisting, and scissor cutting.
- **Setup:** Object meshes were acquired using SAM3D from RGB-D observations. Skills were represented as contact keyframes and relative motion segments obtained via kinesthetic teaching. Grasp generation was guided by transferred contact regions and poses, using GPG for physical feasibility.
- **Success Rates (out of 10 trials):**
 - For simple tasks (Tasks 1 & 2), SemAnCorr (8/10, 9/10) and D3Fields (7/10, 9/10) performed comparably.
 - For complex tasks (Tasks 3, 4, & 5), SemAnCorr (7/10, 7/10, 6/10) substantially outperformed D3Fields (3/10, 1/10, 3/10).
- **Observations:** D3Fields frequently failed in complex tasks because spatially incoherent correspondences led to incorrect local interaction frames, even when the correct semantic part was identified. SemAnCorr's remaining failures were primarily attributed to mesh-to-workspace alignment errors rather than correspondence quality.
- **Conclusion:** The real-world experiments confirmed that semantic accuracy alone is insufficient for successful manipulation skill transfer. Geometric coherence, which SemAnCorr explicitly optimizes for, is equally necessary to produce a robust manipulation-oriented representation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper, through the introduction of Semantic Anchored Correspondence (SemAnCorr), offers a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation and computer vision.

**Core Significance:**
SemAnCorr provides a novel, training-free framework for computing dense, vertex-level correspondences that simultaneously maintain semantic consistency and local geometric coherence across geometrically diverse object instances. This dual preservation of properties addresses a critical gap in existing methodologies for robot skill transfer. Semantic consistency ensures *where* a robot should interact with an object, while geometric coherence dictates *how* that interaction should be executed through the recovery of consistent local geometric frames. The paper rigorously demonstrates that both properties are indispensable for reliable skill transfer.

**Addressing Limitations of Previous Work:**
- **Beyond Sparse Matching:** SemAnCorr moves beyond sparse keypoint matching or coarse alignment methods, which often lack the precision needed for fine-grained manipulation.
- **Overcoming Incoherence in Dense Methods:** It rectifies the issue of spatially incoherent correspondences prevalent in 3D feature field approaches (e.g., D3Fields) by integrating functional map propagation, thereby enforcing global surface smoothness.
- **Enabling 3D Geometric Awareness:** Unlike 2D semantic correspondence methods, SemAnCorr inherently captures 3D geometric orientation and interaction poses, which are crucial for articulated object manipulation.

**Practical Impact on Robotic Manipulation:**
- **Zero-Shot Skill Transfer:** The most immediate impact is the enablement of more reliable zero-shot manipulation skill transfer. Robots can learn a skill once from a single demonstration on a source object and apply it to a wide range of geometrically varied, previously unseen objects that share functional semantics. This significantly reduces the need for extensive, object-specific demonstration collection, a major bottleneck in robot learning.
- **Enhanced Generalization:** By providing a robust object-centric representation that bridges visual demonstrations and executable robot actions, SemAnCorr facilitates greater generalization capabilities for robotic systems in unstructured environments.
- **Foundation for Complex Tasks:** The ability to recover precise local geometric frames opens avenues for transferring more complex and fine-grained manipulation skills, including those involving articulated objects or tools, which require accurate understanding of interaction poses and sequences.

**Potential Future Implications:**
- **Bootstrapping Data for Learned Policies:** A key future direction highlighted is the potential for SemAnCorr to bootstrap data for learned manipulation policies. Successful skill transfers to novel objects could automatically generate additional demonstrations, which could then serve as valuable supervision for fine-tuning deep reinforcement learning or imitation learning policies, further reducing the reliance on human data collection.
- **Advanced Manipulation Scenarios:** Extending the correspondence formulation to more complex manipulation scenarios, such as bimanual or dexterous manipulation, where maintaining geometric consistency across multiple contact points is paramount, represents a promising area for future research.
- **Human-Robot Interaction:** The framework's ability to interpret functional intent and transfer it across object variations could enhance human-robot collaboration, allowing robots to better understand and adapt to human demonstrations involving novel tools or objects.

In conclusion, SemAnCorr represents a notable advancement in providing a robust and flexible framework for achieving generalizable manipulation skills, addressing both the semantic and geometric fidelity required for effective robot interaction with the physical world.
