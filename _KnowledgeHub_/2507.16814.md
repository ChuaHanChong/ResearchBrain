# Semi-off-Policy Reinforcement Learning for Vision-Language Slow-Thinking Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> SOPHIA introduces a semi-off-policy reinforcement learning framework that enhances large vision-language models' (LVLMs) slow-thinking reasoning by leveraging the LVLM's own visual perception to ground external language model reasoning. The framework achieves state-of-the-art performance among open-source LVLMs, surpassing some closed-source models on challenging benchmarks like MathVision and OlympiadBench, while also reducing visual hallucinations.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) struggle with complex, multi-step "slow-thinking" reasoning tasks, often exhibiting pattern memorization rather than deep understanding.
- On-policy reinforcement learning for LVLMs is limited by the current policy's output space, making it difficult to explore and reinforce truly novel reasoning behaviors if initial capabilities are low.
- Direct off-policy reinforcement learning exacerbates visual hallucinations in LVLMs due to mismatches between visual features in external reasoning trajectories and the target LVLM's own visual perception.

## Method

- SOPHIA employs a ==semi-off-policy sampling method== where the trainable LVLM generates its own visual description (on-policy), which then conditions an external reasoning LLM to generate slow-thinking trajectories (off-policy).
- It implements a novel ==reward propagation mechanism==, assigning an outcome-based reward to the reasoning trajectory and propagating an averaged reward to the LVLM's visual description to incentivize useful visual grounding.
- An off-policy dataset is constructed by selecting correct and visually grounded trajectories, prioritizing shorter paths, to fine-tune only the language backbone of the LVLM while keeping the ==visual transformer frozen== to maintain alignment.

## Results

- InternVL3.0-38B + SOPHIA achieved an average gain of **+8.50%** over its base model, attaining state-of-the-art performance among open-source LVLMs on most multimodal reasoning benchmarks.
- SOPHIA outperformed closed-source models like GPT-4.1 on MathVision (**49.08%** pass@1) and OlympiadBench (**49.95%** pass@1), demonstrating enhanced capabilities in complex problem-solving.
- Qualitative analysis showed a significant reduction in both visual hallucinations (from **38%** to **16%**) and logical inconsistencies (from **54%** to **44%**) on MMMU Pro cases, validating the approach's effectiveness in improving reasoning quality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling visual perception (from the target LVLM) and logical inference (from a powerful external LLM) during trajectory generation effectively mitigates visual hallucinations and optimization conflicts in multimodal RL.
> - Propagating rewards back to the LVLM's visual descriptions ensures that the model is incentivized to produce not just accurate, but also *reasoning-relevant* visual interpretations.
> - Freezing the Visual Transformer (ViT) during the RL fine-tuning process is crucial for maintaining vision-language alignment, preventing long reasoning trajectories from degrading the model's visual understanding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) is a critical frontier in AI, aiming to equip machines with human-like understanding and reasoning abilities across visual and textual modalities. While LVLMs have made significant strides in tasks like image captioning and visual question answering, they often struggle with complex, multi-step "slow-thinking" reasoning, which is essential for tackling real-world problems such as mathematical geometry, scientific diagrams, and intricate logical puzzles.

This paper addresses a fundamental challenge in advancing LVLMs: moving beyond mere vision-text alignment and pattern memorization to cultivate genuine visual reasoning capabilities. The existing landscape for enhancing LVLM reasoning can be broadly categorized:

- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** Early attempts focused on SFT using generated reasoning trajectories (e.g., Chain-of-Thought). However, these methods often lead to "pattern memorization" rather than robust reasoning and can suffer from visual hallucinations if the synthetic data is noisy or misaligned with the model's visual perception.
- **On-Policy Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Inspired by successes in Large Language Models (LLMs) like DeepSeek-R1, researchers have explored on-policy RL (e.g., GRPO) for LVLMs. The main limitation here is that on-policy methods are constrained by the *current policy's* output space. If the LVLM initially lacks slow-thinking abilities, it's hard for on-policy RL to sample and reinforce such behaviors, thus reinforcing suboptimal existing behaviors rather than exploring truly novel reasoning paths.
- **Off-Policy Reinforcement Learning:** This paradigm offers the promise of learning from trajectories generated by *any* behavior policy, allowing models to learn beyond their current capabilities. However, a significant hurdle for LVLMs is the "visual hallucination" problem. If off-policy trajectories are generated by external models with different visual perception capabilities, the visual features embedded in these trajectories may not align with the target LVLM's own visual understanding. This mismatch creates optimization conflicts, exacerbating hallucinations and undermining learning.

SOPHIA positions itself as a crucial advancement by proposing a "semi-off-policy" approach that cleverly navigates the limitations of both on-policy and direct off-policy RL. It aims to bridge the gap by leveraging the LVLM's *own* visual perception while benefiting from the superior reasoning capabilities of external LLMs. By achieving state-of-the-art performance among open-source LVLMs on challenging benchmarks and even outperforming some closed-source models, SOPHIA significantly pushes the boundaries of multimodal reasoning, providing a more robust and scalable framework for future LVLM development.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a **simple, scalable, and effective Semi-Off-Policy Reinforcement Learning framework (SOPHIA)** to significantly enhance the visual slow-thinking reasoning capabilities of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs).

The motivations stem from critical shortcomings in existing approaches:

- **Addressing LVLMs' Lack of Genuine Reasoning:** Current LVLMs, primarily trained on vision-language alignment data, excel at recognition but struggle with multi-step, logical "slow-thinking" reasoning required for complex problems like advanced mathematics or scientific inquiry. This often manifests as mere pattern memorization rather than deep understanding.
- **Overcoming On-Policy RL Limitations:** On-policy RL, while successful in LLMs, is fundamentally bounded by the initial policy distribution of LVLMs. If an LVLM's initial policy lacks the ability to generate slow-thinking reasoning steps, on-policy methods cannot easily explore beyond these limited behaviors, hindering the development of true reasoning.
- **Mitigating Visual Hallucinations in Off-Policy RL:** While off-policy RL offers the advantage of learning from diverse trajectories, its direct application to LVLMs is problematic. When trajectories are generated by external (teacher) models, the visual features described in these trajectories may not align with the *target LVLM's own* visual understanding. This "mismatched visual perception" can lead to optimization conflicts and exacerbate visual hallucinations, where the model "sees" or describes things that aren't actually present or are misinterpreted.
- **Achieving Scalability without Expensive Annotations:** Many methods rely on human annotations or expensive closed-source model feedback for reward signals or data generation. The authors aim for a scalable solution that can leverage readily available (or synthesizable) resources without incurring high annotation costs or dependency on proprietary models for fine-grained visual quality assessment.
- **Improving Policy Initialization for Future Training:** The goal is not just to improve reasoning directly, but also to provide a better foundation for subsequent training stages, such as further on-policy reinforcement learning, preventing performance degradation and enabling continuous improvement.

In essence, SOPHIA seeks to empower LVLMs to "think slowly" and reason effectively by combining the best aspects of different RL paradigms while meticulously addressing their respective weaknesses, particularly the nuanced interplay between visual perception and logical inference.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SOPHIA (Semi-Off-Policy RL for vision-language slow-tHInking reAsoning) is structured around two main steps: **semi-off-policy sampling** and **policy optimization with propagated rewards**.

#### 3.1. Semi-Off-Policy Sampling

This step constructs a unique behavior model that blends the strengths of the trainable LVLM's visual understanding with an external LLM's reasoning prowess, circumventing the visual hallucination issue.

1. **On-Policy Visual Understanding from LVLM (π_θ):**
 - For a given image (I_i) and a "description prompt" (designed to elicit detailed descriptions), the *trainable LVLM* (π_θ) is prompted to generate comprehensive descriptions of the image (c_i). This is "on-policy" because the LVLM uses its *own* visual encoder and language generation capabilities to describe what it "sees."
 - **Rationale:** This is crucial for mitigating visual hallucinations. By ensuring that the visual context (c_i) comes directly from the LVLM's own perception, the generated reasoning trajectories will be grounded in the LVLM's inherent visual understanding, preventing conflicts arising from mismatched visual features in external models.

2. **Off-Policy Slow-Thinking Reasoning from LLM (¯π):**
 - Once the LVLM generates its visual description (c_i), this description is combined with the original query (x_q_i) to form an augmented prompt (ˆx_q_i).
 - This augmented prompt is then fed to an *open-source reasoning language model* (¯π, e.g., QwQ, DeepSeek-R1). This LLM generates a slow-thinking reasoning trajectory (y_i) based on the provided visual description and query. This step is "off-policy" as the reasoning LLM is external and not directly the trainable LVLM.
 - **Rationale:** Reasoning LLMs are often much stronger at complex logical inference than initial LVLMs. By conditioning their reasoning on the LVLM's *own* visual understanding, SOPHIA leverages powerful reasoning without imposing an external, potentially misaligned visual perception.

#### 3.2. Reward Evaluation and Propagation

This stage focuses on assigning meaningful rewards to both the reasoning trajectory and, crucially, to the visual understanding that underpins it.

1. **Reward for Slow Thinking (R(y)):**
 - An outcome-based reward (Eq. 4) is used for the generated reasoning trajectory (y_i). If the final answer extracted from the trajectory is correct (verified by rules or an evaluation model), R(y_i) = 1; otherwise, R(y_i) = 0.
 - **Rationale:** Fine-grained step-by-step supervision is often unavailable, making outcome-based rewards a practical choice.

2. **Reward for Visual Understanding (R(c)):**
 - To strengthen the link between the visual understanding (c_i) and the quality of reasoning, a reward (R(c_i)) is propagated back to each visual description.
 - For each description c_i generated by the LVLM, *N* reasoning trajectories are sampled from the reasoning LLM (¯π) conditioned on c_i. The reward for c_i is then the *average outcome score* of these N reasoning trajectories (Eq. 5).
 - **Rationale:** This ensures that the LVLM is incentivized to produce visual descriptions that are not only accurate but also *useful* for downstream reasoning. High-quality descriptions that lead to many correct reasoning paths receive higher rewards.

3. **Reward for Off-Policy Dataset (D) Construction:**
 - The final off-policy dataset D is constructed by selecting trajectories (x_q_i, v_i, y_i) based on a combined reward signal (Eq. 6). A trajectory is included if:
 - The visual understanding reward R(c_i) exceeds a predefined threshold (α).
 - The reasoning trajectory reward R(y_i) is 1 (i.e., the final answer is correct).
 - Among all correct trajectories for a given image-query pair that meet the visual reward threshold, the *shortest* trajectory is chosen.
 - **Rationale:** This selection strategy ensures that the training data contains both correct reasoning *and* visually grounded information. Prioritizing shorter trajectories helps mitigate "overthinking" and encourages concise, efficient reasoning.

#### 3.3. Policy Updating

1. **Off-Policy Optimization:** The LVLM (π_θ) is updated using an off-policy reinforcement learning objective based on importance sampling (Eq. 3).
2. **Policy Gradient:** The policy gradient (Eq. 7) is derived, leveraging a theoretical lemma (Lemma A.3 in Appendix A) which shows that under specific boundedness assumptions on the importance sampling ratio (which are ensured by appropriate sampling and reward design), approximating the off-policy objective by using average reward directly (i.e., treating the importance sampling ratio as 1) introduces only a small, controllable bias. This simplifies the optimization process.
3. **Training Details:** Only the language backbone of the LVLM is unfrozen and updated during training, while the visual transformer (ViT) is typically frozen (based on ablation studies) to maintain vision-language alignment. A warm-up phase using general visual question-answering data is performed to initialize the policy model and enhance its captioning abilities.

**In summary, SOPHIA's methodology is an elegant solution to the challenges of LVLM reasoning by decoupling visual perception from logical inference during trajectory generation. It uses the target LVLM's visual understanding to ground external LLM reasoning, then propagates rewards back to both components, enabling scalable and effective learning.**

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SOPHIA's effectiveness is rigorously demonstrated through extensive experiments on InternVL2.5 and InternVL3.0 models (8B and 38B parameters) across eight challenging multimodal reasoning benchmarks.

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:**
 - InternVL3.0-38B + SOPHIA achieves significant improvements, with an average gain of **+8.50%** over its base model.
 - It attains **state-of-the-art performance among open-source LVLMs** on most benchmarks.
 - Remarkably, it **outperforms some closed-source models** like GPT-4.1 on the highly challenging MathVision and OlympiadBench datasets, achieving 49.08% and 49.95% pass@1 accuracy, respectively. This highlights its strong problem-solving capabilities in complex domains.

- **Superiority over Other Fine-tuning Strategies:**
 - **Outperforms Supervised Fine-tuning (MPO):** SOPHIA consistently surpasses the mixed preference optimization (MPO) method, which typically requires millions of examples, demonstrating more efficient data utilization and greater effectiveness.
 - **Better Policy Initialization for On-Policy RL:** While direct on-policy RL (GRPO) on the base InternVL3.0-38B model shows performance degradation (as hypothesized due to limited initial policy space), SOPHIA not only prevents this drop but also serves as a **superior policy initialization** for subsequent on-policy GRPO training. Combining SOPHIA with GRPO leads to further substantial improvements, achieving an average pass@1 accuracy of 57.28% and performance comparable to Claude-3.7-Sonnet on general college-level questions.

- **Scalability Across Model Sizes:**
 - SOPHIA demonstrates effectiveness on both 8B and 38B parameter models. On the 8B scale, InternVL3.0-8B + SOPHIA achieves the best average performance among smaller open-source models, excelling on MMMU and OlympiadBench.

- **Enhanced Geometry Reasoning:**
 - SOPHIA significantly boosts performance on geometry problems in DynaMath, MathVision, and OlympiadBench, particularly on complex tasks, validating its efficacy in leveraging visual understanding for spatial and mathematical reasoning. For example, InternVL3.0-38B + SOPHIA shows substantial gains in both Plane Geometry and Solid Geometry tasks.

- **Ablation Studies Insights:**
 - **Freezing ViT:** Freezing the Visual Transformer (ViT) during training consistently improved average performance, especially on general-purpose benchmarks like MMMU. This suggests that long reasoning trajectories might interfere with vision-language alignment, and freezing the visual encoder helps maintain stability and efficiency.
 - **Reward Design:** The full reward design (outcome-based reasoning reward, caption-based visual reward, and shortest trajectory selection) is crucial. Removing the caption reward or the shortest trajectory constraint degrades performance, underscoring the importance of both components for effective learning.
 - **Warm-up Training:** Initial fine-tuning with general visual question-answering data (warm-up) consistently enhanced performance, indicating that a strong initial captioning ability is beneficial for generating high-quality reasoning trajectories.

- **Training Data Analysis:**
 - **Versatility:** SOPHIA's performance gains are not solely tied to private datasets; it also shows improvements when trained on public datasets like MathV360K. This indicates its generality.
 - **Quality over Quantity:** Experiments suggest that the *quality and difficulty* of training questions are more critical than sheer data quantity, especially for challenging benchmarks like MathVision and OlympiadBench.
 - **Scaling Law:** Average performance improves with more data initially, but returns diminish, and for some specific benchmarks (MathVista, MathVerse, DynaMath), increasing data size does not always yield consistent gains, possibly due to limited data coverage of certain problem types.

- **Qualitative Reduction in Hallucinations:**
 - Manual evaluation on MMMU Pro cases revealed that SOPHIA significantly reduces both visual hallucinations (from 38% to 16%) and logical inconsistencies (from 54% to 44%). This qualitative finding strongly supports the paper's core motivation that on-policy visual understanding improves the grounding of reasoning.

In summary, the findings demonstrate SOPHIA's exceptional ability to imbue LVLMs with robust slow-thinking reasoning, showcasing its efficiency, scalability, and capacity to address critical limitations of previous RL approaches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SOPHIA represents a significant step forward in the field of multimodal AI, particularly for developing more intelligent and reliable Large Vision-Language Models. Its potential significance and impact are multifaceted:

- **Pioneering a Novel RL Paradigm:** SOPHIA introduces a crucial innovation by demonstrating the efficacy of a semi-off-policy RL framework for LVLMs. It elegantly solves the dilemma of needing diverse reasoning trajectories (off-policy) without compromising the model's own visual grounding (on-policy). This hybrid approach provides a new blueprint for training complex multimodal agents, offering a path beyond the limitations of purely on-policy or direct off-policy methods.
- **Breaking Performance Barriers:** By achieving state-of-the-art results among open-source LVLMs and even outperforming some closed-source giants like GPT-4.1 on challenging reasoning tasks (MathVision, OlympiadBench), SOPHIA pushes the boundaries of what is possible with accessible models. This provides a strong incentive for further open-source development and democratizes advanced AI capabilities.
- **Mitigating Hallucinations and Enhancing Reliability:** The qualitative analysis unequivocally shows a significant reduction in visual hallucinations and logical inconsistencies. This is paramount for real-world deployment, as trustworthy and grounded AI is essential in sensitive applications. SOPHIA's method of propagating visual rewards to encourage the generation of *reasoning-relevant* visual descriptions is a key insight.
- **Scalability and Resource Efficiency:** The framework's ability to achieve high performance without relying on expensive human annotations or proprietary model feedback for visual quality assessment makes it highly scalable and resource-efficient. This is critical for advancing AI research and development, especially for large models that require vast amounts of training data.
- **Improved Foundation for Future Learning:** SOPHIA's role as a superior initialization for further on-policy RL (e.g., GRPO) indicates its foundational strength. It enables continuous performance improvement without the degradation often seen when on-policy methods are applied directly to underdeveloped initial policies. This implies a more robust and adaptive learning curriculum for LVLMs.
- **Broadening AI Applications:** LVLMs with enhanced slow-thinking reasoning can unlock a new generation of AI applications.
 - **Education:** Intelligent tutoring systems capable of solving complex visual math problems (geometry, physics diagrams) and explaining solutions step-by-step.
 - **Science and Engineering:** Assisting researchers in interpreting complex scientific diagrams, experimental results, and engineering blueprints, potentially accelerating discovery and design.
 - **Healthcare:** Aiding medical professionals in analyzing visual diagnostic data (e.g., medical images combined with patient histories) for more accurate diagnoses.
 - **Robotics:** Improving robotic agents' ability to reason about complex environments and plan multi-step actions based on visual observations.
- **Driving Future Research:** The paper identifies several limitations, such as struggles with long-range visual dependencies, fine-grained recognition, and redundant reasoning. These point to clear directions for future research, including stronger visual encoders, adaptive curriculum learning, and more sophisticated reward modeling, ensuring a vibrant research trajectory.

In conclusion, SOPHIA is a landmark contribution that not only achieves impressive empirical results but also offers a fundamentally new perspective on how to effectively train multimodal AI for complex reasoning, paving the way for more capable, reliable, and widely applicable vision-language models.
