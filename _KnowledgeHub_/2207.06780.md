# An Empirical Evaluation of Four Off-the-Shelf Proprietary Visual-Inertial Odometry Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> An empirical evaluation compared four off-the-shelf proprietary Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO) systems—Apple ARKit, Google ARCore, Intel RealSense T265, and Stereolabs ZED 2—across diverse indoor and outdoor real-world environments. The study identified Apple ARKit as the most consistently robust and accurate, while Stereolabs ZED 2 showed the highest drift and inconsistency.

## Problem

- A lack of comprehensive, standardized performance evaluations for widely used closed, proprietary Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO) systems.
- Existing comparative studies often focus on open-source VIO algorithms or are limited to simple, short-range, and controlled environments.
- Engineers and researchers face difficulty in selecting suitable off-the-shelf VIO solutions for real-world robotic and AR/VR applications due to unclear performance characteristics under challenging conditions.

## Method

- Conducted a systematic empirical evaluation of four popular proprietary VIO systems: ==Apple ARKit==, Google ARCore, Intel RealSense T265, and Stereolabs ZED 2.
- Developed a custom data collection rig to capture synchronized motion data across all four devices in six distinct challenging indoor (corridors, stairs) and outdoor (campus, parking lot, urban roads) environments.
- Evaluated VIO performance using quantitative Final Drift Error (FDE) and qualitative trajectory consistency assessments against architectural floorplans or Google Maps, with all SLAM features like loop closure deactivated for a pure VIO comparison.

## Results

- Apple ARKit consistently achieved the lowest Final Drift Error (FDE) and most stable trajectories across nearly all tests, including high-speed urban roads (FDE of **2.68** m) and multi-story stairs (FDE of **0.19** m).
- Google ARCore performed well in indoor walking scenarios (e.g., FDE of **0.09** m for hallways) but experienced significant degradation or failure in high-speed vehicular tests or under abrupt lighting changes (e.g., FDE of **140.08** m for urban roads).
- Intel RealSense T265 showed reasonable indoor performance but suffered from scale inconsistency, while Stereolabs ZED 2 consistently displayed the highest FDE values and most severe rotational drift across most sequences (e.g., FDE of **409.25** m for urban roads).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Proprietary VIO systems exhibit significant variability in robustness, accuracy, and consistency across different real-world scenarios, particularly under rapid motion, poor lighting, or repetitive visual features.
> - Apple ARKit demonstrated superior stability and consistency in 6-DoF pose estimation across almost all challenging conditions, including high-speed vehicular movements, compared to other commercial solutions.
> - Limitations specific to different commercial systems include scale inconsistency (Intel RealSense T265), sensitivity to high speeds and illumination changes (Google ARCore), and significant rotational drift (Stereolabs ZED 2).
