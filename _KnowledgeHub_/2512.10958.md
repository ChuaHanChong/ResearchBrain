# WorldLens: Full-Spectrum Evaluations of Driving World Models in Real World

> [!summary] Summary
> WorldLens introduces a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating generative driving world models, assessing their fidelity across visual realism, geometric consistency, physical plausibility, and functional utility. The benchmark, along with a human-annotated dataset and an AI-powered auto-evaluator, reveals that no single existing model excels universally, and current models are far from human-level realism.

## Problem

- Current evaluation metrics for generative driving world models primarily focus on visual realism (e.g., LPIPS, FVD), which do not adequately assess critical aspects like physical causality, multi-view geometry, or functional reliability in generated environments.
- A lack of standardized and comprehensive evaluation protocols hinders systematic comparison among different generative world models and prevents a balanced understanding of their strengths and weaknesses.
- The absence of a framework to integrate subjective human perception, especially regarding physical plausibility and overall realism, with objective algorithmic metrics creates a gap in evaluating the trustworthiness of generated driving worlds.

## Method

- **WorldLens** establishes a ==full-spectrum benchmark== consisting of 24 metrics across five complementary aspects: Generation, Reconstruction, Action-Following, Downstream Task, and Human Preference, to comprehensively assess driving world models.
- WorldLens-26K, a large-scale dataset of over 26,000 human-annotated videos with quantitative scores and textual rationales, was curated to bridge the gap between objective metrics and subjective human judgments on world fidelity.
- WorldLens-Agent, a vision-language critic agent fine-tuned on WorldLens-26K, was developed to provide scalable, automated, and explainable evaluations that align with human perceptual and physical judgments.

## Results

- All evaluated state-of-the-art models were found to be significantly below an 'Empirical Max' performance, with human preference scores averaging 2-3 out of **10** for overall realism, indicating substantial room for improvement.
- DiST-4D demonstrated the most balanced performance across perceptual, geometric, and downstream utility metrics, while OpenDWM achieved the highest overall perceptual quality but underperformed in some downstream tasks.
- WorldLens-Agent exhibited strong alignment with human annotations in zero-shot tests, accurately predicting human perceptual and physical scores and providing consistent natural-language explanations, confirming its utility as an auto-evaluator.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - No single generative world model consistently excels across all fidelity dimensions (visual realism, geometric consistency, physical plausibility, functional reliability), indicating inherent trade-offs in current model designs.
> - Achieving high perceptual quality does not guarantee functional utility for downstream tasks or reliable behavior in closed-loop simulations, suggesting that physical and causal realism are more critical than mere visual fidelity for practical applications.
> - Human perception of world realism strongly correlates with underlying 3D and 4D geometric and temporal consistency, emphasizing the need for models to generate structurally coherent and stable environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Generative world models are recognized as a transformative development in embodied AI and simulation, capable of synthesizing realistic 4D driving environments. Despite advances in visual realism, a significant gap exists in the systematic evaluation of these models. Existing evaluation methodologies often prioritize frame quality and aesthetic metrics, such as LPIPS (Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity) and FVD (Fréchet Video Distance). These metrics, however, are considered insufficient for assessing critical aspects like physical causality, multi-view geometry, or the functional reliability of generated environments when subjected to control inputs. This deficiency has led to fragmented progress and challenges in comparing results across different models.

The paper identifies the absence of a standardized protocol for evaluating driving world models, analogous to established frameworks like the SAE Levels of Driving Automation for autonomous vehicle benchmarking. Previous work in 3D and 4D world modeling has moved beyond frame-based generation to encode 3D geometry and dynamics but often relies on limited perception metrics or qualitative observations. While some benchmarks evaluate agents within simulated worlds, they do not comprehensively assess the fidelity of the worlds themselves.

"WorldLens" is introduced to address this void by establishing a full-spectrum benchmark. It aims to provide a unified framework that evaluates not only the visual appearance of generated worlds but also their underlying geometric consistency, physical plausibility, and capacity to support reliable decision-making and control. This work attempts to transition the evaluation of driving world models from primarily appearance-driven assessments to a more holistic approach that encompasses both visual and behavioral fidelity.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce "WorldLens," a comprehensive benchmark designed to evaluate the fidelity of generative driving world models across multiple dimensions. The motivation stems from a recognized limitation in the current state of generative AI for embodied systems: while models can generate visually compelling 4D driving environments, existing evaluation methods are often insufficient to assess whether these generated worlds accurately preserve geometry, adhere to physical laws, or can support reliable autonomous control.

Specifically, the key objectives and motivations are:

1. **Address the Evaluation Gap:** Current metrics predominantly focus on visual realism and aesthetic qualities (e.g., frame quality), which provide limited insight into physical causality, multi-view geometry, or functional reliability under control. This gap hinders balanced progress and makes it difficult to compare models comprehensively. The work aims to provide a "standardized way" to overcome this.
2. **Establish a Unified Assessment Framework:** To move beyond fragmented and incomparable results, the authors propose "WorldLens" as a unified benchmark. It is designed to evaluate how well a generative model "builds, understands, and behaves within its generated world" by spanning five distinct aspects: Generation, Reconstruction, Action-Following, Downstream Task, and Human Preference. This holistic approach ensures coverage of visual realism, geometric consistency, physical plausibility, and functional reliability.
3. **Identify Model Trade-offs:** A core motivation is to uncover the inherent trade-offs in existing world models. The authors hypothesize that no single model excels universally across all fidelity dimensions (e.g., models with strong textures might violate physics, while geometry-stable models might lack behavioral fidelity). The benchmark is intended to systematically reveal these strengths and weaknesses.
4. **Align Objective Metrics with Human Perception:** Recognizing that certain aspects of world model quality, such as physical plausibility and overall realism, are inherently subjective, a significant objective is to integrate human judgment into the evaluation process. This led to the creation of "WorldLens-26K," a large-scale dataset of human-annotated videos with numerical scores and textual rationales. The motivation is to "transform subjective evaluation into learnable supervision."
5. **Develop a Scalable and Explainable Auto-Evaluator:** To overcome the scalability challenges of continuous manual human annotation, the research aims to develop "WorldLens-Agent." This evaluation model is distilled from human annotations to enable efficient, automated, and explainable scoring of generative worlds, predicting perceptual and physical scores along with natural-language explanations.
6. **Guide Future Model Development:** Ultimately, the project seeks to establish a "unified ecosystem" for measuring world fidelity. By standardizing evaluation based on how models "look" and how they "behave," the goal is to provide clear guidance for the design of future generative world models, pushing them towards greater physical realism, functional utility, and human alignment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in "WorldLens" involves a multi-faceted benchmarking approach, comprising five complementary evaluation aspects, a large-scale human-annotated dataset, and an AI-powered auto-evaluator.

#### 3.1. WorldLens Benchmark Framework

The benchmark decomposes the evaluation of driving world models into five primary aspects, each with several fine-grained dimensions, totaling 24 metrics:

1. **Generation:** This aspect assesses the fundamental quality of generated video sequences, focusing on visual realism, temporal stability, geometric correctness, and semantic consistency.
- **Subject Fidelity:** Measures the perceptual realism and semantic correctness of individual objects (e.g., vehicles, pedestrians) using class-specific binary classifiers.
- **Subject Coherence:** Evaluates the temporal stability of an object's visual identity (shape, color, texture) across frames using ReID embeddings.
- **Subject Consistency:** Quantifies fine-grained temporal stability of object-level semantics and geometry using DINO features to capture texture and spatial details.
- **Depth Discrepancy:** Measures the smoothness of depth variations over time, capturing geometric coherence, using monocular depth estimation and DINO v2 embeddings.
- **Temporal Consistency:** Quantifies global frame-to-frame smoothness in a learned appearance space (CLIP visual encoder), considering adjacent-frame similarity, jitter suppression, and motion-rate alignment.
- **Semantic Consistency:** Evaluates the smooth evolution of semantic layout using a pretrained SegFormer model to predict frame-wise masks and compute stability across labels, regions, and class distributions.
- **Perceptual Discrepancy:** Quantifies the overall perceptual gap between real and generated videos using Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) on I3D spatiotemporal embeddings.
- **Cross-View Consistency:** Measures geometric and photometric alignment between overlapping regions of adjacent camera views using LoFTR feature correspondences.

2. **Reconstruction:** This aspect evaluates the ability to recover a coherent 4D scene from generated videos. Generated sequences are lifted into a Gaussian Field (or similar 4D representation) and re-rendered.
- **Photometric Error:** Measures how accurately reconstructed scenes reproduce their input frames using LPIPS, PSNR, and SSIM on re-rendered images from original camera poses.
- **Geometric Discrepancy:** Assesses alignment between reconstructed geometry from generated videos and real-world structure using Absolute Relative Error (AbsRel) on rendered depth maps, within regions identified by Grounded-SAM 2.
- **Novel-View Quality:** Evaluates the perceptual realism of re-rendered frames from unseen camera trajectories using the MUSIQ image quality assessor.
- **Novel-View Discrepancy:** Quantifies the perceptual gap between novel-view renderings from generated and real reconstructions using FVD.

3. **Action-Following:** This aspect probes whether generated worlds support plausible driving decisions for pretrained planners, examining if synthesized scenes provide realistic visual and motion cues.
- **Displacement Error:** Measures the functional consistency between trajectories predicted from generated and real videos by a pretrained end-to-end planner (UniAD or VAD), using L2 distance.
- **Open-Loop Adherence:** Evaluates a pretrained policy's performance on generated videos in a non-reactive generative simulator using the Predictive Driver Model Score (PDMS).
- **Route Completion:** Measures long-horizon navigation stability in closed-loop simulation as the percentage of a predefined route completed before termination.
- **Closed-Loop Adherence:** Integrates motion quality and task success into a single metric, the Arena Driving Score (ADS), in a closed-loop simulation where agent decisions influence the environment.

4. **Downstream Task:** This aspect assesses the practical utility of generated videos by measuring how well 3D perception models, pretrained on real data, perform when applied to synthetic content.
- **Map Segmentation:** Evaluates the capacity of generated data to provide spatial and semantic cues for top-down BEV mapping using mIoU.
- **3D Object Detection:** Tests if generated data retains geometric cues for perceiving traffic participants using the nuScenes Detection Score (NDS).
- **3D Object Tracking:** Measures motion consistency and identity preservation across time using Average Multi-Object Tracking Accuracy (AMOTA).
- **Occupancy Prediction:** Evaluates accurate 3D reconstruction of spatial geometry and semantics using the RayIoU metric.

5. **Human Preference:** This aspect captures subjective scores to align algorithmic metrics with human judgment. Each dimension is rated on a 1–10 scale.
- **World Realism:** Overall authenticity and naturalness, including Overall, Vehicle, and Pedestrian Realism.
- **Physical Plausibility:** Whether the scene obeys intuitive physical and causal principles (e.g., continuity of motion, occlusion, object contact).
- **3D & 4D Consistency:** Spatial-temporal coherence of geometry and appearance across frames.
- **Behavioral Safety:** Whether traffic participants behave in predictable and risk-free ways consistent with driving norms.

#### 3.2. WorldLens-26K: Human Annotation Dataset

To bridge the gap between objective metrics and human perception, the authors curated WorldLens-26K, a dataset of 26,808 human-annotated video records. Ten annotators, divided into two independent groups, scored videos across the four Human Preference dimensions. Divergent ratings triggered re-evaluation. Annotators were provided with a structured interface displaying synchronized views of the generated video, semantic mask, depth map, and 3D bounding boxes, along with detailed documentation and examples. Each entry includes a discrete quantitative score and a concise textual rationale.

#### 3.3. WorldLens-Agent: Feedback-Aligned Auto-Evaluator

Leveraging WorldLens-26K, the authors developed WorldLens-Agent, a vision-language critic agent based on Qwen3-VL-8B. Through LoRA-based supervised fine-tuning, human perceptual and physical judgments were distilled into the agent. This allows it to predict perceptual and physical scores and generate natural-language explanations consistent with human reasoning. The agent aims to provide scalable, explainable auto-evaluation for future generative world models.

#### 3.4. Model Evaluation

The benchmark comprehensively evaluates several representative state-of-the-art driving world models, including MagicDrive, DreamForge, DriveDreamer-2, OpenDWM, DiST-4D, and X-Scene, across all five aspects.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The comprehensive evaluation conducted with WorldLens yielded several key findings regarding the current state of generative driving world models:

1. **No Universal Excellence:** A consistent observation across all five evaluation aspects is that no single world model excels universally. Models often exhibit strengths in certain areas (e.g., visual realism) while underperforming in others (e.g., physical plausibility or downstream utility). This divergence underscores that visual realism, geometric consistency, and functional reliability are complementary aspects, necessitating multi-dimensional benchmarking for a complete assessment.

2. **Generation Quality Varies and Needs Improvement:** All existing models were found to be notably below an "Empirical Max" performance, indicating substantial room for improvement in visual and temporal realism. While DiST-4D achieved the lowest Perceptual Discrepancy, it was outperformed by OpenDWM in Subject Fidelity and View Consistency. OpenDWM demonstrated the most balanced overall performance, potentially due to large-scale multi-dataset training. Conditioned approaches, such as DiST-4D and DriveDreamer-2, showed improvements (20–30%) in Depth and Cross-View Consistency through the use of ground-truth frames. This suggests that dataset diversity and effective conditioning strategies are more critical for consistent world modeling than mere perceptual fidelity.

3. **Reconstruction Reveals Geometric Instability:** Models like MagicDrive and DreamForge exhibited the weakest reconstruction capabilities, characterized by high Photometric Error and Geometric Discrepancy, often resulting in "dense floaters and geometric distortions" in 4D Gaussian Field reconstructions. In contrast, OpenDWM and DiST-4D achieved significantly better reconstruction, reducing errors by approximately 55% and producing more structurally coherent sequences. DiST-4D, with its RGB-D generation design, achieved the best Novel-View Quality. These results emphasize that temporal stability and geometric consistency are crucial for physically realistic and reconstructable world models.

4. **Limited Functional Reliability in Action-Following:** Despite some models showing strong open-loop performance (low Displacement Error and reasonable Predictive Driver Model Scores), all evaluated methods experienced significant performance degradation under closed-loop conditions, achieving only marginal Route Completion rates. Frequent failures, such as collisions and off-road drifts, indicate that current synthetic data is largely inadequate for reliable high-level control and planning in closed-loop simulations. This highlights the need for generative world models to achieve a higher degree of physical and causal realism to be effective in practical autonomous driving scenarios.

5. **Downstream Task Utility Dependent on Fidelity:** The practical utility of generated videos for downstream perception tasks varied considerably. DiST-4D demonstrated a leading performance across map segmentation, 3D object detection, and tracking tasks, often averaging 30–40% higher scores than other models. DriveDreamer-2 was notably strong in occupancy prediction. Conversely, MagicDrive performed weakest. Interestingly, OpenDWM, despite its strong perceptual quality, underperformed in detection and tracking (21.9% and 6.9% respectively), suggesting that large-scale, multi-domain training might not guarantee alignment with specific task distributions. This finding indicates that temporal conditioning and precise dataset alignment are critical for task-specific effectiveness.

6. **Human Perception Indicates Low Overall Realism:** Human preference evaluations revealed that current generative world models are "far from human-level realism," with average scores ranging from 2 to 3 out of 10. DiST-4D received the most balanced scores, leading in physical plausibility and behavioral safety. OpenDWM achieved the highest realism score but slightly lower physical consistency. A strong correlation was observed between World Realism and 3D & 4D Consistency, suggesting that human-perceived realism is closely linked to geometric and temporal stability. These subjective evaluations underscore the necessity of human-in-the-loop assessments to complement quantitative metrics.

7. **WorldLens-Agent Demonstrates Alignment with Human Judgment:** Zero-shot tests of the WorldLens-Agent on unseen videos showed strong alignment with human annotations, both in predicted numerical scores and textual rationales. The agent successfully captured nuanced subjective preferences and diagnosed complex failure patterns, demonstrating its ability to generalize beyond its training distribution and provide consistent, interpretable, human-aligned evaluations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of WorldLens represents a significant contribution to the field of embodied AI and generative modeling for autonomous driving. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Standardization of Evaluation:** WorldLens establishes a unified, transparent, and reproducible protocol for evaluating generative world models. By defining 24 standardized metrics across five complementary aspects—Generation, Reconstruction, Action-Following, Downstream Task, and Human Preference—it provides a comprehensive framework that was previously lacking. This standardization facilitates fair comparison between models and helps to move the field beyond fragmented, incomparable results.

2. **Holistic Assessment of World Fidelity:** The benchmark moves beyond superficial visual realism to evaluate deeper aspects of world fidelity, including geometric consistency, physical plausibility, functional reliability, and human perceptual alignment. This holistic approach encourages the development of models that are not only visually convincing but also physically sound and functionally reliable, which is crucial for safety-critical applications like autonomous driving.

3. **Guidance for Future Model Design:** The findings provide concrete insights and guidelines for the design of future generative world models:
- **Complementary Objectives:** The research highlights that visual realism, geometric consistency, and downstream usability are often complementary rather than interchangeable. Future models should aim for a balanced optimization across these dimensions.
- **Usability Beyond Perceptual Quality:** It demonstrates that high perceptual quality does not automatically translate to practical usability in downstream tasks. Aligning generated data with target task distributions is identified as more critical than mere image quality.
- **Importance of Geometry Awareness:** Explicit depth prediction and supervision are shown to significantly enhance reconstruction fidelity and downstream perception, emphasizing geometry as a core objective.
- **Joint Optimization:** The discrepancy between photometric and geometric metrics suggests the need for unified formulations that jointly optimize appearance and geometry through spatiotemporal regularization.
- **Robustness and Autoregressive Resilience:** Guidelines emphasize stabilizing foreground dynamics and enforcing cross-view and temporal consistency to mitigate drift and artifacts, especially crucial for long-horizon stability in closed-loop generation.

4. **Bridging the Human-AI Perception Gap:** The creation of WorldLens-26K, a large-scale human-annotated dataset with quantitative scores and textual rationales, directly addresses the challenge of integrating subjective human judgment into AI evaluation. This dataset serves as a foundational resource for training AI agents to understand and mimic human preferences, transforming subjective evaluation into learnable supervision.

5. **Scalable and Explainable Evaluation:** The development of WorldLens-Agent, an AI-powered auto-evaluator distilled from human preferences, enables scalable, explainable, and human-aligned scoring of generative worlds without continuous manual labeling. This tool has the potential to accelerate research and development by providing consistent and interpretable feedback on model performance.

6. **Impact on AI Safety and Trustworthiness:** By providing rigorous metrics for physical plausibility and behavioral safety, WorldLens has direct implications for AI safety and trustworthy simulation. It helps mitigate the risks associated with deploying models that appear realistic but behave unpredictably or unrealistically. Reliable evaluation of generative simulators can accelerate advancements in safe-driving research, synthetic data generation, and policy testing under controlled, yet realistic, conditions.

7. **Broader Applications:** While focused on driving scenarios, the framework can inspire principled evaluation methods in other embodied AI domains, such as robotics, augmented/virtual reality (AR/VR) simulation, and general world-model research.

Despite its comprehensive nature, the authors acknowledge limitations, including its current focus on driving environments, potential annotator biases in human preferences, and inherent limitations of the AI evaluation agent. However, by releasing the benchmark, dataset, and evaluation agent, WorldLens aims to foster an open and collaborative ecosystem, guiding future world models towards systems that are not only visually authentic but also physically and behaviorally reliable.
