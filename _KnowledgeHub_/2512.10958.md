# WorldLens: Full-Spectrum Evaluations of Driving World Models in Real World

> [!summary] Summary
> WorldLens introduces a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating generative driving world models, assessing their fidelity across visual realism, geometric consistency, physical plausibility, and functional utility. The benchmark, along with a human-annotated dataset and an AI-powered auto-evaluator, reveals that no single existing model excels universally, and current models are far from human-level realism.

## Problem

- Current evaluation metrics for generative driving world models primarily focus on visual realism (e.g., LPIPS, FVD), which do not adequately assess critical aspects like physical causality, multi-view geometry, or functional reliability in generated environments.
- A lack of standardized and comprehensive evaluation protocols hinders systematic comparison among different generative world models and prevents a balanced understanding of their strengths and weaknesses.
- The absence of a framework to integrate subjective human perception, especially regarding physical plausibility and overall realism, with objective algorithmic metrics creates a gap in evaluating the trustworthiness of generated driving worlds.

## Method

- **WorldLens** establishes a ==full-spectrum benchmark== consisting of 24 metrics across five complementary aspects: Generation, Reconstruction, Action-Following, Downstream Task, and Human Preference, to comprehensively assess driving world models.
- WorldLens-26K, a large-scale dataset of over 26,000 human-annotated videos with quantitative scores and textual rationales, was curated to bridge the gap between objective metrics and subjective human judgments on world fidelity.
- WorldLens-Agent, a vision-language critic agent fine-tuned on WorldLens-26K, was developed to provide scalable, automated, and explainable evaluations that align with human perceptual and physical judgments.

## Results

- All evaluated state-of-the-art models were found to be significantly below an 'Empirical Max' performance, with human preference scores averaging 2-3 out of **10** for overall realism, indicating substantial room for improvement.
- DiST-4D demonstrated the most balanced performance across perceptual, geometric, and downstream utility metrics, while OpenDWM achieved the highest overall perceptual quality but underperformed in some downstream tasks.
- WorldLens-Agent exhibited strong alignment with human annotations in zero-shot tests, accurately predicting human perceptual and physical scores and providing consistent natural-language explanations, confirming its utility as an auto-evaluator.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - No single generative world model consistently excels across all fidelity dimensions (visual realism, geometric consistency, physical plausibility, functional reliability), indicating inherent trade-offs in current model designs.
> - Achieving high perceptual quality does not guarantee functional utility for downstream tasks or reliable behavior in closed-loop simulations, suggesting that physical and causal realism are more critical than mere visual fidelity for practical applications.
> - Human perception of world realism strongly correlates with underlying 3D and 4D geometric and temporal consistency, emphasizing the need for models to generate structurally coherent and stable environments.
