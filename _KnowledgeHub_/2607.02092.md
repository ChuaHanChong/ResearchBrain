# Guided Action Flow: Q-Guided Inference for Flow-Matching Vision-Language-Action Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> This research from University College London introduces Guided Action Flow, a method to enhance the performance of frozen flow-matching Vision-Language-Action policies like SmolVLA on robot manipulation tasks. It uses a separately trained action-chunk critic to guide the policy's reverse-time action sampler at inference time, achieving up to a 14.0 percentage point increase in success rate on specific LIBERO tasks and smaller gains on held-out multi-family tasks.

## Problem

- Adapting large Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies to distribution shifts or compounding errors in robot manipulation tasks is challenging.
- Fine-tuning entire VLA policies is computationally intensive, requires significant hardware, and is difficult to validate with limited task-specific data.
- Current VLA policies, while capable, often lack consistent reliability across all task instances, especially under varying conditions.

## Method

- Introduces **Guided Action Flow**, an ==inference-time adaptation== framework for *frozen* ==flow-matching== VLA policies like ==SmolVLA==.
- Trains a smaller, auxiliary ==action-chunk critic== using sparse success/failure rollouts generated by the base VLA policy, conditioned on ==frozen VLM hidden-state task features==.
- During the VLA's ==reverse-time denoising== process, the critic provides ==value-gradients== to adjust the flow velocity (with ==ensemble uncertainty gating==), biasing action generation towards higher-value outcomes without altering the base policy's parameters.

## Results

- Improved success rates by up to **+14.0 percentage points** on single LIBERO spatial tasks, for example, from **68.0%** to **82.0%**.
- Achieved a **+10.0 percentage point** gain on multi-family validation tasks (**46.0%** to **56.0%**) and a **+2.5 percentage point** gain on locked held-out test tasks (**65.0%** to **67.5%**) when using a VLM hidden-state conditioned critic.
- Demonstrated that while the guidance mechanism actively influences closed-loop outcomes, critic generalization across unseen tasks and effective uncertainty-aware guidance remain key bottlenecks for broader robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The iterative nature of flow-matching policies inherently allows for effective test-time guidance, enabling adaptation through external signals without retraining the core policy.
> - A lightweight critic, trained with sparse success/failure labels, can provide actionable gradients that enhance the performance of a complex VLA policy's continuous action-chunk sampler.
> - Effective task conditioning (e.g., using the base VLA's own language model hidden states) and diverse multi-family training data are crucial for the critic's generalization capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of robot manipulation, specifically focusing on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies. VLA policies have emerged as a method for enabling robots to perform tasks conditioned by natural language instructions. Early efforts in language-conditioned manipulation combined pre-trained vision-language features with imitation learning or 3D action representations. More recent VLA systems, such as RT-1, RT-2, PaLM-E, Open X-Embodiment, Octo, OpenVLA, π0, FAST, and SmolVLA, have scaled policy learning by integrating larger robot datasets, advanced language-vision backbones, and sequence-generation architectures. These developments have enhanced robotic capabilities, particularly for benchmarks like LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, and LIBERO-PRO, which test robustness under various distribution shifts.

Despite advancements, a challenge remains in adapting these increasingly capable VLA policies when task distributions shift, compounding errors occur, or multiple action options exist. Traditional fine-tuning of the entire VLA policy can be computationally intensive, hardware-dependent, and difficult to validate with limited task-specific data.

This paper addresses the adaptation challenge by exploring a modular approach that bypasses full policy fine-tuning. It draws inspiration from generative modeling, where guidance methods modify intermediate samples using external gradients or conditional scores, and from control literature on diffusion and flow policies used for planning and policy improvement. Specifically, it builds upon the concept of Q-Guided Flow (QGF), which investigates test-time value-gradient guidance for flow policies in reinforcement learning.

The work distinguishes itself from standard offline reinforcement learning (RL) in that its action generator is a pre-trained VLA with its own language-conditioned flow sampler, rather than a policy trained from scratch for a fixed task family. Furthermore, the adaptation objective is minimally disruptive: to preserve the base VLA policy and alter only its inference trajectory. This approach positions the research as a bridge between established VLA policy development and modular, inference-time adaptation techniques, particularly for resource-constrained environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to empirically investigate whether a Q-guided inference mechanism can be effectively applied to a *frozen* flow-matching VLA policy. Specifically, the authors aim to determine if a learned action-chunk critic, trained from real success and failure rollouts, can guide the reverse-time flow sampler of a pre-trained SmolVLA policy, thereby improving its performance on LIBERO manipulation tasks without retraining the base policy.

The motivation behind this specific approach is multifaceted:
- **Cost-effectiveness and Modularity:** Full fine-tuning of large VLA policies is often resource-intensive, requiring substantial computational power and data. It can also be challenging to validate when only small amounts of task-local data are available. By keeping the base VLA policy frozen and introducing a smaller, separately trained critic, the framework offers a more modular and potentially cost-effective adaptation path. This is particularly relevant for settings like consumer-GPU environments, which informed the choice of SmolVLA as the base policy due to its compact nature.
- **Leveraging Flow-Matching Properties:** Flow-matching and diffusion-style policies generate actions through an iterative transport process from noise to data. This iterative nature inherently creates an opportunity for test-time guidance, where external signals can influence the intermediate sampling steps to steer the action generation towards more desirable outcomes.
- **Improving Competent but Imperfect Policies:** The approach is attractive when a frozen VLA policy is already competent enough to generate plausible action candidates but not consistently reliable to solve every task instance, especially under distribution shifts or accumulating errors. The critic's role is not to replace the policy but to provide a continuous gradient that biases the policy sampler towards higher-value actions.
- **Addressing a Technical Question:** A significant technical challenge is whether a lightweight critic, trained from potentially sparse success and failure rollouts, can produce gradients that are helpful rather than detrimental within a high-dimensional continuous action-chunk sampler of a complex VLA model. The design must correctly account for the specifics of the VLA's internal flow convention, such as SmolVLA's reverse-time sampling.
- **Advancing Inference-Time Adaptation:** This research contributes to the broader goal of developing methods that allow for adaptive behavior in robots without altering their core, pre-trained capabilities. By focusing on inference-time modifications, the work explores a design space distinct from traditional training-time interventions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, Guided Action Flow, operates as an inference-time adaptation layer around a frozen flow-matching VLA policy, specifically SmolVLA. The methodology comprises three main components: data collection and processing, critic training, and Q-guided flow update during inference.

**A. Problem Setting and Base Policy:**
The work considers a frozen VLA policy, denoted π_θ, which generates an action chunk a_0:H-1 from a visual observation, robot proprioception, and language instruction. The base policy, SmolVLA, remains unchanged throughout the process. The critic, Q_ϕ, is trained to predict a value based on policy-side observation/state features (f_o), a candidate action chunk (a_0:H-1), and an optional task feature (e_τ). The deployment constraint dictates that the critic should only utilize features available to the policy at inference time, avoiding privileged simulator state for its inputs, although simulator success signals can be used for training targets.

**B. SmolVLA Reverse-Time Flow Convention:**
A crucial aspect of the methodology is correctly interpreting SmolVLA's reverse-time flow convention. During training, intermediate actions x_t are constructed as x_t = tϵ + (1 − t)a, and the model predicts a velocity v_t = ϵ - a, where ϵ is noise and a is the clean action chunk. During inference, the process integrates from t = 1 towards t = 0. The estimated clean action is derived as ˆa(x_t, v_t, t) = x_t - tv_t. The authors emphasize that understanding this sign convention is critical for applying guidance, as a misplaced sign could misdirect the clean-action estimate. The critic is applied only to the physical action dimensions relevant to LIBERO tasks, leaving padded dimensions unaltered.

**C. Rollout Dataset and Targets:**
Critic datasets are constructed from rollouts generated by the *frozen* SmolVLA policy in the LIBERO environment. For each episode, overlapping action chunks of horizon H are created. The critic's action input is the sequence a_i:i+H-1, and the observation feature is the policy-preprocessed state at the beginning of the chunk. The target for the critic is a sparse success-to-go signal. If success (s_j = 1) is observed at step j, the target for a chunk starting at step i is γ^(j*-i), where j* is the first step of success after i. If no future success occurs, the target is 0. After the first success in an episode, targets are set to 1. Training and validation splits are performed at the episode level to prevent data leakage between adjacent chunks from the same rollout.

**D. Critic Architecture and Task Conditioning:**
The critic is implemented as a multilayer perceptron (MLP). It takes flattened observation features, action chunks, and optional task features as input.
- **Task Conditioning Options:**
 1. **Task-ID Feature:** Useful for same-task tuning but lacks transferability across different task families.
 2. **Hashed Bag-of-Token Feature:** Lightweight and portable but was empirically found to be too weak for reliable cross-task gains.
 3. **Frozen SmolVLA VLM Hidden-State Task Features:** The strongest variant, obtained by mean-pooling hidden states from the frozen SmolVLA VLM text pathway over non-padding task tokens. This approach leverages the base policy's own language representation without introducing a new trainable text encoder.
The critic is trained using a mean-squared error (MSE) objective: L(ϕ) = E_ (f_o,a,e_τ,y) [(Q_ϕ(f_o, a, e_τ) - y)^2].

**E. Q-Guided Flow Update:**
During each reverse-time denoising step of the SmolVLA sampler, Guided Action Flow modifies the flow velocity.
1. **Clean Action Estimation:** The base denoiser output is detached, and the estimated clean action ˆa is computed.
2. **Critic Query:** An ensemble of K critics {Q_ϕ_k} is queried for the estimated clean action, and their mean value, ¯Q(ˆa), is computed.
3. **Action Gradient Calculation:** The gradient g = ∇_ˆa ¯Q(ˆa) is calculated. This gradient represents the direction in the action chunk space that increases the predicted value.
4. **Gradient Clipping and Gating:** The gradient g is clipped to a maximum norm c to prevent instability. It is then gated using an uncertainty-aware mechanism based on ensemble disagreement. The gate value m is calculated as m = max(m_min, exp(-ασ_Q)), where σ_Q is the per-sample standard deviation across critic values, α is an uncertainty scale, and m_min is a minimum gate value. This mechanism reduces guidance when critics disagree significantly.
5. **Velocity Adjustment:** The guided velocity is computed as v_guided_t = v_t - m clip(g, c) / β. The subtraction sign is derived from the reverse-time SmolVLA convention; decreasing v_t in the direction of the positive gradient g results in an increase of the estimated clean action in a direction that raises critic value. The base VLA parameters are not updated.

**F. Runtime Procedure:**
The process is summarized as follows:
1. Initialize x_t from the SmolVLA sampler at t=1.
2. For each reverse-time denoising step:
 a. Compute base velocity v_t with frozen SmolVLA.
 b. Estimate clean action ˆa = x_t - tv_t.
 c. Compute action gradient g from the critic ensemble.
 d. Clip g and gate it with ensemble disagreement.
 e. Update the velocity to v_guided_t = v_t - m clip(g, c) / β.
 f. Integrate the SmolVLA sampler using v_guided_t.
3. Return the final action chunk.

The guidance parameters (β, c, α, m_min) control the trade-off between guidance strength and potential deviation from the base policy. For experiments, a K=3 critic ensemble, β=2, gradient clip norm 1.0, uncertainty scale 10, and minimum gate 0.1 were used, with critics having a hidden dimension of 768 and depth of 4, trained for 30 epochs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Guided Action Flow was structured around three key experimental questions concerning feasibility, transferability, and generalization.

**A. Baseline Anchors:**
Initial evaluations of the frozen SmolVLA baseline checkpoints demonstrated varying success rates:
- **LIBERO vanilla:** 65.0% success (65 out of 100 episodes).
- **LIBERO-Plus spatial subset:** 78.0% success (39 out of 50 episodes).
- **LIBERO-PRO zero-shot:** 1.0% success (1 out of 100 episodes).
These baselines established that the frozen policy is functional on standard LIBERO and some LIBERO-Plus tasks but performs poorly on LIBERO-PRO in a zero-shot configuration, indicating that QGF is primarily applicable to settings where the base policy already exhibits non-trivial competence.

**B. Single-Task QGF:**
The initial assessment of QGF's feasibility focused on a single LIBERO spatial task using an adaptive-gated critic ensemble. This experiment aimed to confirm if the guidance mechanism could improve real closed-loop rollouts.
- **Seed Window 3000:** Success rate improved from 68.0% (34/50) to 82.0% (41/50), a gain of +14.0 percentage points.
- **Seed Window 4000:** Success rate improved from 82.0% (41/50) to 86.0% (43/50), a gain of +4.0 percentage points.
These results support the claim that a learned critic can provide useful gradients during frozen SmolVLA inference, leading to improved task success in a real environment. However, the authors note sensitivity to hyperparameters and critic checkpoint selection, indicating that gains are not universally guaranteed and regressions can occur.

**C. Spatial-Only Multi-Task Critic:**
An experiment testing a critic trained on multiple spatial tasks within a single LIBERO family showed a lack of reliable generalization. In a representative validation split, the frozen baseline achieved 53.3% success (32/60), while QGF candidates achieved at most 51.7% success (<= 31/60). This negative result indicated that simply increasing the number of tasks within one family was insufficient for robust transfer, suggesting issues with critic overfitting, weak gradients, or detrimental guidance on unseen tasks. This finding motivated the subsequent design choices regarding task-description features and multi-family training.

**D. Multi-Family Task-Description Critic:**
The most robust variant involved training the critic on a combination of LIBERO spatial and object task rollouts, leveraging frozen SmolVLA VLM hidden-state task features for conditioning. The training dataset comprised 500 episodes (spatial tasks 0-4 and object tasks 0-4), with 332 successes (66.4%).
- **Validation Performance:** On the validation set, the baseline frozen policy achieved 46.0% success (23/50). QGF improved this to 56.0% success (28/50), representing a clear gain of +10.0 percentage points.
- **Locked Held-Out Test Performance:** On a locked held-out test set, the baseline achieved 65.0% success (26/40). QGF improved this to 67.5% success (27/40), a positive but modest gain of +2.5 percentage points.

**E. Summary of Results:**
The findings collectively indicate:
1. **Active Guidance:** The guidance mechanism is active and demonstrably influences closed-loop outcomes, as evidenced by both single-task improvements and the aggregate gains in multi-family validation.
2. **Task Coverage and Conditioning Importance:** The limited generalization of the spatial-only critic contrasts with the stronger performance of the multi-family critic conditioned on VLM hidden-state task features, highlighting the importance of both data diversity and effective task representation for critic generalization.
3. **Validation vs. Held-Out Performance:** A notable observation is the discrepancy between validation and held-out test performance. The 10.0 percentage point gain on validation tasks reduced to a 2.5 percentage point gain on held-out tasks, suggesting that validation performance can overestimate true generalization.

In conclusion, the results support the hypothesis that critic gradients can enhance a frozen SmolVLA policy's behavior. However, they also reveal that critic generalization and uncertainty-aware guidance are central bottlenecks, preventing broader robustness claims at this stage.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research on Guided Action Flow presents several areas of significance and potential impact for the field of robot learning and artificial intelligence:

- **Modular Policy Adaptation:** The work demonstrates a practical, modular approach to adapting large VLA policies without necessitating expensive and complex full fine-tuning. This "frozen policy" paradigm allows for incremental improvements to a capable base policy using task-local data and a smaller, auxiliary critic. This modularity is particularly relevant for resource-constrained environments or applications where the integrity and stability of the core pre-trained VLA must be preserved.
- **Harnessing Flow-Matching Properties:** By successfully applying Q-guided inference to a flow-matching VLA policy like SmolVLA, the research highlights the inherent suitability of such generative models for inference-time adaptation. The iterative nature of flow-matching provides a natural "hook" for external guidance signals, distinguishing it from traditional policies that directly output actions. This could inspire similar modular adaptation techniques for other diffusion or flow-based control systems.
- **Enabling Localized Improvement:** The framework allows for the addition of task-local correction to a general instruction-conditioned VLA policy. This means that while the VLA retains its broad capabilities, a learned critic can address specific failure modes or improve performance on particular tasks where the base policy struggles, without having to retrain the entire system.
- **Advancing Offline Reinforcement Learning for Adaptation:** While not a traditional offline RL setup, the training of the critic from recorded rollouts of a frozen policy shares principles with offline RL. This work shows that even with sparse success/failure labels, a critic can provide actionable gradients, pushing the boundaries of what can be achieved with limited, real-world interaction data.
- **Identification of Key Challenges and Future Directions:** The modest gains on held-out tasks, despite stronger validation performance, clearly delineate "critic generalization" as a central bottleneck. This finding provides a focused direction for future research, including improving critic quality through richer visual states, more sophisticated reward formulations (e.g., pairwise ranking losses), and better uncertainty estimation (beyond ensemble disagreement).
- **Practical Insights for Implementation:** The paper offers practical lessons such as the critical importance of understanding the base flow sampler's sign and time convention, the necessity of episode-level data splitting for critic training to avoid data leakage, and the discipline required in hyperparameter selection using distinct validation sets. These insights are valuable for researchers and practitioners attempting similar inference-time guidance methods.
- **Conceptual Link to Residual Control:** The approach draws a conceptual parallel to residual reinforcement learning, where a learned component augments an existing controller. Here, the "residual" is a value-gradient correction applied within the action-generation trajectory of a frozen VLA sampler, providing a new perspective on how learned components can interact with and improve complex pre-trained systems.

While the current evaluation budget is limited and the held-out gains are modest, the work establishes the feasibility and potential of Q-guided inference for flow-matching VLA policies. It opens a promising avenue for making advanced robotic systems more adaptable and reliable in dynamic real-world scenarios.
