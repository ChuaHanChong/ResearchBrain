# World Translation: Minimizing Sim-to-Real Gap with Backward Dynamics Extraction and Unpaired Domain Translation

> [!summary] Summary
> The 'World Translation' framework addresses the sim-to-real gap by improving simulated dynamics through backward extraction of unobservable factors from transition outcomes and unpaired domain translation of these learned dynamics features between simulation and reality. This approach yields enhanced dynamics modeling accuracy and superior real-robot policy transfer, exemplified by a 0.1786 rad/s angular velocity tracking error on a Go2 quadruped with payload.

## Problem

- Bridging the 'sim-to-real gap' remains a core challenge, as policies trained in simulation often perform suboptimally or fail in real-world robotic systems.
- Existing learned dynamics models struggle with partial observability, leading to non-deterministic predictions when unobservable factors (e.g., sudden contacts) cannot be inferred from observation history.
- Current sim-to-real methods like Domain Randomization and System Identification have limitations, either producing overly robust but not optimal policies or being unable to model dynamics outside predefined structures.

## Method

- Introduces ==backward dynamics extraction== using a ==VAE== to infer unobservable dynamics features (z_t) directly from complete state transitions (o_t, a_t, o_t+1), rather than relying on incomplete historical observations, as part of the **World Translation** framework.
- Deploys a ==CycleGAN== for ==unpaired domain translation== to align the extracted dynamics features (z_t) between simulation and reality, overcoming the infeasibility of collecting perfectly paired real-sim data.
- Incorporates auxiliary objectives, including a ==blind decoder== and a ==domain classifier==, during VAE training to ensure the latent features explicitly encode meaningful dynamics and domain-specific information without information leakage.

## Results

- World Translation consistently achieved lower single-step prediction error (MSE) across diverse robotic platforms (humanoid, quadruped, manipulator) and conditions, with improvements up to **70%** in high-hidden-variable scenarios (e.g., **0.035** MSE vs. **0.119** for G1 Humanoid Uneven terrain).
- Demonstrated superior stability and maintained the lowest MSE in multi-step autoregressive rollouts for up to **30** steps on the Go2 Quadruped, showing significantly less divergence (**~2%** at 50 steps) compared to baseline methods.
- Achieved improved real-robot policy transfer on a Go2 quadruped with a payload, yielding a lower angular velocity tracking error (**0.1786 rad/s**) compared to both 'no adaptation' (**0.2475 rad/s**) and 'domain randomization' (**0.3306 rad/s**) strategies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unobservable dynamics information, crucial for deterministic state transitions, can be effectively inferred from the *outcome* of a transition, bypassing the limitations of history-based inference when prior observations are uninformative.
> - Translating abstract dynamics features in a latent space, rather than raw observations, is essential for successful unpaired domain translation between simulation and reality.
> - By extracting and translating dynamic features, the framework can correct simulator dynamics with real-world insights, enabling more realistic training environments for robot policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The deployment of robotic policies trained in simulation to real-world environments is a foundational challenge in robot learning, often referred to as the "sim-to-real gap." This work positions itself within the field of mitigating this gap, specifically focusing on improving the realism of simulated environments through learned dynamics.

Current dominant strategies for sim-to-real transfer include:
1. **Domain Randomization (DR)**: This approach involves training policies in simulations where physical parameters are randomized, aiming to make the policy robust to variations in the real world. However, DR can lead to policies that are robust but not optimally performant in any specific real-world scenario.
2. **System Identification (SysID)**: This method calibrates predefined physical models to match real-world observations. Its limitation lies in its inability to account for dynamics that are not captured by the predefined model structure.

More recent advancements involve **Learned Dynamics Models**, which infer transition dynamics directly from real-world data. These models, including residual variants that augment existing simulators with learned corrections, promise to capture complex, unmodeled phenomena that DR and SysID may miss. However, a significant limitation of existing learned dynamics models is the **partial observability problem**. Identical observation-action pairs can lead to different subsequent states due to unobserved factors (e.g., sudden contacts, internal states). Conventional methods often assume these unobservable factors can be recovered from the observation history, an assumption that may fail when the history is uninformative (e.g., instantaneous contact events).

This paper introduces "World Translation" as an approach that addresses this specific limitation. It differentiates itself from prior learned dynamics models by not relying on observation history to infer unobservable factors. Instead, it proposes extracting this information *backward* from observed transition outcomes. Furthermore, while unpaired domain translation methods, such as CycleGAN, have been applied to bridge the visual sim-to-real gap by translating images, this work applies such techniques to learned *dynamics features* themselves, aiming to align the underlying physics rather than just the visual appearance. By doing so, World Translation attempts to leverage the deterministic nature of simulators (which can observe transition outcomes) to inform learned models that struggle with partial observability, thereby offering a complementary solution to existing sim-to-real methodologies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to minimize the sim-to-real gap by enhancing the accuracy and realism of dynamics models used for policy training. This is achieved by specifically tackling a limitation in existing learned dynamics models: their inability to adequately account for unobservable factors that cause non-determinism in state transitions when the observation history is uninformative.

The motivation behind World Translation stems from two key observations regarding the complementary deficiencies of simulators and learned dynamics models:
1. **Simulators are deterministic but physically imperfect**: Simulators maintain a complete internal state and can deterministically predict future states. However, their physical models are often inaccurate or incomplete compared to reality, leading to a domain gap.
2. **Learned dynamics models are accurate but underdetermined under partial observability**: These models can learn accurate transition dynamics from real data. Nevertheless, when critical factors influencing a transition are not directly observable (e.g., internal states, unexpected external forces), and cannot be inferred from historical observations, the models become underdetermined, forcing them to average over possible outcomes and thereby reducing prediction accuracy.

The authors identify that the crucial information lacking in the forward prediction of learned models—the "unobservable dynamics information"—is intrinsically present in the *outcome* of a state transition. A simulator, despite its inaccuracies, *observes* these outcomes (even if they are simulated outcomes). This insight motivates a shift from attempting to predict transitions *forward* from incomplete history to *extracting* unobservable dynamics information *backward* from an observed transition outcome. This backward extraction directly captures the causes of transition uncertainty, even if they were unobservable prior to the transition.

However, a direct transfer of these extracted dynamics features between simulation and reality is not viable because the same underlying hidden factor (e.g., a collision) will manifest differently in the simulated and real domains due to systematic differences (domain characteristics). To address this, the research is motivated by the need for a mechanism to translate these extracted dynamics features across domains. This translation must preserve the semantic content of the hidden dynamics while transforming its "domain style" from simulation to reality, which the authors frame as an *unpaired domain translation problem*. Unpaired translation is chosen because collecting perfectly matched (or "paired") transitions across simulation and reality, especially for complex dynamic events, is practically infeasible.

In summary, the key objectives are to:
- Develop a method for backward dynamics extraction that infers unobservable factors from transition outcomes, rather than observation history, thereby making dynamics modeling more broadly reliable.
- Propose an unpaired domain translation framework that maps these extracted dynamics features between simulation and reality, enabling the use of simulation-observed transitions to predict real-world dynamics.
- Empirically validate this framework across diverse robotic platforms (humanoid, quadruped, manipulator) and demonstrate its ability to improve dynamics modeling accuracy and subsequently enhance real-robot policy transfer.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

World Translation instantiates a framework designed to bridge the sim-to-real gap by extracting dynamics features backward from observed transitions and translating these features across domains. The methodology consists of a formal problem formulation, a specialized VAE for backward dynamics extraction with auxiliary regularization, a CycleGAN for unpaired domain translation, and a specific training and deployment pipeline.

#### 3.1. A. Problem Formulation

The system dynamics are formalized with an inaccessible full state ($s_t$), accessible observation ($o_t$), and accessible action ($a_t$). The full state dynamics are deterministic: $s_{t+1} = g(s_t, a_t)$. However, due to partial observability (information gap between $s_t$ and $o_t$), the mapping $(o_t, a_t) \to o_{t+1}$ is non-deterministic.

To restore determinism in the observation space, two types of unobservable factors are introduced:
- **Time-varying hidden variables ($h_t$)**: These capture unobservable components of the full state that may vary unpredictably (e.g., collision events, specific contact states).
- **Time-invariant domain characteristics ($c$)**: These represent systematic differences between domains (e.g., friction coefficients, motor gains), which are implicitly learned representations.

With these, the dynamics become deterministic: $o_{t+1} = f(o_t, a_t, h_t, c)$. The core idea is that the effect of $h_t$ must be identifiable from an observed transition $(o_t, a_t) \to o_{t+1}$.

#### 3.2. B. Backward Dynamics Extraction

The method introduces a dynamics feature $z_t = \omega(h_t, c)$ in a latent space. This feature is extracted *backward* by an encoder $E_{\phi}$ that maps a full transition tuple to $z_t$:
$(o_t, a_t, o_{t+1}) \xrightarrow{\text{encode}} z_t$.
A decoder $D_{\theta}$ then reconstructs the next observation from the current observation, action, and the extracted feature:
$(o_t, a_t, z_t) \xrightarrow{\text{decode}} o_{t+1}$.

The implementation uses a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) for the encoder-decoder pair, where $E_{\phi}$ maps $(o_t, a_t, o_{t+1})$ to a latent distribution $(\mu, \log \sigma^2)$, and $z_t$ is sampled from $N(\mu, \text{diag}(\sigma^2))$. $D_{\theta}$ reconstructs $\hat{o}_{t+1}$ from $(o_t, a_t, z_t)$.

Two challenges arise during VAE training, addressed by auxiliary objectives:
1. **Challenge 1: Direct encoding of $o_{t+1}$**: The encoder might bypass learning meaningful dynamics features by directly encoding $o_{t+1}$ into $z_t$. To mitigate this, a "blind decoder" $D_{\psi}$ is introduced, which attempts to predict $\tilde{o}_{t+1}$ from $z_t$ alone ($D_{\psi}(z_t)$). The encoder is trained to minimize its reconstruction error while *maximizing* the blind decoder's prediction error through an adversarial objective ($L_{\text{blind}}$).
2. **Challenge 2: Information leakage in $(o_t, a_t)$**: The observation-action pair $(o_t, a_t)$ can contain partial information about both $h_t$ and $c$. While benign for $h_t$, it can prevent the encoder from explicitly encoding domain characteristics ($c$) into $z_t$, which is necessary for domain translation. This is addressed by:
 - A **domain classifier ($C_{\xi}$)**: This classifier predicts the source domain of $z_t$. Training $C_{\xi}$ to succeed forces $z_t$ to encode domain-specific information ($L_{\text{cls}}$).
 - **Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM)**: The decoder $D_{\theta}$ is implemented using FiLM layers, where $z_t$ conditions each layer's computation. This inductive bias encourages the decoder to rely on $z_t$ for domain information, rather than inferring it solely from $(o_t, a_t)$.

The full VAE training objective ($L_{\text{VAE}}$) combines reconstruction loss, KL divergence for regularization, the blind penalty, and the domain classification loss.

#### 3.3. C. Unpaired Domain Translation

Since $z_t = \omega(h_t, c)$ depends on the domain characteristic $c$, a feature $z_t^S$ extracted from the simulation domain (S) needs to be translated to a corresponding feature $z_t^R$ for the real domain (R) while preserving the underlying $h_t$. This is framed as an unpaired translation problem, as perfectly paired sim-real transitions with identical $h_t$ are not available.

The translation is implemented using a **CycleGAN** architecture. It consists of two generators ($G_{S \to R}$ and $G_{R \to S}$) and two discriminators ($D_S$ and $D_R$).
- $G_{S \to R}$ learns to translate $z_t^S$ to $\hat{z}_t^R$.
- $G_{R \to S}$ learns to translate $z_t^R$ to $\hat{z}_t^S$.
- Discriminators distinguish between real and generated latent codes in each domain.
- **Cycle consistency loss**: Ensures that a round-trip translation ($z_t^S \to \hat{z}_t^R \to \tilde{z}_t^S$) reconstructs the original feature ($z_t^S \approx \tilde{z}_t^S$), preserving content.
- **Identity loss (optional)**: Encourages generators to make minimal changes to features already belonging to the target domain.

#### 3.4. D. Training and Deployment Pipeline

- **Training**: The VAE (encoder $E_{\phi}$, decoder $D_{\theta}$, blind decoder $D_{\psi}$, classifier $C_{\xi}$) and CycleGAN (generators $G_{S \to R}, G_{R \to S}$, discriminators $D_S, D_R$) are trained jointly. The encoder-decoder is shared across both simulation and real domains to ensure a common latent space for dynamics features.
- **Deployment (Inference)**: During policy training or deployment, the method works as follows:
 1. A simulator performs a step, yielding a simulated transition $(o_t, a_t, o_t^S)$.
 2. The encoder $E_{\phi}$ extracts a dynamics feature $z_t^S$ from this simulated transition.
 3. The generator $G_{S \to R}$ translates $z_t^S$ to a real-aligned feature $\hat{z}_t^R$.
 4. The decoder $D_{\theta}$ uses $(o_t, a_t, \hat{z}_t^R)$ to predict an aligned real-world next observation $o_{t+1}^{S \to R}$.
 5. This $o_{t+1}^{S \to R}$ then *overwrites* the simulator's predicted state $o_t^S$, ensuring that the policy operates on trajectories consistent with the translated real-world dynamics.
 - During World Translation training, domain randomization in the simulator is disabled to maintain deterministic dynamics for feature extraction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of World Translation involved extensive experiments across multiple robotic platforms, analyzing dynamics modeling accuracy, latent space representation, ablation studies, and real-robot policy transfer.

#### 4.1. A. Dynamics Modeling Accuracy

The method was evaluated on G1 Humanoid, Go2 Quadruped, and R5 Manipulator platforms, each with "low-$h_t$" (minimal hidden variable influence) and "high-$h_t$" (significant hidden variable influence) conditions. Baselines included Direct Prediction (DirectPred), Raw Simulation (RawSim), Residual Dynamics (ResDyn), and Recurrent State Space Model (RSSM).

1. **Single-Step Rollout (Teacher-Forcing)**:
 - World Translation consistently achieved lower prediction error (MSE) than all baselines across all platforms and conditions (Figure 4).
 - The largest improvements were observed in "high-$h_t$" conditions where hidden variables significantly affected transitions (e.g., G1 Humanoid Uneven terrain: World Translation 0.035 vs. DirectPred 0.119; R5 Manipulator with external forces: World Translation 0.401 vs. DirectPred 0.741). This indicates success in capturing unobservable factors.
 - RSSM, a strong history-based model, showed improvements over DirectPred but remained substantially above World Translation in high-$h_t$ tasks (e.g., Go2 Quadruped with Payload: RSSM 0.298 vs. World Translation 0.148; R5 Force: RSSM 0.515 vs. World Translation 0.401), supporting the claim that backward extraction addresses a fundamental limitation of history-based inference.

2. **Multi-Step Rollout (Autoregressive)**:
 - Evaluated on the Go2 Quadruped "high-$h_t$" task to assess error compounding in closed-loop.
 - World Translation maintained stability and the lowest MSE for prediction horizons up to approximately 30 steps. At horizon 50, it exhibited only around 2% divergence (rollouts where base height or velocity exceeded physical bounds), significantly lower than DirectPred and ResDyn which diverged rapidly (Figure 5a).
 - RawSim showed zero divergence but its MSE saturated at a higher level, while RSSM also showed zero divergence but its MSE was higher than RawSim and World Translation (Figure 5b). World Translation demonstrated the best accuracy-stability tradeoff for operationally relevant horizons.

#### 4.2. B. Representation Analysis

Experiments on the ARX R5 manipulator, where external forces ($h_t$) interact with motor parameters ($c$), analyzed the latent representation $z_t$.

1. **Hidden Variable Information ($h_t$)**:
 - Lightweight decoders were trained to predict ground-truth external force magnitude ($h_t$) from various inputs (Table I).
 - The full transition $(o_t, a_t, o_{t+1})$ provided an R^2 of 0.73-0.78 for predicting $h_t$. The combined input $(o_t, a_t, z_t)$ achieved comparable predictive power (0.68-0.81% R^2).
 - This confirms that $z_t$ effectively encodes information about $h_t$, and the auxiliary components in the full model do not compromise this retention.
 - Predictive power for $h_t$ was largely preserved after translation (67-78% R^2).

2. **Domain Characteristic Information ($c$)**:
 - Domain classification accuracy from different inputs was used to assess $c$ encoding (Table II).
 - Without the auxiliary components (blind decoder, domain classifier, FiLM), $z_t$ alone showed poor domain classification (64-67%), and translated $z_{S \to R}$ classified as real at only 43% (near chance).
 - With the auxiliary components, $z_t$ alone achieved 94% domain classification accuracy, and translated $z_{S \to R}$ reached 91% accuracy as real. This indicates that the auxiliary components successfully forced $z_t$ to encode domain-specific information, enabling effective translation.
 - UMAP visualization (Figure 6) confirmed clear domain separation in the original latent space and successful alignment after translation.

#### 4.3. C. Ablation Study

1. **Translation in Latent Space**:
 - An attempt to translate at the observation level (mapping $(o_t, a_t, o_t^A)$ to $o_t^B$) failed, with discriminator losses dropping and generator losses diverging (Figure 7). This was attributed to the discriminators trivially distinguishing domains based on information leakage in $(o_t, a_t)$, preventing meaningful dynamics translation. This result justifies the design choice of abstracting to $z_t$ for translation.

2. **Translation Components**:
 - Ablation of CycleGAN components (Table III) showed that without cycle consistency, cycle error increased significantly (from 0.034 to 0.273) and prediction error degraded substantially (from 0.401 to 0.681).
 - Without identity loss, identity error doubled (0.233 to 0.463), indicating unnecessary modifications to in-domain samples, though prediction error was less affected than by removing cycle consistency.

#### 4.4. D. Downstream Policy Performance on Real Robot

A real-world experiment on a Go2 quadruped with a payload (high-$h_t$ condition) evaluated policy transfer. Policies were fine-tuned with different adaptation strategies.

1. **Results (Table IV)**:
 - World Translation achieved the lowest angular velocity tracking error (0.1786 rad/s) compared to "No Adaptation" (0.2475 rad/s) and "Domain Randomization" (0.3306 rad/s).
 - It maintained comparable orientation stability and average power consumption. Domain Randomization showed lower power but at the cost of responsiveness and higher tracking error.
 - The results confirm that improved dynamics modeling through World Translation translates to better command tracking performance on a physical robot.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents World Translation as a contribution to addressing the sim-to-real gap, particularly in scenarios where unobservable factors render traditional learned dynamics models insufficient. The significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Novel Approach to Partial Observability**: The core contribution of backward dynamics extraction offers a mechanism to infer the effects of hidden variables directly from transition outcomes, circumventing the limitations of history-based inference when prior observations are uninformative. This is particularly relevant for unpredictable events like contacts or external disturbances.
- **Unpaired Dynamics Feature Translation**: The application of cycle-consistent adversarial networks to dynamics features, rather than visual observations, represents a distinct advancement. This allows for aligning the underlying physics between simulation and reality without requiring difficult-to-obtain paired data, which significantly reduces data collection overhead for real-world adaptation.
- **Leveraging Complementary Strengths**: World Translation effectively integrates the strengths of simulators (deterministic prediction of states) with those of learned models (accuracy in capturing real-world dynamics). By correcting simulator dynamics using features translated from real data, it creates a more realistic training environment.
- **Demonstrated Effectiveness Across Diverse Platforms**: The empirical validation across humanoid, quadruped, and manipulator robots, covering various dynamic challenges (uneven terrain, payloads, external forces), suggests the method's generalizability beyond specific robot types or tasks.
- **Improved Real-World Policy Transfer**: The real-robot experiment on the Go2 quadruped demonstrated that the enhanced dynamics modeling directly translates to better policy performance, specifically achieving lower angular velocity tracking error compared to baseline adaptation strategies. This provides practical evidence of the method's utility for robot learning.
- **Potential for Robust Robot Learning**: By providing a more accurate and stable dynamics model, World Translation can enable the training of policies that are more robust to real-world complexities and uncertainties, potentially leading to safer and more capable autonomous robotic systems.
- **Reduced Data Requirements for Adaptation**: The unpaired nature of the domain translation means that real-world data collection for adaptation can be less stringent, requiring only samples of relevant dynamics rather than precise sim-real pairings.
- **Future Research Directions**: The work identifies several avenues for future research, including multi-step training to reduce error accumulation, online adaptation for continuous improvement, and extension to visual observations, which could further broaden its applicability and impact.
- **Addressing Unmodeled Phenomena**: While not recovering phenomena entirely absent from the simulator, the implicit representation of $z_t$ allows the model to capture and translate the *manifestation* of unmodeled dynamics, offering an advantage over strict system identification.

In conclusion, World Translation offers a method for bridging the sim-to-real gap by enhancing the realism of simulation dynamics, particularly in scenarios characterized by partial observability and unpredictable interactions. Its approach of backward dynamics extraction and unpaired feature translation provides a foundation for more robust and effective robot learning.
