# AHA-WAM:Asynchronous Horizon-Adaptive World-Action Modeling with Observation-Guided Context Routing

> [!summary] Summary
> AHA-WAM introduces an asynchronous, horizon-adaptive architecture for robot manipulation, decoupling the temporal scales of visual world planning and action execution. This approach achieves a 4.59x inference speedup and a 24.17 Hz control frequency (up to 56.95 Hz with distillation), while maintaining a 92.80% success rate on RoboTwin 2.0 tasks and 78.33% on real-world tasks.

## Problem

- Existing World-Action Models (WAMs) rigidly couple world prediction and action execution at the same temporal resolution, forcing the video world model to focus on short-horizon, often redundant frame variations.
- This temporal coupling limits the video branch's potential for embodied control, preventing it from forming extended latent plans over future visual states.
- Computational demands of world modeling lead to high inference latency, hindering real-time, closed-loop robot control and deployment.

## Method

- **AHA-WAM** employs a ==dual-Diffusion Transformer== (DiT) architecture with a low-frequency Video DiT world planner for long-horizon scene evolution and a high-frequency Action DiT executor for short action chunks.
- Observation-Guided Video-Context Routing (OVCR) dynamically adapts the planner's layerwise latent context to the latest real-time observations, preventing context staleness during asynchronous execution.
- Horizon-adaptive offset training and rolling planner memory enhance robustness to planner-executor phase shifts and maintain historical scene information for the asynchronous planner.

## Results

- Achieved an average **92.80%** success rate on 50 RoboTwin 2.0 tasks, surpassing the Fast-WAM baseline by **0.97** percentage points and matching performance of generalist VLA models without pretraining.
- Demonstrated significant inference efficiency, reducing closed-loop latency from **190.00 ms** (Fast-WAM) to **41.37 ms** (**4.59x** speedup) for AHA-WAM, and further to **17.56 ms** (**10.82x** speedup) for AHA-WAM-Flash.
- Attained an average **78.33%** success rate across four real-world manipulation tasks, outperforming WAM baselines and exhibiting strong robustness to generalization shifts comparable to large-scale pre-trained VLA models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the temporal scales of world modeling and action execution allows the video branch to form more meaningful, long-horizon plans while the action branch maintains high-frequency, reactive control.
> - Dynamically routing and adapting asynchronous planner context with real-time observations is crucial for maintaining performance when the planner and executor operate at different frequencies.
> - Optimizations like CUDA acceleration and ODE distillation can dramatically reduce inference latency, making complex world-action models viable for real-time robot control without sacrificing performance.
