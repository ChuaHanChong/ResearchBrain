# Hybrid Latent Reasoning with Decoupled Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> The HyLaR framework enhances multimodal large language models by integrating continuous visual latent reasoning with discrete text generation to overcome early semantic collapse. It introduces Decoupled Policy Optimization (DePO), a novel reinforcement learning algorithm that addresses optimization challenges in hybrid action spaces. This approach yields performance improvements of 7.33% on V* and 7.00% on HRBench-4K, and reduces hallucination by 7.11% on HallusionBench.

## Problem

- Conventional MLLMs suffer from 'early semantic collapse,' prematurely compressing high-bandwidth visual signals into discrete text tokens and losing fine-grained details.
- Existing 'Think-with-Images' paradigms, which use external tools for re-perception (e.g., zooming), introduce rigid bottlenecks, potential errors, and increased inference latency.
- Optimizing hybrid discrete-continuous action spaces for MLLMs faces challenges due to variance mismatches in importance sampling ratios and geometric mismatches (Euclidean assumptions on hyperspherical MLLM representations) with standard reinforcement learning.

## Method

- **HyLaR** seamlessly interleaves discrete text generation with ==continuous visual latent representations==, employing control tokens (<|canvas_start|>, <|canvas_end|>) to define an internal ==canvas mode== for visual working memory.
- A two-stage training process begins with ==Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)== to align continuous internal states with compressed visual features, followed by ==Reinforcement Learning (RL)== using the novel **Decoupled Policy Optimization (DePO)** algorithm.
- **DePO** models continuous latent actions using the ==von Mises–Fisher (vMF) distribution==, applies ==decoupled PPO surrogate losses== with separate, tighter clipping ranges for continuous updates, and uses ==closed-form KL regularization== to respect hyperspherical geometry.

## Results

- HyLaR-7B achieved notable performance gains on high-resolution perception and visual search benchmarks, with improvements of **+7.33%** on V*, **+7.00%** on HRBench-4K, and **+14.50%** on HRBench-8K (FCP) compared to Qwen2.5-VL-7B.
- The model enhanced general VQA accuracy across several benchmarks (e.g., **+8.00%** on MMVP, **+2.30%** on MMStar) and reduced visual hallucination by **7.11%** on HallusionBench.
- Ablation studies confirmed DePO's significant outperformance over other RL algorithms and the critical role of decoupled clipping ranges and vMF distribution for optimal and stable learning in the hybrid latent space.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Retaining visual information in a continuous latent space through an internal 'canvas mode' effectively mitigates early semantic collapse, allowing for finer-grained perception during reasoning.
> - Standard reinforcement learning algorithms are suboptimal for hybrid discrete-continuous MLLM action spaces due to inherent variance and geometric mismatches, necessitating a tailored optimization approach like DePO.
> - Modeling continuous latent representations with von Mises–Fisher (vMF) distributions, combined with decoupled trust-region clipping, provides stable and geometrically sound optimization for MLLMs' hyperspherical latent spaces.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning has enhanced the problem-solving capabilities of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) in complex vision-language tasks. However, a limitation in many prevailing MLLMs is the architectural bottleneck of "early semantic collapse." This occurs when high-bandwidth, continuous visual signals are prematurely compressed into discrete text tokens, leading to the loss of fine-grained visual information and an increased reliance on linguistic priors rather than grounded visual facts.

To address this, two primary approaches have emerged in the research landscape:
1. **"Think-with-Images" Paradigm**: This approach utilizes external tools (e.g., zoom, crop) to enable MLLMs to re-perceive image regions. While it can mitigate some issues of semantic collapse, it introduces practical challenges such as rigid bottlenecks, inference latency, and potential tool invocation errors, as reasoning is confined to predefined external operations.
2. **Latent Space Reasoning**: More recent pioneering works, including LVR, SkiLa, and Monet, have explored shifting visual reasoning into a continuous latent space. This approach aims to preserve visual fidelity by avoiding early discretization. However, the optimization of the resulting hybrid discrete-continuous action space poses a significant challenge. Current optimization methods, predominantly supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or vanilla reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms, often perform sub-optimally due to mismatches between their underlying assumptions and the characteristics of MLLM representations. Specifically, standard Gaussian assumptions for continuous spaces do not align with the native hyperspherical geometry of MLLM embeddings, and uniform clipping strategies are ill-suited for actions with distinct variance characteristics in a hybrid space.

HyLaR (Hybrid Latent Reasoning) is positioned within this landscape as a framework designed to overcome the limitations of both textual-only CoT and existing latent reasoning approaches. It aims to seamlessly interleave discrete text generation with continuous visual latent representations. The core contribution is an RL algorithm, Decoupled Policy Optimization (DePO), specifically engineered to address the challenges of optimizing this hybrid discrete-continuous action space, thereby enabling more effective and geometrically rigorous latent reasoning in MLLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The development of HyLaR is motivated by the identified shortcomings in current MLLM reasoning paradigms, particularly concerning their interaction with visual information. The key objectives and underlying motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Overcoming Early Semantic Collapse**: The primary motivation is to resolve the issue of "early semantic collapse" in MLLMs. Conventional MLLMs often convert rich, continuous visual signals into discrete text tokens at an early stage, which leads to the irreversible loss of fine-grained visual evidence. This forces the model to rely more on textual context and linguistic priors, potentially leading to visual grounding errors or inaccurate reasoning, especially in tasks requiring precise visual details. HyLaR aims to circumvent this by allowing visual information to be processed in a continuous latent space.

- **Enabling Seamless Hybrid Reasoning**: The objective is to facilitate a more natural and dynamic interplay between discrete textual logic and continuous visual processing. Current approaches either heavily rely on text or necessitate external tools for visual re-perception. HyLaR seeks to integrate an internal "visual working memory" by allowing the model to dynamically alternate between generating logical text tokens and manipulating continuous visual latent representations, enabling a deeper and more integrated reasoning process within the model itself.

- **Addressing Optimization Challenges in Hybrid Action Spaces**: A significant motivation stems from the limitations of existing optimization techniques when applied to hybrid discrete-continuous action spaces.
 - **Mismatch in Action Variance**: Traditional policy optimization methods often apply uniform trust-region constraints across all action types. However, discrete text tokens and high-dimensional continuous latent vectors exhibit fundamentally different variance characteristics in their importance sampling ratios. Applying a single clipping range can destabilize continuous latent updates or overly restrict textual policy exploration. HyLaR seeks to decouple these optimization processes to ensure stability and effectiveness for both action types.
 - **Geometric Mismatch in Latent Space Modeling**: MLLM hidden states, particularly after normalization layers (e.g., RMSNorm), naturally reside on a high-dimensional hyperspherical manifold. Standard continuous reinforcement learning algorithms typically assume a flat Euclidean geometry, often modeling actions with Gaussian distributions. This geometric mismatch leads to distortions in probability estimation and high sampling variance for KL divergence penalties, making effective policy regularization challenging. HyLaR aims to introduce a geometrically rigorous modeling approach that aligns with the native structure of MLLM latent representations.

- **Improving Fine-grained Perception and Multimodal Understanding**: Ultimately, the motivation is to enhance MLLMs' capabilities in tasks that demand precise visual perception and complex multimodal reasoning. This includes challenges like identifying tiny objects in high-resolution images, understanding subtle spatial relationships, performing accurate counting, and mitigating visual hallucinations that arise from insufficient visual grounding.

- **Maintaining Efficiency**: While striving for enhanced reasoning capabilities, a crucial objective is to achieve these improvements without introducing the computational overhead and latency associated with tool-based approaches that require multiple forward passes, re-encoding, or external API calls. HyLaR intends to operate within a single, efficient forward pass after initial training.

These objectives collectively aim to advance the state of MLLMs by providing a more effective, stable, and geometrically aligned framework for internalizing complex visual reasoning, thereby moving beyond the limitations of text-centric or external-tool-dependent paradigms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HyLaR (Hybrid Latent Reasoning) is a two-stage framework that integrates discrete text generation with continuous visual latent representations. It is built upon a standard MLLM architecture and trained through a supervised fine-tuning (SFT) phase followed by a novel reinforcement learning (RL) algorithm called Decoupled Policy Optimization (DePO).

#### 3.1. Overview of the HyLaR Framework

HyLaR extends a conventional MLLM's discrete decoding space into a hybrid discrete-continuous action space. This is achieved by introducing specialized control tokens: `<|canvas_start|>` and `<|canvas_end|>`. When the model needs to perform fine-grained visual deduction, it enters a "canvas mode." In this mode, textual generation is paused, and the hidden state from the previous layer is recurrently fed back as the input embedding for the next step, effectively bypassing the discrete token vocabulary. This continuous latent recursion functions as an internal visual working memory, continuing until the `<|canvas_end|>` token is emitted or a maximum canvas length is reached. This mechanism enables deep visual "thinking" without generating pixel-level images.

#### 3.2. Stage I: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

The SFT stage equips the MLLM with the ability to alternate between textual reasoning and latent visual thinking by aligning its internal latent states with semantic representations of ground-truth intermediate visual canvases.

- **Canvas Compression and Injection**: During SFT, ground-truth intermediate canvas images are first processed by a frozen SigLIP2 encoder to extract 729 patch tokens. To manage the resulting high sequence length, a learnable cross-attention compressor is employed. This compressor, configured with 2 layers and 16 query tokens, aggregates the patches into 16 compact canvas embeddings, denoted as `e`. These compressed `e` embeddings are then directly injected into the training reasoning trajectory, replacing original intermediate images with `<canvas>` placeholders bounded by `<|canvas_start|>` and `<|canvas_end|>`.
- **Joint End-to-End Optimization**: The model is trained to predict both the next discrete text token and the next continuous latent canvas embedding.
 - For text generation, the standard autoregressive cross-entropy loss (`L_CE`) is applied.
 - For canvas prediction, an MSE-based loss (`L_Canvas`) is used: `L_Canvas = (1/|S|) * Sum_{t∈S} ||h_t - e_{t+1}||^2`, where `h_t` is the LLM's hidden state at a canvas position, and `e_{t+1}` are the target compressed embeddings. Crucially, `e_{t+1}` is not detached during backpropagation, allowing gradients to flow into both the LLM backbone and the Canvas Compressor, thereby actively pulling extracted visual features into the LLM's semantic space.
 - The overall SFT objective is `L_SFT = L_CE + λL_Canvas`, where `λ` balances the two terms.
- **Inference Efficiency**: After SFT, the auxiliary Canvas Compressor is entirely discarded, ensuring no architectural overhead during inference.

#### 3.3. Stage II: Reinforcement Learning with DePO

Following SFT, reinforcement learning with DePO further refines the hybrid generation process. DePO is specifically designed to overcome the limitations of standard RL objectives in hybrid action spaces, which suffer from optimization mismatches (different variance of discrete vs. continuous actions) and geometric mismatches (Gaussian assumption vs. hyperspherical MLLM representations).

- **Hyperspherical vMF Modeling**:
 - For discrete text token positions, the policy follows a standard categorical distribution over the vocabulary.
 - For continuous latent positions (within `<|canvas_start|>` and `<|canvas_end|>`), DePO models the policy density using the von Mises–Fisher (vMF) distribution. This distribution is natural for unit hyperspheres, aligning with the geometry of layer-normalized LLM representations. The latent policy density is defined as `π_θ(a_t|s_t) = C_D(κ) exp(κ(μ_θ_t)^T ˜z_t)`, where `μ_θ_t` is the ℓ2-normalized hidden state, `κ` is a fixed concentration parameter, and `˜z_t` is the previously sampled latent vector from the old policy (the mode).
 - The log-density ratio for latent actions is derived as `log r_t = κ * (cos(μ_θ_t, ˜z_t) - 1)`, where the inner product of ℓ2-normalized vectors is cosine similarity. This formulation provides a deterministic, mode-referenced policy update.

- **Decoupled Surrogate Objectives**: To address the varied volatility of importance sampling ratios for discrete and continuous actions, DePO partitions each response into text positions (Z) and latent positions (S). It applies a dual-clip PPO surrogate independently to each set with separate clipping ranges.
 - `L_PPO(θ) = L_tok(θ | Z; ϵ_tok_l, ϵ_tok_h) + αL_lat(θ | S; ϵ_lat_l, ϵ_lat_h)`, where `ϵ` denotes clipping thresholds and `α` balances the terms.
 - A substantially tighter clipping range is used for latent positions (`ϵ_lat = 0.05`) compared to text positions (`ϵ_tok = (0.2, 0.28)`) to stabilize updates due to the higher volatility of continuous vMF actions.

- **Closed-form vMF KL Regularization**: To prevent policy degradation, position-specific KL penalties are used.
 - For text token positions, a standard sample-based KL penalty is applied.
 - For latent positions, the vMF assumption allows for an exact, closed-form KL divergence. The KL divergence between two vMF distributions with the same concentration `κ` reduces to a scaled cosine distance: `L_lat_KL = W_κ * (1 - cos(μ_θ_t, ˜z_t))`, where `W_κ = κA_D(κ)` is a constant weight. This provides a zero-variance KL estimate that respects the spherical geometry.

- **Total Objective**: The overall RL objective combines the decoupled surrogate losses with the position-specific KL penalties: `L_total = L_PPO + β_tok L_tok_KL + β_lat L_lat_KL`.

- **Practical Relaxation of ℓ2-Normalization (Supplementary Material)**: While the vMF derivations assume strict unit-norm embeddings, practical MLLM hidden states (often from RMSNorm) have meaningful magnitudes. Empirically, HyLaR adopts an "unnormalized surrogate" during implementation, replacing cosine-based terms with inner-product-based scores. This preserves magnitude information alongside directional alignment, leading to improved performance, particularly on fine-grained visual tasks.

- **Reward Design (Supplementary Material)**: The reward function consists of an accuracy reward (binary, evaluated by GPT-5) and a format reward (binary, penalizing deviations from the predefined explicit-implicit reasoning structure).
- **Dynamic Rollout Filtering (Supplementary Material)**: To ensure effective gradient signals, rollout groups with mean accuracies outside the range [0.1, 0.9] are discarded, ensuring sufficient reward variance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across various benchmarks evaluate HyLaR's effectiveness against competitive baselines, analyze component contributions through ablations, and provide insights into visual latent reasoning.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Comparison with Baselines

HyLaR-7B demonstrates consistent performance advantages over both standard MLLMs and state-of-the-art latent reasoning approaches. It also achieves competitive or superior results compared to agent-based models that rely on external tools.

#### 4.2. High-Resolution Image Perception and Visual Search Benchmarks (Table 1)

- HyLaR achieves significant performance gains on V*, HRBench-4K, and HRBench-8K, which involve locating tiny targets within high-resolution images. These tasks are prone to visual hallucinations and perceptual challenges.
- Compared to Qwen2.5-VL-7B, HyLaR-7B shows improvements of 7.33% on V* and 7.00% on HRBench-4K.
- Within the visual latent reasoning category, HyLaR-7B outperforms SkiLa and Monet, for example, by 5.24% and 3.67% on V*, respectively, and similar margins on HRBench benchmarks. This indicates its superior capability in fine-grained perception using internal latent reasoning.

#### 4.3. General VQA and Hallucination Benchmarks (Table 2)

- HyLaR demonstrates improvements in general VQA accuracy across MMVP, MMStar, SeedBench2Plus, and BLINK.
- It also shows significant mitigation of hallucination phenomena on HallusionBench, attributed to its capacity for deep exploration and fine-grained verification of visual content through latent space guidance. For instance, HyLaR-7B improves MMVP by 8.00% and HallusionBench by 7.11% over Qwen2.5-VL-7B.

#### 4.4. Efficiency Analysis (Table 3)

- HyLaR introduces minimal computational overhead. With 16 latent tokens and the SFT canvas extractor discarded at inference, it runs at 0.96x latency and 0.98x memory usage compared to the Qwen2.5-VL-7B long-CoT baseline.
- This contrasts with tool-based methods like DeepEyes, which incur significantly higher costs (2.28x latency, 3.64x memory).

#### 4.5. Scalability to Smaller Model Sizes (Table 4)

- A 3B-parameter variant, HyLaR-3B, consistently outperforms Qwen2.5-VL-3B and LVR-3B across all evaluated benchmarks, indicating that the framework scales effectively without loss of its advantages.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies

- **Ablation on the Number of Latent Steps (Fig. 4)**:
 - Introducing latent tokens (K_test > 0) consistently yields significant performance gains over a zero-latent baseline (e.g., +5.76% on V*).
 - SFT-trained models exhibit "over-thinking" degradation when inference latent steps (K_test) significantly exceed training steps (K_train).
 - RL optimization, particularly with longer training horizons, enables robust length generalization and mitigates "over-thinking," pushing peak accuracy beyond SFT's capabilities and demonstrating reasoning extrapolation.
- **Ablation on Different RL Algorithms (Table 5)**:
 - Models trained with conventional SFT consistently underperform HyLaR, highlighting the necessity of the latent approach.
 - DePO achieves superior performance compared to alternative RL algorithms (GRPO, DAPO, VLPO) initialized from the same HyLaR-SFT checkpoint. GRPO and DAPO even resulted in performance declines on some benchmarks, underscoring DePO's effectiveness for hybrid optimization.
- **Ablation on DePO Hyper-parameters (Tables 6 & 7)**:
 - **Latent loss weight `α`**: Setting `α=0` (no RL signal to latents) results in underperformance. An optimal balance is achieved around `α=0.5`; excessively high values (e.g., `α=2.0`) degrade performance by overwhelming discrete text gradients.
 - **Latent clipping ratio `ϵ_lat`**: Applying a clipping ratio equivalent to text tokens (`ϵ_lat = 0.2`) leads to performance drops, while overly aggressive clipping (`ϵ_lat = 0.01`) suppresses useful updates. An optimal range (e.g., `ϵ_lat = 0.05`) validates the necessity of decoupled, tighter clipping for continuous vMF actions.
- **Ablation on Latent Distribution Assumptions (Table 8)**:
 - The vMF distribution consistently outperforms a standard Gaussian distribution for modeling continuous latent embeddings during RL.
 - This suggests that directional alignment (cosine similarity) is more effective for high-dimensional LLM hidden states, which encode semantic information primarily in vector direction, compared to spatial Euclidean distance.
 - The vMF distribution naturally regularizes against "norm explosion," stabilizing RL training by focusing on angular updates.
- **Effect of ℓ2-Normalization Relaxation (Table S1, Supplementary Material)**:
 - Using an unnormalized surrogate (inner-product based scores) instead of strictly enforcing ℓ2-normalization yielded an average performance gain of +2.87%. This gain was most pronounced on fine-grained, reasoning-intensive tasks, indicating that allowing adaptive magnitude scaling for latent embeddings benefits hybrid policy optimization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HyLaR framework and its accompanying DePO optimization algorithm represent a notable advancement in the field of multimodal large language models (MLLMs), with several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Mitigation of Early Semantic Collapse**: HyLaR provides a principled and effective solution to the "early semantic collapse" problem prevalent in MLLMs. By enabling continuous visual latent reasoning, it allows models to retain and process fine-grained visual evidence, leading to more grounded and accurate understanding of complex visual information. This can reduce reliance on potentially inaccurate linguistic priors.

- **Novelty in Reinforcement Learning for Hybrid Spaces**: The Decoupled Policy Optimization (DePO) algorithm is a key contribution. By addressing the specific challenges of optimizing hybrid discrete-continuous action spaces—namely, the geometric mismatch with hyperspherical MLLM representations and the distinct variance characteristics of discrete vs. continuous actions—DePO introduces a geometrically rigorous, stable, and effective method for policy optimization in such complex environments. This could influence future RL algorithm designs for similar hybrid tasks.

- **Enhanced MLLM Capabilities**: The demonstrated superior performance across high-resolution image perception, visual search, general VQA, and hallucination diagnostic benchmarks indicates that HyLaR significantly improves the core capabilities of MLLMs. This enhancement translates into more reliable and trustworthy AI systems for tasks requiring detailed visual analysis.

- **Efficiency without Architectural Overhead**: Unlike tool-based approaches that incur substantial latency and memory overhead through external invocations and re-encoding, HyLaR integrates latent reasoning internally within a single forward pass during inference. Its minimal computational cost makes it a practical solution for deploying more capable MLLMs.

- **Improved Generalization and Extrapolation**: The ablation studies demonstrate that DePO's RL optimization enables MLLMs to generalize reasoning to longer latent step horizons, a capability that pure supervised fine-tuning lacks. This improved extrapolation suggests that HyLaR-trained models can adapt more flexibly to varying levels of reasoning depth required by different tasks.

- **Broader Impact on Agentic AI**: The ability to seamlessly interleave discrete textual logic with continuous internal "visual thinking" paves the way for more sophisticated and robust agentic MLLMs. Such models could perform complex planning and decision-making in environments that demand both abstract reasoning and fine-grained interaction with visual cues, potentially leading to advancements in robotics, autonomous systems, and advanced human-computer interaction.

- **Foundational for Future Research**: The work opens avenues for future research, including extending this hybrid RL paradigm to open-ended agentic environments and exploring scaling laws for even longer-horizon latent planning. The insights into hyperspherical modeling and decoupled optimization for hybrid action spaces could also inform representation learning and control strategies beyond MLLMs.
