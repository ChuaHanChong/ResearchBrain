# Scaling by Diversified Experience for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced SyVLA, a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that balances robust robotic task execution with preserved visual-language understanding. The model demonstrates an average real-world task success rate of 0.73 in-domain and 0.64 out-of-distribution, while maintaining strong performance on multi-modal benchmarks.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often suffer from 'catastrophic forgetting' of their inherent visual-language understanding and reasoning capabilities when optimized for action learning.
- Imitation learning (IL) in VLA models lacks robustness in closed-loop control, leading to task failures due to accumulated errors and out-of-distribution observations.
- Applying reinforcement learning (RL) to large-scale VLA models with high-dimensional action spaces is prone to instability, policy drift, and performance collapse in real-world scenarios.

## Method

- SyVLA employs a dual-system architecture (VLM and Action Expert) connected by learnable Feature Query Tokens, along with a three-stage training pipeline (pretraining, task fine-tuning, and real-world RL).
- An annotation-free Intention Decoupling algorithm uses gradient-norm masking on Feature Query States to separate control-relevant information from high-level reasoning contexts, enhancing action precision.
- A Similar-Sample Guided RL pipeline stabilizes real-world RL by merging semantically similar expert imitation learning data (with a fixed advantage of 1.0) with online rollout data.

## Results

- SyVLA achieved the highest average success rates on real-world robotic tasks, with **0.73** in-domain and **0.64** out-of-distribution, demonstrating superior generalization compared to baselines.
- The model maintained competitive or superior performance on several multi-modal benchmarks (e.g., leading on AI2D, MME, HallBench), indicating effective preservation of VLM knowledge and reasoning.
- Ablation studies confirmed that Intention Decoupling improved success rates by **0.43**, and the Similar-Sample Guided RL pipeline prevented policy collapse (**0.00** success rate without it), validating their critical roles.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling control intentions from high-level reasoning through gradient-based masking can significantly improve action precision and reduce hesitation in VLA models.
> - Integrating expert demonstrations with a fixed advantage assignment during RL effectively stabilizes policy updates and prevents catastrophic performance collapse in large VLA models.
> - Balancing action competence with preserved visual-language understanding and reasoning is achievable through a combined architectural and training strategy, which enhances out-of-distribution generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent an evolving area within artificial intelligence, aiming to enable robots to understand human instructions, perceive environments, reason, and execute physical actions. Recent advancements in VLA models have been significantly driven by the availability of large-scale, high-quality robotic datasets, often collected through expert human teleoperation, and the development of Visual-Language Models (VLMs). Pretraining on extensive datasets, sometimes exceeding 10,000 hours of teleoperation data, has been shown to facilitate dexterous manipulation.

However, the field faces two primary challenges that hinder the path toward general embodied intelligence. First, VLA models often prioritize action learning, which can result in a degradation or "catastrophic forgetting" of the underlying VLM's inherent visual-language understanding and logical reasoning capabilities. This contrasts with human intelligence, where broad task competence often arises from knowledge transfer across domains. While some prior work has attempted to address this through mixed training on multi-modal datasets or by employing architectures like Mixture of Experts (MoE) or intermediate "Fast Token" representations, these methods have exhibited limitations in achieving a balance between action competence and VLM capability preservation, or in handling dexterous performance without behavioral stalling.

Second, imitation learning (IL), a common training paradigm for VLA models, is constrained by a discrepancy between its training objective and actual task success. During closed-loop control, accumulated errors can lead agents into out-of-distribution (OoD) observations, causing task failures. Reinforcement Learning (RL) is considered a potential remedy for this, as it learns from on-environment rollouts. However, applying RL to multi-billion-parameter VLA models with high-dimensional continuous action spaces presents considerable instability issues, including policy drift and potential capability collapse. Real-robot RL further introduces challenges such as instability, safety risks, and difficulty with deformable object manipulation, which existing solutions (e.g., world models, specific RL algorithms, human-in-the-loop correction) have not fully resolved for VLA-scale models in a generalizable manner. The research thus positions itself within the context of these unresolved issues, seeking to develop a VLA model that maintains strong VLM capabilities while achieving robust and stable real-world robotic task execution.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce SyVLA, a robust Vision-Language-Action model designed to address significant challenges in VLA model deployment in real-world scenarios. This overarching goal is motivated by and broken down into several specific objectives:

1. **Mitigate Entanglement of High-Level Reasoning and Low-Level Control:** Existing VLA models, especially those operating in a "Think-Before-Act" manner, often exhibit imprecise actions or hesitant behavior due to implicit control representations carrying redundant or "leaked" information from higher-level reasoning processes. This entanglement can confuse the action expert, degrading dexterous performance. The objective is to develop a mechanism to isolate control-relevant features from reasoning contexts, thereby making the control intention more precise and effective.

2. **Stabilize Policy Optimization in Real-World Reinforcement Learning:** VLA models trained with imitation learning often lack robustness in closed-loop control due to error accumulation and distribution shift. While reinforcement learning can address this by optimizing for task success through environment interaction, its application to large-scale VLA models with high-dimensional action spaces is prone to instability, policy drift, and catastrophic performance collapse in real-world settings. The motivation is to devise an RL pipeline that can stabilize policy updates, mitigate these instabilities, and achieve higher success rates on long-horizon, sparse-reward tasks on real robotic platforms.

3. **Preserve Vision-Language Understanding and Reasoning Capabilities:** Many VLA models, in their pursuit of action competence, experience "catastrophic forgetting" of the underlying VLM's inherent visual-language understanding and logical reasoning abilities. This work aims to avoid such degradation, arguing that broad task generalization in humans stems from knowledge transfer through vision-language reasoning. Therefore, a key objective is to achieve a satisfactory balance between robust robotic task execution and the preservation of core VLM capabilities, including common-sense knowledge and reasoning.

4. **Achieve Strong Out-of-Distribution Generalization:** The ability to generalize to unseen scenarios and instructions is critical for real-world deployment. The research seeks to develop a model that not only performs well in in-domain tasks but also demonstrates superior out-of-distribution generalization, enabling it to handle complex or abstract user instructions not encountered during training.

5. **Contribute to Open-Source VLA Development:** The authors intend to open-source the pretraining dataset and the full codebase for SyVLA, aiming to be among the first complete open-source VLA releases that integrate visual-language reasoning capabilities. This objective is motivated by a desire to facilitate broader research and development in the community.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SyVLA model integrates a dual-system architecture and a three-stage training pipeline, incorporating novel algorithms for intention decoupling and stabilized reinforcement learning.

#### 3.1. SyVLA Model Architecture

The model comprises two main components:
- **Vision-Language Model (VLM):** Qwen2.5VL-3B is utilized as the high-level perception, language-reasoning core, and control intention generator. It is responsible for interpreting instructions and formulating a plan or "chain-of-thought" (CoT).
- **Action Expert:** A transformer-based Flow Matching model generates continuous actions. This expert receives conditioning inputs from the VLM.

The connection between these two components is established using a fixed set of learnable **Feature Query Tokens**. These tokens are appended to the VLM's input sequence, and their last hidden states (referred to as Feature Query States) are passed through an MLP adapter to produce the control intention representation. This representation then conditions the Flow Matching model, guiding it to generate actions consistent with the VLM's plan. This Feature Query Token approach is described as lightweight compared to methods like KV cache, potentially reducing inference latency and supporting asynchronous inference.

#### 3.2. Training Pipeline

SyVLA follows a three-stage training pipeline:
1. **Pretraining:** The model is initially pretrained on a large-scale robotic dataset (approximately 450 hours, derived from Open X Embodiment, RH20T, and proprietary data), mixed with several high-quality multi-modal datasets (e.g., COCO 2017, VQA-v2, Pixmo, Cambrain) at an approximate ratio of 30-35%. A small fraction (less than 1%) of the robotic data is annotated with CoT, which, combined with multi-modal training, is intended to instill basic "Think-Before-Act" capability and preserve VLM's inherent capabilities.
2. **Task Fine-tuning:** For specific target tasks, several hundred trajectories with detailed CoT annotations are collected. The pretrained SyVLA model is then fine-tuned on this task-specific dataset, mixed with the same multi-modal datasets (reduced ratio of ~30%), to acquire task-specific solutions.
3. **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** To maximize performance and robustness, a reinforcement learning stage is applied. This stage utilizes a custom RL pipeline to optimize the VLA model in the real world.

#### 3.3. Intention Decoupling Algorithm

To address issues of imprecise actions or hesitation caused by the entanglement of high-level reasoning with control intentions, an annotation-free **Intention Decoupling algorithm** is proposed.
- **Mechanism:** During training, the gradient of the action loss (L_action) with respect to the last hidden state of each Feature Query Token (Feature Query State) is computed.
- **Masking:** Feature Query States whose L2-norm of the corresponding gradient falls below an empirically chosen threshold (5th percentile of the gradient-norm distribution) are masked (set to zero). This process reduces the mutual information between the remaining states and reasoning representations.
- **Theoretical Basis:** A theoretical analysis, based on a simplified single-layer transformer Action Expert, suggests that hidden states with small gradient norms contribute minimally to the control intention and primarily contain redundant information. Gradient magnitude is influenced by attention score (relevance) and the distance between the value vector and attention layer output (redundancy).
- **Computational Efficiency:** The algorithm involves an additional forward-backward pass only for the Action Expert, which is a small fraction of the total VLA parameters, resulting in approximately a 10% increase in training time without additional model updates.

#### 3.4. Similar-Sample Guided RL Pipeline

To stabilize real-world RL for VLA models, a **Similar-Sample Guided RL pipeline** is developed.
- **Problem:** Standard policy-gradient updates for large VLA models in sparse-reward, long-horizon tasks are prone to instability and performance collapse due to high-dimensional action/parameter spaces and unreliable value estimates from costly real-robot rollouts.
- **Mechanism:**
 - Observations at each timestep are encoded into feature vectors (using a CLIP model for multi-view images).
 - During each RL update, semantically similar samples are retrieved from the expert imitation-learning (IL) dataset.
 - These retrieved similar samples are merged into the training batch alongside data from online rollouts.
 - **Advantage Assignment:** Crucially, the advantage for samples from the expert IL dataset is uniformly set to 1.0, effectively encouraging the policy to mimic expert behavior and preventing rapid divergence or gradient explosion observed with standard Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE). GAE values for rollout data are clipped to [−0.8, +0.8].
- **Infrastructure:** An RL infrastructure is implemented with HTTP communication between robot and server, where GPU 0 acts as a scheduler and provides inference, while GPUs 1-7 act as workers for RL training. Human-provided 0-1 rewards are used, minimizing extra labor during supervision.

#### 3.5. Evaluation

The methodology includes extensive evaluation on both real-world robotic tasks and multi-modal benchmarks.
- **Real-World Tasks:** Three tasks are defined on a Cobot Magic robotic platform: "Folding Shirts," "Calculating and Wrapping," and "Bagging Snacks." Experiments are conducted in both in-domain and task-specific out-of-distribution (OoD) settings (e.g., novel object placement, unseen instructions). Success rate is the primary metric.
- **Multi-modal Benchmarks:** DocVQA, AI2D, MMMU, MME, and HallBench are used to assess the preservation of VLM's knowledge and reasoning capabilities.
- **Baselines:** Performance is compared against several contemporary VLA models, including Pi0 (both pretrained and from scratch), ChatVLA, OpenVLA-oft, Wall-Oss, and GR00T.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that SyVLA achieves a balance between robotic task competence and visual-language understanding, outperforming baselines in several aspects.

#### 4.1. Real-World Robotic Task Performance

- **Overall Superiority:** SyVLA achieved the highest average success rates across the three real-world robotic tasks in both in-domain (0.73 average success rate) and out-of-distribution (OoD) settings (0.64 average success rate).
- **In-Domain Performance:** SyVLA performed best on "Calculating and Wrapping" (0.68) and "Bagging Snacks" (0.64), and closely matched the performance of a highly pretrained Pi0 model on "Folding Shirts" (0.86 vs 0.93). Notably, SyVLA significantly outperformed a Pi0 model trained from scratch on the same dataset (0.86 vs 0.64 on "Folding Shirts"), indicating that Pi0's advantage largely stems from its proprietary, massive pretraining data.
- **Out-of-Distribution Generalization:** SyVLA demonstrated superior generalization, exhibiting the smallest performance drop in OoD settings and significantly outperforming all baselines (0.64 average success rate compared to the next best, Pi0 pretrained, at 0.48). This suggests improved understanding of complex user instructions.
- **Baseline Analysis:** ChatVLA, despite its MoE architecture for VLM preservation, showed substantial degradation in fine-grained manipulation ability, failing on precise execution even after correct object selection.

#### 4.2. Multi-Modal Understanding Benchmarks

- **Balanced Capabilities:** SyVLA achieved competitive or superior performance on several multi-modal benchmarks, specifically leading on AI2D (67.70), MME (1795), and HallBench (42.53). It maintained comparable performance on DocVQA (80.01) and MMMU (35.78), slightly trailing ChatVLA but substantially outperforming other VLA models that lost VQA capabilities (e.g., Pi0).
- **Trade-offs:** The observed slight decline in document understanding (DocVQA) and multidisciplinary expert reasoning (MMMU) compared to ChatVLA is attributed to SyVLA's focus on VLA task execution and common knowledge, suggesting an inherent trade-off given limited data resources. Pi0, conversely, completely lost its VLM's inherent capabilities.
- **Efficiency:** SyVLA achieved its performance with significantly less pretraining data (less than 5% of Pi0's reported 10k hours), highlighting the effectiveness of its architectural and algorithmic designs.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies conducted on the "Folding Shirts" task supported the necessity of key components:
- **Intention Decoupling:** Removing the Intention Decoupling algorithm ("w/o Intention Decoupling") resulted in a significant performance drop (0.43 success rate from 0.86), confirming that decoupling high-level reasoning from control is crucial for action precision. Disabling "Think-Before-Act" ("w/o CoT") also led to a drop (0.79), supporting the hypothesis that reasoning leakage degrades performance.
- **Reinforcement Learning:** Disabling the RL stage ("w/o RL") reduced success rate (0.71), demonstrating its role in bridging the gap between IL objective and task success.
- **Similar-Sample Guidance:**
 - RL without the expert dataset ("w/o Expert Dataset") showed high instability and performance collapse (0.21 success rate), emphasizing the stabilizing role of expert guidance.
 - Randomly sampling expert data instead of retrieving similar samples ("w/o Similar Sample") resulted in lower performance (0.79), indicating the benefit of semantic similarity for guidance.
 - Using standard GAE for expert data ("w/ standard Advantage") consistently led to rapid gradient explosion and capability collapse (0.00 success rate), validating the fixed advantage assignment for expert samples.

#### 4.4. Hyperparameter Sensitivity

- **Mask Threshold (τ):** Performance was relatively stable for τ values between 3% and 15% (percentile of gradient-norm distribution), with degradation observed at 20%, indicating that masking too much information impairs control intention completeness.
- **Number of Feature Query Tokens (N):** Performance was poor for small N, but stabilized when N ≥ 15, suggesting an information bottleneck for fewer tokens. Further increasing N yielded limited gains but increased cost.
- **Random Seed:** The method exhibited low sensitivity to random seed variations across tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents SyVLA as a contribution to the field of Vision-Language-Action models, offering solutions to persistent challenges in real-world robotic deployment. The significance and potential impact of this work include:

1. **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** By addressing the entanglement between high-level reasoning and low-level control through the Intention Decoupling algorithm, and stabilizing reinforcement learning with the Similar-Sample Guided RL pipeline, SyVLA demonstrates improved robustness and superior out-of-distribution generalization. This enables robots to reliably execute tasks under varying conditions and abstract instructions, which is crucial for real-world applicability beyond controlled laboratory settings.

2. **Balanced Capabilities for Embodied Intelligence:** The work demonstrates a successful approach to maintaining a balance between robotic task competence and the preservation of core visual-language understanding and reasoning capabilities. Unlike some prior VLA models that sacrifice VLM's inherent intelligence for action proficiency, SyVLA's "Think-Before-Act" paradigm allows it to decompose complex tasks while retaining common-sense knowledge. This aligns with the long-term goal of developing general embodied intelligence, where robots can reason about their actions as humans do.

3. **Improved Data Efficiency:** SyVLA achieved competitive performance with a significantly smaller pretraining dataset compared to some state-of-the-art models (e.g., less than 5% of Pi0's data). This suggests that the proposed architectural choices and training algorithms can yield effective VLA models with more accessible data requirements, potentially lowering the barrier to entry for VLA research and development.

4. **Practical Real-World Application:** The robust performance on real-world robotic tasks, including those requiring multi-step, fine-grained manipulations and adaptation to unseen situations, indicates SyVLA's potential for practical deployment in domains such as manufacturing, logistics, or even domestic assistance, where versatility and reliability are paramount. The ability to handle vague user instructions further enhances its utility in human-robot interaction.

5. **Open-Source Contribution to the Community:** The commitment to open-sourcing the pretraining dataset and the full codebase is a significant contribution. This act can accelerate research and development in the VLA community by providing a complete, high-performing, and visually-language-reasoning-capable VLA model and its associated training resources. Such open access facilitates reproducibility, encourages further innovation, and allows other researchers to build upon this work.

Despite its empirical effectiveness, the authors acknowledge a current limitation in the absence of a rigorous theoretical guarantee for the Intention Decoupling algorithm. Addressing this theoretical gap is identified as a future research direction.
