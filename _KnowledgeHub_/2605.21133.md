# Humanoid Whole-Body Manipulation via Active Spatial Brain and Generalizable Action Cerebellum

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Sun Yat-sen University developed a framework for humanoid whole-body manipulation that integrates VLM-driven active spatial perception with a multi-agent system for generalizable action generation. This approach enables robots to perform complex tasks in 3D environments, showing superior generalization compared to data-driven and VLM-driven baselines across diverse real-world scenarios without needing task-specific real-robot data.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle with active spatial understanding in complex 3D environments, often facing occlusions and diverse object configurations.
- Generating generalizable whole-body manipulation actions for humanoids is challenging due to large observation/action spaces and scarcity of costly real-robot training data.
- Prior approaches, including data-driven and LLM/VLM-driven methods, lack active spatial reasoning and whole-body coordination for dynamic 3D scenes.

## Method

- A hierarchical multi-agent architecture, featuring an Active Spatial Brain and a Generalizable Action Cerebellum, processes perception, plans, and controls.
- The Active Spatial Brain uses a VLM for active spatial perception, a memory bank to maintain context, and adaptive task planning for sub-task decomposition and dynamic replanning.
- The Generalizable Action Cerebellum translates high-level commands into executable actions via specialized modules for lower-body locomotion (e.g., A* navigation, end-effector reachable space solver) and upper-body dexterous manipulation (e.g., VLM-based grasp generation, parameterized post-grasp trajectories).

## Results

- VLMs showed high accuracy (up to **98.84%**) in high-level spatial understanding and active target localization (**94.11%**), though they struggled with precise metric perception for navigation.
- The framework consistently outperformed data-driven and VLM-driven baselines in real-world tasks, achieving superior success rates, particularly in challenging out-of-distribution scenarios (e.g., **60.0%** vs. **0%** for baselines in Task 4 Hard).
- It demonstrated robust generalization across varied spatial layouts and object categories, including unseen items (**69.6%** success), with active perception and the end-effector reachable space solver proving crucial for performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLMs are effective for high-level spatial reasoning and active perception decisions (e.g., camera viewpoint adjustment) but require dedicated modules for precise metric control.
> - A hierarchical decomposition, separating active spatial understanding from generalizable action execution, allows for robust humanoid whole-body manipulation without task-specific real-robot training data.
> - Maintaining spatial awareness through active perception and a memory bank is critical for handling occlusions and dynamic environments in complex 3D scenes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Enabling autonomous robots to execute complex tasks in spatial environments is a significant and ongoing challenge within the fields of robotics and computer vision. Humanoid robots are considered particularly promising for these tasks due to their whole-body mobility and dexterous manipulation capabilities, allowing them to operate in diverse three-dimensional spaces, which is relevant for applications such as home assistance and industrial settings.

Previous research in robot manipulation has achieved notable success in tabletop scenarios, often relying on fixed camera positions and constrained workspaces. While these approaches have been extended to mobile manipulation platforms, including wheeled robots, quadrupeds, and humanoids, transitioning to humanoid whole-body manipulation introduces expanded observation and action spaces. This complexity necessitates substantial volumes of high-quality training data, which is both costly and difficult to acquire. Existing strategies to address this data scarcity, such as leveraging human motion capture data, simulation data, or tabletop data, frequently encounter limitations. These limitations include sim-to-real gaps, discrepancies arising from different robot embodiments, and shifts in spatial data distribution. Consequently, learned policies often exhibit restricted robustness and generalization capabilities in varied and unstructured real-world environments.

More recent advancements involve the application of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in robotics. LLMs have been utilized for semantic reasoning and zero-shot generalization, primarily translating semantic instructions into structured sub-goal sequences or synthesizing manipulation policies through code generation. Researchers have also explored enhancing physical grounding by extracting geometric representations from workspaces to guide motion planning. However, these LLM-driven methods are predominantly designed for static tabletop environments with fixed camera perspectives. They typically lack active spatial reasoning abilities and the whole-body coordination mechanisms necessary for managing complex 3D scenes that integrate both locomotion and manipulation.

A third area of related research focuses on spatial intelligence for robotics, aiming to improve 3D perception, spatial reasoning, and precise interaction in complex real-world conditions. This includes explicit spatial modeling, geometric-semantic disentanglement, spatial-aware Vision-Language-Action (VLA) frameworks, and exploiting spatial affordance or keypoint prediction. While these efforts lay foundational groundwork for embodied intelligence, they are also largely confined to tabletop settings or simpler loco-manipulation tasks.

This work positions itself to address the identified gaps by proposing a framework that specifically targets spatial-aware humanoid whole-body manipulation in complex 3D environments. It seeks to overcome the data dependency and generalization limitations of data-driven methods, as well as the active spatial reasoning and whole-body coordination deficiencies of current LLM/VLM-driven approaches. By leveraging the spatial intelligence and generalizable knowledge of multi-agent large models, the research aims to provide a real-robot-data-free solution for robust and generalizable humanoid manipulation in unstructured environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable spatial-aware humanoid whole-body manipulation in complex three-dimensional environments. The authors identify two central challenges inherent to this task, which serve as the key motivations for their proposed framework:

1. **Spatial Understanding in Complex 3D Environments:** Unlike constrained tabletop settings, complex 3D environments present diverse spatial relations and dynamic configurations. Objects may be occluded, situated at various heights and distances, or interact in intricate ways. Traditional perception systems with fixed viewpoints are often inadequate for accurately perceiving and understanding such complex spatial scenes. The challenge lies in developing a system that can actively perceive its surroundings, resolve ambiguities like occlusion, and accurately infer the 3D spatial relationships between objects and the robot itself.

2. **Generalizable Action Generation with Limited Data:** Humanoid whole-body manipulation inherently involves a significantly larger observation and action space compared to simpler robotic tasks. This necessitates vast amounts of high-quality training data for data-driven models to learn effective policies. However, acquiring such data for real humanoid robots is exceptionally costly and time-consuming. The scarcity of diverse, real-robot manipulation data severely restricts the ability of data-driven models to generalize their learned actions to novel tasks, objects, or unstructured environments. The motivation is to achieve robust and adaptable action generation without requiring extensive task-specific real-robot training data, thereby mitigating the sim-to-real gap, embodiment discrepancies, and spatial distribution shifts that affect generalization.

To address these challenges, the authors propose a generalizable humanoid loco-manipulation framework. Their motivation is to leverage the capabilities of multi-agent large models, specifically Vision-Language Models (VLMs), for both advanced spatial perception and generalizable action generation. The framework is designed to provide active spatial perception and decision-making to handle the complexities of 3D environments, while also enabling the generation of executable robot actions in a manner that generalizes across diverse scenarios, circumventing the need for task-specific real-robot data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework adopts a hierarchical multi-agent architecture, operating as a closed-loop system encompassing perception, planning, and control, for humanoid whole-body manipulation in complex environments. It comprises two main components: the Active Spatial Brain and the Generalizable Action Cerebellum. The framework aims to generate whole-body actions, denoted as A = {a_cam, a_dex, a_upper, a_lower}, which are then sent to PD controllers for joint torque output.

#### 3.1. Active Spatial Brain

The Active Spatial Brain functions as the high-level policy, responsible for active spatial perception, environmental understanding, and manipulation planning. It consists of three integrated modules:

- **Active Spatial Perception:** To address occlusions and complex spatial configurations, the humanoid is equipped with a 2-DoF camera neck, whose viewpoint is dynamically adjusted. A VLM makes viewpoint decisions based on visual observations and task instructions, leveraging its spatial reasoning capabilities. The VLM is prompted with the robot's action space, which includes 2-DoF camera neck movements and 4-DoF humanoid base movements (translations along x, y, z axes, and z-axis rotation), enabling active exploration to locate task-relevant objects.

- **Memory Bank:** This module mitigates viewpoint inconsistencies caused by changes in the robot's base position. It selectively archives images captured by the perception module since the last base movement, pairing each image with its corresponding camera pose. Additionally, it maintains a textual log of all commands dispatched from the Brain to the Cerebellum and their feedback. By synthesizing these archived images and the textual history, the VLM can infer the current task stage and maintain consistent spatial awareness.

- **Adaptive Task Planning:** This agent integrates spatial reasoning, sub-task decomposition, and dynamic replanning. Given a user instruction, it initially decomposes the long-horizon goal into a sequence of sub-tasks. During iterative execution, the planner evaluates the sequence against the memory bank to determine the current task stage. It then either triggers active viewpoint adjustment for more comprehensive spatial perception or invokes downstream agents in the Cerebellum to execute specific sub-tasks. The planner is designed to dynamically replan sub-tasks based on memory bank feedback, for instance, re-invoking manipulation agents upon detecting a failed grasp, thus enabling robust closed-loop control and adaptation to environmental disturbances or initial plan infeasibility.

#### 3.2. Generalizable Action Cerebellum

The Generalizable Action Cerebellum translates high-level commands from the Spatial Brain into executable and physically feasible humanoid action sequences, designed for generalizability without task-specific demonstrations. It decomposes whole-body control into lower-body locomotion and upper-body dexterous manipulation.

- **Humanoid Lower-Body Locomotion:** This module manages robot movement to ensure end-effector reachability and obstacle avoidance, with an underlying RL policy (following HOMIE) translating commands into joint actions.
 - **Spatial Navigation Agent:** Handles long-range, obstacle-aware coarse approach. It takes RGB-D observations and a 2D target coordinate. It lifts the depth map into a 3D point cloud, projects it onto a ground plane to create a discretized grid map (marking cells as occupied if point height exceeds a threshold). An A\* algorithm computes a collision-free path, which is then simplified into a waypoint sequence using the Ramer–Douglas–Peucker (RDP) algorithm. The robot tracks these waypoints with closed-loop feedback, generating linear and angular velocities (v_x,t, ω_y,t) and maintaining a nominal base height (h_t).
 - **End-Effector Reachable Space Solver (EES):** Provides precise short-range positioning for reachability. Given the target object position, an optimization problem is solved to compute horizontal (∆d) and vertical (∆z) base adjustments. The objective minimizes adjustment magnitude while bringing the object closer to the shoulder workspace, subject to a reachability constraint (shoulder-to-object distance ≤ ηL, where L is arm length and η=0.8). A kinematic offset model, estimated via MuJoCo simulation, accounts for how base height changes affect the relative shoulder position. The solver outputs refined base displacement and height for the RL policy.
 - **Reinforcement Learning-Based Humanoid Locomotion:** A policy (π_loco) takes the robot's proprioceptive state (s_t) and base commands (c_t = [v_x,t, ω_y,t, h_t]) to output lower-body joint actions (a_lower,t) for stable locomotion.

- **Humanoid Upper-Body Dexterous Manipulation:** This addresses the challenges of high degrees of freedom and limited generalization in arm and hand control, by decomposing manipulation into grasp generation and post-grasp trajectory generation.
 - **Grasp Pose Generation Agent:** Leverages multimodal foundation generative models (MLLMs) to synthesize human hand-object interaction images (I_ho) conditioned on visual observation (I) and task instruction (L). These images provide human grasp strategies. A human-image-to-robot grasp transfer process involves: (1) using a pretrained Hyper3D model to generate a 3D object mesh (M_o) from observation I; (2) applying EasyHOI to estimate a human hand pose (G_mano) from M_o and I_ho; and (3) retargeting the human grasp to the robot's dexterous hand by optimizing fingertip positions, resulting in dexterous hand actions (G_dex).
 - **Post-grasp Trajectory Generation Agent:** Identifies post-grasp trajectories using parameterized action primitives. A large model predicts semantic 2D keypoints and selects a corresponding primitive. These keypoints are lifted to 3D coordinates, and kinematic rules derive target 6-DoF end-effector (EE) poses for the primitive (e.g., Pushing, Pulling, Placing, Rotating). An Inverse Kinematics (IK) solver then converts these sequential poses into continuous joint commands for whole-body execution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings from both a spatial intelligence benchmark for VLMs and real-world robot experiments, assessing the proposed framework's performance and component effectiveness.

#### 4.1. Spatial Intelligence Benchmark for Manipulation Tasks

To evaluate the spatial intelligence capabilities of mainstream VLMs, three tasks were designed:
- **Task 1 (Mapping images to a grid):** Assessed spatial relationship understanding. VLMs demonstrated proficiency, with Gemini-3.1-pro achieving 98.84% correctness, indicating strong capabilities in coarse, relational spatial reasoning.
- **Task 2 (Active perception for target localization):** Evaluated active perception in locating occluded or out-of-view targets. Gemini-3.1-pro achieved 94.11% success with an average of 2.00 observations, demonstrating VLM effectiveness in dynamic viewpoint decisions.
- **Task 3 (Waypoint generation in cluttered scenes):** Tested fine-grained metric perception for navigation. Gemini-3.1-pro achieved 64.91% "Appropriate" paths. However, other VLMs performed significantly lower, with "Fail" rates as high as 66.67% for qwen3.5-plus. This result indicates that while VLMs excel at high-level spatial reasoning, they currently struggle with precise metric perception from 2D observations required for accurate path planning. This finding supports the framework's design of decoupling VLM-based high-level planning from fine-grained action generation.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

Experiments were conducted on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot equipped with Inspire FTP Hands and a RealSense D435i active camera. Five diverse task settings (Different Heights, Different Positions, Different Heights and Positions, Obstacle Avoidance, Occlusion Handling) were designed, each with easy (in-domain) and hard (out-of-distribution) modes.

- **Comparison with Baselines:**
 The proposed framework was compared against two data-driven baselines (TrajBooster, Ψ0) and one VLM-driven baseline (CaP). The results demonstrate that the proposed framework consistently outperformed all baselines across both easy and hard settings.
 - **Easy Setting:** While data-driven methods performed competitively in easy tasks (e.g., TrajBooster 70.0% on Task 1, Ψ0 80.0%, CaP 75.0%), the proposed framework achieved higher success rates (85.0%).
 - **Hard Setting:** Performance of data-driven methods dropped significantly in hard, out-of-distribution tasks (e.g., TrajBooster 0% and Ψ0 0% on Task 4 Hard), indicating limited robustness and generalization. CaP generalized better than data-driven methods but still underperformed the proposed framework, which maintained strong performance (e.g., 60.0% on Task 4 Hard). This suggests that the multi-agent design, combining active spatial perception with generalizable action generation, confers superior generalization capabilities without requiring task-specific real-robot data for training or fine-tuning.

- **Generalization Analysis:**
 - **Spatial Reachability:** Visualizations showed that the proposed framework maintained a broader and more reliable reachable region across varied target locations compared to data-driven baselines (Figure 7), which exhibited significant performance degradation outside their training data distribution.
 - **Object Categories:** The framework demonstrated robust generalization across both seen and unseen object categories (Table 2). For instance, it achieved 69.6% success rate on "Items*" (unseen objects), while TrajBooster achieved 50.0%. This highlights the framework's ability to handle variations in object types and spatial layouts in real-world environments.

- **Ablation Study:**
 An ablation study evaluated the contributions of the Active Perception (AP) and End-Effector Reachable Space Solver (EES) modules on two representative tasks (Different Heights and Positions, Obstacle Avoidance).
 - **Without AP:** Task performance significantly dropped (e.g., from 67.5% to 24.9% for "Diff. Heights and Positions" and 70.0% to 33.4% for "Obstacle Avoidance" when EES was enabled in both cases). This confirms AP's role in expanding the robot's observation space and maintaining visual context during locomotion.
 - **Without EES:** Success rates also decreased (e.g., from 67.5% to 55.6% for "Diff. Heights and Positions" and 70.0% to 16.7% for "Obstacle Avoidance" when AP was enabled in both cases). This module was found to be critical for refining object alignment and positioning, especially where sensor errors might accumulate during complex movements.
 - The study concluded that both AP and EES modules are effective and contribute to the humanoid's whole-body manipulation capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of embodied AI by presenting a generalizable framework for humanoid whole-body manipulation in complex 3D environments. Its primary significance lies in addressing the dual challenges of spatial understanding and action generalization without relying on task-specific real-robot data.

The proposed framework introduces a novel integration of VLM-driven active spatial perception and decision-making with a multi-agent system for executable robot action generation. This approach allows the humanoid to dynamically understand and interact with its environment, adapting to complex spatial relationships and unseen scenarios. By decoupling high-level planning from fine-grained action control, the framework effectively leverages the strengths of large models for reasoning while mitigating their limitations in precise metric control.

The work's impact extends to overcoming several limitations prevalent in prior research. It provides a method to circumvent the significant data bottleneck associated with training data-driven policies for high-dimensional humanoid tasks. Furthermore, it offers a solution to the generalization issues caused by sim-to-real gaps, embodiment discrepancies, and spatial distribution shifts, enabling more robust performance in diverse, unstructured real-world settings. Unlike previous LLM/VLM-driven methods, this framework explicitly incorporates active spatial reasoning and whole-body coordination, moving beyond static tabletop scenarios.

The demonstrated capabilities of reliable spatial perception, adaptive task planning, and generalizable, physically feasible loco-manipulation across various challenging tasks suggest a pathway toward more autonomous and versatile humanoid robots. This could have broad potential impact across several application domains:
- **Home Assistance:** Humanoids could perform complex domestic chores, interacting with objects at different heights and locations, navigating around furniture, and handling occlusions.
- **Industrial and Logistics:** Robots could execute intricate manipulation and transportation tasks in dynamic factory or warehouse environments, adapting to changing layouts and object placements.
- **Exploration and Disaster Response:** Humanoids might be deployed in hazardous or unknown environments, leveraging their whole-body mobility and manipulation to interact with the surroundings, navigate obstacles, and perform specific tasks.

Ultimately, this research highlights the potential of integrating large-model-driven active perception and decision-making for advancing generalizable humanoid manipulation. It suggests a shift in robotic control paradigms, moving away from heavily data-dependent approaches towards systems that can leverage general world knowledge and reasoning capabilities, thereby influencing future research directions in embodied artificial intelligence.
