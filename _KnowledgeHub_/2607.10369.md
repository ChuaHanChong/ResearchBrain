# VINE: Taming Generative Control Policies for Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at AgiBot, in collaboration with The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology and Peking University, developed VINE, an RL-oriented sampling method that enables stable end-to-end value-gradient optimization for generative control policies by redesigning the multi-step denoising process. VINE achieved the highest aggregate score across 40 tasks on the OGBench offline RL benchmark and demonstrated a 100% success rate with reduced human intervention on a real-world robotic socket insertion task.

## Problem

- Generative control policies (e.g., flow-matching, diffusion models) excel at representing complex, multimodal action distributions but suffer from severe training instability when integrated with value-gradient reinforcement learning.
- Prior attempts to stabilize this integration often compromised on the generative policy's expressiveness, iterative refinement capabilities, or the efficiency of value-gradient optimization.
- The root cause of instability was commonly attributed to the iterative generation process itself, hindering effective end-to-end backpropagation of value gradients.

## Method

- **VINE** (Value-gradient Iterative Noise Exploration) redesigns the action sampling process within multi-step generative policies by introducing ==per-step stochastic interpolation==.
- At each ==denoising step==, **VINE** reconstructs a fresh noisy intermediate state by interpolating the current action estimate with a newly sampled ==Gaussian noise==, contrasting with standard samplers that inject noise only once initially.
- This creates a stable, differentiable path for backpropagating ==value gradients== through the entire iterative generation chain, allowing for end-to-end optimization of ==flow-matching== policies without auxiliary models or changes to the generative objective.

## Results

- VINE achieved the **highest aggregate score** across **40 tasks** from **eight** diverse OGBench offline RL domains, outperforming a range of state-of-the-art offline RL methods.
- In a real-world online robotic socket insertion task, VINE demonstrated a **100%** success rate (**20/20** trials), the shortest fine-tuning time (**20 minutes**), and the lowest human intervention ratio (**19.2%**).
- Ablation studies showed VINE stabilized backpropagated gradient norms (reducing spikes) and achieved substantially higher success rates compared to the vanilla Euler sampler, confirming its role in mitigating training instability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The core instability in combining generative policies with value-gradient RL stems not from the iterative generation process itself, but from the 'vanilla sampling strategy' designed for static behavior cloning, which is brittle under dynamic RL optimization.
> - By injecting fresh noise and reconstructing noisy interpolation states at each step, VINE enables smooth, value-driven mode-shifting and provides a stable gradient path compatible with the dynamics of reinforcement learning.
> - VINE successfully preserves the benefits of iterative refinement in generative policies, allowing for a coarse-to-fine action generation process that is crucial for complex tasks, while simultaneously achieving stable RL optimization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

This work operates within the intersection of robot learning, generative modeling, and reinforcement learning (RL). Generative control policies, such as diffusion and flow-matching models, have gained prominence in robot learning due to their capacity to represent complex and multimodal action distributions, a feature particularly beneficial for tasks learned via behavior cloning (BC) from diverse demonstrations. These policies operate by iteratively transforming an initial noise signal into an executable action.

The broader research landscape has increasingly focused on enhancing these generative control policies with reinforcement learning to move beyond mere imitation and optimize for explicit reward maximization. However, integrating value-gradient RL with these highly expressive, multi-step generative policies has historically presented a significant challenge: training instability.

Prior research has attempted to mitigate this instability through various strategies, each involving trade-offs:
1. **Freezing Generative Policy Parameters:** Some methods freeze the generative policy's parameters and employ external guidance signals to steer the denoising process. This approach limits the learning capacity of the generative model itself and constrains its expressiveness.
2. **Shortening Iterative Generation:** Other strategies involve replacing multi-step iterative generation with simpler models, such as residual Gaussian policies or one-step distilled policies. This sacrifices the iterative refinement process, which is considered a key advantage of generative policies.
3. **Discarding Action Gradients:** A third category avoids the critic's action gradient entirely, using scalar value estimates as weighting signals instead. This compromises the efficiency of value-gradient optimization.

VINE positions itself as a method that challenges the common attribution of instability to the iterative generation process itself. Instead, it identifies the root cause in the sampling strategy, which was originally designed for static behavior cloning. By redesigning this sampling process, VINE aims to enable stable end-to-end value-gradient optimization for generative policies without compromising their expressiveness or iterative nature. This situates VINE as a "through-the-chain" approach, seeking to improve the direct backpropagation of value gradients, in contrast to methods that work "around" or "outside" the generative chain or shorten it.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an RL-oriented sampling method, named VINE, that enables stable end-to-end value-gradient optimization for flow-matching policies in reinforcement learning. This objective aims to preserve the inherent expressiveness and iterative generation capabilities of generative policies while overcoming the training instability observed in prior attempts at combining them with value-gradient RL.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations regarding the application of generative control policies in robot learning:

1. **Expressiveness of Generative Policies:** Flow-matching and diffusion models have demonstrated considerable success in behavior cloning due to their ability to model complex and multimodal action distributions. They achieve this by iteratively refining noise into an action, which provides a rich representational capacity for diverse robot behaviors.
2. **Limitations of Behavior Cloning:** While effective for imitation, behavior cloning does not inherently guarantee optimal performance in terms of reward maximization. To further improve policies beyond demonstrated behaviors, fine-tuning with reinforcement learning is necessary to align actions with higher expected returns.
3. **Observed Training Instability:** Integrating value-gradient reinforcement learning with these multi-step generative policies has frequently led to severe training instability. This instability has been a significant barrier to leveraging the full potential of generative models in an RL context.
4. **Revisiting the Source of Instability:** Prior works typically attribute this instability to the iterative generation process itself, leading to methods that circumvent direct value-gradient propagation through the full denoising trajectory. These solutions often necessitate compromises, such as sacrificing the model's expressiveness, eliminating the iterative nature of action generation, or foregoing the efficiency of critic-action gradients.
5. **Hypothesis for Instability:** Contrary to the prevailing belief, the authors hypothesize that the instability does not originate from the iterative generation process fundamentally, but rather from the "vanilla sampling strategy" employed during inference. This standard sampling approach was primarily designed for behavior cloning, where the goal is to accurately reconstruct a fixed, static data distribution.
6. **Mismatch with RL Optimization Dynamics:** Reinforcement learning, however, requires a dynamic adjustment of the policy. The learned critic's gradients direct the policy to shift probability mass towards higher-value actions. A sampling process designed for static distribution reconstruction is poorly equipped to facilitate this dynamic, value-driven mode-shifting, leading to brittleness and instability when subjected to value gradients.
7. **Need for an RL-oriented Sampler:** This fundamental mismatch motivates the need for a redesigned sampling process that is explicitly aligned with the optimization dynamics of reinforcement learning. Such a sampler would need to support smooth transitions between high-value modes and provide a stable path for gradient propagation, thus enabling stable and efficient end-to-end value-gradient optimization for generative control policies.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The methodology proposed in this paper, VINE (Value-gradient Iterative Noise Exploration), aims to stabilize value-gradient reinforcement learning for generative control policies by redesigning the action sampling process. This redesign focuses on how intermediate states are generated during the multi-step denoising process.

**Background on Generative Control Policies (Flow Matching):**
The paper primarily considers flow-matching policies, which parameterize a state-conditioned policy using a time-dependent velocity field, $v_\theta(x, t; s)$. This field transports an initial noise sample ($x_0 \sim N(0, I_d)$) into an executable action. The velocity field is trained using a conditional flow matching objective ($L_{FM}$), which aims for $v_\theta(x_t, t; s)$ to predict $(a-z)$, where $x_t = ta + (1-t)z$ is a noisy interpolation between a data action $a$ and noise $z$ at time $t$.
During standard inference, the policy generates an action by discretizing the ODE into $K$ Euler steps: $x_{k+1} = x_k + \frac{1}{K} v_\theta(x_k, t_k; s)$, starting from a single initial noise sample $x_0$. The final action is $a = x_K$.

**The Instability Challenge of Value-Gradient RL:**
Optimizing these multi-step generative policies with value gradients requires backpropagating the critic's action gradient ($\nabla_a Q_\phi(s, a)$) through all $K$ denoising steps. The actor gradient for parameter $\theta$ is given by the chain rule: $\nabla_\theta Q_\phi(s, a) = \sum_{k=0}^{K-1} \frac{1}{K} \nabla_a Q_\phi(s, a) \frac{\partial x_K}{\partial x_{k+1}} \frac{\partial v_\theta(x_k, t_k; s)}{\partial \theta}$. This direct backpropagation through the entire iterative generation process has been identified as a source of training instability in prior work.

**VINE's Sampling Redesign:**
VINE's core insight is that while flow-matching training teaches the velocity field to reach the same action from various noise paths, the standard Euler sampler only follows one deterministic path. VINE leverages this flexibility to construct an RL-oriented sampling path. It modifies the iterative generation process as follows:

1. **Initial Action Estimate:** The process starts with an initial action estimate $\hat{a}_0 \sim N(0, I_d)$.
2. **Per-Step Stochastic Interpolation:** At each denoising step $k$ (from $k=0$ to $K-1$), VINE reconstructs a fresh noisy intermediate state, $\hat{x}_k$. This is done by interpolating the current action estimate $\hat{a}_k$ with a *freshly sampled* Gaussian noise $z_k \sim N(0, I_d)$ at time $t_k$:
 $\hat{x}_k = t_k \hat{a}_k + (1 - t_k) z_k$.
 A monotonically increasing time schedule $0 \leq t_1 < \dots < t_K \leq 1$ is used, ensuring that the noise weight $(1-t_k)$ decreases over steps, transitioning from coarse exploration to finer refinement. This contrasts with the standard Euler sampler which injects noise only once at the beginning.
3. **Iterative Endpoint Prediction:** The velocity field $v_\theta$ is then applied to this newly constructed noisy intermediate state $\hat{x}_k$ to predict the next refined action estimate, $\hat{a}_{k+1}$:
 $\hat{a}_{k+1} = \hat{x}_k + (1 - t_k) v_\theta(\hat{x}_k, t_k; s)$.
 This formula stems from the property that the optimal velocity field, when combined with an interpolation state, estimates the posterior mean of the final action.
4. **Final Action:** After $K$ such steps, the final action is $a = \hat{a}_K$.

**Consequences and Compatibility:**
This redesigned sampling method has two key implications:
- **Compatibility with Pretrained Policies:** Each query to $v_\theta$ (i.e., $v_\theta(\hat{x}_k, t_k; s)$) remains consistent with the original flow-matching training semantics because the input $\hat{x}_k$ is a noisy interpolation state. This implies that VINE can be applied to existing pretrained flow-matching policies by solely modifying the sampler implementation, without requiring a new generative objective or an auxiliary guidance model.
- **Stable Differentiable Path:** By injecting fresh noise and reconstructing the interpolation state at each step, VINE creates a more stable differentiable path for value-gradient backpropagation through the entire sampling chain. This structured local exploration around the current action estimate is hypothesized to be less brittle under dynamic RL optimization signals.

**Actor-Critic Training:**
VINE integrates with a behavior-regularized actor-critic framework.
- **Critic ($Q_\phi$):** Trained to minimize the squared Bellman error, evaluating completed actions in the action space: $L_{critic}(\phi) = E_{(s,a,r,s')\sim D} [(Q_\phi(s, a) - r - \gamma Q_{\bar{\phi}}(s', \pi_\theta(s')))^2]$.
- **Actor ($\pi_\theta$):** Trained to maximize the critic-estimated value of its generated actions, regularized by a behavior-cloning penalty: $L_{actor}(\theta) = -E_{s\sim D} [Q_\phi(s, \pi_\theta(s))] + \alpha E_{(s,a)\sim D} [F(a, \pi_\theta(s))]$.
Crucially, $\pi_\theta(s)$ is the action produced by the VINE `GENERATE(s)` process, allowing for end-to-end backpropagation of $Q_\phi(s, \hat{a}_K)$ gradients through all 10 denoising steps to update the velocity field $v_\theta$.

**Implementation Details:**
Experiments utilized $K=10$ denoising steps with a fixed MIP grid $t \in \{0.0, 0.1, \dots, 0.9\}$. A standard actor-critic setup with two Q-functions (clipped double Q-learning) was used. The actor was a 4-layer MLP with GELU activations, and the critic was a twin ResNet value network. Offline RL training involved 1 million gradient steps.

### 4. Main findings and results

The experiments conducted evaluated VINE on both benchmark tasks and a real-world robotic manipulation task. The main findings are detailed as follows:

#### 4.1. Performance on OGBench Offline RL Benchmark

- **Overall Superiority:** VINE achieved the highest aggregate score across 40 tasks from eight diverse OGBench domains (antmaze-large, antmaze-giant, humanoidmaze-medium, humanoidmaze-large, scene-sparse, puzzle-3x3-sparse, cube-double, and cube-triple).
- **Strong Gains in Challenging Domains:** The method demonstrated notable performance improvements, particularly on long-horizon navigation tasks such as antmaze-giant and humanoidmaze-large, which are characterized by sparse rewards and require complex action sequences.
- **Comparison to Baselines:** VINE consistently outperformed a range of state-of-the-art offline RL methods, including Gaussian-based policies, and other generative policy methods categorized by how they handle value-gradient propagation ("Around the Chain," "Outside the Chain," and "Through the Chain" approaches like ReBRAC, FQL, FAWAC, IFQL, QAM, DAC, QSM, CGQL variants, DSRL, FEdit, FBRAC, and BAM). This indicates that VINE's approach to direct value-gradient optimization through the generative process yielded superior results compared to alternative strategies.

#### 4.2. Real-world Online RL Performance (Socket Insertion)

- **High Success Rate:** On a real-world socket insertion task, which requires precise, contact-rich control, VINE achieved a 100% success rate (20/20 trials).
- **Efficiency:** VINE demonstrated the shortest fine-tuning time (20 minutes) among the online RL methods evaluated.
- **Reduced Human Intervention:** The method also exhibited the lowest human intervention ratio (19.2%) during online rollouts, indicating a higher degree of autonomous learning and robustness in a physical environment.
- **Baseline Comparison:** VINE outperformed other real-world reinforcement learning baselines such as SAC-Flow, Hil-SERL, DSRL, RLT, and EXPO.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies: Why VINE Helps

The paper included ablation studies to investigate the specific contributions of VINE's design choices:

- **Stabilization of Value-Gradient Backpropagation:**
 - **Methodology:** A comparison was made between VINE and the vanilla Euler sampler typically used by flow-matching policies, focusing on success rates and gradient norms during training. The Euler sampler uses a single initial noise sample and a deterministic denoising path, while VINE reconstructs a noisy interpolation state with fresh noise at each step.
 - **Result:** VINE consistently achieved substantially higher success rates across all five OGBench tasks examined in the ablation. Furthermore, VINE maintained significantly smaller and more stable backpropagated gradient norms throughout training. In contrast, the Euler sampler exhibited frequent, large gradient spikes, which were correlated with unstable optimization and poorer performance.
 - **Conclusion:** These findings support the claim that VINE's redesigned sampling strategy effectively stabilizes end-to-end value-gradient propagation through the generative denoising chain.

- **Preservation of Iterative Refinement for Action Quality:**
 - **Methodology:** The impact of the number of denoising steps ($K$) on action quality was evaluated by testing VINE policies with $K=1, 4, 6, 8, 10$ on the AntMaze task.
 - **Result:** Policies with fewer denoising steps (e.g., $K=1$ or $K=4$) largely failed to solve the long-horizon AntMaze task, with agents remaining near the start region. As the number of refinement iterations increased, the quality of the generated actions improved, leading to higher task success rates. The full 10 steps were essential for optimal performance.
 - **Conclusion:** This demonstrates that VINE not only enables stable training but also successfully preserves and leverages the iterative refinement capability of generative policies, allowing for a coarse-to-fine generation process crucial for complex tasks. The benefits of iterative refinement are not sacrificed but enhanced by VINE's stable gradient propagation.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The research presented in this paper, with the introduction of VINE, addresses a fundamental challenge in the integration of generative control policies with reinforcement learning. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling Stable End-to-End RL for Generative Policies:** VINE directly tackles the long-standing problem of training instability when applying value-gradient reinforcement learning to expressive, multi-step generative control policies. By identifying the root cause in the sampling strategy rather than iterative generation itself, VINE offers a novel solution that allows for stable backpropagation of value gradients through the entire denoising process. This enables the direct and efficient optimization of highly expressive generative policies, which has previously been problematic.

2. **Preservation of Key Generative Policy Advantages:** Unlike previous approaches that often achieved stability by sacrificing the expressiveness, iterative generation, or efficient value-gradient optimization, VINE preserves all these advantages. It retains the multi-step refinement process, which is crucial for modeling complex and multimodal action distributions, while simultaneously allowing for robust policy improvement through value signals. This avoidance of compromise is a notable contribution to the field.

3. **Improved Performance and Robustness in Diverse Tasks:** The empirical results demonstrate that VINE consistently achieves high performance on challenging offline RL benchmarks (OGBench) and exhibits robust, efficient learning in a real-world robotic manipulation task. Its success in complex, long-horizon tasks and in a physical environment with reduced human intervention highlights its practical applicability and effectiveness.

4. **Redefining the Understanding of Instability:** The paper's re-evaluation of the source of RL instability in generative policies—attributing it to the behavior cloning-oriented sampler rather than iterative generation—could influence future research directions. This insight may encourage researchers to focus more on RL-specific design considerations for generative model components, rather than discarding them entirely for RL applications.

5. **Broad Applicability:** The authors note that VINE's design is a "sampling-level redesign" and is not restricted solely to flow-matching policies but "can be applied beyond flow matching to iterative generative policies such as diffusion policies." This suggests a broader potential impact across various generative modeling frameworks used in control.

6. **Foundation for Future Research and Scaling:** VINE's ability to provide stable value-gradient optimization for complex generative policies opens avenues for future research. The conclusion explicitly mentions extending VINE to "large foundation models" and adapting it to "variant RL algorithms." This suggests its potential to serve as a foundational component for scaling advanced generative AI models in robotics and control, enabling them to learn and adapt more effectively in complex, reward-driven environments. The reduced human intervention in real-world settings also points to its potential to accelerate the development and deployment of autonomous robotic systems.
