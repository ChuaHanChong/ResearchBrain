# LaRS: Latent Reasoning Skills for Chain-of-Thought Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> LaRS introduces an unsupervised method that discovers and leverages latent reasoning skills from Chain-of-Thought rationales using a Conditional Variational Autoencoder. This approach consistently improves LLM performance on complex reasoning tasks, achieving up to 4x faster pre-processing and significantly enhanced robustness to noisy example banks compared to prior skill-based selection methods.

## Problem

- Existing Chain-of-Thought (CoT) demonstration selection methods, based on question similarity, often lead to 'skill mismatches' as similar questions may require different reasoning processes.
- Prior skill-based CoT selection methods, like Skill-KNN, rely on expensive LLM inferences to generate explicit textual skill descriptions, leading to high computational costs, poor scalability, and subjective skill representations.
- The quality and consistency of CoT prompting can be severely degraded by suboptimal examples, highlighting a need for more robust selection strategies.

## Method

- LaRS proposes a ==latent skill model== that formulates rationale generation as conditioned on both the question and a latent reasoning skill, providing a theoretical framework for skill-based CoT.
- It implements this model using a ==Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE)== with lightweight MLPs and pre-trained embedding models to unsupervisedly discover latent reasoning skills from question-rationale pairs.
- Demonstration selection for a new query involves inferring its desired ==latent skill==, computing demonstrated skills for examples in the bank, and selecting top-k examples based on ==cosine similarity in the latent skill space==.

## Results

- LaRS consistently outperformed the state-of-the-art `Skill-KNN-zero` in **18 out of 20** experiments across four reasoning benchmarks (TabMWP, GSM8K, Spider, COGS), and matched oracle performance in nearly half of the experiments.
- It achieved a **4x faster** pre-processing time for example banks and eliminated LLM inference costs for skill discovery, saving approximately **$30 per 1k** examples compared to `Skill-KNN-zero`.
- The method demonstrated superior robustness to sub-optimal example banks, showing minimal performance degradation (**0.**1%** to 1.9%**) with **30%** random rationales, whereas `Skill-KNN-zero` experienced drops up to **11.7%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reasoning rationales are not solely dependent on the question but are also governed by underlying 'latent reasoning skills' that can be automatically discovered without explicit textual descriptions.
> - Unsupervised learning with a CVAE can effectively capture abstract reasoning concepts in a continuous latent space, which aligns with human understanding of distinct skills.
> - Representing skills as continuous latent variables and using lightweight models for inference dramatically improves computational efficiency and scalability compared to methods relying on LLM-generated skill descriptions.
