# Reliable and Responsible Foundation Models: A Comprehensive Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> A comprehensive survey synthesizes current challenges, solutions, and future research directions for ensuring the reliability and responsibility of foundation models across nine critical dimensions. It highlights the complex interconnections between issues like bias, security, privacy, and alignment to foster the development of trustworthy AI.

## Problem

- Foundation models' widespread deployment raises urgent concerns about their reliability, defined as performing intended functions accurately, consistently, and robustly.
- Ensuring foundation models align with ethical principles and societal values (responsibility) is critical to prevent adverse socioeconomic consequences and maintain public trust.
- Existing research often addressed specific AI safety and ethical issues in isolation, lacking a holistic, cross-cutting analysis of their interdependencies.

## Method

- The paper systematically reviews and categorizes the challenges and solutions across ==nine core dimensions==: ==bias and fairness==, ==alignment==, ==security==, ==privacy==, ==hallucination==, ==uncertainty==, ==distribution shift==, ==explainability==, and ==AIGC detection==.
- It provides a structured framework within each dimension, defining concepts, reviewing evaluation methods, outlining ==mitigation strategies==, and identifying current limitations.
- The survey offers a holistic, unified examination by explicitly discussing the intersections and synergies between these dimensions, emphasizing complex trade-offs and shared challenges.

## Results

- The survey consolidates existing definitions, evaluation methods, and mitigation strategies for each of the nine dimensions of reliable and responsible foundation models.
- It identifies specific challenges and limitations for each dimension, such as reward hacking in alignment, trade-offs between privacy and utility, and difficulties in ensuring faithful explanations.
- The analysis reveals pervasive interconnections and complex trade-offs between dimensions, exemplified by how adversarial training can impact fairness or jailbreaking attacks can circumvent privacy safeguards.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The field is undergoing a fundamental shift, moving beyond just performance scaling to prioritize the inherent reliability and responsibility of foundation models.
> - The various dimensions of reliability and responsibility are deeply interconnected, requiring comprehensive, systemic solutions rather than isolated approaches.
> - Significant limitations and open challenges persist across all dimensions, necessitating substantial future research and collaborative efforts from academia, industry, and government.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper positions itself squarely at the intersection of the rapidly advancing field of foundation models (FMs) and the increasingly critical domain of AI ethics and safety. Foundation models, encompassing Large Language Models (LLMs), Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), Image Generative Models, and Video Generative Models, have become ubiquitous, driving innovation across sectors like law, medicine, education, and finance. However, their widespread deployment has brought to the forefront urgent concerns regarding their reliability and responsibility.

The research landscape for FMs has traditionally emphasized capabilities and performance scaling. This survey signifies a maturation of the field, highlighting a "fundamental shift" toward a dual imperative: not only developing powerful models but also ensuring they are "inherently reliable and responsible." The authors explicitly state that their work addresses the "critical need for these models to be both reliable and responsible," which they define as the model's capacity to "perform its intended functions accurately, consistently, and robustly" (reliability) and "alignment of a model’s behavior with ethical principles and societal values" (responsibility).

While previous surveys have tackled specific aspects of AI safety, such as hallucination or general safety threats, this paper differentiates itself by offering a "holistic, cross-cutting analysis." It synthesizes knowledge across nine critical dimensions—bias and fairness, alignment, security, privacy, hallucination, uncertainty, distribution shift, explainability, and Artificial Intelligence-Generated Content (AIGC) detection—and explores their interconnections. This comprehensive scope is crucial because these dimensions are often intertwined, and addressing them in isolation can lead to unforeseen trade-offs or incomplete solutions. By highlighting these "synergies and shared challenges," the survey aims to provide a unified perspective essential for advancing the field as a whole. It also deliberately focuses on the "intrinsic properties" of FMs when used as intended, complementing other bodies of work that address malicious misuse.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this comprehensive survey is to systematically analyze the challenges and solutions related to the "reliable and responsible development of foundation models." This overarching goal is broken down into several specific aims, clearly articulated in the abstract and introduction:

1. **Comprehensive Review of Critical Issues:** To explore and summarize the current state of research across nine key dimensions: bias and fairness, security, privacy, uncertainty, explainability, distribution shift, hallucination, alignment, and AIGC detection. This involves defining each issue, reviewing existing evaluation metrics, and outlining mitigation strategies.
2. **Holistic, Cross-Cutting Analysis:** To move beyond specialized reviews by presenting a unified examination that highlights the "intersections between these areas, highlighting their connections and shared challenges." This emphasizes a systemic understanding of how these different aspects of reliability and responsibility interact.
3. **Future Research Directions:** For each area, to "outline concrete future research directions," thereby serving as a roadmap for academics, industry practitioners, and policymakers.
4. **Promote Ethical and Trustworthy AI:** To "foster the development of foundation models that are not only powerful but also ethical, trustworthy, reliable, and socially responsible." This reflects a deep motivation to guide the field towards safer and more beneficial AI systems.

The motivation for undertaking such a monumental survey is rooted in the escalating real-world impact of foundation models. Their rapid integration into critical domains—from healthcare and finance to education and legal systems—necessitates stringent safeguards. The authors underscore that "ensuring their reliability and responsibility has become critical for academia, industry, and government." The unprecedented scale of user adoption, exemplified by the rapid growth of models like ChatGPT, accentuates the urgency of addressing these challenges to prevent adverse socioeconomic consequences and maintain public trust.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a comprehensive survey, the methodology primarily involves an extensive and structured literature review and synthesis. The authors gather, categorize, and critically analyze a vast body of existing research concerning the reliability and responsibility of foundation models.

The approach is highly structured:
- **Taxonomic Organization:** The survey organizes the field by defining "reliability" and "responsibility" as foundational concepts. It then dissects these into nine core dimensions: Bias and Fairness, Alignment, Security, Privacy, Hallucination, Uncertainty, Distribution Shift, Explainability, and AIGC Detection. This robust taxonomy (visualized in Figure 1 and Figure 3) provides a clear framework for understanding complex interdependencies.
- **Categorization by Model Type:** Within each dimension, the analysis differentiates challenges and solutions across four major classes of foundation models: Large Language Models (LLMs), Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), Image Generative Models, and Video Generative Models. This allows for a granular understanding of modality-specific issues.
- **Systematic Review within Each Dimension:** For each of the nine dimensions, the authors systematically cover:
 - **Definitions and Concepts:** Establishing a common language and understanding.
 - **Evaluation Methods:** Reviewing quantitative and qualitative techniques to measure the presence or severity of issues.
 - **Mitigation Strategies:** Summarizing current approaches and proposed solutions to address the identified problems.
 - **Current Limitations and Open Challenges:** Critically assessing the shortcomings of existing methods.
 - **Future Directions:** Proposing concrete research avenues to overcome these limitations.
- **Emphasis on Intersections:** A unique aspect of the methodology is the dedicated section (Section 12) on "Intersection and Conclusion." Here, the authors explicitly discuss how the various dimensions interact (e.g., bias and security, uncertainty and alignment), highlighting complex trade-offs and reinforcing the need for holistic solutions.
- **Timeliness:** The survey explicitly states its coverage of "literature that was publicly available up to May 2025," demonstrating a commitment to being highly current, especially crucial in the fast-paced AI research domain.

This methodological rigor enables the paper to offer not just a collection of research but a coherent, interconnected understanding of the diverse challenges and opportunities in building trustworthy foundation models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

As a survey, the paper's "findings" are not novel experimental outcomes but rather a comprehensive synthesis of the state of research in reliable and responsible foundation models, highlighting key insights, challenges, and future directions across nine critical dimensions and their intersections.

**Overview of Key Findings per Dimension:**

- **Bias and Fairness:** FMs inherit biases from training data, leading to skewed representations and discriminatory outputs. Evaluation methods include analyzing generated text (distribution, classification, word-level metrics), feature embeddings (WEAT, IAT, SEAT), and token selection probabilities. Mitigation involves training data augmentation (CDA, Mix-Debias), alignment with instruction tuning (RLHF-style, InstructGPT), and post-processing (retrieval, content revision). Challenges include autoregressive bias propagation, contextual framing, cross-cultural definition variance, and the tension between fairness and authenticity.
- **Alignment:** FMs' inherent training objectives (e.g., next-token prediction) do not guarantee alignment with human values. Key methods are Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for basic instruction following, Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) for nuanced preference learning, and Prompt Engineering for guiding behavior without parameter changes. RLHF faces tractability and generality issues (reward hacking, human capacity limits), while direct alignment methods can overfit.
- **Security:** FMs are vulnerable to various attacks. LLMs face jailbreak attacks (bypassing safety controls), prompt injection (overriding instructions), and backdoor/poisoning attacks (malicious data during training). MLLMs are also susceptible to image adversarial attacks, and image generative models can be coerced into generating harmful content. Defenses range from output ensembling and prompt preprocessing to provable guarantees (e.g., randomized smoothing) and robust content moderation pipelines. Limitations include the computational cost of attacks/defenses, lack of formal guarantees, and challenges with multimodal robustness.
- **Privacy:** FMs memorize sensitive training data, posing risks like Membership Inference Attacks (identifying if a record was used) and training data extraction. Privacy-preserving techniques include Differential Privacy (DP-SGD) and data sanitization. Challenges include the trade-off between privacy and utility, performance degradation with DP, and the difficulty of ensuring "contextual privacy."
- **Hallucination:** Models generate unfaithful or unverifiable information (factuality hallucination) or content inconsistent with input/context (faithfulness hallucination). Sources include flawed data, knowledge boundaries, architectural choices, and exposure bias. Detection involves retrieval-based fact-checking and uncertainty estimation from internal states. Mitigation uses data-centric approaches (cleaning, augmentation), model-based strategies (RAG, architectural improvements), and decoding methods (visual contrastive decoding).
- **Uncertainty:** Refers to the confidence in predictions. It stems from aleatoric (inherent data randomness) and epistemic (lack of model knowledge) sources. Quantification uses probabilistic methods (entropy, self-consistency) and calibrated confidence scores (ECE, temperature scaling). Models can verbalize uncertainty. Mitigation strategies include selective abstention, deferral to larger models, clarification requests, and statistically rigorous methods like conformal prediction for risk control.
- **Distribution Shift:** Occurs when training and test data distributions differ, degrading performance. Categorized as covariate, label, or concept shift. OOD detection identifies unfamiliar inputs (e.g., via LLM-based likelihood ratios, cosine distance). OOD generalization enhances robustness (data augmentation, adversarial training, invariant learning). Domain adaptation tailors models to new domains (in-context learning, retrieval-augmented generation, fine-tuning, test-time training, model editing). Scalability and catastrophic forgetting remain major limitations.
- **Explainability:** Aims to understand FM predictions. Methods include feature attribution (perturbing input, gradient-based, attention-based), exploring inherent knowledge (probing latent representations, neuron interventions, concept learning, mechanistic interpretability), and analyzing the roles of samples in different learning stages. Evaluation focuses on plausibility (human perception) and faithfulness (accurate reflection of model workings). Challenges include ensuring the faithfulness of explanations and understanding dynamic knowledge storage.
- **AIGC Detection:** A binary classification problem to distinguish AI-generated from human-created content. Methods include zero-shot detectors (statistical, intuitive indicators, pre-trained LLMs), watermark-based detection (training-free, learnable), and neural network detectors (supervised classifiers). Key properties for watermarks are imperceptibility, quality preservation, and robustness. Fairness of detectors (e.g., bias against non-native speakers) and robustness against adversarial attacks are ongoing challenges.

**Intersections as a Core Finding:**
The survey's most critical "finding" is the pervasive and complex interconnections between these dimensions. For instance:
- **Bias and Security:** Adversarial training for robustness can exacerbate fairness issues, while data poisoning can introduce new biases.
- **Bias and AIGC Detection:** Detectors can inherit biases, unfairly flagging content from marginalized groups, and watermarking can be inconsistently applied.
- **Security and Privacy:** Jailbreaking attacks can circumvent privacy safeguards, making compliance with "right to be forgotten" regulations difficult.
- **Uncertainty and Alignment:** Aligning models to express uncertainty accurately and abstain when appropriate is crucial for trustworthiness.
- **Hallucination, Uncertainty, Distribution Shift, and Alignment:** These are deeply intertwined, as OOD data often leads to overconfident hallucinations, and alignment mechanisms must account for evolving knowledge and modality interactions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This comprehensive survey holds significant potential impact across academia, industry, and policy-making for several reasons:

1. **Unified Understanding:** By synthesizing the vast and disparate research on FM reliability and responsibility into a coherent framework, the paper provides a much-needed unified understanding. This helps researchers identify gaps, avoid redundant efforts, and foster interdisciplinary collaboration. For practitioners, it offers a structured guide to navigating the complex landscape of developing and deploying trustworthy AI.
2. **Roadmap for Future Research:** The explicit identification of "Current Limitations and Future Directions" for each of the nine dimensions, alongside the discussion of intersections, serves as a comprehensive research agenda. This is invaluable for guiding funding bodies, academic researchers, and industry labs toward the most pressing and impactful problems.
3. **Informing Policy and Governance:** The detailed analysis of risks (e.g., bias, privacy, security) and mitigation strategies provides crucial evidence and context for policymakers and regulatory bodies. As governments grapple with AI regulation, a clear understanding of these technical challenges is essential for developing effective and informed policies that promote responsible AI development.
4. **Promoting Responsible Development:** By emphasizing the dual imperative of capability and responsibility, the survey reinforces the ethical obligations of the AI community. It aims to "foster the development of foundation models that are not only powerful but also ethical, trustworthy, reliable, and socially responsible," influencing design principles and development practices.
5. **Timeliness and Relevance:** Covering literature up to May 2025, the survey is exceptionally current, addressing the latest advancements and emergent challenges in a rapidly evolving field. This makes it highly relevant for immediate application and future planning in an era where FM capabilities are expanding at an unprecedented pace.
6. **Bridging Theory and Practice:** The discussions on evaluation metrics, mitigation techniques, and real-world deployment challenges offer practical insights for engineers and product developers, helping them translate theoretical safety and ethical principles into actionable strategies.

In essence, this survey is more than just a literature review; it is a strategic document. Its collaborative nature, broad scope, and forward-looking perspective position it as a foundational resource for shaping the discourse and directing efforts towards building a more reliable, responsible, and trustworthy AI ecosystem.
