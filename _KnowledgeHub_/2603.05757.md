# EmboAlign: Aligning Video Generation with Compositional Constraints for Zero-Shot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> EmboAlign integrates video generative models (VGMs) with vision-language models (VLMs) to achieve zero-shot robotic manipulation. The system addresses physical implausibility in VGM outputs and retargeting errors by enforcing VLM-derived compositional constraints, achieving an average success rate of 68.3% across six real-robot tasks, a 43.3 percentage point improvement over existing methods.

## Problem

- Video generative models (VGMs) produce visually coherent but often physically implausible motions, leading to 'physical hallucinations' that hinder precise robotic manipulation.
- Converting pixel-space video motions from VGMs into robot actions via geometric retargeting introduces cumulative errors due to imperfect perception, resulting in execution failures.
- Purely constraint-based manipulation methods suffer from initialization sensitivity, struggling to find feasible solutions for complex, long-horizon tasks without good initial trajectories.

## Method

- The framework automatically extracts task-specific ==compositional constraints== from natural language instructions using a ==Vision-Language Model (VLM)==.
- It filters physically implausible video rollouts generated by a ==Video Generative Model (VGM)== using a ==two-tier scoring system== based on visual plausibility and VLM-derived spatial constraint satisfaction.
- Selected video rollouts are refined into precise, robot-executable trajectories through a ==nonlinear optimization== process that minimizes VLM-derived constraint violations while adhering to the VGM's motion prior.

## Results

- EmboAlign achieved an average success rate of **68.3%** across six distinct real-robot manipulation tasks.
- The system demonstrated a **43.3 percentage point** improvement in average success rate compared to both constraint-only (**21.7%**) and video-only (**25.0%**) baseline methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both constraint-guided video selection and constraint-based trajectory optimization are essential, with the full EmboAlign system achieving the highest success rate of **68.3%** compared to selection-only (**48.3%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The integration of VGMs (for diverse motion priors) and VLMs (for structured physical reasoning) effectively overcomes the individual limitations of each, enabling more robust zero-shot robotic manipulation.
> - Explicitly enforcing VLM-derived compositional constraints at both the video selection and trajectory optimization stages is crucial for significantly enhancing physical plausibility and precision in robot actions.
> - The quality of the underlying video generative model directly impacts the overall success rate of such integrated systems, suggesting that advancements in VGMs can further improve performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is rapidly evolving, with a central challenge being the development of generalizable robotic manipulation policies that can adapt to diverse objects, scenes, and instructions without extensive, costly task-specific retraining. EmboAlign directly addresses this critical area by leveraging recent advancements in large-scale foundation models, specifically video generative models (VGMs) and vision-language models (VLMs).

The work is situated at the intersection of two prominent paradigms in modern robotics:
- **Video Generative Models (VGMs) for Manipulation:** A growing body of research, including works like UniPi, Large Video Planner (LVP), and Robotic manipulation by imitating generated videos, has explored using VGMs trained on vast internet datasets to generate video plans for robotic tasks. These models can produce temporally coherent rollouts that capture rich object dynamics and goal-directed behavior, offering a compelling foundation for "zero-shot" control. However, a significant limitation of these video-only approaches is their propensity for "physical hallucinations"—generating physically implausible motions such as object interpenetration, non-conservative dynamics, or prompt-following drift. Furthermore, converting pixel-space video motion into precise robot actions through geometric retargeting often introduces cumulative errors from imperfect depth estimation and keypoint tracking, leading to execution failures. EmboAlign acknowledges the strength of VGMs in providing diverse motion priors but aims to rectify their inherent physical implausibility. Related works like GVP-WM and GROOT also attempt to ground video plans, but EmboAlign takes a complementary approach by leveraging VLMs for explicit constraint enforcement rather than learned world models or exploration.
- **Constraint-Based Manipulation:** Concurrently, there is a strong interest in using compositional constraints to specify complex manipulation tasks. Methods like ReKep, Code-as-Monitor, SafeBimanual, Maestro, and VLMPC have demonstrated that VLMs can effectively extract and formulate spatial, temporal, and interaction requirements from language instructions. These constraints are then used in optimization frameworks to guide robot actions. The primary drawback of purely constraint-based methods, however, is their sensitivity to initialization. Local optimizers often struggle to find feasible solutions for complex, long-horizon tasks if not provided with a good initial trajectory, frequently converging to poor local optima or failing entirely.

EmboAlign distinguishes itself by **unifying these two complementary paradigms** within a single, data-free framework. It leverages the generative diversity and rich motion priors from VGMs to provide robust initial trajectories (addressing the initialization sensitivity of constraint-based methods), while simultaneously employing the structured physical reasoning and semantic grounding of VLMs to enforce compositional constraints (addressing the physical implausibility of VGM outputs). This integrated approach seeks to overcome the individual weaknesses of both video-only and constraint-only methods, making the outputs of large foundation models more directly actionable and reliable for real-world robotic manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of EmboAlign is to enable precise, safe, and zero-shot robotic manipulation by effectively bridging the gap between the expressive power of large video generative models and the stringent physical demands of real-world tasks.

The motivation for this work stems from two critical and compounding failure modes observed in existing video-based manipulation pipelines:
1. **Physical Implausibility in VGM Outputs:** Pretrained on vast, diverse internet video data, VGMs frequently generate rollouts that, while visually coherent, are physically implausible. These "hallucinations" include objects interpenetrating, non-conservative motion, or drift from the intended prompt. Such errors are particularly detrimental for precise manipulation, where adherence to physical laws and task-specific conditions is paramount. The lack of inherent mechanisms in VGMs to enforce physical or semantic constraints leads to unreliable motion plans.
2. **Cumulative Errors in Geometric Retargeting:** Even when VGMs produce visually plausible motions, the process of converting pixel-space video motion into robot actions via geometric retargeting (e.g., through depth estimation or keypoint tracking) introduces cumulative errors. Imperfect perception can lead to significant deviations between the intended pixel-level motion and the actual robot trajectory, resulting in large execution failures.

The authors identify a crucial unmet need: current VGM-based pipelines lack explicit mechanisms to enforce the compositional constraints (spatial relations, kinematic requirements, safety conditions) that are fundamental to successful manipulation. EmboAlign is motivated by the insight that **VLMs offer a complementary capability to VGMs**: while VGMs excel at generating diverse motion sequences, VLMs provide structured physical reasoning and semantic grounding that can identify and verify these critical physical constraints.

Therefore, the key objectives are to:
- Automatically extract task-specific compositional constraints from natural language instructions using a VLM.
- Develop a mechanism to use these VLM-derived constraints to filter physically implausible rollouts generated by VGMs.
- Design a method to refine the retargeted robot trajectories, using the same constraint set, to correct for perception-induced errors and ensure precise, safe execution.
- Demonstrate that this unified framework can substantially improve the success rate of zero-shot real-robot manipulation tasks without any task-specific training data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EmboAlign is presented as a data-free framework that aligns video generative model outputs with compositional constraints at inference time. The core methodology revolves around a two-stage application of VLM-derived constraints within a video-to-action pipeline.

**A. Problem Formulation**
The problem is framed as generating a feasible end-effector trajectory (ξ) from an initial RGB-D observation (o) and a language instruction (ℓ), ensuring the task is completed while satisfying all relevant physical and geometric constraints. Objects are represented by sparse sets of 3D keypoints (k), which serve as the basis for constraint formulation.

**B. Compositional Constraint Generation**
1. **VLM-driven Constraint Extraction:** A vision-language model (VLM) is used to parse the initial RGB-D observation and the language instruction into a set of compositional constraints (C). The VLM (e.g., leveraging capabilities of models like GPT-4V or similar) is prompted to decompose the task into physical and relational requirements (e.g., "gripper must approach from above," "object not exceed velocity threshold").
2. **Keypoint-Based Representation:** To make constraints object-agnostic and generalizable, the framework adopts a keypoint-based formulation. Segment Anything is applied to the initial RGB image to obtain instance masks for task-relevant entities. Sparse 2D keypoints are sampled from these masks. These keypoints, along with the rendered image, are then fed to the VLM to generate Python functions, where each constraint `c ∈ C` maps a 3D keypoint configuration to a scalar cost. `c(k) ≤ 0` indicates satisfaction.

**C. Constraint-Guided Video Selection**
This stage addresses the issue of physical implausibility in VGM outputs.
1. **VGM Rollout Generation:** A batch of N candidate rollout videos (V) is sampled from a pretrained video generative model (VGM), conditioned on the initial observation and language instruction. The paper uses Large Video Planner (LVP) as its primary VGM.
2. **Two-Tier Scoring and Selection:**
 - **Visual Plausibility Score (`s_vis`):** V-JEPA-2, a self-supervised latent world model, is used to evaluate the temporal coherence and physical plausibility of each rollout. It predicts future latent representations from context frames. Rollouts with lower cosine discrepancy between predicted and observed latent representations are considered more physically plausible, as they align better with the learned dynamics of the world model.
 - **Spatial Constraint Satisfaction Score (`s_spatial`):** For candidates passing the visual plausibility filter, their 2D keypoints are tracked through the rollout using CoTracker. Monocular video depth estimation (RollingDepth) provides per-frame depth maps, which are calibrated and used to back-project 2D keypoints into 3D trajectories. The VLM-derived constraints (C) are then applied to these 3D trajectories to compute a spatial constraint cost (`cost_C(K)`).
 - **Joint Selection:** A sequential evaluation strategy is employed: candidates are first ranked by visual plausibility (`s_vis`). Then, `s_spatial` is computed for the most plausible candidates in order. The first rollout whose spatial constraint cost falls below a predefined threshold (ϵ) is selected (`V*`), prioritizing both visual coherence and task-level constraint satisfaction.

**D. Constraint-Based Trajectory Optimization**
This stage refines the selected video rollout into a precise, robot-executable trajectory.
1. **Grasp Estimation:** AnyGrasp is used to predict stable grasp candidates on the target object from the initial scene point cloud. SAM 3D is utilized to reconstruct a 3D object model for robustness, defining the gripper-object transform (`T_grasp`).
2. **Motion Retargeting:** The 3D keypoint trajectories from `V*` are converted into an initial end-effector trajectory (`ξ^(0)`) by assuming a fixed gripper-object transform. Object poses are recovered by fitting rigid transforms to keypoint correspondences, which then determine the end-effector poses.
3. **Trajectory Optimization:** The initial trajectory `ξ^(0)` often contains errors. A nonlinear program is solved to refine this trajectory:
 `ξ* = arg min(ξ_1:T) [ Σ(c∈C) Σ(t=1 to T) max(0, c(k_t))^2 + λ Σ(t=1 to T) ||ξ_t - ξ^(0)_t||^2 ]`
 This objective function minimizes constraint violations (`c(k_t)`) while staying close to the VGM-generated motion prior (`ξ^(0)_t`). The optimization is performed using Sequential Least Squares Programming (SLSQP), initialized with `ξ^(0)`. The optimized trajectory `ξ*` is then executed by a Dobot Nova2 robot.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

EmboAlign was rigorously evaluated on six real-robot manipulation tasks, each requiring precise and constraint-sensitive execution: "Open the Lid," "Stack the Blocks," "Press the Stapler," "Hammer the Block," "Place the Block Securely," and "Pour the Water."

**A. Quantitative Results**
- **Overall Performance:** EmboAlign achieved an average success rate of **68.3%** across all six tasks (7/10 for Stack, 8/10 for Press, 4/10 for Hammer, 8/10 for Place, 7/10 for Open, 7/10 for Pour).
- **Comparison with Baselines:**
 - **ReKep (Constraint-only):** Achieved an average success rate of 21.7%.
 - **NovaFlow (Video-only):** Achieved an average success rate of 25.0%.
 - EmboAlign demonstrated a **43.3 percentage point improvement** over both ReKep and NovaFlow, highlighting the substantial benefits of its integrated approach.
- **Tasks with Largest Gains:** The method showed the most significant improvements on tasks demanding precise contact geometry, such as "Press the Stapler" (8/10 for EmboAlign vs. 0/10 for NovaFlow and 2/10 for ReKep) and "Place the Block Securely" (8/10 for EmboAlign vs. 4/10 for NovaFlow and 1/10 for ReKep). These results underscore the effectiveness of constraint alignment for sensitive manipulation.

**B. Qualitative Results**
The paper provides a compelling visualization (Fig. 5) of the constraint-based video selection process for the block stacking task. This illustrates how EmboAlign effectively filters out physically implausible or task-inconsistent VGM rollouts. Examples of rejected rollouts include those exhibiting object deformation, object disappearance, misplacement violating spatial alignment, or moving the wrong object. This filtering step ensures that only the most physically plausible and semantically consistent video plans proceed to the execution phase.

**C. Ablation Study**
- **Effect of Video Generative Model (Table II):** While EmboAlign consistently outperforms baselines, its performance is sensitive to the quality of the underlying VGM. LVP (7/10 avg.) performed better than Wan2.2 (6.2/10 avg.) and Cosmos2.5 (5.7/10 avg.), indicating that improved VGMs could further enhance EmboAlign's success rate.
- **Contributions of Pipeline Components (Table III):**
 - **Constraint-only (28.3% avg.)** and **Video-only (23.3% avg.)** methods performed poorly, confirming their individual limitations.
 - Adding **Constraint-guided Selection (+Selection)** improved the average success rate to **48.3%**, demonstrating that filtering implausible video rollouts is crucial.
 - Adding **Constraint-based Trajectory Optimization (+Opt)**, which represents the full EmboAlign system, further boosted the average success rate to **68.3%**. This ablation clearly validates the complementary benefits of both the video selection and trajectory optimization stages, showing how they together mitigate the shortcomings of either approach alone.

**D. Failure Mode Analysis**
An analysis of unsuccessful trials revealed the primary sources of failure:
1. **Video Generation Quality (31.57%):** Residual subtle physical artifacts from the VGM that pass the constraint filter but lead to execution failure, reflecting inherent limitations of current VGMs.
2. **VLM Keypoint Referring (26.31%):** Misinterpretation or confusion of spatially proximate or overlapping keypoint labels by the VLM, especially in cluttered scenes, leading to incorrect constraint instantiation.
3. **Retargeting Failure (15.79%):** Accumulated errors from imperfect keypoint tracking or rigid-body fitting during motion retargeting, resulting in infeasible end-effector trajectories.
4. **Depth Estimation (15.80%):** Inaccuracies in monocular depth prediction causing systematic bias in 3D keypoint reconstruction and misjudgment of spatial relations.
5. **Others (10.53%):** Including grasp estimation errors and overly conservative constraint thresholds.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EmboAlign represents a significant step forward in zero-shot robotic manipulation by offering a principled and effective approach to integrate the complementary strengths of video generative models (VGMs) and vision-language models (VLMs). Its significance and potential impact are multi-faceted:

- **Bridging the Generative-Physical Gap:** The most critical contribution is its ability to bridge the gap between the expressive but often physically naive outputs of large generative models and the precise physical requirements of real-world robotics. By actively aligning VGM rollouts with VLM-derived compositional constraints, EmboAlign transforms abstract video plans into reliable, executable robot trajectories.
- **Enhanced Zero-Shot Generalization:** The framework enables precise manipulation without any task-specific training data, leveraging pre-trained foundation models. This is a crucial step towards truly general-purpose robots that can understand and execute novel instructions in diverse environments, significantly reducing the data and engineering effort traditionally required for new robotic tasks.
- **Improved Robustness and Safety:** By explicitly enforcing physical and safety constraints at both the planning (video selection) and execution (trajectory optimization) stages, EmboAlign dramatically improves the physical plausibility and precision of robot actions. This reduces failures caused by hallucinations, incorrect approach directions, or imprecise movements, leading to more robust and safer robot operations. The success on constraint-sensitive tasks like "Press the Stapler" and "Place the Block Securely" highlights this benefit.
- **Unified and Data-Free Framework:** EmboAlign provides a unified, data-free pipeline that systematically leverages the strengths of two distinct types of foundation models. It demonstrates that structured reasoning from VLMs can effectively "ground" the diverse, free-form generation from VGMs, providing a powerful synergy that is greater than the sum of its parts. This modularity could inspire similar integrations in other AI-driven robotic systems.
- **Guidance for Future Research:** The detailed failure mode analysis offers clear directions for future research. Improvements in video generation quality, more robust VLM keypoint referring (especially in cluttered scenes), and advancements in real-time 3D perception (depth estimation and keypoint tracking) will directly enhance the performance and reliability of systems like EmboAlign. This highlights areas where further foundational AI research can directly benefit robotics.

In conclusion, EmboAlign demonstrates that intelligently combining the generative capabilities of video models with the structured reasoning of vision-language models offers a robust pathway towards generalizable and precise zero-shot robotic manipulation, making large-scale internet-trained models more practical and dependable for complex physical tasks in the real world.
