# Weaving Context Across Images: Improving Vision-Language Models through Focus-Centric Visual Chains

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper introduces the Focus-Centric Visual Chain (FCVC) paradigm and the Focus-Centric Data Synthesis (FCDS) framework to improve Vision-Language Models' (VLMs) ability to reason across multiple images. Fine-tuning VLMs with the resulting VISC-150K dataset led to an average accuracy increase of 3.16% on multi-image benchmarks for LLaVA-OneVision, establishing new state-of-the-art results on four benchmarks while preserving general VLM capabilities.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) demonstrate significant performance degradation when processing and reasoning across multiple images, despite their capabilities with single images.
- VLMs struggle with cross-image correlations and visual discontinuity, making it difficult to grasp intricate relationships and integrate information scattered across multiple visual inputs.
- There is a scarcity of high-quality, scalable, and cost-effective multi-image reasoning data, hindering the development of robust multi-image VLMs.

## Method

- Proposing the ==Focus-Centric Visual Chain (FCVC)==, a multi-step reasoning paradigm that breaks down complex multi-image queries into a sequence of sub-questions with dynamic visual foci.
- Developing ==Focus-Centric Data Synthesis (FCDS)==, a bottom-up framework that uses open-source models (Extractor, Connector, Annotator, Questioner) to generate structured multi-image reasoning data.
- Constructing ==VISC-150K==, a large-scale dataset of 150,000 high-quality multi-image reasoning samples following the FCVC format, to train and evaluate VLMs.

## Results

- Fine-tuning with VISC-150K led to an average accuracy increase of **3.16%** for LLaVA-OneVision and **2.24%** for Qwen2-VL across seven diverse multi-image benchmarks.
- The method established new state-of-the-art results on four benchmarks: MMIU, MIRB, BLINK, and NLVR2, and showed measurable gains on the video benchmark MVBench.
- A quality assessment on synthesized data yielded a **97.5%** overall accuracy, validating the reliability of the FCDS framework, and general VLM abilities were maintained or slightly improved on single-image benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The Focus-Centric Visual Chain (FCVC) paradigm enables VLMs to perform multi-step 'slow thinking' by dynamically focusing on relevant visual subsets and decomposing complex tasks into sequential sub-questions.
> - The Focus-Centric Data Synthesis (FCDS) framework can generate high-quality, reliable, and cost-effective multi-image reasoning data using open-source models, overcoming data scarcity and cost issues.
> - Fine-tuning VLMs with a large-scale, structured dataset like VISC-150K significantly enhances multi-image reasoning capabilities without compromising general vision-language understanding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid evolution of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has revolutionized single-image visual tasks, enabling impressive capabilities such as image captioning, visual question answering, and object detection. Models like GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5-Pro, LLaVA, and Qwen-VL have demonstrated near-human performance in interpreting and generating content based on individual images. However, the broader research landscape acknowledges a significant bottleneck: the performance of these VLMs often degrades dramatically when faced with multi-image inputs. Real-world scenarios rarely present isolated images; instead, information is often fragmented across multiple visual cues that require contextual understanding, comparison, and reasoning.

Existing challenges in multi-image scenarios stem from two core issues: (1) **Cross-image correlations**, where VLMs struggle to grasp the diverse and intricate relationships between multiple images (e.g., temporal sequences, spatial layouts, or thematic connections), and (2) **Visual discontinuity**, where critical information for a complete understanding is scattered across different visual inputs, making it hard for models to integrate a holistic context.

Previous attempts to enhance reasoning in large language models (LLMs) and, by extension, VLMs, have explored strategies like Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Tree-of-Thought, and Graph-of-Thought. While successful for single-modal or simple multimodal reasoning, these approaches often fall short in complex multi-image contexts. Furthermore, data scarcity for multi-image reasoning tasks is a significant hurdle. Methods leveraging powerful multimodal models for direct reasoning chain generation, or distilling data from closed-source models (like GPT-4o), suffer from issues of "insufficient reliability" (even state-of-the-art models are inconsistent on complex multi-image tasks) and "prohibitive costs," limiting scalability.

This paper addresses these critical gaps by proposing a novel paradigm and a scalable data synthesis framework. It moves beyond single-image understanding and rudimentary multi-image processing by enabling VLMs to perform "slow thinking" and structured reasoning over complex visual inputs. By focusing on synthesizing high-quality, reliable, and cost-effective multi-image reasoning data using *open-source models*, this work differentiates itself from expensive, less reliable closed-source alternatives. It contributes to the growing body of research on **multimodal reasoning** and **data-centric AI**, specifically pushing the boundaries of what VLMs can achieve in complex, real-world visual environments where context is woven across multiple discrete images. The work builds upon the success of existing VLM architectures (like LLaVA and Qwen-VL) by providing a specialized training methodology and dataset to unlock their potential in multi-image understanding, thereby pushing the state-of-the-art in this challenging domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to significantly enhance the perception, comprehension, and reasoning abilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) when confronted with intricate multi-image inputs, a common scenario in real-world applications.

The motivation for this work arises from several critical observations within the current landscape of VLMs:
1. **Performance Degradation in Multi-Image Scenarios:** Despite their remarkable success in single-image tasks, current VLMs exhibit a notable performance decline when asked to process and reason across multiple images. This limitation severely restricts their applicability to complex real-world problems.
2. **Challenges of Cross-Image Context:** Multi-image tasks inherently involve two key difficulties: discerning **cross-image correlations** (how images relate contextually) and overcoming **visual discontinuity** (the fragmentation of crucial information across different images). VLMs struggle to disentangle and integrate this scattered critical information.
3. **Scarcity of High-Quality Reasoning Data:** While extensive research exists on reasoning tasks, high-quality reasoning data specifically for multi-image scenarios remains scarce. Existing data generation approaches often suffer from reliability issues (even state-of-the-art models are inconsistent) or are prohibitively expensive due to reliance on closed-source, large-scale models. This data bottleneck impedes the development of robust multi-image VLMs.
4. **Need for Structured Reasoning:** Current VLMs often lack a structured "slow thinking" mechanism necessary to decompose complex multi-image queries into manageable sub-problems and iteratively gather visual evidence.

To address these motivations, the paper sets out to achieve the following key objectives:
- **Propose a Novel Reasoning Paradigm:** Introduce the **Focus-Centric Visual Chain (FCVC)**, a multi-image reasoning paradigm that enables VLMs to progressively focus on key information by decomposing complex tasks into a sequence of sub-processes, each addressing a localized sub-question with a specific visual focus.
- **Develop a Scalable Data Synthesis Framework:** Design and implement **Focus-Centric Data Synthesis (FCDS)**, an efficient, bottom-up framework that generates high-quality, reliable, and reproducible multi-image reasoning data. Crucially, this framework relies exclusively on *open-source models* to ensure cost-effectiveness and scalability.
- **Construct a Large-Scale Dataset:** Leverage the FCDS framework to create **VISC-150K**, a large-scale dataset comprising 150,000 high-quality multi-image reasoning samples structured in the Focus-Centric Visual Chain format.
- **Demonstrate Performance Improvement:** Empirically validate the effectiveness of the proposed paradigm and dataset by showing consistent and significant performance gains across diverse multi-image benchmarks for various VLM architectures, without compromising their general vision-language capabilities.

In essence, the motivation is to bridge the gap between single-image VLM prowess and the complexities of multi-image understanding, driven by the practical need for VLMs that can truly reason in real-world, context-rich visual environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology proposed in this paper revolves around two interconnected innovations: the **Focus-Centric Visual Chain (FCVC)** reasoning paradigm and the **Focus-Centric Data Synthesis (FCDS)** framework.

#### 3.1. Focus-Centric Visual Chain (FCVC)

The FCVC is a multi-step reasoning paradigm tailored for multi-image inputs. Given a collection of images $G = \{I_k\}$ and an initial complex question $Q$, a VLM ($M$) progressively constructs a reasoning chain $R$. At each step $i$, the model dynamically identifies a minimized visual information subset $G_i \subseteq G$ (the "focus") and generates an intermediate sub-question $q_i$ related to $G_i$. This process can be formally represented as: $q_i, G_i = M(Q, G, H_{<i})$, where $H_{<i}$ is the history of previous sub-questions. An intermediate answer $a_i$ is then derived from $q_i$ and $G_i$. The model determines whether to extend the reasoning path or synthesize the final answer $A$ from the accumulated sub-question/answer pairs. This sequential, focused approach allows VLMs to break down complex tasks, iteratively aggregate visual evidence, and perform "slow thinking," akin to human cognitive processes.

#### 3.2. Focus-Centric Data Synthesis (FCDS)

To implement the FCVC paradigm in a data-driven manner, the authors propose the FCDS framework, a cost-effective, bottom-up approach for generating certifiable cross-image reasoning data using open-source models. FCDS incrementally assesses inter-image relationships to formulate coherent reasoning paths and corresponding questions. It consists of four systematically designed modules:

1. **Feature Extraction:**
 - **Purpose:** To create detailed textual profiles for each individual image, serving as fundamental "nodes" in the subsequent reasoning graph.
 - **Approach:** A specialized VLM called `Extractor` (based on LLaVA-OneVision-7B) is used. For each image $I$, `Extractor` first encodes visual features ($X_V$) using a visual encoder ($f_e$). These visual features are then aligned with textual semantics via a vision-language connector ($f_c$). Finally, a large language model ($f_{\phi}$) generates a comprehensive profile $p$ for the image. Each profile contains four core elements: overall view, background descriptions, object attributes, and object interactions.

2. **Pair Connection:**
 - **Purpose:** To identify relevant image pairs, establishing "edges" between potentially related nodes in the reasoning graph, as arbitrary connections may lack semantic validity.
 - **Approach:** An LLM named `Connector` (based on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) analyzes the collection of generated image profiles $P$. It identifies valid pairwise connections based on two criteria: (1) **Object-oriented**, where images share co-occurring objects, and (2) **Event-oriented**, where images depict shared or related events. This step outputs a set of connected image pairs $(i, j)$.

3. **Relevance Annotation:**
 - **Purpose:** To systematically categorize and detail the specific nature of relationships between connected image pairs.
 - **Approach:** A dedicated VLM called `Annotator` (based on LLaVA-OneVision-7B) takes a connected image pair $(I_i, I_j)$ as input. It independently encodes both images, concatenates their visual features, and then maps these combined features to aligned textual tokens using a vision-language connector. Finally, a large language model generates a detailed relationship $r$. Relationships are classified into three types:
 - **Temporal:** Images depict a chronological sequence.
 - **Spatial:** Visual elements exhibit geometric and positional correlations.
 - **Semantic:** Images have intangible associations, covering thematic, logical, or causal relationships. Multiple relation types can coexist for a single pair.

4. **Question Generation:**
 - **Purpose:** To construct logically chained sub-questions and a final composite question, aligning with the established reasoning path.
 - **Approach:** An LLM called `Questioner` (based on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) is employed. It samples a sequential chain of interconnected image nodes. For each connected image pair $s_i$ along this path, `Questioner` generates a targeted sub-question $q_i$ based on their annotated relations $r_i$ and corresponding image profiles. These sub-questions are then aggregated into a coherent overarching question $Q$. This process effectively synthesizes the FCVC structure, including sub-questions, visual foci, and intermediate answers, into a structured dataset.

By leveraging these meticulously designed modules, the FCDS framework allows for the automated synthesis of VISC-150K, a large-scale dataset of 150,000 high-quality multi-image reasoning samples that embody the Focus-Centric Visual Chain paradigm. The use of open-source base models ensures scalability and reproducibility, overcoming the limitations of previous data generation methods.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of the proposed Focus-Centric Visual Chain paradigm and the VISC-150K dataset demonstrates significant advancements in multi-image understanding for VLMs.

#### 4.1. Performance on Multi-Image Benchmarks

The authors fine-tuned two distinct VLM architectures, LLaVA-OneVision-7B and Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct, on the VISC-150K dataset. These models were then evaluated across seven comprehensive multi-image benchmarks: MMIU, MuirBench, MIRB, BLINK, NLVR2, Mantis-Eval, and MVBench.

- **Consistent Improvements:** Both LLaVA-OneVision and Qwen2-VL consistently showed performance improvements across all seven benchmarks after fine-tuning with VISC-150K. This consistency across different VLM architectures validates the effectiveness and universality of the proposed method.
- **Significant Gains:** LLaVA-OneVision achieved an impressive average accuracy increase of 3.16%, with notable benchmark-leading improvements of 6.20% on MMIU and 7.85% on MuirBench. Qwen2-VL, already a strong baseline, still saw an average gain of 2.24%.
- **New State-of-the-Art (SOTA):** The method established new SOTA results on four out of the seven benchmarks: MMIU, MIRB, BLINK, and NLVR2. These improvements are attributed to the dataset's richness of visual information, diversity of inter-image relationships, and complexity of task formulations, which align with the method's enhanced capabilities in visual perception, comprehension, and reasoning.
- **Generalization to Video:** The method also showed measurable performance gains on MVBench, a video benchmark. This suggests that the Focus-Centric Visual Chain reasoning paradigm is domain-agnostic, as video can be viewed as a collection of temporally correlated images, demonstrating robust applicability and transferability.

#### 4.2. Discussion of Specific Findings

The authors conducted further in-depth investigations to understand the method's characteristics:

- **Impact of Data Size (RQ1):** Experiments with varying subsets of VISC-150K (from 25K to 150K instances) showed a non-linear relationship between data size and performance. Rapid improvements were observed from 0 to 125K instances, followed by diminishing but continued returns from 125K to 150K. This suggests a "capability activation process" where the model unlocks stronger multi-image potential with an initial influx of reasoning data, and that the FCDS framework can effectively scale for further performance enhancement.
- **Effect on Different Sub-tasks (RQ2):** A detailed analysis on MuirBench's 12 sub-tasks revealed that significant improvements were achieved in eight tasks, particularly those involving similarity analysis or comparative reasoning at image or feature levels (e.g., visual retrieval, attribute similarity). Crucially, even tasks not explicitly present in VISC-150K (e.g., geographic understanding) showed improvements, highlighting cross-domain generalization. However, tasks requiring complex 3D spatial reasoning (e.g., Scene Understanding) or nuanced semantic interpretation (e.g., Cartoon Understanding) showed less improvement, indicating inherent limitations in the base VLM architectures or foundational language models.
- **Impact of Number of Input Images (RQ3):** The model showed remarkable improvements when handling 3-8 images, successfully identifying cross-image relationships. This capability persisted with larger inputs (11-14 images) without significant performance degradation. However, a slight degradation was observed when processing more than 15 images, potentially due to amplified noise or interference from irrelevant data in prolonged sequences.
- **Effect on General Vision-Language Abilities (RQ4):** To ensure that multi-image specialization did not compromise general VLM capabilities, Qwen2-VL fine-tuned on VISC-150K was evaluated on single-image benchmarks (HallusionBench, MMStar, MMMU, MathVista). The results showed comparable or even slightly superior performance, indicating that the method enhances multi-image understanding without diminishing the model's general visual perception and language understanding abilities.
- **Quality of Synthesized Data (RQ5):** A rigorous quality assessment by three Ph.D. students on a random sample of 200 instances yielded a 97.5% overall accuracy (validity rate) with substantial inter-annotator agreement (Fleiss’κ = 0.637). This robustly validates the reliability and high quality of the data synthesized by the automated FCDS framework.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research represents a significant stride towards developing more robust and capable Vision-Language Models (VLMs) that can effectively handle the complexities of multi-image scenarios prevalent in real-world applications. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Addressing a Critical VLM Limitation:** The core contribution is directly addressing the well-documented performance degradation of VLMs in multi-image contexts. By providing a structured approach to integrate fragmented visual information and infer complex cross-image relationships, the work pushes VLMs beyond single-image understanding, enabling them to tackle more holistic and nuanced visual tasks.
- **Novel Reasoning Paradigm:** The introduction of **Focus-Centric Visual Chain (FCVC)** offers a powerful new paradigm for multi-image reasoning. By mimicking human "slow thinking" through question decomposition and stepwise visual focusing, it provides a structured and interpretable way for VLMs to navigate complex visual information. This paradigm could inspire future research in cognitive-inspired AI reasoning.
- **Scalable and Cost-Effective Data Generation:** The **Focus-Centric Data Synthesis (FCDS)** framework is a major innovation for overcoming the critical data scarcity bottleneck in multi-image reasoning. Its bottom-up design, coupled with the exclusive use of open-source models, ensures that high-quality, reliable, and reproducible reasoning data can be generated at scale and at a significantly lower cost compared to methods relying on expensive closed-source APIs. This democratizes access to complex multimodal reasoning data, accelerating research in the field.
- **Release of a Valuable Dataset (VISC-150K):** The creation and release of VISC-150K, a large-scale dataset of 150,000 multi-image reasoning samples structured according to FCVC, is a direct and impactful contribution to the research community. This dataset serves as a benchmark and a valuable training resource, enabling other researchers to build upon these findings and further advance multi-image VLM capabilities.
- **Empirical Validation and Generalizability:** The consistent and significant performance improvements across diverse VLM architectures (LLaVA-OneVision and Qwen2-VL) and multiple challenging multi-image benchmarks, including generalization to video understanding, strongly validate the effectiveness and broad applicability of the proposed methodology. The preservation of general vision-language capabilities further underscores its practical utility.
- **Actionable Pathways for Future Research:** The identified limitations—such as the quadratic complexity of pairwise relevance annotation, the current focus on real-world photos and comics (vs. structured visual content like charts), and inherited limitations from underlying language models (e.g., complex spatial dynamics)—do not detract from the work but rather highlight clear and promising directions for future research. This includes optimizing data synthesis, expanding to new visual domains, and mitigating inherent architectural constraints.

In summary, this study not only achieves state-of-the-art results in multi-image understanding but also provides a foundational data-driven framework and a novel reasoning paradigm that are crucial for developing the next generation of truly intelligent VLMs capable of operating in rich, contextually diverse visual environments.
