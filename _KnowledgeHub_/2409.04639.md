# High-Speed and Impact Resilient Teleoperation of Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from IHMC, University of West Florida, and Boardwalk Robotics developed an integrated teleoperation system for humanoid robots, combining a minimal user setup, advanced control, and cycloidal actuators. This system enables the Nadia robot to perform high-speed, dynamic tasks with 70 milliseconds latency and withstand significant impacts while maintaining robust control.

## Problem

- High latency and low transparency in humanoid teleoperation hinder the execution of fast, dynamic, and synchronized tasks.
- Humanoid robots often lack the impact resilience needed for physical interaction with the environment or unexpected collisions.
- Existing teleoperation systems typically require complex, cumbersome, and high-maintenance user-side hardware, limiting practical deployment.

## Method

- An integrated system combining a minimal, calibration-free user setup (VR headset, controllers, and 7 IMUs) for motion capture and retargeting.
- A ==Kinematics Streaming Toolbox (KST)== that processes retargeted commands with low latency, incorporating filtering, state estimation, prediction, and quadratic programming-based inverse kinematics for precise and safe execution.
- The Nadia humanoid robot, equipped with high-efficiency, impact-resilient cycloidal actuators, enabling high torque output and durability against shock, controlled by a momentum-based whole-body controller.

## Results

- Achieved an end-to-end latency of approximately 70 milliseconds from the Kinematics Streaming Toolbox receiving VR input to the robot reaching the corresponding pose.
- Demonstrated synchronous high-speed interactions, with the Nadia robot successfully playing ping pong with a human opponent.
- Validated impact resilience and rapid recovery by having the robot execute powerful punches on a 100 lbs punching bag, with the control system re-establishing tracking within approximately one second after impact.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Achieving high-performance teleoperation requires a synergistic integration of motion capture, real-time control software, and robust, impact-resilient hardware.
> - Cycloidal actuators are crucial for enabling humanoid robots to execute high-speed, high-force motions and reliably withstand significant impacts without damage or loss of performance.
> - Effective compensation for system delays through advanced state estimation and prediction within the Kinematics Streaming Toolbox allows for highly responsive control even from lower-rate user inputs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Teleoperation of humanoid robots presents a complex challenge, primarily due to the need for high transparency, low latency, and intuitive control, particularly in scenarios demanding high-speed and dynamic movements. Existing research has explored various strategies to mitigate these issues.

One notable area of focus is **motion anticipation**, where techniques like polynomial interpolation and recurrent neural networks are used to predict operator intentions and future movements, thereby compensating for system delays. While promising for reducing lag, these anticipation methods have not been extensively validated in high-speed motion scenarios, according to the authors.

Another approach involves **bilateral teleoperation**, which aims to synchronize human and robot dynamics through haptic feedback and direct coupling of motion. Examples include using human-machine interfaces for synchronized locomotion or full-body exoskeleton cockpits. While these methods can align human and robot motions, they are often sensitive to network delays and typically require cumbersome, complex, and high-maintenance user-side hardware, limiting their practical deployment.

More recent efforts have focused on **simplifying the user-side setup**, such as "shadowing" techniques that allow robots to mimic human movements using only RGB cameras and pre-trained policies. However, these methods usually rely on extensive datasets for training and have not consistently demonstrated effectiveness in high-speed, synchronized teleoperation.

The present work positions itself as an **integrated solution** that seeks to overcome the limitations of previous approaches by combining multiple techniques. It aims to provide a practical and robust framework for real-time control, emphasizing efficiency, scalability, and impact resilience, particularly for high-speed dynamic tasks. Unlike methods requiring complex exoskeletons or extensive data training, this research proposes a minimal, calibration-free user setup combined with advanced control and actuation systems to achieve high transparency and responsiveness.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary motivation behind this research is to advance the field of humanoid robot teleoperation by addressing significant limitations in operational efficiency, specifically regarding control transparency, speed, and robustness. The authors aim to enable humanoid robots to perform complex, dynamic tasks with human-like agility and responsiveness.

The key objectives and motivations can be detailed as follows:

- **Achieve High Transparency and Responsiveness:** A central goal is to minimize the perceived delay between operator input and robot movement, making the robot's actions feel as immediate and fluid as human motion. This necessitates overcoming inherent system latencies and ensuring that the robot can track high-bandwidth human movements.
- **Enable High-Speed Motion Execution:** The research specifically targets applications requiring rapid and dynamic movements, such as punching or playing sports. This requires a teleoperation framework capable of generating and executing commands at a high control bandwidth (e.g., 1 kHz) even when user input is acquired at a lower rate (e.g., 60 Hz).
- **Ensure Impact Resilience:** For tasks involving interaction with the environment or dynamic forces (e.g., punching a bag), the robot's hardware must be able to withstand significant impacts without damage or loss of performance. The motivation is to integrate robust actuation systems that can endure such forces.
- **Simplify the User-Side Setup:** The authors aim to create a practical and accessible teleoperation system by reducing the complexity and intrusiveness of the operator interface. This involves developing a minimal and calibration-free motion capture system that does not require extensive physiological measurements or elaborate exoskeletons.
- **Develop an Integrated and Robust Solution:** The work is motivated by the need for a comprehensive framework that combines advancements across hardware and software domains. This includes efficient motion capture and retargeting, low-latency kinematics streaming, and high-bandwidth, impact-resilient actuators, all working synergistically to enhance teleoperation performance.
- **Validate Performance in Demanding Scenarios:** The motivation extends to demonstrating the system's effectiveness through rigorous experimental validation in challenging tasks that stress test its capabilities in terms of speed, synchronization, and impact handling.

In summary, the research aims to push the boundaries of humanoid teleoperation by developing an integrated system that is fast, robust, intuitive, and practical, thereby expanding the potential applications of humanoid robots in dynamic and interactive environments.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The presented teleoperation system integrates several hardware and software components to achieve high-speed and resilient control of a humanoid robot, Nadia. The overall architecture follows a structured pipeline from user input to robot execution.

**Overall System Architecture:**
The system comprises three main stages:
1. **Motion Retargeting:** Translates human operator movements into robot-specific references. This includes `Footstep Streaming` for locomotion and `Kinematics Retargeting` for upper body movements.
2. **Kinematics Streaming Toolbox (KST):** Processes the retargeted commands, ensuring real-time, safe, and precise execution. It incorporates filtering, state estimation, prediction, and inverse kinematics.
3. **Whole-Body Controller:** Executes the processed commands on the robot, maintaining balance and interacting with the environment.
Visual feedback is provided via a digital twin 3D scene and stereo vision, though these are acknowledged as relying on existing state-of-the-art and are not the primary focus of this paper.

**Motion Retargeting:**
The system employs a minimal, wireless, and calibration-free setup for motion capture, consisting of a Vive Focus 3 VR headset, two VR controllers, and four Vive Ultimate trackers placed on the sternum, waist, and ankles. This setup is adaptable to any system using seven Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs).

The retargeting process involves:
- **Pelvis Retargeting:** The robot's pelvis position and orientation are updated based on the waist tracker's movements. Vertical movement is scaled proportionally to the robot's pelvis height, while roll variations are ignored to prevent unnatural motions, particularly during double support phases.
- **Hands Retargeting:** User shoulder positions are approximated using the chest tracker and headset data. The distance between the sternum and head is used to estimate head length, which in turn approximates shoulder offset. Hand positions (VR controllers) relative to shoulders are scaled to match the robot's arm length, and the orientation of the VR controllers directly maps to the robot's hand orientation.
- **Center of Mass (CoM) Retargeting:** The human's CoM ground projection is transferred to the robot using a normalized offset method. The waist tracker's ground projection and ankle tracker data are used to compute an offset relative to the feet, which is then applied to the robot's feet positions to reconstruct its desired CoM ground position.
- **Footstep Streaming:** This algorithm continuously monitors ankle tracker poses. A step is identified if a foot moves significantly beyond a "step threshold" and is lifted above a "lift threshold." The new footstep position is determined by scaling the normalized movement direction by a predefined stride length and applying it to the robot's current foot position. Yaw adjustment is also incorporated based on tracker yaw changes. The feasibility of the computed footstep pose is checked before being sent to the walking controller. A stability threshold is used to reset the stepping state, preventing unintended steps during leg swing.

**Kinematics Streaming Toolbox (KST):**
The KST is designed for low-latency whole-body motion streaming, operating in a separate thread on the robot side to ensure consistent execution frequency and minimize network delays.
- **Filter and Safety Limits:**
 - **Bounding Box:** Restricts robot commands to a specified 3D volume relative to the robot’s mid-feet reference frame, which dynamically updates with the robot’s stance.
 - **Rate of Change and Velocity Limits:** Invalidates inputs that exceed predefined thresholds to maintain stability and safety.
- **State Estimation and Prediction:** Smoothes and anticipates future states to compensate for delays.
 - **First-order Estimation:** Uses first-order finite differences to estimate input velocities, followed by first-order extrapolation to predict future states. Velocities decay towards zero when no new input is received.
 - **Feedback Controller-based Estimation:** Aims for pose continuity by adding a correction term (computed from pose error) to the finite difference velocity estimation. This correction term linearly cancels the error over a short duration during the prediction phase.
- **Inverse Kinematics (IK) Module:** Solves a Quadratic Program (QP) at each update tick to determine desired joint velocities, ensuring high tracking responsiveness and safe execution.
 - **Objective Function:** Minimizes a combination of nominal joint configuration deviation, kinematic tracking error, and joint velocity magnitude.
 - **Constraints:** Includes joint velocity limits, centroidal momentum constraints (to keep CoM within the support region), and self-collision avoidance (using geometric primitives and evaluating maximum relative velocity along collision axes).
 - **Motion Tasks:** Kinematic objectives (p_i) are computed from feedback controllers to track references. These can include:
 - **Robot CoM:** Desired CoM position in the world, driving towards a constant position relative to feet or tracking the user's CoM.
 - **Spatial Pose of a Rigid Body:** Desired position and orientation of end-effectors (e.g., hands), chest, and pelvis, tracked using a proportional feedback law.
 - **Momentum of the Robot:** Driven to zero to minimize resultant momentum, prioritizing joints with less impact on momentum.
 - The QP solution `v_d` (desired joint velocities) is then integrated to update the desired robot state `q`.
- **Post-Processing:** Refines IK outputs for continuity and adjustable responsiveness.
 - **Velocity Down-scaling:** Dampens execution for smoother motion.
 - **Estimating Joint Accelerations:** Uses a PD-control law to compute desired accelerations for each 1-DoF joint, integrating them to update estimated velocities and positions. A similar strategy is used for the floating base.
 - **Low-Pass Filtering:** Provides an additional control knob for smoothness at the cost of minor delays.
 - **Initial Blending:** Manages the start of the stream to account for discrepancies between IK solution and actual robot configuration.

**Whole-Body Controller:**
The robot employs a momentum-based whole-body controller framed as a QP. It primarily tracks a desired rate of change of momentum while simultaneously tracking external motion objectives (pelvis height, chest orientation, end-effector poses, arm configurations). The QP outputs joint accelerations and contact wrenches, which are converted to desired actuator torques via inverse dynamics. For locomotion, a dynamic walking behavior with a state machine (standing, transfer, swing states) is used, employing the instantaneous capture point (ICP) concept for balance and generating natural foot and pelvis trajectories.

**Cycloidal Actuators for High-Speed and Impact-Resilient Operations:**
The humanoid robot Nadia is equipped with advanced cycloidal actuators. These actuators are chosen for their high efficiency, robustness, and ability to withstand high speeds and impacts. They provide high torque output (up to 158 Nm peak torque in arms, supporting 10 kg payload) due to their unique design, which distributes load across multiple contact points. This load distribution, combined with smooth rolling motion, reduces stress on components, minimizes backlash, and enhances durability against shock and impact.

### 4. Main findings and results

The effectiveness of the integrated teleoperation system was evaluated through experiments conducted on the humanoid robot Nadia, specifically focusing on dynamic and impact-intensive tasks.

**Experimental Demonstrations:**
- **Ping Pong:** Nadia was teleoperated to play ping pong with a human opponent (Fig. 7). This experiment was designed to stress-test the system's roundtrip latency, specifically the time from the operator perceiving the ball's position to adjusting the racket's position and orientation. The demonstration aimed to show the system's synchronization capabilities in a dynamic, high-speed interaction scenario.
- **Punching Motions:** Experiments involving Nadia punching a 100 lbs punching bag (Fig. 8) were conducted to test the system's speed, transparency, and impact resilience. Punching is characterized as a fast, whole-body motion requiring precise coordination and places significant stress on the robot's motors, challenging the tracking performance of teleoperated references.

**Quantitative and Qualitative Results:**
- **Latency:** The measured delay from the Kinematics Streaming Toolbox (KST) receiving a new VR input to the robot reaching the corresponding pose was approximately **70 milliseconds**. This figure represents the system's overall responsiveness, from processing operator commands to physical execution.
- **Tracking Performance (Free Motion):** During free-motion punching, where the operator threw straight punches without contact, the robot demonstrated good tracking performance across a large range of velocities (Fig. 5). The system effectively mapped the operator's hand movements to the robot's, even with the robot's arms having only four degrees of actuation, which made tracking all six degrees of freedom of the hand a challenge.
- **Tracking Performance (Impact):** When the robot performed a left hook punch impacting a punching bag (Fig. 6), the tracking data showed the system's response to external forces. The instant of impact (around the 21.9-second mark in the y-axis position and linear velocity plots) caused a rapid reversal of hand motion due to the bounce. Despite this significant perturbation, the system demonstrated recovery, with tracking re-establishing itself within approximately one second. This highlights the cycloidal actuators' ability to withstand impacts and the control system's capacity to regain control.
- **Impact Resilience:** The experiments, particularly the punching demonstration, validated the effectiveness of the integrated cycloidal actuators in enabling the robot to withstand high-impact forces. The actuators' design, which distributes load and minimizes backlash, contributed to the robot's ability to maintain structural integrity and functional performance during forceful interactions.
- **Synchronization:** The ping pong experiment qualitatively demonstrated the system's ability to synchronize robot movements with human actions at speeds required for interactive tasks.

The results indicate that the integrated solution provides high transparency and synchronization, allowing the robot to perform fast and dynamic motions, even under impact conditions, with a notable low delay of 70 milliseconds.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

This research presents an integrated teleoperation framework that addresses several long-standing challenges in humanoid robotics, offering substantial significance and potential impact across various domains.

**Significance:**

- **Advancement in Teleoperation Transparency and Speed:** The demonstrated low latency (70 milliseconds from VR input to robot pose) and the ability to track high-speed, dynamic human movements represent a notable improvement in teleoperation transparency. This level of responsiveness is critical for enabling humanoid robots to perform tasks requiring fine motor control and rapid reactions, previously limited by system delays.
- **Enhanced Impact Resilience for Dynamic Tasks:** The integration of cycloidal actuators, designed for high torque capacity, efficiency, and robustness, directly enables the robot to withstand significant impacts. This is a crucial development for applications where robots interact dynamically with their environment or experience unexpected collisions, such as disaster response, construction, or even sports. The ability to recover quickly from impacts, as demonstrated in the punching experiment, contributes to the robot's operational reliability and safety.
- **Practical and User-Friendly Interface:** The adoption of a minimal, calibration-free user setup (7 IMUs with a VR headset and controllers) significantly lowers the barrier to entry for teleoperation. This simplicity contrasts with more complex and cumbersome full-body exoskeletons or extensive calibration procedures, making the system more accessible for a wider range of operators and facilitating faster deployment in various scenarios.
- **Robustness in Dynamic Environments:** The combination of filtering, state estimation, and prediction techniques within the Kinematics Streaming Toolbox ensures smooth and stable robot control even with noisy or low-rate input signals. This robustness is essential for reliable operation in real-world, less-than-ideal environments.
- **Integrated System Approach:** The work highlights the importance of an integrated approach, where advancements in motion capture, control software (KST, whole-body controller), and hardware (cycloidal actuators) collectively contribute to superior performance. This synergistic design paradigm is a key takeaway for future humanoid robot development.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Expansion of Humanoid Robot Applications:** The demonstrated capabilities could unlock new applications for humanoid robots in fields requiring high agility, interaction, and resilience. This includes complex manipulation tasks, industrial assembly, hazardous environment exploration (e.g., search and rescue, nuclear decommissioning), and even human-robot collaborative tasks in dynamic settings.
- **Improved Safety and Reliability:** By enabling robots to react quickly and withstand impacts, the system could lead to safer robot operation, both for the robot itself and for nearby humans. Reduced latency means more predictable robot behavior, while impact resilience lessens the risk of damage or failure during unexpected events.
- **Foundation for Advanced Human-Robot Interaction:** The high transparency and intuitive control could foster more natural and effective human-robot interaction. Operators can feel more "connected" to the robot, potentially reducing cognitive load and training time, and enabling more complex collaborative tasks.
- **Benchmarking for Future Research:** The claims of achieving "the fastest teleoperation experiments ever performed on a real humanoid robot" (to the best of the authors' knowledge) establish a new benchmark for speed and transparency in the field, likely motivating further research and development in high-performance teleoperation systems.
- **Broader Adoption of Teleoperated Humanoids:** The combination of intuitive control, robust performance, and simplified user interface could accelerate the adoption of teleoperated humanoid robots beyond highly specialized research labs into more general industrial, service, and even domestic applications.

In essence, this research significantly pushes the operational envelope of humanoid robots under human teleoperation, moving closer to the vision of robots that can seamlessly and robustly extend human capabilities into challenging environments.
