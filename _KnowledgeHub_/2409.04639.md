# High-Speed and Impact Resilient Teleoperation of Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from IHMC, University of West Florida, and Boardwalk Robotics developed an integrated teleoperation system for humanoid robots, combining a minimal user setup, advanced control, and cycloidal actuators. This system enables the Nadia robot to perform high-speed, dynamic tasks with 70 milliseconds latency and withstand significant impacts while maintaining robust control.

## Problem

- High latency and low transparency in humanoid teleoperation hinder the execution of fast, dynamic, and synchronized tasks.
- Humanoid robots often lack the impact resilience needed for physical interaction with the environment or unexpected collisions.
- Existing teleoperation systems typically require complex, cumbersome, and high-maintenance user-side hardware, limiting practical deployment.

## Method

- An integrated system combining a minimal, calibration-free user setup (VR headset, controllers, and 7 IMUs) for motion capture and retargeting.
- A ==Kinematics Streaming Toolbox (KST)== that processes retargeted commands with low latency, incorporating filtering, state estimation, prediction, and quadratic programming-based inverse kinematics for precise and safe execution.
- The Nadia humanoid robot, equipped with high-efficiency, impact-resilient cycloidal actuators, enabling high torque output and durability against shock, controlled by a momentum-based whole-body controller.

## Results

- Achieved an end-to-end latency of approximately 70 milliseconds from the Kinematics Streaming Toolbox receiving VR input to the robot reaching the corresponding pose.
- Demonstrated synchronous high-speed interactions, with the Nadia robot successfully playing ping pong with a human opponent.
- Validated impact resilience and rapid recovery by having the robot execute powerful punches on a 100 lbs punching bag, with the control system re-establishing tracking within approximately one second after impact.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Achieving high-performance teleoperation requires a synergistic integration of motion capture, real-time control software, and robust, impact-resilient hardware.
> - Cycloidal actuators are crucial for enabling humanoid robots to execute high-speed, high-force motions and reliably withstand significant impacts without damage or loss of performance.
> - Effective compensation for system delays through advanced state estimation and prediction within the Kinematics Streaming Toolbox allows for highly responsive control even from lower-rate user inputs.
