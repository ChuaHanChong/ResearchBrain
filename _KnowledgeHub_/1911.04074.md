# SUMMIT: A Simulator for Urban Driving in Massive Mixed Traffic

> [!summary] Summary
> SUMMIT is an open-source driving simulator, built on CARLA, that auto-generates dense unregulated urban traffic at any OpenStreetMap location. It converts real maps into lane and sidewalk networks, then drives heterogeneous agents with Context-GAMMA, a velocity-space optimizer with road-context constraints. It ships a Context-POMDP expert planner and three real-world benchmark scenes, producing faster, less congested traffic than rule-based baselines.

## Problem

- Real driving datasets like KITTI or BDD100K are non-interactive: exo-agents cannot react to robot decisions, so they poorly support robust planning and learning.
- Photorealistic simulators depend on hand-authored predefined maps with sparse, rule-based traffic, missing complex layouts and aggressive unregulated crowd behaviors.
- Traffic simulators supporting real maps use simple reactive rules, while sophisticated motion models are restricted to predefined maps and lack sensor realism.

## Method

- Map pipeline converts OpenStreetMap data via SUMO into a directed lane network, a sidewalk poly-line network, occupancy-grid interface, and randomizable landmark textures for ==domain randomization==.
- **Context-GAMMA** extends ==GAMMA== by casting road contexts as extra half-plane constraints in velocity space, optimizing over kinematic, geometric, and contextual sets, solvable in linear time.
- **Context-POMDP** pairs a factored ==histogram belief tracker== over agent intention and attentive/distracted type with ==HyP-DESPOT== belief-tree search, forward-simulating exo-agents using Context-GAMMA.

## Results

- Across three real scenes with **120** heterogeneous agents, Context-GAMMA sustained higher agent speeds and low congestion over **20 minutes**, while time-to-collision baseline congestion grew rapidly.
- Simulation scales near-linearly on a laptop: **28.4 Hz** at **150** agents down to **7.1 Hz** at **400** agents, update time rising from **35.3 ms** to **141.8 ms**.
- Context-POMDP reached **0.00069** collisions per step at **4.53 m/s**, versus roll-out planning at **2.1 m/s** and Context-GAMMA control at **0.002** collisions per step.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Encoding map structure as constraints, not just goals, keeps optimization-based crowd models from getting trapped by complex real road geometry.
> - Reciprocal velocity-space optimization models cooperation that reactive time-to-collision rules cannot, yielding smoother and less congested dense traffic.
> - Belief-space planning over hidden intention and attention buys the aggressive/conservative trade-off that pure collision avoidance or rollout heuristics miss.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of robust autonomous driving systems faces significant challenges, particularly in complex, unregulated urban environments characterized by dense, heterogeneous, and often aggressive traffic. Traditional approaches to developing and testing these systems often rely on real-world data, which is expensive and difficult to acquire due to equipment costs, regulatory hurdles, and safety concerns. Furthermore, real-world datasets, while rich in sensor inputs, are typically non-interactive, meaning they cannot capture the reactions of other traffic participants to an autonomous vehicle's decisions—a crucial aspect for training and validating planning and learning algorithms.

Driving simulators have emerged as a promising alternative, offering a controlled, interactive, and virtually limitless source of data. However, existing simulators exhibit limitations that SUMMIT aims to address. Current simulators can be broadly categorized:
- **Multi-car simulators** like TORCS focus on interactions between a limited number of robotic vehicles and do not scale to dense urban crowds.
- **High-fidelity visual and physics simulators** such as CARLA, Sim4CV, and GTA provide realistic rendering and sensor models essential for end-to-end learning. However, they typically rely on predefined maps, limiting environmental variety, and simulate traffic with low density and simplified, rule-based behaviors.
- **Traffic simulators** like SUMO and SimMobilityST support real-world maps and dense traffic, but their agent behaviors are often based on simple rules, lacking the complexity and unpredictability of human drivers.
- Other simulators, such as AutonoViSim and force-based models, offer more sophisticated motion models but are often restricted to predefined maps.

This landscape indicates a gap: a simulator capable of combining real-world map complexity, dense and heterogeneous traffic, sophisticated unregulated behaviors, and high-fidelity physics and visuals in an interactive environment. SUMMIT positions itself to fill this gap by leveraging real-world map data and an advanced crowd behavior model to generate realistic, interactive data for developing and testing autonomous driving algorithms in challenging urban scenarios. The work also builds upon existing crowd simulation algorithms like Social Force, Velocity Obstacles (VO), Reciprocal Velocity Obstacles (RVO), and notably, GAMMA, extending them to incorporate explicit road context for enhanced realism.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop **SUMMIT (Simulator for Urban Driving in Massive Mixed Traffic)**, a high-fidelity simulator designed to facilitate the development and testing of crowd-driving algorithms. This simulator aims to generate realistic, interactive data for autonomous driving in challenging, unregulated urban environments characterized by dense and heterogeneous traffic.

The motivation behind SUMMIT stems from several critical challenges and limitations in the field of autonomous driving:

1. **Complexity of Unregulated Urban Environments:** Driving in crowded urban settings, especially in less-developed regions with uncontrolled roads or unsignalized intersections, presents an outstanding challenge for autonomous vehicles. Human drivers in these contexts often exhibit aggressive behaviors, disregard or are unaware of traffic rules (e.g., close following, inappropriate overtaking, illegal turns/crossings), leading to chaotic road conditions. This complexity is compounded by diverse road layouts (multi-lane roads, intersections, roundabouts) and a heterogeneity of traffic agents (cars, buses, bicycles, motorcycles, pedestrians, each with different kinematics, dynamics, and behavioral types ranging from conservative to aggressive, attentive to distracted). Such environments pose significant difficulties for perception, control, planning, and decision-making for robotic vehicles.

2. **Limitations of Real-World Data Acquisition:** Obtaining high-quality, interactive data for developing, training, and testing crowd-driving algorithms in these complex scenarios is expensive and difficult. This is due to the high cost of specialized equipment, stringent regulations, and safety constraints. While publicly available datasets (e.g., KITTI, BDD100K, Oxford RobotCar) provide rich sensor data from real-world driving, they lack interactivity, preventing the modeling of other agents' reactions to an autonomous vehicle's actions. Interactive data is considered crucial for developing robust planning and learning algorithms.

3. **Deficiencies of Existing Simulators:** Current driving simulators do not fully capture the complete complexity required for robust crowd-driving research. Many rely on predefined maps, limiting environmental diversity. Others simulate traffic with relatively low density or employ overly simplistic rule-based behaviors that fail to mimic the nuanced, unregulated, and often aggressive interactions observed in real-world urban crowds. This makes them insufficient for thoroughly testing or training algorithms intended for challenging real-world scenarios.

To address these motivations, SUMMIT aims to:
- Leverage open-source OpenStreetMap (OSM) to provide a virtually unlimited source of complex, real-world map environments.
- Automatically generate crowds of heterogeneous traffic agents with sophisticated, unregulated behaviors, guided by topological road contexts.
- Be built upon an existing high-fidelity simulator (CARLA) to inherit realistic physics, rendering, and sensor models.
- Provide a Python-based API to expose rich sensor data, semantic information, and road contexts, enabling diverse applications in perception, control, planning, and end-to-end learning.

As a secondary objective, the paper also provides a **context-aware planner, Context-POMDP**, as a reference for future crowd-driving algorithms. This planner is motivated by the extreme challenge of planning in dense, unregulated traffic, which requires explicit modeling of interactions and uncertainty in human intentions and driving types, alongside long-term belief-space reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SUMMIT's methodology integrates real-world map data with a sophisticated crowd behavior model, built upon an existing high-fidelity simulation platform, to generate realistic, interactive driving scenarios.

**A. Foundation and Architecture**
SUMMIT is implemented as an extension of CARLA, leveraging its high-fidelity physics engine, realistic rendering capabilities, weather control, and rich sensor suite. This foundation allows SUMMIT to focus on augmenting environmental variety and realistic crowd behaviors. An overview (Fig. 2) illustrates SUMMIT's capacity to simulate massive mixed traffic globally.

**B. Representing Real-world Maps (Section III.A)**
SUMMIT acquires real-world map data from the open-source OpenStreetMap (OSM) database. This enables the simulation of environments at any worldwide location supported by OSM. The simulator then constructs various contextual representations from this data:
- **Lane Network:** SUMO is used to automatically convert OSM maps into a directed lane network, defining road connectivity at the individual lane level. This network comprises directed lane segments and their connections, allowing traffic agents to locate themselves and navigate effectively.
- **Sidewalk Network:** Pedestrian behavior is defined by a sidewalk network, consisting of poly-lines along road edges and cross-able road connections. This structure guides pedestrian movement, including road crossings.
- **Occupancy Map Interface:** A top-down projection of drivable road geometry is provided, aligned with the ego-vehicle's perspective. This interface is designed for collision checking in planning algorithms or as input for neural networks.
- **Landmarks:** OSM data for buildings and forests is utilized to create structurally rich and visually realistic environments. The system also supports randomization of landmark textures for increased visual versatility, potentially benefiting domain randomization techniques.

**C. Crowd Behavior Modelling (Section III.B)**
SUMMIT employs **Context-GAMMA**, a context-aware crowd behavior model, to generate sophisticated interactive behaviors for heterogeneous traffic agents. Context-GAMMA extends the existing GAMMA model by incorporating road contexts as objectives and constraints in velocity space.

- **GAMMA's Core:** GAMMA formulates agent motion as a constrained geometric optimization problem in velocity space. Each agent optimizes its velocity to reach a navigation goal while adhering to:
 - **Kinematic Constraints ($K_A$):** Representing an agent's physical motion limits (e.g., non-holonomic motion for cars).
 - **Geometric Constraints ($G_{\tau A}$):** Ensuring collision avoidance with nearby agents for a time horizon $\tau$.
 The chosen velocity minimizes the difference from a preferred velocity ($v_{pref A}$) within the feasible velocity space ($K_A \cap G_{\tau A}$). GAMMA also considers agent responsibility and attention for human-like motions and handles heterogeneous agents with different kinematics and geometry.

- **Context-GAMMA's Enhancements:**
 - **Road Context Integration:** Context-GAMMA addresses GAMMA's limitation in complex urban roads by explicitly using road contexts.
 - **Preferred Velocity Extraction:** The preferred velocity ($v_{pref}$) for traffic agents is extracted from the lane and sidewalk networks. To introduce diversity, the model randomly selects a feasible lane ahead and points $v_{pref}$ towards a look-ahead waypoint on that lane.
 - **Contextual Constraints ($C_A$):** Traffic rules (e.g., preventing wrong-direction driving) are modeled as half-planes in the agents' velocity space. For instance, preventing a car from driving into an opposite lane within a time window $\tau_1$ translates to a lateral speed constraint. Collision avoidance with static obstacles like sidewalks also forms velocity half-planes.
 - **Augmented Feasible Velocity Space:** The agent's new velocity is optimized within an augmented feasible space: $K_A \cap G_{\tau A} \cap C_A$. This intersection remains convex, and the objective function (minimizing deviation from $v_{pref}$) is quadratic, allowing for efficient linear-time optimization.
 - **Heterogeneous Static Obstacles:** The model distinguishes how different agent types perceive static obstacles (e.g., pedestrians see sidewalks as open space, vehicles see them as obstacles).

**D. Interfaces (Section III.C)**
SUMMIT provides a Python API that extends CARLA's interface. This API exposes sensor data, agent states, and crucial road context information (lane networks, sidewalk networks, occupancy grids) to external algorithms. It also allows external algorithms to send vehicle control commands (steering, acceleration, braking, reversing) back to the simulation, supporting diverse applications like perception, sensor-based control, model-based reasoning, and end-to-end learning.

**E. Context-Aware POMDP Planning (Section IV)**
The paper also presents **Context-POMDP**, an expert planner for autonomous driving within SUMMIT, formulated as a context-aware Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) and solved using online belief tree search (HyP-DESPOT).
- **Belief Tracking:** A factored histogram filter maintains a joint belief over exo-agents' hidden states, including their **intention** (sampled path candidates from road contexts) and **type** (distracted or attentive). This belief is updated using Context-GAMMA for mean motion generation and Bayes' rule based on observed motion.
- **Context-POMDP Model:**
 - **State:** Comprises the ego-vehicle's state (position, heading, intended path), observable exo-agent states (position, velocity), and hidden exo-agent states (type, intended path).
 - **Observation:** The ego-vehicle observes its own state and discretized observable states of exo-agents.
 - **Action:** The action space is decoupled for tractability. Steering is handled by a pure-pursuit algorithm, while the POMDP computes acceleration (ACC, MAINTAIN, DEC) along the intended path.
 - **Transition:** Distracted agents follow their path at current speed. Attentive agents use Context-GAMMA for local motion. All agents' motions are constrained by kinematics and include Gaussian noise for stochasticity.
 - **Reward:** The reward function balances safety (penalties for collision), efficiency (penalties for low speed), and smoothness (penalties for frequent deceleration).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to assess SUMMIT's capability to simulate realistic dense traffic on complex maps, compare its behavior model (Context-GAMMA) against rule-based approaches, and validate the performance of the Context-POMDP planner.

**A. Real-world Benchmark Scenarios**
Three distinct real-world urban scenarios were selected from OpenStreetMap and imported into SUMMIT to evaluate performance:
- **Singapore-Highway:** A multi-lane highway requiring frequent overtaking.
- **Magic-Roundabout:** A complex roundabout in Swindon, England, demanding coordination among agents.
- **Meskel-Intersection:** A complex intersection in Addis Ababa, characterized by aggressive, unregulated traffic from multiple directions.
Each scenario involved 120 heterogeneous traffic agents, with agent density maintained by replacing agents moving out of the region of interest.

**B. Simulation on Benchmark Scenarios (Qualitative)**
Qualitative analysis of the simulations (Fig. 1d-f and accompanying video) indicated that SUMMIT, utilizing Context-GAMMA, generated traffic behaviors that closely represented the complexities observed in the real-world benchmark scenarios. This suggests that the integration of topological road contexts with the optimization-based behavior model contributes to realistic crowd dynamics in complex urban settings.

**C. Comparison with Rule-based Simulation (Quantitative)**
Context-GAMMA was quantitatively compared against a reactive rule-based model that used Time-To-Collision (TTC) for speed regulation, with agents moving along lane center-curves. Performance was measured by average agent speed and a "congestion factor" (percentage of agents stalled).
- **Improved Efficiency and Flow:** Context-GAMMA consistently generated faster and smoother traffic flow across all benchmark scenarios over a 20-minute simulation period.
- **Reduced Congestion:** The congestion factor for TTC-controlled traffic increased rapidly, indicating agents' failure to coordinate and navigate dense crowds. In contrast, Context-GAMMA maintained higher average agent speeds and significantly lower congestion factors for all agent types.
These results were attributed to Context-GAMMA's explicit modeling of cooperation between agents and its ability to derive optimal collision-avoidance motions through both steering and acceleration, which rule-based models often lack.

**D. Efficiency and Scalability of Simulation**
Tests on the computational efficiency of Context-GAMMA demonstrated its scalability with crowd density:
- The simulator achieved simulation frequencies of 28.4 Hz for 150 agents and 7.1 Hz for 400 agents on a laptop with an i7-9750H CPU and an RTX 2060 GPU.
- The growth of computation time per update (milliseconds) was observed to be approximately linear as the number of agents increased, indicating that the model scales well for massive crowds until the map becomes saturated.

**E. Driving Performance of the Context-POMDP Planner**
The Context-POMDP planner was evaluated against two baselines:
- **Context-GAMMA:** Direct local collision avoidance using Context-GAMMA to control the ego-vehicle.
- **Roll-out:** A simple planning algorithm based on multiple roll-outs with a default policy (accelerate if agents far, maintain half-speed if cautious range, decelerate if close).
Measurements included collision rate per step, average vehicle speed, and deceleration frequency.
- **Balance of Safety and Efficiency:** Context-POMDP achieved a balance between aggressiveness and conservativeness. It demonstrated a significantly higher average speed (4.53 m/s) compared to the over-conservative Roll-out baseline (2.1 m/s), indicating improved efficiency.
- **Enhanced Safety:** Crucially, Context-POMDP maintained a lower collision rate (0.00069 collisions/step) than both Roll-out (0.00095 collisions/step) and Context-GAMMA (0.002 collisions/step), despite driving faster than Roll-out.
- **Smoothness:** The deceleration frequency of Context-POMDP (0.16 decelerations/step) was comparable to Roll-out (0.15 decelerations/step), suggesting smooth driving behavior.
The findings indicate that sophisticated long-term planning, as implemented in Context-POMDP, is instrumental for achieving safe, efficient, and smooth autonomous driving in dense, unregulated traffic environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SUMMIT represents a significant contribution to the field of autonomous driving research by addressing several critical limitations of existing simulation platforms. Its potential impact spans across multiple dimensions:

1. **Bridging the Simulation Gap:** SUMMIT fills a previously identified gap in driving simulators by combining complex real-world map environments, high-density heterogeneous traffic, sophisticated unregulated behaviors, and high-fidelity physics/visuals. This comprehensive approach provides a more realistic and challenging testbed for autonomous driving algorithms than what was typically available.

2. **Facilitating Algorithm Development and Testing:** By generating high-fidelity, interactive data for unregulated, dense urban traffic, SUMMIT enables researchers to develop, train, and test a wide range of autonomous driving algorithms. This includes advancements in perception (e.g., handling occlusions in dense crowds), vehicle control, long-term planning (as demonstrated by Context-POMDP), and end-to-end learning in environments previously difficult to simulate accurately.

3. **Providing Controllable and Unlimited Data:** SUMMIT offers a crucial alternative to the costly, time-consuming, and often non-interactive nature of real-world data acquisition. Researchers can generate virtually unlimited and controllable scenarios, allowing for systematic exploration of diverse traffic conditions, corner cases, and customized environments without safety risks or regulatory constraints. This capability is vital for iterative development and robust validation.

4. **Promoting Benchmarking and Standardization:** The provision of real-world benchmark scenarios and a reference context-aware planner (Context-POMDP) within SUMMIT can serve to standardize evaluations for crowd-driving algorithms. This fosters a more objective comparison of different approaches and accelerates progress in the field by providing common metrics and environments.

5. **Scalability for Massive Traffic:** The demonstrated efficiency and scalability of SUMMIT's crowd behavior model (Context-GAMMA) allows for the simulation of hundreds of agents in real-time. This capability is essential for studying complex emergent traffic behaviors and testing algorithms under truly massive mixed traffic conditions, which are prevalent in many global urban centers.

6. **Enhancing Autonomous Vehicle Safety:** By enabling more thorough testing and refinement of autonomous driving systems in highly challenging and interactive environments, SUMMIT contributes to the development of safer autonomous vehicles. The ability to simulate high-risk scenarios repeatedly and safely is paramount for identifying and mitigating potential failure modes before deployment.

7. **Open-Source Contribution:** The open-source release of both the SUMMIT simulator and the Context-POMDP planner is a significant impact multiplier. It encourages wider adoption, community collaboration, further development, and integration with other research efforts, thereby accelerating overall progress in autonomous driving and intelligent transportation systems.
