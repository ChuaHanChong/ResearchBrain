# Conditional Advantage Estimation for Reinforcement Learning in Large Reasoning Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Conditional Advantage Estimation (CANON) introduces an adaptive framework for reinforcement learning in large reasoning models that dynamically identifies and utilizes beneficial trends in intrinsic training metrics. This method achieves up to a 1.9-point gain in math accuracy and a 5.2-point improvement on challenging logic tasks, while also reducing token consumption by 33.8% compared to prior methods.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods for LLMs incorporate intrinsic metrics (like entropy or response length) through rigid, directional reward shaping.
- These pre-defined directional priors are often biased, inflexible, and can lead to suboptimal training or failure if the assumed direction doesn't align with true beneficial trends.
- The optimal direction for a metric is complex and task-dependent, making robust performance difficult without extensive hyperparameter tuning.

## Method

- **CANON** employs a "==conditional regrouping==" strategy where sampled responses for a query are sorted by a target metric and divided into distinct groups.
- It calculates a unified advantage from a weighted sum of "inter-group advantage" (comparing a response's reward to the opposite metric group's mean reward) and "intra-group advantage" (comparing to its own group's mean reward).
- A dynamic scheduling mechanism is implemented to adaptively adjust the balance between inter-group and intra-group advantages during training based on task type or training stage.

## Results

- On math reasoning tasks, entropy-based CANON-Inter achieves an average accuracy of **57.6%** across six benchmarks, a 1.9-point gain over the DR.GRPO baseline, and improves high-complexity logic by up to 5.2 points.
- Length-based CANON reduces token cost by **33.8%** (from 1522 to 1008 tokens) on math tasks while preserving performance, establishing a superior performance-efficiency trade-off.
- CANON-Dynamic, with its adaptive scheduling, consistently surpasses DR.GRPO across multiple LLMs and reasoning tasks, effectively balancing exploitation and exploration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Ample benefits can be gained by dynamically discerning and amplifying beneficial metric trends during RL training rather than imposing fixed, directional priors.
> - Inter-group advantage effectively promotes exploitation and certainty (beneficial for math tasks), while intra-group advantage encourages robust exploration and reflection (crucial for complex logic tasks).
> - Existing group-relative advantage estimation methods like DR.GRPO can be formulated as a special case within the more general CANON framework.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models (LLMs) has witnessed remarkable progress in recent years, particularly in enhancing their reasoning capabilities across diverse tasks. A pivotal technique driving this advancement is Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), a specialized form of Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) where human preferences are replaced by objective, verifiable correctness criteria (e.g., for mathematical or logical problems). State-of-the-art models like Gemini 2.5 Pro, DeepSeek-R1, and OpenAI-o1 leverage RLVR and its variants, such as GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) and DR.GRPO (Liu et al., 2025a), to refine their multi-step reasoning processes and achieve superior performance.

Within this landscape, researchers have observed that various intrinsic training metrics—such as token generation entropy (a measure of uncertainty or exploration) or response length (related to efficiency)—often correlate with different LLM reasoning behaviors. Prior approaches to incorporate these valuable "human priors" typically involved reward or advantage shaping. This usually entailed adding hand-crafted penalty or bonus terms, explicitly guiding the model in a predefined direction (e.g., "higher entropy is better for exploration" or "shorter responses are better for efficiency").

However, this traditional, directional approach presents a significant challenge: these hand-crafted priors can be overly biased and inflexible. Without extensive and careful hyperparameter tuning for each specific scenario, they risk driving metrics in suboptimal directions or even leading to training failures. For instance, while high entropy might be beneficial for exploration in complex problems, low entropy might be crucial for exploiting learned knowledge and achieving high certainty in simpler cases. The "optimal" direction of a metric can be context-dependent and difficult to predefine.

CANON addresses this fundamental limitation by proposing a novel advantage estimation framework that amplifies the impact of target metrics *without presuming their direction*. Instead of imposing a fixed "higher-is-better" or "lower-is-better" prior, CANON dynamically identifies and leverages the inherent trends within the model's rollouts that contribute most effectively to performance. This work builds upon and generalizes existing group-relative advantage estimation methods like DR.GRPO, which serves as a baseline for comparison and is shown to be a special case of CANON. By enabling more adaptive and robust integration of training metrics, CANON represents a significant step forward in optimizing RLVR for complex reasoning tasks, bridging the gap between rigid human priors and the nuanced, dynamic nature of LLM learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind the Conditional Advantage Estimation (CANON) framework stems from a critical limitation in current Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods for Large Language Models (LLMs). While RLVR has been highly effective in boosting LLM reasoning capabilities, existing techniques that incorporate human priors—such as generation entropy or response length—often do so through rigid, directional reward or advantage shaping. These methods typically assume a fixed "higher-is-better" or "lower-is-better" relationship for a given metric.

The authors highlight that such pre-conceived directional priors are problematic:
1. **Bias and Inflexibility:** They can be "overly biased" and "drive specific metrics up or down directly," potentially leading to suboptimal training or even failure if the assumed direction does not align with the true beneficial trend for a given task or training stage.
2. **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** Robust performance often necessitates "careful hyper-parameter tuning," which is computationally expensive and not easily transferable across different tasks or models.
3. **Nuance of Metrics:** The optimal behavior related to a metric can be complex. For example, both high-entropy (exploratory) and low-entropy (certain) responses have their merits, depending on the problem's complexity or the model's current capabilities. Similarly, while shorter responses are generally more efficient, overly aggressive length penalties can degrade reasoning quality.

Given these challenges, the key objectives of this work are to:
- **Amplify Metric Impact Without Presuming Direction:** Develop an advantage estimation method that can leverage the information encoded in training metrics (like entropy or response length) to guide RL training more effectively, without requiring explicit, pre-defined assumptions about whether the metric should be maximized or minimized.
- **Dynamically Identify Beneficial Trends:** Enable the learning algorithm to naturally discover which trends in a given metric (e.g., increasing or decreasing entropy) actually contribute to better performance, based on the model's current rollouts.
- **Enhance LLM Reasoning Robustly:** Improve the reasoning capabilities of LLMs on tasks with verifiable correctness, such as mathematical and high-complexity logic reasoning, in a more stable and robust manner compared to prior methods.
- **Improve Reasoning Efficiency:** Specifically, for metrics like response length, use this adaptive framework to achieve better token efficiency, leading to a more favorable performance-cost trade-off.
- **Generalize Existing Methods:** Provide a unifying framework that can explain and potentially improve upon existing successful RLVR algorithms, particularly showing that DR.GRPO can be seen as a special case.

In essence, the authors aim to introduce a more intelligent and adaptive way for LLMs to learn from intrinsic metrics, moving beyond simplistic directional biases to harness the full, nuanced potential of these signals for improved reasoning and efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CANON introduces a novel conditional advantage estimation framework built upon the principles of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and group-relative advantage estimation. The core innovation lies in its "conditional regrouping" strategy, which allows the model to dynamically discern and leverage beneficial metric trends without predefined directional priors.

The methodology can be broken down into the following key steps:

1. **Foundation in PPO and Group-Relative Advantage:**
 - The work operates within the PPO framework, widely used for stable policy optimization in LLMs.
 - It leverages the concept of group-relative advantage estimation, similar to GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) and DR.GRPO (Liu et al., 2025a). These methods estimate advantage by comparing a response's reward to the average reward of other sampled responses for the same query, circumventing the need for a separate critic model. DR.GRPO, a direct predecessor, removes standard deviation normalization and uses a token-level loss.

2. **Conditional Regrouping:**
 - For each query, a batch of sampled responses (Gq) is generated by the policy.
 - Instead of treating all responses uniformly, CANON introduces a "conditional regrouping" step. Responses are sorted based on the value of a chosen target metric (e.g., per-token generation entropy or response length).
 - These sorted responses are then divided into two distinct, non-overlapping groups (C+q and C-q), typically of equal size, representing "higher" and "lower" values of the metric, respectively. This splitting introduces an explicit comparison target.

3. **Dual Advantage Estimation:** CANON computes two types of advantages based on these regrouped responses:
 - **Inter-group Advantage (Â_inter):** For a given response `o`, its inter-group advantage is calculated by comparing its reward (Ro) against the mean reward of the *other* group (the group it does *not* belong to).
 - If `o` is in the "lower metric" group (C-q), it's compared to the mean reward of the "higher metric" group (C+q).
 - If `o` is in the "higher metric" group (C+q), it's compared to the mean reward of the "lower metric" group (C-q).
 - This inter-group comparison is designed to reveal which *trend* of the metric (e.g., higher vs. lower entropy) generally leads to better performance.
 - **Intra-group Advantage (Â_intra):** For a given response `o`, its intra-group advantage is calculated by comparing its reward (Ro) against the mean reward of *its own group*.
 - If `o` is in C+q, it's compared to the mean reward of C+q.
 - If `o` is in C-q, it's compared to the mean reward of C-q.
 - This intra-group comparison identifies superior responses within the same metric trend, prioritizing correct answers from groups that generally perform worse (e.g., rewarding a correct, high-entropy exploratory response more if high-entropy responses are generally less accurate).

4. **Unified Advantage Formulation:**
 - A combined CANON advantage (Â_CANON) is formulated as a weighted sum of the inter-group and intra-group advantages: Â_CANON = μ * Â_inter + (1 - μ) * Â_intra.
 - The hyperparameter μ controls the balance between emphasizing inter-group comparisons (identifying beneficial trends) and intra-group comparisons (improving within trends).

5. **Theoretical Justification:**
 - Theorem 1 theoretically proves that when the two conditional groups (C+q and C-q) are of equal size, the inter-group advantage amplifies the signal-to-noise ratio compared to DR.GRPO.
 - Crucially, the authors demonstrate that DR.GRPO can be expressed as a special case of CANON when μ = 0.5 and the groups are of equal size, highlighting CANON's generality.

6. **Weighted Advantage for Specific Targets (e.g., Efficiency):**
 - CANON further allows for fine-grained control by introducing a weighting factor `α` within the inter-group advantage calculation. For instance, to encourage shorter responses, the reward contribution from longer responses (the C+q group if C+q represents longer responses) can be slightly reduced by setting `α < 1`.

7. **Dynamic Scheduling (CANON-Dynamic):**
 - To overcome the trade-off between math reasoning (benefiting more from inter-group comparison, i.e., high μ) and complex logic reasoning (benefiting more from intra-group comparison, i.e., low μ), the authors introduce strategies to dynamically schedule the μ coefficient during training. These strategies can be based on training accuracy or cosine annealing functions over training steps, adapting the balance between exploration and exploitation.

The methodology is rigorously evaluated using various open-weight LLMs (Qwen2.5-Math-7B/1.5B, Llama3.1-8B) on comprehensive benchmarks for math reasoning (e.g., MATH, GSM8K, AIME) and high-complexity logic tasks (ZebraLogic), using verifiable 0-1 rewards.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of CANON reveals significant improvements over prior methods across various LLMs and reasoning tasks, demonstrating its effectiveness in both enhancing performance and improving efficiency.

1. **Superior Performance on Math Reasoning Tasks:**
 - **Entropy-based CANON-Inter (μ=1.0):** Achieves an average accuracy of 57.6% across six math benchmarks (AIME 24/25, OlympiadBench, AMC, MATH-500, GSM8k), marking a 1.9-point gain over the DR.GRPO baseline (55.7%). This variant excels at exploiting established beneficial patterns, leading to a stable decrease in generation entropy during training.
 - CANON-Inter (Entropy) shows the best performance in four out of six math benchmarks and is highly competitive in the others, notably a 5.0-point increase over DR.GRPO in AIME24.

2. **Enhanced Performance on High-Complexity Logic Tasks:**
 - **Entropy-based CANON-Intra (μ=0.0):** Proves crucial for high-complexity reasoning problems (ZebraLogic Mid, Large, XLarge subsets). While CANON-Inter excels in math, CANON-Intra shows increasing performance gains with task complexity. On the most challenging XLarge subset, it achieves a 5.2-point improvement over DR.GRPO, promoting effective exploration as evidenced by increased generation entropy and higher reflection gains.
 - CANON-Intra (Entropy) also reduces response length by 36.6% compared to DR.GRPO in logic tasks.

3. **Improved Reasoning Efficiency with Length-based CANON:**
 - **CANON-Inter (Length):** Significantly reduces token cost by 33.8% compared to DR.GRPO (from 1522 to 1008 tokens on math tasks) while maintaining nearly identical performance (55.3% vs. 55.7%). This demonstrates its ability to guide models towards more concise and efficient reasoning.
 - **CANON-Eff (α < 1.0):** By weighting conditions based on response length (`α < 1`), CANON establishes a new, superior Pareto frontier in the performance-efficiency trade-off. It consistently outperforms baselines, achieving up to 2.63 times higher performance at low token budgets and reducing token consumption by 45.5% for the same performance level. Furthermore, CANON-Eff demonstrates greater stability in exploring this frontier compared to other length-controlling methods (e.g., length penalty).

4. **Comprehensive Performance with Dynamic Advantage Scheduling (CANON-Dynamic):**
 - By adaptively scheduling the balance coefficient μ (e.g., using cosine annealing based on training steps or accuracy), CANON-Dynamic achieves robust and superior performance across *both* math and complex logic reasoning tasks simultaneously.
 - It consistently outperforms DR.GRPO across all three tested LLMs (Qwen2.5-Math-7B, Llama3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B) and task types, effectively synergizing the strengths of inter-group (exploitation for math) and intra-group (exploration for logic) advantages.
 - Analysis shows that CANON-Dynamic successfully combines the positive gains from rethinking (exploration) with high training scores (exploitation), explaining its overall leading performance.

5. **Insights from Analysis:**
 - Ablation studies confirm that intelligent metric-based regrouping is crucial; random regrouping offers no benefits.
 - Different μ values indeed drive distinct trends in target metrics (e.g., high μ decreases entropy, low μ increases it), validating the mechanism of CANON in controlling model behavior.

In summary, CANON offers a more intelligent, adaptive, and effective framework for integrating intrinsic metrics into RLVR, leading to marked improvements in reasoning accuracy, exploration, and efficiency across a range of LLMs and challenging tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Conditional Advantage Estimation (CANON) marks a significant advancement in the field of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for Large Language Models (LLMs). Its impact is multi-faceted, addressing critical limitations in current methodologies and opening new avenues for research and application.

1. **Robust and Adaptive RL for LLMs:** CANON provides a more robust and adaptive mechanism for incorporating human priors and intrinsic training metrics (like entropy and response length) into the RL training loop. By moving beyond rigid, directional assumptions ("higher-is-better" or "lower-is-better"), it allows the learning algorithm to dynamically discover and leverage beneficial metric trends. This reduces the need for extensive, often brittle, hyperparameter tuning associated with traditional reward/advantage shaping, making RLVR more stable and easier to deploy.

2. **Enhanced Reasoning Capabilities:** The consistent outperformance of CANON across diverse math reasoning and high-complexity logic tasks demonstrates its ability to significantly enhance LLM reasoning. The framework's capacity to strategically balance exploitation (via inter-group advantage for certainty/math tasks) and exploration (via intra-group advantage for complex logic/reflection) is crucial for tackling problems that require nuanced cognitive behaviors. This pushes the boundaries of what LLMs can achieve in verifiable reasoning domains.

3. **Improved Model Efficiency and Resource Management:** The demonstrated ability of CANON to achieve a superior Pareto frontier in the performance-efficiency trade-off is particularly impactful. By reducing token consumption significantly without compromising accuracy, CANON addresses a major practical concern in deploying large models: computational cost and inference latency. This "efficient reasoning" capability makes advanced LLM applications more economically viable and environmentally sustainable.

4. **Deeper Understanding of LLM Learning Dynamics:** The theoretical grounding and empirical analysis of CANON provide valuable insights into how different advantage estimation strategies drive specific behavioral changes in LLMs. The distinct roles of inter-group advantage (favoring exploitation and certainty) and intra-group advantage (promoting effective exploration and reflection) offer a more granular understanding of the learning process. This understanding can inform the design of future RL algorithms and guide the development of more sophisticated LLM training paradigms.

5. **Generalizability and Broad Applicability:** The framework's demonstrated effectiveness across multiple open-source LLMs (Qwen, Llama) and various reasoning benchmarks suggests its broad generalizability. This indicates that CANON could be applied to a wide array of verifiable tasks beyond math and logic, potentially including scientific problem-solving, code generation, and complex planning.

6. **Future Research Directions:** As noted by the authors, CANON opens up exciting avenues for future research, such as exploring more complex and non-numerical conditions for regrouping, simultaneously incorporating multiple training metrics, and potentially leveraging this adaptive learning framework to enhance the security and trustworthiness of LLMs.

In conclusion, CANON represents a methodological leap in RLVR, fostering LLMs that are not only more intelligent and capable of complex reasoning but also more efficient and adaptable in their learning process. This work has the potential to significantly influence the development of next-generation reasoning AI.
