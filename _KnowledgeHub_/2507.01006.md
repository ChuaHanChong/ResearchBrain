# GLM-4.5V and GLM-4.1V-Thinking: Towards Versatile Multimodal Reasoning with Scalable Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The GLM-V Team from Zhipu AI and Tsinghua University developed GLM-4.5V and GLM-4.1V-Thinking, a family of vision-language models designed for versatile multimodal reasoning. These models, trained with a scalable reinforcement learning framework, achieve state-of-the-art performance across 42 diverse public benchmarks, including complex STEM problem-solving, GUI agents, and VLM coding, while maintaining competitive results against closed-source models.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often specialized in narrow domains, lacking general-purpose reasoning capabilities for complex tasks.
- There was a need for VLMs capable of advanced multimodal reasoning in areas such as STEM problem-solving, autonomous GUI agents, coding from designs, and interpreting long documents.
- Unlocking the full potential of strong vision foundation models required sophisticated and scalable reinforcement learning (RL) techniques, which were primarily explored for large language models (LLMs).

## Method

- A three-stage, reasoning-centric training framework was employed, including vision foundation model pre-training, supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and reinforcement learning (RL).
- Pre-training involved a unified architecture with an ==AIMv2-Huge vision encoder== and GLM-4-9B-0414 or GLM-4.5-Air LLMs, supporting arbitrary resolutions and diverse multimodal data.
- The core Reinforcement Learning stage combined ==Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)== and Human Feedback (RLHF) with a multi-domain reward system and ==Reinforcement Learning with Curriculum Sampling (RLCS)== for efficiency and stability.

## Results

- GLM-4.5V achieved state-of-the-art performance on nearly all **42** public benchmarks among open-source models of comparable size, outperforming models like Step-3 (321B-A38B) and Qwen-2.5-VL-72B.
- The model demonstrated competitive or superior results compared to closed-source models such as Gemini-2.5-Flash on challenging tasks including VLM Coding and GUI Agents.
- The smaller GLM-4.1V-9B-Thinking model achieved superior results to the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B on **29 out of 42** benchmarks, highlighting an excellent balance of performance and efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-domain reinforcement learning promotes robust cross-domain generalization and mutual facilitation, where training on one domain positively impacts performance in others.
> - Dynamically selecting informative rollout problems through Reinforcement Learning with Curriculum Sampling (RLCS) is critical for maximizing learning gains and overall efficiency.
> - A precise and hack-resistant multi-domain reward system is essential for the stability and effectiveness of large-scale RL training across diverse multimodal tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has become a cornerstone of modern intelligent systems, evolving rapidly from basic visual content perception to complex multimodal understanding and reasoning. Historically, VLMs have excelled at tasks like image captioning and visual question answering (VQA). However, the broader research landscape increasingly demands VLMs capable of more advanced reasoning, such as solving scientific problems, developing autonomous agents for graphical user interfaces (GUIs), coding from designs, and interpreting long, complex documents with interleaved visuals.

Previous efforts to enhance VLM reasoning often focused on specific domains or utilized paradigms like long-form reasoning and scalable reinforcement learning primarily for large language models (LLMs). While some works attempted similar enhancements for VLMs, they frequently specialized in particular areas, leaving a gap for a general-purpose multimodal reasoning model that consistently outperforms traditional models across a wide range of scenarios and scales in the open-source community.

This paper directly addresses this gap by presenting a family of VLMs (GLM-4.1V-Thinking, GLM-4.5V, and GLM-4.6V) explicitly designed for *general-purpose multimodal understanding and reasoning*. It integrates state-of-the-art vision foundation models with sophisticated reinforcement learning techniques to achieve comprehensive capability enhancement across a diverse range of challenging tasks. By open-sourcing models of varying sizes, the authors significantly contribute to the open-source community, providing powerful baselines and tools for further research in versatile multimodal AI. The emphasis on "thinking" modes, scalable reinforcement learning, and broad cross-domain performance positions this work at the forefront of multimodal AI research, pushing beyond mere perception towards true multimodal intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance general-purpose multimodal understanding and reasoning through the development of a capable family of vision-language models. The authors are motivated by several key observations and challenges in the VLM landscape:

- **Increasing Complexity of Multimodal Tasks:** Modern applications demand VLMs that can perform far beyond simple visual content perception, requiring advanced reasoning abilities for tasks like STEM problem-solving, autonomous agents, and long document interpretation.
- **Gap in General-Purpose Multimodal Reasoning:** While large language models have shown significant advancements in complex problem-solving through long-form reasoning and scalable reinforcement learning, a robust, open-source multimodal reasoning model that consistently excels across a broad spectrum of visual and language tasks at comparable parameter scales is lacking.
- **Unlocking Full Potential through Scalable RL:** The authors hypothesize that a strong vision foundation model, while crucial, sets only the *upper bound* for performance. To truly *unlock* its full potential and achieve comprehensive capability enhancement, a sophisticated and scalable reinforcement learning framework is essential.
- **Advancing Research and Open-Source Community:** A core motivation is to contribute to the research community by open-sourcing their models and key components, thereby facilitating further exploration and development in multimodal reasoning.

In essence, the project aims to build highly versatile VLMs that can "think" and reason across diverse multimodal inputs, overcoming the limitations of previous models that often specialized in narrow domains or lacked consistent generalizability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The GLM-V team employs a three-stage, reasoning-centric training framework designed to comprehensively enhance multimodal reasoning capabilities:

#### 3.1. Vision Foundation Model Pre-training

This initial stage focuses on building a robust foundation with strong general multimodal capabilities.
- **Architecture:** The GLM-V series shares a unified architecture comprising a vision encoder (AIMv2-Huge initialization), an MLP adapter to align visual features, and a large language model (GLM-4-9B-0414 or GLM-4.5-Air) as the decoder.
- **Visual Adaptations:**
 - **Arbitrary Resolution/Aspect Ratios:** 2D-RoPE is integrated into the ViT's self-attention layers, allowing effective processing of extreme aspect ratios and high resolutions. Original learnable absolute position embeddings are retained and dynamically adapted via bicubic interpolation based on normalized patch coordinates.
 - **Temporal Modeling for Video:** For video inputs, 3D convolutions replace 2D convolutions in the vision encoder for temporal downsampling. A time index token is inserted after each frame token, encoding timestamps as strings to explicitly inform the model about temporal relationships.
- **Diverse Pre-training Data:** A broad and diverse corpus is curated:
 - **Image Caption Data:** Over 10 billion image-text pairs from sources like LAION, DataComp, DFN, and Wukong, refined through heuristic filtering, CLIP-based relevance filtering, concept-balanced resampling (MetaCLIP-inspired), and factual-centered recaptioning using an iteratively trained model to denoise and enrich captions.
 - **Interleaved Image-Text Data:** Large volumes of high-quality interleaved data from web pages (MINT, MMC4, OmniCorpus) and over 100 million academic books (STEM domains from arXiv), processed with specialized pipelines for noise reduction, relevance filtering (CLIP-Score), identification of high-information-value content (e.g., charts, diagrams), and PDF parsing.
 - **OCR Data:** 220 million images comprising synthetic document images (rendered text on diverse backgrounds), natural scene text images (processed with Paddle-OCR), and academic documents (LaTeX source normalized to HTML, parsed, segmented, and rasterized, inspired by Nougat).
 - **Grounding Data:** A hybrid dataset including natural image grounding (40 million annotations from LAION-115M using GLIPv2 to predict bounding boxes for noun phrases) and novel GUI grounding (over 140 million QA pairs from webpages captured via Playwright, parsing DOM elements and bounding boxes).
 - **Video Data:** Large-scale, high-quality video-text data from academic, web, and proprietary sources, with fine-grained human annotation for complex actions, in-scene text, and cinematic elements, undergoing rigorous filtering for integrity and semantic deduplication.
 - **Instruction Tuning Data:** 50 million high-quality samples covering general perception, multimodal reasoning (STEM), long documents, GUI agents, and UI coding, with fine-grained taxonomy, complex scenario augmentation (synthetically generated data), and contamination checks.
- **Training Recipe:** Conducted in two stages: multimodal pre-training (8,192 sequence length, 120,000 steps, diverse data excluding video) and long-context continual training (32,768 sequence length, video/long-sequence interleaved data, additional 10,000 steps, 131,072 for GLM-4.6V).

#### 3.2. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

This stage aligns the pre-trained VLM with effective "chain-of-thought" (CoT) reasoning and response styles.
- **SFT Data:** Curated high-quality, long CoT reasoning examples spanning verifiable (STEM) and non-verifiable (instruction following) tasks, primarily in Chinese and English. Difficulty is moderated by filtering using the pre-trained model.
- **Response Formatting:** A standardized structure `<think>{think_content}</think><answer>{answer_content}</answer>` is enforced, with final answers for verifiable tasks boxed with `<|begin_of_box|>` and `<|end_of_box|>`. Special tokens are added to the tokenizer. GLM-4.5V and GLM-4.6V support both thinking and non-thinking modes.
- **Tool Use Formatting (GLM-4.6V):** A rigorous XML schema `<tool_call>{function_name}<arg_key>{arg-key-1}</arg_key><arg_value>{arg-value-1}</arg_value>...</tool_call>` is used for tool invocation, defined in system prompts to mitigate structural hallucinations.
- **Response Curation & Iterative Enhancement:** Rigorous data cleaning enforces formatting conventions and removes inconsistent or noisy reasoning. High-quality examples from later RL checkpoints are iteratively incorporated back into the SFT dataset.
- **Training Recipe:** Full-parameter fine-tuning with a sequence length of 32,768 tokens (131,072 for GLM-4.6V), including both multimodal and high-quality text-only long-form examples to preserve core language understanding.

#### 3.3. Reinforcement Learning (RL)

The core stage for performance enhancement, combining Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) and Reinforcement Learning with Human Feedback (RLHF) across all multimodal domains.
- **Data Preparation:** Sub-tasks suitable for verifiable or model-based rewards are identified, and large volumes of high-quality, difficulty-graded question-answer pairs are filtered or generated (e.g., converting MCQs to fill-in-the-blank).
- **Reward System:** A multi-domain, unified reward system is meticulously designed to assign accurate and robust rewards across diverse tasks (OCR, grounding, video, GUI, charts, STEM, logical reasoning).
 - **Answer Extraction:** Explicit box markers (`<|begin_of_box|>...<|end_of_box|>`) are used for final answer extraction to ensure precision, addressing inaccuracies found with LLM-based extraction in diverse open-domain scenarios.
 - **Avoiding Reward Hacking:** Fine-tuned, hack-resistant verifiers are critical, as even slight weaknesses in a single domain's reward signal can collapse training across all domains.
 - **Domain-Specific Rewards:** Reward logic varies by domain (e.g., numeric tolerance for math, edit distance for OCR, IoU for grounding, LLM semantic equivalence for textual answers), built with shared verification functions and domain-specific modules with unit testing.
 - **Format and Style Checking:** Rule-based and model-based penalties are applied for incorrect formatting or poor reasoning style (e.g., mixed languages, repetitive text).
- **Reinforcement Learning with Curriculum Sampling (RLCS):** An adaptive curriculum adjusts training sample difficulty dynamically.
 - **Difficulty Grading:** Offline assessment (pass@k, human annotations) and online grading (recording rollout outcomes) inform difficulty tiers.
 - **Adaptive Sampling:** Sampling ratios are continuously re-weighted to prioritize mid-range difficulties, where learning gains are maximized, by down-sampling trivial or excessively challenging examples.
- **Improving Effectiveness:**
 - **Larger Batch Size:** Recommended for higher performance ceiling.
 - **Dynamic Sampling Expansion via Ratio EMA:** Oversampling factor (expansion_ratio) is dynamically adjusted using Exponential Moving Average (EMA) to select batches with balanced correct/incorrect responses, improving stability and efficiency.
 - **Force Answering:** A `</think>` token is forcibly inserted for overlong reasoning paths to prompt a final answer, allowing fair reward assignment and dynamic thinking budget control.
 - **Discard KL Loss:** Removed due to observed constraints on VLM capabilities despite faster KL divergence increase.
 - **Clip-higher:** Increasing the upper clipping bound of the importance sampling ratio improves off-policy performance and prevents entropy collapse.
- **Improving Stability:**
 - **High-Quality Cold-Start SFT Data:** Crucial for stable RL; meaningless thinking paths lead to instability.
 - **Removed Entropy Loss:** Found to cause garbled output and training collapse.
 - **Top-p = 1 for Rollouts:** Produces more stable training by ensuring full vocabulary coverage and preventing garbled outputs in later iterations, despite increased randomness.
 - **Per-Sample Loss Computation:** Yields more stable training compared to per-token loss.
 - **Format Learning in SFT:** Emphasized that required output formats should be fully learned during SFT, rather than relying on RL format-based rewards, to prevent training destabilization.
- **Infrastructure:** Extensive optimizations for RL training efficiency: load balancing sequence lengths across DP ranks, intra-rank training with sequence packing and gradient accumulation, efficient sample re-packing, and dynamic sampling expansion.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The GLM-V series models, particularly GLM-4.5V and GLM-4.1V-9B-Thinking, demonstrate remarkable performance, establishing new state-of-the-art results across a broad spectrum of multimodal benchmarks.

- **Overall State-of-the-Art Performance:** GLM-4.5V achieves state-of-the-art performance on nearly all of the 42 public benchmarks among open-source models of similar size. It consistently outperforms strong open-source models like Step-3 (321B-A38B) and Qwen-2.5-VL-72B.
- **Competitiveness with Closed-Source Models:** GLM-4.5V demonstrates competitive or even superior results compared to closed-source models such as Gemini-2.5-Flash on challenging tasks like Coding and GUI Agents, and matches or outperforms it across multiple other tasks.
- **Significant Performance Gains:** GLM-4.5V advances the state-of-the-art for open-source VLMs of comparable size by roughly 10% or more across a wide range of tasks, including:
 - **General VQA:** MMBench, MMStar, GeoBench.
 - **STEM Problem Solving:** MMMU Pro, MathVerse, WeMath.
 - **Chart Understanding:** ChartQAPro, ChartMuseum.
 - **Long Document Understanding:** MMLongBench-Doc.
 - **Visual Grounding:** TreeBench, Ref-L4-test.
 - **Spatial Reasoning:** ERQA.
 - **GUI Agents:** OSWorld, AndroidWorld, WebVoyagerSom, WebQuest.
 - **VLM Coding:** Design2Code, Flame-React-Eval.
 - **Video Understanding:** VideoMME, LVBench, MotionBench, MVBench.
- **Efficiency of Smaller Models:** The smaller GLM-4.1V-9B-Thinking (9 billion parameters) remains highly competitive, achieving superior results to the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B on 29 out of 42 benchmarks. This highlights an excellent trade-off between performance and efficiency.
- **Cross-Domain Generalization in RL:** Experiments with GLM-4.1V-9B-Thinking reveal robust cross-domain generalization and mutual facilitation during reinforcement learning.
 - Training on one domain often boosts performance in others (e.g., STEM training enhances grounding, GUI-agent interaction, and general VQA; GUI-agent training improves performance across all evaluated domains).
 - Joint training across multiple domains ("Mix-all" setting) yields even greater improvements in STEM, OCR & Chart, and general VQA, showcasing a synergistic effect. This synergy is a key factor in the models' extraordinary performance.
- **Key Insights from Development:**
 - Multi-domain reinforcement learning promotes robust cross-domain generalization and mutual facilitation.
 - Dynamically selecting informative rollout problems through Reinforcement Learning with Curriculum Sampling (RLCS) is crucial for efficiency and performance.
 - A robust and precise multi-domain reward system is critical, as weaknesses in a single capability's reward signal can destabilize the entire training process.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GLM-V series, particularly GLM-4.5V and GLM-4.1V-Thinking, represents a significant step forward in the pursuit of general-purpose multimodal reasoning, with several profound implications and potential impacts:

- **Advancing Multimodal AI Capabilities:** By achieving state-of-the-art performance across an exceptionally diverse range of benchmarks, including highly challenging reasoning, agentic, and coding tasks, this work demonstrates that general-purpose multimodal reasoning is becoming increasingly feasible. The "thinking" mode enables more transparent and complex problem-solving.
- **Democratizing High-Performance VLMs:** The open-sourcing of GLM-4.1V-9B-Thinking and GLM-4.5V (including base models and reward systems) is a major contribution. It provides researchers and developers with powerful, readily available tools that can serve as strong foundations for further innovation, reducing the barrier to entry for developing advanced multimodal applications. The efficiency of the 9B parameter model makes high-performance VLMs accessible for real-world deployment with constrained computational resources.
- **Validation of Scalable Reinforcement Learning for VLMs:** The sophisticated RL framework, including RLCS and the multi-domain reward system, proves highly effective in unlocking the full potential of VLMs. The detailed insights on improving RL effectiveness and stability, such as dynamic sampling expansion, force answering, and careful reward design, offer valuable lessons for future large-scale RL implementations in multimodal contexts.
- **Insights into Cross-Domain Generalization:** The empirical demonstration of positive cross-domain generalization and mutual facilitation during RL training provides fundamental insights into how different multimodal skills can reinforce each other. This understanding can guide the design of more efficient and powerful multimodal learning paradigms.
- **Impact on AI Agents and Human-Computer Interaction:** The strong performance in GUI Agents and VLM Coding indicates a significant stride towards more capable AI agents that can interact with digital environments and generate functional code directly from visual input. This has immense potential for automating complex tasks, improving productivity in software development, and enabling more intuitive human-computer interfaces.
- **Catalyst for New Benchmarks and Evaluations:** The authors acknowledge limitations, such as RL not consistently improving reasoning *quality* (only outcomes) and the saturation of some current benchmarks. Their work highlights the need for more challenging and diagnostic evaluation frameworks that can explicitly detect failure modes like shortcut reasoning or hallucinations, thereby inspiring the development of next-generation benchmarks.

Despite its achievements, the paper openly discusses limitations, including reasoning quality not always matching outcome correctness, RL training instability, and struggles in highly complex visual scenarios. Addressing these will be crucial for future work, focusing on advanced reward mechanisms for intermediate reasoning steps, more robust RL optimization, and simultaneous improvements in perception and reasoning. The exploration of multimodal training benefits for text-only tasks also points to exciting future research directions in general reasoning.
