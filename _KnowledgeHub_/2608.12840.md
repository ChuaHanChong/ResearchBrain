# ASPIRE-VINS: Adaptive Spline-based Visual-inertial Navigation System With Robust 3D Measurement Residuals

> [!summary] Summary
> ASPIRE-VINS is a continuous-time visual-inertial navigation framework that couples adaptive knot placement, multi-resolution SE(3) B-splines, and 3D measurement-space residuals in one Lie-group optimization. Knots follow local motion intensity, bounded refinement levels add local detail, and visual constraints enforce ray-orthogonal bearing consistency instead of pixel reprojection. It cuts mean ATE RMSE to 0.212 m on a VIO benchmark at 56 ms per frame.

## Problem

- Discrete-time keyframe VINS ties state updates to selected timestamps, so residuals cannot be evaluated flexibly at native sensor times or varying temporal resolution.
- Continuous-time splines with uniformly spaced knots underfit rapid or nonlinear motion while over-parameterizing static intervals, wasting parameters and losing dynamic detail.
- Standard 2D image-plane reprojection residuals stay sensitive to pixel noise, feature localization error, and photometric degradation during fast motion or weak texture.

## Method

- ==Adaptive knot placement== sets knot spacing inversely to a filtered velocity-change statistic, clipped between 0.03 s and 0.25 s, giving finer support in dynamic intervals.
- ==Multi-resolution splines== sum three levels of local SE(3) pose coefficients with ==bounded refinement activations== from a 95th-percentile-normalized velocity-change ratio, retracted onto SE(3).
- ==3D measurement-space residuals== project the transformed reference feature orthogonally to the calibrated unit observation ray, with a ==tangent-space smoothness prior== on angular-velocity and acceleration derivatives.

## Results

- Mean ATE RMSE across nine VIO benchmark sequences is **0.212 m**, a **5.36%** gain over the best baseline OKVIS-CT, with the largest gains on head-turning sequences.
- On handheld plus Hilti-Oxford sequences, mean RMSE falls to **0.454 m** versus **0.650 m** for OKVIS-CT and **1.316 m** for the discrete-time filtering reference.
- Controlled ablation under identical feature tracks: full backend reaches **0.126 m** on square-fast and **0.552 m** on rotation-normal, beating uniform-knot and 2D-reprojection variants; runtime **56 ms** per frame.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Allocating spline resolution by local motion statistics pays off mainly in fast or rotation-heavy segments; low-dynamic intervals see little change, confirming adaptivity is cheap insurance.
> - Ray-orthogonal bearing residuals improve both discrete-time and continuous-time backends, so the residual formulation and the trajectory representation give largely independent gains.
> - Ray-orthogonal constraints leave the along-ray component unconstrained, so depth stays unobservable under pure rotation or low parallax and must come from prior triangulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Visual-inertial navigation systems (VINS) are a foundational technology for estimating the six-degree-of-freedom (6-DoF) motion of a sensor platform by fusing data from cameras and inertial measurement units (IMUs). The research landscape for VINS is broadly divided into discrete-time and continuous-time approaches, each with distinct advantages and limitations.

**Discrete-time VINS** frameworks, such as MSCKF, VINS-Mono, PL-VINS, and modern square-root filters like √VINS, are recognized for their practical accuracy and computational efficiency in real-time applications. However, their state updates are inherently tied to discrete timestamps, typically corresponding to camera frames. This can limit flexibility when evaluating sensor residuals at their native, often asynchronous, timestamps or when the required temporal resolution of the trajectory varies with motion intensity.

**Continuous-time VINS** methods emerged to address these limitations by modeling the platform's motion as a smooth, continuous function of time. This approach allows for residual evaluation at arbitrary timestamps, accommodating high-rate IMU data and lower-rate camera data within a unified representation. Existing continuous-time systems (e.g., those using cubic B-splines in SE(3) like Furgale et al., Mueggler et al., OKVIS-CT, and Ctrl-VIO) have demonstrated improved temporal consistency. A common challenge for these methods, however, is their reliance on uniformly spaced knots for spline representation. This can lead to either underfitting rapid, dynamic motion segments (failing to capture nuances) or over-parameterizing static intervals (introducing redundant parameters and computational burden). While some work, like CT-UIO, explored non-uniform B-splines and adaptive knot placement, it was developed for UWB-inertial odometry and not directly tailored for tightly coupled visual-inertial optimization with feature-level visual residuals.

Another significant aspect of the VINS landscape concerns the formulation of **visual constraints**. Many VINS frameworks traditionally rely on 2D image-plane reprojection errors. While effective in many scenarios, these residuals can be sensitive to various factors, including pixel noise, inaccuracies in feature localization, and visual degradation caused by rapid motion, poor illumination, or feature-deprived environments. This sensitivity can lead to reduced robustness, particularly in challenging real-world conditions. Consequently, a growing body of research is exploring **3D residual modeling** in visual-inertial systems. Approaches like DiT-SLAM, D-VINS, GaussianFlow SLAM, AIM-SLAM, and A, B, VINS have investigated using implicit 3D depth, semantic cues, dense priors, or Euclidean residuals. However, these often face challenges such as increased real-time computational burden, reliance on specific front-end priors, or limitations in robustness under highly nonlinear motion or severe visual degradation.

ASPIRE-VINS positions itself within this landscape by integrating three components that collectively aim to overcome the aforementioned limitations. It combines adaptive spline resolution (via Adaptive Knot Placement and Multi-Resolution Splines) to address the inflexibility and inefficiency of uniform-knot continuous-time representations, with a 3D measurement-space residual formulation to enhance the robustness of visual constraints beyond traditional 2D reprojection errors. This work seeks to provide a continuous-time, motion-adaptive backend that offers improved accuracy and consistency under diverse and challenging motion and sensing conditions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an adaptive, robust, and efficient continuous-time visual-inertial navigation system capable of accurately estimating 6-DoF trajectories under varying motion dynamics and challenging visual conditions. This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Enhancing Temporal Modeling Flexibility and Efficiency:**
 - **Motivation:** Traditional discrete-time VINS frameworks (e.g., MSCKF, VINS-Mono) update states at fixed, discrete timestamps, primarily dictated by camera frames. This limits their flexibility when needing to evaluate residuals at the native, potentially higher or asynchronous, rates of sensors (like IMUs) or when the required temporal resolution of the trajectory needs to adapt to the intensity or nature of motion.
 - **Objective:** To leverage a continuous-time trajectory representation that allows for smooth interpolation and evaluation of sensor measurements at any arbitrary timestamp. This improves the consistency of fusing multi-rate sensor data.

2. **Addressing Limitations of Uniform Continuous-Time Representations:**
 - **Motivation:** While continuous-time methods offer advantages over discrete-time, many existing implementations (e.g., OKVIS-CT, Ctrl-VIO) use uniformly spaced knots for their spline representations. This uniform spacing leads to two main issues:
 - **Underfitting:** Rapid, highly dynamic, or nonlinear motion segments may not be adequately captured, leading to inaccuracies.
 - **Over-parameterization:** In static or low-dynamic intervals, a uniform, high-resolution knot distribution introduces redundant parameters, increasing computational load without providing additional detail, thus reducing efficiency.
 - **Objective:** To develop adaptive mechanisms that dynamically adjust the temporal resolution of the spline representation. This involves placing more knots in segments with high motion variation and fewer in static or smoothly moving segments, thereby achieving a compact yet expressive trajectory model that accurately reflects motion complexity.

3. **Improving Robustness of Visual Constraints:**
 - **Motivation:** A significant challenge for many VINS frameworks is the reliance on 2D image-plane reprojection errors for visual constraints. These residuals are known to be sensitive to:
 - **Measurement Noise:** Pixel noise and inaccuracies in feature localization.
 - **Motion Degradation:** Motion blur during rapid movement.
 - **Environmental Factors:** Visual degradation due to abrupt illumination changes (e.g., indoor-to-outdoor transitions), feature-deprived regions (e.g., plain walls, narrow corridors), or surfaces with challenging photometric properties (e.g., transparent or reflective materials).
 - **Objective:** To formulate visual residuals in a more robust manner, specifically within a 3D measurement space. This approach aims to reduce direct dependence on pixel-level accuracy and provide bearing consistency by aligning transformed 3D features with calibrated observation rays, thereby enhancing robustness under diverse and challenging visual conditions.

By integrating adaptive spline placement, multi-resolution trajectory modeling, and robust 3D visual residuals within a unified continuous-time optimization framework, ASPIRE-VINS aims to deliver a VINS solution that is both accurate and resilient across a wider range of real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ASPIRE-VINS is designed as a continuous-time optimization framework that estimates the platform's trajectory, velocity, inertial biases, and 3D landmark positions. It employs a Lie group formulation, drawing inspiration from MSCKF-DVIO, and represents the trajectory using a continuous-time B-spline. The core of the methodology lies in the integration of three main components: Adaptive Knot Placement (AKP), Multi-Resolution Splines (MRS), and 3D Measurement-Space Residuals (3D-MSR).

**A. State Variables and Optimization**
The system's state `X` includes the continuous-time pose trajectory `T_s_w(t)` (representing the sensor frame 's' with respect to the world frame 'w'), linear velocity `v(t)`, accelerometer and gyroscope biases (`b_a(t)`, `b_g(t)`), and 3D landmark positions `P`. The trajectory `T_s_w(t)` is modeled by spline coefficients in local SE(3) coordinates and mapped back to SE(3) using retraction, ensuring a valid rigid-body motion. The angular velocity is derived from the rotation spline, avoiding redundant state variables.

Optimization is performed by minimizing a loss function `J(X)` using the Ceres solver. This loss function comprises:
1. **Inertial Residuals:** Compares bias-compensated gyroscope and accelerometer measurements against predictions derived analytically from the spline-based trajectory.
2. **Visual Residuals (3D-MSR):** Incorporates the 3D measurement-space residuals, detailed below.
3. **Smoothness Prior:** A tangent-space prior regularizes angular velocity variation and translational acceleration (`˙ω(t)` and `¨p(t)`), preventing implausible motion.

Lie group retraction is utilized for state updates in the tangent space, which aids in consistency and efficient Jacobian computation.

**B. Adaptive Knot Placement (AKP)**
AKP is responsible for dynamically determining the timestamps of spline knots. Its purpose is to tailor the temporal resolution of the trajectory representation to the local motion intensity.
- **Mechanism:** Knot timestamps `t_q` are accumulated from local intervals `Δt_r`. The duration of each interval `Δt_r` is inversely proportional to a local motion intensity statistic `ρ_r`, which is calculated from the magnitude of velocity change between filtered velocity estimates.
- **Adaptivity:** In high-dynamic intervals, `ρ_r` will be large, leading to smaller `Δt_r` and thus shorter knot spacings (higher temporal resolution). Conversely, in low-dynamic intervals, `ρ_r` will be small, resulting in larger `Δt_r` and longer knot spacings (lower temporal resolution), reducing parameter redundancy.
- **Constraints:** The `Δt_r` values are bounded between `Δt_min` and `Δt_max` to ensure numerical stability and practical resolution limits. This approach generates a non-uniform knot sequence that serves as the temporal support for MRS.

**C. Multi-Resolution Splines (MRS)**
MRS models the continuous-time trajectory `T_s_w(t)` across multiple temporal scales or resolution levels (`H = {0, 1, ..., L}`).
- **Representation:** The trajectory is represented as a weighted sum of local pose coefficients `c_h_μ` (in R^6) and effective interpolation weights `w_h_μ(t)`. This sum operates in the local coordinate representation before being mapped to SE(3) via an exponential map update.
- **Effective Interpolation Weight:** `w_h_μ(t)` is constructed from standard B-spline basis functions (`B_h_μ,k(t)`) and incorporates bounded refinement coefficients (`γ_h_μ`, `γ_h_μ+1`). These refinement coefficients are activated based on a locally filtered velocity-change statistic (`δ_h_μ`), derived from the platform's velocity.
- **Hierarchy:** Coarse resolution levels maintain global smoothness, while finer levels, activated by `γ_h_μ` according to local motion variation, add localized detail for sudden turns or nonlinear motions. The `γ_h_μ` values are bounded to limit abrupt changes in local refinement. This hierarchy allows for expressive trajectory representation without introducing discontinuous changes in spline basis functions during optimization.

**D. 3D Measurement-Space Residuals (3D-MSR)**
3D-MSR provides robust visual constraints by enforcing feature alignment directly in 3D Euclidean space, rather than relying on 2D image-plane reprojection errors.
- **Formulation:** For each visual observation, the residual `f_P` measures the consistency between a transformed 3D reference feature `P_r` and its corresponding back-projected measurement ray direction `P_m`. `P_r` is obtained from multi-view triangulation and refined by global bundle adjustment. `P_m` represents the calibrated observation direction from the camera model.
- **Calculation:** The residual is defined as `f_P = (I - V)(R_m_r P_r + t_m_r)`. Here, `T_m_r = (R_m_r, t_m_r)` is the relative transformation from the reference frame to the measurement frame, `I` is the identity matrix, and `V = rr^T / ||r||^2` is a projection matrix onto the unit observation-ray direction `r = P_m / ||P_m||`.
- **Bearing Consistency:** The term `(I - V)` effectively removes the component of the transformed point that lies parallel to the observation ray, thus evaluating only the ray-orthogonal consistency error. This means the residual constrains the feature to lie *on* the observation ray, rather than matching its 2D projection.
- **Depth Observability:** It is noted that 3D-MSR does not directly make feature depth observable under pure rotation or insufficient parallax; the depth of `P_r` is established by prior multi-view triangulation.

**E. Experimental Setup**
The methodology is evaluated using:
- **VIO benchmark dataset:** For UAV-like motion patterns (square, circle, infinity trajectories with normal, fast, and head-turning variants), conducted indoors with stable conditions.
- **Custom handheld sequences:** Collected in unconstrained real-world environments with abrupt illumination changes, feature-deprived areas, and reflective surfaces. Ground truth for these sequences is generated using PALoc, which fuses LiDAR odometry, IMU, and prior map constraints.
- **Hilti-Oxford dataset:** Selected sequences (exp04, exp05, exp06, exp18) representing large-scale architectural environments with repetitive structures (e.g., spiral staircases) and challenging natural lighting.

Comparisons are made against five VINS systems: MSCKF-DVIO, PL-VINS, √VINS (discrete-time baselines), and Ctrl-VIO, OKVIS-CT (fixed-resolution continuous-time baselines). An ablation study isolates the contributions of AKP, MRS, and 3D-MSR by employing identical visual front-ends and feature tracks across variants.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated ASPIRE-VINS against a range of baselines across diverse datasets and motion conditions, supported by a controlled ablation study.

**A. Pose Estimation Accuracy on VIO Benchmark Dataset (Table II)**
- ASPIRE-VINS achieved the lowest mean Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) RMSE of 0.212 m across all nine VIO benchmark sequences, representing a 5.36% improvement over the next best baseline, OKVIS-CT.
- The system demonstrated improved accuracy, particularly in sequences involving high-dynamic motion (e.g., `square-fast`: 0.111 m RMSE for ASPIRE-VINS vs. 0.135 m for OKVIS-CT) and aggressive head-turning (e.g., `circle-head`: 0.259 m vs. 0.290 m for OKVIS-CT; `infinity-head`: 0.418 m vs. 0.450 m for OKVIS-CT).
- While fixed-resolution continuous-time baselines like OKVIS-CT performed comparably in smooth infinity trajectories and Ctrl-VIO slightly surpassed ASPIRE-VINS in one low-dynamic case (`square-normal`), the adaptive modeling of ASPIRE-VINS provided advantages in nonlinear and rapidly varying motions.

**B. Controlled Backend Ablation (Table III)**
This study isolated the contributions of the proposed components using identical visual feature tracks:
- **Impact of 3D-MSR:** Replacing 2D reprojection residuals with 3D-MSR consistently improved accuracy for both discrete-time (DT) and continuous-time (CT) variants. For example, in a DT backend, 3D-MSR reduced the `rot-normal` RMSE from 0.814 m to 0.746 m. This indicates the benefit of bearing-consistency constraints in 3D measurement space, especially in rotation-dominant scenarios, by reducing sensitivity to image-plane perturbations.
- **Impact of AKP and MRS:** Comparing "CT uniform + 3D-MSR" with the "Full proposed backend" (which includes AKP/MRS) revealed that motion-adaptive trajectory modeling significantly enhanced performance in fast translation (`square-fast` RMSE decreased from 0.156 m to 0.126 m) and rotation-heavy motion (`rot-fast` RMSE decreased from 0.736 m to 0.612 m). The minimal change in `square-normal` indicated stability in low-dynamic motion.
- **Comparison to CT-UIO-style knot placement:** The full proposed backend, combining AKP and MRS, achieved lower errors (e.g., 0.126 m for `square-fast`) compared to a variant using only a CT-UIO-style adaptive knot span rule (0.143 m), supporting the effectiveness of the combined refinement strategy.

**C. Runtime Analysis (Table IV)**
- ASPIRE-VINS processed data at approximately 56 ms per frame (18 FPS) on the specified hardware.
- This places its computational speed slower than highly optimized discrete-time filters like √VINS (18 ms, 56 FPS) but faster than other continuous-time baselines like OKVIS-CT (87 ms, 11 FPS) and comparable to Ctrl-VIO (63 ms, 16 FPS). This suggests the proposed continuous-time backend maintains practical processing speed for real-time applications.

**D. Pose Estimation Accuracy on Custom Handheld and Hilti-Oxford Datasets (Table V)**
- **Custom Handheld Sequences:** Under realistic visual degradation, such as repetitive stair geometries (`Multistory Stairs`) and abrupt photometric transitions (`Indoor-to-Outdoor`), ASPIRE-VINS achieved the lowest RMSE and maximum error in `Multistory Stairs` (0.240 m RMSE, 0.420 m Max). In `Indoor-to-Outdoor`, it achieved the lowest maximum error (0.448 m), while OKVIS-CT had a slightly lower RMSE (0.308 m). This indicates that adaptive temporal resolution is beneficial in visually degraded segments and at transition boundaries.
- **Hilti-Oxford Datasets:** In large-scale architectural environments with prolonged structural repetition and challenging illumination (exp04, exp05, exp06, exp18), ASPIRE-VINS consistently achieved the lowest RMSE and maximum error across all selected sequences. The improvement was particularly pronounced in `exp18` (spiral staircases, strong structural degeneracy, severe illumination contrast), where ASPIRE-VINS reported an RMSE of 0.379 m compared to OKVIS-CT's 0.568 m. This demonstrates the robustness of the motion-adaptive continuous-time trajectory under sustained geometric and photometric challenges.

In summary, the findings indicate that ASPIRE-VINS generally achieves competitive or lower trajectory errors than compared baselines across a variety of motion and sensing conditions. The adaptive temporal modeling (AKP/MRS) and robust 3D-MSR contribute to improved accuracy, particularly in dynamic, head-turning, and visually challenging scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of ASPIRE-VINS holds significance for the field of visual-inertial navigation and could have several potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Robustness and Adaptability in VINS:** ASPIRE-VINS advances VINS by directly addressing two critical limitations of existing systems: the rigidity of discrete-time state representations and the suboptimality of uniformly spaced knots in continuous-time models, as well as the fragility of 2D image-plane residuals. By integrating adaptive knot placement and multi-resolution splines, the framework can dynamically adjust its temporal resolution to better represent varying motion dynamics. This leads to more accurate trajectory estimates, particularly in scenarios involving rapid, nonlinear, or complex motions where fixed-resolution methods may underfit.

2. **Improved Performance in Challenging Environments:** The introduction of 3D Measurement-Space Residuals (3D-MSR) offers a more robust visual constraint mechanism. By focusing on bearing consistency in 3D space rather than direct 2D pixel reprojection errors, ASPIRE-VINS demonstrates reduced sensitivity to common visual degradations such as pixel noise, motion blur, abrupt illumination changes, and feature scarcity. This makes the system more reliable for real-world deployments in unconstrained environments (e.g., indoor-to-outdoor transitions, narrow corridors, repetitive structures) that frequently challenge conventional VINS.

3. **Practical Application Potential:** Despite the added complexity of adaptive continuous-time modeling, ASPIRE-VINS maintains a practical processing speed (18 FPS), making it suitable for online operation in various robotic and autonomous applications. Its demonstrated performance across diverse benchmark and real-world datasets—from UAV-like movements to handheld exploration in architecturally complex and visually degraded settings—suggests its readiness for integration into platforms requiring robust and accurate state estimation, such as autonomous robots, drones, and augmented/virtual reality devices.

4. **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework provides a principled approach to combining motion-adaptive continuous-time trajectory representation with robust geometric constraints. This could serve as a foundation for further research in several directions. For instance, the authors acknowledge limitations such as sensitivity to knot initialization, numerical conditioning over very long trajectories, and limited depth observability under pure rotation. These points suggest avenues for future work, such as exploring alternative interpolation methods like Chebyshev-based barycentric rational interpolation (CBRI) for enhanced stability and investigating mechanisms for improved depth estimation.

In summary, ASPIRE-VINS contributes to the ongoing evolution of visual-inertial odometry by offering a more flexible, accurate, and robust solution for motion estimation. Its innovations in adaptive continuous-time trajectory modeling and 3D visual residuals provide a step towards more reliable autonomous navigation systems in a wider range of challenging operational scenarios.
