# Fauna Sprout: A lightweight, approachable, developer-ready humanoid robot

> [!summary] Summary
> Fauna Sprout is a lightweight (22.7 kg, 1.07 m tall), expressive humanoid robot developed by Fauna Robotics to provide an accessible and robust platform for general-purpose robotic control development. The robot integrates safety features for human environments and a modular, developer-friendly software stack to support advanced embodied AI research and application prototyping.

## Problem

- A scarcity of robust, accessible, and safe humanoid robot platforms hinders progress in general-purpose robotic controllers and embodied AI research.
- Current humanoid robots are either proprietary industrial systems inaccessible to developers or academic prototypes lacking manufacturability, robustness, and safety for sustained real-world deployment.
- Developing robots for safe, expressive, and long-term interaction in human environments remains challenging due to hardware and software limitations of available platforms.

## Method

- Developed **Fauna Sprout**, a lightweight (22.7 kg, 1.07 m) humanoid robot with a "defense-in-depth" safety approach, including soft exteriors and conservative torque limits.
- Implemented a modular, component-based software architecture utilizing Docker and ROS 2, providing explicit APIs for developer accessibility and custom service integration.
- Designed for expressivity through an integrated head with LED arrays and motorized eyebrows, alongside a robust whole-body control system based on learned reinforcement learning policies.

## Results

- Successfully realized a functional, integrated humanoid robot (**1.07** m tall, **22.7** kg) capable of various whole-body behaviors, including locomotion and object manipulation.
- Demonstrated robust whole-body control via learned policies trained in IsaacSim/IsaacLab, complemented by an intuitive VR-based teleoperation system for complex tasks.
- Implemented an effective mapping and navigation system, featuring fused odometry and a volumetric mapping system that reportedly outperformed RTAB-Map with **30%** less compute.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Broad adoption and accelerated research in embodied AI depend on creating accessible, safe, and expressive humanoid platforms for diverse users, mirroring the impact of personal computing.
> - A "defense-in-depth" safety strategy, integrating physical design constraints with real-time software monitoring and compliant control, is critical for robots operating safely alongside humans.
> - Designing robots with integrated non-verbal expressive capabilities and a developer-friendly modular software stack fosters richer human-robot interaction and enables rapid application prototyping.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of the Fauna Sprout robot is directly positioned to address a critical gap in the rapidly evolving field of humanoid robotics. Recent advancements in learned control, large-scale simulation, and generative AI have ignited renewed interest in creating general-purpose humanoid robots. However, as the paper articulates, progress is hampered by the scarcity of platforms suitable for safe, expressive, and sustained deployment in real-world human environments.

The existing humanoid robot landscape can be broadly categorized:

- **Academic Prototypes:** Projects like Toddlerbot, OP3, Berkeley Humanoid, and ARTEMIS showcase impressive technical capabilities, especially in bipedal locomotion. However, they often lack the manufacturability, robustness, and safety features required for long-term deployment around non-specialist users, limiting their impact beyond specialized labs.
- **Industrial Humanoids:** Companies such as Boston Dynamics, Tesla, Agility, Figure, and XPENG are developing large, full-scale humanoids primarily for industrial applications like logistics and manufacturing. These systems are typically closed, proprietary, and inaccessible to independent developers, restricting broad experimentation. While some, like Disney Research, focus on entertainment, the general trend is utilitarian.
- **Emerging Commercial Platforms:** A newer generation of startups, particularly from China (e.g., Unitree, Fourier Intelligence, LimX, Booster, AgiBot), emphasizes rapid manufacturability and cost-effectiveness, leading to broader adoption among researchers due to their compact size and reliability.

Sprout deliberately carves out a niche by aiming to be a "developer platform" that combines the accessibility of smaller academic systems with the manufacturability and robust engineering principles seen in commercial efforts, all while prioritizing safety and expressivity in human environments. The authors draw a parallel to the transformative impact of personal computers, smartphones, and virtual reality, arguing that robotics needs similar accessible, general-purpose platforms to reach its inflection point.

In terms of control systems, the paper acknowledges the paradigm shift towards GPU-accelerated simulation and empirical optimization through reinforcement learning (e.g., IsaacLab, MuJoCo, Genesis). It also contextualizes Sprout within the debate between monolithic (e.g., RT-1, Gemini Robotics, Physical Intelligence) and modular/hierarchical (e.g., NVIDIA N1/N1.5, Figure Helix, MenteeBot, Flexion) language-conditioned robotic control systems. Sprout's software architecture is designed to support both approaches by offering modular, containerized services with explicit APIs, allowing developers to leverage or replace components as needed.

Furthermore, Sprout's "character-like" appearance and non-screen expressive head (using lights and actuated eyebrows, akin to Reachy Mini) differentiate it from most industrial humanoids that often feature featureless heads or other expressive robots that rely on digital screens for non-verbal communication. This design choice directly addresses the underexplored domain of social interaction on utilitarian humanoids.

In essence, Fauna Sprout aims to bridge the gap between high-capability but inaccessible industrial systems and open but often non-robust academic prototypes, by offering a safe, expressive, and developer-friendly platform tailored for real-world human-robot interaction and embodied intelligence research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective behind the development of Fauna Sprout is to **accelerate progress toward general-purpose robotic controllers** by providing a much-needed, accessible, and robust humanoid platform. The authors identify a significant "gap in the physical infrastructure" required for developing embodied intelligence in real human environments.

The core motivations and specific objectives can be summarized as follows:

- **Address Platform Limitations:** To overcome the limitations of existing humanoid robots, which are either "closed industrial systems or academic prototypes that are difficult to deploy and operate around people," thus hindering progress in robotics.
- **Prioritize Safety for Human Environments:** A fundamental motivation is to design a robot that can safely operate in close proximity to people. This includes minimizing kinetic energy, designing soft exteriors, limiting joint torques, and implementing compliant control to mitigate risk during interaction. The goal is to enable "routine operation in shared human spaces rather than isolated or tightly controlled settings."
- **Enhance Expressivity for Social Interaction:** To facilitate new modes of human-robot interaction that rely on social connection. This involves integrating an expressive head with features like articulated gaze, actuated eyebrows, and LED arrays to enable non-verbal cues, a domain often neglected in utilitarian humanoids.
- **Ensure Developer Accessibility and Ease of Use:** To lower "physical and technical barriers to deployment" for a broad range of users, including researchers, developers, educators, designers, and creative technologists. This means providing a unified hardware-software stack with clear APIs, modular services, and intuitive tools for deployment, monitoring, and data collection, without requiring deep expertise in low-level control.
- **Support General Robotics Research and Application Prototyping:** To serve as a versatile platform capable of supporting diverse research, from low-level motor control to high-level planning, manipulation, and autonomy, by offering core system services that can be leveraged or replaced.
- **Enable Long-Term and Reproducible Deployment:** To provide a platform robust enough for day-to-day use and structured to support reproducible deployment, moving beyond short-horizon behaviors in sanitized settings.
- **Achieve Scalable Manufacturability:** To design the robot from the outset for cost-effective manufacturing, allowing it to scale from low-volume builds to mass production, making it widely available.

In essence, Fauna Robotics aims to democratize access to capable humanoid robotics, fostering an ecosystem of broad participation and experimentation that they believe is crucial for the field's advancement, mirroring the success trajectories of personal computing and smartphones.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Fauna Sprout's development combines robust hardware engineering with a modular, developer-centric software architecture, guided by principles of safety, expressivity, and manufacturability.

**Hardware Design:**
The hardware design emphasizes a "defense-in-depth" approach to safety. Sprout is intentionally **compact and lightweight** (1.07 m tall, 22.7 kg), significantly reducing kinetic energy and potential impact forces. Its mechanical design minimizes pinch points, hot surfaces, and electrical hazards, incorporating **soft, deformable exterior materials**. Embedded software acts as a secondary safety layer, with a dedicated subsystem running on independent processors for real-time monitoring and enforcement of system-level constraints, even under application faults. The third layer involves application- and policy-level safety mechanisms like compliant motor control and vision-based safe navigation.

Mechanical integrity is balanced with low mass and compliance through extensive **simulation-based optimization** for weight, power, and thermal performance. Actuators are selected and tuned per-joint to be appropriately sized, reducing mass and improving controllability. The arms feature custom **single-degree-of-freedom grippers** prioritizing durability and consistent grasping, with grip force software-limited to 12 N to prevent injury.

The core compute is an **NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin**, handling perception, planning, and high-level decision-making. This interfaces with a custom motherboard for power distribution, sensor acquisition, and deterministic motor control. Communication with actuators occurs over Ethernet using real-time protocols, ensuring bounded latency. A custom-designed **Li-ion battery** with a commercial Battery Management System provides high energy density.

Manufacturing principles from consumer electronics are applied, focusing on **low part count, standardized fasteners, and co-developed mechanical/electronic designs** to enable scalable and cost-effective production. Aesthetically, Sprout's design is "character-like," incorporating an **LED array and motorized eyebrows** for nonverbal communication, and an integrated audio system with a four-microphone array for speech recognition and output.

**Software Architecture:**
Sprout adopts a **component-based AI platform** architecture, prioritizing modularity and explicit APIs. Services are containerized using **Docker**, allowing developers to select and deploy specific functionalities. Core platform services for safety and real-time control run with explicit resource isolation (CPU affinity, scheduling policies) to ensure stability.

Inter-process communication primarily leverages **ROS 2**, with RMW Zenoh for efficient on- and off-board compute bridging. Other protocols include CBOR over Ethernet for motor control and JSON/WebSocket/WebRTC for teleoperation. **Foxglove** is integrated for visualization and debugging, supported by a structured logging pipeline. The **SDK** provides stable APIs (ROS 2, Python, C++) and templates for custom service packaging.

**Core Software Services:**

1. **Motor Control:** Utilizes a **finite state machine** to orchestrate discrete control modes (e.g., standing, walking, kneeling), each encapsulating specific behaviors and safety checks. Learned reinforcement learning policies, trained in **IsaacSim/IsaacLab**, implement these modes, mapping proprioceptive observations to intermediate control targets. Transitions between modes are treated as distinct components, implemented as learned tracking controllers via imitation learning. **Compliance** is a fundamental property, trained into policies by exposing the robot to disturbances and rewarding stable, non-aggressive responses to external forces. **Sim-to-real calibration** is ensured through zero-referencing, camera calibration, and refined actuator models. Standard robot description formats (USD, URDF, MJCF) are provided for consistent physical modeling.

2. **Whole-Body Teleoperation:** A **VR-based system** (using Meta Quest devices and the "Embody" Unity application) enables intuitive full-body control. It performs **isomorphic retargeting** to map user morphology to the robot's dimensions. The system uses a compliant whole-body policy, and includes a **data collection system** for logging, annotating, and playing back demonstrations, supporting DAgger-style interventions for robust policy training.

3. **Mapping & Navigation:** Features a **Fused Odometry system** (Extended Kalman Filter integrating ZED2i camera, IMU, and learned proprioceptive velocity estimates) for low-latency state estimation. **Volumetric Mapping** uses a Truncated Signed Distance Field (TSDF) and divides the environment into locally consistent "maplets" to manage drift and optimize computation. Global alignment is achieved via a hierarchical pose graph and a custom ML-based loop-closure service, with **GTSAM** for optimization. The **Navigation system** builds an occupancy grid (static + dynamic layers), uses a **Hybrid A*** planner for global and local paths, and a **pure-pursuit tracker** for velocity commands.

4. **Human-Robot Interaction (HRI):** A dedicated subsystem communicates system status and expressive intent through lights, sounds, and mechanical movements. It uses a **hierarchical graph of computation nodes** that interpret robot state and propose actions in independent "slots." A merge policy blends or arbitrates these proposals, allowing for additive effects, exclusivity, and prioritization (e.g., safety alerts preempting expressions).

5. **Conversation and Reasoning:** The platform does not provide a turnkey conversational agent but exposes core services (ROS 2 topics, **Model Context Protocol (MCP) server**) that developers can integrate with their own **LLM-based agents**. These services include wake-word detection (openWakeWord), ASR (Deepgram Flux, ElevenLabs v2, NVIDIA Riva), TTS, and MCP tools for commanding motor control modes, high-level behaviors, and navigation goals.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper primarily *introduces* and *describes* the Fauna Sprout platform, articulating its design philosophy, architectural components, and capabilities rather than presenting comparative experimental results in a traditional sense. The "findings" are therefore the successful realization of its design objectives and the functional demonstration of its intended features.

The key results and capabilities presented are:

- **A Fully Integrated, Functional Humanoid Platform:** Sprout is presented as a complete, working hardware-software system capable of a range of humanoid behaviors (Fig. 1 demonstrates standing, high-fives, kneeling, object manipulation, dancing, expressive movements).
- **Demonstrated Safety in Design:** Through its lightweight and compact form factor (1.07 m, 22.7 kg), soft exteriors, conservative torque limits, and compliant control, Sprout inherently supports safer interactions in shared human spaces. While quantitative safety metrics are not extensively detailed, the design choices are a clear outcome of prioritizing safe deployment.
- **Achieved Expressivity for Social HRI:** The robot successfully integrates an expressive head with actuated eyebrows, LED arrays, and an audio system, enabling non-verbal communication and supporting social interactions. This moves beyond the purely utilitarian designs of many industrial humanoids.
- **Robust Whole-Body Control:** The motor control system, leveraging learned reinforcement learning policies and state-machine orchestration, enables various whole-body behaviors like walking, kneeling, and crawling. The explicit design for compliant interaction ensures stability and safety during physical contact.
- **Effective VR-Based Teleoperation:** The "Embody" VR application provides an intuitive interface for full-body teleoperation, facilitating complex loco-manipulation tasks and demonstrating seamless control for tasks like reaching objects on the ground. The integrated data collection system supports DAgger-style interventions, which is crucial for training robust policies.
- **Reliable Mapping and Navigation:** The fused odometry system, combining visual, inertial, and kinematic data, provides stable state estimates. The Volumetric Mapping system effectively constructs dense 3D maps using maplets and loop closures, achieving strong reconstruction consistency with efficient onboard compute (e.g., outperforming RTAB-Map with 30% less compute). The navigation stack allows Sprout to plan and execute paths to targets within mapped environments.
- **Modular and Extensible Software Stack:** The component-based architecture, utilizing Docker, ROS 2, and explicit APIs, demonstrates a highly modular and developer-friendly system. This allows for rapid iteration, replacement of individual services, and integration of custom components without destabilizing the core control stack.
- **Foundational Support for LLM-Based Agents:** By exposing core robot services and an MCP server, Sprout provides the necessary infrastructure for developers to build and deploy complex, conversational, and reasoning agents, indicating a forward-looking compatibility with cutting-edge AI.
- **Designed for Manufacturability:** The adoption of consumer electronics design principles ensures that the platform can scale from prototypes to mass production, addressing a key limitation of many academic robotic systems.

In essence, Fauna Sprout presents itself as a fully realized platform that embodies its design principles and offers a comprehensive set of capabilities essential for embodied AI research and real-world deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Fauna Sprout robot represents a significant contribution to the field of humanoid robotics, primarily due to its strategic focus on **accessibility, safety, and expressivity in human environments**. Its potential impact spans several critical areas:

- **Democratization of Humanoid Robotics:** By consciously lowering the "physical and technical barriers to deployment," Sprout aims to make capable humanoid platforms available to a much broader audience. This includes not only advanced robotics researchers but also educators, designers, and creative technologists who previously lacked the specialized expertise or resources to operate complex robots. This broadens participation, fostering a more diverse and innovative ecosystem akin to how personal computers transformed computing.
- **Acceleration of Embodied AI Research:** The absence of reliable, deployable platforms has hindered research into long-horizon autonomy and learning from real-world experience. Sprout's emphasis on safety, durability, and sustained operation in shared human spaces directly addresses this. It enables rigorous research into human-robot interaction (HRI), social robotics, learning in unstructured environments, and developing agents that can operate reliably over extended periods. This shifts the focus from sanitized lab settings to dynamic, everyday contexts.
- **Advancement in Human-Robot Interaction:** With its expressive head, audio capabilities, and HRI subsystem, Sprout is specifically designed to facilitate more natural and engaging interactions. This feature is crucial for developing robots that can build trust, convey intent, and integrate seamlessly into human society, opening new avenues for research into social cues, non-verbal communication, and human perception of robots.
- **Catalyst for Application Development:** The developer-ready hardware-software stack, complete with APIs, modular services, and robust tooling, will enable rapid prototyping and deployment of new robotic applications. This could lead to unforeseen uses and innovations in service robotics, education, entertainment, and assistance, moving beyond current industrial or highly controlled applications.
- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** The meticulous attention to sim-to-real calibration, accurate actuator models, and training policies in environments with disturbances significantly enhances the transferability of learned behaviors from simulation to the real world. This is a perpetual challenge in robotics, and Sprout's approach promises to make robot learning more efficient and reliable.
- **Commercial Viability and Scalability:** The design-for-manufacturability approach, inspired by consumer electronics, positions Sprout for scalable production and potential widespread commercial adoption. This focus on cost-effectiveness and reliability suggests it could become a standard platform, much like specific drone or mobile robot platforms have become in their respective domains.
- **New Data Collection Paradigms:** The integrated teleoperation and data collection system, with support for DAgger-style interventions, provides a powerful tool for efficiently gathering high-quality, on-policy data. This is critical for training robust learned controllers and accelerating the development of autonomous capabilities.

In conclusion, Fauna Sprout is not merely another humanoid robot; it represents a strategic effort to overcome the infrastructural bottlenecks currently impeding the progress of general-purpose robotics. By making safe, expressive, and capable humanoid technology broadly accessible, Fauna Robotics aims to be a foundational element that catalyzes a new era of innovation and deployment in embodied AI.
