# Dynamics Are Learned, Not Told: Semi-Supervised Discovery of Latent Dynamics Geometries For Zero-Shot Policy Adaptation

> [!summary] Summary
> Latent Dynamics Geometries (LDG) is presented as an outcome-centric framework for zero-shot policy adaptation, using semi-supervised contrastive learning within a variational inference setup to construct a smooth and structured latent space of interaction outcomes. The method enables policies to robustly adapt to unmodeled, time-varying, and even catastrophic dynamics shifts in robotics, outperforming parameter-centric baselines on MuJoCo tasks, especially under structural failures.

## Problem

- Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) policies exhibit significant performance degradation when deployed in real-world scenarios with shifts in physical dynamics (out-of-distribution generalization).
- Existing parameter-centric adaptation methods are limited by relying on explicit, pre-specified physical parameters, which are often incomplete, unmodeled, or unknown in real-world robotic systems.
- The most relevant information for adaptation often lies in the *realized effects of dynamics on interaction outcomes* rather than the specific physical parameters themselves, a nuance often overlooked.

## Method

- **LDG** adopts an outcome-centric framework that infers dynamics-relevant context directly from interaction histories without explicit physical parameter supervision.
- It integrates multi-positive ==contrastive learning== into a variational inference framework to discover a geometrically structured ==latent== dynamics manifold.
- The approach establishes a theoretical grounding by demonstrating that minimizing an encoder's Lipschitz constant (LEL_ELE) and local consistency metric (eta_e) is crucial for robust zero-shot adaptation.

## Results

- LDG achieved high in-distribution stability, obtaining the highest mean cumulative reward on Hopper (3239.2) and Walker2d (4883.7) with substantially lower variance compared to baselines.
- The method demonstrated superior zero-shot generalization, outperforming all baselines across unmodeled parameters, time-varying dynamics, and notably in structural failure scenarios (e.g., disabled actuator) where parameter-centric methods catastrophically failed.
- LDG learned interpretable latent dynamics manifolds, visualizing as smooth, monotonic arcs correlating with physical parameters, and discovered functional equivalences, allowing adaptation to unseen physical properties based on similar observable effects.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The sub-optimality of cross-domain adaptation monotonically increases with the encoder's Lipschitz constant and a local consistency metric, emphasizing the importance of smooth and locally consistent latent representations.
> - Contrastive learning, specifically the InfoNCE loss, effectively shapes the latent dynamics geometry by enforcing local consistency, promoting global uniformity, and implicitly filtering nuisance variables irrelevant to dynamics.
> - An 'outcome-centric' perspective, which learns from how dynamics affect interaction outcomes, is more robust to unmodeled and catastrophic dynamics shifts than 'parameter-centric' methods that rely on explicit physical parameters.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) agents often excel in specific training environments but exhibit significant performance degradation when deployed in real-world scenarios with shifts in physical dynamics. This challenge, known as out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization or dynamics adaptation, is a critical area of research in robotics.

Existing approaches to dynamics adaptation can be broadly categorized:
- **Domain Randomization (DR):** Trains a single policy across a diverse range of dynamics to improve robustness, though it may compromise optimality in specific domains.
- **Off-Dynamics Reinforcement Learning:** Utilizes limited target domain interactions or domain classifiers to adjust source policies.
- **Meta-Reinforcement Learning (Meta-RL):** Learns dynamics priors that facilitate rapid adaptation, often requiring online gradient updates during deployment.
- **Parameter-Centric Adaptation (e.g., Rapid Motor Adaptation - RMA):** A prominent paradigm where policies are conditioned on a compact representation (latent context) of explicit physical parameters (e.g., mass, friction). This latent context is typically inferred from short interaction histories by an adaptation module. While effective for known parameter variations, this approach is limited by its reliance on pre-specified axes of variation and can be brittle under unmodeled or time-varying dynamics shifts. Furthermore, identifying and explicitly providing these physical parameters can be complex or infeasible in certain real-world scenarios.
- **Implicit Latent Inference:** Infers dynamics-relevant context directly from interaction histories (e.g., trajectory dynamics encoders) without explicit parameter supervision. This area often leverages variational inference to create compact latent representations.

This paper positions itself within the implicit latent inference paradigm but introduces a novel *outcome-centric* perspective. It argues that prior parameter-centric methods are limited by focusing on *what* the dynamics are, rather than *how* they affect interaction outcomes. The work draws inspiration from representation learning techniques in computer vision, particularly contrastive learning, to explicitly shape the geometry of the latent dynamics space. By grounding its approach in theoretical stability analysis related to the encoder's Lipschitz constant, this research aims to provide a principled mechanism for robust zero-shot adaptation that addresses the limitations of both parameter-centric and purely reconstructive implicit methods. It contributes to the understanding of how latent space properties, specifically smoothness and topological structure, influence the generalization capabilities of DRL policies in non-stationary environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent brittleness of DRL policies to real-world dynamics shifts. Policies tightly optimized for nominal environments frequently fail catastrophically when physical conditions change (e.g., unknown payload, mechanical wear).

The paper identifies two fundamental intricacies overlooked by the prevalent parameter-centric adaptation paradigm:
1. **Incompleteness of Explicit Parameters:** Real robotic systems are subject to a wide array of unmodeled, coupled, and time-varying factors for which no fixed set of physical parameters can provide a complete description. Relying on pre-specified parameters limits adaptability to unforeseen changes.
2. **Functional Equivalence of Dynamics:** Distinct physical phenomena can induce similar effects on motion. For example, increased payload and higher friction can both manifest as "resistance to acceleration." Consequently, the most relevant information for adaptation is not the physical parameters themselves, but their *realized effects on interaction outcomes*.

Given these limitations, the key objectives of this work are:

- **Develop an Outcome-Centric Adaptation Framework:** Shift the paradigm from "telling policies what the dynamics are" to "enabling them to learn how dynamics affect interaction outcomes." This involves inferring dynamics-relevant context directly from interaction histories without explicit physical parameter supervision.
- **Establish a Theoretical Grounding for Robust Adaptation:** Mathematically demonstrate that the sub-optimality (regret) of cross-domain adaptation is monotonically increasing with respect to the encoder's Lipschitz constant ($L_E$) and a local consistency metric ($\delta_e$). This implies that controlling the sensitivity and smoothness of the latent representation is a principled mechanism for robust adaptation.
- **Engineer a Smooth and Structured Latent Dynamics Manifold:** Practically achieve a smooth, task-relevant latent topology. This involves:
 - **Enforcing Local Consistency:** Ensuring that similar interaction histories map to nearby regions in the latent space (minimizing $\delta_e$).
 - **Promoting Global Uniformity and Separability:** Structuring the latent space such that distinct dynamics regimes are distinguishable and organized, forming a "smooth manifold" (bounding $L_E$).
 - **Filtering Nuisance Variables:** Ensuring the latent space encodes only task-relevant dynamics and remains invariant to task-irrelevant variations.
- **Validate Zero-Shot Generalization under Severe Dynamics Shifts:** Empirically demonstrate the method's superiority over existing parameter-centric baselines in challenging scenarios, including unmodeled dynamics parameters, time-varying environments, and structural failures.
- **Improve In-Distribution Stability and Latent Interpretability:** Show that the learned geometry leads to more stable policy performance within the training distribution and produces interpretable latent structures that reflect physical properties.

By pursuing these objectives, the authors aim to move beyond the limitations of pre-specified parameter spaces and foster more flexible and robust adaptation capabilities in DRL agents for real-world robotic applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Latent Dynamics Geometries (LDG), adopts an outcome-centric approach to dynamics adaptation. It integrates contrastive learning into a variational inference framework to discover a geometrically structured latent dynamics manifold.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Framework: Performance Gap Under Dynamics Shift

The paper establishes a theoretical connection between an adaptive policy's performance in a target domain and the properties of its trajectory encoder.

- **Problem Formulation:** Two Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), $M_S$ (source) and $M_T$ (target), share state, action, and reward functions but differ in transition dynamics ($p_S$ vs. $p_T$) due to physical parameter changes. An encoder $E$ maps a history window $w_t$ to a latent context $z_t$, which conditions a policy $\pi(\cdot|s, z)$.
- **Key Definitions:**
 - **$\epsilon_p$-close MDPs:** Target and source dynamics are "close" in total variation distance.
 - **$L_\pi$-smooth policy:** Small changes in latent context $z$ lead to bounded changes in policy output (Total Variation divergence).
 - **Latent Centroid $\mu_t(\pi, p)$:** The expected encoding of a window at time $t$ under policy $\pi$ and dynamics $p$.
 - **$\delta_e$-consistent encoder:** The encoding of any specific window is close to its corresponding latent centroid. This implies local consistency in the latent space.
 - **$L_E$-smooth encoder (Lipschitz constant):** Controls how trajectory-level distribution shifts translate into latent perturbations. A smaller $L_E$ means similar window distributions map to nearby latent regions.
- **Target Domain Regret Bound (Theorem 1):** The paper decomposes the target domain regret (difference between oracle policy in target domain and adaptive policy in target domain) into three terms:
 - **(I) Oracle Difference:** Performance gap between source and target oracle policies (bounded by Simulation Lemma, related to $\epsilon_p$).
 - **(II) Training Error:** How well the adaptive policy approximates the source oracle in the source domain (bounded by $\delta_{train}$).
 - **(III) Policy Adaptation:** The performance gap between the adaptive policy rolled out in the source domain and the target domain. This term is shown to be monotonically increasing with respect to the encoder's $\delta_e$ and $L_E$.
- **Implication:** Minimizing $\delta_e$ (local consistency) and $L_E$ (encoder smoothness) is crucial for reducing target domain regret. A smooth encoder maps trajectories from similar dynamics to proximate latent regions, ensuring bounded changes in latent codes for small dynamics variations, which in turn promotes policy stability.

#### 3.2. Contrastive Learning for Latent Geometry

The paper proposes using contrastive learning to control the latent geometry and thus bound $L_E$.

- **InfoNCE Loss:** The InfoNCE loss (Equation 8) is adopted, which asymptotically decomposes into two components:
 - **Alignment Loss ($L_{align}$):** Encourages positive pairs (samples from the same dynamics regime) to be mapped to nearby features.
 - **Uniformity Loss ($L_{uniform}$):** Encourages features to be uniformly distributed on a unit hypersphere, preventing mode collapse.
- **Upper-bounding $L_E$ (Theorem 2):** Minimizing the Alignment Loss ($L_{align}$) is theoretically shown to upper-bound $L_E$. This is achieved by:
 - **Intra-Cluster Tightness:** $L_{align}$ bounds the variance of latent embeddings for samples within the same dynamics cluster.
 - **Inter-Cluster Closeness (Bridge Samples):** If the supports of two marginally different dynamics distributions overlap (P(S) > $\alpha$), there exist "bridge samples" that link their respective latent clusters, allowing the distance between cluster centroids to be bounded.
- **Nuisance Variable Filtering (Theorem 3):** The InfoNCE loss also encourages the encoder to focus on dynamics-relevant features while being invariant to nuisance variables. If a trajectory window can be factorized into dynamics-relevant features ($\mu$) and nuisance features ($s$), minimizing InfoNCE implies minimizing the Frobenius norm of $\partial E/\partial s$. This ensures the latent space encodes only task-relevant dynamics.
- **Role of $L_{uniform}$:** While $L_{align}$ bounds $L_E$, the Uniformity Loss is critical in practice to prevent the encoder from collapsing all latents to a single point, thereby ensuring a meaningful, globally structured latent space.

#### 3.3. Practical Algorithm (Latent Dynamics Geometries - LDG)

LDG combines a variational inference framework with multi-positive contrastive learning.

- **Variational Autoencoder (VAE) Objective ($L_{VAE}$):** A standard VAE setup is used, where an amortized inference network $q_\phi(z_t|w_t)$ maps history windows $w_t$ to a latent distribution, and a generative decoder $p_\theta(s_{t+1}|s_t, a_t, z_t)$ reconstructs the next state. The objective is to maximize the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO), including a reconstruction loss and a KL divergence term regularizing $q_\phi$ towards a prior $p(z_t) = N(0, I)$.
- **Multi-Positive InfoNCE Loss ($L_{contrast}$):** For a mini-batch of trajectory segments, positive pairs are defined as segments generated under the *same dynamics parameters*. This objective provides three training signals:
 - **Temporal Consistency:** Segments from the same trajectory (and thus same dynamics) are mapped to proximate latent points, minimizing $\delta_e$.
 - **Manifold Continuity:** "Bridge samples" with marginally different dynamics pull similar clusters closer, bounding $L_E$.
 - **Global Structure:** The uniformity component ensures the latent manifold maximally spans the available space, creating organized global structure.
- **Total Training Objective:** The overall loss function is a weighted sum of the reinforcement learning loss ($L_{rl}$), the VAE loss ($L_{VAE}$), and the contrastive loss ($L_{contrast}$): $L = L_{rl} + \lambda_1 L_{VAE} + \lambda_2 L_{contrast}$. The $L_{rl}$ also influences the encoder, promoting task-relevant latent representations.
- **Network Architectures:**
 - **Dynamics Encoder ($q_\phi$):** A Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) processes the trajectory history window, followed by a linear projection to predict latent mean and log-standard deviation.
 - **Dynamics Decoder ($p_\theta$):** A multi-layer perceptron (MLP) takes the latent $z_t$ and current state-action $(s_t, a_t)$ to predict the next state residual. Layer Normalization is applied to latent vectors.
 - **Policy ($\pi_\psi$) and Critic ($Q_\omega$):** MLPs that receive the concatenation of the current state $s_t$ and the latent context $z_t$ as input. Layer Normalization is also applied to the input latent vectors.
- **Training Details:** Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) is used for $L_{rl}$. Latent dropout is applied to enhance policy robustness. An "Anchor Dynamics" sampling strategy is employed during training to stabilize contrastive learning by ensuring fixed dynamics parameters are present in each batch. KL annealing is used for VAE training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The LDG method was evaluated on four MuJoCo continuous-control benchmarks (Hopper, Walker2d, HalfCheetah, Ant) under various dynamics randomization schemes. Comparisons were made against SAC+DR, RMA (Phase 1 - oracle, Phase 2 - learned), SO-CMA (test-time optimization), and a VAE ablation.

#### 4.1. In-Distribution Stability

- **Performance:** LDG achieved the highest mean cumulative reward on Hopper (3239.2) and Walker2d (4883.7) and ranked second on Ant. On HalfCheetah, LDG was outperformed by RMA baselines.
- **Variance:** LDG exhibited substantially lower variance across dynamics parameters compared to RMA (Phase 2) and VAE, suggesting improved stability. This supports the theoretical claim that bounding $L_E$ reduces policy adaptation error.
- **Latent Geometry Visualization (Figure 2):** T-SNE visualizations of the latent space for Walker2d and Ant showed that LDG learns a smooth, monotonic arc where latent coordinates correlate linearly with physical parameters (e.g., mass, damping). In contrast, RMA (Phase 2) produced scattered, unstructured latents, and the VAE ablation suffered from topological disconnectedness or mode collapse. This demonstrates that contrastive learning successfully creates an ordered and continuous manifold, facilitating extrapolation to OOD dynamics. The Uniformity Loss was empirically found to be crucial for separating clusters and avoiding collapse, leading to this continuous arc.

#### 4.2. Zero-Shot Generalization

LDG was tested in three out-of-distribution (OOD) settings:

- **Unmodeled Parameter:** LDG consistently outperformed SAC+DR, RMA (Phase 1 & 2), and VAE for OOD mass and damping scales across most environments (Table 2).
- **Time-Varying Dynamics (Var. Env):** When dynamics parameters shifted every 200 steps within an episode, LDG generally outperformed RMA (Phase 2). LDG's local consistency allows the encoder to treat dynamics switches as transitions between latent clusters, enabling smooth policy adaptation without being constrained to a single global identification.
- **Structural Failure (Struct. Fail.):** In scenarios where an actuator was disabled mid-episode, LDG significantly outperformed all baselines, including test-time optimization (SO-CMA) and RMA. Parameter-centric methods failed catastrophically because a "broken joint" does not correspond to any valid combination of dynamics parameters in their pre-defined latent space. LDG, being outcome-centric, mapped the resulting trajectory pattern to a functionally equivalent, albeit extrapolative, region of its learned manifold (e.g., extremely high damping/mass on that joint), enabling adaptation.

- **Failure Mode Analysis (HalfCheetah):** LDG underperformed baselines on HalfCheetah in Var. Env and Struct. Fail. settings. The authors hypothesize that HalfCheetah's highly reactive gaits, which often require large, immediate force commands, might be over-regularized by LDG's Lipschitz smoothness constraint. The latent space visualizations for HalfCheetah showed less overlapping regions for different dynamics, hindering the formation of "bridge samples" crucial for robust adaptation.
- **Outcome-Centric vs. Parameter-Centric:** Observations that RMA Phase 2 (learned adaptation) often outperformed RMA Phase 1 (oracle parameters) further supported the paper's argument for conditioning on interaction history rather than explicit, static physical parameters.

#### 4.3. Structure Discovery Via Functional Equivalence

- LDG demonstrated the ability to discover the structure of physical parameters (e.g., joint damping) even when they were *not* explicitly randomized during training (Figure 3). This is attributed to functional equivalence; high joint damping and increased mass both manifest as "resistance to motion." The encoder projects unseen damping variations onto a pre-existing "resistance" manifold learned from mass variations. This supports LDG's robustness to unmodeled events by mapping them to known functional coordinates.

#### 4.4. Alternative Regularizer (Spectral Normalization)

- An ablation study compared contrastive learning (CL) with Spectral Normalization (SN), a technique for explicitly bounding Lipschitz constants (Table 3). While SN achieved strong in-distribution performance on Ant, it struggled significantly in OOD and structural failure scenarios. This suggests that while SN enforces mathematical smoothness (low $L_E$), it fails to promote a *meaningful topological structure* in the latent space, which is critical for extrapolation. Contrastive learning's Uniformity Loss, though not theoretically necessary to bound $L_E$, provides this essential global organization. An empirical approximation of $L_E$ (Table D.2) showed LDG achieved a substantially lower $L_E$ than VAE, and SN achieved the lowest, but its lack of structure harmed OOD performance.

#### 4.5. Hyperparameter Sensitivity

- LDG was found to be generally robust to hyperparameters, with sensitivity primarily observed for reward scale, VAE encoder $\beta$, encoder horizon $H$, and latent dimension $d_z$.
- **Latent Dimension ($d_z$):** A trade-off was observed between information bottlenecking and optimization complexity. Too small ($d_z=2$) led to information loss, while too large ($d_z \ge 7$) introduced sparsity that destabilized training in some environments. Optimal performance was found at intermediate dimensions (e.g., $d_z=3$ for Walker2d, $d_z=5$ for Ant).
- **Temporal Context Horizon ($H$):** Extremely short horizons ($H=10$) provided insufficient context, leading to volatile training. Excessively long horizons ($H=70$) diluted the importance of recent transitions. An intermediate horizon ($H=50$) consistently yielded the best performance, balancing context accumulation and reactivity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Latent Dynamics Geometries (LDG) as a novel and principled framework for robust zero-shot policy adaptation in reinforcement learning. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Shift in Adaptation Paradigm:** By moving from a "parameter-centric" to an "outcome-centric" view, LDG addresses a fundamental limitation of many existing methods. This shift allows policies to learn directly from the *manifestations* of dynamics in interaction outcomes, rather than relying on explicit knowledge or inference of underlying physical parameters. This is particularly impactful for real-world robotics where physical parameters might be unknown, unmodeled, or constantly changing.
- **Theoretical Grounding for Representation Learning in RL:** The work provides a formal theoretical link between the Lipschitz smoothness of a trajectory encoder ($L_E$) and the regret in target-domain adaptation. This theoretical foundation offers a principled guideline for designing robust representation learning objectives in RL, emphasizing the importance of controllable latent geometry.
- **Enhanced Zero-Shot Generalization:** LDG demonstrates strong empirical performance in zero-shot adaptation across a range of challenging dynamics shifts, including unmodeled parameters, time-varying environments, and catastrophic structural failures. Its ability to adapt to structural failures, where parameter-centric methods fail, is a notable achievement with significant implications for robot resilience.
- **Interpretability and Functional Equivalence:** The method generates a smooth and interpretable latent dynamics manifold where nearby points correspond to functionally similar dynamics. The discovery of functional equivalences (e.g., high damping behaving like increased mass) allows the system to generalize to entirely unseen physical properties if they induce similar observable effects, enhancing the adaptability to novel situations.
- **Leveraging Contrastive Learning for Robustness:** The effective integration of contrastive learning to simultaneously enforce local consistency ($\delta_e$) and global topological structure (bounding $L_E$ and promoting uniformity) in the latent space provides a practical and effective mechanism for creating robust representations. The ablation study against Spectral Normalization highlights that mere mathematical smoothness is insufficient; meaningful *structure* is crucial for OOD generalization.
- **Broader Impact on Real-World Robotics:** The ability of policies to adapt to unforeseen and complex dynamics changes without requiring extensive retraining or explicit system identification could significantly accelerate the deployment of DRL agents in real-world robotic applications, reducing development costs and increasing system autonomy and safety.

**Future Work:**
The authors identify several promising avenues for future research, including:
- **Adaptive Smoothness Constraints:** Investigating mechanisms to adaptively relax smoothness constraints for highly reactive tasks (e.g., HalfCheetah) where over-regularization might hinder optimal performance.
- **Fully Unsupervised Settings:** Moving towards a fully unsupervised framework that removes the need to explicitly determine whether dynamics parameters are shared across samples during contrastive learning.
- **Extension to More Complex Domains:** Applying LDG to more complex scenarios, such as interactive tasks with external objects or human collaboration, where dynamic environments are the norm.

Overall, LDG represents a substantial contribution to building more robust and generalizable reinforcement learning systems for dynamic real-world environments.
