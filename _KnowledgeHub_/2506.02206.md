# Reinforcement Learning with Data Bootstrapping for Dynamic Subgoal Pursuit in Humanoid Robot Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> A hierarchical framework enables humanoid robots to navigate cluttered environments by integrating a high-level reinforcement learning planner for dynamic subgoals with a low-level Model Predictive Control for stable gait generation, augmented with data bootstrapping. This system demonstrated superior navigation success rates (93.3% in seen, 89.3% in unseen environments) and more efficient goal-reaching compared to previous model-based and learning-based methods in simulation.

## Problem

- Humanoid robot navigation in complex, cluttered environments faces challenges in balancing computational efficiency with locomotion precision.
- High-dimensional, underactuated dynamics of bipedal robots make stable navigation difficult, with discrepancies between planned and executed paths potentially leading to instability.
- Existing model-based methods struggle with integrating detailed environmental information for real-time computation, while learning-based methods suffer from sample inefficiency and generalization issues for complex bipedal systems.

## Method

- A hierarchical framework is proposed, featuring a high-level ==Reinforcement Learning (RL)== planner that generates dynamic subgoals based on local occupancy maps and robot state.
- A low-level Linear Inverted Pendulum (LIP)-based Model Predictive Control (MPC) controller translates these subgoals into dynamically stable walking gaits.
- A data bootstrapping technique utilizes an expert RRT-LMPC to pre-populate the RL replay buffer, progressively reducing reliance on expert data during training to improve sample efficiency and stability.

## Results

- The proposed "Subgoal Pursuit" method achieved the highest success rates (**93.3%** in testing-seen, **89.3%** in testing-unseen environments) and highest accumulated rewards among all compared methods.
- It demonstrated superior performance over baseline model-based methods (e.g., LMPC, RRT+LMPC) and other learning-based methods (PPO, SAC without demonstration, CQL) in terms of navigation efficiency and goal-reaching time.
- The RL high-level planner operated in real-time (**1.3** ± **0.5** ms), ensuring compatibility with the low-level MPC's real-time gait generation, enabling robust navigation and generalization to unseen environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining high-level RL for complex environmental decision-making with low-level MPC for dynamic stability effectively addresses the trade-off between computational efficiency and locomotion precision in humanoid navigation.
> - Data bootstrapping from a model-based expert significantly accelerates and stabilizes RL training for complex continuous control tasks, mitigating sample inefficiency without sacrificing adaptability.
> - The proposed reward function and comprehensive state representation for the RL planner enable the robot to learn robust strategies for goal pursuit and obstacle avoidance in cluttered scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robot navigation in complex and cluttered environments is a fundamental yet challenging area of robotics research. The broader landscape involves developing methods that enable bipedal robots to move safely and efficiently while maintaining dynamic stability, despite their underactuated and high-dimensional dynamics. Current navigation frameworks often compartmentalize geometric path planning and real-time locomotion control, which can limit their adaptability and lead to discrepancies between planned and executed paths, particularly for underactuated systems prone to instability.

Traditional model-based methods in bipedal locomotion control formulate and solve constrained optimization problems, often using Model Predictive Control (MPC) or Quadratic Programming (QP) with simplified center-of-mass (CoM) or foot-placement models. While these methods have improved motion performance and stability, they face challenges in integrating high-dimensional environmental information (e.g., occupancy maps) due to increased state space and constraints, which can hinder real-time computation. Nonlinear coupling of states further complicates these optimization processes. For example, the authors refer to their previous work which precomputed heading angles to mitigate nonlinearities but still relied on a geometric approach.

More recent advancements in learning-based methods, particularly Reinforcement Learning (RL), offer an alternative approach by enabling robots to learn navigation strategies through trial and error. RL has shown promise in adaptive decision-making and motion planning, handling nonlinearities and high-dimensional control problems. However, existing RL navigation frameworks are often designed for robots with simpler kinematics (e.g., wheeled robots) and suffer from sample inefficiency. Training from scratch without leveraging prior knowledge of robotic dynamics or the environment can be challenging, especially in continuous action spaces with sparse or delayed reward signals, potentially leading to suboptimal solutions. Imitation learning, another learning-based approach, depends heavily on high-quality expert data and lacks active environmental interaction, limiting its generalization capabilities.

This work positions itself as a bridge between these two paradigms. It addresses the limitations of model-based methods (computational load with complex environments, difficulty with nonlinearities) and learning-based methods (sample inefficiency, generalization issues for complex bipedal systems) by proposing a hierarchical framework. This framework combines the adaptability of RL for high-level decision-making in complex environments with the stability and predictive control of MPC for low-level gait generation. Furthermore, it incorporates a data bootstrapping technique to enhance the efficiency and stability of RL training, leveraging existing model-based knowledge to overcome the sample inefficiency of learning from scratch.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust, safe, and real-time navigation framework for humanoid robots operating in cluttered environments. This overarching goal is motivated by several identified challenges and limitations of existing approaches:

1. **Balancing Computational Efficiency and Locomotion Precision:** Humanoid robots require precise control for stable locomotion, but integrating detailed environmental data into this control often leads to computationally intensive problems that are not suitable for real-time operation. Current frameworks struggle to balance these two critical aspects. The motivation is to create a system that can accommodate the nonlinear dynamics of bipedal robots while maintaining real-time performance.
2. **Addressing High-Dimensional and Underactuated Dynamics:** Bipedal robots possess high-dimensional, underactuated dynamics, making navigation inherently difficult. Discrepancies between planned paths and executed trajectories, caused by model inaccuracies, disturbances, or dynamic constraints, can critically impact stability. A key motivation is to develop a method that can inherently account for these dynamics to prevent instability or falls.
3. **Limitations of Model-Based Approaches:** Existing model-based methods, while effective for locomotion control, face significant hurdles when tasked with high-dimensional environmental navigation. Integrating occupancy maps into optimization problems increases the state space and constraints, slowing down real-time computation. The nonlinear coupling of robot states further complicates these methods. The paper aims to overcome these limitations by offloading complex environmental decision-making to a learning agent.
4. **Limitations of Learning-Based Approaches for Bipedal Robots:** While learning-based methods, particularly RL, show promise in handling nonlinearities, they are often tailored for robots with simpler kinematics and suffer from sample inefficiency. Training complex bipedal robots from scratch via RL can be time-consuming and prone to converging to suboptimal solutions due to sparse rewards and continuous action spaces. The motivation here is to enhance RL training for bipedal navigation, making it more practical and robust.
5. **Synergizing Strengths of RL and MPC:** The research is motivated by the potential benefits of combining the adaptive decision-making capabilities of RL with the predictive stability and constraint handling of MPC. This synergy is hypothesized to mitigate risks of instability in challenging scenarios by allowing RL to handle complex environment interaction at a high level, while MPC ensures dynamic stability at a low level.
6. **Expediting and Stabilizing RL Training:** To address the sample inefficiency challenge of RL, a specific motivation is to incorporate a data bootstrapping technique. By leveraging data from an existing model-based navigation approach, the goal is to provide a diverse and informative starting point for RL, thereby accelerating the learning process, stabilizing training, and enhancing the robustness of learned policies.

In summary, the key objective is to enable safe, efficient, and reliable humanoid robot navigation in cluttered environments by developing a hierarchical RL-MPC framework that explicitly addresses the computational, dynamic, and learning efficiency challenges inherent in this domain, bolstered by a data bootstrapping strategy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is a novel hierarchical framework designed for robust bipedal navigation and obstacle avoidance. It consists of two main levels: a high-level Reinforcement Learning (RL) planner and a low-level Model Predictive Control (MPC) based gait planner, further augmented by a data bootstrapping technique for efficient training.

**A. High-Level Reinforcement Learning for Dynamic Subgoal Planner**

The high-level planner is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) to learn dynamic subgoals.

- **State Space (S):** The robot's state `s` is comprehensive, including both environmental perception and locomotion dynamics.
 - **Occupancy Map (O):** A 64x64 local occupancy grid map, centered on the robot, covers a 4.5m forward, 1.5m backward, and 6m lateral range. This provides structured local obstacle information.
 - **Robot CoM State:** The robot's Center of Mass (CoM) position (`px`, `py`), velocity (`vx`, `vy`), and heading angle (`θ`) in the world frame are included.
 - **Stance Foot (il):** Indicates whether the left (-1) or right (1) foot is currently supporting.
 - **Goal Information:** Euclidean distance (`dg`) and relative heading (`δθg`) to the global goal.
 - **Obstacle Information:** Distance to the nearest obstacle (`do`) in the local map.
 - **Global Goal Position (G):** The ultimate goal position in the world frame.

- **Action Space (A):** The high-level planner outputs a dynamic subgoal `a` in a robot-centric polar coordinate system.
 - **Subgoal Definition:** `a = (dc, ϕc)`, where `dc` is the distance to the subgoal and `ϕc` is the direction relative to the robot's current heading.
 - **Constraints:** `dc` is limited to meters, and `ϕc` is limited to [−π/4, π/4] radians, to ensure compatibility with the low-level controller and the robot's physical capabilities.
 - **RL Algorithm:** The Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) algorithm is employed due to its effectiveness in continuous action spaces and its entropy-regularized objective, which balances optimality and exploration. The policy `π(a|s)` is modeled as a Gaussian distribution.

- **Network Structure:**
 - **Actor Network:** Processes the heterogeneous state input. A Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) handles the occupancy map (`O`), consisting of three convolutional layers, ReLU activations, and max pooling, followed by two fully connected (FC) layers reducing features to 64 dimensions. These 64-dimensional features are then concatenated with the other state variables and fed into four additional FC layers to output the mean and standard deviation of the Gaussian action distribution.
 - **Critic Network:** Has a similar architecture but outputs a single scalar value representing the state-action value.

- **Reward Function:** A weighted sum of sub-rewards encourages goal-directed movement, obstacle avoidance, and smooth locomotion, along with terminal rewards.
 - **Goal Proximity Reward (rg):** Rewards reduction in distance to the goal.
 - **Heading Alignment Reward (rθ):** Penalizes deviations from the goal heading using a cubic function.
 - **Action Smoothness Reward (ra):** Encourages forward progress and penalizes large changes in consecutive actions.
 - **Velocity Reward (rv):** Incentivizes desired longitudinal velocity and discourages lateral movement.
 - **Collision Avoidance Reward (ro):** Rewards increasing distance from obstacles, inspired by control barrier functions.
 - **Terminal Reward (RF):** Provides a large positive reward for reaching the goal (with an exponential decay for faster completion), a significant penalty for failure (collision/fall), and a slightly smaller penalty for timeout.

**B. LIP-based Linear MPC for Stable Gait Planning**

The low-level MPC controller receives a subgoal from the high-level RL planner and generates dynamically stable walking gaits.

- **Robot Model:** The Linear Inverted Pendulum (LIP) model is used to approximate the CoM dynamics in the x-y plane, assuming constant CoM height and centroidal momentum. The step-to-step dynamics are treated as discrete.
- **State Vector (x):** `[px, vx, py, vy, θ]^T`, representing CoM position, velocity, and heading angle.
- **Control Inputs (u):** Swing foot stepping positions (`fx`, `fy`) and turning rate (`ω`).
- **Dynamics:** A discrete-time linear control system `x_k+1 = A_L * x_k + B_L * u_k`.
- **Optimization Problem:** An LIP-based MPC (LIP-MPC) formulates an optimal control problem over a prediction horizon `N` (set to 3 steps).
 - **Cost Function:** A quadratic cost minimizes the Euclidean distance between the robot's CoM and the received subgoal (`cx`, `cy`) at each prediction step.
 - **Constraints:** Kinematic and path constraints (velocity, reachability, maneuverability) are included to ensure feasible and safe motions. These constraints, inherently nonlinear in local coordinates, are linearized by relating the subgoal direction `ϕc` to the turning rate `ωk` within the MPC.
 - **Solver:** The problem reduces to a Linear Constraints Quadratic Problem (LCQP), allowing for real-time solution at 20 Hz or faster update frequencies.
- **Parameters:** Desired CoM height `H = 1m`, step duration `T = 0.4s`.

**C. Policy Learning with Data Bootstrapping**

To enhance learning efficiency and overcome sample inefficiency in RL, a data bootstrapping approach is used.

- **Training Environment:** The Agility Robotics Digit humanoid robot is simulated in MuJoCo. Training occurs across 50 randomly generated environments, each 10x10m with varying numbers (0, 1, 2, 6, 8) and types (circles, ellipses, polygons) of obstacles.
- **Demonstration Dataset Generation:** An "expert" navigation approach, RRT-LMPC (a linear LIP-MPC augmented with a Rapidly-exploring Random Tree for global exploration), is used to generate an offline dataset. This dataset comprises 10,000 state-action transitions from various training environments.
- **Bootstrapping Procedure:**
 - **Initialization:** The RL replay buffer is initially populated with samples from this offline demonstration dataset.
 - **Early Training:** During the early phase, 80% of samples drawn for training batches come from the offline dataset, and 20% from online interactions with the simulation.
 - **Progressive Reduction:** As training progresses over 10,000 episodes, the reliance on the demonstration dataset is gradually reduced until the replay buffer is entirely composed of online samples.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the proposed hierarchical framework with data bootstrapping was evaluated in simulation using the Agility Robotics Digit humanoid robot. Performance was assessed in both "testing-seen" environments (used during training) and "testing-unseen" environments (newly generated scenarios with maximum difficulty: 8 obstacles).

**A. Comparisons against Baseline Model-Based Approaches**

The proposed "Subgoal Pursuit" method was compared against three model-based baselines: a model-based nonlinear LIP-MPC controller, a linear LIP-MPC controller (LMPC), and the RRT-LMPC (the expert used for data bootstrapping).

- **Real-Time Computation:**
 - The nonlinear LIP-MPC exhibited an average computation time of 110 ± 57 ms per optimization, which is insufficient for the required real-time update frequency of 20 Hz for stable walking.
 - The linear LIP-MPC (LMPC) achieved an efficient computation time of 0.94 ± 0.07 ms, enabling real-time reactive gait generation.
 - The high-level RL planner of the proposed method computed a subgoal in 1.3 ± 0.5 ms, which is adequate for its chosen update frequency of 2.5 Hz (once per walking step).

- **Navigation Performance (Testing-Seen Environments, Table III, Fig. 4):**
 - **LMPC:** Achieved the lowest success rate (84%), lowest accumulated reward (91.06 ± 0.0), and the slowest average goal-reaching time (Time Ratio of 1.17 relative to the proposed method). Its conservative nature and limited maneuverability often led to timeouts or local minima.
 - **RRT+LMPC:** Demonstrated improved success rate (90 ± 1.4%) and accumulated reward (96.11 ± 3.64), with a faster time ratio (1.02). The global RRT planner enhanced flexibility, but its inherent randomness and lack of dynamic consideration sometimes resulted in overly aggressive or unnecessarily long subgoals.
 - **Proposed Subgoal Pursuit:** Achieved the highest success rate (93.3 ± 2.1%) and accumulated reward (101.07 ± 2.41), and the fastest average goal-reaching time (Time Ratio of 1). The method effectively regulated walking speed and turning rate, leading to more efficient trajectories.

The findings indicate that the data-bootstrapped RL approach, despite relying only on local map data unlike the global map used by RRT, outperformed the original model-based methods. This suggests the effectiveness of RL in learning robust navigation strategies for complex bipedal dynamics, even with a slightly higher variance due to policy stochasticity.

**B. Comparison against Other Learning-Based Methods**

The proposed SAC with demonstration method was compared against Conservative Q-Learning (CQL), Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), and standard Soft Actor-Critic (SAC).

- **Training Performance (Fig. 5):**
 - **PPO:** Showed consistent underperformance, attributed to its on-policy nature and limited sample efficiency for complex tasks.
 - **SAC (without demonstration):** Its training performance was sensitive to the quality of collected transitions. In scenarios where the agent rarely reached the goal, SAC often exhibited circular wandering behavior leading to timeouts. While its episodic reward could converge comparably to CQL when good transitions were available, its average success rate remained low.
 - **CQL:** Benefited from its offline RL formulation and efficient use of demonstration data, leading to faster initial improvements in both episodic reward and success rate. However, its lack of continuous environmental interaction during training hindered its adaptability to dynamic changes, occasionally leading to obstacle collisions.
 - **SAC with demonstration (Proposed):** Outperformed all other baselines by leveraging both demonstration bootstrapping and interactive exploration. This combination directed the agent toward promising strategies early on, reduced reliance on random exploration, avoided suboptimal behaviors, accelerated learning, and stabilized the training process, resulting in improved performance.

- **Testing Performance (Table IV - Testing-Seen, Table V - Testing-Unseen):**
 - **PPO:** Achieved 0% success rate in both testing-seen and testing-unseen environments, consistently yielding low negative accumulated rewards.
 - **SAC (without demonstration):** Demonstrated low success rates (9% in testing-seen, 5% in testing-unseen) and negative accumulated rewards, performing only marginally better than PPO.
 - **CQL:** Showed better performance than PPO and SAC, with success rates of 44.3% in testing-seen and 14% in testing-unseen. While its accumulated rewards were lower than the proposed method, it achieved the fastest average goal-reaching time when successful (Time Ratio 0.91-0.92). However, its generalization to unseen environments was limited.
 - **SAC with demonstration (Proposed):** Achieved the highest success rates (93.3% in testing-seen, 89.3% in testing-unseen) and highest accumulated rewards (101.07 ± 2.41 in seen, 101.47 ± 4.38 in unseen) across both testing environments. This indicates superior overall performance and generalization capabilities compared to the other learning-based methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The proposed Subgoal-Pursuit method represents a significant contribution to humanoid robot navigation by introducing a novel hierarchical framework that effectively combines the strengths of reinforcement learning and model predictive control, enhanced by a data bootstrapping technique.

### 6. Significance of Contributions

- **Bridging the Gap:** The work successfully addresses the fundamental challenge of balancing computational efficiency with the precision required for stable bipedal locomotion. It provides a viable approach to integrate complex environmental information into robot navigation without being bogged down by the computational costs often associated with fully model-based optimization for high-dimensional systems.
- **Enhanced Navigation Capabilities:** By using an RL-based high-level planner for dynamic subgoal generation, the framework enables the robot to adapt to cluttered and potentially unpredictable environments. The low-level MPC ensures dynamic stability by generating robust walking gaits that adhere to physical constraints, mitigating the risk of falls or instability inherent in underactuated bipedal robots.
- **Efficient Learning:** The incorporation of a data bootstrapping technique, leveraging expert demonstrations from a model-based approach, significantly improves the sample efficiency and stability of RL training. This addresses a common limitation of RL, particularly for complex continuous control tasks like bipedal navigation, by providing a foundation for faster convergence and more robust policies.
- **Demonstrated Superiority:** The simulation results confirm that the proposed method surpasses both traditional model-based approaches and other state-of-the-art learning-based methods in terms of navigation success rates, accumulated rewards, and goal-reaching efficiency. Its ability to generalize to unseen environments also highlights its robustness.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Advancement in Humanoid Robotics:** This research can significantly advance the practical deployment of humanoid robots in real-world scenarios such as logistics, public services, and exploration. By enabling safe, efficient, and reliable navigation in human-centric environments, it expands the range of tasks these robots can perform autonomously.
- **Hybrid Control Architectures:** The framework serves as a model for integrating learning-based and model-based control strategies in other complex robotic systems. It illustrates how hierarchical designs can decompose challenging problems, allowing each layer to leverage its inherent strengths (e.g., RL for high-level decision-making, MPC for low-level dynamics).
- **Reinforcement Learning Applications:** The successful application of data bootstrapping with SAC for bipedal navigation provides valuable insights for improving RL training in other challenging robotic control domains. It demonstrates a practical approach to mitigating the "cold start" problem and sample inefficiency in complex continuous action spaces.
- **Future Research Directions:** The work lays the groundwork for further enhancements in trajectory optimization and generalization. Future efforts could focus on validating the approach on physical hardware, addressing navigation in truly dynamic and completely unseen environments, and refining the learning framework to incorporate more explicit safety guarantees.

In conclusion, this research presents a robust and effective solution for humanoid robot navigation, with implications for both the fundamental understanding of complex robot control and the practical deployment of advanced robotic systems.
