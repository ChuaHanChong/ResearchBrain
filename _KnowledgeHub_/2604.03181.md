# Multi-View Video Diffusion Policy: A 3D Spatio-Temporal-Aware Video Action Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Beihang University, Tsinghua University, and collaborators developed the Multi-View Video Diffusion Policy (MV-VDP) for robotic manipulation, which integrates 3D structural priors and explicit temporal dynamics using a multi-view video diffusion model. This approach achieved an average success rate of 89.1% on Meta-World tasks with five demonstrations and 57.1% on real-world tasks with ten demonstrations, outperforming existing methods and enabling safer deployment through interpretable video predictions.

## Problem

- Existing robotic policies often rely on 2D visual observations, leading to a mismatch with 3D physical actions and limiting generalization under low-data regimes.
- Many current approaches use backbones pretrained on static image-text pairs, failing to explicitly model temporal dynamics and anticipate environmental changes.
- Contemporary Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often require large-scale datasets, hindering data efficiency for complex manipulation skills.

## Method

- The **MV-VDP** (==Multi-View Video Diffusion Policy==) leverages ==3D-aware multi-view projections== to implicitly encode 3D spatial structure from point clouds and robot end-effector poses.
- A ==multi-view video diffusion transformer==, built upon a 5B-parameter video foundation model (Wan2.2), jointly predicts future multi-view RGB videos and robot ==end-effector heatmap sequences==.
- Actions are decoded from the predicted multi-view heatmaps (for position) and ==denoised latent representations== (for rotation and gripper state) to form ==continuous action chunks==.

## Results

- **MV-VDP** achieved an average success rate of **89.1%** on seven Meta-World tasks with only five demonstrations per task, outperforming baselines like Diffusion Policy (**37.7%**).
- On real-world basic manipulation tasks, it demonstrated a **57.1%** average success rate with ten expert trajectories, surpassing DP3 (**0%**), UVA (**5.7%**), and BridgeVLA (**41.42%**).
- The model exhibited robustness to hyperparameter changes and achieved comparable performance with significantly fewer denoising steps (e.g., one step for ~**89%** success rate), enabling 5Hz inference speed, and reduced collision events to zero during human-validated deployments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling both 3D spatial structure and temporal dynamics through a multi-view video diffusion framework significantly improves data efficiency and generalization in robotic manipulation.
> - Aligning the representation format of large-scale video pretraining with action fine-tuning via multi-view projections and heatmap representations helps bridge the perception-control gap.
> - Jointly predicting future visual observations alongside actions provides a mechanism for action validation and enhances interpretability and safety during robot deployment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen advancements through large-scale representation learning, including behavioral cloning, diffusion-based policies, and Vision–Language–Action (VLA) models. These approaches have demonstrated performance across various tasks and embodiments. However, the authors identify a general limitation: most existing policies do not possess a unified understanding of the environment's 3D spatio-temporal state, encompassing both the underlying 3D structure and its evolution over time.

This limitation is attributed to two common design choices. First, many methods primarily rely on 2D visual observations, failing to explicitly model 3D geometric structure while actions are executed in a 3D physical space. This mismatch between observation and action often necessitates large amounts of training data and can hinder generalization under limited demonstration regimes. Second, many current approaches utilize backbones pretrained on static image-text pairs. Such pretraining is not inherently designed to capture temporal dynamics, which limits a policy's ability to anticipate future environmental states. The authors contrast this with cognitive science insights, which suggest that effective action selection involves anticipating environmental changes in response to actions.

This work addresses these identified gaps by proposing a policy that explicitly models both 3D spatial structure and temporal dynamics. It builds upon existing paradigms such as video prediction for manipulation (distinguished by its implicit 3D priors and alignment with video pretraining), 3D structural priors (distinguished by using a video foundation model and predicting continuous actions/future observations), and VLA models (distinguished by integrating explicit dynamics modeling and 3D information for enhanced data efficiency). By leveraging a multi-view video diffusion approach with a video foundation model, the research aims to bridge the perception-control gap and enable more data-efficient, robust, generalizable, and interpretable manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to design a spatio-temporal-aware manipulation policy that integrates 3D structural priors while explicitly modeling environmental dynamics. This overarching goal is driven by the identified limitations of existing robotic manipulation policies:

- **Addressing the 2D Observation-3D Action Mismatch:** Current policies often rely on 2D visual observations, which may not adequately capture the 3D geometric structure of the environment where robot actions take place. This discrepancy can lead to a substantial learning gap, particularly in low-data regimes, and restrict generalization. A key motivation is to implicitly encode 3D spatial structure to reduce this gap.
- **Incorporating Temporal Dynamics:** Many state-of-the-art models utilize backbones pretrained on static image-text data, which are not designed to understand how scenes evolve over time. This limits a robot's ability to anticipate the consequences of its actions. The research aims to explicitly model environmental dynamics to enable anticipatory control, drawing inspiration from cognitive science.
- **Improving Data Efficiency:** The need for large-scale datasets in many contemporary VLA models is a practical barrier. By aligning the representation format of video pretraining with action finetuning and integrating spatio-temporal understanding, the authors aim to significantly reduce the data requirements for learning complex manipulation skills.
- **Enhancing Robustness and Generalization:** Policies that lack a comprehensive understanding of 3D spatio-temporal states may struggle with variations in the environment, such as changes in background, object properties, or lighting. A goal is to develop a policy that demonstrates strong robustness to hyperparameters and generalizes effectively to out-of-distribution settings.
- **Enabling Interpretability:** Understanding why a robot takes a particular action can be challenging with abstract numerical action representations. By simultaneously predicting future visual observations alongside actions, the research aims to provide an interpretable preview of action consequences, facilitating safer and more reliable deployment.

To achieve these objectives, the core idea is to align the representation format of video pretraining with action finetuning. This involves using 3D-aware multi-view projections, representing robot states and actions as multi-view heatmaps, and employing a unified video foundation model to jointly predict future RGB sequences and heatmap sequences.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Multi-View Video Diffusion Policy (MV-VDP) is structured into three main components: a multi-view projection module, a multi-view video diffusion transformer, and an action decoding procedure.

#### 3.1. Multi-View Projection

The MV-VDP takes cropped colored point clouds and the robot end-effector pose as initial inputs.
- **Point Cloud to Multi-View Images:** The cropped point clouds are projected onto three fixed, orthographic 2D views. This projection implicitly encodes 3D spatial structure. The projection process involves computing depth and pixel coordinates for each point, applying Z-ordering to select the closest point for each pixel, and using screen-space splatting to reduce visual noise by modeling points as discs.
- **End-Effector Pose to Multi-View Heatmaps:** The robot end-effector pose is transformed into multiple heatmaps, one for each projection view. This is achieved by identifying the end-effector's pixel location on each view and generating a Gaussian heatmap centered at these locations. These heatmaps are then colorized to have 3 RGB channels, allowing them to be processed by the VAE encoder of the Video Foundation Model (VFM), thereby reducing the representation gap and leveraging pretrained knowledge.

#### 3.2. Multi-View Video Diffusion

The core of MV-VDP is a multi-view video diffusion transformer, built upon Wan2.2, a 5B-parameter video foundation model pretrained on single-view video datasets.
- **Architecture Adaptation:** To extend Wan2.2 to the multi-view setting, each Diffusion Transformer (DiT) block is augmented with a view-attention module.
- **Input Processing:** Input multi-view RGB images and multi-view heatmap images are encoded into latent representations using a pretrained 3D VAE encoder. This VAE performs both spatial (H x W to H/8 x W/8) and temporal (1+T frames to [1+T/4] frames) compression. The resulting latents are concatenated along the view dimension, then patchified and flattened across spatial and temporal dimensions, forming token sequences.
- **Cross-View Interaction:** For the view-attention module, token sequences are reshaped to enable explicit interactions across views at each timestep. After view attention, tokens are reshaped back and processed by standard transformer layers, minimizing disruption to pretrained knowledge.
- **Diffusion Process:** The transformer is trained to predict the noise added to latent representations, enabling multi-step denoising to recover clean latents. This process predicts the velocity between latent frames using flow matching. The denoised latents are then decoded by the VAE decoder to generate future multi-view RGB videos and heatmap videos.
- **Training Loss:** The multi-view video diffusion transformer is trained with an MSE loss to predict the injected Gaussian noise. The total loss is a weighted sum of the diffusion loss for video sequences ($L_{vid}$) and heatmap sequences ($L_{heat}$), with a weighting parameter $\lambda$. LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) fine-tuning is used for computational efficiency.

#### 3.3. Action Decoding

Following the generation of predicted heatmap sequences and denoised latent representations:
- **Position Prediction:** The peak locations of the three predicted heatmaps at each timestep are back-projected into a 3D position in the workspace using known camera parameters. This recovers a continuous 3D end-effector trajectory.
- **Rotation and Gripper Prediction:** A separate, lightweight 170M rotation and gripper predictor module takes the denoised latent representations as input. These latents are temporally upsampled. Global features from the entire latent representation and local features centered around heatmap peak locations are extracted using convolutional networks. These features are fused and aggregated along the view dimension. Cross-attention is applied between predicted latents and a conditioning latent (from the first frame) to incorporate conditioning information. A four-layer transformer encodes these representations, followed by two separate MLP heads to predict discretized Euler angles for rotation (72 bins) and a binary variable for gripper state.
- **Action Chunks:** The predicted positions, rotations, and gripper states are combined to form continuous action chunks, which are then executed by the robot controller.

#### 3.4. Training & Inference

- **Training:** SE(3) augmentations are applied to point clouds and end-effector poses. The multi-view RGB images and heatmaps are encoded by a shared VAE and concatenated. Gaussian noise is added to future frame latents, and the diffusion transformer predicts this noise. The rotation and gripper predictor uses ground-truth latents (with minor noise injection) and is trained with cross-entropy loss on discretized rotation and gripper states.
- **Inference:** The current point cloud and end-effector pose are projected and encoded to obtain a conditioning latent. Future frame latents are initialized with random noise and iteratively denoised by the multi-view diffusion transformer, keeping the conditioning latent fixed. Predicted latents are decoded in two parallel branches: one for 3D position recovery from heatmaps, and another for rotation and gripper state estimation. The combined action chunks are then executed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on both the Meta-World simulation benchmark and real-world robotic platforms, assessing data efficiency, robustness, generalization, and interpretability.

#### 4.1. Meta-World Experiments (Q1)

- **Low-Data Regime Performance:** On seven Meta-World tasks with only five demonstrations per task, MV-VDP achieved an average success rate of 89.1%. This significantly outperformed behavioral cloning methods (BC-Scratch: 26.2%, BC-R3M: 35.4%, Diffusion Policy: 37.7%) and prior video-prediction-based methods (UniPi: 11.4%, AVDC: 58.9%, Track2Act: 67.4%, DreamZero: 61.1%).
- **Comparison with Baselines:** MV-VDP surpassed all baselines on five out of seven tasks. The results indicate that video prediction is beneficial for data-efficient learning and that MV-VDP's multi-view video diffusion with a video foundation model is more effective than other video-prediction approaches. The comparison with DreamZero, which uses the same video foundation model but lacks 3D-aware multi-view prediction, highlights the critical role of the latter.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments (Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4)

- **Basic Task Performance:** On three basic real-world manipulation tasks (Put Lion, Push-T, Scoop Tortilla) with ten expert trajectories, MV-VDP achieved a 57.1% average success rate. It outperformed DP3 (0%), π0.5 (1.4%), and UVA (5.7%). BridgeVLA was the strongest competitor (41.42%).
 - **DP3 (3D-based):** DP3 failed completely, demonstrating overfitting and struggles with real-world sensor noise and variability due to its MLP-based point cloud encoder. This addresses Q2.
 - **π0.5 (VLA):** π0.5 exhibited limited spatial generalization, failing on tasks where the target was outside the training distribution, or executing partially successful actions (e.g., scooping but failing to place). This addresses Q3.
 - **UVA (Video-prediction):** UVA showed reasonable action tendencies but lacked execution accuracy, leading to errors like premature gripper closure or over-pushing. This addresses Q1.
 - **BridgeVLA (3D VLA):** BridgeVLA, which uses multi-view images and heatmap prediction like MV-VDP, performed well on Put Lion but struggled with Push-T and Scoop Tortilla. Its reliance on a static image-text pretrained VLM and prediction of a single key pose limited its ability to handle continuous actions and complex contact-rich tasks. This contrasts with MV-VDP's video foundation model, which predicts continuous action chunks and future video frames, highlighting the advantage of video foundation models over vision-language models for continuous action prediction, further addressing Q2 and Q3.
- **Generalization to Unseen Tasks:** MV-VDP demonstrated strong generalization on four unseen tasks (Put-B, Put-H, Push-L, Scoop-C), achieving the best performance on Push-L and Scoop-C. On Put-B and Put-H, BridgeVLA slightly outperformed MV-VDP, potentially due to its larger-scale image-text pretraining. This addresses Q4.
- **Failure Analysis:** The primary failure mode of MV-VDP was attributed to inaccurate action decoding, such as scraping instead of lifting in the Scoop Tortilla task, potentially due to the finite resolution of heatmaps (256x256 pixels, approx. 4mm/pixel).

#### 4.3. Robustness Analysis (Q5)

- **Hyperparameter Robustness:** MV-VDP demonstrated robustness to variations in the RGB loss weight (λ) and heatmap standard deviation (σ). Varying λ by 80% resulted in only a 3.3% change in success rate, and varying σ by 133% resulted in a 2.5% change on Meta-World.
- **Denoising Steps:** Critically, MV-VDP achieved comparable success rates (around 89%) even with a single denoising step, in contrast to typical diffusion processes requiring 50 steps. This is attributed to the simpler distribution modes of heatmaps and the reliance on peak locations for action prediction, making overall heatmap quality less critical. A recommendation of 5 denoising steps balances performance and computational efficiency, allowing 5Hz inference.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies (Q6)

- **LoRA vs. Full Fine-tuning:** LoRA fine-tuning (89.1%) achieved comparable performance to full fine-tuning (87.4%) with significantly lower computational overhead.
- **View Concatenation:** Concatenating heatmap and image sequences along the view dimension (89.1%) outperformed channel concatenation (81.1%), indicating better preservation of multi-view information.
- **Joint Video Prediction:** Training the model to predict only future heatmap sequences (61.1%) resulted in a significant performance drop compared to joint video and heatmap prediction (89.1%), emphasizing the importance of modeling temporal evolution through video prediction.
- **Pretrained Weights:** Removing pretrained weights from the video foundation model led to near-complete failure (4.6%), underscoring the crucial role of large-scale video pretraining for data-efficient manipulation.

#### 4.5. Safer Deployment and Enhanced Explainability (Q7)

- MV-VDP generates realistic, temporally consistent multi-view RGB videos and heatmaps. This allows visual inspection of predicted rollouts before execution, providing a mechanism for action validation.
- A study with evaluators reviewing predicted videos demonstrated that video-based inspection significantly reduced collision events (0 collisions with checking vs. 6 collisions without checking in 140 trials). This indicates that MV-VDP's predicted RGB videos enhance interpretability and provide a practical tool for safer deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces MV-VDP, a novel multi-view video diffusion policy that makes several contributions to robotic manipulation.
- **Concept and Insight:** The paper highlights the limitations of existing manipulation policies regarding 3D spatio-temporal understanding and proposes leveraging video foundation models to construct a "3D Video-Action-model" (3D VAM). This is presented as a new conceptual framework in the field.
- **Methodological Advance:** MV-VDP jointly captures spatial structure through 3D-aware multi-view projections and temporal dynamics using a video foundation model, translating these predictions into executable robot actions via multi-view heatmaps. This unified approach reduces the gap between perception and control.
- **Demonstrated Performance:** Extensive experiments in both simulation (Meta-World) and real-world environments demonstrate MV-VDP's capabilities:
 - **Data Efficiency:** Achieves high success rates with a limited number of demonstrations (e.g., 10 trajectories for complex real-world tasks).
 - **Robustness:** Maintained strong performance across variations in key hyperparameters and even with significantly reduced diffusion denoising steps, enabling faster inference.
 - **Generalizability:** Demonstrated effectiveness in unseen scenarios with changes in background, object category, height, and lighting conditions.
 - **Interpretability and Safety:** Provides realistic predicted videos that allow for visual validation of actions, enhancing safety during deployment by reducing collision events.
- **Advancement over Baselines:** MV-VDP established new performance benchmarks in data-efficient multi-task manipulation, consistently outperforming state-of-the-art video-prediction-based, 3D-based, and vision-language-action models.

The potential impact of this work includes making robotic manipulation more accessible by reducing data requirements, enabling more reliable and safer robot deployments through interpretable predictions, and providing a framework for developing generalizable robot policies that better understand and interact with the 3D, dynamic world.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
Despite its strengths, MV-VDP faces limitations in inference speed, taking approximately 4.6 seconds to generate a 24-frame action chunk on an NVIDIA A100 GPU. This speed is insufficient for high-frequency dexterous tasks. Future work is planned to integrate advanced diffusion acceleration techniques, such as TurboDiffusion (aiming for 100-200x speedup), and real-time chunking into the pipeline to enable real-time control for practical robotic applications.
