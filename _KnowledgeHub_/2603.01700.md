# TacMamba: A Tactile History Compression Adapter Bridging Fast Reflexes and Slow VLA Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> TacMamba is a hierarchical hardware-software architecture that bridges high-frequency tactile reflexes (100Hz) with low-frequency VLA visual planning (~1Hz) via a Mamba-based encoder compressing tactile history into a soft prompt with O(1) latency, achieving 100% success on ambiguous contact tasks versus visual-only baselines.

## Problem

- Transformers process high-frequency tactile histories with quadratic O(N^2) cost, making them computationally prohibitive for control loops exceeding 100Hz on embedded hardware.
- LSTMs offer fast inference but suffer catastrophic forgetting over thousands of time steps, failing to retain fine-grained physical details across long interaction histories.
- Existing tactile-augmented VLAs feed tactile tokens synchronously into the heavy backbone, bottlenecking sensory processing to the VLA's low frequency and discarding high-frequency transients like snap-through vibrations.

## Method

- **Mamba-based Tactile History Compressor**: a ==selective state-space== encoder discretized via ==Zero-Order Hold== that recursively updates a compact hidden state from 1D force streams with constant-time O(1) inference.
- **Ternary Temporal Discrimination**: self-supervised pretraining where a discriminator classifies state pairs as early, simultaneous, or late using relation-balanced sampling to embed causal contact structure without labels.
- **Phase-Uniform Sampling**: a VLA fine-tuning strategy that segments trajectories into sub-task phases and samples uniformly across phases, preventing transient contact events from being drowned by static frames.

## Results

- TacMamba reaches **88.89%** accuracy at **0.45ms** latency, a **21x** speedup over Bi-LSTM (**9.20ms**) and **25x** over Transformer (**11.37ms**), while 1D-CNN and Single-LSTM trail at **56.25%** and **65.33%**.
- On sequential button pressing, TacMamba achieves **100%** success versus pi_0.5 collapsing from **37.5%** at 5k steps to **0%** at 20k steps, and versus MemoryVLA's **58%** ceiling.
- Phase-Uniform Sampling nearly doubles global success on fries packing at 8k steps (**82.5%** versus **47.5%** uniform baseline), roughly a **2.5x** sample-efficiency gain.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling a constant-time reflexive module from a slow reasoning backbone lets high-bandwidth sensing inform low-frequency planning without joint pre-training or architecture surgery.
> - Standard random sampling over long horizons biases policies toward dominant static frames, causing temporal overfitting that erases dynamic skills as training converges.
> - When a modality's informative content is rare and transient, sampling strategy design can matter as much as model architecture for downstream task success.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge in robotic manipulation related to integrating different levels of intelligence: the "System 1" of fast, reflexive reactions and the "System 2" of slow, semantic planning, a distinction often drawn in human cognition. Recent advancements in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have improved System 2 capabilities in robots, enabling high-level semantic reasoning in open-world scenarios. However, these models typically operate at low control frequencies (e.g., 1-10Hz), limiting their ability to incorporate high-frequency sensory feedback required for rapid, reflexive adjustments.

This frequency mismatch becomes particularly relevant in visually ambiguous manipulation tasks, such as detecting a button's "click" or identifying stripped threads, where tactile feedback is a primary source of ground truth. Unlike vision, which captures discrete snapshots, tactile perception often relies on continuous temporal profiles of contact. Current memory-augmented VLA models, while capable of handling long-horizon tasks, primarily rely on low-frequency visual or proprioceptive states, thereby lacking the necessary high-bandwidth physical feedback for real-time reflexes.

Prior attempts to integrate tactile and force modalities into VLA architectures, such as ForceVLA and OmniVTLA, have been bottlenecked by their synchronous designs. These approaches often feed tactile tokens directly into the VLA backbone, constraining sensory processing to the VLA's low inference speed and leading to downsampling or loss of critical high-frequency tactile transients.

The challenge of modeling extensive historical sequences efficiently is also a long-standing issue in deep learning. Transformers, while effective for long-term time-series forecasting, incur a quadratic computational cost (O(N²)) with sequence length, making them unsuitable for high-frequency control loops. Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) like LSTMs offer faster inference but are prone to catastrophic forgetting over thousands of time steps. Structured State Space Models (SSMs) and their selective variant, Mamba, have emerged as a potential solution, offering linear-time sequence modeling and constant-time (O(1)) inference by maintaining a compressed hidden state. TacMamba positions itself within this landscape by applying the Mamba architecture to high-frequency tactile streams to bridge the spatiotemporal gap, decoupling fast tactile reflexes from slow visual planning, and thereby enabling loss-less, real-time processing of micro-scale contact dynamics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work arises from the identified spatiotemporal mismatch between human-like dexterous manipulation and the capabilities of current robotic systems. Human dexterity synergizes slow, semantic planning (System 2) with fast, reflexive reactions (System 1). While Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have advanced System 2 capabilities, they often fail to replicate the reflexive nature of System 1 due to their inability to efficiently process high-frequency tactile history.

This limitation is particularly critical in manipulation scenarios where visual information is ambiguous or insufficient. Examples include detecting the subtle "click" of a button, which provides only transient vibration feedback, or identifying physical states like stripped threads, where continuous force curves and temporal contact profiles are essential ground truth. Traditional VLA models, operating at low control frequencies, cannot effectively integrate the high-frequency (e.g., >100Hz) tactile data streams necessary for such tasks.

Existing architectural approaches present specific limitations:
- **Transformers:** Their quadratic computational complexity (O(N²)) with respect to sequence length makes them computationally prohibitive for processing the hundreds or thousands of tactile tokens generated over extended interaction histories, thus preventing their use in high-frequency control loops.
- **LSTMs:** While offering faster inference than Transformers, LSTMs are susceptible to catastrophic forgetting, failing to retain fine-grained physical details over long interaction sequences.

Beyond architectural constraints, multi-modal training for such systems faces two challenges:
- **Computational Expense:** Integrating high-frequency tactile streams into large VLA models through end-to-end co-training is computationally demanding and may risk diminishing the pre-established semantic priors of the VLA.
- **Data Imbalance:** Critical interaction events (e.g., button "snap-throughs") are typically brief and sparse within longer trajectories. Standard data sampling methods tend to over-represent trivial reaching motions, leading to policies that fail during critical contact phases.

To address these spatiotemporal and data-related challenges, the paper introduces TacMamba with three core objectives:
1. **Develop a Hierarchical Architecture for Frequency Alignment:** To align high-bandwidth tactile reflexes (100Hz) with low-frequency visual reasoning (approximately 1Hz) without downsampling high-frequency data. This aims to enable lossless, real-time processing of micro-scale contact dynamics.
2. **Propose a Mamba-based Tactile Encoder:** To resolve the accuracy-latency trade-off in long-sequence modeling. This encoder is designed to encode continuous force history into a compact state with constant-time O(1) inference latency, making it suitable for real-time embedded hardware and enabling plug-and-play fusion with VLA models without joint pre-training.
3. **Implement a Robust Dual-Stage Training Strategy:** To circumvent the need for expensive VLA joint pre-training and mitigate data sparsity. This strategy involves self-supervised pre-training via Ternary Temporal Discrimination for learning causal contact dynamics, and VLA fine-tuning augmented with a Phase-Uniform Sampling strategy to address the imbalance of critical interaction frames.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TacMamba is presented as a unified hardware-software framework designed to bridge the frequency gap between high-speed tactile reflexes (System 1) and low-frequency visual planning (System 2).

#### 3.1. Hardware System

The system incorporates a modular, morphology-based tactile fingertip. This design mechanically projects distributed surface contacts onto a single sensing axis through an integrated compliant contact body, inspired by soft transmission principles. The module comprises the compliant contact body, a single-axis force sensing unit, lateral side panels, and a reconfigurable dual-clamp mounting interface for generic parallel grippers. The fingertip and fingerpad regions are realized as a single continuous compliant structure, funneling interactions from both regions to the force sensor as a unified 1D tactile stream. Finite-element analysis (FEM) was conducted to characterize force projection, confirming an approximately linear mapping for contacts at different surface regions onto the single sensing axis. The adoption of a single-axis force sensor is a deliberate system-level trade-off. The rationale is that coarse contact location is provided by the VLA backbone's visual observations, while the 1D force signal primarily complements this by supplying precise magnitude and dynamic feedback for high-frequency temporal variations (e.g., click-through, snap-through, release events) that are often visually ambiguous or imperceptible.

#### 3.2. Tactile Encoder via Mamba Architecture

The core of the software component is a Mamba-based tactile encoder, leveraging the Selective State Space Model (SSM) mechanism.
- **Continuous-Discrete Modeling:** The 1D tactile signal `x(t)` is treated as a continuous-time forcing function for a latent physical system, which maps the input to a latent state `h(t)` via a linear time-invariant (LTI) ordinary differential equation (ODE): `ḣ(t) = Ah(t) + Bx(t)`, with `y(t) = Ch(t)`. For integration with discrete robotic control cycles, this ODE is discretized using the Zero-Order Hold (ZOH) principle, yielding `h_t = Āh_{t-1} + B̄x_t`, where `Ā = exp(ΔtA)` and `B̄` are the discretized system parameters.
- **Capturing Hybrid Dynamics via Selectivity:** To account for the hybrid dynamics common in manipulation tasks, the Mamba architecture's selective mechanism is utilized. This involves parameterizing the system dynamics as a function of the input, specifically computing input-dependent parameters (Δt, B_t, C_t) via `Linear(x_t)` before discretization. This allows the model to act as an adaptive physical filter, adjusting its behavior to suppress noise during static grasping or aggressively update its internal state upon detecting transient contact events like a "click."
- **Efficient Hierarchical Architecture with O(1) Inference:** The network processes multivariate non-stationary force signals. It employs RevIN and Channel Independence initially to extract universal physical features. The backbone consists of stacked Mamba blocks that hierarchically capture features from high-frequency impulses to long-term stability trends. A critical feature is its constant-time O(1) inference capability, achieved by exploiting the recurrent formulation (`h_t` is updated from `h_{t-1}`). This ensures the high-frequency tactile system (100Hz) remains decoupled from the latency of the low-frequency visual policy.

#### 3.3. Training Strategy

A two-stage training protocol is employed to address the scarcity of labeled tactile data and the temporal sparsity of critical contact events.
- **Stage 1: Self-Supervised Pre-training via Temporal Discrimination:**
 - **Automatic Segmentation:** Long trajectories are automatically segmented into discrete sub-task phases based on the magnitude of the tactile gradient `||∇x_t||`, specifically detecting transitions from non-contact to contact.
 - **Ternary Temporal Discrimination:** A discriminator `D` is trained to classify the temporal relationship between pairs of hidden states `(h_i, h_j)` into three categories: `t_i < t_j` (Early), `t_i ≈ t_j` (Simultaneous), or `t_i > t_j` (Late). A Relation-Balanced Sampling strategy is employed to ensure a uniform distribution across these three classes, compelling the encoder to embed the causal structure of physical interactions into its hidden state. The discriminator is trained by minimizing a standard Cross-Entropy loss.
- **Stage 2: Tactile-Guided VLA Tuning with Phase Sampling:**
 - The pre-trained Mamba encoder is frozen, and its compressed state `h_t` is projected as a "Soft Prompt" (`z_tac`) into the VLA.
 - **Phase-Uniform Sampling:** To address the challenge of critical interaction frames being sparse compared to long idle modes, a Phase-Uniform Sampling strategy is introduced. Leveraging the automatic segmentation from Stage 1, each trajectory is decomposed into a sequence of `K` discrete sub-task phases `P = {P1, P2, ..., PK}`. This strategy enforces a uniform sampling distribution across these phases, meaning `p(t ∈ P_k) ∝ 1 / (K * |P_k|)`. This ensures that each sub-task mode, regardless of its duration, contributes equally to the training batch, thereby weighting transient but critical phases (e.g., snap-through) equally with stable modes and improving the VLA's ability to learn complete manipulation logic.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted using an AgileX PiPER 6-DoF manipulator with a parallel gripper, equipped with a custom single-point high-frequency force sensor integrated into the fingertip, streaming 1D force data at 100Hz. The evaluation addressed two main research questions: (1) the efficiency and scalability of TacMamba for real-time high-frequency control with long tactile histories, and (2) its robustness in manipulation scenarios characterized by visual occlusion or state ambiguity.

#### 4.1. Baselines

For tactile encoder evaluation, 1D-CNN, LSTM (single and bi-directional), and Transformer architectures were used. For end-to-end policy comparison, π₀.₅, a VLA model, served as the system-level baseline.

#### 4.2. Performance Evaluation: Accuracy & Efficiency (Offline Dataset)

A quantitative comparison of TacMamba against other architectures on an augmented real-world dataset was performed, assessing accuracy, inference latency, and memory consumption (Table I and Fig. 4).
- **TacMamba:** Achieved an accuracy of 88.89% with an inference latency of 0.45 ms and memory consumption of 60.85 MB. Its O(1) inference complexity provided a substantial speedup (21× faster than Bi-LSTM), meeting the high-frequency control requirements.
- **RNNs (LSTM):**
 - Bi-LSTM achieved 82.50% accuracy but with a latency of 9.20 ms, which breaches strict real-time streaming constraints (100Hz implies 10ms per cycle).
 - Single-LSTM was faster (0.54 ms latency) but exhibited lower accuracy (65.33%) due to catastrophic forgetting over high-frequency sequences.
- **Transformer:** Reached 81.25% accuracy but suffered from an O(L²) complexity, resulting in a latency of 11.37 ms and high memory usage (2164.71 MB), making it unsuitable for edge computing.
- **1D-CNN:** Was computationally efficient (0.23 ms latency, 19.14 MB memory) but showed the lowest accuracy (56.25%) due to its limited receptive field failing to contextualize local vibrations.

#### 4.3. Real-World Validation: Sequential Interaction under Occlusion

Three real-world manipulation tasks were designed to validate performance against the visual-only π₀.₅ baseline under varying visual occlusion and state ambiguity. Transformers and LSTMs were excluded from these real-world closed-loop tasks due to their inference latency exceeding the 10ms control cycle as tactile history grows, making TacMamba the only architecture capable of sustaining the required high-frequency control loop.

- **Task 1: Sequential Button Pressing:** The robot was required to press three buttons in a specific color sequence. Challenges included visual occlusion by the end-effector, visually imperceptible "click" feedback (state ambiguity), and perceptual aliasing due to identical-looking buttons (requiring memory of past interactions).
 - **π₀.₅ Baseline:** Demonstrated temporal degradation, achieving 37.5% success at 5k training steps but dropping to 0% by 20k steps. This was attributed to overfitting to static, non-contact frames which dominate long-horizon sequences, causing the policy to lose the ability to execute dynamic pressing actions. For comparison, external visual memory architectures (e.g., MemoryVLA) plateaued at 58% success.
 - **TacMamba:** Maintained a 100% success rate upon convergence (by 8k steps), overcoming static-frame overfitting by anchoring its policy on high-frequency tactile reflexes.

- **Task 2: Blind French Fry Packing:** The robot had to sequentially pick three fries and place them into an opaque box, requiring cumulative state estimation (implicit counting) without visual confirmation of task progress inside the box.
 - **π₀.₅ Baseline:** Achieved a 47.5% global success rate, limited by visual occlusion.
 - **TacMamba:** Achieved 95% global success, demonstrating reliable implicit counting through long-horizon tactile history.

- **Task 3: Deformable Clothes Folding:** This task involved a five-step folding workflow for a garment. Challenges included precise control for thin fabric, visual ambiguity from dynamic wrinkles, and long-horizon overfitting.
 - All models achieved 100% macro-folding success at full convergence (20k steps).
 - The visual baseline and the uniform-sampling ablation suffered from micro-failures (e.g., "Failed to grasp," "Shape crumpled") due to ambiguous visual cues.
 - The full TacMamba model maintained robust reflexes, reliably pinching the fabric edge based on tactile feedback.

#### 4.4. Ablation on Phase-Uniform Sampling

The comparison between TacMamba with Phase-Uniform Sampling and a variant using Standard Uniform Sampling highlighted the benefits of the sampling strategy. Phase-Uniform Sampling significantly reduced micro-level execution errors (e.g., overshoot, phantom grasps), leading to a higher atomic action success rate. In the fries task, it yielded an 82.5% global success rate at 8k steps, compared to 47.5% for the uniform baseline, indicating an approximate 2.5× improvement in sample efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents TacMamba as a hardware-software architecture that effectively addresses the critical spatiotemporal discrepancy between high-frequency tactile reflexes and low-frequency visual reasoning in robotic manipulation. By leveraging a Mamba-based architecture for continuous 1D tactile streams, the system achieves a high accuracy of 88.89% with a constant-time O(1) inference latency of 0.45 ms. This performance represents a significant speedup (25×) compared to Transformer baselines and overcomes their latency bottlenecks, making TacMamba the first architecture demonstrated to process ultra-long tactile histories (up to 5000 time steps) within the hard real-time constraints of embedded hardware. It also mitigates the catastrophic forgetting issues observed in standard LSTMs, enabling lossless, real-time processing of micro-scale contact dynamics.

The proposed phase-uniform sampling strategy is significant for addressing the pervasive data imbalance problem where critical contact events are sparse. This strategy substantially improves sample efficiency during VLA fine-tuning, leading to more robust and precise manipulation policies.

The extensive physical evaluations demonstrated that TacMamba successfully overcomes the temporal overfitting to static frames that causes purely visual baselines (e.g., π₀.₅) to fail in challenging scenarios. By satisfying 100Hz control loop constraints and maintaining fine-grained physical precision, the system achieves robust task execution across visually occluded and long-horizon scenarios, including sequential button pressing, implicit state counting, and deformable clothes folding.

The potential impact of TacMamba lies in enabling more dexterous and reliable robotic manipulation in real-world environments where precise physical interaction and real-time responsiveness are crucial. Its decoupled architecture supports plug-and-play integration with existing VLA frameworks, offering a modular solution to augment generalist robot policies with essential tactile intelligence.

The paper acknowledges certain limitations, such as the use of a 1D force sensor which restricts the extraction of fine-grained spatial topologies, and an evaluation primarily focused on specific edge cases rather than large-scale benchmarking. Future work aims to address these by integrating high-dimensional vision-based tactile sensor data (e.g., GelSight image sequences) directly into the Mamba network for spatially rich contact tasks and to explore its application in facilitating zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for multi-fingered dexterous hands. These future directions suggest a path toward further enhancing robotic dexterity and generalization through advanced tactile perception.
