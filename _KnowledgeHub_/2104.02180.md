# AMP: Adversarial Motion Priors for Stylized Physics-Based Character Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at UC Berkeley developed Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP), a framework that allows physically simulated characters to perform tasks in specific styles learned from unstructured motion data. This approach automatically composes diverse skills like locomotion and actions, demonstrating high-fidelity motion quality comparable to state-of-the-art tracking methods without explicit phase synchronization.

## Problem

- Designing effective objective functions for physics-based characters to produce natural and graceful behaviors is challenging and often requires extensive manual tuning.
- Existing data-driven animation techniques (e.g., motion tracking) rely on carefully designed, hand-tuned tracking objectives and often require pre-processed or annotated motion data, limiting their scalability and generalizability.
- Prior applications of adversarial imitation learning to physics-based character control struggled with instability and produced lower motion quality compared to tracking-based methods.

## Method

- A dual reward system combines a high-level, manually-designed task reward with a learned, task-agnostic style reward derived from an adversarial motion prior (**AMP**).
- ==The AMP== is an adversarial discriminator trained using a Least-Squares GAN (LSGAN) objective and a gradient penalty, which operates on state transitions to distinguish between generated and reference motions.
- The control policy is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), utilizing reference state initialization and a replay buffer to enhance exploration and training stability.

## Results

- The AMP framework generates high-quality, diverse stylized motions (e.g., 'Locomotion', 'Zombie', 'Stealthy') and automatically composes disparate skills (e.g., gait transitions, walking to punch) from unstructured motion data.
- AMP achieves motion quality comparable to state-of-the-art motion tracking methods, without requiring manually designed tracking objectives or phase synchronization.
- Ablation studies demonstrate that a gradient penalty is vital for training stability and that explicit velocity features in the discriminator's observations are crucial for accurately imitating dynamic motions like rolling.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adversarial imitation learning can achieve high-fidelity physics-based character control when carefully designed with components like LSGAN objectives, gradient penalties, and robust observation features.
> - Learning a motion prior directly from unstructured data decouples task and style, enabling automatic composition of diverse skills without explicit motion planners.
> - Discriminators operating on state transitions are effective for imitation learning when demonstrator actions are unknown, capturing essential motion characteristics relevant to style.
