# InternVideo-Next: Towards General Video Foundation Models without Video-Text Supervision

> [!summary] Summary
> InternVideo-Next, a two-stage self-supervised pretraining framework, learns general video representations from unlabeled data, achieving state-of-the-art performance on benchmarks like Kinetics-400 and SSv2. The model exhibits strong generalization in tasks requiring physical and 3D spatial intelligence, along with competitive performance in multimodal applications.

## Problem

- Current text-supervised video models are limited by noisy, biased captions, hindering their ability to capture non-semantic visual features like motion and 3D priors.
- Self-supervised Masked Video Modeling (MVM) faces challenges with pixel-level reconstruction converging poorly and latent prediction methods encouraging "shortcut learning" instead of genuine spatiotemporal dynamics.
- Existing MVM architectures struggle to reconcile the learning of fine-grained pixel details with the extraction of high-level semantic abstraction.

## Method

- Introduces **InternVideo-Next**, a two-stage pretraining framework utilizing a disentangled Encoder-Predictor-Decoder (EPD) architecture to form a "Latent ==World Model==."
- Stage 1 employs semantic-guided pixel reconstruction, using a conditional ==diffusion== decoder to balance fine-grained detail with semantics and leveraging a frozen image semantic model (SigLIP) for semantic alignment.
- Stage 2 focuses on semantically coherent latent prediction, using a multi-block masking strategy and a frozen teacher (initialized from Stage 1) to learn robust spatiotemporal dynamics and prevent shortcut learning.

## Results

- InternVideo-Next achieves state-of-the-art results among video-only models on action recognition benchmarks like Kinetics-400 (**78.1%**) and SSv2 (**59.4%**), outperforming video-text pretrained counterparts.
- The model demonstrates capabilities in physical and 3D spatial intelligence, achieving superior performance in depth estimation on ScanNet and KITTI, and higher mean IOU for object tracking on Waymo Open.
- It exhibits strong multimodal compatibility, achieving competitive zero-shot video-text retrieval and serving as an effective foundation encoder for video-LLM frameworks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The performance gap in self-supervised video understanding stems from architectural limitations, not inherent paradigm flaws, particularly regarding pixel-level reconstruction and latent prediction.
> - Disentangling the Encoder-Predictor-Decoder (EPD) structure enables the Predictor to function as a "Latent World Model," fostering a semantically consistent and detail-preserving latent space.
> - Integrating image-level semantic priors and a conditional diffusion decoder in Stage 1, combined with a frozen, semantically rich teacher for latent prediction in Stage 2, effectively bridges pixel-level fidelity with high-level semantic abstraction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Video, as a dynamic and information-rich modality, offers insights into the physical world, embedding spatiotemporal dynamics, causal relationships, 3D geometrical priors, and physical cues. These elements are considered fundamental for developing advanced video understanding models, which are relevant for areas such as embodied AI, procedural reasoning, and multimodal large language models.

Current progress in large-scale video representation learning predominantly follows two paradigms:
- **Text-supervised methods:** These approaches leverage extensive video-text alignment data. They have demonstrated strong performance on semantic and human-centric tasks, such as action recognition. However, their reliance on often noisy, synthetically generated captions, which have limited semantic coverage, can introduce external biases. This limitation makes it challenging for these models to capture non-semantic visual features like detailed object motion, causal structures, or 3D geometrical priors.
- **Self-supervised video pretraining (Masked Video Modeling - MVM):** In contrast, MVM methods aim to learn directly from the inherent spatiotemporal structures within videos. This approach holds potential for unbiased and scalable world modeling. Despite this promise, existing MVM techniques have generally trailed text-supervised methods on benchmarks that require robust semantic understanding, such as Kinetics-400.

The authors propose that the performance disparity observed in self-supervised video pretraining is not an inherent limitation of the paradigm itself. Instead, they attribute it to specific architectural challenges within current MVM designs. They identify that pixel-level reconstruction in MVM often faces convergence issues and that its low-level requirements can conflict with the learning of higher-level semantics. Furthermore, they note that latent prediction methods frequently encourage "shortcut learning," where models identify superficial correlations instead of genuine spatiotemporal dynamics.

This work builds upon and differentiates itself from prior efforts, including the InternVideo series (e.g., InternVideo and InternVideo2), which previously explored integrating unsupervised video learning with text alignment to reconcile pixel-level fidelity and high-level semantic abstraction. While these earlier methods involved weight- or embedding-level fusion, InternVideo-Next re-examines video-only pretraining from a task-level perspective, aiming to resolve the detail-semantics conflict by integrating image-level semantic priors into an augmented video reconstruction framework. By addressing these architectural and learning mechanism challenges, InternVideo-Next aims to advance self-supervised video understanding to a level competitive with or surpassing text-supervised approaches on a broader range of tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop "world understanding video models" by leveraging large-scale, unlabeled video data to learn general video representations. The authors identify specific shortcomings in existing self-supervised video pretraining methods, which motivate their proposed approach.

The key objectives are detailed as follows:
1. **Bridging Pixel-Level Fidelity with High-Level Semantic Abstraction:** Traditional Masked Video Modeling (MVM) methods, particularly those employing pixel-level reconstruction, aim to recover fine-grained details. However, the authors argue that this low-level task often conflicts with the extraction of high-level semantic information. The requirement for a decoder to linearly project predicted latent representations back to pixel space can force the latent space to be linearly separable in the pixel domain, which is posited to be detrimental to the representation of semantic abstractions. This leads to a scenario where models struggle to simultaneously maintain intricate details and a semantically rich understanding of the video content.
2. **Learning Robust Spatiotemporal Dynamics, Causal Relations, and 3D Geometric Priors without Shortcut Solutions:** Current latent prediction methods in self-supervised learning, while attempting to capture semantic abstraction by predicting feature representations, are susceptible to "shortcut learning." This occurs when models learn trivial or superficial correlations within the data to satisfy the prediction objective, rather than developing a genuine understanding of complex spatiotemporal dynamics, causal relationships, or implicit 3D geometric information embedded in videos. The authors note that symmetric teacher-student structures, common in some latent prediction models, can exacerbate this issue, leading to semantic drift or a failure to learn robust predictive world knowledge.

The motivation for these objectives stems from the authors' analysis of the "Encoder-Decoder" paradigm prevalent in existing MVM frameworks. They contend that the conventional design, where a decoder directly reconstructs pixels from an encoder's output, overlooks the crucial role of the intermediate latent space. By explicitly disentangling this into an Encoder-Predictor-Decoder (EPD) framework, the research aims to enable a focused examination and cultivation of the Predictor's output latent space. The central insight is that this latent space should be both semantically rich and faithful to fine-grained details, effectively serving as a "Latent World Model." This design principle is hypothesized to compel the Predictor to infer missing content based on genuine spatiotemporal relationships and implicit world knowledge, thereby mitigating shortcut learning and fostering the acquisition of a more comprehensive and robust video representation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces **InternVideo-Next**, a two-stage pretraining framework built upon a disentangled **Encoder-Predictor-Decoder (EPD)** architecture. This framework aims to establish a semantically consistent yet detail-preserving latent space for a "latent world model."

#### 3.1. The EPD Disentanglement

The traditional MVM Encoder-Decoder design is decoupled into three distinct components:
- **Encoder (E):** A Vision Transformer that processes input video to extract spatiotemporal representations.
- **Predictor (P):** A lightweight transformer responsible for predicting latent representations for masked video regions, utilizing information from the visible tokens. This component is conceptualized as the "Latent World Model."
- **Decoder (D):** A reconstruction module that maps the Predictor’s output latent representation to a target space, which can be either pixels or another target latent representation.

#### 3.2. Stage 1: Semantic-Guided Pixel Reconstruction

The first stage focuses on constructing a semantically grounded, detail-preserving, and structurally consistent latent space.
- **Pixel Reconstruction as Foundation:** This stage leverages a pixel reconstruction objective to enable the model to learn fine-grained pixel details and fundamental spatiotemporal priors from the raw video data.
- **Conditional Diffusion Decoder:** Instead of a conventional linear decoder head, a lightweight conditional diffusion generative decoder is employed. This diffusion decoder models the distribution of each masked patch independently, conditioned on a vector `z` produced by the Predictor. It uses a small MLP for denoising. This design is intended to preserve high semantics in the Predictor's output space, avoiding the conflict that a linear decoder might introduce by forcing latent outputs to be linearly separable in pixel space, potentially compromising the balance between semantic information and fine-grained details.
- **Semantic Alignment Loss (L_sem):** To inject reliable image-level semantic priors, a cosine similarity loss is introduced. This loss aligns the student model’s embedding of visible video regions with the corresponding embeddings from a *frozen pretrained image semantic model* (specifically, a SigLIP encoder) applied to the full, unmasked image. This approach leverages cleaner and higher-quality semantic information from large image-text corpora, thereby reducing reliance on noisy video captions and accelerating convergence by focusing learning on temporal-centric information.
- **Semantic-Aware Masking:** The masking strategy in Stage 1 prioritizes temporally informative regions. This is achieved by using attention scores derived from the semantic teacher, selecting top-k regions for masking.
- **Text-Decoder Initialization:** The Predictor (P) is initialized with weights from a pretrained text decoder (e.g., ModernBert-Large). This initialization is designed to provide richer semantic priors and facilitate a smoother translation of high-level semantics between the latent spaces, potentially reducing the number of layers required for the predictor.

#### 3.3. Stage 2: Semantically Coherent Latent Prediction

Building upon the semantically aligned and detail-preserving latent space established in Stage 1, the second stage aims to further enhance temporal abstraction and representation generalization.
- **Initialization:** Both the student (Encoder + Predictor) and teacher networks for Stage 2 are initialized with the weights learned during Stage 1. The Predictor from Stage 1 is also retained.
- **Multi-Block Masking:** To foster robust temporal reasoning and prevent shortcut learning, this stage employs a multi-block masking strategy. Large, contiguous spatiotemporal blocks within a video are masked. This increases the difficulty of the prediction task, thereby encouraging the model to learn more profound spatiotemporal dynamics and causal relations.
- **Latent Prediction Objective:** The student model is tasked with predicting the teacher's latent representations for the masked regions. This objective operates purely in the latent space, guiding the model to focus on abstract semantic and temporal patterns rather than direct pixel reconstruction.
- **Frozen Teacher Target:** A key aspect of Stage 2 is that the teacher network is *frozen* with its Stage 1 initialization. This differs from approaches like V-JEPA, which often use a momentum-updated teacher. The frozen teacher provides a stable, high-quality target that already encapsulates both fine-grained details and high-level semantics from Stage 1, which is intended to prevent "shortcut solutions" and promote the learning of genuine predictive world knowledge.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

InternVideo-Next demonstrates robust performance across a diverse range of video understanding tasks, achieving state-of-the-art results without relying on explicit video-text supervision, using only public and unlabeled video data.

**Overall Achievements:**
- **Performance Leadership:** InternVideo-Next is reported to be the first video model trained exclusively with video data (without explicit video-text supervision) that surpasses video-text pretrained counterparts on benchmarks such as Kinetics-400 (action recognition) and SSv2 (fine-grained motion recognition).
- **Implicit World Knowledge:** The learned representations exhibit generalization capabilities in tasks requiring physical and 3D spatial intelligence, including depth estimation and object-centric tracking, suggesting the acquisition of implicit world knowledge inherent in videos.
- **Multimodal Friendliness:** With minimal fine-tuning (LiT-style), the model achieves competitive zero-shot video-text retrieval performance and shows potential as a foundation encoder for multimodal video chat models.

**Ablation Studies (Tables 1, 2, 3, 4):**
- **Stage 1 Design:**
 - The pixel reconstruction baseline yielded 47.2% on K400, highlighting its limited semantic abstraction.
 - Semantic alignment alone with SigLIP boosted K400 accuracy to 70.7%.
 - Naively combining pixel reconstruction and semantic alignment negatively impacted performance. However, the introduction of the conditional diffusion decoder reversed this trend, leading to a 4.4% performance gain and demonstrating complementarity between the supervision signals.
 - Initializing the Predictor with a pretrained text decoder (ModernBert-Large) and using the diffusion decoder resulted in the strongest Stage 1 performance (75.8% K400, 36.9% SSv2).
 - Different diffusion-based MLP decoder configurations were evaluated, with Depth 6, Width 1536 providing the optimal balance.
- **Semantic Prior Sources:** Using image semantic models like SigLIP for semantic alignment consistently provided the best performance across recognition and 3D understanding tasks, outperforming DINOv2 or CLIP-ViT, and showing superiority over InternVideo2's multi-teacher alignment strategy.
- **Stage 2 Configuration:**
 - The full Stage 2 configuration (latent prediction with frozen Stage 1-initialized teacher, multi-block masking) significantly boosted performance (e.g., K400 from 75.8% to 76.9%, SSv2 from 36.9% to 56.9%), indicating enhanced temporal abstraction.
 - Alternatives such as zero-initialized V-JEPA predictor, momentum teachers, or other frozen target initializations resulted in performance degradation, underscoring the importance of semantically coherent initialization and a stable, detail-preserving target.
 - Adding unmasked token alignment or pixel reconstruction loss in Stage 2 provided marginal or negative benefits.
- **Masking Strategy and Temporal Window:** Semantic masking in Stage 1 was beneficial for high-level semantics, while multi-block masking in Stage 2 improved learning of robust spatiotemporal dynamics. Increasing the number of input frames consistently improved performance, with F32 frames in Stage 2 yielding the highest results (78.1% K400, 59.4% SSv2).

**Single-modality Tasks:**
- **Video Classification (Table 5):** Achieved state-of-the-art attentive probing results on K400, SSv2, and COIN with significantly less training data and computational cost compared to other models, including those pretrained with video-text pairs.
- **Depth Estimation (Table 6, Figure 3):** Demonstrated superior performance on ScanNet and KITTI datasets, surpassing both image models and other video models in capturing 3D spatial information. In some settings, it achieved results comparable to specialized depth estimation models like VideoDepthAnything, even with a simpler probing head.
- **Object Tracking (Table 7):** Obtained the highest mean IOU on the Waymo Open dataset, indicating superior capability in capturing object-level motion.
- **Video Prediction (Table 8):** Showed improved mean-class recall-at-5 on the EK100 dataset for future action prediction.

**Multi-modality Tasks:**
- **Zero-shot Action Recognition (Table 9):** Outperformed InternVideo2s2, particularly on the motion-intensive SSv2-Multiple Choice task, demonstrating better separability and alignment in text space.
- **Zero-shot Retrieval (Table 10):** Achieved comparable performance with other leading models on various text-to-video retrieval benchmarks (MSRVTT, DiDeMo, ActivityNet, LSMDC, MSVD), indicating effective implicit alignment between video embeddings and semantic text space.
- **Chat-centric Tasks (Table 11):** When integrated as a frozen encoder with a frozen large language model (Qwen2-7B) via a trainable MLP, InternVideo-Next demonstrated better performance on general spatiotemporal perception (MVBench, Perception Test) and captioning tasks (Dream1K), validating its compatibility and transferability within generative video-LLM frameworks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "InternVideo-Next: Towards World Understanding Video Models" introduces a significant advancement in self-supervised video pretraining. Its primary significance lies in the development of a unified two-stage framework that effectively addresses long-standing challenges in learning comprehensive video representations from unlabeled data.

**Key Contributions and Impact:**
- **Unified and Coherent Learning Framework:** The proposed Encoder-Predictor-Decoder (EPD) disentanglement, combined with the two-stage pretraining scheme, provides a novel approach to integrate pixel-level reconstruction and latent prediction. This unification occurs within a semantically coherent latent space, which is critical for balancing the learning of fine-grained details with high-level semantic abstraction. This design potentially resolves the conflict between these two aspects that has hindered previous MVM methods.
- **Mitigation of Architectural Limitations:** By specifically designing Stage 1 with a conditional diffusion decoder and semantic priors, the work tackles the convergence issues and the conflict between low-level pixel requirements and semantics inherent in traditional pixel-level reconstruction. Stage 2, with its frozen teacher and multi-block masking, is designed to counteract "shortcut learning" common in latent prediction methods, thereby fostering the acquisition of genuine spatiotemporal dynamics and implicit world knowledge.
- **Scalable and Unbiased World Understanding:** The model's ability to achieve state-of-the-art performance across a wide array of video understanding tasks using only publicly available, unlabeled videos signifies a scalable and unbiased pathway toward building robust video foundation models. This approach reduces the dependency on expensive, noisy, or synthetically generated video-text annotations, which is a major bottleneck for large-scale video model development.
- **Broad Applicability and Generalization:** InternVideo-Next demonstrates strong generalization capabilities, excelling not only in traditional action recognition but also in tasks requiring deeper physical and 3D spatial intelligence, such as depth estimation and object tracking. Its performance in multimodal tasks, including zero-shot retrieval and chat-centric applications, indicates its potential as a versatile foundation encoder adaptable to diverse downstream applications.
- **Advancement of Self-Supervised Learning:** By surpassing previous video-only and even some video-text pretrained methods, InternVideo-Next establishes a new benchmark for self-supervised video representation learning. This suggests that the identified architectural improvements and the two-stage learning strategy are effective in unlocking the full potential of learning from raw video data.

**Potential Future Directions:**
The authors indicate plans to investigate the scalability of this method to larger datasets and model architectures. They also aim to extend the framework toward more complex multi-modal and interactive world modeling scenarios, with a focus on efficient scaling and open-world generalization. This implies a trajectory toward developing even more versatile video foundation models capable of understanding and interacting with the world in sophisticated ways.
