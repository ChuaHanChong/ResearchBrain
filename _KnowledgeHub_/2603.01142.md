# ArtLLM: Generating Articulated Assets via 3D LLM

> [!summary] Summary
> ArtLLM, a framework by researchers from ShanghaiTech University, Tencent Hunyuan, and HKUST, generates high-quality articulated 3D assets by reformulating 3D articulation understanding as a language modeling problem. It leverages a 3D multimodal LLM to predict geometric layouts and kinematic structures, then synthesizes high-fidelity geometry and applies physics-based joint limit correction, achieving superior accuracy and significantly faster inference compared to existing methods while demonstrating strong real2sim performance for robotics.

## Problem

- Existing optimization-based methods for articulated object reconstruction are slow, produce low-fidelity geometry, and are limited to simple kinematic structures.
- Retrieval-based methods suffer from geometric repetitiveness and poor generalization due to reliance on predefined part libraries.
- Many 3D generative models lack an understanding of kinematic structures, leading to a disconnect between generated geometry and its intended motion, making objects mechanically incoherent.

## Method

- A 3D Articulation Language Model (ArtLLM) leverages a 3D multimodal LLM to autoregressively predict an object's kinematic structure (parts, joints, hierarchy) as a sequence of discrete tokens from point cloud inputs.
- Predicted part bounding boxes condition a part-aware generative model for high-fidelity geometry synthesis, capable of creating novel and diverse shapes.
- A physics-based joint limit correction module refines predicted joint ranges to prevent inter-part collisions during articulation, enhancing physical plausibility.

## Results

- ArtLLM achieved superior quantitative performance on the PartNet-Mobility dataset, with a Part Layout mIoU of 0.6884, Joint Type Accuracy of 0.9084, and Kinematic Hierarchy Graph Acc of 0.7741, outperforming baselines such as Articulate-Anything and SINGAPO across most metrics.
- The framework demonstrated significantly faster inference times (19 seconds) compared to baselines (84-522 seconds) while generating high-fidelity, novel part geometries with accurate and coherent articulations.
- Real2sim evaluations in robotics showed ArtLLM's ability to reconstruct real-world objects into URDF-format articulated assets that accurately reproduce real articulation behavior in simulation, bridging the sim-to-real gap for robot learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reformulating 3D articulation understanding as a language modeling problem enables the use of Large Language Models for joint reasoning about geometry and kinematics, handling variable numbers of parts and joints.
> - Quantization of continuous geometric and kinematic parameters allows their effective prediction within a discrete token-based LLM framework, trained via a multi-stage and multi-task supervised fine-tuning strategy that begins with geometric foundation and progressively integrates kinematic reasoning for more robust and accurate model learning.
> - Post-processing with physically-constrained joint limit correction ensures that generated assets are free from self-collisions and are simulation-ready.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of articulated 3D objects is a foundational component for creating interactive digital environments across various domains, including gaming, robotics, and simulation. Articulated objects, characterized by their part-based geometry and kinematic structures, are essential for realistic manipulation and animation. Existing research in this area broadly falls into two categories, both of which face notable limitations.

One prominent paradigm involves **optimization-based reconstruction** from inputs such as multi-view images or videos. Methods in this category often leverage neural representations like NeRF or 3D Gaussian Splatting to estimate both geometry and joint parameters. While capable of reconstructing specific objects, these approaches are typically hindered by slow, per-object optimization processes, tend to produce lower-fidelity geometry, and are frequently restricted to simpler objects with a single joint or limited articulation complexity. Examples include works focusing on part-level reconstruction and motion analysis or those reconstructing articulated objects from monocular video.

The second paradigm encompasses **retrieval-based methods**, which train feedforward networks to predict part layouts and joint parameters by assembling components from a predefined library. These methods offer faster inference compared to optimization-based techniques. However, their reliance on a fixed database of parts severely constrains their ability to generate novel shapes, leading to geometrically repetitive assets and limited generalization capabilities. Techniques like SINGAPO and Articulate-Anything fall into this category. Some studies have attempted surface reconstruction from generated Signed Distance Fields (SDFs) to enable novel geometry, but often result in lower quality.

Concurrently, the broader field of **3D object generation** has experienced significant advancements, leading to high-fidelity synthesis from diverse inputs. Recent extensions have enabled part-level generation. However, a fundamental challenge persists: a **disconnect between geometry and motion**. Most 3D generative models are unaware of the underlying kinematic structures that dictate how parts should move, potentially leading to a mismatch between a part's visual semantics and its intended mechanical function. This indicates a need for a unified approach capable of jointly reasoning about geometry and articulation.

In parallel, the emergence and rapid development of **3D Large Language Models (LLMs)** have shown promise in integrating 3D representations into language models, enabling native 3D reasoning, generation, and scene grounding. These models demonstrate strong capabilities for multimodal understanding and generation. However, existing 3D LLM-based approaches for articulated object modeling often predict continuous float parameters, are trained on limited data, leading to poor generalization, or rely on point cloud-based mesh reconstruction, which can constrain output quality.

ArtLLM positions itself within this landscape by addressing these combined challenges. It proposes a novel framework that bridges the gap between high-fidelity 3D geometry generation and articulated kinematic structure inference. By leveraging a 3D multimodal LLM and an articulation-aware generative pipeline, ArtLLM aims to overcome the limitations of slow optimization, repetitive geometry, and the geometry-motion disconnect, thereby providing a more scalable, generalized, and high-quality solution for generating functional articulated 3D assets.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient and robust framework, named ArtLLM, capable of generating high-quality, physically grounded articulated 3D assets directly from complete 3D meshes, and subsequently from various modalities such as images or text.

This overarching goal is motivated by several critical limitations identified in existing articulated object generation techniques:

1. **Addressing the limitations of optimization-based reconstruction methods:** Current optimization-based approaches, while capable of reconstructing articulated objects from multi-view inputs, are inherently slow due to their per-object fitting nature. They often yield low-fidelity geometry and are typically restricted to objects with simpler kinematic structures, often only a single joint. The motivation is to create a method that bypasses these speed and fidelity constraints, enabling rapid and high-quality generation of complex articulated objects.

2. **Overcoming the geometric repetitiveness of retrieval-based methods:** Retrieval-based techniques, which assemble objects from a fixed library of parts, suffer from limited geometric novelty and poor generalization to unseen shapes. This work aims to develop a system that can synthesize novel and diverse part geometries rather than merely retrieving them, thereby expanding the creative scope and realism of generated assets.

3. **Bridging the geometry-motion disconnect in general 3D generation:** While general 3D object generation has advanced significantly, most models lack an understanding of the kinematic structures that govern how parts interact and move. This can lead to visually plausible but mechanically incoherent objects. A core motivation is to integrate kinematic reasoning directly into the generation process, ensuring that the synthesized geometry is inherently compatible with its intended mechanical role and motion.

4. **Leveraging the advanced capabilities of Large Language Models for 3D articulation:** Inspired by the success of LLMs in complex reasoning and sequence modeling, the authors are motivated to reformulate 3D articulation understanding as a language modeling problem. This allows the framework to harness the LLM's ability to autoregressively predict a variable number of parts and joints, inferring their kinematic structure in a unified manner from point cloud data. This approach aims to provide a more robust and flexible solution compared to direct regression of continuous parameters used in some prior 3D LLM works.

5. **Enabling scalable content creation for interactive digital environments:** The demand for high-quality articulated assets is substantial in areas such as gaming, robotics simulation (e.g., for robot training and digital twins), and virtual reality. The motivation is to provide a scalable and efficient pipeline for automatically generating these assets, significantly reducing manual effort and accelerating the development of complex interactive worlds.

By addressing these motivations, ArtLLM seeks to deliver a stable and generalizable pipeline that generates high-fidelity, physically grounded articulated assets, thereby narrowing the real-to-simulation gap and facilitating advancements in scalable robot learning and digital twin construction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ArtLLM is a novel framework designed to generate articulated assets by first predicting their geometric layouts and kinematic structures, and then synthesizing their high-fidelity geometries. The pipeline consists of three main stages: a 3D Articulation Language Model (ArtLLM) for structural prediction, a part-aware generative model for geometry synthesis, and a physics-based joint limit correction module.

#### 3.1. 3D Articulation Language Model (ArtLLM)

The core of the framework is the ArtLLM, which reformulates 3D articulation understanding as a language modeling problem. This allows it to leverage the reasoning and sequence modeling capabilities of Large Language Models to autoregressively predict an object’s kinematic structure as a unified sequence of discrete tokens.

- **Input Representation:** The model takes a point cloud as input. For text or image inputs, off-the-shelf generative models (e.g., Hunyuan3D 2.5, TripoSG) are used to produce an initial 3D mesh. For direct mesh inputs (generated or provided), 32,768 surface points with corresponding normals are uniformly sampled after ensuring the mesh is watertight.
- **Bridging 3D Geometry and Language:** An encoder-projector architecture connects the 3D point cloud to the LLM. Point Transformer v3, pre-trained with P3SAM (for part segmentation), serves as the point cloud encoder. Its final layer features are augmented with position embeddings to preserve spatial information. These features are then projected by a two-layer MLP for modality alignment with the LLM backbone, which is Qwen3 0.6B.
- **Generating Articulation as Languages:** The articulated structure is represented as a language sequence using a concise text template.
 - **Parts:** Each part is defined by an ID and its 3D axis-aligned bounding box (AABB) with `min_x, y, z` and `max_x, y, z` coordinates.
 - **Joints:** Kinematic joints are defined by their type (Revolute, Continuous, Prismatic, Screw), parent/child link IDs, 3D direction vector, 3D position vector, and motion range (limit).
 - **Autoregressive Generation Order:** The model first predicts all part bounding boxes, followed by a separator token, and then all joint definitions. This ensures joint prediction is conditioned on the complete part layout.
- **Quantized Predictions:** To handle continuous geometric and kinematic parameters within a discrete token-based LLM framework, quantization is applied:
 - **Bounding Box Coordinates and Joint Origin:** Normalized range `[-1, 1]` is discretized into 128 bins per axis.
 - **Rotational Limits:** Angles are discretized into 48 bins over `[-2π, 2π]`.
 - **Translational Distances:** Discretized into 64 bins over `[-2, 2]`.
 - **Joint Axis:** A 128-entry hierarchical codebook is used, constructed by sampling points uniformly from unit circles on XY, YZ, XZ planes, supplemented by Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) on a Fibonacci sphere for broader coverage.
- **Multi-Task and Multi-Stage Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):**
 - **Multi-Task:** Three SFT tasks are defined: 1) Part Layout Prediction (only part bounding boxes), 2) Kinematic Prediction (joints, conditioned on point cloud and ground-truth part layout), and 3) End-to-End Articulation Prediction (both parts and joints).
 - **Multi-Stage:**
 - **Stage 1:** Train only on Task 1 (Part layout prediction) to establish a robust geometric foundation for the 3D encoder, initialized with weights from P3SAM.
 - **Stage 2:** Initialize the point encoder and projector with weights from Stage 1, then perform SFT using all three tasks. This refines the model's kinematic reasoning capabilities.
 This strategy decouples geometric understanding from kinematic reasoning initially, leading to more robust learning.

#### 3.2. Training Corpus for ArtLLM

A large-scale dataset is curated by aggregating and refining existing articulation datasets and procedurally generated data:
- **Sources:** PartNet-Mobility, PhysX3D, and 12,000 synthetic assets from Infinite-Mobility.
- **Total:** 20,673 articulated objects across 43 categories.
- **Preprocessing:**
 - **Filtering:** Remove objects with >20 joints or categories with excessively small/detailed parts (e.g., keyboards, buttons).
 - **Structure Simplification:** Merge fixed joints and their connected links; combine revolute and prismatic components into single screw joints.
 - **Normalization:** All joint parameters and geometry normalized to `[-0.9, 0.9]`.
 - **Normal Correction:** Watertight reconstruction applied to models with incorrect surface normals (e.g., some PartNet-Mobility objects).
- **Data Ordering:** Quantized bounding boxes are sorted by their minimum z-y-x coordinates, and joints are sorted by their child link IDs to ensure deterministic ordering for autoregressive training stability.

#### 3.3. Part-Aware Geometry Synthesis

The predicted structural blueprint (part bounding boxes) conditions a part-based generative model for high-fidelity geometry synthesis.
- **Backbone:** XPart is adopted as the geometry generation backbone (compatible with OmniPart).
- **Bounding Box Expansion:** To prevent truncated or incomplete generated parts if predicted bounding boxes do not perfectly encompass ground-truth geometry, a post-processing step expands each bounding box. Any point in the input point cloud not contained within a predicted box is assigned to its nearest box, and that box is expanded to enclose all newly assigned points.

#### 3.4. Physically-Constrained Joint Limit Correction

A post-processing step is introduced to refine predicted joint limits and prevent inter-part collisions during articulation.
- **Method:** For each joint, its child part is articulated through its initially predicted range. Collision volume against all other static parts is computed at discrete steps. Significant collisions are identified by sharp spikes in the derivative of the collision volume. A hierarchical search pinpoints the precise angle/distance of initial contact, which then sets the refined, collision-free joint limit. This procedure applies to both revolute and prismatic joints.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ArtLLM demonstrates its effectiveness in generating articulated assets, showing improvements over existing state-of-the-art methods and robust generalization capabilities.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Comparisons

ArtLLM was quantitatively evaluated on a held-out test set from PartNet-Mobility, comprising 77 objects across 7 categories (Storage, Table, Refrigerator, Dishwasher, Oven, Washer, Microwave), and compared against URDFormer, SINGAPO, and Articulate-Anything.

- **Superior Performance:** ArtLLM achieved a clear advantage across most metrics:
 - **Part Layout Accuracy (mIoU):** ArtLLM scored 0.6884, significantly higher than Articulate-Anything (0.3381), original SINGAPO (0.4330), retrained SINGAPO (0.4705), and URDFormer (0.1225).
 - **Joint Prediction Accuracy:**
 - **Type Accuracy (Type Acc):** ArtLLM achieved 0.9084, outperforming Articulate-Anything (0.8457), original SINGAPO (0.7649), retrained SINGAPO (0.9065, very close but still slightly lower), and URDFormer (0.6068).
 - **Joint-Axis-Err:** ArtLLM demonstrated a lower error of 0.1271, compared to Articulate-Anything (0.4529), original SINGAPO (0.2445), retrained SINGAPO (0.2463), and URDFormer (0.7377).
 - **Joint-Pivot-Err:** ArtLLM showed the lowest error at 0.0801, significantly better than Articulate-Anything (0.5361), original SINGAPO (0.2567), retrained SINGAPO (0.1465), and URDFormer (0.6095).
 - **Joint-Range-IoU:** ArtLLM achieved 0.7398, performing well, though Articulate-Anything (0.8653) showed higher performance in this specific metric, likely due to its rule-based limit prediction. Retrained SINGAPO scored 0.6184.
 - **Kinematic Hierarchy (Graph Acc):** ArtLLM scored 0.7741, outperforming Articulate-Anything (0.6142), original SINGAPO (0.4564), retrained SINGAPO (0.6851), and URDFormer (0.0791).
- **Inference Speed:** ArtLLM demonstrated significantly faster inference times (19 seconds) compared to Articulate-Anything (522 seconds), SINGAPO (84 seconds), and URDFormer (183 seconds), indicating its efficiency for large-scale applications.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Comparisons

Qualitative results show that ArtLLM generates articulated objects whose shapes closely match the input images, producing accurate and novel part geometries. It successfully recovers correct and coherent articulations with appropriate joint types and aligned positions. In contrast:
- URDFormer often produces results that differ significantly from ground truth due to fixed assumptions about object structure and frequently fails to accurately predict articulated parts and their motion.
- Articulate-Anything and SINGAPO, while capable of retrieving similar geometry from their part databases, exhibit limitations in joint prediction, with Articulate-Anything often misidentifying axis directions and SINGAPO frequently predicting inaccurate part scales and axis positions.
- ArtLLM also demonstrates robust generalization to real-world images, accurately reconstructing articulated assets where baselines often fail to retrieve accurate geometry or miss essential parts.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of key components of ArtLLM:
- **Quantization:** Directly predicting continuous part layouts and articulation parameters (Experiment A) significantly degraded coordinate and direction-related attribute inference, confirming the necessity of token-based quantization.
- **Multi-Task Setup:** Removing the multi-task learning configuration (Experiment B) generally degraded performance across most metrics, indicating its role in reinforcing part and articulation understanding.
- **3D Augmentation:** Eliminating random scaling and rotation (Experiment C) led to lower part-IoU, highlighting the benefit of 3D augmentation for spatial perception.
- **Multi-Stage Training:** Removing the progressive two-stage training strategy (Experiment D) and directly using a P3SAM-initialized encoder for all tasks lowered both part and joint prediction accuracy, validating the efficacy of pre-training the encoder on part-layout prediction.

#### 4.4. Physically-Constrained Joint Limit Correction

Qualitative results for the physical limit correction step demonstrated its ability to effectively adjust predicted joint ranges that initially caused self-collisions. After correction, articulated parts moved smoothly and collision-free, enhancing physical plausibility and stability.

#### 4.5. Application in Robotic Area (Real2Sim Evaluation)

ArtLLM's utility for robotics was demonstrated through a real2sim evaluation. Real-world robot manipulation tasks (e.g., closing a laptop, a box, moving a bucket handle) were recorded, and the corresponding real objects were reconstructed into URDF-format articulated assets using ArtLLM. Replaying the recorded robot pose sequences in a simulated environment (SAPIEN) with these generated assets successfully reproduced the real articulation behavior, showcasing the high fidelity of ArtLLM's assets in terms of structure, joint constraints, and resulting motion.

#### 4.6. Limitations

Despite its strengths, ArtLLM has some limitations:
- **Limited Category Diversity:** While trained on a large dataset, the diversity of object categories remains somewhat limited. The model generalizes well to common household items but struggles with more complex categories like vehicles or robots.
- **Lack of Joint Physical Property Modeling:** The framework does not jointly model physical properties such as mass or friction, which are crucial for comprehensive physical simulations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ArtLLM represents a significant advancement in the field of articulated 3D object generation, offering a novel and effective solution to long-standing challenges. Its significance and potential impact span several domains:

- **Advancement in 3D Articulation Generation:** The work introduces a unified framework that successfully integrates high-fidelity geometry synthesis with robust kinematic structure prediction. By reformulating articulation understanding as a language modeling problem, ArtLLM harnesses the power of 3D LLMs to jointly reason about part layouts and joint parameters, a capability that distinguishes it from prior art.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Methods:** ArtLLM directly addresses and largely overcomes the key limitations of previous approaches:
 - It bypasses the slow, per-object optimization processes and low-fidelity geometry often associated with reconstruction-based methods.
 - It transcends the geometric repetitiveness and poor generalization of retrieval-based methods by enabling the synthesis of novel and diverse part geometries.
 - It resolves the fundamental disconnect between geometry and motion by ensuring that generated parts are inherently compatible with their predicted kinematic roles.
- **Generation of High-Quality, Physically Grounded Assets:** The framework is capable of producing detailed, novel geometries and, critically, physically plausible articulations. The physics-based joint limit correction mechanism is particularly impactful, preventing self-collisions and ensuring that the generated assets are "simulation-ready" for robust physical interactions.
- **Scalability and Efficiency:** Achieving superior performance while maintaining significantly faster inference times compared to state-of-the-art baselines makes ArtLLM a scalable solution. This efficiency is crucial for rapid content creation in large-scale digital environments.
- **Impact on Robotics and AI:** The ability to rapidly generate high-fidelity, functionally accurate articulated digital twins from real-world observations is transformative for robotics. ArtLLM's successful real2sim demonstrations show its potential to:
 - **Accelerate Robot Learning:** Provide realistic and diverse training environments for robot manipulation tasks, reducing the need for costly and time-consuming real-world data collection.
 - **Bridge the Sim-to-Real Gap:** Improve the fidelity of simulated environments, making robot policies trained in simulation more directly transferable to the real world.
 - **Enable Digital Twin Construction:** Facilitate the creation of accurate digital representations of real-world objects, which is vital for advanced manufacturing, predictive maintenance, and complex system design.
- **Broader Applications in Interactive Digital Environments:** Beyond robotics, ArtLLM has substantial implications for:
 - **Gaming:** Rapidly populate virtual worlds with complex, interactive objects, enhancing immersion and gameplay.
 - **Virtual Reality/Augmented Reality:** Create more realistic and manipulable virtual objects for various applications, from training to entertainment.
 - **Content Creation:** Empower designers and artists with tools to quickly prototype and generate complex 3D assets with inherent functionality.
- **Advancing 3D LLM Capabilities:** This work further demonstrates the utility and strong potential of 3D LLMs for complex, structured generative tasks beyond traditional conversational understanding, showcasing their capacity for intricate geometric and kinematic reasoning.

In conclusion, ArtLLM represents a substantial leap in the automated generation of articulated 3D objects, offering a powerful, efficient, and versatile framework that is poised to significantly impact various fields requiring high-fidelity interactive digital assets.
