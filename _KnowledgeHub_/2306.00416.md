# Interactive Character Control with Auto-Regressive Motion Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed Auto-Regressive Motion Diffusion Models (A-MDM) that enable real-time, frame-by-frame character motion generation by leveraging lightweight MLPs and few denoising steps. This approach generates more diverse and high-fidelity motions than previous auto-regressive techniques, providing a versatile framework for interactive control.

## Problem

- Most state-of-the-art motion diffusion models are space-time models, generating entire sequences at once, making them unsuitable for real-time interactive character control.
- Interactive applications (games, VR) require high-fidelity, diverse, and responsive character motions that can react to continuous, time-varying user inputs.
- Prior auto-regressive methods for real-time motion often struggled with motion quality, diversity, long-term coherence, and susceptibility to drift over extended sequences.

## Method

- Introduced **Auto-Regressive Motion Diffusion Models (A-MDM)** that predict the next motion frame based on the previous one, using a lightweight ==MLP== architecture and ==few denoising steps== for efficiency.
- Employed ==scheduled sampling== during training to mitigate error accumulation and ==drift==, enhancing long-horizon motion coherence.
- Integrated various control strategies, including ==task-oriented sampling==, ==conditional inpainting==, and ==hierarchical reinforcement learning== (PPO-trained ==residual== steering), to adapt the base A-MDM for diverse interactive tasks.

## Results

- A-MDM outperforms prior auto-regressive generative models (MVAE, HuMoR) in motion diversity (higher **APD**), fidelity (lower **ADE/FDE**), and physical plausibility on datasets like AMASS and 100STYLE.
- Achieves real-time inference speeds (e.g., approximately **0.021 seconds per frame** for **40 denoising steps**), demonstrating practical applicability for interactive systems.
- The proposed control strategies successfully enable diverse interactive tasks, including accurate trajectory following via conditional inpainting and superior task completion with diverse behaviors using hierarchical reinforcement learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diffusion models, when designed with an auto-regressive architecture and lightweight components (MLPs, fewer denoising steps), can achieve real-time performance suitable for interactive character control without sacrificing substantial motion quality.
> - Scheduled sampling is effective in addressing long-term drift in auto-regressive generative models, enabling stable, unbounded motion generation.
> - A single pre-trained auto-regressive diffusion model can serve as a flexible generative prior, adaptable to a wide range of interactive control tasks through various steering mechanisms like inpainting or hierarchical reinforcement learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "Interactive Character Control with Auto-Regressive Motion Diffusion Models" positions itself at the forefront of data-driven character animation, specifically addressing a critical gap in the application of powerful generative models for real-time interactive experiences.

**Evolution of Motion Synthesis:** The field of computer animation has continuously sought to synthesize realistic and controllable motions for virtual characters. Historically, this involved motion capture data, keyframe animation, or procedural methods. With the advent of deep learning, data-driven approaches have gained prominence, drawing inspiration from successes in text and image synthesis.

**Rise of Diffusion Models:** Recent years have seen diffusion models (e.g., DDPM, Latent Diffusion Models) emerge as state-of-the-art generative models, particularly for high-fidelity image and text generation. Their capacity to produce diverse and high-quality outputs has naturally led researchers to explore their application in motion synthesis. Several recent works have indeed applied diffusion models to motion generation, yielding impressive results in terms of visual quality.

**The Identified Gap: Real-time Interaction vs. Offline Generation:** The authors highlight a significant limitation in the majority of existing motion diffusion models: they are predominantly *space-time models*. These models synthesize an entire motion sequence of a pre-specified length simultaneously, making them suitable for *offline applications* but inherently unsuitable for *real-time interactive scenarios* where continuous, time-varying controls are necessary. Applications like video games, virtual reality, and real-time physics simulations demand immediate responsiveness and the ability to generate unbounded motion sequences frame-by-frame.

**This Work's Contribution to Bridging the Gap:** This paper directly addresses this challenge by proposing **Auto-Regressive Motion Diffusion Models (A-MDM)**. A-MDM represents a fundamental redesign of motion diffusion models to operate in an auto-regressive, frame-by-frame manner. This allows for:
- **Real-time responsiveness:** By generating motion incrementally, the model can react to user inputs or environmental changes as they happen.
- **Time-varying controls:** The sequential nature enables dynamic adjustments to character behavior.
- **Unbounded motion sequences:** Unlike fixed-length space-time models, A-MDM can generate motions of arbitrary duration.

**Comparison with Existing Paradigms:**
- **Against other Auto-regressive Models (e.g., VAE-based like MVAE, HuMoR, NSM):** Previous auto-regressive methods often struggled with motion quality, diversity, or long-term coherence (e.g., drift, convergence to mean pose). A-MDM leverages the expressive power of diffusion models to produce significantly higher fidelity and more diverse motions, surpassing these prior methods while also addressing drift through techniques like scheduled sampling.
- **Against Space-Time Diffusion Models (e.g., MDM, GMD):** While space-time diffusion models can achieve very high overall sequence quality (as acknowledged by the authors in section 7.7), A-MDM makes a deliberate trade-off. It prioritizes the real-time, interactive capabilities by employing a lightweight architecture and fewer denoising steps. This sacrifices some global consistency or peak fidelity found in offline space-time models but makes interactive use practical. A-MDM also boasts the ability to generate long-horizon motions from short training clips, a challenge for space-time models.

In essence, this work pioneers the adaptation of the high-fidelity generative capabilities of diffusion models to the demands of real-time, interactive character control, filling a critical void in the current landscape of motion synthesis research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of this research is to enable **real-time, interactive character control using the generative power of diffusion models.** This overarching goal is driven by several motivations and broken down into specific technical objectives:

- **Motivation 1: The Demand for Interactive Motion:** Real-time character control is fundamental for a vast array of interactive applications, including video games, virtual reality, physics simulations, and digital puppetry. These applications require motions that are not only high-fidelity and diverse but also responsive to dynamic user inputs and environmental changes.
- **Motivation 2: Leveraging Diffusion Models' Success:** Diffusion models have demonstrated unprecedented success in generating high-quality and diverse data in other domains (e.g., images, text). Researchers recognize their immense potential for motion synthesis to produce more natural and varied human movements than previous generative models.
- **Motivation 3: Overcoming Limitations of Existing Motion Diffusion Models:** The primary hurdle preventing widespread adoption of diffusion models in interactive character control is their computational cost and design. Most current motion diffusion models are *space-time models* that generate entire sequences simultaneously, precluding real-time interactivity and the ability to respond to time-varying controls.
- **Motivation 4: Improving upon Prior Auto-regressive Methods:** While auto-regressive models are inherently suited for real-time, frame-by-frame generation, prior VAE-based (e.g., MVAE, HuMoR) and non-generative models (e.g., NSM) often struggle with maintaining high fidelity, diversity, and long-term coherence (prone to drift or convergence to mean poses) over extended periods.

**Specific Technical Objectives:**

1. **Develop an Auto-Regressive Motion Diffusion Model (A-MDM):** Design a novel diffusion model architecture that can generate motion frame-by-frame, conditioning the current frame on the previous one.
2. **Achieve Real-time Inference Speed:** Drastically reduce the computational burden of diffusion models for real-time applications by employing a lightweight network architecture (simple MLPs) and significantly fewer denoising steps (e.g., 10-50 steps instead of 1000).
3. **Ensure Long-Horizon Motion Coherence:** Implement strategies like scheduled sampling to mitigate error accumulation and drift, enabling the generation of stable and natural motions over arbitrary durations.
4. **Incorporate Flexible Interactive Control Mechanisms:** Develop a suite of techniques that allow a pre-trained A-MDM to be efficiently adapted for various downstream tasks without extensive retraining or fine-tuning of the base model. These include:
 - **Task-Oriented Sampling:** For high-level goal-directed behaviors.
 - **Conditional Inpainting:** For precise, fine-grained control over specific joint trajectories or keyframe inbetweening.
 - **Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning:** For training robust, task-specific controllers that can steer the base A-MDM to achieve complex objectives while maintaining motion quality and diversity.
5. **Demonstrate Competitive Performance:** Conduct comprehensive experiments to quantitatively and qualitatively validate A-MDM's effectiveness in terms of motion quality, diversity, realism, and responsiveness against state-of-the-art auto-regressive and, where applicable, space-time models.

By achieving these objectives, the authors aim to introduce a robust, efficient, and versatile framework for interactive character animation that harnesses the advanced generative capabilities of diffusion models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers around the **Auto-Regressive Motion Diffusion Model (A-MDM)**, designed to generate high-fidelity kinematic motion sequences frame-by-frame in real-time. The framework then integrates various control strategies to adapt this base model for diverse interactive tasks.

#### 3.1. Auto-Regressive Motion Diffusion Model (A-MDM) Core

- **Motion Representation:** A character's state `x_f` at frame `f` is represented by a comprehensive set of features: planar linear velocity of the root, angular velocity around the up-axis, joint positions, joint velocities, and 6D joint orientations. These features are defined in the character's local coordinate frame.
- **Pipeline Overview:** A-MDM predicts the next state `x_f` given the previous state `x_{f-1}`. The training procedure follows the principles of Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM) [Ho et al. 2020]:
 - **Forward Diffusion Process:** Gaussian noise is progressively added to the ground truth `x_f` over `T` steps, transforming it into an isotropic Gaussian distribution `x_T_f`.
 - **Backward Reconstruction Process (Denoising):** A neural network `p_θ` (the A-MDM itself) is trained to iteratively remove this noise. At each diffusion step `t`, the network takes the noisy target state `x_t_f`, the previous character state `x_{f-1}`, and a time embedding `e_t` as input. Its objective is to predict the noise vector `ε_t_f` that was added.
 - **Loss Function:** A simple Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss is applied between the predicted noise `^ε_t_f` and the ground truth noise `ε_t_f`.
- **Inference:** During inference, the process starts from pure Gaussian noise `ε_T_f`. The denoising network iteratively predicts and removes noise, gradually reconstructing `x_f` over `T` steps. This `^x_f` then becomes the `x_{f-1}` for the next frame, enabling auto-regressive generation.

#### 3.2. Key Design Decisions for Real-time Performance

- **Lightweight Network Architecture:** Unlike prior work often using computationally expensive Transformers, A-MDM employs a streamlined architecture based on **Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs)**. It consists of 10 fully-connected layers, each with 1024 hidden units, SiLU activations, and layer normalization. This simplicity significantly reduces computational cost.
- **Fewer Denoising Steps:** A critical optimization for real-time performance is using a significantly smaller number of diffusion steps `T` during inference (e.g., 10-50 steps) compared to the 1000 steps commonly used in DDPM. This is viable because the output dimension for a single frame is much smaller than an entire motion sequence, allowing effective denoising with fewer iterations.

#### 3.3. Drift Mitigation via Scheduled Sampling

- **Problem:** Auto-regressive models are prone to error accumulation and "drift" over long sequences.
- **Solution:** During training, A-MDM incorporates **scheduled sampling**. Instead of always using ground-truth `x_{f-1}` (teacher forcing), it occasionally uses the model's own previously predicted state `^x_{f-1}` (student forcing) as input for subsequent frame prediction. This forces the model to learn to recover from its own errors, improving long-horizon stability.

#### 3.4. Control Strategies for Downstream Tasks

Once the base A-MDM is trained, it acts as a flexible generative prior that can be steered for various tasks:

- **Random Sampling:** Generates diverse, unbounded motions without external constraints, showcasing the model's inherent motion manifold.
- **Task-Oriented Sampling (Beam Search):** For goal-directed tasks, multiple candidate future trajectories are generated by sampling from A-MDM. A user-defined score function (e.g., distance to target) evaluates these candidates, and the first frame of the best-scoring trajectory is chosen. This is repeated for subsequent frames.
- **Conditional Inpainting:** This technique allows users to specify precise constraints on the generated motion:
 - **Spatial Inpainting:** Users define desired trajectories or positions for specific joints (e.g., root, head) via a binary mask. During each denoising step, the noisy output is explicitly updated with these user-specified values, forcing adherence.
 - **Temporal Inpainting (Keyframe Inbetweening):** For generating transitions between sparse keyframes, the denoising process is initialized with the target keyframe, but the starting diffusion step `t_0` is varied based on proximity to the keyframe. Closer to the keyframe, fewer denoising steps are applied, ensuring closer adherence, while earlier frames allow more flexibility for natural transitions.
- **Hierarchical Control (Reinforcement Learning):** For more robust and flexible task completion, a task-specific high-level controller is trained using PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization). This controller does not directly control the character's joints but instead predicts **residual vectors `a_t_f`** for different denoising steps. These residuals perturb the A-MDM's output (`^x_t_f+1` is modified by `w_t * a_t_f`), effectively "steering" the denoising process towards desired task objectives. An annealing schedule `w_t` gradually attenuates perturbations at later denoising steps to prevent artifacts.

This multi-faceted methodology combines the strengths of diffusion models with architectural and training innovations for real-time performance, and a rich set of control strategies for practical interactive character animation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling findings across several experiments, demonstrating the effectiveness and advantages of A-MDM:

#### 4.1. Superior Generative Performance for Auto-Regressive Models

- **Quantitative Metrics:** A-MDM consistently outperforms prior VAE-based auto-regressive models (MVAE, HuMoR) and non-generative models (NSM) across various datasets (AMASS, 100STYLE, LaFAN1) in random sampling and motion continuation tasks.
 - **Diversity (APD ↑):** A-MDM achieves significantly higher Average Pairwise Distance (APD), indicating its ability to generate a wider variety of motions from similar initial states. For instance, on 100STYLE, A-MDM achieved an APD of 102.52±1.17 compared to HuMoR's 68.25±1.45.
 - **Fidelity (ADE ↓, FDE ↓):** A-MDM exhibits lower Average Displacement Error (ADE) and Final Displacement Error (FDE), signifying that its generated motions are closer to the distribution of real motion data. On AMASS, A-MDM achieved ADE 10.40±0.66 and FDE 21.12±1.16, considerably better than MVAE (ADE 24.42±1.04, FDE 42.22±3.64).
 - **Physical Plausibility (Bone.Err ↓, Pen.Freq ↓, Pen.Dist ↓, FS ↓, Jnt.Accel →):** A-MDM produces motions with fewer artifacts. It shows lower Bone Length Error, significantly reduced Foot Penetration Frequency and Distance, and competitive Foot Sliding (FS) and Joint Acceleration (Jnt.Accel) compared to baselines. This indicates more robust and natural-looking motions.

#### 4.2. Real-time Efficiency and Denoising Step Analysis

- **Inference Speed:** A-MDM successfully achieves real-time inference speeds. For example, with 40 denoising steps on 100STYLE, it generates a frame in approximately 0.021 seconds, which is well within real-time requirements (e.g., 30 FPS demands ~0.033s per frame).
- **Optimal Denoising Steps:** Experiments exploring the impact of the number of diffusion steps (T) reveal a trade-off between motion diversity/quality and inference time. While more steps generally improve diversity, the gains attenuate quickly. Around 40-50 steps were identified as an effective balance, providing robust quality without excessive computational cost.

#### 4.3. Effectiveness of Control Strategies

- **Random Sampling:** A-MDM demonstrates the ability to generate diverse, long-horizon, plausible motions, including transitions from static to dynamic behaviors (walking, running, jumping) and context-consistent motions (e.g., hopping from a hopping pose).
- **Task-Oriented Sampling:** For simple tasks like target reaching, A-MDM-generated trajectories are more direct and efficient than those produced by VAE-based models (e.g., HuMoR), which often exhibited "wandering" or circling behaviors.
- **Conditional Inpainting:** This method proves highly effective for precise control:
 - **Spatial Inpainting:** A-MDM can synthesize coherent full-body motions that accurately follow user-specified trajectories for individual joints (e.g., root, head) without additional fine-tuning.
 - **Temporal Inpainting (Keyframe Inbetweening):** A-MDM generates natural and realistic transitions between sparse user-defined keyframes, showcasing its ability to interpolate smoothly while adhering to target poses.
- **Hierarchical Control via Reinforcement Learning:**
 - **Superior Task Performance:** High-level controllers trained on top of A-MDM achieve consistently higher returns in complex tasks (Target Reaching, Joystick Control, Path Following) compared to hierarchical controllers built on other auto-regressive models (MVAE, HuMoR).
 - **Preservation of Diversity:** Crucially, the hierarchical control approach for A-MDM allows the generation of diverse motion strategies for the same task, unlike some VAE-based hierarchical methods that might lead to deterministic, less varied motions.
 - **Flexibility vs. Precision:** Hierarchical control offers greater flexibility than strict inpainting; while inpainting adheres exactly to specified trajectories (potentially leading to unnatural full-body motions if the trajectory is awkward), RL allows the character to deviate slightly to produce more natural, plausible movements.

#### 4.4. Comparison with Space-time Diffusion Models

- **Trade-offs:** When evaluated on unconditional generation on HumanML3D, space-time diffusion models (MDM, GMD) generally show stronger performance (lower FID, better Diversity, lower Foot Skat. Ratio). This is an expected trade-off, as A-MDM prioritizes real-time performance and unbounded length generation using a lighter architecture and fewer steps, whereas space-time models can optimize for overall sequence quality.
- **Unique Advantage:** A-MDM's auto-regressive nature allows it to generate long-horizon motions even when trained on datasets primarily composed of short clips, a challenge for space-time models which struggle to generalize beyond their maximum training sequence length.

Overall, the findings strongly support A-MDM as a highly effective and versatile framework for interactive character control, demonstrating significant advancements in motion quality, diversity, efficiency, and controllability compared to previous auto-regressive approaches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Auto-Regressive Motion Diffusion Models (A-MDM) represents a significant advancement in the field of character animation and interactive AI, with substantial potential impact across various domains:

1. **Enabling High-Fidelity Interactive Character Control:** The most immediate and profound impact is bridging the gap between the high-quality generative capabilities of diffusion models and the stringent real-time requirements of interactive applications. A-MDM provides a robust framework for game developers, VR/AR creators, and simulation engineers to implement characters that move with unprecedented realism and diversity while remaining fully responsive to user input. This moves beyond pre-canned animations or less diverse generative methods.

2. **New Paradigm for Motion Synthesis:** By successfully adapting diffusion models for auto-regressive, frame-by-frame generation, this work introduces a novel paradigm for motion synthesis. It challenges the prevailing focus on space-time models for diffusion-based motion and demonstrates that efficiency and interactivity can be achieved without sacrificing substantial motion quality and diversity, particularly when compared to other auto-regressive generative models.

3. **Versatile and Adaptable Control Framework:** The suite of control strategies (random sampling, task-oriented sampling, conditional inpainting, and hierarchical reinforcement learning) built on top of a single pre-trained A-MDM makes the system exceptionally versatile. This flexibility means a single base model can be reused for numerous downstream tasks without requiring extensive retraining or fine-tuning, significantly streamlining development workflows in animation and game design.

4. **Advancements in Reinforcement Learning for Character Control:** The integration of A-MDM with hierarchical reinforcement learning offers a powerful approach for training intelligent character controllers. It demonstrates that RL policies can effectively "steer" a complex generative model like a diffusion model, leading to more robust and flexible task-oriented behaviors while preserving the inherent diversity of the generative prior. This could pave the way for more sophisticated and generalized character AI.

5. **Practical Industry Applications:** The involvement of Xmov and NVIDIA, combined with the focus on real-time performance, highlights the strong potential for industrial adoption. A-MDM could become a foundational technology for next-generation character animation tools, powering more lifelike NPCs in video games, more immersive avatars in virtual reality, and more realistic simulations. The ability to generate unbounded motions from short clips (as shown in section 7.7) is also highly valuable in production environments.

6. **Future Research Directions:** This work opens several avenues for future research:
 - **Integration of Acceleration Techniques:** Exploring the integration of advanced diffusion model acceleration techniques (e.g., Consistency Models) into A-MDM could further boost real-time performance.
 - **Robustness Improvements:** Enhancing A-MDM's robustness to extreme or unnatural user controls to mitigate artifacts like foot sliding or jittering.
 - **Multi-frame Auto-regressive Models:** Investigating the use of multi-frame predictions within the auto-regressive framework to improve temporal coherence and tackle even more complex tasks.
 - **Broader Applications:** Extending the auto-regressive diffusion concept to other continuous, real-time generation tasks beyond human motion.

In conclusion, "Interactive Character Control with Auto-Regressive Motion Diffusion Models" is a landmark paper that not only introduces a highly effective method for interactive character animation but also demonstrates the adaptability and power of diffusion models in a domain previously dominated by different generative paradigms. Its impact will likely be felt across both academic research and practical industry applications, setting a new standard for responsive and realistic virtual characters.
