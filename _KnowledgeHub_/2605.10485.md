# VEGA: Visual Encoder Grounding Alignment for Spatially-Aware Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> VEGA (Visual Encoder Grounding Alignment) enhances the spatial reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation by aligning the VLA's visual encoder output with features from a 3D-aware teacher model. This method achieved a new state-of-the-art on the RoboTwin 2.0 benchmark with 67.5% on Easy and 30.7% on Hard tasks, surpassing previous baselines by up to 3.3%, and improved performance in real-world robotic tasks without adding inference overhead.

## Problem

- VLA models often lack precise 3D spatial awareness for robotic manipulation due to their visual backbones being primarily pre-trained on 2D image data.
- Explicit spatial grounding methods, while effective, introduce practical challenges such as hardware dependency, sensor noise, error propagation, and the high cost of 3D annotated datasets.
- Existing implicit spatial grounding methods rely on empirical layer searches and conflate geometric structure with linguistic semantics by aligning visual features at the LLM-token level.

## Method

- **VEGA** introduces a ==representation alignment framework== that directly aligns the output of the VLA's ==DINOv2 visual encoder== with features from a frozen, 3D-aware ==DINOv2-FiT3D teacher== model.
- A lightweight projector (==LayerNorm + two-layer MLP==) transforms student features to bridge representational gaps, minimizing additional computational complexity.
- Spatial alignment is enforced during training using a ==cosine similarity loss== applied to patch-level features, combined with the VLA's standard action prediction loss.

## Results

- **VEGA** achieved **state-of-the-art** performance on the RoboTwin 2.0 benchmark, with **67.5%** average success on Easy tasks and **30.7%** on Hard tasks, outperforming the strongest baseline (OFT+SF) by **+3.3%** and **+2.9%** respectively.
- The method demonstrated accelerated policy learning, achieving performance comparable to OpenVLA-OFT at **60k steps in just 10k steps**, and improved data efficiency, showing a **+10%** absolute gain in data-scarce settings.
- Consistent outperformance was observed in real-world robotic manipulation experiments on the AgileX ALOHA platform across various single-arm and bimanual tasks, particularly in scenarios requiring precise 3D spatial reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Injecting spatial awareness at the visual encoder output level, prior to linguistic entanglement, provides a purer and more geometrically interpretable spatial signal for VLA models.
> - The DINOv2-FiT3D teacher model, fine-tuned with multi-view consistent 3D Gaussian Splatting, offers architecturally compatible and spatially consistent patch-level features ideal for supervising VLA encoders.
> - An optimal balance for the alignment loss coefficient (λ=0.1) is crucial, enhancing both learning speed and final performance by correctly weighting spatial supervision and task-specific learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation requires precise spatial reasoning, including understanding object depths, relative positions, and geometric configurations in a 3D environment. Recent advancements in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have enabled robots to follow complex instructions by leveraging large-scale vision-language pretraining. However, the visual backbones of many contemporary VLA models, such as those employing SigLIP or DINOv2, are predominantly pretrained on 2D image data. This 2D-centric pretraining typically results in visual representations that lack explicit 3D geometric supervision, limiting the models' inherent spatial awareness. Consequently, while these models demonstrate proficiency in tasks relying on coarse 2D appearance cues, they often exhibit reduced generalization and performance in manipulation tasks that demand fine-grained spatial understanding, such as estimating object height, inferring precise relative positions, or adapting to novel viewpoints.

To address this deficiency, existing research generally pursues two categories of solutions:
1. **Explicit Spatial Grounding:** This approach involves augmenting VLA model inputs with explicit 3D information, such as depth maps or point clouds derived from additional sensors, or estimated from 2D images using depth estimators. Some methods also fine-tune VLAs on datasets enriched with human-annotated 3D bounding boxes or spatial coordinates. While effective, these methods present practical challenges, including hardware dependency, susceptibility to sensor noise, potential for error propagation from depth estimators, and the high cost and scarcity of large-scale 3D annotated datasets.
2. **Implicit Spatial Grounding:** This category aims to instill 3D awareness without requiring additional sensors or annotations at inference time. It typically involves integrating features from frozen 3D-aware foundation models (e.g., VGGT) into VLA representations. Techniques in this area include representation alignment (e.g., Spatial Forcing, ROCKET, GLaD) or feature fusion (e.g., Evo-0). While this approach offers a hardware-agnostic alternative, existing implicit methods exhibit two primary limitations:
 - **Empirical Layer Search:** They often rely on heuristic layer search to identify optimal intermediate layers for feature alignment, making the process sensitive to hyperparameters and potentially limiting generalizability across different VLA architectures or tasks.
 - **LLM-level Token Alignment:** Alignment is commonly performed on visual tokens at the Large Language Model (LLM) level, where visual features have already been integrated with linguistic context. This entanglement can conflate geometric structure with linguistic semantics, reducing geometric interpretability and potentially hindering precise spatial reasoning.

The presented work, VEGA, positions itself within the implicit spatial grounding paradigm. It aims to overcome the identified limitations of existing implicit methods by introducing a more principled and geometrically interpretable approach to injecting spatial awareness into VLA visual encoders.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the spatial reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation by introducing a method for implicit spatial grounding that is both effective and addresses the limitations of prior approaches.

The motivation for developing VEGA stems from several identified shortcomings in the current landscape of VLA models and their spatial grounding strategies:

1. **Inadequate 3D Spatial Awareness in VLA Models:** VLA models, despite their advancements in instruction following, generally lack accurate 3D spatial awareness due to their visual backbones being predominantly pretrained on 2D image data without explicit 3D geometric supervision. This limitation impedes their performance in fine-grained robotic manipulation tasks that necessitate understanding object depths, spatial configurations, and viewpoint variations. For instance, tasks requiring precise object placement, obstacle avoidance, or adapting to new perspectives often fail when only 2D cues are available.

2. **Limitations of Explicit Spatial Grounding:** While explicit methods, such as augmenting inputs with depth sensors or 3D annotations, can inject spatial information, they introduce practical drawbacks. These include increased hardware complexity, susceptibility to sensor noise, propagation of estimation errors, and the high cost and scarcity of comprehensive 3D annotated datasets, making them less scalable or generalizable for real-world robotics.

3. **Limitations of Existing Implicit Spatial Grounding Methods:** Current implicit spatial grounding approaches, though appealing for their hardware-agnostic nature, possess fundamental flaws:
 - **Empirical Layer Search:** The reliance on empirical searches to determine optimal alignment layers is a heuristic process. Such searches are sensitive to hyperparameter choices, time-consuming, and may not generalize well across different VLA architectures or manipulation tasks, leading to suboptimal or brittle solutions.
 - **Linguistic Entanglement at LLM-level Alignment:** Performing feature alignment at the LLM token level, where visual features are already fused with linguistic context, compromises geometric interpretability. The spatial structure becomes conflated with semantic associations, making it difficult for the model to purely disentangle and leverage precise geometric information crucial for manipulation. This late-stage alignment means spatial awareness is injected after its pure geometric properties have been diluted or merged with semantic meaning.

To overcome these issues, the key motivation behind VEGA is to establish a more direct and principled method for spatial grounding. The authors propose to:
- **Inject spatial awareness at an earlier stage:** By aligning features at the output level of the VLA's visual encoder, before any linguistic processing or entanglement occurs, VEGA aims to provide a purer and more geometrically interpretable spatial signal.
- **Leverage a robust 3D-aware teacher model:** The selection of DINOv2-FiT3D as the spatial teacher is motivated by its demonstrated ability to produce spatially consistent and semantically rich patch-level features. These features are acquired through a fine-tuning process with multi-view consistent 3D Gaussian Splatting supervision, making DINOv2-FiT3D a suitable source of explicit 3D knowledge.
- **Ensure efficiency and practicality:** The alignment mechanism is designed to be lightweight and introduces no additional computational overhead during inference, making it suitable for real-time robotic applications.

In essence, the research is motivated by the need for VLA models to possess robust 3D spatial reasoning for effective robotic manipulation, combined with the recognition that current approaches either incur significant practical costs or dilute the geometric signal through suboptimal alignment strategies. VEGA aims to provide a more direct, interpretable, and efficient solution to this fundamental challenge.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VEGA (Visual Encoder Grounding Alignment) is designed as a representation alignment framework to instill spatial awareness into VLA models. The methodology focuses on directly aligning the output of the VLA's visual encoder with features from a 3D-aware foundation model.

**VLA Model and Teacher Model Selection:**
The base VLA model used is OpenVLA-OFT, which incorporates a visual backbone composed of a DINOv2 encoder (ε\_DINO) and a SigLIP encoder (ε\_SigLIP), alongside a Llama 2 7B LLM. VEGA specifically targets the DINOv2 branch for spatial alignment, as it is considered responsible for fine-grained spatial and structural representations.
The spatial teacher model is a frozen DINOv2-FiT3D encoder (ε\_FiT3D). DINOv2-FiT3D is a DINOv2 model fine-tuned using a two-stage framework that leverages multi-view consistent 3D Gaussian Splatting supervision. This process injects 3D spatial awareness into the 2D visual foundation model, producing dense, patch-level features that are spatially consistent and semantically rich.

**Feature Extraction and Alignment:**
Given an input image *I*, patch-level visual tokens are extracted from both the student and teacher encoders:
- **Student Features (F\_DINO):** These are extracted from the second-to-last transformer block of the VLA's DINOv2 encoder (ε\_DINO). This layer is chosen to bypass FiLM language conditioning, ensuring that the extracted features primarily represent vision-specific information before linguistic entanglement.
- **Teacher Features (F\_FiT3D):** These are extracted from the final transformer block of the frozen DINOv2-FiT3D encoder (ε\_FiT3D). This layer is selected because it contains the most complete and refined 3D spatial representations from the teacher. The use of the teacher's strongest signal to supervise the student's working layer is a practice in knowledge distillation.
Both F\_DINO and F\_FiT3D are of dimension *N* x *d*, where *N* is the number of patch tokens and *d* is the feature dimension (1024).

**Lightweight Projector:**
To bridge the potential representational gap between the student's and teacher's feature spaces, a lightweight projector (ϕ) is introduced. This projector consists of a LayerNorm layer followed by a two-layer MLP with GELU activation. The projector transforms the student's features:
`ˆF_DINO = ϕ(F_DINO)`
The projector has approximately 2.1 million parameters, designed to be computationally efficient.

**Alignment Loss:**
The spatial alignment is enforced using a cosine similarity loss. This loss measures the average cosine distance between the projected DINOv2 features (`ˆF_DINO`) and the DINOv2-FiT3D features (`F_FiT3D`) across all patch tokens:
`L_align = (1/N) * Σ (1 - (ˆF_DINO_i · F_FiT3D_i) / (||ˆF_DINO_i|| ||F_FiT3D_i||))`
This loss encourages the VLA's visual encoder to produce representations that are geometrically similar to those of the 3D-aware teacher.

**Overall Training Objective:**
The final training objective for VEGA combines the standard action prediction loss (`L_action`) of the VLA model with the proposed alignment loss (`L_align`):
`L_VEGA = L_action + λ * L_align`
Here, `λ` is a scalar coefficient that balances the contribution of spatial alignment against the primary task of action prediction. Through ablation studies, `λ = 0.1` was identified as an optimal value.

**Inference Time Efficiency:**
A key design principle of VEGA is to introduce no additional computational overhead during inference. At inference time, both the lightweight projector (ϕ) and the DINOv2-FiT3D teacher encoder (ε\_FiT3D) are discarded. The VLA model then operates identically to its standard form, but with an enhanced visual encoder due to the spatially-aware training.

**Implementation Details:**
The models are trained for 100k steps on 4 NVIDIA H100 GPUs using a batch size of 4 per GPU. LoRA fine-tuning with a rank of 32 is applied to the LLM backbone. Image augmentation, proprioception input, and FiLM language conditioning are utilized as per the OpenVLA-OFT protocol. Baselines are reproduced under identical training configurations for fair comparison.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of VEGA involved extensive experiments across simulation benchmarks and real-world robotic manipulation tasks, supported by ablation studies and feature analyses.

**Motivation for DINOv2-FiT3D Features:**
- **Feature Visualization:** PCA visualizations comparing original DINOv2 and DINOv2-FiT3D features on robotic data (RoboTwin 2.0, Bridge Dataset v2) demonstrated that DINOv2-FiT3D produces more spatially consistent and structured feature maps with clearer object boundaries. This suggests that the 3D spatial awareness from FiT3D transfers effectively to robotic domains.
- **VLA Performance Improvement:** A controlled pretraining experiment within the OpenVLA framework showed that VLA models initialized with DINOv2-FiT3D as the visual encoder consistently and substantially outperformed those with the standard DINOv2 encoder on the "Move Playingcard Away" task. This finding indicated that richer spatial features at the encoder level directly benefit downstream manipulation performance.

**Simulation Experiments (RoboTwin 2.0 Benchmark):**
- **Main Results:** On the RoboTwin 2.0 benchmark, VEGA achieved an average success rate of 67.5% on "Easy" tasks and 30.7% on "Hard" tasks, establishing a new state-of-the-art among implicit spatial grounding methods. VEGA surpassed the strongest baseline, OFT+SF, by 3.3% on Easy and 2.9% on Hard settings. Consistent improvements were observed across most tasks compared to the base OpenVLA-OFT model, with particularly notable gains on tasks requiring precise spatial localization, such as "Move Card Away," "Turn Switch," and "Place Shoes." The pronounced advantage on "Hard" settings, which include severe domain randomization, suggests that visual encoder-level spatial grounding leads to more robust representations under distribution shift.
- **Training Efficiency:** VEGA demonstrated accelerated policy learning, achieving a success rate at 10k training steps comparable to OpenVLA-OFT at 60k steps on the "Move Playingcard Away" task. Both models converged around 80k–90k steps, but VEGA converged to a higher performance plateau, indicating that injected spatial awareness enhances both learning speed and final performance.
- **Data Efficiency:** VEGA consistently outperformed OpenVLA-OFT across varying fractions of demonstration data (25%, 50%, 75%, 100%) on the "Move Playingcard Away" task. In the most data-scarce setting, VEGA showed a 10% absolute improvement over the base model, suggesting that spatial alignment provides a strong inductive bias that partially compensates for limited training data.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Effect of Alignment Loss Coefficient (λ):** An ablation on the alignment loss coefficient λ revealed that λ = 0.1 yielded the best overall performance. Deviations (λ = 0.2 or λ = 0.05) resulted in performance degradation, suggesting that an appropriate balance between spatial alignment supervision and task learning is crucial.
- **Spatial Teacher Model:** The choice of spatial teacher significantly impacted performance. Removing the teacher entirely resulted in lower performance. Using VGGT-at-encoder as a teacher led to a catastrophic drop in performance on "Hard" settings (0.04%), attributed to architectural mismatch between VGGT's scene-level geometry and the patch-level features required for ViT token alignment. In contrast, DINOv2-FiT3D as the teacher model achieved the best performance, particularly on "Hard" settings (0.43%), due to its architecturally homogeneous patch-level features derived from multi-view consistent 3D Gaussian Splatting supervision.

**Real-World Experiments (AgileX ALOHA Platform):**
- **Performance:** VEGA consistently outperformed both OpenVLA-OFT and OFT+SF across four real-world manipulation tasks, including single-arm tasks ("Close Laptop," "Handover Cucumber") and bimanual tasks ("Pick Dual Carrots into Dual Bowls," "Pick Dual Flowers into Vase"). The performance gains were most notable on spatially demanding tasks like "Close Laptop" and "Pick Dual Flowers into Vase," which require reasoning about 3D articulated structures and precise depth estimation for narrow insertions.
- **Generalization:** These results validated VEGA’s robust generalization to physical robot settings, confirming the practical benefits of visual encoder-level spatial grounding.

**Feature Representation Analysis (Appendix B.1):**
Qualitative PCA visualizations and quantitative Adjusted Rand Index (ARI) comparisons of patch-level features indicated that DINOv2-FiT3D produced more spatially structured features than original DINOv2. Notably, the DINOv2 branch of OpenVLA-7B, after VLA training, exhibited a feature structure closer to DINOv2-FiT3D than to the original DINOv2. This suggested that VLA training itself inherently pushes the encoder towards more spatially organized representations, further supporting the use of DINOv2-FiT3D as an effective spatial teacher.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of VEGA represents an advancement in the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation by addressing a fundamental limitation: the lack of precise 3D spatial awareness in visual representations.

**Significance:**
- **Principled Spatial Grounding:** VEGA introduces a more principled approach to implicit spatial grounding by performing alignment directly at the visual encoder output level. This strategy ensures that spatial awareness is injected before linguistic entanglement occurs, offering a more geometrically interpretable and pure signal compared to previous methods that aligned at LLM-level tokens.
- **Overcoming Prior Limitations:** The framework effectively resolves two key issues identified in existing implicit spatial grounding techniques: the reliance on empirical layer search and the conflation of geometric structure with linguistic semantics. By leveraging a 3D-aware teacher model (DINOv2-FiT3D) that provides architecturally compatible, patch-level spatial features, VEGA eliminates the need for exhaustive hyperparameter tuning for alignment layers.
- **Enhanced Performance and Robustness:** The consistent outperformance of VEGA over existing implicit spatial grounding baselines in both simulation (RoboTwin 2.0) and real-world manipulation tasks establishes a new state-of-the-art. This indicates that grounding spatial awareness at the visual encoder stage leads to more accurate, robust, and generalizable manipulation policies, especially under domain randomization (Hard settings) and in physically demanding real-world scenarios.
- **Efficiency:** The design of VEGA, where the alignment projector and teacher model are discarded at inference time, ensures that there is no additional computational overhead. This aspect is critical for real-time robotic applications where latency is a significant concern.
- **Improved Training and Data Efficiency:** VEGA demonstrates accelerated policy learning and more effective utilization of available training data, reducing the computational resources and data required to achieve high performance. This contributes to the broader goal of democratizing access to capable robotic systems.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Advancements in Robotic Autonomy:** Improved spatial reasoning can lead to more capable and reliable robots for a wide range of applications. This includes sophisticated manufacturing, logistics, and service robotics where precise interaction with objects in 3D space is paramount.
- **Assistive Robotics:** Robots with enhanced spatial understanding could provide better assistance in domestic environments, healthcare settings, or for individuals with physical disabilities, performing complex tasks with greater accuracy and safety.
- **Human-Robot Collaboration:** More spatially aware robots could collaborate more effectively and safely with humans, interpreting complex spatial cues from their environment and adapting their actions accordingly.
- **Foundation Model Development:** This work provides insights into how 2D-pretrained vision backbones can be effectively enhanced with 3D understanding, potentially influencing the design and pretraining strategies for future vision-language foundation models in robotics.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge a limitation in VEGA's current reliance on DINOv2-FiT3D as the spatial teacher. The transferability of this teacher model might degrade in highly unstructured or out-of-distribution environments that significantly differ from its training data. Future work could explore stronger, more general purpose 3D-aware teacher models or develop methods to enhance the robustness of the teacher itself. Additionally, extending the visual encoder alignment paradigm to a broader range of VLA architectures and exploring its applicability in even more complex, dynamic, or human-interactive robotic scenarios remains a promising direction for further research.
