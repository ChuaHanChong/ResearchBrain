# Mind Your Steps: A General Learning Framework for Accurate Humanoid Foothold Tracking

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Politecnico di Milano, Tongji University, and Technische Universität Darmstadt developed a general learning framework for accurate 3D humanoid foothold tracking, demonstrating robust performance on complex terrains in simulation and a 93.08% real-world success rate on a Booster T1 humanoid by mitigating state estimation noise.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle with precise and robust locomotion in complex, dynamic real-world environments due to sensitivity of model-based methods and implicit foot placement in velocity-commanded policies.
- Existing explicit foothold-tracking policies are often limited to simulation, rely on unrealistic state assumptions (e.g., perfect contact flags, precise localization), or lack generality and modularity for real-world deployment.
- The inherent mismatch between continuous velocity commands and discrete foot placement decisions often leads to degraded tracking accuracy and potentially unsafe behaviors.

## Method

- Introduced a lightweight, model-free framework (**π_FT**) for training general-purpose 3D humanoid foothold-tracking policies using an asymmetric ==Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)== algorithm.
- Designed a novel ==foothold target representation== expressed in the current stance-foot frame, which remains constant during a swing phase, reducing target variability and eliminating reliance on precise base state estimation.
- Developed a procedural ==goal sampler== for training to generate feasible 3D foothold targets across various terrains and a reward function balancing tracking accuracy, natural locomotion, and sufficient swing foot clearance.

## Results

- In simulation, the foothold-tracking policy (π_FT) consistently outperformed velocity-tracking baselines, reaching targets significantly faster and maintaining a **100%** success rate on narrow bridges (0.15m width) where baselines achieved only **36.6%**.
- The `π_FT` policy successfully traversed complex simulated environments including cluttered workspaces (**90-99%** success rates), straight and spiral stairs, and continuous ramps, demonstrating robust generalization to terrains not explicitly trained on.
- Achieved a **93.08%** success rate in real-world deployment on a Booster T1 humanoid, stepping on vision-based targets in zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, validating the practical viability and robustness of the approach.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing foothold targets in the stance-foot frame significantly enhances robustness to noise in base state estimation, making policies suitable for real-world deployment without perfect localization.
> - A general-purpose, learning-based low-level foothold controller can seamlessly integrate with diverse high-level planners, allowing for modular robotics system design for complex loco-manipulation tasks.
> - Model-free reinforcement learning, when combined with carefully designed observation spaces, goal sampling, and reward structures, can yield highly generalizable and accurate 3D locomotion skills on challenging and varied terrains.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

Enabling humanoid robots to operate autonomously and reliably in complex, dynamic real-world environments is a significant challenge in robotics. A fundamental requirement for such deployment is the ability to navigate safely, accurately, and robustly, particularly in scenarios involving obstacles, human interaction, uneven terrain, slopes, and stairs. In these conditions, stable locomotion is highly dependent on precise foot placement, as valid footholds can be sparse and constrained.

**Challenges in Humanoid Locomotion:**
Traditional model-based methods for legged locomotion, while offering interpretability and high performance, often suffer from sensitivity to model discrepancies and violations of modeling assumptions, especially in real-world scenarios where accurate terrain modeling is difficult. This can lead to unreliable or unsafe behavior.

**Existing Paradigms: Velocity-Commanded vs. Foothold-Tracking:**
To overcome the limitations of model-based control, model-free Reinforcement Learning (RL) has gained prominence. Many successful RL-based approaches in humanoid and quadrupedal locomotion utilize velocity-commanded policies, often integrated with vision pipelines that implicitly select footholds. While these methods have demonstrated remarkable robustness and generalization (e.g., agile dynamic behaviors like backflips), they lack explicit control over foot placement. This can result in degraded tracking accuracy, inefficiency in reaching targets, and potentially unsafe behaviors (e.g., stepping on unintended areas) dueising the mismatch between continuous velocity commands and discrete foot placement decisions.

Conversely, explicit foothold-commanded policies have emerged as an alternative, offering direct control over target foot poses. These approaches aim to enhance foot placement accuracy and enable more flexible gait generation and motion capabilities.

**Limitations of Current Foothold-Tracking Approaches:**
Despite their promise, existing foothold-tracking implementations have generally been limited in scope and practicality:
- Many have been studied exclusively in simulation, relying on unrealistic state assumptions (e.g., perfect binary ground contact flags, precise base state estimation).
- Some are restricted to simple movements like forward walking on flat terrain.
- Often, they are embedded as low-level components within specific, monolithic pipelines, making them tightly coupled to particular downstream tasks and lacking general-purpose utility or modularity.
- Their reliance on hard-to-estimate information in real-world observations (e.g., precise localization or contact flags) hinders direct deployment on physical hardware.

This research addresses these limitations by proposing a novel framework that aims to train general-purpose 3D foothold-tracking policies designed for real-world applicability and seamless integration with various high-level planners, without requiring unrealistic state assumptions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work is to achieve stable and accurate locomotion for humanoid robots using foothold-conditioned policies. The authors identify a gap in the current state of humanoid locomotion control where either robustness is achieved at the cost of precise foot placement (velocity-commanded policies) or precise foot placement is achieved with policies that are not generalizable or directly deployable in the real world.

The key objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Overcoming Limitations of Velocity-Commanded Policies:** The research is motivated by the inherent mismatch between continuous velocity commands and discrete foot placement, which leads to reduced tracking accuracy and efficiency in reaching targets. Explicit foothold control is sought to address these issues.
- **Addressing Deficiencies in Existing Foothold-Tracking Policies:** Current foothold-tracking policies are often limited by:
 - **Unrealistic State Assumptions:** They frequently rely on information (e.g., precise base state estimation, binary ground contact flags) that is difficult to obtain accurately in real-world environments. This compromises their real-world deployment.
 - **Lack of Generality and Modularity:** They are often studied in simulation, restricted to simple scenarios, or are part of staged pipelines tied to specific downstream tasks, preventing them from being general-purpose low-level controllers.
- **Developing a General-Purpose, Real-World Ready Framework:** The core objective is to propose a lightweight, model-free framework for training 3D foothold-tracking policies that are:
 - **Agnostic to Specific Terrains:** By dynamically providing footstep support through a goal sampler during training, the learned policy can generalize to various terrains.
 - **Independent of High-Level Planners:** The policy should function as a standalone low-level controller that can be seamlessly paired with diverse high-level foothold generators, promoting modularity.
 - **Robust to Real-World Challenges:** A new target representation is designed to mitigate issues arising from noisy and inaccurate pose estimation and foot contact estimation, making it suitable for direct real-world transfer.
 - **Enabling Omnidirectional Locomotion:** The policy should be able to track full 3D foothold targets, allowing for forward, backward, lateral, and rotational movements, as well as controlling swing foot height and orientation.

In essence, the work aims to bridge the gap between robust but imprecise velocity control and precise but limited foothold control, by creating a universally applicable and practical foothold-tracking policy for complex humanoid loco-manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework introduces a novel, lightweight, and model-free approach for training general-purpose 3D foothold-tracking policies designed for direct real-world transfer and modular integration with high-level planners.

**Problem Formulation:**
The problem is framed as a goal-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) task within a Markov Decision Process (MDP). The agent receives partial observations `o_t` and a goal `g_t` at each timestep. The objective is to learn an optimal policy `π_ζ` that maximizes the expected discounted return by sampling actions `a_t` (joint position targets for a low-level PD controller).

**Goal and Observation Formulation:**
A critical aspect of the methodology is the formulation of foothold commands and observations:
- **Foothold Target Representation (`g_t`):** The goal vector `g_t` specifies the desired next left and right footholds. It includes 3D position offsets (`s_l_p_t`, `s_r_p_t`) and yaw orientations (`s_l_ψ_t`, `s_r_ψ_t`), all expressed **in the current stance foot frame**.
 - **Key Design Choice:** Expressing targets in the stance-foot frame means the target remains constant throughout each swing phase. This significantly reduces target variability compared to base-frame or specific foot-frame methods, eliminating the need for precise base state estimation (localization) to update targets in real-time.
- **Observation Space (`x_t`):** The policy observes proprioceptive information (`o_proprio,t` – base orientation and angular velocity, joint positions and velocities), the previous action (`a_t-1`), gait phase information (`ϕ_t` as sine/cosine components), and the foothold goal (`g_t`).
 - **Avoidance of Binary Contact Flags:** Unlike prior works, the framework explicitly avoids binary ground contact flags, which are difficult to estimate reliably on hardware.
 - **3D Control:** By commanding full 3D positions and yaw orientations, the policy is designed for general-purpose navigation on complex terrain, supporting omnidirectional locomotion and swing foot height/orientation control.

**Training Architecture:**
- **Algorithm:** Asymmetric Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is employed, building on the LocoMuJoCo library.
- **Agent Structure:** An asymmetric actor-critic architecture is used.
 - **Actor (`π_ζ`):** A neural network (512x256x128) receives the actor's observation `x_t`.
 - **Critic:** Shares the same network structure but receives a privileged state (including robot base linear velocity) for training, which is not available to the actor during deployment.
- **Action Space:** The policy outputs target joint positions for an underlying low-level PD controller.

**Goal Sampling (for Training):**
During training, a procedural **Goal Sampler (GS)** generates feasible 3D foothold targets:
- **Episode Initialization:** At the start of each episode, global locomotion heading (`θ_move`) and nominal feet orientation (`θ_feet`) are sampled and remain constant for consistency.
- **Target Generation at Gait Switch:** At every gait phase switch:
 1. **Planar Displacement:** A deviation angle `α` and step length `d` are sampled. The planar displacement (`Δp`) is computed by perturbing `θ_move`, ensuring the robot handles lateral and angular variations.
 2. **Target Yaw:** The swing foot yaw (`w_ψ_tgt`) is generated by perturbing `θ_feet` with an offset `β`.
 3. **Vertical Component:** A vertical offset `z` is sampled relative to the current stance foot height.
 4. **Terrain Adaptation:** Crucially, the simulation environment consists of moving pillars that automatically adjust their height to match the sampled `z`-coordinate of the target, guaranteeing a valid contact surface for training.
- **Target Transformation:** The world-frame target is transformed into the current stance foot frame and clipped to a feasible region to prevent leg crossing, forming the `g_t` vector.
- **"Hold-Still" Mode:** With a certain probability (`p_hold`), the GS enters a "hold-still" mode, commanding the robot to maintain a nominal stance width and align with the stance foot, freezing the gait phase.

**Reward Design:**
The reward function balances foothold tracking accuracy with natural locomotion style.
- **Foothold-Tracking Terms (`r_track`):**
 - `r_sw`: Rewards tracking of the 3D target foothold (planar, height, and yaw components) for the swinging foot. Planar and height components are decoupled with different sharpness coefficients.
 - `r_st`: For the stance phase, this term retains the `r_sw` value achieved at touchdown, incentivizing accurate placement.
- **Feet Swing Term (`r_feet`):** Encourages the swing foot to lift off the ground during a specified temporal window within each gait phase, ensuring adherence to the commanded gait schedule.
- **Swing Knee Height Term (`r_k`):** Rewards the robot for raising the knee of the swing leg above a certain threshold relative to the target foothold height, ensuring sufficient clearance for obstacles.
- **Penalization Terms:** Includes terms from the LocoMuJoCo framework to enforce natural locomotion (e.g., penalizing large action changes, foot velocity during ground contact, and deviations from a reference upper body pose and CoM height).

**Additional Training Details:**
- **Domain Randomization:** Extensive randomization is applied to link geometries, masses, friction, observation noise, and external force disturbances to enhance sim-to-real transfer.
- **Curriculum Learning:** A two-stage curriculum is used: initially, the agent trains on flat terrain to learn planar tracking, then fine-tuned on full 3D terrain.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of the proposed framework was evaluated through extensive experiments in both simulation and the real world, comparing the Foothold-Tracking (FT) policy (`π_FT`) against a state-of-the-art Velocity-Tracking (VT) baseline (`π_VT`) and an ablation study (`π_FT-R`).

**Simulation Experiments:**

- **Goal Reaching Task:**
 - **FT vs. VT:** `π_FT` consistently outperformed `π_VT` in reaching a target precisely and efficiently across different acceptance radii (0.2m, 0.1m, 0.05m). `π_FT` reached targets significantly faster, with a smaller increase in time-to-goal as the success threshold decreased (5.3% for FT vs. 18.9% for VT). This was attributed to the explicit foot control of `π_FT`, allowing for more aggressive and precise maneuvering.
 - **Frame Ablation (`π_FT-R`):** The `π_FT-R` policy (root-frame targets) showed slightly better accuracy than `π_FT` (stance-foot frame targets) under ideal, noise-free conditions (1.66cm vs. 2.15cm mean accuracy). However, `π_FT-R`'s performance degraded sharply even with small base state estimation noise (e.g., 3e-2m position noise and 2.0 deg yaw noise led to 2.07cm accuracy, 6e-2m and 3.0 deg to 2.86cm accuracy). In contrast, `π_FT` was unaffected by such noise, validating the robustness of the stance-foot frame formulation for real-world deployment.

- **Narrow Bridge Traversal:**
 - `π_FT` demonstrated superior motion accuracy in traversing narrow bridges of varying widths (0.15m to 0.25m) and lengths (2m to 4m). For less challenging configurations, `π_FT` completed traversal in less time.
 - In critical scenarios (0.15m width, narrower than robot's foot), `π_VT`'s success rate dropped drastically, while `π_FT` maintained almost the same performance (e.g., 100% success for FT vs. 36.6% for VT on 0.15m width, 4m length). This highlights the explicit, precise step-level control offered by `π_FT`, crucial for safety-critical, constrained navigation.

- **Traversing Cluttered Environments:**
 - `π_FT` was integrated with an Anytime Repairing A* (ARA*) planner to navigate a 6x6m workspace with 10, 15, or 20 traffic cones.
 - The system achieved high success rates (99% for 10 cones, 98% for 15 cones, 90% for 20 cones) with minimal falls (1% for 10 cones, 0% for 15/20 cones). Failures were primarily due to the planning algorithm not finding a feasible path within the time budget, rather than policy failure. This demonstrated modularity and robust integration with high-level planners.

- **Traversing Stairs (Straight and Spiral):**
 - **Straight Stairs (5 ascending, 5 descending):** `π_FT` was evaluated on staircases with varying step lengths (0.300m to 0.375m) and heights (0.01m to 0.13m). Performance was influenced by step geometry: shorter steps and higher risers increased riser-contact failures, while longer steps provided more safety margin but could lead to balance-related falls due to wider stance. The policy demonstrated robust 3D tracking.
 - **Spiral Stairs (rotating steps with -10° yaw offset per step):** This task validated the omnidirectional capabilities. Similar trends were observed, with longer steps generally reducing riser-contact failures. However, due to step rotation and non-uniform lateral/vertical distances, there was an optimal step length (around 0.35m), beyond which balance failures increased. These results confirmed the policy's ability for complex omnidirectional locomotion using only proprioception.

- **Traversing Ramps:**
 - `π_FT` generalized effectively to continuous ascending and descending ramps of varying inclinations, even though it was trained on dynamic pillars, not sloped surfaces.
 - Longer steps generally yielded higher success rates, up to an optimal length (around 0.35m). At high inclinations, short steps led to balance loss due to steep vertical rise, while excessively long steps increased balance failures by extending the gait cycle. This demonstrated the strong generalization capabilities of the framework.

**Real-World Deployment:**

- **Setup:** `π_FT` was deployed on a Booster T1 humanoid. A vision-based foothold tracking task was set up where the robot stepped on manually placed circular markers in a corridor. An onboard RealSense D455 camera detected targets, which were then transformed into the stance-foot frame using robot kinematics. A delay compensation mechanism adjusted the gait phase until sufficient foot clearance was detected.
- **Results:** Across 20 real-world trials, including forward and backward traversals, the robot achieved a **93.08% success rate** in stepping on designated foothold targets while maintaining stable locomotion.
- **Sources of Error:** Primary errors originated from motion-induced image blur affecting target detection, depth sensing artifacts, and the remaining sim-to-real gap despite extensive domain randomization.
- **Verification:** This experiment successfully verified the practical viability and zero-shot sim-to-real transfer of the `π_FT` controller, demonstrating its seamless integration with vision-based high-level planners and its potential for real-world loco-manipulation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a general learning framework for humanoid foothold tracking that addresses several critical limitations of prior work, presenting a notable contribution to the field of robotic locomotion.

The significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Enhanced Robustness and Accuracy:** By explicitly controlling 3D foothold placement and expressing targets in the stance-foot frame, the framework achieves demonstrably higher accuracy and agility compared to velocity-commanded policies. This precision is crucial for navigation in constrained and safety-critical environments.
- **Improved Real-World Deployability:** The core design choice of stance-foot frame targets effectively mitigates challenges posed by noisy and inaccurate base state estimation and eliminates reliance on hard-to-estimate binary ground contact flags, which are common issues in real-world deployments. This directly contributes to bridging the sim-to-real gap.
- **Generality and Modularity:** The developed policy acts as a standalone, low-level controller that is agnostic to specific terrains and high-level planners. Its demonstrated ability to integrate seamlessly with various upstream planners (e.g., goal-reaching, ARA*, deterministic staircase planners) highlights its modularity and general-purpose utility. This allows for flexible system design where planning and control modules can be developed independently.
- **Foundational Block for Complex Tasks:** The framework provides a robust foundation for more complex loco-manipulation tasks where precise foot placement is paramount. By enabling accurate and adaptive locomotion in challenging settings (cluttered environments, stairs, ramps), it paves the way for humanoids to perform intricate interactions with their environment.
- **Advancement in Learning-Based Locomotion:** The work advances model-free reinforcement learning by demonstrating how carefully designed observation spaces, goal sampling strategies, and reward functions can lead to powerful and generalizable locomotion skills, especially in scenarios requiring fine-grained contact control.
- **Empirical Validation on Hardware:** The successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and real-world deployment on a Booster T1 humanoid using an imperfect vision-based planner validates the practical viability and robustness of the approach, demonstrating its potential for real-world applications beyond simulation.

In summary, this framework offers a practical and robust solution for precise humanoid locomotion, moving the field closer to enabling autonomous humanoid operation in unstructured and dynamic real-world environments.
