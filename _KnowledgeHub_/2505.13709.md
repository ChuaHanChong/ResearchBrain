# Policy-Driven World Model Adaptation for Robust Offline Model-based Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Proposes a unified framework for offline model-based RL that dynamically adapts the world model alongside the policy under a single learning objective. Addresses the classical "objective mismatch" between maximum-likelihood WM training and policy-centric reward optimization by formulating the joint problem as a maximin and solving it via Stackelberg learning dynamics. Achieves state-of-the-art robustness on twelve noisy D4RL MuJoCo tasks and three stochastic Tokamak Control tasks.

## Problem

- Offline MBRL typically trains the world model by maximum likelihood, then optimizes a policy on the frozen model. This **objective mismatch** means the world model is not tuned for policy performance.
- Policies trained this way are brittle: small adversarial noise in deployment degrades performance substantially.
- Jointly optimizing WM and policy naively can collapse — the WM overfits to the current policy's state distribution.

## Method

- Reformulates offline MBRL as a ==maximin optimization== problem over the joint (world model, policy) pair under a ==unified robust objective==.
- Solves the maximin via ==Stackelberg learning dynamics==: the leader (WM) anticipates the follower's (policy) best response.
- **Policy-Driven WM Adaptation** dynamically updates the world model based on policy-induced state visitation, explicitly aligning WM fidelity with policy deployment needs.
- Provides theoretical analysis of convergence under the Stackelberg formulation.
- Keeps computation tractable by exploiting closed-form inner-loop approximations.

## Results

- Achieves state-of-the-art performance on **12** noisy D4RL MuJoCo benchmark tasks.
- Outperforms baselines on **3** stochastic Tokamak Control tasks.
- Demonstrates substantial robustness gains under adversarial environmental perturbations compared to two-stage MBRL baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Objective mismatch in MBRL is empirically significant — WM likelihood ≠ policy performance.
> - Joint WM-policy optimization via Stackelberg dynamics is a principled way to break the mismatch.
> - Robustness to deployment noise benefits from *aligning* the WM's training signal with the policy's downstream objective.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the field of offline reinforcement learning (RL), specifically focusing on offline model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL). Offline RL aims to train policies using static datasets of previously collected transitions without further interaction with the environment. MBRL explicitly learns a world model from this offline data, which serves as a surrogate simulator for policy optimization, offering advantages in data efficiency and potential generalization beyond the dataset.

However, existing offline MBRL methods typically employ a two-stage training process: initially learning a world model by maximizing the likelihood of observed transitions, followed by optimizing a policy to maximize its expected return within this learned model. This sequential approach can lead to an objective mismatch, as the world model's optimization objective (likelihood maximization) does not directly align with the policy's objective (expected return maximization). In online MBRL, this mismatch is often addressed by dynamically adapting the world model alongside the policy to enhance current policy returns. However, directly applying such strategies in an offline setting is problematic because the real environment is inaccessible, potentially causing the world model to diverge from true dynamics and mislead policy updates. Some prior offline MBRL research has indicated that return-driven model adaptation can sometimes underperform non-adaptive approaches in offline contexts.

A second challenge in the current landscape is the observed lack of robustness in policies learned via offline MBRL. These policies frequently exhibit performance degradation when deployed in environments with minor adversarial noise or deviations from the training data, suggesting overfitting to static datasets or their derived dynamics models. While some model-free robust offline RL algorithms exist, they typically focus on robust value estimation, conservative smoothing, or handling data corruption, rather than explicit dynamics mismatch or explicit generalization beyond the dataset.

The paper identifies RAMBO (Rigter et al., 2022) as the closest related work, which also targets robust offline MBRL through adversarial dynamics. However, RAMBO utilizes alternating updates, which implicitly treats the interaction as a symmetric zero-sum game. This approach may overlook the bi-level nature of the problem, where the worst-case world model is an implicit function of the policy, potentially lacking theoretical convergence guarantees and leading to over-conservatism. Additionally, RAMBO does not address the issue of outdated rollouts in the replay buffer when the world model is dynamically updated.

This work contributes to the field by proposing a unified framework that dynamically adapts the world model alongside the policy under a shared objective designed to improve robustness. It addresses the objective mismatch by formulating the problem as a constrained maximin optimization, utilizing Stackelberg learning dynamics to account for the bi-level interaction between policy and model, and offering theoretical guarantees on convergence and suboptimality.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The research is driven by two primary objectives:

1. **To address the objective mismatch between world model learning and policy optimization in offline MBRL.**
 - **Motivation:** Traditional offline MBRL methods first learn a world model by maximizing the likelihood of observed transitions from a static dataset. Subsequently, a policy is optimized to maximize its expected return within this fixed, learned model. This two-stage process means the world model is not intrinsically optimized to facilitate effective policy learning. The authors observe that this lack of alignment can hinder the learning of high-performing policies. While online MBRL attempts to resolve this by adapting the model to improve policy returns, applying this offline is difficult due to the inability to interact with the real environment, which could lead to a model that diverges from reality. The paper aims to develop a framework where the world model and policy are optimized concurrently under a unified objective.

2. **To enhance the robustness of policies learned via offline MBRL, particularly against environmental noise and dynamics mismatch during deployment.**
 - **Motivation:** The authors empirically observe that policies derived from current state-of-the-art offline RL algorithms (both model-free and model-based) often lack robustness in deployment. They note that even small adversarial noise in the environment can cause significant performance degradation. This suggests that learned policies may overfit to the static offline dataset or the data-driven dynamics models. The goal is to develop an approach that is less susceptible to these issues, enabling more reliable real-world application of offline RL. A core aspect of offline RL is conservatism (Levine et al., 2020), which motivates maximizing the worst-case performance of the policy.

To achieve these objectives, the authors are specifically motivated to:
- **Formulate the problem as a maximin optimization:** This approach aims to maximize the policy's expected return in the worst-case scenario over a set of plausible world models. This directly targets robustness by training the policy to be resilient against adversarial model variations.
- **Utilize Stackelberg learning dynamics:** The problem is inherently bi-level, where the adversarial world model's optimal response depends on the policy, and the policy, in turn, optimizes against this response. Standard alternating updates, as seen in some prior work, may not accurately capture this leader-follower relationship, lacking theoretical guarantees for convergence and potentially leading to overly conservative policies. Modeling the policy as the leader and the world model as the follower in a Stackelberg game provides a principled framework with established convergence properties.
- **Incorporate a constrained optimization for the world model:** To prevent the adversarially updated world model from diverging unrealistically from the true dynamics (or the maximum likelihood estimate derived from the data), an uncertainty set (`Φ`) is introduced. This constraint ensures that the adversarial model remains within a "reasonable" proximity to the empirically observed dynamics, which is crucial when the true environment is inaccessible.
- **Develop computationally efficient implementations:** Given the complex second-order derivatives involved in Stackelberg dynamics and constrained optimization, practical algorithms require efficient methods for their computation and inversion.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology developed in this paper, termed **ROMBRL (Robust Offline Model-based Reinforcement Learning)**, is structured around a constrained maximin optimization problem solved using Stackelberg learning dynamics.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The core problem is formulated as follows:
`max_θ J(θ, ϕ')` subject to `ϕ' ∈ arg min_{ϕ∈Φ} J(θ, ϕ)`
Here, `J(θ, ϕ)` represents the expected return of a policy `π_θ` in an environment governed by the world model `P_ϕ`. The leader (policy `π_θ`) aims to maximize its expected return, while the follower (world model `P_ϕ`) attempts to minimize it, thereby creating an adversarial training dynamic.
The uncertainty set `Φ` for the world model is defined by a KL-divergence constraint:
`Φ = {ϕ ∈ M | E_{(s,a)~D_μ} [D_KL(P_¯ϕ(·|s,a) || P_ϕ(·|s,a))] ≤ ϵ}`
where `¯ϕ` is the maximum likelihood estimator of the world model learned from the offline dataset `D_μ`, and `ϵ` is the uncertainty radius. This constraint ensures that the adversarial model `ϕ` remains within a specified divergence from the empirically observed dynamics.

#### 3.2. Theoretical Analysis

The paper provides theoretical guarantees for the proposed framework:
- **Suboptimality Gap:** Theorem 3.1 establishes an upper bound on the suboptimality gap between the learned policy `π_ˆθ` and an optimal policy `π_θ*` in the true environment `M_ϕ*`. This gap is shown to depend on factors such as a concentrability coefficient (`C_ϕ*,θ*`), the discount factor (`γ`), the uncertainty radius (`ϵ`), and the size of the offline dataset (`N`).
- **Uncertainty Radius (ϵ) Bounds:** Theorems 3.2 and 3.3 derive concrete expressions for `ϵ` that ensure `ϕ* ∈ Φ` with high probability, for both tabular MDPs and MDPs with continuous state/action spaces using diagonal Gaussian world models. For tabular MDPs, `ϵ` depends on the alphabet size and dataset density. For continuous MDPs, `ϵ` scales with the state space dimension and dataset size.

#### 3.3. Learning Dynamics: Constrained Stackelberg Game

The problem is modeled as a Stackelberg game where `π_θ` is the leader, and `P_ϕ` and its associated Lagrange multiplier `λ` (for the KL-divergence constraint) are the followers. The update rules for `θ`, `ϕ`, and `λ` are derived from Stackelberg learning dynamics (Equation 9), which involve second-order derivatives:
- **Policy Update (`θ`):** `θ_k+1 = θ_k + η_θ [∇_θ J(θ_k, ϕ_k) - ∇^2_{ϕθ} L(θ_k, ϕ_k, λ_k)^T H(θ_k, ϕ_k, λ_k)∇_ϕ J(θ_k, ϕ_k)]`. This update incorporates a term derived from the sensitivity of the optimal follower response (`ϕ*`, `λ*`) to changes in the leader's strategy (`θ`). `H` is a matrix derived from second-order derivatives of the Lagrangian `L`.
- **World Model Update (`ϕ`):** `ϕ_k+1 = ϕ_k - η_ϕ ∇_ϕ L(θ_k, ϕ_k, λ_k)`. This is a gradient descent step on the Lagrangian, where `L(θ, ϕ, λ) = J(θ, ϕ) + λE_{D_μ}[KL(P_¯ϕ || P_ϕ) - ϵ]`.
- **Lagrange Multiplier Update (`λ`):** `λ_k+1 = [λ_k + η_λ ∇_λ L(θ_k, ϕ_k, λ_k)]_+`. This is a projected gradient ascent step to enforce the KL-divergence constraint, ensuring `λ` remains non-negative.
- **Two-Timescale Learning:** For theoretical convergence, the learning rates must satisfy `η_ϕ ≫ η_λ ≫ η_θ`, meaning the follower (model and multiplier) adapts significantly faster than the leader (policy).

#### 3.4. Practical Algorithm (ROMBRL)

To implement these dynamics efficiently, ROMBRL incorporates several practical considerations:
- **Derivative Computation:** Theorem 5.1 provides formulas for first and second-order derivatives of `J(θ, ϕ)` and `L(θ, ϕ, λ)` for episodic MDPs. These are based on expectations over trajectories, which are approximated by sample means in practice.
- **Second-Order Derivative Approximations:** Computing exact second-order derivatives can be costly. The algorithm approximates `∇^2_ϕ Ψ(τ, ϕ)` and `∇^2_ϕ log P_ϕ` using terms involving the Fisher Information Matrix (`-F`), leveraging the property `E_x~P_ϕ [∇^2_ϕ log P_ϕ(x) + ∇_ϕ log P_ϕ(x)∇_ϕ log P_ϕ(x)^T] = 0`. This approximation helps manage computational complexity.
- **Efficient Matrix Inversion (Woodbury Matrix Identity):** The inversion of the Hessian matrix `A = ∇^2_ϕ L(θ, ϕ, λ)` is a computational bottleneck. `A` is represented as a sum of low-rank matrices (`U V^T - XY^T + ZZ^T`). The Woodbury matrix identity is then applied recursively to efficiently compute `A^-1` without explicit inversion, reducing the time complexity of policy updates from `O(M N_ϕ^2 + N_ϕ^ω + mN_θ)` to `O(M^2 N_ϕ + mN_θ)`, where `N_ϕ` is the number of model parameters, `m` is the number of sampled trajectories, and `M` is the number of sampled transitions.
- **Training Enhancements (Algorithm 2):**
 - **Initial Model:** A maximum likelihood world model `¯ϕ` is pre-trained on the offline dataset.
 - **Value Function Training:** Value functions (`Q_υ`, `V_υ`) are trained using off-policy samples stored in a replay buffer (D), with target networks (`V_¯υ`), similar to Soft Actor-Critic (SAC).
 - **Truncated On-Policy Rollouts:** Policy and world model gradients are computed using short, on-policy rollouts (length `l < h`) starting from states sampled from `D_μ`. This helps mitigate compounding errors from inaccurate long-horizon model predictions. Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) is used with learned value functions to estimate returns.
 - **Gradient Mask Mechanism:** Inspired by Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), gradient masks are applied during multi-epoch updates for policy and world model parameters. These masks clip importance sampling ratios, ensuring that updates from potentially outdated data (due to the continually adapting world model) remain conservative and stable, maintaining sample efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ROMBRL was conducted across 15 continuous control tasks, including the D4RL MuJoCo suite and stochastic Tokamak Control tasks, with a focus on deployment robustness under added noise.

#### 4.1. Performance on D4RL MuJoCo (Noisy Environments)

- ROMBRL achieved the highest average normalized score of 77.7 across 12 D4RL MuJoCo tasks when subjected to 5% measurement noise in state transitions during deployment. This performance surpassed all other evaluated baselines.
- The algorithm secured the top rank in 7 out of 12 tasks and the second rank in 4 tasks.
- Statistical significance was quantified using Cohen's d, with ROMBRL demonstrating values (e.g., 18.9 against CQL, 4.1 against MOBILE, 22.9 against RAMBO) that indicate a very large and statistically significant improvement over comparative methods.

#### 4.2. Robustness under Noiseless vs. Noisy D4RL Environments

- In a direct comparison of performance on standard (noiseless) D4RL benchmarks, ROMBRL's average score (92.8) was competitive, performing on par with leading SOTA methods like MOBILE (95.9) and outperforming the robust baseline RAMBO (82.7).
- The crucial indicator of robustness, "Performance Drop" (the percentage change in score from noiseless to noisy environments), showed ROMBRL with a minimal drop of -0.6%. This contrasts sharply with other baselines, which experienced significant degradation (e.g., MOBILE: 11.1%, RAMBO: 20.2%, EDAC: 26.6%). This finding supports that ROMBRL maintains performance under perturbations, validating its design for deployment robustness without a substantial compromise in ideal conditions.

#### 4.3. Performance on Tokamak Control Tasks

- ROMBRL was evaluated on three challenging and highly stochastic Tokamak control tasks, which simulate nuclear fusion processes. The evaluation metric was negative episodic tracking error.
- ROMBRL ranked first in 2 out of 3 tasks (βN and Rotation tracking) and second in the remaining task (Density tracking).
- It achieved the best average performance across all three tasks (-47.1) with comparatively low variance, indicating consistent and stable learning.
- Cohen's d values further confirmed the statistical significance of ROMBRL's improvements over other baselines (e.g., d=1.9 over RAMBO, d=3.2 over MOBILE).
- Several state-of-the-art model-based methods, including RAMBO, MOBILE, and BAMCTS, exhibited significantly poorer performance and higher variance in this stochastic and noisy benchmark, suggesting limited robustness in such complex settings.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study: Effectiveness of Constrained Stackelberg Gradients

- An ablation study compared three gradient update mechanisms on `walker2d-medium`:
 - **Naive Alternating (Eq. 7):** Yielded the lowest return.
 - **Unconstrained Stackelberg (Eq. 8):** Showed only marginal improvement over the Naive approach.
 - **Constrained Stackelberg (Ours, Eq. 9):** Achieved a substantial performance increase.
- This empirical evidence indicates that merely adapting the model is insufficient; explicitly incorporating the dynamics of constraint enforcement (through the dual variable `λ` and the `H` matrix) is critical for effective robustness in constrained RL settings.

#### 4.5. Hyperparameter Sensitivity

- **Uncertainty Radius (ϵ):** Experiments demonstrated that the optimal `ϵ` value correlates with the magnitude of environmental disturbance. Larger noise levels necessitated a larger `ϵ` to maintain robustness, while an overly small `ϵ` could lead to sub-optimal performance.
- **Learning Rates (η_θ, η_ϕ):** The study confirmed the theoretical requirement of two-timescale learning, where the model's learning rate should be significantly higher than the policy's (`η_ϕ ≫ η_θ`). Performance collapsed when this condition was not met (`η_θ ≥ η_ϕ`).

#### 4.6. Computational Cost Analysis

- ROMBRL's GPU memory usage was found to be comparable to other model-based offline RL methods like MOBILE and RAMBO.
- Its runtime per training epoch was higher than MOBILE but comparable to RAMBO, suggesting that the computational overhead is within an expected range for algorithms designed for robustness in offline MBRL. The algorithm also requires no additional components during deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents ROMBRL, an offline model-based reinforcement learning algorithm designed to enhance both the effectiveness and robustness of learned policies. The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

- **Addressing Core Offline MBRL Challenges:** The paper directly tackles the prevalent objective mismatch problem in offline MBRL by proposing a unified learning objective that jointly optimizes the world model and policy. This represents a methodological advancement over traditional two-stage approaches, potentially leading to more intrinsically aligned and performant learning.
- **Enhancing Policy Robustness:** A central contribution is the significant improvement in deployment robustness. Policies learned by ROMBRL demonstrated minimal performance degradation under environmental noise, a critical factor for successful real-world application. This robust performance, validated across diverse and challenging benchmarks (D4RL MuJoCo and Tokamak Control tasks), suggests a more reliable transfer of learned policies from offline data to real-world deployment scenarios, which often involve unforeseen perturbations or dynamics discrepancies.
- **Strong Theoretical Foundations:** The work introduces a principled formulation of robust offline MBRL as a constrained maximin optimization problem. The application of Stackelberg learning dynamics, which models the policy as a leader and the world model as a follower, provides theoretical convergence guarantees. This theoretical grounding distinguishes ROMBRL from some existing methods that may rely on heuristic or alternating update schemes, fostering greater confidence in its behavior. The derivation of suboptimality bounds further solidifies the theoretical understanding of the algorithm's performance characteristics.
- **Practical Computational Efficiency:** Despite the inherent complexity of second-order optimization in Stackelberg games, the paper develops practical implementations. The use of Fisher information matrix approximations and the recursive application of the Woodbury matrix identity for efficient Hessian inversion demonstrates methods for making such theoretically advanced approaches computationally tractable. Furthermore, the inclusion of training stability mechanisms like gradient masks and truncated rollouts contributes to the empirical effectiveness and sample efficiency of the algorithm.
- **Broadening Real-World Applicability:** The demonstrated state-of-the-art performance and robustness on both standard robotic control tasks and complex, highly stochastic Tokamak control problems highlight ROMBRL's potential applicability in various safety-critical and data-scarce domains. Such domains, including autonomous systems, medical robotics, and fusion energy research, require high levels of reliability and robustness where online data collection is costly, dangerous, or impractical. By mitigating the sim-to-real gap, ROMBRL offers a more viable pathway for deploying RL agents trained exclusively on offline data.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a new benchmark for robust offline MBRL and provides a robust framework that can serve as a foundation for future research in areas such as adaptive uncertainty sets, more complex adversarial model formulations, and further improvements in computational efficiency for bi-level optimization in RL.
