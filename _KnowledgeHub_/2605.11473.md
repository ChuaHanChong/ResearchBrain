# TOPPO: Rethinking PPO for Multi-Task Reinforcement Learning with Critic Balancing

> [!summary] Summary
> TOPPO reformulates Proximal Policy Optimization for Multi-Task Reinforcement Learning by introducing "Critic Balancing" modules to resolve critic-side gradient ill-conditioning. This approach achieved a 90.88% mean success rate on MT50 with 22.7 times fewer parameters than top baselines, significantly improving performance on challenging tail tasks.

## Problem

- On-policy PPO methods historically underperformed off-policy SAC variants in Multi-Task Reinforcement Learning (MTRL), often attributed to inherent algorithmic limitations.
- Existing MTRL approaches, including strong off-policy methods, typically failed on challenging "tail tasks" in benchmarks like Meta-World, achieving 0% success.
- A key, overlooked issue within PPO was critic-side gradient ill-conditioning, where dominant gradients from easy tasks biased the shared critic and hindered learning for harder tasks.

## Method

- Introduced **TOPPO** (Tail-Optimized PPO), a reformulation of PPO that incorporates a suite of ==Critic Balancing== modules.
- Integrated ==PopArt== for per-task value-target normalization to address critic-side gradient scale spread (D1).
- Applied ==LayerNorm-Critic (LN-c)== to improve hidden-feature conditioning and ==FairGrad-Critic (FG-c)== for fair gradient aggregation to mitigate co-linear collapse (D2) and unfair aggregation (D3), along with ==PCGrad-Actor (PCGrad-a)== for actor-side conflict.

## Results

- TOPPO achieved a **90.88 ± 1.59%** mean success rate on MT50 (Meta-World V2), surpassing larger state-of-the-art baselines by nearly **+2pp** while using **22.7 times fewer parameters**.
- It significantly improved performance on the worst-10 tail tasks of MT50, reaching **56.50 ± 7.76%** success, compared to **0%** for most non-ARS SAC methods and **29.3%** for ARS-LoRA.
- Ablation studies confirmed the additive benefits of each "Critic Balancing" module, with LayerNorm-Critic providing the largest individual gain and PopArt proving essential for robust performance on diverse reward scales.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Critic-side gradient ill-conditioning, characterized by up to 497x spread in gradient norms and high co-linearity, is the primary impediment to PPO's performance in MTRL, more so than actor-side issues.
> - PopArt's per-task value normalization and LayerNorm on critic hidden layers are crucial for stabilizing gradient scales and improving feature conditioning across tasks.
> - FairGrad (α=1α=1) provides scale-invariant gradient aggregation for the critic, theoretically absorbing PopArt's rescaling effects and ensuring fair updates across tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Multi-Task Reinforcement Learning (MTRL) has predominantly relied on off-policy methods, particularly Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) and its variants. This preference stems from SAC's reported sample efficiency when operating with online data collection budgets, as its replay buffer reuses past experiences and its slow target network mitigates cross-task gradient imbalance. On Meta-World, a widely recognized benchmark for multi-task robotic manipulation, SAC-style training has become a near-universal backbone for architectural variants (e.g., Soft Modularization, CARE, PaCo, MOORE) and gradient-aggregation methods (e.g., PCGrad, CAGrad, FAMO, FairGrad, Nash-MTL). However, the paper observes that most published non-ARS multi-task SAC methods exhibit a 0% success rate on the ten hardest Meta-World MT50 tasks, with the strongest ARS variant clearing this floor only at a substantially higher parameter cost.

In contrast, on-policy methods, such as Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), have remained largely underexplored in MTRL. Early evaluations of Multi-Task PPO (MT-PPO) on Meta-World reported a success rate of approximately 30% on MT10, and this comparison had not been comprehensively revisited with architectures comparable to modern MTRL methods. The authors position PPO as a valuable testbed for investigating cross-task gradient ill-conditioning due to its on-policy Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE), which directly exposes task imbalance within each minibatch without the smoothing effects of replay buffers or target network lag.

This work aims to challenge the prevailing reliance on SAC by demonstrating that, with appropriate optimization strategies, on-policy methods can achieve performance comparable to or superior to off-policy approaches in MTRL. It shifts the focus to an overlooked optimization bottleneck within PPO itself, specifically critic-side gradient conditioning, rather than relying on modular architectures or larger models characteristic of some prior work.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to diagnose and address a previously overlooked issue in the application of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) to Multi-Task Reinforcement Learning (MTRL): critic-side gradient ill-conditioning. The authors' motivation stems from the observed underperformance of PPO in MTRL compared to Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) variants, and a desire to explore if PPO's limitations are due to inherent algorithmic properties or solvable optimization challenges.

The paper identifies a "self-reinforcing trap" on the critic side as a core problem. Early in training, tasks that are easier to learn generate larger critic gradients. These gradients then dominate updates to the shared critic network, biasing its features towards reducing the TD errors of these easy tasks. Consequently, harder tasks contribute little to the aggregate update and are forced to fit their value functions through features primarily optimized for other tasks. This leads to distorted advantage estimates for the harder tasks, which in turn guides the actor updates toward less informative policies. In the online learning loop, this results in the collection of worse or less useful rollouts from these tail tasks, further reducing their signal for subsequent critic updates. This feedback loop explains why approaches solely focusing on actor-side or loss-level reweighting might not effectively resolve the issue when the critic's gradient geometry remains uncorrected.

Through empirical diagnostics, the authors motivate their focus on the critic:
- **Gradient Norm Spread:** Per-task critic gradient norms were found to span approximately 497 times across tasks, significantly wider than the approximately 4.1 times spread observed in per-task actor gradient norms (after per-task advantage normalization). This asymmetry indicates that the critic is the primary source of cross-task gradient conflict.
- **Decomposition of Critic Ill-conditioning:** The critic's ill-conditioning is further decomposed into three distinct aspects:
 - **(D1) Per-task Scale Spread:** Large differences in the magnitudes of per-task critic gradients (represented by the diagonal entries of the Gram matrix) allow high-gradient tasks to disproportionately influence the mean update, diminishing the impact of smaller-gradient tasks.
 - **(D2) Co-Linear Collapse:** A high mean off-diagonal cosine similarity in the critic Gram matrix indicates that per-task critic gradients tend to align along a limited set of shared feature directions, suggesting a lack of independent learning for diverse tasks.
 - **(D3) Unfair Aggregation:** Even if scale spread and co-linearity were partially mitigated, the default mean aggregation rule can still disproportionately allocate the combined gradient direction to a few tasks, rather than distributing the update budget fairly according to gradient geometry.

Based on this diagnosis, the key objective is to propose TOPPO (Tail-Optimized PPO), a reformulation of PPO that incorporates a set of "Critic Balancing" modules. These modules are specifically designed to address the identified critic-side ill-conditioning aspects (D1-D3) and a residual actor-side conflict, with the overarching goal of enabling on-policy methods to achieve strong mean and tail-task performance in MTRL, thereby challenging the established dominance of off-policy SAC variants.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose TOPPO (Tail-Optimized PPO) as a reformulation of PPO designed to mitigate critic-side gradient ill-conditioning and improve learning dynamics across multiple tasks. The methodology involves a critic-centered diagnosis of MT-PPO failures and the integration of four specific modules into the PPO training pipeline, targeting distinct stages of the per-minibatch update.

**Core PPO Setup:**
The foundation of TOPPO is a standard MT-PPO framework. It involves training a task-aware policy $\pi_{\theta_a}(a|s,i)$ and a shared state-value critic $V_{\theta_c}(s,i)$ across $K$ tasks. The objective is to maximize the average cross-task expected discounted return. For each task, rollouts are collected, and Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) advantages $\hat{A}^{(i)}_t$ and return targets $\hat{V}^{(i),targ}_t$ are calculated. The vanilla MT-PPO objective averages per-task losses, each comprising a clipped policy surrogate, a squared value loss, and an entropy bonus. Critically, per-task advantage normalization (computed within each task's slice of a minibatch) is applied to $\hat{A}^{(i)}$ before the actor surrogate, which contributes to the observed actor-critic gradient asymmetry.

**Diagnosis Methodology:**
To pinpoint gradient ill-conditioning, the authors compute per-task gradients $g^a_i := \nabla_{\theta_a} L^a_i$ and $g^c_i := \nabla_{\theta_c} L^c_i$ for shared actor and critic parameters, respectively. They analyze Gram matrices $G_{\bullet} \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times K}$, where $G_{\bullet,ij} = \langle g_{\bullet,i}, g_{\bullet,j} \rangle$. The diagonal entries $G_{\bullet,ii}$ quantify per-task scale (gradient norm squared), and off-diagonal cosines $G_{\bullet,ij}/\sqrt{G_{\bullet,ii}G_{\bullet,jj}}$ measure pairwise direction conflict. This analysis empirically revealed that critic gradient norms exhibit a significantly larger spread (approximately 497x) compared to actor gradient norms (approximately 4.1x), leading to the diagnosis of three critic-side issues:
- **(D1) Per-task Scale Spread:** Large variations in $\Vert g^c_i \Vert^2$.
- **(D2) Co-linear Collapse:** High average off-diagonal $|\cos|$ values in $G_c$.
- **(D3) Unfair Aggregation:** The default mean update disproportionately favoring a few tasks.

**TOPPO Modules for Critic Balancing:**
TOPPO integrates four modules to address these diagnosed issues:

1. **PopArt: Per-task Value-Target Normalization (Addresses D1: Scale Spread):**
 - PopArt (Preserving Outputs Precisely, Adaptively Rescaling Targets) directly tackles extreme per-task value-target variation.
 - It introduces a per-task affine layer $V_{\theta_c}(s,i) = \sigma_i z_{\theta_c}(s,i) + \mu_i$, where $z_{\theta_c}(s,i)$ is the shared critic's output, and $(\mu_i, \sigma_i)$ are running estimates of each task's return target mean and standard deviation. These statistics are updated online using Welford's algorithm.
 - The shared critic learns to predict normalized targets $(\hat{V}^{(i),targ}_t - \mu_i)/\sigma_i$, effectively placing each task's value loss on a comparable scale.
 - The "Pop" component ensures that raw value predictions are preserved during updates to $(\mu_i, \sigma_i)$.
 - This module rescales shared-critic gradients, with $g^c_{PopArt,i} = \sigma_i^{-2} g^c_{raw,i}$, directly impacting scale spread.

2. **LayerNorm-Critic (LN-c) (Addresses D2: Co-Linear Collapse):**
 - Pre-activation LayerNorm is applied to the hidden linear layers of the critic network.
 - This stabilizes task-wise hidden activation scales before forming the Gram matrix, empirically reducing the mean off-diagonal $|\cos|$ in $G_c$, thus improving hidden-feature conditioning and mitigating co-linear collapse.

3. **FairGrad-Critic (FG-c) with $\alpha=1$ (Addresses D3: Unfair Aggregation):**
 - The default mean critic update is replaced by FairGrad aggregation, $d_c = \sum_i w_i g^c_i$, where weights $w \in \mathbb{R}^K_{>0}$ are determined by an $\alpha$-fairness objective.
 - For $\alpha=1$ (log utility), the KKT condition $G_c w = w^{-1}$ (componentwise) is solved.
 - **Theorem 1:** This solution guarantees that the combined critic gradient $d_c$ has a fixed $L^2$ norm equal to $\sqrt{K}$ on every minibatch and is invariant to positive task-wise rescaling of individual gradients.
 - **Corollary 1:** This invariance implies that the gradient-rescaling effect of PopArt (i.e., $\sigma_i^{-2}$) is absorbed at the FG-c aggregator output, making the combined critic gradient identical whether raw or PopArt-rescaled gradients are used.
 - A projected Newton solver with a closed-form diagonal warm start is introduced to efficiently solve the KKT condition, addressing the computational bottleneck for $K=50$.

4. **PCGrad-Actor (PCGrad-a) (Addresses Secondary Actor-Side Conflict):**
 - Given that per-task advantage normalization reduces actor-side scale variation, the main residual issue for the actor is direction conflict.
 - PCGrad is applied to aggregate actor gradients, projecting out conflicting gradient components.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Benchmark:** Meta-World V2 (MT10 and MT50) and V1 (MT50) with task identifiers.
- **Architecture:** A fully task-shared [400x3] ReLU MLP for both actor and critic networks (separate), totaling 685K (MT10) / 717K (MT50) parameters. This is consistent with common SAC-family backbones but is significantly smaller than some ARS variants.
- **Training:** 20M (MT10) or 100M (MT50) total environment steps, using stratified minibatches, per-task advantage normalization, value clipping, and a post-aggregation gradient-norm clip.
- **Baselines:** Performance is compared against published SAC-family (e.g., SAC-MT, MOORE, PCGrad) and ARS-family (e.g., ARS, ARS-LN, ARS-LoRA) methods on Meta-World V2 and V1, using their reported metrics (mean ± std or IQM ± std).
- **Ablation Studies:** Detailed ablations isolate the contribution of each TOPPO module and analyze their interactions, including how PopArt's effect changes with FG-c and sensitivity to V1 vs. V2 reward structures.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding TOPPO's performance and the effectiveness of its constituent modules.

**Overall Performance on MT50 (Meta-World V2):**
- TOPPO, with 717K parameters, achieved a final-checkpoint mean success rate of 90.88 ± 1.59% on MT50. This performance surpassed ARS-LN-1024 (16.27M parameters) by 1.98 percentage points, using 22.7 times fewer parameters.
- On the challenging worst-10 tail tasks of MT50, TOPPO attained 56.50 ± 7.76% success, which is 27.2 percentage points higher than ARS-LoRA (16.12M parameters), which achieved 29.3%. Other published non-ARS multi-task SAC methods reportedly scored 0.0% on these tasks.
- In terms of training efficiency, TOPPO matched or exceeded the full-budget mean performance of strong SAC baselines, including ARS-LN-1024, at approximately 40% of its own 100M-step training budget (around 40M steps).

**Performance on MT10 (Meta-World V2):**
- On MT10, TOPPO's final-checkpoint mean success rate was 97.2 ± 3.1%. While slightly below ARS-LoRA's 99.5 ± 0.8%, every TOPPO seed reached at least 99% success at its best checkpoint. This suggests that the observed gap in final mean is primarily attributable to PPO's known late-training instability rather than a performance ceiling, as the reported results intentionally avoid learning rate schedules to maintain consistency with baseline reporting.

**Ablation Studies on MT50 (Meta-World V2):**
- **Cumulative Effect:** The study systematically demonstrated the incremental benefits of each module:
 - Vanilla MT-PPO (78.5% mean, 6.6% worst-10 tail).
 - Adding LN-c significantly improved performance (87.5% mean, 42.3% worst-10 tail), marking the largest single-step gain.
 - Adding PopArt (to LN-c) further increased performance (88.9% mean, 48.9% worst-10 tail).
 - Adding FG-c ($\alpha=1$) (to LN-c + PopArt) provided additional gains (90.1% mean, 52.4% worst-10 tail).
 - The full TOPPO configuration (+PCGrad-a) achieved the headline performance (90.9% mean, 56.5% worst-10 tail).
- **PopArt and FG-c Interaction:** The empirical results supported Corollary 1: PopArt's marginal gain on worst-10 performance (over LN-c) collapsed from 6.64% (when FG-c was absent) to a decrease of 0.22% (when FG-c was present). This aligns with the theoretical prediction that FairGrad ($\alpha=1$) absorbs PopArt's gradient-rescaling effect.
- **Per-Side Combiner Asymmetry:** PCGrad-a's contribution was concentrated on the tail tasks, lifting the worst-10 tail tasks by 4.08 percentage points when added to the LN-c+PopArt+FG-c stack. This supports the asymmetric design, addressing modest actor-side direction conflict after advantage normalization.

**V1 Reward Sensitivity (MT50 Meta-World V1):**
- TOPPO achieved an Interquartile Mean (IQM) of 79.3% on MT50 V1, outperforming the strongest published V1 baseline (MOORE, 61.8%) by 17.6 percentage points, while utilizing 14.5 times fewer trainable parameters.
- On V1 rewards, which exhibit substantially wider per-task scale heterogeneity than V2, PopArt's non-rescaling channels (e.g., running statistics, Adam moments) became more critical. The PopArt marginal over LN-c+FG-c increased to 17.01% on V1 IQM (compared to 0.32% on V2 IQM).
- Notably, FG-c without PopArt slightly degraded performance on V1, demonstrating that PopArt's target normalization is essential for FG-c to operate effectively under heterogeneous reward scales.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of Multi-Task Reinforcement Learning (MTRL) by providing a comprehensive analysis and an effective solution for Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) in multi-task settings.

**Re-evaluating On-Policy Methods:** The work directly challenges the prevailing assumption that off-policy methods, particularly Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) and its variants, are inherently superior for MTRL due to their sample efficiency. By demonstrating that PPO can achieve competitive or superior performance when properly optimized, the paper suggests that prior conclusions about MT-PPO's limitations were influenced by insufficient attention to its optimization challenges, rather than inherent algorithmic constraints. This could encourage renewed exploration and development of on-policy algorithms for MTRL.

**Identifying a Critical Bottleneck:** The diagnosis of critic-side gradient ill-conditioning as a central bottleneck in MT-PPO is a significant contribution. This diagnosis, supported by empirical evidence of large gradient norm spread and co-linear collapse, provides a mechanistic understanding of why tail tasks often stall. This specific focus on the critic differentiates TOPPO from prior MTRL methods that primarily addressed actor-side or loss-level gradient issues.

**Principled Optimization Framework:** The proposed "Critic Balancing" framework, comprising PopArt, LayerNorm-Critic (LN-c), and FairGrad-Critic (FG-c), offers a principled approach to addressing these bottlenecks. The theoretical results (Theorem 1 and Corollary 1) provide a clear justification for the combination and interaction of these modules, particularly the scale-invariance properties of FairGrad ($\alpha=1$) in conjunction with PopArt. This theoretical grounding enhances the interpretability and robustness of the proposed method.

**Improved Performance and Efficiency:** TOPPO achieves stronger mean and tail-task performance on Meta-World benchmarks than published SAC-family and ARS-family baselines. Crucially, it does so with substantially fewer parameters (e.g., 22.7 times fewer than ARS-LN-1024) and competitive or superior sample efficiency, matching or surpassing baselines early in training. This demonstrates that architectural complexity or large model sizes are not the sole routes to high performance in MTRL.

**Impact on Hard Tasks:** The significant improvement in success rates for previously unsolved or poorly performing "tail tasks" is particularly impactful. Addressing these challenging tasks is crucial for developing generalist agents capable of robustly handling diverse environments, moving beyond merely solving easy tasks.

**Future Research Directions:** The findings suggest that focusing on fundamental optimization issues within existing RL algorithms, rather than solely on architectural innovations or large models, can yield substantial gains. This could guide future research towards deeper analysis of gradient dynamics in MTRL and the development of more robust and efficient optimization techniques across different algorithm families. The method's robustness across Meta-World V1 and V2 reward variants also suggests its adaptability to environments with varying reward heterogeneity.
