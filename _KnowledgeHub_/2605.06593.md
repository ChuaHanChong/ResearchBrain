# ReActor: Reinforcement Learning for Physics-Aware Motion Retargeting

> [!summary] Summary
> ReActor, a framework from Disney Research, uses a bilevel optimization scheme to adapt human kinematic motion to diverse robotic morphologies by integrating reinforcement learning with physics simulation. This approach generates physically plausible motions with zero ground or self-penetration, which significantly improves downstream reinforcement learning success rates by up to 15.22 percentage points on various humanoid and quadruped robots.

## Problem

- Existing motion retargeting methods often generate physically implausible motions (e.g., foot sliding, self-penetration) that degrade downstream imitation learning performance.
- A significant 'embodiment gap' exists between human motion data and robots with fundamentally different kinematic structures and dynamics, making direct application challenging.
- Current methods frequently require extensive manual tuning, predefined contact patterns, or large paired datasets, limiting their scalability and generalization across diverse morphologies.

## Method

- **ReActor** uses a ==bilevel optimization== framework that jointly optimizes learnable retargeting parameters (upper-level) and trains a ==motion tracking policy== via reinforcement learning (lower-level) within a physics simulation.
- A ==simplified gradient estimation== for the upper-level loss is derived, avoiding the computationally intensive implicit function theorem typically required for bilevel problems with an RL-based lower-level.
- The system requires only ==sparse semantic rigid-body correspondences== and automatically identifies optimal retargeting parameters, including local position/orientation adjustments and a per-motion vertical offset.

## Results

- ReActor achieved **zero ground and self-penetration** (0.00% time, 0.00 cm depth) and significantly reduced foot sliding (e.g., **0.17 cm/s** on G1) and floating compared to GMR and OmniRetarget baselines.
- Policies trained with ReActor-retargeted data consistently achieved higher downstream RL success rates (e.g., **97.45%** for G1, **95.07%** for Lima) compared to baselines (79.85–95.51%) — up to **+15.22 pp** improvement.
- The method demonstrated effective generalization to unseen motion data from diverse datasets and successfully applied retargeted motions to quadruped robots and physical hardware in real-time interactive settings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating motion retargeting directly within a physics simulation, combined with bilevel optimization, inherently ensures physical plausibility, accounts for contact dynamics, and significantly reduces artifacts like ground or self-penetration.
> - A computationally tractable, simplified gradient estimation enables the practical application of bilevel optimization to complex reinforcement learning problems for motion retargeting.
> - The iterative refinement of both retargeting parameters and the RL policy leads to more natural and physically consistent robot motions, directly correlating with improved performance in downstream imitation learning tasks.
