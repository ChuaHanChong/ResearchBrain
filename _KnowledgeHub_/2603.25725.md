# SoftMimicGen: A Data Generation System for Scalable Robot Learning in Deformable Object Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The system SOFTMIMICGEN generates extensive synthetic datasets for robot learning in deformable object manipulation, leveraging non-rigid registration to adapt demonstrations to new scenes. This approach enables the training of robust visuomotor policies that achieve high success rates and demonstrate effective zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

## Problem

- Acquiring large-scale datasets for robot learning, particularly for deformable objects, is costly, time-consuming, and labor-intensive due to reliance on human teleoperation.
- Existing synthetic data generation methods for robots were largely limited to rigid objects, failing to address the complexities of deformable objects which lack a static frame of reference and exhibit high-dimensional, non-linear dynamics.
- The computational challenges and difficulty in creating realistic simulation assets for deformable objects have hindered the scalable development of robot learning solutions in this domain.

## Method

- Introduced SOFTMIMICGEN, an automated data generation pipeline specifically designed for deformable object manipulation that uses non-rigid registration to adapt small sets of human demonstrations to diverse new scenes.
- Generates thousands of demonstrations from just 1-3 human examples by transforming end-effector poses based on a continuous deformation field, thereby maintaining local spatial relationships between the end-effector and the deforming object.
- Leverages a high-fidelity simulation environment suite in Isaac Lab, encompassing various deformable objects, manipulation behaviors, and multiple robot embodiments for comprehensive data generation.

## Results

- SOFTMIMICGEN generated 1,000 demonstrations per task from 1-3 human examples with 70-100% success rates, significantly reducing the human effort and cost for data collection.
- Policies trained on SOFTMIMICGEN-generated data achieved 25% to 97% higher success rates across various tasks compared to policies trained solely on source demonstrations, and substantially outperformed rigid-body focused methods for deformable tasks.
- Policies demonstrated effective zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, with performance (e.g., 63.3% for YAM - Bag Loading) often comparable to or exceeding real-only training, and further improved with sim-real co-training (e.g., 93.3% for YAM - Bag Loading).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Non-rigid registration effectively extends object-centric demonstration adaptation beyond rigid-body limitations, enabling scalable data generation for tasks involving continuous and high-dimensional deformable objects.
> - Training visuomotor policies on large, diverse synthetic datasets generated from few human demonstrations leads to significantly improved performance and robustness in deformable object manipulation tasks.
> - Robust visuomotor policies can be trained to operate directly from visual inputs on clean simulated data, enabling successful zero-shot transfer to the real world without requiring complex or noisy online non-rigid registration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The broader research landscape in robotics is increasingly focused on developing generalist robot capabilities and foundation models. These models, often trained on extensive datasets of robot manipulation, have demonstrated proficiency across a range of complex tasks. However, the acquisition of such large-scale datasets remains a significant challenge, typically requiring substantial human effort, time, and financial investment through methods like robot teleoperation.

Simulation and synthetic data generation have emerged as a promising alternative to address this data scarcity. Recent advancements have shown that synthetic datasets can significantly reduce the need for real-world data and enhance generalization to novel scenarios. This paradigm has been successfully applied to rigid-body manipulation tasks, where objects maintain a fixed shape and are computationally less demanding to simulate. Existing synthetic data generation systems, such as MIMICGEN, exploit rigid object-centric invariances to adapt demonstrations to new scenes.

However, a notable gap exists in the domain of deformable object manipulation. Deformable objects (e.g., cloth, rope, tissue) constitute a substantial portion of real-world manipulation tasks but pose considerable challenges for simulation and data generation. These challenges include the computational complexity of simulating their non-linear dynamics, the difficulty of creating realistic simulation assets, and the inability of rigid-body assumptions to capture their continuous, high-dimensional configurations. Conventional data generation methods, which rely on rigid frames of reference, are inadequate for deformable objects.

This work directly addresses this gap by extending the synthetic data generation paradigm to deformable object manipulation. It aims to bridge the current limitation of simulation-driven robot learning, which has predominantly focused on rigid-body interactions, and pave the way for more comprehensive robot foundation models capable of handling the complexities of soft materials and dynamic deformations. By providing a scalable method for generating data for deformable objects, SOFTMIMICGEN contributes to making robot learning more applicable to a wider array of real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the recognized challenges in scaling robot learning, particularly for tasks involving deformable objects. While large-scale datasets have propelled progress in robot manipulation, their collection through human teleoperation is costly, labor-intensive, and limits the development of more generalist robot foundation models. Synthetic data generation offers an alternative, but its application has been largely confined to rigid-body tasks due to the inherent difficulties in simulating and generating data for deformable objects.

The specific motivations driving the development of SOFTMIMICGEN include:
- **Addressing Data Scarcity:** The prohibitive cost and effort associated with collecting real-world data for robot manipulation, especially for complex deformable objects.
- **Expanding Synthetic Data Utility:** The limitation of existing synthetic data generation paradigms to rigid-body tasks, leaving a significant portion of real-world manipulation (involving deformable objects) unaddressed.
- **Computational Challenges of Deformable Objects:** The difficulty in simulating deformable object interactions in real-time and obtaining realistic physics, coupled with the complexity of sourcing and annotating deformable assets.
- **Algorithmic Limitations:** The breakdown of rigid-body assumptions, which are central to prior data generation methods like MIMICGEN, when applied to objects that do not maintain a static frame of reference.

Given these motivations, the key objectives of the SOFTMIMICGEN project are:
- **To develop and introduce SOFTMIMICGEN:** An automated data generation pipeline specifically designed for deformable object manipulation tasks, capable of synthesizing large-scale datasets from a small number of human demonstrations.
- **To create and release a high-fidelity simulation environment suite:** This suite is intended to encompass a broad range of deformable objects (e.g., stuffed animals, ropes, tissues, towels) and diverse manipulation behaviors (e.g., high-precision threading, dynamic whipping, folding, pick-and-place), across various robot embodiments (single-arm, bimanual, humanoid, surgical robots).
- **To demonstrate the efficacy of SOFTMIMICGEN:** By applying the pipeline to generate thousands of demonstrations across the developed task suite, training high-performing visuomotor policies from this generated data, and conducting a systematic analysis of the data generation system's capabilities.
- **To validate real-world deployment:** To evaluate policies trained on SOFTMIMICGEN-generated data in real-world setups, demonstrating zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and showing that these policies can be further improved through sim-real co-training with limited real-world data.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to reduce the barrier to studying deformable object manipulation in the context of imitation learning and robot foundation models, making scalable robot learning more accessible for a wider range of complex tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of SOFTMIMICGEN addresses the challenge of scalable data generation for deformable object manipulation by extending concepts from prior rigid-object data generation systems, primarily MIMICGEN, through the integration of non-rigid registration techniques.

**A. Foundation in Behavioral Cloning:**
The system is designed to generate data for training visuomotor policies via Behavioral Cloning (BC). Tasks are modeled as Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs). BC trains a policy (π: O → A) to map observations (o) to actions (a) by minimizing the maximum likelihood objective on a dataset of expert demonstrations.

**B. Problem Statement for Data Generation:**
Given a small source dataset (D_src), typically 1 to 10 human demonstrations, the goal is to generate a large dataset (D) for the same task or a task with a different, broader initial state distribution (D'). This involves: (1) sampling a start state from D', (2) adapting one or more source demonstrations (τ) to produce a new robot trajectory (τ'), and (3) adding successful trajectories to D. The core technical challenge lies in step (2), the trajectory generation mechanism for deformable objects.

**C. Assumptions for Deformable Objects:**
SOFTMIMICGEN operates under specific assumptions:
- **(A0) Deformable Object Representation:** Every deformable object is represented as a collection of 3-dimensional node positions, O = {n_i}, where n_i ∈ R^3. This is analogous to a point cloud and can also represent rigid bodies.
- **(A1) Action Space:** The action space consists of pose commands for end-effector controllers and gripper commands for each robot arm.
- **(A2) Object-Centric Subtasks:** Each task is divisible into sequential object-centric subtasks S_k(o_k), where an arm primarily interacts with one object. Unlike MIMICGEN, manipulation does not require a static object coordinate frame.
- **(A3) Observable Object Configurations:** Object configurations (node positions for deformable, pose for rigid) are observable or estimable before each subtask.

**D. Limitations of MIMICGEN for Deformable Objects:**
MIMICGEN parses source demonstrations into object-centric segments and transforms these segments using a constant SE(3) transform (T_o'_i_W * (T_o_i_W)^-1) to preserve the relative pose between the end-effector and a *rigid* object's canonical coordinate frame. This approach is unsuitable for deformable objects, which lack a single canonical coordinate frame and exhibit high-dimensional, non-linear deformations.

**E. SOFTMIMICGEN's Core Mechanism: Non-Rigid Registration:**
SOFTMIMICGEN overcomes MIMICGEN's limitation by employing non-rigid registration.
- **Deformable Object Representation:** Deformable objects are represented as a set of 3D node positions.
- **Non-Rigid Registration Process:** For two different configurations of a deformable object, O1 = {a_i} and O2 = {b_i}, non-rigid registration finds a smooth function f: R^3 → R^3 that maps points from O1 to O2. This is achieved by minimizing a cost function comprising point-wise distances between f(a_i) and b_i, combined with a regularization term to ensure smoothness. This process establishes correspondence and a continuous deformation field.

**F. Data Generation Pipeline Steps:**
To generate a demonstration in a novel scene, SOFTMIMICGEN proceeds subtask by subtask:
1. **Source Demonstration Selection:** For a given subtask, the system observes the target object's configuration (O'i). It then compares O'i against the initial object configurations of all relevant source segments using non-rigid registration. The source segment yielding the lowest registration cost is selected, analogous to a nearest-neighbor approach but adapted for deformable objects.
2. **Trajectory Adaptation:** Once a source segment (τ_i) is selected, non-rigid registration is performed between the object configuration (O_i) at the start of τ_i and the target scene's configuration (O'i). This yields a continuous deformation field f(·). Each pose T_t = (p_t, R_t) in the source segment is then transformed using this field:
 - Position: p_t → f(p_t)
 - Rotation: R_t → orth(J_f(p_t)R_t), where J_f is the Jacobian of f, and orth(·) orthonormalizes the result.
 This transformation preserves the local spatial relationship between the end-effector and the deforming object.
3. **Trajectory Execution:** A linear interpolation segment is added to the beginning of the warped trajectory to ensure a smooth transition from the robot's current pose. The robot then executes the adapted trajectory segment. This process is repeated for subsequent subtasks, and successful demonstrations are added to the dataset.

**G. Simulation Environments and Policy Training:**
- **Task Suite:** A suite of 10 high-fidelity deformable object manipulation tasks was developed in Isaac Lab, featuring various deformable objects (stuffed animal, rope, tissue, towel), manipulation types (threading, whipping, folding, pick-and-place), and four robot embodiments (GR1 humanoid, Franka Panda arm, dVRK surgical robot, bimanual YAM arms).
- **Source Data Collection:** 1 to 3 human demonstrations per task were collected using an Apple Vision Pro and retargeted to robot motions.
- **Policy Training:** Visuomotor policies were trained on the generated datasets using two imitation learning algorithms: BC-RNN-GMM and Diffusion Policy.

**H. Real-World Evaluation:**
- Policies were evaluated on three real-world deformable manipulation tasks.
- **Sim-to-Real Bridge:** Point Bridge was used, which extracts unified, domain-agnostic point-based representations from RGB-D camera observations in the real world (via a VLM-guided pipeline) and ground-truth masks/depth maps in simulation. Policies operate on these point-based observations directly, without explicit online non-rigid registration.
- Three training settings were compared: real-only (30 demos), zero-shot sim-to-real (1000 generated sim demos), and sim-real co-training (1000 sim + 30 real demos).

This comprehensive approach allows SOFTMIMICGEN to generalize demonstration adaptation beyond rigid-body limitations, creating a scalable method for generating diverse, high-quality data for learning deformable object manipulation skills.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted with SOFTMIMICGEN demonstrate its capabilities in generating large-scale synthetic datasets for deformable object manipulation and the effectiveness of policies trained on this data.

**A. SOFTMIMICGEN Features:**
- **Reduced Data Collection Burden:** SOFTMIMICGEN effectively reduces the need for extensive human teleoperation. From a small set of 1 to 3 human-collected demonstrations, the system generated 1,000 demonstrations per task, sampling from broader initial state distributions. This generation process achieved success rates ranging from 70% to 100% across the diverse task suite. This capability is noted to significantly decrease the human effort and cost involved in data collection for complex deformable manipulation tasks.
- **Improved Policy Performance:** Policies trained on datasets generated by SOFTMIMICGEN consistently exhibited higher success rates compared to policies trained solely on the small set of human source demonstrations. Improvements in success rates ranged from 25% to 97% across various tasks (Table I). For instance, the Franka - Rope task saw an increase from 2.0% (source) to 99.3% (generated, BC-RNN-GMM) and 100.0% (generated, Diffusion Policy). This indicates that the generated data provides sufficient diversity and quantity to train more robust and effective policies.
- **Wide Applicability:** The system successfully generated demonstrations across a comprehensive suite of tasks involving four distinct robot embodiments: the GR1 humanoid, Franka Panda arm, dVRK surgical robot, and bimanual YAM arms. The tasks encompassed a range of deformable objects (e.g., stuffed animal, rope, tissue, towel) and manipulation behaviors (e.g., high-precision threading, dynamic whipping, folding, pick-and-place). Notably, SOFTMIMICGEN also demonstrated applicability to rigid object manipulation (Franka – Rigid Cube Stack), achieving 90.7% success, indicating its generalization beyond purely deformable scenarios.

**B. SOFTMIMICGEN Analysis:**
- **Comparison with MIMICGEN:** In a direct comparison on the Franka – Rope Manipulation task, SOFTMIMICGEN significantly outperformed its rigid-body predecessor, MIMICGEN. When manipulating a rope into a U-shape, MIMICGEN achieved only 4 out of 50 successful demonstrations, primarily limited to configurations very similar to the source. In contrast, SOFTMIMICGEN achieved 49 out of 50 successful demonstrations, demonstrating its ability to handle the continuous deformations inherent in rope manipulation by leveraging non-rigid registration. This highlights the limitation of rigid-centric methods for deformable tasks.
- **Impact of Dataset Size:** An analysis of policy performance across different dataset sizes (50, 250, 500, 750 demonstrations) showed a general trend of increasing success rates with larger datasets (Table II). For example, Humanoid - Teddy improved from 24.0% with 50 demos to 44.0% with 500/750 demos. This finding reinforces the importance of scalable data generation for training performant policies, particularly for tasks requiring dexterity and dynamic control of deformable objects.
- **Replay-based Mechanisms vs. Visuomotor Policies:** The paper clarifies that while SOFTMIMICGEN uses precise ground-truth nodal information for non-rigid registration during the *data generation* phase in simulation, the trained visuomotor policies operate directly from raw image inputs *at inference time*. This approach avoids the challenges of noisy real-world point clouds for online registration, which can negatively impact direct replay-based controllers, by learning robust visuomotor mappings from the clean simulated data.

**C. Real-World Evaluation:**
- **Effective Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Policies trained purely on SOFTMIMICGEN-generated simulation data (1,000 demonstrations) demonstrated successful zero-shot transfer to real-world tasks. For example, the YAM - Bag Loading task achieved a 63.3% success rate in the real world with zero-shot transfer, outperforming real-only training with 30 demonstrations (33.3%). The Franka - Towel task achieved 70.0% zero-shot success, comparable to real-only training at 76.6%.
- **Performance Improvement with Sim-Real Co-training:** Combining SOFTMIMICGEN-generated data (1,000 demonstrations) with a small amount of real-world data (30 demonstrations) through sim-real co-training consistently further improved policy performance in the real world (Table III). For example, YAM - Bag Loading reached 93.3% success with co-training, significantly surpassing both real-only (33.3%) and zero-shot sim-to-real (63.3%). Similarly, Franka - Rope saw its success rate increase from 33.3% (zero-shot sim) and 46.7% (real-only) to 76.6% with co-training. This indicates that synthetic data from SOFTMIMICGEN can effectively supplement limited real-world data, leading to more robust real-world policies.

In summary, the results position SOFTMIMICGEN as a robust and generalizable system for generating scalable datasets for deformable object manipulation, enabling the training of high-performing visuomotor policies with demonstrated real-world applicability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of SOFTMIMICGEN represents a notable advancement in the field of robot learning, particularly for tasks involving deformable objects. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enabling Scalable Learning for Deformable Objects:** SOFTMIMICGEN addresses a critical bottleneck in robot learning by providing a method to generate large-scale, diverse datasets for deformable object manipulation. This is significant because deformable objects are ubiquitous in real-world scenarios, but their simulation and data generation have historically been challenging. By making it feasible to create thousands of demonstrations from a few human examples, the system democratizes access to data for this complex domain.
- **Reduced Human Data Collection Burden:** The system dramatically reduces the reliance on costly, time-consuming, and labor-intensive human teleoperation for data collection. This efficiency gain can accelerate research and development in robot manipulation by freeing up human operators and resources.
- **Generalization of Data Generation Paradigms:** SOFTMIMICGEN generalizes previous synthetic data generation systems, like MIMICGEN, which were limited to rigid objects. By incorporating non-rigid registration, it extends the applicability of object-centric trajectory transfer to a broader class of objects and tasks, including those with continuous and high-dimensional deformations. The demonstration that it can also handle rigid objects further establishes its versatility.
- **Advancing Robot Foundation Models:** The availability of scalable datasets for deformable object manipulation is crucial for the development of more comprehensive and generalist robot foundation models. These models aim to equip robots with broad manipulation capabilities across diverse objects and environments. SOFTMIMICGEN contributes directly by filling the data gap for a previously underserved category of objects, fostering the creation of more capable robotic systems.
- **High-Fidelity Simulation Resources:** The release of a suite of high-fidelity simulation environments for deformable object manipulation, built on Isaac Lab, provides a valuable resource for the robotics community. This open-source contribution can lower the barrier for practitioners and researchers to engage with and explore deformable manipulation challenges, fostering further innovation.
- **Validated Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The demonstration of successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and the further improvement through sim-real co-training are critical for practical robot deployment. This finding suggests that policies trained on SOFTMIMICGEN-generated synthetic data are robust enough to generalize to the real world, reducing the need for extensive real-world experimentation and calibration.
- **Robust Policy Learning:** The systematic analysis confirms that larger datasets generated by SOFTMIMICGEN lead to higher-performing visuomotor policies. Furthermore, by training policies to operate directly from visual input without explicit online registration, the approach circumvents potential issues arising from noisy real-world sensory data, enhancing real-world policy robustness.

While the current system assumes a fixed sequence of object-centric subtasks, the authors acknowledge that extending SOFTMIMICGEN to support less structured tasks with conditional transitions is a promising area for future work. Overall, SOFTMIMICGEN is positioned to significantly impact the field by enabling more scalable, efficient, and robust learning for complex robot manipulation skills involving deformable objects.
