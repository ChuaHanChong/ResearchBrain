# Reducing Temporal Redundancy for Efficient Vision-Language-Action Inference

> [!summary] Summary
> An approach to accelerate Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models reduces inference latency by addressing temporal redundancy in both visual perception and iterative action generation. Developed by a collaborative team including members from Tianji KernalMind co ltd. and Tsinghua University, this framework achieved over 2x end-to-end inference speedup in simulation and 1.6x on real robot platforms, while sustaining task success rates.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models suffer from high inference latency, preventing real-time, closed-loop control in robotic manipulation.
- Consecutive visual frames in robotic perception exhibit high temporal redundancy, yet VLA pipelines repeatedly re-encode entire frames at each step.
- Iterative action generation methods, like flow matching, incur high computational costs due to numerous steps, even though action dynamics often show low-rank structure.

## Method

- Introduced a unified system-level framework to accelerate VLA models without modifying their core backbone architectures.
- Implemented **Temporal Token Reuse** for perception, where only dynamic ==visual tokens== are recomputed, while static tokens are cached and reused across layers.
- Developed a **2-step efficient policy** for action generation, compressing multi-step ==flow matching== into two steps by leveraging ==low-rank velocity approximations== and a lightweight ==adaptor network==.

## Results

- The proposed method achieved an end-to-end inference speedup of over **2x** in simulation (e.g., from **3.5 FPS** to **8.2 FPS** for π0.5) and **1.6x** on real robot platforms.
- Task success rates remained competitive (e.g., **93.8%** on LIBERO for π0.5 with **2 steps**, compared to **94.4%** for the 10-step baseline).
- Real-world evaluation showed an improved time-constrained success rate (**SR@30s** from **77.1%** to **82.3%**) on a Marvin Pro robot, confirming practical benefits for faster task completion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual perception in VLA models contains substantial temporal redundancy, as evidenced by high cosine similarity (mostly above 0.98) between visual tokens in adjacent frames.
> - Action generation via flow matching exhibits low-rank dynamics, where velocity evolution is largely driven by a few dominant components, allowing for significant step compression.
> - The action generation module, particularly iterative sampling, constitutes the primary computational bottleneck in VLA pipelines, making its optimization critical for overall efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models has seen significant advancements, with systems like RT-2, OpenVLA, and π0 demonstrating strong generalization for robotic manipulation tasks through the integration of large-scale vision-language pre-training. These models enable robots to interpret natural language instructions and execute multi-step physical tasks, pushing the boundaries of robotic autonomy.

However, a significant challenge to their real-world deployment is high inference latency. Modern VLA pipelines typically rely on computationally intensive components such as large Vision Transformer (ViT) backbones for visual encoding and iterative inference mechanisms like diffusion models or flow matching for action generation. This high latency conflicts with the demands of real-time, high-frequency, closed-loop control necessary for safe and responsive operation in dynamic robotic environments.

Existing research efforts aimed at VLA acceleration often focus on optimizing isolated components. Examples include TinyVLA and DeeR-VLA, which propose lightweight architectures but are difficult to apply to existing large pre-trained models. Other methods, such as VLA-Cache, exploit visual redundancy through token caching but primarily target the perception module, leaving the computational overhead of the language model (LLM) core and the iterative action head largely unaddressed. Furthermore, many acceleration studies are primarily evaluated in simulation environments and focus on VLM components, with less exploration of action generation efficiency. Consequently, a gap remains in addressing the end-to-end latency of VLA agents at a systemic level.

This work positions itself to bridge this gap by proposing a system-level acceleration strategy that simultaneously addresses efficiency bottlenecks in both the perception and action generation components of VLA pipelines, thereby enabling real-time deployment without sacrificing performance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient acceleration method for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) systems to enable their real-time deployment in robotic manipulation tasks. The motivation stems from the recognition that current VLA models, despite their strong generalization capabilities, suffer from high inference latency, which is a major impediment to their practical application in dynamic, closed-loop control scenarios.

The authors identify two main sources of temporal redundancy within existing VLA pipelines that contribute to this latency:
1. **Redundancy in Visual Perception**: During continuous operation, consecutive frames captured by a robot's camera often exhibit high similarity. Existing VLA pipelines, however, repeatedly re-encode entire frames at each time step, even when only small portions of the scene have changed. The paper analyzes this by showing that visual tokens extracted from adjacent frames have extremely high cosine similarity (mostly above 0.98) across multiple ViT layers (Figure 1a). Additionally, token dissimilarity maps reveal that significant changes occur only in a sparse subset of spatial tokens, while the majority remain nearly constant over time (Figure 1b). This indicates substantial temporal redundancy in streaming robotic perception.
2. **Redundancy in Action Generation**: Modern VLA policies, particularly those based on diffusion or flow matching, rely on multi-step iterative sampling to generate actions. Each iteration requires a forward pass through the policy network, leading to a linear increase in inference cost with the number of steps. The research team investigates the singular value spectrum of velocity trajectories in flow matching, observing a rapid decay with energy concentrated in the top two dominant components (Figure 1c). This suggests that velocity evolution follows a low-dimensional structure, implying that multiple diffusion iterations primarily produce highly correlated refinements rather than independent directional updates.

Motivated by these identified structural redundancies across the entire VLA pipeline, the authors propose a unified acceleration strategy. The key objectives are:
- To reduce computation in the perception module by incrementally updating only visual tokens corresponding to dynamic scene regions, rather than re-encoding entire frames.
- To compress the iterative action generation process by exploiting the low-rank structure of flow velocities, thereby reducing the number of sampling steps required while maintaining action precision.
- To achieve significant end-to-end inference speedup (e.g., over 2x) on VLA models, enabling real-time closed-loop control without compromising task performance or success rate.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces a unified system-level acceleration framework for flow-based VLA models, targeting both perception and action generation. The approach avoids altering the backbone architecture, focusing instead on optimizing the inference process.

**A. Preliminaries: Vision-Language-Action Models**
The work focuses on transformer-based VLA models that map multimodal observations (image *I*, language tokens *L*, robot state *s*) to an action sequence *a* over a horizon *H*. These models typically consist of a vision encoder (e.g., SigLIP/ViT), a large language backbone for multimodal fusion, and a policy head that generates actions using diffusion or flow matching. In flow matching, action generation involves solving an Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) (Eq. 1) where *v_θ* is a learned velocity field. This is typically done by integrating backward from Gaussian noise *x1* to *t=0* using numerical solvers (e.g., Euler discretization, Eq. 2), often requiring 8 to 10 steps (*N*) per control cycle, which is a major latency bottleneck.

**B. Temporal Token Reuse (Perception Side)**
To address temporal redundancy in visual perception, the method proposes selectively updating visual tokens. The mechanism is based on an odd-even chunking strategy (Figure 2):
- **Reference Representation**: On "odd" chunks (e.g., every other frame), a full forward pass is performed, and the resulting key projections (*K*) from the Vision Transformer are cached as reference representations.
- **Dynamic Region Identification**: On "even" chunks (frames between full passes), cosine similarity is computed between the current frame's keys *K(i)t* and the previously cached reference keys *K(i)ref* (Eq. 3).
- **Selective Update**: Only a subset of tokens (e.g., the top-ρN tokens with the lowest cosine similarity) are selected for re-computation. These tokens correspond to dynamic regions where significant changes have occurred. The parameter *ρ* controls the update ratio.
- **Layer-wise Reuse**: Based on empirical observations that token similarity patterns are consistent across ViT layers (Figure 1a,b), the selection of indices for updated tokens is performed only in the first Transformer layer and then reused across all subsequent layers, minimizing computational overhead.
- **Feature Assembly**: For subsequent layers, updated tokens are recomputed from their corresponding queries (*Q*), keys (*K*), and values (*V*) (Eq. 4). The remaining tokens directly reuse their cached key-value pairs from the reference representation. The full representation is then reassembled (Eq. 5).
- **Attention and MLP**: Attention mechanisms are computed only for the updated tokens, while reused tokens inherit cached outputs. After residual connections, all tokens are processed by the MLP layer to ensure full forward propagation and stable training. This design ensures that static scene elements are efficiently preserved while dynamic changes are captured. The empirical observation indicated that typically about 60% of tokens need recomputing per frame.

**C. Efficient Flow Matching Policy with Step Compression (Action Generation Side)**
The methodology addresses the high computational cost of iterative flow matching by exploiting the low-rank nature of learned flow dynamics (Figure 1c, Figure 4).
- **Low-Rank Approximation**: The velocity sequence *vt* is modeled as a low-rank process. Specifically, the paper proposes approximating each velocity *vt* as a linear combination of a small number of basis directions (Eq. 6). In practice, two anchor velocities, *v0* (initial global direction) and *v7* (mid-trajectory refinement), are chosen as a basis (Eq. 7).
- **2-Step Formulation**: Under Euler integration, the final action state *xT* can be approximated as a function of the initial state *x0* and the weighted sum of these anchor velocities (Eq. 9). This formulation effectively compresses the multi-step solver (e.g., 10 steps) into a compact 2-step process (Figure 3).
- **Adaptor Training**: Since the coefficients (α, β, b) for this linear combination are not directly available, a lightweight adaptor *gφ* is introduced to predict these coefficients directly from the initial velocity *v0* (Eq. 10).
- **Efficiency-Oriented Training**: The adaptor *gφ* is trained by minimizing the discrepancy between the trajectory generated by the compressed 2-step formulation (*x(2)T*) and the full 10-step original trajectory (*x(10)T*) (Eq. 11). This training objective ensures that the compressed policy accurately reconstructs the trajectory despite the aggressive step reduction, thereby maintaining trajectory precision and stable control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

**A. Experimental Setup**
The proposed framework was evaluated on both simulated and real-world robotic platforms.
- **Simulation Benchmarks**: LIBERO and RobotWin, which are widely used suites for tabletop manipulation. The VLA model processed 224x224 RGB observations and natural language instructions, outputting 7-DoF joint space actions. The primary metric was task success rate (SR).
- **Real Robot Platform**: A Marvin Pro robotic platform, featuring a bimanual setup with two 7-DoF arms. Visual observations were captured by three RGB-D cameras (head-mounted Intel RealSense D435 and two wrist cameras SHW 5G). Demonstration data was collected via teleoperation using a Meta Quest 3. The policy generated actions in Cartesian space.
- **Hardware**: Simulation experiments utilized an NVIDIA A100 GPU, while real-world deployment ran on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU. Both used FP16 precision.

**B. Simulation Results**
(Table I) On the LIBERO benchmark, the method consistently achieved competitive task performance while significantly reducing the number of sampling steps.
- When applied to π0.5, the proposed method achieved a mean success rate of 93.8%, which is comparable to the original π0.5 (94.4%) and X-VLA (94.1%), despite reducing the sampling process from 10 steps to 2. This suggests that a substantial portion of iterative updates in flow-based policies can be considered redundant.
- Efficiency metrics showed a notable improvement: inference latency decreased from 286.9 ms to 121.2 ms, leading to an increase in control frequency from 3.5 FPS to 8.2 FPS.
- Similar trends were observed when applying the method to π0, demonstrating the generalizability of the step compression strategy across different policy backbones. The approach achieved 91.0% SR with 2 steps, 156.2 ms latency, and 6.4 FPS, compared to the baseline π0's 92.3% SR, 276.4 ms latency, and 3.6 FPS.

**C. Inference Efficiency Analysis**
(Table II) An analysis of inference time breakdown across perception (ViT), language reasoning (LLM), and action generation (Action Expert) modules revealed that the Action Expert was the primary computational bottleneck in the evaluated VLA models.
- For π0.5, the Action Expert accounted for 212.6 ms out of a total 293.2 ms latency, while ViT and LLM contributed 40.1 ms and 42.5 ms, respectively.
- The proposed method, while keeping ViT and LLM latencies largely unchanged (e.g., 38.6 ms for ViT and 42.3 ms for LLM with our method), dramatically reduced the Action Expert cost to 40.9 ms. This resulted in an overall end-to-end latency of 121.8 ms, representing a 241% increase in FPS (from 3.4 to 8.2) and a 64% reduction in TFLOPs (from 4.48 to 1.23).
- This analysis highlights that optimizing the action generation module is crucial for achieving substantial end-to-end speedup, particularly when it dominates computational cost. Similar results were observed for the RoboTwin 2.0 benchmark.

**D. System Level Ablation**
(Table III) A system-level ablation study clarified the individual contributions of temporal token reuse and the 2-step efficient policy.
- **Token Reuse alone**: Reduced ViT latency from 40.1 ms to 28.5 ms with a minor success rate degradation (93.9% vs. 94.4% for baseline π0.5). This confirmed the existence of substantial temporal redundancy in visual tokens.
- **Efficient Policy (2-step) alone**: Significantly reduced Action Expert latency from 212.6 ms to 41.5 ms, leading to a total inference time reduction from 293.2 ms to 123.9 ms, while maintaining a comparable success rate (94.1%). This indicates that aggressive compression of the multi-step flow matching process can be achieved without compromising action performance.
- **Combined (Ours)**: Combining both components yielded the lowest latency (111.6 ms, 9.0 FPS) with a minimal performance drop (93.8% SR). This demonstrates that the two components provide complementary gains by targeting different parts of the VLA pipeline.

**E. Real World Evaluation**
(Table IV, Figure 5, Figure 6) The method was validated on a Marvin Pro Robot across six representative manipulation tasks.
- The 2-step policy achieved a comparable overall success rate to the original π0.5 (95.4% vs. 97.2%).
- Notably, the time-constrained success rate within 30 seconds (SR@30s) consistently improved from 77.1% to 82.3%. This suggests that reduced policy latency enables the robot to react more promptly and complete long-horizon tasks within the allotted time.
- Qualitatively, the compressed policy maintained stable and precise manipulation behavior across multi-stage execution tasks (Figure 5), without introducing new failure modes. Task rollout comparisons (Figure 6) further confirmed that the 2-step policy produces smooth and goal-directed behaviors under time constraints, with trajectories remaining stable and coherent.

**F. Appendix Results**
- (Table VI) The latency reduction scaled approximately linearly with the decrease in solver steps, confirming the significance of step compression.
- (Table VII) Different token update ratios showed that updating approximately 40% of tokens provided a good balance between visual representation accuracy and computational efficiency.
- (Table VIII, IX) Detailed RoboTwin results confirmed competitive success rates and over 2x speedup compared to the base model, outperforming other acceleration methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes a systemic solution to the critical problem of high inference latency in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, a key bottleneck for their real-time deployment in robotics. By identifying and addressing temporal redundancies across both the perception and action generation modules, the proposed framework offers a unified and effective acceleration strategy.

The significance of this work lies in several aspects:
- **Enabling Real-Time Deployment**: The reported over 2x end-to-end inference speedup in simulation (up to 2.6x) and 1.6x acceleration on real robot platforms, coupled with the preservation of high task success rates (e.g., 93.8% on LIBERO), directly facilitates the practical application of large-scale VLA models in dynamic, real-world robotic environments. This allows for higher control frequencies, which are essential for responsive and precise manipulation.
- **Systemic Optimization**: Unlike many prior approaches that focus on isolated components (e.g., lightweight architectures for perception), this work presents a holistic system-level optimization. By simultaneously addressing redundancy in visual encoding through temporal token reuse and in action generation through 2-step flow matching compression, it effectively tackles the dominant computational bottlenecks.
- **Maintaining Performance**: A key achievement is the ability to achieve substantial efficiency gains with negligible degradation in manipulation performance. The improved time-constrained success rates on real robots demonstrate that faster inference directly translates to more reliable task completion within practical time limits, without sacrificing accuracy or introducing new failure modes.
- **Cost-Benefit Analysis**: The detailed analysis of computational costs highlights the disproportionate contribution of the action generation module to overall latency. This insight emphasizes that future research efforts targeting VLA efficiency should prioritize optimizations in policy execution alongside perception and language understanding.

**Limitations and Future Work**:
The authors acknowledge several limitations. The compressed policy has primarily been validated on short-horizon manipulation tasks. Its effectiveness for long-horizon planning or highly dynamic and complex environments requires further investigation. Future work will explore adaptive step scheduling strategies, where the number of inference steps could dynamically adjust based on task complexity or scene dynamics. Additionally, extending the framework to more diverse and complex tasks would further solidify its generalizability and practical utility.

Overall, this research represents a step towards making advanced, generalized robotic manipulation capabilities, powered by large VLA models, more accessible for practical, real-world applications by addressing a fundamental challenge of computational efficiency.
