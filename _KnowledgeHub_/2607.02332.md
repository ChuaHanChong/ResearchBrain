# HEFT: Heavy-Payload Full-size Humanoid Teleoperation with Privileged Motion Guidance and Windowed Payload Curriculum

> [!summary] Summary
> HEFT enables full-size humanoid robots to perform heavy-payload teleoperation, handling up to 24 kg on a 175 cm, 65 kg robot (L7), by integrating Privileged Motion Guidance for noisy VR inputs and a Windowed Payload Curriculum to adapt to motion-dependent load limits. This single policy achieves robust, diverse tasks on the physical L7 humanoid hardware without task-specific controllers.

## Problem

- Full-size humanoids are highly sensitive to noise, drift, and retargeting errors from commodity VR trackers due to their increased inertia and reduced balance margins, making real-time teleoperation challenging.
- Existing teleoperation systems underutilize the heavy-payload potential of full-size humanoids, as load feasibility dynamically varies with specific motions, hindering general whole-body tracking.
- Offline human-motion reconstruction methods, while improving motion quality, introduce computational delays that make them unsuitable for low-latency online robot control in teleoperation.

## Method

- **Privileged Motion Guidance (PMG)**: An ==asymmetric actor-critic reinforcement learning== framework where the deployable policy trains on raw, noisy VR inputs, but the critic and reward computation utilize physically plausible, offline-reconstructed "clean" motion references.
- **Windowed Payload Curriculum (WPC)**: A ==curriculum== that divides motions into 5-second windows and assigns ==expert-guided feasible payload caps== to each window, allowing the policy to progressively learn robust heavy-payload tracking by adapting to motion-dependent load limits.
- **Teacher-Student Distillation**: An ==RMA-style== architecture ==distills== privileged training-time information (like clean references and payload state) from a "teacher" policy into a deployable "student" controller, which then operates solely from raw VR inputs and proprioception.

## Results

- PMG effectively reduced long-horizon root tracking error on the full-size L7 humanoid with noisy VR inputs, achieving **0.560 m**, outperforming mocap-only and generic motion-noise training baselines.
- WPC enabled HEFT to maintain high success rates for heavy-payload tracking on the L7 humanoid, achieving **90%** success at **25 kg** and **75%** at **30 kg**, significantly outperforming baseline methods like TWIST2+FC.
- The single policy demonstrated robust teleoperation on the L7 hardware with real payloads up to **24 kg**, successfully performing diverse tasks including walking with two **12 kg** kettlebells, squatting with **24 kg**, and multi-stage object handling using raw VR references.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training a robot policy with noisy deployable inputs (raw VR) but evaluating it against clean, physically plausible targets (reconstructed motion) during learning allows the policy to robustly interpret operator intent while suppressing structured noise artifacts.
> - Systematically integrating the dynamic variability of payload limits across different motion segments, through a windowed curriculum with expert-guided caps, is crucial for teaching a single policy to handle heavy loads robustly across diverse movements.
> - A teacher-student distillation approach can effectively transfer complex, privileged training-time information to a deployable controller, enabling it to operate robustly in real-world conditions without requiring high-fidelity real-time inputs or task-specific modifications.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics is progressing towards more general-purpose and scalable skill acquisition, with teleoperation emerging as a method for operators to impart task intent and motion styles to robots. Existing research has explored teleoperated demonstrations for manipulation and mobile manipulation, extending to immersive teleoperation and whole-body imitation for humanoid systems. However, much of this prior work has focused on compact robot platforms or tasks that do not involve significant physical payload interaction.

This paper addresses a gap in the literature by focusing on full-size humanoids operating at human scale, where tasks frequently involve carrying and manipulating substantial objects. The work positions itself against the backdrop of challenges associated with scaling teleoperation to full-size humanoids, namely their increased inertia and reduced balance margins, which render them highly susceptible to noise, drift, and retargeting errors from commodity VR trackers. Furthermore, the payload potential of these larger robots often remains underutilized in current teleoperation frameworks.

Previous efforts in motion reconstruction aim to generate complete and plausible human motion from sparse and noisy data, such as VR inputs. While these techniques can improve motion quality, their iterative nature makes them unsuitable for low-latency online control required for teleoperation. Similarly, payload-aware whole-body control has been studied, but often within specific tasks, command spaces, or prescribed force curricula, which may not generalize to diverse whole-body tracking scenarios where payload feasibility varies dynamically with the motion itself.

HEFT contributes to this landscape by proposing a framework that specifically enables a single policy to handle both noisy VR-derived inputs and significant payloads on full-size humanoids. It aims to bridge the gap between human intent captured via deployable, noisy VR systems and the physical capabilities of large, dynamically complex humanoid robots under heavy loads. By integrating a training-time guidance mechanism for motion cleanliness and a motion-adaptive payload curriculum, the research seeks to advance general motion tracking and teleoperation capabilities for real-world applications of full-size humanoids.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a teleoperation framework for full-size humanoids that can effectively handle heavy payloads while tracking motions derived from noisy virtual reality (VR) inputs. The authors aim to enable a single, deployable policy to achieve robust performance across a range of dynamic motions, including locomotion, turns, and squats, with real payloads up to 24 kg on a 175 cm, 65 kg humanoid robot.

This objective is motivated by two compounding challenges identified by the authors:

1. **Sensitivity to Noisy VR References on Full-Size Humanoids**: Full-size humanoids possess greater inertia and operate with tighter balance margins compared to smaller platforms. Consequently, their motion tracking is highly sensitive to the inherent noise, drift, and retargeting errors that typically characterize data from commodity VR trackers. While offline human-motion reconstruction methods can generate more physically plausible motions from imperfect observations, their computational delay makes them impractical for real-time online robot command. The deployable controller therefore needs to interpret raw, noisy VR streams in real-time. This necessitates a training approach that allows the policy to learn from these noisy inputs while still being guided towards dynamically feasible and globally consistent motion targets, without imposing real-time reconstruction delays during deployment.

2. **Underutilization and Dynamic Variability of Payload Potential**: Full-size humanoids are designed to interact with human-scale objects, often involving significant payloads. However, their payload potential is frequently underutilized in existing teleoperation systems. The challenge lies in the fact that the feasibility of carrying a given load is not constant; it varies dynamically with the specific motion being performed. For instance, a load that is manageable during static support might destabilize the robot during turns, squats, or rapid arm movements. Current methods often target specific tasks or use prescribed force settings, which are not conducive to broad whole-body tracking across diverse teleoperated motions. This indicates a need for a payload curriculum that can adapt to motion-dependent feasibility constraints while remaining compatible with a wide library of whole-body teleoperation references.

To address these motivations, the paper sets out to develop two specific technical contributions:
- A **Privileged Motion Guidance (PMG)** formulation to train the deployable policy on raw VR inputs by leveraging physically plausible reconstructed references exclusively during training.
- A **Windowed Payload Curriculum (WPC)** that uses expert-guided payload caps assigned to short motion windows to enable the acquisition of robust heavy-payload tracking capabilities.

The overarching goal is to enable a single, general-purpose controller to perform diverse heavy-payload teleoperation tasks on full-size humanoids using standard VR inputs, thereby reducing the need for task-specific controllers and expanding the utility of humanoid robots in practical applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HEFT integrates Privileged Motion Guidance (PMG) and a Windowed Payload Curriculum (WPC) within an asymmetric actor-critic reinforcement learning framework to enable heavy-payload teleoperation on full-size humanoids using noisy VR references.

#### 3.1. Reference-Conditioned Payload Teleoperation

The framework learns a whole-body policy that tracks teleoperated reference motions while carrying two-hand payloads. The deployable policy observes `o_dep_t = (S_prop,t, S_raw,t)`, which includes proprioception, action history, and near-past/future targets from the raw VR reference stream. The policy outputs a joint-position command `a_t` for a low-level PD controller.

During training, each episode uses a reference motion `m_i` and a windowed payload schedule `F_window`. A key aspect is the use of two aligned motion streams: `S_i_raw` (the deployable, potentially noisy VR stream) and `S_i_clean` (a training-only, physically plausible reconstructed stream or clean mocap). Payloads are applied as external forces to the robot's wrist links in simulation, affecting dynamics but not directly observed by the deployed policy. The training objective is to maximize reward based on `S_clean` under payload-disturbed dynamics, with the policy acting on `S_raw`.

Training references are built from a large mocap library (SEED, 100STYLE, LaFAN1) where `S_raw = S_clean`, and a smaller paired VR dataset. For VR clips, `S_raw` comes from raw VR data, while `S_clean` is derived from offline reconstruction using models like RoHM and retargeting. An offline clean-reference tracking expert is also trained to filter inconsistent mocap clips and provide labels for WPC.

#### 3.2. Privileged Motion Guidance (PMG)

PMG addresses the discrepancy between the noisy `S_raw` and the clean `S_clean` reference streams. VR references often contain structured artifacts (global drift, body-frame bias, latency, end-effector offsets) that generic noise models do not capture. By using paired raw/reconstructed VR clips, the policy learns to differentiate operator intent from tracker artifacts.

In the asymmetric actor-critic setup:
- The **actor** (deployable policy) receives reference targets exclusively from `S_raw`.
- The **critic** and **reward computation** utilize `S_clean` as the tracking target.
- For mocap clips, `S_raw` is set to `S_clean`.

This allows the policy to train on the type of noisy input it will encounter online, while grounding its reward and value estimation in physically plausible motion. The `S_clean` stream for VR data is generated offline using a human-motion reconstruction model (e.g., RoHM) to remove physically implausible drift while preserving operator style and timing.

#### 3.3. Windowed Payload Curriculum (WPC)

WPC manages payload robustness by acknowledging that payload limits vary across different motion segments.
- **Motion Windows and Caps**: Each motion is divided into 5-second windows (`w_i,k`). An expert-guided feasible payload cap (`F_i,k`) is assigned to each window.
- **Expert Labeling**: To generate these caps, a reference-tracking expert (trained with `S_clean` and privileged simulator information) performs rollouts. The expert is tested under various downward forces (from 30 kg down to 0 kg in 5 kg steps) applied to the wrist links. The largest load for which the expert successfully completes a window without termination becomes the cap (`F_i,k`).
- **Payload Sampling**: During policy training, the total two-hand payload (`F_i,k`) for a sampled window `w_i,k` is drawn from a uniform distribution `U[0, F_i,k * clip(p/0.8, 0, 1)]`, where `p` is the training progress. This means payloads are gradually scaled up to the full cap as training progresses. The sampled load is split randomly between the two wrists and applied within a 12-degree cone around downward gravity. The payload state is included in privileged observations during training to accelerate learning, but not during deployment.

#### 3.4. Deployable Controller Training

The training process adopts an RMA-style teacher-student architecture to distill privileged training-time information into a deployable controller.
- **Teacher Policy**: The teacher policy conditions on a privileged latent `z_t = E_p(S_clean,t, S_priv,t)`. `S_priv,t` includes simulator-only information such as tracking errors, contact signals, payload state, and randomization variables.
- **Adapter**: An adapter network learns to predict the same latent space (`^z_t = E_a(S_prop,t, S_raw,t)`) from only policy-visible history (`S_prop,t, S_raw,t`). It is supervised by `L_adapt = ||^z_t - z_t||^2_2`.
- **Training Stages**:
 1. The teacher policy is first optimized using PPO with PMG and WPC.
 2. The learned latent from the teacher is distilled into the adapter.
 3. The student policy is fine-tuned, conditioned on the adapter-predicted latent `^z_t`.
- **Deployment**: At deployment, only the student policy and the adapter are used. All privileged information, reconstructed references, and window caps are removed, allowing the system to operate solely from raw VR references and proprioception.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates HEFT's performance in simulation on both G1 (a compact humanoid) and L7 (a full-size humanoid) and on L7 hardware. The evaluation focuses on two main questions: the effectiveness of PMG for noisy VR data and the impact of WPC on heavy-payload tracking.

#### 4.1. Privileged Motion Guidance (PMG)

PMG was compared against two mocap-only ablations (one with, one without motion noise), SONIC, and TWIST2 baselines.

- **Adaptation to Noisy VR Data (D_VR dataset)**: On the `D_VR` dataset, which contains structured VR noise, PMG demonstrated reduced long-horizon root tracking error.
 - On G1, PMG achieved a final horizontal root error of 0.544 m.
 - On L7, PMG achieved 0.560 m.
 - These values were lower than those of mocap-only training, generic motion-noise training, and TWIST2, indicating that PMG effectively interprets noisy VR references and suppresses structured VR artifacts.
- **General Tracking Performance (D_random dataset)**: On the `D_random` dataset with noise-free reference inputs, PMG maintained high success rates and close tracking accuracy to clean-reference training.
 - PMG showed MPJPE values of 0.021 m on G1 and 0.036 m on L7, comparing favorably against SONIC (0.043 m on G1) and TWIST2 (0.061 m on G1, 0.099 m on L7).
These results suggest that PMG enhances the system's ability to handle noisy VR inputs without significantly compromising tracking accuracy on clean motions.

#### 4.2. Windowed Payload Curriculum (WPC)

WPC was evaluated on L7, comparing it with a TWIST2+FC baseline (TWIST2 with a FALCON-style force curriculum) and a `w/o expert` ablation (WPC without expert-guided caps).

- **High-Payload Tracking (D_random dataset)**: WPC demonstrated improved success rates at higher payload levels.
 - HEFT (Ours) maintained high success up to 20 kg, achieving 90% success at 25 kg and 75% at 30 kg.
 - The `w/o expert` ablation dropped to 80% and 62% at 25 kg and 30 kg, respectively.
 - TWIST2+FC showed lower success, falling to 35% and 29% at these loads.
 - In terms of tracking metrics (MPJPE and velocity errors), HEFT remained comparable to the `w/o expert` ablation and generally more accurate than TWIST2+FC, indicating that expert-guided window caps primarily extend the feasible high-load range rather than reducing baseline accuracy.
- **Unloaded Dynamic Motion Tracking (D_dynamic dataset)**: WPC preserved and even improved tracking performance on challenging high-dynamic motions without payloads.
 - HEFT achieved a success rate of 73%, an improvement over the `w/o expert` ablation (64%).
 - It also showed slight reductions in MPJPE (0.057 m vs. 0.060 m) and root linear velocity error (0.743 m/s vs. 0.872 m/s).
 - TWIST2+FC exhibited significantly lower stability (10% success) and larger tracking errors on these motions.

#### 4.3. Real-Robot Teleoperation

The developed policy was deployed on the L7 hardware without switching policies or task-specific controllers.
- The single policy demonstrated robust performance across various tasks, including multi-stage object handling (e.g., picking up a 5 kg loaded basket), asymmetric carrying (e.g., a 10 kg water bottle), walking with heavy loads (e.g., two 12 kg kettlebells), squatting with 24 kg, backpack pickup and placement, desk lifting, and rack pushing.
- These real-world validations confirm the ability of the PMG to enable control from raw VR references and the WPC to allow the same whole-body mimic policy to tolerate significant real payloads and contact-rich behaviors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HEFT framework represents an advancement in the field of humanoid teleoperation, particularly for full-size platforms interacting with the physical world. Its significance lies in addressing two core challenges that have historically limited the practical deployment of teleoperated humanoids: operating reliably from noisy, deployable VR inputs and handling substantial, dynamically varying payloads with a single, generalizable policy.

**Key areas of significance include:**

- **Robustness to Noisy VR Inputs**: By introducing Privileged Motion Guidance (PMG), HEFT enables the robot to learn to interpret and act upon raw, noisy VR data streams. This is crucial for practical teleoperation, as commodity VR trackers are prone to artifacts like drift, misalignment, and latency. The ability to abstract away these imperfections during online operation, without relying on computationally expensive real-time reconstruction, makes VR-based teleoperation more viable for real-world scenarios. This can lower the barrier to entry for developing and deploying humanoid skills, as it reduces the need for specialized motion capture systems.
- **Enhanced Payload Capability with Generalization**: The Windowed Payload Curriculum (WPC) allows a single whole-body policy to robustly handle heavy payloads up to 24 kg, adapting to the varying feasibility of loads across different motion segments. This represents a substantial improvement over methods that require task-specific controllers or rigid force curricula. The ability to perform complex, dynamic movements like walking, turning, and squatting while carrying significant weights expands the utility of humanoids for physically demanding tasks, such as logistics, construction, or disaster response.
- **Scalable Skill Acquisition**: HEFT's approach contributes to scalable humanoid skill acquisition. By enabling human operators to teleoperate a full-size humanoid for complex, heavy-payload tasks using a general controller, it facilitates the collection of diverse, high-fidelity demonstration data. This data can then be used to train more autonomous policies, accelerating the development of advanced humanoid behaviors.
- **Hardware Validation on Full-Size Humanoid**: The validation on L7, a 175 cm, 65 kg humanoid, demonstrates the framework's applicability to human-scale robots. This is important because full-size humanoids pose greater control challenges due to their larger inertia and tighter balance margins. The successful real-robot teleoperation of multi-stage object handling, asymmetric carrying, and heavy-load locomotion and squatting tasks validates the practical viability of the proposed methods.

**Potential impacts include:**

- **Broader Adoption of Humanoids**: By making teleoperation more robust and capable for demanding physical tasks, HEFT could accelerate the adoption of humanoids in industries requiring physical labor, where heavy lifting and stable locomotion under load are essential.
- **Development of More Autonomous Systems**: The framework provides a foundation for training more capable autonomous humanoid policies. By generating high-quality demonstrations of heavy-payload manipulation and locomotion, it can inform reinforcement learning algorithms, leading to robots that can independently perform complex tasks in unstructured environments.
- **Advancements in Human-Robot Interaction**: Improvements in teleoperation fidelity and capability enhance the operator's sense of presence and control, fostering more intuitive and effective human-robot collaboration.

While the current work has limitations, such as requiring offline reconstructed references and expert labels for training and approximating object interaction as wrist-applied forces, it lays significant groundwork for future research in robust, general-purpose humanoid control. The demonstrated ability of a single policy to handle diverse heavy-payload tasks from noisy VR inputs marks a step towards more versatile and deployable humanoid robots.
