# DEFT: Dexterous Fine-Tuning for Real-World Hand Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> DEFT learns dexterous real-world manipulation on a soft anthropomorphic hand without robot demonstrations. A task-conditioned affordance model trained on 76.5K internet human-video clips predicts grasp contact point, wrist rotation, and finger pose; a CEM-style residual sampling loop then fine-tunes these parameters online in under an hour, and a conditional VAE distills elite rollouts into a generalizing residual policy across 9 tabletop tasks.

## Problem

- Reinforcement and imitation learning for dexterous hands need vast data and privileged rewards, staying limited to simple tasks despite heavy real-world interaction budgets.
- Soft, deformable objects and soft hands cannot be simulated accurately, so sim-to-real pipelines that dominate dexterous manipulation are unavailable for these settings.
- Rigid hands break under contact-rich practice, and learning high-dimensional hand policies from scratch explores uselessly without a prior over sensible grasps.

## Method

- Task-conditioned ==affordance model==: ==R3M== visual encoder plus ==CLIP== text embedding feed a ==transformer== predicting GMM contact heatmap, wrist euler angles, and ==MANO== grasp pose, retargeted to 16 soft-hand joints.
- Real-world residual fine-tuning via ==Cross-Entropy Method (CEM)==: sample noise on the 22 grasp parameters, rank 30 rollouts by reward, refit the elite distribution over 10 elites.
- ==Conditional VAE== residual policy distills the top-ten CEM trajectories, conditioned on the initial image and affordance output, to generalize residuals to new object placements. Together these stages form **DEFT**.

## Results

- DEFT reaches **0.5-0.8** train success across six tasks versus **0.0-0.7** for real-world-only heuristic priors and **0.0-0.5** for the zero-shot affordance model alone.
- Success climbs steadily over **10-30** CEM rollouts on pick cup, pour, open drawer, pick spoon, scoop, and stir, plus soft-object tasks flip bagel and squeeze lemon.
- Ablations: MLP residual head scores **0.0** on pour versus VAE **0.8**; direct parameter estimation drops to **0.1**; R3M-embedding automated reward stays competitive except on pour.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Internet human video supplies the contact point and wrist rotation prior that heuristic object-center grasping cannot, turning intractable high-dimensional hand exploration into a small local search.
> - Learning residuals over a prior, rather than absolute parameters, is what makes distillation work; direct estimation discards the affordance information within the same data budget.
> - Compliant soft hardware substitutes for tactile sensing and simulation, enabling hours of contact-rich practice, but limited finger curl still caps grasps on thin or small objects.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "DEFT" operates within the field of robot learning, specifically addressing dexterous manipulation in real-world environments. A longstanding objective in robot learning is to develop autonomous agents capable of performing complex, long-horizon tasks. Many such tasks, when performed by humans, necessitate intricate hand movements and tool operation, underscoring the importance of dexterous manipulation in robotics.

Current approaches to robot learning for real-world tasks, such as deep reinforcement learning (RL) and imitation learning (IL), frequently encounter challenges related to data efficiency. Learning complex behaviors from scratch often demands substantial amounts of data and can be resource-intensive. These methods have achieved some success in sim-to-real transfer or tasks amenable to extensive real-world practice, but they typically struggle with tasks that are difficult to simulate (e.g., manipulation of soft objects), involve sparse rewards, or are prone to hardware failure due to repetitive contact.

The fragility of robotic hardware, particularly complex dexterous hands, during continuous real-world interaction is another significant impediment. The paper addresses this by utilizing a soft anthropomorphic hand, which offers robustness to continuous contact and simplifies the retargeting of human hand grasps due to its human-like morphology. However, the softness that provides robustness also makes accurate simulation challenging, further complicating simulation-based learning.

To overcome the data inefficiency and simulation difficulties, the research leverages the growing availability of human motion data from internet videos. This aligns with a broader trend in robotics and computer vision to extract priors from human demonstrations or observations, which can then guide robot learning, thereby making exploration more efficient and behaviors more human-like. Prior work has explored learning general priors using contrastive learning or direct retargeting of human motion to robots.

DEFT integrates these themes by combining human-driven priors, specifically visual affordances derived from internet videos, with real-world online optimization. This hybrid approach aims to bridge the gap between human capabilities and robotic execution, particularly for tasks involving complex interactions and deformable objects, positioning it as a contribution to efficient and robust real-world dexterous manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient and robust approach for learning complex, dexterous manipulation tasks in the real world. The authors aim to enable a robotic system, equipped with a soft anthropomorphic hand, to perform tasks that involve manipulating both rigid and soft, deformable objects, including tool use, without requiring extensive robot demonstrations or vast amounts of real-world practice data.

The motivation behind DEFT stems from several identified challenges in current robot learning paradigms:

- **Data Inefficiency in Real-World Learning:** Learning dexterous behaviors from scratch in the real world is inherently data-inefficient due to the high-dimensional action spaces of robotic hands and the complexity of real-world dynamics. The authors highlight the need for a strong prior to guide exploration and make real-world learning tractable.
- **Challenges with Soft Object Manipulation:** Many real-world tasks, such as food preparation, involve manipulating soft and deformable objects. These objects are difficult to model accurately in simulation, making sim-to-real transfer approaches less effective. A method capable of learning directly in the real world, especially with robust hardware, is thus motivated.
- **Hardware Robustness and Dexterous Hand Control:** Traditional rigid robot hands can be susceptible to damage during continuous contact, which is common in dexterous tasks like tool use. The paper explicitly motivates the use of a soft anthropomorphic hand for its inherent robustness to collisions and its ability to handle delicate object interactions, enabling prolonged real-world data collection.
- **Leveraging Human Knowledge:** Humans perform a vast array of dexterous tasks proficiently. The authors are motivated by the idea of using the readily available corpus of human demonstration videos from the internet to impart initial knowledge to robots. This human-driven prior can establish a baseline for "reasonable behavior" and provide guidance on how to interact with objects (e.g., where to grasp and how to hold).
- **Efficient Online Adaptation:** While human priors provide a useful starting point, direct application often fails due to differences in robot morphology, inaccuracies in human pose estimation, or variations in task setup. Therefore, the research is motivated to develop an efficient online optimization procedure that can refine these human-derived priors to successfully execute tasks in the robot's specific real-world environment. The goal is for this fine-tuning to occur rapidly, within a few trials or less than an hour, to be practical.
- **Generalization to Diverse Tasks and Objects:** The authors aim for an approach that can generalize to a variety of objects and scenarios, moving beyond task-specific training to a more versatile manipulation capability.

In summary, DEFT is motivated by the need for a practical robot learning framework that can efficiently acquire complex dexterous skills in real-world settings, particularly for challenging scenarios involving soft objects and tool use, by synergistically combining human observational learning with targeted real-world adaptation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DEFT employs a two-phase approach: first, learning grasping affordances as a prior from human videos, and second, fine-tuning these priors through real-world interaction using an online optimization procedure.

**Phase 1: Learning Grasping Affordances from Human Videos**

The objective of this phase is to extract actionable information from a large corpus of human interaction videos to generate an initial, task-conditioned grasp policy.

- **Data Collection and Extraction:**
 - The model is trained on three large-scale, egocentric human video datasets: Ego4D, HOI4D, and EPIC Kitchens, totaling over 76,000 clips.
 - For each video clip, the first frame where the human hand contacts an object is identified using an off-the-shelf hand-object detection model.
 - Three key pieces of information are extracted for each interaction:
 1. **Grasp Location ($\mu$):** A set of contact points on the object surface is extracted. Detic is used to crop the object from the initial frame. A Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) is fitted to these contact points.
 2. **Human Grasp Pose ($\mathbf{P}$):** The human hand grasp pose, represented by MANO parameters (a 45-dimensional vector), is extracted using Frankmocap at the moment of contact. The wrist orientation ($\theta_{wrist}$) in the camera frame is also recorded.
 3. **Task Description ($\mathbf{T}$):** A text description of the action occurring in the video clip.
- **Affordance Model (f) Architecture:**
 - A task-conditioned affordance model $f$ is trained to predict $(\hat{\mu}, \hat{\theta}_{wrist}, \hat{\mathbf{P}})$ given a cropped object image ($v'_1$) and a task description ($\mathbf{T}$).
 - The object image $v'_1$ is encoded into a latent vector $z_v$ using a pre-trained visual model (R3M). $z_v$ is then passed through deconvolutional layers to generate a heatmap, from which $\hat{\mu}$ (contact point) is estimated using spatial softmax.
 - The text description $\mathbf{T}$ is encoded into $z_T$ using the CLIP text encoder.
 - A transformer encoder takes the concatenated $z_v$ and $z_T$ as input to predict $\hat{\theta}_{wrist}$ (wrist rotation, 3 Euler angles) and $\hat{\mathbf{P}}$ (hand joint angles, initially 45-dimensional MANO parameters).
 - The model is optimized using an L2 loss on the predicted values against the ground truth.
- **MANO Retargeting and Post-Grasp Trajectory:**
 - The 45-dimensional MANO parameters for human hand pose are retargeted to a 16-dimensional pose compatible with the soft robot hand. This involves mapping anatomical human joint axes (bending, spreading) to the robot hand's motor-controlled joint movements.
 - For the task-specific post-contact trajectory, a single human demonstration of the target robot task is collected separately. Frankmocap is used to extract the wrist trajectory, which is then represented as a sequence of wrist pose displacements for the first 40 timesteps after grasp.

**Phase 2: Fine-Tuning via Interaction (Online Optimization)**

This phase refines the learned affordance prior in the real world to account for discrepancies between human and robot morphology, detection inaccuracies, and task setup variations.

- **Residual Policy Learning with CEM:**
 - A residual policy is learned for the grasp parameters: grasp location ($\mu$), wrist rotation ($\theta_{wrist}$), and soft hand joint angles ($\mathbf{P}$). The post-grasp trajectory ($\tau$) remains fixed from the human demo.
 - The Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) is employed for efficient online optimization.
 - **Procedure (Algorithm 1):**
 1. An initial distribution $D$ (Gaussian, $\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$) for the residuals ($\epsilon$) is set.
 2. **Warm-up Episodes (M):** An initial set of $M$ episodes are executed by sampling residuals $\epsilon$ from $D$, adding them to the affordance prior $\xi$ (from the affordance model), performing the combined action ($\xi + \epsilon$), and collecting a human-assigned continuous reward $R_k$ (0 to 1). The environment is then reset.
 3. **CEM Iterations (N):** For $N$ subsequent iterations, the episodes collected so far are ranked by their rewards. The residuals from the top-performing 'elite' episodes ($\Omega$) are used to refit the distribution $D$. New actions are then sampled from this refined $D$.
 4. In the experiments, $M=10$ warmup episodes and $N=20$ CEM iterations (total 30 episodes) were used, which typically takes less than an hour per task.
- **Generalization with Conditional VAE (cVAE):**
 - To generalize the learned residuals to unseen initial object configurations at test time, a Conditional Variational Autoencoder (cVAE) is trained.
 - The cVAE learns to output residuals $\delta_j$ conditioned on an encoding of the initial image $\phi(I_{j,0})$ and the affordance model's outputs $\xi_j$ from the top-performing CEM episodes.
 - At test time, given a new initial image and affordance output, the cVAE samples a latent vector $z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ and predicts a residual $\hat{\delta}$. The final policy executes the trajectory determined by $\xi + \hat{\delta}$.

**Hardware Setup:**
Experiments are conducted using a 6-DOF UFactory xArm6 robot arm equipped with a 16-DOF Dexterous Anthropomorphic Soft Hand (DASH). An egocentric RGBD camera provides visual input for 3D object localization. The soft hand's flexibility is highlighted for its robustness to collisions.

**Tasks:**
The method is evaluated on 9 tabletop tasks, including "Pick Cup," "Pour Cup," "Open Drawer," "Pick Spoon," "Scoop Grape," "Stir Spoon," "Pick Grape," "Flip Bagel," and "Squeeze Lemon." These tasks involve both rigid and soft objects, requiring varied dexterous manipulation and tool use. Object positions are randomized, and different objects are used for training and testing generalization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding DEFT's efficacy in dexterous manipulation:

- **Overall Performance of DEFT:** DEFT, which combines human-driven affordance priors with real-world fine-tuning, consistently achieved higher success rates across the evaluated 9 tasks compared to baselines. For example, on "Pick Cup" (train/test), DEFT achieved 0.8/0.8 success rates, while "Real-World Only" (0.0/0.1) and "Affordance Model Only" (0.1/0.4) were significantly lower. This trend was observed across most tasks, with DEFT demonstrating 0.8/0.9 for "Pour Cup," 0.5/0.4 for "Open Drawer," and 0.8/0.6 for "Pick Spoon."
- **Importance of Affordance Model:**
 - The "Real-World Only" baseline, which used heuristics (bounding box center for contact, random rotation, half-closed hand) instead of the learned affordance prior, exhibited poor performance (e.g., 0.0-0.3 success rates across tasks). This indicated that a strong prior, especially for hand rotation, is crucial for stable grasps in tool manipulation.
 - The "Affordance Model Only" baseline, representing zero-shot execution of the prior without fine-tuning, performed better than "Real-World Only" but struggled with tasks requiring robust power grasps or precise interactions, such as "Stir" and "Scoop" (0.0/0.3 and 0.0/0.0 respectively). This suggested that while the prior provided a reasonable starting point, real-world adaptation was necessary for task completion.
- **Effectiveness of CEM Fine-Tuning:** The results demonstrated a steady improvement in success rates as more CEM episodes were collected (Figure 6). This indicated that the online fine-tuning procedure effectively optimized the initial priors over a relatively small number of real-world interactions (30 episodes).
- **Automated vs. Human Rewards:**
 - An ablation study on reward functions showed that using an R3M encoder to calculate visual rewards (negative embedding distance between episode's final image and a human-demonstrated goal image) yielded competitive performance compared to human-assigned continuous rewards on most tasks.
 - A ResNet18 Imagenet encoder for visual rewards was less effective. This finding suggests potential for reducing human intervention by leveraging robust visual reward models.
- **Policy Architecture Ablations:**
 - Replacing the conditional VAE for learning residuals with a simpler MLP resulted in significantly lower success rates for tasks like "Pour Cup" (0.0/0.0), indicating the MLP's difficulty in learning the multi-modal nature of successful data.
 - A transformer-based offline method, conditioned on reward to predict residuals, showed competitive but generally slightly lower performance than DEFT, suggesting it might require more data for equivalent results.
 - A VAE trained to directly estimate grasp parameters ($\xi$) instead of residuals ($\delta$) performed poorly (e.g., 0.1/0.1 for "Pour Cup"), underscoring the benefit of learning residuals which build upon the pre-trained affordance prior.
- **Complex and Soft Object Manipulation:**
 - DEFT demonstrated reasonable success on challenging tasks involving soft objects that are difficult to simulate, such as "Flip Bagel" (0.6 success rate after fine-tuning) and "Squeeze Lemon" (0.4 success rate). This highlights the advantage of real-world learning with a soft robotic hand for such scenarios.
 - The "Pick Grape" task proved particularly challenging for DEFT (0.2 success rate). This was attributed to a physical limitation of the soft hand, specifically its inability to curl fingers fully, which made it difficult to secure small objects like grapes or maintain a strong grip on heavier objects like spatulas.

In summary, DEFT successfully integrates human priors and real-world fine-tuning, demonstrating an ability to learn dexterous manipulation for a variety of tasks, including those with soft objects. The efficiency of the fine-tuning and the potential for automated rewards were identified as key strengths, while physical hardware limitations were noted as factors impacting performance on certain tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DEFT approach contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by offering a data-efficient and robust pathway for learning dexterous skills directly in the real world. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap and Data Efficiency:** By leveraging large-scale human video data as a prior, DEFT addresses the problem of data inefficiency in real-world robot learning, particularly for complex dexterous tasks. This reduces the need for extensive robot-specific data collection or high-fidelity simulations, which are often impractical for deformable objects. The ability to fine-tune a policy in under an hour for a new task represents a step towards practical deployment.
- **Enabling Soft Object Manipulation:** The paper demonstrates a method capable of handling soft and deformable objects (e.g., bagels, lemons) directly in the real world. This is a significant impact as such objects are notoriously difficult to model and manipulate accurately in simulation, a common limitation of many robot learning frameworks.
- **Robustness through Soft Robotics:** The integration of a soft anthropomorphic hand (DASH) enhances the system's robustness to continuous contact and unexpected interactions, which are common in dexterous tasks. This contributes to the feasibility of long-term real-world robot operation, as it mitigates issues of hardware damage.
- **Foundation for General Dexterous Manipulation:** By combining task-conditioned grasp affordances from human videos with iterative real-world refinement, DEFT provides a framework for acquiring a range of dexterous manipulation skills. This approach could move towards more generalizable robotic agents that can adapt human skills to their own embodiment and environment.
- **Reduced Reliance on Expert Demonstrations:** While a single human demonstration is used for the post-grasp trajectory, the core grasp affordances are learned from observational internet videos, reducing the need for costly and time-consuming expert robot demonstrations. The finding that visual rewards can approximate human-assigned rewards further suggests a path towards greater autonomy in the fine-tuning process.
- **Implications for Human-Robot Collaboration:** A system that can learn and adapt dexterous skills efficiently could enhance human-robot collaboration by allowing robots to perform more nuanced tasks that currently require human intervention.

Despite these contributions, the work also highlights current limitations that represent areas for future research:
- **Grasp Multi-modality:** The dependency on noisy human hand detections in affordance pretraining currently limits the diversity of learned grasps, indicating a need for improved human hand detection models.
- **Human-in-the-Loop for Resets:** The requirement for human intervention during task resets in the fine-tuning phase restricts the autonomy and scalability of data collection. Developing autonomous reset mechanisms or more robust failure recovery could further enhance efficiency.
- **Physical Hardware Limitations:** The noted physical constraints of the soft hand (e.g., inability to fully curl fingers) impact performance on certain object types. Future robot hand designs could aim to overcome these limitations.

Overall, DEFT represents a significant step towards practical and generalizable dexterous robot manipulation in unstructured real-world environments, particularly for tasks involving challenging object properties.
