# Distill What the Student Can See: Fisher-Projected On-Policy Distillation for Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Tianjin University introduced Fisher-Projected On-Policy Distillation (FP-OPD) to address the capacity mismatch in distilling Vision-Language Models, projecting teacher corrections onto a student's locally realizable visual response space. This method improved average performance by 2.77 points over the base student and 1.60 points over standard On-Policy Distillation for 8B-to-2B VLM distillation across seven multimodal benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing on-policy distillation (OPD) for Vision-Language Models (VLMs) assumes the full teacher distribution is a universally appropriate target, failing to account for student model capacity.
- Compact VLM students often cannot perceive or represent the nuanced visual distinctions underlying a larger teacher's corrections.
- This capacity mismatch leads to unrealizable distillation targets, impeding effective knowledge transfer and potentially reducing student performance.

## Method

- A new method, Fisher-Projected On-Policy Distillation (**FP-OPD**), introduces a ==capacity-aware distillation target== for VLMs by filtering the teacher's correction.
- It approximates the student's local visual response space by applying continuous ==visual perturbations== to image tokens and observing the student's log-probability changes.
- The teacher's log-probability correction is projected onto this estimated ==visual tangent space== using the ==Fisher metric==, retaining only components the student can visually express.

## Results

- FP-OPD improved average performance by **2.77 points** over the base student and **1.60 points** over standard OPD in 8B-to-2B VLM distillation across seven multimodal benchmarks.
- The method showed consistent gains across mathematical reasoning tasks (e.g., **+2.95** on MMMU, **+2.55** on WeMath) and general multimodal benchmarks in the 8B-to-2B setting.
- FP-OPD scaled effectively to larger models (32B-to-8B distillation), achieving an average score **0.80 points** higher than standard OPD, with particular improvements in mathematical reasoning tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A 'second mismatch' in VLM on-policy distillation exists where teacher corrections, often visually grounded, can be beyond the student's representational capacity, requiring capacity-aware targets.
> - Projecting teacher corrections using the Fisher metric provides a principled framework for defining a locally realizable target, consistent with the KL divergence objective.
> - Perturbing student visual embeddings with fixed spatial masks can effectively approximate the student's visual response geometry, allowing for empirical tangent space estimation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of knowledge distillation, particularly focusing on its application to Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and the refinement of on-policy distillation (OPD) techniques.

Knowledge distillation (KD) is a conventional method for transferring knowledge from a larger, more capable "teacher" model to a smaller, more compact "student" model by matching the student's predictive distribution to that of the teacher. For autoregressive reasoning models, a known challenge is the mismatch between the fixed trajectories used during distillation training and the states the student might encounter during its own inference generation.

On-policy distillation (OPD) was introduced to address this mismatch by sampling trajectories directly from the current student policy and aligning the student's and teacher's next-token distributions at prefixes along these student-generated trajectories. This ensures that supervision is applied to states that the student is likely to visit during its own generation process. Existing OPD methods have explored various aspects, such as directly matching distributions (GKD-OPD), deriving learning signals from log-probability ratios (PG-OPD), combining multiple teachers (MOPD), or using self-distillation under privileged contexts (OPSD). Other work has focused on improving the efficiency or reliability of OPD, for example, by restricting supervision to early reasoning tokens (Prefix OPD) or transferring regional perception through self-distillation (Vision-OPD).

Vision-Language Models themselves represent a rapidly evolving area, with advancements in stronger visual encoders, native-resolution processing, and large-scale multimodal pretraining. Models like LLaVA-OneVision, Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3, and Qwen3-VL have demonstrated increasing capabilities across various multimodal tasks. These advancements have also led to the development of model families with substantial differences in visual capacity across different scales, setting the stage for the challenges addressed in this paper.

The present work identifies a limitation in existing OPD approaches when applied to VLMs: while OPD aligns *where* supervision is applied (student-visited prefixes), it does not account for *what* supervision requests. Specifically, teacher corrections can rely on visual evidence or distinctions that a compact student model may not be capable of representing. Prior research in distillation has shown that simply increasing teacher capacity does not always lead to better student performance, especially when there is a significant capacity gap or misalignment in reasoning patterns.

This paper introduces a modification to the OPD framework by adapting the *distillation target* rather than altering the core optimization or the source of on-policy supervision. It leverages principles from Fisher information geometry, which characterizes the local geometry of predictive distributions and is foundational to methods like natural-gradient optimization. While some related work has explored Sobolev training or Jacobian matching to transfer derivative information, this paper uniquely employs continuous visual perturbations to estimate a local output space and uses Fisher geometry to project the teacher correction, ensuring the target correction is locally realizable by the student's visual pathway.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the effectiveness of on-policy knowledge distillation for compact Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by addressing a specific mismatch between the teacher's complete supervisory signal and the student's representational capacity.

The motivation stems from an identified limitation in standard on-policy distillation (OPD) when applied to VLMs. While OPD successfully aligns the distillation states with the student’s own generation distribution by sampling trajectories from the student policy, it operates under the assumption that the complete teacher distribution, including all its corrections, constitutes an appropriate target across varying student capacities.

The authors contend that this assumption is often incorrect, particularly in vision-language reasoning tasks. They highlight a "second mismatch" where teacher corrections might implicitly depend on nuanced visual distinctions or evidence that a compact student model is unable to process or represent accurately. For instance, a teacher might correctly identify an angle in an image as 65 degrees, leading to a specific next-token prediction, while a smaller student might misread it as 55 degrees due to its more limited visual processing capabilities. In such a scenario, distilling the complete teacher's correction would ask the student to account for visual information it cannot perceive, leading to an unrealizable target.

Empirical evidence is presented to support this motivation:
1. **Illustrative Example:** Figure 1(a) visually demonstrates a case where a teacher correctly identifies a visual feature (an angle of 65 degrees), while a student misinterprets it (55 degrees), leading to differing subsequent reasoning and final answers despite sharing the same generated prefix. This suggests that the teacher's next-token distribution is grounded in visual details inaccessible to the student.
2. **Target-Scaling Study:** The authors conducted a study controlling the interpolation strength (α) of the distillation target, effectively scaling how far the target moves from the student distribution towards the teacher distribution. They observed that for an 8B-to-2B VLM distillation, using the complete teacher target (α=1) resulted in the student realizing only 46.6% of the prescribed KL gap after one epoch, compared to 56.3% with an intermediate target (α=0.5). Furthermore, the intermediate target (α=0.5) yielded better downstream performance on the MathVista Geometry subset (57.03 vs. 55.29). This outcome indicates that a complete teacher target is both harder for the student to fit and can lead to degraded downstream reasoning performance, implying that portions of the teacher's target are not locally realizable by the student.

These observations motivated the development of a "capacity-aware" OPD target. The objective is not merely to uniformly weaken the teacher's supervision, which would diminish both realizable and unrealizable corrections. Instead, the goal is to selectively preserve only the component of the teacher's correction that is supported by the student’s *current visual response geometry*. The authors propose that the Fisher metric, which describes the local geometry of predictive distributions induced by KL divergence, offers a principled mathematical framework for identifying the teacher-directed changes that the student can express through its visual pathway.

Therefore, the key objective is to introduce a novel distillation method that:
- Identifies and isolates the "locally realizable" components of the teacher's supervisory signal.
- Projects the teacher's log-probability correction onto the student's visual response space under the Fisher metric.
- Constructs a capacity-aware distillation target based on this projection.
- Integrates this target seamlessly into the existing on-policy reverse KL distillation framework without altering its core loss function or training protocol.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Fisher-Projected On-Policy Distillation (FP-OPD), refines the standard On-Policy Distillation (OPD) by making the distillation target capacity-aware. This is achieved by projecting the teacher’s next-token distribution corrections onto the student’s local visual response space using the Fisher metric.

#### 3.1. From OPD to Capacity-Aware Distillation

Standard OPD minimizes the token-level reverse KL divergence, $KL(p_{\theta,t} \parallel p_{T,t})$, where $p_{\theta,t}$ is the student's distribution and $p_{T,t}$ is the teacher's distribution at a student-generated prefix $(x, y_{<t})$. The teacher's target distribution $p_T$ can be expressed as $softmax(\ell_\theta + g)$, where $\ell_\theta = \log p_\theta$ are the student's log-probabilities and $g = C_p[\ell_T - \ell_\theta]$ is the centered teacher-student log-probability gap. This gap $g$ represents the complete correction that standard OPD asks the student to match. FP-OPD aims to filter $g$ to retain only the components supported by the student's visual representation.

#### 3.2. Local Visual Reachability

The method conceptualizes a local visual representation perturbation space ($V_Z$) around the student's visual embeddings $Z$. Mapping this space through the student's Jacobian defines the locally reachable output space, $T_\theta(x, y_{<t})$. This space theoretically captures all first-order distributional changes that the student can express through its current visual state.

#### 3.3. Finite-Difference Tangent Approximation

Directly constructing $T_\theta$ is impractical. FP-OPD approximates this space by perturbing the student's visual representations and observing the resulting changes in its log-probabilities.
1. **Spatial Perturbations:** The image tokens are reshaped into an $H \times W$ grid. A set of $K$ spatial weighting fields, $U_K = \{m_1, \dots, m_K\}$, are defined. These fields are binary or soft masks (e.g., top, bottom, left, right boundary bands) that encode spatial patterns.
2. **Feature Perturbation:** For each field $m_k$, a perturbation $\delta Z_k = m_k \odot (\bar{Z} - Z)$ is applied to the visual embeddings $Z$, where $\bar{Z}$ is the per-image mean. This creates perturbed embeddings $Z^{(k)} = Z + \epsilon_{fd} \delta Z_k$, where $\epsilon_{fd}$ is a scalar finite-difference step magnitude.
3. **Finite-Difference Response:** The student's log-probabilities $\ell^{(k)}_\theta$ are computed with the perturbed embeddings $Z^{(k)}$. A detached forward pass ensures no gradients flow back through this probe. The finite-difference response $d_k$ is then calculated as the centered difference: $d_k = C_p[(\ell_\theta - \ell^{(k)}_\theta)/\epsilon_{fd}]$.
4. **Empirical Tangent Space:** The collection of these responses $D_K = [d_1, \dots, d_K]$ forms the basis for the empirical tangent space, $\hat{T}_{\theta,K} = span(D_K)$, which approximates $T_\theta$.

#### 3.4. Fisher Projection of the OPD Correction

To select the "realizable" component of the teacher correction $g$, FP-OPD projects $g$ onto the estimated tangent space $\hat{T}_{\theta,K}$. The projection is performed using the Fisher metric, which is locally induced by the KL divergence loss itself, ensuring consistency between the projection geometry and the objective function.
1. **Fisher Inner Product:** For two centered score displacements $a, b$, the Fisher inner product is defined as $\langle a, b \rangle_p = \sum_{v \in V} p(v) a_v b_v$.
2. **Projection Calculation:** The goal is to find $\hat{g}_R$, the closest point in $\hat{T}_{\theta,K}$ to $g$ under the Fisher metric: $\hat{g}_R = \text{arg min}_{u \in \hat{T}_{\theta,K}} \langle g - u, g - u \rangle_p$. This is solved by constructing a system of linear equations:
 - $A_{ij} = \langle d_i, d_j \rangle_p$ (Fisher Gram matrix of probes)
 - $b_i = \langle d_i, g \rangle_p$ (Alignment of probes with $g$)
3. **Ridge-Stabilized Solution:** The system $(A + \lambda I)c = b$ is solved for coefficients $c$, where $\lambda = \rho \frac{1}{K} tr(A) + \epsilon_{num}$ is a ridge regularization term to handle potential correlations or near-degeneracies among the finite-difference directions.
4. **Projected Correction:** The Fisher-projected correction is then $\hat{g}_R = D_K c = \sum_{k=1}^K c_k d_k$. The residual $g_\perp = g - \hat{g}_R$ is discarded as it lies outside the estimated student capacity.

#### 3.5. Fisher-Projected OPD Objective

The final step is to incorporate $\hat{g}_R$ into the OPD loss. Instead of using the complete teacher distribution $p_T = softmax(\ell_\theta + g)$, FP-OPD defines a new, capacity-aware target distribution:
$q_R = softmax(\text{stopgrad}(\ell_\theta) + \hat{g}_R)$.
The $\text{stopgrad}(\ell_\theta)$ term ensures that the current student distribution acts as a fixed anchor, and $\hat{g}_R$ moves this anchor only within the estimated visual tangent space.
The FP-OPD loss function then becomes:
$L_{FP-OPD}(x, y) = \frac{1}{|T_y|} \sum_{t \in T_y} KL(p_{\theta,t} \parallel q_{R,t})$.
This maintains the token-level reverse KL divergence over the full vocabulary, modifying only the target distribution. Gradients flow through the current student distribution $p_{\theta,t}$, while the teacher model, probe responses, projection calculation, and $q_R$ are detached to prevent gradients from back-propagating through these parts. The on-policy sequence generation determines the prefixes, without policy-gradient or reward weighting.

#### 3.6. Experimental Setup

- **Models:** Qwen3-VL models of different sizes were used: Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct as student, with Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct (and its Geo3K-GRPO variant) as teacher, and Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct as student with Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct as teacher.
- **Training Data:** Geo3K, a dataset for mathematical reasoning, was used as the training corpus.
- **Training Parameters:** Training ran for two epochs with a step-wise linear learning rate decay and no warmup. BF16 precision, one example per GPU, gradient accumulation of 8, and four on-policy rollouts per prompt were employed. Maximum sequence length was 4,096 tokens, and completion length was 2,048 tokens.
- **FP-OPD Specifics:** Four fixed, unsampled binary spatial fields (top, bottom, left, right boundary bands, each spanning 25% of the axis) were used for visual perturbations. The finite-difference step $\epsilon_{fd}$ was set to 0.05.
- **Baselines:** Performance was compared against the base student model, direct GRPO post-training, and Standard OPD.
- **Benchmarks:** Evaluation covered seven benchmarks: WeMath, MathVista, MathVerse, MathVision (mathematical reasoning), and MMMU, HallusionBench, MMStar (general multimodal). Scores primarily used AVG@8 with temperature 1.0 and top-p = 0.95.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrated the effectiveness of Fisher-Projected On-Policy Distillation (FP-OPD) across various teacher-student scales and multimodal reasoning benchmarks.

#### 4.1. 8B-to-2B Distillation

In the primary setting of distilling Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct into Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct, FP-OPD achieved an average score of 51.27 across the seven evaluated benchmarks. This represented an improvement of 2.77 points over the base student model and 1.60 points over standard On-Policy Distillation (OPD). FP-OPD outperformed both baselines on all seven individual benchmarks.
- Significant improvements relative to Standard OPD were observed on MMMU (+2.95 points), WeMath (+2.55 points), and MathVision (+2.24 points). Other benchmarks such as MathVista (+1.14), MathVerse (+1.24), HallusionBench (+0.17), and MMStar (+0.92) also showed gains. These results indicate that FP-OPD strengthens mathematical reasoning without compromising general multimodal performance.
When using a stronger teacher (Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct-GRPO, a variant fine-tuned on Geo3K), FP-OPD further improved, achieving an average of 52.78. This result was the highest in the 2B-student block, surpassing FP-OPD with the standard 8B-Instruct teacher by 1.51 points, direct GRPO post-training of the student by 1.52 points, and Standard OPD with the same teacher by 0.73 points. This suggests FP-OPD can leverage stronger teachers effectively while maintaining broad performance improvements.

#### 4.2. Scaling to Larger Models

The efficacy of FP-OPD was also tested in a larger distillation setting, from Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct to Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct. In this scenario, FP-OPD attained an average score of 64.06, which was 1.46 points higher than the base 8B student and 0.80 points higher than Standard OPD.
- FP-OPD improved all four mathematical benchmarks, showing gains over Standard OPD of +2.23 on WeMath, +0.81 on MathVista, +1.05 on MathVerse, and +0.97 on MathVision. MMMU also improved by +1.32 points. While small decreases were observed on HallusionBench (-0.53) and MMStar (-0.21) compared to Standard OPD, the overall trend demonstrated that FP-OPD remains effective even as model sizes increase, leading to improved reasoning capabilities.

#### 4.3. Decoding Robustness

An analysis of decoding robustness (Table 2) showed that FP-OPD's benefits were consistent under both sampled decoding (AVG@8, temperature 1.0) and deterministic greedy decoding (Acc@1). For the 32B-to-8B setting, FP-OPD's greedy average reached 63.95, outperforming Standard OPD by 1.12 points and winning on six of seven benchmarks. This consistency indicates that the observed improvements reflect a stronger underlying student model rather than statistical variance from sampling.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies were conducted to evaluate the impact of different FP-OPD components (Table 3):
- **Finite-Difference Step ($\epsilon_{fd}$):** The default value of 0.05 yielded the best average performance (51.27). Values of 0.025 and 0.10 produced slightly lower averages (51.12 and 50.86, respectively). This indicates stability around the chosen default, with 0.05 providing a suitable balance for overall performance.
- **Number of Tangent Probes (K):** Using four probes (K=4) achieved the highest average (51.27) and led on all four mathematical reasoning benchmarks. Two and eight probes remained competitive (51.16 and 51.09), suggesting that performance is not monotonically related to the number of probes within this range.
- **Projection Geometry and Probe Construction:**
 - Replacing the Fisher metric with Euclidean projection resulted in a substantial decrease in average score, from 51.27 to 42.48 (an 8.79-point drop). This outcome affirms the importance of using a projection geometry consistent with the local geometry induced by the KL distillation objective.
 - Maintaining the Fisher metric but substituting the fixed boundary-band visual probes with random probes yielded an average of 50.90. The smaller reduction of 0.37 points compared to the default shows that the Fisher-consistent projection is responsible for the majority of the benefit, while spatially structured visual probes provide an additional, systematic improvement.

#### 4.5. Mechanism Analysis

An analysis of training diagnostics for the 32B-to-8B distillation (Figure 3) examined the evolution of "gap energy" ($E_{gap} = \langle g, g \rangle_{p_\theta}$, the squared Fisher norm of the complete teacher-student correction) and "target-shift JS" ($JS(p_\theta \parallel q_R)$, the Jensen–Shannon divergence from the current student to the Fisher-projected target).
Both quantities decreased rapidly during the first epoch and remained low during the second. The median gap energy fell by 43.3% (from 0.299 to 0.169), and the median target-shift Jensen–Shannon divergence fell by 35.8% (from 0.0987 to 0.0634). This joint decline indicates that the student effectively absorbed the projected supervision, and the reachable target $q_R$ progressively moved closer to the student’s current predictive neighborhood, signifying continued and effective learning rather than an inactive distillation target.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of knowledge distillation, particularly for Vision-Language Models (VLMs), by addressing a fundamental limitation in existing on-policy distillation (OPD) paradigms.

The primary significance lies in identifying and empirically demonstrating the "second mismatch" in OPD for VLMs. While OPD successfully aligns supervision to student-generated prefixes, this work shows that the *content* of that supervision—the complete teacher distribution—can contain corrections that exceed the compact student's representational capacity, especially regarding visual distinctions. This observation challenges the conventional assumption that a more powerful teacher's full distribution is always the optimal target for a weaker student. The target-interpolation study provides direct evidence that attempting to fit unrealizable teacher corrections can hinder student learning and lead to reduced downstream performance.

The proposed solution, Fisher-Projected On-Policy Distillation (FP-OPD), offers a principled and effective method to mitigate this issue. By projecting the teacher's log-probability correction onto a locally estimated visual tangent space using the Fisher metric, FP-OPD creates a capacity-aware distillation target. This approach is significant because:
- **Principled Foundation:** The use of the Fisher metric is theoretically grounded in information geometry, aligning the projection with the local geometry induced by the KL divergence objective. This provides a robust basis for determining which teacher corrections are "locally realizable" by the student.
- **Demonstrated Efficacy:** FP-OPD consistently improved performance across a suite of seven multimodal benchmarks, including both mathematical reasoning and general multimodal understanding tasks. These gains were observed across different teacher-student scales (8B-to-2B and 32B-to-8B) and persisted under both sampled and deterministic decoding strategies, indicating a more robust and capable student model.
- **Architectural Compatibility:** FP-OPD integrates into the standard OPD framework by modifying only the target distribution, retaining the full-vocabulary reverse KL loss and the on-policy training protocol. This facilitates its adoption and application to existing OPD setups.
- **Mechanism Insight:** The mechanism analysis during training illustrates that the student effectively absorbs the projected supervision, leading to a progressive reduction in the discrepancy between the student and the capacity-aware target, validating the learning process.

The potential impact of this work extends to improving the efficiency and effectiveness of training smaller, more deployable VLMs without sacrificing performance. By distilling only the relevant and realizable knowledge, FP-OPD can lead to:
- **More Efficient Knowledge Transfer:** Avoiding the imposition of unrealizable learning targets can make the distillation process more efficient and stable.
- **Improved Student Model Performance:** Compact models can achieve higher performance by focusing on corrections within their capabilities, enhancing their reasoning and understanding abilities across various multimodal tasks.
- **Broader Applicability:** The insights regarding capacity-aware distillation targets could be relevant beyond VLMs, to other complex multimodal or multi-component models where significant capacity gaps might exist in specific sub-modules or modalities.

Future work suggested by the authors includes exploring adaptive tangent-space estimation and more efficient projection strategies. These directions aim to further optimize the FP-OPD approach, potentially by reducing the additional computational cost associated with the multiple forward passes required for probe generation. Overall, this research advances the understanding and methodology of knowledge distillation, particularly in the context of compact Vision-Language Models operating with complex, multimodal information.
