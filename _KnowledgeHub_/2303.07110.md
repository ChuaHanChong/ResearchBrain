# Upcycling Models under Domain and Category Shift

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tongji University and collaborators introduce Global and Local Clustering (GLC), a finetuning-free approach for Universal Domain Adaptation (UniDA) that operates without source data. This method adapts a standard pre-trained closed-set model to various category shifts, achieving a 14.8% H-score improvement over prior methods on the VisDA dataset for open-partial-set adaptation.

## Problem

- Deep neural networks suffer performance degradation under simultaneous domain and category shifts, especially when the type of category shift is unknown.
- Existing Source-free Domain Adaptation (SFDA) methods are largely restricted to closed-set scenarios, failing to address partial, open, or open-partial label space discrepancies.
- Current Universal Domain Adaptation (UniDA) solutions often rely on access to source data or specialized model architectures, hindering their practicality and broad applicability with standard pre-trained models.

## Method

- A ==Global and Local Clustering (GLC)== technique is proposed, which adapts a standard pre-trained closed-set source model to an unlabeled target domain without source data.
- ==Adaptive One-vs-All Global Clustering== generates ==pseudo-labels==, separates known from unknown samples, and includes ==confidence-based suppression== for source-private classes.
- A ==Silhouette-based== method dynamically estimates the unknown number of target categories, while local ==k-NN consensus clustering== refines pseudo-labels for semantic consistency.

## Results

- GLC achieves a state-of-the-art H-score of **73.1%** on the VisDA dataset for Open-Partial-Set Domain Adaptation (OPDA), surpassing previous methods like UMAD by **14.8%**.
- It demonstrates superior performance in Open-Set Domain Adaptation (OSDA) and competitive results in Partial-Set Domain Adaptation (PDA) across multiple standard benchmarks.
- The approach significantly reduces target-side training time (e.g., **48.4%** for wild animal classification, **75.9%** for single-cell identification) and shows strong practical utility in real-world applications.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Universal Domain Adaptation can be achieved without source data by adaptively generating pseudo-labels, distinguishing known and unknown classes, and dynamically estimating target categories.
> - A combination of global one-vs-all clustering and local k-NN consistency is effective in mitigating negative transfer and managing diverse category shifts.
> - Utilizing a standard pre-trained closed-set source model is sufficient for SF-UniDA, eliminating the need for specialized architectures.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Deep neural networks (DNNs), despite their remarkable success, often suffer from significant performance degradation when deployed in environments different from their training data, a phenomenon known as "domain shift." This paper addresses an even more challenging scenario by also considering "category shift," where the label spaces of the source and target domains are not identical.

The research landscape can be broadly categorized as follows:
- **Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA):** This is a long-standing field that aims to adapt models from a labeled source domain to an unlabeled target domain. Traditional UDA methods typically require access to both labeled source data and unlabeled target data during adaptation.
- **Source-free Domain Adaptation (SFDA):** A more recent and challenging variant, SFDA addresses growing concerns about data privacy, storage costs, and computational efficiency by requiring only a pre-trained source model (without access to raw source data) and unlabeled target data for adaptation. Most existing SFDA methods, however, assume a "closed-set" scenario, meaning the source and target domains share the exact same set of classes.
- **Domain Adaptation with Category Shift:** Beyond closed-set, researchers have explored scenarios with differing label spaces:
 - **Partial-set DA (PDA):** The target label space is a subset of the source label space ($Y_s \supset Y_t$).
 - **Open-set DA (OSDA):** The target label space contains "unknown" classes not present in the source ($Y_s \subset Y_t$). The goal is to classify known classes and reject unknown ones.
 - **Open-partial-set DA (OPDA):** The most complex, where both source and target domains have unique classes ($Y_s \cap Y_t \ne \emptyset$, $Y_s \not\subseteq Y_t$, $Y_s \not\supseteq Y_t$). The goal is to identify common "known" classes and reject target-private "unknown" classes.
- **Universal Domain Adaptation (UniDA):** This aims to develop a single method applicable to all category shift scenarios (PDA, OSDA, OPDA) without prior knowledge of the specific shift type. While some data-dependent UniDA methods exist (e.g., GATE, DANCE, OVANet), they still require source data during adaptation.

This paper tackles the highly ambitious and novel problem of **Source-free Universal Domain Adaptation (SF-UniDA)**. This combines the challenges of source-free adaptation with the generality of universal adaptation. Critically, the authors highlight that existing SFDA methods for category shift (e.g., UMAD, specifically designed for OPDA) often require *dedicated source model architectures* (e.g., two-branch open-set recognition frameworks), limiting their practical applicability. The current work distinguishes itself by aiming to achieve SF-UniDA using **only a standard pre-trained closed-set source model**, making it significantly more practical and broadly deployable than prior approaches. By addressing this gap, the paper pushes the frontier of domain adaptation towards more realistic and resource-constrained scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the inherent limitations of deep neural networks (DNNs) when encountering real-world variability, specifically **domain shift** (differences in data distribution) and **category shift** (differences in label space). Current state-of-the-art domain adaptation techniques fall short in addressing these challenges in a unified and efficient manner:

1. **Limited Applicability of Traditional SFDA:** While Source-free Domain Adaptation (SFDA) has emerged as a promising solution to mitigate data privacy and access issues by relying only on a pre-trained source model, most existing SFDA methods are designed for **closed-set scenarios**. This means they assume the source and target domains share the exact same set of classes, which is often not true in real-world applications.
2. **Lack of Universality in Category-Shift DA:** Research has explored various category-shift scenarios (Partial-set DA, Open-set DA, Open-partial-set DA), but most developed methods are tailored to a *specific* type of shift. A method designed for open-set DA, for instance, might perform poorly in a partial-set scenario. Furthermore, in practice, the type of category shift (e.g., partial, open, or open-partial) is often unknown in advance, necessitating an adaptive solution.
3. **Dependency on Source Data or Specialized Architectures:** Existing Universal Domain Adaptation (UniDA) methods, which aim to handle multiple category shifts, still typically require access to raw source data during adaptation. Other attempts at source-free adaptation under category shift demand *dedicated model architectures*, which significantly constrains their practical deployment with standard pre-trained models.

Given these limitations, the **key objective** of this paper is to **"upcycle" existing deep neural networks** by adapting them to target tasks under *both domain shift and category shift*, without access to source data, and critically, using **only a standard pre-trained closed-set source model**.

More specifically, the paper aims to:
- **Achieve Source-free Universal Domain Adaptation (SF-UniDA):** Develop a unified framework that can adapt models across all common category shift scenarios (partial-set, open-set, and open-partial-set) without needing source data.
- **Identify "Known" and Reject "Unknown" Samples:** Within the target domain, the adapted model must accurately identify data samples belonging to classes that were present in the source domain ("known" samples) and robustly reject those belonging to target-private classes ("unknown" samples).
- **Operate without Prior Category Shift Knowledge:** The method should be adaptive and not require knowing in advance which specific category shift scenario it is facing.
- **Utilize Standard Pre-trained Models:** Overcome the limitation of requiring specialized model architectures, making the solution more general and easier to integrate into existing AI pipelines.

By achieving these objectives, the authors motivate a more practical, flexible, and resource-efficient approach to deploying DNNs in dynamic and uncertain real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose an innovative technique called **Global and Local Clustering (GLC)** to address the challenging problem of Source-free Universal Domain Adaptation (SF-UniDA). The approach builds upon existing source-free closed-set domain adaptation principles but extends them significantly to handle diverse category shifts.

#### 3.1. Preliminary Setup

- The problem assumes a K-way classification with a pre-trained source model $f_s = h_s \circ g_s$, where $g_s$ is the feature extractor and $h_s$ is the classifier.
- The source domain $D_s$ (with labeled data) is *unavailable* during adaptation.
- An unlabeled target domain $D_t$ is provided.
- The goal is to learn a target-specific feature module $g_t$ (by fine-tuning $g_s$) while keeping the source classifier $h_s$ frozen ($h_t = h_s$).
- The method must identify "known" samples (common classes $Y$) and reject "unknown" samples (target-private classes $\bar{Y}_t$) from $D_t$, without prior knowledge of the category shift type.

#### 3.2. Core Components of GLC

1. **Adaptive One-vs-All Global Clustering (for "Known" vs. "Unknown" Separation):**
 - **Motivation:** Traditional pseudo-labeling struggles with inconsistent label spaces and noisiness under domain shift. The goal is to derive reliable pseudo-labels for target samples, distinguishing between known source classes and unknown classes.
 - **Mechanism:** For each source class 'c' from $C_s$:
 - **Positive/Negative Grouping:** Target instances are initially grouped. The top-K instances with the highest softmax probability for class 'c' ($\delta_c(f_t(x_t))$) are designated as "positive" ($P_c$), and the rest as "negative" ($N_c$). K is set to $N_t/C_t$.
 - **Prototype Learning:** A single positive prototype ($p_c$) is computed by averaging features in $P_c$. Multiple negative prototypes ($\{n_i^c\}_{i=1}^M$) are derived by applying K-means clustering on features in $N_c$. (M is set to $C_t$, not $C_t-1$, to account for hard positive samples within negatives).
 - **Pseudo-Label Assignment:** For a target sample $x_t$, it's assigned to class 'c' if its feature similarity to $p_c$ is greater than its maximum similarity to any $n_i^c$. Otherwise, it's considered not to belong to class 'c'.
 - **Ambiguity Resolution:** If a sample is assigned to multiple known classes, it's assigned to the class with maximum similarity. If it's not assigned to *any* known class (all-zero encoding), it's treated as an "unknown" sample and assigned a uniform encoding (e.g., $1/C_s$) to facilitate rejection.

2. **Confidence-based Source-private Suppression:**
 - **Motivation:** In PDA or OPDA scenarios, the source domain ($Y_s$) might contain classes not present in the target domain ($\bar{Y}_s$). The global clustering might mistakenly assign pseudo-labels for these "source-private" categories to target samples.
 - **Mechanism:** A suppression weight $\epsilon_c$ is introduced for each class 'c'. This weight is calculated based on the *mean prediction confidence* of the positive data group $P_c$ for that class. Classes with lower mean confidence (indicating they might be source-private in the target domain) receive a smaller $\epsilon_c$, effectively reducing their influence in the pseudo-label assignment process. A hyper-parameter $\rho$ controls the base weight (set to 0.75).

3. **Silhouette-based Target Domain $C_t$ Estimation:**
 - **Motivation:** The previous steps require the number of target categories ($C_t$), which is unknown in SF-UniDA.
 - **Mechanism:** The method enumerates a range of possible $C_t$ values (e.g., $[1/3C_s, 1/2C_s, C_s, 2C_s, 3C_s]$). For each hypothesized $C_t$, K-means clustering is applied to the target features. The **Silhouette criterion** is then used to evaluate the quality of the clustering for each $C_t$. The $C_t$ that yields the highest average Silhouette score is selected as the estimated number of target categories ($\tilde{C}_t$). This estimation is performed once at the beginning and remains fixed.

4. **Local k-NN Consensus Clustering:**
 - **Motivation:** Even with global clustering, noisy pseudo-label assignments can occur due to domain and category shift, leading to "negative transfer."
 - **Mechanism:** A memory bank stores target features and their corresponding prediction scores. For each target sample, its k-nearest neighbors (k-NN) in the feature space are identified. A local consensus loss ($L_{loc}^{tar}$) is applied, minimizing the cross-entropy between the sample's prediction and the average predictions of its nearest neighbors. This encourages local semantic consistency and smooths out noisy assignments.

#### 3.3. Optimization Objective

The overall training loss is a weighted sum of the global clustering cross-entropy loss ($L_{glb}^{tar}$) and the local k-NN consensus clustering loss ($L_{loc}^{tar}$), controlled by a trade-off hyper-parameter $\eta$.

#### 3.4. Inference Details

- During inference, a normalized Shannon Entropy ($I(x_t)$) is used as an uncertainty metric. Higher entropy suggests the model is uncertain about the class, often indicating an "unknown" sample.
- A pre-defined threshold $\omega$ (e.g., 0.55) is used: if $I(x_t) \ge \omega$, the sample is rejected as "unknown"; otherwise, it's classified as the `argmax` of the model's output.

In summary, GLC combines adaptive global clustering with confidence-based suppression and local consensus learning, along with an initial estimation of target classes, to provide a comprehensive and robust solution for SF-UniDA using only a standard pre-trained source model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the strong performance and versatility of the Global and Local Clustering (GLC) technique across various benchmarks and category shift scenarios.

1. **State-of-the-Art Performance in Open-Partial-Set Domain Adaptation (OPDA):**
 - **VisDA:** GLC achieves a remarkable H-score of 73.1%, significantly outperforming previous methods like UMAD (by 14.8%) and GATE (by 16.7%). This is a substantial gain in the most challenging DA setting.
 - **DomainNet:** On this largest and most complex benchmark, GLC consistently improves upon UMAD by approximately 8.0% and GATE by 3.0%, confirming its scalability and robustness.
 - **Office-Home and Office-31:** GLC achieves competitive, often state-of-the-art, H-scores on these standard benchmarks, demonstrating its efficacy across different dataset sizes and complexities.

2. **Superior Performance in Open-Set Domain Adaptation (OSDA):**
 - GLC maintains its strong performance in OSDA scenarios, where only target-private unknown classes exist.
 - **Office-Home:** GLC obtains a 69.8% H-score, showing a 3.4% improvement over UMAD.
 - **VisDA:** GLC reaches a 72.5% H-score, a 5.7% improvement over UMAD. This indicates its robust ability to identify "known" classes while effectively rejecting "unknown" ones.

3. **Competitive Results in Partial-Set Domain Adaptation (PDA):**
 - Even though GLC is designed for universal adaptation, it performs comparably to or better than methods specifically tailored for PDA.
 - GLC significantly outperforms UMAD in PDA, with gains of 6.2% on Office-31, 4.6% on Office-Home, and 7.7% on VisDA. This highlights its generality and ability to avoid negative transfer even when the target domain is a subset of the source.

4. **Ablation Study:**
 - The ablation study confirms that both the global and local clustering components are crucial and complementary to the overall performance.
 - The global one-vs-all clustering loss ($L_{glb}^{tar}$) is particularly vital, demonstrating its primary role in distinguishing between "known" and "unknown" categories. For example, on VisDA, using only $L_{glb}^{tar}$ boosts the H-score from the source model's 25.7% to 66.0%.

5. **Robustness and Stability:**
 - **Hyper-parameter Sensitivity:** Experiments show that GLC's performance is relatively stable around the chosen hyper-parameters ($\eta$ and $\rho$) and the uncertainty threshold ($\omega$), indicating its practical applicability without extensive fine-tuning.
 - **Varying Unknown Classes:** GLC consistently achieves more stable and superior performance compared to existing methods when the number of "unknown" classes in the target domain varies.

6. **Performance in Realistic Applications (from Appendix):**
 - **Remote Sensing (PDA):** GLC (64.6% accuracy) significantly outperforms data-dependent methods like DCC (55.2%), ETN (54.9%), DANCE (62.0%), and BA3US (59.6%) in adapting models from high-resolution to low-resolution satellite imagery, even under varying overlapping scenes.
 - **Wild Animal Classification (OSDA):** GLC achieves 79.1% H-score, outperforming DANN (70.1%), OVANet (70.8%), OSBP (72.2%), and DANCE (74.5%). It also demonstrates a 48.4% reduction in target-side training time compared to source data-dependent methods. Visualizations confirm better localization of relevant features for animal identification.
 - **Single-Cell Identification (OPDA):** GLC obtains a 62% H-score for identifying cell types from scRNA-seq to scATAC-seq data, outperforming scJoint (56%) and Seurat (58%). This application also shows a remarkable 75.9% reduction in target-side training time.

In summary, the main findings clearly establish GLC as a highly effective, robust, and generalizable solution for SF-UniDA, providing significant performance improvements over prior methods, especially in complex open-partial-set scenarios, and demonstrating its utility in diverse real-world applications.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The proposed Global and Local Clustering (GLC) technique represents a significant advancement in the field of domain adaptation, particularly for its ability to enable **Source-free Universal Domain Adaptation (SF-UniDA)**. Its potential impact is multi-faceted:

1. **Bridging a Critical Research Gap:** GLC is the first to effectively address SF-UniDA using *only a standard pre-trained closed-set source model*. This is a crucial distinction from prior works that either required source data or specialized model architectures. By doing so, it pushes the boundaries of domain adaptation research into more realistic and challenging settings.

2. **Enhanced Practicality and Deployability:**
 - **Data Privacy and Security:** By eliminating the need for raw source data during adaptation, GLC is highly beneficial for applications with strict data privacy regulations (e.g., medical imaging, confidential datasets) or where proprietary source data cannot be shared.
 - **Resource Efficiency:** It bypasses the need for large-scale source data storage and complex data transfer, making model deployment more efficient and cost-effective.
 - **Accessibility for Non-Experts:** The ability to adapt models using standard pre-trained networks, without requiring specialized architectures or prior knowledge of the exact category shift, significantly lowers the barrier to entry for practitioners in various fields.

3. **Robustness in Real-World Scenarios:** Real-world data often exhibits both domain and category shifts simultaneously, and the exact nature of these shifts is usually unknown. GLC's universal adaptation capability, demonstrated across partial-set, open-set, and open-partial-set scenarios, makes it exceptionally robust for dynamic environments. Its superior performance on challenging benchmarks like VisDA and DomainNet underscores this.

4. **Acceleration of Scientific Discovery and Application:**
 - The successful application of GLC to diverse realistic problems (remote sensing, wild animal classification, single-cell RNA sequence identification) is a strong indicator of its broad utility. This can accelerate scientific discovery in fields where data collection and annotation are expensive or limited, such as in biology (identifying novel cell types or species), environmental monitoring, or autonomous systems.
 - The demonstrated reduction in target-side training time (e.g., 48.4% for wild animal classification, 75.9% for single-cell identification) means faster iteration cycles and quicker deployment of adapted models.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:**
 - GLC introduces novel ideas for adaptive pseudo-labeling (one-vs-all global clustering, confidence-based suppression) and target class estimation (Silhouette-based). These techniques can serve as strong baselines and inspire further research into more sophisticated clustering, prototype learning, and uncertainty quantification methods for domain adaptation.
 - The work opens avenues for exploring meta-learning or lifelong learning approaches in source-free universal settings, where models can continually adapt to evolving domains and categories.

In conclusion, GLC's ability to "upcycle" models universally and without source data is not just an incremental improvement but a paradigm shift that makes advanced domain adaptation more accessible, efficient, and robust for a wide array of practical and scientific applications.
