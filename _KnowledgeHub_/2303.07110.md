# Upcycling Models under Domain and Category Shift

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tongji University and collaborators introduce Global and Local Clustering (GLC), a finetuning-free approach for Universal Domain Adaptation (UniDA) that operates without source data. This method adapts a standard pre-trained closed-set model to various category shifts, achieving a 14.8% H-score improvement over prior methods on the VisDA dataset for open-partial-set adaptation.

## Problem

- Deep neural networks suffer performance degradation under simultaneous domain and category shifts, especially when the type of category shift is unknown.
- Existing Source-free Domain Adaptation (SFDA) methods are largely restricted to closed-set scenarios, failing to address partial, open, or open-partial label space discrepancies.
- Current Universal Domain Adaptation (UniDA) solutions often rely on access to source data or specialized model architectures, hindering their practicality and broad applicability with standard pre-trained models.

## Method

- A ==Global and Local Clustering (GLC)== technique is proposed, which adapts a standard pre-trained closed-set source model to an unlabeled target domain without source data.
- ==Adaptive One-vs-All Global Clustering== generates ==pseudo-labels==, separates known from unknown samples, and includes ==confidence-based suppression== for source-private classes.
- A ==Silhouette-based== method dynamically estimates the unknown number of target categories, while local ==k-NN consensus clustering== refines pseudo-labels for semantic consistency.

## Results

- GLC achieves a state-of-the-art H-score of **73.1%** on the VisDA dataset for Open-Partial-Set Domain Adaptation (OPDA), surpassing previous methods like UMAD by **14.8%**.
- It demonstrates superior performance in Open-Set Domain Adaptation (OSDA) and competitive results in Partial-Set Domain Adaptation (PDA) across multiple standard benchmarks.
- The approach significantly reduces target-side training time (e.g., **48.4%** for wild animal classification, **75.9%** for single-cell identification) and shows strong practical utility in real-world applications.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Universal Domain Adaptation can be achieved without source data by adaptively generating pseudo-labels, distinguishing known and unknown classes, and dynamically estimating target categories.
> - A combination of global one-vs-all clustering and local k-NN consistency is effective in mitigating negative transfer and managing diverse category shifts.
> - Utilizing a standard pre-trained closed-set source model is sufficient for SF-UniDA, eliminating the need for specialized architectures.
