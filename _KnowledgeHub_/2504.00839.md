# Context-Aware Human Behavior Prediction Using Multimodal Large Language Models: Challenges and Insights

> [!summary] Summary
> This research systematically evaluates pre-trained Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for context-aware human behavior prediction from a third-person robot perspective. Researchers found that MLLMs can predict complex, multi-label human activities up to 3 seconds into the future, with the optimal configuration of GPT-4o achieving 66.1% accuracy and 92.8% semantic similarity using MLLM-generated scene captions and In-Context Learning.

## Problem

- Traditional data-driven methods for human behavior prediction struggle with generalization to unseen environments, new activities, and longer prediction horizons.
- Standalone Large Language Models (LLMs) lack visual grounding, leading to "hallucinations" when applied to real-world visual tasks.
- Applying general-purpose MLLMs for complex prediction tasks is challenging due to input sequence limitations, sensitivity to prompt design, and the high cost of task-specific fine-tuning.

## Method

- A modular framework was developed to systematically benchmark pre-trained MLLMs, investigating various input types, ==In-Context Learning (ICL)== examples, and autoregressive prediction strategies for human behavior prediction.
- Different visual context representations were utilized, including single images, sequences of images, and MLLM-generated scene captions, alongside historical interaction labels.
- The approach incorporated In-Context Learning to direct general-purpose MLLMs to the specific prediction task without fine-tuning, and explored autoregressive prediction by generating intermediate activity labels.

## Results

- The highest prediction accuracy of **66.1%** and a semantic similarity of **92.8%** were achieved by GPT-4o using MLLM-generated scene captions, 15 In-Context Learning examples, and autoregressive prediction.
- Visual context in the form of image sequences and scene captions consistently outperformed single images and text-only (blind) inputs, confirming the benefit of temporal and descriptive visual information.
- Autoregressive prediction provided only marginal improvements (approximately **1%** average accuracy gain), indicating it serves as a minor refinement rather than a major performance booster for the tested MLLMs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Incorporating visual context, particularly MLLM-generated scene captions, consistently improves human behavior prediction accuracy compared to purely text-based inputs.
> - In-Context Learning (ICL) substantially enhances MLLM performance for this task, with prediction accuracy generally improving as the number of ICL examples increases, up to a saturation point.
> - Pre-trained MLLMs are capable of predicting non-trivial human activity sequences from a third-person view, handling scenarios with multiple concurrent labels and changes in activity over a 3-second horizon.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The ability to accurately predict human behavior is a cornerstone for the safe, efficient, and natural operation of robotic systems in environments shared with humans. Traditionally, human behavior prediction has relied on data-driven methods, often utilizing Deep Learning (DL) models like Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs). These methods process spatiotemporal patterns from visual or sensor data to predict activities and trajectories. Examples include Social LSTM and Social GAN, which focus on pedestrian trajectory prediction. However, these conventional approaches face significant limitations: they struggle with generalization to unseen environments or activities not present in their training data, and their performance often degrades with increasing prediction horizons or the complexity of human-object interactions.

The advent of Large Language Models (LLMs) and, more recently, Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), has opened new avenues for addressing these limitations. LLMs possess vast common-sense knowledge and impressive generalization capabilities, enabling them to act as zero-shot reasoners across diverse domains. However, standalone LLMs are inherently unimodal (text-based) and lack visual understanding, making them prone to "hallucinating" scene content in real-world visual tasks. MLLMs overcome this by integrating visual encoders and adapters, allowing them to process and ground information from various modalities (text, image, sometimes audio) in the real world. This makes MLLMs particularly promising for complex vision-enabled robotics applications, as they can combine rich visual scene understanding with sophisticated natural language reasoning.

This paper strategically positions itself within this evolving landscape by focusing on a systematic analysis of *pre-trained, general-purpose MLLMs* for *context-aware human behavior prediction* from a *third-person robot perspective*. This contrasts with much of the contemporary LLM-based action anticipation research, which often relies on egocentric (first-person) views (e.g., Epic-Kitchens, Ego4D datasets), or requires extensive fine-tuning for specific domains. While some LLM-based methods like PALM and AntGPT leverage visual inputs for action sequences, they are often still tailored or optimized for specific tasks. The authors highlight a notable gap: research utilizing LLMs to predict human behaviors from a third-person perspective, particularly anticipating *multiple human activities simultaneously* within a future frame, is relatively uncommon. This work addresses the challenge of applying powerful, off-the-shelf MLLMs directly, without fine-tuning, to generate flexible, multi-label human behavior predictions in robotic contexts, making a significant contribution to bridging the gap between general-purpose AI models and specific robotic applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to **systematically analyze the applicability and performance of pre-trained Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for context-aware human behavior prediction.** This analysis is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Enhancing Human-Robot Interaction (HRI) Safety and Efficiency:** Accurate human behavior prediction is critical for robots operating in shared spaces, enabling functionalities like predictive collision avoidance, anticipatory HRI, and safe navigation in applications such as autonomous driving, intralogistics, and home automation.
2. **Overcoming Limitations of Traditional Methods:** Traditional data-driven methods struggle with generalization to unseen environments, activity types, and longer prediction horizons. They are often pre-trained on domain-specific datasets and produce single action labels, limiting their flexibility.
3. **Leveraging LLM Generalization Capabilities:** The recent breakthroughs in LLMs promise open-ended, cross-domain generalization, allowing them to describe various human activities and make predictions in diverse contexts, which is a significant advantage over domain-specific models.
4. **Harnessing MLLM Contextual Awareness:** MLLMs, by integrating visual information, offer improved contextual awareness and scene understanding compared to unimodal LLMs, making them suitable for real-world visual prediction tasks where grounding in the environment is crucial.
5. **Addressing MLLM Application Challenges:** Directly applying general-purpose MLLMs for prediction tasks is challenging due to limitations in processing large input sequences, sensitivity to prompt design, and the high cost of fine-tuning. This work aims to investigate how to effectively leverage pre-trained MLLMs despite these hurdles.

To guide their systematic analysis, the authors formulate four key hypotheses:

- **H1: MLLMs are capable of correctly predicting non-trivial sequences of human activities** (i.e., such where the target label is different from the latest observation, or where multiple correct activity labels are given as the ground truth).
- **H2: Adding visual context yields more accurate predictions, compared to using purely text-based LLMs.**
- **H3: Prediction accuracy generally improves with the number of In-Context Learning (ICL) examples, compared to zero-shot prediction.**
- **H4: Predicting intermediate actions between the current and the target time frame in an autoregressive manner improves the accuracy.**

By investigating these hypotheses, the research aims to provide insights into building performant, generalized human behavior prediction systems using MLLMs without the need for expensive task-specific fine-tuning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a **modular multimodal human activity prediction framework** designed to systematically benchmark various pre-trained MLLMs, input variations, In-Context Learning (ICL) techniques, and autoregressive strategies for context-aware human behavior prediction.

**A. Problem Definition:**
The task is defined as predicting future human interaction labels ($\hat{L}_3$) at a 3-second horizon, based on the current visual scene context ($V_0$), historical interaction labels ($L_{-2:0}$ from up to 2 seconds in the past), and a set of ICL examples ($E_n$). An interaction label is a "verb-noun" pair (e.g., "touch-table"), and a behavior can be a list of multiple labels. The 3-second horizon is chosen to ensure a non-trivial prediction problem, where the target label is more likely to differ from the latest observation. Autoregressive prediction is also explored, where intermediate predictions ($\hat{L}_1$, $\hat{L}_2$) at 1s and 2s are generated and incorporated.

**B. System Overview:**
The framework consists of several key components:
1. **MLLM Module:** The core component that interprets visual context to identify humans, activities, and relevant objects.
2. **Input Structure:** Includes historical interaction labels, visual input, and optional scene captioning.
3. **Prompt Design:** Guides the MLLM's reasoning and constrains output format, often utilizing ICL.
4. **Autoregressive Prediction:** Generates intermediate predictions to refine the final output.

**C. Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs):**
The study evaluates state-of-the-art MLLMs, representing proprietary and open-source models of varying sizes:
- **GPT-4o and GPT-4o-mini (OpenAI):** Proprietary models known for multimodal processing (text, image, audio).
- **Qwen2-VL-72B-Instruct and -7B-Instruct (Alibaba Cloud):** Open-source models designed for images, text, and bounding box inputs/outputs, noted for strong performance in document understanding.
- **LLaVA-NeXT-Video-34B-DPO-hf and -7B-DPO-hf:** Extensions of the LLaVA series, focusing on video comprehension and strong zero-shot performance, even when trained on image data.
These models are chosen based on their performance in video analysis benchmarks. GPT models are deployed via Azure OpenAI Service, while Qwen and LLaVA models run on Nvidia H200 GPUs.

**D. Visual Context Representations:**
Four types of visual context representations are ablated:
- **Blind:** Textual baseline, relying solely on historical interaction labels (no visual input).
- **Single Image:** A static image representing the current visual scene.
- **Image Sequence:** Multiple images captured over time (3 past time steps) to provide richer temporal context and motion information.
- **Scene Caption:** A textual description of the person, objects, and actions generated by the MLLM itself, enhancing scene understanding.

**E. In-Context Learning (ICL):**
ICL is employed to direct general-purpose MLLMs to the specific prediction task without fine-tuning. Examples of input-output pairs (interaction label history and ground truth future label) are included in the prompt. The number of ICL examples is varied from 1 to 15, limited by GPT-4o's image processing capacity. ICL aims to demonstrate the desired output format, define domain-specific labels, and highlight common temporal patterns.

**F. Autoregressive Prediction:**
Inspired by Chain-of-Thought (CoT), this approach guides behavior prediction by adding intermediate prediction steps. Instead of natural language reasoning, these steps involve predicting intermediate interaction labels at 1s and 2s before the final 3s prediction. This leverages the MLLM's autoregressive generation process.

**G. Datasets:**
The evaluation utilizes the **PROX (Proximal Relationships with Object eXclusion)** and **PROX-S** datasets.
- **PROX:** Provides RGB image sequences of human motion and scene interaction in indoor environments from a third-person view.
- **PROX-S:** Extends PROX with frame-level semantic interaction labels (action-object pairs, 17 actions, 42 unique interactions). Crucially, a single frame can have multiple interaction labels.
The evaluation dataset comprises 329 data sequences from the PROX-S test split, ensuring a 3-second prediction horizon where 47.4% of sequences have different target and latest historical labels, and 25.8% have multiple interaction labels in the target frame, thus enabling a non-trivial assessment of H1.

**H. Metrics:**
Three commonly-used metrics are employed:
- **Cosine Similarity:** Measures semantic similarity between vector embeddings of predicted and ground truth strings (value between -1 and 1).
- **Edit Distance:** Minimum operations (deletions, insertions, substitutions) to transform prediction to ground truth, indicating character-level similarity (value between 0 and 1, 0 is a full match).
- **Accuracy Score:** Measures overlap between predicted and ground truth interaction label sets, considering exact and partial matches for multiple labels (value between 0 and 1, 1 is a full match).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The systematic evaluation provides quantitative results across various MLLMs, visual representation types, and ICL configurations, offering insights into each hypothesis.

**A. Visual Context Representations (Zero-Shot Prediction):**
The initial experiments focused on zero-shot prediction performance across different visual input types.
- **Blind (Text-only):** Generally the weakest performance, confirming that the absence of visual input limits human behavior prediction. Qwen-7B surprisingly achieved the highest accuracy (0.575) in this setting, slightly outperforming GPT-4o (0.569).
- **Single Image:** Performance slightly improved over the blind setting for most models, with GPT-4o achieving the highest accuracy (0.584). GPT-4o-mini showed the most significant percentage gain in accuracy (11.9%). However, Qwen-7B's accuracy slightly decreased, suggesting a single image might introduce ambiguity without sufficient context.
- **Image Sequence:** Generally outperformed both single-image and blind settings. Qwen-7B showed a substantial accuracy increase (9.6% over single image, reaching 0.584), and achieved the best cosine similarity. This confirms that temporal context from a sequence of images is crucial for dynamic human behaviors.
- **Scene Caption:** Consistently outperformed the image sequence representation by approximately 2-3% in accuracy for most models. GPT-4o achieved the highest zero-shot accuracy at 0.611 with caption representation. This indicates that adding a textual description of the scene further enhances MLLM understanding and helps reduce ambiguity.

**Confirmation of H2:** The findings robustly confirm H2, demonstrating that incorporating visual inputs (image, sequence, or caption) generally enhances model performance compared to purely text-based (blind) predictions. The best zero-shot performance was achieved with caption representation, highlighting the value of descriptive information.

**B. Number of In-Context Learning Examples:**
- **General Trend:** For GPT and Qwen models, performance consistently improved across all three metrics (accuracy, cosine similarity, edit distance) as the number of ICL examples increased from 0 to 15, thus supporting H3.
- **Qwen Models:** Qwen-7B showed an initial drop in accuracy when moving from zero-shot to 5 ICL examples, especially in blind and image settings, before recovering. Both Qwen models exhibited performance saturation beyond 10 ICL examples, suggesting diminishing returns or sensitivity to example quality.
- **LLaVA Models:** LLaVA models were found to be unsuitable for ICL due to their prompt template's image processing order, leading to mismatched examples and corrupted outputs. Consequently, ICL results for LLaVA models were not reported.
- **Confirmation of H3:** For the well-performing MLLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, Qwen2-VL-72B-Instruct), H3 is confirmed, showing that increasing the number of ICL examples generally improves prediction accuracy.

**C. Autoregressive Prediction:**
- **Marginal Improvement:** Autoregressive prediction, which involves predicting intermediate interaction labels at 1s and 2s before the 3s horizon, yielded only minimal improvement (circa 1% average accuracy gain) for GPT and Qwen models with 15 ICL examples, and for LLaVA models in zero-shot settings. It had no major impact on cosine similarity.
- **Refinement Step:** This suggests autoregressive prediction acts as a minor refinement step rather than a significant performance booster.

**Refutation of H4:** At this point, the authors conclude that it is not possible to definitively confirm H4, as the improvements were minimal.

**D. Summary and Qualitative Results:**
- **Best Performance:** The combination of **GPT-4o with caption representation, 15 ICL examples, and autoregressive prediction** achieved the highest accuracy at **66.1%** and a semantic similarity of **92.8%**. Edit distance values consistently reached competitive levels (over 0.2).
- **Non-Trivial Prediction (H1):** The evaluation dataset was designed with a 3-second horizon, ensuring 47.4% of target labels differed from the latest observation and 25.8% involved multiple interaction labels. The competitive performance across various configurations (with cosine similarity often over 0.9 and accuracy up to 66.1%) indicates that MLLMs are indeed capable of predicting such non-trivial sequences.
- **Confirmation of H1:** The results provide evidence in favor of H1, demonstrating that MLLMs can successfully predict non-trivial sequences, including those with multiple ground truth labels, in challenging third-person view scenarios (cluttered environments, occlusions, limited visibility).

**E. Limitations from the Evaluation:**
- **ICL Scaling:** While beneficial, ICL performance gains plateau after about 10 examples, while computational costs continue to rise. Its effectiveness also varies across models and depends on example quality.
- **MLLM Visual/Spatial Reasoning:** Zero-shot performance was notably weaker, indicating that pre-trained MLLMs heavily depend on additional context (ICL) for higher accuracy. Qwen models showed minimal difference between blind and visual representations, struggling to extract meaningful spatial information without strong textual guidance, and exhibiting a ceiling effect in spatial reasoning. LLaVA models struggled with structured output and ICL implementation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This paper presents a highly significant contribution to the field of human-robot interaction and multimodal AI, particularly by systematically exploring the capabilities of off-the-shelf Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for a critical robotics task: context-aware human behavior prediction.

The key significance and potential impact areas are:

1. **Advancing Human-Robot Interaction (HRI):** The ability to predict human behavior in shared spaces is fundamental for robots to operate safely, efficiently, and collaboratively. This work directly contributes to making robots more "human-aware," enabling proactive collision avoidance, anticipatory actions, and seamless integration into human environments across various applications (autonomous vehicles, industrial robotics, smart homes).
2. **Leveraging General-Purpose MLLMs for Robotics:** This research demonstrates a novel approach of applying pre-trained, general-purpose MLLMs without costly task-specific fine-tuning. This "zero-shot" or "few-shot" capability is highly impactful, as it dramatically reduces the data collection and training burden for deploying AI in diverse robotic scenarios, potentially accelerating the development and deployment of intelligent robotic systems.
3. **Systematic Framework for MLLM Evaluation:** The proposed modular framework offers a robust methodology for benchmarking MLLMs, input variations, and learning strategies. This framework is valuable for researchers aiming to understand and optimize MLLM performance in complex, multimodal prediction tasks.
4. **Insights into MLLM Capabilities and Limitations:** The comprehensive evaluation provides crucial insights into how different visual contexts (single image, sequence, caption), the number of In-Context Learning (ICL) examples, and autoregressive techniques influence MLLM performance. The findings clarify that while visual context and ICL significantly enhance prediction, the effectiveness varies across MLLM architectures, and spatial reasoning capabilities remain a challenge for some. This informs future MLLM development and application strategies.
5. **Addressing Non-Trivial Prediction Challenges:** By focusing on a 3-second prediction horizon and handling scenarios with multiple ground truth labels and changes in activity, the work tackles a more challenging and realistic human behavior prediction problem compared to simpler, short-horizon, single-label tasks. The achievement of 66.1% exact label accuracy and 92.8% semantic similarity on such complex predictions is a notable benchmark.
6. **Future Research Directions:** The paper explicitly outlines future work, including extending evaluation to real-world experiments, incorporating other input modalities (audio, depth), and analyzing computational efficiency. These directions underscore the practical relevance and the pathway toward even more robust and deployable human-aware robotic systems.

In conclusion, this research not only pushes the boundaries of human behavior prediction with MLLMs but also provides a practical blueprint for integrating cutting-edge AI models into real-world robotic applications. By systematically dissecting the factors influencing MLLM performance in this domain, the authors lay crucial groundwork for the next generation of intelligent, human-centric robotic systems.
