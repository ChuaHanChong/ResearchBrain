# EMERGE-Policy: A Robot Mind Emerges Beyond a Single Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> EMERGE-Policy is a graph-structured agentic framework for robot manipulation where a Main Agent plans criterion-grounded subgoals and orchestrates isolated Sub Agents for perception, monitoring, verification, and memory, invoking heterogeneous VLA and world-model backends through a unified Operational/Imagination/Evaluation Skill interface with Branch Stack recovery. Without fine-tuning it reaches 99.2% on LIBERO and 93.9% on LIBERO-Plus.

## Problem

- End-to-end policies produce rollouts without explicit task decomposition, outcome verification, localized recovery, or persistent task state across long-horizon execution.
- Existing harness and code-as-policy systems orchestrate mainly capability selection and control transfer, passing dense evidence directly into the high-level planner's context.
- Errors at perception or execution stages invalidate later stages, yet planners lack structured failure diagnosis and must instead replan globally over full interaction histories.

## Method

- Hierarchical context engineering plus role-isolated **Sub Agents** (Perception, Verification, Monitor) that run asynchronously and return only structured, subgoal-conditioned evidence to the **Main Agent**.
- Unified functional **Skill** library splitting backends into Operational, Imagination, and Evaluation roles under a shared schema with ==lazy metadata loading==, so one model serves multiple roles.
- **Branch Stack** recovery with ==Reflexion-style textual failure diagnosis==, plus ==token-aware memory consolidation== across PLAN.md, HISTORY.md, MEMORY.md and ENVIRONMENT.md files.

## Results

- LIBERO average **99.2%** with the world-model pathway versus **98.5%** for the Cosmos Policy backend; the non-WM variant reaches **98.8%** versus **96.8%** for the pi-0.5 backend.
- LIBERO-Plus average rises to **93.9%** from **82.2%**; on LIBERO-Pro implicit instructions, Spatial-S jumps **20.0%** to **95.7%** and Long-10-S **8.0%** to **59.1%**.
- Disabling imagination drops LIBERO-Plus to **85.1%**; real-robot cup stacking hits **94-99%** per stage, and task priors cut planning steps **24.7%** and wall-clock **16.3%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Categorize models by their function in the decision loop, not architecture: the same backend can act as actor, imaginer, and evaluator through one interface.
> - Isolating evidence-heavy processing in Sub Agents keeps the planner's context small, which matters more for long-horizon reliability than any single backend's strength.
> - System-level gains here are not attributed to specific components; extra cameras, longer step budgets, and a strong closed-source planner confound the comparisons.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a central challenge in embodied AI and robotics: enabling intelligent behavior that extends beyond the capabilities of a single monolithic policy. The paper posits that a robot's effective "mind" can emerge from the coordinated interaction of specialized components rather than residing in one unified model. This perspective grounds intelligence in a distributed system, encompassing perception, prediction, task reasoning, physical control, external memory, and environmental feedback.

Current research in robot manipulation has explored several complementary areas:
- **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models** (e.g., RT-2, OpenVLA, π0, π0.5) have advanced in directly mapping visual observations and language instructions to robot actions, leveraging large-scale pretraining. While effective for visuomotor control, these models typically do not inherently manage complex aspects such as task decomposition, explicit outcome verification, failure recovery, or persistent task state.
- **World-Action Models (WAMs)** (e.g., Cosmos Policy, Fast-WAM) integrate action generation with future state prediction, offering predictive capabilities. However, their primary focus is often on the prediction and generation aspects, rather than comprehensive system orchestration.
- **Code-based and Harness-based methods** (e.g., Code as Policies, RoboCodeX, CaP-X, RoboClaw, HarnessVLA, RoboHarness) represent robot behavior as executable programs or orchestrate various learned policies and analytical controllers. These systems acknowledge the need for higher-level control but often pass detailed, low-level information directly to the central planner, potentially leading to information overload.
- **Agent engineering techniques** (e.g., Reflexion, Statler, ASPIRE) have focused on enabling language models to learn from feedback, maintain explicit world states, and consolidate successful procedures into reusable skills.
- **Hierarchical and closed-loop robot agents** (e.g., SayCan, Inner Monologue, Hi Robot) have demonstrated the value of separating high-level planning from low-level skill execution and incorporating feedback for replanning.

EMERGE-Policy distinguishes itself by explicitly structuring the *coordination and information flow* among these specialized capabilities. It addresses the limitation that existing orchestration systems often pass voluminous, detailed outputs from various components directly to a high-level planner. Instead, EMERGE-Policy proposes a graph-structured agentic framework where functional roles, information boundaries, capability invocation, verification, and recovery are explicitly defined. This design aims to control the information load on the high-level planner by having role-specific Sub Agents process evidence in isolation and return only structured, decision-relevant results, allowing a coherent system-level policy to emerge from their orchestrated interactions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation behind EMERGE-Policy stems from the observation that intelligent behavior in embodied agents is a result of complex coordination among diverse cognitive and physical components. The paper argues that reducing this complexity to a single policy oversimplifies the nature of robust robotic intelligence, particularly in dynamic and error-prone environments. The central challenge identified is the need to skillfully coordinate specialized capabilities while simultaneously managing the volume of information the high-level planner must process at each decision step. An error at one stage in a manipulation task can cascade, invalidating subsequent actions, highlighting the importance of robust coordination and feedback mechanisms.

The key objectives of this work are:

1. **To establish a principled, graph-structured agentic framework for robot manipulation**: The aim is to move beyond monolithic policies by enabling specialized components (for perception, reasoning, prediction, action, verification, and memory) to operate within a shared, structured orchestration process.
2. **To explicitly define functional roles and information boundaries**: The framework seeks to delineate the responsibilities of different components, with a "Main Agent" for task-level state and coordination, and "Sub Agents" for role-specific, evidence-intensive processing in isolated contexts. This is motivated by the need to control information load on the high-level planner by exposing only decision-relevant, structured evidence.
3. **To unify the invocation of heterogeneous backends through a Skill interface**: The objective is to compose diverse capabilities (e.g., VLA models, world models, analytical controllers) by categorizing them into functional roles: Operational, Imagination, and Evaluation Skills. This allows for flexible integration without requiring identical internal architectures.
4. **To implement robust error detection and recovery mechanisms**: The framework aims to provide localized correction through criterion-grounded verification, textual failure diagnosis, and a "Branch Stack" recovery mechanism. This ensures that the system can adapt to execution deviations without necessitating a full replan for every minor issue.
5. **To maintain task-relevant state through token-aware external memory**: A critical objective is to prevent information loss and manage context growth in long-horizon tasks by employing a multi-tiered memory system that preserves essential task state and relevant evidence, triggered by context window capacity.

Collectively, these objectives are designed to allow a coherent, system-level policy to "emerge" from the closed-loop interaction of these components, enabling robots to perform complex manipulation tasks more reliably and robustly.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EMERGE-Policy is presented as a graph-structured agentic framework that orchestrates robot manipulation through the coordinated interaction of a Main Agent, specialized Sub Agents, and a Unified Skill Library (Figures 1, 2). The architecture emphasizes explicit functional roles, information boundaries, and structured communication.

#### 3.1. Main Agent: Planning, Recovery, and Memory

The Main Agent functions as an LLM-based orchestration policy (π_M) responsible for high-level task planning, invoking capabilities, verifying outcomes, and managing failure recovery.

- **Active Context Window (C_t)**: This acts as the Main Agent's working memory, containing the task instruction, current subgoal, plan status, recovery state (from the Branch Stack), relevant memory facts, recent structured results from Sub Agents, and compact signatures of available Skills. The system minimizes context growth by lazy-loading detailed Skill parameters only upon invocation and offloading intensive evidence processing to Sub Agents.
- **Hierarchical Task Planner**: The Main Agent decomposes a high-level language instruction (T) into an ordered sequence of executable subgoals (g_i). Each subgoal is defined by a target entity, an intended state change, and an explicit completion criterion (⟨Target_i, Change_i, Criterion_i⟩). This sequence and its execution status are recorded in `PLAN.md`.
- **Branch Stack Recovery**: Upon detection of a failure (i.e., a subgoal criterion not being met), the Main Agent generates a textual diagnosis of the discrepancy. Based on this, it formulates a localized recovery subgoal and pushes it onto a "Branch Stack." The system prioritizes execution of the stack-top recovery subgoal; if successful, it is removed, and execution resumes the main plan. Global replanning is reserved for cases where localized recovery attempts repeatedly fail.
- **Token-Aware Memory Consolidation Engine**: EMERGE-Policy employs a multi-tiered external memory system to manage task state:
 - `PLAN.md`: Stores the subgoal sequence and completion status for the current episode.
 - `HISTORY.md`: An append-only chronological log of summarized observations, actions, and outcomes.
 - `MEMORY.md`: A consolidated belief state of task-relevant facts (spatial, environmental, procedural), which can be revised.
 - Branch Stack: Temporarily stores recovery subgoals.
 Memory consolidation is triggered when the active context window approaches its token capacity. A Memory Integration Agent processes evicted chronological summaries into `HISTORY.md`, merges facts into `MEMORY.md`, and reconstructs `C_{t+1}`. All memory structures are reinitialized for each new episode, preventing cross-episode adaptation.

#### 3.2. Sub Agents: Role-Specific Evidence Processing

Sub Agents handle computationally or information-intensive operations, providing structured, concise evidence to the Main Agent without exposing raw observations or detailed processing traces. They operate asynchronously in isolated, role-specific contexts.

- **Categorized Roles**:
 - **Perception Sub Agent**: Extracts task-relevant scene evidence (e.g., object identification, state estimation, spatial relationships) using vision-language or spatial-grounding tools.
 - **Verification Sub Agent**: Assesses conditions, such as preconditions before action execution or outcome satisfaction against `Criterion_i` after execution.
 - **Monitor Sub Agent**: Observes physical interaction, detects deviations from expected execution, and reports anomalies.
- **Failure Handover**: If a Sub Agent identifies a failure, it returns a structured report containing evidence, failure type, state changes, and likely cause. The Main Agent then uses this report to inform diagnosis and recovery decisions.

#### 3.3. Unified Skill and Tool Orchestration

EMERGE-Policy exposes all robot capabilities through a unified "Skill" interface, abstracting callable functions with defined inputs and outputs. Tools are concrete API implementations of these skills.

- **Functional Skill Categorization**: Skills are categorized by their function in the decision loop:
 - **Operational Skills**: Execute actions (e.g., motion primitives, VLA policies, WAM policies).
 - **Imagination Skills**: Predict outcomes without environment modification (e.g., world models, action-conditioned prediction models).
 - **Evaluation Skills**: Assess feasibility, progress, or completion (e.g., VLM, geometric checks).
- **Standardized Schema**: Each callable interface (K) is defined by its metadata (I_meta), runtime parameters (Θ_param), preconditions (Φ_pre), and postconditions (Ψ_post). The Main Agent only sees `I_meta` initially and lazy-loads full details upon selection.
- **Concrete Tool APIs**: These include APIs for execution and workspace management (Agent Mode, Embodied Action, File System, Shell, Message) and perceptual grounding (Scene Graph, external vision interfaces).
- **Dual-Mode Execution Lifecycle**: The system operates through distinct phases:
 1. **Planning Phase**: Main Agent decomposes tasks, updates `PLAN.md`, and may use Imagination/Evaluation Skills.
 2. **Perceptual Grounding Phase**: Perception Sub Agent uses APIs to obtain spatially grounded evidence.
 3. **Physical Dispatch Phase**: Main Agent invokes selected Operational Skills through the Embodied Action API.
 4. **Outcome Verification Phase**: Verification Sub Agent assesses observed outcomes against `Criterion_i`, leading to plan advancement or localized recovery.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

The framework was evaluated on the LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, LIBERO-Pro benchmarks for tabletop manipulation, and RoboDojo for bimanual manipulation. The high-level planner used was a closed-source Codex model. Two pre-trained robot policies served as execution backends: Cosmos-Policy-LIBERO-Predict2-2B (for WAM, serving as Operational and Imagination Skills) and pi05_LIBERO (for VLA, serving as Operational Skill). The evaluation environments were augmented with additional camera views for high-level perception (not low-level policy inputs), and episode step limits were increased to accommodate multi-stage execution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments aimed to assess the system-level benefits of EMERGE-Policy and identify contributions from its design choices across various benchmarks and real-world tasks.

#### 4.1. System-level Benchmark Performance (RQ1)

- **LIBERO (Standard tasks)**: EMERGE-Policy demonstrated consistent, albeit incremental, improvements over its underlying execution backends. With the Cosmos Policy world-model pathway, it achieved a 99.2% average success rate, compared to 98.5% for the standalone Cosmos Policy (+0.7%). Using π0.5 as the execution backend, EMERGE-Policy achieved 98.8% versus 96.8% for standalone π0.5 (+2.0%).
- **LIBERO-Plus (Robustness under perturbations)**: The system exhibited enhanced robustness. The world-model variant achieved a 93.9% average success rate under seven types of perturbations (e.g., state, language, camera changes), a substantial increase from the 82.2% achieved by the raw Cosmos Policy (+11.7%). The π0.5 variant also showed an improvement, reaching 88.3% versus 85.7% (+2.6%).
- **LIBERO-Pro (Instruction robustness, explicit vs. implicit instructions)**: EMERGE-Policy significantly improved performance on tasks with implicit instructions. For instance, on Spat-S, it achieved 95.7% compared to 20.0% for π0.5 (+75.7%). On L10-S, it reached 59.1% versus 8.0% for π0.5 (+51.1%). This indicates the effectiveness of high-level re-grounding and closed-loop orchestration for ambiguous instructions.

#### 4.2. Evidence for the Sub-Agent Workflow (RQ2)

The performance gains on LIBERO-Pro, particularly with implicit instructions, are consistent with the hypothesized benefits of the Sub-Agent workflow, where role-specific processing and structured evidence reduce the burden on the Main Agent, enabling more robust task re-grounding and planning.

#### 4.3. Action Selection and Failure Avoidance via Imagination (RQ3)

- The integration of Imagination Skills (predicting outcomes with a world model) and Evaluation Skills (scoring predicted outcomes against subgoal criteria) contributed to failure avoidance.
- In the LIBERO-Plus experiments, the world-model variant (leveraging Imagination/Evaluation Skills) significantly outperformed the raw Cosmos Policy. When the Imagination Skill was disabled, leading to open-loop action selection, the average success rate on perturbed tasks dropped to 85.1%, from 93.9% with imagination. This supports that forward prediction and evaluation enable the system to anticipate and mitigate failures before physical execution.

#### 4.4. Memory Architecture (RQ4)

- **RoboDojo Simulation**: EMERGE-Policy achieved high performance in the "Memory" and "Open" settings of the RoboDojo benchmark (25.0% and 19.98% success rate, respectively), outperforming other VLAs. This suggests strong capabilities in encoding historical information, non-Markovian reasoning, skill reorganization, and aligning open-vocabulary semantics.
- The three-tiered memory mechanism (persistent `PLAN.md`/`ENVIRONMENT.md` files, structured Sub-Agent evidence, and asynchronous visual verification) was instrumental. This architecture ensures the Main Agent operates within a bounded context while maintaining comprehensive awareness of task progress and physical conditions, dynamically refining its state based on perceptual feedback.

#### 4.5. Physical Deployment (RQ5)

- **"Multi-layered Cup Stacking" task**: EMERGE-Policy was deployed on a physical robot for a long-horizon, multi-step task, achieving an overall 94% success rate across 100 trials.
- **Robustness to disturbances**:
 - **Interrupted execution**: Achieved 86% success after partial structures were dismantled, demonstrating online response and replanning capabilities.
 - **Changed cup size (5%-15%)**: Maintained 92% success, indicating tolerance for moderate perceptual errors.
 - **Changed camera viewpoint/quantity**: Showed the greatest performance decline, dropping to 85% success, suggesting the importance of robust visual input for positioning.
 - **Lighting/color change**: Remained highly stable at 93%-94% success, indicating effective object identification despite environmental variations.

#### 4.6. Task-Specific Priors and Trajectory Optimization (RQ6)

- Incorporating user-provided task-specific prior knowledge into the Main Agent's planning process significantly improved execution efficiency.
- Without such priors, the Main Agent tended to insert conservative, redundant `move_to_pose` commands for re-localization and verification, resulting in fragmented, zigzag trajectories.
- With priors, the Main Agent compressed the planning horizon, eliminating superfluous `move_to_pose` cycles. This reduced the average number of planning steps per subgoal by 24.7% and decreased total wall-clock time by 16.3% per episode, while maintaining comparable success rates (96.7% vs. 95.2%). This indicates that aligning high-level reasoning with low-level actuator dynamics through priors leads to more fluent and efficient motions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EMERGE-Policy represents a methodological contribution to the field of robot learning and embodied AI, particularly in how intelligence is structured and deployed in complex robotic systems. Its significance and potential impact include:

- **Advancing System-Level Robot Intelligence**: The framework shifts the paradigm from relying on a single, monolithic policy to enabling intelligence to "emerge" from the orchestrated interaction of specialized, functionally distinct components. This approach addresses the limitations of end-to-end models in handling complex task decomposition, robust verification, and adaptive recovery.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Reliability in Real-World Scenarios**: By providing explicit mechanisms for criterion-grounded verification, textual failure diagnosis, and localized Branch Stack recovery, EMERGE-Policy significantly improves the robot's ability to operate robustly in dynamic and unpredictable environments. The real-robot experiments demonstrate its resilience to various perturbations and unexpected events, which is crucial for practical deployment.
- **Efficient Information Management for Large Language Models (LLMs)**: The design with role-specific Sub Agents and structured evidence handover tackles a critical challenge in LLM-based robot control: managing excessive information load. By isolating dense perceptual and execution traces from the Main Agent's active context, the framework enables more scalable and focused reasoning.
- **Flexible Integration of Heterogeneous Capabilities**: The unified Skill interface allows for modular integration of diverse state-of-the-art backends (VLA policies, world models, analytical controllers) based on their functional roles (Operational, Imagination, Evaluation). This promotes flexibility, extensibility, and the ability to leverage advancements in specialized models without needing to redesign the entire system.
- **Improved Performance on Long-Horizon Tasks**: The multi-tiered, token-aware external memory system (PLAN.md, HISTORY.md, MEMORY.md, Branch Stack) enables the agent to maintain coherent task state and adapt to progress over extended periods, a key hurdle for complex, multi-step manipulation sequences.
- **Transparency and Debuggability**: The explicit organization of agent roles, information boundaries, and externalized memory structures provides a higher degree of transparency into the agent's decision-making process. This can facilitate debugging, understanding failure modes, and auditing system behavior, which are important for safety-critical applications.
- **Guidance for Future Research and Development**: The work identifies critical future directions, such as developing reproducible evaluation protocols for harness systems, quantifying the trade-offs between accuracy, latency, and token usage, and investigating the transferability of the orchestration graph across different domains. It also highlights the importance of distinguishing within-episode adaptation from persistent cross-episode learning to ensure fair benchmark evaluations. EMERGE-Policy thus provides a foundational framework and a clear research agenda for the continued development of generalist robot agents.

The findings suggest that EMERGE-Policy offers a promising architectural paradigm for building more capable and robust robotic systems, demonstrating that structured agentic orchestration can yield performance benefits beyond the individual strengths of its constituent models.
