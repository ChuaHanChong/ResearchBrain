# Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning for Pre-Trained Vision Models: A Survey and Benchmark

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University and collaborators introduce a comprehensive survey and the V-PEFT Bench, a unified benchmark for Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) in vision models. The work demonstrates that PEFT methods often outperform full fine-tuning on limited data across diverse vision tasks, achieving comparable performance with as little as 0.05% of the trainable parameters.

## Problem

- Full fine-tuning large Pre-trained Vision Models (PVMs) demands prohibitive computational resources, memory, and storage for each new task.
- Traditional full fine-tuning often leads to overfitting and reduced generalization performance when adapting PVMs to smaller downstream datasets.
- The visual domain lacked a systematic survey and a standardized benchmarking framework for Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) methods.

## Method

- A systematic survey categorizes visual PEFT methods into four main types: ==addition-based, partial-based, unified-based, and multi-task tuning==.
- The ==V-PEFT Bench==, a unified benchmark, evaluates **25** representative PEFT algorithms across various vision tasks including image recognition, video action recognition, and dense prediction.
- A novel ==Performance-Parameter Trade-off (PPT) metric== is introduced to holistically assess PEFT methods based on both performance and parameter efficiency.

## Results

- PEFT algorithms consistently outperformed full fine-tuning across **19** VTAB-1k image recognition datasets, demonstrating superior generalization on limited data, with DTL showing the best performance-parameter trade-off (PPT=**0.75**).
- On video action recognition tasks, PEFTs strongly surpassed full fine-tuning on data-scarce HMDB51, and methods like BAPAT achieved leading results on both large and small video datasets.
- For dense prediction, PEFTs outperformed full fine-tuning on smaller datasets such as PASCAL VOC, and select methods like Mona even exceeded full fine-tuning on the large MS-COCO dataset.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning methods consistently outperform full fine-tuning when adapting large vision models to tasks with limited data by preventing overfitting.
> - Different PEFT approaches present distinct trade-offs in terms of added modules, structural changes, and efficiency during inference and memory usage.
> - The effectiveness of PEFT is influenced by factors like the pre-training strategy of the base vision model and the data abundance of the downstream task.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in this paper is positioned at the intersection of two critical and rapidly evolving areas in artificial intelligence: large-scale Pre-trained Vision Models (PVMs), often referred to as Vision Foundation Models, and Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT).

In recent years, deep learning has witnessed a paradigm shift towards large foundation models, initially popularized in Natural Language Processing (NLP) with models like BERT and GPT, and now increasingly prevalent in computer vision with models such as Vision Transformers (ViT), Swin Transformers, and generative models like Stable Diffusion and Segment Anything Model (SAM). These PVMs, trained on massive datasets, demonstrate exceptional performance and adaptability across a wide array of downstream vision tasks, including image recognition, video action recognition, semantic segmentation, and image generation.

However, the sheer scale of these models, often comprising billions or even trillions of parameters, poses significant challenges for traditional full fine-tuning. Full fine-tuning demands prohibitive computational resources, extensive memory, and large storage for each new task. Moreover, it can lead to overfitting when applied to smaller downstream datasets and becomes impractical for scenarios requiring adaptation to numerous tasks, each necessitating a separate set of full model weights.

To address these limitations, Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) methods emerged. Originating in NLP, PEFT aims to achieve performance comparable to full fine-tuning while modifying or introducing only a minimal fraction of the model's parameters. This approach capitalizes on the robust generalizability of large pre-trained models, which have learned rich representations applicable across diverse tasks. The core idea is that most of the pre-trained knowledge can be retained, with only a small, task-specific adjustment needed.

This paper critically fills a crucial gap in the burgeoning field of visual PEFT. While PEFT has been extensively surveyed and integrated into libraries in NLP, the visual domain lacks a consolidated, up-to-date overview and a standardized benchmarking framework. Existing surveys, as highlighted in Table 1 of the paper, often focus predominantly on NLP or offer less comprehensive taxonomies. This work differentiates itself by providing a vision-centric, exhaustive survey, a detailed categorization of methods, an in-depth analysis of relevant datasets and applications, and, most importantly, introducing "V-PEFT Bench" – a unified benchmark for consistent and fair evaluation of visual PEFT methods. By doing so, the authors aim to standardize comparisons, facilitate reproducible research, and accelerate advancements in the application of PEFT to computer vision tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivations for this comprehensive survey and benchmark arise from the rapid evolution and growing complexity of the visual PEFT landscape, coupled with a noticeable lack of standardized resources for the community.

**Key Motivations:**
1. **Explosion of Pre-trained Vision Models (PVMs):** The past few years have seen an unprecedented growth in the size and capabilities of PVMs, which have become the de facto standard for achieving state-of-the-art performance across various computer vision tasks.
2. **Impracticality of Full Fine-Tuning:** The sheer scale of these PVMs makes conventional full fine-tuning computationally expensive, memory-intensive, and storage-demanding. This is particularly problematic for edge devices, multi-task learning, or scenarios with limited computational resources.
3. **Risk of Overfitting on Small Datasets:** For downstream tasks with limited data, full fine-tuning large models often leads to overfitting, diminishing generalization performance.
4. **Emergence of PEFT as a Solution:** Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning has proven to be an effective alternative, demonstrating the ability to match or exceed full fine-tuning performance with significantly fewer trainable parameters and resources.
5. **Lack of Comprehensive Visual PEFT Resources:** Unlike NLP, where PEFT methods have been well-surveyed and integrated into unified libraries, the visual PEFT domain lacks a systematic review, a clear categorization of diverse methodologies, and a standardized benchmark for fair comparison.

**Corresponding Objectives (as outlined in the paper's contributions):**
1. **Comprehensive Problem Definition and Background:** To provide a clear understanding of the PEFT problem, including a review of widely used vision foundation models and foundational pre-training techniques that underpin PEFT.
2. **Systematic Survey of Visual PEFT Methods:** To conduct a thorough review of existing visual PEFT methods, particularly transformer-based approaches, and categorize them into a systematic taxonomy (addition-based, partial-based, unified-based, and multi-task tuning) to provide a structured overview of the field.
3. **Analysis of Datasets and Applications:** To analyze prominent datasets and real-world applications where visual PEFT methods have been successfully applied, offering context on their practical utility.
4. **Development of a Unified Benchmark (V-PEFT Bench):** To introduce and implement V-PEFT Bench, a rigorous benchmark designed to standardize the evaluation of PEFT methods across a diverse set of vision tasks, ensuring consistency, fairness, and reproducibility in comparisons. This includes proposing a novel "Performance-Parameter Trade-off (PPT)" metric to holistically evaluate methods.
5. **Identification of Future Research Directions:** To outline potential avenues and challenges for future research, such as interpretability of PEFT methods, application to visual generative models, simplification of hyperparameter selection, and integration with differential privacy, thereby guiding future innovations in the field.

These objectives collectively aim to provide a foundational resource for researchers, standardize evaluation practices, and stimulate further advancements in parameter-efficient adaptation of large vision models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopted a two-pronged methodology: a comprehensive survey of existing visual PEFT methods and the development of a novel, unified benchmark called V-PEFT Bench.

#### 3.1. Survey Methodology

The survey component involved a systematic review and categorization of the latest advancements in visual PEFT, with a particular focus on transformer-based architectures. The authors structured the diverse landscape of PEFT techniques into four primary categories:

1. **Addition-based Tuning:** This category focuses on methods that introduce additional trainable neural modules or parameters into the Pre-trained Vision Models (PVMs). Sub-categories include:
 - **Adapter Tuning:** Involves inserting small bottleneck-like neural modules (adapters) into transformer layers, where only the adapter parameters are fine-tuned. The paper discusses various adapter designs (e.g., AdaptFormer, Convpass) and optimization techniques (e.g., LoRand, SCT).
 - **Prompt Tuning:** Injects learnable parameters or perturbations either into the image embedding space (embedding-level prompts like VPT) or directly around the input image (pixel-level prompts like VP).
 - **Prefix Tuning:** Similar to prompt tuning, but introduces learnable prefix matrices directly into the Multi-Head Attention (MHA) mechanism of transformers to modify key and value representations.
 - **Side Tuning:** Employs a smaller, detached side network that runs alongside the frozen PVM. Its outputs are combined with PVM representations, offering both parameter and memory efficiency (e.g., ControlNet, LST).

2. **Partial-based Tuning:** These methods selectively fine-tune a small subset of parameters within the original PVMs, without introducing new modules. Sub-categories include:
 - **Specification Tuning:** Directly optimizes specific, critical parameters within the PVM, such as bias terms (e.g., BitFit) or LayerNorm parameters (e.g., LN-Tune), or selects parameters based on gradients (e.g., GPS).
 - **Reparameterization Tuning:** Introduces new learnable parameters during training that are later integrated into the original PVM weights during inference via reparameterization (e.g., LoRA, KronA, FacT).

3. **Unified-based Tuning:** This category encompasses approaches that consolidate multiple PEFT methods or integrate PEFT with other advanced techniques. Examples include hybrid PEFT methods that combine adapters, LoRA, and prompts (e.g., NOAH, V-PETL), and cross-tech & PEFT fusion methods that integrate dynamic token activation or sparsity strategies (e.g., DyT, Sparse Tuning).

4. **Multi-task Tuning:** These methods are designed to adapt PVMs for multiple downstream tasks simultaneously, aiming to leverage task interdependencies and learn both shared and task-specific knowledge (e.g., Polyhistor, VMT-Adapter, MT-LoRA).

Beyond categorization, the survey includes a comparative analysis of these methods based on characteristics such as "No-Additional Modules," "Structure Preserving," "Inference Efficient," and "Memory Efficient," along with an analysis of their trainable parameter counts (Table 2).

#### 3.2. Benchmark Methodology (V-PEFT Bench)

The V-PEFT Bench is designed as a unified and rigorous platform for evaluating PEFT methods in visual tasks. Its construction involves:

1. **Scope and Coverage:** The benchmark evaluates 2 conventional (Full Fine-tuning, Linear Probing) and 25 representative PEFT algorithms across a diverse set of vision tasks:
 - **Image Recognition:** 19 datasets from the VTAB-1k benchmark, covering Natural, Specialized, and Structured domains.
 - **Video Action Recognition:** 2 datasets (Something-Something-v2, HMDB51).
 - **Dense Prediction:** 3 datasets (MS COCO, PASCAL VOC, ADE20K).
 - Pre-trained models include ViT-B/16 (ImageNet-21K), VideoMAE ViT-B, Video Swin transformer (Kinetics 400), Swin-B (ImageNet-22K), and Swin-L (ImageNet-22K).

2. **Codebase Structure:** The benchmark is built with a modular, four-layer architecture (Figure 5) to ensure flexibility, extensibility, and reproducibility:
 - **Core Layer:** Implements fundamental functions, datasets, data loaders, and pre-trained models.
 - **Algorithm Layer:** Contains base classes for PEFT algorithms, loss functions, and specific configurations, supporting 25 algorithms.
 - **Extension Layer:** Provides tools for advanced analysis, such as attention map visualization and feature distribution (t-SNE) visualization.
 - **API Layer:** Offers a user-friendly interface with configuration files for all supported algorithms to facilitate experimentation and result reproduction.

3. **Unified Evaluation Metric:** Recognizing the need for a holistic metric in PEFT, the authors propose the **Performance-Parameter Trade-off (PPT)** metric (Equation 14). This metric quantifies how efficiently a PEFT algorithm improves task performance relative to its number of trainable parameters, allowing for direct comparison across methods that balance these two aspects. Standard metrics like accuracy, mAP, and mmIoU are also used.

4. **Visualization Tools:** The benchmark integrates visualization capabilities, including t-SNE for feature distribution analysis and attention maps, to provide qualitative insights into how different PEFT methods affect model behavior and representations.

This systematic approach ensures a comprehensive overview of the field and provides practical tools for standardized evaluation, making V-PEFT Bench a valuable resource for researchers.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents significant findings from both its comprehensive survey and the empirical evaluations conducted using the V-PEFT Bench.

#### 4.1. Survey Findings

The systematic categorization and analysis of visual PEFT methods revealed several key insights:
- **Parameter Efficiency is Achieved:** All surveyed PEFT methods successfully reduce the number of trainable parameters significantly, typically requiring only 0.05% to 10% of the parameters compared to full fine-tuning. Despite this reduction, they often achieve comparable or even superior performance.
- **Diverse Approaches:** PEFT methods are highly diverse, ranging from introducing external modules (addition-based) to selectively tuning internal parameters (partial-based). This diversity allows for tailored solutions depending on specific task requirements, model architectures, and resource constraints.
- **Trade-offs in Characteristics:** A comparative analysis (Table 2) showed distinct trade-offs:
 - **No-Additional Modules (NAM):** Only specification tuning entirely avoids introducing new modules.
 - **Structure Preserving (SP):** Prompt, prefix, side, and reparameterization tuning largely preserve the original model structure, while adapter tuning modifies it.
 - **Inference Efficiency (IE):** Reparameterization tuning excels here by merging parameters during inference, avoiding latency overhead. Others might incur slight latency due to additional modules.
 - **Memory Efficiency (ME):** Side tuning is particularly memory-efficient as its gradient backpropagation bypasses the large PVM backbone.
- **Emerging Trends:** The survey highlighted unified and multi-task tuning as promising directions, indicating a shift towards combining PEFT strategies and optimizing for multiple tasks simultaneously.

#### 4.2. Benchmark Results (V-PEFT Bench)

The V-PEFT Bench provided quantitative results across various vision tasks:

##### 4.2.1. Image Recognition (VTAB-1k Datasets, Table 4)

- **PEFT Outperforms Full Fine-tuning on Limited Data:** Across the 19 VTAB-1k datasets, almost all PEFT algorithms (e.g., Adapter, LoRA, AdaptFormer, SCT, FacT, Hydra, DTL, HST, GPS, LAST, SNF) outperformed full fine-tuning. This is a critical finding, demonstrating that full fine-tuning of large PVMs on limited data (1k examples per task in VTAB) often leads to overfitting and catastrophic forgetting, whereas PEFT effectively preserves the model's generalizability.
- **Top Performers:** DTL achieved the highest PPT metric (0.75), indicating the best balance between performance and parameter efficiency. HST and GPS also showed very strong performance.
- **Domain Gap Challenges:** PEFT methods generally performed well on "Natural" and "Specialized" image recognition tasks, where the objectives align closer with ImageNet pre-training. However, performance was notably lower on "Structured" tasks (e.g., object counting, depth prediction), suggesting that these tasks' significant domain gap requires more extensive adaptation or specialized PEFT techniques. Simpler PEFTs like BitFit and VPT-Shallow struggled more in these challenging domains.

##### 4.2.2. Video Action Recognition (SSv2 and HMDB51, Table 5)

- **Data Sufficiency vs. Scarcity:**
 - On the larger SSv2 dataset, full fine-tuning of ViT-B from VideoMAE was competitive, and some PEFTs still outperformed it (e.g., AdaptFormer). This suggests that with ample data, the overfitting risk of full fine-tuning decreases.
 - On the smaller HMDB51 dataset, most PEFT algorithms significantly outperformed full fine-tuning, reinforcing that PEFT is crucial for limited-data scenarios to prevent overfitting.
- **Pre-training Impact:** Self-supervised pre-training (VideoMAE) demonstrated robust feature learning on data-rich tasks, while supervised pre-trained models (Video Swin transformer) showed better adaptability to smaller datasets.
- **Effective PEFTs:** BAPAT, which integrates multiple PEFT strategies, achieved outstanding performance on both SSv2 and HMDB51, highlighting the potential of hybrid approaches.

##### 4.2.3. Dense Prediction (MS COCO, PASCAL VOC, and ADE20K, Table 6)

- **Large vs. Small Datasets:**
 - On the large MS-COCO dataset, full fine-tuning generally outperformed most PEFT algorithms, although Mona impressively surpassed full fine-tuning, demonstrating the potential for highly effective parameter-efficient solutions even on large datasets.
 - On the smaller PASCAL VOC dataset, all evaluated PEFT algorithms outperformed full fine-tuning, again emphasizing PEFT's strength in preventing overfitting in data-limited settings.
 - On ADE20K, which is larger than PASCAL VOC but smaller than MS-COCO, full fine-tuning slightly outperformed PEFTs, but PEFTs still achieved competitive results.
- **Top Performer:** LN TUNE showed strong performance on PASCAL VOC and ADE20K by tuning only LayerNorm parameters, indicating the high impact of subtle parameter adjustments. E³VA demonstrated superior memory efficiency.

##### 4.2.4. Visualization (Figures 6 & 7)

- **Feature Distribution:** t-SNE visualizations illustrated a direct correlation between the distinguishability of feature clusters for different classes and the downstream task performance. Higher-performing PEFT methods produced more distinct class boundaries in the embedding space.
- **Attention Maps:** Attention map visualizations revealed how different PEFT methods influence the model's focus, with methods like SCT showing more targeted attention to relevant objects compared to full fine-tuning.

In summary, the benchmark results provide strong empirical evidence for the efficacy and efficiency of PEFT methods across a wide spectrum of visual tasks and conditions, consistently outperforming or matching full fine-tuning, particularly in data-scarce environments, while significantly reducing resource demands.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This paper, "Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning for Pre-Trained Vision Models: A Survey and Benchmark," holds substantial significance and is poised to have a broad impact on the field of computer vision and machine learning.

#### 5.1. Significance for Research and Development

1. **Standardization and Unification:** The most significant contribution is the V-PEFT Bench itself. By providing a unified, rigorous benchmark across diverse visual tasks and models, the authors address a critical need for standardized evaluation. This will enable researchers to compare new PEFT methods fairly and consistently, accelerating the pace of innovation and fostering reproducible research. The proposed Performance-Parameter Trade-off (PPT) metric is particularly impactful, offering a holistic measure that quantifies the efficiency of a method beyond just raw performance or parameter count.
2. **Comprehensive Resource:** The detailed survey and systematic categorization of existing visual PEFT methods provide an invaluable and up-to-date resource for researchers entering or working within this rapidly evolving domain. The clear taxonomy, characteristic analysis, and discussion of different tuning strategies offer a structured understanding that was previously fragmented across numerous individual papers.
3. **Guidance for Method Selection and Design:** The empirical results from the V-PEFT Bench offer practical guidance on which PEFT methods are most effective for specific types of tasks, data regimes (data-rich vs. data-scarce), and pre-trained model architectures. This insight can inform the selection of appropriate PEFT strategies for new applications and guide the design of novel, more efficient PEFT techniques.
4. **Identification of Open Challenges:** By explicitly outlining future research directions—such as improving interpretability, extending to visual generative models, simplifying hyperparameter selection, and integrating differential privacy—the paper sets a clear agenda for the PEFT community. This forward-looking perspective can stimulate new research efforts and collaborations.
5. **Bridging the NLP-CV Gap:** The paper effectively bridges the gap between PEFT advancements in NLP and their application in computer vision, tailoring the discussion to the unique challenges and opportunities presented by visual data and models.

#### 5.2. Potential Practical Impact

1. **Democratization of Large Models:** PEFT makes the immense power of large Pre-trained Vision Models (PVMs) accessible to a wider range of practitioners and organizations, including those with limited computational resources. By dramatically reducing the hardware requirements for fine-tuning, it enables broader adoption of state-of-the-art models in various industries.
2. **Efficient Deployment:** For real-world applications where models need to be adapted to numerous downstream tasks (e.g., in robotics, medical imaging, or large-scale content moderation), PEFT significantly reduces the storage and deployment overhead. Instead of storing full copies of models for each task, only small, task-specific PEFT modules need to be managed.
3. **Faster Experimentation and Iteration:** The reduced training time and resource requirements associated with PEFT allow for quicker experimentation cycles. Researchers and developers can iterate on model designs and fine-tuning strategies more rapidly, accelerating product development and scientific discovery.
4. **Improved Robustness and Generalization:** The findings indicate that PEFT methods often lead to better generalization performance, especially on smaller datasets, by preventing the catastrophic forgetting and overfitting associated with full fine-tuning. This enhances the reliability of adapted models in practical scenarios.
5. **New Application Areas:** The focus on PEFT for visual generative models and other complex tasks could unlock new applications in creative AI, content generation, and specialized domain adaptation, further expanding the utility of foundation models.

In conclusion, this paper serves as a seminal work in the visual PEFT domain, providing both a foundational understanding and critical tools for future advancements. Its comprehensive nature and practical contributions are expected to significantly influence how large vision models are developed, evaluated, and deployed in the coming years.
