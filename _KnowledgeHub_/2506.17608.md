# HIRE: Lightweight High-Resolution Image Feature Enrichment for Multimodal LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Adobe's Media and Data Science Research Lab developed HIRE, a lightweight feature enrichment pipeline for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to achieve high-resolution visual understanding. This method attains competitive performance on fine-grained tasks, reducing computational costs by up to 35% and inference times by over 50% compared to existing multi-crop approaches.

## Problem

- Existing high-resolution MLLMs rely on processing multiple crops of an image through a large vision encoder, leading to substantial computational costs and extended inference times.
- Achieving fine-grained visual understanding in MLLMs typically incurs a quadratic increase in FLOPs, hindering practical deployment and scalability, especially with larger vision backbones.
- A challenge exists in balancing high accuracy for detailed visual tasks with the necessity for computational efficiency and real-time application in MLLMs.

## Method

- ==HIRE== employs a ==lightweight feature enrichment pipeline== that directly generates high-resolution visual features from a single low-resolution vision encoder output, eliminating the need for multiple, computationally expensive ViT forward passes.
- It utilizes a compact ==UNet module== (approximately 250k parameters) to extract multi-scale semantic and high-frequency details from the original high-resolution image as guidance signals.
- A stack of ==Joint Bilateral Upsampling (JBU) modules== progressively enriches the low-resolution features, guided by the UNet's multi-scale outputs, with the final enriched features average-pooled and concatenated with original features to prevent quadratic scaling of LLM context length.

## Results

- HIRE reduces computational costs (FLOPs) by up to **35%** and accelerates inference time by over **50%** on benchmarks like VQA_T compared to existing multi-crop high-resolution MLLM methods.
- The framework demonstrates approximately **10-15%** improvements in fine-grained tasks such as pixel-level localization and animal keypoint detection, and about a **5%** reduction in hallucination, on the MMT-Bench.
- HIRE's computational overhead remains nearly constant across different scales of ViT backbones and input resolutions, showcasing superior scalability compared to methods whose overhead increases significantly with larger backbones.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-resolution visual understanding in MLLMs can be efficiently achieved by enriching features from a single low-resolution vision encoder pass, rather than through multiple, independent crop processing.
> - A small, specialized UNet module can effectively extract crucial multi-scale semantic guidance from high-resolution images, facilitating accurate and efficient feature upsampling without adding significant computational overhead.
> - Strategically average-pooling high-resolution enriched features before concatenation and projection to the LLM is critical for maintaining overall computational efficiency by avoiding a quadratic increase in the LLM's context length.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has rapidly advanced, enabling large language models to process, reason about, and generate content involving both text and visual information. Seminal works like LLaVA and Flamingo demonstrated the power of integrating visual tokens from pre-trained vision encoders (e.g., CLIP, DINO, ViT) into the language embedding space of LLMs through a projector. These early successes established a powerful paradigm for general-purpose visual understanding.

A significant recent development within MLLMs is the recognition of the "resolution gap." While initial MLLMs often processed images at relatively low resolutions (e.g., 224x224 or 336x336 pixels), many real-world tasks require much finer-grained visual understanding. This has led to an increasing focus on incorporating high-resolution visual information, with works like Dragonfly, S2, and CogVLM demonstrating substantial performance improvements across various benchmarks that demand detailed perception.

The prevalent approach to infuse high-resolution information, however, has involved splitting the input image into multiple crops. Each crop is then independently processed by the vision encoder, and the resulting features are subsequently merged. While effective in enhancing detail perception, this method incurs a substantial computational cost. Multiple forward passes through large vision encoders, such as Vision Transformers (ViTs), lead to a significant increase in FLOPs (floating point operations) and extended inference times. For instance, methods handling resolutions up to 2000 pixels might require dozens of ViT passes, making them computationally intensive and less practical for real-time applications or environments with limited resources.

HIRE directly addresses this critical challenge. Instead of repeatedly calling the vision encoder for different image crops, it proposes a lightweight feature enrichment pipeline. This work builds upon existing research in feature upsampling and image-guided deep feature enrichment (e.g., FeatUp, LIFT), adapting these concepts to the MLLM domain. By aiming to achieve competitive performance with vastly reduced computational overhead, HIRE positions itself as a crucial advancement for making high-resolution MLLMs more efficient, scalable, and deployable. It offers a practical solution to bridge the gap between the demand for fine-grained visual understanding and the computational constraints of current high-resolution MLLM architectures.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **develop a lightweight and efficient method for infusing high-resolution visual information into Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs)**, thereby enabling fine-grained visual understanding without the prohibitive computational costs associated with existing approaches.

The motivation for HIRE stems from several critical observations and challenges in the MLLM landscape:

- **Computational Bottleneck of High-Resolution Processing:** Modern MLLMs, while benefiting immensely from high-resolution image inputs for tasks requiring fine-grained detail, typically achieve this by processing multiple crops of an image through a large vision encoder (e.g., ViT). This "multiple ViT calls" strategy leads to a quadratic increase in computational cost (FLOPs) and significantly extended inference times, making these models expensive to train and slow to deploy. For example, processing resolutions up to 2000 pixels can demand as many as 40 forward passes through the vision encoder.
- **Balancing Performance and Efficiency:** There is a strong need to maintain, if not improve, the performance of MLLMs on detailed visual tasks while drastically reducing their computational footprint. Existing high-resolution methods often prioritize accuracy over efficiency, creating a barrier to broader adoption and real-world application.
- **Scalability Challenges:** The computational overhead of current methods becomes even more pronounced when scaling to larger, more powerful ViT backbones. A solution is needed that maintains efficiency even as the underlying vision encoders grow in complexity and capacity.
- **Generic Applicability:** The authors aim for a solution that can be seamlessly integrated into existing MLLM training pipelines with minimal changes, making it a broadly applicable plug-and-play module for various MLLM architectures.
- **Reducing Hallucination:** Implicitly, by providing more accurate and detailed visual features, the work aims to reduce "hallucination," a common problem in MLLMs where the model generates factually incorrect information about an image.

In essence, HIRE seeks to overcome the trade-off between high-resolution visual perception and computational efficiency, making sophisticated MLLMs more accessible and practical for a wider range of applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The HIRE (High-Resolution Image Feature Enrichment) method is designed as a lightweight feature enrichment pipeline that directly generates high-resolution visual features from a single low-resolution vision encoder output, eliminating the need for multiple, computationally expensive ViT forward passes on image crops.

#### 3.1. Motivation and Problem Statement

Current high-resolution MLLMs commonly tile a high-resolution image into smaller crops, process each crop independently with a vision encoder (E), and then merge the resulting features. This multi-inference approach leads to substantial computational overhead. HIRE proposes an alternative: given a high-resolution image *I* and the low-resolution feature map *E(i)* obtained from *E* processing a downsampled version of *I*, HIRE generates an enriched feature map *E_enr(I)* at or near the original image resolution. Critically, this enricher is designed to be extremely lightweight and independent of the architecture of the vision encoder *E*.

#### 3.2. Approach Details

The core of HIRE comprises two main components: a small UNet module and a stack of Joint Bilateral Upsampling (JBU) modules, which work in conjunction to enrich features.

1. **UNet as a Semantic Detail Retriever (U):**
 - A compact UNet architecture is employed. It features an encoder with 5 downsampling modules, producing multi-scale features (1x, 1/2x, 1/4x, 1/8x, and 1/16x resolution relative to the input image).
 - A corresponding decoder stack with upsampling layers and skip connections brings these features back to a high resolution (e.g., 672x672 pixels).
 - The UNet's role is to extract and accumulate strong semantic and high-frequency details from the original high-resolution image *I* at various scales. These multi-scale features serve as crucial "guidance signals" for the subsequent feature enrichment process.
 - The UNet module is notably small, contributing only about 250k parameters to the overall model, ensuring its "lightweight" claim.

2. **Joint Bilateral Upsampling (JBU) Modules (J):**
 - Inspired by existing image-guided deep feature upsampling techniques, HIRE uses a parameterized stack of JBU modules.
 - This stack takes the low-resolution features *E(i)* (e.g., 24x24x1024) and the multi-scale guidance features from the UNet.
 - The JBU stack consists of multiple enriching layers (e.g., 5 layers). Each layer takes low-resolution features of resolution *x* and guidance features of resolution *2x*.
 - This guidance signal is used to generate a locality-aware joint bilateral kernel, which is then applied to the low-resolution features to produce enriched features at a higher resolution (e.g., *2x*). This process progressively upsamples the features, step by step.
 - The combined output of the UNet and JBU modules results in the enriched features, formulated as: *x_en = J(U(I; ϕ), E(i); θ)*, where *ϕ* and *θ* are the parameters of the UNet and JBU, respectively.

3. **Feature Combination and Projection:**
 - The final enriched features (*x_en*) are then average-pooled back to the original feature scale of *E(i)* (e.g., 24x24x1024).
 - These pooled enriched features are concatenated along the hidden dimension with the original low-resolution features *E(i)*. This concatenated feature vector, *E_enr(I) = Concat(E(i), pool(x_en))*, is then passed to the multimodal projector, which connects to the LLM.
 - The crucial step of *average-pooling* the high-resolution enriched features back to the lower resolution of *E(i)* before concatenation is a key design choice for computational efficiency. It prevents the quadratic scaling in the context length of the LLM that would occur if the full high-resolution enriched features were directly tokenized and fed to the LLM, thereby saving significant computational overhead.

#### 3.3. Training Regimen

HIRE is integrated into a standard MLLM training pipeline, specifically demonstrated with LLaVA-1.5:

- **Architecture:** It uses a CLIP-336 as the vision encoder and a 2-layer MLP as the projector, with Vicuna-7B or Qwen-7B as the LLM, using LoRA for memory efficiency.
- **Two-Stage Training:**
 1. **Pre-training:** Both the projector and the HIRE module (UNet and JBU) are unfrozen and trained.
 2. **Fine-tuning:** The same modules remain unfrozen, and trainable LoRA modules are added to the LLM layers for fine-tuning.
- **No Independent Pre-training:** A notable advantage is that HIRE does not require any independent pre-training, making its integration straightforward.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors demonstrate HIRE's significant advantages in computational efficiency while maintaining or exceeding competitive performance across a range of multimodal tasks.

#### 4.1. Computational Efficiency

- **Reduced FLOPs:** HIRE achieves a substantial reduction in computational costs. Compared to methods like S2 (which relies on multiple ViT calls for high resolution), HIRE provides up to a 1.5x saving in FLOPs, equating to a 35% reduction in high-resolution MLLM pipelines. For instance, LLaVA1.5-7B-HIRE at 672px resolution requires 4.296 TFLOPs for first token generation, only marginally more than vanilla LLaVA1.5 at 336px (4.278 TFLOPs), but significantly less than S2 at 672px (5.013 TFLOPs) or 1008px (6.664 TFLOPs).
- **Faster Inference Time:** HIRE drastically reduces inference time. On the VQA_T test set, S2 takes 43 minutes, more than double the time of LLaVA-1.5. In contrast, HIRE completes the same task in 23.2 minutes, representing only a marginal increase (0.1% relative increase) over the vanilla LLaVA1.5, despite enabling higher resolution processing.
- **Minimal Overhead:** The HIRE module itself adds only 0.25M parameters, contributing negligible overhead to the overall MLLM architecture.

#### 4.2. Performance on Benchmarks

HIRE demonstrates strong performance, often outperforming vanilla baselines and remaining competitive or superior to other state-of-the-art high-resolution methods like S2, even when operating at a lower effective resolution.

- **Fine-grained Visual Understanding (MMT-Bench):**
 - HIRE significantly outperforms S2 across multiple subtasks. It shows approximately a 10% improvement in pixel-level localization and dense captioning, and about a 15% improvement for animal keypoint detection, all of which are fine-grained tasks.
 - An ~5% improvement in the hallucination task indicates a reduction in factual errors, demonstrating HIRE's ability to provide more accurate visual context.
- **Visual Detail Understanding (V*):**
 - HIRE, even at 672px resolution, outperforms the vanilla LLaVA1.5-7B (336px) on V* spatial (6% relative increase) and V* attention (7% relative increase), which are benchmarks highly dependent on high-resolution information. It is competitive with S2 (1008px).
- **General Visual Question Answering (VQA):**
 - HIRE shows improvements over vanilla LLaVA1.5 on various VQA benchmarks, including VizWiz, ScienceQA, VQAv2, SEED, and GQA. For instance, it achieves 70.1 on ScienceQA compared to S2's 69.5 (with LLaVA1.5-7B backbone).
- **Areas for Future Research:** The paper notes that HIRE performs below S2 and vanilla variants on VQA_T and InfographicsVQA. The authors posit this might be due to a lack of quality in enriching text-heavy images, suggesting an area for future improvement.

#### 4.3. Scaling Capabilities

- **Efficient Scaling with Larger ViTs:** HIRE's computational overhead remains nearly constant across different scales of ViT backbones (Large, Huge, Giant) and resolutions (224, 336). In stark contrast, the computational overhead for S2 increases significantly as the size and resolution of the ViT are scaled up, due to its multiple ViT forward passes. This demonstrates HIRE's superior scalability for building larger, more powerful MLLMs.

- **Importance of UNet:** Ablation studies (Table 4) confirm the critical role of the UNet module. Using the UNet substantially improves performance on the V* benchmark, demonstrating that it effectively brings in stronger semantic features to enrich the vision encodings, leading to better downstream task performance. The learning rate for the UNet also plays a role in optimal performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HIRE framework represents a significant step forward in the development and deployment of Multimodal Large Language Models, particularly for applications requiring fine-grained visual understanding. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Democratization of High-Resolution MLLMs:** By drastically reducing the computational cost and inference time associated with high-resolution visual processing, HIRE makes sophisticated MLLMs more accessible. This means smaller research labs, startups, and developers with less access to massive computational resources can now build and deploy MLLMs capable of detailed visual analysis.
- **Enhanced Practicality and Real-world Adoption:** The efficiency gains translate directly into more practical real-world applications. Faster inference times are crucial for interactive MLLM systems, real-time visual analysis, and integration into consumer-facing products where latency is a key concern. This broadens the applicability of MLLMs in fields like medical imaging, industrial inspection, retail analytics, and advanced creative design tools.
- **Sustainable AI Development:** Reduced FLOPs contribute to more energy-efficient AI models, aligning with growing concerns about the environmental impact of large-scale AI training and deployment. HIRE offers a more sustainable pathway for advancing high-resolution MLLMs.
- **Improved User Experience:** For users interacting with MLLMs, HIRE promises faster responses and more accurate, detailed understanding of visual inputs, leading to a superior overall experience, especially for tasks requiring minute details.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** HIRE introduces a generic and lightweight feature enrichment technique that can be adapted and integrated into various MLLM architectures. This model-agnostic nature provides a robust foundation for future research into efficient high-resolution multimodal understanding, encouraging innovation in other areas like video processing or 3D scene understanding within MLLM contexts.
- **Advancement of Adobe's AI Capabilities:** For Adobe, this research directly contributes to enhancing the AI capabilities within its creative suite. Enabling MLLMs to process high-resolution images efficiently can lead to more intelligent tools for image editing, content generation, visual search, and contextual understanding within creative workflows, ultimately empowering users with more powerful and responsive AI-driven features.

In conclusion, HIRE effectively addresses a critical bottleneck in high-resolution MLLMs, demonstrating that significant performance can be achieved without the previously assumed trade-off with computational efficiency. Its lightweight design and strong empirical results position it as an influential contribution that will likely inspire further research and accelerate the practical adoption of advanced multimodal AI.
