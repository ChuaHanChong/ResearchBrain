# WorldSculpt: Generating Compositional Worlds from Grounded Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> WorldSculpt generates a cluttered scene as hundreds of separate object meshes in one shared world frame. It canonicalizes each object into an anchor-aligned cube, lifts per-view DINOv3 features into that voxel volume, fuses them with a permutation-invariant IBR aggregator, and injects the result into a frozen Pixal3D flow-matching prior via zero-initialized layers and LoRA. Trained only on single objects, it beats baselines on the new 701-object UE-MeshyScene benchmark.

## Problem

- Scene-level world generators like Marble output one fused mesh or Gaussian soup, so individual objects cannot be selected, moved, or handed to a physics simulator.
- Geometry-centric reconstructors recover accurate visible surfaces but leave holes wherever occlusion hides geometry, since they carry no generative prior over unobserved regions.
- Native-3D object priors take a single pose-free image and no known scene frame; compositional extensions were only demonstrated on tabletops or sparse furniture layouts.

## Method

- ==Anchor-aligned canonicalization==: an isotropic virtual cube, oriented by the most-visible anchor view and grown until it covers every mask, gives each object a similarity canonical-to-world transform and crop-aware projections.
- ==Multi-view conditioning pathway==: per-view ==DINOv3== features are lifted into canonical voxels and fused by an ==IBRNet-style permutation-invariant aggregator== computing cross-view mean, variance, and a softmax-weighted zero-initialized residual.
- Frozen ==Pixal3D== geometry stages are adapted by ==zero-initialized injection layers== plus rank-32 ==LoRA==, trained on single objects with a curriculum of occlusion, pose-jitter, mask-error, and downsampling augmentations.

## Results

- On Toys4k at **75%** per-view occlusion, 16 views cut CD-l2 to **2.25** versus **63.95** for single-view Pixal3D and **34.39** for multi-view TRELLIS.
- Compositional world-frame evaluation without ICP: CD-l2 **0.28** on HouseCat6D and **0.61** on Toys4k-Scene, against **1.26** and **8.38** for the strongest baseline ShapeR.
- On UE-MeshyScene's 2,299 objects across six scenes, F-Score rises **0.813** to **0.951** over ShapeR; the learned IBR aggregator beats mean fusion by **12%** CD-l2.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A single-object generative prior can scale to hundred-object scenes with no scene-level training, provided observations are mapped into a per-object canonical frame first.
> - Learned view weighting only pays off when views differ in reliability; under clean or well-observed conditions plain mean fusion of lifted features matches it.
> - Grounding is only as good as upstream masks, poses, and boxes; geometry-only static-scene output still blocks appearance-dependent and dynamic downstream simulation use.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the problem of generating compositional 3D representations of cluttered scenes, a challenge within computer vision and 3D content creation. Current generative world models, such as Marble and HY-World 2.0, are capable of synthesizing persistent and explorable 3D environments. However, their output is typically a unified scene representation (e.g., a fused mesh or a set of Gaussians), which does not explicitly separate individual objects. This monolithic representation limits the utility of these models for downstream applications like gaming, augmented/virtual reality (AR/VR), simulation, and robotics, which require scenes to be decomposed into independent, editable object meshes.

Existing approaches to 3D reconstruction and generation can be broadly categorized:
- **Geometry-centric methods:** These approaches (e.g., per-scene optimization, volumetric/Gaussian regressors, pointmap models) aim for geometric accuracy in observed regions but often produce a single, fused representation and leave occluded areas incomplete.
- **Generative priors:** These models learn distributions over plausible 3D content, allowing them to infer coherent geometry for unobserved regions.
 - **Scene-level world generation models:** While capable of synthesizing unobserved regions, these models (like Marble) still output a unified scene representation.
 - **Native-3D generative priors:** Models like Point-E, Shap-E, and Pixal3D learn complete individual objects. However, they are typically designed for single, pose-free images of one object and do not inherently handle multi-view consistency or placement in a known scene coordinate frame.
 - **Recent compositional approaches:** Methods such as InstPIFu, PartCrafter, and SceneGen extend object-level generative priors to multi-object scenes, often by jointly generating instances or combining object generation with scene layout estimation. However, these have primarily been demonstrated on simpler scenes with limited object counts or less severe occlusion, often from a single image.

WorldSculpt positions itself by proposing an approach that leverages a strong *single-object 3D generative prior* and adapts it to *multi-view observations* from *cluttered scenes containing hundreds of objects*. This approach aims to overcome the limitations of monolithic scene representations and the scalability issues of prior compositional methods. A key aspect is the demonstration that such an adapted object-level prior can generalize to complex scenes without requiring explicit scene-level training, thus addressing a gap in current capabilities for generating editable 3D assets from real-world captures.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a method for generating compositional 3D representations of densely cluttered scenes, where each object is represented as an individual mesh placed within a shared world frame.

The motivation for this objective stems from several limitations in current 3D content generation paradigms:
1. **Mismatch with Downstream Applications:** Many critical applications, including gaming, AR/VR, simulation, and robotics, do not operate on a single, undifferentiated surface geometry. Instead, they require scenes to be broken down into their constituent objects, each with its own mesh. This enables independent selection, manipulation (e.g., moving a chair), and re-simulation of objects. Current generative world models that produce fused representations fall short of this requirement.
2. **Challenges in Complex Scenes:** Generating compositional 3D content from multiple images of real cluttered scenes presents significant technical difficulties:
 - **Heavy Occlusion:** Objects are often densely packed and mutually occluding, meaning that any single view provides only partial observations of each object's geometry.
 - **Geometry Recovery:** The method must infer plausible and coherent geometry for unobserved regions, going beyond just reconstructing visible parts.
 - **Consistency:** The recovered geometry must align accurately with visible evidence across multiple views.
 - **Independent Assets:** The output must preserve each object as an independently usable asset, which current monolithic representations do not facilitate.
3. **Limitations of Existing Generative Priors:** While native-3D generative priors can create complete individual objects, they are typically designed for isolated objects and single-image inputs. Applying them to complex, multi-view, multi-object scenes requires additional mechanisms to handle pose, scale, and multi-view consistency. Scene-level generative models, while handling synthesis beyond observation, still produce non-compositional outputs. Prior compositional methods have generally been applied to less complex scenarios.

To address these motivations, WorldSculpt sets forth the following specific objectives:
- To propose a framework that can generate hundreds of individual object meshes from multi-view, grounded video observations of a cluttered scene.
- To adapt a strong single-object 3D generative prior (Pixal3D) with a multi-view conditioning pathway to enable generation of complete objects within a known scene coordinate frame.
- To demonstrate that such an object-level prior, finetuned exclusively on individual objects in canonical space, can generalize effectively to large-scale, densely occluded multi-object scenes without requiring scene-level training data.
- To introduce a new benchmark, UE-MeshyScene, to facilitate meaningful evaluation of compositional 3D generation methods in photorealistic, densely cluttered environments with exact per-object ground-truth meshes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WorldSculpt processes a set of posed RGB images, instance-level 2D masks, and coarse 3D bounding boxes to produce a compositional scene representation. This representation consists of individual meshes (M_c_k) in their canonical frames and transformations (T_ow_k) that map these canonical frames to the world coordinate system, resulting in world-space meshes (M_w_k). The method comprises three primary stages: anchor-aligned object canonicalization, multi-view conditioned object generation, and scene composition.

### 4. Anchor-aligned Object Canonicalization

This stage standardizes the input observations for each object into a consistent canonical frame, making them suitable for the object generative prior.
- **Anchor View and Canonical Cube:** For each object *k*, an "anchor" view *a_k* is selected (most fully observed during inference, random during training). This anchor defines the orientation of a virtual canonical frame. A normalized cubic domain `V = [-0.5, 0.5]^3` serves as the canonical spatial domain. A canonical-to-world transformation `T_ow_k` is derived, which includes an isotropic scale `s_k` (initially based on the largest side of the coarse localization box `B_loc_k`) and a rotation `R_k` induced by the anchor camera. The scale `s_k` is then adaptively increased until the projection of the canonical cube covers the object masks in all selected views, preventing clipping. The center `c_k` and `R_k` remain fixed during this adjustment.
- **Canonical Observations and Crop-Aware Projection:** For each view *n* observing object *k*, the image is cropped to the object's projected extent, masked by *S_kn*, and resized to the generative model's input resolution (yielding `I_kn`). The original camera intrinsics `K_n` are adjusted to `K_kn` to account for cropping and resizing. A projection function `π_kn(x)` is defined to map a canonical voxel *x* through the object's canonical-to-world transformation and camera extrinsics into the resized, cropped image coordinates, ensuring spatial alignment across views.

### 5. Multi-View Conditioned Object Generation

This stage leverages a pretrained single-object generative prior and augments it with multi-view conditioning.
- **Generative Prior:** WorldSculpt uses Pixal3D as its core generator. Pixal3D is a native-3D generative model, built on the TRELLIS.2 architecture, that generates object geometry through a cascade of flow-matching stages: a sparse structure stage (coarse occupancy on a 64^3 grid) and a shape stage (high-resolution sparse structured latent over occupied voxels). This research focuses on geometry generation.
- **Multi-View Feature Lifting:** Each canonical observation `I_kn` is encoded independently using DINOv3 to produce a dense 2D feature map `F_kn`. For every voxel *x* in the canonical volume, features `g_n(x)` are sampled from `F_kn` at the projected image location `π_kn(x)`. This process lifts 2D features into a 3D feature grid, where spatial coordinates are consistent across all views.
- **Permutation-Invariant View Aggregation:** The lifted features from selected conditioning views *J_k* are aggregated using an IBRNet-style module. This module is designed to accept a variable number of views and is permutation-invariant. For each canonical voxel *x*, it computes cross-view mean `μ(x)` and variance `σ^2(x)`. Two lightweight MLPs refine individual features `g'_n(x)` and predict aggregation logits `w_n(x)`. The final aggregated voxel feature `g_out(x)` is computed as the mean plus a softmax-weighted residual sum of the refined features. The aggregator's final layer for `MLP_feat` is zero-initialized, causing it to start as a simple mean and learn view-dependent corrections during training. Global image tokens from the anchor view `I_ka_k` are also used, preserving the original Pixal3D conditioning convention.
- **Conditioning Injection:** The aggregated 3D conditioning grid `G_k` is injected into both Pixal3D geometry stages. At each transformer block, `G_k` is aligned with the current 3D token locations, projected to the block's feature dimension, and added. The original Pixal3D parameters are kept frozen, while low-rank adapters (LoRA) are used to adapt its attention and per-block projection layers. The multi-view aggregator and zero-initialized conditioning projections are trained from scratch.

### 6. Training

- **Random-View Training:** The sparse-structure and shape stages are trained independently using ground-truth latent targets. During training, a variable number of conditioning views and a random anchor are sampled for each object, defining the canonical orientation. This exposes the model to diverse camera configurations while the aggregation remains permutation-invariant.
- **Conditioning-View Augmentation:** To bridge the domain gap between clean training renders and real-world cluttered scenes, a curriculum of augmentations is applied to input observations. These include simulating occlusion (random 2D masks or 3D-consistent occluders), perturbing non-anchor camera poses, introducing mask errors, and downsampling observations. The strength of these degradations is gradually increased during training.

### 7. Inference and Scene Composition

During inference, each object is processed independently. An anchor view is selected as the one with the most complete observation. The anchor-aligned canonical cube is constructed, and its side length is adjusted if necessary to cover all object masks. A bounded number of conditioning views, prioritizing larger visible regions, are selected to generate masked crops and crop-aware projection functions. The two Pixal3D geometry stages are run with the multi-view conditioning to produce an individual canonical-space mesh `M_c_k`. Finally, each `M_c_k` is transformed into the world frame using `T_ow_k` to form the final compositional scene `M_w`.

### 8. Implementation Details

The Pixal3D geometry stages are finetuned independently on the TexVerse dataset. Conditioning images are resized to 512px for the sparse-structure stage and 1024px for the shape stage. LoRA adapters with rank `r=32` and scaling factor `α=32` are applied to DiT attention and per-block projection layers. Training occurs for 15k iterations in bf16 using AdamW, with a learning rate of 10^-4 and a batch size of 48 for sparse-structure and 12 for shape. 1-20 views are randomly sampled for conditioning. Gradient checkpointing and token capping are used for memory efficiency. The augmentation curriculum reaches maximum strength over the first 3k iterations.

### 9. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates WorldSculpt across three settings of increasing complexity: canonical-space single-object generation, multi-object scene generation, and large-scale compositional generation. Metrics include Chamfer distance (CD-ℓ2, CD-ℓ1), Earth Mover's Distance (EMD), and F-Score. For compositional scenes, evaluation is performed in the world frame without per-object alignment, assessing both geometry and placement.

### 10. UE-MeshyScene Dataset

A new benchmark, UE-MeshyScene, is introduced to address limitations of existing datasets in scale, occlusion, and ground-truth availability. It comprises six large-scale, photorealistic indoor and outdoor environments rendered in Unreal Engine 5.8, containing 93 to 701 objects (2,299 total) per scene. Each scene is captured along orbiting camera trajectories (265–1758 views per scene, 5,964 total) at 2560x1440 resolution. The dataset provides RGB images, camera poses, per-object instance masks, 3D bounding boxes, and ground-truth meshes, enabling direct world-frame evaluation under complex mutual occlusion.

### 11. Canonical-Space Single-Object Generation (Toys4k)

- **Setup:** Evaluation on 500 Toys4k objects under varying input views (1–16) and per-view occlusion levels (0–75%). Compared against Pixal3D (single-view), TRELLIS (single/multi-image), and TRELLIS.2 (single-view).
- **Results:** With a single view, WorldSculpt achieves competitive performance with the dedicated single-view Pixal3D pipeline. Performance consistently improves as the number of input views increases. The method demonstrates robustness to occlusion; with 16 views, quality remains largely stable even with 50% per-view occlusion, significantly outperforming baselines which degrade substantially. This indicates that multi-view conditioning and the augmentation curriculum effectively handle partial observations.

### 12. Multi-object Scene Generation (Toys4k-Scene and HouseCat6D)

- **Setup:** Evaluated compositional scene generation on synthetic Toys4k-Scene (cluttered layouts, strong inter-object occlusion) and real-world HouseCat6D (tabletop scenes, scanned ground-truth, milder occlusion). Compared against SAM3D, SceneGen, SceneMaker, RecGen, and ShapeR.
- **Results:** WorldSculpt consistently outperforms all prior approaches across all reported metrics on both datasets. Gains are particularly pronounced on Toys4k-Scene, which features denser occlusion. Qualitative comparisons (Figures 4 and 5) show WorldSculpt producing more detailed and accurately registered object geometry, with lower angular errors and fewer spurious or missing surfaces compared to baselines.

### 13. UE-MeshyScene Generation

- **Setup:** Evaluation on the six large-scale UE-MeshyScene environments, which present hundreds of objects and severe mutual occlusion. Comparison primarily with ShapeR, a multi-view baseline capable of handling complex scenes.
- **Results:** WorldSculpt significantly outperforms ShapeR across all reported metrics, including mean and median distance errors and F-Score. The improvement in median values suggests consistent gains across typical objects, not just a few outliers. Qualitative results (Figure 6) illustrate that WorldSculpt recovers more detailed object geometry and maintains more accurate placement in the world frame, particularly for small and heavily occluded objects, where baselines often produce over-smooth or misplaced shapes.

### 14. Ablation: Multi-View Fusion

- **Canonical-space (Toys4k):** Replacing the learned IBR aggregator with a simple arithmetic mean ("Avg") shows that the learned IBR aggregator generally performs comparably or better, with its advantage becoming more pronounced as the number of input views and occlusion levels increase (Table 5). This suggests learned view weighting is beneficial when observations have varying reliability.
- **Scene-level (HouseCat6D, Toys4k-Scene, UE-MeshyScene):** The benefit of the learned IBR aggregation scales with scene complexity (Table 6). On HouseCat6D (sparse, mild occlusion), performance is similar. On Toys4k-Scene (denser), IBR shows consistent improvement. On UE-MeshyScene (hundreds of objects, severe occlusion), IBR achieves its largest gains, reducing CD-ℓ2 by 12% and increasing F-Score. This reinforces that adaptive view weighting is critical in highly cluttered and occluded environments.

### 15. Application: Converting Generated 3D Worlds into Compositional Meshes

WorldSculpt's applicability is demonstrated by converting 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) worlds generated by models like Marble and HY-World 2.0 into compositional mesh scenes without additional training. The process involves:
1. Rendering posed RGB frames and metric depth maps from the 3DGS.
2. Recovering per-object mask tracks using SAM3 and estimating coarse 3D localizations from masks and depth, including merging fragmented tracks.
3. Applying the standard WorldSculpt inference pipeline to generate individual object meshes.
This application highlights the method's potential to enable editable 3D assets from existing generative world models.

### 16. Significance and Potential Impact

WorldSculpt presents a significant advancement in the field of 3D content generation, particularly for complex and cluttered environments. Its primary significance lies in demonstrating a scalable and effective approach to generating compositional 3D representations from multi-view inputs, where each object is an individual, editable mesh.

The potential impact of this work is multifaceted:
- **Enabling Downstream Applications:** By providing scenes as collections of independent object meshes, WorldSculpt directly addresses a fundamental requirement of applications in gaming, AR/VR, simulation, and robotics. This allows for greater interactivity, reusability of assets, and integration with physics engines, which is not feasible with unified scene representations.
- **Scalability and Generalization:** The finding that a strong single-object generative prior, adapted with multi-view conditioning, can generalize to complex scenes with hundreds of densely occluded objects without scene-level training is a notable technical contribution. This approach reduces the need for expensive and difficult-to-acquire scene-level training data, potentially accelerating research and development in this area.
- **Robustness to Real-World Conditions:** The multi-view conditioning pathway, combined with the conditioning-view augmentation curriculum, enhances the model's robustness to partial and degraded observations commonly encountered in real-world captures. This increases the practical utility of the method in less controlled environments.
- **New Benchmark for Progress:** The introduction of UE-MeshyScene provides a valuable resource for the research community. This photorealistic dataset with dense clutter, numerous objects, and precise per-object ground-truth meshes offers a rigorous standard for evaluating future compositional 3D generation methods, pushing the boundaries of what these systems can achieve.
- **Interoperability with Existing Generative Models:** The demonstration of converting 3DGS worlds from other generative models (like Marble) into compositional mesh scenes expands WorldSculpt's utility. This capability could serve as a "compositionalization" layer, making outputs from other 3D content pipelines more editable and application-ready.

While the work has limitations, such as its reliance on upstream predictions for camera poses, masks, and bounding boxes, and its current focus on geometry without appearance, these are recognized as avenues for future research. The core contribution of enabling practical, compositional 3D generation for complex scenes positions WorldSculpt as a foundational step toward more versatile and functional 3D world models.
