# $\textbf{Re}^{2}$: Unlocking LLM Reasoning via Reinforcement Learning with Re-solving

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces Reinforcement Learning with Re-solving (Re²), a framework that allows large language models to strategically abandon unpromising reasoning paths and restart the problem-solving process. It consistently improved reasoning accuracy across various models and benchmarks, demonstrating superior test-time scaling compared to existing reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) methods.

## Problem

- Existing RLVR-trained large language models (LLMs) often struggle to recover from suboptimal early reasoning steps, leading to "overthinking" or "underthinking."
- Current LLM reasoning paradigms typically enforce a single, continuous Chain of Thought (CoT), making it difficult for models to escape early errors or unproductive trajectories.
- Blindly extending a flawed reasoning path is inefficient and frequently fails to yield correct answers, especially when initial reasoning is misdirected.

## Method

- A novel Reinforcement Learning with ==Re-solving== (Re²) framework is developed, enabling LLMs to either finalize a solution or explicitly choose to restart the problem-solving process.
- The method involves sampling multiple partial reasoning trajectories (prefixes) and their continuations, then training the model to predict a 're-solve' action when appropriate.
- A specialized reward strategy is designed, where the reward for a 're-solve' action is based on the estimated expected accuracy of solving the problem from scratch, derived from out-of-group completions.

## Results

- Re² consistently improved reasoning performance across five diverse benchmarks (including AIME, AMC, GSM8K, GPQA) and five LLM types, showing an average gain of 5.8 percentage points on Qwen2.5-**7B** Base over DAPO.
- The framework demonstrated superior test-time scaling, effectively leveraging increased inference-time compute to unlock further performance potential beyond other RLVR baselines.
- Re² exhibited difficulty-adaptive behavior, with higher re-solving rates for more challenging problems and substantial accuracy gains (e.g., from **51.2%** to **81.7%**) on questions where RLVR models were unstable.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Equipping LLMs with an explicit re-solve mechanism, learned via RL, allows them to dynamically decide when to abandon unpromising paths, mirroring human problem-solving strategies.
> - The proposed rational reward strategy for re-solving encourages more self-aware model behavior, prompting models to indicate uncertainty and restart rather than forcing potentially incorrect answers.
> - Early errors in reasoning significantly hinder recovery, making a re-solving capability crucial for improving overall reasoning robustness and avoiding inefficient computation on flawed trajectories.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of large language models (LLMs) has witnessed significant advancements in reasoning capabilities, often achieved by scaling test-time compute through techniques like generating longer Chains of Thought (CoTs) that incorporate planning, reflection, and self-correction. Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has emerged as a prominent post-training paradigm for enhancing these reasoning abilities, encouraging models to produce more elaborate and sophisticated CoTs. State-of-the-art models frequently adopt RL to strengthen their deep-thinking capabilities, particularly in complex domains such as coding and mathematics.

However, existing RLVR-trained LLMs continue to exhibit limitations, including tendencies towards "overthinking" (generating unnecessary or redundant steps) and "underthinking" (failing to explore sufficiently), both of which can lead to suboptimal reasoning trajectories and degraded performance. Current paradigms often restrict models to generating a single, continuous CoT, making recovery from early errors difficult. Some recent efforts have explored backtracking or terminating low-confidence chains, primarily through supervised fine-tuning or decoding strategies, but these have not fully leveraged the potential of reinforcement learning to enable more flexible reasoning control.

This paper addresses these limitations by introducing a novel reasoning paradigm that allows LLMs to strategically abandon unpromising reasoning paths and restart the problem-solving process from scratch. This approach contrasts with traditional single-chain reasoning and extends the concept of test-time scaling by providing an explicit "re-solve" mechanism within the RL framework, aiming to improve robustness and accuracy when initial reasoning is misdirected. The work positions itself as the first to integrate such a re-solving capability into LLMs through pure reinforcement learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the reasoning performance of Large Language Models (LLMs) by addressing a fundamental limitation observed in existing RLVR-trained models: their struggle to recover from suboptimal early reasoning steps. Despite extensive RL training and increased test-time compute, current LLMs frequently generate inefficient or low-quality Chain-of-Thought (CoT) steps, leading to "overthinking" or "underthinking" behaviors that negatively impact both efficiency and answer quality.

The motivation for this work stems from several key observations:
- **Ineffectiveness of prolonged suboptimal reasoning:** An analysis of CoT length and accuracy revealed a negative correlation, indicating that longer CoTs often result from early critical mistakes. When the initial reasoning direction is suboptimal or misdirected, LLMs tend to generate significantly more tokens and reasoning steps, yet frequently fail to converge on the correct answer (as illustrated in Figure 2a). This suggests that blindly extending an already flawed reasoning path is inefficient and largely unproductive.
- **Critical impact of early reasoning quality:** Experiments demonstrated that even truncating and attempting to complete incorrect responses from early prefixes (as short as 20% of the full response) led to substantial drops in accuracy compared to starting from scratch. This highlights that once early reasoning is misguided, the model rarely recovers, emphasizing the need for a mechanism to escape such trajectories.
- **Analogy to human problem-solving:** Complex problems often require initial exploration, which may involve pursuing seemingly promising but ultimately unfruitful paths. Humans frequently re-evaluate and restart their approach when a current strategy proves unproductive. The researchers were motivated to imbue LLMs with a similar capability to dynamically decide when to abandon an unpromising path and "re-solve" the problem.

Therefore, the key objective is to equip LLMs with the ability to flexibly abandon unproductive reasoning paths and restart the solution process when necessary, rather than being compelled to commit to a final answer from a potentially flawed trajectory. This capability is hypothesized to improve overall accuracy by allowing models to escape dead ends and pursue more fruitful approaches, leading to a more rational and effective reasoning process.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Reinforcement Learning with Re-solving (Re²), enables LLMs to either finalize an answer or restart the problem-solving process. This is achieved through a pure reinforcement learning framework that does not require preliminary supervised fine-tuning.

#### 3.1. Prefix Group Generation

To facilitate the learning of re-solving behavior, Re² operates by first sampling multiple partial reasoning trajectories, termed "prefixes," for each query. Specifically, for each query, `n` full responses are sampled, and each response is randomly truncated at a proportion uniformly drawn from [0, 0.8]. These `n` diverse prefixes serve as intermediate reasoning states. For each of these `n` prefixes, the model then generates `m` CoT continuations. All `m` continuations originating from the same prefix are grouped together, which is crucial for subsequent advantage calculation. The model is incentivized to produce re-solve actions through a specialized prompting strategy (detailed in Appendix A), which explicitly guides it to state "It's better to redo the question." when choosing to restart.

#### 3.2. Reward Strategy with Re-solving

A distinct reward strategy is designed to encourage the model to rationally decide between continuing a reasoning path and re-solving. For each continuation `O_i,j` of the `i`-th prefix `Pre_i`, there are three potential outcomes:
1. **Correct final answer:** The continuation leads to the correct solution. Reward assigned is 1.
2. **Incorrect final answer:** The continuation leads to an incorrect solution. Reward assigned is 0.
3. **Choose to re-solve (resolve):** The model explicitly indicates a decision to abandon the current path and restart.

For a continuation that chooses to re-solve, the reward is assigned based on the *expected accuracy of solving the problem from scratch*. This expectation is estimated using out-of-group CoT completions—i.e., completions derived from prefixes other than `Pre_i`. If `P_!=i(correct)`, `P_!=i(incorrect)`, and `P_!=i(resolve)` represent the empirical probabilities of the three outcomes among the `(n-1) * m` out-of-group continuations, and `R` is the maximum allowed redo rounds, the reward `r_i,j` for a "resolve" action is:

`r_i,j = P_!=i(correct) * (1 - P_!=i(resolve)^R) / (1 - P_!=i(resolve))`

This formulation encourages re-solving when the current reasoning prefix is suboptimal, as the expected accuracy of restarting might be higher than continuing a flawed trajectory. Conversely, if the current path is promising, direct completion yields a higher reward.

#### 3.3. Advantage Computation and Parameter Update

Following the reward assignment, group-wise advantages are computed, similar to DAPO. For the `j`-th continuation `O_i,j` of prefix `Pre_i`, the advantage `A_i,j` is calculated by normalizing its reward `r_i,j` with respect to the mean and standard deviation of rewards within its group:

`A_i,j = (r_i,j - mean({r_i,j}_m_j=1)) / std({r_i,j}_m_j=1)`

Groups where all continuations yield identical outcomes (and thus zero advantage) are filtered out. This advantage is then broadcast to all tokens in the corresponding continuation. The policy parameters are updated by maximizing a clipped objective function, which is a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), to stabilize training. The training algorithm iteratively samples mini-batches, generates prefixes and continuations, computes rewards and advantages, and updates the policy.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Training Datasets:** DAPO-Math-17K, comprising 17,000 mathematical problems from AoPS and competition sources, with answers transformed into integers for reliable rule-based reward signals.
- **Baselines:** Vanilla model (before RL training) and DAPO (Yu et al., 2025), a recent RLVR method. Both Re² and DAPO were trained with comparable generated token budgets. Additional baselines for test-time scaling include GRPO, DLER, and DeepConf.
- **Models:** Qwen-7B-Base, Qwen-14B-Base, Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (base and instruction-tuned LLMs), and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B (a reasoning model).
- **Benchmarks:** AIME 2024, AIME 2025 (challenging mathematics), AMC 2023 (moderate mathematics), GSM8K (elementary math word problems), and GPQA-Diamond (graduate-level scientific reasoning). AIME25 is noted for its low risk of data contamination.
- **Training Details:** Learning rate of 1e-6. Re² uses 32 queries per batch, 8 prefixes (`n=8`), and 8 continuations per prefix (`m=8`). Maximum sequence length 8192 tokens. Clipping thresholds `ε_low` = 0.2, `ε_high` = 0.28. Maximum redo rounds `R=5`. DAPO uses 128 queries per batch, 20 samples per query to match token budget.
- **Evaluation Details:** Maximum sequence length 16384 tokens, temperature 0.6, top-p 0.95. For Re² models, if a redo action is sampled, sampling restarts until a final answer is generated, with the first valid answer taken as output. For test-time scaling, accuracy is measured using majority voting over sampled outputs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Re² across various models and benchmarks yielded several key findings:

#### 4.1. Consistent Performance Improvements

- Re² consistently improved reasoning performance across all five benchmark datasets (AIME24, AIME25, AMC23, GSM8K, GPQA) and all five model types (base, instruction-tuned, and reasoning-optimized models ranging from 3B to 14B parameters) compared to DAPO under the same training budget (Table 1).
- On pretrained models like Qwen2.5-7B and Qwen2.5-14B, Re² achieved larger gains relative to DAPO. For instance, Qwen2.5-7B Base saw an average improvement of 5.8 percentage points, and Qwen2.5-14B Base improved by 5.5 points.
- The improvements were robust across different domains (mathematics, scientific reasoning) and difficulty levels. Notably, Re² demonstrated substantial gains on the challenging AIME24 and AIME25 benchmarks, with AIME25 being particularly significant due to its reduced risk of data contamination.

#### 4.2. Superior Test-Time Scaling

- Re² effectively leveraged increased test-time compute, demonstrating superior scaling compared to DAPO and other baselines (GRPO, DLER, DeepConf) as the number of samples increased (Figures 1b, 6, 10). When the number of samples exceeded 64, Re² consistently surpassed RLVR-trained models, which often showed saturated performance.
- This indicates that Re² unlocks further performance potential with more inference-time resources. However, it was noted that with a small number of samples, Re² might trigger more redo actions on difficult problems, leading to fewer valid final answers and potentially lower accuracy than RLVR in those specific low-sample scenarios.

#### 4.3. Training Dynamics and Learned Behavior

- Analysis of training dynamics (Figure 7) showed that both the average reward and the reward for the 'resolve' action steadily increased throughout training.
- The probability of choosing to re-solve initially rose sharply within the first 20 training steps, reaching over 30% from a baseline of 0.5% in vanilla models, and then gradually decreased with further training. Concurrently, the probability of producing incorrect answers substantially dropped, while correct final answers slowly increased.
- This suggests Re² rapidly learns to activate and then refine the re-solving behavior, enabling the model to abandon unproductive paths while continuing to explore challenging, correct trajectories.

#### 4.4. Mechanisms Behind Re²'s Effectiveness

- **Improved CoT Quality:** Manual inspection revealed that Re² produces reasoning chains of consistently higher quality by allowing models to restart when a trajectory is unpromising. This reduces the tendency to force a final answer from flawed reasoning.
- **Rational Reward Modeling:** The reward strategy in Re² is considered more rational than standard 0/1 end-rewards. It encourages the model to output a final answer only when confident and to honestly indicate the need to restart when reasoning is unreliable. This self-aware behavior helps avoid optimizing for trajectories that accidentally guess correct answers, a common issue in traditional RLVR.
- **Difficulty-Adaptive Behavior:** Re²'s re-solving rate correlates with problem difficulty (Figure 8). For problems the base model could not solve (Group 1), Re² almost always attempted to re-solve. For difficult but solvable questions (Group 2), Re² achieved more than twice the accuracy of DAPO. The largest improvement over DAPO occurred on questions where RLVR models were capable of solving but were highly unstable (Group 4), with accuracy rising from 51.2% to 81.7%. This indicates Re² helps models successfully navigate complex problems by allowing restarts.
- **CoT Length Stabilization:** Unlike DAPO, which showed increasing CoT length due to self-reflection and error correction in single-chain reasoning, Re² gradually stabilized its CoT lengths. The lengths of correct, wrong, and redo CoTs did not show substantial differences (Figure 13), indicating that Re² avoids over-committing to unpromising trajectories and generates more focused reasoning.
- **Resolve Reward Estimator Accuracy:** The Re² reward estimator for re-solving accuracy was shown to be accurate, exhibiting lower bias and variance than a baseline EMA estimator across various sampling configurations (Figure 11).

#### 4.5. Efficiency and Application

- Re² showed an 11% increase in rollout time compared to DAPO for the same number of rollout samples. However, this additional cost was deemed acceptable given the performance improvements, as Re² still outperformed DAPO under the same training time budget (Figure 14).
- The method was successfully applied to OpenPangu-Embedded-1B, a smaller model designed for edge devices, demonstrating its effectiveness across different model scales and hardware (Table 3).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Reinforcement Learning with Re-solving (Re²), a novel paradigm that significantly advances the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs). By allowing models to flexibly abandon unproductive reasoning paths and restart the problem-solving process, Re² addresses a critical limitation of existing LLMs, which often struggle to recover from suboptimal early reasoning.

The **significance** of this work lies in several key aspects:
- **Novel Reasoning Paradigm:** Re² moves beyond the traditional single-chain, committed-answer approach in LLM reasoning. It introduces a multi-chain, self-correction mechanism explicitly learned through reinforcement learning, akin to human problem-solving where re-evaluation and restarts are common.
- **Enhanced Reasoning Robustness:** The ability to "re-solve" problems makes LLMs more robust to initial misdirections or low-quality reasoning steps, leading to consistently higher accuracy across diverse and challenging benchmarks, including complex mathematical and scientific reasoning tasks.
- **Improved Efficiency in Complex Scenarios:** By preventing models from "overthinking" along flawed trajectories, Re² promotes more efficient exploration of solution space, reducing the generation of spurious steps and incoherent logic that are common in traditional RLVR approaches.
- **Superior Test-Time Scaling:** Re² effectively harnesses increased test-time compute, demonstrating better performance scaling as more samples are generated compared to existing RLVR methods. This unlocks a higher potential ceiling for performance in scenarios where computational resources can be expanded during inference.
- **More Rational Model Behavior:** The specialized reward strategy encourages a more self-aware and rational decision-making process within the LLM, prompting it to indicate uncertainty and a need to restart rather than forcing an uncertain or incorrect answer. This inherently leads to higher-quality reasoning traces.

The **potential impact** of Re² is broad:
- **Advancing LLM Capabilities:** This paradigm could enable LLMs to tackle even more complex, multi-step, and open-ended problems where initial approaches are highly uncertain.
- **Foundation for Future AI Systems:** The concept of flexible re-solving could be integrated into more advanced AI agents, particularly those involved in planning, scientific discovery, or complex decision-making, allowing them to adapt and recover from errors more effectively.
- **Broader Applications:** While primarily demonstrated on reasoning benchmarks, the core idea of learning when to restart could be extended to other generative modalities (e.g., vision, multi-modal reasoning) or more complex tasks like tool use and search-intensive problems, where iterative refinement and re-evaluation are crucial.
- **Improved Trustworthiness and Explainability:** By making models more "self-aware" of their reasoning quality and capable of explicitly indicating a need to restart, Re² could contribute to more trustworthy AI systems that are transparent about their limitations and decision processes.

The authors acknowledge limitations, such as the current lack of explicit control over re-solve probability during inference and the focus on language models and reasoning benchmarks. However, the foundational shift introduced by Re² provides a promising direction for developing more flexible, reliable, and intelligent reasoning capabilities in LLMs.
