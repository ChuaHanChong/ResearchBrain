# Statistically Assuring Safety of Control Systems using Ensembles of Safety Filters and Conformal Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> This research from Washington University in St. Louis introduces a two-phase framework that integrates conformal prediction with Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) reachability analysis to provide statistical safety guarantees for learning-enabled control systems. The framework enables principled switching between controllers by calibrating learned safety functions and offers trajectory-level probabilistic safety assurances, demonstrating robust performance with ensemble models in a 10-dimensional highway overtaking scenario.

## Problem

- Scalability issues in Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) reachability analysis for high-dimensional systems make formal safety verification computationally intensive.
- Learning-based approximations of HJ value functions lack guarantees on accuracy, creating uncertainty in their safety predictions.
- Current methods for switching between nominal and learned safe controllers often rely on unprincipled thresholds, lacking statistical assurance for the combined policy.

## Method

- A two-phase framework using ==one-sided conformal prediction== to calibrate learned ==Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) value functions==, establishing a statistically principled ==switching condition== between a nominal and a learned safe controller.
- The framework introduces an ==ensemble of independently trained HJ value functions==, employing distinct strategies (single vs. multiple switching) to aggregate their safety predictions and enhance robustness.
- ==Trajectory-level statistical safety guarantees== are provided for the deployed switched policy, quantifying the probability of avoiding failure states using a ==Beta distribution== based on certification runs.

## Results

- In a 10-dimensional highway takeover environment, individual Hamilton-Jacobi safety filters reduced safety violations from **46%** (nominal controller) to as low as **2%** and **8%** for the best-performing models.
- The ensemble approach achieved a **96%** success rate and **4%** violation rate, matching the best individual model's performance and demonstrating robustness against poorly performing ensemble members.
- The multiple switching strategy within the ensemble consistently yielded higher success rates and lower violation rates compared to the single switching strategy, and the statistical safety guarantees were empirically validated, showing increased precision with more certification trajectories.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Conformal prediction effectively quantifies the uncertainty in learned Hamilton-Jacobi value functions, providing a statistically sound basis for switching between nominal and safe control policies.
> - An ensemble of independently trained HJ value functions significantly improves robustness, consistently matching the performance of its best individual member even when some members perform poorly.
> - Dynamically selecting the safest policy from an ensemble at each timestep (multiple switching strategy) yields superior safety performance compared to committing to a single safe policy for an entire unsafe episode.
