# Evo-Depth: A Lightweight Depth-Enhanced Vision-Language-Action Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Evo-Depth introduces a lightweight Vision-Language-Action (VLA) framework that enhances robotic manipulation by implicitly deriving depth information from multi-view RGB images without additional 3D sensors. This framework demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across various simulation benchmarks and achieves a 90% average success rate in complex real-world tasks, maintaining high inference efficiency with only 0.9 billion parameters.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models primarily rely on 2D visual representations, leading to degraded performance in manipulation tasks requiring precise spatial understanding.
- Methods using explicit 3D inputs (e.g., depth maps) require additional sensing hardware and increase system complexity, making them less practical for real-world deployment.
- Implicit 3D-aware models often depend on large geometry foundation models, resulting in substantial model sizes and high computational costs, limiting deployment efficiency.

## Method

- **Evo-Depth** employs a lightweight ==Implicit Depth Encoding Module (IDEM)== initialized from a pre-trained multi-view depth encoder to extract ==compact implicit depth features== from multi-view RGB observations.
- A ==Spatial Enhancement Module (SEM)== uses ==FiLM-style modulation== to efficiently inject these implicit depth features into the 2D vision-language representations from an ==InternVL3-1B backbone==.
- A ==Progressive Alignment Training== strategy, involving three stages (initial, spatial, and joint optimization), is used to align the implicit depth-enhanced representations with the downstream action expert for improved end-to-end performance.

## Results

- Evo-Depth achieved an average success rate of **84.4%** on Meta-World, **41.1%** on VLA-Arena, and **95.4%** on LIBERO, demonstrating superior performance over baselines in simulation, especially for tasks requiring robust spatial perception.
- In real-world experiments on a UFACTORY xArm6 robot, the model attained a **90%** average success rate across three manipulation tasks, with performance advantages widening on tasks of increased spatial complexity.
- The framework maintains high deployment efficiency with **0.9B parameters**, **3.2 GB GPU memory** usage, and an inference frequency of **12.3 Hz**, while exhibiting enhanced robustness against various real-world environmental disturbances.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Implicit depth cues, derived from multi-view RGB images using a lightweight encoder, effectively capture relative spatial relationships and object layouts to enhance VLA models without explicit 3D data.
> - A FiLM-style modulation within the Spatial Enhancement Module (SEM) provides an efficient and lightweight method for fusing spatial information with 2D vision-language representations.
> - A progressive, multi-stage training strategy is crucial for effectively aligning implicitly learned spatial representations with downstream action prediction, optimizing cross-module compatibility and overall task performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied intelligence, particularly robotic manipulation, has increasingly focused on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models aim to unify perception, language grounding, and action generation, enabling robots to understand natural language instructions and execute complex tasks based on visual observations. Current VLA models, such as OpenVLA, GR00T, and π\*0.6, have demonstrated progress through large-scale multimodal pretraining and end-to-end policy learning.

However, a significant limitation of many existing VLA models is their primary reliance on 2D visual representations. This reliance often leads to performance degradation in manipulation scenarios that demand precise spatial understanding, such as accurate object localization, fine-grained placement, or tasks requiring an understanding of detailed spatial relationships.

To address the lack of 3D information, the broader research landscape has explored two main directions:

1. **Explicit 3D Information Integration:** This approach involves incorporating explicit 3D inputs like depth maps or point clouds into robotic policy learning. Examples include 3D-VLA, SpatialVLA, and PointVLA. While these methods enhance spatial understanding, they typically increase system complexity by requiring additional sensing hardware (e.g., depth cameras, LiDAR) or specialized geometry-processing pipelines. This can make them susceptible to sensing noise, reconstruction errors, and higher latency, limiting their practicality and scalability for real-world deployment.
2. **Implicit 3D-Aware Spatial Modeling from RGB:** This alternative explores deriving 3D-aware spatial information directly from RGB observations, thus avoiding the need for additional sensors. However, current methods in this category often depend on large geometry foundation models, such as VGGT, which can lead to substantial model sizes and consequently higher training and deployment costs. Furthermore, many existing fusion designs in this area are computationally heavy, leaving lightweight yet effective spatial-semantic fusion largely unexplored.

Evo-Depth positions itself as a response to these challenges. It aims to bridge the gap by offering a lightweight and deployment-efficient VLA framework that enhances spatial perception *implicitly* from multi-view RGB images, without requiring additional 3D sensing hardware or relying on computationally expensive large-scale geometry models. The work specifically addresses the need for effective alignment of spatial enhancements with downstream action learning, an aspect that remains an open challenge in the field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the Evo-Depth research is to develop a lightweight, depth-enhanced Vision-Language-Action (VLA) framework that improves spatially grounded robotic manipulation. This objective is pursued while adhering to two critical constraints: avoiding reliance on additional sensing hardware (such as depth cameras or LiDAR) and preserving deployment efficiency (i.e., maintaining a compact model size, low memory usage, and high inference frequency).

The motivation behind these objectives stems from several limitations observed in the current state of VLA models and their existing solutions for spatial understanding:

- **Deficiency in 2D-based VLA Models:** Many contemporary VLA models primarily process 2D visual representations. While effective for certain tasks, this approach inherently lacks robust depth information and detailed spatial relationships. This deficiency significantly impairs performance in manipulation scenarios demanding precise spatial understanding, such as accurately locating objects, performing fine-grained placements, or executing spatially consistent actions, leading to noticeable performance drops.
- **Drawbacks of Explicit 3D Information:** Approaches that incorporate explicit 3D inputs (e.g., depth maps, point clouds) provide richer geometric information and can improve spatial understanding. However, they introduce several practical challenges. These include the necessity for additional specialized sensing hardware, increased system complexity due to extra processing pipelines, and heightened vulnerability to sensing noise and reconstruction errors. These factors often compromise the practicality and scalability of such methods in real-world robotic deployments.
- **Computational Burden of Implicit 3D-Aware Models:** A different line of research aims to extract implicit 3D-aware spatial information solely from RGB observations, circumventing the need for extra sensors. While conceptually appealing, these methods frequently leverage large-scale geometry foundation models. This reliance typically results in significantly larger model sizes, leading to higher training costs, increased computational demands during deployment, and reduced suitability for real-time robotic applications.
- **Need for Efficient Spatial-Semantic Fusion and Alignment:** Beyond the input modality, there is an underexplored area regarding lightweight and effective designs for fusing spatial and semantic information within VLA models. Furthermore, how to optimally align these enhanced spatial representations with the subsequent action learning process during training presents a considerable challenge, often leading to suboptimal end-to-end performance.

In response, Evo-Depth is motivated to develop a framework that can inherently derive and integrate spatial cues from readily available multi-view RGB observations. This is achieved through a lightweight mechanism for depth feature extraction and a novel modulation-based fusion strategy, coupled with a progressive training scheme to ensure proper alignment with action generation. The overarching goal is to enable VLA models to perform precise manipulation more reliably and robustly in diverse environments, without the compromises associated with existing 3D perception methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Evo-Depth proposes a lightweight, depth-enhanced VLA framework designed to improve spatially grounded manipulation using implicit depth cues from multi-view RGB observations, without requiring additional 3D sensors. The architecture consists of four main components: an Implicit Depth Encoding Module (IDEM), a Vision-Language Backbone, a Spatial Enhancement Module (SEM), and an Action Expert. A Progressive Alignment Training strategy is introduced to optimize the model.

#### 3.1. Overview of Evo-Depth Architecture

Given multi-view RGB observations, a language instruction, and the robot state, Evo-Depth processes these inputs to predict a target action trajectory. The IDEM first extracts implicit depth features from the multi-view RGB images. Concurrently, a Vision-Language Backbone encodes the RGB images and language instruction into 2D vision-language representations. These two sets of features are then integrated by the SEM to produce spatially enhanced vision-language representations. Finally, an Action Expert uses these enhanced representations along with the robot state to generate the action trajectory.

#### 3.2. Model Design

- **Implicit Depth Encoding Module (IDEM):**
 - **Purpose:** To extract compact implicit depth features directly from multi-view RGB observations, avoiding explicit 3D inputs or additional sensors. These features aim to capture relative spatial relationships and object layouts without constructing heavy geometry-rich scene representations.
 - **Architecture:** It is built upon a single, lightweight Vision Transformer backbone, comprising approximately 0.13 billion parameters.
 - **Initialization:** The weights of the IDEM are initialized from a multi-view depth encoder, specifically "Depth Anything 3," which is pretrained for joint any-view depth estimation under large-scale supervision. This provides a strong initial spatial understanding.
 - **Cross-View Information Exchange:** The IDEM employs a progressive attention mechanism. Initial layers (first l0 layers) apply self-attention independently within each view to preserve local image structure. Subsequent layers alternate between within-view and cross-view attention, progressively aggregating spatial cues from different views into a unified latent feature space.
 - **Output:** The latent transformer embeddings serve as the implicit depth features (Dt), encoding object layout and relative positional relationships.

- **Vision-Language Backbone:**
 - **Purpose:** To encode multi-view RGB observations and the language instruction into 2D vision-language representations.
 - **Instantiation:** This backbone is instantiated using a pretrained InternVL3-1B model. InternVL3-1B is trained under a native multimodal paradigm, optimizing text corpora and diverse multimodal data jointly, which ensures strong cross-modal alignment.
 - **Components:** Its visual encoder is InternViT-300M, and its language branch leverages Qwen2.5-0.5B. Only the first 14 layers of the language branch are retained, as intermediate layers are found to have stronger cross-modal alignment relevant for visuomotor control.
 - **Output:** Hidden representations (Zt) serve as the 2D vision-language representations for spatial enhancement.

- **Spatial Enhancement Module (SEM):**
 - **Purpose:** To inject the implicit depth features (Dt) from IDEM into the 2D vision-language representations (Zt), thereby enriching them with spatial information without heavy fusion.
 - **Mechanism:** The SEM first transforms the implicit depth features (Dt) to align with the hidden space of the 2D vision-language representations. These transformed features are then averaged across tokens to create a compact global descriptor (gt). A lightweight modulation head (f_mod) predicts a pair of feature-wise modulation parameters, γ (scaling) and β (shifting), based on gt.
 - **Application:** These parameters are applied to the 2D vision-language representations (Zt) in a FiLM-style manner: ˆZt = γ ⊙ Zt + β (element-wise multiplication).
 - **Benefit:** This modulation approach is designed to preserve the original vision-language representations while subtly injecting complementary spatial cues, making the fusion efficient and lightweight.
 - **Output:** Spatially enhanced vision-language representations (ˆZt).

- **Action Expert:**
 - **Purpose:** To predict the future action sequence based on the spatially enhanced representations and the current robot state.
 - **Instantiation:** It is implemented as a conditional denoising policy using the flow-matching paradigm, specifically with a Diffusion Transformer.
 - **Training:** The module learns a time-dependent vector field that transforms noisy actions towards the ground-truth action sequence, conditioned on ˆZt and the robot state st. The training objective follows the flow-matching formulation (Equation 8).
 - **Inference:** During inference, the policy starts with a random action initialization and iteratively refines it through multiple denoising steps to produce the final executable robot actions.

#### 3.3. Progressive Alignment Training

To effectively align the implicit depth-enhanced spatial representations with downstream action learning, Evo-Depth employs a three-stage progressive alignment training strategy:

- **Stage 1 (Initial Alignment):** The SEM and Action Expert are optimized, while the IDEM and Vision-Language Backbone remain frozen. This stage establishes an initial functional alignment between the newly introduced fusion and action modules and the pretrained representation spaces.
- **Stage 2 (Spatial Alignment):** The IDEM is unfrozen and optimized jointly with the SEM and Action Expert, while the Vision-Language Backbone remains frozen. This stage allows the implicit depth representations to align more effectively with the 2D vision-language features, improving cross-module spatial consistency.
- **Stage 3 (Joint Optimization):** All modules (Vision-Language Backbone, IDEM, SEM, and Action Expert) are unfrozen and jointly optimized end-to-end. This final stage enables the entire model to achieve full feature coordination and task-specific adaptation, leveraging the established alignments from previous stages.

This progressive strategy aims to improve representation compatibility, facilitate deeper fusion between depth-aware and vision-language features, and ultimately enhance the overall action modeling capability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Evo-Depth was evaluated through extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world settings, demonstrating its effectiveness, robustness, and deployment efficiency.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

Evo-Depth was benchmarked against existing VLA models on four simulation platforms: Meta-World, VLA-Arena, LIBERO, and LIBERO-Plus.

- **Meta-World:** Evo-Depth achieved an average success rate of 84.4%, which is presented as state-of-the-art among the compared models. It demonstrated particularly strong performance on the medium (84.7%), hard (87.3%), and very hard (82.4%) task splits, indicating improved capabilities in scenarios requiring accurate localization and fine-grained object placement.
- **VLA-Arena:** The model attained a total success rate of 41.1%. It exhibited strong performance in specific challenging settings, notably in the distractor (52.7%) and long horizon (46.5%) task dimensions, suggesting enhanced robustness in cluttered environments and for multi-stage tasks.
- **LIBERO:** Evo-Depth achieved a high average success rate of 95.4%, outperforming other models on this benchmark. Its performance was balanced across different task categories (spatial, object, goal, long), with notable results in object (99.2%) and long (91.3%) categories.
- **LIBERO-Plus:** On this robustness benchmark, Evo-Depth demonstrated strong generalization, achieving an impressive overall average success rate of 69.6%. It performed well across diverse perturbation dimensions, including camera, robot, language, light, background, noise, and layout variations.

Overall, simulation results indicate that the implicit depth perception strategy effectively enhances VLA performance across a range of benchmarks, particularly in tasks requiring robust spatial perception, long-horizon execution, and generalization under distribution shifts.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

Evo-Depth was tested on a physical UFACTORY xArm6 robot across three real-world manipulation tasks: Orange Placement, Tennis Deposit, and Cup Stacking, which progressively increase in spatial complexity and demand for precise manipulation.

- **Task Performance:** Evo-Depth achieved the highest average success rate of 90%. Specifically, it obtained 95.0% for Orange Placement, 90.0% for Tennis Deposit, and 85.0% for Cup Stacking. The performance gap between Evo-Depth and baseline methods widened as task spatial complexity increased (e.g., Cup Stacking). This suggests that implicit depth cues provide more informative spatial representations crucial for accurate action generation in challenging real-world scenarios.
- **Inference Efficiency:** Evo-Depth demonstrated a favorable trade-off between efficiency and performance. With only 0.9 billion parameters and 3.2 GB GPU memory usage, it achieved an inference frequency of 12.3 Hz. This performance significantly surpassed other compared VLA models (OpenVLA-OFT, π0) in terms of efficiency while maintaining the highest success rates in real-world manipulation.

#### 4.3. Generalization Experiments (Real-World)

To assess real-world generalization, Evo-Depth was subjected to four types of environmental disturbances: background disturbance, unseen distractor objects, horizontal position disturbance, and height position disturbance.

- **Robustness to Disturbances:** Evo-Depth consistently achieved higher success rates than the baseline model (π0) across all four disturbance settings and all three real-world tasks. Its advantage was particularly significant in scenarios requiring accurate spatial reasoning, such as position perturbations and precise object-object interaction. These results indicate that implicit depth-aware representations enhance spatial perception and improve the robustness of VLA policies against real-world visual and spatial variations.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Implicit Depth Perception Analysis:** Removing the implicit depth perception module from Evo-Depth led to a performance decrease on LIBERO-Plus. The gains from implicit depth cues were particularly pronounced on camera, robot, and layout dimensions, confirming its effectiveness in handling viewpoint changes, embodiment variations, and object arrangement shifts.
- **Multi-Stage Fusion Training:** The proposed Progressive Alignment Training strategy, with its three-stage variant, achieved the best performance on the LIBERO Long benchmark, outperforming one-stage and two-stage training variants. This suggests that gradual cross-module alignment effectively integrates implicit depth cues into the VLA framework. Qualitative visualizations indicated that progressive alignment training resulted in more task-focused IDEM spatial attention.
- **Fusion Strategy Analysis:** Comparing different fusion strategies on LIBERO Long, the SEM-based FiLM-style modulation achieved the best performance. This approach outperformed direct concatenation and cross-attention, suggesting that injecting spatial information through feature-wise modulation is more effective for complementing 2D vision-language representations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Evo-Depth research presents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation by addressing critical limitations in current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Spatial Understanding without Hardware Additions:** Evo-Depth demonstrates that robust spatial perception, crucial for precise robotic manipulation, can be effectively achieved by implicitly extracting depth information from multi-view RGB images alone. This eliminates the need for additional, often expensive and complex, 3D sensing hardware (e.g., depth cameras, LiDAR). This finding is particularly impactful for reducing the cost and complexity of robotic systems, making advanced manipulation capabilities more accessible.
- **Lightweight and Deployment-Efficient VLA Framework:** The research introduces a VLA model that maintains high deployment efficiency. With a compact model size (0.9 billion parameters), low GPU memory usage (3.2 GB), and a high inference frequency (12.3 Hz), Evo-Depth is well-suited for real-time robotic deployment. This contrasts with existing implicit 3D-aware models that often rely on large geometry foundation models, leading to higher computational costs. This efficiency is critical for practical applications where latency and resource constraints are paramount.
- **Improved Robustness and Generalization:** Evo-Depth exhibits enhanced robustness to various visual and spatial disturbances, as demonstrated in real-world generalization experiments. Its ability to consistently outperform baselines under changes in background, distractors, and object positions indicates that the implicit depth-aware representations contribute to more stable and adaptable VLA policies. This improvement is vital for robots operating in unstructured or dynamic real-world environments.
- **Effective Cross-Modal Fusion and Training Strategy:** The introduction of the Spatial Enhancement Module (SEM) and the Progressive Alignment Training strategy provides a novel and effective method for integrating implicit depth features into vision-language representations. The FiLM-style modulation employed by SEM allows for lightweight injection of spatial cues while preserving the primary vision-language information. The multi-stage training strategy ensures that newly introduced spatial understanding capabilities are progressively and effectively aligned with downstream action learning, optimizing end-to-end performance.
- **Advancement in VLA Research:** By demonstrating that lightweight implicit depth enhancement is a practical and effective solution, Evo-Depth opens new avenues for future research in VLA models. It encourages further exploration into efficient spatial perception techniques that do not rely on explicit 3D data or large, heavy models, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable with resource-constrained robotic platforms.

**Potential Impact:**
In the long term, Evo-Depth's contributions could significantly benefit application scenarios requiring reliable and efficient robotic manipulation. This includes:
- **Laboratory Automation:** For tasks like precise liquid handling, object sorting, or intricate assembly, where accuracy and repeatability are critical.
- **Industrial Assistance:** In manufacturing or logistics, where robots need to handle varied objects, adapt to minor spatial changes, and operate in real-time.
- **Structured Manipulation Environments:** Any setting where robots interact with objects and require fine motor control and spatial awareness, such as in service robotics or assistive technologies.

The authors acknowledge the importance of deploying such systems with appropriate safety constraints and human oversight, as errors in perception or action prediction could lead to failed grasps or object damage. This consideration for broader impacts emphasizes responsible development and deployment of advanced robotic systems.
