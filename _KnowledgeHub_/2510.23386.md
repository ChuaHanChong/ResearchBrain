# Full-Dynamics Real-Time Nonlinear Model Predictive Control of Heavy-Duty Hydraulic Manipulator for Trajectory Tracking Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Tampere University developed and experimentally validated a real-time Nonlinear Model Predictive Control framework for heavy-duty hydraulic manipulators. This NMPC operates at a 1 kHz control frequency, strictly enforces physical and operational constraints, and achieves high-accuracy trajectory tracking on the full nonlinear system dynamics.

## Problem

- Heavy-duty hydraulic manipulators (HHMs) exhibit complex nonlinear dynamics, unmodeled uncertainties, and external disturbances, making precise real-time control challenging.
- Existing control methods for HHMs struggle to simultaneously address full nonlinear dynamics, ensure strict constraint satisfaction, and achieve high real-time control frequencies like 1 kHz.
- Violations of operational constraints (e.g., position, velocity, force limits) in powerful HHMs pose significant safety hazards and can cause severe system damage, an area underexplored for real-time control frameworks.

## Method

- A Nonlinear ==Model Predictive Control== (NMPC) framework is developed using a multiple-shooting strategy for trajectory tracking, explicitly handling a comprehensive set of physical and operational constraints.
- The NMPC achieves 1 kHz real-time execution by combining optimized symbolic C++ code generation (CASADI), a numerical solver (IPOPT) with warm-starting, high-frequency sensor updates, and limiting the solver to a single iteration per NMPC cycle.
- The framework integrates efficient UDP communication for sensor feedback and command streaming, providing joint references to a robust low-level Virtual Decomposition Control (VDC) controller.

## Results

- The NMPC framework successfully operated at a real-time control frequency of 1 kHz on a full-scale heavy-duty hydraulic manipulator, consistently computing optimal solutions within the 1 ms sensor sampling interval.
- High-accuracy trajectory tracking was demonstrated on both circumference and spiral paths, achieving average radial tracking errors of less than 5 mm and radial velocity errors less than **0.02** m/s.
- The system effectively managed and enforced explicit Cartesian and joint space constraints during a challenging spiral trajectory, adapting the robot's path to respect boundaries without violating any predefined limits.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Real-time NMPC can be successfully implemented on the full nonlinear dynamics of heavy-duty hydraulic manipulators at a 1 kHz control frequency by judiciously employing techniques like symbolic code generation, warm-starting, and minimal solver iterations per control cycle.
> - The NMPC framework demonstrates the ability to dynamically adapt the manipulator's trajectory to strictly respect a wide range of safety-critical constraints, including Cartesian workspace, joint positions, velocities, accelerations, and actuator force limits.
> - A robust low-level controller (VDC) is capable of accurately tracking the high-frequency, constraint-compliant references generated by the NMPC, enabling precise end-effector and joint-level movements in challenging industrial environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Heavy-duty hydraulic manipulators (HHMs) are integral to various demanding industries, including mining, agriculture, forestry, offshore operations, and field robotics, primarily due to their high power-to-weight ratio. These systems often operate under two modes: predefined motion paths or teleoperation. Effective execution in these modes necessitates robust control systems capable of high performance under diverse operational conditions.

However, HHMs present significant control challenges arising from their structural complexities, the nonlinear dynamics of their fluid power systems, inherent unmodeled uncertainties, and susceptibility to external disturbances. These challenges are amplified in real-time applications where computational efficiency and robustness are critical. Previous research has explored various control strategies, including PID control often augmented with observers or optimized using genetic algorithms, and reinforcement learning (RL) methods for automation and end-effector velocity control. Virtual Decomposition Control (VDC) has also demonstrated success in HHM control, achieving sub-centimeter precision, robust performance against disturbances, and computational efficiency suitable for real-time industrial deployment.

Despite advancements in HHM control, safety-aware control strategies, particularly those that explicitly manage physical constraints, remain an underexplored area. Given the substantial size and power of these machines, any violation of state or actuator constraints (e.g., velocity, force, or position limits) can result in severe system damage and pose significant safety hazards. Approaches to enforce constraints include designing specific control laws and using optimization-based control techniques like Model Predictive Control (MPC). MPC is notable for its ability to explicitly account for system dynamics and constraints. A key trade-off in MPC design is between linear and nonlinear formulations. Linear MPC offers computational efficiency but struggles with the complex nonlinear dynamics of hydraulic systems. In contrast, Nonlinear MPC (NMPC) provides higher fidelity and improved constraint satisfaction, but traditionally incurs high computational costs, making real-time deployment challenging.

Recent attempts to bridge this gap have involved NMPC on linearized models, data-driven NMPC with machine learning prediction models (operating at 50 Hz), or NMPC with sway compensation for forestry cranes (with average cycle times of 37.6 ms). These studies indicate the potential of NMPC for meeting HHM safety and performance requirements. However, these implementations often rely on simplified models or operate at relatively low frequencies, particularly when considering the high degrees of freedom and complex nonlinear dynamics of HHMs. Prior research has not successfully implemented NMPC based on the complete nonlinear dynamics of HHMs at a control frequency of 1 kHz, a frequency demonstrated on smaller-scale robotic platforms.

This research aims to address this gap by developing and experimentally validating a real-time NMPC framework for HHMs that fully incorporates their nonlinear dynamics and achieves a 1 kHz control frequency while ensuring strict constraint satisfaction. The work also positions itself as a foundational step for future integration of learning-based skill transfer methods in HHMs, envisioning the NMPC as a safety-critical interface between high-level learning algorithms and low-level actuation, thus enabling safe deployment of intelligent behaviors in industrial settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and experimentally validate a real-time Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) framework capable of ensuring constraint satisfaction throughout the full nonlinear dynamics of heavy-duty hydraulic manipulators (HHMs) while operating at a control frequency of 1 kHz for trajectory tracking tasks.

The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from several critical factors:

- **Demanding Industrial Applications:** HHMs are indispensable in industries requiring high power-to-weight ratios and the ability to handle heavy payloads beyond human physical limits. Their reliable and precise operation is crucial for these sectors.
- **Complex System Dynamics and Control Challenges:** HHMs possess intricate structural complexities, highly nonlinear actuator dynamics governed by fluid power systems, inherent unmodeled uncertainties, and susceptibility to external disturbances. These characteristics make their control challenging, particularly in real-time scenarios where robustness and computational efficiency are paramount.
- **Safety-Critical Operation and Constraint Violation Risks:** The substantial size and power of HHMs mean that any violation of operational constraints, such as limits on joint positions, velocities, accelerations, or actuator forces/torques, can lead to severe system damage, equipment failure, and significant safety hazards for personnel and property. Despite this, safety-aware control strategies that explicitly account for and enforce these physical and operational limits are underexplored in real-time control frameworks for HHMs.
- **Limitations of Existing Control Approaches:** While various control methods (e.g., PID, RL, VDC) have been applied to HHMs, a significant gap exists in simultaneously addressing the full nonlinear dynamics, ensuring strict constraint satisfaction, and achieving high real-time control frequencies (e.g., 1 kHz). Traditional NMPC, while offering high fidelity and constraint handling, has historically been too computationally intensive for real-time deployment on complex HHM systems. Previous NMPC implementations for HHMs have either relied on simplified system models or operated at lower control frequencies, failing to meet the combined requirements of full-dynamics consideration, high-frequency execution, and explicit constraint enforcement.
- **Enabling Future Autonomous Systems:** The development of a robust, real-time, and safety-aware NMPC framework is identified as a critical foundation for integrating advanced learning-based skill transfer methods into HHMs. By acting as a safety-critical interface, this NMPC can translate high-level commands from learning algorithms into constraint-compliant, executable trajectories, thereby facilitating the safe deployment of intelligent and autonomous behaviors in real-world and industrial environments. This bridges the gap between data-driven autonomy and the strict physical limitations inherent in large-scale hydraulic systems.

In summary, the research is motivated by the need for a high-performance, safety-critical control solution for HHMs that can manage their complex nonlinear dynamics and strict physical constraints in real-time, thereby improving operational safety, precision, and enabling the integration of advanced autonomous capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology centers on a Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) framework designed for heavy-duty hydraulic manipulators (HHMs). This framework integrates a multiple-shooting strategy, real-time sensor feedback, and a robust low-level controller to achieve high-accuracy trajectory tracking while adhering to physical and operational constraints at a 1 kHz control frequency.

**System Dynamics Formulation:**
The dynamics of a parallel-serial heavy-duty manipulator are expressed in generalized coordinates as an inverse dynamics problem:
$f_x = H(\theta)\ddot{\theta} + h(\theta, \dot{\theta})$
where $\theta$ is the configuration vector, $n$ is the number of degrees of freedom, $f_x$ represents generalized joint forces/torques, $H(\cdot)$ is the generalized inertia matrix, and $h(\cdot)$ contains gravitational, centrifugal, and nonlinear effects. For the multiple-shooting approach, the forward dynamics solution is required:
$\ddot{\theta} = H(\theta)^{-1}(f_x - h(\theta, \dot{\theta}))$
This is represented by the analytic function $\ddot{\theta} = FD(\theta, \dot{\theta}, f_x)$, derived using geometric recursive algorithms based on screw theory. The robot's Tool Center Point (TCP) pose $x(t)$ and twist $\dot{x}(t)$ are computed via forward kinematics from $\theta$ and $\dot{\theta}$.

**Optimal Control Problem (OCP) Transcription to Nonlinear Programming (NLP):**
The continuous-time, infinite-dimensional OCP for tracking a reference TCP trajectory $x_r(t)$ and $\dot{x}_r(t)$ with minimum effort is transcribed into a finite-dimensional NLP.
The time horizon is discretized into $N$ steps with increment $\Delta_t$. The decision variable $z$ concatenates joint forces, positions, and velocities over the horizon.
The objective function $J$ minimizes a combination of running costs $L_k$ and a terminal cost $L_N$. The running cost at time $t_k$ penalizes tracking deviations in Cartesian position and velocity and minimizes actuator effort:
$L_k(\cdot) = ||x_k - x_{r_k}||^2_{Q_x} + ||\dot{x}_k - \dot{x}_{r_k}||^2_{Q_{\dot{x}}} + ||f_{x_k}||^2_R$
where $Q_x$, $Q_{\dot{x}}$, and $R$ are weighting matrices. A similar terminal cost $L_N$ is applied at $t_N$.

The NLP is formulated as:
$\arg \min_z J$
subject to a set of constraints:
- **Initial State:** $\theta_0 = \theta_{msr}$ and $\dot{\theta}_0 = \dot{\theta}_{msr}$, anchoring the problem to current sensor measurements for closed-loop feedback.
- **Multiple-Shooting Matching:** $\theta_{k+1} = \hat{\theta}_{k+1}$ and $\dot{\theta}_{k+1} = \hat{\dot{\theta}}_{k+1}$, where $\hat{\theta}_{k+1}$ and $\hat{\dot{\theta}}_{k+1}$ are approximations obtained by numerically integrating the forward dynamics (e.g., Euler step, Runge Kutta, trapezoidal). This constraint ensures consistency between propagated states and decision variables.
- **Cartesian Space Constraints:** Bounds on TCP position ($x_{min} \leq x_k \leq x_{max}$) and velocity ($\dot{x}_{min} \leq \dot{x}_k \leq \dot{x}_{max}$).
- **Joint Space Constraints:** Bounds on joint position ($\theta_{min} \leq \theta_k \leq \theta_{max}$), velocity ($\dot{\theta}_{min} \leq \dot{\theta}_k \leq \dot{\theta}_{max}$), and acceleration ($\ddot{\theta}_{min} \leq \ddot{\theta}_k \leq \ddot{\theta}_{max}$).
- **Actuator Effort Constraints:** Bounds on generalized joint forces/torques ($f_{x_{min}} \leq f_{x_k} \leq f_{x_{max}}$).

**Real-Time NMPC Implementation:**
The framework employs a combination of techniques to achieve real-time execution at 1 kHz:
1. **Offline Setup:**
 - The NLP and its first and second partial derivatives are automatically generated as optimized symbolic C++ code using CASADI.
 - This symbolic code is integrated into a numerical nonlinear solver, IPOPT.
2. **Initialization (Warm-Start Preparation):**
 - Initial joint position ($\theta_{msr}$) and velocity ($\dot{\theta}_{msr}$) measurements are received from sensors.
 - A reference trajectory for the entire horizon is computed.
 - The solver is called with a small tolerance and a high number of iterations to obtain a refined optimal solution, which serves as the initial warm-start.
3. **Online RT NMPC Loop (1 kHz):**
 - **High-Frequency Sensor Feedback:** Current $\theta_{msr}$ and $\dot{\theta}_{msr}$ are read at 1 kHz via UDP.
 - **Efficient Data Management:** Custom C++ routines manage UDP buffers to prevent data saturation, avoid queues, and eliminate old information, ensuring deterministic algorithm execution time.
 - **Reference Trajectory Update:** The reference TCP position and velocity trajectories are updated in real-time based on the current time, leveraging their continuous function definition.
 - **Warm-Starting:** The solver is warm-started using a shifted version of the optimal solution from the previous NMPC iteration as the primal solution. This provides an informed initial guess, significantly reducing convergence time.
 - **Limited Iterations:** The NLP is solved with a fixed and very small number of solver iterations (e.g., one iteration of Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP)). This is justified by the relatively slow dynamics of HHMs, meaning the NLP does not change substantially between consecutive 1 ms NMPC iterations. The high sampling rate allows the overall optimization to occur over multiple NMPC cycles rather than within a single solver call.
 - **Command Streaming:** Optimal motion commands (joint position $\theta^*_1$ and velocity $\dot{\theta}^*_1$ for the next instant) are extracted from the NMPC solution and streamed via UDP to the low-level controller.
 - **Solution Shifting:** The current optimal decision variable $z^*$ is shifted to prepare the warm-start for the subsequent NMPC iteration.

**Low-Level Controller:**
A robust low-level controller based on Virtual Decomposition Control (VDC) is employed. This VDC controller, implemented on a Beckhoff CX2030 industrial PC, operates at a 1 ms sampling rate and commands voltage signals to the hydraulic valves based on the NMPC-generated joint references.

**Experimental Setup:**
Experiments are conducted on a full-scale heavy-duty hydraulic manipulator. The control hardware includes Bosch 4WRPEH10 electro-hydraulic servo valves, SICK AFS60 absolute encoders (18-bit resolution) for joint angles, and Unix 5000 pressure transmitters. The NMPC runs on an industrial-grade computing platform (Nuvo-9160GC with Intel Core i9, NVIDIA RTX 3050 GPU, 32 GB RAM, 1 TB SSD, Linux OS, C++ implementation). The IPOPT solver is configured to use BFGS for Hessian approximation, with a tolerance of $1e-6$ and a maximum of 1 iteration per NMPC step. Prediction horizons $N=3$ (circumference) and $N=8$ (spiral) with $\Delta_t=0.3$ and Euler integration are used. BOOST library is used for UDP communication management.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental validation of the proposed NMPC framework was conducted on a full-scale hydraulic manipulator using two distinct trajectory tracking tasks: a circumference path and a spiral-shaped trajectory with increasing tangential speed. The primary findings are detailed as follows:

**Real-Time Execution and Computational Performance:**
The NMPC framework successfully operated at a real-time control frequency of 1 kHz. This was achieved by leveraging optimized symbolic code generation (CASADI), integrating with a numeric solver (IPOPT), using warm-starting techniques, high-frequency sensor updates, efficient UDP buffer management, and limiting the solver to a single iteration of SQP per NMPC cycle. This configuration allowed the optimal solution to be computed within the 1 ms sensor sampling interval, fulfilling the deterministic computation time requirement for real-time control.

**Circumference Trajectory Tracking:**
The hydraulic robot's Tool Center Point (TCP) was controlled to follow a circumference with a 0.4 m radius and 0.5 m/s tangential velocity in the xy-plane.
- **Tracking Accuracy (NMPC optimized trajectory vs. reference):**
 - RMS Cartesian position error: 0.021258 m
 - RMS Cartesian velocity error: 0.009776 m/s
 - RMS Joint position error (Lift joint): 0.025287 rad
 - RMS Joint position error (Tilt joint): 0.033505 rad
 - RMS Joint velocity error (Lift joint): 0.035976 rad/s
 - RMS Joint velocity error (Tilt joint): 0.044383 rad/s
- **Low-Level Control Performance (VDC tracking NMPC output):**
 - Average radial tracking error: less than 5 mm.
 - Average radial velocity error: less than 0.02 m/s.
These results indicate that the NMPC framework generated constraint-compliant and accurate references, which the low-level VDC controller was able to track with high precision.

**Spiral Trajectory Tracking with Constraint Handling:**
A spiral-shaped trajectory with increasing tangential speed was used to evaluate the NMPC's ability to handle Cartesian and joint space constraints. Explicit limits were set for the working space (x, y), joint positions (Lift, Tilt), joint velocities, joint accelerations, and actuator forces.
- The TCP initially followed the spiral path.
- Upon reaching a Cartesian space limit along the x-axis, the NMPC adapted the trajectory to move along the boundary of that limit.
- Subsequently, as the spiral continued, joint limits were encountered. The NMPC framework effectively adjusted the Cartesian reference to respect these joint constraints, demonstrating a coordinated response to multiple constraint types.
- Ultimately, the TCP reached combined Cartesian and configurational limits in the plane. This demonstrated the NMPC's capability to enforce various constraints dynamically during an aggressive maneuver.
- **Tracking Accuracy (NMPC optimized trajectory vs. reference):**
 - RMS Cartesian position error: 0.006944 m
 - RMS Cartesian velocity error: 0.006474 m/s
 - RMS Joint position error (Lift joint): 0.019402 rad
 - RMS Joint position error (Tilt joint): 0.0250045 rad
 - RMS Joint velocity error (Lift joint): 0.021604 rad/s
 - RMS Joint velocity error (Tilt joint): 0.0246659 rad/s
- **Low-Level Control Performance (VDC tracking NMPC output):**
 - Average radial tracking error: less than 5 mm.
The NMPC effectively managed to keep the robot within the specified constraints while maintaining tracking performance.

In summary, the experiments demonstrated that the proposed NMPC framework is capable of high-accuracy trajectory tracking while strictly respecting a comprehensive set of safety-critical constraints in both joint and Cartesian spaces. Furthermore, it achieves these results with deterministic computation times compatible with a 1 kHz real-time control loop on a full-scale heavy-duty hydraulic manipulator.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the control of heavy-duty hydraulic manipulators (HHMs) with several notable implications and potential impacts:

- **Enabling Real-Time NMPC on Full Nonlinear Dynamics:** The successful implementation of NMPC on the complete nonlinear dynamics of HHMs at a 1 kHz control frequency represents an advancement. Previous efforts typically relied on simplified models or operated at significantly lower frequencies, which limited their applicability to complex, fast-changing scenarios. This work demonstrates that high-fidelity NMPC is now feasible for these powerful systems, overcoming prior computational barriers.

- **Enhanced Safety and Reliability:** By explicitly incorporating and enforcing a comprehensive set of physical and operational constraints—including joint position, velocity, acceleration, actuator force limits, and Cartesian workspace boundaries—the proposed NMPC framework contributes to enhanced safety and reliability. Violations of these constraints in HHMs can lead to severe equipment damage and safety hazards. The ability to guarantee constraint satisfaction, validated experimentally, directly mitigates these risks, making HHM operations safer and more predictable in demanding industrial environments.

- **Improved Trajectory Tracking and Performance:** The experimental results demonstrate high-accuracy trajectory tracking for both end-effector and joint-level movements, even under challenging conditions involving constraint activation. This precision, combined with the real-time execution, can lead to improved operational efficiency, higher quality task execution, and reduced wear and tear on machinery due to smoother, more controlled movements.

- **Foundation for Advanced Automation and Autonomy:** The NMPC framework is positioned as a safety-critical interface between high-level decision-making processes and low-level actuation. This is particularly impactful for the integration of learning-based skill transfer methods and other advanced autonomous capabilities. By ensuring that learned commands are translated into constraint-compliant and executable trajectories, the framework facilitates the safe and robust deployment of intelligent robotic behaviors in complex, unstructured, and industrial settings. This bridges a critical gap between data-driven intelligence and the physical limitations of large-scale hydraulic systems, paving the way for the next generation of intelligent, adaptive heavy machinery.

- **Technological Contribution to Optimization-Based Control:** The specific technical strategies employed—such as optimal symbolic code generation, solver warm-starting, high-frequency sensor updates with efficient buffer management, and solving the NLP with a minimal number of iterations—provide a blueprint for achieving real-time NMPC in other complex, high-DoF robotic systems with challenging dynamics. These techniques are critical for pushing the boundaries of what is computationally feasible for model predictive control in real-world applications.

- **Industrial Relevance:** The advancements are directly applicable to various heavy industries, including mining, agriculture, forestry, offshore operations, and construction. In these sectors, HHMs are critical for productivity, and improved control performance, safety, and the potential for increased autonomy can translate into substantial economic and operational benefits.
