# Optimal UGV-UAV Cooperative Partitioning and Inspection of Shortest Paths

> [!summary] Summary
> A cooperative framework for Unmanned Ground Vehicles (UGVs) and Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) optimizes path planning in environments with unknown road blockages, minimizing UGV travel time by strategically partitioning path inspection tasks. This approach reduced UGV travel times by an average of 17% compared to UGV-only methods, demonstrating improved efficiency in uncertain conditions.

## Problem

- UGVs face substantial delays and re-planning overhead when encountering unknown road blockages in uncertain environments, such as post-disaster scenarios.
- Traditional Canadian Traveller Problem (CTP) models often consider only single ground vehicles and simplified blockage detection at vertices, limiting their realism for modern robotic teams.
- Effectively leveraging the distinct capabilities of heterogeneous robots (e.g., UGV for transport, UAV for reconnaissance) in dynamic, uncertain environments remains a challenge.

## Method

- A **UGV-UAV cooperative framework** is developed where the UGV navigates a path, while the UAV inspects path segments for blockages and communicates findings in real-time.
- An ==O(m^2) algorithm== is introduced for optimally partitioning a candidate path of `m` edges into UGV and UAV inspection segments, minimizing the maximum time taken by either vehicle.
- UAV inspection of a path suffix is modeled as a ==minimum-weight T-join problem==, enabling efficient coverage of required edges while accounting for initial transit and ==deadheading costs==.

## Results

- Theoretical analysis established a tight competitive ratio of `2k - 1` for UGV-only navigation on `k` disjoint paths, which was reduced to `2 * (v_G / (v_A + v_G)) * k - 1` with UAV cooperation.
- Simulations on OpenStreetMap road networks demonstrated that the optimal partitioning strategy reduced average UGV travel times by **17%** (ranging from **1.15%** to **30.67%**) compared to a UGV-only approach.
- The proposed optimal partitioning algorithm consistently outperformed both the UGV-Only and a UAV Bi-directional inspection strategy across various city maps and scales.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - For a single UGV navigating `k` disjoint paths in an adversarial CTP setting, the worst-case competitive ratio is `2k-1`, quantifying the inherent cost of uncertainty.
> - UGV-UAV cooperation analytically improves the competitive ratio to ``v_A` * (v_G / (v_A + v_G)) * k - `k`` for `k` disjoint paths, highlighting how higher UAV speeds (`v_A`) reduce UGV costs.
> - The optimal deadheading strategy for a UAV inspecting a path suffix involves adding at most one additional edge to enable an Eulerian inspection walk.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the fields of robotic path planning, cooperative multi-robot systems, and online algorithms under uncertainty, specifically extending the Canadian Traveller Problem (CTP).

The **Canadian Traveller Problem (CTP)**, introduced by Papadimitriou and Yannakakis, models path planning in graphs where edge availability is unknown until physically traversed. Prior work on CTP often assumes a single ground vehicle and that edge status is revealed immediately upon reaching a vertex incident to that edge. Variants include stochastic CTP (with known edge blockage probabilities) and k-CTP (at most k blocked edges). This paper distinguishes itself from these by focusing on the original CTP without probabilistic information, where competitive ratios are typically analyzed. Furthermore, it introduces a more realistic sensor model where blockages are discovered precisely at the point of obstruction along an edge, rather than at a vertex.

The paper integrates principles of **UGV-UAV cooperation**, an area of robotics that has seen applications in target tracking, exploration, and search and rescue. While previous studies have explored UGV-UAV path planning in stochastic networks (e.g., Bhadoriya et al.) or CTP with remote sensing (e.g., Bnaya et al.), these often rely on probabilistic models or assumptions that edges remain passable, which may not hold in post-disaster scenarios. This work addresses the cooperative path planning problem for a UGV and a UAV in a CTP-like environment, where edges can be completely impassable and the traversability status is unknown a priori.

The subproblem of efficiently inspecting a path segment by a UAV relates to **arc routing and line coverage problems**. These classical formulations focus on covering all required edges in a graph with minimum cost. This research specializes this concept to a single simple path and partitions the inspection tasks between heterogeneous agents (UGV and UAV), proposing a polynomial-time solution for this specific configuration.

Overall, this work addresses a gap in the literature by combining the challenges of the original Canadian Traveller Problem with the capabilities of heterogeneous UGV-UAV teams, offering both theoretical analysis and practical algorithms for path planning in uncertain, real-world environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable a Unmanned Ground Vehicle (UGV) to navigate from a start vertex to a goal vertex in an environment with unknown road blockages, while minimizing the UGV's total travel time, by utilizing the assistance of an Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV).

The motivation for this work stems from the practical challenges encountered in emergency response, logistics, and disaster relief scenarios. In such environments, road networks can be significantly damaged by events like earthquakes, floods, or wildfires, leading to unknown and unpredictable road conditions. UGVs are critical for transporting supplies or rescuing individuals, while UAVs can provide rapid reconnaissance and situational awareness. A central challenge is to plan efficient and safe paths when the status of road segments (edges) is uncertain and blockages are only discovered when a vehicle physically encounters them.

Specifically, the paper outlines several key objectives:

1. **Generalize the Canadian Traveller Problem (CTP):** The authors aim to extend the original CTP formulation by:
 - Introducing a cooperative UGV-UAV team instead of a single ground vehicle.
 - Adopting a more realistic blockage detection model where obstructions are discovered at the specific damaged point along an edge, not just upon reaching an incident vertex.
2. **Analyze UGV-only performance:** To establish a baseline, the first objective is to provide a tight worst-case competitive ratio for a single UGV navigating between a start and a goal connected by `k` disjoint paths. This analysis helps quantify the inherent difficulty of the problem without assistance.
3. **Quantify the benefits of UGV-UAV cooperation:** A core objective is to analytically demonstrate how UAV assistance improves the UGV's performance in worst-case scenarios. This involves deriving an improved competitive ratio for UGV-UAV teams in graphs with `k` disjoint paths, considering the speed differential between the vehicles.
4. **Develop an optimal path partitioning strategy for general graphs:** For more complex, general road networks, the work aims to design an efficient algorithm that optimally divides the path inspection task between the UGV and UAV. This strategy must consider practical aspects such as the UAV's initial transit cost to reach its inspection area and any deadheading (non-inspection travel) required to complete its assigned segment.
5. **Validate the proposed strategy empirically:** The final objective is to evaluate the performance of the developed optimal partitioning algorithm using simulations on realistic road network datasets, comparing it against alternative strategies to demonstrate its efficacy in reducing UGV travel times.

The motivation is to overcome the limitations of single-agent CTP models and simple cooperative approaches by leveraging the distinct capabilities of UGVs and UAVs to navigate uncertain environments more efficiently and robustly, thereby enhancing response efforts in critical situations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a combination of theoretical analysis, problem decomposition, algorithmic design, and simulation-based evaluation.

**A. Problem Formulation and Assumptions:**
The core task is to minimize the UGV's total travel time from a start vertex `s` to a goal vertex `g` in a weighted undirected graph `G = (V, E, w)`, with UAV assistance.
- **UGV Motion:** Constrained to graph edges at constant speed `v_G`.
- **UAV Motion:** Flies in continuous 2D space at constant speed `v_A`, can deadhead (fly directly) between any two vertices, and inspects edges by flying along their full length.
- **Edge Status:** Unknown (open or blocked) until inspected.
 - **UGV Inspection:** If a UGV attempts a blocked edge `(u,v)`, it discovers the blockage at the obstruction point (distance `d` from `u`), then must return to `u` (cost `2d/v_G`).
 - **UAV Inspection:** Inspects full edge length. Detects blockage, communicates status to UGV, allowing UGV to avoid the blocked edge without physical traversal.
- **Adversary Model:** A standard CTP adversary determines which edges are blocked to maximize the UGV's travel cost.
- **Competitive Ratio:** Performance is measured by the competitive ratio `ρ(π) = sup_{I} (T_UGV(π, I) / (L*(I)/v_G))`, comparing the online strategy to the offline optimum.

**B. Problem Decomposition for General Graphs:**
For a candidate shortest path `P_i` from `s` to `g`, the problem is reduced into two subproblems:
1. **UGV-UAV Path Partitioning (Subproblem 1):** Choose a split vertex `u_j` on `P_i` to assign the prefix `(s, ..., u_j)` to the UGV and the suffix `(u_j, ..., g)` to the UAV. The objective is to minimize `max(T_UGV(j), T*_UAV(j))`, where `T_UGV(j)` is the UGV's inspection time for its prefix (including return time `τ_ret_G` to `s` if a previous path failed) and `T*_UAV(j)` is the minimum time for the UAV to inspect its suffix.
2. **UAV Inspection of a Subpath (Subproblem 2):** Given a path suffix `R = (v_0, ..., v_ℓ)` and the UAV's current position `x_A`, compute an optimal UAV plan to inspect all edges in `R` in minimum time. This includes initial transit, inspection, and deadheading costs.

**C. Worst-Case Analysis for Disjoint Paths (Simplifying Assumption):**
To make the problem tractable for analytical bounds, the authors first simplify the environment to `k` disjoint paths between `s` and `g`.
- **UGV-Only Case:** The "shortest-first" strategy is analyzed, where the UGV attempts paths in non-decreasing order of length. The adversary blocks `k-1` paths near `g`, forcing repeated backtracking. This analysis leads to a competitive ratio `ρ = 2k - 1`.
- **Cooperative UGV-UAV Case (Negligible UAV Costs):** Under the assumption of negligible UAV initial transit and deadheading costs, the analysis again uses a shortest-first strategy. The benefit of cooperation is that the UGV's wasted travel on a blocked path is reduced because the UAV shares the inspection load. The competitive ratio is derived as `ρ = 2 * (v_G / (v_A + v_G)) * k - 1`.

**D. Optimal UAV Inspection of a Path Suffix (Subproblem 2 - General Graphs):**
This subproblem focuses on minimizing `T_UAV = T_init + T_inspect + T_dead`.
- **Motion Graph for UAV:** The UAV's movement is modeled on a complete graph `G_A`, where edge weights `w_A(p,q)` are original graph weights if `(p,q)` is an edge, or Euclidean distance otherwise.
- **T-join Formulation for Minimum Deadheading:** To efficiently inspect all required edges `E_R` of the path suffix `R`, the UAV may need to deadhead. This is framed as a minimum-weight T-join problem, similar to the Chinese Postman Problem.
 - An Euler trail (path that traverses every edge exactly once) exists if exactly two vertices have odd degrees.
 - By carefully selecting a set `T` of vertices whose degree parity needs to be flipped, deadheading edges (a T-join `J`) are added to the required edges `E_R` to create an augmented graph `H'` that admits an Euler trail starting at a chosen `v_q` and ending at a chosen `v_j`. The minimum-weight T-join corresponds to the shortest path between these vertices.
- **Selecting Optimal Start Vertex for UAV:** The UAV's current position `x_A` and the structure of `R` influence the optimal start vertex `v_q` on `R` and the deadheading `D(v_q, γ)`. The authors analyze three cases for the UAV's starting point (`v_0`, `v_ℓ`, or an interior vertex `v_i`) and potential stopping points, showing that optimal deadheading involves adding at most one additional edge connecting `v_0`, `v_i`, or `v_ℓ` to establish an Eulerian trail.

**E. Optimal Partition Algorithm (Algorithm 1):**
This algorithm implements Subproblem 1 for general graphs:
1. Iterate `j` from `0` to `m` (number of edges in the current path `P`).
2. For each `j`, calculate `T_UGV(j)` based on prefix length and `v_G`.
3. For each `j`, calculate `T*_UAV(j)` by solving Subproblem 2 for the corresponding suffix `R = (u_j, ..., u_m)`, considering `x_A` and the optimal deadheading strategy.
4. Determine `T(j) = max(T_UGV(j), T*_UAV(j))`.
5. Select `j*` that minimizes `T(j)`.
- **Complexity:** The algorithm has a time complexity of `O(m^2)` for a path of `m` edges, as `T*_UAV(j)` takes `O(m)` time for each of the `m+1` possible splits.

**F. Experimental Evaluation:**
- **Datasets:** OpenStreetMap road networks from 50 major cities globally, at two scales (1kmx1km and 3kmx3km).
- **Instances:** 50 random start/goal/UAV positions per map, ensuring path existence.
- **Speeds:** `v_G = 20 m/s`, `v_A = 40 m/s`.
- **Blockage Model:** Blockages are assumed at the midpoint of an edge.
- **Strategies Compared:**
 1. **Full Observation:** Lower bound (perfect knowledge).
 2. **UGV Only:** Baseline, UGV replans after encountering blockage.
 3. **UAV Bi-directional:** UAV inspects backwards from path end.
 4. **Optimal Partition:** Proposed `O(m^2)` algorithm.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research produced both theoretical competitive ratio bounds and empirical validation results.

**A. Worst-Case Analysis for Disjoint Paths:**
- **UGV-Only Path Planning:** The analysis demonstrated that for a single UGV navigating `k` disjoint paths, using a shortest-first strategy, the worst-case competitive ratio is `ρ = 2k - 1`. This bound was proven to be tight, achieved when an adversary blocks `k-1` of the shortest paths near their respective goals, leaving only the longest path open.
- **UGV-UAV Cooperative Path Planning:** Under the simplifying assumption of negligible initial transit and deadheading costs for the UAV, and an adversarial strategy that forces full inspection of each attempted path, the competitive ratio for the UGV-UAV team was shown to improve to `ρ = 2 * (v_G / (v_A + v_G)) * k - 1`. This bound is also tight and indicates that the competitive ratio decreases as the UAV speed (`v_A`) increases relative to the UGV speed (`v_G`), demonstrating the analytical benefit of cooperation.

**B. Optimal UAV Inspection and Path Partitioning:**
- **Optimal UAV Inspection of a Subpath (Subproblem 2):** The study established an optimal polynomial-time algorithm for a UAV to inspect a given path suffix (required edges). This algorithm correctly accounts for the UAV's initial transit from its current position `x_A` and any necessary deadheading to cover all required edges. The critical insight is that the optimal deadheading strategy involves adding at most one edge to the path suffix to enable an Eulerian inspection walk, connecting either endpoints `(v_0, v_ℓ)`, a start vertex `v_i` to `v_0`, or `v_i` to `v_ℓ`.
- **Optimal UGV-UAV Path Partitioning (Subproblem 1):** An `O(m^2)` algorithm was developed that optimally determines the split point (`j*`) on a candidate path (`m` edges) to balance the UGV's prefix inspection time with the UAV's suffix inspection time (which includes initial transit and deadheading). This algorithm guarantees minimum `max` time for the inspection tasks.

**C. Simulation Results on OpenStreetMap Road Networks:**
The empirical evaluation on road networks from 50 major cities demonstrated the practical effectiveness of the proposed Optimal Partition strategy.
- **Performance Improvement:** The Optimal Partition strategy consistently outperformed the UGV-Only and UAV Bi-directional strategies in terms of average UGV travel time.
 - The proposed method reduced UGV travel times by an average of 17% compared to the UGV-Only strategy, with reductions ranging from 1.15% to 30.67% across various cities and map scales.
 - Compared to the UAV Bi-directional strategy, the Optimal Partition also showed improvements in UGV travel time.
- **Detailed City Results:** Tables II and III provide representative results for 10 cities, illustrating these improvements. For instance, on small maps, Lagos showed a reduction from 79.138s (UGV-Only) to 54.865s (Optimal Partition), and Istanbul from 82.987s to 63.808s. On large maps, Tokyo reduced from 201.017s to 152.556s, and Delhi from 184.308s to 151.179s.
- **Outlier Case:** Only one instance, a small map of Bogota, showed a slightly lower cost for the UGV-Only strategy (54.718 seconds) than the Optimal Partition strategy (56.883 seconds). The authors attribute this to the UGV-Only strategy avoiding potential waiting time incurred when the UAV must complete an edge traversal before the UGV can replan.

In summary, the research provides theoretical guarantees for improved performance with cooperation and validates these benefits through simulations, demonstrating a substantial reduction in UGV travel time in realistic, uncertain environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research has both theoretical and practical significance, with potential impacts on the design and deployment of robotic systems in complex, uncertain environments.

**A. Theoretical Contributions:**
- **Extension of the Canadian Traveller Problem:** The work significantly extends the classical CTP by introducing a multi-robot, heterogeneous team and a more realistic blockage detection model. This generalization broadens the applicability of CTP-like problems to modern robotics.
- **Tight Competitive Ratio Bounds:** The derivation of tight competitive ratio bounds for both UGV-only and UGV-UAV cooperative scenarios in graphs with disjoint paths provides a fundamental understanding of the inherent costs and benefits of cooperation under uncertainty. The explicit dependence of the competitive ratio on vehicle speeds (`v_G`, `v_A`) offers clear insights into how UAV capabilities translate into performance gains.
- **Optimal Subproblem Solutions:** The formulation of UAV inspection as a minimum-weight T-join problem and the subsequent development of an optimal solution is a notable contribution to arc routing and inspection path planning for heterogeneous robots.

**B. Practical Implications and Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Emergency Response and Disaster Relief:** The primary practical impact lies in improving the efficiency and safety of UGV operations in post-disaster scenarios where road networks are highly uncertain. By reducing UGV travel times by up to 30%, the proposed methods can accelerate critical tasks such as delivering supplies, assessing damage, or conducting search and rescue operations, potentially saving lives and resources.
- **Real-time Applicability:** The development of an `O(m^2)` optimal partitioning algorithm is crucial for practical implementation. Its polynomial time complexity makes it suitable for online, real-time path replanning as new information about blockages is acquired.
- **Foundation for Multi-Robot Coordination:** This work lays a foundation for more advanced multi-robot coordination strategies in uncertain environments. By providing a principled way to partition tasks between a UGV and a UAV, it enables more intelligent and adaptive resource allocation.
- **Guidance for System Design:** The analytical bounds and simulation results can inform the design and deployment of UGV-UAV teams, helping engineers understand the performance gains achievable with specific vehicle speed ratios and system architectures.

**C. Future Research Directions:**
The authors identify several promising avenues for future work, indicating the potential for further advancements in this area:
- **Scaling to Multiple Agents:** Extending the framework to teams with multiple UAVs and UGVs, potentially starting and finishing at different locations, would increase realism and complexity.
- **Distributed and Decentralized Control:** Exploring how multiple robots can coordinate and share information without a centralized planner is a critical challenge for large-scale deployments.
- **Integration of Real-world Constraints:** Incorporating practical limitations such as UAV battery life, recharge requirements, UGV fuel limits, and communication range constraints would move the models closer to real-world operational scenarios.

In conclusion, this research presents a robust and analytically sound approach to UGV-UAV cooperative path planning under uncertainty, offering significant improvements over traditional single-agent or naive cooperative strategies. Its contributions have the potential to enhance the effectiveness of robotic deployments in critical applications.
