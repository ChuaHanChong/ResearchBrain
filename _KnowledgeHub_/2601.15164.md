# V-CAGE: Context-Aware Generation and Verification for Scalable Long-Horizon Embodied Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> The V-CAGE framework generates high-fidelity synthetic data for long-horizon embodied tasks by combining LLM planning with VLM-guided verification and context-aware scene instantiation. This approach led to downstream policies achieving a 64.58% average success rate, a 17.72% improvement over baselines, and 100% success for top-performing models.

## Problem

- The scarcity of high-quality, large-scale demonstration data hinders the training of embodied AI for complex, long-horizon tasks.
- Existing synthetic data generation methods frequently produce geometrically inconsistent (physically implausible) and semantically misaligned ("silent failures") data.
- Effectively translating abstract, high-level natural language instructions into concrete, executable robotic actions poses a significant challenge.

## Method

- V-CAGE implements a closed-loop framework that integrates LLMs for high-level planning with VLM-guided rejection sampling for rigorous, step-by-step semantic verification of generated trajectories.
- A context-aware scene instantiation mechanism dynamically manages a "map of prohibited spatial areas" to prevent object interpenetration and unstable configurations during scene synthesis.
- The framework utilizes an LLM (Pangu) to decompose abstract instructions into parameterized motion primitives and employs a five-stage pipeline for asset retrieval, visual-conditioned rearrangement, scene refinement, and subtask code generation.

## Results

- Policies trained on V-CAGE data achieved a 64.58% average success rate across 35 manipulation tasks, representing a 17.72% improvement over the Vanilla baseline's 46.86%.
- The framework enabled policies to attain 100% success rates for both Top-5 and Top-10 performing checkpoints, demonstrating exceptional robustness and consistency in learning from the generated data.
- VLM-guided verification effectively pruned semantically incorrect trajectories, significantly reducing "silent failures" and producing a much "purer" training dataset compared to methods lacking such a filtering mechanism.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Rigorous, VLM-guided semantic verification is critical for filtering out "silent failures" in synthetic data, leading to significantly more robust and generalizable downstream robotic policies.
> - Dynamic, context-aware geometric scene instantiation ensures physical plausibility and executability, preventing simulator crashes and improving the realism of generated environments.
> - Treating data synthesis as an optimization and rejection sampling process, rather than open-loop generation, is crucial for achieving high data fidelity for complex, long-horizon tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The V-CAGE paper addresses a critical bottleneck in the advancement of embodied AI: the scarcity of high-quality, large-scale demonstration data for learning long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks. The broader research landscape is characterized by two major trends: the impressive capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in reasoning and planning, and the increasing reliance on synthetic data for robot learning due to the cost and complexity of real-world data collection.

This work directly builds upon and extends several active research lines:

- **LLMs for Robotic Planning and Control:** Many contemporary approaches leverage LLMs to translate high-level natural language instructions into executable robot plans. Papers like PaLM-SayCan, DELTA, and RoboHorizon demonstrate LLMs' utility in decomposing complex goals into sub-goals or skill sequences. V-CAGE incorporates an LLM (Pangu) for semantic instruction grounding, aligning with this trend but extending it by ensuring the *quality* of the generated plans through subsequent verification.
- **Generative Simulation for Robotic Data:** The paradigm of using LLMs and generative models to automatically construct simulation tasks and generate training data has gained significant traction. Works such as GenSim, GenSim2, RoboTwin, RoboTwin 2.0, and RoboGen illustrate efforts to scale data generation beyond manual engineering. These pipelines often follow an "open-loop generate-and-execute" approach, where success is primarily judged by the absence of execution errors. V-CAGE critically identifies and addresses a key limitation of these prior generative works: the lack of closed-loop verification and spatial awareness.
- **VLM-Driven Manipulation:** VLMs are increasingly being used to enable robots to understand and interact with their environments more intelligently. Projects like VILA and OmniManip focus on pre-training VLMs for strong reasoning in long-horizon tasks or developing generalist policies. MOKA utilizes VLMs for visual prompting and grounding high-level plans. V-CAGE innovatively positions VLMs not just as planners or perception modules, but as *rigorous, automated verifiers* within the data generation loop. This differentiates it from existing VLM applications that primarily focus on inference-time reasoning or policy learning, assuming the availability of reliable data.

V-CAGE distinguishes itself by tackling two fundamental "quality gaps" prevalent in naively generated synthetic data:
1. **Geometric Inconsistency:** Existing procedural generation methods often fail to account for the dynamic evolution of a cluttered workspace, leading to physically implausible object placements (interpenetration, unstable configurations). V-CAGE introduces a novel context-aware instantiation mechanism to mitigate this.
2. **Semantic Misalignment ("Silent Failures"):** Language-conditioned code generation can produce scripts that execute without runtime errors but fail to achieve the intended visual or semantic goal. Training on such "false positive" data degrades learning. V-CAGE directly confronts this by integrating a VLM-based semantic verification loop.

In essence, V-CAGE pushes the frontier of synthetic data generation for robotics by shifting the focus from merely generating data *at scale* to generating data *at scale with high fidelity and reliability*. It acknowledges that for complex, long-horizon tasks, data *quality* is paramount, and without robust verification, increased data quantity can even be detrimental due to noise. This work provides a crucial closed-loop paradigm that will likely influence future developments in scalable robot learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the V-CAGE framework is to enable the creation of highly robust, semantically aligned manipulation datasets at scale, thereby facilitating the training of embodied agents for complex, long-horizon tasks. This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Bridging the "Scaling Laws" Gap in Robotics:** While Large Language Models and Vision-Language Models have demonstrated impressive scaling laws in NLP and Vision, applying similar principles to robotics is hampered by the prohibitive cost and safety concerns associated with collecting large-scale, high-quality real-world robotic demonstration data. Synthetic data generation has emerged as a promising alternative, but it often suffers from quality issues.
2. **Addressing the "Quality Gap" in Synthetic Data:** The primary motivation stems from the inherent limitations of existing synthetic data generation pipelines, which often produce data that is either physically implausible or semantically incorrect. This "quality gap" manifests in two critical failure modes:
 - **Geometric Inconsistency:** Standard procedural generation methods frequently place objects without considering their physical interactions and the evolving workspace dynamics. This leads to unrealistic scenarios, such as object interpenetration, unstable arrangements, or configurations that are impossible for a robot to reach or manipulate, often causing physics simulator crashes. The motivation here is to ensure that synthetic scenes are physically plausible and functionally executable.
 - **Semantic Misalignment (Silent Failures):** A significant challenge arises when language-driven program generation produces code that, while syntactically correct and executable without runtime errors, fails to achieve the intended high-level semantic goal (e.g., a switch is not fully toggled, an object is placed *near* a target but not *on* it). Training on such "false positive" data injects noise into the learning process, hindering the agent's ability to learn accurate cause-and-effect relationships and leading to brittle policies. The motivation is to filter out these deceptive failures to ensure semantic correctness.
3. **Grounding Abstract Intent into Executable Actions:** Long-horizon tasks require a coherent decomposition of high-level goals (e.g., "get ready for work") into a sequence of low-level, executable action primitives. The framework aims to provide a robust mechanism for this hierarchical planning, ensuring that abstract instructions are effectively translated and grounded in the physical environment.
4. **Improving Downstream Policy Performance and Generalization:** Ultimately, the motivation is to generate synthetic datasets that significantly boost the success rate, robustness, and generalization capabilities of downstream policies trained on this data. By tackling the root causes of data quality issues, V-CAGE seeks to enable the development of more capable and reliable embodied agents.

In summary, V-CAGE is motivated by the need for scalable, high-quality robotic demonstration data, aiming to overcome the twin challenges of physical implausibility and semantic incorrectness in synthetic environments through a novel closed-loop generation and verification framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

V-CAGE (VLM-Guided Context-Aware Generation for Embodied Planning) is presented as a closed-loop framework designed to synthesize high-fidelity manipulation datasets. Its core insight lies in treating data synthesis as a rigorous optimization and rejection sampling process, aggressively pruning invalid trajectories to ensure only high-quality data contributes to the training buffer. The framework operates through three hierarchical modules and a detailed five-stage pipeline.

**A. Three Hierarchical Modules:**

1. **Semantic Instruction Grounding via LLMs (Planning Level):**
 - A Large Language Model, specifically Pangu (Chen et al., 2025a), serves as the high-level planner.
 - It decomposes abstract user instructions (e.g., "Get ready for work") into a structured sequence of executable subtasks (T = {T_1, T_2..., T_k}).
 - Each subtask is then mapped to a parameterized motion primitive (e.g., "turn switch," "place object"), effectively transforming the long-horizon problem into a series of manageable short-horizon grounding problems.

2. **Context-Aware Scene Instantiation (Geometric Level):**
 - This module addresses geometric inconsistencies during scene synthesis, particularly in cluttered environments.
 - It proposes a mechanism that dynamically maintains a "map of prohibited spatial areas" or "prohibited volumes" as objects are progressively placed within the scene.
 - By continuously updating this map, the system ensures that newly instantiated objects are positioned only in feasible, collision-free, and reachable regions, preventing interpenetration and unstable configurations that could lead to physics simulator failures. This effectively solves a dynamic packing problem.

3. **VLM-Guided Rejection Sampling (Verification Level):**
 - This crucial module tackles semantic misalignment and "silent failures."
 - A Vision-Language Model, specifically Gemini3 (Gemini Team, Google, 2025), is employed as a "visual critic."
 - After the execution of *each* subtask (T_i), the VLM evaluates the visual outcome by analyzing a post-execution image (I_img) in conjunction with the subtask description.
 - The VLM outputs a binary verification (ϕ_VLM(I_img, T_i) → {0, 1}). If the VLM determines that the subtask's semantic goal has *not* been met (ϕ_VLM = 0), the entire trajectory generation episode is immediately aborted, and the scene is reset or re-sampled.
 - Only trajectories where *every* subtask passes this rigorous visual verification (product of all ϕ_VLM = 1) are accepted into the final dataset, ensuring that the data is both physically plausible and semantically correct.

**B. Five-Stage Generative Scene and Task Synthesis Pipeline:**

To operationalize the above modules, V-CAGE employs a detailed five-stage pipeline:

1. **Language-Guided Asset Retrieval and Scene Planning:**
 - Given a high-level user prompt (e.g., "organize the breakfast table"), the LLM (Pangu) acts as a semantic planner.
 - It retrieves compatible object assets (O = {o_1..., o_n}) from an annotated database, considering object semantics and grasp affordances.
 - Concurrently, the LLM generates a detailed natural language scene description (D_scene) specifying spatial constraints and layout logic (e.g., "place the bread inside the wooden box").

2. **Visual-Conditioned Rearrangement:**
 - The selected objects are initially instantiated in the simulation using a randomized collision-free scattering, generating an initial top-down orthographic view (I_init).
 - This initial image, along with D_scene, is fed into an image-inpainting model (NanoBanana-Pro).
 - The model "hallucinates" a goal configuration image (I_goal) that visually respects the semantic spatial relations from D_scene while maintaining consistency with the object assets.

3. **Scene Refinement and Grounding:**
 - 2D pixel coordinates of objects are extracted from I_goal via template matching against I_init and then projected back into the 3D simulation space.
 - A Refine Agent then optimizes the final 6-DoF pose configuration (P*) for all objects. This optimization minimizes a cost function that penalizes object collisions (L_collision(P)) and deviations from the semantic description (λL_semantic(P, D_scene)), ensuring physical plausibility and correcting any artifacts from the image generation.

4. **Subtask Code Generation:**
 - A library of parameterized skill templates (e.g., `pick_and_place(obj, container)`) is utilized.
 - The LLM identifies actionable relationships within the refined scene (pairing objects with grasp annotations to containers with placement annotations) and instantiates executable Python code for each potential subtask (T_i).

5. **Simulation-Based Verification:**
 - This stage acts as an initial filter for the robustness of the generated subtask code itself. Each generated subtask (T_i) is executed in the physics simulator multiple times.
 - A success rate (SR(T_i)) is measured, and a subtask is committed to a subtask database only if its SR exceeds a robustness threshold (τ > 0.5). This ensures that the foundational motion primitives are reliably executable before they are strung together into full long-horizon trajectories, complementing the real-time VLM-guided verification during trajectory generation.

**C. Pangu-7B Supervised Fine-Tuning (Appendix):**
- The LLM (Pangu-7B) used for motion planning is fine-tuned via Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT).
- A high-quality SFT dataset of approximately 900 instruction-code pairs was created by augmenting 50 expert motion planning scripts from the RoboTwin benchmark using the DeepSeek model to generate functionally equivalent code with diverse structures.
- This fine-tuning process was crucial to adapt Pangu-7B to the specific API calls and physical constraints of the simulation environment, enabling it to generate correct and robust low-level control policies.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted for V-CAGE were designed to empirically validate the framework's effectiveness in generating high-quality synthetic data and its subsequent impact on downstream policy performance.

**A. Experimental Setup:**
- **Task Suite:** Evaluation was performed on 35 diverse manipulation tasks derived from the RoboTwin benchmark (Mu et al., 2025). These tasks varied from simple object rearrangement to intricate, multi-stage activities in cluttered environments (e.g., table setting, semantic sorting).
- **Comparison:** V-CAGE (the full proposed pipeline, incorporating context-aware geometric constraints and VLM-based semantic verification) was compared against a "Vanilla" baseline. The Vanilla baseline utilized the same LLM planner and code generation module but critically lacked the closed-loop VLM verification and dynamic context-aware instantiation. In the Vanilla setting, task success was determined solely by the absence of runtime execution errors (e.g., Python exceptions, physics simulator crashes), a common success proxy in prior open-loop generation methods.
- **Policy Training and Evaluation:** Diffusion-based manipulation policies were trained using datasets generated by both V-CAGE and the Vanilla baseline. Policy performance was assessed using three key metrics:
 - **Average Success Rate:** The overall success rate across all 35 tasks.
 - **Top-5 Success Rate:** The success rate of the top 5 performing checkpoints.
 - **Top-10 Success Rate:** The success rate of the top 10 performing checkpoints. These Top-K metrics gauge the reliability and consistency of the best-performing models.

**B. Quantitative Results:**
The experimental results clearly demonstrated the superior performance of policies trained on data generated by V-CAGE:

| METRIC | V-CAGE (OURS) | VANILLA |
| :-------------------- | :------------ | :-------- |
| AVERAGE SUCCESS RATE | 64.58% | 46.86% |
| TOP 5 SUCCESS RATE | 100.00% | 92.00% |
| TOP 10 SUCCESS RATE | 100.00% | 77.00% |

- **Significant Improvement in Average Success Rate:** V-CAGE achieved an Average Success Rate of 64.58%, representing a substantial increase of +17.72% over the Vanilla baseline's 46.86%. This direct comparison highlights the overall superior quality of the dataset produced by the V-CAGE framework.
- **Exceptional Robustness in Top-K Metrics:** The most striking difference was observed in the Top-K success rates. V-CAGE maintained a perfect 100% success rate for both Top-5 and Top-10 performing checkpoints. In contrast, while the Vanilla baseline achieved a respectable 92% for Top-5, its performance dropped significantly to 77% for Top-10.

**C. Critical Role of VLM Verification:**
The disparity in Top-K metrics provides crucial insight into the impact of VLM verification:
- The dramatic drop in the Vanilla baseline's Top-10 performance compared to its Top-5 indicates a lack of stability and a significant amount of "noisy" data in its training set.
- This noise stems from "silent failures," where generated code executes without system errors, but the actual semantic goal of the subtask is not achieved (e.g., an object slips, a switch is partially but not fully actuated). The Vanilla approach labels these as successes, injecting incorrect associations into the policy's learning process.
- V-CAGE's consistent 100% success rate across both Top-5 and Top-10 demonstrates that the VLM-guided rejection sampling effectively acts as a rigorous semantic filter. By pruning these "silent failures" before they enter the training buffer, the VLM ensures a much higher "purity" and fidelity of valid demonstrations. This allows downstream policies to learn more robust, causally correct, and generalizable behaviors without being confused by erroneous supervision.

In conclusion, the main findings unequivocally demonstrate that incorporating context-aware geometric constraints and, most critically, VLM-based semantic verification, dramatically improves the quality of synthetic training data, leading to significantly higher success rates and enhanced generalization for downstream robotic manipulation policies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The V-CAGE framework presents a significant advancement in the field of embodied AI and robotics, with a profound potential impact on how robotic agents are trained and deployed for complex tasks.

**A. Enhanced Data Fidelity for Robot Learning:**
The most immediate and critical significance of V-CAGE lies in its ability to generate synthetic datasets with "superior physical and semantic fidelity." By addressing the pervasive issues of geometric inconsistency and semantic misalignment ("silent failures"), V-CAGE moves beyond mere data *quantity* to emphasize data *quality*. This paradigm shift is crucial for long-horizon tasks, where errors can compound, and noisy training data can lead to brittle, unreliable policies. The demonstrated performance gains in downstream policies highlight that intelligent filtering and verification are more impactful than simply scaling up unverified data.

**B. Scalability and Accessibility for Robot Learning:**
V-CAGE provides a scalable solution to the long-standing problem of data scarcity in robotics. Real-world data collection is inherently expensive, time-consuming, and often unsafe. By offering a method to autonomously generate high-quality, verified synthetic data, V-CAGE democratizes access to large datasets, allowing researchers and developers to accelerate the training and iteration cycles for complex robotic skills without incurring prohibitive real-world costs and risks. This significantly lowers the barrier to entry for developing advanced robotic systems.

**C. Robustness and Generalization of Embodied Agents:**
The empirical results clearly show that policies trained on V-CAGE data exhibit significantly higher success rates and improved generalization across diverse and cluttered scenarios. This means robots trained with this methodology are more likely to perform reliably in varied real-world environments, which are inherently complex and dynamic. The ability to generalize to unseen tasks and cluttered settings is a hallmark of truly intelligent embodied agents, and V-CAGE directly contributes to achieving this.

**D. A New Paradigm for Generative Robotics:**
V-CAGE introduces a novel "closed-loop" framework for synthetic data generation, moving beyond the traditional "open-loop generate-and-execute" approaches. The integration of context-aware spatial reasoning and VLM-guided semantic verification directly into the generation pipeline establishes a new best practice for creating reliable training data. This framework can serve as a blueprint for future generative simulation systems, inspiring further research into more sophisticated validation mechanisms.

**E. Advancing LLM/VLM Integration in Robotics:**
The work innovatively leverages state-of-the-art LLMs (Pangu) for hierarchical planning and VLMs (Gemini3) for rigorous, step-by-step visual verification. This demonstrates a powerful synergistic approach where foundation models are not just used for high-level reasoning but also for meticulous ground-truth validation, ensuring that the generated data accurately reflects human intent and physical reality. This successful integration paves the way for more intelligent and self-correcting autonomous data generation systems.

**F. Future Directions and Broader Implications:**
The paper acknowledges limitations, such as the computational expense of rejection sampling for very complex tasks. This opens avenues for future research into more efficient verification strategies, like using VLMs to provide dense reward signals for in-the-loop trajectory correction rather than discarding entire failed episodes. Extending V-CAGE to handle dynamic interactions, deformable objects, or fluids would further broaden its applicability. Ultimately, the impact of V-CAGE extends to various domains requiring robust robotic manipulation, from manufacturing and logistics to assistive robotics and exploration. By fostering the development of more capable and reliable embodied agents, V-CAGE contributes to a future where robots can perform increasingly intricate tasks autonomously and safely in human-centric environments.
