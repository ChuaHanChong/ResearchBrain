# Motion-Omni: End-to-End Joint Speech and Full-Body Motion for Spoken Dialogue

> [!summary] Summary
> Motion-Omni is an end-to-end spoken dialogue framework whose model natively emits full-body co-speech motion (face, hands, upper and lower body) directly from the Speech Generator's hidden states instead of from decoded audio. Joint speech-and-motion training over 422,856 teacher pseudo-labeled pairs (1,402 hours) lets Motion-Omni-Q7 match a teacher cascade's motion quality while responding 5.4 times faster at 2.62% word error rate.

## Problem

- Spoken dialogue models and co-speech motion models are separate families, so the standard cascade runs a second full inference pass over finished audio.
- In that cascade no motion objective can update speech or dialogue parameters, and prior joint systems freeze the speech pathway or cover only the face.
- No corpus pairs open-ended responses in a single consistent target voice with full-body motion, and no public benchmark evaluates stochastic open-ended full-body spoken dialogue.

## Method

- Four-component framework (Speech Projector, LLM Backbone, Speech Generator, Part-Aware Motion Generator) with a ==dual-input interface==: motion decoders attend to speech hidden states as ==keys/values== and emitted speech-token embeddings as ==queries==.
- Four-stage curriculum (ASR, TTS, TTS-with-Motion, joint four-task mixture) co-adapts projector, LLM, Speech Generator and four part-specific decoders emitting 30 Hz ==VQ codes== under ==dimension-weighted cross-entropy==.
- ==Model-agnostic pseudo-labeling pipeline== runs a replaceable motion teacher over consistent-voice response audio, then a ==dual-metric score== (VQ reconstruction error plus beat correlation) ranks samples into quality quantiles.

## Results

- On SwDA-500 it reaches the best beat correlation (**7.59**) and diversity (**13.67**) among systems not running the teacher at inference, and the lowest teacher-referenced FGD (**3.03**).
- It completes speech plus motion in **4.32 s** (RTF **0.78**) versus **23.35 s** for the same-audio teacher cascade, a **5.4 times** speedup, with **2.62% WER** on Seed-TTS-Eval.
- Human raters prefer it over a same-audio EMAGE cascade (**45 wins, 10 ties, 20 losses**); freezing the speech pathway leaves motion loss plateaued and visibly misaligned.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Conditioning motion on the speech model's hidden states, not its rendered waveform, removes a whole inference stage while keeping motion quality nearly unchanged.
> - Cross-modal gradients must reach the speech pathway: freezing it for stability costs alignment, so co-adaptation is the mechanism, not an optimization detail.
> - Teacher pseudo-labeling buys scale and voice consistency but caps quality at the teacher, and unranked training diverges without a quality-quantile curriculum.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence seeks to develop conversational agents that can interact with humans in a natural and multimodal manner. This paper positions itself at the intersection of several rapidly advancing domains: spoken dialogue models (SDMs), co-speech motion generation, and integrated multimodal synthesis.

Traditionally, the generation of spoken responses and accompanying body motion by AI avatars has been approached through a cascaded pipeline. In this setup, a spoken dialogue model first generates a verbal response, which is then converted into audio. This audio is subsequently fed into a separate co-speech motion generation model to produce the corresponding movements. This cascaded approach has been the standard baseline, producing outputs from models such as OpenAI's GPT-4o, LLaMA-Omni, GLM-4-Voice for speech, and TalkShow, EMAGE, or LOM for motion generation when given audio input.

The primary limitation of this cascaded structure is a lack of joint optimization and increased latency. Since motion generation occurs in a separate, subsequent inference pass after speech is finalized, there is no direct mechanism for motion objectives to influence or co-adapt the parameters of the speech or dialogue generation pathway. This separation restricts the potential for reciprocal transfer through shared states, where motion supervision could refine speech-generating states, and vice-versa.

Prior research has explored aspects of integrated speech and gesture synthesis, primarily for prescribed text scripts rather than open-ended dialogue. Models like Diff-TTSG and FastTalker jointly predict speech acoustics and 3D gesture from text, utilizing shared representations. In the domain of spoken dialogue, models such as SpeechGPT, LLaMA-Omni, and Moshi have focused on enhancing speech input and output capabilities for Large Language Models (LLMs) but do not natively produce body motion.

More recent work has begun to bridge this gap, introducing "spoken motion models." SOLAMI and U-Mind aim for joint prediction of speech and body/hand motion tokens, but some either generate facial animation post-hoc or do not explicitly detail how motion supervision affects speech generation states. Ex-Omni focuses on joint optimization for facial animation with an LLM and Speech Generator, but not full-body motion. ViBES combines a frozen speech expert with trainable face and body experts, which preserves the speech model but prevents motion gradients from updating it. MIBURI generates full-body gestures and facial expressions from streaming states but does not report motion-loss adaptation of the speech model.

Motion-Omni contributes to this landscape by proposing an end-to-end framework that natively integrates facial expression, hand, upper-body, and lower-body motion generation directly within a spoken dialogue model. This integration includes co-adaptation of the LLM, Speech Generator, and Motion Generator, addressing the joint optimization limitation. Furthermore, it introduces a scalable data construction pipeline and a public evaluation protocol, addressing known challenges in data availability and standardized assessment for open-ended, full-body spoken dialogue.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop an end-to-end framework, named Motion-Omni, that enables a spoken dialogue model (SDM) to natively generate both a spoken response and synchronized full-body co-speech motion, including facial expression, hand, upper-body, and lower-body movements, directly from the hidden states that produce the speech. This objective is pursued without compromising the quality of either the generated speech or the conversational dialogue abilities of the underlying language model.

The motivation for this research stems from inherent limitations observed in existing approaches to creating conversational avatars:

1. **Inefficiency of Cascaded Systems:** Current methods typically involve a two-stage cascade: first, an SDM generates speech, and then its audio is passed to a separate co-speech motion model. This sequential process necessitates a second full inference pass for motion after the audio is complete, leading to increased response latency. The researchers aim to remove this separate stage to achieve faster, more efficient responses.

2. **Absence of Joint Optimization:** In cascaded systems, the motion objective cannot update or influence the parameters of the speech or dialogue generation pathway. This prevents any reciprocal transfer of information or co-adaptation between the modalities. The motivation is to explore whether end-to-end joint optimization can improve the synchronization and naturalness of both speech and motion by allowing motion-relevant timing and contextual cues to be preserved in the states that generate speech, while speech supervision constrains those states to maintain spoken-dialogue ability.

3. **Data Scarcity for Joint Training:** Jointly training such a multimodal model requires a large-scale corpus of dialogue responses paired with consistent-voice speech and corresponding full-body motion. Existing audiovisual corpora often record diverse speakers and voices, which is not suitable for models aiming to produce speech in a consistent target voice. Therefore, a key motivation is to develop a scalable method for generating such supervision data.

4. **Lack of Standardized Evaluation:** There is a recognized absence of public benchmarks specifically designed for stochastic, open-ended full-body spoken dialogue. Existing benchmarks typically assume fixed, supplied speech or focus only on facial animation. This makes fair evaluation challenging, as it requires assessing a model's own valid but unpredictable spoken response accompanied by appropriate whole-body motion. A motivation is to establish a robust and reproducible evaluation protocol for this complex task.

By addressing these motivations, the authors aim to advance the development of more expressive, natural, and efficient multimodal conversational AI agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Motion-Omni is structured as an end-to-end spoken motion framework, comprising four distinct components designed to process user speech/text input and autoregressively generate synchronized spoken responses with full-body co-speech motion.

**System Architecture:**
1. **Speech Encoder:** Utilizes a frozen Whisper-large-v3 encoder to map 16 kHz waveforms into continuous representations.
2. **Speech Projector:** A two-layer MLP that concatenates every five consecutive frames from the Speech Encoder and projects them into the LLM embedding space, effectively downsampling the sequence.
3. **LLM Backbone:** An instantiated Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct model. For speech input, projected Whisper features replace a placeholder in the token sequence, allowing the LLM to process continuous speech segments and surrounding text.
4. **Speech Generator:** A Qwen2-style transformer, initialized from Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, which autoregressively emits GLM-4-Voice discrete speech units at 12.5 Hz. It incorporates a Token-as-Query Gated Fusion (TQGF) block, enabling speech token embeddings to query the LLM's contextualized hidden states.
5. **Motion Generator:** Consists of four parallel, part-specific decoders (face, hands, upper body, lower body) that emit LOM VQ codes at 30 Hz. It is conditioned on the Speech Generator’s last-layer hidden states (key/value) and on a learned, interpolated speech-token query embedding. This dual-input conditioning interface allows motion generation directly from speech-producing representations without requiring a rendered waveform.

**Training Curriculum:**
The model is trained through a four-stage progressive curriculum, designed to co-adapt the Speech Projector, LLM, Speech Generator, and Motion Generator while maintaining module distinctness and preserving spoken-dialogue capabilities. The Whisper speech encoder and LOM VQ-VAE are frozen throughout.
1. **Stage 1 (ASR):** Trains only the Speech Projector using ASR supervision. Projected Whisper features replace speech placeholders, and next-token cross-entropy is applied to transcript tokens, training the projector to produce LLM-decodable representations. The LLM is frozen.
2. **Stage 2 (TTS):** Trains the Speech Generator on TTS-style pairs, with the LLM remaining frozen. The LLM generates response representations, and the Speech Generator learns to produce corresponding discrete speech units.
3. **Stage 3 (TTS-with-Motion - TTSM):** The Motion Generator is attached and jointly trained with the Speech Generator on the TTSM corpus. The LLM and projector remain frozen. This stage employs a four-substage curriculum that progressively exposes the network to larger quantiles of quality-ranked data, warm-starting each substage from the previous one. Ablations confirmed that jointly updating the Speech Generator and Motion Generator is crucial for effective audio/motion alignment.
4. **Stage 4 (Joint Mixture):** The LLM backbone, Speech Projector, Speech Generator, and Motion Generator are simultaneously unfrozen. The model is optimized using a four-task mixture: ASR, TTS, Speech-to-Speech-with-Motion (S2SM), and Text-to-Text (T2T). The T2T component is included to preserve general text-response planning and multi-turn dialogue behavior during fine-tuning.

**Data Construction Pipeline:**
To address the data scarcity for joint training, a scalable, model-agnostic pseudo-labeling pipeline is introduced.
1. **Motion Supervision via Teacher:** A pre-trained co-speech motion generation teacher (LOM in this instance) is run on every response waveform from an open-ended speech-instruction corpus (InstructS2S-200K). Its four per-part VQ code streams (face, hand, upper, lower) serve as the motion target.
2. **Dual-Metric Quality Scoring and Curriculum:** Each generated sample is scored using two metrics:
 - **Weighted VQ-VAE Reconstruction Error (L_vq_1):** Measures how faithfully the frozen LOM VQ-VAE codebook represents the generated motion, flagging motions outside its expressive range or with noisy labels.
 - **Beat Correlation (BC):** Measures the rhythmic coupling between the generated motion and the speech, identifying motions poorly aligned with the audio.
 A combined quality score, `s(x) = α(1 − f_L_vq_1) + (1 − α) f_BC` (with α=0.5), drives the four-substage Stage 3 curriculum and selects the S2SM pool for Stage 4. This process yielded 422,856 quality-ranked speech-motion samples (1,402 hours).

**Evaluation Protocol:**
The paper introduces and releases **SwDA-500**, an evaluation set and protocol for stochastic open-ended full-body spoken dialogue.
- **Prompt Generation:** Uses external 500-prompt dialogue-text from the Switchboard Dialog Act Corpus (SwDA).
- **Matched Audio:** Controls response content and prosody across different motion systems.
- **Shared Renderer:** Ensures consistent visual output for all systems being compared.
- **Metrics:** Includes reference-aware automatic metrics (FGD, Diversity, BC, LSE-C, LSE-D, MSE, LVD), paired human evaluation, and latency measurements (Total Response Time, Real-Time Factor).

At inference, speech units are converted to mel spectrograms by a CosyVoice chunk-aware flow-matching decoder and then to waveforms by a HiFi-GAN-style vocoder. Motion codes are decoded by the frozen LOM VQ-VAE into SMPL-X body/hand parameters and FLAME facial-expression coefficients, which are then rendered into videos.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Motion-Omni-Q7, an instance of the framework using a Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct backbone, against various baselines yielded several key findings across speech quality, motion quality, and system efficiency.

**Spoken Dialogue Quality:**
- **VoiceBench Evaluation:** Motion-Omni-Q7 achieved an Overall score of 47.63 on VoiceBench, a nine-subset benchmark for open-ended dialogue, factual QA, reasoning, instruction-following, and safety. This score surpassed other omni-modal LLMs like LLaMA-Omni (41.12), Ex-Omni (43.57), Mini-Omni2 (33.49), and Moshi (29.51), indicating that the joint motion training did not degrade the model's core spoken dialogue abilities.
- **Text-to-Speech (WER):** On the Seed-TTS-Eval benchmark (English split), Motion-Omni-Q7 achieved a Word Error Rate (WER) of 2.62%. This was the lowest WER among the omni-modal LLMs compared, demonstrating strong speech intelligibility. Dedicated TTS systems generally reported lower WERs.
- **Speech Naturalness Proxy:** An automatic proxy for speech naturalness, UTMOSv2, on SwDA-500 generated audio, scored 3.77 for Motion-Omni-Q7. This places it above GLM-TTS, VoxCPM1.5, F5-TTS, and CosyVoice, and below Qwen3-TTS and CosyVoice 3, suggesting competitive naturalness in conversational contexts.

**Speech-to-Motion Quality (Automatic Evaluation on SwDA-500):**
Motion-Omni-Q7 was compared against controlled cascades using either its own audio (MO-audio) or Qwen2.5-Omni audio, combined with various audio-to-motion generators (MambaTalk, GestureLSM, EMAGE, LOM).
- **Overall Performance:** Motion-Omni-Q7 placed first or second on seven of the eight evaluated metrics (Diversity, Beat Correlation (BC), Bidirectional Beat Correlation (BC_bi), Lip-Sync Error Confidence (LSE-C), Lip-Sync Error Distance (LSE-D), Fréchet Gesture Distance (FGD), Mean Squared Error (MSE), and Landmark Velocity Difference (LVD)).
- **Reference-Free Motion Metrics:** Among systems that do not run the motion teacher (LOM) at inference time, Motion-Omni-Q7 achieved the highest beat correlation (BC = 7.59) and diversity (13.67). It also obtained the best scores on all facial and lip-sync metrics (LSE-C = 7.011, LSE-D = 7.023).
- **Teacher-Referenced Metrics:** Motion-Omni-Q7 recorded the lowest teacher-reference FGD (3.03), indicating a high fidelity to the distribution generated by the LOM teacher.
- The results indicate that the integrated Motion-Omni-Q7 system can match the motion quality of strong cascaded baselines while avoiding a separate audio-to-motion inference stage.

**Human Evaluation:**
A paired A/B/tie human preference study involving four annotators rated 25 clips for each of four comparison arms, focusing on rhythm, semantic alignment, and body naturalness.
- **Against MO-audio + EMAGE:** Motion-Omni-Q7 exhibited a pooled margin of +25 (45 wins, 10 ties, 20 losses), suggesting a preference for Motion-Omni-Q7's motion.
- **Against MO-audio + LOM (Teacher):** The comparison resulted in 25 wins, 27 ties, and 23 losses for Motion-Omni-Q7. The high number of ties suggests broad comparability in perceived motion quality with its teacher model.
- The human evaluation indicated that Motion-Omni-Q7's motion was often preferred over EMAGE and broadly comparable to LOM, partly attributed to Motion-Omni's training on dialogue-paired pseudo-motion, leading to a more conversational motion style compared to baselines trained on data that includes presentation-style behaviors.

**Latency:**
- Motion-Omni-Q7 completed full responses (speech + motion) in 4.32 seconds, achieving a Real-Time Factor (RTF) of 0.78, which is faster than real time.
- This performance represents a significant efficiency gain, being 5.4 times faster than the matched baseline MO-audio + LOM cascade (23.35 seconds), which uses the same speech model but invokes the motion model as a separate stage.

**Ablation Studies:**
- An ablation confirmed that co-adaptation of the Speech Generator and Motion Generator is necessary for achieving proper speech-motion alignment. When the Speech Generator was frozen during Stage 3, the motion loss plateaued at a higher level, and rendered motions showed visible misalignment with speech.
- It was also observed that conditioning the Motion Generator on Speech Generator context was more effective than direct LLM context when using the TQGF operator.

In summary, Motion-Omni-Q7 demonstrated proficiency in retaining spoken dialogue capabilities, generating intelligible speech, and producing high-quality, synchronized full-body motion with significantly reduced latency compared to cascaded approaches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Motion-Omni framework and its reference instance, Motion-Omni-Q7, represent a notable advancement in the development of multimodal conversational AI agents. The work's significance and potential impact span several areas:

1. **Advancement in End-to-End Multimodal Generation:** By natively integrating full-body motion generation (facial expressions, hands, upper, and lower body) directly into a spoken dialogue model, Motion-Omni addresses a fundamental limitation of traditional cascaded systems. This end-to-end approach, where motion is generated from the same hidden states producing speech, represents a step towards more holistic and biologically plausible human-AI interaction.

2. **Increased Efficiency and Responsiveness:** The removal of a separate audio-to-motion inference stage leads to a substantial reduction in response latency. Motion-Omni-Q7 achieves faster-than-real-time responses (RTF = 0.78) and is 5.4 times faster than comparable cascaded teacher models. This efficiency is critical for developing interactive and fluid conversational avatars, enabling more natural turn-taking and reducing user perception of delay.

3. **Improved Speech-Motion Synchronization and Naturalness:** The demonstrated necessity of jointly optimizing the LLM, Speech Generator, and Motion Generator for effective speech-motion alignment highlights the importance of co-adaptation in multimodal systems. This joint training enables reciprocal transfer through shared states, potentially leading to more coherent and natural expressive behaviors that reinforce the spoken content. The human evaluation results, indicating comparability with and even preference over some cascaded baselines, support the effectiveness of this approach.

4. **Contribution to Research Infrastructure:** The release of SwDA-500 and a comprehensive, reproducible evaluation protocol for stochastic open-ended full-body spoken dialogue is a significant contribution. This benchmark addresses a known gap in the research landscape, providing a standardized means for comparing future models, fostering transparent research, and accelerating progress in this complex domain.

5. **Scalable Data Generation Methodology:** The introduction of a model-agnostic pseudo-labeling pipeline, which generated 1,402 hours of quality-ranked supervision data, offers a practical solution to the scarcity of large-scale, consistent-voice audiovisual dialogue corpora. This methodology can be adopted and improved with stronger motion teachers, allowing for continuous data enrichment and model development without the need for extensive new human motion capture.

6. **Foundation for Future Research:** Motion-Omni's modular design, where components are swappable, sets a foundation for future work. This includes exploring stronger LLM backbones, more advanced motion teachers, continuous motion heads, and explicit conditioning on speaker identity or emotion. The identified limitations, such as the current focus on English and offline response generation, also open clear avenues for extending the framework to multilingual/multicultural contexts and real-time streaming interactions.

In practical applications, Motion-Omni could significantly enhance the realism and engagement of virtual assistants, digital humans in gaming or entertainment, educational avatars, and customer service interfaces. By enabling avatars to not only speak but also move in a natural and synchronized manner, it contributes to building more intuitive and human-like AI experiences.
