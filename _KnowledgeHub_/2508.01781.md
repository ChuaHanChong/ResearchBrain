# A comprehensive taxonomy of hallucinations in Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A detailed taxonomy of hallucinations in Large Language Models is presented, formally defining them as an inherent, inevitable characteristic of computable models and categorizing their various types, underlying causes, evaluation methods, and mitigation strategies, alongside considerations for human factors and monitoring.

## Problem

- Large Language Models frequently generate plausible yet factually incorrect or fabricated content, hindering their reliable deployment.
- The research landscape lacks a unified and comprehensive taxonomy for categorizing different types of LLM hallucinations.
- Inconsistent definitions and categorizations across literature impede comparative research and the development of standardized mitigation strategies.

## Method

- A ==systematic review and taxonomic analysis== approach is used to consolidate and structure existing knowledge on LLM hallucinations.
- The work establishes a ==formal definition of hallucination== and incorporates a theoretical framework positing its ==inherent inevitability== in computable LLMs.
- A ==multi-layered taxonomy== categorizes hallucinations based on ==core distinctions==, ==specific manifestations==, ==underlying causes==, and integrates ==human factors==.

## Results

- A comprehensive multi-layered taxonomy details core distinctions such as intrinsic vs. extrinsic and factuality vs. faithfulness hallucinations, along with specific manifestations.
- Underlying causes are categorized into data-related (e.g., quality, bias), model-related (e.g., auto-regressive nature, training issues), and prompt-related factors.
- Existing evaluation benchmarks and mitigation strategies, including RAG and guardrails, are surveyed, with a recommendation for hybrid, context-aware mitigation systems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Hallucination is formally defined as an inconsistency with computable ground truth and is an inherently inevitable characteristic of computable LLMs.
> - The inherent nature of hallucination mandates a shift in research focus from elimination to robust detection, management, and continuous human oversight.
> - Human cognitive biases and over-reliance on LLMs significantly influence the impact and perception of hallucinations, highlighting the need for user-centric design.
