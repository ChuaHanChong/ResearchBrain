# Perceptive Hierarchical-Task MPC for Sequential Mobile Manipulation in Unstructured Semi-Static Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> A Perceptive Hierarchical-Task Model Predictive Control (HTMPC) framework is presented that enables robust sequential mobile manipulation in unstructured, semi-static environments by tightly integrating online perception with whole-body control. This system utilizes Bayesian inference for object-level change detection to maintain an accurate 3D map and incorporates Control Barrier Function (CBF)-based safety constraints, demonstrating improved safety and reactivity in real-world experiments.

## Problem

- Most existing mobile manipulation planners assume static environments, relying on precomputed maps that become inaccurate in real-world scenarios with moving or new objects.
- Long-horizon sequential tasks in dynamic environments suffer from map-reality drift, leading to suboptimal or unsafe robot behaviors and reduced long-term autonomy.
- Traditional collision avoidance methods (e.g., Euclidean Distance Fields) can be less robust to perception limitations, potentially leading to higher approach speeds and collision risks.

## Method

- A Perceptive HT**==MPC==** framework integrates an online Perception and Mapping module with a Perceptive HTMPC Controller for closed-loop whole-body control.
- The Perception and Mapping module constructs an object-level 3D volumetric map, continuously updated via Bayesian inference for explicit change detection and maintaining object consistency scores.
- The Perceptive HTMPC Controller formulates whole-body motion planning as a lexicographic optimization problem, incorporating Control Barrier Function (CBF)-based safety constraints derived from the online 3D map for enhanced collision avoidance.

## Results

- Object-aware mapping with change detection eliminated 'phantom obstacles,' enabling shorter, more efficient paths compared to baseline voxel-level updates that resulted in suboptimal robot behaviors.
- CBF constraints consistently reduced robot speed near obstacles and achieved larger traverse clearance, leading to a higher percentage of collision-free cases (**87%** for 2-box, **60%** for 5-box scenarios) compared to EDF under perception limitations.
- The system demonstrated reactive collision avoidance in safety-critical scenarios, achieving a minimum Distance To Collision (DTC) for successful avoidance of **0.6** m for the end-effector and **1.48** m for the base, closely approaching theoretical limits.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit object-level change detection, using Bayesian inference to assess object stationarity, is crucial for maintaining artifact-free environment maps and enabling efficient path planning in semi-static environments.
> - Control Barrier Function (CBF)-based safety constraints offer superior safety performance over traditional EDF constraints by adaptively limiting approach speeds and enforcing larger clearances, particularly under perception uncertainties.
> - The integrated framework allows effective exploitation of kinematic redundancy for efficient sequential task execution (e.g., active perception during navigation) while ensuring safety against dynamic obstacles.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of mobile manipulation, specifically addressing the challenges of sequential tasks in environments that undergo changes over time. Mobile manipulation involves tasks requiring both navigation and physical interaction with objects, areas that have seen substantial progress with methods for generating coordinated whole-body motions. Existing approaches can be broadly categorized into heuristic-based methods and hierarchical optimization frameworks, such as Hierarchical-Task Model Predictive Control (HTMPC). These frameworks aim to manage the redundancy of mobile manipulators and ensure kinematic consistency during task transitions.

However, a significant limitation in much of the prior work across both streams is the assumption of static environments, often relying on precomputed maps. This assumption restricts their applicability in real-world scenarios, particularly for long-horizon sequential tasks common in warehousing or service robotics, where objects may be moved, introduced, or removed (semi-static changes). Such discrepancies between the assumed static map and the actual dynamic environment can render pre-generated plans suboptimal or unsafe, leading to failures or reduced efficiency.

The broader research landscape also includes advancements in object-aware mapping and perceptive control. Object-aware mapping systems have evolved from purely geometric representations (e.g., occupancy grids, Euclidean Distance Fields) to incorporating semantic and object-level information. While these systems enhance scene understanding, many still assume a stationary world, making them vulnerable to map corruption in changing environments. Strategies to address dynamic scenes include excluding dynamic objects, tracking dynamic objects, or explicitly targeting semi-static environments through methods like Panoptic Multi-TSDF or Bayesian consistency estimation. Concurrently, perceptive control aims to derive collision avoidance specifications online from onboard sensing, enabling reactive behaviors in unknown or changing environments. These methods range from mapless approaches that react to immediate sensor observations to map-based approaches that build consistent geometric representations for longer-horizon decision-making.

This work integrates these advancements by proposing a perceptive HTMPC framework that explicitly closes the loop between online environment understanding and whole-body control. It leverages Bayesian inference for object-level change detection to maintain a temporally accurate 3D map, which is then used to inform a lexicographic optimization problem for sequential task execution. This systematic integration, particularly its demonstration on real robots for sequential mobile manipulation in unstructured, semi-static environments, positions it as an effort to bridge the gap between theoretical capabilities of advanced motion planning and the practical demands of long-term autonomy in dynamic real-world settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust control framework that enables efficient and reliable sequential mobile manipulation in unstructured, semi-static environments. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations and aims to address specific limitations of existing approaches:

1. **Addressing the Static Environment Assumption:** A core motivation stems from the observation that most prior mobile manipulation planners, particularly for sequential tasks, assume static environments and rely on precomputed maps. This assumption is often invalid in real-world operational settings such as warehouses or service environments, where objects are frequently rearranged, introduced, or removed. The paper aims to overcome this limitation by developing a system that does not rely on prior maps and can continuously adapt to environmental changes.
2. **Enabling Long-Term Autonomous Operation:** Sequential mobile manipulation tasks, by their nature, often extend over significant periods. During these extended operations, the environment can undergo semi-static changes unobserved by the robot, leading to a drift between its internal map and reality. The motivation is to ensure that robots can maintain task completion successfully over long durations by continuously perceiving their surroundings, tracking these changes, and adapting their motion plans online.
3. **Maintaining Temporally Accurate Environmental Representation:** To facilitate adaptation, a key objective is to explicitly model and track object-level changes in the 3D environment. This involves developing a mechanism, leveraging Bayesian inference, to assess the "stationarity score" of objects and account for changes that might not be directly observed, thereby maintaining an up-to-date and consistent representation of the scene.
4. **Integrating Perception with Hierarchical Task Control:** The research aims to coherently embed this continuously updated environmental representation within a hierarchical task control framework, specifically HTMPC. This integration is designed to enable reliable execution of sequential tasks by ensuring that whole-body control decisions (like collision avoidance) are informed by the most current understanding of the environment, even as it evolves.
5. **Enhancing Reactivity and Efficiency in Unstructured Scenes:** The motivation includes improving the robot's reactivity to sudden environmental changes (e.g., unexpected obstacles) and maintaining efficiency in task execution. This is pursued by formulating collision avoidance as Control Barrier Function (CBF)-based safety constraints derived directly from the online-built 3D map, within the lexicographic optimization problem of HTMPC. This approach seeks to exploit the robot's kinematic redundancy for efficient task execution while guaranteeing safety.
6. **Validation in Realistic Settings:** A practical objective is to validate the proposed perceptive HTMPC framework through both simulated and real-robot experiments. These experiments are designed to demonstrate the system's ability to systematically account for moved and phantom obstacles, achieve higher efficiency and reactivity compared to baseline methods, and operate autonomously without reliance on prior maps or external infrastructure.

In summary, the motivation is to advance mobile manipulation beyond static-world assumptions, enabling truly adaptive and safe long-term autonomous operation in dynamic real-world environments by tightly coupling online perception with sophisticated whole-body motion planning and control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology centers on a **Perceptive Hierarchical-Task Model Predictive Control (HTMPC) framework** that establishes a closed-loop system between online environment understanding and whole-body control for sequential mobile manipulation tasks. The framework is composed of two primary modules: Perception and Mapping, and Perceptive HTMPC.

**A. Perception and Mapping Module:**
This module is responsible for estimating the robot's state and constructing an object-level 3D volumetric map of the scene, which is continuously updated via scene change detection.
1. **State Estimation:**
 - Arm joint angles ($q_{a,k}$) and velocities ($v_{a,k}$) are obtained from onboard joint encoders.
 - Base pose ($q_{b,k}$) and velocity ($v_{b,k}$) are estimated using a modified ORB-SLAM3. This modification incorporates wheel odometry alongside synchronized RGB-D frames from onboard cameras. Wheel odometry enhances robustness in featureless environments and extrapolates state estimates to compensate for camera latency and low frame rates.
2. **Object Mapping and Change Maintenance:**
 - Building on POCD, RGB-D frames are unprojected into the global frame, segmented into pointcloud observations ($Y$), and merged into an object library ($O$) through optimal association.
 - Each object $O_i$ in the library is modeled as a submap containing:
 - A 3D position and 1D heading in the global frame.
 - An Euclidean Distance Field (EDF) voxel grid submap ($M_i$).
 - A state distribution $p(l_i, v_i)$, which models the object's current geometric change ($l_i$) and its consistency ($v_i$, likelihood of no change). This distribution is parameterized as a product of a Gaussian for $l_i$ and a Beta distribution for $v_i$.
 - **Bayesian Update for Change Detection:** New observations are matched with the object library. For each matched object:
 - **Geometric Consistency ($\Delta_{i,k}$):** Computed as the average difference between the object's and observation's EDFs. Modeled as a Gaussian-Uniform mixture, where the Gaussian component corresponds to an inlier (unchanged object) and the uniform component to an outlier (moved object), weighted by $v_i$.
 - **Semantic Label ($s_{i,k}$):** A binary label inferred using a classification network, indicating whether an object is likely static or changing. This is modeled as a sample from a Bernoulli process controlled by $v_i$.
 - Matched observations are integrated, and object state distributions are propagated using an approximated, closed-form Bayesian update rule.
 - Unmatched observations are added as new objects. If an object's expected consistency ($E[v_i]$) falls below a predefined threshold, it is considered changed and is either removed or relocated in the map, ensuring an artifact-free representation.

**B. Perceptive HTMPC Controller:**
This module generates coordinated whole-body motion plans for sequential mobile manipulation tasks, explicitly handling semi-static map changes and incorporating safety constraints.
1. **HTMPC Formulation:**
 - The problem is framed as a lexicographic optimization problem, solved in a receding horizon fashion over a fixed time interval $[t_k, t_k + T]$.
 - The robot's state includes generalized coordinates ($q$) and velocities ($v$) for both base and arm.
 - For a sequence of tasks $[T_1, \ldots, T_L]$, the controller minimizes a sequence of cost functions $[J_1, J_2, \ldots, J_L]$, where each $J_l$ is the accumulated pose tracking error for task $T_l$.
 - **Lexicographic Optimization:** The problem is solved by iteratively optimizing for each task $T_l$. For iteration $l$, the current task $J_l$ is minimized, subject to maintaining optimality (or staying within a certain bound of the optimal error) for all preceding tasks $T_i$ ($i < l$). An auxiliary cost function ($J_{aux}$) is also included for control performance and numerical stability.
 - **System Dynamics:** The optimization is constrained by the robot's dynamics ($\dot{x} = A(q)x + Bu$), state and input limits ($x \in X, u \in U$), and the initial state ($x(t_k) = x_k$).
2. **Handling Semi-Static Map Changes:**
 - To mitigate infeasibility arising from sudden object appearances, all constraints (except control input and lexicographic optimality) are softened with slack variables. These slack variables are penalized in the cost function.
 - CBF safety constraints are explicitly integrated to steer the robot away from safety boundaries or correct it if it enters an unsafe region.

**C. Construction of CBF Constraints from 3D Map:**
Safety constraints are derived online from the tracked object library ($O$) to enable real-time collision avoidance.
1. **EDF Local Map Construction:** A local 3D EDF voxel grid ($M_{edf}$) centered at the robot is generated. For each object $O_i$ in $O$, its surface (zero-level set $\partial M_i$) is extracted, and $M_{edf}$ stores the minimum distance from any voxel to any object surface. The map is then truncated at a distance $\theta_{cutoff}$.
2. **CBF Constraints:**
 - The robot's body is approximated using a set of collision spheres, each centered at $P_{S_j}$ with radius $r_j$.
 - The safety function $h_j(x)$ for each sphere is defined as the interpolated distance from the sphere's surface to the nearest obstacle in $M_{edf}$, minus a desired safe clearance $\delta_{safe}$: $h_j(x) = \text{Interp3D}(M^\dagger_{edf})(P_{S_j}) - r_j - \delta_{safe}$.
 - The **CBF constraint** is formulated as $\dot{h}_j(x, u) \geq -\gamma h_j(x)$. Unlike traditional EDF constraints that focus on position, CBF constrains the rate at which the robot can approach obstacles.
 - The parameter $\gamma$ acts as a decay factor, allowing tuning of the robot's aggressiveness: a larger $\gamma$ permits faster approaches, while a smaller $\gamma$ enforces more conservative behaviors (reduced speed, larger traverse clearance), thereby enhancing robustness against perception delays and uncertainties.

The entire framework operates on onboard sensing and compute, without relying on prior maps or external infrastructure. The controller is implemented using ACADOS, running at 10 Hz with commands sent at 100 Hz, while state estimation (50 Hz) and dense mapping (5-10 Hz) run in parallel.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated the proposed perceptive HTMPC framework through extensive experiments in both simulation and on a real robot (Universal Robots UR10 arm on a Clearpath Robotics Ridgeback base), demonstrating its safety, reactivity, and efficiency in semi-static unstructured environments.

**A. Object-Aware vs Voxel-level Mapping:**
- **Experiment:** Robot performing a round-trip navigation. Obstacles were rearranged outside the robot's Field of View (FoV) during the return trip (Phase 2).
- **Comparison:** The proposed object-aware mapping with explicit change detection was compared against a baseline using only voxel-level updates with a static environment assumption (similar to Kimera).
- **Findings:** The baseline method, lacking explicit object-level change detection, failed to clear previously occupied voxels of relocated objects, resulting in persistent "phantom obstacles." This forced the robot to take longer, suboptimal paths. In contrast, the proposed framework actively updated object consistency scores (e.g., as shown in Fig. 5, consistency drops below a threshold), leading to the removal or relocation of changed objects. This resulted in an artifact-free map (Fig. 4), enabling the robot to plan shorter, more efficient paths.

**B. CBF vs EDF Safety Constraints:**
- **Experiment:** Whole-body navigation tasks avoiding obstacles, conducted in simulation (45 scenarios with varying speeds and clutterness) and on a real robot. Ablation studies compared CBF and EDF formulations with different parameter settings, using both ground truth (GT) and perceived maps.
- **Findings on Safety Behavior:**
 - CBF consistently reduced the robot's speed when near obstacles while maintaining nominal speed far away, promoting safer behaviors (Fig. 6, 7b).
 - CBF also resulted in larger traverse clearance compared to EDF under identical safety margin settings (Fig. 7a).
 - EDF, even with increased safety margins, permitted higher approach speeds near obstacles and operated with minimal clearance, increasing collision risks, especially under partial observations.
- **Findings on Collision Avoidance Performance (under Perception Limitations):**
 - Both methods performed well with complete GT maps. However, with incomplete (limited FoV) or inaccurate (sensor noise) perceived maps, CBF yielded a higher percentage of collision-free cases (Fig. 8b). CBF's larger clearance reduced exposure to both observed and unobserved parts of obstacles.
 - The CBF parameter $\gamma$ allowed tuning of safety performance: increasing $\gamma$ made CBF behavior converge towards EDF.
 - Excessive safety margins in EDF to improve collision avoidance sometimes led to unnecessary constraint activations, increasing acceleration in tight spaces and potentially compromising traversability (Fig. 9).
- **Real-World Robustness:** In experiments with severely limited perception range (Fig. 10), CBF enabled safe passage around obstacles where EDF, without an inflated safety margin, resulted in collisions. This was attributed to CBF's ability to limit approach speed and encourage larger clearances, making it more resilient to perception and control delays/inaccuracies.

**C. Perceptive-HTMPC for Reactive Whole-Body Navigation (Safety-Critical Scenarios):**
- **Experiment:** Robot navigating at its maximum speed (1m/s) towards a waypoint. Stacks of boxes were suddenly pushed and scattered in front of the robot, requiring rapid collision avoidance. Experiments were conducted with two (15 trials) and five boxes (15 trials) at varying distances to collision (DTC).
- **Findings:** The system demonstrated reactive capabilities, achieving a minimum DTC for successful avoidance of 0.6 m for the end-effector (EE) and 1.48 m for the base, closely approaching the theoretical minimum (approximately 0.5 m given system delays). The collision-free rate was 87% for the two-box scenario and 60% for the more challenging five-box scenario. Observed collisions were attributed to perception delays and map incompleteness, rendering physical avoidance impossible. The system adapted its prediction EE and base paths in real-time to avoid new obstacles (Fig. 11).

**D. Perceptive-HTMPC for Long-Term Sequential Tasks:**
- **Experiment:** Robot tasked with navigating a circular aisle three times, performing object receiving tasks at two locations. Boxes were dynamically rearranged during the robot's operation (Fig. 1).
- **Findings:** The perception module successfully observed, associated, and updated the state models of changed boxes, leading to their reconstruction at new locations. The robot reacted by deviating its base path and adjusting its arm (retracting or lifting) to ensure safe passage. This experiment also showcased HTMPC's ability to leverage kinematic redundancy for efficiency: for navigation, the hand camera looked ahead for earlier collision information, and for manipulation, tasks could be performed on a moving base.

Overall, the results indicate that the perceptive HTMPC framework effectively closes the perception-action loop, providing a robust solution for sequential mobile manipulation in environments characterized by semi-static changes, outperforming baselines in terms of map accuracy, safety, and reactivity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of mobile manipulation, particularly in extending the capabilities of robots to operate autonomously and reliably in dynamic, real-world environments.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**
1. **Systematic Integration of Perception and Control:** The most notable significance lies in systematically closing the perception-action loop for sequential mobile manipulation in unstructured, semi-static environments. This integration addresses a fundamental limitation of prior work that often assumes static environments, thereby paving the way for more robust and adaptable long-term autonomy.
2. **Enhanced Environmental Awareness through Object-Level Change Detection:** By leveraging a Bayesian inference framework for explicit object-level change detection, the proposed method maintains a temporally accurate 3D environment representation. This allows the robot to adapt to significant environmental shifts, such as object removal, introduction, or relocation, which are common in real-world scenarios but often ignored by traditional static mapping approaches. The elimination of "phantom obstacles" directly translates to more efficient and safer navigation.
3. **Robust Safety Guarantees with Perceptive CBF:** The integration of Control Barrier Function (CBF)-based safety constraints, derived online from the continuously updated 3D map, represents a significant advancement. Unlike simpler EDF-based constraints, CBF allows for better control over the robot's approach speed and traverse clearance around obstacles, offering a tunable trade-off between safety and efficiency. This is particularly crucial in scenarios with perception delays, noise, or occlusions, where more conservative (yet efficient) behaviors are essential for collision avoidance.
4. **Efficient Sequential Task Execution via HTMPC:** The use of a lexicographic optimization framework within HTMPC enables the robot to execute complex sequences of interleaved navigation and manipulation tasks while exploiting its kinematic redundancy. This ensures consistent motion generation during task transitions and allows for flexible task prioritization, such as using the end-effector camera for active perception during navigation.
5. **Practical Real-World Applicability:** The validation through both extensive simulations and real-robot experiments on a mobile manipulator demonstrates the practical viability of the framework. The ability to operate without relying on prior maps or external localization infrastructure makes the system highly autonomous and suitable for deployment in previously unknown or evolving environments.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Advancement in Robotics Autonomy:** This work moves mobile manipulation closer to truly autonomous operation in complex, human-centric environments, where changes are inherent.
- **Impact on Service and Industrial Robotics:** Applications in warehousing, logistics, and service robotics (e.g., tidying, delivery) can benefit directly. Robots can safely perform long-horizon tasks, adapting to reconfigured layouts or dynamic elements like moving pallets, without requiring manual map updates or intervention.
- **Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction:** As robots become more adept at navigating and manipulating in dynamic environments, they can operate more safely alongside humans, adapting to human movements or rearranged objects without collision.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework's modularity opens avenues for future work, such as integrating active perception strategies to minimize map uncertainties, or combining with language-based task planners for more intuitive and complex task execution. The systematic approach to closing the perception-action loop provides a strong foundation for developing next-generation intelligent robotic systems.

In conclusion, this research significantly contributes to bridging the gap between theoretical robotic capabilities and the demands of real-world dynamic environments, offering a robust and adaptive solution for sequential mobile manipulation that holds substantial promise for future autonomous applications.
