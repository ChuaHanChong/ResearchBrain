# Perceptive Hierarchical-Task MPC for Sequential Mobile Manipulation in Unstructured Semi-Static Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> A Perceptive Hierarchical-Task Model Predictive Control (HTMPC) framework is presented that enables robust sequential mobile manipulation in unstructured, semi-static environments by tightly integrating online perception with whole-body control. This system utilizes Bayesian inference for object-level change detection to maintain an accurate 3D map and incorporates Control Barrier Function (CBF)-based safety constraints, demonstrating improved safety and reactivity in real-world experiments.

## Problem

- Most existing mobile manipulation planners assume static environments, relying on precomputed maps that become inaccurate in real-world scenarios with moving or new objects.
- Long-horizon sequential tasks in dynamic environments suffer from map-reality drift, leading to suboptimal or unsafe robot behaviors and reduced long-term autonomy.
- Traditional collision avoidance methods (e.g., Euclidean Distance Fields) can be less robust to perception limitations, potentially leading to higher approach speeds and collision risks.

## Method

- A Perceptive HT**==MPC==** framework integrates an online Perception and Mapping module with a Perceptive HTMPC Controller for closed-loop whole-body control.
- The Perception and Mapping module constructs an object-level 3D volumetric map, continuously updated via Bayesian inference for explicit change detection and maintaining object consistency scores.
- The Perceptive HTMPC Controller formulates whole-body motion planning as a lexicographic optimization problem, incorporating Control Barrier Function (CBF)-based safety constraints derived from the online 3D map for enhanced collision avoidance.

## Results

- Object-aware mapping with change detection eliminated 'phantom obstacles,' enabling shorter, more efficient paths compared to baseline voxel-level updates that resulted in suboptimal robot behaviors.
- CBF constraints consistently reduced robot speed near obstacles and achieved larger traverse clearance, leading to a higher percentage of collision-free cases (**87%** for 2-box, **60%** for 5-box scenarios) compared to EDF under perception limitations.
- The system demonstrated reactive collision avoidance in safety-critical scenarios, achieving a minimum Distance To Collision (DTC) for successful avoidance of **0.6** m for the end-effector and **1.48** m for the base, closely approaching theoretical limits.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit object-level change detection, using Bayesian inference to assess object stationarity, is crucial for maintaining artifact-free environment maps and enabling efficient path planning in semi-static environments.
> - Control Barrier Function (CBF)-based safety constraints offer superior safety performance over traditional EDF constraints by adaptively limiting approach speeds and enforcing larger clearances, particularly under perception uncertainties.
> - The integrated framework allows effective exploitation of kinematic redundancy for efficient sequential task execution (e.g., active perception during navigation) while ensuring safety against dynamic obstacles.
