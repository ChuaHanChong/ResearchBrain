# A Survey on Test-Time Scaling in Large Language Models: What, How, Where, and How Well?

> [!summary] Summary
> A comprehensive survey introduces a unified, four-axis taxonomy to systematically organize the rapidly growing field of Test-Time Scaling (TTS) in Large Language Models (LLMs). This work provides a structured framework for classifying methods, offers practical guidelines for deployment, and identifies critical open challenges for future research in enhancing LLM performance during inference.

## Problem

- Traditional pre-training scaling of Large Language Models (LLMs) faces diminishing returns due to prohibitive resource intensity and finite high-quality data.
- Existing LLMs possess latent reasoning capabilities that are not fully elicited or utilized during standard inference.
- The burgeoning research into Test-Time Scaling (TTS) for LLMs lacked a unified, multi-dimensional framework to systematically organize and compare diverse methods.

## Method

- Introduced a novel ==four-axis taxonomy== ("what to scale," "how to scale," "where to scale," and "how well to scale") to provide a structured framework for Test-Time Scaling (TTS) methods.
- Systematically organized and analyzed a vast body of existing TTS literature by mapping methods to this multi-dimensional taxonomy.
- Distilled practical guidelines for researchers and practitioners on deploying TTS, including task applicability, ==implementation pathways==, and efficiency improvements.

## Results

- Identified a clear evolutionary trajectory for Test-Time Scaling (TTS), progressing from structured inference (e.g., CoT prompting) to advanced autonomous reasoning through techniques like process reward models and reinforcement learning.
- Provided hands-on guidelines for practical TTS deployment, emphasizing its utility across a wide range of tasks, particularly complex reasoning, and outlining key considerations for efficiency and method integration.
- Articulated critical open challenges and future research opportunities, focusing on advancing the limits of different scaling types, enhancing theoretical understanding, optimizing for efficiency beyond raw accuracy, and generalizing TTS methods across diverse domains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Test-Time Scaling (TTS) is an emerging paradigm for enhancing LLM capabilities by allocating additional computation during inference, similar to System 2 thinking.
> - Different TTS techniques (e.g., parallel, sequential, hybrid, internal scaling) are often complementary, with frontier strategies involving integrated pipelines of methods.
> - The distinction between tuning-based and inference-based TTS is becoming less clear, as high-quality data generated from inference strategies is increasingly used for model fine-tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper contextualizes Test-Time Scaling (TTS) as a critical paradigm shift in the evolution of Large Language Models (LLMs). Historically, the primary driver for advancements in LLM capabilities has been "training-time scaling," involving the continuous increase of computational resources, data, and parameters during the pre-training phase (e.g., models like GPT-3). However, the authors note a diminishing enthusiasm for this approach due to its exorbitant resource intensity and the bounded availability of high-quality human-generated data.

In response to this slowdown, researchers have begun to pivot towards "test-time scaling," also referred to as "test-time computing." This new focus aims to maximize the real-world effectiveness of already-trained LLMs by eliciting their latent intelligence during the inference phase, rather than solely relying on pre-training scale. The core idea is that, similar to human cognition where deeper, more deliberate thinking leads to better outcomes (analogous to System 2 thinking in cognitive science), allocating additional computation *during inference* can significantly boost an LLM's problem-solving capabilities. Recent empirical evidence, particularly from models like OpenAI's o1 and DeepSeek-AI's R1, demonstrates that increasing inference-time compute can yield consistent performance improvements, exhibiting patterns akin to scaling laws observed in pre-training.

Despite the recent explosion of research in TTS, the authors identify a critical void: a comprehensive and systematically organized survey. Prior efforts have touched upon aspects of TTS (e.g., input modification, output verification, System 2 AI), but they often adopt specific lenses or trace developments chronologically. This paper aims to fill this gap by providing a unified, hierarchical framework that dissects TTS across multiple orthogonal dimensions, offering a structured perspective for both theoretical inquiry and practical deployment. By doing so, it positions itself as a foundational resource for understanding, comparing, and guiding future research in this rapidly evolving domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this survey stems from the evolving landscape of Large Language Model (LLM) development and the inherent limitations of traditional scaling paradigms. As the authors articulate, the once-unbridled enthusiasm for scaling LLMs during their pre-training phase has begun to wane. This is largely due to the prohibitive resource intensity (computational power, energy) required for further increases in data and parameters, coupled with the finite availability of high-quality human-generated data. This bottleneck necessitates a new approach to fully unlock the problem-solving capabilities already encoded within existing LLMs.

Inspired by human cognitive processes, where deeper, more deliberate thinking often leads to superior outcomes, researchers have begun to allocate additional computation during the inference or "test-time" phase. This "Test-Time Scaling" (TTS) has shown remarkable success in enhancing LLM performance across complex tasks, including specialized reasoning (e.g., mathematics, coding) and general applications (e.g., open-ended Q&A). The emergence of models like OpenAI's o1 and DeepSeek-AI's R1 further amplified interest in TTS, showcasing its potential as a key driver for advancing LLM reasoning and utility.

However, despite this surge in research activity, the field lacked a unified and systematic framework to synthesize the burgeoning insights, compare diverse techniques, and identify consistent trends in TTS. This critical gap hindered comprehensive understanding and organized progress.

To address these motivations, the paper sets out three core objectives:

1. **To Propose a Unified, Multi-Dimensional Taxonomy:** The central objective is to introduce a novel, four-axis taxonomy—"what to scale," "how to scale," "where to scale," and "how well to scale"—that provides a structured framework for classifying, comparing, and extending TTS methods. This taxonomy aims to offer a fine-grained, decomposition-based understanding of the field.
2. **To Systematically Organize Literature and Provide Pragmatic Analysis:** Utilizing the proposed taxonomy, the survey aims to extensively review the existing TTS landscape, analyze representative methods, and distill practical guidelines for both research application and real-world deployment. This involves mapping individual contributions within the broader TTS ecosystem.
3. **To Identify Challenges, Offer Insights, and Outline Forward Directions:** Building upon the organized perspective provided by the taxonomy, the paper seeks to uncover critical open challenges in TTS (such as advancing scalability and clarifying the functional essence of techniques) and to outline promising future research directions. This structured approach facilitates more targeted and impactful advancements in the field.

In essence, the paper's motivation is to bring order and clarity to a rapidly expanding but fragmented research area, thereby accelerating the development and practical application of more intelligent and efficient LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a comprehensive survey paper, the methodology of this research revolves around the development and application of a novel, unified framework to systematically organize and analyze the burgeoning field of Test-Time Scaling (TTS) in Large Language Models (LLMs). The core of their approach is the introduction of a hierarchical taxonomy structured along four fundamental dimensions: "What to Scale," "How to Scale," "Where to Scale," and "How Well to Scale."

The authors meticulously apply this four-axis taxonomy to existing literature, decomposing and mapping representative works to highlight their unique contributions, trade-offs, and developmental trajectories.

**The Four-Axis Taxonomy:**

1. **What to Scale:** This dimension addresses the specific form or aspect of LLM inference that is expanded or adjusted to enhance performance. It categorizes existing TTS methods based on their fundamental approach to increasing computation:
 - **Parallel Scaling:** Involves generating multiple outputs concurrently and then aggregating them into a final answer (e.g., Self-Consistency, Multi-Agents).
 - **Sequential Scaling:** Explicitly directs later computations based on intermediate steps, iteratively refining a solution (e.g., Self-Refine, ReAct).
 - **Hybrid Scaling:** Combines the benefits of both parallel and sequential approaches, often mimicking human problem-solving strategies of divergent and convergent thinking (e.g., Tree-of-Thoughts, Graph-of-Thoughts, Monte Carlo Tree Search).
 - **Internal Scaling:** Elicits the model to autonomously determine computation allocation during testing within its internal parameters, often through fine-tuning on multi-step reasoning tasks (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, OpenAI-o1).

2. **How to Scale:** This dimension details the algorithmic and technical mechanisms used to implement TTS. It distinguishes between approaches that involve offline parameter adjustments and those that dynamically adjust computation during deployment:
 - **Tuning-based Approaches:** Involve adjusting the model's parameters offline to activate its test-time capabilities.
 - **Supervised Finetuning (SFT):** Training LLMs on synthetic or distilled long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) examples to internalize structured reasoning patterns (e.g., Imitation, Distillation, Warmup strategies).
 - **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Updating the policy model based on feedback from a reward model on inference tasks, encouraging autonomous CoT generation (categorized into reward model-free and reward model-based methods).
 - **Inference-based Approaches:** Dynamically adjust computation during model deployment. These are further broken down into four essential components often used in combination:
 - **Stimulation:** Encouraging the model to generate longer or multiple candidate outputs (e.g., Prompt Strategy, Decode Strategy, Latent Strategy, Self-Repetition, Mixture-of-Model).
 - **Verification:** Filtering or scoring outputs based on correctness or other criteria (e.g., Outcome Verification, Process Verification).
 - **Search:** Systematically exploring the sample space of potential solutions (e.g., Tree Search, Graph Search, MCTS, Beam Search).
 - **Aggregation:** Consolidating multiple outputs into a final decision (e.g., Selection, Fusion techniques like Majority Voting or Weighted Best-of-N).

3. **Where to Scale:** This dimension covers the diverse application scenarios, tasks, and datasets where TTS techniques are applied and demonstrate utility. It categorizes them into:
 - **Reasoning-intensive Tasks:** Requiring structured, explicit, multi-step logic (e.g., Mathematical Reasoning, Programming & Code Generation, Game Playing, Scientific Reasoning, Medical Reasoning).
 - **Agentic Tasks:** Involving interactive environments, complex planning, and tool utilization (e.g., WebShop, WebArena).
 - **Other Tasks:** Broader applications including General purpose, Open-Ended, Knowledge-intensive, Evaluation, and Multi-Modal tasks.

4. **How Well to Scale:** This dimension focuses on the metrics used to evaluate the effectiveness of TTS methods, spanning performance, efficiency, controllability, and scalability:
 - **Performance:** Measures correctness (e.g., Pass@1, Pass@k, Cons@k, Arena-based Evaluation, Task-Specific Metrics like Elo Rating).
 - **Efficiency:** Quantifies computational cost and reasoning quality (e.g., Token Cost, FLOPs-based analysis, KV Cache size, and novel reasoning efficiency metrics like Underthinking Score, Outcome Efficiency, Process Efficiency).
 - **Controllability:** Assesses adherence to pre-defined resource constraints or output targets (e.g., Control Metric, Length Deviation Metrics, k-ε Controllability).
 - **Scalability:** Measures how effectively performance improves with increased compute (e.g., Scaling Metric, Scaling Curves).

Beyond this structured classification, the methodology includes distilling major developmental trends, offering hands-on guidelines for practical deployment, and identifying persistent open challenges along with promising future research directions, all grounded in the multi-dimensional taxonomy. The authors also maintain a public repository to continuously update the taxonomy, ensuring its evolving relevance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey's comprehensive analysis yields several key findings, tracing the evolution of Test-Time Scaling (TTS) and outlining its current state, practical implications, and future directions.

**Developmental Trajectories and Trends:**
The paper observes a clear evolutionary path for TTS. Early efforts (2022-2023) primarily focused on structured inference to guide LLMs towards more complex solutions, exemplified by Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting and techniques like Self-Consistency and Self-Refine. By 2024, the field progressed with methods like Process Reward Models (PRM) and Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS), which enabled automatic supervision of intricate reasoning trajectories. This, in turn, generated richly annotated data that could be used for fine-tuning LLMs, further enhancing TTS performance. More recently (late 2024-early 2025), approaches like OpenAI's o1 and DeepSeek-AI's R1 demonstrated that pure Reinforcement Learning (RL) can also elicit comprehensive and logically sound reasoning capabilities within LLMs.

A crucial insight is that these techniques are not mutually exclusive but rather complementary. For instance, DeepSeek-R1, an RL-trained model, still benefits from Supervised Finetuning (SFT) based on rejection sampling as a "warmup" step. This suggests that achieving more powerful and robust TTS often requires systematically integrating various methods. The authors also highlight a trend towards focusing on "optimal-scaling solutions," indicating that researchers are moving beyond naive scaling to consider efficiency and effectiveness trade-offs. Furthermore, the boundary between inference-based and tuning-based approaches is blurring, with high-quality data generated from inference-based strategies increasingly being used for model tuning.

**Hands-on Guidelines for Practical Deployment:**
The survey provides practical advice for researchers and practitioners looking to implement TTS:
- **Task Applicability:** TTS can enhance performance across "almost any task," particularly excelling in reasoning-intensive areas like Olympiad-level mathematics and complex coding, but also showing promise in open-ended and long-horizon scenarios (e.g., medical, legal reasoning).
- **Implementation Pathways:** For quick implementation or minimal cost, users can either directly utilize models already trained with RL-based incentivization (to understand upper bounds) or start with deliberate inference-time reasoning procedures (for a baseline). These can then be refined by imitating complex reasoning trajectories.
- **Integration is Key:** Frontier-level TTS strategies involve seamlessly integrating different pipelines (e.g., SFT for initialization, RL for sharpening, structured inference).
- **Training-time Impact:** Training-time choices (SFT, RL) significantly influence the effectiveness ceiling of TTS methods by providing strong reasoning priors or incentivizing concise chains.
- **Efficiency Improvement:** Strategies for improving TTS efficiency include model-based approaches (fine-tuning for concise traces, distillation), output-based methods (early stopping based on verifier confidence), and prompt-based controls (token budgets, step limits).
- **Representative Baselines:** The survey lists widely-used TTS methods across categories: Self-Consistency, Best-of-N (Parallel); STaR, Self-Refine, PRM (Sequential); MCTS, ToT (Hybrid); Distilled-R1, R1 (Internal).
- **No Single Optimal Solution:** The authors emphasize that "No free lunch," indicating that the optimal TTS solution is highly dependent on the task's hardness and openness.
- **Holistic Evaluation:** Beyond standard accuracy, efficiency is a critical evaluation aspect. Researchers should also consider secondary attributes like robustness, safety, bias, and interpretability as TTS becomes more general-purpose.
- **Internal vs. External Scaling:** Internal scaling can be more efficient post-tuning (single prompt), but requires significant tuning resources, making external methods (multiple trials) more accessible for practitioners.

**Open Challenges and Future Opportunities:**
The survey identifies several critical challenges and promising future directions:
- **More Scaling is the Frontier:** Continued efforts are needed to push the limits of parallel (smart coverage expansion, verifier-augmented), sequential (structured self-refinement, verification-enhanced iterative scaling), hybrid (generalized architectures, multi-agent interactive scaling), and internal scaling (effective compute allocation, stability, interpretability, controllability).
- **Clarifying the Essence of Techniques:** Deeper theoretical understanding is required regarding how core techniques (SFT, RL, reward modeling) contribute to TTS, their optimal combinations, the role of PRMs, mathematical properties of scaling, adaptive TTS, and the underlying "thoughtology" or reasoning patterns.
- **Optimizing Scaling is the Key:** Beyond accuracy, systematic evaluation and optimization for efficiency, robustness, bias, safety, and interpretability are crucial as TTS methods proliferate.
- **Generalization Across Domains is the Mainstream:** Extending TTS to diverse, high-stakes domains like medicine, finance, and law is a major opportunity. This requires balancing cost and accuracy, ensuring domain-specific interpretability, and integrating external knowledge and real-world constraints. Future research must focus on generalizable TTS strategies that are robust across various reasoning tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey represents a significant contribution to the field of Large Language Models (LLMs) and artificial intelligence for several reasons:

1. **Unified and Structured Understanding:** Its most profound impact lies in providing the first comprehensive, multi-dimensional taxonomy for Test-Time Scaling (TTS). Before this work, TTS research was rapidly growing but lacked a cohesive framework. By systematically categorizing "what to scale," "how to scale," "where to scale," and "how well to scale," the survey brings much-needed order and clarity. This structured perspective allows researchers to better classify, compare, and understand the unique contributions of individual techniques, facilitating more informed research design and reducing redundant efforts.

2. **Roadmap for Future Research:** By meticulously identifying critical open challenges and outlining promising future directions within each dimension of the taxonomy, the survey serves as a vital roadmap. It highlights specific areas for advancement, such as pushing the frontiers of different scaling types, deepening the theoretical understanding of underlying techniques, optimizing TTS beyond raw accuracy, and generalizing methods across diverse domains. This clear articulation of research gaps and opportunities is likely to inspire and guide future investigations, accelerating progress in TTS and, by extension, towards Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).

3. **Practical Guidance for Deployment:** Beyond theoretical organization, the survey offers invaluable "hands-on guidelines" for practitioners. It addresses practical questions regarding task applicability, efficient implementation pathways, the interplay between training-time and inference-time strategies, and holistic evaluation metrics. This pragmatic advice lowers the barrier to entry for developers and researchers seeking to apply or develop TTS methods, fostering broader adoption and experimentation.

4. **Emphasis on Efficiency and Controllability:** The survey’s detailed focus on "How Well to Scale" is particularly impactful. By introducing and consolidating metrics for efficiency (e.g., token cost, FLOPs, KV cache, reasoning efficiency scores) and controllability (e.g., adherence to budgets, length deviation), it shifts the evaluation paradigm beyond mere accuracy. This encourages the development of not just powerful, but also practical and deployable TTS solutions that are resource-aware and align with real-world constraints.

5. **Legitimizing a New Paradigm:** The paper reinforces the idea that TTS is a legitimate and increasingly vital approach to enhancing LLM intelligence, especially as the returns from traditional pre-training scaling diminish. By framing TTS as a deliberate, System 2-like thinking process for LLMs, analogous to human cognition, it lends theoretical grounding and practical urgency to this research direction. The mention of scaling laws for inference-time compute further solidifies TTS as a robust path forward.

In summary, this survey is not merely a summary of existing work; it is a foundational piece that structures a nascent but critical field. Its comprehensive scope, clear taxonomy, practical guidelines, and forward-looking identification of challenges will undoubtedly influence the direction and pace of LLM research, ultimately leading to more capable, efficient, and versatile AI systems that can address increasingly complex real-world problems.
