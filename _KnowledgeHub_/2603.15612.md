# HSImul3R: Physics-in-the-Loop Reconstruction of Simulation-Ready Human-Scene Interactions

> [!summary] Summary
> HSImul3R, developed by researchers from S-Lab, Nanyang Technological University, introduces a unified framework for reconstructing simulation-ready 3D human-scene interactions (HSI) from visual captures. The system achieves significantly higher interaction stability (e.g., 53.68% for Easy cases versus 10.52% for the baseline HSfM) and reduces 3D scene penetration by more than half, enabling successful deployment of refined human motions to a Unitree G1 humanoid robot.

## Problem

- Existing 3D reconstruction methods for human-scene interactions suffer from a "perception-simulation gap," where visually plausible outputs often violate physical laws, making them unstable in physics engines.
- Current approaches typically model human motion and scene geometry separately or prioritize 2D visual fidelity over precise 3D geometric and physical consistency, failing to capture explicit physical coupling.
- Physically inconsistent reconstructions hinder their utility in embodied AI applications, preventing stable simulation, training, and direct transfer to real-world robotic systems.

## Method

- **HSImul3R** employs a ==physics-in-the-loop bi-directional optimization== pipeline, integrating a physics simulator as an active supervisor to ensure physically consistent human-scene interactions.
- A forward-pass uses ==scene-targeted reinforcement learning== to refine human motion for stable interaction under dual supervision of motion fidelity and contact stability.
- A reverse-pass leverages ==Direct Simulation Reward Optimization (DSRO)== to fine-tune 3D object generative models, correcting structural deficiencies based on physics-based feedback on gravitational and interaction stability.

## Results

- HSImul3R achieved substantially higher HSI stability rates (**53.68%** for Easy, **30.56%** for Medium, **13.92%** for Hard interactions) compared to HSfM's baseline (**10.52%**, **4.50%**, **2.66%**).
- The framework reduced 3D scene penetration (SP-3D) to **22.9%** from HSfM's **69.51%** and improved human motion quality (W-MPJPE of **4.09** and PA-MPJPE of **2.17**).
- Refined human motions generated by HSImul3R were successfully retargeted to a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, demonstrating robust robot-scene interactions in real-world deployment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly incorporating a physics simulator into a closed-loop optimization framework is effective for bridging the perception-simulation gap and ensuring simulation-readiness of HSI reconstructions.
> - Jointly optimizing human dynamics and scene geometry through coupled forward and reverse passes ensures physical consistency, where refined human motions improve interaction stability and simulator feedback refines object geometry.
> - Utilizing 3D structural priors from generative models during initial alignment, combined with 3D explicit constraints, significantly mitigates penetration artifacts crucial for robust simulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied artificial intelligence (AI) aims to integrate intelligent agents into real-world environments through physically grounded systems. A fundamental challenge within embodied AI is the accurate modeling of humanoid–scene interactions (HSI), which necessitates a comprehensive understanding of human motion, spatial layouts, and the stability of interactions. Reconstructing HSI from visual data, such as images or videos, offers a scalable method for generating high-fidelity, simulation-ready datasets that can facilitate the transition from passive observation to active robotic deployment.

Existing methodologies for 3D reconstruction of human-scene interactions encounter a significant limitation termed the "perception–simulation gap." This gap arises because reconstructions that appear visually plausible often violate fundamental physical constraints, rendering them unstable or unfeasible when introduced into physics engines. This instability subsequently hinders their utility in embodied AI applications, including robotic control.

Current approaches to HSI modeling typically fall into three distinct categories, each with inherent limitations:
1. **3D Scene Reconstruction:** Methods like NeRF, Gaussian Splatting, and DUSt3R primarily focus on environmental geometry, often disregarding dynamic human elements.
2. **Human Motion Estimation:** Techniques such as those based on SMPL aim for robust motion reconstruction, even under occlusion, but frequently model human motion in isolation, without adequately accounting for physical contact or environmental constraints.
3. **Interaction Modeling:** Approaches relying on SMPL-driven HSI datasets are often limited in scale, diversity, and physical validation.

More recent unified frameworks, such as HOSNeRF and HSfM, attempt to model humans and scenes jointly. However, these methods typically optimize within the 2D image space, prioritizing visual alignment over precise geometric and physical validity. Consequently, the reconstructions derived from these methods often lack the metric and contact fidelity required for stable simulation and practical embodied deployment, thereby preserving the perception–simulation gap.

HSImul3R directly addresses this perception–simulation gap by introducing a unified framework that formulates HSI reconstruction as a bi-directional, physics-aware optimization problem. This approach integrates a physics simulator as an active supervisor, enabling a closed-loop refinement process between human motion and scene geometry. By focusing on physical consistency from the outset, HSImul3R aims to produce reconstructions that are not only visually accurate but also robust and stable for use in physics-based simulations and real-world robotic applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop **HSImul3R**, a unified framework capable of generating simulation-ready 3D reconstructions of human-scene interactions (HSI) from casual visual captures, including sparse-view images and monocular videos.

The motivation for HSImul3R stems directly from the critical **"perception-simulation gap"** identified in current HSI reconstruction methods. Existing techniques, while often producing visually plausible results, frequently yield reconstructions that fail to adhere to physical laws. This physical inconsistency leads to several issues:
- **Instability in Physics Engines:** Reconstructed human-scene configurations, when imported into physics simulators, often result in unintended object displacement, penetrations, or overall structural collapse, making them unsuitable for training or evaluating embodied AI agents.
- **Failure in Embodied AI Applications:** The inability to generate physically valid interactions prevents the direct transfer of learned motions or scene understanding to real-world robotic systems, thus limiting the practical utility of these reconstructions.
- **Fragmented Modeling:** Previous approaches tend to model humans and environments separately or prioritize 2D visual fidelity over 3D geometric and physical accuracy, failing to capture the explicit physical coupling between humans and their surroundings. This results in reconstructions that lack the necessary metric and contact fidelity for robust simulation.

To address these limitations, HSImul3R is motivated by the following specific objectives:
1. **Bridge the Perception-Simulation Gap:** By introducing a physically-grounded bi-directional optimization pipeline that actively incorporates a physics simulator as a supervisor.
2. **Jointly Refine Human Dynamics and Scene Geometry:** This involves a closed-loop process where the understanding of human motion informs scene geometry adjustments, and vice-versa, ensuring physical consistency.
3. **Optimize Human Motion with Scene-targeted Reinforcement Learning:** This forward optimization aims to refine human motion to achieve interaction stability under dual supervision of motion fidelity and contact stability, accounting for environmental constraints.
4. **Refine Scene Geometry via Direct Simulation Reward Optimization (DSRO):** This reverse optimization leverages direct feedback from the physics simulator regarding gravitational stability and interaction success to correct structural deficiencies and artifacts in the reconstructed scene geometry.
5. **Develop a Comprehensive Benchmark (HSIBench):** To facilitate training, evaluation, and comparison of physically-grounded HSI reconstruction methods across diverse objects and interaction scenarios.
6. **Enable Direct Deployment to Real-world Robotics:** To demonstrate the practical utility of simulation-ready reconstructions by seamlessly transferring and deploying refined human motions onto humanoid robots.

By achieving these objectives, HSImul3R aims to produce the first stable, simulation-ready HSI reconstructions that are directly applicable to embodied AI and robotic control, moving beyond merely visually acceptable results.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HSImul3R is presented as a unified framework that reconstructs simulation-ready human-scene interactions (HSI) from casual captures by employing a physics-in-the-loop bi-directional optimization pipeline. The approach initially focuses on sparse-view inputs and is extensible to monocular videos.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

The framework builds upon existing techniques. **DUSt3R** is utilized for 3D scene reconstruction, which predicts depth maps and camera poses from uncalibrated image pairs. While effective for global alignment, DUSt3R is acknowledged to struggle with human subjects and often produces artifacts and non-watertight topologies unsuitable for stable simulation.

#### 3.2. Human-Scene Interaction Reconstruction and Alignment

The initial stage involves the independent reconstruction of the static scene geometry and dynamic human motion, followed by their alignment:
- **Scene Reconstruction:** DUSt3R is used to recover the 3D structure of the environment.
- **Human Motion Estimation:**
 - **Individual Detection & Tracking:** SAM2 detects and associates individuals across frames, generating masks and identity tracks.
 - **3D SMPL-based Motion & 2D Keypoints:** 4DHumans extracts initial 3D SMPL-based motion sequences, and ViTPose extracts 2D keypoints.
- **Initial Alignment:** A joint optimization unifies human and scene into a common coordinate space. This includes human-centric bundle adjustment guided by 2D keypoints and global human-scene alignment minimizing reprojection error between ViTPose keypoints and projected 3D SMPL joints.

**Alignment via Explicit 3D Structural Prior:** To overcome structural artifacts in scene geometry and improve the robustness of human-scene alignment (which can be vulnerable to occlusions when solely based on 2D projections), HSImul3R leverages 3D structural priors from pre-trained generative models.
- For each object, the most prominent view is identified, and SAM extracts its segmentation mask.
- This segmented view is then fed into a pre-trained image-to-3D generative model (e.g., MIDI) to synthesize a high-fidelity 3D representation with improved structural accuracy.
- With these refined 3D object representations, a more robust human-scene alignment is performed using explicit 3D constraints to mitigate penetration artifacts, which are critical for simulation stability.
 - For **non-contact** scenarios, `ℓ_non-contact` minimizes the Euclidean distance between the human body part closest to the object and the object's vertices.
 - For **contact** scenarios, `ℓ_contact` uses a signed distance function to penalize penetration depth of human vertices into the object surface.

#### 3.3. Forward-Pass: Scene-Targeted Motion Optimization

After initial 3D reconstruction and alignment, the raw human motions often fail to produce stable dynamics in a physics simulator (e.g., humanoids displacing objects). To address this:
- HSImul3R integrates a **scene-targeted supervision signal** into a reinforcement-learning-based motion tracking framework (specifically, PHC).
- This supervision, `ℓ_scene`, encourages physically plausible contact by enforcing spatial proximity between the humanoid and relevant scene objects. It is defined as the average Euclidean distance between human contact keypoints and their nearest sampled object surface points within the local contact region. This objective guides the reinforcement learning agent to optimize human motion for stable interaction.

#### 3.4. Reverse-Pass: Simulator-Guided Object Refinement

Even with the forward-pass, simulation stability can be compromised by structural defects in generated objects (e.g., missing table legs, surface distortions from occlusion). To mitigate these issues:
- HSImul3R introduces **Direct Simulation Reward Optimization (DSRO)**, which uses physics-based simulation feedback to refine the 3D explicit object generation process.
- The `ℓ_DSRO` objective fine-tunes the generative model (e.g., MIDI) based on a stability feedback score `l(x_0)`.
- **Stability criteria** for `l(x_0)` are defined based on three conditions:
 1. The object must remain physically stable under gravity alone.
 2. The HSI scene must reach a stable final state during human interaction.
 3. The interaction must involve actual contact, rather than the object merely resting independently.

**HSIBench Dataset:** To support the training and evaluation of HSImul3R, a new benchmark dataset, **HSIBench**, was collected.
- It comprises 19 diverse objects and over 50 motion sequences from three participants, resulting in 300 distinct HSI cases.
- Each case is captured from 16 synchronized viewpoints, providing rich multi-view supervision.
- Pseudo ground truth for object geometry (multi-view 2DGS) and human motion (SMPL estimation) is derived for quantitative evaluation.
- For training signals, the full reconstruction and simulation pipeline is run 15 times for each captured case under different random seeds to collect diverse simulation outcomes.

#### 3.5. Extension to Monocular Videos

The framework is extended to monocular video inputs due to the dynamic nature of the motion tracking techniques used in the forward-pass.
- MegaSAM and TRAM are employed for scene reconstruction and human motion estimation, respectively.
- The approach assumes a static scene with the human as the only moving entity.
- Dynamic 3D alignment is achieved by using SAM2 to extract 2D bounding boxes for both the person and specific scene objects, enabling preliminary interaction identification and accurate 3D alignment throughout the sequence.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

HSImul3R was evaluated across reconstruction fidelity, simulation stability, and the impact of DSRO fine-tuning, comparing against state-of-the-art baselines and ablated variants.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Evaluations

- **HSI Reconstruction and Simulation Quality (Table 1):** HSImul3R demonstrated substantial improvements over the baseline HSfM and various ablated variants.
 - **Stability-HSI (%):** HSImul3R achieved significantly higher interaction stability across difficulty levels: 53.68% (Easy), 30.56% (Medium), and 13.92% (Hard). In contrast, HSfM managed only 10.52% (Easy), 4.50% (Medium), and 2.66% (Hard). This indicates HSImul3R's ability to produce robustly stable interactions.
 - **Scene Penetration (SP-3D) (%):** HSImul3R reduced 3D penetration to 22.9%, a substantial improvement over HSfM's 69.51%, highlighting the effectiveness of the 3D explicit constraints.
 - **Human Motion Quality (W-MPJPE, PA-MPJPE):** The method also yielded better human motion quality post-simulation, with lower W-MPJPE (4.09) and PA-MPJPE (2.17) compared to HSfM (5.02 and 2.79, respectively).
- **Image-to-3D Generation Quality (Table 2):** The integration of DSRO into the framework notably improved the quality of generated 3D objects.
 - **Stability-HSI (%) and Stability-Gravity (SG) (%):** The DSRO-enabled model showed superior physical plausibility, with an SG of 91.50% (compared to MIDI at 79.19%) and improved interaction stability (53.68% Easy HSI, compared to MIDI's 29.56%).
 - **Geometric Accuracy (CD, F-Score):** DSRO also led to better geometric accuracy (lower Chamfer Distance (0.173) and higher F-Score (88.25) compared to MIDI (0.198, 81.95) and DSO* (0.191, 86.26)).

#### 4.2. Qualitative Evaluations

- **Comparison with HSfM (Fig. 5):** HSfM frequently failed to achieve stable human-object interactions in the simulator, often resulting in objects being displaced by the humanoid. HSImul3R, by contrast, consistently produced stable interaction states.
- **Image-to-3D Object Reconstruction (Fig. 4):** Compared to MIDI and DSO, the DSRO fine-tuned model generated objects with more accurate geometric structures and reduced surface distortions, which are crucial for stable simulation.

#### 4.3. Real-world Robotics Deployment (Fig. 6)

- The refined human motions generated by HSImul3R were successfully retargeted to Unitree G1 humanoid robots using GMR.
- These motions served as a prior for training a whole-body control policy via diffusion-guided reinforcement learning in the IsaacGym simulator.
- The trained policy was successfully deployed onto the physical Unitree G1 robot, demonstrating robust robot-scene interactions. This suggests that the framework facilitates the use of large, cost-effective datasets from sources like YouTube for training embodied AI models.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Scene-targeted Simulation Loss (Eq. 5) (Fig. 7):** Removing the `ℓ_scene` distance-minimization term resulted in unstable humanoid behavior, often leading to exaggerated motions and unintended object displacement.
- **Number of Input Views (Table 3):** While increasing the number of input views (from 4 to 10 or 16) slightly improved human motion quality, it did not significantly impact penetration handling or overall simulation stability, indicating competitive performance even with sparse inputs.

#### 4.5. Limitations and Failure Cases

- **Success Ratio:** The method's success ratio, particularly for complex interactions or scenarios involving multiple objects, remains moderate.
- **Interaction Fidelity:** In some failure cases, the humanoid and objects achieved stable individual states but did not engage in meaningful interactions.
- **Generalizability:** The fine-tuned image-to-3D model may inherit biases from its original training dataset (MIDI) and the collected HSIBench, potentially limiting its generalizability to out-of-domain interaction scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

HSImul3R represents a notable advancement in the field of 3D reconstruction for human-scene interactions, offering a direct solution to the persistent "perception-simulation gap." Its significance and potential impact span several key areas:

1. **Bridging the Perception-Simulation Gap:** The most significant contribution is the development of the first framework capable of producing *simulation-ready* HSI reconstructions from casual captures. By explicitly integrating a physics simulator into a bi-directional optimization loop, HSImul3R ensures that reconstructions are not only visually accurate but also physically consistent and stable, addressing a fundamental limitation in previous work.

2. **Advancement in Embodied AI:** This work provides a crucial tool for training and evaluating embodied AI agents. The ability to generate high-fidelity, physically plausible HSI data from real-world observations offers a scalable pathway for developing more robust and intelligent agents that can interact effectively and safely within physical environments.

3. **Direct Applicability to Robotics:** The successful deployment of HSImul3R's refined human motions to a real-world humanoid robot (Unitree G1) demonstrates its practical utility in robotics. This framework can facilitate the learning of complex robot-scene interaction tasks, enabling robots to perform actions like sitting on chairs, manipulating objects, or maintaining balance in dynamic environments, which are essential for future autonomous systems.

4. **Enabling Scalable, Physically Valid Datasets:** HSImul3R opens avenues for leveraging vast, cost-effective datasets from unconstrained sources (e.g., YouTube videos) to augment training data for large-scale embodied AI models. By transforming casual captures into simulation-ready assets, the framework can significantly expand the scope and diversity of physically validated training data, accelerating research in robot learning and interaction.

5. **Improved 3D Reconstruction Quality:** The introduction of the Explicit 3D Structural Prior and Direct Simulation Reward Optimization (DSRO) contributes to improving the geometric and physical plausibility of reconstructed scene objects. DSRO, in particular, offers a novel approach to fine-tune generative models based on physics simulation feedback, leading to more structurally faithful and stable 3D assets.

6. **Foundation for Future Research:** HSImul3R establishes a robust foundation for future research in several directions, including addressing more complex interaction scenarios, enhancing the generalizability of physically-grounded reconstruction to out-of-domain cases, improving success rates for intricate multi-object interactions, and exploring methods to ensure meaningful rather than merely stable interactions.

In conclusion, HSImul3R marks a critical step towards creating a robust pipeline for understanding and replicating human-scene interactions in simulated and real-world robotic contexts, holding significant implications for the future development of intelligent, embodied agents.
