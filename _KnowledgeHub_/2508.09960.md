# GBC: Generalized Behavior-Cloning Framework for Whole-Body Humanoid Imitation

> [!summary] Summary
> A GBC framework unifies human motion imitation for diverse humanoid robots, incorporating an adaptive data pipeline with a differentiable IK network and a DAgger-MMPPO algorithm using a Motion Mimic Transformer. This system facilitates robust policy learning for whole-body control, demonstrating precise imitation of complex human motions and effective transferability between simulation environments.

## Problem

- Humanoid control methods are fragmented, lacking universality and generalization across diverse robot morphologies and configurations.
- Acquiring and retargeting human motion capture (MoCap) data into physically feasible robot actions is complex due to Inverse Kinematics (IK) multi-solutions, configuration-specific adjustments, and real-time conversion challenges.
- Existing imitation learning policies struggle with robustness (e.g., covariate shift) and effectively processing high-dimensional, multi-modal reference observations for high-fidelity human-like performance.

## Method

- An adaptive data pipeline employs a differentiable ==Inverse Kinematics (IK)== network and post-processing steps to convert diverse human MoCap datasets into physically feasible robot demonstration data for various humanoid morphologies.
- A Motion Mimic Transformer (MMTransformer) with grouped embeddings serves as the policy backbone to effectively fuse multi-modal robot observations and reference motion states, overcoming limitations of traditional MLPs.
- The DAgger-MMPPO algorithm, a two-stage PPO framework, integrates an expert motion prior with Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) for robust policy learning, enhanced by curriculum learning (e.g., 'toddler' curriculum) for stability and convergence.

## Results

- The data pipeline achieved precise kinematic mapping with average end-effector position errors between **0.44** cm and **0.91** cm across varied robots, and successfully retargeted Out-of-Distribution (O.O.D.) motions with median errors ranging from **1.42** cm to **2.54** cm.
- The MMTransformer with Grouped Embeddings V2 significantly outperformed MLP and other Transformer variants in imitation learning, achieving an Action Similarity Score of **0.89** and substantially faster convergence (e.g., 1870 Well-Trained Epochs compared to MLP failure).
- The DAgger-MMPPO algorithm with the Toddler Curriculum produced highly robust and high-performing policies, achieving high success rates (e.g., **91.3%** on Medium Task), strong generalization to O.O.D. motions (e.g., **0.724** Joint Index for O.O.D. dancing), and successful sim-to-sim transfer.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A differentiable IK network, combined with auxiliary losses, provides a universal, real-time method for physically feasible retargeting of human motion to diverse robot morphologies.
> - An Encoder-Only Transformer architecture with grouped embeddings effectively processes and fuses multi-modal inputs, enabling robust and high-fidelity imitation compared to simpler network architectures.
> - A two-stage DAgger-MMPPO framework, enhanced with curriculum learning, offers an efficient strategy to learn robust policies by building a behavioral prior in a simplified environment and adapting it to full-physics simulations.
