# Towards Large Reasoning Models: A Survey of Reinforced Reasoning with Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This Tsinghua University-led survey synthesizes advancements in reinforced reasoning with Large Language Models, identifying the emerging paradigm of "Large Reasoning Models (LRMs)." It outlines how these models integrate train-time improvements through reinforcement learning and automated data construction, particularly with Process Reward Models, alongside test-time computational scaling for enhanced reasoning.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) historically excelled at text generation but lacked robust, human-like complex reasoning capabilities.
- A significant bottleneck for training LLMs on reasoning tasks was the high cost and difficulty of obtaining human-annotated, step-by-step reasoning data.
- Bridging the gap between LLM language generation and sophisticated multi-step cognitive reasoning processes presented a key challenge.

## Method

- Systematically reviews the field of "reinforced reasoning with LLMs," categorizing methods for data construction, learning, and test-time inference.
- Focuses on the transition from human-annotated to LLM-driven ==automated data generation== for reasoning trajectories.
- Explores the application of reinforcement learning, particularly ==Process Reward Models (PRMs)==, and ==test-time computational scaling== to enhance reasoning in LLMs.

## Results

- The survey synthesizes advancements to define "Large Reasoning Models (LRMs)" as a new paradigm for advanced AI reasoning.
- It maps the critical role of automated process data generation and reinforcement learning with Process Reward Models (PRMs) in enabling LLMs to learn complex reasoning.
- It highlights the "test-time scaling law" where increased inference-time computation, guided by PRMs, consistently improves reasoning accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Automated, LLM-driven data construction, particularly for generating step-by-step reasoning trajectories (Process Annotation), is crucial for scaling reasoning capabilities.
> - Reinforcement learning, especially through Process Reward Models (PRMs) providing dense, step-wise rewards, is pivotal for training LLMs to master complex reasoning processes.
> - Increasing computational effort during inference time through PRM-guided search methods can substantially enhance LLM reasoning accuracy, indicating a "test-time scaling law."

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This survey paper is positioned at the forefront of the rapidly evolving field of Large Language Models, specifically addressing their burgeoning capacity for complex reasoning. It critically examines the shift from LLMs primarily acting as powerful text generators to becoming sophisticated "reasoning AI" systems. The broader landscape of LLM research has seen an explosion of interest since the advent of transformer architectures and massive pre-training datasets, leading to emergent abilities such as in-context learning and zero-shot reasoning. Initially, techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting demonstrated that simply eliciting step-by-step thinking could significantly enhance LLMs' reasoning accuracy without model modifications.

This paper, however, situates itself beyond these initial prompting techniques, focusing on an *emerging trend*: "learning to reason" by applying **reinforcement learning (RL)** to train LLMs. This signifies a transition from merely *prompting* LLMs to *learn* reasoning processes directly. The authors argue that this trend, coupled with advancements in automated data generation and test-time computational scaling, defines a new research frontier: the development of **Large Reasoning Models (LRMs)**. They explicitly link this direction to **OpenAI's o1 series**, which they present as a significant milestone, reinforcing OpenAI's vision of moving LLMs from "conversational AI" (level 1) to "reasoning AI" (level 2) on the path toward Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).

By surveying these interwoven advancements—automated data construction, sophisticated RL fine-tuning, and advanced test-time search—the paper identifies and structures the key components driving the next generation of LLMs. It moves beyond generic performance metrics to focus specifically on the mechanisms that enable LLMs to mimic complex human reasoning processes like tree search and reflective thinking. Thus, this work doesn't just describe a part of the LLM landscape; it attempts to *define* a significant and rapidly growing sub-field that is critical for future AI development.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this survey is to provide a comprehensive review of recent progress in the emerging field of "reinforced reasoning with Large Language Models," guiding researchers through the key technical components and open challenges.

The core motivations behind this survey are multi-faceted:

- **Bridging the Gap from Generation to Reasoning:** While LLMs have demonstrated remarkable language generation capabilities, their ability to perform complex, human-like reasoning has been a more recent, and often emergent, phenomenon. The paper is motivated by the desire to understand how LLMs can move beyond simple autoregressive token generation to mimic intricate cognitive processes, aligning with Noam Chomsky's view on the link between language and thought.
- **Addressing Data Scarcity for Reasoning:** A significant challenge in training LLMs for reasoning is the prohibitive cost and difficulty of human-annotated, step-by-step reasoning trajectories. The survey highlights the motivation to overcome this bottleneck by focusing on automated, LLM-driven search algorithms that can generate high-quality reasoning data at scale.
- **Leveraging Reinforcement Learning:** The authors emphasize the growing trend of applying RL to train LLMs for reasoning. They are motivated to review how RL, particularly with "Process Reward Models (PRMs)," enables models to learn effective reasoning processes through trial-and-error, reducing reliance on expensive human supervision and providing substantially more training data.
- **Exploring Computational Scaling for Reasoning:** Beyond train-time improvements, the survey is motivated by the observation that increasing test-time compute can also significantly boost reasoning accuracy. This "test-time scaling law" suggests a promising direction to fully unleash LLMs' reasoning capabilities.
- **Defining the "Large Reasoning Model" Paradigm:** The ultimate motivation is to crystallize and frame the concept of a "Large Reasoning Model" as a distinct and critical research frontier, drawing inspiration from pioneering efforts like OpenAI's o1 series. This involves integrating train-time scaling (via RL and automated data) and test-time scaling (via PRM-guided search) into a cohesive vision for advanced AI reasoning.
- **Providing a Structured Overview:** Given the rapid pace of research, the authors are motivated to offer a structured and systematic review, covering foundational background, technical components, open-source projects, benchmarks, and future directions, thereby serving as a valuable resource for the research community.

### 3. Methodology and Approach (of the Survey Paper)

As a survey paper, its "methodology" is the structured and comprehensive approach taken to review, categorize, and synthesize the existing literature on reinforced reasoning with LLMs. The authors explicitly outline their systematic approach across several key sections:

1. **Foundational Background (Section 2):** The survey begins by establishing a common understanding of LLMs, detailing their core training phases:
 - **Pre-training:** Discussing how LLMs acquire linguistic and world knowledge, and the role of diverse datasets (e.g., code, math) in cultivating reasoning abilities.
 - **Fine-tuning:** Explaining how Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) refines model output and enhances reasoning, particularly with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) techniques.
 - **Alignment:** Delving into the use of Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and AI Feedback (RLAIF) for aligning LLMs with desired behaviors, and the evolution to Direct Preference Optimization (DPO).
 - **Prompting for Advanced Reasoning:** Categorizing and explaining various prompting strategies (step-by-step, multi-path exploration, decomposition-based) that elicit complex reasoning without model modification.
 - **Agentic Workflow:** Introducing how structured "thinking patterns" can be programmed into LLMs at test-time to enhance reasoning.

2. **Key Technical Components (Sections 3, 4, 5):** The core of the survey systematically dissects the enabling technologies for large reasoning models:
 - **Data Construction (Section 3):** Analyzing the critical shift from expensive human annotation to scalable LLM-driven automation. This includes a detailed review of "Outcome Annotation" (labeling final results) and the more crucial "Process Annotation" (labeling intermediate steps), highlighting methods like Monte Carlo simulation and Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) for automated generation and self-refinement.
 - **Learning to Reason (Section 4):** Reviewing the evolution of fine-tuning from supervised approaches to reinforcement learning. This section deeply explores how RL is applied, distinguishing between "Outcome Reward Models (ORM)" and "Process Reward Models (PRM)" for multi-step reasoning, and discussing methods like PPO and DPO in this context. It also introduces "Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT)" as a novel, data-efficient approach.
 - **Test-time Scaling (Section 5):** Examining techniques that enhance reasoning at inference time. This covers prompt-based methods (CoT, ToT) and, more importantly, PRM-guided search algorithms like Majority Vote, Tree Search, Beam Search, and Lookahead Search, which represent a "test-time scaling law."

3. **Analysis of Large Reasoning Models (Section 6):** The survey dedicates a section to contextualizing these technical advancements through the lens of concrete implementations:
 - **OpenAI's o1 Series:** Providing an in-depth analysis of o1's reported capabilities (knowledge integration, problem decomposition, coherent reasoning) and its implications for new scaling laws in both training and inference.
 - **Open-source Attempts:** Reviewing projects like OpenR, Rest-MCTS*, Journey Learning, and LLaMA-Berry that attempt to replicate or explore the core methods of o1, offering insights into their distinct strategies for data construction, pre-training, post-training, and test-time improvements.

4. **Other Enhancing Techniques and Evaluation (Sections 7, 8):**
 - **Additional Test-time Enhancements (Section 7):** Summarizing other training-free test-time methods like Verbal Reinforcement Search, Memory-based Reinforcement, and Agentic System Search, broadening the scope beyond PRM-guided approaches.
 - **Evaluation Benchmarks (Section 8):** Categorizing and detailing popular benchmarks across Math, Logic, Commonsense, Coding, and Agent problems, underscoring their importance in documenting LLM capabilities.

This structured breakdown allows the authors to systematically trace the development of LLM reasoning, highlighting key paradigms, technical innovations, and the overarching shift towards complex, reinforced reasoning, culminating in the vision of Large Reasoning Models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results (of the Survey)

The survey synthesizes a rapidly advancing research domain into several key findings and identifies crucial trends shaping the future of LLM reasoning:

1. **Emergence of "Thought" as a Key Concept:** The survey highlights that breakthroughs in LLM reasoning move beyond simple token generation by introducing the concept of "thought" – a sequence of intermediate tokens representing steps in a reasoning process. This shift, exemplified by Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and Tree-of-Thought (ToT) prompting, enables LLMs to mimic complex human cognitive processes.

2. **Paradigm Shift in Data Construction:** A central finding is the move from prohibitively expensive human annotation to **LLM-driven automated data construction**. This automation, particularly for generating step-by-step reasoning trajectories (Process Annotation), is deemed crucial for scaling LLM reasoning capabilities. Methods like Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) simulations are shown to be highly effective in generating high-quality reasoning data, even outperforming human annotations in some cases, and enabling self-refining mechanisms.

3. **Reinforcement Learning as the Core of "Learning to Reason":** The survey identifies reinforcement learning (RL) as the pivotal technology for training LLMs to master reasoning processes. It distinguishes between:
 - **Outcome Reward Models (ORMs):** Where feedback is only provided for the final solution, presenting credit assignment challenges for intermediate steps.
 - **Process Reward Models (PRMs):** Which provide dense, step-wise rewards, offering fine-grained feedback throughout the reasoning trajectory. PRMs are shown to be significantly more effective for multi-step reasoning, enabling more efficient optimization and better alignment with human reasoning preferences.

4. **The Rise of Test-Time Scaling:** Beyond train-time optimization, the survey emphasizes that increasing computational effort during inference can substantially boost reasoning accuracy. Techniques like PRM-guided search (e.g., Lookahead Search combining PRMs with Beam Search) allow LLMs to "think" more deliberately at test-time, leading to improved performance. This points to a "test-time scaling law" where more computation during inference directly translates to higher accuracy.

5. **Convergence Towards "Large Reasoning Models":** The most significant finding is the proposed concept of "Large Reasoning Models (LRMs)," which integrate the advancements in train-time (RL-driven learning with automated data) and test-time scaling (PRM-guided search). OpenAI's o1 series is presented as a leading example of this paradigm, demonstrating advanced capabilities in knowledge integration, systematic problem decomposition, and coherent reasoning across complex tasks, validated by empirical studies showing distinct scaling patterns.

6. **Growing Open-Source Efforts:** The survey documents various open-source initiatives (e.g., OpenR, Rest-MCTS*, Journey Learning, LLaMA-Berry) attempting to replicate and further explore the techniques underpinning LRMs. These efforts validate the feasibility of the proposed methodologies and contribute to the broader understanding and accessibility of advanced reasoning capabilities.

7. **Diverse Test-Time Enhancement Techniques:** Beyond PRM-guided search, the survey identifies other training-free test-time methods—such as verbal reinforcement search (for individual, multi-agent, and embodied agents), memory-based reinforcement (experiential, reflective, and concept learning), and agentic system search (prompt, module, and agent level optimization)—demonstrating the breadth of approaches to enhance LLM reasoning without altering model parameters.

8. **Comprehensive Benchmarking Taxonomy:** The survey provides a detailed taxonomy of evaluation benchmarks across various reasoning domains (mathematics, logic, commonsense, coding, and agent problems), highlighting the evolving standards for assessing LLMs' cognitive abilities.

In essence, the survey's main "result" is a structured map of the emerging LRM landscape, illustrating how the convergence of advanced data generation, sophisticated reinforcement learning, and intelligent test-time computation is paving the way for significantly more capable and human-like AI reasoning systems.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey paper holds significant importance for the field of artificial intelligence, particularly in shaping the future trajectory of Large Language Models. Its potential impact is multi-faceted:

1. **Defining a New Frontier: Large Reasoning Models (LRMs):** The paper formally introduces and consolidates the concept of "Large Reasoning Models," framing the convergence of train-time and test-time scaling for reasoning as a coherent and promising research direction. This provides a clear conceptual framework for future work, moving beyond the traditional focus on model size or pre-training data scale alone.

2. **Highlighting Critical Technical Bottlenecks and Solutions:** By dissecting the core components—data construction, learning algorithms, and inference strategies—the survey pinpoints key challenges (e.g., data scarcity for step-by-step reasoning) and reviews innovative solutions (e.g., LLM-automated process annotation, Process Reward Models, PRM-guided search). This offers researchers a targeted roadmap for addressing current limitations.

3. **Accelerating Development of Reasoning AI:** The emphasis on reinforcement learning for "learning to reason" and the "test-time scaling law" suggests a paradigm shift in how LLMs will be developed and deployed. This could lead to more efficient and effective methods for imbuing LLMs with robust reasoning capabilities, potentially unlocking new performance ceilings without perpetually increasing model parameters. The insights from OpenAI's o1 series, and the validation through open-source projects, indicate that these approaches are not merely theoretical but practically achievable.

4. **Towards More Human-like AI and AGI:** The paper's alignment with OpenAI's vision of moving to "reasoning AI" (level 2 on the AGI roadmap) and its discussion of mimicking human "System 1 + System 2" thinking underscore its long-term significance. By fostering systematic problem decomposition, knowledge integration, and coherent reasoning, LRMs bring AI closer to exhibiting more sophisticated, human-like cognitive abilities, which is a crucial step towards Artificial General Intelligence.

5. **Enabling Advanced Downstream Applications:** Enhanced reasoning capabilities will have a profound impact across various domains. The paper alludes to applications in competitive programming, scientific discovery (e.g., optimizing chemical reactions), complex problem-solving (e.g., urban planning, logistics scheduling), and autonomous agents. LRMs could lead to more reliable AI assistants for engineering, healthcare, finance, and other fields requiring intricate logical deduction and multi-step planning.

6. **Guiding Future Research and Benchmarking:** The comprehensive review of existing benchmarks provides a valuable resource for evaluating progress and identifying gaps in assessment. The discussion of open problems—such as the need for theoretical analysis of slow-thinking, the potential for scaling laws in LLM agents, and the development of complementary problem types—lays out clear avenues for future research.

In summary, this survey acts as a pivotal document, synthesizing diverse threads of research into a cohesive vision for "Large Reasoning Models." Its significance lies in not only documenting past achievements but, more importantly, in providing a conceptual and technical blueprint for the next generation of LLMs capable of truly sophisticated and human-like reasoning, with far-reaching implications for both AI research and its real-world applications.
