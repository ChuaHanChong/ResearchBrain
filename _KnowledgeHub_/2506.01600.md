# WoMAP: World Models For Embodied Open-Vocabulary Object Localization

> [!summary] Summary
> Princeton University researchers develop WoMAP, a framework that combines VLM-guided high-level reasoning with learned world models for embodied object localization, achieving 9× higher success rates than VLM-only baselines and 2× improvement over diffusion policy approaches by using Gaussian Splatting to generate photorealistic training data from short real-world videos, training a reconstruction-free world model with reward signals distilled from GroundingDINO object detection confidence, and employing model predictive control to optimize VLM action proposals in latent space, while demonstrating strong sim-to-real transfer on physical TidyBot robots and zero-shot generalization to novel objects, scenes, and lighting conditions with less than 22% performance degradation across visual and semantic distribution shifts.

## Problem

- Existing imitation learning methods for robot object localization require vast expert demonstrations and struggle to generalize beyond their training distribution
- Vision-Language Models provide intelligent semantic guidance but lack physical grounding and generate infeasible low-level actions
- Current approaches fail to combine high-level reasoning with precise motor control for embodied navigation tasks
- Scalable data generation for robot learning remains prohibitively expensive without expert human intervention

## Method

- ==Gaussian Splatting==-based data generation pipeline that creates diverse photorealistic training trajectories from short real-world videos without expert demonstrations
- ==Reconstruction-free latent world model== trained on dense reward signals from ==GroundingDINO== object detection confidence rather than image reconstruction objectives
- ==VLM==-guided planning scheme that uses ==GPT-4o== to propose high-level actions, then optimizes these proposals through ==model predictive control== in learned latent space
- Integration of frozen ==DINOv2== features as observation encoder with ==ViT==-based dynamics and reward predictors for robust state representation

## Results

- Achieves **9×** higher success rates than VLM-only baselines and **2×** improvement over diffusion policy across PyBullet and Gaussian Splat simulation environments
- Demonstrates successful sim-to-real transfer on physical TidyBot robot with comparable performance to simulation despite training entirely on synthetic data
- Shows robust zero-shot generalization to novel objects, lighting conditions, and backgrounds with less than **22%** performance degradation
- Maintains high performance across varying scene complexity and initial pose difficulty, with smaller performance drops (**23.6%** in PyBullet, **40.2%** in Gaussian Splat) compared to baselines (over **50%**)

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dense reward distillation from object detectors enables stable world model training without reconstruction losses, improving sample efficiency and avoiding training instability
> - Optimizing VLM action proposals in latent space through MPC successfully grounds abstract semantic reasoning into physically feasible robot movements
> - Gaussian Splatting provides sufficient photorealism for sim-to-real transfer while enabling scalable synthetic data generation from minimal real-world footage
> - Frozen pre-trained visual encoders (DINOv2) outperform trainable alternatives for world model observation encoding, providing better stability and performance

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical challenge in embodied AI and robotics: **language-instructed active object localization in partially observable and previously unseen environments.** This task requires robots to not only understand natural language commands but also actively explore their surroundings, make informed decisions, and execute physically grounded actions to locate a target object.

The current research landscape in this domain is broadly characterized by two main approaches, each with significant limitations:
- **End-to-end methods (e.g., Imitation Learning (IL) and Reinforcement Learning (RL)):** While powerful, these methods typically demand vast quantities of expert demonstrations (IL) or extensive online interactions in highly accurate simulation environments (RL). A major drawback is their struggle to generalize effectively to novel environments or tasks outside their training distribution, making real-world deployment challenging.
- **Modular approaches incorporating foundation models (e.g., Vision-Language Models (VLMs)):** VLMs excel at high-level semantic understanding and commonsense reasoning, providing useful heuristics for exploration (e.g., "look in the fruit basket for a banana"). However, they inherently lack physical grounding and often struggle to translate abstract reasoning into precise, executable low-level actions in a 6-DoF (degrees-of-freedom) robotic system. Directly grounding VLM proposals to physical actions is a non-trivial problem, often resulting in unrealistic or unfeasible trajectories.

WoMAP aims to bridge this gap by offering a novel "recipe" that combines the strengths of both paradigms while mitigating their weaknesses. It seeks to leverage the commonsense reasoning of VLMs for high-level guidance and integrate it with the robust grounding capabilities of a learned world model.

Key trends in the broader landscape that WoMAP contributes to and leverages include:
- **Foundation Models in Robotics:** The increasing adoption of large pre-trained models (DINOv2, GroundingDINO, GPT-4o) as powerful perception and reasoning modules for robots.
- **Data Efficiency and Scalability:** The pressing need for methods that can learn robust policies without prohibitive amounts of real-world data or expert human intervention. WoMAP's Gaussian Splatting-based data generation directly addresses this.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** A perpetual challenge in robotics, where policies trained in simulation often fail to perform well in the physical world. WoMAP's focus on photorealistic synthetic data and robust latent representations is designed to enhance this transferability.
- **World Models in Robotics:** A growing area of research where robots learn an internal predictive model of their environment dynamics, enabling long-horizon planning and foresight without direct interaction. WoMAP's reconstruction-free world model with reward distillation is a significant step in this direction.

By integrating a novel data generation pipeline, a reconstruction-free world model, and VLM-guided latent space planning, WoMAP positions itself as a state-of-the-art solution for open-vocabulary active perception, pushing the boundaries of generalization, data efficiency, and physical grounding in embodied AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of WoMAP is to enable **efficient, open-vocabulary object localization for robots instructed by natural language**, specifically in partially observable and novel environments. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations and aims to address critical limitations in existing approaches:

- **To overcome the generalization limitations of imitation learning methods:** Existing IL methods struggle to perform beyond the specific demonstration datasets they are trained on, limiting their applicability in diverse, real-world scenarios. WoMAP aims for strong zero-shot generalization to unseen objects, scenes, and task instructions.
- **To provide physical grounding for high-level VLM action proposals:** While VLMs can offer intelligent, commonsense-based guidance for exploration (e.g., "Look behind the bowl"), they notoriously fail to generate physically feasible or precise low-level actions. WoMAP aims to ground these abstract proposals into concrete, executable robot movements.
- **To achieve scalable data generation without expert demonstrations:** Collecting large, diverse datasets of expert robot trajectories is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming. WoMAP seeks a data generation recipe that leverages readily available real-world videos and synthetic rendering to produce training data at scale without human expert intervention.
- **To enable data-efficient and robust world model training:** Traditional world models often rely on image reconstruction objectives, which can lead to training instability and require massive datasets. WoMAP aims to achieve robust learning by distilling dense reward signals from open-vocabulary object detectors, avoiding the need for reconstruction and improving sample efficiency.
- **To facilitate strong sim-to-real transfer:** For practical robotic deployment, policies trained in simulation must transfer effectively to the real world. WoMAP's use of photorealistic Gaussian Splats for data generation and a robust latent representation aims to minimize the sim-to-real gap.
- **To combine high-level reasoning with low-level execution:** The paper's core motivation is to create a system that can intelligently reason about where an object might be (VLM) and then precisely execute the necessary movements to find it (world model). This fusion of cognitive and motor intelligence is essential for truly autonomous and adaptive robots.

In essence, WoMAP seeks to develop a comprehensive framework that allows robots to "think" intelligently about object search and then "act" precisely in the physical world, all while being trainable efficiently and generalizable to new situations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WoMAP proposes a novel "recipe" for open-vocabulary active object localization, integrating three key components: scalable data generation, a reconstruction-free latent world model, and a VLM-guided planning scheme.

#### 3.1. Scalable Data Generation via Gaussian Splatting (Section 3.2)

The core idea is to generate diverse, photorealistic training data efficiently from a few real-world videos, bypassing the need for expert demonstrations or extensive manual labeling.
- **Process:**
 1. **Video Capture:** A short real-world video of a scene is captured.
 2. **Gaussian Splatting (GS) Reconstruction:** A 3D Gaussian Splat model is trained from this video. GS enables real-time photorealistic novel-view synthesis from arbitrary camera poses.
 3. **Semantic Annotation:** Using semantic Gaussian Splats (which distills CLIP language semantics), the location and physical dimensions of target objects within the reconstructed 3D scene are automatically annotated.
 4. **Trajectory Generation:** Collision-free trajectories are synthetically generated within the trained Gaussian Splat. These trajectories start from random initial positions and navigate towards sampled target objects using a planning algorithm like RRT*. Random linear and angular perturbations are added for data augmentation and diversity.
 5. **Data Collection:** For each generated camera pose along these trajectories, an image observation (`o_i`) is rendered, its spatial camera pose (`P_i`) is recorded, and a dense reward (`r_i`) is computed.
- **Reward Computation:** The reward (`r_i`) for each frame is generated by an open-vocabulary object detector (GroundingDINO). It's based on the detection confidence of the target object, scaled by the associated bounding-box size. This provides a rich, task-relevant signal.
- **Outcome:** A dataset `D = {(o_i, r_i, P_i), ∀i∈[M]}` is created. Crucially, the world model can be trained on `(o_i, o_j, a_ij, r_j)` tuples, where `a_ij` is the action to transition from `P_i` to `P_j`, allowing for non-sequential data usage.

#### 3.2. World Models for Active Perception (Section 3.3)

This component learns a latent representation of environment dynamics and reward prediction, crucial for planning.
- **Architecture (Figure 4):** Consists of three main parts:
 1. **Observation Encoder (h_θ):** Maps high-dimensional camera observations (`o_t`) to a compact latent state (`z_t`). It uses a *frozen, pre-trained DINOv2 model* (specifically, its flattened patch embeddings) to leverage robust visual and spatial features learned from large-scale pre-training. This design choice is validated in ablations as superior and more stable than training from scratch or finetuning.
 2. **Dynamics Predictor (q_ψ):** Models the transition distribution `p(z_{t+1}|z_t, a_t)` in the latent space. It's a ViT-based architecture that takes the current latent state (`z_t`) and action (`a_t`) to predict the next latent state (`z_{t+1}`). It's trained by minimizing KL divergence. To improve multi-step prediction, it's supervised recurrently on sequences of observation-action pairs without teacher forcing.
 3. **Rewards Predictor (v_φ):** Estimates the reward (`r_t`) for a given latent state (`z_t`), conditioned on the language embedding (`e_g`) of the target object. It uses a cross-attention mechanism and is trained using binary cross-entropy loss, distilling the ground-truth rewards (`r_gt`) generated during data collection.
- **Reconstruction-Free Training:** A central innovation. Unlike many world models that use image reconstruction losses (which can lead to instability and poor generalization), WoMAP relies solely on the dense reward signals distilled from the object detector. This simplifies the model and improves robustness.

#### 3.3. Planning with WoMAP (Section 3.4)

This combines the high-level reasoning of VLMs with the grounded predictive power of the world model for effective action selection.
- **VLM Action Proposals:** Given a task instruction and current observation, a VLM (GPT-4o) is prompted to generate high-level action proposals. The VLM is given a fixed set of "action primitives" (e.g., "move directly forward," "look to the left") as a multiple-choice question to simplify spatial reasoning and avoid VLM hallucination of numerical actions.
- **Latent Space Optimization (Model Predictive Control - MPC):**
 1. Each VLM proposal is transformed into a coarse action.
 2. The world model simulates rollouts of these actions (and their refinements) in its latent space over a planning horizon `T`.
 3. For each rollout, the world model predicts the sequence of future latent states and their corresponding rewards.
 4. An optimization objective (Eq. 2) is used to maximize the sum of expected predicted rewards while incentivizing smoothness of the robot's trajectories. This refinement process allows the world model to "ground" the VLM's high-level suggestions into precise 6-DoF actions.
 5. The action sequence that yields the highest predicted reward after optimization is selected and executed on the robot.

This modular yet integrated approach allows WoMAP to leverage the best of both symbolic reasoning (VLM) and learned dynamics (world model) while being trainable from efficiently generated synthetic data.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

WoMAP demonstrates superior performance and generalization capabilities across various experiments in both simulation and real-world settings.

- **Overall Performance (Simulation):** In PyBullet (PB) and Gaussian Splat (GS) environments, WoMAP significantly outperforms baselines. It achieves **more than 9× higher success rates compared to VLM-only baselines and 2× higher success rates compared to diffusion policy (DP) baselines.** This highlights its ability to generate grounded actions and explore effectively, unlike VLM's lack of physical understanding or DP's struggle with generalization.
- **Generalization to Varying Task Difficulty:**
 - **Scene Difficulty:** As scene complexity increases, all methods show a performance drop, but WoMAP's drop is significantly smaller (23.6% in PB, 40.2% in GS) compared to VLM and DP (over 50%). This indicates WoMAP's robustness and strong planning capabilities even in cluttered, challenging environments.
 - **Initial-Pose Difficulty:** WoMAP consistently maintains high performance across easy, medium, and hard initial conditions, showcasing its ability to handle occlusions and varied starting viewpoints effectively by leveraging VLM guidance to mitigate world model hallucinations.
- **Comparison with Other World Model-Based Planners:**
 - **WM-CEM (sampling-based):** Less efficient due to its reliance on sampling.
 - **WM-Grad (gradient-based):** Often generates inefficient, circuitous paths, indicating reliance on myopic local gradients.
 - **WM-HR (heuristic-based):** Lacks intelligent high-level guidance, limiting performance in complex problems.
 - WoMAP's strategy of optimizing VLM proposals explicitly addresses the limitations of these world model-only planners, demonstrating the synergy between VLM reasoning and world model grounding.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer (TidyBot):** Despite being trained *entirely in Gaussian Splat simulation*, WoMAP transfers effectively to a physical TidyBot robot. It achieves similar or even higher success rates in real-world GS-Office and GS-Random scenes compared to simulation, with only a moderate drop in GS-Kitchen. In stark contrast, the VLM baseline experiences substantial performance drops (62% or more) due to its generated actions violating physical constraints and joint limits. This is a critical finding, validating the photorealistic data generation and robust latent representation.
- **Generalization to Novel Task Conditions:**
 - **Visual Generalization (Lighting & Backgrounds):** WoMAP shows strong zero-shot generalization under out-of-distribution lighting and background conditions, with less than a 22% decrease in success rate and efficiency. This suggests the learned latent features are robust to visual domain shifts.
 - **Semantic Generalization (Unseen Objects/Instructions):** WoMAP successfully localizes unseen target objects (e.g., "pear," "pliers," "beaker") and responds to unseen language instructions, even for objects it was not explicitly trained on. Performance correlates positively with semantic similarity (cosine distance) between the novel query and trained objects, indicating a learned understanding of object categories and descriptions.
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Data Scalability:** Training with data generated from Gaussian Splats (GSplat-Data) significantly outperforms training only on real-world video frames (Video-Only), underscoring the importance of diverse viewpoints provided by the GS pipeline.
 - **Observation Encoder:** Using a *frozen, pre-trained DINOv2* encoder yields better and more stable performance than training a ViT from scratch or finetuning DINOv2. This highlights the benefit of powerful pre-trained features and avoids training instability.
 - **Reconstruction Objective:** Training *without* an image reconstruction objective is shown to be beneficial, avoiding training instability and degradation in performance associated with reconstruction-based losses.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

WoMAP presents a significant advancement in embodied AI and robotic perception, offering a pragmatic and high-performing solution to the critical problem of open-vocabulary object localization. Its contributions hold substantial significance and potential impact:

- **Addressing Core Limitations in Embodied AI:** WoMAP directly tackles the long-standing challenges of data scarcity (by providing a scalable data generation recipe), generalization (by enabling zero-shot performance in novel environments and tasks), and robust physical grounding (by optimizing VLM proposals within a learned world model). This moves robots closer to real-world deployment in unstructured, human-centric environments.
- **Pioneering Data Generation with Gaussian Splatting:** The innovative use of Gaussian Splatting for generating photorealistic training data is a key methodological contribution. This approach reduces the reliance on costly, time-consuming real-world data collection or labor-intensive expert demonstrations, democratizing access to high-quality training data for robot learning. This could set a new standard for synthetic data generation pipelines in robotics.
- **Robust and Data-Efficient World Model Learning:** The reconstruction-free training strategy, leveraging dense reward signals from off-the-shelf object detectors, significantly improves the stability and sample efficiency of world model learning. This is a crucial step towards making world models more practical for robotics, where interaction data is often limited.
- **Effective Fusion of High-Level Reasoning and Low-Level Control:** WoMAP's unique planning scheme, combining VLM-derived commonsense guidance with the world model's precise dynamics and reward predictions, represents a powerful paradigm for intelligent robotic behavior. It enables robots to "think" semantically and then "act" physically, leading to more intelligent and efficient exploration strategies.
- **Strong Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The demonstrated strong sim-to-real performance on a physical robot (TidyBot) is a testament to the robustness of WoMAP's design. This capability is paramount for rapid prototyping and deployment of learned policies in real-world applications, accelerating the development cycle for autonomous systems.
- **Enabling General-Purpose Robots:** The ability for open-vocabulary localization, combined with strong generalization, moves towards robots that can understand and execute a wide range of commands for diverse objects in varied settings. This is a foundational capability for truly general-purpose household, industrial, or assistive robots.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Broader Adoption of Embodied AI:** By making robot learning more data-efficient and generalizable, WoMAP could accelerate the adoption of embodied AI in various industries, from logistics and manufacturing to assistive robotics and home automation.
- **New Research Directions:** The paper identifies important future work, such as interactive object localization, uncertainty quantification in reward distillation and world models, and risk-sensitive planning. These areas highlight fertile ground for further research, building upon WoMAP's foundations.
- **Standardization of Data Pipelines:** The success of Gaussian Splatting in this context could inspire the development of standardized, scalable, and high-fidelity synthetic data generation pipelines, fostering reproducibility and comparison across different robot learning frameworks.

In summary, WoMAP offers a comprehensive and effective solution for a central problem in robotics, pushing the boundaries of data efficiency, generalization, and physical grounding through an intelligent combination of cutting-edge AI models and novel data generation techniques. Its findings pave the way for more capable and adaptable autonomous robotic systems.
