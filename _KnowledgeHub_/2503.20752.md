# Reason-RFT: Reinforcement Fine-Tuning for Visual Reasoning of Vision Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Reason-RFT introduces a two-stage reinforcement fine-tuning framework to enhance the visual reasoning and generalization capabilities of Vision-Language Models. The method achieves state-of-the-art or highly competitive performance across various visual reasoning tasks, particularly demonstrating strong generalization under domain shifts while maintaining data efficiency.

## Problem

- Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for Vision-Language Models often leads to cognitive rigidity and poor generalization to domain-shifted visual reasoning tasks due to over-reliance on extensive Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data.
- Pure reinforcement learning (RL) methods, while dynamic, may lack robustness to visual domain shifts and struggle with initial acquisition of complex reasoning skills.
- Current visual reasoning approaches are often data-intensive, requiring meticulously annotated CoT data that can be costly and difficult to scale.

## Method

- A two-stage framework, ==Reason-RFT==, is proposed, starting with ==SFT-based Visual Reasoning Activation== to establish foundational task-specific priors using CoT data.
- The second stage employs Reinforcement Learning using ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== to dynamically refine the model and enhance generalization.
- ==Task-specific reward functions== are designed for diverse visual reasoning types (discrete, mathematical, function-based), combining format adherence and accuracy, with configurable partial credit.

## Results

- Reason-RFT achieved state-of-the-art or highly competitive performance across visual counting, structural perception, and spatial transformation tasks, outperforming models like GPT-4o and Gemini-1.5-Pro.
- Demonstrated superior generalization under domain shift conditions, with the **2B** Reason-RFT model surpassing GPT-4o by **31.7%** in spatial transformation under perspective shifts.
- Exhibited high data efficiency, achieving **70%** of the peak performance with only **3%** of the training data for spatial transformation tasks in the 2B model, and over **92%** for the 7B model.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The synergistic combination of SFT for initial skill activation and RL for dynamic refinement effectively overcomes the generalization limitations of purely supervised methods in visual reasoning.
> - SFT provides crucial inductive biases, especially for smaller models and tasks with complex output structures, enabling more effective RL adaptation.
> - Stricter 'hard rewards' that penalize partial matches in RL can improve generalization under domain shifts more effectively than allowing partial credit, indicating a better signal for robust serialized reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Visual reasoning is a cornerstone of artificial general intelligence (AGI) and is critical for understanding complex multimodal data. The field has seen significant advancements, particularly with the rise of Vision-Language Models (VLMs). This paper positions itself at the intersection of VLM development and reinforcement learning (RL) for enhanced reasoning capabilities.

Historically, approaches to visual reasoning fall into two main categories:
1. **Neural-symbolic methods:** These integrate symbolic reasoning with neural networks, aiming for interpretability and modularity. However, they often suffer from high complexity and reliance on explicit program generation.
2. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) based on VLMs:** These methods leverage end-to-end training with meticulously annotated Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data to improve reasoning. While effective, SFT-based approaches are limited by their dependence on high-quality, large-scale CoT data. This dependency can lead to issues such as overfitting to specific data distributions, cognitive rigidity (lack of flexibility in reasoning), and poor generalization to unseen or domain-shifted visual reasoning tasks, thereby restricting their real-world applicability.

Recent breakthroughs in large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated the power of reinforcement learning during post-training (e.g., GPT-o1, DeepSeek-R1, Kimi-1.5) to enhance reasoning in domains like coding and mathematics. RL offers a dynamic, feedback-driven optimization alternative to SFT, potentially improving performance with limited labeled data and promoting exploration. However, pure RL methods historically face challenges in robustness to domain shifts, limiting their generalization in complex real-world visual scenarios.

"Reason-RFT" directly addresses these limitations. It bridges the gap between the structured reasoning benefits of CoT-SFT and the dynamic, generalization-enhancing potential of RL, particularly in visual contexts. By proposing a two-stage reinforcement fine-tuning framework, the authors aim to overcome the cognitive rigidity and generalization issues inherent in purely SFT-based VLMs, and adapt RL methodologies (like DeepSeek-R1) to the visual domain, thus pushing the boundaries of VLM reasoning capabilities under diverse and challenging conditions. This work is at the forefront of efforts to make VLMs more robust, adaptable, and data-efficient, which are crucial steps toward achieving more capable and generalizable AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the Reason-RFT framework are driven by a recognized gap in existing visual reasoning methods, particularly concerning generalization and robustness.

The key objectives are:

1. **Enhance Visual Reasoning Generalization:** To enable Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to effectively transfer visual reasoning skills across varied visual conditions and domain shifts, overcoming the cognitive rigidity and overfitting often observed in supervised fine-tuning (SFT) approaches.
2. **Improve Performance on Complex Visual Reasoning Tasks:** To achieve state-of-the-art or highly competitive performance on a range of visual reasoning tasks, including visual counting, structural perception, and spatial transformation.
3. **Boost Data Efficiency:** To allow VLMs to achieve strong reasoning capabilities with fewer labeled examples, making the training process more efficient and applicable in scenarios with limited high-quality annotated data.
4. **Introduce a Robust Training Paradigm:** To establish a stable and effective fine-tuning framework that synergistically combines the strengths of SFT (for initial reasoning activation) and reinforcement learning (for generalization enhancement).

The motivations stemming from the limitations of current methods include:

- **Overcoming SFT Limitations:** Traditional SFT, while improving reasoning, heavily relies on meticulously annotated Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data. This dependence can lead to models overfitting to training data and struggling to generalize when faced with variations or shifts in visual appearance, configurations, or problem structures.
- **Addressing Cognitive Rigidity:** SFT-trained models can become "rigid" in their reasoning, making it difficult for them to adapt to novel problem formulations or unexpected visual contexts, which is crucial for real-world applicability.
- **Leveraging RL for Dynamic Optimization:** Reinforcement learning (RL) has shown promise in enhancing reasoning in other domains (e.g., text, math, coding) by enabling feedback-driven exploration and optimization. The authors are motivated to adapt this dynamic optimization capability to the visual domain, which is inherently more complex due to the richness of visual inputs.
- **Filling the Domain Shift Gap:** Pure RL methods, while dynamic, may also lack robustness to visual domain shifts. The motivation is to design a hybrid approach that can mitigate this, ensuring both dynamic learning and robust generalization.

In essence, the authors are motivated to develop a more adaptive, less data-intensive, and more generalizable visual reasoning system that moves beyond the inherent limitations of purely supervised methods, bringing VLMs closer to robust real-world applicability and foundational AGI capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Reason-RFT introduces a novel **two-stage reinforcement fine-tuning framework** designed to enhance the generalization capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in visual reasoning tasks. The methodology meticulously integrates supervised learning for initial skill acquisition with reinforcement learning for dynamic refinement and robustness.

**Stage 1: SFT-based Visual Reasoning Activation**

The first stage focuses on "activating" the VLM's potential reasoning capabilities by training it on high-quality, domain-specific Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data.
- **Objective:** To enable the model to decompose complex tasks into logical, step-by-step reasoning processes. This stage establishes a strong foundation of task-specific priors.
- **Data:** Curated CoT datasets are used, where each sample includes input images (`x`), a question (`q`), detailed reasoning steps (`r`), and the final answer (`a`).
- **Training:** The model is fine-tuned using a standard supervised learning objective, maximizing the likelihood of generating the reasoning steps and the final answer given the visual input and question. The resulting model, denoted as `π_CoT`, serves as a robust initialization for the subsequent RL stage.

**Stage 2: RL-based Reasoning Enhancement**

Building upon the activated reasoning potential from Stage 1, this stage employs reinforcement learning to further refine the model, specifically targeting enhanced generalization and adaptability.
- **Algorithm:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is utilized. GRPO is chosen over more computationally intensive algorithms like PPO due to its efficiency. It calculates relative advantages by comparing rewards within a group of sampled actions, thereby reducing computational overhead and simplifying optimization.
- **Sampling Action Groups:** For each input state (image-question pair), GRPO samples a group of diverse reasoning-response pairs (`a_1..., a_G`) from the current policy. This promotes exploration and prevents premature convergence to suboptimal strategies.
- **Reward Evaluation:** Each sampled action `a_i` receives a reward `R(a_i)`, which is a composite of two crucial components:
 - **Format Reward (`R_format`):** A binary reward (1 or 0) that enforces adherence to a predefined structured output format (e.g., `<think>...</think><answer>...</answer>`). This ensures consistent and interpretable reasoning traces.
 - **Accuracy Reward (`R_acc`):** This component is meticulously tailored to the specific characteristics of different visual reasoning tasks:
 - **Discrete-valued Type (e.g., Visual Counting):** A binary reward (1 if predicted answer matches ground truth, 0 otherwise) for tasks with unambiguous integer or multiple-choice answers.
 - **Mathematical Type (e.g., Structure Perception):** A tolerance-based reward using a cosine similarity function. It provides a full reward (1) for exact matches within a small tolerance (`ϵ_1`) and partial rewards for answers within a broader range (`ϵ_2`), smoothly transitioning to zero reward for incorrect answers. This accounts for minor numerical deviations.
 - **Function-based Type (e.g., Spatial Transformation):** Evaluates the alignment between predicted and ground truth sequences of transformation functions. It rewards complete matches (function, object, value) and partial matches (function+object/value, or only function), with weighting coefficients (`α`, `β`) for flexible adjustment of partial credit.
- **Policy Update:** Based on the calculated rewards, relative advantages are computed by normalizing rewards within the sampled group. The policy is then updated to reinforce actions with positive advantages, while a KL divergence constraint between the updated and reference policies maintains training stability.

**Dataset Reconstruction for Evaluation:**
To systematically evaluate Reason-RFT's visual reasoning capabilities, the authors reconstructed a comprehensive benchmark dataset across three core dimensions:
- **Visual Counting:** Derived from CLEVR-Math, with added domain-shift samples from Super-CLEVR for enhanced complexity (direct arithmetic, mixed arithmetic).
- **Structure Perception:** Curated from Geo170K and Math360K, with Geometry3K used for domain-shift evaluation (geometric problems involving angles, lengths, areas).
- **Spatial Transformation:** Generated from TRANCE, with viewpoint changes (left/right) used to assess generalization under perspective shifts (inferring object transformations).

This multi-faceted methodology, combining a structured SFT initialization with dynamic, reward-driven RL refinement and a carefully constructed evaluation benchmark, forms a robust approach to advancing visual reasoning in VLMs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the Reason-RFT framework yielded several significant findings, demonstrating its effectiveness across multiple dimensions of visual reasoning.

#### 4.1. Overall Evaluation of Reason-RFT Framework (RQ1)

- **Performance Enhancement:** Reason-RFT achieved state-of-the-art or highly competitive results across all three visual reasoning tasks (visual counting, structure perception, spatial transformation) compared to mainstream open-source and proprietary models (including GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5-Pro). Notably, in visual counting, Reason-RFT-Zero (RL-only) performed best in the 7B setting, suggesting strong inherent RL capacity.
- **Generalization Superiority:** Under domain shift (DS) conditions, Reason-RFT showed substantial gains over both traditional baselines and alternative training paradigms. For instance, in visual counting, it outperformed ANS-SFT by a notable margin (e.g., 10.95% for 2B models). In spatial transformation, the 2B Reason-RFT model surpassed GPT-4o by an impressive 31.7%, highlighting its remarkable adaptability to changes in visual perspective.
- **Data Efficiency:** Reason-RFT demonstrated high training efficiency. For the spatial transformation task, the 2B model achieved 70% of Reason-RFT-Zero's final performance with only 3% of the training data (1,600 samples) and 82.5% with 9% of the data. The 7B model showed even better efficiency, reaching over 92% of Reason-RFT-Zero’s performance with just 3% of the data. This indicates strong potential for few-shot learning scenarios.
- **General Benchmarks:** Evaluation on general benchmarks (MMMU, RealWorldQA, MathVision, AI2D) showed that Reason-RFT not only preserves but often improves general reasoning capabilities, indicating no observable performance degradation outside its specific target domains.

#### 4.2. Effect of STAGE 1 on Initialization (RQ2)

- **Consistent Gains from CoT-SFT:** Stage 1, involving SFT with CoT reasoning, consistently improved performance for Reason-RFT compared to Reason-RFT-Zero (RL without SFT initialization). This improvement was more pronounced for smaller models and tasks with complex output structures, such as spatial transformation, where the 2B Reason-RFT surpassed Reason-RFT-Zero by 33.84%.
- **Benefit for Smaller Models:** Smaller models (2B) benefited more significantly from the CoT-SFT priors, especially under challenging domain shifts (e.g., visual counting DS-M, 31.6% improvement over Reason-RFT-Zero). This indicates that CoT-SFT provides crucial inductive biases for adaptation that pure RL struggles to acquire independently.

#### 4.3. Effect of STAGE 2 on Generalization (RQ3)

- **Reinforcement Fine-Tuning Improves Generalization:** RL in Stage 2 significantly enhanced generalization beyond what CoT-SFT alone could achieve. Reason-RFT consistently outperformed CoT-SFT across all domain-shift settings. For example, in visual counting, the 7B Reason-RFT showed a 12.8% higher combined DS-D and DS-M score than CoT-SFT. In spatial transformation, the 2B Reason-RFT exceeded CoT-SFT by 21.04% on average across DS-L and DS-R. These results highlight RL's ability to facilitate better adaptation to compositional and layout-dependent variations.

#### 4.4. Exploration on Reward Design (RQ4)

- **Format Reward:** Extending the format reward with `<summary>` and `<caption>` tokens helped Reason-RFT-Zero more than Reason-RFT. This suggests that Reason-RFT, having undergone CoT supervision, already internalizes caption-like structures, while RL-only models benefit from explicit structural cues.
- **Accuracy Reward (Partial Credit):** Intriguingly, allowing "partial credit" (e.g., `α=0.50, β=0.25`) in the spatial transformation task improved in-domain performance but *harmed* generalization under domain shift. Conversely, "hard rewards" (penalizing partial matches, e.g., `α=-0.25, β=-0.50`) improved generalization under domain shift, indicating that a stricter reward signal better supports robust serialized reasoning.

#### 4.5. Training Dynamics and Reasoning Behavior Analysis (RQ5)

- **Greedy Reward Stratification:** Especially in Reason-RFT-Zero, models initially prioritize easier-to-optimize rewards (e.g., format reward), leading to rapid format compliance before accuracy significantly improves. Reasoning token length initially decreases, then gradually increases and stabilizes.
- **Transient Adaptation Gap:** Reason-RFT-Zero exhibited a temporary performance degradation early in training when shifting from direct answer prediction to structured reasoning, leading to a sharp decline and gradual recovery in accuracy. This highlights the sensitivity of early-stage RL to format constraints.
- **Reasoning Redundancy:** Reason-RFT, despite achieving similar accuracy to Reason-RFT-Zero in some tasks (e.g., structure perception), generated significantly longer reasoning traces. This is attributed to the verbose CoT data derived from advanced models (like GPT-4o) used in Stage 1, which encourages verbosity that persists in the absence of length penalties in RL.

In summary, Reason-RFT successfully addresses the generalization challenge in visual reasoning by strategically combining SFT for initial skill activation and GRPO-based RL for robust refinement. Its data efficiency and superior performance under domain shifts make it a highly promising framework for advancing VLM capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Reason-RFT framework represents a significant advancement in the field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and visual reasoning, with broad implications for both research and real-world applications.

**Scientific Significance:**

- **Novel Hybrid Paradigm:** Reason-RFT introduces the first two-stage reinforcement fine-tuning framework for visual reasoning, effectively integrating the complementary strengths of supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL). This provides a new, robust training paradigm for multimodal learning.
- **Enhanced Generalization:** By addressing the issues of overfitting and cognitive rigidity inherent in purely SFT-based methods, Reason-RFT significantly improves cross-domain transferability. This is a crucial step towards developing more robust and versatile VLMs that can function reliably in diverse and unconstrained environments.
- **Data Efficiency:** The framework's ability to achieve high performance with limited labeled data (few-shot learning) is highly impactful. It reduces the immense resource requirements for curating large-scale, high-quality CoT datasets, making advanced visual reasoning more accessible and scalable.
- **Systematic Analysis:** The paper provides a thorough comparative analysis of SFT and RL paradigms in visual reasoning, shedding light on their respective limitations and advantages. The exploration of reward design and training dynamics offers valuable insights for future research in RL for multimodal models.
- **Comprehensive Benchmark:** The reconstruction of a high-quality dataset spanning visual counting, structure perception, and spatial transformation provides a rigorous and systematic benchmark for evaluating visual cognition, geometric understanding, and spatial generalization, facilitating further research and fair comparison of methods.

**Potential Impact and Applications:**

- **Advancing Artificial General Intelligence (AGI):** Visual reasoning is a core component of human intelligence. By improving this capability in VLMs, Reason-RFT contributes directly to the long-term goal of building more general and intelligent AI systems.
- **Robotics and Embodied AI:** Enhanced visual reasoning enables robots to better understand complex scenes, plan multi-step actions, and adapt to changing environments. This could lead to more capable and autonomous robots for manufacturing, logistics, exploration, and domestic assistance. The authors' prior work on RoboBrain further underscores this direct impact.
- **Autonomous Driving:** Robust visual reasoning is critical for autonomous vehicles to interpret dynamic road conditions, understand complex interactions, and make safe driving decisions, especially in novel or ambiguous situations (domain shifts).
- **Healthcare:** Applications in medical image analysis, such as diagnosing diseases or assisting in surgical procedures, could benefit from more reliable and generalizable visual reasoning for interpreting complex medical visuals.
- **Education and Scientific Discovery:** Tools capable of advanced visual and mathematical reasoning could aid in teaching, scientific analysis, and accelerating discovery by processing and interpreting complex diagrams, charts, and experimental data.
- **Improved Human-AI Collaboration:** More reliable and interpretable reasoning processes, facilitated by frameworks like Reason-RFT, can foster greater trust and effectiveness in human-AI collaborative tasks.

**Ethical Considerations:**

The authors acknowledge potential limitations and societal impacts in their appendix, including biases in training data and the need for responsible deployment. While Reason-RFT aims for more robust and reliable AI, the broader implications of powerful AI systems, such as potential misuse or unintended consequences (e.g., in surveillance or automated decision-making), remain important ethical considerations that require ongoing diligence and mitigation strategies during development and deployment.

In conclusion, Reason-RFT offers a significant step forward in making VLMs more capable, adaptable, and efficient in understanding and reasoning about the visual world, laying a crucial groundwork for future advancements in AGI and impacting a wide array of real-world applications.
