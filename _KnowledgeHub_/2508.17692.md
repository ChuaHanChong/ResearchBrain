# LLM-based Agentic Reasoning Frameworks: A Survey from Methods to Scenarios

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from multiple international institutions propose a unified methodological taxonomy and formal language for LLM-based agentic reasoning frameworks, systematically surveying their progress, application scenarios, and evaluation strategies across diverse domains like scientific research and healthcare. This work provides a structured view of single-agent, tool-based, and multi-agent approaches, clarifying the rapidly evolving landscape.

## Problem

- The explosive growth of LLM-based agent systems has led to blurred conceptual boundaries, making it difficult to differentiate them from traditional multi-agent or autonomous systems.
- It is challenging to attribute performance enhancements in agent systems to specific framework designs versus underlying model improvements or other technological advancements.
- Existing surveys are often either model-centric, focusing on training or planning abilities, or domain-specific, lacking a unified taxonomic approach for framework-side developments.

## Method

- Developed a ==three-level progressive taxonomy== (Single-Agent, Tool-Based, Multi-Agent methods) to systematically organize and analyze agentic reasoning frameworks.
- Introduced a ==unified formal language== and a general reasoning algorithm to precisely describe complex, multi-step reasoning processes within these frameworks.
- Conducted a ==scenario-driven review== of applications, analyzing representative works and their evaluation strategies across scientific research, healthcare, software engineering, and social simulations.

## Results

- A novel **three-level** taxonomy (Single-Agent, Tool-Based, Multi-Agent methods) was successfully proposed and elaborated, providing an organizational framework for the field.
- A unified formal language and a general reasoning algorithm were introduced, offering a rigorous foundation for understanding and analyzing agentic systems.
- The utility of the proposed framework was demonstrated through a comprehensive review of diverse methodologies and their application across high-impact scenarios, including scientific research, healthcare, and software engineering.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLM-based agentic reasoning can be systematically deconstructed into single-agent, tool-based, and multi-agent components, each contributing progressively to complex task execution.
> - A formal language and algorithm can rigorously define how different methodologies enhance an agent's multi-step reasoning cycle.
> - Effective agent systems often integrate methods from multiple taxonomic levels, demonstrating synergistic potential for addressing complex, real-world problems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The emergence of Large Language Models (LLMs) has profoundly impacted various domains, but their intrinsic limitations—such as hallucinations, outdated knowledge, and high operational costs—have spurred the development of LLM-based agent systems. These agents aim to overcome single-step reasoning constraints by integrating modules like planning, memory, and tool-use, enabling complex, multi-step task execution. This field has witnessed an explosive growth since 2023, attracting significant academic and industrial interest, as evidenced by the proliferation of research papers and the integration of agentic workflows into commercial products like Microsoft’s AutoGen and autonomous AI software engineers like Devin.

However, this rapid expansion has also led to blurred conceptual boundaries, making it difficult to differentiate LLM-based agents from traditional multi-agent or autonomous systems. Moreover, attributing performance enhancements to specific framework designs versus underlying model improvements or technological advancements remains a challenge. Existing surveys often fall into two categories: those that are model-centric, focusing on training methodologies, smaller agentic models, or planning abilities (the "LLM side" of development), and those that are domain-specific, reviewing applications within fields like scientific discovery or healthcare but lacking a unified taxonomic approach.

This paper addresses these critical gaps by providing the *first survey* that systematically organizes "framework-side" developments in LLM-based agentic reasoning. Its unique contribution lies in proposing a unified methodological taxonomy that clarifies core reasoning mechanisms and methods, specifically focusing on training-free approaches. By categorizing methods into single-agent, tool-based, and multi-agent dimensions, and employing a formal language to describe their influence on multi-step reasoning, the survey offers a structured lens through which to analyze the field. Furthermore, it adopts a novel scenario-driven approach, comparing evaluation setups and datasets across diverse application domains, thus bridging the theoretical understanding of agentic frameworks with their practical implementation and performance in real-world contexts. This positions the survey as a timely and essential guide for navigating the complexities and opportunities in the rapidly evolving landscape of LLM-based agent research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this survey stems from the dual challenge presented by the rapid advancement of LLM-based agentic systems: their immense potential to overcome intrinsic LLM limitations in critical applications, juxtaposed with the conceptual ambiguity and lack of systematic classification that has emerged due to their explosive growth. While LLMs offer powerful generalization and reasoning, issues like hallucinations, reliance on outdated information, and high computational costs limit their dependability in sensitive fields such as healthcare and software engineering. Agentic reasoning frameworks, by enabling LLMs to perform complex, multi-step tasks through enhanced components like planning, memory, and tool-use, offer a pathway to more reliable and consistent outcomes.

However, the authors identify a pressing need for clarity in this burgeoning field. The boundaries of what constitutes an LLM-based agent are often unclear, overlapping with traditional multi-agent systems and autonomous systems. Critically, it is often difficult to discern whether an agent's enhanced capabilities arise from superior framework design, model-level improvements, or other technological advancements, hindering objective comparison and a deeper understanding of foundational principles.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the survey are:

- To **systematically summarize** the latest progress and application scenarios of agentic reasoning frameworks, providing a comprehensive overview of the field.
- To **clearly define the boundaries** of LLM-based agentic frameworks, thereby reducing ambiguity and facilitating focused research.
- To **propose a unified methodological classification system** that organizes the core reasoning mechanisms and methods within these frameworks.
- To **analyze the application and evaluation strategies** of these methods across a diverse array of real-world scenarios, thereby illustrating their practical utility and performance.
- To **provide a clear roadmap** for the standardized and safe development of agentic systems, which is crucial for their responsible deployment.
- Ultimately, to **equip the research community with a panoramic view**, enabling a better understanding of the strengths, suitable application contexts, and effective evaluation practices of different agentic reasoning frameworks.

By addressing these objectives, the authors aim to consolidate fragmented knowledge, establish a common lexicon, and guide future research towards more structured, efficient, and impactful development in LLM-based agentic AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopt a systematic and comprehensive approach to survey LLM-based agentic reasoning frameworks, grounded in a novel taxonomy and a formal language. Their methodology is structured as follows:

**A. Foundational Taxonomy:**
The core of their approach is a proposed three-level, progressive taxonomy for agentic reasoning frameworks, designed to systematically decompose and analyze existing methods:
1. **Single-Agent Methods:** Focus on enhancing the cognitive and decision-making abilities of an individual agent. This category is further divided into:
 - **Prompt Engineering:** Methods that enrich the agent's initial context (`C0`) through carefully crafted prompts, including role-playing (`P_role`), environment simulation (`P_env`), detailed task clarification (`P_task`), and in-context examples (`P_icl`).
 - **Self-Improvement:** Mechanisms that enable agents to dynamically adapt and refine their strategies based on experience. This includes Reflection (post-hoc analysis of actions), Iterative Optimization (refining outputs against a predefined standard until a condition is met), and Interactive Learning (dynamically adjusting high-level goals based on environmental feedback).
2. **Tool-Based Methods:** Extend an agent's reasoning capabilities by enabling interaction with external tools (`T = {t1, t2..., tn}`). This involves three stages:
 - **Tool Integration:** How tools are made accessible, categorized into API-based, Plugin-based (e.g., RAG), and Middleware-based integration.
 - **Tool Selection:** How agents choose the most suitable tool for a task, including Autonomous Selection (LLM's intrinsic reasoning), Rule-Based Selection (predefined explicit rules), and Learning-Based Selection (online refinement through feedback).
 - **Tool Utilization:** How selected tools are employed, encompassing Sequential Use (ordered tool-chains), Parallel Use (concurrent invocation of multiple tools), and Iterative Use (repeated interaction with a single tool for fine-grained objectives).
3. **Multi-Agent Methods:** Leverage the collective intelligence of multiple agents (`M = {A_r1, A_r2..., A_rn}`) to tackle complex tasks, focusing on coordination. This is broken down into:
 - **Organizational Architecture:** Defines the macro-level structure for coordination, including Centralized (a central agent manages others), Decentralized (agents have equal status and peer-to-peer communication), and Hierarchical (agents organized in a structured tree for task decomposition).
 - **Individual Interaction:** Governs how agents' goals evolve in response to others, categorized as Cooperation (maximizing collective interests), Competition (maximizing individual interests, often adversarial), and Negotiation (balancing cooperation and competition to reach consensus).

**B. Unified Formal Language:**
To ensure precision and clarity in describing the complex, multi-step reasoning processes, the authors introduce a general reasoning algorithm (Algorithm 1) and a notation table (Table 1). This formal language defines key components such as user query (`P_U`), termination condition (`Q`), goals (`g`), external tools (`t`), internal context (`C`), and various action types (`a_reason`, `a_tool`, `a_reflect`). Each method discussed in the taxonomy is then mapped to how it modifies or adjusts specific steps within this general algorithm, providing a rigorous and consistent analytical framework.

**C. Scenario-Driven Review:**
Building on the methodological taxonomy, the survey proceeds to review the application of these frameworks across diverse, real-world scenarios. These scenarios include:
- Scientific Research (Mathematics, Astrophysics, Geo-science, Biochemistry & Material Science, General Research)
- Healthcare (Diagnosis Assistance, Clinical Management, Environmental Simulation)
- Software Engineering (Code Generation and Testing, Program Repair, Full-cycle Development)
- Social and Economic Simulation (Social Simulation, Economic Perception and Simulation)
- Other emerging domains like Embodied Interaction, GUI Operation, and Strategic Reasoning.

For each scenario, the authors analyze representative works, highlighting how different methods from their taxonomy are integrated and adapted to meet specific domain requirements. They also meticulously collect and present the evaluation strategies, including metrics, benchmarks, and datasets used in these various fields.

**D. Delimitation:**
Crucially, the authors explicitly state that their survey maintains a clear focus on *framework-level reasoning*, excluding orthogonal developments in model architectures and fine-tuning techniques (e.g., Supervised Fine-Tuning, Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) to avoid diluting the primary objective of analyzing agentic design principles.

This systematic and structured approach allows the survey to provide a clear, in-depth understanding of LLM-based agentic reasoning frameworks, their underlying mechanisms, and their practical manifestations across a wide spectrum of applications.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The primary "findings" of this survey are not experimental results but rather a comprehensive synthesis and structured categorization of the rapidly evolving field of LLM-based agentic reasoning. The authors' main contributions and results can be summarized as follows:

- **A Unified, Progressive Taxonomy:** The survey successfully proposes and elaborates on a novel three-level taxonomy (Single-Agent, Tool-Based, and Multi-Agent methods). This taxonomy systematically deconstructs agentic reasoning, providing a much-needed organizational framework for a field previously characterized by blurred boundaries. Each level is progressively complex, demonstrating how foundational single-agent capabilities are augmented by external tools and further scaled through multi-agent collaboration.
- **Formalization of Reasoning Processes:** By introducing a unified formal language and a general reasoning algorithm (Algorithm 1), the authors provide a rigorous foundation for understanding agentic systems. This formalization clearly illustrates how different methodologies, from prompt engineering to complex multi-agent interactions, modify and enhance specific steps within an agent's multi-step reasoning cycle. This is a significant intellectual contribution, offering a precise analytical tool for future research.
- **Comprehensive Review of Methodologies:** The survey meticulously details various techniques within each taxonomic category.
 - **Single-Agent:** Explores how Prompt Engineering (role-playing, environment simulation, task description, in-context learning) enriches context, and Self-Improvement (reflection, iterative optimization, interactive learning) enables dynamic adaptation.
 - **Tool-Based:** Dissects Tool Integration (API, Plugin, Middleware), Tool Selection (autonomous, rule-based, learning-based), and Tool Utilization (sequential, parallel, iterative), demonstrating how agents extend their boundaries through external resources.
 - **Multi-Agent:** Analyzes Organizational Architectures (centralized, decentralized, hierarchical) and Individual Interactions (cooperation, competition, negotiation), revealing patterns for collective intelligence and task decomposition.
- **Scenario-Driven Application Analysis:** A key result is the demonstration of the taxonomy's utility by systematically applying it across diverse, high-impact application scenarios:
 - **Scientific Research:** Agents are shown to automate various stages of scientific inquiry, from mathematical problem-solving and astrophysical hypothesis generation to geospatial analysis and biochemical experiment design, often using multi-agent and tool-based strategies.
 - **Healthcare:** Agents enhance diagnostic precision, clinical management, and patient care through multi-agent dialogues, integration of knowledge graphs and multi-modal data, and sophisticated environmental simulations for training.
 - **Software Engineering:** Agents are leveraged for code generation, program repair, and full-lifecycle software development, employing structured collaboration, test-driven approaches, and dynamic interaction with development environments.
 - **Social and Economic Simulation:** LLM-based agents are capable of modeling complex human-like behaviors and market dynamics, from small-scale social interactions to large-scale economic simulations, incorporating sophisticated cognitive modules and collaborative structures.
- **Collection of Evaluation Practices:** The survey provides a valuable compilation of evaluation metrics, benchmarks, datasets, and methodologies employed across different domains. This aids researchers in selecting appropriate evaluation strategies and fosters consistency in assessing agent performance.
- **Integration of Methods:** It highlights that effective agent systems often integrate methods from multiple levels of the taxonomy (e.g., a multi-agent system where individual agents use prompt engineering and external tools). This underscores the interconnectedness and synergistic potential of the proposed framework.

In essence, the survey's main results are the successful creation of a clear, actionable, and comprehensive framework for understanding LLM-based agentic reasoning, coupled with a detailed mapping of its practical manifestations and evaluation landscapes across critical scientific and industrial domains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey on LLM-based agentic reasoning frameworks holds significant importance for the rapidly evolving field of artificial intelligence, with potential impacts across several dimensions:

- **Bringing Order to a Nascent Field:** The explosive growth of LLM-based agents has created a fragmented and often ambiguous research landscape. By introducing a unified methodological taxonomy, formal language, and systematic review, this survey acts as a crucial organizing principle. It clarifies conceptual boundaries, distinguishes framework-level innovations from model-level improvements, and provides a much-needed common vocabulary for researchers. This structured understanding is essential for enabling coherent discourse and fostering cumulative scientific progress.

- **Guiding Future Research and Development:** The comprehensive overview of methods and their applications across diverse scenarios offers a clear roadmap for researchers and developers. It helps in identifying the strengths and weaknesses of different agentic frameworks, informing the selection of appropriate methodologies for specific tasks, and guiding the design of new, more robust, and efficient agent systems. The detailed collection of evaluation setups, datasets, and metrics across domains is particularly valuable, promoting standardized assessment practices and facilitating reproducible research.

- **Accelerating Interdisciplinary Innovation:** By meticulously surveying applications in scientific discovery, healthcare, software engineering, and social/economic simulations, the paper highlights the immense cross-domain potential of LLM-based agents. This interdisciplinary perspective can inspire novel applications, encourage the transfer of best practices between fields, and foster collaborative research that tackles complex, real-world challenges. For instance, the successful deployment of agents in drug discovery or automated scientific hypothesis generation can profoundly accelerate breakthroughs in those fields.

- **Informing Ethical and Safety Considerations:** The burgeoning autonomy of LLM agents necessitates careful consideration of ethical implications, fairness, reliance, and safety. The survey's identification of future prospects explicitly addresses these concerns, calling for research into proactive bias management, ethical justification, framework-level security, and uncertainty-aware confidence estimation. By framing these as integral components of future framework design, the survey directly contributes to building more responsible and trustworthy AI systems, which is critical for their societal acceptance and deployment in sensitive areas.

- **Promoting Autonomous and Open-Ended Learning:** The vision of agents moving from "users" to "creators" of knowledge and tools is a powerful one. This survey lays the groundwork for achieving open-ended autonomous learning by advocating for frameworks capable of dynamic tool generation, adaptive resource scheduling, and continuous self-improvement. Such advancements could lead to truly adaptive and creative AI agents, capable of operating in unconstrained, evolving environments.

In conclusion, "LLM-based Agentic Reasoning Frameworks: A Survey from Methods to Scenarios" is a timely and invaluable contribution that distills the current state of LLM agent research into an accessible, organized, and forward-looking analysis. Its significance lies in its ability to clarify a rapidly expanding field, guide future research directions, foster interdisciplinary innovation, and underscore the critical importance of developing ethical, safe, and explainable agent systems for the future.
