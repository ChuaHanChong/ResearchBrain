# LLM-based Agentic Reasoning Frameworks: A Survey from Methods to Scenarios

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from multiple international institutions propose a unified methodological taxonomy and formal language for LLM-based agentic reasoning frameworks, systematically surveying their progress, application scenarios, and evaluation strategies across diverse domains like scientific research and healthcare. This work provides a structured view of single-agent, tool-based, and multi-agent approaches, clarifying the rapidly evolving landscape.

## Problem

- The explosive growth of LLM-based agent systems has led to blurred conceptual boundaries, making it difficult to differentiate them from traditional multi-agent or autonomous systems.
- It is challenging to attribute performance enhancements in agent systems to specific framework designs versus underlying model improvements or other technological advancements.
- Existing surveys are often either model-centric, focusing on training or planning abilities, or domain-specific, lacking a unified taxonomic approach for framework-side developments.

## Method

- Developed a ==three-level progressive taxonomy== (Single-Agent, Tool-Based, Multi-Agent methods) to systematically organize and analyze agentic reasoning frameworks.
- Introduced a ==unified formal language== and a general reasoning algorithm to precisely describe complex, multi-step reasoning processes within these frameworks.
- Conducted a ==scenario-driven review== of applications, analyzing representative works and their evaluation strategies across scientific research, healthcare, software engineering, and social simulations.

## Results

- A novel **three-level** taxonomy (Single-Agent, Tool-Based, Multi-Agent methods) was successfully proposed and elaborated, providing an organizational framework for the field.
- A unified formal language and a general reasoning algorithm were introduced, offering a rigorous foundation for understanding and analyzing agentic systems.
- The utility of the proposed framework was demonstrated through a comprehensive review of diverse methodologies and their application across high-impact scenarios, including scientific research, healthcare, and software engineering.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLM-based agentic reasoning can be systematically deconstructed into single-agent, tool-based, and multi-agent components, each contributing progressively to complex task execution.
> - A formal language and algorithm can rigorously define how different methodologies enhance an agent's multi-step reasoning cycle.
> - Effective agent systems often integrate methods from multiple taxonomic levels, demonstrating synergistic potential for addressing complex, real-world problems.
