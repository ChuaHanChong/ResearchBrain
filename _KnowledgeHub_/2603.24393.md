# 3D-Mix for VLA: A Plug-and-Play Module for Integrating VGGT-based 3D Information into Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed 3D-MIX, a plug-and-play module that integrates geometry-aware 3D features into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models using a semantic-conditioned adaptive gating mechanism. The module consistently improved task success rates across various VLA architectures and MLLM backbones, achieving up to +12.51% average gains on out-of-domain benchmarks.

## Problem

- Current VLA models, heavily reliant on MLLMs pre-trained on 2D image-text datasets, exhibit limited 3D spatial perception critical for precise robotic manipulation tasks.
- The lack of adequate 3D spatial intelligence hinders VLA models in tasks requiring accurate object localization, grasp pose estimation, and understanding spatial relationships.
- Existing attempts to integrate specialized 3D vision models into VLA architectures have employed varied and often unsystematic fusion strategies, leaving optimal integration mechanisms and injection points unclear.

## Method

- A systematic pilot study compared nine distinct ==VGGT-derived 3D feature fusion== schemes within a GR00T-style VLA framework to empirically identify the most effective integration strategy.
- The 3D-MIX module was developed based on the ==GatedFusion== scheme, employing a ==semantic-conditioned adaptive gating mechanism== to dynamically blend VGGT-derived 3D geometric features with MLLM-extracted 2D semantic states.
- Designed as a plug-and-play component, 3D-MIX integrates seamlessly into existing VLA architectures (GR00T-style and π-style) without requiring modifications to the MLLM or action expert components.

## Results

- The pilot study identified GatedFusion as the superior strategy, achieving **68.23%** success on the SIMPLER benchmark (**+10.42%** over baseline) and **98.05%** on LIBERO.
- 3D-MIX consistently improved task success rates across all evaluated MLLM backbones and VLA architectures, delivering an average **+7.0%** gain on the SIMPLER benchmark for GR00T-style models, with RynnBrain-8B showing a notable **+12.51%** increase.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that freezing VGGT was comparable or superior to fine-tuning, and sparse 3D feature fusion in π-style architectures achieved similar performance with reduced memory compared to full layer-wise fusion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A semantic-conditioned adaptive gating mechanism is highly effective for fusing 3D geometric and 2D semantic features, outperforming other integration strategies investigated.
> - Pre-trained 3D feature extractors, such as VGGT, can be effectively frozen during VLA training, providing robust geometric representations without requiring task-specific fine-tuning.
> - The performance gains observed are genuinely attributable to the utilization of 3D geometric information, as evidenced by significant performance degradation when VGGT features are corrupted.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a paradigm in robotic manipulation, integrating visual perception, natural language understanding, and action generation into end-to-end learned systems. These models typically leverage Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) as semantic encoders alongside specialized action prediction modules, enabling robots to interpret natural language instructions and perform diverse tasks.

Despite advancements, a recognized limitation of current VLA models stems from the MLLMs being predominantly pre-trained on 2D image-text datasets. This training regimen often results in inadequate 3D spatial perception, which is a critical capability for robotic manipulation tasks requiring precise localization, grasp pose estimation, and understanding of spatial relationships.

To address this deficiency, researchers have begun incorporating specialized 3D vision models into VLA architectures. The Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer (VGGT) has been identified as a relevant option for extracting geometric features from multi-view images. However, prior efforts to integrate VGGT into VLA systems have employed varied and often ad-hoc fusion strategies without systematic comparison. This lack of comprehensive evaluation has left open questions regarding the optimal mechanisms for combining 3D geometric information with 2D semantic understanding, including the ideal injection points and fusion techniques within the VLA pipeline. This paper aims to systematically investigate these integration strategies and propose a generalized solution.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research arises from the identified limitation of current VLA models: their restricted spatial intelligence due to the 2D nature of MLLM training data. While MLLMs excel at semantic understanding and language processing, they often struggle with the precise 3D spatial reasoning required for complex physical manipulation tasks. This deficiency manifests as difficulties in accurately localizing objects, determining appropriate grasp poses, and understanding the spatial relationships between objects in a 3D environment.

Existing approaches that attempt to mitigate this by integrating specialized 3D vision models, such as VGGT, into VLA architectures have been characterized by a diverse range of integration mechanisms. These prior works have adopted various fusion strategies without systematic investigation or comparative analysis, leading to an unclear understanding of which methods are most effective. This context motivates several key questions:
- At what point within the VLA processing pipeline should 3D features be introduced?
- How can 3D geometric information and 2D semantic information be effectively combined?
- What fusion mechanisms facilitate robust and effective cross-modal interaction between these different types of information?

In response to these motivations and questions, the paper outlines the following key objectives:
1. **Systematic Evaluation of VGGT Integration Strategies:** To conduct a comprehensive empirical study comparing different methods of integrating VGGT-derived 3D features into VLA models. This involves evaluating a range of distinct fusion schemes across the entire VLA pipeline.
2. **Identification of Optimal Fusion Strategy:** To identify the most effective architectural pattern for 3D feature integration by analyzing the performance of the evaluated fusion schemes on standardized benchmarks.
3. **Development of a Plug-and-Play Module (3D-MIX):** To propose and implement 3D-MIX, a lightweight, modular component that integrates VGGT-derived 3D features into VLA systems using a principled semantic-conditioned adaptive gating mechanism. The module is designed to be plug-and-play, requiring no modifications to existing MLLM or action expert components.
4. **Demonstration of Generalizability:** To validate the proposed 3D-MIX module's effectiveness and generalizability across various VLA architectures (specifically GR00T-style and π-style) and a diverse set of MLLM backbones, spanning different model series and parameter scales.
5. **Provision of Practical Guidelines:** To offer empirical insights and practical recommendations for enhancing spatial intelligence in VLA systems, thereby contributing to the broader research community.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a multi-stage approach, commencing with a pilot study to systematically evaluate various 3D feature fusion schemes, followed by the development and extensive validation of the proposed 3D-MIX module.

#### 3.1. Pilot Study: Comparative Analysis of VGGT Fusion Schemes

**Base VLA Architecture:**
A GR00T-style VLA framework was adopted for the pilot study. This architecture consists of an MLLM backbone (specifically Qwen3-VL) responsible for encoding multi-view images and language instructions into hidden states. These hidden states then serve as input for a Diffusion Transformer (DiT)-based flow-matching action expert, which predicts future robotic actions. This modular design facilitates the isolated evaluation of 3D fusion mechanisms.

**VGGT Fusion Schemes:**
Nine distinct fusion schemes for integrating VGGT-derived 3D geometric features were investigated, varying in their injection point and mechanism:
1. **AE-Fusion:** Augments the action expert with a secondary cross-attention head to attend to VGGT features in parallel with MLLM semantic features during denoising.
2. **Early Fusion:** Injects projected VGGT tokens directly into the MLLM's input sequence, allowing implicit interaction via self-attention.
3. **Concat Fusion:** VGGT features, preprocessed by a GateMixer module, are directly concatenated with the MLLM's output hidden states.
4. **CrossAttn Fusion:** Similar to Concat Fusion, but applies an explicit residual cross-attention between GateMixer-processed VGGT features and MLLM outputs before concatenation.
5. **Gated Fusion:** Employs learnable gates to dynamically balance semantic and geometric features at each token position, conditioned on a global semantic context derived from the MLLM output.
6. **3D-Tokens:** Appends a special learnable token `⟨|vggt|⟩` to the MLLM input, supervised to encode 3D geometry via an auxiliary alignment loss during training. VGGT is not required at inference.
7. **Middle Layer Injection:** Injects projected VGGT features into an intermediate MLLM layer via an adapter-style cross-attention mechanism.
8. **Spatial Forcing:** Supervises intermediate MLLM layers with an alignment loss to geometric features during training. VGGT is discarded at inference.
9. **Visual Fusion:** Fuses VGGT 3D tokens with the MLLM’s 2D visual tokens via cross-attention before processing by the MLLM backbone.

**Experimental Setup for Comparative Evaluation:**
- **Models:** Qwen3-VL-4B as the MLLM backbone and VGGT-1B as the frozen 3D feature extractor.
- **Training:** All models trained for 60,000 steps on 8 NVIDIA H100 GPUs, using DeepSpeed ZeRO-2. A per-device batch size of 16 (global batch size 128) was used. MLLM backbone learning rate was 10⁻⁵, while action expert and fusion parameters used 10⁻⁴, both with AdamW optimizer and cosine learning rate schedule.
- **Datasets:** Trained on the BridgeV2 subset of Open X-Embodiment. Evaluated on SIMPLER (a real-to-simulation out-of-domain benchmark) for cross-domain generalization, and LIBERO (an in-domain multi-task benchmark across four task suites: Spatial, Object, Goal, Long-Horizon) for in-domain multi-task learning.
- **Metric:** Average task success rate.

#### 3.2. 3D-MIX Module Design

Based on the pilot study results, **GatedFusion** was selected as the foundation for the 3D-MIX module.
- **Feature Extraction and Projection:** VGGT extracts geometry-aware patch tokens from multi-view RGB images. These are then linearly projected to align with the MLLM's hidden dimension. The MLLM concurrently processes images and language to produce semantic hidden states.
- **Semantic-Conditioned Gating:** A global semantic summary is derived by mean-pooling the MLLM hidden states. This context is broadcast to each geometric token position. A position-specific gating weight is computed by passing the concatenated semantic context and geometric feature through a learnable gating network with sigmoid activation.
- **Adaptive Feature Fusion:** The fused representation at each position is obtained by a weighted blending of independently projected semantic and geometric features, using the computed gating weight.
- **Enriched Conditioning Sequence:** The set of fused geometric tokens is concatenated with the original MLLM hidden states to form an enriched conditioning sequence.

#### 3.3. Integration with VLA Architectures

- **GR00T-style Architecture:** 3D-MIX is inserted as a bridge between the MLLM and the action expert. The MLLM's final-layer hidden states and VGGT features are input to 3D-MIX, which outputs the enriched conditioning sequence. The DiT action expert then performs cross-attention over this sequence during denoising.
- **π-style Architecture:** This architecture utilizes layer-wise cross-attention, extracting hidden states from multiple MLLM layers and connecting them to corresponding DiT layers. For π-style, 3D-MIX applies its gating mechanism independently at each layer. VGGT features are extracted once and reused across all layers, but layer-specific semantic contexts are used to generate layer-specific fused features. Each DiT transformer block then attends to its corresponding layer-specific enriched conditioning sequence.

#### 3.4. Implementation as a Plug-and-Play Module

The 3D-MIX module is designed to be plug-and-play, requiring only the MLLM hidden states and VGGT-extracted geometric features as inputs. It operates without modifying the MLLM or action expert source code. The integration protocol involves loading a frozen pre-trained VGGT, inserting 3D-MIX, and training end-to-end with standard flow-matching objectives.

#### 3.5. Main Experiments and Ablation Studies

- **Main Experiments:** 3D-MIX was evaluated on both GR00T-style (six MLLM series, nine model variants from 2B to 8B parameters, including Qwen2.5-VL, Qwen3-VL, RoboBrain2/2.5, MimoEmbodied, RynnBrain) and π-style (a representative subset of five model variants) architectures. The same training and evaluation protocols as the pilot study were used, with a reduced batch size for π-style models due to higher memory demands.
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **VGGT Frozen vs. Trainable:** Investigated whether to freeze or fine-tune the pre-trained VGGT model.
 - **3D Information Sensitivity:** Corrupted VGGT features at inference (replaced with zeros or Gaussian noise) to assess dependency on geometric information.
 - **Sparse Layer Fusion Depth (π-style):** Explored fusing 3D features at skipped layers (every k layers) in π-style architectures to analyze performance-memory trade-offs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the integration of 3D geometric information into VLA models.

#### 4.1. Pilot Study Results

- **Identification of GatedFusion:** Among the nine systematically evaluated VGGT fusion schemes, GatedFusion demonstrated the highest average success rates on both benchmarks. On the SIMPLER (out-of-domain) benchmark, GatedFusion achieved 68.23% success, a 10.42% increase over the base model's 57.81%. On the LIBERO (in-domain) benchmark, it also achieved the highest performance at 98.05%. This indicated that a semantic-conditioned adaptive gating mechanism was effective in selectively incorporating 3D geometric cues based on task context. This finding served as the empirical basis for the design of the 3D-MIX module.

#### 4.2. 3D-MIX Performance Across Architectures and MLLM Backbones

- **Consistent Performance Gains:** 3D-MIX consistently improved performance across all evaluated MLLM backbones in both GR00T-style and π-style VLA architectures.
- **GR00T-style Architecture:** On the SIMPLER benchmark, 3D-MIX delivered an average gain of +7.0% across all nine GR00T-style variants. Notable improvements were observed for RynnBrain-8B (+12.51%), RoboBrain2.0-7B (+11.39%), MimoEmbodied-7B (+10.41%), and Qwen3-VL-4B (+10.42%). Even strong baselines like RoboBrain2.5-8B (64.58%) showed a gain of +1.05% with 3D-MIX. On the LIBERO benchmark, 3D-MIX also provided consistent, albeit smaller, gains (e.g., Qwen3-VL-4B improved by +1.55%).
- **π-style Architecture:** 3D-MIX similarly showed consistent gains for π-style architectures on SIMPLER, including Qwen3-VL-4B (+6.77%), Qwen3-VL-2B (+5.38%), and RoboBrain2.5-4B (+2.60%). These results indicated the module's generalizability across diverse backbone capacities and baseline strengths.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies Results

- **VGGT Frozen vs. Trainable:** Freezing the pre-trained VGGT model yielded performance comparable to or superior to fine-tuning it. This suggests that the pre-trained geometric representations from VGGT are sufficiently robust and transferable to manipulation tasks without requiring task-specific adaptation of the 3D encoder's weights.
- **3D Information Sensitivity:** Corrupting VGGT features at inference time, either by replacing them with zero vectors or random Gaussian noise, consistently led to performance degradation. This indicates that the observed performance improvements attributed to 3D-MIX are genuinely due to its utilization of 3D geometric information, rather than merely an increase in feature dimensionality.
- **Sparse Layer Fusion Depth (π-style):** For π-style architectures, sparse fusion (injecting 3D features every *k* skipped layers) achieved performance comparable to or better than full layer-wise fusion, with reduced memory costs. This finding suggests that sparse fusion is a practical option for resource-constrained deployments of π-style VLA models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by systematically addressing the challenge of integrating 3D spatial intelligence into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The work clarifies effective strategies for combining 2D semantic information from Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) with 3D geometric features from specialized vision models like VGGT.

The systematic pilot study of nine different fusion schemes provides empirical evidence for the efficacy of semantic-conditioned adaptive gating. This finding offers specific guidance for researchers and practitioners, moving beyond ad-hoc integration methods towards a more principled approach.

The developed 3D-MIX module represents a practical and extensible contribution. Its plug-and-play design ensures compatibility with a range of existing VLA architectures (both GR00T-style and π-style) and diverse MLLM backbones (across various scales and series) without necessitating modifications to the core MLLM or action expert components. This design characteristic lowers the barrier to entry for incorporating 3D spatial understanding into existing VLA systems.

The consistent performance improvements observed across multiple benchmarks, including significant gains on the out-of-domain SIMPLER benchmark, highlight 3D-MIX's ability to enhance robustness and generalization in robotic manipulation tasks. The ablation studies further support the conclusion that these gains are attributable to the effective integration of genuine 3D geometric information.

The central finding that effective 3D integration depends on the placement of fusion and the adaptive balancing of semantic and geometric information provides a foundational insight for future research. This work may facilitate the development of more spatially aware robot learning systems, potentially leading to robots capable of performing more precise and robust manipulation in complex, real-world environments. The availability of 3D-MIX as a modular component could accelerate the adoption of advanced 3D perception in the broader robotics research community.
