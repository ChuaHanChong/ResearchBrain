# Xiaomi-Robotics-1: Scaling Vision-Language-Action Models with over 100K Hours of Real-World Trajectories

> [!summary] Summary
> Xiaomi-Robotics-1, developed by Xiaomi Robotics, scales vision-language-action (VLA) models using over 100,000 hours of real-world manipulation trajectories, demonstrating improved out-of-the-box performance and efficient adaptation to novel tasks. This work provides empirical evidence that increasing data and model size enhances generalization, achieving state-of-the-art results across various robot manipulation benchmarks.

## Problem

- Robotics faces a critical data bottleneck, as traditional real-robot data collection is slow, expensive, and yields limited diversity in tasks and environments.
- Developing generalist robot policies capable of performing diverse tasks in unseen environments remains challenging due to insufficient broadly generalizable training data.
- Aligning broad action generation capabilities learned from descriptive state-transition language with imperative human instructions is necessary for practical robot deployment.

## Method

- Introduced **Xiaomi-Robotics-1**, an end-to-end VLA model based on a ==Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT)== architecture, integrating a pre-trained Vision-Language Model with a ==Diffusion Transformer==.
- Developed a large-scale data curation strategy, collecting over 100,000 hours of real-world ==UMI trajectories==, enhanced by an automated VLM-based ==auto-labeling pipeline==.
- Implemented a ==two-stage training recipe==: pre-training for broad action generation and post-training for cross-embodiment transfer and alignment with imperative instructions, using a unified action vector for heterogeneous embodiments.

## Results

- The 5B model's out-of-the-box success rate in novel environments increased from **26%** (without action pre-training) to **75%** (with 100% of 20k pre-training data), showing monotonic improvement with data scale.
- Scaling model size from 2B to 10B increased overall success rates in novel environments from **61%** to **79%**, indicating that both data and model size are effective for performance enhancement.
- Achieved state-of-the-art results across multiple simulation benchmarks, including a **10.8 percentage point** improvement on RoboCasa365 (**57.4%** success) and a **7%** absolute improvement on RoboDojo (**20.07** score).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Scaling pre-training data volume consistently decreases validation action errors and translates to significantly improved out-of-the-box performance in novel environments during post-training.
> - Increasing model size also contributes to improved action prediction precision and post-training success rates, acting as a complementary factor to data scaling for enhanced generalization.
> - The combination of large-scale pre-training on diverse UMI data and targeted post-training enables efficient adaptation to new, complex tasks with minimal fine-tuning data, outperforming previous baselines.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large models, such as large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs), has demonstrated that scaling data and model size can lead to significant performance improvements and emergent capabilities. This observation has motivated researchers in robotics to explore similar scaling paradigms for vision-language-action (VLA) models and world-action models (WAMs), with the goal of developing generalist robot policies.

However, robotics faces a distinct data bottleneck. Traditional real-robot teleoperation for data collection is often slow, expensive, and limited by hardware availability. This typically results in datasets that are redundant, narrow in task scope, and restricted in environmental diversity. Previous efforts to address this bottleneck include leveraging portable Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI) devices to collect diverse real-world manipulation trajectories without requiring physical robots. Another approach involves using egocentric human manipulation data, which is then converted for robot use through representation alignment or motion retargeting.

This paper positions Xiaomi-Robotics-1 as a foundational VLA model that aims to overcome the data limitation by adopting a large-scale data collection and training approach. It draws inspiration from the success of large models by scaling data volume, leveraging UMI devices, and introducing an auto-labeling pipeline to create a diverse and semantically rich dataset. The work contributes to the growing field of robot foundation models, which seek to enable robust generalization across various environments and efficient adaptation to new tasks by training on extensive datasets. It specifically contributes to the VLA paradigm, which utilizes pre-trained vision-language models as backbones for action prediction, and investigates the scaling behaviors of such models empirically.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop **Xiaomi-Robotics-1**, a foundational vision-language-action (VLA) model capable of:
1. Following diverse language instructions to perform a wide range of mobile manipulation tasks in environments not encountered during training ("out-of-the-box" capability).
2. Efficiently adapting to novel downstream tasks with minimal fine-tuning data.

The motivation for pursuing these objectives is multi-faceted:

- **Leveraging Scaling Principles:** The success of large language models and vision-language models has demonstrated that substantial performance gains and generalizable capabilities can emerge when training data and model sizes are scaled. The authors are motivated to apply these scaling principles to robot learning, anticipating similar benefits for generalist robot policies.
- **Addressing the Robotics Data Bottleneck:** Conventional robot data collection methods, such as real-robot teleoperation, are resource-intensive, slow, and typically yield datasets lacking broad diversity in tasks and environments. This limits the generalizability of trained policies. A key motivation is to overcome this bottleneck by developing scalable data collection and annotation techniques, specifically using UMI devices and an automated labeling pipeline.
- **Bridging Descriptive and Imperative Language:** The initial broad action generation capabilities learned from diverse, descriptive state-transition language need to be aligned with the imperative instructions humans naturally use to command robots. This alignment is critical for practical deployment and intuitive human-robot interaction.
- **Investigating Scaling Properties:** A central motivation is to systematically investigate the scaling properties of VLA models. This includes understanding how model performance improves with increasing data scale and model size during pre-training, and crucially, whether these improvements translate to enhanced out-of-the-box performance and data efficiency in real-world robot applications after post-training. This empirical investigation aims to guide future research directions in scalable robot learning.
- **Advancing General-Purpose Robot Manipulation:** By demonstrating strong generalization, adaptation, and state-of-the-art performance on various benchmarks, the work aims to contribute to the long-term goal of developing truly general-purpose robots capable of operating effectively in unconstrained, real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology for Xiaomi-Robotics-1 involves a two-stage training recipe: pre-training and post-training, supported by a specialized model architecture and a large-scale data curation strategy.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

Xiaomi-Robotics-1 employs an end-to-end VLA model built upon a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture. This architecture integrates a pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM) (specifically Qwen3-VL) with a Diffusion Transformer (DiT).

- **Input Processing:** The VLM receives the current observation (image) and a language instruction.
- **Action Generation:** The DiT generates action chunks, conditioned on the robot's proprioceptive state and the key-value (KV) cache produced by the VLM. Action generation is performed via flow-matching. The loss function for flow-matching is defined as $L_{Flow}(\theta) = ||v_{\theta}(o_t, l, s_t, \tilde{a}_{\tau_{t:t+H}}, \tau) - u(\tilde{a}_{\tau_{t:t+H}}, a_{t:t+H}, \tau)||_2^2$, where $\tau$ is the flow-matching timestep sampled from a Beta distribution, $\tilde{a}_{\tau_{t:t+H}}$ is noisy action, and $u$ is the target velocity field. Adaptive normalization layers (adaLN) are used to inject the timestep condition into the DiT. Inference uses a 5-step Euler integration for action recovery.
- **Auxiliary Supervision:** To accelerate convergence, an auxiliary action-generation supervision is introduced on the VLM using Choice Policies. The VLM predicts multiple candidate action chunks and associated scores. A winner-takes-all approach selects the candidate with the smallest L1 loss against the ground truth. The regression loss $L_{Regression}(\theta)$ minimizes the L1 distance for the chosen action and L2 loss for score prediction.
- **Attention Mechanism:** The DiT tokens are specifically constrained to attend only to the VLM's representations of language instructions and visual observations. Action-related tokens from the VLM are excluded from DiT's attention computation to prevent shortcut learning where the DiT might simply copy VLM-generated actions instead of learning to ground its own generation in visual and textual context.
- **Model Scaling Variants:** Three model sizes are developed: 2B (2.6B total parameters), 5B (5.1B total parameters), and 10B (10.5B total parameters), varying VLM and DiT hidden sizes and layers.

#### 3.2. Training & Data: Pre-training

The objective of pre-training is to endow the model with broad and generalizable action-generation capabilities.

- **Data Source:** Over 100,000 hours of real-world manipulation trajectories are collected using Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI) handheld grippers with egocentric cameras. This dataset spans diverse environments (households, commercial premises, industrial sites, offices, outdoor spaces) and tasks, aiming for high volume and diversity.
- **Auto-labeling Pipeline:** To scale language annotation, a pipeline is developed that automatically divides each trajectory into fixed-length segments. A pre-trained VLM (Qwen3.5-27B) is then used to caption the state transitions of both the grippers and interacting objects within each segment. This process uses a producer-consumer architecture for efficiency, labeling the entire corpus in approximately two weeks. The model learns to generate actions that transition the scene from the current state to the state described by these language annotations.
- **Pre-training Objective:** The model is optimized by jointly minimizing the flow-matching loss ($L_{Flow}$) from the DiT and the regression loss ($L_{Regression}$) from the VLM choice policy. To preserve the VLM's vision-language capabilities, the model is co-trained on a high-quality vision-language dataset using a next-token prediction objective ($L_{NTP}$). The overall loss is $L = L_{Flow} + L_{Regression} + \lambda L_{NTP}$, with $\lambda=0.1$. Vision-language data and UMI trajectories are sampled at a 1:9 ratio. Training throughput is maximized by packing tokens and amortizing VLM computation by sampling four flow-matching timesteps per sample.

#### 3.2.2. Post-training

The goal of post-training is two-fold: to transfer UMI-based action generation capabilities to robot embodiments and to align language conditioning from state-transition descriptions to imperative instructions.

- **Data Source:** The post-training dataset comprises approximately 10,000 hours of cross-embodiment manipulation trajectories. This includes:
 - Over 7,200 hours of in-house robot data collected with mobile manipulators and dual-arm robots across diverse household environments, with language instructions annotated by Qwen3.5.
 - Over 1,000 hours of human-annotated UMI data, labeled with temporal segments and imperative language instructions.
 - Open-source robot datasets such as Bridge V2, RT-1, and DROID. Idle segments are filtered out.
- **Action Space Alignment:** For arm actions, relative delta end-effector (EE) poses are used, and the orientation of EE frames is unified across all robot and UMI data to ensure consistent action values for similar motions regardless of hardware. For mobile robot data, base velocity and relative delta of the waist position are used. A unified action vector is adopted for all trajectory data, with masking for dimensions corresponding to missing action components in heterogeneous embodiments.
- **Post-training Objective:** The model is trained with the same objective function as in pre-training (Equation 1). Data sources (vision-language, open-source robot, instruction-labeled UMI, in-house robot) are sampled at a ratio of 0.5:0.5:0.5:8.5.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments investigate the scaling properties of Xiaomi-Robotics-1 and its performance in various settings.

#### 4.1. Pre-training: Data and Model Scaling

- **Data Scaling:** Experiments with the Xiaomi-Robotics-1-5B variant, trained on progressively larger subsets of 20,000 hours of UMI data (12.5%, 25%, 50%, 100%), demonstrated a monotonic decrease in validation action errors (MSE) as the data scale increased. Smaller data subsets (12.5%, 25%) showed signs of overfitting, with validation error increasing after an initial decrease, while larger subsets (50%, 100%) exhibited consistent error reduction.
- **Model Scaling:** Training the 2B, 5B, and 10B variants on 20,000 hours of UMI data revealed that action prediction precision consistently improved with larger model sizes. However, the performance gains from increasing model size were less pronounced than those observed with data scaling. This suggests that for the current dataset, data volume may be a more significant bottleneck for further generalization than model capacity.

#### 4.2. Post-training: Out-of-the-Box Evaluation in Novel Environments

Post-training experiments assessed the model's performance in unseen environments without task-specific or environment-specific fine-tuning. Evaluation was conducted on four tasks: shoe storage, bag packing, table organization, and sofa tidying.

- **Effectiveness of Scaling Pre-training Data:** The benefits of scaling pre-training data transferred to post-training performance. The overall success rate monotonically increased from 26% (baseline without action pre-training) to 75% (with 100% of the 20k pre-training data) for the 5B model. Performance gains were particularly evident in contact-rich manipulation tasks, such as shoe tidying, where the model pre-trained on 100% data achieved a 75% success rate compared to the baseline's complete failure. The performance continued to improve, without signs of saturation, as pre-training data increased.
- **Effectiveness of Scaling Model Size:** Scaling the model size during post-training also led to improved performance. The overall success rate increased from 61% (2B variant) to 75% (5B variant) and 79% (10B variant), all initialized from models pre-trained on 20k hours of UMI data. Significant gains were observed in tasks like shoe tidying (58% for 2B to 92% for 10B). These results indicate that pre-training data scale and model size are complementary factors for enhancing out-of-the-box performance in out-of-distribution settings.

#### 4.3. Downstream Fine-tuning: Efficient Adaptation to New Tasks

The model's ability to adapt to novel tasks with minimal data was evaluated on four new, challenging tasks: phone packing, laundry loading, printer refilling, and box packing. Fine-tuning was performed in both high-data (144 hours total) and low-data (36 hours total, averaging <10 hours/task) settings.

- **Performance vs. Baselines:** Xiaomi-Robotics-1 consistently outperformed baseline methods (π0.5 and Xiaomi-Robotics-0) in both low-data and high-data settings. In the low-data setting, it achieved an average success rate of 75% and average progress of 90%, substantially higher than π0.5 's 40% success rate and 66% progress.
- **Task-Specific Strengths:** The model demonstrated strong performance in tasks requiring dexterous manipulation (phone packing), handling deformable objects (printer refilling, improving success from 20% to 70% in low-data), and long-horizon mobile manipulation (laundry loading, 80% success rate in low-data). These results indicate the data efficiency benefits derived from large-scale pre-training and careful post-training alignment.

#### 4.4. Simulation Benchmarks

Xiaomi-Robotics-1 established state-of-the-art results across four simulation benchmarks:

- **RoboCasa:** Achieved an average success rate of 74.5%.
- **RoboCasa365:** Attained a 57.4% success rate, a 10.8 percentage point improvement over the previous best of 46.6%. The most significant improvement was observed on the challenging Composite-Unseen split, demonstrating generalization capabilities to novel task compositions.
- **VLABench:** Secured the highest average success rate (59.1%) and progress score (70.3%), while maintaining a competitive intention score (69.9%). The model showed robustness to semantic and distribution shifts, with notable outperformance (6.0 and 15.2 percentage points in success rate) under cross-category and texture shifts, respectively.
- **RoboDojo:** Achieved an average score of 20.07, an absolute improvement of 7% over the second-best method (13.07). It ranked first in four out of five capability dimensions (Generalization, Precision, Long-Horizon, Open), demonstrating strong overall performance across diverse task types. Although not explicitly modeling memory, it still significantly outperformed other models on the memory dimension.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Xiaomi-Robotics-1 demonstrates the viability and benefits of scaling vision-language-action models for robotics. Its significance and potential impact are multifold:

- **Empirical Validation of Scaling Laws in Robotics:** The work provides empirical evidence that scaling data and model size leads to consistent performance improvements in VLA models, a finding consistent with observations in large language and vision models. This suggests that continued investment in large-scale data collection and model development can further advance robot capabilities.
- **Advancement in Generalizable Robot Policies:** Xiaomi-Robotics-1 exhibits the ability to perform a broad range of mobile manipulation tasks in unseen environments without prior task-specific fine-tuning (out-of-the-box performance). This represents a step towards creating generalist robots that are less constrained by specific environments or tasks.
- **Efficient Adaptation to Novel Tasks:** The model's capacity for efficient fine-tuning on new, complex, and dexterous tasks with minimal data is notable. This characteristic is crucial for practical robot deployment, reducing the time and resources required to adapt robots to new operational demands.
- **Overcoming the Data Bottleneck:** The proposed scalable auto-labeling pipeline for UMI trajectory data addresses a critical challenge in robotics data collection. By efficiently annotating massive amounts of real-world data with semantically rich descriptions, this approach paves the way for curating larger and more diverse datasets for future robot learning research.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** Achieving state-of-the-art results across multiple challenging real-robot and simulation benchmarks (RoboCasa, RoboCasa365, VLABench, RoboDojo) validates the effectiveness of the proposed architecture and training methodology. The improvements, particularly in generalization to unseen task compositions and robustness to environmental shifts, signify progress in the field.
- **Foundation for Future Research and Deployment:** By releasing code and model checkpoints, the authors intend to contribute to the broader robotics research community, fostering further exploration and development of scalable robot policies. The findings suggest a pathway toward robots capable of autonomous operation in complex, unconstrained real-world settings, potentially impacting industries ranging from logistics and manufacturing to personal assistance.
