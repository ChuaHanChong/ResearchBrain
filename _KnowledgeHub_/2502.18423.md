# Retrieval Dexterity: Efficient Object Retrieval in Clutters with Dexterous Hand

> [!summary] Summary
> The "Retrieval Dexterity" system, developed by researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University and Peking University, enables a dexterous arm-hand robot to efficiently retrieve target objects from cluttered environments. Trained with large-scale reinforcement learning, it achieved significantly higher success rates and reduced retrieval steps by up to 90% in simulation and over 50% in real-world scenarios compared to baselines.

## Problem

- Traditional robotic methods for object retrieval in clutter are time-consuming, often requiring sequential grasping and removal of occluding items.
- Existing approaches struggle with generalization to diverse and novel objects, frequently relying on accurate pose estimation which is prone to errors and hinders adaptation.
- Manipulating cluttered environments with high-dimensional dexterous hands presents significant challenges due to complex contact dynamics and large state-action spaces.

## Method

- A "**Retrieval Dexterity**" system leverages a 6-DOF robotic arm integrated with a multi-finger dexterous hand, trained using large-scale parallel reinforcement learning.
- A fine-grained, potential-based reward function incentivizes the robot to learn emergent manipulation skills like pushing, stirring, and poking to efficiently clear occlusions.
- Policies are trained in diverse simulated cluttered environments using ==Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)== and then transferred to a real robot via behavior cloning and a Transformer-based student policy, relying on simplified visual observations.

## Results

- In simulation, the method achieved up to a **59.4%** higher retrieval success rate for unseen large objects and reduced retrieval steps by **90%** compared to baselines.
- Real-world experiments demonstrated successful zero-shot transfer, reducing retrieval time by an average of **51.2%** against VMP and **61.9%** against sequential grasping and removal.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of specific reward components, such as `r_stir` for displacing clutter and `r_clean` for focused clearing, and highlighted that potential-based reward shaping improved generalization by an average of **100%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Active, skill-based manipulation (e.g., pushing, stirring) with a dexterous hand is substantially more efficient for clearing clutter than conventional sequential grasping and removal of occluding objects.
> - Careful design of a potential-based reward function is crucial for successfully learning complex, contact-rich dexterous manipulation skills and enhancing generalization in reinforcement learning.
> - Effective zero-shot Sim2Real transfer for high-dimensional dexterous manipulation can be achieved by distilling expert policies into a student network that uses simplified observations, combined with robust real-time vision tracking.
