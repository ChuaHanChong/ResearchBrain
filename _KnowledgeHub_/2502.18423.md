# Retrieval Dexterity: Efficient Object Retrieval in Clutters with Dexterous Hand

> [!summary] Summary
> The "Retrieval Dexterity" system, developed by researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University and Peking University, enables a dexterous arm-hand robot to efficiently retrieve target objects from cluttered environments. Trained with large-scale reinforcement learning, it achieved significantly higher success rates and reduced retrieval steps by up to 90% in simulation and over 50% in real-world scenarios compared to baselines.

## Problem

- Traditional robotic methods for object retrieval in clutter are time-consuming, often requiring sequential grasping and removal of occluding items.
- Existing approaches struggle with generalization to diverse and novel objects, frequently relying on accurate pose estimation which is prone to errors and hinders adaptation.
- Manipulating cluttered environments with high-dimensional dexterous hands presents significant challenges due to complex contact dynamics and large state-action spaces.

## Method

- A "**Retrieval Dexterity**" system leverages a 6-DOF robotic arm integrated with a multi-finger dexterous hand, trained using large-scale parallel reinforcement learning.
- A fine-grained, potential-based reward function incentivizes the robot to learn emergent manipulation skills like pushing, stirring, and poking to efficiently clear occlusions.
- Policies are trained in diverse simulated cluttered environments using ==Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)== and then transferred to a real robot via behavior cloning and a Transformer-based student policy, relying on simplified visual observations.

## Results

- In simulation, the method achieved up to a **59.4%** higher retrieval success rate for unseen large objects and reduced retrieval steps by **90%** compared to baselines.
- Real-world experiments demonstrated successful zero-shot transfer, reducing retrieval time by an average of **51.2%** against VMP and **61.9%** against sequential grasping and removal.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of specific reward components, such as `r_stir` for displacing clutter and `r_clean` for focused clearing, and highlighted that potential-based reward shaping improved generalization by an average of **100%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Active, skill-based manipulation (e.g., pushing, stirring) with a dexterous hand is substantially more efficient for clearing clutter than conventional sequential grasping and removal of occluding objects.
> - Careful design of a potential-based reward function is crucial for successfully learning complex, contact-rich dexterous manipulation skills and enhancing generalization in reinforcement learning.
> - Effective zero-shot Sim2Real transfer for high-dimensional dexterous manipulation can be achieved by distilling expert policies into a student network that uses simplified observations, combined with robust real-time vision tracking.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation in cluttered and unstructured environments is a core area of embodied intelligence research. A significant challenge arises when target objects are heavily occluded, preventing robots from obtaining essential information like precise poses or feasible grasp points through non-contact sensing alone. Existing robotic manipulation systems often struggle with tasks requiring active interaction with clutter to expose hidden objects.

Prior work has explored combining non-prehensile and prehensile actions, typically within hierarchical or sequential frameworks. These approaches often involve preparatory actions, such as pushing, to create favorable conditions for subsequent grasping. However, these interactions are frequently treated as isolated auxiliary steps, leading to inefficiencies. Many methods are limited by the capabilities of parallel-jaw grippers, simplifying pushing motions to linear clearing actions. Even studies employing dexterous hands have primarily focused on learning a limited set of discrete interaction-grasp combinations or on the sequential removal of occluding objects. These approaches often assume a relatively complete world state before decision-making, which is not always available in heavily occluded scenes, and they typically do not model scene exploration and target retrieval as a continuous and deeply coupled process.

RetrDex addresses these limitations by developing a framework that leverages the full potential of dexterous hands for a wider range of continuous interactions beyond discrete pre-grasp motions. Instead of relying on sequential removal or treating physical interactions as auxiliary steps, RetrDex enables robots to learn diverse manipulation skills—including stirring, poking, and reorienting—that actively clear occluders. This approach shifts from a perception-planning-action paradigm that assumes complete state information to one that actively explores and perceives through physical interaction, aiming for a more efficient and robust object retrieval process in cluttered environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient retrieval policy for target objects in cluttered scenes using a dexterous arm-hand system. This policy is designed to be trained in simulation and subsequently achieve zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to a physical robot.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in object retrieval within cluttered environments:
- **Heavy Occlusion:** Target objects are often buried under other items, making it difficult for robots to perceive their precise location or grasp points using standard visual or non-contact sensors. This limitation highlights a fundamental drawback of traditional perception-planning-action paradigms.
- **Inefficiency of Sequential Methods:** Current methods that rely on sequentially removing occluding objects or performing preparatory pushes as isolated steps are often time-consuming and inefficient. They typically simplify interactions due to limitations of parallel-jaw grippers or treat physical interactions as auxiliary actions before grasping.
- **Underutilization of Dexterous Hands:** While dexterous hands offer the potential for complex, multi-fingered manipulation, existing robotic systems often do not fully exploit this capability for active clutter manipulation. Instead, their use is frequently restricted to a narrow set of interaction-grasp combinations.
- **Need for Continuous Interaction:** To operate effectively in highly cluttered scenes, robots require the ability to actively explore and perceive by continuously interacting with the environment, rather than passively observing. This necessitates developing policies that can learn a wide range of flexible, fine-grained manipulation skills.

By addressing these challenges, the authors aim to enable robots to autonomously discover efficient strategies for resolving occlusion and retrieving objects, moving beyond the restrictions of sequential decision frameworks and fully leveraging the dexterity of multi-fingered hands.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RetrDex is a framework for dexterous object retrieval in cluttered scenes, comprising three main components: diverse clutter construction, reinforcement learning (RL) problem formulation, and retrieval policy training, with a subsequent sim-to-real transfer step.

#### 3.1. Task Construction

The research simulates realistic cluttered scenarios by dropping 20+ household objects of varying masses, sizes, and shapes into a box, with the target object initially placed at the bottom. For each simulation episode, the target object and its pose are varied. To ensure reliable reward computation and prevent hand interference, the robot hand uses a "suspending pose" during object dropping and reward evaluation, returning to a "prepare pose" to begin training. During training, if the hand occludes the target in the top-down camera view, it temporarily moves to the suspending pose every 10 timesteps to compute a pixel reward, which is then restored. Domain randomization is applied during environment reset, varying object masses, small perturbations to object initial positions, and camera mounting positions to enhance robustness and generalization.

#### 3.2. RL Problem Design

The object retrieval task is formulated as a finite-horizon Markov Decision Process (MDP).
- **Observation Space:** The policy receives proprioceptive, visual, and privileged information. Proprioceptive inputs include arm and hand joint positions (`q_t`). Visual inputs are derived from the target object’s segmentation mask, providing its bounding box, area, and depth at the center pixel. As privileged information in simulation, the system includes a spatially aware representation (`h_o*_t`), poses of the five fingertips, target object pose, joint velocities, target object linear and angular velocities, and the positions of the five nearest objects to the target.
- **Action Space:** The action space consists of 13-dimensional target joint positions for the robot arm and hand. Hand actions are smoothed using a factor `λ` to reduce abrupt movements, while arm actions represent relative joint position changes.
- **Reward Function:** The reward function encourages the hand to actively expose the target object through five components:
 - **Distance Reward (`r_dist`):** Encourages the palm to approach occluded regions, defined as `exp(−5 · min(d − 0.15, 0))`.
 - **Stir Reward (`r_stir`):** Promotes displacement of surrounding objects, especially under complete occlusion, calculated as `400 * ||p_all_t - p_all_t-1||^2`.
 - **Proximity Clearance Reward (`r_clean`):** Rewards clearing occluders near the target, defined as `100 * r_dist * Σ(d_i)` for the k nearest neighbors.
 - **Pixel Visibility Reward (`r_pixel`):** A vision-based signal rewarding the number of visible target pixels in the top-down segmentation mask, calculated as `C/15`.
 - **Penalty Terms:** Includes penalties for action changes, contacts, and displacing the target object.
 The reward function incorporates potential-based reward shaping, where the total reward `R(s, a, s') = Φ(s') - Φ(s) - r_penalty`, with `Φ(s)` being the sum of `r_dist`, `r_clean`, `r_pixel`, and `r_stir`.

#### 3.3. Retrieval Policy Training

The training consists of two stages:
- **Spatially Aware Representation Learning:** A Graph Neural Network (GNN) module is trained to predict target visibility and extract a spatially aware representation. A graph `G=(V, E)` is constructed with nodes for the target, robot hand, and K (default 5) nearest neighboring objects. Each node's point cloud (256x3 points) is encoded into a 64-dimensional feature vector using PointNet++. Two layers of EdgeConv propagate features, and the embedding of the target node after message passing (`h_o*_t`) serves as the spatially aware representation. This module is trained by regressing predicted visibility against ground-truth visible pixel count using Mean Squared Error (MSE). Once trained, this module is frozen, and `h_o*_t` is integrated into the policy observation space.
- **Retrieval Policy Training:** A closed-loop retrieval policy is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with an actor-critic architecture. Leveraging IsaacGym's parallel simulation, training occurs across 512 environments. Domain randomization is applied by varying target objects, target poses, target mass, clutter layouts, and camera positions. PPO hyperparameters include a learning rate of 3e-4, discount factor 0.99, GAE parameter 0.95, and a clipping range of 0.1.

#### 3.4. Sim-to-Real Transfer

For real-world deployment, a student policy is distilled from the trained teacher policy via behavior cloning (BC). Successful rollout trajectories from the teacher policy are collected, filtering for stable demonstrations (e.g., fingers maintaining 2 cm clearance from the box bottom). The student policy’s observation space `o_t = (q_arm_t, x_t, y_t)` comprises arm joint positions and target pixel coordinates from a top-down camera. Target tracking in the real world uses the Segment Anything Model (SAM) for initial segmentation and Cutie for continuous tracking, with pixel coordinates converted to world coordinates via camera calibration at 30 Hz. The student policy is implemented as a transformer network that processes a history of 15 timesteps of `o_t` to capture temporal dependencies, outputting target joint positions for both the arm and dexterous hand. It uses six transformer layers, six attention heads, a hidden dimension of 384, and a feed-forward dimension of 2048, trained with Adam optimizer for 10,000 iterations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted experiments in both simulated and real-world settings to evaluate the RetrDex framework, focusing on effectiveness, efficiency, and generalization.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance in Simulation

The method was evaluated on retrieval success rate (RSR), retrieval steps (RS), and Increase in Exposure Ratio (IER) across seen and unseen target objects (categorized as small and large).
- **Superior Performance:** RetrDex consistently achieved higher RSR and required fewer RS across all scenarios compared to baselines. For seen objects, it achieved 91.4% RSR and 91.2 RS.
- **Generalization to Unseen Objects:** On unseen small targets, RetrDex achieved 86.2% RSR and 107.8 RS, and on unseen large targets, 73.9% RSR and 133.2 RS. This represented a significant improvement, with 25.3% higher RSR on small targets (compared to the SAC variant) and 285.0% higher RSR on large targets (compared to the gripper variant).
- **Comparison with Baselines:**
 - **VMP (Visual-based Motion Planning):** This heuristic method exhibited lower success rates (e.g., 25.3% on seen objects) and efficiency due to its reliance on predefined rules and segmentation masks, limiting adaptation.
 - **Grasp-Pick:** This sequential method, even with oracle knowledge of object stacking, performed poorly (e.g., 48.9% on seen objects), suffering from error accumulation and inefficiency over long horizons.
 - **Ours (SAC):** Replacing PPO with SAC resulted in an 80.2% RSR on seen objects but poor generalization to unseen targets (68.8% on small, 9.9% on large), suggesting that SAC's maximum-entropy objective might lead to overfitting to training objects.
 - **Ours (Gripper):** Substituting the dexterous hand with a parallel-jaw gripper significantly reduced performance (52.4% RSR on seen, 45.0% on small unseen, 19.2% on large unseen objects). The gripper variant frequently pushed targets deeper or became stuck, highlighting the advantage of a dexterous hand for complex interactions.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Effectiveness of GNN Module:** Incorporating the spatially aware GNN module consistently improved retrieval success. An ablation comparing a GNN with K=5 nearest neighbors to K=3 (83.4% RSR on small unseen) and K=7 (78.2% RSR on small unseen) showed K=5 generally performed better, particularly for small targets. Increasing K beyond 5 for larger targets did not yield significant marginal gains and could lead to degraded performance due to redundant obstacle information.
- **Impact of Reward Design:**
 - **`w/o Shaping`:** Removing potential-based reward shaping significantly destabilized learning, leading to a 55.4% RSR on seen objects (compared to 91.4% with shaping) and increased retrieval steps by 39.0%, confirming its role in efficient learning.
 - **`w/o r_stir`:** Ablating the stir reward led to a 52.8% drop in RSR on large objects (from 73.9% to 34.9%), indicating its importance for resolving deep occlusions by displacing clutter.
 - **`w/o r_clean`:** Removing the proximity clearance reward caused a notable decline, especially on smaller objects (from 86.2% to 63.5% RSR on small unseen), where local decluttering is critical.

#### 4.3. Additional Evaluations

- **Generalization to Unseen Clutter and Quantity:** RetrDex maintained high success rates (L2: ~84% RSR, L3: ~74% RSR) even in scenarios with unseen clutter configurations (L2) and increased clutter quantity (L3). Grasp-Pick and gripper-based policies showed sharp performance drops under these conditions.
- **Retrieval Efficiency:** RetrDex achieved higher efficiency in simulation, reducing retrieval steps by an average of 38% compared to VMP and 90% compared to Grasp-Pick across small targets, large targets, and complex clutter.
- **Impact of Occlusion Rate (Appendix):** As occlusion increased from 30% to 100%, RSR decreased, and RS increased for both small and large target objects. Smaller objects generally achieved higher RSR and fewer steps at most occlusion levels, likely due to less surrounding clutter requiring clearance.

#### 4.4. Real-World Experiments

The distilled student policy was deployed on a physical robot system (Inspire Hand on Realman RM-75 arm with RealSense D435 camera) with zero-shot transfer.
- **Retrieving Diverse Target Objects:** RetrDex achieved high success rates (60-90% success across 10 trials) on various everyday objects (cuboids, cylinders, spheres, small/large instances like milk cartons, soap, yogurt, bags, balls), outperforming VMP (0-40% success).
- **Generalization Across Target Positions:** The method generalized well across different initial target positions within the box, with higher success rates for centrally and bottom-located targets (e.g., 80% for center, 70% for bottom-left) and lower rates for top corners (e.g., 50% for top-right) due to kinematic limitations. VMP showed poorer performance across all positions (0-30% success).
- **Real-World Retrieval Efficiency:** RetrDex reduced retrieval time by an average of 51.2% compared to VMP and 61.9% compared to sequential grasp-and-remove, leveraging skill-based actions like pushing and stirring.

#### 4.5. Discussion and Limitations

The authors acknowledge several limitations:
- **Teacher-Student Gap:** The student policy for deployment, trained via behavior cloning, primarily transfers stirring and poking trajectories but not explicit occlusion reasoning present in the privileged teacher. Spatial context about clutter remains largely implicit in the student's observation space.
- **Perception and Failures:** SAM/Cutie tracking can drift under heavy occlusion or in challenging corners. Contact can also perturb segmentation. Common failures include tracking loss, kinematic difficulties in corners, and over-stirring that displaces the target.
- **Efficiency, Dexterity, and Task Scope:** While simulation efficiency benefits from the teacher's stirring strategy and dexterous hand, real-world gains come from closed-loop interactions. The thumb and index fingers tend to dominate contacts, with other fingers providing compliance. The method’s sensitivity to clutter density (as indicated by L3 performance and stir reward ablation) suggests that very heavy occluders may still necessitate sequential removal strategies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RetrDex presents a significant contribution to robotic manipulation in cluttered environments by demonstrating a framework for efficient object retrieval using dexterous hands. Its core significance lies in moving beyond sequential, isolated interaction steps towards learning diverse, continuous manipulation skills that actively clear occlusions.

The potential impact of this work is multi-faceted:
- **Enhanced Robotic Autonomy:** By enabling robots to robustly retrieve objects from highly cluttered scenes, RetrDex contributes to the development of more autonomous and capable robotic systems for real-world applications such as household service, logistics, and industrial automation.
- **Leveraging Dexterous Manipulation:** The research highlights the true advantage of dexterous multi-fingered hands, showing that they can learn a wide array of interactions (stirring, poking, reorienting) that go beyond the limitations of parallel-jaw grippers or discrete interaction sequences. This could drive further research and development in dexterous robotics.
- **Efficient Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The demonstration of zero-shot transfer from simulation to a physical robot is crucial for practical applicability, reducing the need for extensive real-world training which is often costly and time-consuming. The teacher-student framework and spatially aware representation contribute to this robust transfer.
- **Advanced Reinforcement Learning for Contact-Rich Tasks:** The use of large-scale parallel RL in diverse cluttered scenes, combined with a spatially aware representation, provides a robust methodology for learning complex, contact-rich manipulation policies in high-dimensional action spaces. The refined reward function design, including potential-based shaping, also offers insights for training efficient RL agents in similar domains.
- **Foundation for Future Work:** The identified limitations, particularly regarding robust perception under occlusion and distilling richer spatial representations to student policies, provide clear directions for future research. Extending the approach to heavier clutter and more complex manipulation tasks could further broaden its utility.

Overall, RetrDex advances the state of the art in cluttered object manipulation by providing an efficient, generalizable, and deployable framework that effectively utilizes the dexterity of multi-fingered robotic hands.
