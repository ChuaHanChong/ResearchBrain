# ACID: Action-Conditional Implicit Visual Dynamics for Deformable Object Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> ACID introduces an end-to-end framework for robot manipulation of volumetric deformable objects, leveraging implicit neural representations for 3D geometry and action-conditional dynamics, alongside a geodesics-based contrastive learning approach for robust correspondence. This enabled a 30% increase in manipulation task success rate in simulation and demonstrated successful real-world deployment on a physical robot.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation of volumetric deformable objects is challenging due to their complex non-linear dynamics, infinite-dimensional configuration spaces, and difficulty in estimating parameters from raw sensory data.
- Existing methods for deformable object manipulation are often limited to 1D or 2D objects, struggling to generalize to complex 3D shapes and internal structures with high fidelity.
- Establishing robust, dense point-wise correspondences on highly deforming objects from visual data is difficult, hindering effective state tracking and goal-conditioned planning.

## Method

- The framework learns implicit neural representations for full 3D object geometry and action-conditional flow-based dynamics directly from partial RGB-D observations and robot actions.
- A novel geodesics-based contrastive learning method establishes robust, dense point-wise correspondences by using geodesic distances on the object surface to define positive and negative pairs.
- A model-based planning approach utilizes the learned implicit dynamics to roll out action sequences and evaluates costs using a correspondence-aware state distance for goal-conditioned manipulation.

## Results

- ACID achieved a task success rate of 55.6% in simulation, representing a 30% increase over the strongest baseline (22.2%) for unseen volumetric deformable object manipulation tasks.
- The geodesics-based contrastive learning yielded state-of-the-art correspondence performance for deformable objects, with a Feature-Match Recall (FMR) of 0.815 and accuracy of 0.257.
- The model, trained solely in simulation, successfully transferred to real-world scenarios, enabling a physical robot arm to manipulate actual plush toys into target configurations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Implicit Neural Representations can effectively model high-fidelity 3D geometry and action-conditional dynamics for complex volumetric deformable objects from partial visual observations, surpassing explicit representations.
> - Using geodesic distances in contrastive learning is crucial for learning deformation-consistent point-wise correspondences that are robust to large non-rigid transformations.
> - Jointly learning geometry, dynamics, and deformation-aware correspondence within a single framework leads to improved prediction accuracy and more effective model-based manipulation planning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The robotic manipulation of deformable objects (e.g., cloth, ropes, plush toys, food items) represents a significant and persistent challenge in robotics. Unlike rigid objects, deformable objects possess infinite-dimensional configuration spaces, exhibit complex non-linear dynamics, are prone to partial observability due to self-occlusion, and undergo self-collision. These characteristics make traditional model-based control approaches, which often rely on explicit physical models, extremely difficult to apply in real-world scenarios due to the complexity of estimating their parameters from raw sensory data.

The current research landscape can be broadly categorized as follows:

- **Traditional Model-Based Approaches:** Historically, these relied on high-fidelity mathematical models (e.g., finite element methods) or simplified geometric representations (e.g., line segments for ropes, surface meshes for cloth). While precise, these models are computationally expensive and challenging to estimate directly from visual data, limiting their real-world applicability for novel, complex objects.
- **Data-Driven and Learning-Based Methods:**
 - **Simplified Deformable Objects:** Prior successes have largely been confined to 1D deformable linear objects (ropes, cables) or 2D flat objects (cloth, paper). These often rely on specialized representations like point clouds or graph structures for dynamics modeling. Some work has extended to primitive 3D objects (e.g., sponges) using 2D contours. However, methods struggle to generalize to *volumetric* deformable objects with complex 3D shapes and internal structures.
 - **Pixel-space and Latent-space Models:** These approaches learn dynamics directly from raw pixel observations or encoded latent state vectors. While end-to-end trainable and successful in some domains, they often lack explicit 3D structural understanding of the object, which can be crucial for precise manipulation planning and generalization across novel configurations.
 - **Physics-informed Learning:** Some works aim to learn approximations of physics simulators. While powerful, they often require access to underlying physical states (mass, velocity) which are not directly observable from standard camera inputs.
- **Implicit Neural Representations (INR) in 3D Vision:** Recent breakthroughs in 3D computer vision have leveraged INRs (e.g., Occupancy Networks, DeepSDF, NeRF) to represent high-fidelity 3D geometry and even dynamic scenes using coordinate-based neural networks. These methods excel at continuous, high-resolution shape representation and reconstruction from partial views. However, most existing INR work in 3D vision has focused on rigid shapes or specific deformable categories (e.g., human bodies) and has not typically integrated action-conditional dynamics for general robot manipulation planning.

**ACID's Contribution to the Landscape:**
ACID strategically positions itself to address the critical gap of *volumetric, action-conditional deformable object manipulation* by synthesizing advancements from both robotics and 3D computer vision. It leverages the strengths of implicit neural representations to overcome the limitations of explicit 3D representations (like point clouds or voxels) in terms of memory footprint and resolution for complex shapes. By learning an *action-conditional* model directly from visual data, ACID moves beyond purely observational dynamics models or physics-constrained learning. The introduction of *geodesics-based contrastive learning* for correspondence is a novel solution to accurately track highly non-rigid deformations, an area where Euclidean distance-based methods fail.

Thus, ACID represents a significant step forward in bringing sophisticated 3D visual understanding (via INRs) directly into the robot manipulation pipeline for a challenging class of objects previously deemed largely intractable, thereby bridging the gap between high-fidelity 3D perception and practical robot control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching motivation behind ACID is to enable autonomous robots to reliably perceive, predict the dynamics of, and manipulate complex, volumetric deformable objects prevalent in everyday environments. This capability is crucial for advancing robotics in domestic, industrial, and medical applications.

The key objectives of the ACID research are:

1. **Develop a Robust Action-Conditional Visual Dynamics Model for Volumetric Deformable Objects:** The primary goal is to create a model that can take partial visual observations (RGB-D) and a robot action as input, and accurately predict the resulting 3D deformation and future state of a volumetric deformable object. This specifically targets objects like plush toys and pizza dough, which have complex 3D structures and non-rigid motions that are challenging for existing methods.
2. **Leverage Implicit Neural Representations for High-Fidelity 3D Understanding:** To overcome the limitations of explicit 3D representations (e.g., point clouds, voxels) regarding memory and resolution, ACID aims to employ implicit neural representations. This includes learning an implicit occupancy field for full 3D geometry reconstruction and an implicit flow field for predicting object dynamics continuously in 3D space from partial observations.
3. **Establish Dense, Deformation-Consistent Correspondence:** A critical challenge in manipulating deformable objects is understanding how different parts of the object relate to each other across various configurations. The objective is to learn a correspondence embedding field that can accurately identify pointwise correspondences even under large, non-rigid deformations and self-occlusions. This requires a novel approach beyond simple Euclidean distances.
4. **Integrate Geodesics-based Contrastive Learning for Correspondence:** To achieve deformation-consistent correspondence, a specific objective is to develop and incorporate a geodesics-based contrastive loss function. This loss uses the intrinsic geodesic distance on the object's surface as a metric for defining positive and negative pairs during contrastive learning, thus making the learned features robust to non-rigid transformations.
5. **Enable Goal-Conditioned Deformable Object Manipulation:** The ultimate objective is to apply the learned dynamics model to practical manipulation tasks. This involves using the predicted future states and the learned correspondence (for state distance measurement) to plan action sequences that effectively transform a deformable object from a starting configuration to a desired target configuration.
6. **Develop a Realistic Simulation and Benchmarking Environment:** To train and rigorously evaluate the proposed model, an essential objective is to create a high-fidelity simulation environment (PlushSim) capable of generating diverse action trajectories, complex deformable object physics, and photorealistic rendering. This also serves as a benchmark for future research.
7. **Demonstrate Sim-to-Real Transferability:** A crucial validation objective is to show that the model trained solely in simulation can be directly applied to manipulate real-world deformable objects using a physical robot arm, highlighting the practical utility and robustness of the learned dynamics.

The motivation stems from the inadequacies of prior work in handling the full complexity of volumetric deformable objects in an action-conditional manner. Existing models either lack high-fidelity 3D representations, struggle with dynamic state estimation under severe deformation, or fail to establish robust correspondences necessary for effective manipulation planning. ACID seeks to fill these gaps by combining advanced implicit neural representation techniques with novel learning strategies for dynamics and correspondence, thereby moving closer to intelligent robotic interaction with the diverse soft objects found in human environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ACID (Action-Conditional Implicit Dynamics) proposes an end-to-end framework that jointly learns implicit representations for object geometry, flow-based dynamics, and a correspondence embedding field from partial RGB-D observations and robot actions.

**A. Problem Formulation:**
The goal is to build a model-based robotic gripper controller that rearranges deformable objects. It learns:
- **State Representation ($\psi$):** Maps observation $o_t$ to full object geometry $s_t$.
- **State Transition ($\phi$):** Maps current state $s_t$ and action $a_t$ to next state $s_{t+1}$.
- **State Distance Measure ($d$):** Quantifies similarity between states, crucial for planning.

**B. Implicit Geometry Module ($\psi$): Predicting Geometry from Partial Observation:**
This module reconstructs the complete 3D geometry of the deformable object from partial RGB-D data, moving beyond explicit representations like point clouds or voxels that struggle with resolution-memory trade-offs.
1. **Observation Encoder ($\psi_{enc}$):**
 - Back-projects an RGB-D image into a 3D point cloud with per-point RGB colors.
 - Uses a shallow PointNet with local pooling to map these points and colors into a latent feature space.
 - Orthographically projects these point cloud features onto three canonical 2D feature planes (xy, xz, yz).
 - Processes these 2D feature maps through a U-Net to produce refined feature planes (e.g., 128x128x64).
2. **Implicit Occupancy Decoder ($\psi_{dec}$):**
 - For any query point $p \in \mathbb{R}^3$, it bilinearly interpolates features from the three canonical feature planes.
 - Feeds the summed feature to a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) with skip connections to predict the occupancy probability at point $p$.
 - The full 3D object geometry $s_t$ is extracted by aggregating points whose predicted occupancy probability exceeds a threshold.

**C. Action-Conditional Flow-based Dynamics Field ($\phi$):**
This module predicts the 3D flow (displacement) of every point on the object conditioned on a given action, allowing for the simulation of object deformation.
1. **Dynamics Encoder ($\phi_{enc}$):**
 - Uses the same network architecture as $\psi_{enc}$.
 - Fuses the action command $a_t = (p_g, p_r)$ (grasp point, release point) with the partial point cloud observation. For each point $p_i$ in the point cloud, its feature is augmented with its relative position to the grasp point $(p_g - p_i)$ and the release point $p_r$.
 - Encodes this action-featurized point cloud into three canonical feature planes.
2. **Implicit Flow Decoder ($\phi_{dec}$):**
 - For any query point $p \in \mathbb{R}^3$, it queries a feature vector from $\phi_{enc}$'s canonical planes.
 - **Representation Fusion:** Crucially, it also integrates features from the implicit geometry module: the per-point feature $\psi_{enc}(o_t)|_p$ and intermediate representations $r_p$ from $\psi_{dec}$. This fusion allows the dynamics prediction to leverage the object's intrinsic geometric features.
 - Predicts a one-step forward 3D flow vector for point $p$.
 - Future states $s_{t+1}$ are computed by displacing each point in $s_t$ according to the predicted flow. For future time steps where $o_t$ is unavailable, the model approximates $o_t$ by projecting the rolled-out geometry $s_t$ and reuses initial geometric features.

**D. Geodesics-based Contrastive Learning for Correspondence:**
To establish robust, dense point-wise correspondences under significant non-rigid deformation, where Euclidean distance is misleading (e.g., a bent arm touching a body), ACID introduces a novel approach.
1. **Implicit Correspondence Decoder ($\chi_{dec}$):**
 - Shares the `Observation Encoder` $\psi_{enc}$ with the geometry module.
 - Predicts a 32-dimensional embedding $f_p$ for any query point $p$.
2. **Geodesics-based Contrastive Loss ($L_{geo}$):**
 - Learns point embeddings such that corresponding points have similar embeddings and non-corresponding points have dissimilar embeddings.
 - Instead of Euclidean distance, it uses the *geodesic distance* $d_O(p,q)$ (shortest path on the object's surface) to define positive and negative pairs.
 - A pair $(p,q)$ is positive if their geodesic distance is below a threshold $d_{thres}$; otherwise, it's negative. This makes the correspondence features robust to deformation.
 - The object is approximated as a graph of tetrahedrons for pre-calculating geodesic distances.
3. **Correspondence-Aware State Distance ($d_{corr}$):**
 - Once embedding fields are learned, a correspondence mapping $\xi_{corr}$ between two states $s_t$ and $s_{t'}$ is found by minimizing aggregated feature distance.
 - The state distance $d_{corr}$ is then calculated as the mean squared Euclidean distance between corresponding points: $\frac{1}{|s_t|} \sum_{p \in s_t} \|p - \xi_{corr}(p)\|^2$.

**E. Planning with Dynamics Model:**
ACID employs a model-based planning approach for goal-conditioned manipulation tasks.
1. **Target and Initial State Recovery:** The implicit geometry module reconstructs the initial state $s_0$ and the target state $s_{target}$ from their respective RGB-D images.
2. **Action Sequence Roll-out:** Given an action sequence $(a_1..., a_n)$, the implicit dynamics module iteratively predicts future states $s_1..., s_n$.
3. **Cost Function:** The cost of an action sequence is defined as the learned correspondence-aware distance $d_{corr}(s_n, s_{target}, \xi_{corr})$, where $\xi_{corr}$ is established between the initial and target states.
4. **Optimal Action Selection:** The action sequence with the lowest predicted cost is selected for execution.

**F. PlushSim Environment:**
To facilitate training and evaluation, the authors developed PlushSim, a simulation framework built on NVIDIA Omniverse Kit's PhysX engine.
- Uses GPU-accelerated finite element method (FEM) for volumetric deformable body simulation (tetrahedron graphs).
- Generates a dataset of 17,665 action trajectories with 6 types of plush toys (78 variants) in randomized realistic scenes (attic, furniture obstacles, lighting).
- Provides a manipulation API for grasp, move, and release actions, enabling diverse action sampling.

This comprehensive methodology allows ACID to model complex deformable object behavior with high fidelity, learn robust correspondences, and leverage these capabilities for effective manipulation planning in both simulated and real-world environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the PlushSim environment and real-world robot deployment demonstrate ACID's superior performance across geometry, dynamics, correspondence prediction, and ultimately, manipulation task success.

1. **Geometry and Dynamics Prediction:**
 - **Geometry:** ACID (specifically ACID-No Corr) achieved a mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) of **0.796** for shape reconstruction, significantly outperforming the voxel-based baseline Voxel-flow (0.643). This confirms the advantage of implicit representations for high-fidelity deformable object geometry.
 - **Dynamics (Flow Prediction):** ACID (Ours), trained jointly with correspondence, demonstrated the best flow prediction performance with Mean Squared Error (MSE) of **0.425** for visible surfaces and **0.372** for the full object. This is a substantial improvement over Voxel-flow (0.492 vis, 0.488 full) and Pixel-flow (0.525 vis), indicating the effectiveness of implicit representations for dynamics and the benefit of 3D reasoning.
 - **Synergy of Joint Learning:** The full ACID model, incorporating geodesics-based correspondence, *outperformed* ACID-No Corr in dynamics prediction (0.425 vs. 0.434 vis MSE), suggesting that learning robust correspondence features aids in predicting object motion. Ablation (ACID-w/o fusion) further confirmed the importance of representation fusion for dynamics.

2. **Correspondence Evaluations:**
 - ACID's novel geodesics-based contrastive learning approach achieved state-of-the-art results for deformable object correspondence. It reached a Feature-Match Recall (FMR) of **0.815** and a correspondence accuracy of **0.257**.
 - This significantly surpassed strong baselines like FCGF (0.665 FMR, 0.204 accuracy), InfoNCE, FPFH, and Deep-Shells.
 - Ablation studies on the contrastive loss function revealed the critical role of geodesics: using InfoNCE or FCGF loss *without* geodesics (ACID-e2e-w/ and ACID-e2e-w/) led to worse correspondence and even *decreased* dynamics prediction, underscoring that deformation-aware distance metrics are essential for effective joint learning.

3. **Manipulation Evaluations:**
 - **Action Ranking:** ACID variants consistently showed strong agreement with ground truth action sequence rankings, with an average Kendall’s τ score (e.g., 0.544 for ACID-Ours) demonstrating better predictive power than Voxel-Flow (0.278).
 - **Plan Execution Results:** ACID achieved superior results across multiple metrics after executing the selected action sequences: highest mIoU (0.438), F1-score (0.713), and lowest Chamfer distance (0.426) and $d_{corr}$ (0.936).
 - **Task Success Rate:** Most notably, ACID achieved a task success rate of **55.6%**, representing a **30% increase** over the strongest baseline, Voxel-Flow (22.2%).
 - **Contribution of $d_{corr}$:** The ablation comparing `ACID (Ours)` with `ACID (Chamfer)` demonstrated that using the learned correspondence-aware distance ($d_{corr}$) as the cost function for planning further improved the success rate (55.6% vs. 54.3%), beyond just the benefits of better feature learning.
 - **Per-Type Performance:** ACID consistently outperformed baselines across different toy types, though smaller objects presented slightly greater challenges, suggesting areas for future scale-aware modeling.

4. **Real Robot Deployment (Sim-to-Real):**
 - The ACID model, trained exclusively in simulation, was successfully deployed on a physical Franka Emika Panda robot arm to manipulate real-world plush toys.
 - It demonstrated the ability to correctly establish shape correspondences on real objects and manipulate them into target configurations, a crucial validation of its practical utility and generalization capabilities from simulation.

In summary, the main findings overwhelmingly support ACID's innovative approach. Its use of implicit neural representations for geometry and dynamics, combined with a novel geodesics-based contrastive loss for correspondence, leads to significantly improved perception, prediction, and control for complex volumetric deformable objects. The successful sim-to-real transfer is a particularly strong result, showcasing the potential for practical applications.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ACID represents a substantial advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for deformable objects, and has several significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Enabling Manipulation of Complex Volumetric Deformable Objects:** This is ACID's most direct and impactful contribution. By effectively modeling the geometry, dynamics, and correspondence of *volumetric* deformable objects (like plush toys, dough, or even soft tissues), ACID pushes the boundaries beyond previous work that primarily focused on 1D or 2D deformables. This opens up vast possibilities for robots to handle a wider, more diverse range of objects in unstructured environments.
2. **Pioneering the Use of Implicit Neural Representations for Robot Manipulation:** ACID effectively bridges the gap between state-of-the-art 3D computer vision (Implicit Neural Representations for high-fidelity shape and motion) and robotic control. It demonstrates that these continuous, high-resolution representations are not just powerful for static scene understanding but can be leveraged for dynamic, action-conditional modeling in complex robotic tasks. This could inspire a new wave of research applying INRs to other areas of robotics requiring fine-grained spatial and temporal understanding.
3. **Robust Correspondence Learning for Deformable Objects:** The novel geodesics-based contrastive learning approach is a significant methodological contribution. Accurate and deformation-consistent correspondence is fundamental for various tasks beyond manipulation, such as medical imaging, animation, and augmented reality. ACID provides a principled way to learn these correspondences from visual data, which can be applied to other domains dealing with highly non-rigid shapes.
4. **Advancement in Model-Based Learning for Robotics:** While data-driven, ACID's core is a learned dynamics model that enables planning. The substantial improvement in task success rate (30% over strong baselines) highlights the power of learning robust 3D models of interaction. This reinforces the value of model-based approaches, especially when combined with sophisticated perception.
5. **Robust Simulation and Benchmarking:** The development of PlushSim, a high-fidelity, randomized simulation environment for volumetric deformable objects, is a valuable contribution to the research community. It provides a much-needed benchmark for future work, allowing for quantitative comparison and accelerating research in this challenging area.
6. **Strong Sim-to-Real Transferability:** The successful deployment of the simulation-trained ACID model directly on real-world robots is a critical validation. It suggests that the learned implicit dynamics generalize well from simulated physics to real physics, offering a promising pathway for developing practical robotic systems without extensive real-world data collection, which is often a bottleneck for deformable object tasks.
7. **Potential for Broader Applications:**
 - **Domestic Robotics:** Enabling robots to perform tasks like folding laundry, cooking (e.g., kneading dough, handling fresh produce), or tidying up plush toys.
 - **Industrial Automation:** Handling delicate or irregularly shaped components, packaging, or quality control.
 - **Medical Robotics:** Assisting in surgical procedures involving soft tissues, where precise and deformation-aware manipulation is paramount.
 - **Logistics and Warehousing:** Managing and organizing soft goods or irregularly shaped items.

**Limitations and Future Directions (as discussed by authors):**
The authors acknowledge limitations, primarily concerning the sim-to-real gap for volumetric deformable objects, which is arguably larger than for rigid bodies, and the current dataset's lack of fine-grained manipulation behaviors. Future work will focus on closing this reality gap through advanced sim2real transfer techniques, modeling finer-grained dynamics (e.g., twisting), and exploring how to incorporate complex, long-tail actions into training to enable more sophisticated manipulation capabilities.

In conclusion, ACID's comprehensive approach, combining advanced 3D vision techniques with robust learning for dynamics and correspondence, significantly pushes the boundaries of what robots can achieve with deformable objects. Its success lays a strong foundation for future research in general-purpose robotics, with a high potential for real-world impact across diverse industries.
