# PAL: Program-aided Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Program-aided Language Models (PAL) from Carnegie Mellon University enhance Large Language Models' reasoning by prompting them to generate Python programs, which are then executed by an external interpreter. This approach achieves state-of-the-art few-shot accuracy across 13 mathematical, symbolic, and algorithmic datasets, significantly improving robustness to numerical complexity compared to existing methods like Chain-of-Thought.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) excel at decomposing complex problems into logical steps (e.g., via Chain-of-Thought prompting) but frequently make errors during the execution or calculation phase.
- LLMs, being pattern matchers, are not inherently reliable for precise arithmetic, symbolic, or logical computation, especially with complex numbers or intricate manipulations.
- This limitation leads to situations where an LLM's reasoning chain might be conceptually correct, but its final numerical or logical answer is erroneous due to calculation mistakes.

## Method

- PAL introduces a novel prompting strategy where the Large Language Model generates an ==interleaved sequence of natural language comments and executable Python code== as intermediate reasoning steps.
- Instead of the LLM performing internal calculations, the generated Python program is passed to and executed by a ==standard, external Python interpreter==.
- The final solution is derived directly from the interpreter's output, effectively ==decoupling the LLM's problem decomposition capabilities== from the precise execution of computational steps.

## Results

- PAL achieved state-of-the-art few-shot accuracy across all **13** evaluated mathematical, symbolic, and algorithmic reasoning datasets, outperforming larger models like PaLM-540B and Minerva-540B using Chain-of-Thought.
- On GSM8K, PAL reached **72.0%** accuracy, surpassing CoT with Codex (**65.6%**); on the harder GSM-HARD dataset, PAL maintained **61.2%** accuracy, demonstrating superior robustness compared to CoT's drop to **23.1%**.
- PAL showed substantial gains across BIG-Bench Hard symbolic and algorithmic tasks, with improvements ranging from **8.8%** to **23.7%** over Chain-of-Thought on datasets such as COLORED OBJECTS, PENGUINS, OBJECT COUNTING, and REPEAT COPY.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating Large Language Models (LLMs) with external symbolic interpreters, such as a Python interpreter, is an effective strategy for achieving accurate and robust reasoning.
> - The primary benefit of PAL comes from offloading precise computation to a deterministic external interpreter, rather than solely from prompting LLMs to generate structured code for internal execution.
> - The inclusion of natural language comments and meaningful variable names within the Python code prompts is crucial for the LLM's performance and its ability to generate correct and grounded programs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Program-aided Language Models (PAL) is situated at a critical juncture in the evolution of Large Language Models (LLMs), specifically addressing their limitations in complex reasoning. Recent years have seen LLMs like GPT-3, PaLM, and LaMDA achieve unprecedented success across a wide range of natural language tasks, including common sense, mathematical, and symbolic reasoning. A key enabler of this success has been "few-shot prompting" and advanced prompting methods such as "Chain-of-Thought" (CoT) (Wei et al., 2022), "scratchpads" (Nye et al., 2021), and "least-to-most" prompting (Zhou et al., 2022). These methods encourage LLMs to generate explicit intermediate reasoning steps, mimicking human problem-solving processes.

While LLMs have demonstrated remarkable ability in *decomposing* complex problems into smaller, manageable steps (the "thought" part of CoT), a persistent challenge remains: their susceptibility to logical and arithmetic errors during the *execution* or *calculation* phase. Even models fine-tuned on vast amounts of mathematical content, such as Minerva, reportedly struggle with "incorrect reasoning" and "incorrect calculation." This limitation becomes particularly pronounced with complex arithmetic or large numbers, as highlighted by prior research (Hendrycks et al., 2021; Madaan & Yazdanbakhsh, 2022). LLMs, being pattern matchers trained on sequences, are not inherently symbolic calculators, leading to unreliability when precise computation is required.

PAL directly addresses this fundamental limitation by integrating symbolic reasoning capabilities with the LLM's natural language understanding. It represents a significant step within the broader paradigm of **neuro-symbolic AI**, which seeks to combine the strengths of neural networks (for pattern recognition, generalization, and understanding natural language) with the precision and reliability of symbolic systems (for logical inference, calculation, and knowledge representation). Prior work has explored equipping neural models with external tools (e.g., calculators, specialized modules for dates), but PAL proposes a more general solution: using a standard, general-purpose programming language interpreter (Python) as the symbolic "solver." This distinguishes it from ad-hoc solutions or pre-training on execution results. By offloading the deterministic computational burden to a Python interpreter, PAL aims to bridge the gap between LLMs' impressive generative capabilities and their inherent limitations in exact computation, pushing towards more robust and general AI reasoners.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the "PAL: Program-aided Language Models" research is to significantly enhance the accuracy and robustness of Large Language Models (LLMs) on tasks requiring precise arithmetic, symbolic, and algorithmic reasoning.

The core motivation stems from a critical observation about the current state of LLMs:
1. **LLMs excel at problem decomposition:** Modern LLMs, especially when prompted with Chain-of-Thought like examples, are adept at understanding natural language problems and breaking them down into logical, step-by-step reasoning processes. They can effectively transform a high-level problem statement into a sequence of intermediate "thoughts."
2. **LLMs struggle with precise execution:** Despite their proficiency in decomposition, LLMs frequently make mistakes when performing the actual calculations or logical deductions required by these steps. This is particularly true for complex arithmetic operations, dealing with large numbers, or intricate symbolic manipulations. Their neural architecture, based on statistical patterns, is not designed for exact, deterministic computation. This leads to scenarios where the *reasoning chain* generated by the LLM might be conceptually correct, but the *final numerical or logical answer* is erroneous.

Therefore, PAL is motivated by the desire to **synergize the strengths of neural LLMs with symbolic interpreters.** The LLM is tasked with what it does best: understanding, generating, and decomposing natural language problems into programmatic steps. The Python interpreter, a reliable and deterministic symbolic system, is then delegated the task of accurate calculation and execution of these steps. This division of labor aims to overcome the inherent "calculation weakness" of LLMs, leading to more accurate and trustworthy reasoning outputs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PAL introduces a novel prompting strategy that leverages an LLM to generate Python programs as intermediate reasoning steps, with the ultimate solution derived by executing these programs using an external Python interpreter.

The methodology can be broken down as follows:

1. **Program Generation as Intermediate Steps:** Instead of the free-form natural language "thoughts" seen in Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, PAL prompts the LLM to generate an interleaved sequence of natural language comments (for readability and guidance) and executable Python code.
- **Prompt Structure:** For few-shot learning, the input prompt for PAL consists of a series of example pairs `(x_i, t_i)`, where `x_i` is the natural language problem and `t_i` is the sequence of interleaved natural language and Python statements representing the reasoning steps. Crucially, PAL examples *do not* include the final numerical answer, as this will be derived from the interpreter.
- **Role of Natural Language in Prompts:** While the core is code generation, PAL prompts still include natural language comments (e.g., using Python's `#` syntax) and encourage meaningful variable names (e.g., `money_initial` instead of `a`). Ablation studies confirm these NL elements are vital for the LLM's understanding and generation of correct code.
2. **Offloading Solution to an External Interpreter:** Once the LLM generates the Python program (`t_test`) for a given test problem (`x_test`), this program is passed to a standard Python interpreter. The interpreter executes the code, and its output is taken as the final answer (`y_test`). This contrasts with CoT, where the LLM is expected to perform both the reasoning and the final calculation internally.
3. **Experimental Setup:**
- **Base LLM:** The primary LLM used in the experiments is **CODEX (code-davinci-002)**, an OpenAI model specifically pre-trained on a vast corpus of code, making it well-suited for code generation tasks. The paper also investigates other Codex models and, notably, general natural language models (text-davinci series) to assess PAL's generalizability.
- **Prompt Engineering:** For benchmarks where CoT prompts were available, PAL adapted these by converting the natural language reasoning steps into their corresponding programmatic equivalents. Otherwise, a fixed set of 3-6 examples was randomly selected and augmented with Python code.
- **Decoding Strategy:** Greedy decoding with a temperature of 0 was primarily used for single-sample results. For multi-sample generation, nucleus sampling (p=0.95) with k=40 samples and temperature 0.7 was employed, followed by majority voting, similar to prior work.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Performance is measured by the "solve rate" (accuracy) on each task.
- **Baselines:** Performance is compared against:
 - **DIRECT Prompting:** Standard few-shot prompting with just input-output pairs.
 - **Chain-of-Thought (COT):** Prompting with explicit natural language intermediate reasoning steps. Results for various strong LLMs (Codex, PaLM-540B, LaMDA-137B, Minerva-540B) from prior literature are included for comparison.
- **Datasets:** A comprehensive evaluation across 13 diverse reasoning tasks:
 - **Mathematical Reasoning:** GSM8K, SVAMP, ASDIV, and four MAWPS sub-datasets (SINGLEEQ, SINGLEOP, ADDSUB, MULTIARITH). Crucially, the authors introduced **GSM-HARD**, a harder version of GSM8K with larger numbers, to specifically test robustness to numerical complexity.
 - **Symbolic Reasoning (from BIG-Bench Hard):** COLORED OBJECTS, PENGUINS, DATE.
 - **Algorithmic Problems (from BIG-Bench Hard):** OBJECT COUNTING, REPEAT COPY.

This methodology effectively decouples the "reasoning decomposition" capability of LLMs from the "precise execution" capability, leading to a robust neuro-symbolic approach.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that PAL consistently achieves state-of-the-art few-shot accuracy across a wide range of reasoning tasks, significantly outperforming existing LLM prompting strategies, including Chain-of-Thought (CoT) with much larger models.

1. **Overall State-of-the-Art Accuracy:**
- PAL, using the Codex LLM, establishes a new few-shot state-of-the-art top-1 decoding accuracy across *all 13 mathematical, symbolic, and algorithmic reasoning datasets* evaluated.
- It surpasses larger models like PaLM-540B and Minerva-540B (which was explicitly fine-tuned on mathematical content) that use CoT prompting.

2. **Mathematical Reasoning Performance:**
- **GSM8K:** PAL achieves 72.0% accuracy, significantly outperforming CoT with Codex (65.6%) and PaLM-540B (56.9%).
- **GSM-HARD:** This dataset, designed with larger numbers, highlights PAL's robustness. While DIRECT prompting and CoT showed dramatic accuracy drops (from 19.7% to 5.0% and 65.6% to 23.1%, respectively), PAL remained remarkably stable at 61.2% (a mere 14.3% drop from its GSM8K performance). Analysis shows CoT's failures on GSM-HARD were primarily due to calculation errors, not incorrect reasoning decomposition.
- **Other Math Datasets:** Consistent improvements were observed across SVAMP, ASDIV, and various MAWPS sub-datasets, with PAL achieving accuracies like 96.1% on MAWPS-SINGLEEQ and 99.2% on MAWPS-MULTIARITH.
- **Multi-sample Generation (Majority Voting):** When using majority voting from 40 samples, PAL's accuracy on GSM8K further improved to 80.4%, surpassing Minerva-540B (78.5%), reinforcing its strength even with advanced sampling techniques.

3. **Symbolic Reasoning & Algorithmic Tasks Performance:**
- PAL also demonstrated substantial gains in symbolic and algorithmic tasks from BIG-Bench Hard.
- **COLORED OBJECTS:** PAL achieved 95.1%, an 8.8% absolute improvement over CoT.
- **PENGUINS:** PAL gained 14.1% over CoT, reaching 93.3%.
- **DATE:** PAL improved by 11.4% over CoT, reaching 76.2%.
- **OBJECT COUNTING:** PAL achieved 96.7%, a significant 23.7% improvement over CoT.
- **REPEAT COPY:** PAL reached 90.6%, outperforming CoT by 21.8%.
- **Robustness to Complexity (COLORED OBJECTS):** As the number of objects in the question increased, PAL maintained consistently high accuracy (close to 100%), whereas CoT's performance was unstable and declined.

4. **Ablation Studies and Analysis:**
- **Weaker LMs:** PAL's relative performance gain over CoT was consistent even when using weaker Codex models (e.g., code-cushman-001, code-davinci-001), indicating the general applicability of the approach.
- **Natural Language LMs:** PAL was effective with stronger natural language models (text-davinci-002 and text-davinci-003) that possess sufficient code-modeling ability, showing it's not restricted to code-specific LLMs.
- **Python Prompt vs. Interpreter:** A crucial finding was that having the LLM generate Python code but *then forcing the LLM to "execute" it internally* (without an interpreter) resulted in accuracy near DIRECT prompting (23.2% on GSM8K). This strongly supports the hypothesis that the benefit of PAL comes from the synergy with the external, deterministic interpreter, not just the structured prompt.
- **Variable Names:** Meaningful variable names in the prompts were found to be critical for PAL's performance. Removing natural language comments slightly reduced accuracy, but substituting variable names with random characters significantly decreased performance, underscoring the importance of grounding variables to entities for the LLM.

In summary, PAL's results demonstrate that leveraging LLMs for problem decomposition into executable code, and then offloading the actual computation to a reliable interpreter, is a highly effective strategy for achieving accurate and robust reasoning capabilities across a diverse set of challenging tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Program-aided Language Models (PAL) represents a significant advancement in the field of artificial intelligence, particularly at the intersection of natural language processing and symbolic reasoning. Its implications are far-reaching:

1. **Advancement in Neuro-Symbolic AI:** PAL is a compelling demonstration of the power of tightly integrating neural and symbolic AI approaches. By clearly delineating roles—LLMs for natural language understanding, problem decomposition, and code generation, and symbolic interpreters for precise execution—PAL effectively combines the generalization capabilities and natural language fluency of neural networks with the deterministic accuracy and reliability of symbolic systems. This synergy is a crucial step towards building more robust, transparent, and trustworthy AI reasoners that can tackle complex real-world problems.

2. **Addressing Core LLM Limitations:** PAL directly confronts and overcomes a fundamental weakness of current LLMs: their unreliability in precise arithmetic and logical computation. The consistent outperformance of PAL over even much larger CoT-based models, especially on tasks involving large numbers or intricate logic, highlights its ability to circumvent these inherent limitations. This means LLMs can now reliably handle tasks that previously required human oversight for numerical accuracy, expanding their practical applicability.

3. **Enhanced Robustness and Reliability:** The dramatic performance increase on the GSM-HARD dataset, where PAL maintains high accuracy while CoT struggles with large numbers, underscores its superior robustness. This increased reliability is vital for deploying AI systems in sensitive domains like finance, engineering, scientific research, and healthcare, where even minor computational errors can have significant consequences.

4. **Scalability of Reasoning:** By offloading computation, PAL allows LLMs to tackle problems of increasing complexity without being bottlenecked by their internal computational limits. The ability to generate complex Python programs, including loops, conditionals, and data structures (as shown in examples like COLORED OBJECTS and PENGUINS), means that LLMs can now effectively "reason" through multi-step, multi-conditional problems with greater precision and scalability.

5. **New Avenues for LLM Application:** The success of PAL opens up new possibilities for LLMs in domains that require not just understanding and generation, but also verifiable, accurate computation. This could lead to more effective AI assistants for data analysis, complex problem-solving, educational tools for math and logic, and even automated code generation for specific tasks, with a higher guarantee of functional correctness.

6. **Future Research Directions:** PAL's success invites further exploration into more dynamic neuro-symbolic interactions. The authors note that their current implementation uses "single, post-hoc execution." Future work could involve "incrementally running PL segments and feeding the execution results back to the LLM to generate the following blocks." This would enable more interactive, adaptive reasoning, allowing the LLM to dynamically adjust its subsequent steps based on intermediate computational outcomes, moving towards truly adaptive problem-solving agents. The study also prompts further research into how LLMs learn to generate correct and robust programs, including the optimal ways to structure prompts and the interplay between natural language and code within these prompts.

In conclusion, PAL marks a significant leap forward in empowering LLMs with accurate reasoning capabilities by strategically integrating them with external symbolic tools. It lays a strong foundation for the next generation of AI systems that are not only fluent in human language but also precise and reliable in their logical and computational tasks, bringing the vision of general and robust AI reasoners closer to reality.
