# 3D Diffusion Policy: Generalizable Visuomotor Policy Learning via Simple 3D Representations

> [!summary] Summary
> DP3, developed by researchers at Shanghai Qi Zhi Institute, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, and Tsinghua University, introduces a 3D Diffusion Policy that learns robust robotic skills from significantly fewer demonstrations by integrating compact 3D representations with diffusion models, achieving higher success rates and generalization across varied scenes and objects while enhancing deployment safety.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art visual imitation learning methods require large volumes of human demonstrations, creating a significant data collection bottleneck.
- 2D image-based policies lack explicit spatial understanding, hindering generalization across varying viewpoints and object instances, and leading to unsafe behaviors.
- Prior 3D-based methods for robotics often suffer from slow inference speeds or are ill-suited for continuous, high-dimensional control tasks.

## Method

- A two-module architecture, comprising a ==Perception module== for 3D representations and a ==Decision module== using a ==conditional diffusion model==, is trained end-to-end.
- The Perception module utilizes single-view depth images, converting them to cropped and downsampled ==point clouds==, then processed by a lightweight ==MLP-based encoder==.
- The Decision module, built on **Diffusion Policy**, generates ==action sequences== conditioned on 3D visual features and robot poses, optimized via a ==conditional denoising process==.

## Results

- Achieves an average **74.4%** success rate in **72** simulated tasks (**24.2%** relative improvement over baseline Diffusion Policy) with only **10** demonstrations per task.
- Demonstrates high real-world success rates (average **85%**) across **4** challenging dexterous manipulation tasks using only **40** demonstrations.
- Exhibits exceptional generalization abilities across spatial, appearance, instance, and viewpoint variations, along with near-zero safety violations in real-world deployment, surpassing baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Simple, lightweight MLP encoders for point clouds, rather than complex pre-trained models, are highly effective for visuomotor control tasks.
> - Explicit 3D representations, specifically point clouds processed without color information, inherently lead to superior generalization across spatial, appearance, instance, and view variations.
> - Integrating compact 3D representations with diffusion policies enables significantly higher data efficiency and enhanced safety, reducing erratic behaviors in real-world robot deployment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of several critical and rapidly evolving areas in robotics and artificial intelligence: imitation learning, visual perception for robotics, diffusion models, and dexterous manipulation.

**Imitation Learning (IL) for Robotics** has emerged as a highly effective paradigm for teaching robots complex motor skills by observing human or expert demonstrations. Its advantage lies in circumventing the often challenging and data-intensive process of reinforcement learning, especially for tasks that are difficult to define with explicit reward functions.

However, a significant hurdle for **Visual Imitation Learning**, which relies on high-dimensional visual observations like images or depth maps, has been its **high data requirement**. State-of-the-art methods, such as the original Diffusion Policy, often necessitate hundreds of demonstrations per task in real-world scenarios, making data collection a time-consuming and expensive bottleneck. While online learning approaches exist to mitigate this, they introduce their own complexities, including safety concerns, the need for automated resetting, and extensive human intervention.

Concurrently, **Diffusion Models**, initially celebrated for their prowess in high-fidelity image generation, have recently gained traction in robotics. Their expressive generative capabilities make them well-suited for modeling complex, multi-modal, and conditional distributions, which is highly beneficial for generating coherent action sequences in visuomotor policies. Diffusion Policy pioneered the application of these models for action generation in visuomotor control, marking a significant advancement.

Furthermore, the importance of **3D Visual Representations** in robotics is increasingly recognized. Traditional 2D image-based policies, while popular, can struggle with ambiguities inherent in projection and lack explicit spatial understanding. Recent works like PerAct, GNFactor, and ACT3D have showcased the benefits of 3D information for robotic manipulation, particularly for low-dimensional control tasks. However, these 3D-based methods often come with drawbacks:
1. **Impractical Settings:** Many convert imitation learning into a prediction-and-planning problem using keyframe pose extraction, which is less suitable for high-dimensional, continuous control.
2. **Slow Inference Speed:** Their intricate architectures can lead to prohibitively slow inference rates (e.g., PerAct at 2.23 FPS), making them impractical for tasks requiring rapid, dense command generation in dynamic environments.

**Dexterous Manipulation**, a particularly challenging domain in robotics due to the high degrees of freedom and complex interactions with objects (especially deformable ones), often relies on reinforcement learning or specialized human-to-robot retargeting techniques. The ability to learn these skills from minimal demonstrations through improved imitation learning algorithms remains a significant open problem.

**This paper, DP3, directly addresses these limitations.** It pioneers the integration of efficient 3D visual conditioning into diffusion policies. By combining the expressiveness of diffusion models for action generation with the spatial understanding and generalization capabilities of 3D representations, DP3 aims to overcome the data inefficiency of 2D diffusion policies and the speed/generality limitations of prior 3D-based methods. This work seeks to enable robust, generalizable, and safe robot skill learning from minimal human demonstrations, thereby making complex robotic manipulation more practical and accessible for real-world deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an **offline imitation learning algorithm capable of learning robust and generalizable robotic skills with as few demonstrations as possible**, specifically targeting practical real-world robot learning challenges.

The motivation behind DP3 stems from several critical limitations observed in the current landscape of visual imitation learning:

1. **Excessive Data Requirements:** Current state-of-the-art visual imitation learning methods, such as Diffusion Policy, demand a large volume of human-collected demonstrations (e.g., 100-200 per task in real-world scenarios). Collecting such extensive datasets, especially for long-horizon or failure-prone tasks, can take several days, making the process highly inefficient and a significant bottleneck for deploying new skills.
2. **Challenges of Online Learning:** While online learning can adapt policies through interaction, it introduces its own set of complexities in real-world environments, including safety considerations, the necessity for automated resetting mechanisms, human intervention during failures, and additional hardware costs. The authors aim for an effective *offline* solution that avoids these overheads.
3. **Limitations of 2D Visual Representations:** Standard visual imitation learning often relies on 2D image-based observations. While convenient, 2D images inherently lose depth and true spatial relationships, which can hinder generalization across varying viewpoints, object instances, and appearances, and may lead to unsafe behaviors in 3D environments.
4. **Inefficiencies of Existing 3D Approaches:** Although the value of 3D representations is recognized, current 3D-based policies often suffer from impractical task formulations (e.g., prediction-and-planning instead of direct continuous control) and slow inference speeds, making them unsuitable for real-time, high-dimensional control tasks.

To address these challenges, the authors propose **DP3** with the following key objectives:
- **Efficiency & Effectiveness:** To achieve superior task success rates with significantly fewer demonstrations and training steps compared to existing methods.
- **Generalizability:** To enable robust performance across diverse aspects of real-world variability, including changes in space (unseen positions), viewpoint (camera angles), instance (different objects of the same category), and appearance (colors, textures), leveraging the inherent advantages of 3D information.
- **Safe Deployment:** To minimize erratic and potentially hardware-damaging behaviors during real-world robot operation, enhancing the practicality and trustworthiness of learned policies.
- **Universal Applicability:** To demonstrate the method's versatility across a broad range of simulated and real-world robotic tasks, from simple parallel gripper manipulations to complex, high-dimensional dexterous manipulation of deformable objects.

The core motivation is thus to fill a critical gap: to create a visuomotor policy that marries the expressive power of diffusion models with the robust spatial understanding of 3D representations, resulting in an imitation learning approach that is highly data-efficient, generalizable, and safe for practical robot deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3) is designed as a simple yet effective visual imitation learning algorithm that fundamentally consists of two interconnected modules: **Perception** and **Decision**. These modules are trained end-to-end using expert demonstrations.

**A. Perception Module: Compact 3D Representations from Point Clouds**

The perception module is responsible for extracting a compact, task-relevant 3D representation from the robot's environment.

- **Single-View Depth Input:** Unlike many prior works that rely on multiple cameras or RGB-D images, DP3 opts for a single-view depth camera (e.g., RealSense L515 in real-world experiments) for practical applicability. The depth images, initially 84x84 resolution, are converted into 3D point clouds using camera intrinsics and extrinsics. Crucially, **color channels are explicitly excluded** from the input to promote better appearance generalization, a design choice later validated through ablations.
- **Point Cloud Processing:**
 - **Cropping:** To remove redundant points from static elements like tables and grounds, a bounding box is applied to crop the point cloud, focusing on the relevant workspace.
 - **Downsampling:** The potentially dense point cloud is downsampled to a sparse set of points (e.g., 512 or 1024 points) using **Farthest Point Sampling (FPS)**. FPS is chosen over uniform sampling to ensure sufficient coverage of the 3D space and reduce randomness in point selection, contributing to the robustness of the representation.
- **Encoding Point Clouds (DP3 Encoder):** The processed sparse point cloud is then fed into a lightweight **MLP-based encoder**, termed the "DP3 Encoder." This conceptually simple network consists of:
 - A three-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) for feature extraction.
 - A max-pooling function applied across the points to aggregate features into a global representation. Max-pooling ensures order-equivariance, meaning the output is invariant to the permutation of input points.
 - A projection head that maps the aggregated features into a compact 64-dimensional vector.
 - LayerNormalization layers are interleaved within the MLP to stabilize training.
 The simplicity and efficiency of this encoder are a key design choice, as ablation studies demonstrate its superior performance compared to more complex or pre-trained point cloud encoders.

**B. Decision Module: Conditional Action Generation via Diffusion Policy**

The decision module utilizes a conditional denoising diffusion model as its backbone, building upon the framework of Diffusion Policy.

- **Conditional Denoising Process:** The module is designed to generate a sequence of actions (`a`) by denoising a random Gaussian noise (`a_K`) over `K` iterations. The denoising process is *conditioned* on the compact 3D visual features (`v`) extracted by the perception module and the current robot poses (`q`).
 - Specifically, a denoising network `ϵθ` is trained to predict the noise added to a data point at a given timestep. The inference process iteratively refines a noisy action sequence into a coherent, noise-free action sequence.
- **Training Objective:** The model is trained using a Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss, aiming to predict the noise components that transform a clean action sequence into a noisy one. This adheres to the standard training methodology for diffusion probabilistic models.
- **Implementation Details:**
 - **Noise Scheduler:** DDIM (Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models) is employed as the noise scheduler.
 - **Prediction Type:** The policy uses "sample prediction" instead of "epsilon prediction," which is found to be more effective for high-dimensional action generation.
 - **Timesteps:** 100 timesteps are used during training, reduced to 10 for faster inference.
 - **Action Horizon:** A relatively short action prediction horizon (H=4, predicting 4 future actions) and execution horizon (N_act=3, executing the last 3 actions) are used. This choice, while seemingly short, is competitive with longer horizons and beneficial for immediate policy switching in dynamic real-world scenarios.
 - **Normalization:** Action and observation dimensions are independently scaled to `[-1, 1]` for stable training and compatibility with diffusion models.

**C. End-to-End Training and Evaluation**

DP3 is trained end-to-end, allowing the perception and decision modules to co-adapt and optimize for the downstream robotic control task. Evaluation involves determining actions based on real-time visual observations from the environment. The method is benchmarked extensively across a large suite of simulated tasks and challenging real-world dexterous manipulation tasks, often with very few demonstrations (e.g., 10 in simulation, 40 in real-world).

The methodology highlights a deliberate design philosophy: prioritize simplicity and efficiency in the 3D representation and encoder, and integrate it seamlessly with a powerful generative policy backbone, aiming for superior performance without unnecessary complexity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive evaluation of DP3 across 72 simulated tasks and 4 real-world robotic tasks demonstrates its significant advantages in efficiency, effectiveness, generalizability, and safety compared to baseline methods.

**A. Simulation Experiments (72 Tasks across 7 Domains)**

- **Superior Accuracy and Efficiency:**
 - DP3 achieves an average success rate of **74.4%**, a **24.2% relative improvement** over the baseline Diffusion Policy (59.8%).
 - It consistently achieves success rates exceeding 90% in nearly 30 tasks, whereas Diffusion Policy does so in fewer than 15. Critically, DP3 never recorded a task with a success rate below 10%, unlike Diffusion Policy, which had more than 10 such instances.
 - Most tasks were trained with **only 10 demonstrations**, highlighting DP3's remarkable data efficiency.
- **Faster Learning Convergence:** DP3 typically converges to high accuracy within approximately **500 training epochs**, significantly faster than Diffusion Policy, which either converges much slower or to sub-optimal results.
- **Efficient Scaling with Demonstrations:** DP3 shows strong performance even with limited data (e.g., 1 demonstration) and generally improves with more demonstrations, achieving higher accuracy than Diffusion Policy when demonstrations are sufficient, especially in complex MetaWorld tasks.
- **Competitive Inference Speed:** Contrary to the common assumption that 3D-based policies are slower, DP3 achieves an inference speed marginally surpassing Diffusion Policy. A simplified version, "Simple DP3," demonstrates a **~2x inference speed improvement** with only a minor (6%) drop in average accuracy, making it even more practical.

**B. Ablation Studies (Simulation)**

- **Choice of 3D Representations:** Point clouds prove to be the most effective 3D representation. When compared to RGB-D, depth, and voxel representations, point clouds consistently outperform them. Intriguingly, point clouds perform comparably to oracle states (perfect object and robot state information), suggesting their effectiveness in capturing task-critical information.
- **Choice of Point Cloud Encoders:** The simple, lightweight **DP3 Encoder** (a 3-layer MLP with max-pooling and a projection head) surprisingly outperforms more complex and widely used pre-trained point encoders like PointNet, PointNet++, PointNeXt, and Point Transformer. Further analysis suggests that elements like T-Nets and BatchNorm layers, common in standard PointNet architectures, can inhibit performance in control tasks, possibly due to fixed camera views not requiring explicit feature transformations.
- **Key Design Choices:** Several minor design choices contribute to DP3's success:
 - **Cropping point clouds** for irrelevant data significantly improves accuracy.
 - **LayerNormalization** stabilizes training.
 - **Sample prediction** in the noise sampler leads to faster convergence.
 - The **projection head** in the encoder accelerates inference without compromising accuracy.
 - **Removing color channels** from point clouds ensures robust appearance generalization.

**C. Real-World Experiments (4 Tasks on Allegro Hand and Gripper)**

- **High Real-World Success Rates:** DP3 demonstrates precise control and high success rates (average **85%**) across challenging real-world tasks, including dexterous manipulation of deformable objects (e.g., "Roll-Up," "Dumpling" with Allegro hand) and rigid objects ("Drill," "Pour"), using **only 40 demonstrations** per task.
- **Exceptional Generalization Abilities:**
 - **Spatial Generalization:** DP3 successfully generalizes to unseen object positions in 3D space, succeeding in 4 out of 5 novel test positions for the "Pour" task, where baselines failed.
 - **Appearance Generalization:** By operating without color information, DP3 robustly generalizes to objects of different colors (e.g., various colored cubes in "Drill"), outperforming baselines.
 - **Instance Generalization:** DP3 effectively adapts to diverse instances of objects with varying shapes and sizes (e.g., different everyday objects for "Drill"), a significant challenge for vision-based policies.
 - **View Generalization:** The method shows robustness to minor alterations in camera viewpoint, which is crucial for real-world deployment where camera positions may not be perfectly fixed.
 - **Cluttered Scenes:** DP3 performs well in cluttered "pick & place" tasks, demonstrating its ability to operate in complex, realistic environments and generalize to unseen objects/colors within those scenes.
- **Enhanced Deployment Safety:** A remarkable and practical finding is DP3's **near-zero safety violation rate** in real-world experiments. Unlike image-based and depth-based baselines that frequently exhibited erratic behaviors requiring human intervention to prevent hardware damage, DP3 rarely gave unpredictable commands, highlighting its practical and hardware-friendly nature. This suggests that explicit 3D understanding inherently contributes to safer robot operation by aiding collision avoidance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3) makes several significant contributions that could substantially impact the field of robotic learning, particularly in bridging the gap between theoretical advancements and practical, real-world robot deployment.

**1. Revolutionizing Data Efficiency in Imitation Learning:** The most immediate and profound impact of DP3 is its ability to achieve high success rates with **significantly fewer demonstrations**—as low as 10 in simulation and 40 in complex real-world dexterous tasks. This directly tackles the most notorious bottleneck in imitation learning: data collection. By drastically reducing the need for extensive human teleoperation, DP3 makes it more feasible and time-efficient for researchers and practitioners to teach new, complex skills to robots. This efficiency could accelerate the development and deployment of robotics solutions in various domains.

**2. Unprecedented Generalization Capabilities:** DP3’s strong performance in generalizing across diverse aspects—spatial variations, appearance changes, different object instances, and altered viewpoints—is a critical step towards truly robust and adaptable robot policies. Real-world environments are inherently variable, and a policy’s ability to generalize beyond its training data is paramount for practical applicability. The inherent nature of 3D representations, especially point clouds processed without color information, proves to be a powerful enabler for this robust generalization, reducing the need for extensive data augmentation or specific domain randomization.

**3. Enhancing Robot Deployment Safety:** The observation that DP3 rarely violates safety requirements in real-world experiments, unlike baseline methods that frequently necessitate human intervention, is a compelling and highly practical benefit. This "hardware-friendliness" translates to reduced risks of robot damage, less human supervision, and greater trustworthiness in autonomous operation. For industrial or domestic applications, the ability to deploy robots with high confidence in their safe operation is invaluable. This finding also suggests that direct 3D understanding can inherently contribute to safer motion planning and collision avoidance.

**4. Advancing Dexterous Manipulation:** By demonstrating successful and data-efficient learning of complex, high-dimensional dexterous skills (e.g., manipulating deformable plasticine with an Allegro hand), DP3 pushes the frontier of what can be achieved with imitation learning in this challenging domain. This opens up new possibilities for robots to perform intricate tasks that typically require human-level dexterity, traditionally a stronghold for reinforcement learning with extensive trials.

**5. Debunking Performance Myths about 3D Representations:** DP3 effectively challenges the prevailing assumption that 3D-based policies are inherently slower or more computationally intensive for real-time control. By employing simple yet effective 3D representations (sparse point clouds) and a lightweight encoder, DP3 achieves competitive, or even superior, inference speeds compared to 2D-based diffusion policies, making it suitable for tasks requiring dense, high-frequency commands. The "Simple DP3" variant further optimizes this, making the approach even more attractive for resource-constrained deployments.

**6. Guiding Future Research:** The comprehensive ablation studies provide valuable insights into the design choices for visual representations in robot learning. The finding that a simple, purpose-built MLP encoder outperforms complex pre-trained models highlights the importance of task-specific architecture design for visuomotor control. This work establishes a strong foundation for future research into optimal 3D representations for control and exploring its application to extremely long-horizon tasks, which remain a challenge.

In summary, DP3 represents a significant step forward in visuomotor imitation learning by effectively addressing data scarcity, generalization, and safety concerns. Its practical efficiency and robust performance across diverse simulated and real-world tasks position it as a highly impactful method with the potential to accelerate the adoption of advanced robotic manipulation capabilities.
