# EmbodiedMind: Adaptive Data Curation and Prefix-Tree Reinforcement Learning for Efficient Embodied Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> EmbodiedMind is an 8B embodied foundation model built on Qwen3-VL-8B through a three-stage post-training recipe: rejection-sampling SFT that keeps only informative samples, Iterative Rejection GRPO with task-specific difficulty-balanced queues and hybrid rewards, and Trie-GRPO, which computes step-level advantages over an action prefix tree. Using 522K samples, it averages 70.02% across 18 benchmarks and reaches 66.67% on VLM-PlanSim-99.

## Problem

- Embodied foundation models such as RoboBrain 2.5 depend on massive corpora (12M trajectories, 512 GPUs), yet full-data SFT wastes compute on low-informative or already-learned samples.
- DAPO-style dynamic sampling filters zero-advantage samples without task-level calibration, discarding easy tasks and starving hard ones, causing a seesaw effect across capabilities in multi-task RL.
- Standard GRPO applies one trajectory-level advantage to every token, penalizing correct intermediate plan steps for downstream errors, while MCTS-style tree search needs reversible environments and explodes combinatorially.

## Method

- **Rejection Sampling-based Fine-Tuning (RSFT)**: k ==high-temperature generations== per sample stratify data into ==Easy/Uncertain/Hard==; SFT keeps Uncertain plus a controlled share of Easy, excluding Hard to avoid erroneous trajectories.
- **Iterative Rejection GRPO (IR-GRPO)**: per-task ==FIFO queues== with quotas inversely scaled by ==EMA-smoothed effective-sample rate==, plus ==hard-sample re-queueing==, balance ==non-zero-advantage samples== across tasks, scored by five output-type-specific ==hybrid rewards==.
- **Trie-GRPO**: rollouts are parsed into canonical action tuples and merged into an ==Action Trie==; discounted returns set node ==Q-values==, and advantages are normalized among sibling actions sharing a prefix.

## Results

- EmbodiedMind-8B averages **70.02%** over 18 benchmarks versus **67.98%** for RoboBrain2.5-8B, using **522K** training samples against **12.4M**; MSMU reaches **85.81%** versus **66.96%**.
- On VLM-PlanSim-99, Trie-GRPO scores **66.33±0.82** versus **61.50** for GRPO and **58.17** after Stage 2; on ALFWorld unseen it reaches **60.45%** success versus SEEA-R1's **46.27%** (different controllers).
- IR-GRPO rounds take **155** versus **352** minutes for DAPO and avoid DAPO's Q-Spatial collapse (**66.05%** to **56.83%**); real-robot pick-and-place reaches **62%** versus **42%** for RoboBrain2.5.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Defining GRPO groups at shared action prefixes rather than whole trajectories yields step-level credit without a value network, simulator rollbacks, or environment reversibility, provided plans parse into canonical actions.
> - Zero-advantage filtering needs per-task calibration: uniform filtering silently starves hard, long-tail tasks, so multi-task RL should budget effective samples per task and replay hard ones.
> - Difficulty-based curation can substitute for data scale, but it depends on cheap verifiable rewards; on unstructured data, LLM-judged rejection sampling consumed 85% of stage time with little gain.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

An embodied agent in an AI2-THOR kitchen receives the instruction: cut bread, heat the slice in a microwave, and place it on the counter. A long plan may include several correct decisions before one downstream mistake, but standard trajectory-level reinforcement learning assigns credit—or blame—across the entire sequence. EmbodiedMind's central change is to compare alternative next actions after the same action history, so that an early correct decision can still receive positive credit even if the overall plan fails. The reported payoff is a 70.02% reported average across 18 benchmarks for the 8B model, trained on 522K samples versus RoboBrain2.5's 12.4M.

An AI2-THOR execution trace for the bread-cutting, microwave-heating, and counter-placement task. Each row shows a successive kitchen viewpoint; the ordered panels document changing bread and microwave states across the plan.

### 2. Curate the learning diet

EmbodiedMind begins with rejection-sampling fine-tuning (RSFT). The base model generates multiple answers at elevated temperature for each sample. A sample is **Easy** when all generations match the ground truth, **Uncertain** when only some match, and **Hard** when none match. RSFT retains uncertain samples plus a controlled proportion of easy samples, and excludes hard samples during early supervised fine-tuning to avoid training on trajectories far beyond current capability.

After RSFT, the RL stage (IR-GRPO) uses uncertain and hard samples. Separate task-specific FIFO queues preserve task identity; each iteration estimates the fraction of sampled examples producing nonzero advantages. An exponential moving average with β=0.8 smooths that rate, increases the next quota for difficult tasks, and re-queues hard samples that were not useful rather than discarding them. The closest baseline, DAPO, dynamically oversamples until prompts have nontrivial group variation, but EmbodiedMind argues this can undersample difficult or long-tail tasks when applied uniformly across heterogeneous embodied tasks. Across CrossPoint, Q-Spatial, EgoPlan-1, and a 14-benchmark average, IR-GRPO remains stable while DAPO often rises and then degrades; on Q-Spatial, DAPO falls from 66.05% at step 200 to 56.83% at step 1000, whereas IR-GRPO continues the more stable trajectory. The paper's aggregate Stage-2 table reports 69.89 for IR-GRPO versus 68.04 for DAPO.

### 3. Give each action its own credit

A "Cut the bread" task feeds the policy model; sampled trajectories receive reward labels; actions are parsed into a trie; Q-values and local advantages appear at sibling nodes; GRPO updates the policy, while task batching and policy/reward-model co-training participate in the iterative loop.

Standard GRPO compares rewards within a group of complete responses and applies the resulting relative advantage broadly across the response. Trie-GRPO instead parses each sampled plan into canonical action tuples—action type, target object, and receptacle—and merges trajectories sharing the same action prefix into an Action Trie. Each node represents a prefix; sibling children represent alternative next actions after the same history.

The training pipeline samples K trajectories, assigns each a binary majority-vote reward from M reward-model votes, builds the trie, propagates the final reward backward with temporal discounting to compute node Q-values, normalizes each child's Q-value against its siblings to obtain a local advantage, and discards near-zero-advantage examples using the threshold $|A| \leq 0.001$. The policy update masks the loss to tokens belonging to the selected action step, rather than applying the signal to all context tokens.

Applied to the bread task: when several sampled plans share "pick up the bread" but diverge at "slice" versus an incorrect next action, those branches are compared locally. A plan that eventually fails can still give positive credit to an earlier action if that action's sibling branch has lower estimated value. This is the authors' mechanism-level interpretation; the reported evidence is benchmark and ablation performance.

On VLM-PlanSim-99—tasks of up to 25 planning steps with an average trajectory length of 10.73 steps—Stage 2 scores 58.17 ± 0.41, GRPO scores 61.50 ± 0.84, and Trie-GRPO scores 66.33 ± 0.82 across five inference seeds. The full pipeline reaches 66.67 on VLM-PlanSim-99, compared with 36.36 for Cosmos3-Nano, a 30.31-point difference.

### 4. Credit, not just scale

EmbodiedMind's central change is to spend training effort selectively—curating examples by difficulty, rebalancing RL data by task, and comparing decisions at the point where alternatives diverge. The stronger planning results and stable task-balanced RL curves align with this design. The authors note that unstructured general multimodal data remain difficult to stratify and reward reliably, and the results show improved measured benchmark performance and training-stage behavior without establishing that the trie is the sole cause of every gain.
