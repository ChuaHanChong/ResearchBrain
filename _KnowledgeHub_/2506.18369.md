# RePIC: Reinforced Post-Training for Personalizing Multi-Modal Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> RePIC introduces a reinforcement learning framework for post-training Multi-Modal Large Language Models (MLLMs), enhancing their ability to generate personalized image captions, particularly in complex multi-concept scenarios. This approach significantly outperforms existing supervised fine-tuning methods in robustness and generalizability while being more data-efficient.

## Problem

- Multi-Modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) lack robust personalization capabilities for incorporating user-specific concepts from reference images into captions.
- Existing Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) based personalization methods are data-intensive, costly, and struggle with faithfulness, especially in complex multi-concept images and out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios.
- SFT approaches tend to exhibit poor visual recognition, often failing to differentiate objects or merely replicating provided text, even when visually inaccurate.

## Method

- A reinforcement learning (RL)-based post-training framework, **RePIC**, is introduced, employing ==Group Relative== Policy Optimization (GRPO) to enhance MLLM personalization.
- Three verifiable rewards are designed: Object Consistency Tuning (OCT) for robust visual recognition, Visual Localization Tuning (VLT) for improved object localization, and Identity Consistency Tuning (ICT) for consistent personalized generation.
- GRPO utilizes these rewards to guide MLLM learning and updates, incorporating KL-divergence regularization to maintain base model capabilities while reducing the need for large-scale supervised data.

## Results

- RePIC demonstrated superior performance over supervised fine-tuning baselines in multi-concept personalized grounding, particularly excelling in 2-concept and 4-concept out-of-domain scenarios.
- Human preference evaluations indicated higher quality, faithfulness, and detail in RePIC's generated personalized captions compared to other models.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of all verifiable reward components (OCT, VLT, ICT) for robust personalization, showing increased resistance to incorrect visual demonstrations and reduced "copy-and-paste" behavior from reference texts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning with carefully designed verifiable rewards offers a data-efficient and robust approach to MLLM personalization, outperforming supervised fine-tuning.
> - Targeted reinforcement of visual recognition and identity consistency is essential for MLLMs to generate faithful personalized captions, particularly in complex, multi-concept scenarios.
> - KL-divergence regularization during RL post-training allows for enhanced personalization capabilities without degrading the base MLLM's existing general image captioning abilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the rapidly evolving field of Multi-Modal Large Language Models (MLLMs), which have extended the impressive capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) to integrate and process visual information alongside text. MLLMs like LLaVA and Qwen-VL have demonstrated strong performance in various vision-language tasks, including image-grounded dialogue and captioning.

However, a critical limitation of current MLLMs is their struggle with **personalization**. This refers to the ability to recognize and incorporate user-specific concepts, such as specific individuals or objects, from reference images and associated descriptions into their generated outputs. Existing MLLM personalization methods generally fall into two categories:
1. **Retraining-required approaches:** Methods like MyVLM and Yo’LLaVA require retraining or learning new concept embeddings whenever a new personal concept is introduced. This approach lacks scalability and is impractical for real-world applications where new concepts continuously emerge.
2. **Post-training (SFT-based) approaches:** Methods like PVIT and RAP-MLLM fine-tune MLLMs using Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on large-scale, label-annotated datasets of question-answering pairs. While these methods allow for personalization without retraining for every new concept, they are heavily data-centric. They rely on proprietary MLLMs (e.g., GPT-4o, Gemini) to generate high-quality captions for training, which is costly, labor-intensive, and often leads to models that still struggle with "faithful descriptions" in complex, real-world scenarios, particularly those involving "multi-concept image captioning" (e.g., images with 3-4 distinct identities). SFT-based methods also exhibit limitations in visual recognition and generalization to out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios.

RePIC addresses these limitations by introducing the **first reinforcement learning (RL)-based post-training framework for personalized image captioning**. While RL has been successfully applied to LLMs for alignment and reasoning, and to MLLMs for hallucination mitigation and visual reasoning (e.g., Visual-RFT, Vision-R1), its application to the specific challenge of *personalized image captioning* in MLLMs is novel. By leveraging RL, RePIC aims to reduce the reliance on vast amounts of meticulously curated, high-quality personalized caption data that SFT methods demand, and instead learn more robust and generalizable personalization capabilities. This shift towards RL-based post-training represents a significant departure from prevailing SFT-centric paradigms in MLLM personalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a more effective and data-efficient method for personalizing Multi-Modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for image captioning tasks, particularly in challenging multi-concept scenarios. The authors identify two key capabilities that MLLMs must possess for successful personalized image captioning:

1. **Robust visual recognition ability:** The model must consistently identify the same object or person across different images, even when variations in pose, location, lighting, and background are present. This ensures that the MLLM accurately and faithfully describes the visual content of the query image.
2. **Consistent personalized generation ability:** The model must effectively incorporate personal information (e.g., names, specific details) from demonstrations into its generated responses, correctly referencing the provided identities.

The motivation behind pursuing a reinforcement learning (RL)-based approach stems from the observed shortcomings of existing Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) methods:
- **Data scarcity and cost:** SFT-based methods heavily depend on large-scale, high-quality, human-annotated captions, especially for complex, multi-concept images. Acquiring such data is prohibitively costly and difficult.
- **Lack of faithfulness in complex scenarios:** Even after extensive SFT, current MLLMs often fail to generate faithful personalized captions in real-world settings, particularly when multiple distinct concepts are present (e.g., 3-4 concepts). The authors observe that SFT methods underperform due to the scarcity of multi-concept captions in training data (e.g., only 5.4% of a 210K dataset in RAP-MLLM).
- **Poor generalization to OOD scenarios:** SFT methods tend to overfit to the training data, exhibiting limited visual recognition and poor generalization to out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios, such as images with a higher number of concepts than seen during training.
- **Vulnerability to incorrect demonstrations:** SFT-based models struggle to differentiate objects or resist merely duplicating provided textual information, even if it contradicts the visual content.

To overcome these data-centric and generalization limitations of SFT, RePIC proposes an RL-based post-training framework that can learn from targeted feedback signals, thereby enhancing both visual recognition and personalized generation without requiring vast amounts of perfect, human-curated caption data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RePIC proposes a Reinforced post-training for Personalized Image Captioning (RePIC) framework, which leverages **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**, an RL algorithm, to enhance MLLM capabilities. The core idea is to guide the MLLM's learning through carefully designed verifiable rewards (VRs) that directly address the identified shortcomings of SFT in visual recognition and personalized generation.

#### 3.1. Preliminary: Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)

GRPO is an improved RL algorithm built upon PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization). It optimizes a clipped surrogate objective while simultaneously minimizing the Kullback-Leibler (KL) Divergence between the current policy (πθ) and a reference policy (πref). This KL regularization helps prevent policy drift and maintain the base MLLM's general capabilities. Key features of GRPO for RePIC's application:
- **Group-based learning:** For each task, the model generates a group of responses (rollouts), and each response is assigned a reward. GRPO leverages these group-level relative preferences.
- **Verifiable Rewards (VR):** RePIC uses VRs, which are directly computed from the model's output based on predefined rules, eliminating the need for an auxiliary reward model (e.g., human feedback model).
- **Stability and data efficiency:** GRPO is noted for its stability and ability to learn effectively even with sparse reward signals, which is beneficial in early stages when the MLLM's personalization ability is nascent.

#### 3.2. Proposed Verifiable Rewards (VRs)

RePIC introduces three distinct VRs, each targeting a specific aspect of personalization:

1. **Object Consistency Tuning (OCT):**
 - **Purpose:** To strengthen the MLLM's robust visual recognition ability.
 - **Mechanism:** Positive-negative pairs of images are constructed. Positive pairs contain the same object across different images (e.g., varying pose, lighting), while negative pairs contain different objects. The MLLM is prompted with binary questions like "Is `<name>` present in the second image?".
 - **Reward:** A VR of 1 is assigned for a correct "yes" (for positive pairs) or "no" (for negative pairs) response, and 0 otherwise.
 - **Data:** Uses real datasets (COCO, Objects365, CelebA) and high-quality synthetic images from Subject 200K+ (generated via diffusion models) to ensure visual diversity and variations in attributes.

2. **Visual Localization Tuning (VLT):**
 - **Purpose:** To further reinforce the MLLM's visual recognition by improving its ability to localize objects within an image.
 - **Mechanism:** Adapts an IoU-based accuracy reward template from prior work (VLM-R1). The MLLM is prompted to predict bounding box (BBox) coordinates based on a query instruction (e.g., "Provide the bounding box coordinate of...").
 - **Reward:** A VR of 1 is assigned if the predicted BBox has an Intersection over Union (IoU) score greater than or equal to 0.5 with the ground truth BBox, and 0 otherwise.
 - **Data:** Utilizes Refcoco/+/g datasets, commonly used for referring expression comprehension (REC). Empirical findings suggest this task is crucial for RL training stability.

3. **Identity Consistency Tuning (ICT):**
 - **Purpose:** To enhance the MLLM's ability to generate personalized responses by consistently incorporating provided names and information.
 - **Mechanism:** Involves positive pairs of reference images and a query image. Each reference image is assigned a unique name (e.g., `<name>`). The MLLM is prompted with descriptive questions like "Describe the query image while referencing the reference images."
 - **Reward (Single-ICT):** For a single reference image, a VR of 1 if the `<name>` appears in the output, 0 otherwise.
 - **Reward (Multi-ICT):** For `m` (up to 3 in experiments) given names, the reward is `n/m` if `n` names are correctly mentioned in the output, 0 otherwise. This encourages mentioning all given identities.
 - **Data:** Uses positive pair images from OCT, and manually curated multi-object images from COCO and Objects365.
 - **Additional strategies:** Descriptive prompts (e.g., "describe this image in detail") and output length regularization (minimum length of 100 tokens) are applied to encourage richer and more informative captions and prevent "reward hacking" (e.g., merely outputting "This is `<name>`.").

#### 3.3. Overall Framework

During training, the frozen vision encoder processes images, and the LLM decoder generates responses. These responses are then evaluated by the verifiable rewards. The GRPO algorithm uses these rewards, along with the KL-divergence term, to update the LLM's parameters. During inference (both skip-retrieval and retrieval settings), the post-trained MLLM generates personalized captions based on query images and reference information.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The RePIC framework demonstrates significant improvements in personalized image captioning, particularly in complex multi-concept scenarios, consistently outperforming existing SFT-based baselines.

#### 4.1. Personal Grounding Performance

- **Single-Concept Setting (Table 1):**
 - RAP-Qwen (SFT-based, 210K samples) achieves the highest F1 scores in skip-retrieval.
 - RePIC (RL-based, 2K samples) performs comparably to RAP-Qwen and significantly outperforms RAP-LLaVA (2K samples), highlighting its data efficiency.
 - PVIT-LLaVA (reproduced baseline) shows notably low performance, attributed to its human-centric training data.
 - RAP-LLaVA trained on only 2K samples shows a significant performance drop, underscoring the data dependency of SFT.
- **Multi-Concept Setting (Table 2):**
 - This is where RePIC truly excels. SFT-based methods (RAP-LLaVA, RAP-Qwen) show significant performance drops in both 2-concept and especially 4-concept scenarios, highlighting their struggle with generalization.
 - The zero-shot Qwen model sometimes even outperforms SFT-based models in OOD (4-concept) personal grounding, indicating the limitations of SFT.
 - **RePIC (2K samples) consistently and substantially outperforms all SFT-based baselines in both 2-concept (in-domain) and 4-concept (out-of-domain) settings.** The performance gap is most pronounced in the challenging 4-concept setting, demonstrating superior generalizability.

#### 4.2. Preference Evaluation (Human-level Quality with GPT-4o/Gemini 2.0 Flash)

- (Figure 3, Figure A.11) RePIC consistently achieves higher preference scores than all other baselines and ablation variants in both single-concept and 2-concept settings. This indicates that RePIC generates higher-quality, more faithful, and detailed personalized captions according to human-aligned criteria.
- Qualitative examples (Figure 4) visually confirm RePIC's ability to accurately recognize all identities and produce faithful descriptions, while SFT methods often miss identities or generate inaccurate captions.

#### 4.3. Image Caption Quality Evaluations

- **Reference-based (Table 3):** RePIC shows strong competitive performance, achieving the best scores in METEOR and BLEU, and second-best in CIDEr, SPICE, and BERTScore against GT captions generated by GPT-4o. This indicates good overall caption quality.
- **Reference-free (Table 4):** RePIC achieves the highest CLIPScore and the second-best ImageReward score, suggesting better image-text alignment and human preference alignment for general caption quality.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies (Table 6)

- **Necessity of RL:** SFT-based methods struggle significantly when provided with *incorrect visual demonstrations* (Table 5), demonstrating their inability to distinguish objects and their tendency to merely duplicate provided information. RePIC, conversely, yields lower scores with incorrect demonstrations, showing robustness in grounding captions visually. Furthermore, SFT's poor performance in multi-concept scenarios (Table 2), despite comparable multi-identity training data proportion to RePIC, highlights RL's superiority in learning from diverse, complex data.
- **Efficacy of Each Component:**
 - Removing **Identity Consistency Tuning (ICT)** causes a drastic decline in personal grounding performance, confirming its critical role.
 - Removing **Object Consistency Tuning (OCT)** weakens scores, emphasizing the importance of reinforced visual recognition.
 - Removing **Visual Localization Tuning (VLT)** also leads to a performance drop, suggesting its contribution to personal grounding by improving object localization.
 - Using **reasoning templates** (<think>, <observe>) degrades personal grounding performance, leading to longer, less faithful descriptions (Figure A.5), contrary to their typical use in visual reasoning tasks.
 - Additional components like **length regularization** and **detailed prompts** enhance caption quality by preventing short, uninformative outputs (Figure A.4).
- **Training Stability (Figure 5, Figure A.12):** RePIC demonstrates stable RL training, with verifiable rewards for OCT, Single-ICT, and Multi-ICT showing clear upward trends as training progresses, indicating effective learning of personalization capabilities. KL-divergence regularization helps maintain stability (Figure A.18).
- **Copy-and-Paste Analysis (Table A.2, A.3, A.4):** RePIC exhibits significantly less copy-and-paste behavior from reference texts (even incorrect ones) compared to SFT baselines. It also generates more diverse captions, further confirming its robust visual understanding and ability to avoid mere textual duplication.
- **General Image Captioning (Figure A.15, A.16, A.17):** RePIC enhances faithfulness and preferability under general query prompts without degrading the original MLLM's zero-shot capabilities, thanks to KL-divergence regularization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RePIC framework introduces a significant advancement in the field of Multi-Modal Large Language Model (MLLM) personalization, offering a compelling alternative to prevalent Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) approaches. Its key significance and potential impacts are:

- **Pioneering RL-based MLLM Personalization:** RePIC is the first work to successfully apply a reinforcement learning framework to the task of personalized image captioning in MLLMs. This opens up a new paradigm for tackling personalization challenges beyond the limitations of SFT.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Generalizability:** The core strength of RePIC lies in its ability to achieve superior performance, especially in complex multi-concept scenarios and out-of-distribution (OOD) settings (e.g., 4-concept images not seen during training). This demonstrates that RL-based post-training can equip MLLMs with more robust visual recognition and personalized generation capabilities, which are crucial for real-world applications.
- **Data-Efficient Learning:** By leveraging verifiable rewards, RePIC significantly alleviates the heavy reliance on costly and labor-intensive large-scale, high-quality personalized caption datasets that SFT methods demand. This makes MLLM personalization more accessible and scalable.
- **Improved Faithfulness and Quality of Captions:** Through its meticulously designed verifiable rewards (OCT, VLT, ICT) and strategies like length regularization, RePIC generates more faithful, detailed, and human-preferred personalized captions, as evidenced by both quantitative metrics and human preference evaluations. It also shows less "copy-and-paste" behavior, indicating deeper visual grounding.
- **Broader Applicability:** The demonstrated ability of RePIC to handle diverse personalized image captioning tasks, including virtual try-on, multi-human personalization, and style-driven personalization (Figure A.2), suggests its potential for wide application across various domains requiring fine-grained, personalized visual understanding.
- **Maintaining Base Model Capabilities:** The use of KL-divergence regularization in GRPO ensures that the RL-based fine-tuning enhances personalization without degrading the MLLM's original general image captioning and instruction-following abilities.

**Potential Impact:**
- **More User-Centric AI:** RePIC paves the way for more intelligent and user-aware MLLMs that can understand and articulate specific user concepts in visual data, leading to more natural and helpful AI assistants.
- **Advanced Applications:** It could enable advancements in personalized content generation, virtual assistants, accessibility tools, and customized product recommendations by improving the AI's ability to precisely describe user-specific entities.
- **Future Research Directions:** The work highlights the power of RL in multimodal contexts and encourages further exploration of RL for other personalized tasks (e.g., multi-turn conversations) and other modalities (audio, video), aligning with the development of "Omni" models. It also points to research in reducing retrieval noise and exploring alternatives to simple length regularization for generating diverse and informative captions.

**Limitations Acknowledged by Authors:**
The authors transparently discuss several limitations:
- The baselines may not have undergone exhaustive hyperparameter tuning.
- The primary focus was on personalized image captioning, suggesting future work could extend to other personalized tasks.
- Reliance on GPT-4o for generating GT captions for quality evaluation might introduce bias, and human refinement would be ideal.
- RePIC's current limitations in fine-grained visual perception (e.g., hallucinating non-existent clothes) and handling significant differences between reference and query images (e.g., back vs. front view of a person) or corner cases where concepts are absent from the query image. These suggest areas for further improvement, possibly through higher-fidelity vision encoders or more robust reward mechanisms for penalizing incorrect information.

In summary, RePIC represents a significant step forward in MLLM personalization by demonstrating the effectiveness and efficiency of an RL-based post-training approach, particularly for complex, multi-concept visual understanding tasks.
