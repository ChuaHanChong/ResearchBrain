# SG-VLA: Learning Spatially-Grounded Vision-Language-Action Models for Mobile Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The SG-VLA framework facilitates Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for mobile manipulation in complex household environments by integrating multi-modal sensory inputs and a progressive multi-task learning scheme with auxiliary decoders. This approach achieved an average success rate of 0.73 on the SetTable subset of the ManiSkill-HAB benchmark, representing a 22% improvement over VLA models without auxiliary tasks.

## Problem

- VLA models typically exhibit strong performance in constrained tabletop environments but struggle to adapt to the complexities of mobile manipulation in real-world domestic settings.
- Mobile manipulation tasks require navigating high-dimensional continuous action spaces and demand robust spatial and scene-level reasoning, which is not adequately addressed by standard VLA configurations.
- Direct imitation learning, often used for VLA models, provides an insufficient supervisory signal for acquiring the rich, multi-faceted understanding necessary for complex mobile manipulation.

## Method

- SG-VLA enhances a pre-trained VLM backbone by incorporating multi-modal inputs, including multi-view RGB observations, explicit depth cues, and short temporal history for comprehensive spatial understanding.
- The framework employs five auxiliary decoders to provide dense supervisory signals by reconstructing interpretable intermediate states such as global robot position, joint configurations, grasp affordances, target-object relative pose, and segmentation masks.
- A progressive multi-stage training scheme is utilized to effectively integrate auxiliary decoders and an optional flow matching action head, adapting decoders first, then jointly refining representations, and finally training the action head in isolation.

## Results

- SG-VLA achieved an average success rate of 0.73 on the SetTable subset of ManiSkill-HAB, which is a 22% improvement over the best input modality configuration without auxiliary tasks.
- Individual auxiliary tasks, particularly joint position prediction, provided substantial improvements, yielding an average success rate of 0.71 and excelling in place tasks (0.67) and drawer operations (0.70 Open, 0.90 Close).
- The flow matching action head improved pick tasks (from 0.13 to 0.27) and place tasks (0.70 to 0.80), demonstrating its value for precise manipulation, though it showed decreased performance on some mobility-oriented tasks like opening fridges or drawers.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Enriching VLA models with multi-view RGB and explicit depth information significantly improves performance in mobile manipulation, leading to a substantial increase in success rates from 0.04 to 0.60.
> - Auxiliary tasks provide crucial dense supervision, enabling the VLM backbone to learn more spatially grounded and manipulation-aware latent representations that are critical for complex tasks.
> - A progressive multi-stage training approach is essential for effectively integrating diverse auxiliary objectives without degrading the VLM's foundational capabilities, outperforming naive co-training.
