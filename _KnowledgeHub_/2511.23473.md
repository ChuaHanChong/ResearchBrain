# ThetaEvolve: Test-time Learning on Open Problems

> [!summary] Summary
> ThetaEvolve is an open-source framework that simplifies AlphaEvolve to a single small LLM. It uses a large program database, batch sampling and lazy penalties, and optionally trains the model with reinforcement learning at test time on a verifiable evaluator. An 8B model sets new best-known bounds on circle packing and the first autocorrelation inequality, and RL beats inference-only runs.

## Problem

- AlphaEvolve is closed-source and depends on ensembles of frontier LLMs, so it is unclear which pipeline components matter or whether small open models can improve bounds on open problems.
- Open reimplementations such as OpenEvolve and ShinkaEvolve are complex, with many hyperparameters left unablated, and remain paired with large closed-source models rather than small local ones.
- AlphaEvolve is inference-only and never updates model weights, so effective exploration strategies discovered during evolution cannot be internalized or transferred to new tasks.

## Method

- A simplified ==dynamic environment==: one LLM instead of an ensemble, a much larger ==program database== (population 10000 versus 70), and optional parent-only prompts. ==Batch sampling== draws B parents and n responses each per step.
- Early-check ==lazy penalties== give negative scores (-0.4 to -0.1) for missing diffs, unchanged programs, or failed or invalid solutions, and also penalize children duplicating any database program.
- Optional ==test-time RL== uses ==GRPO== with asymmetric clipping. A ==reward-shaping== function clips a linear map of the score to [0,1], applies a power alpha, and scales by 3 to stabilize narrow score ranges.
- The RL loop trains on programs evolved in the dynamic environment rather than always restarting from the initial program.

## Results

- Distill-Qwen3-8B reaches circle packing **2.63598308**, above AlphaEvolve's **2.63586276**, and first autocorrelation inequality **1.503133** versus **1.503164**. Its circle-packing program runs in about **3 seconds** versus roughly **75** for ShinkaEvolve.
- RL beats inference-only across two models and four tasks. On ProRL-1.5B-v2 circle packing, mean **2.3498** and best **2.5225** versus **2.0991** and **2.2491**. A checkpoint trained on circle packing also transfers to unseen tasks.
- Static-environment RL scores **1.5985** versus **2.3498** on circle packing, and a format-only reward gives **1.6783** versus **1.6412** on the third autocorrelation task. Batch sampling cuts run time from **63.6** to **5.4 hours**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Test-time RL only helps when the environment keeps evolving. Training on a trajectory of incremental improvements from a growing database beats restarting from the initial program, which is too far from the frontier.
> - Scaling test-time compute matters more than pipeline sophistication, and a larger database pays off only at high compute. Small databases progress faster early but plateau, so scale both together.
> - Small open models can reach frontier bounds, and RL on one task yields transferable evolutionary skill. Reward-shaping ranges should be set from a no-RL run, with alpha=1 and a narrow range as the safe default.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

ThetaEvolve operates at the confluence of several rapidly advancing fields: large language models (LLMs), reinforcement learning (RL), and automated scientific discovery, particularly in mathematics and optimization. It directly responds to limitations identified in pioneering works and seeks to democratize and enhance these capabilities.

**Emergence of LLMs in Scientific Discovery:**
The paper acknowledges the transformative role of LLMs in scientific findings, citing examples in mathematical discovery (Romera-Paredes et al., Charton et al.). A key predecessor is **AlphaEvolve** (Novikov et al., 2025; Georgiev et al., 2025), a closed-source system developed by Google DeepMind. AlphaEvolve demonstrated the power of LLMs in iteratively evolving programs to improve bounds on open mathematical problems, achieving state-of-the-art results. Its success, alongside similar program search initiatives like FunSearch (Romera-Paredes et al., 2024), established a new paradigm for using LLMs as generative agents for scientific exploration.

**Limitations of Prior Art and the Gap Addressed:**
Despite its breakthroughs, AlphaEvolve (and its open-source variants like OpenEvolve and ShinkaEvolve) presented several limitations that ThetaEvolve aims to address:
1. **Closed-Source Nature:** AlphaEvolve's proprietary nature hindered systematic study and community contribution. ThetaEvolve's open-source release directly tackles this.
2. **Reliance on Frontier LLM Ensembles:** AlphaEvolve typically leveraged ensembles of powerful, closed-source LLMs (e.g., Gemini-2.0-Flash/Pro). This suggested that smaller, open-source models might not be capable of similar achievements, limiting accessibility. ThetaEvolve challenges this by demonstrating SOTA with a single open-source 8B model.
3. **Pure Inference System:** Crucially, AlphaEvolve was an inference-only pipeline. While it could *find* effective programs, the underlying LLM itself did not *learn* from these evolutionary experiences. The performance relied entirely on prompt design and search heuristics.

**Integration of Reinforcement Learning:**
The paper draws a strong connection to the success of reinforcement learning (RL) in enhancing LLM reasoning capabilities (Gao et al., Lambert et al.). Specifically, it highlights **AlphaProof** (Hubert et al., 2025), which couples a pre-trained LLM with an AlphaZero-style RL loop within a verifiable environment (the Lean proof assistant). AlphaProof's use of Test-Time RL (TTRL) for problem-specific adaptation is a direct inspiration. ThetaEvolve extends this concept to the program evolution domain, treating the AlphaEvolve-style pipeline as an "adaptive verifiable environment" suitable for continuous-reward RL optimization.

**Distinction from Prompt Optimization/Agentic LLMs:**
While related to prompt optimization (Yang et al., Khattab et al.) and agentic LLMs that manage context and feedback (Shinn et al., Zhang et al.), ThetaEvolve is distinguished by its specific focus on *program optimization for continuous objectives on challenging open problems*. Unlike general agentic systems, ThetaEvolve integrates RL directly into the evolutionary search process, allowing the model to internalize problem-solving strategies rather than merely executing inference-time heuristics or refining prompts.

In essence, ThetaEvolve aims to bridge the gap between powerful LLM-driven evolutionary search and the model-updating capabilities of RL. It seeks to make cutting-edge scientific discovery tools more accessible, efficient, and, most importantly, *intelligent* by enabling LLMs to genuinely learn and improve their evolutionary strategies over time.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind ThetaEvolve stems from the desire to overcome the limitations of existing LLM-driven scientific discovery frameworks, particularly AlphaEvolve, and to unlock new avenues for model improvement and accessibility.

**Motivation:**
1. **Democratization of Scientific Discovery:** The success of closed-source systems like AlphaEvolve, while impressive, created a high barrier to entry for academic researchers and smaller teams. The reliance on expensive, proprietary, and often frontier LLM ensembles further limited broader adoption and systematic study. There was a clear need for an open-source, resource-efficient alternative that could still achieve competitive or even superior results.
2. **Addressing the "Inference-Only" Limitation:** A critical observation was that AlphaEvolve, despite its iterative nature, was purely an inference system. The underlying LLM did not learn from the generated programs or the feedback from evaluators. This meant that any "evolving strategy" or "search-on-the-edge" behavior remained external to the model, relying solely on prompt engineering and external search algorithms. This represented a missed opportunity for the LLM itself to internalize and improve its problem-solving intelligence.
3. **Enhancing Efficiency and Scalability:** Existing open-source variants of AlphaEvolve were often complex, with many unablated hyperparameters, leading to inefficiencies. There was a need to simplify the pipeline, improve throughput, and scale test-time compute more effectively.

**Key Objectives of ThetaEvolve:**
Based on these motivations, ThetaEvolve sets out to achieve the following core objectives:

1. **Develop an Open-Source Framework:** To provide a transparent, accessible, and extensible platform for program evolution on open problems, fostering collaborative research and community development.
2. **Enable Smaller Open-Source Models to Achieve State-of-the-Art (SOTA) Bounds:** To challenge the prevailing notion that only frontier, large-scale, closed-source LLM ensembles can push the boundaries of scientific discovery. The goal was to demonstrate that smaller, more accessible models could achieve new best-known bounds.
3. **Integrate Test-Time Reinforcement Learning (RL) for Continuous Learning:** To enable LLMs to continually learn from their experiences in improving open optimization problems. This allows the model to internalize effective evolutionary strategies, moving beyond mere inference to genuine adaptation and skill acquisition.
4. **Introduce Novel Architectural and Algorithmic Enhancements:** To simplify the pipeline (e.g., single LLM), improve inference efficiency (e.g., batch sampling), enhance exploration (e.g., large program database), and stabilize RL training (e.g., lazy penalties, reward shaping).
5. **Demonstrate Transferability of Learned Capabilities:** To investigate whether the evolutionary capabilities learned through RL on one task can generalize and improve performance on other, unseen tasks, hinting at a more general post-training recipe for LLMs.

In essence, ThetaEvolve seeks to evolve not just programs, but also the *intelligence* of the LLM itself, making the process of scientific discovery more autonomous, efficient, and widely available.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ThetaEvolve builds upon the foundation of the AlphaEvolve/OpenEvolve pipeline, introducing a series of strategic simplifications, architectural enhancements, and a novel integration of test-time reinforcement learning.

**Core AlphaEvolve/OpenEvolve Pipeline (Baseline):**
The framework starts with the established structure of program evolution:
- **Manual Preparation:** Requires a manually designed, unhackable evaluator (mapping solutions to scalar scores), an initial program example, and meta-information (problem description, improvement directions) for the prompt.
- **Program Database:** Stores high-scoring or diverse programs discovered during evolution.
- **Prompt Builder:** Constructs prompts using meta-information, prior programs (or parent program), their evaluation scores, and instructions.
- **LLM (Ensemble):** Generates responses (CoT reasoning + SEARCH/REPLACE diff blocks).
- **Verifier:** Parses LLM responses, applies diffs to create child programs.
- **Evaluator:** Task-specific function to check validity and compute objective scores.
- **Dynamic Environment:** Child programs and results are added back to the database, continuing the evolutionary loop.

**ThetaEvolve's Key Features and Modifications:**

1. **Direct Adjustments and Simplifications:**
 - **Single LLM:** Unlike AlphaEvolve's reliance on LLM ensembles, ThetaEvolve successfully employs a single LLM, significantly reducing computational overhead and complexity.
 - **Large Program Database:** ThetaEvolve scales the program database significantly (population size = 10,000) compared to OpenEvolve (70), enhancing exploration by maintaining a wider diversity of candidate programs.
 - **(Optional) Iterative Refinement:** Simplifies prompt construction by only including the immediate "parent program" (and its result) rather than multiple prior programs from the database, reducing context length and improving efficiency.

2. **Batch Sampling and Generation:**
 - To overcome the inefficiency of sequential sampling in AlphaEvolve (one prompt, one response per iteration), ThetaEvolve introduces **batch sampling**. At each step, it samples *B* parent programs from the database, generates *n* responses for each, yielding a total of *B x n* child programs. This approach leverages optimized batched inference engines (e.g., vLLM, SGLang) for significantly higher throughput.
 - Generated programs are then added sequentially to the database, which is re-organized after each insertion.

3. **Early Check and Lazy Penalty:**
 - To make the evaluation process more robust and provide better signals for RL, ThetaEvolve implements a series of early checks before full program evaluation. Programs are assigned penalty scores for:
 - Missing SEARCH/REPLACE diff blocks (-0.4)
 - No valid changes (child program identical to parent) (-0.3)
 - No solution (compilation/execution errors, timeouts) (-0.2)
 - Invalid solution (fails evaluator's validity checks) (-0.1)
 - Crucially for RL, an additional **lazy penalty** is applied to any child program equivalent (up to comment removal) to *any* program already in the database. This discourages the model from merely repeating previously found strong solutions without attempting improvements.

4. **(Optional) RL Reward Shaping:**
 - For tasks with narrow objective ranges or minimization objectives, a normalized reward function is introduced to provide a more stable and effective training signal for RL.
 - The reward `R(s)` is scaled by `k` and derived from a shaping function `F(s) = {clip(H(s), 0, 1)}^alpha`, where `H(s)` linearly maps the raw score `s` from a task-specific range `[L, U]` to ``. A larger `alpha` aggressively rewards scores closer to the best-known value. This allows fine-tuning the reward signal to the specific characteristics of the problem and the model's current capabilities.

**Reinforcement Learning (RL) Training Details:**
- ThetaEvolve integrates GRPO (Generalized Policy Optimization) as its RL algorithm.
- Enhancements like asymmetric clipping (clip low = 0.2, clip high = 0.28) and truncated importance sampling are used to improve training stability.
- The learning rate is set to 10^-6 with a weight decay of 0.1.
- No dynamic sampling, KL-divergence, or entropy regularization is included to ensure a fair comparison with inference baselines.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Models:** Two open-source LLMs: ProRL-1.5B-v2 and DeepSeek-R1-0528-Qwen3-8B (referred to as Distill-Qwen3-8B).
- **Tasks:** Five open mathematical problems from AlphaEvolve: CirclePacking-T (N=26), First/Second/Third Autocorrelation Inequality, and HadamardMatrix (N=29). Notations regarding evaluation differences for CirclePacking-T and typos in ThirdAutoCorrIneq are noted.
- **Multi-Seed Runs:** Experiments are conducted with three random seeds (42, 1234, 3407) to reduce variance.

The methodology strategically combines efficient inference scaling with robust RL training mechanisms, all within a flexible, open-source framework designed to tackle challenging scientific optimization problems.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ThetaEvolve demonstrates compelling results, successfully addressing its core objectives of enabling smaller open-source models to achieve SOTA, integrating effective test-time RL, and improving the efficiency of program evolution.

1. **Achieving New Best-Known Bounds with a Small Open-Source LLM:**
 - A single open-source 8B model, **Distill-Qwen3-8B**, powered by ThetaEvolve, achieved new best-known bounds on two open problems mentioned in AlphaEvolve: **CirclePacking** and the **First Auto-correlation Inequality**.
 - For Circle Packing, ThetaEvolve surpassed AlphaEvolve's result (2.63586276) with a score of 2.6359857 (with RL) and 2.6359834 (without RL). This is particularly notable as AlphaEvolve achieved its results using ensembles of frontier LLMs like Gemini-2.0-Flash/Pro.
 - The program discovered for circle packing is also significantly faster, taking only 3 seconds to find the best solution compared to 75 seconds for ShinkaEvolve (which uses an ensemble of six advanced closed-source models).
 - For the First Auto-correlation Inequality, the model achieved a score of 1.5068, surpassing the previous human SOTA (1.5097), though slightly worse than AlphaEvolve-v2 (1.5032). When initialized with AlphaEvolve-v2's SOTA solution, ThetaEvolve could still make slight further improvements (1.5031324360 vs 1.5031635547).

2. **Reinforcement Learning Consistently Outperforms Inference-Only:**
 - Across two open-source models (ProRL-1.5B-v2 and Distill-Qwen3-8B) and four open tasks, ThetaEvolve with RL consistently achieved better mean and best objective scores compared to inference-only baselines, even with fewer training steps.
 - Both RL and inference-only settings showed significant improvements over initial programs, underscoring the general effectiveness of the evolutionary pipeline.

3. **Model Learns Transferable Evolutionary Capabilities:**
 - **Internalization of Strategy:** When an RL-trained checkpoint (e.g., ProRL-1.5B-v2 on CirclePacking-T) was used for *pure inference* on the same task, it demonstrated significantly faster progress and achieved better scores than the original, untuned base model. This provides strong evidence that the RL process meaningfully updated the model parameters, teaching it effective evolutionary strategies.
 - **Cross-Task Generalization:** This learned evolutionary capability was found to transfer to *unseen tasks* (HadamardMatrix and ThirdAutoCorrIneq). The RL-trained checkpoint improved average performance and sometimes matched or surpassed dedicated RL runs on these new tasks. This suggests a potential for developing a more general post-training recipe for evolutionary AI.

4. **Importance of Dynamic Environment and Ground-Truth Rewards:**
 - **Dynamic vs. Static RL:** RL applied in ThetaEvolve's dynamic environment (continually learning from updated program databases) drastically outperformed RL in a static environment (always starting from the initial program). The static environment performed even worse than pure inference with ThetaEvolve, highlighting the necessity of an adaptive environment for tackling challenging, multi-step open problems where direct optimal solutions are extremely sparse.
 - **Beyond Format Learning:** A "format reward" baseline (rewarding only syntactically correct output) proved ineffective for challenging open problems, performing worse than pure inference. This confirms that ThetaEvolve with a ground-truth evaluator enables the model to learn non-trivial problem-solving capabilities, not just code generation heuristics.

5. **Ablation Studies Validate Key Components:**
 - **Scaling Database Size:** Larger program databases (population size 10,000) led to better final performance, especially when scaling test-time compute, by promoting diversity in the evolutionary search. Smaller databases showed faster initial progress but quickly plateaued.
 - **Batch Sampling Efficiency:** ThetaEvolve's batch sampling approach achieved significantly faster inference (5.4 hours for 204.8K programs) compared to OpenEvolve's sequential sampling (63.6 hours), demonstrating substantial throughput improvements.
 - **Reward Shaping:** Reward shaping was found to be beneficial, particularly for tasks with narrow objective ranges. However, optimal parameters (U, L, α) were model-dependent, emphasizing the need for tuning based on observed performance.
 - **Database Management:** The MAP-Elites algorithm and island-based models for database management (inherited from AlphaEvolve/OpenEvolve) were shown to be important, with a simplified priority queue leading to weaker performance.

Overall, the findings conclusively demonstrate that ThetaEvolve provides an efficient, learnable, and open-source framework that empowers smaller LLMs to achieve SOTA in scientific discovery by effectively integrating reinforcement learning with an adaptive evolutionary environment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ThetaEvolve represents a significant advancement in the field of AI for scientific discovery, with far-reaching implications across research and practical applications.

1. **Democratization of Scientific Discovery with LLMs:** By enabling a single, small, open-source LLM (like DeepSeek-R1-0528-Qwen3-8B) to achieve new best-known bounds on open mathematical problems, ThetaEvolve drastically lowers the barrier to entry for LLM-driven scientific research. This open-source framework makes advanced discovery tools accessible to a wider community of researchers who may not have access to frontier closed-source models or extensive computational resources for large ensembles. This shift is crucial for fostering innovation and diverse contributions to scientific progress.

2. **Advancement in LLM Autonomy and Learning Capabilities:** The most profound impact lies in demonstrating that LLMs can *learn to evolve* rather than just *execute evolutionary prompts*. The fact that an RL-trained checkpoint performs better even in pure inference mode and generalizes to unseen tasks suggests that the model internalizes a deeper understanding of problem-solving strategies. This pushes LLMs beyond mere pattern matching or sophisticated text generation towards genuine adaptive intelligence, capable of acquiring new skills directly from experiential feedback in complex, dynamic environments. This capability is foundational for building truly autonomous AI agents.

3. **A Novel Paradigm for Optimization and Scientific Research:** ThetaEvolve offers a robust framework for tackling open-ended optimization problems with continuous rewards. This goes beyond mathematical problems, potentially extending to diverse real-world applications in materials science, drug discovery, engineering design, and algorithm optimization. The dynamic, verifiable environment coupled with RL provides a powerful mechanism for systematic exploration and continuous improvement, where the AI itself learns how to best navigate the search space.

4. **Implications for Future LLM Post-Training:** The observed task-transfer phenomenon—where evolutionary capabilities learned on one task generalize to others—is highly significant. This suggests that RL within dynamic evolutionary environments could become a general post-training recipe for LLMs, enhancing their foundational reasoning and problem-solving skills across a spectrum of domains. This could lead to more versatile and robust AI models that require less task-specific fine-tuning.

5. **Enhanced Efficiency and Scalability:** The introduction of batch sampling and a large program database significantly improves the efficiency and scalability of the evolutionary process. This enables researchers to explore larger solution spaces and achieve higher performance with fewer computational resources and in less time, making the approach more practical for real-world applications.

6. **Contribution to Reproducibility and Transparency:** The open-sourcing of ThetaEvolve, including its code and methodology, is a direct contribution to scientific transparency and reproducibility. This allows the broader research community to validate results, understand underlying mechanisms, and build upon this work, accelerating progress in AI and scientific discovery.

In summary, ThetaEvolve is not just an incremental improvement; it is a conceptual leap in how LLMs can be utilized for scientific discovery. By integrating active learning into the core evolutionary loop, it empowers LLMs to become more capable, adaptive, and autonomous partners in pushing the boundaries of human knowledge.
