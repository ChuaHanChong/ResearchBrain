# BEHAVIOR-1K: A Human-Centered, Embodied AI Benchmark with 1,000 Everyday Activities and Realistic Simulation

> [!summary] Summary
> BEHAVIOR-1K is a new embodied AI benchmark with 1,000 everyday activities, chosen by human survey, set in the OMNIGIBSON photorealistic simulation environment, featuring advanced physics and a commonsense knowledge base. Experiments show current AI methods achieve low success rates on these complex, long-horizon tasks, underscoring the importance of realistic physics and robust planning for real-world robotic assistance.

## Problem

- Developing robots capable of performing diverse, everyday tasks in home environments.
- Existing embodied AI benchmarks often compromise between task diversity and simulation realism, limiting their utility for practical robotic assistance.
- Current benchmarks typically feature researcher-defined tasks, which may not align with activities humans genuinely desire robots to perform.

## Method

- Introduces BEHAVIOR-1K, a benchmark comprising 1,000 everyday activities derived from an extensive human preference survey.
- Developed OMNIGIBSON, a next-generation simulation environment built on Nvidia's Omniverse, featuring advanced physics (rigid, deformable, fluid, thermal dynamics) and photorealistic rendering.
- Includes a comprehensive commonsense knowledge base that annotates objects with physical, functional, state, and hierarchical properties, enabling semantic reasoning within activities.

## Results

- Current state-of-the-art algorithms (PPO, SAC) achieve very low success rates on BEHAVIOR-1K activities, with even single tasks proving extremely challenging without specific domain knowledge.
- The use of higher-level action primitives and including a history of observations (memory) improved baseline performance, particularly for long-horizon tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that simplified physics, such as "magnet" grasping or non-realistic object dynamics, severely degrade task performance, underscoring the importance of OMNIGIBSON's detailed physical simulations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Grounding embodied AI benchmarks in human-identified needs can accelerate the development of truly useful robotic assistants.
> - Realistic physics simulation, including deformable objects and fluids, is crucial for developing policies that transfer to real-world scenarios, as simplified physics significantly degrades performance.
> - Current state-of-the-art reinforcement learning algorithms struggle significantly with complex, long-horizon everyday activities, indicating a need for more advanced planning and reasoning capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied AI researchers face a fundamental challenge: developing robots that can perform everyday tasks in our homes. While significant advancements have been made in robot perception and control, creating systems that can assist humans with daily activities remains an elusive goal. Current benchmarks for embodied AI often face a trade-off between diversity and realism, limiting their utility for developing truly helpful robotic assistants.

BEHAVIOR-1K addresses this challenge by creating a comprehensive benchmark grounded in actual human needs, featuring 1,000 everyday activities and a realistic simulation environment. Developed by a multidisciplinary team from Stanford University and other institutions, this benchmark represents a significant advancement over its predecessor, BEHAVIOR-100, by expanding the activity scope tenfold and substantially improving simulation realism.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

BEHAVIOR-1K distinguishes itself through its human-centered approach. Rather than relying on researcher-defined tasks, the benchmark's activities originate from an extensive survey of over 1,400 participants across different demographics.

Participants were asked to rate activities based on how beneficial it would be if a robot could perform them. This approach ensured that the benchmark focuses on tasks that people genuinely want robots to perform, rather than tasks that are merely convenient for research purposes.

The survey revealed interesting demographic patterns in robot task preferences:

- Age distribution of respondents spanned from 20 to 80 years, with a peak around 35-40 years
- While the survey included participants from diverse racial backgrounds, there was a higher representation of white respondents
- Income levels were distributed across different brackets, with the highest count in the $60k-$80k range

This human-centered approach aligns the benchmark with real-world needs, potentially accelerating the development of robotic systems that can address actual human priorities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

BEHAVIOR-1K contains 1,000 everyday activities derived from human preferences, organized with formal predicate logic definitions, making it the most comprehensive embodied AI benchmark to date. Each activity is formally specified with:

- Initial conditions (the starting state of the environment)
- Goal conditions (what constitutes successful task completion)
- Relevant objects and their properties
- State transitions required for completion

The benchmark activities span a wide spectrum of complexity, including:

1. Simple object manipulation (picking up or placing items)
2. Multi-stage tasks (preparing meals, cleaning rooms)
3. Activities involving rigid objects, deformable materials, and fluids
4. Tasks requiring complex physical interactions and tool use

The activities cover common household domains such as cooking, cleaning, organization, and maintenance. Unlike previous benchmarks that focus on a narrow set of interactions, BEHAVIOR-1K encompasses the full range of everyday tasks that would make robots truly useful in home environments.

#### 3.1. OMNIGIBSON Simulation Environment

To support the diverse activities in BEHAVIOR-1K, the researchers developed OMNIGIBSON, a next-generation simulation environment built on Nvidia's Omniverse platform. OMNIGIBSON stands out for its exceptional physics fidelity and rendering capabilities:

1. **Advanced Physics Simulation**: OMNIGIBSON leverages PhysX 5 to provide realistic simulation of:
 - Rigid body dynamics (objects that don't change shape)
 - Deformable materials (cloth, soft objects)
 - Fluid simulation (liquids, powders)
 - Thermal effects (cooking, freezing)
 - Transition machines (state changes like toasting bread)

2. **High-Quality 3D Assets**: The environment includes:
 - 50 detailed indoor scenes (homes, offices, etc.)
 - Over 9,000 unique object models
 - Comprehensive object annotations with physical and semantic properties

3. **Realistic Rendering**: OMNIGIBSON provides photorealistic graphics with accurate lighting, reflections, and material properties, creating visual inputs similar to what a robot would encounter in the real world.

The simulation environment also supports multi-camera setups, depth sensing, segmentation masks, and other sensor inputs that are critical for embodied AI research. This level of physical and visual realism helps address the simulation-to-reality gap that has traditionally plagued robotics research.

#### 3.2. Commonsense Knowledge Base

A unique feature of BEHAVIOR-1K is its comprehensive commonsense knowledge base that captures the properties and relationships of objects involved in everyday activities. This knowledge base was created through a systematic process:

1. Starting with survey-selected activities from WikiHow
2. Extracting and filtering relevant noun phrases
3. Creating synsets (synonym sets) for object terms
4. Annotating properties through multiple methods:
 - Human annotation (99% accurate)
 - GPT-3 annotation (98.8% accurate)
 - WordNet-generated hierarchies

The knowledge base includes detailed information about:

- Physical properties (mass, material, etc.)
- Functional properties (what the object can do)
- State properties (possible states of the object)
- Spatial relationships (where objects are typically found)
- Hierarchical relationships (object taxonomies)

This rich semantic understanding goes beyond basic physics simulation, enabling AI agents to reason about objects in ways that align with human commonsense knowledge. For example, knowing that a cup is designed to hold liquids, or that bread becomes toast when heated.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The researchers conducted extensive experiments to evaluate the challenge posed by BEHAVIOR-1K and to establish baseline performance levels for current AI approaches. The evaluation focused on both the task completion capabilities and the physical realism of the simulation.

#### 4.1. Baseline Algorithms

Two state-of-the-art reinforcement learning algorithms were evaluated:

1. **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)**: A policy gradient method with a neural network architecture
 ```
   Image Obs → Feature Extractor →
   Feature embedding → Policy Net → Action
   ```

2. **Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)**: An off-policy actor-critic method with separate policy and value networks
 ```
   Image Obs → Feature Extractor →
   Feature embedding → Actor + Critic → Action + Q-Value
   ```

#### 4.2. Task Performance

The evaluation revealed several important findings:

1. **Task Difficulty**: Even a single activity from BEHAVIOR-1K proved extremely challenging for current algorithms. Success rates were very low without significant domain knowledge injection.

2. **Key Challenges**:
 - Long-horizon planning: Activities with multiple steps were particularly difficult
 - Complex physical manipulation: Tasks involving precise control were challenging
 - Environmental diversity: Generalizing across different scenes proved hard

3. **Action Representation**: Using higher-level action primitives that leverage sampling-based motion planning significantly improved performance compared to raw visuomotor control.

4. **Memory Importance**: Including a history of observations improved performance, particularly for long-horizon activities where the state at a single time step is insufficient for decision-making.

#### 4.3. Physics Realism Evaluation

The researchers conducted ablation studies to evaluate the importance of physics realism:

1. **Grasping Simplification**: When realistic grasping physics was replaced with simplified "magnet" grasping, performance degraded significantly.

2. **Object Dynamics**: Realistic object dynamics (how objects move, collide, and interact) proved crucial for successful task completion.

3. **Deformable Objects and Fluids**: Activities involving deformable materials or fluids were nearly impossible without proper physical simulation.

The results underscore the importance of physical realism in embodied AI benchmarks. Simplified physics can lead to policies that don't transfer to real-world scenarios.

#### 4.4. Simulation-to-Reality Transfer

A critical aspect of any simulation-based benchmark is how well solutions learned in simulation transfer to real robots. The researchers conducted initial experiments to evaluate the simulation-to-reality gap in BEHAVIOR-1K:

1. **Physical Setup**: Tasks from BEHAVIOR-1K were recreated in a real-world environment with a physical robot.

2. **Depth Image Comparison**: Depth images from both simulation and reality were compared to evaluate visual fidelity.

3. **RGB Image Comparison**: Color images were also compared to assess visual realism.

The study revealed that while OMNIGIBSON provides strong visual and physical realism, a sim-to-real gap still exists. The primary failure modes when transferring policies to real robots were:

1. **Grasping difficulties**: Real-world grasping proved more challenging than simulation
2. **Perception issues**: Visual perception in the real world introduced additional noise
3. **Actuation inaccuracies**: Real robots had mechanical limitations not fully captured in simulation

These findings highlight areas for future improvement while demonstrating that BEHAVIOR-1K provides a more realistic simulation environment than previous benchmarks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

BEHAVIOR-1K reveals several important challenges for the field of embodied AI:

1. **Long-Horizon Planning**: Current algorithms struggle with activities requiring many sequential steps. New approaches to hierarchical planning and memory are needed.

2. **Physics Understanding**: Robots need better intuitive physics models to manipulate diverse materials and understand their properties.

3. **Common Sense Reasoning**: The ability to leverage commonsense knowledge about objects and their affordances remains limited in current systems.

4. **Generalization**: Transferring skills across different environments, objects, and task variations presents a significant challenge.

5. **Sim-to-Real Transfer**: While OMNIGIBSON reduces the gap, further work is needed to ensure policies learned in simulation work reliably in the real world.

The benchmark also points to promising research directions:

- Developing better hierarchical reinforcement learning approaches
- Creating more effective representations of physical dynamics
- Integrating large language models for commonsense reasoning
- Improving domain adaptation techniques for sim-to-real transfer

#### 5.1. Conclusion

BEHAVIOR-1K represents a significant advancement in embodied AI benchmarks through its human-centered approach, unprecedented scale, and realistic simulation. By providing 1,000 everyday activities derived from human preferences and a rich simulation environment with advanced physics, BEHAVIOR-1K offers a comprehensive platform for developing and evaluating embodied AI systems.

The benchmark challenges the research community to develop algorithms that can handle the full complexity of everyday activities in realistic settings. While current state-of-the-art methods struggle with these challenges, BEHAVIOR-1K provides a clear roadmap for progress toward robots that can truly assist humans in their daily lives.

By grounding research in tasks that people actually want robots to perform, BEHAVIOR-1K has the potential to accelerate the development of practical robotic assistants that can make a meaningful difference in people's lives. At the same time, the benchmark's complexity ensures that researchers will continue to be challenged for years to come, driving innovation in reinforcement learning, computer vision, robotics, and artificial intelligence.
