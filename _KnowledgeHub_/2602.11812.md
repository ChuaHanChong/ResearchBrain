# Predicting LLM Output Length via Entropy-Guided Representations

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from King Abdullah University of Science and Technology and Mohamed bin Zayed University of Artificial Intelligence developed a framework for predicting LLM output length by leveraging the model's internal hidden states, rather than auxiliary models. This approach achieved an average 29.16% reduction in Mean Absolute Error (MAE) over baselines and, when integrated into LLM serving, reduced padding ratios by up to 3x and halved Job Completion Time (JCT).

## Problem

- Large Language Model (LLM) serving in batches suffers from the "barrel effect" due to varying output lengths, leading to significant wasted computation from padding.
- Existing length prediction methods relying on separate auxiliary models introduce high computational overhead, exhibit limited accuracy, and struggle to generalize to complex reasoning tasks or long sequences.
- Stochastic "one-to-many" generation scenarios, common in reinforcement learning (RL), lack effective prediction mechanisms, as static prompt-based predictors cannot adapt to highly variable output lengths.

## Method

- The Entropy-Guided ==Token Pooling== (**EGTP**) mechanism reuses the main LLM's internal hidden states, weighting tokens by their next-token prediction entropy to create an aggregated representation for static length prediction.
- A Progressive Length Prediction (PLP) component dynamically refines remaining length estimates during token generation by incorporating the hidden states of already generated tokens.
- A robust training strategy converts continuous target lengths into soft probability distributions over bins, optimizing a joint loss function combining cross-entropy and Mean Squared Error (MSE).
- The ForeLen benchmark was constructed, featuring challenging long-sequence, reasoning, and stochastic RL sampling scenarios, to rigorously evaluate length prediction performance.

## Results

- The Entropy-Guided Token Pooling (EGTP) method reduced Mean Absolute Error (MAE) by an average of **29.16%** over the best baseline across diverse LLMs and tasks on the new ForeLen benchmark.
- When integrated into an LLM serving backend with a Shortest Job First (SJF) scheduler, the framework achieved higher throughput, more than halved Job Completion Time (JCT), and reduced padding ratios by nearly **3x** (e.g., from **0.51** to **0.18**).
- The proposed methods incurred minimal computational overhead, with an average inference latency of approximately **0.66ms** and only 5-7MB of additional GPU memory, significantly outperforming auxiliary model-based baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLM internal hidden states implicitly encode sufficient information for predicting output length, and leveraging token entropy can effectively identify the most informative states for accurate pooling.
> - Dynamic, progressive refinement of length predictions during generation (PLP) significantly improves accuracy in stochastic output scenarios where static, pre-generation predictions are insufficient.
> - Combining a soft label distribution with direct regression in the loss function provides a more stable and accurate training signal for length prediction compared to pure MSE or simple classification.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge in the efficient serving and training of large language models (LLMs). LLMs are widely adopted across various applications, including chatbots, code assistants, knowledge databases, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), and intelligent agents. Efficient LLM serving infrastructure is crucial for these applications, typically relying on autoregressive next-token prediction. Concurrently, LLM inference is being integrated into online reinforcement learning (RL) frameworks, where multiple independent samples are generated from the same prompt to construct reward signals.

A core mechanism for boosting hardware utilization and throughput in LLM serving is batching, where multiple requests are processed in parallel. However, the output sequence lengths of different requests within a batch often vary significantly. This variability leads to the "barrel effect," where shorter sequences are padded to match the longest sequence in a batch, resulting in redundant computations, increased GPU time consumption, and reduced effective computation. This computational waste is particularly acute in RL sampling scenarios, where stochastic decoding from identical prompts can yield highly variable response lengths.

Existing research on LLM output length prediction primarily employs lightweight auxiliary models, such as DistilBERT or OPT, fine-tuned to predict output length based on the input prompt. While this approach aims to enable length-aware scheduling to mitigate padding overhead, it has several limitations. These include non-negligible computational and deployment overhead due to running a separate model for each request, limited accuracy, and poor generalization to complex reasoning tasks or long sequences. Crucially, these static, prompt-based predictors are often ineffective in stochastic "one-to-many" generation scenarios common in RL, where the same prompt can lead to completions of widely differing lengths.

This paper positions itself by proposing an alternative to these auxiliary model-based approaches. It aims to develop a lightweight framework that reuses the internal hidden states of the main LLM for length prediction. This strategy seeks to reduce overhead, improve generalization, and address the challenges of stochastic generation, thereby complementing existing LLM serving optimizations like continuous batching and efficient KV cache management. The introduction of a dedicated benchmark, ForeLen, further addresses the absence of standardized evaluation for challenging length prediction scenarios, particularly in RL.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient, accurate, and adaptable framework for predicting LLM output lengths. This objective is motivated by several challenges inherent in current LLM serving and reinforcement learning (RL) sampling paradigms:

1. **Computational Waste from Padding:** In batched LLM inference, requests with varying output lengths require padding to conform to uniform tensor shapes. This leads to inefficient resource utilization, where shorter sequences waste significant computational resources (GPU cycles and memory) on padding. Such inefficiencies directly reduce system throughput and increase operational costs in both online serving and RL training. Accurate length prediction before or early in the inference process could enable length-aware scheduling, grouping requests with similar predicted lengths, and thereby reducing padding overhead.

2. **Limitations of Auxiliary Predictor Models:** Existing length prediction methods typically rely on separate, fine-tuned auxiliary models (e.g., DistilBERT). These approaches suffer from:
 - **High Overhead:** Each request necessitates running an additional, separate predictor model, incurring extra computational cost and deployment complexity, rather than leveraging the computations already performed by the main LLM.
 - **Limited Accuracy and Generalization:** These auxiliary models are often trained on benchmarks like LMSYS, which may not adequately represent the diversity of real-world LLM usage, particularly long sequences or complex Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. Consequently, their prediction accuracy tends to degrade in more challenging and realistic settings.

3. **Instability in Stochastic "One-to-Many" Generation:** A significant challenge arises in scenarios like online RL training, where a single input prompt can lead to multiple distinct outputs with widely varying lengths due to stochastic sampling. Traditional static predictors, which generate a single length estimate based solely on the prompt, are fundamentally ill-suited for these dynamic environments. A mechanism capable of adapting its length estimate as generation progresses is required.

To address these motivations, the paper proposes a framework that reuses the LLM's internal hidden states. The underlying hypothesis is that the LLM's internal states implicitly encode signals about when to emit the end-of-sequence (`<eos>`) token, and thus, information related to the eventual output length. By tapping into these internal activations, the framework aims to provide more accurate length predictions with minimal additional computational cost, overcome generalization issues, and adapt to stochastic generation processes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces a lightweight framework for LLM output length prediction, leveraging the model's internal activations. It comprises two core components: Entropy-Guided Token Pooling (EGTP) for static prediction and Progressive Length Prediction (PLP) for dynamic scenarios, along with a novel training strategy.

#### 3.1. Entropy-Guided Token Pooling (EGTP)

EGTP is designed for efficient and accurate static length prediction from input prompts.
- **Motivation:** The core idea is that an LLM's internal states contain implicit signals about the eventual output length. Reusing these states avoids the overhead of auxiliary models. However, aggregating a sequence of hidden states into a single vector for prediction requires an effective pooling mechanism. The authors hypothesize that tokens where the model is uncertain (high entropy) are more informative for length prediction.
- **Empirical Validation:** An experiment using a BERT model demonstrated a positive correlation (Pearson's r = 0.451) between a token's entropy (from its next-token probability distribution) and its importance for length prediction (measured by L2 norm of the gradient of MSE loss with respect to its hidden state). This finding supports the entropy-guided pooling strategy.
- **Mechanism:**
 1. For each input token's hidden state $h_i$, the entropy $H_i$ is computed from the next-token probability distribution $P(v|x_{<i})$ over the vocabulary.
 2. These entropy values are transformed into attention weights $w_i$ using a softmax function, scaled by a temperature parameter $\alpha$.
 3. The final aggregated representation $h$ is calculated as a weighted sum of the hidden states, where the weights are the entropy-derived $w_i$. This aims to focus on the most informative parts of the input.

#### 3.2. Length Regression via Soft Label Distribution

This section describes the prediction head and training strategy for converting the aggregated feature vector into a length prediction.
- **Motivation:** Standard Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss can be sensitive to outliers and heavy-tailed distributions typical of sequence lengths, while classification via binning loses distance information. The goal is a robust and distance-aware prediction.
- **Mechanism:**
 1. The continuous target length $y$ is converted into a soft probability distribution $p$ over $K$ predefined bins. For a true length in bin $i$, the probability $p_j$ for any bin $j$ is inversely proportional to its distance from bin $i$, using an exponential decay function.
 2. The model uses the feature vector $h$ from EGTP to produce two outputs: a $K$-dimensional probability distribution $\hat{p}$ (via a softmax layer) and the final regression prediction $\hat{y}$. $\hat{y}$ is computed as the expected value of $\hat{p}$ across the bin centers.
 3. The model is trained using a joint loss function: $L = \lambda L_{CE}(p, \hat{p}) + (1-\lambda) L_{MSE}(y, \hat{y})$. $L_{CE}$ encourages $\hat{p}$ to align with the soft label distribution $p$, providing stable gradients, while $L_{MSE}$ directly minimizes the error between $\hat{y}$ and $y$. The hyperparameter $\lambda$ balances these two losses.

#### 3.3. Progressive Length Prediction (PLP)

PLP is designed to handle dynamic, stochastic generation scenarios, such as RL sampling.
- **Motivation:** Static, pre-generation predictions are insufficient when a single prompt can yield multiple completions of varying lengths. PLP aims to dynamically refine length estimates during the decoding process.
- **Mechanism:**
 1. At each decoding timestep $t$, PLP predicts the *remaining* number of tokens to be generated, $y^{(t)}_{rem}$.
 2. It forms a dynamic input representation $z_t$ by concatenating the initial prompt feature vector $h$ with the hidden states of all already generated tokens $\{h'_1, \ldots, h'_t\}$.
 3. This $z_t$ is then passed through the same prediction head described in Section 4.2 to yield $\hat{y}^{(t)}_{rem}$.
 4. The model is trained by minimizing the average loss across all timesteps: $L_{PLP} = \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^T L(y^{(t)}_{rem}, \hat{y}^{(t)}_{rem})$, where $T$ is the total sequence length.

#### 3.4. Dataset Construction (ForeLen Benchmark)

To rigorously validate the framework, especially in challenging scenarios, the authors constructed ForeLen, a comprehensive benchmark.
- **Scenario 1: Long-Sequence and Complex Reasoning Generation:**
 - **Data Sources:** Prompts from LongBench, ZeroSCROLLS, and IFEval.
 - **Model Responses:** Generated by Qwen2.5 (0.5B-7B) and Llama3.2 (1B, 3B) for long-sequence tasks, and Qwen2.5 and DeepSeek-R1-Distill for reasoning tasks.
- **Scenario 2: Dynamic RL Sampling:**
 - **Data Sources:** Collected from actual GRPO training pipelines of Qwen2.5 and Llama3.2. Prompts from CRUXEval, GSM8K, LiveCodeBench, MATH, MBPP, and MMLU-STEM.
 - **Generation:** Grouped sampling (K=4) was used for each prompt to create multiple candidate responses with varying lengths, simulating stochastic environments.
- **Data Splits and Statistics:** Datasets are split into train/val/test following a 3:1:1 ratio, ensuring unseen prompts for evaluation. The benchmark features a diverse range of prompt and response lengths, with the RL scenario constituting the majority (93.0%) of unique prompts.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Baselines:** SSJF-Reg, SSJF-MC, S3, PiA, TPV, TRAIL, LTR-C.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for prediction accuracy; Throughput, Job Completion Time (JCT), and Padding Ratio for end-to-end system performance.
- **Training:** AdamW optimizer, learning rate 2e-5, 10 epochs max, batch size 16. $\lambda=0.95$ for soft label regression, K=20 bins. Experiments used 1 V100 GPU.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents empirical results demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed framework across various evaluation metrics and scenarios.

#### 4.1. Prediction Accuracy (EGTP)

EGTP consistently achieved lower Mean Absolute Error (MAE) compared to baseline methods.
- **On LMSYS Benchmark:** EGTP reported MAEs of 87.32 for GPT-4 and 68.33 for Claude-2. These figures represent MAE reductions of 9.1% and 11.3% respectively, over the next best baseline methods on this dataset.
- **On ForeLen Benchmark:** EGTP demonstrated substantial improvements across Long-Sequence, Reasoning, and RL scenarios for various LLMs (Qwen2.5 3B/7B, Llama3.2 1B/3B). For Qwen2.5 3B, EGTP achieved an average MAE of 110.75, a 24.4% reduction compared to the strongest baseline, TRAIL (146.42). Similar reductions were observed for other models: 25.0% for Qwen2.5 7B (103.47 vs 137.96), 30.8% for Llama3.2 1B (105.08 vs 151.80), and 35.7% for Llama3.2 3B (101.53 vs 157.88).
- **Overall:** EGTP reduced the MAE by an average of 29.16% over the best baseline and 55.09% over SSJF-Reg across all tested models and scenarios. This indicated EGTP's ability to generalize across diverse models and complex tasks.

#### 4.2. Effect of Progressive Length Prediction (PLP)

The integration of PLP consistently improved prediction accuracy, particularly in dynamic generation environments.
- **MAE Improvements:** Figure 3 illustrated MAE reductions across Long Sequence, Reasoning, and RL tasks. The most notable improvement was observed in the RL task, where MAE dropped from 95.24 to 80.85. Reasoning and Long Sequence tasks also showed reductions.
- **Convergence Pattern:** All tasks exhibited rapid initial gains in MAE reduction during the first few decoding steps, followed by a gradual stabilization, suggesting that PLP effectively leveraged already-generated tokens for iterative refinement of length predictions.

#### 4.3. End-to-End System Performance

When integrated with a Shortest Job First (SJF) scheduler within a vLLM serving backend, EGTP demonstrated improvements in end-to-end system metrics.
- **Long Sequence Workload:** EGTP achieved the highest throughput and more than halved the Job Completion Time (JCT) compared to the best baseline, TRAIL. This was associated with a padding ratio of 0.18, representing a nearly 3x reduction compared to TRAIL's 0.51.
- **Reasoning Workload:** EGTP again led in throughput and JCT, reducing the padding ratio to 0.09. This represented a 36% relative reduction in wasted computation compared to the strongest baseline, LTR-C.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Effectiveness of Entropy-Guided Pooling:** An ablation study on pooling methods (Table 3) showed that EGTP achieved an average MAE of 103.47, outperforming Max Pooling (119.69), Average Pooling (155.39), and Last Token Pooling (126.64). This indicated the effectiveness of entropy-guided weighting in capturing informative features.
- **Loss Weighting Parameter (λ):** An analysis of the $\lambda$ hyperparameter (Figure 7, Appendix D.1) showed that a balanced joint loss function was optimal. Pure MSE ($\lambda=0$) performed worst (MAE 82-84), while pure CE ($\lambda=1$) yielded an MAE around 70. The optimal performance (MAE approx 68) was achieved at $\lambda=0.99$, indicating the benefit of combining stable CE supervision with fine-grained MSE optimization.
- **Loss Function Variants:** EGTP consistently outperformed alternative loss function designs like log-scale regression, label smoothing, and label noise across LongSeq, Reasoning, and RL tasks, particularly in LongSeq and RL scenarios (Table 6, Appendix D.2).
- **Layer Selection Strategy:** Using the hidden states from the final transformer layer (Layer 24) showed comparable performance (MAE 73.93) to the best intermediate layers (e.g., Layer 12, MAE 70.26) on the GSM8K dataset for Qwen2.5-0.5B. This supports using the final layer for simplicity and to avoid over-engineering (Table 7, Appendix D.3).

#### 4.5. Generalization to Super-Long Sequences (Out-of-Distribution)

Experiments on the `euclaise/writingprompts` dataset, containing sequences over 17k tokens, demonstrated EGTP's strong generalization capabilities. EGTP achieved the lowest MAE (195.89) and RMSE (257.49), outperforming baselines which struggled to scale effectively (Table 8, Appendix E.2).

#### 4.6. Computational Efficiency Analysis

EGTP incurred minimal overhead compared to baseline methods requiring auxiliary models (Tables 9 & 10, Appendix E.3).
- **Inference Latency:** EGTP's average inference time was approximately 0.66ms, significantly lower than auxiliary model-based methods (e.g., SSJF: 2-4ms, LTR-C: 12ms, PiA: 60ms).
- **VRAM Consumption:** EGTP required only 5-7 MB of additional GPU memory for its lightweight linear head, whereas auxiliary models consumed 238-270 MB for their weights.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a novel framework for LLM output length prediction that offers several contributions to the field of efficient LLM inference and serving.

**Technical Contribution:** The introduction of Entropy-Guided Token Pooling (EGTP) and Progressive Length Prediction (PLP) represents a shift from relying on external auxiliary models to directly leveraging the internal hidden states of the main LLM. EGTP's use of token entropy to guide pooling is a novel mechanism that exploits intrinsic signals within the model, leading to improved accuracy for static predictions while incurring negligible computational overhead. PLP addresses the long-standing challenge of stochastic "one-to-many" generation, particularly relevant in reinforcement learning, by dynamically updating length predictions during decoding. This progressive refinement capability allows for more adaptable resource management in unpredictable generation scenarios.

**Enhanced Evaluation Baseline:** The development and release of ForeLen, a comprehensive benchmark for length prediction, addresses a gap in existing evaluation practices. ForeLen includes challenging long-sequence, Chain-of-Thought (CoT), and RL sampling data, providing a more rigorous and realistic environment for assessing predictor performance than previous benchmarks. This benchmark can serve as a new standard for future research in this domain.

**Improved LLM Serving Efficiency:** The main findings indicate that the proposed methods lead to notable improvements in end-to-end LLM serving performance. By integrating EGTP with a length-aware scheduler, the system achieved higher throughput, reduced Job Completion Time (JCT), and significantly decreased padding ratios. These practical gains directly translate to lower operational costs and better resource utilization for LLM deployments, benefiting a wide range of applications from chatbots to complex intelligent agents. The minimal computational overhead of EGTP and PLP further enhances their practical applicability, as they do not significantly compete for resources with the main LLM.

**Broad Applicability:** The framework's ability to handle both static and dynamic/stochastic generation scenarios expands its relevance across diverse LLM use cases. Its specific attention to reinforcement learning training, where output length variability is high, highlights its potential to optimize resource management in crucial model alignment and development pipelines.

**Future Research Direction:** This work establishes a new technical and evaluation baseline for efficient LLM inference, suggesting avenues for further research into exploiting internal model representations for various inference optimization tasks. The validation that sufficient signals for length determination are already encoded within the LLM's hidden states opens possibilities for developing other lightweight, in-situ prediction mechanisms.
