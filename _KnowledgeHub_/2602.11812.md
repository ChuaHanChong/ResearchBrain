# Predicting LLM Output Length via Entropy-Guided Representations

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from King Abdullah University of Science and Technology and Mohamed bin Zayed University of Artificial Intelligence developed a framework for predicting LLM output length by leveraging the model's internal hidden states, rather than auxiliary models. This approach achieved an average 29.16% reduction in Mean Absolute Error (MAE) over baselines and, when integrated into LLM serving, reduced padding ratios by up to 3x and halved Job Completion Time (JCT).

## Problem

- Large Language Model (LLM) serving in batches suffers from the "barrel effect" due to varying output lengths, leading to significant wasted computation from padding.
- Existing length prediction methods relying on separate auxiliary models introduce high computational overhead, exhibit limited accuracy, and struggle to generalize to complex reasoning tasks or long sequences.
- Stochastic "one-to-many" generation scenarios, common in reinforcement learning (RL), lack effective prediction mechanisms, as static prompt-based predictors cannot adapt to highly variable output lengths.

## Method

- The Entropy-Guided ==Token Pooling== (**EGTP**) mechanism reuses the main LLM's internal hidden states, weighting tokens by their next-token prediction entropy to create an aggregated representation for static length prediction.
- A Progressive Length Prediction (PLP) component dynamically refines remaining length estimates during token generation by incorporating the hidden states of already generated tokens.
- A robust training strategy converts continuous target lengths into soft probability distributions over bins, optimizing a joint loss function combining cross-entropy and Mean Squared Error (MSE).
- The ForeLen benchmark was constructed, featuring challenging long-sequence, reasoning, and stochastic RL sampling scenarios, to rigorously evaluate length prediction performance.

## Results

- The Entropy-Guided Token Pooling (EGTP) method reduced Mean Absolute Error (MAE) by an average of **29.16%** over the best baseline across diverse LLMs and tasks on the new ForeLen benchmark.
- When integrated into an LLM serving backend with a Shortest Job First (SJF) scheduler, the framework achieved higher throughput, more than halved Job Completion Time (JCT), and reduced padding ratios by nearly **3x** (e.g., from **0.51** to **0.18**).
- The proposed methods incurred minimal computational overhead, with an average inference latency of approximately **0.66ms** and only 5-7MB of additional GPU memory, significantly outperforming auxiliary model-based baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLM internal hidden states implicitly encode sufficient information for predicting output length, and leveraging token entropy can effectively identify the most informative states for accurate pooling.
> - Dynamic, progressive refinement of length predictions during generation (PLP) significantly improves accuracy in stochastic output scenarios where static, pre-generation predictions are insufficient.
> - Combining a soft label distribution with direct regression in the loss function provides a more stable and accurate training signal for length prediction compared to pure MSE or simple classification.
