# SPIDER: Scalable Physics-Informed Dexterous Retargeting

> [!summary] Summary
> SPIDER introduces a scalable physics-informed framework that transforms kinematic human demonstrations into dynamically feasible robot trajectories, achieving an order-of-magnitude faster generation speed than previous methods while ensuring robust execution across diverse robot embodiments and tasks. The framework successfully generated 2.4 million frames of robot data for dexterous manipulation and humanoid control, demonstrating deployment on physical hardware.

## Problem

- Robot control for dexterous manipulation and whole-body tasks faces data scarcity due to the high cost and complexity of collecting robot-specific demonstration data.
- Transferring abundant human motion data to robots is challenging due to the 'embodiment gap' (morphology, dynamics, actuation differences) and the lack of crucial dynamic information in human data.
- Existing retargeting methods often lack physical realism, scalability, robustness to out-of-distribution motions, or computational efficiency for large datasets.

## Method

- Formulates retargeting as a constrained optimization problem, minimizing the distance to human motion references and control effort, subject to physics simulator constraints.
- Employs a scalable sampling-based optimizer with an annealed sampling kernel and parallelized simulation for efficient exploration and refinement of control sequences.
- Introduces 'virtual contact guidance' with curriculum-style relaxation to steer the optimizer toward human-intended contact modes in complex, contact-rich tasks.

## Results

- Generated 2.4 million frames (800 hours) of dynamically feasible robot data for 5 dexterous hands and 4 humanoids across 6 human motion datasets.
- Achieved task success rates up to **100%** in challenging manipulation, with virtual contact guidance improving performance by approximately **18%** over sampling alone.
- Demonstrated an order-of-magnitude faster trajectory generation (2.5-**1.5** FPS) compared to state-of-the-art RL-based methods (0.1-**0.05** FPS) while maintaining competitive task success and validating on physical hardware.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physics-informed sampling, combined with an annealed kernel, effectively navigates the non-convex landscape of contact-rich robot control, achieving high success rates.
> - Curriculum-style virtual contact guidance resolves ambiguity in contact-rich tasks, enabling convergence to desired contact modes even with noisy human references.
> - Optimizing for worst-case cost across perturbed dynamics parameters (pessimistic objective) enhances trajectory robustness, allowing direct deployment on physical hardware.
