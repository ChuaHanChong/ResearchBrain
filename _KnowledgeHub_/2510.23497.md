# VOLD: Reasoning Transfer from LLMs to Vision-Language Models via On-Policy Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tuebingen AI Center, University of Tuebingen, MIT-IBM Watson AI Lab, Inria, École Normale Supérieure, CNRS, and PSL Research University developed VOLD, a framework that enables Vision-Language Models to acquire complex reasoning skills by training solely on text-only data using on-policy distillation from a text-only teacher LLM and reinforcement learning. The approach achieved 28.0% on MathVision and 45.0% on LogicVista, outperforming prior methods, including those fine-tuned with visual reasoning data.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) demonstrate strong reasoning capabilities due to abundant text-based data, a proficiency largely absent in Vision-Language Models (VLMs).
- High-quality, multi-step image-text reasoning datasets are scarce, making it challenging and expensive to train VLMs for complex logical reasoning tasks.
- Existing text-to-vision reasoning transfer methods often underutilize powerful text-only teacher models for dynamic guidance during reinforcement learning (RL).

## Method

- A two-stage post-training pipeline, **VOLD**, integrates supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for initial policy alignment followed by unified reinforcement learning (RL) and on-policy knowledge distillation (KD).
- The 'cold-start' SFT phase uses teacher-generated text-only reasoning traces to align the student VLM's policy with the text-only teacher LLM, establishing a foundational understanding.
- The second stage combines Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with on-policy reverse KL distillation, applying dense, token-level teacher guidance on the student's own generated text-only trajectories; reward-guided masking selectively applies distillation only for incorrect rollouts.

## Results

- VOLD attained **28.0%** on MathVision and **45.0%** on LogicVista, showing improvements over baseline VLMs and the X-Reasoner text-only training method.
- The framework demonstrated higher training rewards (**0.58** vs. **0.51** for vanilla GRPO) on text-only data and better validation accuracy (**0.38** vs. **0.32** on Geo3K) on visual tasks, confirming effective cross-modal knowledge transfer.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both initial SFT for policy alignment and the combination of RL with on-policy distillation are essential for optimal reasoning transfer, with the benefits of distillation scaling with SFT duration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Initial 'cold-start' policy alignment via Supervised Fine-Tuning is a critical prerequisite for effective and stable on-policy distillation between a text-only teacher and a VLM student.
> - On-policy knowledge distillation provides dynamic, token-level guidance, significantly improving sample efficiency and overall reasoning performance compared to static teacher supervision.
> - Reward-guided KL masking, which selectively applies distillation only to incorrect student trajectories, prevents interference with the VLM's ability to solidify novel and correct reasoning strategies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The advancement of large language models (LLMs) has demonstrated sophisticated reasoning capabilities, largely due to the availability of extensive text-based reasoning data and scalable training methods like reinforcement learning (RL). Extending these reasoning abilities to vision-language models (VLMs) for complex visual tasks remains a significant challenge, primarily due to the scarcity of high-quality, diverse image-text reasoning datasets. Most existing image-text datasets are limited to basic perception tasks, lacking the complexity required for multi-step reasoning. In contrast, text-based reasoning data, particularly for domains such as mathematics and programming, is abundant and can be generated and verified automatically, facilitating large-scale RL training.

Current approaches to address VLM reasoning in data-limited regimes include:
1. **Synthetic Visual Reasoning Data Generation:** Methods such as Vision-R1, OpenVLThinker, and R1-VL augment text-based reasoning with visual descriptions. A limitation of this approach is the modality gap, where textual descriptions may not fully capture the richness of direct visual perception.
2. **Training on Challenging Benchmarks:** Approaches like VLAA-Thinker and VLM-R1 involve training on carefully selected, challenging image-text pairs from existing benchmarks. This strategy faces challenges related to data scarcity, scalability, and potential test data contamination.
3. **Text-Only Reasoning Transfer:** Some methods, such as X-Reasoner, explore training VLMs on text-only data to transfer reasoning capabilities to visual tasks. However, these methods typically do not fully leverage teacher models for continuous guidance during training.

Concurrently, research in text-to-text reasoning transfer has demonstrated the effectiveness of combining RL with knowledge distillation. KDRL and Qwen3 illustrate that on-policy knowledge distillation can enhance RL sample efficiency by providing additional teacher-based guidance. The Thinking Machines Lab blog further showed that on-policy distillation can achieve performance comparable to RL at reduced computational cost for text-only reasoning.

The VOLD framework contributes to this landscape by building upon the insights from text-to-text on-policy distillation and extending them to the cross-modal domain. It specifically addresses the limitation of previous text-based transfer methods by integrating a text-only teacher LLM to guide a VLM student *during* the training process. VOLD represents a novel approach to achieving text-to-vision reasoning transfer through a unified objective that combines RL and on-policy distillation, utilizing purely text-based training data. This strategy aims to overcome the bottlenecks posed by limited visual reasoning data, offering a scalable pathway for developing sophisticated reasoning VLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enable Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to perform complex, multi-step reasoning tasks by effectively transferring reasoning capabilities from advanced text-only Large Language Models (LLMs). This goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing Data Scarcity in VLM Reasoning:** A primary motivation is the significant challenge of acquiring high-quality, complex image-text reasoning data. Unlike text-based reasoning, which benefits from abundant and scalable datasets, visual reasoning data curation is labor-intensive and difficult to automate. The authors aim to circumvent this bottleneck by leveraging the vast quantities of existing text-based reasoning resources.

2. **Leveraging Advanced LLM Reasoning:** State-of-the-art LLMs have demonstrated remarkable reasoning abilities, partly due to training on extensive text-based reasoning traces. The research is motivated by the potential to transfer these well-developed capabilities from text-only teachers to VLMs, thereby bootstrapping their reasoning prowess without direct visual reasoning data.

3. **Improving Training Efficiency and Effectiveness for VLMs:** While reinforcement learning (RL) has proven effective for training LLMs, applying it to VLMs for reasoning is hindered by data limitations. The paper proposes to enhance VLM training by integrating on-policy knowledge distillation with RL. This integration is motivated by prior work demonstrating that dense teacher guidance can significantly improve RL sample efficiency and learning stability in text-to-text contexts. The goal is to extend this benefit to the cross-modal setting.

4. **Establishing Policy Alignment for Cross-Modal Distillation:** The authors identify a critical gap in existing cross-modal transfer methods: the potential for divergence between the student VLM's and teacher LLM's policy distributions. A core motivation is to demonstrate that an initial "cold-start" alignment phase, using Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), is essential for ensuring that the VLM student can effectively interpret and benefit from the text-only teacher's on-policy guidance. Without sufficient distributional overlap, distillation signals can become diffuse and ineffective.

5. **Developing a Scalable and Generalizable Framework:** The research aims to develop a framework (VOLD) that is not only effective but also scalable and complementary to other RL advancements. By training exclusively on text-only reasoning data, VOLD seeks to provide a generalizable approach that can be integrated with various RL algorithms and potentially serve as a robust foundation for future VLM development.

In summary, the core objectives are to propose and validate VOLD as a unified framework for text-to-vision reasoning transfer, demonstrate the necessity of initial policy alignment, and show that combined RL and on-policy distillation significantly outperforms standalone RL in equipping VLMs with complex reasoning capabilities using only text-based training resources.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VOLD employs a two-stage post-training pipeline designed to transfer reasoning capabilities from text-only teacher LLMs to VLM students, exclusively using text-based training data. The pipeline consists of a Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) stage for policy alignment, followed by a unified objective that combines Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with on-policy knowledge distillation.

**Technical Preliminaries and Notation:**
- **Notation:** An input prompt `q` generates a trajectory `τ` of tokens. `y_t` is the token at step `t`, and `h_t` is the history `(q, y_<t)`.
- **Policies:** `π_θ` denotes the student policy (parameterized by `θ`), and `π_ϕ` denotes the fixed teacher policy (parameterized by `ϕ`).
- **Reward:** `r(τ)` is a binary scalar reward ({0, 1}) for a completed trajectory.
- **GRPO:** A value-function-free RL algorithm. It samples a group of `K` trajectories from an old policy, computes group-relative advantages `A_i` based on relative rewards, and minimizes a clipped importance ratio loss (Equation 2). An asymmetric clipping strategy is used. KL regularization (`D_KL(π_θ || π_ref)`) is typically included but noted to be sometimes omittable.
- **On-Policy Knowledge Distillation:** Aims to minimize the reverse KL divergence (`D_KL(π_ϕ(·|h_t) || π_θ(·|h_t))`) between teacher and student distributions at each step `t` of the student's own generated trajectories `τ ~ π_θ`. This means the teacher provides distributional targets that adapt to the student's evolving policy. The "k2" estimator is used for practical KL divergence computation. A shared tokenizer and vocabulary between teacher and student are necessary for meaningful KL computations.

**VOLD Framework - Two-Stage Pipeline:**

**Stage 1: SFT for Policy Alignment (Cold-Start)**
- **Goal:** To establish an initial distributional alignment between the student VLM and the text-only teacher LLM. This is considered critical for effective subsequent on-policy distillation.
- **Data Generation:** Reasoning prompts from the "Mixture-of-Thoughts" (MoT) dataset are fed to the Qwen3-8B teacher model to generate reasoning traces (`τ^*`). These `(q_j, τ^*_j)` pairs form the "MoT-Teacher-8B" dataset. Only trajectories under 8192 tokens are retained, without answer verification.
- **Training Objective:** The student VLM is fine-tuned to minimize the negative log-likelihood of the teacher-generated trajectories (Equation 4).
- **Model Configuration:** The VLM's vision encoder remains frozen during this stage to preserve visual capabilities, with alignment efforts focused on the language modeling components.
- **Rationale for Alignment:** Without initial alignment, the student's sampled trajectories (`h_t ~ π_θ`) may fall outside the teacher's policy support (`π_ϕ`), leading to diffuse or uninformative teacher distributions and high-variance gradients for the KL divergence term. SFT ensures that `h_t` corresponds to regions where `π_ϕ` has low entropy, making distillation effective and stable.

**Stage 2: Unified RL and On-Policy Distillation**
- **Goal:** To enhance the student's reasoning capabilities by combining the sparse, trajectory-level rewards from RL with dense, token-level guidance from the teacher.
- **Data:** The text-only "orz-57k" dataset, comprising mathematical problems with ground truth answers, is used for RL training. Binary rewards are computed via exact match verification.
- **Core Mechanism:** The same on-policy rollouts sampled from the student (`τ ~ π_θ`) are reused for both GRPO and on-policy knowledge distillation, minimizing computational overhead.
- **Unified Objective (L_VOLD):** The combined loss function (Equation 5) integrates the GRPO loss (`L_GRPO`) with the reverse KL distillation loss, weighted by a hyperparameter `β`. The paper replaces the standard GRPO KL-divergence penalty against the old policy (`π_θ_old`) with the teacher-student reverse KL term.
- **Reward-Guided KL Masking:** To prevent potential conflicts where the student discovers correct reasoning paths that diverge from the teacher's approach, a masking mechanism is introduced. The distillation term is multiplied by `(1 - r(τ))`, meaning it is active only for incorrect rollouts (reward `r=0`) and masked out for correct rollouts (reward `r=1`). This allows the student to retain successful novel strategies (Equation 6).

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Models:** Student is Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct, teacher is Qwen3-8B. Vision tower frozen.
- **Hyperparameters:** SFT: batch size 256, LR 5e-5, 4000 steps. RL: batch size 256, LR 6e-6, 60 steps, 5 rollouts/prompt, KL coeff `β = 0.001`, `ϵ_upper = 0.3`, `ϵ_lower = 0.2`.
- **Evaluation:** Zero-shot inference on diverse benchmarks: MMMU-Pro, MMStar (general multimodal); MathVision, MathVista, MathVerse, WeMath, DynaMath (multimodal math); LogicVista (multimodal logic). Accuracy is reported using vLLM and VLMEvalKit with GPT-4o-mini for answer extraction. Geo3K is used for validation during RL training to monitor text-to-vision transfer.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings supporting the effectiveness and underlying principles of the VOLD framework:

1. **State-of-the-Art Performance in Multimodal Reasoning (Table 1):**
 - VOLD achieves competitive performance across a range of multimodal reasoning benchmarks, notably outperforming baselines that require training with image-text data (VLAA-Thinker, VLM-R1-Math) on several tasks, despite VOLD being trained exclusively on text-only data.
 - Specifically, VOLD achieved 28.0% on MathVision, exceeding VLAA-Thinker (24.4%) and the base model (21.9%). On LogicVista, VOLD reached 45.0%, surpassing VLM-R1 (40.5%) and the base model (40.3%).
 - Comparison with X-Reasoner, which also uses text-only training but without on-policy distillation, demonstrates VOLD's significant gains (e.g., MMMU-Pro: 32.0% vs 31.0%; MathVision: 28.0% vs 24.4%), highlighting the critical role of teacher guidance.

2. **Critical Role of Policy Alignment (Table 2):**
 - An ablation study revealed that the initial SFT stage, specifically aligning the student VLM with the *same* text-only teacher LLM used for distillation, is essential.
 - When the student was SFT-aligned with a different teacher's traces (DeepSeek-R1 via the original MoT dataset), on-policy distillation provided no discernible benefit, yielding performance similar to SFT+RL alone. This supports the hypothesis that a sufficient overlap in output distributions between student and teacher is a prerequisite for effective on-policy knowledge transfer.

3. **Component Contribution to Performance (Table 3):**
 - A component analysis demonstrated that the full VOLD pipeline (SFT + RL + On-Policy Distillation) consistently yielded the best performance across all benchmarks.
 - SFT-only training on teacher-generated traces sometimes showed a temporary performance degradation compared to the base model, attributed to the inclusion of unfiltered, potentially incorrect reasoning paths in the teacher traces. However, this stage was still crucial for establishing alignment.
 - RL-only (SFT followed by GRPO without distillation) showed moderate improvements but significantly underperformed the full VOLD, confirming the synergistic effect of combining sparse rewards with dense teacher guidance.

4. **Positive Learning Dynamics (Figure 3):**
 - Visualizations of learning dynamics during RL training showed that VOLD consistently converged to higher performance levels than vanilla GRPO, both in text-only training reward (on orz-57k: 0.58 vs 0.51) and validation accuracy on a visual reasoning dataset (Geo3K: 0.38 vs 0.32).
 - The widening performance gap indicated that on-policy distillation provided increasingly valuable guidance throughout training, leading to more stable and effective reasoning transfer, including cross-modal generalization.

5. **Impact of Cold Start Duration (Supplementary Material, Figure 4):**
 - Further ablations on SFT duration confirmed that insufficient SFT (e.g., 500 steps) led to poor alignment and negated the benefits of distillation. As SFT steps increased, the student's ability to leverage on-policy distillation improved, resulting in greater performance gains over the GRPO-only baseline. Performance plateaus were observed after approximately 3000 SFT steps, suggesting sufficient policy internalization.

6. **Teacher Model Size (Supplementary Material, Table 5):**
 - Evaluating different teacher model sizes (4B, 8B, 14B) showed that larger teachers generally provided better guidance, with the 8B teacher outperforming the 4B teacher. However, performance gains saturated beyond the 8B scale, suggesting that the 3B student VLM has inherent capacity limitations in fully leveraging guidance from excessively large teachers.

7. **Effectiveness of Reward-Guided KL Masking (Supplementary Material, Figure 5):**
 - The reward-guided KL masking mechanism, which applies distillation only to incorrect rollouts, further improved training reward compared to VOLD without masking. This suggests that allowing the student to retain successful reasoning paths while receiving guidance on failures optimizes the balance between exploration and exploitation.

8. **General Multimodal Capabilities and Trade-offs (Supplementary Material, Table 6):**
 - VOLD maintained or improved general multimodal capabilities, with an overall increase in MME score and MMMU-Pro. A perception-reasoning trade-off was observed, where perception scores slightly decreased (e.g., MMStar Perception: 58.4% to 54.4%) while reasoning scores improved (e.g., MMStar Reasoning: 53.5% to 55.9%). This trade-off was noted to be consistent with other reasoning-focused methods, regardless of image-based training. Hallucination scores on HallusionBench also improved.

9. **VOLD as a Foundation for Image-Based RL (Supplementary Material, Table 7):**
 - Applying RL on image-text data starting from a VOLD checkpoint (VOLD + RL on images) yielded superior performance compared to both text-only VOLD and image-trained baselines (e.g., MathVista: 63.4% vs VOLD 61.9% vs VLAA-Thinker 61.0%), suggesting VOLD provides a strong pre-training foundation.

10. **Generalization to Other RL Algorithms (Supplementary Material, Figure 6):**
 - The on-policy distillation component of VOLD was shown to be compatible with and improve other RL algorithms, such as Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO), confirming its orthogonality and generalizability beyond GRPO.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VOLD framework represents a notable contribution to the field of multimodal AI, particularly in addressing the challenge of reasoning in Vision-Language Models. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

1. **Addressing Data Scarcity and Scalability:** VOLD offers a scalable solution for training VLMs for complex reasoning tasks by effectively leveraging abundant text-based reasoning data. This strategy mitigates the reliance on scarce, costly, and labor-intensive high-quality image-text reasoning datasets. This approach can democratize access to advanced VLM training, making it more feasible for researchers and practitioners without extensive visual data annotation resources.

2. **Pioneering Cross-Modal Reasoning Transfer:** The framework establishes a novel paradigm for transferring sophisticated reasoning capabilities from text-only LLMs to VLMs. By demonstrating successful text-to-vision transfer through a unified RL and on-policy distillation objective, VOLD opens new avenues for knowledge leverage across modalities, bridging a significant gap in current multimodal AI research.

3. **Highlighting Policy Alignment as a Prerequisite:** The explicit identification and empirical validation of initial policy alignment (via SFT) as a critical prerequisite for effective teacher-student reasoning transfer is a fundamental insight. This finding provides clear guidance for future work on distillation in cross-modal or even single-modal settings, ensuring that teacher guidance is meaningful and stable.

4. **Enhanced Training Efficiency and Effectiveness:** VOLD demonstrates that integrating dense, token-level teacher guidance through on-policy distillation significantly improves the sample efficiency and overall effectiveness of RL for VLM reasoning, outperforming standalone RL methods. This can lead to faster development cycles and more capable models.

5. **Modular and Generalizable Framework:** The framework's orthogonality to specific RL algorithms means that VOLD can be seamlessly integrated with future advancements in RL methods. This adaptability ensures its continued relevance and potential for further performance enhancements as the field evolves. Its ability to serve as a robust pre-training foundation for subsequent image-based fine-tuning also suggests its utility in multi-stage training pipelines.

6. **Broader Impact on AI Capabilities:** By enabling VLMs to acquire robust reasoning abilities without direct visual reasoning data, VOLD contributes to the development of more intelligent and versatile multimodal AI systems. Such systems can better understand and interact with the world, performing complex tasks that require both visual perception and intricate logical thinking. This could find applications in diverse areas such as scientific discovery, educational tools, and advanced robotics.

7. **Cost Reduction in Model Development:** The reduction in the need for specialized visual reasoning datasets can significantly lower the computational and human resource costs associated with developing and deploying advanced VLMs, fostering innovation and wider adoption of these technologies.

In conclusion, VOLD represents a substantial step towards building more capable and scalable reasoning VLMs by strategically combining established techniques in a novel cross-modal context, with a clear focus on practical and impactful contributions to the field.
