# Skill Self-Play: Pushing the Frontier of LLM Capability with Co-Evolving Skills

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Alibaba and affiliated universities introduce Skill Self-Play (Skill-SP), a reinforcement learning framework that enables Large Language Models to autonomously improve by co-evolving a task proposer, a solver, and a dynamic library of modular skills. The framework consistently enhanced the performance of five diverse LLM backbones on complex tool-calling and logical reasoning benchmarks, demonstrating its ability to generate high-quality, verifiable curricula and significantly improve initially misaligned models.

## Problem

- Existing LLM self-evolution methods face a dilemma between achieving broad task diversity and maintaining reliable task verification.
- Environment-bound methods provide verifiable feedback but restrict learning to narrow domains, limiting generalization.
- Open-ended task generation offers diversity but often lacks reliable verification, leading to ill-posed tasks, overfitting, or synthetic data collapse.

## Method

- **Skill Self-Play (Skill-SP)** is proposed, a reinforcement learning framework orchestrating the ==co-evolution== of a proposer, a solver, and a dynamic, evolving ==skill library==.
- The proposer synthesizes challenging, valid tasks guided by dynamically sampled skills and ==open-ended exploration==, ensuring both structural priors and novelty.
- A ==skill controller== continuously refines existing skills, archives obsolete ones, and induces new skills from successful exploration, creating an ==adaptive curriculum==.

## Results

- Skill-SP universally improved all **five** evaluated LLM backbones across diverse tool-calling and logical reasoning tasks, consistently outperforming an unguided self-play baseline.
- The framework notably "rescued" initially misaligned models (e.g., Ministral-3-8B gained **+42.9** absolute points in tool-calling), turning them into capable agents.
- Ablation studies confirmed that dynamic skill orchestration, mixing open-ended exploration, and the co-evolutionary updates of both proposer and solver are crucial for the observed performance gains and curriculum quality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modular agent skills effectively bridge the gap between verifiable deep execution and open-ended task variety, allowing for both precise feedback and broad exploration.
> - The continuous co-evolution of the proposer, solver, and skill library enables sustainable and adaptive curriculum construction, preventing issues like synthetic data collapse or mode collapse.
> - Proactive structural guidance from skills, combined with rigorous validity verification, ensures high-quality training data and focuses learning on the solver's true learning frontier.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Model (LLM) training is transitioning from human-designed datasets and manual annotations towards autonomous, interaction-driven self-evolution. Within this paradigm shift, self-play has emerged as a key technique, enabling LLMs to improve by generating tasks, finding solutions, and evaluating outcomes through various role-playing agents and multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL). Existing self-play methods have demonstrated successes in diverse domains, including logical reasoning, deep search, visual question answering, and software engineering.

However, the paper identifies a fundamental tension in current self-evolutionary approaches: the dilemma between task diversity and verification reliability.
- **Environment-bound methods** offer precise feedback and reliable verification by grounding task generation in specialized external environments (e.g., code executors, game simulators, retrieval engines). Examples include Absolute Zero for code, SPIRAL and MARSHAL for games, and Search Self-play for retrieval. While ensuring accuracy, these methods inherently constrain the learning agent to narrow, predefined operational domains, limiting their ability to generalize to broader, open-ended scenarios.
- **Open-ended self-generation methods** aim to expand the task space by generating tasks without strict environmental confinement, often paired with post-hoc filtering (e.g., format checks, majority voting). While broadening coverage, this approach frequently compromises quality control. Unguided generation often synthesizes ill-posed tasks, overfits to simple templates, or leads to synthetic data collapse over successive training iterations due to accumulated errors and biases. Post-hoc filtering acts passively, rejecting only obvious errors without proactively guiding the generator to produce diverse, valid, and reusable task patterns.

This research positions "agent skills" as a means to reconcile this tension. A skill, defined as a modular unit of procedural knowledge, bundles task-specific instructions and resources into a reusable block. Prior work has explored skill interfaces for agent inference-time execution, focusing on retrieval, compression, or progressive disclosure. Some research also investigates automatic skill construction from interaction traces. This paper, however, distinguishes itself by proposing an **evolving skill library as a training-time interface for task synthesis**. This enables skills to provide structural guidance and verification that co-evolves with the self-play curriculum, addressing the shortcomings of both narrow environment-bound methods and unreliable open-ended generation. The work aims to bridge the gap between structured verification and open-ended exploration by integrating skill-driven task generation into a continuous reinforcement learning loop.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the fundamental dilemma in current LLM self-evolution methodologies: the conflict between achieving broad task diversity and maintaining reliable task verification. Existing approaches struggle to simultaneously excel in both aspects. Environment-bound self-play, while providing precise and verifiable feedback, restricts the LLM's learning to narrow domains, impeding its ability to generalize. Conversely, open-ended task generation, while broadening the scope of potential tasks, often suffers from a lack of reliable verification, leading to the creation of ill-posed, trivial, or malformed tasks. This compromises the quality of synthetic training data and can result in data collapse, where accumulated errors and biases degrade the model's capabilities. The paper argues that post-hoc filtering mechanisms are insufficient as they only passively reject bad tasks rather than proactively guiding the generation of high-quality, diverse, and reusable tasks.

To resolve this dilemma, the key objectives of this work are:
1. **Introduce Agent Skills as a Middle Ground:** Propose that modular agent skills can act as an effective bridge, allowing for deep, verifiable execution within specific scenarios while simultaneously enabling open-ended task variety through dynamic routing.
2. **Develop a Co-Evolutionary Framework (Skill Self-Play - Skill-SP):** Design a reinforcement learning framework where a Proposer, a Solver, and a dynamic Skill Controller continuously co-evolve.
 - The **Proposer** aims to synthesize challenging and valid tasks, guided by dynamically sampled skills from a library.
 - The **Solver** learns to execute and solve these tasks, pushing its capability boundaries.
 - The **Skill Controller** analyzes execution feedback to refine existing skills, archive obsolete ones, and induce entirely new skills, thereby continuously expanding the task frontier.
3. **Ensure High-Fidelity Curriculum Generation:** Leverage skills to inject structural priors into the task generation process and provide explicit executable mechanisms for rigorous verification. This approach seeks to prevent the synthesis of ill-posed tasks and guide the generator toward producing reusable, logically valid, and diverse task patterns.
4. **Demonstrate Effectiveness Across Verifiable Agent Tasks:** Empirically validate the Skill-SP framework on diverse domains, specifically tool-use (API-Bank, BFCL) and logical reasoning (ZebraLogic), to show that it consistently enhances LLM performance and capability across models of varying initial proficiency.
5. **Achieve Sustainable and Open-Ended Self-Evolution:** Establish a paradigm where the self-play loop can autonomously generate and verify diverse tasks without collapsing into either narrow domains or chaotic synthetic noise, thereby unlocking a sustainable pathway for LLM self-improvement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this research is Skill Self-Play (Skill-SP), a reinforcement learning framework that orchestrates the co-evolution of a proposer policy (𝜋_propose), a solver policy (𝜋_solve), and an evolving skill library (S). This framework transforms generic self-play into a proactive curriculum-construction process.

#### 3.1. Skill Self-Play Objective

A verifiable agent task is formalized as a tuple (𝒙, 𝒄), where 𝒙 is the prompt for the solver, and 𝒄 is a hidden, machine-readable verification contract. The environment provides a reward R_solve(𝒙, 𝒚, 𝒄) for the solver's response 𝒚. The solver's expected success rate is 𝑣_solve(𝒙, 𝒄; 𝜋_solve).

The proposer's objective is to generate tasks that target the solver's learning frontier, defined by a medium-difficulty score (1 - 2|𝑣_solve - 0.5|). To prevent reward hacking (where the proposer generates ill-posed tasks to artificially inflate difficulty), a binary task quality filter, 𝟙{(𝒙,𝒄) is valid}, gates the proposer's reward. The overall objective is a bi-level optimization problem: the outer objective optimizes the skill library and proposer to synthesize valid, frontier-targeted tasks, while the inner objective optimizes the solver to maximize its execution success on these tasks.

#### 3.2. Proposer Optimization via Skill-Conditioned Generation

The proposer's role is to synthesize valid, frontier-targeted tasks. This process involves three stages:

- **Skill-Conditioned Generation:** Each skill 𝒔 ∈ S is a tuple ⟨𝑚, 𝑟, ℎ, 𝑒, 𝜈, 𝜎⟩, comprising routing metadata, procedural rules, generation hints, few-shot examples, executable validators, and historical usage statistics.
 - **Dynamic Skill Sampling:** Skills are sampled (𝒔 ∼ S) based on their statistics (𝜎), balancing exploitation of high-yield skills with exploration of under-tested ones.
 - **Dual-Stream Generation:** The proposer synthesizes candidates via two streams:
 - **Skill Stream:** (𝒙, 𝒄) ∼ 𝜋_propose(· | 𝒔), injecting strong structural priors.
 - **Open-ended Exploration Stream:** (𝒙, 𝒄) ∼ 𝜋_propose(· | ∅), for charting novel task spaces and preventing mode collapse.
- **Validity Verification:** The binary task quality filter 𝟙{(𝒙,𝒄) is valid} enforces rigorous structural and logical checks.
 - For skill-stream tasks, a task is valid if it is schema compliant, contract valid (passes skill-defined validators 𝜈), and probe consistent (𝐾 rollouts from the current solver yield a unique majority answer matching 𝒄).
 - For exploration-stream tasks, the skill-specific validation is omitted.
- **Policy Update:** At each iteration, the current solver (𝜋^(t)_solve) acts as an empirical evaluator. The proposer computes the gated reward R_propose, and its policy (𝜋^(t)_propose) is updated using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to maximize this expected reward. This ensures the proposer dynamically tracks the solver's learning frontier.

#### 3.3. Solver Optimization via Dynamic Curriculum Construction

The solver is optimized on a dynamic, high-quality curriculum:

- **Curriculum Construction:** Valid candidate tasks (T^(t)_skill from the skill stream, T^(t)_explore from the exploration stream) are ranked by their proposer reward R_propose, identifying the solver's learning frontier. A training pool D^(t) of size 𝑀 is formed by selecting the top-scoring tasks according to a blending ratio 𝛼.
- **Policy Update:** The solver policy (𝜋^(t)_solve) is updated using GRPO to maximize the environment verification reward R_solve on the constructed curriculum D^(t).

#### 3.4. Continuous Evolution of the Skill Library

To prevent curriculum stagnation, the skill library S^(t) dynamically co-evolves with the proposer and solver policies:

- **Skill Refinement:** Tracking statistics (𝜎) of each sampled skill are updated. Invalid generation attempts are analyzed to diagnose systematic failures and refine the skill's content, maintaining it as a robust repository of structural priors.
- **Skill Pruning:** Obsolete skills that consistently yield trivial tasks (expected frontier reward below a threshold 𝛾_prune) are identified as S^(t)_prune and archived, conserving generation budget.
- **Skill Induction and Update:** Novel candidates from the exploration stream that meet a minimum frontier reward threshold (𝛾_induce) form an induction set T^(t)_induce. A skill controller (𝜋_control, instantiated by the base policy) abstracts underlying task patterns into candidate skill packages. These are filtered for structural integrity and semantic novelty against the current library to yield newly induced skills S^(t)_new. The active library is then updated: S^(t+1) = (S^(t) \ S^(t)_prune) ∪ S^(t)_new. This mechanism distills successful open-ended exploration into reusable structural priors, expanding the curriculum.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Task Families:** Two domains were chosen:
 - **Tool-call prediction:** Evaluated on API-Bank Levels 1-3 and four BFCL categories (JavaScript, Python, Java, Live). Measures schema adherence and tool selection.
 - **Logical reasoning:** Evaluated on ZebraLogic, a benchmark for constraint-satisfaction grid puzzles across four complexity scales. Measures ability to satisfy complex constraints and deduce unique solutions.
- **Backbones:** Five open-source LLM backbones (Qwen3-4B-Instruct, Qwen3-8B, Ministral-3-8B-Instruct, Ministral-3-14B-Instruct, Granite-4.1-3B) ranging from 3B to 14B parameters were used. The same checkpoint initialized both proposer and solver.
- **Baselines:** Performance was compared against initial "Base" checkpoints and an "Unguided SP" baseline, which relies solely on passive post-hoc filtering without proactive skill guidance. Unguided SP was only evaluated on tool-calling due to its inability to synthesize valid logical puzzles.
- **Training Protocol:** All experiments ran for five iterations, using GRPO. Specific parameters were set for training pool size, solver probes, and update steps for both domains. Solver rewards targeted exact correctness and format compliance, while the proposer received a penalty for structural parsing failures.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluations demonstrate that Skill-SP consistently pushes the performance of LLMs across verifiable agent tasks.

#### 4.1. Tool-call Prediction (Table 1)

- **Universal Improvement:** Skill-SP universally improved all five evaluated LLM backbones across diverse tool-calling scenarios. For example, Qwen3-4B-Ins saw an average gain of +6.5 absolute points, and Ministral-3-8B, an initially misaligned model, achieved a substantial +42.9 absolute points overall.
- **Superior to Unguided SP:** Unguided SP yielded consistently smaller and sometimes inconsistent gains, occasionally causing performance degradation on specific subtasks. In contrast, Skill-SP maintained strictly positive and superior gains across the board.
- **Rescuing Misaligned Models:** Skill-SP notably "rescued" initially misaligned models, such as Ministral-3-8B, which had severe zero-shot schema adherence failures. For these models, Unguided SP often stagnated, failing to synthesize valid tasks and thus starving the learning loop. Skill-SP's proactive skill orchestration enabled significant performance turnarounds, confirming its effectiveness in generalizing tool-use capabilities.

#### 4.2. Logical Reasoning (Table 2)

- **Consistent Enhancement:** Skill-SP successfully transferred its benefits to rigorous constraint-satisfaction reasoning, universally improving overall accuracy across all five backbones.
- **Turnarounds for Weak Models:** While competent models like Qwen3-4B-Instruct showed steady enhancements across all task scales, Skill-SP delivered striking turnarounds for logically misaligned models like Ministral-3-14B, achieving up to +12.0 points overall grid-level gains and over +35 points on smaller puzzles.
- **Unguided SP Failure:** Unguided SP was unable to bootstrap a viable training loop in the reasoning domain, as it failed to synthesize valid logical puzzles, underscoring the necessity of proactive structural guidance.
- **Progress on Complexity:** Progress on extreme Large and X-Large scales remained limited for initially weak models, suggesting a minimal capability threshold is required for pure self-play to bootstrap valid learning signals.

#### 4.3. Ablations (Figure 4, Tables 3 & 4)

Ablation studies on Qwen3-4B-Instruct validated the contribution of each design choice:
- **Naive Self-Play (Unguided SP):** Removing skill orchestration entirely (Unguided SP) led to early performance plateaus and a 2.6 absolute point overall performance drop compared to Skill-SP, confirming that standard self-play is insufficient for complex tasks without structural guidance.
- **Over-Specialization (Skill-only pool):** Generating tasks exclusively from the skill library (Skill-only) degraded generalized evaluation performance, indicating that mixing open-ended exploration is crucial for preserving task diversity and preventing mode collapse.
- **Static Skill Orchestration (Uniform routing, Frozen skills):** Both uniform skill routing and a static (frozen) skill library underperformed the full system by 1.9 and 2.3 points respectively, demonstrating that dynamic curriculum allocation and continuous library evolution are essential for the observed performance gains.
- **Co-evolutionary Updates (Frozen proposer, Frozen feedback solver, Frozen both):** Fixing the proposer at initialization resulted in a 2.1-point overall drop, while fixing the solver used for probing and proposer rewards caused a severe 3.0-point degradation. Disabling both yielded the largest drop (-3.2 points). This confirmed that both proposer and solver policies must co-evolve: the proposer to synthesize harder tasks, and the feedback solver to provide accurate difficulty evaluations.

#### 4.4. Data-Loop Diagnostics (Figure 5 & 6)

- **Curriculum Quality:** The skill-routed stream consistently generated tasks closer to the solver’s learning frontier (mean 𝑣_solve ≈ 0.57) compared to the Unguided SP (0.70) and exploration streams (0.75). PCA visualization of task embeddings showed that the Skill-SP mixed pool provided broader semantic coverage than Unguided SP, indicating simultaneous improvements in task difficulty and structural diversity.
- **Library Evolution:** The skill library dynamically evolved, continuously inducing approximately 20 new packages per round, updating existing ones based on execution traces, and retiring obsolete skills across five iterations. This demonstrates the library's function as an adaptive curriculum engine.
- **Skill Utilization:** The number of active skills (yielding ≥ 1 accepted record) expanded to 86, and the number of effective skills (measured by exponentiated Shannon entropy) steadily grew to 46, confirming that Skill-SP translates an expanding library into a broad and balanced curriculum, preventing over-reliance on a few dominant patterns.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Skill Self-Play (Skill-SP) as a significant advancement in the field of LLM self-evolution. Its core contribution lies in resolving the fundamental tension between task diversity and verification reliability, a persistent challenge in previous self-improvement methods. By introducing a dynamically evolving library of modular skills, Skill-SP provides a proactive and structured mechanism for guiding LLMs to generate high-fidelity, challenging, and diverse curricula that are precisely tailored to the solver's current learning frontier.

The potential impacts of this work are multifaceted:
- **Enhanced LLM Capabilities:** The demonstrated performance gains across complex tool-calling and logical reasoning tasks indicate that Skill-SP can systematically expand the operational horizons of various language models. This is particularly impactful for initially misaligned or weaker models, which showed striking capability turnarounds.
- **Sustainable Self-Improvement:** By integrating skill induction, refinement, and pruning into a continuous co-evolutionary loop, Skill-SP offers a pathway for sustainable and truly open-ended LLM self-evolution. This approach mitigates issues like synthetic data collapse and mode collapse often observed in unguided generation methods.
- **Improved Data Quality and Efficiency:** The use of skill-driven structural priors and executable validators ensures that synthesized tasks are structurally sound and verifiable, leading to higher-quality training data. Dynamic curriculum allocation focuses learning on challenging, frontier-targeted tasks, thereby maximizing sample efficiency.
- **Reduced Human Supervision:** The framework minimizes the reliance on manual design and annotation by enabling autonomous task generation and verification, thereby reducing the human effort required for post-training LLM improvement.
- **Foundation for Future Agent Development:** Skill-SP provides a robust training-time scaffold for developing more capable and general-purpose LLM agents. The abstraction of knowledge into reusable skills could facilitate the transfer of learned capabilities across different model architectures or even the bootstrapping of smaller, efficient agents from stronger frontier models.

In conclusion, Skill-SP represents a paradigm shift towards more structured, autonomous, and effective self-improvement for large language models, opening new avenues for scalable LLM development and alignment.
