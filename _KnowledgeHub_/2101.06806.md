# MP3: A Unified Model to Map, Perceive, Predict and Plan

> [!summary] Summary
> MP3 is an end-to-end mapless self-driving model that predicts an interpretable online map (drivable area, reachable lanes, intersections) and a probabilistic dynamic occupancy flow directly from raw LiDAR, then feeds both as cost functions into a retrieval-based neural motion planner, beating baselines with 74.39% closed-loop success versus 22.56% for the best prior method.

## Problem

- HD maps are costly to build and maintain and demand centimeter-level localization everywhere, making them a fragile single point of failure for self-driving stacks.
- Prior mapless imitation-learning methods output raw controls without interpretable intermediate representations, making decisions unexplainable and brittle to distributional shift.
- Existing online mapping and occupancy methods rely on discrete decisions like confidence thresholding or NMS, discarding uncertainty and risking missed lanes or objects in safety-critical driving.

## Method

- ==Probabilistic online map==: predicts drivable area and intersection as ==Bernoulli fields== plus reachable-lane distance transform (==Laplacian==) and direction (==Von Mises==) per BEV cell without HD map input.
- ==Dynamic occupancy flow==: parameterizes each class's current occupancy and a ==multi-modal temporal motion field== that iteratively warps occupancy forward, keeping future predictions consistent with motion.
- ==Retrieval-based neural motion planner==: samples kinematically feasible trajectories from expert demonstration clusters, predicts a command-conditioned route mask via ==CoordConv routing CNNs==, and scores trajectories with interpretable map and occupancy cost functions.

## Results

- In **164** closed-loop simulation scenarios, MP3 reaches **74.39%** success rate versus **22.56%** for the best baseline (NMP), over **3x** improvement.
- MP3 goes off-route only **14.63%** of the time versus **47.56-99.39%** for baselines, and progresses roughly **3x** farther per safety event than any baseline.
- Ablations show warped-occupancy supervision nearly doubles progress-per-collision over motion-only supervision, and adding route distance input cuts off-route rate from **44.51%** to **14.63%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dense probabilistic intermediate representations preserve uncertainty that discrete thresholded detections destroy, which materially improves downstream planning safety.
> - Closed-loop evaluation exposes distributional-shift failures that open-loop imitation metrics hide, since plans compound errors as the vehicle unrolls its own trajectory.
> - Coupling perception outputs directly as differentiable-style cost functions in a planner, rather than post-processing them, yields more interpretable and robust decisions than direct control regression.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

MP3 comes from Uber ATG's Toronto office, led by Raquel Urtasun in close collaboration with the University of Toronto — a group with a strong track record in 3D object detection, motion forecasting, and integrating learned perception with traditional planning (e.g. IntentNet, Fast and Furious, and Perceive-Predict-and-Plan). PPP in particular introduced interpretable semantic representations for safe motion planning, a concept MP3 builds on and extends to the mapless driving problem, leveraging vast real-world fleet data to tackle it at unprecedented scale.

Self-driving has traditionally relied heavily on High-Definition (HD) maps for lane topology, traffic-light locations, crosswalks, and traffic rules. HD maps are expensive and slow to build and maintain, require centimeter-level localization at all times (a single point of failure), and go stale as roads and rules change — severely limiting deployment to pre-mapped areas. Prior alternatives fall into a few camps: modular pipelines (interpretable but map-dependent and error-propagating across modules), purely end-to-end imitation learning like ALVINN and Conditional Imitation Learning (simple but brittle, black-box, and hard to certify safe), online mapping (often simplistic or highway-limited, and discards uncertainty), and occupancy-grid perception/prediction (often lacks velocity or multi-modal predictions needed for dynamic planning).

MP3 positions itself at the intersection of these paradigms: an end-to-end, mapless system that takes raw sensor data plus a high-level command and produces interpretable, *probabilistic* intermediate representations (an online map and a dynamic occupancy field) rather than raw controls or discrete detections, then couples them to a planner as explicit, uncertainty-aware cost functions. This bridges the interpretability of modular systems with the generalization of end-to-end learning, while removing HD-map dependency entirely.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind MP3 stems from the inherent limitations and challenges associated with High-Definition (HD) maps in autonomous driving systems. The authors identify several key issues that their proposed model aims to address:

1. **Scalability and Cost Reduction:** The primary objective is to enable self-driving solutions to scale globally by eliminating the need for expensive and labor-intensive HD map creation and maintenance. Current HD mapping processes are a significant bottleneck for widespread deployment.
2. **Increased Failure Tolerance:** By operating without HD maps, MP3 aims to make self-driving systems more robust to localization failures (where the SDV cannot precisely determine its position on a map) or situations where existing maps are outdated. It serves as a crucial fail-safe mechanism.
3. **Enhanced Interpretability:** Unlike many end-to-end imitation learning approaches that produce black-box control outputs, MP3 seeks to generate interpretable intermediate representations. This objective is critical for safety-critical systems, allowing engineers to understand the reasons behind the SDV's decisions, diagnose issues, and build trust in the autonomy stack.
4. **Robust Decision-Making with Uncertainty:** The authors aim to develop a system that can reason about and incorporate uncertainty in its understanding of both the static environment (e.g., lane boundaries) and dynamic agents (e.g., future paths of other vehicles). This probabilistic approach is vital for making safer and more cautious planning decisions.
5. **Safe and Comfortable Driving in Complex Environments:** The model is motivated to produce trajectories that are not only safe (avoiding collisions and adhering to traffic rules, even without explicit map priors) but also comfortable for passengers, while effectively following high-level navigation commands in challenging urban scenarios.

In summary, MP3's key objective is to provide an end-to-end, mapless driving solution that is scalable, robust, interpretable, and capable of making safe and comfortable decisions by explicitly modeling and leveraging uncertainty in its perception and prediction of the driving environment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MP3 proposes a unified, end-to-end neural network architecture that processes raw sensor data and a high-level command to produce a safe and comfortable trajectory. The approach is characterized by its generation of interpretable, probabilistic intermediate representations which directly inform a neural motion planner.

#### 3.1. End-to-End Architecture Overview

The MP3 model integrates four main components:
1. **Voxelized LiDAR Backbone Network:** This module processes a history of raw LiDAR point clouds to extract rich geometric and semantic features from the scene in a Bird's Eye View (BEV).
2. **Interpretable Scene Representations:** From the backbone features, two key probabilistic representations are predicted:
 - **Online Map:** Captures static environmental elements.
 - **Dynamic Occupancy Field:** Models the current and future states of dynamic agents.
3. **Routing Network:** Given a high-level command (e.g., "turn left") and the online map, this network predicts a probabilistic route.
4. **Neural Motion Planner:** This module leverages the predicted online map, dynamic occupancy field, and route to sample kinematically feasible trajectories, score them based on learned cost functions, and select the optimal, lowest-cost trajectory for the SDV.

#### 3.2. Interpretable Scene Representations

A core aspect of MP3 is its focus on producing intelligible intermediate representations that enhance interpretability and allow the motion planner to reason about uncertainty.

##### 3.2.1. Online Map

The online map captures critical static environmental information in BEV, modeled probabilistically:
- **Drivable Area:** Modeled as a Bernoulli random variable for each grid cell, indicating the probability of a cell being part of the road surface.
- **Reachable Lanes:** To guide the SDV to drive close to lane centers and in the correct direction, for each grid cell, the model predicts:
 - **Distance Transform:** The unsigned distance to the closest reachable lane centerline, modeled as a Laplacian distribution.
 - **Angle:** The angle of the closest reachable lane centerline segment, modeled as a Von Mises distribution, suitable for directional data.
- **Intersections:** Modeled as a Bernoulli random variable, indicating the probability of a cell being part of an intersection, crucial for traffic light/sign reasoning.

##### 3.2.2. Dynamic Occupancy Field

This representation focuses on the current and future states of dynamic objects (vehicles, pedestrians, bicyclists). It addresses the limitations of traditional object detection and prediction pipelines by avoiding discrete decisions like non-maximum suppression (NMS) and providing velocity estimates.
- **Initial Occupancy:** A BEV grid indicating the probability of each cell being occupied by dynamic objects at the current time (t=0), modeled as Bernoulli random variables. Separate occupancy grids are maintained for different object classes.
- **Temporal Motion Field:** For each occupied pixel, this field predicts 2D BEV velocity vectors for future time steps. Crucially, it models multi-modal behaviors by predicting a categorical distribution over *K* possible motion vectors for each spatio-temporal location. The motion field is predicted for *T=11* time steps (up to 5 seconds).
- **Occupancy Flow:** The initial occupancy is "warped" through the temporal motion field to iteratively compute future occupancies. This ensures consistency between predicted motion and future occupancy and allows for querying how initial occupancy evolves over time.

##### 3.2.3. Probabilistic Modeling and Uncertainty

By using distributions like Bernoulli, Laplacian, and Von Mises, MP3 explicitly models uncertainty in its predictions. This probabilistic information (e.g., standard deviation for distance, concentration for angle) is directly fed into the motion planner, allowing it to adapt its behavior (e.g., driving slower in areas of high map uncertainty).

#### 3.3. Neural Motion Planner

The motion planner generates safe, comfortable, and goal-directed trajectories by minimizing a learned cost function.

##### 3.3.1. Trajectory Sampling

Since HD maps are unavailable to guide trajectory generation, MP3 uses a novel retrieval-based approach:
- **Expert Demonstrations:** A large-scale dataset of real-world human driving trajectories is used.
- **Binning and Clustering:** Trajectories are binned based on the SDV's initial state (velocity, acceleration, curvature) and then clustered.
- **Retrieval:** During planning, kinematically feasible trajectories are generated by retrieving acceleration and steering rate profiles from cluster prototypes that match the SDV's current state and rolling out a bicycle model. This provides a diverse yet realistic set of candidate trajectories.

##### 3.3.2. Route Prediction

Given a high-level driving command (e.g., "turn right at the intersection" with an approximate distance to action), a dedicated routing network predicts a dense probabilistic "route map" in BEV. This network uses Coordinate Convolutions (CoordConv) and conditioned on the discrete action via a switch-like mechanism, helping to reason about spatial relationships and the goal.

##### 3.3.3. Trajectory Scoring

Each sampled trajectory is evaluated using a linear combination of learned cost functions:
- **Routing and Driving on Roads:** Costs ensure the SDV stays on the predicted route, close to reachable lane centers (using the distance transform and angle), and within the drivable area. Uncertainty in map predictions is explicitly penalized (e.g., higher cost for fast driving in uncertain lane regions). It also penalizes violating red traffic lights in predicted intersection areas.
- **Safety:** Costs are derived from the dynamic occupancy field. Trajectories overlapping with occupied regions are heavily penalized. A "headway" cost encourages maintaining a safe distance from objects based on their predicted motion and relative speeds.
- **Comfort:** Penalties are applied for high jerk, lateral acceleration, curvature, and its rate of change to promote smooth driving.

#### 3.4. Learning Strategy

MP3 is trained in two stages for stability:
1. **Stage 1 (Perception/Prediction):** The backbone, mapping, dynamic occupancy field, and routing networks are trained jointly using negative log-likelihood for map components, cross-entropy for occupancy (with hard negative mining), and a combination of categorical cross-entropy and Huber loss for multi-modal motion fields. Critically, future occupancy (derived from flow) also receives supervision, which significantly improves safety.
2. **Stage 2 (Planning):** With the perception/prediction components frozen, the weights for the linear combination of planning cost functions are trained using a max-margin loss. This loss encourages the human expert trajectory to have a lower cost than other (unsafe or non-expert) sampled trajectories, specifically penalizing unsafe actions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of MP3 is conducted on a large-scale real-world dataset called URBANEXPERT and in challenging closed-loop simulations, comparing its performance against several state-of-the-art baselines.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup and Baselines

- **Dataset:** URBANEXPERT, a large-scale dataset of 5000 training, 500 validation, and 1000 test scenarios (25 seconds each), focused on challenging urban driving. Geographically non-overlapping splits ensure generalization evaluation.
- **Baselines:**
 - **Imitation Learning (IL):** Direct prediction of future SDV positions.
 - **Conditional Imitation Learning (CIL):** Similar to IL but conditioned on a high-level driving command.
 - **Neural Motion Planner (NMP):** Predicts a planning cost-volume, detection, and prediction in a multi-task fashion.
 - **Trajectory Classification (TC):** Predicts a cost-volume for a probability distribution over trajectories.
 - **Conditional Neural Motion Planner (CNMP):** An extension of NMP incorporating high-level commands.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** A comprehensive set covering safety (collisions, off-road, oncoming traffic), adherence to commands (off-route, L2 distance to expert, progress), and comfort (jerk, lateral acceleration).

#### 4.2. Closed-Loop Simulation Performance

Closed-loop simulations are considered the most critical evaluation as they reflect real-world execution, where the SDV's own actions influence future states. MP3's performance in these simulations is overwhelmingly superior:
- **Success Rate:** MP3 achieves a 74.39% success rate, which is over 3 times higher than the next best baseline (NMP at 22.56%). This indicates MP3's ability to complete scenarios without critical failures.
- **Safety:** MP3 shows significantly lower "Off-Route" (14.63% vs. NMP's 64.02%) and other incident rates. Its "Progress per event (m)" is dramatically higher across all categories (any event, collision, off-road, off-route, oncoming traffic), meaning it drives much further before encountering any issue. For instance, it progresses 1037.08m per collision event, far surpassing any baseline.
- **Comfort:** MP3 exhibits lower jerk (1.64 m/s^3) and lateral acceleration (0.10 m/s^2) compared to most baselines, indicating smoother and more comfortable driving.
- **Imitation:** While not directly optimized for L2 distance, MP3 achieves an L2 distance of 12.95m, outperforming all baselines except the pure imitation learning models (IL/CIL) which are optimized for this but are highly unsafe.

The results strongly demonstrate MP3's robustness to distributional shifts and its superior performance in safety, comfort, and command following under realistic interactive conditions.

#### 4.3. Open-Loop Evaluation Against Expert Demonstrations

Open-loop evaluations assess how well the model predicts plans from expert states, without its own actions influencing the scene.
- **Safety:** MP3 produces the safest trajectories, with the lowest collision rates (0.21% at 0-3s, 2.07% at 0-5s), significantly better than all baselines.
- **Progress:** MP3 achieves the most progress (25.15m at 0-5s), indicating efficient movement towards the goal.
- **Comfort:** MP3 consistently ranks among the most comfortable, with low lateral acceleration (1.23 m/s^2) and jerk (1.88 m/s^3).
- **Map Understanding:** MP3 shows comparable performance to the best baselines (NMP/CNMP) in terms of off-route, off-road, and oncoming traffic violations, demonstrating accurate understanding of road rules and geometry.
- **Imitation vs. Safety:** Pure imitation models (IL, CIL) have lower L2 distances to expert trajectories but are notably unsafe, highlighting the trade-off. MP3 strikes a better balance, prioritizing safety while maintaining reasonable imitation.

The authors emphasize that open-loop results, while included for completeness, do not fully capture real-world performance. MP3's stark improvements in closed-loop simulations underscore its robustness when executing its own plans.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Insights

Figure 5 and the appendix provide qualitative examples, showcasing MP3's ability to:
- Accurately predict the online map (drivable areas, lane guidance, intersections) from sensor data.
- Generate highly expressive dynamic occupancy fields, capturing multi-modal behaviors of other vehicles and accurately predicting pedestrian movements, even from occlusion.
- Plan safe stops for newly appearing dynamic objects.
- Accurately predict routes for various high-level commands (keep lane, turn left/right) and execute safe maneuvers in crowded scenes, closely resembling human driving.

These visualizations provide strong evidence for the interpretability of MP3's intermediate representations and their direct contribution to safe and rational planning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MP3 represents a significant advancement in autonomous driving research, particularly in addressing the long-standing challenges of scalability, robustness, and interpretability. Its potential impact is multi-faceted.

#### 5.1. Addressing Scalability and Robustness

The most immediate and profound impact of MP3 is its potential to unlock self-driving at scale. By removing the fundamental dependency on expensive and labor-intensive HD maps, MP3 offers a pathway for autonomous vehicles to operate in previously unmapped areas or rapidly changing environments. This capability drastically reduces deployment costs and logistical hurdles, paving the way for wider adoption of autonomous technology across diverse geographies. Furthermore, its "mapless" nature inherently increases failure tolerance; the system is less vulnerable to errors in localization or outdated map information, making it a more robust and reliable solution for real-world deployment. This robustness, particularly demonstrated in closed-loop simulations, is critical for public acceptance and regulatory approval.

#### 5.2. Interpretability and Safety Assurance

MP3's commitment to producing interpretable probabilistic intermediate representations is a major step forward for safety-critical AI systems. Unlike opaque, black-box end-to-end models, MP3 allows engineers and regulators to understand *why* the SDV made a particular decision. The online map and dynamic occupancy field provide clear insights into the system's understanding of the static environment and the predicted behavior of other agents. This interpretability is invaluable for:
- **Debugging and Diagnostics:** Easier identification of failure modes and root causes.
- **Trust and Certification:** Building confidence in the system's decision-making process for developers, users, and regulatory bodies.
- **Risk Management:** The probabilistic nature of its representations allows for explicit reasoning about uncertainty, enabling the planner to make cautious decisions proportional to perceived risks. This is a crucial element for ensuring safety in complex, unpredictable driving scenarios.

#### 5.3. Future Implications

MP3's unified approach and innovative representations lay a strong foundation for future research in autonomous driving:
- **Foundation for General AI in Driving:** By learning to infer environmental semantics and dynamics directly from raw sensors, MP3 moves closer to a more general form of driving intelligence that is less reliant on engineered priors and more adaptive to novel situations.
- **Hybrid Systems:** The interpretable outputs of MP3 could potentially be integrated into hybrid autonomy stacks, serving as robust inputs for advanced planning algorithms or as a fallback system for traditional map-dependent solutions.
- **Advancements in Online Mapping and Prediction:** The novel online map and occupancy flow representations could inspire further research in real-time environmental understanding and multi-modal behavior prediction, pushing the boundaries of what's possible with sensor-only data.
- **Reduced Computational Overhead:** While not explicitly detailed as a primary focus, the end-to-end nature and unified learning could, in principle, lead to more efficient systems by avoiding redundant processing steps and enabling joint optimization.

In conclusion, MP3 represents a significant stride towards realizing truly scalable, safe, and robust autonomous driving. By successfully demonstrating mapless driving with interpretable, uncertainty-aware reasoning, it provides a compelling vision for the future of self-driving technology.
