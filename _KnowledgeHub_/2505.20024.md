# ReasonPlan: Unified Scene Prediction and Decision Reasoning for Closed-loop Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called ReasonPlan fine-tunes multimodal large language models for closed-loop autonomous driving, integrating self-supervised next scene prediction and explicit decision reasoning. The system achieves a Driving Score of 64.01 on the Bench2Drive benchmark, a 16.1-point improvement over prior state-of-the-art imitation learning, and exhibits strong zero-shot generalization in unseen scenarios.

## Problem

- Traditional end-to-end autonomous driving methods, often based on imitation learning, suffer from causal confusion and poor generalization to out-of-distribution scenarios in complex interactive environments.
- Existing E2E approaches are predominantly evaluated in open-loop settings, which fails to adequately assess their reactive behavior and continuous adaptation in real-world closed-loop conditions.
- Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have untapped potential in closed-loop autonomous driving due to insufficient utilization of visual information, lack of explicit reasoning processes, and the absence of suitable planning-oriented reasoning datasets.

## Method

- The **ReasonPlan** framework integrates a self-supervised ==Next Scene Prediction (NSP)== task to enhance scene understanding and a supervised ==Decision Chain-of-Thought (DeCoT)== process for interpretable decision-making and trajectory generation.
- A two-stage training strategy is employed to first align visual and textual modalities via projection and context encoding, then jointly fine-tune the entire ==MLLM== framework.
- A novel ==Planning-oriented Decision Reasoning (PDR)== dataset was curated, providing 210k samples with detailed, structured reasoning labels for complex closed-loop planning scenarios.

## Results

- ReasonPlan achieved an L2 error of **0.61** for open-loop trajectory prediction on Bench2Drive, outperforming prior state-of-the-art E2E imitation learning models.
- In closed-loop evaluations, the framework recorded a Driving Score of **64.01** on Bench2Drive, representing a **16.1-point improvement** over the leading non-distillation imitation learning method, MomAD.
- The system demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization on the DOS benchmark, achieving the highest average Driving Score of **78.02** across challenging out-of-distribution scenarios, surpassing other MLLM-based and imitation learning baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multimodal large language models can be effectively fine-tuned with structured reasoning tasks to achieve superior performance and generalization in challenging closed-loop autonomous driving scenarios.
> - Integrating self-supervised future scene prediction with explicit, supervised decision chain-of-thought enhances both contextual understanding and the interpretability of an MLLM's driving policy.
> - Developing high-quality, planning-oriented datasets with detailed reasoning traces is crucial for training MLLMs to perform complex, interactive decision-making in autonomous driving.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Research Landscape and Context

#### 1.1. Challenges in End-to-End Autonomous Driving

The field of autonomous driving has seen a growing interest in end-to-end (E2E) approaches, which aim to directly map raw sensor inputs to driving actions or trajectories. This paradigm offers advantages in simplifying the complex driving pipeline, making it more scalable and data-driven. However, mainstream E2E methods, largely based on imitation learning, face significant limitations when confronted with intricate, dynamic, and interactive closed-loop environments. Key challenges include:

- **Causal Confusion:** Imitation learning models often struggle with causal confusion, meaning they may learn spurious correlations from demonstrations rather than true causal relationships, leading to unsafe or suboptimal behavior in interactive scenarios.
- **Limited Generalization:** These models frequently exhibit poor generalization capabilities, struggling to adapt to out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios that differ from their training data. This is a critical hurdle for real-world deployment, where unforeseen situations are common.
- **Open-Loop Evaluation Bias:** Most existing E2E approaches are primarily evaluated in open-loop settings, where the model's generated actions do not influence the environment. This can mask deficiencies in reactive behavior and lead to models overfitting to specific ego-vehicle states, failing catastrophically in closed-loop settings where continuous adaptation is paramount.

#### 1.2. Role of Multimodal Large Language Models

The emergence of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) has presented new avenues for addressing these challenges. MLLMs, with their powerful vision-language reasoning and zero-shot generalization abilities, offer the potential to enhance E2E autonomous driving systems by integrating high-level semantic understanding with perceptual inputs. Recent efforts in this direction include:

- Dual-system frameworks that use MLLMs for high-level intentions.
- LLM distillation techniques to inject abstract reasoning into lightweight planning modules.
- Direct trajectory prediction in textual forms.

Despite these promising explorations, the application of MLLMs to **closed-loop autonomous driving remains largely underexplored**, and their performance has not yet demonstrated a clear superiority over traditional imitation learning techniques in rigorous closed-loop evaluations. The paper identifies three primary reasons for this gap:

1. **Insufficient Utilization of Visual Information:** Many MLLM-based methods either supervise perception/decision using only text or use predicted images, leading to limited scene comprehension and error accumulation.
2. **Lack of Explicit Reasoning Process:** While some use multi-turn Q&A, they often fail to fully engage the MLLMs' Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning capabilities, which are crucial for interpretable and robust decision-making.
3. **Absence of Planning-Oriented, High-Quality Reasoning Datasets:** Existing datasets are often tailored for scene understanding in open-loop settings and lack the specific annotations required for complex closed-loop planning and decision reasoning.

#### 1.3. ReasonPlan's Position and Contribution

"ReasonPlan" directly positions itself to tackle these limitations. It aims to harness the advanced reasoning and generalization capabilities of MLLMs to develop a robust, interpretable, and high-performing system for **closed-loop autonomous driving**. By focusing on holistic reasoning, self-supervised next scene prediction, and explicit decision CoT, coupled with a novel planning-oriented dataset, ReasonPlan seeks to demonstrate that MLLMs can indeed offer clear advantages over traditional E2E imitation learning methods in challenging, interactive environments. This work represents a significant step towards bridging the gap between sophisticated language-vision AI and the practical demands of real-world autonomous vehicles.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind ReasonPlan stems from the inherent limitations of current end-to-end (E2E) autonomous driving systems, particularly their struggles with causal confusion, generalization to unseen scenarios, and inadequate performance in closed-loop evaluations. While multimodal large language models (MLLMs) offer compelling vision-language reasoning and zero-shot generalization capabilities, their full potential for robust, closed-loop autonomous driving has remained untapped due to several critical challenges.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the ReasonPlan research are:

1. **To develop a novel MLLM fine-tuning framework (ReasonPlan):** The primary goal is to create a framework specifically designed to adapt MLLMs for complex, closed-loop driving scenarios, enabling more comprehensive and robust decision-making.
2. **To enhance holistic reasoning capabilities:** This involves integrating a dual mechanism into the MLLM:
 - **Self-supervised Next Scene Prediction (NSP):** To encourage the model to align visual representations with actionable driving context, thereby enriching its contextual understanding of dynamic driving scenarios.
 - **Supervised Decision Chain-of-Thought (DeCoT):** To promote an interpretable and causally grounded decision-making process, allowing the model to explicitly reason through its choices, akin to human drivers.
3. **To curate a planning-oriented, high-quality reasoning dataset (PDR):** A significant objective is to address the lack of suitable training data by creating a large-scale dataset tailored for closed-loop planning, featuring detailed reasoning traces.
4. **To demonstrate clear superiority over mainstream E2E imitation learning methods:** The research aims to empirically prove that the ReasonPlan framework can achieve significantly better performance on challenging closed-loop benchmarks compared to existing state-of-the-art E2E imitation learning approaches.
5. **To exhibit strong zero-shot generalization:** A crucial objective is to show that ReasonPlan can generalize effectively to unseen, out-of-distribution corner cases, highlighting its adaptability and robustness for real-world deployment.

In essence, the motivation is to leverage the advanced cognitive abilities of MLLMs to overcome the interpretability and generalization shortcomings of current E2E driving systems, ultimately paving the way for more intelligent and safer autonomous vehicles.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ReasonPlan introduces a unified scene prediction and decision reasoning framework, meticulously designed to leverage multimodal large language models (MLLMs) for closed-loop autonomous driving. Its methodology can be broken down into three main components: the ReasonPlan framework itself, its two-stage training strategy, and the specialized PDR dataset.

#### 3.1. ReasonPlan Framework

The core of ReasonPlan consists of two synergistic modules: the self-supervised Next Scene Prediction (NSP) task and the supervised Decision Chain-of-Thought (DeCoT) process.

##### 3.1.1. Self-supervised Next Scene Prediction (NSP)

The NSP task is designed to enhance the model's scene understanding and spatially align visual features with the language semantic space.

- **Input Processing:** The system takes multi-view RGB images (e.g., six surround-view cameras) and ego vehicle context (current velocity, acceleration, and driving command) as input. An AnyRes partitioning strategy is applied to the front-view images, dividing them into spatial grids to capture fine-grained details efficiently. A SigLIP model acts as the vision encoder, processing these images into visual feature tensors.
- **Feature Projection and Context Encoding:** The visual features are then mapped into the language embedding dimension using a two-layer MLP projection module. Simultaneously, a separate two-layer MLP context encoder processes the ego vehicle's dynamic states and high-level driving intent (e.g., "turn left") into a contextual representation.
- **Future Scene Prediction:** The encoded context and visual features are concatenated and fed into the MLLM. The MLLM is trained to predict the latent visual embedding of the scene three seconds into the future.
- **Self-Supervision:** The ground truth for this prediction is generated by processing the actual future multi-view images (specifically, the front-view for computational efficiency, as validated by ablation studies) through the identical SigLIP encoder and MLP projector. A Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss is applied in the latent feature space to enforce consistency between the predicted and actual future visual representations. This objective trains the LLM to anticipate future perceptual states, integrating ego motion and scene context, thereby enhancing spatial awareness and context-aware forecasting.

##### 3.1.2. Supervised Decision Chain-of-Thought (DeCoT)

Following NSP, the DeCoT process focuses on generating interpretable decision-making and executable trajectories in textual form, exploiting the LLM's pre-trained knowledge.

- **Multimodal Input Integration:** The system prompt, navigation instructions, and ego context are tokenized and encoded into textual embeddings. Crucially, special `<image>` tokens are dynamically substituted with the visual embeddings obtained from the vision encoder, ensuring seamless integration of visual and textual modalities. Specific tokens ([BOI], [EOI], [BOT], [EOT]) are introduced to mark the boundaries of image inputs and reasoning processes.
- **Structured Reasoning:** ReasonPlan enforces explicit textual supervision over intermediate reasoning steps, mimicking human cognitive processes. This "planning-oriented thinking" includes:
 - **Scene Understanding:** Identifying lane topology, drivable areas, and feasible maneuvers.
 - **Traffic Sign Recognition:** Interpreting traffic signals, warning signs, and construction indicators.
 - **Critical Object Identification for Risk Assessment:** Analyzing nearby dynamic objects (cars, pedestrians) for category, position, motion, and potential risks.
 - **Meta Action:** Generating high-level lateral (e.g., lane follow, change left) and longitudinal (e.g., accelerate, stop) driving decisions.
 Unlike traditional Q&A, this is performed in a single forward pass, leveraging the LLM's common-sense reasoning.
- **Output and Loss:** The final output is a textual representation of the planned trajectory. A Cross-Entropy (CE) loss is computed on the generated textual sequence, including both the reasoning steps and the trajectory.

#### 3.2. Training Strategy

ReasonPlan adopts a two-stage training strategy to gradually align modalities and refine reasoning capabilities:

- **Stage I:** The projection module (for visual features) and the context encoder are initially trained using non-decision supervision. This stage focuses on aligning the visual feature space with the textual semantic space and extracting relevant contextual cues.
- **Stage II:** In the second stage, the entire framework—including the projection module, context encoder, and the MLLM backbone—is jointly fine-tuned using the PDR dataset. This stage transfers the MLLM's pre-trained common-sense reasoning to complex driving scenarios. The total objective is a weighted combination of the MSE loss for image prediction (L_image) and the CE loss for language reasoning (L_text).

#### 3.3. Planning-oriented Decision Reasoning (PDR) Dataset

Recognizing the inadequacy of existing datasets for closed-loop MLLM-based driving, the authors constructed PDR, a large-scale, high-quality dataset of 210k samples.

- **Source and Annotation:** PDR is built upon the Bench2Drive dataset, which provides rich ground-truth annotations from the CARLA simulator. An automated annotation pipeline is employed to generate structured reasoning labels for each sample, which are then human-verified.
- **Reasoning Trace Components:** The dataset meticulously annotates the complete decision reasoning process for training scenarios, covering:
 - **Scene Understanding:** Details on lane types, connectivity, drivable areas, and candidate routes derived from HD map information.
 - **Traffic Sign Recognition:** Annotations for various traffic signs and their implications, including traffic light states.
 - **Critical Object Identification for Risk Assessment:** Attributes (category, position, motion state) and risk estimations for all influential obstacles.
 - **Meta Action:** Structured lateral and longitudinal expert meta-actions, filtered for stability, and serialized into language-based formats.
- **Purpose:** PDR is designed to leverage the LLM's reasoning and generalization capabilities in dynamic driving environments, providing explicit supervision for the DeCoT process.

The vision encoder used is SigLIP, and the language model is Qwen-0.5B, chosen for its balance of efficiency and reasoning capability, making it suitable for potential real-time deployment. This comprehensive methodology ensures that ReasonPlan can effectively perceive, understand, reason, and plan in complex driving scenarios.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ReasonPlan's performance was rigorously evaluated on two benchmarks, Bench2Drive and DOS, demonstrating significant advancements over existing methods in both open-loop and closed-loop settings, as well as in zero-shot generalization.

#### 4.1. Superior Performance on Bench2Drive

On the Bench2Drive benchmark, which focuses on challenging interactive scenarios and multi-ability assessment in a closed-loop manner, ReasonPlan achieved leading performance across multiple metrics.

- **Open-Loop Trajectory Prediction:** ReasonPlan recorded an L2 error of 0.61, indicating superior trajectory prediction accuracy. This outperforms prior state-of-the-art E2E imitation learning models such as MomAD (0.82 L2) and UniAD-Base (0.73 L2), establishing a new benchmark for trajectory prediction.
- **Closed-Loop Driving Performance:**
 - **Driving Score (DS):** ReasonPlan achieved a DS of 64.01, marking a substantial improvement of 16.1 points over MomAD (47.91), the leading non-distillation IL-based SOTA method. It also demonstrated competitive performance against distillation-based methods like DriveAdapter (64.22 DS).
 - **Success Rate (SR):** The model significantly improved the success rate by 16.44% compared to MomAD, highlighting its enhanced reliability and safety in completing routes without infractions.
 - **Efficiency (Effi):** ReasonPlan achieved the highest efficiency score of 180.64 among all evaluated methods, reflecting a proactive and effective driving policy that maintains optimal speed relative to surrounding traffic.
 - **Mean Ability Score:** With a Mean Ability score of 36.66% across diverse driving scenarios (including Merging, Overtaking, Emergency Brake, Give Way, Traffic Sign), ReasonPlan substantially outperformed all E2E baselines (e.g., VAD 16.75%, UniAD-Base 15.94%), validating its robust reasoning capabilities across a spectrum of driving intents.
 - **Comfort (Comf):** A comfort score of 25.63 was reported, indicating a common trade-off between agile trajectory generation and motion smoothness, suggesting an area for future optimization (e.g., through post-smoothing).

#### 4.2. Strong Zero-Shot Generalization on DOS

To assess its out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization capabilities, ReasonPlan was evaluated zero-shot on the DriveOcclusionSim (DOS) benchmark, which features complex scenarios requiring reasoning about occluded information.

- ReasonPlan achieved the highest average Driving Score of 78.02 across the four challenging DOS scenarios (Parked Cars, Sudden Brake, Left Turn, Red Light Infraction). This performance significantly surpassed other MLLM-based methods like LMDrive (64.53) and IL-based baselines like UniAD-Base (71.09).
- These results underscore ReasonPlan's strong capacity for robust and safe decision-making in unseen, critical corner cases, a direct benefit of its holistic reasoning pipeline.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Insights

Qualitative comparisons (Figure 5 and A3 in the supplementary material) further illustrate ReasonPlan's superior decision-making. For instance:

- In a signalized junction on Bench2Drive, ReasonPlan accurately detected a green light transition and proceeded safely, whereas baselines stalled due to misinterpretation.
- In a pedestrian emerging scenario on DOS, ReasonPlan anticipated risk by decelerating early and stopping promptly, avoiding collisions that other methods failed to prevent.
- Additional cases showed effective navigation through complex lane changes in "AccidentTwoWays" and proactive pedestrian avoidance in "PedestrianCrossing," where baselines exhibited conservative halts or collisions.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study Validation

Ablation studies on the Dev10 subset of Bench2Drive validated the effectiveness and synergistic nature of ReasonPlan's core components:

- **Next Scene Prediction (NSP):** NSP alone improved planning (DS 52.61 vs. 41.84 without), demonstrating enhanced spatial understanding through dense visual supervision. However, without DeCoT, it resulted in lower infraction scores. Ablation showed front-view NSP performed on par with all-view, confirming front-view's semantic richness.
- **Decision Chain-of-Thought (DeCoT):** DeCoT alone improved the ability to handle intricate scenarios (DS 53.97 vs. 41.84 without), proving the benefit of structured textual reasoning.
- **NSP and DeCoT Synergy:** The integration of both NSP and DeCoT yielded the best overall performance (DS 57.83), as NSP provided crucial visual context while DeCoT enforced structured, traffic-compliant reasoning.
- **Individual Reasoning Steps:** Removing any single component from the DeCoT reasoning pipeline (Scene Understanding, Traffic Sign Recognition, Critical Object Identification, Meta Action) degraded performance, with Meta Action omission leading to the most significant drop. This confirms the importance of a complete and structured reasoning process, and the quality of the PDR dataset.

In summary, ReasonPlan demonstrates a significant step forward in MLLM-based autonomous driving, showcasing superior performance and generalization in challenging closed-loop scenarios through its innovative architecture and dedicated dataset.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "ReasonPlan" research marks a notable advancement in the field of autonomous driving, particularly in its novel integration of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for complex closed-loop scenarios. The work offers several key areas of significance and potential impact:

#### 5.1. Contributions to Autonomous Driving

- **Bridging the MLLM-AD Gap:** ReasonPlan successfully addresses a critical limitation of previous MLLM-based autonomous driving methods, which largely operated in open-loop settings or showed suboptimal performance in closed-loop evaluations. By demonstrating clear superiority in closed-loop benchmarks, it effectively validates the utility of MLLMs for real-world interactive driving.
- **Enhanced Reasoning and Generalization:** The synergistic integration of the self-supervised Next Scene Prediction (NSP) task and the explicit Decision Chain-of-Thought (DeCoT) process is foundational. NSP enriches visual context and spatial understanding, while DeCoT provides interpretable, causally grounded, and human-like decision-making. This combination leads to robust performance in complex, multi-intent scenarios and, crucially, strong zero-shot generalization capabilities in unseen corner cases (DOS benchmark), which is paramount for the safety and reliability of autonomous vehicles.
- **Interpretability and Trust:** The explicit, structured reasoning steps facilitated by DeCoT offer a significant advantage in interpretability. By verbalizing its understanding of the scene, traffic signs, critical objects, and meta-actions, ReasonPlan provides a transparent decision-making process. This enhances the trustworthiness and explainability of autonomous systems, a vital factor for public acceptance and regulatory approval.
- **Dataset Contribution:** The creation of the Planning-oriented Decision Reasoning (PDR) dataset is a valuable public contribution to the research community. This large-scale, high-quality dataset, specifically tailored for closed-loop planning and reasoning, provides a crucial resource for training and evaluating future MLLM-based autonomous driving models, fostering further innovation in the field.
- **Paving the Way for Cognitive AD Systems:** The work highlights the promise of MLLM-based frameworks in seamlessly integrating high-level cognitive reasoning with low-level motion planning. This approach suggests a paradigm shift towards more "cognitive" and "generalizable" autonomous driving systems that can understand, anticipate, and react to complex, dynamic environments more intelligently than purely reactive or imitation-based systems.

#### 5.2. Identified Limitations and Future Directions

The authors thoughtfully acknowledge several limitations, which also illuminate promising avenues for future research:

- **Inference Latency:** While the chosen Qwen-0.5B MLLM is deemed acceptable for real-time deployment, larger MLLMs (e.g., 7B) pose significant inference latency challenges. Future work could explore techniques like latent-space reasoning, model compression, or hardware acceleration to improve efficiency.
- **Unimodal Action Output:** ReasonPlan's actions are currently represented as unimodal textual outputs. A more modular design, decoupling LLM-based reasoning from specialized generative models for multimodal trajectory synthesis, could enhance flexibility and scalability, aligning with trends in robotics.
- **Reliance on Offline Datasets and Collisions:** Like many E2E frameworks, ReasonPlan relies on supervised fine-tuning over offline datasets. This approach inherently limits the model's ability to learn from interactive feedback, occasionally leading to unavoidable collisions. Integrating reinforcement learning (RL) or environment-aware adaptation mechanisms could enable models to learn from online experience and mitigate this limitation.
- **Suitability of MLLMs as Foundation Models:** The paper also raises a fundamental question about whether MLLMs are the optimal foundation models for E2E autonomous driving and if they can truly align visual language and actions effectively. This calls for further investigation into dedicated Vision-Language-Action (VLA) foundation models specifically designed for autonomous driving.

In conclusion, ReasonPlan represents a significant step forward in integrating the advanced capabilities of MLLMs into the complex domain of closed-loop autonomous driving. Its emphasis on interpretable reasoning, strong generalization, and robust performance in challenging scenarios provides a strong foundation for the development of safer and more intelligent autonomous systems, while also clearly outlining the next frontiers for research in this exciting area.
