# CATCH-FORM-3D: Compliance-Aware Tactile Control and Hybrid Deformation Regulation for 3D Viscoelastic Object Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> CATCH-FORM-3D is a dual-loop control framework for manipulating viscoelastic 3D objects (silicone, foam, and similar compliant materials) that explicitly models the object's time-dependent stress-strain behavior — stiffness, damping, *and* stress relaxation — rather than treating contact as an instantaneous force-deformation relationship. A PDE-driven observer fuses vision and tactile feedback to estimate material parameters online, and an admittance outer loop plus a reaction-diffusion inner loop hold both force and surface deformation within tight bounds across a range of material hardnesses.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation of soft, viscoelastic objects requires regulating both contact force and surface deformation, but conventional grasp controllers treat the object as a static, instantaneous force-deformation map.
- Real viscoelastic materials exhibit spatiotemporal coupling — stiffness, strain-rate damping, *and* stress relaxation act together — that a purely elastic (Hookean) or single-mechanism model cannot capture, producing unstable or object-specific manual tuning.
- No unified controller had combined a physically-grounded 3D contact model with closed-loop visual-tactile parameter estimation across a range of material hardnesses (from soft silicone to near-rigid ceramics/glass) without per-object recalibration.

## Method

- Derives a unified ==3D partial differential equation (PDE)== over the object's surface, $\dot\phi = \epsilon\Delta\phi + a_1 f + a_2 \dot f + \lambda\phi$ — a 3D continuum generalization of the 1D **Kelvin-Voigt**/**Maxwell** (Burgers) force-displacement model, with an added spatial diffusion term ($\epsilon\Delta\phi$). $a_1, a_2$ carry the stiffness/damping coefficients from that reduction, while $\lambda\phi$ ($\lambda=-\beta^{-1}k$, tracing back to the spring constant $k$) is a first-order relaxation/decay term, so the controller's internal state carries the material's *relaxation* dynamics, not just its current deformation.
- A **dual-loop control** structure: an outer ==admittance control== loop uses force feedback to adaptively update the reference deformation target, while an inner reaction-diffusion loop tracks that deformation via geometric surface conformance.
- A **PDE-driven observer** fuses visual and tactile streams to estimate the object's material parameters ($\epsilon, a_1, a_2, \lambda$) online, so the same controller adapts across materials without hand-tuned, object-specific gains.

## Results

- Demonstrated on a PaXini robotic hand across five material classes (Shore 40A / 60A silicone, nano-foam, ceramic plate, glass tube): steady-state deformation-tracking error of **0.68-0.85 mm²** (composite spatial-distribution + area index) and force-tracking error held below **5%** across all materials (absolute force error **0.15-0.55 N** depending on material).
- The reaction-diffusion inner loop achieves sub-millimeter deformation accuracy while the outer admittance loop keeps contact force within its target band, without re-tuning between the five tested material classes.
- Results are reported over continuous manipulation episodes (up to 17 s), with n=5 independent trials/condition for statistical reliability (repeated-measures ANOVA, α=0.05); these are independent replicates, not sequential regrasps — the paper never carries material state across a sequence of regrasps on the same object instance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A physically-grounded viscoelastic PDE (a Kelvin-Voigt/Maxwell Burgers reduction, extended with a spatial diffusion term) is tractable as a real-time robot controller — the closest existing instance of genuine contact-memory dynamics (a state that depends on the *history* of applied force, not just its current value) in a deployed manipulation policy.
> - The relaxation/decay term ($\lambda\phi$) is exactly the mathematical object that would let a controller anticipate stress *decay* between loading events — but the paper only ever runs it within one continuous manipulation episode, never resets or carries the material state across separate, temporally-separated grasps on the same object instance.
> - Because the observer estimates material parameters online rather than assuming them fixed, the same machinery is a plausible substrate for tracking a *slowly drifting* parameter (e.g., a fatigue-degraded stiffness) across repeated handling — a use the paper does not explore.
