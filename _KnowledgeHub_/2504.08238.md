# CATCH-FORM-3D: Compliance-Aware Tactile Control and Hybrid Deformation Regulation for 3D Viscoelastic Object Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> CATCH-FORM-3D is a dual-loop control framework for manipulating viscoelastic 3D objects (silicone, foam, and similar compliant materials) that explicitly models the object's time-dependent stress-strain behavior — stiffness, damping, *and* stress relaxation — rather than treating contact as an instantaneous force-deformation relationship. A PDE-driven observer fuses vision and tactile feedback to estimate material parameters online, and an admittance outer loop plus a reaction-diffusion inner loop hold both force and surface deformation within tight bounds across a range of material hardnesses.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation of soft, viscoelastic objects requires regulating both contact force and surface deformation, but conventional grasp controllers treat the object as a static, instantaneous force-deformation map.
- Real viscoelastic materials exhibit spatiotemporal coupling — stiffness, strain-rate damping, *and* stress relaxation act together — that a purely elastic (Hookean) or single-mechanism model cannot capture, producing unstable or object-specific manual tuning.
- No unified controller had combined a physically-grounded 3D contact model with closed-loop visual-tactile parameter estimation across a range of material hardnesses (from soft silicone to near-rigid ceramics/glass) without per-object recalibration.

## Method

- Derives a unified ==3D partial differential equation (PDE)== over the object's surface, $\dot\phi = \epsilon\Delta\phi + a_1 f + a_2 \dot f + \lambda\phi$ — a 3D continuum generalization of the 1D **Kelvin-Voigt**/**Maxwell** (Burgers) force-displacement model, with an added spatial diffusion term ($\epsilon\Delta\phi$). $a_1, a_2$ carry the stiffness/damping coefficients from that reduction, while $\lambda\phi$ ($\lambda=-\beta^{-1}k$, tracing back to the spring constant $k$) is a first-order relaxation/decay term, so the controller's internal state carries the material's *relaxation* dynamics, not just its current deformation.
- A **dual-loop control** structure: an outer ==admittance control== loop uses force feedback to adaptively update the reference deformation target, while an inner reaction-diffusion loop tracks that deformation via geometric surface conformance.
- A **PDE-driven observer** fuses visual and tactile streams to estimate the object's material parameters ($\epsilon, a_1, a_2, \lambda$) online, so the same controller adapts across materials without hand-tuned, object-specific gains.

## Results

- Demonstrated on a PaXini robotic hand across five material classes (Shore 40A / 60A silicone, nano-foam, ceramic plate, glass tube): steady-state deformation-tracking error of **0.68-0.85 mm²** (composite spatial-distribution + area index) and force-tracking error held below **5%** across all materials (absolute force error **0.15-0.55 N** depending on material).
- The reaction-diffusion inner loop achieves sub-millimeter deformation accuracy while the outer admittance loop keeps contact force within its target band, without re-tuning between the five tested material classes.
- Results are reported over continuous manipulation episodes (up to 17 s), with n=5 independent trials/condition for statistical reliability (repeated-measures ANOVA, α=0.05); these are independent replicates, not sequential regrasps — the paper never carries material state across a sequence of regrasps on the same object instance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A physically-grounded viscoelastic PDE (a Kelvin-Voigt/Maxwell Burgers reduction, extended with a spatial diffusion term) is tractable as a real-time robot controller — the closest existing instance of genuine contact-memory dynamics (a state that depends on the *history* of applied force, not just its current value) in a deployed manipulation policy.
> - The relaxation/decay term ($\lambda\phi$) is exactly the mathematical object that would let a controller anticipate stress *decay* between loading events — but the paper only ever runs it within one continuous manipulation episode, never resets or carries the material state across separate, temporally-separated grasps on the same object instance.
> - Because the observer estimates material parameters online rather than assuming them fixed, the same machinery is a plausible substrate for tracking a *slowly drifting* parameter (e.g., a fatigue-degraded stiffness) across repeated handling — a use the paper does not explore.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The manipulation of viscoelastic materials, which exhibit complex time-dependent stress-strain behaviors combining elastic recovery and viscous flow, presents a significant challenge in robotics. Such materials are prevalent in applications like industrial assembly (e.g., workpiece installation), surgical robotics (e.g., organ retraction), and domestic automation (e.g., cloth folding). Current robotic strategies often encounter difficulties in achieving desired deformations and maintaining stable contact forces due to inadequate handling of spatiotemporal coupling effects, including stress relaxation, strain-rate damping, and geometric nonlinearity.

Existing viscoelastic models, such as the Kelvin–Voigt and Maxwell models, typically decouple elastic and viscous behaviors and are primarily designed for one-dimensional (1D) scenarios. While hybrid models like Burgers models attempt to unify these effects, they generally do not resolve the complexities of 3D spatiotemporal stress-strain coupling during dynamic robotic manipulation. This limitation hinders their applicability in real-world, complex manipulation tasks.

Furthermore, parameter identification methods for viscoelastic materials frequently rely on offline calibration, which restricts their utility in dynamic robotic manipulation. Traditional approaches, including frequency-domain techniques and optimization-based methods, are often computationally intensive or require specific loading conditions, preventing real-time adaptation. Black-box methods, such as neural networks, often demand extensive training datasets and can lack interpretability.

In the domain of force and deformation control for 3D soft objects, challenges arise from the dynamic couplings between deformations, viscoelastic properties, and contact forces. Finite element methods (FEM), physics simulations, and machine learning (ML) are effective for offline modeling and design but often suffer from high computational demands, rendering them impractical for real-time control in time-critical applications. Traditional admittance control strategies, while offering rapid response, tend to oversimplify or localize the working region, making them less effective in complex 3D spatial uncertain regimes.

This research addresses these gaps by proposing a framework that coordinates deformation shaping with compliant interaction forces through closed-loop control. It introduces a unified 3D continuum model that integrates Kelvin–Voigt and Maxwell dynamics, an observer-based real-time parameter identification method, and a physics-guided admittance control framework. This approach aims to provide adaptive, safe, and efficient manipulation of viscoelastic materials by explicitly accounting for their 3D spatiotemporal characteristics, thereby advancing the capabilities of compliant robotic interactions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a comprehensive framework, named CATCH-FORM-3D, for achieving precise contact force control and accurate surface deformation regulation during the manipulation of 3D viscoelastic objects. This overarching goal is motivated by several identified limitations and challenges in the current state of robotic manipulation:

1. **Complexity of Viscoelastic Materials:** Viscoelastic materials exhibit hybrid mechanical behaviors, combining elastic recovery and viscous flow, which makes their precise manipulation challenging for robots. There is a need for control strategies that can effectively manage these complex, spatiotemporal coupled effects, including stress relaxation, strain-rate damping, and geometric nonlinearity, which are often insufficiently addressed by existing robotic approaches.
2. **Limitations of Existing Material Models:** Current 1D viscoelastic models, such as Kelvin–Voigt and Maxwell, often decouple elastic and viscous behaviors or oversimplify transient deformations. While some hybrid models exist, they generally fail to accurately capture the intricate 3D spatiotemporal stress-strain coupling critical for robotic manipulation. The motivation is to develop a unified 3D continuum model that explicitly governs the interplay of energy storage (stiffness), viscous dissipation (damping), and stress redistribution (diffusion).
3. **Inefficiency of Offline Parameter Identification:** Existing parameter identification methods for viscoelastic materials largely rely on offline calibration, which restricts their real-time applicability in dynamic robotic tasks. These methods can be time-consuming, require specific testing conditions, or demand extensive datasets, hindering adaptive manipulation. The motivation is to develop a real-time, interpretable, and data-driven parameter identification scheme embedded within the physical model.
4. **Challenges in Real-time Force-Deformation Control:** Controlling both contact force and deformation in 3D soft objects is complex due to dynamic couplings. While methods like finite element analysis, physics simulations, and machine learning offer high fidelity, their computational demands typically make them unsuitable for real-time dynamic control in time-critical applications. Traditional admittance control strategies often simplify the problem, struggling in 3D spatial uncertain regimes. This motivates the development of a physics-guided compliance modulation and a control architecture that can achieve rapid response and adaptation without extensive computational overhead.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of the CATCH-FORM-3D framework are:

- To establish a unified 3D viscoelastic continuum model that integrates Kelvin–Voigt and Maxwell dynamics through governing partial differential equations (PDEs), capturing energy storage, viscous dissipation, and stress redistribution across multiple parameter fields.
- To design an observer-based parameter identification method that leverages this interpretable physical model to dynamically regress historical actuation forces and visual-tactile signals for real-time estimation of stiffness, damping, and diffusion coefficients.
- To develop a physics-guided compliance modulation strategy for robotic manipulation that effectively interplays deformation modulation with compliant force regulation via viscoelastic dynamics.
- To propose a boundary control strategy that synthesizes Dirichlet boundary conditions using analytical geometric templates to ensure globally convergent strain fields and stabilize contact forces even under large deformations.
- To construct an inner-outer admittance control architecture that achieves low-error force profiles and precise geometry transformations in dynamic tasks (e.g., industrial shaping, organ retraction), balancing computational efficiency with physical fidelity.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The CATCH-FORM-3D framework is constructed around a multi-layered approach encompassing material modeling, real-time parameter identification, and a dual-loop control architecture for force and deformation regulation.

**A. Unified 3D Viscoelastic Continuum Model**
The foundational element is a unified 3D viscoelastic continuum model, which extends classical 1D Kelvin–Voigt and Maxwell models. The 1D Kelvin–Voigt model, represented by `f(t) = kϕ(t) + b˙ϕ(t)`, captures elastic and damping effects, while the Maxwell model, `f(t) = b˙ϕ(t) - α˙f(t)`, accounts for stress relaxation. These are generalized into a Burgers model: `f(t) = kϕ(t) + β˙ϕ(t) - γ˙f(t)`.

To capture 3D spatiotemporal dynamics, the paper proposes a partial differential equation (PDE):
`˙ϕ(t, x, y, z) = ϵ∆ϕ(t, x, y, z) + a_1 f(t, x, y, z) + a_2 ˙f(t, x, y, z) + λϕ(t, x, y, z)`
where:
- `ϕ` is the deformation scalar field, representing displacement.
- `f` is a scalar field describing the contact force's effect projected onto the deformation gradient `∇ϕ`.
- `∆ϕ` is the Laplace operator, representing spatial variation and diffusion.
- `ϵ > 0`, `a_1 = β⁻¹`, `a_2 = β⁻¹γ`, `λ = -β⁻¹k` are parameters characterizing diffusion, stiffness, damping, and stress redistribution, respectively.
This formulation simplifies contact forces and deformations to scalar fields, assuming small contact areas or flat contact surfaces, with shear forces being considered negligible or compensated. Visual-tactile sensors provide the necessary 3D surface position and force data. The model operates within a cylindrical domain `Ω`, with Dirichlet boundary conditions `ϕ(x, y, z) = 0` on `∂Ω \ {x = δ}` and actuation applied at `x = δ`.

**B. Observer-Based Parameter Identification**
For real-time estimation of the mechanical parameters (`ϵ`, `a_1`, `a_2`, `λ`), an adaptive observer is designed based on the proposed PDE. The observer equation for the estimated deformation `ˆϕ` is:
`˙ˆϕ = ˆϵ∆ϕ + ˆa_1 f + ˆa_2 ˙f + ˆλϕ + L(ϕ − ˆϕ)`
where `ˆϵ`, `ˆa_1`, `ˆa_2`, `ˆλ` are the estimated parameters, and `L` is a feedback gain. The parameter update laws are proportional to the observer error (`ϕ − ˆϕ`) and a regressor signal `ψ`:
`˙ˆϵ = -Kψ_1(ϕ − ˆϕ)`, `˙ˆa_1 = -Kψ_2(ϕ − ˆϕ)`, `˙ˆa_2 = -Kψ_3(ϕ − ˆϕ)`, `˙ˆλ = -Kψ_4(ϕ − ˆϕ)`
The regressor signal `ψ` is generated by filtering the input signals `Ψ = [∆ϕ, f, ˙f, ϕ]ᵀ` to ensure sufficient information content. To guarantee convergence of parameter estimates, a "historical data replay" strategy is employed. This involves combining current and historical actuation force inputs and visual-tactile sensor outputs to ensure the "persistent excitation condition" is met, meaning the autocorrelation matrix `ψψᵀ` is full rank over a time interval, which aids in resolving parameter ambiguities and provides robustness against noise.

**C. Physics-Guided Deformation Planning (Outer Loop)**
A dual-loop control architecture is implemented. The outer loop manages compliant interaction through a physics-guided admittance control law. After parameter identification, the ideal contact force and deformation are designed using the established PDE. For tracking errors `f_e` (force) and `ϕ_e` (deformation), an admittance control law is formulated:
`a_1 f_e + a_2 ˙f_e = λ_1 ϕ_e + λ_2 ˙ϕ_e`
This law relates the force errors to the desired deformation error response. The parameters `λ_1` and `λ_2` are chosen to ensure passivity and stability of the admittance loop, specifically requiring `λ_1 > 0` and `λ_2 > 0`. This allows the reference deformation `ϕ_d` to be modulated online in response to external interactions, bypassing computationally intensive simulations or machine learning. The term `a_2˙f_e` explicitly incorporates sensitivity to the derivative of force error, addressing the Maxwell effect.

**D. Boundary Control by Geometric Templates (Inner Loop)**
The inner loop focuses on rapidly tracking the dynamically updated reference deformation `ϕ_d`. By applying the outer-loop admittance control law, the PDE for the tracking error `ϕ_e` is transformed into a reaction-diffusion PDE:
`˙ϕ_e = ϵ*∆ϕ_e + λ*ϕ_e`
where `ϵ* = (1 - λ_2)⁻¹ϵ` and `λ* = (1 - λ_2)⁻¹(λ + λ_1)`. The goal is to drive `ϕ_e` to zero exponentially. This is achieved through a boundary control strategy where the Dirichlet boundary condition at the actuation surface (`x = δ`) is defined as a control input `U_e(t, y, z)`.
The control law `U_e(t, y, z)` is formulated as a weighted integral of the current deformation error `ϕ_e(ξ, y, z)` over the object's depth `[0, δ]`, involving a first-order modified Bessel function `I_1`. This design ensures that the deformation tracking error `ϕ_e` exponentially converges to zero. The paper uses a nonlinear transformation to show that an equivalent system `w` converges to zero, thereby proving the stability of `ϕ_e`. The proposed control strategy is stated to be extensible to more general geometric settings and multiple robotic end-effectors due to the linearity of the reaction-diffusion PDE, allowing for superposition of solutions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents CATCH-FORM-3D, a framework designed for precise manipulation of viscoelastic materials. The main findings and results are derived from both the theoretical development and experimental validation.

**A. Framework Development:**
- **Unified 3D Viscoelastic Model:** A 3D spatiotemporal partial differential equation (PDE) was successfully proposed, integrating the Kelvin–Voigt (stiffness-damping) and Maxwell (diffusion) effects. This model captures the material’s viscoelastic behavior across three dimensions and time, bridging classical distributed parameter models with robotic manipulation requirements.
- **Real-time Parameter Identification:** A PDE-driven observer was developed and validated for real-time estimation of key mechanical parameters (stiffness `a_1`, damping `a_2`, diffusion `ϵ`, and stress redistribution `λ` coefficients). This observer fuses visual-tactile sensor data with historical experimental force inputs to generate robust regressor signals, enabling dynamic adaptation and estimation of material properties.
- **Dual-Loop Control Architecture:** A robust inner-outer loop control structure was established. The outer loop implemented a novel admittance control law using a proportional-derivative (PD) feedback based on contact force measurements to adaptively update reference deformation. The inner loop formulated a reaction-diffusion PDE for deformation tracking error, which was then exponentially stabilized by enforcing Dirichlet boundary conditions through analytical geometric configurations. This architecture facilitates effective deformation regulation in dynamic contact environments.

**B. Experimental Validation:**
The CATCH-FORM-3D framework was experimentally validated using a PaXini robotic hand (DexH13) mounted on a Realman RM arm, equipped with high-precision tactile arrays (PX6AX-GEN2-DP-M2826) with a spatial resolution of 2.0–2.5 mm and a force resolution of 0.01 N, sampling at 60Hz. Experiments were conducted on a range of materials, including silicone (40A, 60A), nano-foam, ceramic plate, glass tube, and thin porcelain, covering elastic, viscoelastic, and rigid properties.

- **Force Tracking Performance:**
 - The framework demonstrated stable force tracking across all tested materials under dynamic target force fields.
 - Total force tracking errors consistently decreased from initial contact to a stable state.
 - For viscoelastic materials: nano-foam showed errors reducing from 0.43 ± 0.08 N to 0.35 ± 0.07 N; silicone 40A maintained 0.45 ± 0.10 N; silicone 60A maintained 0.55 ± 0.12 N.
 - For rigid materials: ceramic plates achieved 0.20 ± 0.04 N; glass tubes achieved 0.15 ± 0.03 N.
 - Overall, force control errors remained below ±5% deviation across all material types, demonstrating precise force maintenance.

- **Deformation Control Precision:**
 - The tactile sensor array provided continuous 3D surface topography data, enabling precise quantification of contact morphology evolution.
 - A dual-validation index, combining spatial distribution accuracy and absolute area size (weighted sum `ϵ_total = α·ϵ_dist + β·ϵ_area` with `α=0.4, β=0.6`), was used to assess deformation errors.
 - Sub-millimeter deformation accuracy was achieved.
 - For viscoelastic materials: nano-foam deformation tracking error gradually improved from 0.88 ± 0.16 to 0.85 ± 0.16; silicone 40A maintained 0.82 ± 0.15; silicone 60A maintained 0.78 ± 0.14.
 - For rigid materials: ceramic plates maintained 0.71 ± 0.13; glass tubes achieved 0.68 ± 0.12.
 - The controller demonstrated precise tracking of complex deformation profiles, maintaining synchronization with reference trajectories even during rapid transitions.
 - Statistical analysis indicated that the standard deviations of temporal error distributions were less than 5.0% of the target values under all experimental conditions.

These results indicate that the CATCH-FORM-3D framework effectively achieves both precise contact force maintenance and accurate deformation regulation in dynamic environments with diverse viscoelastic materials.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CATCH-FORM-3D framework presents several significant advancements in the field of robotic manipulation of deformable objects, with broad potential impacts across various industries and applications.

**A. Advancing Compliant Robotic Interactions:**
The development of a unified 3D viscoelastic continuum model directly addresses the limitations of prior 1D models that often decouple elastic and viscous behaviors. By integrating Kelvin–Voigt and Maxwell dynamics into a single PDE, the framework provides a more comprehensive and accurate representation of material behavior in 3D space and time. This enhanced modeling capability allows for a deeper understanding and more precise control of complex spatiotemporal stress-strain coupling effects, which are critical for compliant interactions with soft and viscoelastic objects.

**B. Real-time Adaptability and Efficiency:**
The observer-based parameter identification method is a notable contribution, enabling real-time estimation of crucial mechanical parameters (stiffness, damping, diffusion coefficients). This real-time adaptation capability, facilitated by the fusion of visual-tactile sensor data and historical force inputs, overcomes the computational burdens and offline calibration requirements of traditional methods. Consequently, robots can dynamically adjust their manipulation strategies based on changing material properties or environmental conditions, which is essential for unpredictable and dynamic operational settings. The dual-loop control architecture, by avoiding time-consuming simulations, machine learning, or black-box optimization, offers a balance between computational efficiency and physical fidelity, leading to more practical and deployable robotic solutions.

**C. Enhanced Precision and Stability:**
Experimental validation demonstrated sub-millimeter deformation accuracy and stable force tracking with a deviation of ±5%. These quantitative results signify a high level of precision and robustness in controlling robot-object interactions. The ability to achieve such accurate control over both deformation and force profiles simultaneously is critical for tasks requiring fine motor skills and gentle handling, which are often challenging for conventional robotic systems. The exponential stabilization of deformation tracking errors through boundary control contributes to the system's reliability and predictability.

**D. Broad Applicability and Industry Impact:**
The CATCH-FORM-3D framework has potential for promotion to a wide range of application scenarios:
- **Industrial Manufacturing and Assembly:** Precision tasks involving the manipulation of metal sheets, polymers, or composite materials can benefit from compliant force and deformation control, improving product quality and reducing material damage.
- **Polymer Shaping and Molding:** The precise regulation of deformation can be instrumental in the automated shaping of polymers, enabling consistent and complex geometries.
- **Medical Procedures and Surgical Robotics:** The framework can enhance surgical robots' ability to interact with delicate biological tissues and organs, providing compliant retraction, palpation, and manipulation with reduced risk of damage. This is particularly relevant for minimally invasive surgery where precise force and deformation control are paramount.
- **Household Service and Domestic Automation:** Tasks such as folding laundry, handling fragile items, or interacting with soft furniture can be performed with greater adaptability and safety, contributing to the development of more capable and user-friendly domestic robots.

**E. Foundation for Future Research:**
The methodology, particularly the unified PDE model and the PDE-based boundary control, provides a theoretical and practical foundation for future research in adaptive control for distributed parameter systems in robotics. It opens avenues for exploring more complex material behaviors, anisotropic properties, and scenarios involving multiple robotic manipulators interacting with large, deformable objects, as suggested by the principle of superposition mentioned in the paper.

In summary, CATCH-FORM-3D contributes to the field by offering a comprehensive, efficient, and robust solution for compliant robotic manipulation of 3D viscoelastic objects, addressing critical limitations in modeling, parameter estimation, and real-time control.
