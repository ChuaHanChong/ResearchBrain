# VideoPhy: Evaluating Physical Commonsense for Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> The VIDEOPHY benchmark systematically evaluates the physical plausibility of videos generated by text-to-video (T2V) models, revealing that current state-of-the-art models exhibit severe deficiencies in physical commonsense, with the best model achieving less than 40% combined semantic and physical accuracy. The study also introduces a finetuned automatic evaluator, VIDEOCON-PHYSICS, to scale this assessment.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art text-to-video (T2V) models lack specific evaluation benchmarks for their adherence to physical laws.
- Existing T2V evaluation metrics primarily focus on visual quality and semantic alignment, neglecting fundamental physical plausibility.
- Directly comparing generated videos to traditional physics simulations is challenging due to difficulties in extracting 3D geometry and tuning material parameters.

## Method

- A new benchmark, **VIDEOPHY**, was created with 688 human-verified captions describing solid and fluid interactions, each annotated for simulation difficulty by physics experts.
- 11,330 videos were generated across twelve state-of-the-art T2V models and rigorously evaluated by qualified human annotators for semantic adherence and physical commonsense.
- VIDEOCON-PHYSICS, an automatic evaluator, was developed by finetuning a video-text language model on human annotations to provide scalable assessment of physical plausibility.

## Results

- The top-performing T2V model, CogVideoX-5B, achieved only 39.6% combined semantic adherence and physical commonsense accuracy, with most other models scoring below 20%.
- Performance for both semantic adherence and physical commonsense consistently decreased as the perceived complexity of the captions increased, highlighting a struggle with intricate physics.
- `VIDEOCON-PHYSICS` achieved an 82% ROC-AUC for semantic adherence and 73% for physical commonsense, significantly outperforming zero-shot baselines like GPT-4Vision and Gemini-Pro-Vision-1.5.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current T2V models demonstrate a severe lack of physical commonsense, consistently performing poorly on physically challenging interactions like solid-solid dynamics.
> - Achieving high semantic adherence in video generation does not guarantee physical plausibility, indicating a distinct evaluation dimension that models struggle with.
> - The `VIDEOCON-PHYSICS` auto-evaluator effectively assesses physical commonsense, demonstrating strong agreement with human judgments and generalizing well to unseen prompts and generative models.
