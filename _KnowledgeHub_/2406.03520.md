# VideoPhy: Evaluating Physical Commonsense for Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> The VIDEOPHY benchmark systematically evaluates the physical plausibility of videos generated by text-to-video (T2V) models, revealing that current state-of-the-art models exhibit severe deficiencies in physical commonsense, with the best model achieving less than 40% combined semantic and physical accuracy. The study also introduces a finetuned automatic evaluator, VIDEOCON-PHYSICS, to scale this assessment.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art text-to-video (T2V) models lack specific evaluation benchmarks for their adherence to physical laws.
- Existing T2V evaluation metrics primarily focus on visual quality and semantic alignment, neglecting fundamental physical plausibility.
- Directly comparing generated videos to traditional physics simulations is challenging due to difficulties in extracting 3D geometry and tuning material parameters.

## Method

- A new benchmark, **VIDEOPHY**, was created with 688 human-verified captions describing solid and fluid interactions, each annotated for simulation difficulty by physics experts.
- 11,330 videos were generated across twelve state-of-the-art T2V models and rigorously evaluated by qualified human annotators for semantic adherence and physical commonsense.
- VIDEOCON-PHYSICS, an automatic evaluator, was developed by finetuning a video-text language model on human annotations to provide scalable assessment of physical plausibility.

## Results

- The top-performing T2V model, CogVideoX-5B, achieved only 39.6% combined semantic adherence and physical commonsense accuracy, with most other models scoring below 20%.
- Performance for both semantic adherence and physical commonsense consistently decreased as the perceived complexity of the captions increased, highlighting a struggle with intricate physics.
- `VIDEOCON-PHYSICS` achieved an 82% ROC-AUC for semantic adherence and 73% for physical commonsense, significantly outperforming zero-shot baselines like GPT-4Vision and Gemini-Pro-Vision-1.5.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current T2V models demonstrate a severe lack of physical commonsense, consistently performing poorly on physically challenging interactions like solid-solid dynamics.
> - Achieving high semantic adherence in video generation does not guarantee physical plausibility, indicating a distinct evaluation dimension that models struggle with.
> - The `VIDEOCON-PHYSICS` auto-evaluator effectively assesses physical commonsense, demonstrating strong agreement with human judgments and generalizing well to unseen prompts and generative models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself at the critical intersection of rapid advancements in text-to-video (T2V) generative AI and the growing need for robust, comprehensive evaluation metrics.

**Current Landscape of T2V Models:** The introduction acknowledges the "recent advances in internet-scale video data pretraining" that have propelled T2V models like Sora, Lumiere, Dream Machine, and others to generate "high-quality videos across a broad range of visual concepts, synthesize realistic motions and render complex objects." These models are increasingly perceived as having "the potential to become general-purpose simulators of the physical world." This ambition, particularly highlighted by OpenAI's claims regarding Sora, underscores the drive to imbue AI with an understanding of physical laws.

**Identified Gap in Evaluation:** Despite these impressive capabilities, the authors point out a significant lacuna: "it is unclear how far we are from this goal with the existing text-to-video generative models." While existing benchmarks like VBench and EvalCrafter assess various qualities such as visual fidelity, motion smoothness, temporal consistency, and text-video alignment, they "do not specifically address the generated videos’ adherence to physical laws." This is the core gap VIDEOPHY aims to fill.

**Relevance to Intuitive Physics and World Simulators:** The paper explicitly connects its work to the concept of "intuitive physics," a human ability to understand object interactions without formal education. It argues that for T2V models to truly act as "world simulators" or enable agents to "act, plan, and solve goals in the real world" (as suggested by works like Genie), they must internalize this intuitive physical understanding. Without it, generated videos might be visually compelling but physically nonsensical, limiting their utility in real-world applications.

**Challenges in Physical Simulation-Based Evaluation:** The authors thoughtfully address why direct comparison with traditional physics simulations is "non-trivial." Challenges include the difficulty of generating accurate 3D geometries from single-view inputs, the need for expert-level tuning of material parameters, and the complexities of lighting and rendering. Crucially, they note that "physical simulations are not equivalent to ground truth" but rather approximations. This justification underscores the necessity of a human-centric evaluation approach for "intuitive physics."

**Contribution to Evaluation Methodology:** By introducing "physical commonsense" as a distinct and measurable evaluation criterion, VIDEOPHY pioneers a new dimension in assessing generative AI. It shifts the focus beyond purely aesthetic or semantic qualities to fundamental mechanistic understanding. Furthermore, the development of `VIDEOCON-PHYSICS` as an automatic evaluator addresses the scalability issues inherent in human-based assessments, aiming to provide a practical tool for the rapid development cycle of T2V models. In summary, this work pushes the frontier of T2V evaluation by emphasizing physical intelligence, a crucial step towards truly intelligent and reliable generative AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **systematically evaluate the extent to which text-to-video (T2V) generative models adhere to physical commonsense in their generated content.** This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

- **Assessing the "World Simulator" Ambition:** Recent breakthroughs in T2V models have led to claims and aspirations that these models could serve as "general-purpose simulators of the physical world." The core motivation is to rigorously test this hypothesis and quantify how far current models are from this ambitious goal.
- **Filling an Evaluation Gap:** Existing comprehensive benchmarks for T2V models (e.g., VBench, EvalCrafter) primarily focus on visual quality, motion smoothness, background consistency, and semantic alignment to text prompts. However, they largely neglect the fundamental aspect of physical plausibility. The authors are motivated to bridge this critical gap, recognizing that a video can be semantically accurate and visually stunning yet still violate basic laws of physics.
- **The Importance of "Intuitive Physics" for AI:** Humans develop an intuitive understanding of how objects interact in the physical world without formal physics education. This "intuitive physics" is essential for real-world reasoning and action. For AI agents to effectively interact with and plan within physical environments (e.g., in robotics or virtual simulations), they must possess a similar understanding. The research is motivated by the need to determine if current generative models are learning this crucial cognitive capacity.
- **Lack of Reliable Physical Evaluation Methods:** The paper highlights the inherent difficulties in directly comparing generated videos with precise physical simulations (due to issues like 3D geometry extraction, material parameter tuning, and rendering complexities). This motivated the development of a human-centric evaluation approach, focusing on "intuitive physics" rather than exact dynamical equations, and subsequently an automated tool to scale this evaluation.
- **Driving Future Research and Development:** By exposing the severe deficiencies of current T2V models in physical commonsense, the authors aim to provide a clear benchmark and actionable insights. This motivation is to guide future research and development efforts, encouraging model builders to focus on enhancing physical intelligence in their generative architectures.
- **Scalable and Cost-Effective Evaluation:** Recognizing the expense and time involved in human evaluation, a secondary but important motivation is to develop an automatic evaluator (`VIDEOCON-PHYSICS`). This tool is intended to offer a reliable, cheaper, and scalable alternative for assessing physical commonsense and semantic adherence, thus democratizing access to this crucial benchmark for model developers.

In essence, the paper is motivated by the desire to push T2V research beyond mere visual synthesis towards genuinely intelligent systems that comprehend and realistically simulate the physical world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in VIDEOPHY is multi-faceted, encompassing a rigorous dataset curation process, extensive video generation from diverse models, human-based evaluation, and the development of an automatic evaluator.

#### 3.1. VIDEOPHY Dataset Curation

The dataset, named VIDEOPHY, is designed to evaluate physical commonsense by focusing on the behavior and dynamics of solids and fluids in real-world interactions. Its curation follows a three-stage pipeline:

1. **LLM-Generated Captions (Stage 1):**
 - **Tool:** GPT-4 was prompted to generate candidate captions.
 - **Focus Areas:** Captions depict interactions between diverse states of matter: solid-solid, solid-fluid, and fluid-fluid.
 - **Specifics:** 500 captions for solid-solid and solid-fluid interactions each, and 200 for fluid-fluid interactions.
 - **Guidelines:** Prompts for GPT-4 emphasized realistic scenes, common materials, avoidance of static scenes, exclusion of non-physical actions (e.g., "celebrating"), and specific interaction types (contact/friction for solids, mixing/laying for fluids).

2. **Human Verification (Stage 2):**
 - **Process:** Authors manually reviewed the LLM-generated captions.
 - **Criteria:** Captions had to be clear, understandable, avoid excessive complexity (overly varied objects or intricate dynamics), and accurately reflect the intended interaction categories.
 - **Outcome:** 688 high-quality, human-verified captions (289 solid-solid, 291 solid-fluid, 108 fluid-fluid). The authors prioritized data quality over quantity, drawing parallels to other high-quality, smaller benchmarks.

3. **Difficulty Annotation (Stage 3):**
 - **Process:** Two experienced graphics researchers (senior Ph.D. students in physics-based simulation) independently classified each caption as "easy" or "hard."
 - **Basis for Difficulty:** Perceived complexity in simulating the objects and motions using state-of-the-art physics engines. Factors considered included material type (e.g., deformable vs. rigid bodies), velocity (slower is easier), and numerical stability challenges.
 - **Outcome:** 366 "easy" and 322 "hard" captions, with disagreements resolved through discussion.

#### 3.2. Video Generation

- **Models Tested:** Videos were generated from a diverse set of twelve state-of-the-art T2V models, including both open-source models (e.g., CogVideoX-2B/5B, OpenSora, VideoCrafter2, LaVIE, SVD-T2I2V, ZeroScope) and closed-source models (e.g., Lumiere-T2V/T2I2V, Dream Machine, Pika, Gen-2).
- **Scale:** In total, 11,330 videos were generated corresponding to the 688 captions.

#### 3.3. Human Evaluation

- **Metrics:** Two binary metrics (0/1) were used:
 - **Semantic Adherence (SA):** Assesses if the video accurately depicts the actions, events, and entities described in the text caption.
 - **Physical Commonsense (PC):** Evaluates if the depicted actions and object states follow intuitive physical laws, independent of semantic adherence.
- **Annotators:** 14 qualified Amazon Mechanical Turk (AMT) workers, who had studied high-school physics and passed a qualification test, were compensated at $18/hour.
- **Procedure:** Annotators were shown a caption and the corresponding generated video (without model information) and asked to provide independent SA and PC scores. Clear instructions and examples were provided. For the test set, three annotators judged each video, and majority voting determined the final score. For the training set of the auto-evaluator, one annotator scored each video.
- **Inter-annotator Agreement:** 75% for SA, 70% for PC, indicating PC judgment is more subjective.

#### 3.4. Automatic Evaluation: VIDEOCON-PHYSICS

- **Motivation:** To address the scalability and cost of human evaluation.
- **Base Model:** `VIDEOCON` (a 7B parameter open video-text language model trained for robust semantic adherence on real videos).
- **Finetuning:** `VIDEOCON` was finetuned on the generated videos and human annotations collected as part of VIDEOPHY's training split (344 prompts) for both SA and PC tasks. Low-rank adaptation (LoRA) was applied to attention blocks.
- **Evaluation:** ROC-AUC was used to measure the agreement between the model's judgments and human annotations on unseen prompts and unseen generative models.
- **Baselines:** Performance was compared against zero-shot `VIDEOCON`, GPT-4Vision (multi-image reasoning), and Gemini-Pro-Vision-1.5 (native video input).
- **Templates:** Specific multimodal templates (text prompts) were designed to query the model for SA and PC, aiming for "Yes/No" responses.

This comprehensive approach ensures a robust dataset, a wide-ranging evaluation of current T2V capabilities, and the development of a practical tool for future research.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study reveals significant insights into the current capabilities and limitations of text-to-video generative models regarding physical commonsense.

- **Overall Deficiency in Physical Commonsense:** The most striking finding is the severe lack of physical commonsense in existing T2V models. The best-performing model, CogVideoX-5B, achieved a joint performance score (Semantic Adherence (SA)=1 and Physical Commonsense (PC)=1) of only **39.6%**. Most other models scored below 20%. This definitively shows that current models are "far from accurately simulating the physical world."

- **CogVideoX-5B Leads the Pack:** CogVideoX-5B consistently outperformed other models, achieving 53% accuracy for physical commonsense alone. Its superior performance is attributed to high-quality data curation, including detailed captions and filtering of low-motion/poor-quality videos. The scaling effect is also observed, with CogVideoX-5B outperforming its 2B parameter counterpart (34.1% PC).

- **Discrepancy Between Semantic Adherence and Physical Commonsense:** Several models demonstrated a notable gap between their ability to follow a text caption and their capacity for physical plausibility. For instance, Dream Machine achieved a high semantic adherence score (61.9%) but a poor physical commonsense score (21.8%). This highlights that optimizing for semantic alignment does not automatically lead to an understanding of physical laws.

- **Open-Source vs. Closed Models:** While CogVideoX-5B (an open model) was the overall best, Pika (a closed model) was the best among closed models with 19.7% joint SA, PC performance. OpenSora, an open-source replication attempt of Sora, performed the worst, indicating substantial room for improvement in open-source implementations.

- **Varying Performance Across States of Matter:** Models performed particularly poorly on **solid-solid interactions** (e.g., "bottle topples off the table," "ball bouncing on the floor"). CogVideoX-5B, despite being the best overall, only achieved 24.4% joint SA, PC for this category. Conversely, Pika performed relatively better in captions depicting **fluid-fluid interactions** (e.g., "rain splashing on a pond"). This points to specific areas where model development needs focused attention.

- **Impact of Task Complexity:** Both semantic adherence and physical commonsense performance consistently **decreased as the perceived complexity of the captions increased**. Captions deemed "hard" by graphics simulation experts proved harder for T2V models to generate accurately and physically plausibly. This suggests a direct correlation between the complexity of underlying physics and the generative models' ability to represent it.

- **Common Failure Modes (Qualitative Analysis):** Qualitative analysis revealed recurring physical commonsense violations, including:
 - **Conservation of Mass:** Object volume or texture inconsistent over time (e.g., milk level not rising when poured, objects appearing/disappearing).
 - **Newton's Laws of Motion:** Objects changing velocity without external force (First Law), or violating momentum conservation (Second Law, e.g., static water droplets, unnatural splashes).
 - **Constitutive Laws:** Solids deforming unnaturally (e.g., rigid wood bending, metal spoon deforming) or fluids exhibiting unnatural flow (e.g., static glue-like lemon juice).
 - **Non-physical Penetration:** Objects unnaturally passing through each other.
 - Models often struggled with correctly identifying individual objects and comprehending their material properties.

- **Effectiveness of VIDEOCON-PHYSICS (Auto-Evaluator):**
 - **Superior Performance:** `VIDEOCON-PHYSICS` significantly outperformed all baselines (zero-shot `VIDEOCON`, GPT-4Vision, Gemini-Pro-Vision-1.5) in ROC-AUC for both SA and PC, demonstrating its effectiveness in judging generated videos. It achieved 82% ROC-AUC for SA and 73% for PC, compared to Gemini-Pro-Vision-1.5's 73% for SA and 58% for PC.
 - **Generalization:** The auto-evaluator successfully generalized to *unseen prompts* and, critically, to *unseen generative models*, making it a robust tool for evaluating future T2V models.
 - **Reliable Leaderboard Tracking:** The automatic leaderboard generated by `VIDEOCON-PHYSICS` closely matched the human-annotated leaderboard, validating its reliability.

- **Finetuning T2V Models with VIDEOPHY (Exploratory):** Initial attempts to finetune Lumiere-T2I2V using physically plausible video-caption pairs from VIDEOPHY's training set resulted in a *decrease* in semantic adherence while physical commonsense remained largely unchanged. This suggests that naive finetuning with this specific dataset (possibly due to limited samples, mixed-source videos, or optimization challenges) is not straightforward for immediately improving physical commonsense.

In conclusion, the findings firmly establish that current T2V models, despite their photorealistic capabilities, lack a fundamental understanding of physical commonsense, presenting a clear and challenging research direction for the AI community.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VIDEOPHY research holds significant implications for the development and evaluation of generative AI, particularly in the realm of text-to-video synthesis.

- **Pioneering a New Evaluation Paradigm:** The most significant contribution is the introduction of "physical commonsense" as a crucial, previously overlooked, metric for evaluating T2V models. This moves the benchmark beyond purely aesthetic quality, visual consistency, or semantic alignment, and closer to assessing true "intelligence" in physical understanding. It establishes a new standard for what it means for a video generative model to be truly capable.

- **Crucial for "World Simulators" and Embodied AI:** By rigorously demonstrating the severe lack of physical commonsense, the paper highlights a fundamental hurdle for T2V models aspiring to be "general-purpose world simulators." For AI agents in robotics, autonomous systems, or virtual environments, a robust understanding of physics is non-negotiable for realistic interaction, planning, and problem-solving. VIDEOPHY provides the necessary tools to measure progress towards this critical goal.

- **Driving Future Research Directions:** The findings provide clear, actionable insights for researchers. The identified weaknesses, particularly in solid-solid interactions and complex physical dynamics, will likely steer future model development efforts. Researchers can now specifically target these areas, perhaps by integrating physics priors, incorporating simulation-based training data, or developing architectures that explicitly model material properties and dynamics.

- **Scalable and Cost-Effective Benchmarking:** The development of `VIDEOCON-PHYSICS` is highly impactful. Manual human evaluation is expensive, time-consuming, and difficult to scale, especially as T2V models evolve rapidly. `VIDEOCON-PHYSICS` offers a reliable, automatic, and generalizable solution, enabling researchers and developers to quickly and frequently benchmark their models, accelerate iteration cycles, and track progress without incurring prohibitive costs. This democratizes access to robust evaluation.

- **Diagnostic Tool for Model Failures:** The qualitative analysis and classification of common failure modes (e.g., violations of conservation of mass, Newton's laws, constitutive laws) offer a diagnostic framework for understanding *why* models fail. This detailed insight is invaluable for designing targeted improvements in model architectures, training data, or optimization strategies.

- **Foundation for Multimodal AI Development:** The paper contributes to the broader field of multimodal AI by demonstrating that even advanced vision-language models like Gemini-Pro-Vision-1.5 struggle significantly with physical commonsense reasoning. This underscores that true multimodal intelligence requires more than just high-level semantic understanding; it demands a deeper, grounded comprehension of the physical world.

- **Ethical Considerations and Responsible AI:** While acknowledging its own limitations (e.g., cultural biases in human annotations), the paper implicitly encourages a more responsible approach to generative AI. Ensuring physical plausibility is not only about realism but also about safety and predictability, especially if these models are to inform real-world applications. Understanding biases in physical generation could be a future ethical imperative.

In summary, VIDEOPHY is a cornerstone work that redirects the focus of text-to-video research towards a more profound understanding of the physical world. By providing a novel benchmark and a scalable evaluation tool, it lays the groundwork for developing the next generation of generative models that are not just visually impressive but also physically intelligent.
