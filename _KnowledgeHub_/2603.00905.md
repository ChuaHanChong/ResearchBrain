# pySpatial: Generating 3D Visual Programs for Zero-Shot Spatial Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The pySpatial framework equips Multi-modal Large Language Models with explicit and robust 3D spatial reasoning by generating executable Python programs that orchestrate specialized 3D vision tools. This approach achieved 58.56% accuracy on the MINDCUBE benchmark, outperforming GPT-4.1-mini by 12.94%, and successfully guided real-world robot navigation.

## Problem

- Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) consistently struggle with robust 3D spatial reasoning, especially in multi-view settings, limiting their application in robotics and embodied AI.
- Current MLLM approaches often rely on implicit 'imagination' or 2D cognitive maps, which lack explicit geometric grounding and robustness for complex 3D tasks.
- Sparse and costly explicit 3D supervision in MLLM training data hinders the development of reliable language-to-3D correspondences.

## Method

- Developed ==pySpatial==, a visual programming framework that enables MLLMs to generate executable Python programs, orchestrating a defined ==API of specialized spatial tools==.
- The API includes functions for ==3D scene reconstruction== from 2D image sequences, natural language descriptions of camera motion, novel view synthesis, and camera manipulation (e.g., rotate, move).
- Implemented a multi-stage process where an MLLM generates a program, an interpreter executes it to produce explicit intermediate outputs (text or images), and the MLLM then integrates these outputs with the original query to form the final response.

## Results

- Achieved an overall accuracy of **58.56%** on the MINDCUBE benchmark, outperforming the strongest proprietary model (GPT-4.1-mini) by **12.94%** and specialized spatial models by **16.5%**.
- Successfully demonstrated zero-shot real-world robot navigation, guiding a quadrupedal robot through complex indoor environments with geometrically consistent motion plans, where MLLM baselines struggled.
- Showed robustness to different MLLM code agents and 3D reconstruction backbones, with performance improvements consistently attributed to the 3D visual programming paradigm rather than the specific underlying MLLM.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Grounding MLLMs' spatial reasoning in explicit 3D geometry via reconstructive vision tools and visual programming yields superior performance compared to implicit or 2D cognitive map methods.
> - A modular API for 3D spatial operations allows MLLMs to generate interpretable, compositional programs for multi-step, multi-view spatial queries, enhancing transparency and verifiability.
> - The zero-shot visual programming paradigm effectively leverages advanced 3D computer vision techniques without gradient-based fine-tuning, demonstrating strong generalizability across various MLLMs and reconstruction backbones.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The `pySpatial` framework operates at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs), 3D computer vision, embodied AI, and visual programming.

**Challenges in MLLMs for Spatial Reasoning:** MLLMs have achieved remarkable success in diverse tasks like image captioning, referring grounding, and video understanding. However, the paper highlights a significant limitation: their struggle with robust 3D spatial reasoning. This includes basic tasks like judging relative object positions from a single image to more complex reasoning involving egocentric motion and multi-view relations. This deficiency is a well-recognized bottleneck for deploying MLLMs in critical applications such as robotics, augmented reality, and embodied intelligence, which demand precise spatial understanding. Existing MLLMs often rely on implicit "imagination" or 2D cognitive maps, which provide limited effectiveness, especially in multi-view settings where MLLMs perform only slightly better than random chance.

**Advances in 3D Reconstruction:** A key enabler for `pySpatial` is the recent progress in feed-forward 3D reconstruction techniques (e.g., DUSt3R, MASt3R, CUT3R, VGGT). These methods can recover scene geometry, camera parameters, and depth maps directly from sparse 2D views, transforming limited 2D inputs into explorable 3D scenes. This advancement provides the geometric foundation necessary for explicit spatial reasoning.

**Modular Visual Reasoning and Visual Programming:** The paper builds upon the paradigm of modular visual reasoning, where vision specialists (like object detectors or segmentation models) are treated as symbolic operators and composed to solve complex problems. More specifically, `pySpatial` leverages the concept of "visual programming," a more advanced form of tool-use where MLLMs generate executable Python programs to invoke a predefined set of APIs. Prior works like Visual ChatGPT, MM-REACT, HuggingGPT, VisProg, ViperGPT, and VADAR have explored this direction for various visual tasks, including single-view spatial reasoning. `pySpatial` distinguishes itself by explicitly designing a framework for *multi-view* 3D spatial reasoning, equipping MLLMs with compositional 3D tools to handle complex spatial scenarios that go beyond single-image understanding.

In summary, `pySpatial` addresses a critical gap by providing MLLMs with explicit, geometrically-grounded 3D reasoning capabilities, moving beyond the limitations of implicit methods and single-view approaches. It synergizes the strengths of large language models for code generation with advanced 3D computer vision techniques to enable structured, interpretable, and effective spatial understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **equip Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) with explicit and robust 3D spatial reasoning capabilities**, particularly in challenging multi-view settings, where current MLLMs exhibit significant limitations.

The motivations driving this work are multi-faceted:

- **Addressing MLLM Limitations in 3D Spatial Understanding:** Despite their general-purpose perception and reasoning abilities, MLLMs consistently struggle with tasks requiring a deep understanding of the 3D world, especially when it comes to judging relative object positions from multiple perspectives, estimating depth, or reasoning about egocentric motion. This weakness is a major impediment to their deployment in real-world applications.
- **Overcoming Implicit Reasoning and Sparse 3D Supervision:** Existing approaches often rely on implicit "imagination" mechanisms or 2D cognitive maps, which offer only limited effectiveness and lack robustness for complex 3D tasks. Furthermore, MLLMs' training data (internet-scale image-caption pairs) is largely 2D, with explicit 3D supervision being sparse and costly, making it difficult for models to learn reliable language-to-3D correspondences.
- **Leveraging Advances in 3D Reconstruction for Explicit Geometric Foundation:** Recent advancements in feed-forward 3D reconstruction make it feasible to convert limited 2D views into explorable 3D scenes. This provides a crucial geometric foundation, allowing models to perform spatial transformations like camera translation and rotation, and to query previously occluded regions, grounding reasoning in concrete geometric evidence rather than implicit inference.
- **Enabling Flexible and Context-Aware Tool Composition:** A key challenge remains in how MLLMs can flexibly compose these 3D spatial tools and interact seamlessly with reconstructed 3D environments in a context-aware manner. The paper aims to address this by adopting a visual programming paradigm, allowing MLLMs to generate explicit, interpretable programs that orchestrate these tools.
- **Achieving Zero-Shot Performance and Interpretability:** A crucial goal is to enable these capabilities in a fully zero-shot setting, without the need for gradient-based fine-tuning on specific spatial reasoning datasets. The visual programming approach is inherently interpretable, providing transparent reasoning steps that can be easily inspected, debugged, and modified, making it suitable for safety-critical applications.
- **Bridging the Gap to Embodied AI and Robotics:** Ultimately, the work is motivated by the need for MLLMs to reliably support embodied intelligence tasks such as navigation and manipulation, where precise spatial understanding is paramount. Demonstrating effectiveness in real-world robot navigation highlights this practical ambition.

In essence, `pySpatial` seeks to elevate MLLMs from passive 2D observers to active 3D reasoners, capable of explicit, verifiable, and generalizable spatial understanding.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

`pySpatial` is designed as a visual programming framework that enables MLLMs to reason explicitly in 3D space by generating and executing Python code that orchestrates a set of specialized spatial tools. The approach can be broken down into several key components:

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The framework addresses the problem where an MLLM is given an image sequence ($I = \{I_n\}_{n=1}^N$) providing partial observations of a 3D scene, along with a natural-language query ($q$) about spatial relations or camera movements. The goal is to produce a correct response ($r^*$) from an answer space ($A$).
This is achieved by converting 2D views into an explorable 3D scene via feed-forward reconstruction, yielding depth estimates ($D$), camera intrinsics ($K$), and extrinsics ($G$), which together form a point cloud ($P$). An MLLM acts as a code agent ($F$) to synthesize a Python program ($z$) that invokes spatial tools through a defined API. An interpreter ($E$) executes this program to produce an intermediate output ($O$), which can be text or images. Finally, the MLLM integrates $I$, $O$, and $q$ to generate the final response ($r$).

#### 3.2. Spatial Tools and API

The core of `pySpatial` lies in its well-defined API (Code 1 in the paper), which exposes a set of spatial tools for MLLMs to interact with 3D environments:

- **3D Reconstruction (`reconstruct(scene)`):** This tool converts raw 2D image sequences into a structured 3D scene representation. The framework uses feed-forward reconstruction models like **CUT3R** for metric-scale scenes or **VGGT** for normalized unit space, depending on task requirements. It processes input images to generate a point cloud in world coordinates, along with camera intrinsics and extrinsics for each view. This reconstructs the scene geometry, serving as the basis for all downstream spatial operations.
- **Camera Description (`describe_camera_motion(recon)`):** This tool translates raw camera pose matrices into interpretable natural language descriptions of egocentric motion. Given two camera poses, it computes their displacement and discretizes the yaw angle into eight canonical motion categories (e.g., "forward," "backward," "left," "right," "diagonally forward and left"), making camera movements understandable to the language model.
- **Novel View Synthesis (`synthesize_novel_view(recon, new_camera_pose)`):** This enables active exploration of the reconstructed 3D scene. Given the point cloud and a new camera pose (extrinsics), it rasterizes the point cloud to render an RGB image from that arbitrary viewpoint. This provides interactive visual feedback, allowing the MLLM to "see" the scene from different perspectives.
- **Camera Manipulation Functions (`rotate_right`, `rotate_left`, `move_forward`, `move_backward`, `turn_around`):** These high-level actions allow the MLLM to programmatically modify a camera's pose within the 3D scene. For instance, `rotate_right` applies a yaw rotation (e.g., 45 degrees by default) about the world y-axis to the current camera-to-world transform, and then inverts it back to a world-to-camera pose. These functions facilitate exploring the scene to answer perspective-dependent queries.

#### 3.3. 3D Visual Programming

- **Program Generation:** An MLLM (defaulting to **GPT-4o** due to its strong code generation capabilities) acts as the code agent ($F$). Given a natural language query ($q$), it synthesizes a Python program ($z$) by composing function calls from the `pySpatial` API. The agent interacts only with the public interface, abstracting away internal implementation details. In-context examples are used to guide program synthesis, providing interface documentation and query-code pairs. The MLLM is prompted to first generate free-form natural language reasoning, followed by the Python code, which serves as an explicit and interpretable intermediate representation.
- **Program Execution:** The generated Python program ($z$) is then executed by a standard Python interpreter ($E$) over the input image sequence ($I$). The execution yields an intermediate output ($O$), which could be text (e.g., camera motion description) or images (e.g., novel views). This output provides direct, observable evidence grounding the program's reasoning steps.
- **Final Response Generation:** In the final step, the MLLM ($M$) integrates the original visual inputs ($I$), the program's explicit output ($O$), and the initial natural language query ($q$) to generate the final answer ($r$). This multi-stage process allows the MLLM to leverage its language understanding and reasoning abilities while offloading complex 3D geometric operations to specialized tools.

This methodology ensures that spatial reasoning is explicit, geometrically consistent, interpretable, and compositional, enabling MLLMs to tackle complex multi-view 3D tasks effectively in a zero-shot manner.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The `pySpatial` framework demonstrates superior performance across various benchmarks and real-world scenarios, substantiating its effectiveness in zero-shot 3D spatial reasoning.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

- **MINDCUBE Benchmark:** `pySpatial` achieved an overall accuracy of **58.56%** on the full MINDCUBE dataset, significantly outperforming all baseline categories. It surpassed the best open-weight model (DeepSeek-VL2-Small) by 10.94% and the strongest proprietary model (GPT-4.1-mini) by **12.94%**. Notably, `pySpatial` achieved 60.54% accuracy in the "Among" category, which requires complex reasoning about object relations, considerably outperforming baselines. It also outperformed specialized spatial models like VLM-3R by 16.5%, despite operating in a zero-shot setting.
- **MINDCUBE-1k Subset:** On the MINDCUBE-1k dataset, `pySpatial` recorded an impressive **62.35%** overall accuracy. It substantially outperformed spatial mental models (e.g., Chain-of-Thought, Cognitive Map) by roughly 20%, indicating that explicit 3D reasoning is far more effective than implicit "imagination." Furthermore, it surpassed existing visual programming approaches like VADAR by 21.9%, even when VADAR was augmented with `pySpatial`'s 3D reconstruction module (VADAR w/ Recon.), highlighting the robustness of `pySpatial`'s API design.
- **OMNI3D-BENCH (Single-View Spatial Reasoning):** The framework also generalized effectively to single-view settings, achieving **44.2%** on OMNI3D-BENCH. It outperformed prior visual programming approaches like VADAR (3.8% gain) and ViperGPT (17.5% gain), and even surpassed GPT-4o on the total score, demonstrating its broad generalizability beyond multi-view tasks.
- **MMSI-Bench (Appendix A.1):** Further evaluations on MMSI-Bench showed `pySpatial` improving overall performance by an average of 6.4%, reinforcing its efficacy across different spatial reasoning tasks.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

- **Interpretable 3D Visual Programs:** Qualitative examples from MINDCUBE (Figure 2, Figure A1) illustrate that `pySpatial` generates high-quality, executable, and interpretable Python programs. These programs explicitly orchestrate spatial tools (e.g., 3D reconstruction, camera rotation, novel view synthesis) to perform multi-step 3D operations. The generated code includes well-structured comments that capture the reasoning process, providing transparency and verifiability. This explicit grounding in geometric evidence leads to responses that closely align with ground-truth annotations.
- **Complex Control Flow:** Figure A1 further demonstrates `pySpatial`'s capability to compose visual programs with expressive control-flow constructs like `for-loops`, conditionals, and lambda-style functional compositions, enabling it to iteratively explore viewpoints and apply geometric predicates.

#### 4.3. Real-World Robot Navigation

- **Successful Navigation in Complex Environments:** In real-world indoor navigation experiments (Figure 3, Figure A2), `pySpatial` successfully guided a quadrupedal robot to traverse complex environments, including navigating through doorways and making correct turns, to reach a target object. The framework reconstructed an explorable 3D scene, inferred camera poses, and generated structured motion plans with precise rotations and translations.
- **Superiority over MLLM Baselines in Robotics:** In contrast, MLLM baselines like GPT-4.1 struggled with relative direction and absolute metric distance estimates, leading to navigation errors and collision-prone trajectories. `pySpatial`'s geometrically consistent plans proved reliable for physical robot platforms, showcasing its practical effectiveness and potential for embodied AI.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies and Discussions

- **Code Agent Robustness:** Ablation studies on the code agent (Table 4, Table A2) showed that augmenting various MLLM baselines (GPT-4o, GPT-4.1-mini, GPT-4.1, Qwen3-Coder, DeepSeek-v3) with `pySpatial` consistently led to substantial performance improvements. This indicates that the gains stem primarily from the 3D visual programming paradigm rather than the specific underlying MLLM used for code generation.
- **3D Reconstruction Backbone Robustness:** `pySpatial` also proved robust to the choice of 3D reconstruction backbone (Table A3), with VGGT, Pi3, and CUT3R all leading to significant improvements over the baseline.
- **In-Context Learning:** The number of in-context learning examples (Table A4) positively influenced performance, with 2-4 examples yielding the best results, though even zero-shot demonstrations provided strong improvements.
- **Failure Case Analysis:** Manual analysis of failure cases (Figure 4) revealed that only 6% were due to incorrect program generation. A larger portion stemmed from MLLM errors in the final reasoning step (20%) or limitations in the 3D reconstruction models (13%), suggesting future avenues for improvement in those areas.
- **Efficiency:** `pySpatial` completed MINDCUBE-1k in 2.17 hours (7.45 seconds per query), demonstrating efficiency comparable to or better than prior visual programming approaches like VADAR (17.25 seconds per query).

Overall, the findings highlight `pySpatial`'s ability to significantly enhance MLLMs' 3D spatial reasoning in a zero-shot, interpretable, and practical manner, consistently outperforming state-of-the-art baselines.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of `pySpatial` marks a significant advancement in the field of multi-modal AI, offering a robust and interpretable solution to a critical limitation of current MLLMs: 3D spatial reasoning. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enabling Explicit and Interpretable 3D Spatial Reasoning:** `pySpatial` fundamentally shifts MLLM spatial understanding from implicit "imagination" or 2D cognitive maps to explicit, geometrically-grounded reasoning in 3D space. By generating Python programs, it provides a transparent, step-by-step reasoning process that can be inspected, debugged, and understood. This interpretability is crucial for high-stakes applications where trust and accountability are paramount.
- **Zero-Shot Generalization and Adaptability:** The framework operates in a fully zero-shot setting, requiring no gradient-based fine-tuning for new spatial reasoning tasks. This plug-and-play nature makes it highly adaptable to various MLLMs (both open and closed-source) and different 3D reconstruction backbones. This broad generalizability means `pySpatial` can be readily integrated into existing systems to enhance their spatial intelligence.
- **Bridging the Gap to Embodied AI and Robotics:** The most immediate and impactful application demonstrated is in embodied AI, specifically real-world robot navigation. By enabling robots to generate geometrically consistent route plans from limited 2D views, `pySpatial` pushes the frontier of truly intelligent autonomous agents. This could lead to more robust navigation, manipulation, and human-robot interaction in complex, unstructured environments, addressing a long-standing challenge in robotics.
- **Advancing Multi-View Understanding:** The framework's superior performance on multi-view spatial reasoning benchmarks (like MINDCUBE) is particularly noteworthy. Many real-world scenarios involve fragmented observations, and the ability to synthesize an explorable 3D scene from sparse 2D inputs unlocks richer contextual understanding that was previously difficult for MLLMs to achieve.
- **Foundation for Future AI Agents:** `pySpatial` exemplifies the growing trend of AI agents that leverage external tools to augment their capabilities. It provides a blueprint for how MLLMs can be equipped with specialized domain-specific tools (in this case, 3D vision tools) to solve problems that are beyond their inherent training data. This modular, compositional approach paves the way for building more powerful and versatile AI systems.
- **Potential for Augmented Reality and Virtual Environments:** Beyond robotics, `pySpatial`'s ability to reconstruct and reason within 3D scenes from 2D inputs has strong implications for augmented reality (AR) and virtual reality (VR) applications. It could enable more intelligent AR assistants that understand spatial relationships in real-time, or generate dynamic content grounded in the user's physical environment.
- **Identifying Future Research Directions:** The failure analysis, pointing to limitations in the final MLLM reasoning step and 3D reconstruction quality, provides clear avenues for future research. Continuous improvements in these underlying components will directly translate into further enhancements for frameworks like `pySpatial`.

In conclusion, `pySpatial` is a seminal work that not only addresses a critical weakness in MLLMs but also lays a strong foundation for the next generation of intelligent systems capable of genuinely understanding and interacting with our complex 3D world.
