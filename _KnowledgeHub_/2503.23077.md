# Efficient Inference for Large Reasoning Models: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from National University of Singapore and collaborators provide the first comprehensive survey on efficient inference for Large Reasoning Models (LRMs), establishing a taxonomy of methods that reduce token consumption and improve inference time while maintaining reasoning quality. The work categorizes approaches into explicit compact Chain-of-Thought and implicit latent Chain-of-Thought, identifying current limitations and future research avenues.

## Problem

- Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) generate extensive intermediate reasoning steps, leading to high token consumption and increased inference time.
- The deliberative nature of LRMs results in significant memory overhead, making their deployment costly and resource-intensive.
- Traditional efficiency techniques for Large Language Models (LLMs) do not specifically target the token inefficiency inherent in LRMs' verbose reasoning chains.

## Method

- The authors conduct a systematic review, proposing a ==hierarchical taxonomy== for efficient LRM inference methods focused on token efficiency.
- The taxonomy classifies methods into =="explicit compact Chain-of-Thought"== (reducing visible tokens) and =="implicit latent Chain-of-Thought"== (encoding reasoning in hidden states).
- The survey provides empirical analyses of existing methods, detailing their performance across reasoning scenarios, objective functions, and ==efficiency metrics== like accuracy and token costs.

## Results

- The survey established a comprehensive hierarchical taxonomy for efficient LRM inference, categorizing methods based on their approach to compacting reasoning steps.
- Empirical analyses on datasets like GSM8K demonstrated a clear trade-off between reasoning accuracy and token cost, highlighting the practical utility of distilled models.
- The work identified four key open challenges: achieving user-centric controllable reasoning, balancing interpretability with efficiency, ensuring the safety of efficient LRMs, and expanding application domains beyond math and code.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Efficient LRM inference approaches primarily diverge into explicit compact CoT, which maintains visible reasoning steps, and implicit latent CoT, which embeds reasoning in hidden representations for greater token efficiency.
> - Research on LRM inference efficiency is predominantly focused on mathematical reasoning benchmarks due to their structured nature, simplifying objective function design.
> - An inherent trade-off exists between achieving high reasoning accuracy and minimizing token consumption, with model distillation presenting a viable path to retaining performance while reducing resource use.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This survey paper is strategically positioned at a critical juncture in the evolution of Large Language Models (LLMs) and their specialized offshoots, Large Reasoning Models (LRMs). While LLMs have excelled in "fast-thinking" applications like chatbots, there's a growing demand for models capable of "slow-thinking" scenarios, such as complex mathematical problem-solving or in-depth research, which require advanced analytical and deliberative reasoning. LRMs, exemplified by OpenAI's o1/o3, DeepSeek R1, and Kimi k1.5, have emerged to address this need by learning to explicitly reason.

However, the very process that makes LRMs powerful—their intermediate, deliberative reasoning steps—also makes them highly resource-intensive. This manifests in significant token consumption, high memory overhead, and increased inference time. Traditional methods for LLM efficiency, such as model compression, efficient model design, and system-level optimizations, primarily tackle memory and inference time but do not adequately address the *token inefficiency* inherent in LRMs' verbose reasoning chains. The paper explicitly states that previous efforts are "not specially designed for LRMs" and focuses on this critical gap.

Therefore, this survey fills a crucial void by specifically examining "efficient inference methods designed specifically for LRMs," with a particular emphasis on mitigating token inefficiency while rigorously preserving reasoning quality. The rapid pace of research in this niche is highlighted by the survey's chronological overview of selected highly-cited papers published predominantly between July 2024 and July 2025, demonstrating that this is an extremely active and fast-evolving area. By systematizing this nascent field, providing a taxonomy, empirical analyses, and outlining open challenges and future directions, the survey aims to serve as a foundational guide. It contextualizes the development of LRMs within the broader AI landscape, acknowledging their capabilities while directly addressing the practical barriers to their scalable and cost-effective deployment. This work is essential for fostering responsible and economically viable development of next-generation reasoning AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this survey stems directly from the inherent trade-off in Large Reasoning Models (LRMs): their impressive reasoning capabilities come at a significant computational cost. While LRMs excel at complex, "slow-thinking" tasks by generating explicit intermediate reasoning steps (often referred to as Chain-of-Thought or CoT), this deliberative process leads to substantial inefficiencies in:
1. **Token Consumption**: Each reasoning step translates to generated tokens, rapidly increasing output length.
2. **Memory Overhead**: Longer sequences demand more memory.
3. **Inference Time**: Sequential generation of numerous tokens prolongs response times.

These inefficiencies translate into higher operational costs for service providers and degraded user experience, hindering the widespread deployment of powerful LRMs in real-world applications. Crucially, the authors highlight that existing efficiency techniques for general Large Language Models (LLMs) often do not specifically target the unique problem of *token inefficiency* in LRMs, where "thinking tokens are treated like regular output tokens without cost differentiation."

Given this critical challenge, the key objectives of this survey are clearly defined:

- **Provide a Comprehensive Review**: Systematically review recent methods for efficient inference in LRMs, with a core focus on improving token efficiency while maintaining reasoning quality.
- **Develop a Hierarchical Taxonomy**: Categorize existing approaches into a structured taxonomy, specifically distinguishing between "explicit compact Chain-of-Thought (CoT)" (reducing tokens while keeping explicit structure) and "implicit latent CoT" (encoding reasoning in hidden representations). This helps organize a diverse and rapidly growing research landscape.
- **Analyze Strengths and Weaknesses**: Discuss the characteristics, advantages, and disadvantages of the identified methods within the proposed taxonomy.
- **Conduct Empirical Analyses**: Provide insights into existing methods from multiple perspectives, including the reasoning scenarios they address, the objective functions they optimize, and their observed performance (accuracy) and efficiency (token costs).
- **Identify Open Challenges**: Articulate the current limitations and crucial future challenges in the field, covering aspects like human-centric controllable reasoning, the trade-off between interpretability and efficiency, ensuring the safety of efficient reasoning, and expanding the application breadth of LRMs.
- **Highlight Future Directions**: Propose promising avenues for further improvement, such as new architectural designs (e.g., hybrid autoregressive-diffusion models, memory-efficient transformers), model merging techniques, and agent routing strategies.
- **Serve as a Guiding Resource**: Ultimately, the authors aim for this survey to be a valuable guide for researchers and engineers, helping them navigate the complexities and overcome the challenges of enhancing LRM inference efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a survey paper, the methodology employed is primarily a systematic review and synthesis of existing literature rather than presenting novel primary research. The authors adopt a structured and comprehensive approach to cover the burgeoning field of efficient LRM inference:

1. **Scope Definition**: The survey explicitly defines its scope to "recent efficient inference methods designed specifically for LRMs," with a particular focus on the past year (July 2024 to July 2025), emphasizing the dynamic nature of this research area. It distinguishes LRM-specific efficiency from general LLM efficiency methods.

2. **Literature Collection and Curation**: The authors implicitly conducted an extensive literature search, as evidenced by the numerous citations to very recent papers (many from 2025). The accompanying GitHub repository further indicates a structured effort to compile and organize relevant papers and their associated code.

3. **Hierarchical Taxonomy Development**: A core part of the methodology is the creation of a novel, hierarchical taxonomy to categorize the diverse approaches. This taxonomy is divided into two main classes:
 - **Explicit Compact Chain-of-Thought (CoT)**: Methods that reduce explicit reasoning tokens while maintaining a visible reasoning structure. This category is further subdivided into:
 - *CoT Compression*: Directly constraining or dynamically adapting the reasoning process.
 - *Fine-Tuning on Compact Reasoning Chains*: Training models to generate concise reasoning.
 - *Reward-Based Incentivization*: Using reward signals to encourage brevity.
 - **Implicit Latent CoT**: Methods that encode reasoning steps within hidden representations, moving away from explicit tokens.

4. **Detailed Method Description and Analysis**: For each category and sub-category, the survey provides a concise description of representative methods, often accompanied by flowcharts (e.g., Figure 4 for CoT Compression, Figure 5 for Fine-Tuning, Figure 6 for Reward-Based Incentivization) to visually illustrate their core steps and mechanisms. The authors also discuss the strengths and weaknesses of these approaches, touching upon aspects like reasoning quality and efficiency.

5. **Empirical Analyses**: To provide a data-driven perspective, the authors conduct "empirical analyses" by systematically reviewing the experimental settings and results reported in the surveyed papers. This involves:
 - **Reasoning Scenarios Analysis**: Categorizing benchmarks used by the methods into ten distinct reasoning scenarios (e.g., Mathematical, Code, Logical, Commonsense, Visual, Agent Reasoning). This helps to understand the applicability and focus of different techniques. Tables 4 and 5 explicitly map methods to the benchmarks they employ.
 - **Performance & Efficiency Analysis**: Presenting quantitative comparisons of accuracy and token costs, primarily focusing on the widely used GSM8K dataset, across multiple methods, models (e.g., GPT-4o, LLaMA-3.1, DeepSeek-R1), and experimental settings. Tables 6 and 7 consolidate these results, providing a structured basis for comparison.
 - **Objective Functions Analysis**: Examining the mathematical objective functions (e.g., prompt-based, SFT-based, DPO, RL, self-training, inference-time intervention) employed by representative methods. Tables 8, 9, and 10 in the Appendix detail these formulations, offering insights into the underlying optimization principles.

6. **Identification of Limitations and Challenges**: Based on the comprehensive review, the authors synthesize current limitations and articulate four key "open challenges" (Figure 7): user-centric controllable reasoning, interpretability vs. efficiency trade-off, safety implications, and the need for broader applications.

7. **Future Improvement Directions**: Finally, the survey concludes by outlining specific "further improvement" strategies (Figure 8), including new architectural paradigms (hybrid autoregressive-diffusion, memory-efficient transformers), model merging techniques, and the use of agent routers.

In essence, the methodology is robust for a survey, providing a structured overview, detailed classification, comparative analysis of reported empirical data, and forward-looking insights into a rapidly evolving and critical area of AI research.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey synthesizes a vast and rapidly expanding body of literature on efficient inference for Large Reasoning Models (LRMs), yielding several key findings and insights:

1. **Categorical Understanding of Efficiency Methods**: The primary result is a clear and comprehensive taxonomy of efficient inference methods for LRMs, divided into two main approaches:
 - **Explicit Compact CoT**: These methods aim to reduce the verbosity of explicit reasoning chains. They are further sub-categorized into CoT compression (e.g., `Sketch-of-Thought`, `TrimR`), fine-tuning on compact reasoning chains (e.g., `C3oT`, `TokenSkip`), and reward-based incentivization (e.g., `Kimi k1.5`, `S-GRPO`). The strength lies in preserving explicit reasoning structure, which can aid interpretability, but they risk sacrificing transparency if too much logic is abstracted.
 - **Implicit Latent CoT**: These methods internalize reasoning into hidden representations, moving away from explicit tokens (e.g., `Soft Thinking`, `CCoT`, `Heima`). They offer significant token efficiency and latency reduction, often matching or surpassing explicit CoT methods in accuracy. However, a major trade-off is the reduced interpretability, as the reasoning steps are no longer human-readable.

2. **Empirical Landscape of Applications**: The survey reveals that "Mathematical Reasoning" (using benchmarks like GSM8K, MATH, AIME) is overwhelmingly the most prevalent application domain for efficient LRM research, followed by "Logical Reasoning" and "Code Reasoning." This focus is attributed to the fixed nature of answers in these domains, which simplifies the construction of objective functions and reward signals. Other emerging areas include "Commonsense Reasoning" and "General Reasoning," with "Visual Reasoning" and "Agent Reasoning" also gaining traction.

3. **Performance and Efficiency Trade-offs**: The empirical analysis, particularly on the GSM8K dataset, underscores a consistent trade-off: higher accuracy often correlates with increased token costs. Distilled models (e.g., `R1-Distill-LLaMA`, `DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen`) consistently retain much of their teacher models' accuracy while significantly reducing latency and token consumption, making them practical for resource-constrained environments. Proprietary models like GPT-4o typically achieve top-tier accuracy but at higher token costs. The analysis highlights that methods like `CoD`, `FlashThink`, and `AALC` can achieve high accuracy while keeping token costs relatively low under specific settings, but direct, apples-to-apples comparisons are challenging due to varied experimental setups.

4. **Diversity in Optimization Objectives**: The review of objective functions demonstrates a wide array of strategies:
 - **Prompt-based methods**: Modify decoding scores without parameter updates (e.g., `Budget Guidance`, `ThinkLess`).
 - **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**: Minimizing cross-entropy loss over curated concise reasoning trajectories (e.g., `C3oT`, `SOLAR`).
 - **Preference Optimization (DPO, SimPO)**: Learning from pairwise preferences to balance brevity and correctness (e.g., `TALE-PT`, `ConCISE`).
 - **Reinforcement Learning (RL)**: Using explicit reward functions to incentivize efficiency and correctness, often with length penalties or adaptive reward shaping (e.g., `Kimi k1.5`, `S-GRPO`, `LASER`).
 - **Self-Training Frameworks**: Iteratively generating pseudo-labels to reduce annotation reliance (e.g., `SF`).
 - **Inference-time Intervention**: Manipulating the decoding process dynamically (e.g., `ASC`, `CTS`). This diversity indicates the multi-faceted nature of the efficiency problem and the numerous avenues being explored.

5. **Critical Challenges Identified**: The survey pinpoints several overarching limitations:
 - **User-centric Controllable Reasoning**: A lack of fine-grained, dynamic control mechanisms for users to adjust reasoning depth and verbosity.
 - **Interpretability vs. Efficiency Trade-off**: Efficient methods, especially implicit ones, often sacrifice the transparency of LRM reasoning, which is crucial for high-stakes applications.
 - **Safety of Efficient Reasoning**: Existing efficiency methods, particularly training-based ones, risk breaking the safety alignment of LRMs, potentially leading to jailbreaking or privacy leakage.
 - **Broader Application**: Current applications are concentrated in domains with fixed answers (math, code), limiting LRM utility in open-ended or time-sensitive scenarios (e.g., robotics, finance).

These findings collectively highlight the significant progress made in LRM inference efficiency while underscoring the complex, multi-dimensional challenges that still require substantial research and development.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey paper holds significant importance and is poised to have a substantial impact on the ongoing development and deployment of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) for several reasons:

1. **Structuring a Nascent Field**: The field of efficient LRM inference is extremely new and rapidly evolving, as evidenced by the high concentration of papers from late 2024 and 2025 cited in the survey. By providing the first comprehensive review and a well-defined hierarchical taxonomy, the authors bring much-needed structure and clarity to this complex landscape. This is invaluable for new researchers entering the domain and helps experienced practitioners navigate the diverse array of techniques.

2. **Addressing a Critical Bottleneck**: The core problem of LRM inefficiency (high token consumption, memory, and inference time) is a major barrier to their practical and scalable deployment. By systematically analyzing methods to mitigate token inefficiency—the focus of this survey—the work directly contributes to making advanced reasoning AI more accessible, cost-effective, and deployable in real-world scenarios. This has profound implications for the economic viability of LRM-powered services.

3. **Guiding Future Research**: The survey's identification of "Limitations & Challenges" and "Further Improvement" strategies provides a clear roadmap for future research.
 - **User Control**: Emphasizing user-centric controllable reasoning will push for more adaptable and user-friendly LRMs.
 - **Interpretability vs. Efficiency**: Highlighting this trade-off forces researchers to consider hybrid approaches and external verification mechanisms, ensuring that progress in efficiency doesn't come at the cost of explainability, particularly in critical domains like healthcare or legal applications.
 - **Safety**: Drawing attention to the potential for efficient methods to compromise safety alignment is paramount. This will encourage the integration of robust safety constraints and the development of stronger reasoning-based safeguards, ensuring responsible AI development.
 - **Broader Applications**: By showing how efficient reasoning can unlock new domains (e.g., real-time robotics, financial trading, open-ended creative tasks), the survey inspires wider applicability of LRMs beyond current niches like math and code.

4. **Promoting Innovation in Architectures and Training**: The forward-looking suggestions—such as new architectures (hybrid autoregressive-diffusion, memory-efficient transformers), model merging, and agent routers—are likely to catalyze novel research directions. These areas represent significant shifts from current paradigms and could lead to breakthroughs in how LRMs are designed, trained, and utilized.

5. **Resource for the Community**: The accompanying GitHub repository for papers and codes serves as a tangible resource, fostering collaboration and accelerating research by providing a centralized and updated collection of materials. This practical contribution enhances the survey's utility beyond academic discussion.

In summary, "Efficient Inference for Large Reasoning Models: A Survey" is a timely and essential contribution that not only consolidates the current state of a vital research area but also actively shapes its future trajectory. By focusing on practical challenges and highlighting avenues for innovation, it paves the way for the development of more powerful, efficient, and responsibly deployed reasoning AI systems.
