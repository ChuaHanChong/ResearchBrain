# Efficient Inference for Large Reasoning Models: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from National University of Singapore and collaborators provide the first comprehensive survey on efficient inference for Large Reasoning Models (LRMs), establishing a taxonomy of methods that reduce token consumption and improve inference time while maintaining reasoning quality. The work categorizes approaches into explicit compact Chain-of-Thought and implicit latent Chain-of-Thought, identifying current limitations and future research avenues.

## Problem

- Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) generate extensive intermediate reasoning steps, leading to high token consumption and increased inference time.
- The deliberative nature of LRMs results in significant memory overhead, making their deployment costly and resource-intensive.
- Traditional efficiency techniques for Large Language Models (LLMs) do not specifically target the token inefficiency inherent in LRMs' verbose reasoning chains.

## Method

- The authors conduct a systematic review, proposing a ==hierarchical taxonomy== for efficient LRM inference methods focused on token efficiency.
- The taxonomy classifies methods into =="explicit compact Chain-of-Thought"== (reducing visible tokens) and =="implicit latent Chain-of-Thought"== (encoding reasoning in hidden states).
- The survey provides empirical analyses of existing methods, detailing their performance across reasoning scenarios, objective functions, and ==efficiency metrics== like accuracy and token costs.

## Results

- The survey established a comprehensive hierarchical taxonomy for efficient LRM inference, categorizing methods based on their approach to compacting reasoning steps.
- Empirical analyses on datasets like GSM8K demonstrated a clear trade-off between reasoning accuracy and token cost, highlighting the practical utility of distilled models.
- The work identified four key open challenges: achieving user-centric controllable reasoning, balancing interpretability with efficiency, ensuring the safety of efficient LRMs, and expanding application domains beyond math and code.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Efficient LRM inference approaches primarily diverge into explicit compact CoT, which maintains visible reasoning steps, and implicit latent CoT, which embeds reasoning in hidden representations for greater token efficiency.
> - Research on LRM inference efficiency is predominantly focused on mathematical reasoning benchmarks due to their structured nature, simplifying objective function design.
> - An inherent trade-off exists between achieving high reasoning accuracy and minimizing token consumption, with model distillation presenting a viable path to retaining performance while reducing resource use.
