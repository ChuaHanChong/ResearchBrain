# Recti-Q: Feature-Space Rectification for Out-of-Distribution-Robust Quantized Perception in Edge Robotics

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces Recti-Q, a method that restores out-of-distribution (OOD) robustness in deep learning models after Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) by rectifying feature space distortions. It addresses the 'Quantization-Induced Robustness Gap' using a lightweight, trainable adapter with minimal overhead, achieving significant OOD accuracy recovery while maintaining the efficiency benefits of quantization.

## Problem

- Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) in deep learning models, while efficient for edge robotics, leads to a 'Quantization-Induced Robustness Gap' where OOD performance degrades despite high in-distribution accuracy.
- Existing methods for robust quantization (e.g., QAT) demand extensive retraining or full training data, undermining the efficiency advantages of PTQ.
- Robotic systems operate in dynamic, unpredictable environments, necessitating robust perception against sensor noise, adverse weather, and domain shifts that quantized models often fail to handle reliably.

## Method

- **Recti-Q** employs a lightweight, parameter-efficient ==Classifier-LoRA adapter==, attached at the pre-classifier feature level of a frozen, already quantized backbone model.
- The adapter is trained to correct quantization-induced feature distortions using a combined ==cross-entropy== and optional ==knowledge distillation loss==, with training possible even without a full-precision teacher model.
- The minimal size of the adapter enables efficient, low-bandwidth ==Over-The-Air (OTA) updates== for deployed robotic systems, allowing robustness patches with negligible overhead.

## Results

- Recti-Q consistently recovered OOD accuracy, with gains up to **+1.62 percentage points** on the PACS domain shift benchmark and significant recovery on ImageNet-C common corruptions, occasionally surpassing the original full-precision model's performance.
- The method preserved over **99%** of the memory savings from quantization, with adapter sizes as small as **6 KB** (rank=**4**), demonstrating extreme parameter efficiency.
- Ablation studies showed that a full-precision teacher model for knowledge distillation is not always necessary, with 'teacher-free' Recti-Q achieving competitive or superior performance in some cases.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - PTQ creates a substantial 'Quantization-Induced Robustness Gap,' where high in-distribution accuracy masks severe degradation in out-of-distribution robustness.
> - Efficient recovery of OOD robustness in quantized models can be achieved by a small, parameter-efficient adapter modifying the feature space, rather than full model retraining.
> - Feature-space rectification is crucial for addressing OOD degradation, as a simple logit-space adaptation provides negligible gains.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the intersection of model efficiency, robustness, and deployment in resource-constrained edge computing environments, particularly within robotic perception pipelines. Modern robotic systems often rely on large vision models for tasks such as autonomous navigation and object recognition. Deploying these models on embedded and edge platforms necessitates efficiency in terms of memory, latency, and energy consumption. Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) is a widely adopted technique to achieve this efficiency by reducing model size and computational demands without extensive retraining.

Historically, PTQ research has primarily focused on preserving in-distribution (ID) accuracy, maximizing compression, or improving inference throughput. While effective in these areas, the reliability of quantized models under real-world distribution shifts—such as sensor noise, adverse weather conditions, or novel operating environments—has received less attention. Existing methods designed to enhance robustness in compressed models, such as Robust Quantization-Aware Training (QAT) or robust distillation, often require access to full training data and computationally expensive end-to-end retraining, which undermines the practical advantages of PTQ for edge deployment.

This paper positions itself by identifying and quantifying a "Quantization-Induced Robustness Gap," where PTQ models maintain high ID accuracy but exhibit significant degradation in out-of-distribution (OOD) robustness. This gap is particularly critical for robotic perception, where unpredictable environmental conditions are common, and perception failures can propagate to downstream autonomy modules, affecting safety and performance.

The proposed solution, Recti-Q, integrates into the broader landscape of Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) and adapter-based tuning. PEFT methods, such as LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation), modify small auxiliary parameters while freezing a larger, pre-trained backbone, offering an efficient way to adapt models to new tasks or domains. While PEFT has been applied to various problems, including domain adaptation and transfer learning, Recti-Q specifically applies this paradigm to rectify quantization-induced feature distortions in vision models. This approach differentiates it from related work on mitigating quantization issues in Large Language Models (LLMs) or improving adversarial robustness, by focusing on OOD robustness for real-time visual perception in physical-world settings. The framework aims to provide a lightweight, architecture-agnostic solution that allows for efficient, low-bandwidth updates for deployed systems, a capability that remains underexplored in robot learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address a critical and under-explored challenge: the degradation of out-of-distribution (OOD) robustness in deep learning models after Post-Training Quantization (PTQ), specifically in the context of edge robotics. The authors term this phenomenon the "Quantization-Induced Robustness Gap."

The motivation for this work stems from several factors pertinent to the deployment of robotic perception systems:

1. **Efficiency Requirements of Edge Robotics:** Robotic platforms, such as autonomous vehicles or mobile robots, operate under stringent Size, Weight, and Power (SWaP) constraints. This necessitates the use of efficient models with low memory footprints, minimal computational demands, and low power consumption. PTQ is an attractive solution because it significantly reduces model size and inference costs without requiring computationally intensive full retraining, making it suitable for real-time inference on embedded and edge devices.

2. **Unpredictable Operating Environments:** Robots typically operate in open, dynamic, and often harsh environments. Their visual sensors are susceptible to various forms of degradation, including sensor noise, motion blur, changes in illumination, precipitation (rain, snow), fog, and lens artifacts (e.g., glare, splatter). A perception module must function reliably under these diverse and often unseen conditions. The paper highlights that a full-precision model might robustly handle such perturbations, but its quantized counterpart may fail on the same input, leading to potentially critical errors (e.g., misrecognizing a stop sign in adverse weather).

3. **Limitations of Current PTQ Research:** Existing PTQ research primarily emphasizes metrics like in-distribution (ID) accuracy, model compression rates, or inference throughput. While these are important, the authors demonstrate that high ID accuracy post-quantization can be misleading. A model performing well on clean, in-distribution data may still be highly vulnerable to realistic distribution shifts, a fact often overlooked in standard evaluation protocols.

4. **Inefficiency of Robustness-Preserving Quantization Methods:** Methods that specifically aim to preserve robustness during quantization, such as Robust Quantization-Aware Training (QAT) or robust distillation, typically require significant computational resources, full access to training data, and extensive retraining. These requirements negate the primary advantage of PTQ, which is its ability to compress models post-training with minimal additional cost. This creates a practical dilemma for deploying robust, yet efficient, models.

5. **Need for a Lightweight and Flexible Solution:** The authors aim to develop a method that can repair OOD robustness on a *frozen, already quantized* model without introducing significant computational overhead or requiring a full-precision teacher model during deployment. Such a solution would enable practical scenarios like "Over-The-Air (OTA) robustness patching" for deployed robotic fleets, where only a small update needs to be transmitted to enhance resilience to new environmental challenges. The solution should also be architecture-agnostic, applicable to both traditional Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and modern Vision Transformers.

In essence, the core motivation is to ensure that the efficiency gains from PTQ do not come at the cost of unreliable perception in real-world, unpredictable robotic deployment scenarios, by providing an efficient and effective mechanism to restore OOD robustness.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Recti-Q framework is designed to repair out-of-distribution (OOD) robustness in already quantized models without requiring full model retraining or substantial computational overhead. The core approach involves freezing the quantized backbone and attaching a small, trainable adapter module at the classifier head.

**A. Overall Strategy: Decoupling Compression from Robustness**
The methodology begins with a pre-trained, full-precision vision model $f$, which is then converted into an efficient, frozen quantized backbone $f_q$ using Post-Training Quantization (PTQ). The key insight is that PTQ, while preserving in-distribution accuracy, perturbs the model's internal feature representations, leading to OOD degradation. Recti-Q aims to rectify these feature perturbations rather than re-training the entire compressed model.

**B. Adapter Design: Classifier-LoRA Adapter**
The central component of Recti-Q is a parameter-efficient adapter, $g_\phi$, which intervenes at the feature level, specifically on the pre-classifier features $u = \text{PRECLASSIFIERFEATURES}(f_q, x)$. This adapter is implemented as a standard Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) module, comprising a down-projection matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times d}$ and an up-projection matrix $B \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times r}$, where $d$ is the feature dimension, $C$ is the number of classes, and $r$ is the rank.

The adapter computes a logit-space correction:
$g_\phi(u) = B(A(u)) \cdot (\alpha/r)$
where $\alpha$ is a scaling factor.

The final logits $z$ are obtained by adding this correction to the original quantized model's logits $z_q$:
$z = z_q + g_\phi(u) = f_q(x) + g_\phi(u)$

Conceptually, this operation is equivalent to applying a low-rank update to the quantized classifier head, $(W_q + (\alpha/r)BA)u$, while keeping the original quantized head $W_q$ frozen. A crucial design choice is the zero-initialization of the up-projection matrix $B$, which ensures that at the start of training, $g_\phi(u)$ is zero, leading to $z = z_q$. This design promotes stable training, allowing the adapter to progressively learn corrections.

**C. Recti-Q Training and Inference**
The training procedure for Recti-Q is as follows:
1. **Frozen Components:** The quantized student backbone $f_q$ remains frozen throughout training. An optional full-precision teacher model $f_t$ (if used for knowledge distillation) also remains frozen.
2. **Trainable Component:** Only the lightweight adapter $g_\phi$ is updated during training.
3. **Loss Function:** The optimization objective $L$ combines two terms:
 - **Cross-Entropy Loss ($L_{CE}$):** Standard classification loss applied to the adapted logits $z$ against the true source labels $y$.
 - **Knowledge Distillation Loss ($L_{KD}$):** An optional term, weighted by $\lambda$, which computes the Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence between the temperature-scaled softmax outputs of the adapted student model ($s_z = \text{softmax}(z/T)$) and the teacher model ($s_t = \text{softmax}(f_t(x)/T)$). The teacher is used only if $\lambda > 0$. The framework supports a "teacher-free" mode where $\lambda = 0$, relying solely on $L_{CE}$.
 - The total loss is $L = L_{CE} + \lambda L_{KD}$.
4. **Optimizer and Schedule:** AdamW optimizer is used with a small learning rate, light weight decay, and a short cosine learning rate schedule, allowing for rapid convergence.
5. **Source-Only Adaptation Protocol:**
 - **ImageNet-C:** Adapters are trained on a fixed 5% class-balanced subsample of the ImageNet-1k training set and validated on the ImageNet-1k validation split. No corrupted data is used during training or validation.
 - **PACS:** A leave-one-domain-out (LODO) protocol is followed. For each target domain, adapters are trained on data from the other three source domains and validated on source splits. No target domain images are used for training or model selection. This protocol reflects real-world "Lab-to-Wild" or "Sim-to-Real" deployment scenarios.

For inference, the full-precision teacher model (if used during training) is discarded. The forward pass involves a single pass through the frozen quantized backbone $f_q$ to obtain $z_q$ and $u$, followed by the computation of the correction $g_\phi(u)$ by the trained adapter. The final prediction is $\hat{y} = \text{arg max}(z_q + g_\phi(u))$.

**D. Complexity and Deployment Considerations**
Recti-Q minimizes overhead:
- **Parameter Overhead:** The LoRA adapter adds parameters proportional to $r(d+C)$, which is significantly smaller than the full model. The authors report less than 1% parameter overhead.
- **Memory Footprint:** The quantized backbone remains unchanged, so the overall memory footprint is largely preserved, maintaining over 99% of PTQ memory savings.
- **Computational Cost:** The adapter involves a minimal number of Multiply-Accumulate (MAC) operations ($r \times d$ and $C \times r$), which is negligible compared to the visual backbone's operations.
- **Deployment Advantage:** The small size of the adapter enables low-bandwidth Over-The-Air (OTA) updates. Once a base quantized model is deployed, only the compact adapter parameters need to be transmitted to update its robustness for new environmental conditions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings across various benchmarks, substantiating the existence of the "Quantization-Induced Robustness Gap" and demonstrating Recti-Q's ability to mitigate it.

**A. The Quantization-Induced Robustness Gap**
The authors first establish that standard 4-bit Post-Training Quantization (W4 PTQ) maintains high in-distribution (ID) accuracy but significantly degrades out-of-distribution (OOD) robustness.
- **ID Accuracy Preservation:** Table I shows that W4 PTQ models exhibit negligible accuracy drops (less than 1.0 percentage point) on standard ID test sets like ImageNet validation and PACS ID splits. This indicates that traditional metrics alone are insufficient to assess real-world reliability.
- **OOD Robustness Degradation:**
 - **ImageNet-C (Common Corruptions):** Table V quantifies severe accuracy drops for W4 PTQ models on ImageNet-C at severity 5. For the DeiT-s model, the accuracy decreased by -5.62 percentage points on 'contrast' corruption, -4.30 pp on 'gaussian noise', and similar drops for other corruptions.
 - **PACS (Domain Shift):** Table IV shows a pronounced robustness loss on the PACS dataset. For instance, DeiT-s on the 'sketch' domain experienced a -4.02 pp drop, while DeiT-t on 'cartoon' and 'art painting' domains saw drops of -1.36 pp and -1.27 pp, respectively.
These results confirm the existence of a substantial "Quantization-Induced Robustness Gap" under realistic OOD shifts.
- **Breakdown Point Analysis:** Further analysis revealed that while W3 (3-bit quantization) still allows for some robustness recovery, W2 (2-bit quantization) causes ID accuracy to collapse significantly (to 4.61%), rendering feature rectification largely ineffective. This suggests an empirical breakdown point where information loss becomes too severe for head-only adaptation.

**B. Recti-Q Recovers OOD Robustness**
Recti-Q consistently recovers a substantial portion of the lost OOD robustness while preserving the efficiency benefits of quantization.
- **Performance on PACS (Domain Shift):** As detailed in Table II, Recti-Q not only recovers the accuracy of the degraded W4 baseline but, in several instances, surpasses the original FP32 model's performance. For example, DeiT-t on the 'cartoon' domain achieved 73.34% accuracy with Recti-Q, exceeding the FP32 baseline of 73.08%. Similarly, on 'art painting', it improved from 73.63% (W4) to 75.15% (Recti-Q), surpassing FP32's 74.90%. The robustness recovery (Ours vs W4) was notable, with gains up to +1.62 pp.
- **Performance on ImageNet-C (Common Corruptions):** Table III demonstrates strong and consistent recovery on ImageNet-C, particularly for severe corruptions. For the 'contrast' corruption on DeiT-s, Recti-Q recovered +2.68 percentage points of accuracy, closing approximately 50% of the initial -5.62 pp gap. Consistent positive gains were observed across other corruption types.
- **Efficiency Preservation:** A critical finding is that these robustness gains are achieved with minimal overhead. The total model size with Recti-Q (e.g., 25.74 MB for DeiT-s on PACS, 26.50 MB on ImageNet-C) remains nearly identical to the W4 baseline (25.64 MB, 26.16 MB respectively), retaining over 99% of the memory savings from quantization.

**C. Ablation Studies**
Ablation studies validated key design choices of Recti-Q:
- **Necessity of FP32 Teacher (KD):** Table VI shows that the FP32 teacher for Knowledge Distillation (KD) is not always essential. The "teacher-free" (λ=0) Recti-Q variant was highly competitive and, in some cases (e.g., DeiT-t on 'cartoon' and 'art painting'), achieved the best performance. This confirms the method's practicality in scenarios where a teacher model is unavailable or too costly.
- **Necessity of Feature-Space Rectification:** Table VII compares the Classifier-LoRA Adapter (feature-space) with a Logit-Space Adapter (simple MLP on final logits). The Logit-Space Adapter provided no measurable gain (+0.00 pp) for DeiT-s on 'sketch', whereas Recti-Q yielded a +1.17 pp gain. This finding supports the hypothesis that OOD degradation is a deep, feature-level problem requiring intervention before the final logits.
- **Effect of Adapter Rank (r):** Table VIII analyzed the impact of LoRA rank on robustness and parameter efficiency. Even a very small rank, such as r=4 (adding only 0.006 MB, or 6 KB), recovered +0.77 pp, representing over 74% of the gain achieved by the r=64 adapter. Increasing rank to r=16 (0.025 MB) captured nearly 90% of the maximum recovery. This demonstrates the extreme parameter efficiency of Recti-Q, making it highly suitable for low-bandwidth OTA updates.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Recti-Q framework addresses a fundamental tension between model efficiency and real-world deployment robustness, offering several significant contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Bridging the Efficiency-Robustness Gap:** The work formally identifies and quantifies the "Quantization-Induced Robustness Gap," a critical oversight in much of the existing PTQ literature. By demonstrating that PTQ can severely degrade OOD robustness despite preserving ID accuracy, the research highlights a practical vulnerability for edge AI systems. Recti-Q provides a targeted solution to bridge this gap, allowing practitioners to leverage the efficiency benefits of PTQ without compromising reliability in dynamic environments.

2. **Enhanced Reliability for Edge Robotics:** For robotic perception systems deployed on SWaP-constrained platforms, Recti-Q offers a mechanism to significantly improve reliability under unpredictable conditions (e.g., sensor noise, adverse weather, varied visual styles). By recovering lost robustness, it reduces the risk of perception failures that could propagate to downstream planning and control modules, thereby enhancing the safety and operational robustness of autonomous systems.

3. **Cost-Effective Robustness Improvement:** Recti-Q challenges the conventional wisdom that achieving robust quantization necessitates computationally expensive methods like Quantization-Aware Training (QAT) or robust distillation. Instead, it provides a lightweight, post-deployment robustness patching mechanism. The ability to train a small adapter using only source data, often without requiring a full-precision teacher, makes the process efficient in terms of computational cost, memory, and training time.

4. **Enabling Low-Bandwidth Over-The-Air (OTA) Updates:** The extremely small parameter footprint of the Recti-Q adapter (as small as 6 KB) is a key innovation for deployment. This enables low-bandwidth OTA updates for deployed robotic fleets. Instead of transmitting entirely new, large models or retraining on device, only the compact adapter parameters need to be sent, allowing for rapid adaptation to new environments or discovered failure modes with minimal network and computational overhead. This paradigm can extend the operational lifetime and adaptability of deployed AI systems.

5. **Architecture Agnosticism and Flexibility:** The framework's architecture-agnostic nature, demonstrated across CNNs (ResNet) and Transformers (DeiT), broadens its applicability. The option to operate in a "teacher-free" mode further increases its flexibility, making it usable even when a full-precision teacher model is not readily available or too expensive to run.

6. **Addressing a Feature-Level Problem:** The ablation study confirming the necessity of feature-space rectification, as opposed to simple logit re-weighting, offers insight into the nature of quantization-induced degradation. This suggests that PTQ primarily distorts internal representations, and corrective measures need to operate at this deeper level.

While Recti-Q offers significant advantages, the authors acknowledge its limitations. It corrects quantization-induced feature distortion but cannot reconstruct information fundamentally lost by aggressive quantization. Its effectiveness relies on the quantized backbone retaining usable class structure in its output features. Furthermore, the current evaluation is limited to closed-set covariate and domain/style shifts, and its performance on semantic or open-set OOD scenarios or entirely novel categories is yet to be fully explored. Despite these limitations, Recti-Q represents a practical step towards deploying more reliable and adaptable perception systems in real-world edge robotics.
