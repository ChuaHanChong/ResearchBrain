# RainyGS: Efficient Rain Synthesis with Physically-Based Gaussian Splatting

> [!summary] Summary
> RainyGS, a framework from Peking University, integrates a physics-based shallow-water model and screen-space rendering techniques with 3D Gaussian Splatting to synthesize dynamic, photorealistic, and physically accurate rain effects in "in-the-wild" scenes. This approach achieves interactive frame rates exceeding 30 frames per second, demonstrating superior visual fidelity and 3D consistency compared to existing methods.

## Problem

- Difficulty in synthesizing dynamic, photorealistic, and physically accurate rain effects in "in-the-wild" 3D scenes due to complex physical phenomena and their concurrent evolution.
- Limitations of existing methods: 3D scene representations (e.g., 3DGS) lack sophisticated physics-based simulation for fluids, while traditional physics simulations are too manual and inflexible for broad application.
- Computational inefficiency for achieving interactive frame rates (e.g., 30+ fps) for widespread dynamic rain in open-world environments.

## Method

- **RainyGS** uses a multi-stage pipeline integrating scene modeling (using ==PGSR== with normal priors) and auxiliary map extraction to establish a robust 3D scene representation.
- A height map-based ==shallow-water model== for efficient simulation of dynamic water accumulation, ripples, and raindrop impacts on surfaces.
- A two-pass ==reflection-aware rasterization== strategy utilizing ==Screen-Space Reflection== (SSR) for surface effects and explicit 3D Gaussian spheres for raindrops and splashes, optimized for rendering efficiency.

## Results

- Achieves **photorealistic and physically accurate dynamic rain effects**, including streaks, dynamic water accumulation, ripples, splashes, and complex optical phenomena like reflections and refractions, while maintaining 3D consistency across views.
- Renders dynamic rain at interactive frame rates exceeding **30 FPS**, demonstrating superior efficiency and minimal additional computational cost compared to a baseline 3DGS setup and significantly outperforming ray tracing or video-based generation methods.
- Outperforms baseline methods like Rain Motion, Runway Gen-3 Alpha, and Instruct-GS2GS in terms of physical accuracy, multi-view consistency, and overall visual fidelity, as supported by qualitative comparisons and a user study indicating significant preference.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating an efficient physics-based shallow-water model with 3D Gaussian Splatting enables dynamic and physically accurate water accumulation on diverse surfaces within 3D scenes.
> - Leveraging screen-space rendering techniques, such as Screen-Space Reflection (SSR) and image-based refraction, offers a computationally efficient approach to achieve photorealistic high-order optical effects for water surfaces.
> - Representing rain elements like streaks, drops, and splashes with specialized Gaussian configurations (e.g., aligned Gaussians for streaks, single Gaussians for splashes) allows for consistent and adaptable rain rendering within the 3DGS pipeline.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "RainyGS" is situated at the intersection of 3D scene modeling and physics-based simulations, specifically addressing the challenge of generating dynamic and physically accurate weather effects in "in-the-wild" scenes.

Recent advancements in 3D scene representations, such as Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), have demonstrated proficiency in novel view synthesis and have begun to enable certain scene editing tasks. However, these methods typically encounter difficulties when confronted with complex, physics-based editing tasks like rain simulation, which require modeling intricate interactions and dynamic phenomena.

Concurrently, traditional physics-based simulations are capable of generating realistic rain effects, including individual raindrops and splashes, by adhering to physical principles. The limitation of these approaches lies in their reliance on manual setup by skilled artists, which often requires significant effort to create high-fidelity scenes. This dependency restricts their flexibility and scalability, making them less suitable for broader, open-world environments where manual scene construction is impractical.

The research landscape has seen initial attempts to combine these two domains. Examples include ClimateNeRF, which integrates scene modeling with physics-based weather modeling, and Gaussian Splashing, which applies physics-based animations of fluids with 3D Gaussian Splatting. However, ClimateNeRF primarily focuses on static weather conditions, while Gaussian Splashing employs particle simulations that can be computationally inefficient for dynamic effects. These limitations suggest a gap in existing methodologies for efficient, dynamic, and physically accurate rain synthesis in diverse, real-world scenarios.

RainyGS positions itself to address this gap by developing a framework that leverages the strengths of both physics-based modeling and the efficiency of Gaussian Splatting. It aims to provide a unified approach for generating photorealistic and dynamic rain effects in open-world scenes with physical accuracy, surpassing the limitations of prior methods in terms of both realism and computational efficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method, termed RainyGS, for adding dynamic and photorealistic rain effects to "in-the-wild" scenes in a physically correct manner. This involves synthesizing rainy conditions from multi-view inputs of scenes originally captured under clear weather (sunny or cloudy).

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in achieving realistic rain synthesis for open-world environments:

- **Complexity of Physical Phenomena:** Generating realistic rain requires simulating a multitude of complex, high-order physical and rendering effects. These include the formation of rain streaks in the atmosphere, the accumulation of water on various surfaces, and intricate optical phenomena such as realistic reflections, refractions, and specular highlights.
- **Dynamic and Concurrent Evolution:** These diverse effects must not only be simulated accurately but also evolve dynamically over time and appear concurrently within the scene, adding significant computational and modeling complexity.
- **Efficiency Requirements:** To be applicable in a wide range of fields such as augmented reality (AR/VR), gaming, robotics, and autonomous driving, rain simulation must be computationally efficient, capable of operating at interactive frame rates (e.g., over 30 frames per second). The problem of efficient and dynamic rain synthesis for open-world scenes remains largely underexplored due to these combined requirements.
- **Limitations of Existing Scene Modeling Techniques:** While neural radiance fields (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) have advanced novel view synthesis and some scene editing tasks, they generally lack the inherent capabilities for advanced, physics-based simulations required for realistic rain. Their geometric representations may not be optimized for complex fluid interactions.
- **Inflexibility of Traditional Physics Simulations:** Conversely, traditional physics-based simulations, despite their capacity for realism in effects like raindrops and splashes, typically demand significant manual effort from artists to set up high-fidelity scenes. This manual intervention renders them inflexible and difficult to scale for arbitrary "in-the-wild" environments.
- **Gaps in Hybrid Approaches:** Prior works that attempt to combine scene modeling with physics, such as ClimateNeRF and Gaussian Splashing, have demonstrated progress but possess specific limitations. ClimateNeRF primarily addresses static weather conditions, while Gaussian Splashing, although dynamic, relies on particle simulations that can be inefficient for widespread, dynamic fluid effects like rain over large scenes.

RainyGS aims to bridge these gaps by offering a framework that integrates the precision of physics-based rain simulation with the rendering efficiency of 3D Gaussian Splatting. Its specific objectives include enabling efficient simulation of raindrop behavior, splashes, reflections, and water accumulation, providing flexible control over rain intensity, and demonstrating superior photorealism and physical accuracy compared to existing state-of-the-art methods in real-world outdoor and large-scale driving scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RainyGS employs a multi-stage pipeline to achieve efficient and photorealistic rain synthesis by integrating physics-based simulation with 3D Gaussian Splatting. The overall process begins with multi-view input images, progresses through scene modeling and auxiliary map extraction, conducts rain simulation, and concludes with a specialized rasterization approach for rendering the rain effects.

**Scene Modeling:**
The method first constructs a 3D representation of the scene. It utilizes PGSR (Planar-based Gaussian Splatting for efficient and high-fidelity surface reconstruction) as a unified module to recover both the appearance and precise geometry of the scene from the multi-view input images. To further enhance geometric quality, the system incorporates normal priors derived from a pretrained monocular normal estimation model to supervise the rendered normal maps. This is reported to improve rendering fidelity and geometric accuracy, particularly in challenging areas. The authors note that PGSR is chosen over alternatives like combining GaussianShader and GOF due to its superior quality and efficiency, despite sacrificing explicit Physically-Based Rendering (PBR) properties (though environment maps can still be replaced).

**Auxiliary Map Extraction:**
Following scene modeling, several auxiliary maps essential for rain simulation are generated.
- **Height Map:** A height map ($H$) of the scene, particularly the ground plane, is created by rasterizing the PGSR model from a top-down orthographic view. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is used to align the 3DGS point cloud with the XY ground plane. A depth map is rendered from an orthographic camera, and the height map is derived by subtracting these depth values from the camera's height above the XY plane.
- **Depth and Normal Maps:** For the current view, per-pixel depth ($D$) and normal ($N$) maps are generated by applying alpha blending techniques (as in standard 3DGS) to the depths and normals of the 3D Gaussian points. These maps serve as base layers for rasterizing subsequent rainy effects.
- **Raindrop Modeling:** Rain is modeled using isotropic 3D Gaussian spheres. For water surface Gaussians, positions correspond to height map pixels, with a fixed radius and normals derived from the normal map. Rain streaks are modeled as densely aligned lines of 3D Gaussian spheres, each with a small radius and a near-white color. Splashes are represented by single Gaussians at impact points, with their scale determined by raindrop volume and longest axis aligned with the projected raindrop velocity.

**Rain Simulation on Height Maps:**
The core rain dynamics are simulated using a height map-based shallow-water model, which is efficient for representing phenomena such as water accumulation, ripples, and large-scale flooding on surfaces. This approach bypasses the need for detailed internal geometric representations of the fluid body, which are often problematic within 3DGS.
- **Shallow Water Equations (SWEs):** The simulation is based on shallow water equations, which model the horizontal velocity ($\mathbf{u}$) and depth ($h$) of water. The total height field ($\eta$) is $\eta = H + h$.
- **Numerical Scheme:** An explicit numerical scheme by Chentanez and Müller is adopted for efficiency and stability. Velocity fields are handled with a semi-Lagrangian method, while height fields are treated with a first-order upwind scheme, ensuring mass conservation.
- **Raindrop Impact and Accumulation:** During each time step, new raindrops are randomly generated with user-defined density, velocity, and direction. Raindrop radii follow an exponential distribution. Raindrops are explicitly updated in position. Upon collision with the ground (i.e., when a raindrop's height is less than or equal to the ground height at its horizontal position), the raindrop disappears, and its volume contributes to the surface water accumulation by updating the corresponding height field pixel. If a raindrop hits an occlusion layer (defined by a second height map $H'$), it disappears without contributing to the ground water.

**Reflection-Aware Water Rasterization:**
The rendering of water and rain involves a two-pass rasterization strategy consistent with the splatting approach.
- **Pass 1 (Water Surface Effects):** This pass renders the combined effects of reflection ($I_{\text{spec}}$), highlight ($I_{\text{highl}}$), and refraction ($I_{\text{refra}}$) from the water surface. The blending is controlled by the Fresnel term ($F$), using the equation $I_0 = (1 - F)I_{\text{refra}} + F(I_{\text{spec}} + I_{\text{highl}})$.
 - **Specular Reflection ($I_{\text{spec}}$):** Screen-Space Reflection (SSR) is employed to approximate reflections efficiently. This technique casts rays from reflective pixels, marches them in screen space using the digital differential analyzer (DDA) algorithm, recovers 3D points, performs collision detection against the depth map, and fetches colors from the rasterized image. A fallback to the environment map is used if no collision is detected or the ray leaves the screen. SSR is highlighted as significantly more efficient than full ray tracing while maintaining comparable visual quality.
 - **Highlights ($I_{\text{highl}}$):** Glossy reflections of a directional light source (sun) extracted from the environment map are computed using the Blinn-Phong shading model.
 - **Refraction ($I_{\text{refra}}$):** An image-based approximation is used to distort the source image based on the screen-projected normal components and water depth, given the shallow nature of the accumulated water.
 - **Fresnel Term ($F$):** The Fresnel-Slick equation determines the ratio of reflection and refraction intensities based on the view angle.
- **Pass 2 (Raindrops and Splashes):** This pass rasterizes the raindrops ($I_1$), including both rain streaks and splash effects. Rain streak color is adjusted based on the base image's normalized brightness to enhance realism under varying lighting.
- **Final Blending:** The outputs from both passes ($I_0$ and $I_1$) are blended together based on their respective depths using screen-space depth information to produce the final rainy image ($I$).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The RainyGS framework demonstrates capabilities in synthesizing dynamic, photorealistic, and physically accurate rain effects across various "in-the-wild" scenes. The main findings and results are summarized as follows:

- **Photorealistic and Physically Accurate Rain Synthesis:** RainyGS successfully generates a comprehensive set of rain phenomena, including flying rain streaks, dynamic water accumulation on surfaces (puddles), ripples formed by raindrops, splashes, and high-order rendering effects such as reflections and refractions. These effects are integrated to produce visually convincing and physically plausible rainy conditions.
- **3D Consistency:** The method maintains 3D consistency across multiple views, ensuring that the synthesized rain effects, water accumulation, and surface interactions remain coherent regardless of the camera viewpoint. This is a distinguishing feature compared to several baseline methods.
- **Efficiency and Interactive Performance:** RainyGS exhibits computational efficiency, enabling the rendering of dynamic rain effects at over 30 frames per second (fps). Quantitative performance metrics show that the method introduces only a small additional time and memory cost compared to the base PGSR method, and is significantly faster than a PGSR implementation with 3D ray tracing or video-based generation models like Runway-V2V (e.g., 0.032s per frame vs. 1.942s for PGSR+RT, and ~0.4s for Runway-V2V in the Garden scene). The computational cost scales approximately linearly with the number of Gaussians.
- **Precise User Control:** The framework provides flexible user control over key rain parameters, including rain intensity (from light drizzle to heavy downpour), water volume, and scene brightness. This interactive control allows users to generate diverse rainy scenarios effectively.
- **Qualitative Comparisons with Baselines:**
 - **Rain Motion:** This video-based method struggles with 3D consistency and produces superficial, unrealistic 2D rain streaks without hydrops or realistic puddles.
 - **Runway Gen-3 Alpha:** While generating general rainy styles, Runway-V2V often alters the original scene geometry and appearance (e.g., changing object materials or colors) and lacks physical accuracy in its dynamic rain, producing random ripples and nearly static rain content that does not respond to specific droplet impacts. It also tends to produce oversaturated colors.
 - **Instruct-GS2GS:** This text-driven 3DGS editing method is limited to static edits and does not support advanced dynamic rain effects such as water accumulation, rain streaks, or dynamic reflections.
 - In contrast, RainyGS consistently outperforms these baselines by preserving scene geometry, producing physically accurate rain dynamics, ensuring multi-view and temporal consistency, and delivering higher overall visual fidelity.
- **Validation on Diverse Datasets:** The effectiveness of RainyGS is demonstrated across a variety of complex, "in-the-wild" scenes from datasets such as MipNeRF360 (Garden, Treehill, Bicycle) and Tanks and Temples (Family, Truck), confirming its robustness in diverse environments.
- **User Study Results:** A user study conducted on Amazon Mechanical Turk indicated a significant user preference for RainyGS over the compared baselines. Across image and video comparisons, RainyGS was preferred over Rain Motion (70.9% for images, 67.5% for videos), Runway-V2V (70.7% for images, 72.0% for videos), and Instruct-GS2GS (65.2% for images, 65.7% for videos).
- **Applications:** The framework's utility extends to practical applications such as augmenting training scenes for autonomous driving systems (demonstrated on Waymo driving scenarios) by creating challenging rainy environments, and for improving complex weather scene modeling.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of RainyGS represents a notable advancement in the field of 3D scene modeling and weather simulation, with several points of significance and potential impact:

- **Novelty and Integration:** RainyGS is presented as the first unified framework for open-world rain synthesis that achieves efficient, realistic, and physically accurate dynamic rain effects within the 3D Gaussian Splatting paradigm. It successfully integrates shallow-water dynamics and screen-space rendering techniques with 3DGS, addressing the limitations of prior approaches that struggled with either physical accuracy, dynamic interaction, or computational efficiency in complex, in-the-wild scenes.
- **Bridging Gaps in Scene Editing:** The work effectively bridges a critical gap between advanced 3D scene representations (like 3DGS) that excel at novel view synthesis but lack sophisticated physics-based editing capabilities, and traditional physics simulations that are realistic but inflexible for arbitrary real-world environments. By leveraging the strengths of both, RainyGS enables a new level of dynamic scene editing for weather effects.
- **Enhanced Realism for Virtual Environments:** The ability to synthesize photorealistic and physically accurate rain effects dynamically has significant implications for virtual reality (VR), augmented reality (AR), and gaming. It can create more immersive and believable virtual environments, enhancing user experience and narrative depth.
- **Impact on Robotics and Autonomous Driving:** For autonomous systems, RainyGS offers a valuable tool for generating diverse and challenging weather conditions in simulation environments. This allows for more robust training and testing of perception and control algorithms under adverse weather, which is crucial for improving safety and reliability in real-world applications. The demonstrated application to Waymo driving scenarios highlights this potential.
- **Efficiency for Real-Time Applications:** Achieving interactive frame rates (over 30 fps) is a key strength of RainyGS. This efficiency makes it suitable for real-time applications where rapid environmental changes or dynamic interactions are required, pushing the boundaries of what is possible with physically-based weather rendering.
- **Flexible User Control:** The provision of precise user control over rain intensity and other parameters offers significant utility for artists, developers, and researchers. It reduces the need for extensive manual effort or reliance on pre-rendered assets, allowing for on-the-fly customization of weather conditions.
- **Foundational Work for Future Weather Simulations:** While the paper acknowledges limitations regarding complex deep-wave interactions or occluded reflections/refractions, RainyGS establishes a strong foundation. The approach to integrating physics within a modern 3D representation like 3DGS can serve as a template for simulating other complex dynamic weather phenomena or environmental interactions. Future research building upon this framework could further enhance realism and address current limitations.

In summary, RainyGS contributes a robust and efficient methodology for dynamic rain synthesis, broadening the applicability of 3D scene modeling for realistic environmental effects and offering practical benefits across multiple domains requiring high-fidelity virtual environments and robust simulation.
