# CKT-WAM: Parameter-Efficient Context Knowledge Transfer Between World Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework named CKT-WAM enables parameter-efficient knowledge transfer from a large teacher World Action Model to a lightweight student, achieving strong zero-shot generalization and robust real-world robotic manipulation with only 1.17% trainable parameters. The system overcomes architectural heterogeneity by injecting a compact contextual representation through the student's native conditioning pathway.

## Problem

- World Action Models (WAMs) often incur high computational overhead and test-time latency due to explicit video synthesis.
- Efficiently transferring action-relevant world knowledge between WAMs with heterogeneous architectures is difficult, as traditional methods struggle with mismatched latent interfaces and high adaptation costs.
- Existing knowledge distillation or fine-tuning approaches are either computationally expensive, unstable with disparate latent parameterizations, or impose rigid architectural constraints.

## Method

- **CKT-WAM** constructs a compact ==contextual interface== by extracting hidden states from an intermediate layer of a powerful teacher WAM, repurposing it as a single-pass observation encoder.
- A parameter-efficient ==CKT module== compresses teacher states using ==learnable-query cross-attention== and adapts them via both a generalized adapter and ==sparsely-routed specialized adapters==.
- Context is injected into the student WAM's frozen backbone by appending to its textual embeddings, serving as keys and values for cross-attention without modifying the student's core architecture or query mechanism.

## Results

- CKT-WAM achieved **86.1% total success** on the LIBERO-Plus zero-shot generalization benchmark, outperforming all prior VLA and WAM baselines, while utilizing only **1.17% trainable parameters**.
- The framework nearly matched full fine-tuning (**86.1% vs. 86.3%**) with a drastically smaller parameter budget, outperforming other parameter-efficient adaptation methods.
- In real-world multi-step, long-horizon manipulation tasks, CKT-WAM achieved the best average success rate of **83.3%** across four diverse tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Efficient knowledge transfer between heterogeneous WAMs can be achieved by extracting and injecting compact, task-relevant contextual information rather than relying on full representation matching.
> - A combination of a generalized knowledge transfer branch and sparsely-routed, instance-dependent specialized adapters significantly improves robustness and generalization to diverse out-of-distribution shifts.
> - Freezing both teacher and student backbones, and only training a lightweight context transfer module, allows for high parameter efficiency and architectural flexibility while effectively leveraging teacher knowledge.
