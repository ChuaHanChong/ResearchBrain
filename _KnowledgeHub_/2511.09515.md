# WMPO: World Model-based Policy Optimization for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> WMPO introduces a framework for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policy optimization that uses a pixel-space video-generative world model to enable reinforcement learning entirely in imagination, overcoming the need for real-world interactions. The method facilitates self-correction and efficient task execution, achieving superior sample efficiency and robustness in both simulation and real-world robotic tasks.

## Problem

- VLA models trained via imitation learning (IL) are brittle, lack self-correction, and struggle with out-of-distribution states, leading to compounding errors.
- Applying reinforcement learning (RL) directly to real robots for VLAs is severely constrained by high sample complexity, making it impractical, unsafe, and costly.
- Many existing model-based RL approaches use latent world models, which creates a representational mismatch with powerful VLA foundation models pretrained on pixel-rich images.

## Method

- A high-fidelity, pixel-space ==video-generative world model==, based on a modified ==OpenSora architecture==, is developed to generate imagined trajectories conditioned on VLA policy actions.
- A lightweight, learned ==reward model== is trained on real trajectories to provide binary success/failure feedback for the automated evaluation of imagined rollouts.
- An on-policy ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== algorithm is applied within the imagined environment, leveraging ==dynamic sampling== to enable scalable and principled policy updates.

## Results

- WMPO achieved superior sample efficiency and overall performance in simulation, outperforming established RL baselines by up to **+15.2 points** in mean success rate.
- Qualitative analysis revealed emergent self-correction capabilities and more efficient, smoother task execution (significantly shorter imagined trajectories), with robust generalization to novel disruption scenarios (spatial, background, texture changes) and stable improvements in a lifelong learning setup.
- Deployed on a real Cobot Mobile ALOHA platform, WMPO achieved a **70%** success rate on a fine-grained manipulation task, surpassing a base policy (**53%**) and offline DPO (**60%**) over **30** trials.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Operating a world model in pixel space is crucial for aligning imagined trajectories with the rich visual features that VLA foundation models are pre-trained to understand.
> - Fine-tuning the world model on real rollout trajectories collected from the policy itself enables accurate simulation of policy-specific behaviors, including failure modes, which is essential for effective RL.
> - Decoupling on-policy RL from physical interaction, by performing it entirely within a learned world model, allows for scalable learning of complex behaviors like self-correction and efficient execution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen a significant paradigm shift with the advent of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models, exemplified by works like RT-2, OpenVLA, and π0.5, aim to enable general-purpose robots to understand natural language instructions and execute complex tasks by mapping visual inputs to actions. The dominant training approach for these VLAs has been imitation learning (IL) from large-scale human demonstrations. While IL has been remarkably effective in mimicking demonstrated behaviors, it inherently suffers from brittleness: policies trained via IL often fail when encountering states not present in the training data, leading to compounding errors and an inability to recover or self-correct. This limitation severely restricts their robustness and adaptability in real-world, open-ended environments.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) offers a natural solution to the brittleness of IL by allowing agents to learn through active interaction and self-improvement. RL-trained policies can become more robust and capable of recovering from failures. However, applying RL directly to real robots faces significant challenges, primarily high sample complexity. Training on real robots requires millions of interactions, which is impractical, unsafe, and time-consuming (Fig. 1b). Prior efforts to address this have involved human intervention [7–9] (labor-intensive and not scalable) or direct simulation (limited by simulator accuracy and engineering overhead).

This paper positions itself at the intersection of VLA models, model-based RL, and generative world models. The emergence of large-scale generative models, especially video-generative world models, provides a compelling alternative to costly real-world interactions. These models can learn environment dynamics from data and simulate future transitions, enabling policies to learn "in imagination." However, integrating these world models with existing VLAs is not straightforward. Many classical model-based RL approaches [15–20] operate in abstract latent spaces, which creates a fundamental mismatch with powerful VLA foundation models pretrained on real-world images. The rich, pretrained visual understanding of VLAs cannot be directly applied within a mismatched latent dynamics model. WMPO directly addresses this gap by proposing a pixel-space video-generative world model, arguing that this approach is crucial for aligning imagined trajectories with the features VLAs are pretrained on, thereby effectively bridging the world model with the policy's pre-trained knowledge. This focus on pixel-level fidelity for VLA-compatible world models is a key differentiator from prior model-based RL works that primarily focused on latent space dynamics [15–18].

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of WMPO is to enable **on-policy Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Reinforcement Learning (RL) without direct interaction with the real physical environment**, thereby overcoming the inherent limitations of current VLA training paradigms.

The core motivations driving the development of WMPO are:

1. **Addressing the Brittleness and Lack of Self-Correction in Imitation Learning (IL)-trained VLAs:** Current VLA models, largely trained via IL, struggle with out-of-distribution states, leading to suboptimal actions, compounding errors, and an inability to recover from failures (Fig. 1a). WMPO aims to instill robustness and self-correction capabilities in VLA policies.
2. **Mitigating the Sample Inefficiency of Real-World RL for VLAs:** While RL offers a solution to IL's brittleness, its application to real robots is severely constrained by the enormous number of interactions required, which are often impractical, unsafe, and costly (Fig. 1b). WMPO seeks to achieve sample-efficient RL by performing policy optimization entirely "in imagination."
3. **Bridging the Gap between World Models and Pretrained VLA Features:** Many existing model-based RL approaches use latent world models. This creates a representational mismatch with powerful VLA foundation models that are pretrained on web-scale, pixel-rich real-world images. The motivation is to develop a world model that operates in pixel space, ensuring that the "imagined" trajectories are consistent with the VLA's pretrained visual understanding, thereby maximizing the leverage of VLA's capabilities.
4. **Enabling Scalable and Principled On-Policy RL:** WMPO is motivated to provide a principled framework for on-policy RL, which typically offers stronger performance than off-policy methods but is harder to implement for VLAs due to data collection constraints. By decoupling RL from physical interaction, WMPO aims to make scalable on-policy optimization feasible for VLAs.

In essence, WMPO is motivated by the desire to unlock the full potential of VLA models for general-purpose robotic manipulation by combining the self-improvement benefits of RL with the data efficiency of world models, all while maintaining compatibility with the rich visual representations of modern VLAs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WMPO introduces a novel, principled framework for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policy optimization that operates entirely within a learned, pixel-space world model, eliminating the need for real-world interactions during policy learning.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The VLA manipulation task is framed as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) M = (S, A, P, R).
- **State Space (S):** Composed of image observations (sequences I$_{0:K}$) and a language instruction (g). The assumption is that robot states are solely defined by image observations.
- **Action Space (A):** Consists of "action chunks," which are sequences of D-DoF control vectors. The VLA policy π$_{θ}$ learns to sample these action chunks. Each dimension is discretized into 256 bins for optimization.
- **Transition Function (P):** Realized by a parameterized world model p$_{ϕ}$(s$_{t+1}$ | s$_t$, a$_t$), which generates future observations. Imagined trajectories τ are sampled by iteratively interacting the world model with the VLA policy, starting from an initial real environment state.
- **Reward Function (R):** A learned model R$_{ψ}$(τ) that provides a binary outcome ({0, 1}) for task success based on the full imagined trajectory.

The objective is to maximize the expected cumulative return of imagined trajectories: max$_{θ}$ E$_{τ∼π_θ,p_ϕ}$[R$_{ψ}$(τ)].

#### 3.2. Generative World Model

The core of WMPO is a high-fidelity, pixel-space video-generative world model (p$_{ϕ}$).

- **Imagined Trajectory Generation:** Given initial frames (I$_{0:c}$) and a language instruction (g), the policy (π$_{θ}$) predicts an action chunk (a$_{i:i+K}$). The world model then generates the next K frames (I$_{i:i+K}$) conditioned on the last 'c' observed frames and the predicted action chunk. This autoregressive process continues until a full trajectory (τ) is generated, which is then evaluated by the reward model.
- **Model Architecture:** The world model builds upon the OpenSora video diffusion backbone. Critically, it replaces OpenSora's 3D VAE with the 2D VAE from SDXL. This modification is chosen to better preserve fine-grained motion details and avoid temporal distortions, ensuring pixel-level fidelity important for VLAs. The diffusion process operates in the VAE's latent space, but images are decoded back into pixel space for VLA optimization to leverage pretrained knowledge.
- **Robustness Techniques for Long-Horizon Generation:**
 - **Noisy-Frame Conditioning:** To combat error accumulation in long autoregressive rollouts, conditional frames (I$_{i−m:i}$) are perturbed with diffusion noise during training. This improves robustness to imperfect conditioning and enables stable generation of hundreds of frames without significant quality loss.
 - **Frame-Level Action Control:** To achieve precise action conditioning and better action-frame alignment, WMPO incorporates a mechanism inspired by Zhu et al.. This extends the AdaLN block to inject action signals and diffusion timestep embeddings at the frame level, modulating feature representations (x$_i$) within transformer blocks.
- **Policy Behavior Alignment:** The world model is initially pretrained on the large-scale Open X-Embodiment (OXE) dataset, providing broad knowledge of physical dynamics. However, to faithfully simulate policy-specific behaviors, especially failure modes which are underrepresented in expert demonstrations, the world model is fine-tuned on **real rollout trajectories collected from the policy itself**. This adapts the world model to the policy's (state, action) distribution, making its imagination of failures more accurate.

#### 3.3. Reward Model

A lightweight reward model (R$_{ψ}$) is crucial for automated success/failure judgment.
- It is trained on real trajectories. Successful trajectories' terminal clips (c$_N$) serve as positive samples, while arbitrary clips from failed trajectories and non-terminal clips from successful ones are negatives.
- Implemented as a VideoMAE encoder with a linear head, trained with binary cross-entropy loss.
- At inference, it uses a sliding window to compute clip-wise success probabilities. A trajectory is deemed successful if any clip's probability exceeds a validation-selected threshold (τ$_{thr}$). This design avoids complex reward shaping and reward hacking while reliably distinguishing outcomes (F1 score > 0.95).

#### 3.4. On-Policy Reinforcement Learning with WMPO

WMPO leverages its imagined environment to perform on-policy RL.
- **Decoupling RL from Real-World Interaction:** Costly real-world rollouts are replaced by model-generated trajectories, enabling scalable online learning.
- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** GRPO is chosen as the policy optimization algorithm due to its demonstrated stability and scalability, particularly in sparse reward settings. WMPO naturally supports repeated rollouts from the same initial state, a feature crucial for large-scale GRPO training that is difficult in the physical world.
- **Trajectory Sampling:** From an initial real state (s$_0$), a group of imagined trajectories ({τ$_1$..., τ$_G$}) is sampled using the current policy (π$_{θ_{old}}$) within the world model. The reward model evaluates each trajectory (R$_i$ ∈ {0, 1}). A "Dynamic Sampling" strategy discards groups where all trajectories are either successful or unsuccessful, ensuring a balanced training signal for relative optimization. Log-probabilities for action chunks under π$_{θ_{old}}$ are pre-computed.
- **Policy Update:** The policy parameters (θ) are updated using an objective (Eq. 4) derived from DAPO, which removes KL divergence regularization. This encourages exploration of novel behaviors and reduces memory consumption. The update maximizes the expected advantage (ˆA$_i$), which normalizes the reward (R$_i$) relative to other trajectories in the group.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

WMPO was rigorously evaluated through extensive experiments in both Mimicgen simulation environments and real-world settings, demonstrating its effectiveness across multiple dimensions.

1. **Superior Sample Efficiency and Overall Performance in Simulation:**
 - WMPO consistently outperformed established RL baselines (GRPO and DPO) across four fine-grained manipulation tasks (Coffee, StackThree, ThreePieceAssembly, Square) in the Mimicgen simulation (Table 1).
 - With a small rollout budget (P=128), WMPO surpassed the strongest baseline by +9.8 points in mean success rate. When the budget increased (P=1280), the performance margin expanded to +15.2 points, demonstrating robust scalability and efficient utilization of additional data.
 - GRPO often underperformed with limited updates, while DPO plateaued due to static data reuse. WMPO's iterative improvement contrasts sharply, highlighting its ability to leverage its learned world model for sustained gains.
 - The learned reward model achieved an F1 score above 0.95 across all tasks, reliably distinguishing success from failure and preventing reward hacking.

2. **Emergent Behaviors: Self-Correction and Efficient Execution:**
 - Qualitative analysis revealed two key emergent behaviors in WMPO-trained policies, absent in imitation learning baselines.
 - **Self-Correction:** For tasks like "Square" (inserting the square into a stick), WMPO-trained policies learned to recover from near-failure states, such as collisions (Fig. 3). Instead of continuously pushing against an obstacle like the base policy, WMPO autonomously lifted, realigned, and correctly inserted the square. This ability is difficult to acquire through imitation alone.
 - **Efficiency and Smoothness:** WMPO policies executed tasks more efficiently and smoothly. Successful trajectories generated by WMPO were significantly shorter (Fig. 5), indicating that the policy rarely became "stuck" in suboptimal states, thus avoiding timeouts and completing tasks faster.

3. **Robust Generalization to Novel Scenarios:**
 - WMPO demonstrated strong generalization capabilities across three novel disruption scenarios: spatial (random stick position), background (grey background), and texture (different base color) (Fig. 4).
 - WMPO consistently achieved the best performance across all disruption types compared to base policy, GRPO, and DPO (Table 2). DPO, while showing modest in-distribution gains, degraded significantly under distribution shifts, suggesting reliance on spurious cues. WMPO, trained in the world model, learned more generalizable manipulation strategies.

4. **Capacity for Lifelong Learning:**
 - WMPO exhibited stable and substantial improvements in a lifelong learning setup, where the policy iteratively collected real trajectories and then optimized itself within the world model.
 - On the StackThree task (Fig. 6), WMPO consistently improved its success rate over iterative updates, outperforming both DPO (which suffered from unstable training) and a base policy trained with an increasing amount of human-collected expert demonstrations (which is less scalable than self-collected data).

5. **Effectiveness in Real-World Robotics:**
 - WMPO was successfully deployed on a real Cobot Mobile ALOHA platform for a challenging "Insert the square into the stick" task with tight tolerances (5 mm clearance).
 - Compared to the base policy (53% success) and offline DPO (60% success), WMPO achieved a superior success rate of 70% over 30 trials.
 - The world model accurately predicted future outcomes, including failure cases, even for trajectories never observed during training, demonstrating its ability to capture precise task dynamics in the real world (Fig. 7, Fig. 8). While some rare failure cases for prediction were observed (Fig. 9), the overall reliability was high enough for stable policy optimization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

WMPO represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic learning, particularly for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Its key significance lies in providing a principled, scalable, and data-efficient framework that addresses several long-standing challenges in robot reinforcement learning.

Firstly, by grounding policy optimization entirely within a video-generative world model, WMPO effectively **decouples RL from costly real-world interactions**. This breakthrough eliminates the primary bottleneck of sample inefficiency, making complex VLA RL tasks feasible and drastically reducing hardware costs, safety concerns, and training time. This has a profound impact on the scalability of robotic learning, enabling researchers and practitioners to iterate on policies much faster and at a lower cost.

Secondly, WMPO's focus on **pixel-based predictions** directly addresses the fundamental mismatch between abstract latent world models and the rich, pre-trained visual features of VLA foundation models. By operating in the pixel space, WMPO ensures that the "imagined" trajectories are consistent with what VLA models are designed to understand, maximizing the leverage of their extensive pre-trained knowledge. This design choice is critical for effectively integrating powerful general-purpose VLAs with model-based RL.

Thirdly, the framework's ability to facilitate **on-policy Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)** in imagination provides stronger performance benefits compared to often-used off-policy methods, which can suffer from value estimation biases. The dynamic sampling and robust autoregressive generation techniques further ensure stable and effective learning from imagined experiences, including failure scenarios.

The demonstrated **emergent behaviors, such as self-correction**, are particularly impactful. Policies that can recover from errors are far more robust and deployable in unstructured, dynamic environments. The ability to learn these complex behaviors, which are difficult to capture through imitation learning alone, signifies a major step towards more intelligent and autonomous robots. Furthermore, the **improved efficiency and smoothness of task execution** derived from WMPO-trained policies suggest a pathway to more natural and human-like robotic operations.

WMPO's strong **generalization capabilities** to novel disruption scenarios (spatial, background, texture changes) and its support for **iterative lifelong learning** further underscore its potential. This means robots can adapt to unforeseen environmental variations and continuously improve their skills over time, moving beyond static, task-specific training.

Finally, the validation of WMPO in **real-world robotic experiments** (on the Cobot Mobile ALOHA platform) confirms its practical applicability and effectiveness beyond simulations. Achieving a 70% success rate on a fine-grained manipulation task with tight tolerances highlights its readiness for real-world deployment.

In conclusion, WMPO offers a transformative paradigm for advancing VLA RL. It makes significant strides in sample efficiency, policy robustness, and generalizability, thereby accelerating the development of truly capable and adaptable general-purpose robotic systems. While the current work focuses on discretized action representations, its extensibility to more expressive policy classes (e.g., flow-matching based policies) points towards even broader future applicability and impact across diverse robotic manipulation challenges.
