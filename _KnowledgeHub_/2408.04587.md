# FORGE: Force-Guided Exploration for Robust Contact-Rich Manipulation under Uncertainty

> [!summary] Summary
> FORGE trains force-aware sim-to-real assembly policies with RL in IsaacGym. Policies are conditioned on a scalar maximum-force threshold, penalized for exceeding it, and trained under randomized controller gains, part properties, and actuation dead-zones. A shared-weight success predictor enables early termination and automatic threshold tuning. Across 1000+ real trials, FORGE beats IndustReal on gear meshing and nut threading under 5mm pose error.

## Problem

- Prior sim-to-real assembly policies such as IndustReal neither observe nor adapt to contact forces, so safe force profiles depend entirely on careful controller design and gain tuning.
- Heuristic contact search such as spiral search bounds applied force but is task-specific and inefficient, while RL work typically assumes accurate part poses rather than millimeter-scale estimation error.
- Earlier force-threshold methods fix one large threshold during training, around 40N, too coarse for small parts that slip far below it, and run policies for a fixed duration.

## Method

- **FORGE** uses ==force-threshold conditioning==: the policy takes a scalar maximum allowable force as input and is penalized proportionally to force exceeding it, so the threshold is tunable at deployment without retraining.
- ==Dynamics randomization== over controller stiffness and action scale, giving max commandable force 6.4-20.0N, plus part mass and friction and a randomized actuator dead-zone clamping small commanded forces.
- ==Success prediction== as an extra ==early-termination action head== sharing policy weights, trained by a prediction-error penalty under ==recurrent PPO== with ==asymmetric actor-critic==, driving automatic force-threshold escalation.

## Results

- On M16 nut threading FORGE reached **0.69** success versus **0.40** for the non-force baseline and **0.36** for IndustReal, with successful-episode duration **11.4s** versus **22.3s**.
- Gear meshing reached **0.98** success versus **0.87** for IndustReal; mean contact force fell to **5.5-8.0N** from **9.5-12.6N**, and termination delay dropped up to **66%**.
- Ablations: dropping force observations cut nut success to **0.40** and termination precision to **0.33**; automatic tuning inserted a **15N** snap-fit **10/10**, full gearbox assembly **3/5**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Force feedback is the cheap modality that turns pose uncertainty into recoverable search, and it also signals task completion better than proprioception alone in contact-rich tasks.
> - Randomizing controller gains shifts deployment tuning from opaque control parameters to one interpretable physical scalar, a transferable recipe for cutting sim-to-real tuning burden.
> - A single global force limit is a genuine limitation: phased tasks need phase-dependent limits, since alignment demands low force while snap-fit engagement demands high force.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses challenges in robotic assembly, specifically focusing on the sim-to-real transfer of force-aware manipulation policies for contact-rich tasks under significant pose uncertainty. Sim-to-real techniques have advanced dexterous manipulation and legged locomotion, but their application to robotic assembly has been more recent due to the requirement for efficient and accurate simulation of detailed, low-clearance parts.

Existing methods for robotic assembly often face issues with policies exhibiting aggressive behavior, leading to part slip or damage, especially when contact-based search behaviors are necessary under pose uncertainty. Heuristic approaches like spiral search can limit force but are task-specific and potentially inefficient. Reinforcement learning (RL) offers a more general paradigm, but previous RL-based approaches for assembly, such as the IndustReal framework, typically rely on careful controller design and gain tuning to manage force profiles, rather than enabling policies to observe and adapt to contact forces directly. Some works optimize or adapt gains online, but the desired force profile is task-dependent (e.g., threading a nut vs. snap-fit insertion).

This research positions itself by proposing a framework, FORGE, that specifically addresses these limitations by integrating force awareness directly into the policy learning process. It aims to reduce the reliance on external tuning procedures and develop policies that can inherently modulate their actions to maintain desired force profiles while being robust to pose uncertainty and variations in dynamics. The work contributes to the broader field of robust robot learning and sim-to-real transfer, particularly for industrial manipulation and assembly applications where safety and efficiency are critical. It also extends the application of success prediction in sim-to-real settings, providing mechanisms for efficient task termination and autonomous parameter tuning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for developing FORGE are:

- **Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer for Contact-Rich Assembly under Uncertainty**: The core objective is to enable policies learned entirely in simulation to transfer effectively to real-world robots for complex assembly tasks involving contact and tight tolerances, even when there is significant uncertainty in part poses (up to 5mm position estimation error). The motivation stems from the observation that naive policies can be too aggressive, causing part damage or failure, especially when searching for contact points under uncertainty.
- **Developing Force-Aware Policies**: A key goal is to enable policies to use external force observations to achieve efficient and gentle interaction behaviors. This is motivated by the need for policies to adapt their actions based on contact forces to avoid excessive forces that lead to slip or damage, while still applying sufficient force when required for tasks like snap-fit insertion. Existing RL approaches often lack this explicit force awareness.
- **Simplifying Deployment and Reducing Tuning**: The research aims to simplify the deployment of learned policies by reducing the amount of manual tuning required. This is motivated by the complexity and time-consuming nature of traditional controller gain tuning or system identification for each new task or set of parts. FORGE seeks to achieve this by using dynamics randomization to make policies robust to a range of underlying dynamics parameters, effectively distilling tuning down to an interpretable scalar force-threshold.
- **Enabling Adaptive Force Profiles**: The paper seeks to provide a mechanism for policies to adapt their force profile based on task requirements. This is motivated by the understanding that different assembly tasks (e.g., delicate nut threading vs. forceful snap-fit) demand different levels of applied force. The proposed force threshold mechanism, combined with automatic tuning, addresses this need.
- **Efficient Policy Termination**: The work aims to develop a method for policies to autonomously determine task completion and terminate efficiently. This is motivated by the inefficiency of fixed-duration policy execution in industrial settings, which can lead to premature termination or unnecessary delays, affecting cycle times.
- **Automatic Force Threshold Tuning**: For tasks where the required force is not known a priori (e.g., snap-fit connectors with unknown material properties), an objective is to provide an automatic procedure for tuning the force threshold. This is motivated by the practical challenge of setting optimal force parameters for novel tasks.

In summary, the motivation behind FORGE is to enhance the safety, robustness, efficiency, and adaptability of sim-to-real robot assembly by embedding force-awareness, dynamics randomization, and success prediction directly into the policy learning and execution framework, thereby addressing critical limitations of prior approaches.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FORGE operates within a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) framework and integrates several components to achieve robust sim-to-real transfer for contact-rich manipulation.

#### 3.1. A. POMDP Formulation

- **States (S)**: Comprise the end-effector (EE) pose and velocities, fixed part pose, held part pose, held part velocity, contact force experienced by the EE (F_ee), and time-invariant dynamics properties of the robot, controller, and parts (Ψ).
- **Observations (Ω)**: Policies observe noisy EE pose and velocity, estimated contact force (F_ee), noisy estimate of the fixed part’s pose, and the previous action. Pose and velocity of the held part, and the dynamics parameters (Ψ), are not observed due to real-world estimation difficulties.
- **Actions (A)**: Control targets for a task-space impedance controller, specifically for (x, y, z, yaw) dimensions, given the assumption of upright part orientations.
- **Transition Function (T_Ψ)**: Modeled using the IsaacGym simulator, parameterized by dynamics parameters Ψ. The sim-to-real gap is attributed to the mismatch between simulated and real-world Ψ.
- **Observation Function (O)**: Incorporates up to 5mm error for the fixed part's position and Gaussian noise for other observations.
- **Reward Function (R)**: A keypoint reward, R_kp, is used, modified for small, threaded geometries (coarse-to-fine kernel). Two discrete bonus rewards, R_bonus, are given upon part centering and task success to aid learning under pose uncertainty.

#### 3.2. B. Force Threshold

- **Mechanism**: Policies are conditioned on a force threshold, F_th. During training, an additional penalty term, R_contact_pen = −β \* max(0, ||F_ee|| − F_th), is added to the reward function. This encourages the policy to avoid exceeding the specified threshold.
- **Purpose**: At deployment, F_th can be set or tuned according to task requirements, allowing for modulation between gentle and forceful interactions (e.g., low for nut threading, higher for snap-fit).

#### 3.3. C. Dynamics Randomization (DR)

- **Objective**: To reduce the sim-to-real gap by training policies to be robust to a wide range of dynamics parameters, making them less dependent on precise system identification. The training objective optimizes expected return over a distribution of dynamics parameters, P(Ψ).
- **Randomized Variables**:
 - **Controller Randomization**: Varies the impedance controller parameters (proportional gain k_p and action scale λ), which collectively determine the maximum commandable forces. This makes policies robust to different controller implementations and gain tunings.
 - **Part Randomization**: Randomizes part mass and friction coefficients to account for material variations.
 - **Robot Dynamics Randomization**: Introduces a randomized "dead-zone" in simulation for applied forces. This simulates phenomena like joint friction, where small commanded forces might not result in actual motion, encouraging the policy to increase its target when necessary.

#### 3.4. D. Success Prediction

- **Mechanism**: A success predictor is trained jointly with the policy by expanding the policy's action space to include an early termination action, a_ET ∈. A penalty, R_ET = -|a_ET - y_t|, is added to the reward, where y_t is the true success label. This also encourages exploratory actions to verify task completion.
- **Early Termination**: At deployment, episodes can terminate when the predicted success confidence, a_ET, exceeds a predefined threshold, p_term, leading to dynamic and efficient episode lengths.
- **Force Threshold Tuning**: For tasks where the optimal F_th is unknown, success prediction enables an automatic tuning procedure. Starting with a conservative low threshold, F_th is iteratively increased if the policy times out without predicting success. This process continues until success is predicted, without manual resets.

#### 3.5. E. Policy Training and Experimental Setup

- **Simulator**: All policies are trained in IsaacGym, leveraging its GPU-accelerated physics simulation.
- **RL Algorithm**: Recurrent PPO with asymmetric actor-critic is used to handle partial observability.
- **Observations and Actions**: Relative to the fixed part to promote generalization across the workspace.
- **Robot System**: Franka Panda robot with FrankaPy for impedance control, using joint-torque sensing for EE-frame force estimation.
- **Evaluation**: Policies are evaluated on tasks including Peg Insertion, Gear Meshing, Nut Threading, and Snap-Fit Insertion. Performance is compared against an IndustReal baseline, a generic baseline, and ablations of FORGE's key components, across various pose estimation error levels and controller gains. Multi-stage assembly of a planetary gearbox is also demonstrated.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings and results, derived from extensive real-world trials and simulated experiments:

#### 4.1. A. Baseline Comparisons (Q1, Q2)

- **Robustness (Q1)**: FORGE exhibited improved sim-to-real transfer performance, outperforming the generic Baseline for all tasks (Peg Insertion, Gear Meshing, M16 Nut) and IndustReal for Gear Meshing and Nut Threading. For Peg Insertion, FORGE's success rate was comparable to IndustReal (0.84 vs 0.82), while for Gear Meshing and M16 Nut, FORGE achieved higher success rates (0.98 vs 0.87 and 0.69 vs 0.36, respectively).
- **Behavioral Properties (Q2)**: FORGE policies generally resulted in lower mean and maximum contact forces compared to both IndustReal and the generic Baseline. For example, in Peg Insertion, FORGE's F_mean was 5.51N compared to 9.45N for IndustReal and 11.81N for Baseline. This indicates gentler interactions. FORGE also led to significantly shorter trial durations for successful episodes compared to IndustReal, particularly for Gear Meshing (3.14s vs 8.44s) and M16 Nut (11.38s vs 22.27s).
- **Ablation Studies**: Ablations demonstrated that both force observations and the excessive force penalty are important for FORGE's performance gains, especially for success rate and force profiles. Dynamics randomization, while not always directly affecting success rate, contributed to robustness across controller gains.

#### 4.2. B. Noise Analysis (Q3)

- **Performance with Pose Uncertainty**: FORGE policies maintained good performance across various levels of position estimation error (Low: 0-1mm, Medium: 1-2.5mm, High: 2.5-5mm). While performance generally degraded with error > 2.5mm, FORGE still outperformed IndustReal at all noise levels for the more complex Gear Meshing and Nut Threading tasks. This indicates that force feedback is a valuable modality for recovering from substantial pose uncertainty.

#### 4.3. C. Force Analysis (Q4, Q5)

- **Excessive-Force Penalty (Q4)**: The ablation study (FORGE No FP) showed that without the excessive-force penalty, policies (even with force observations) achieved lower success rates. Policies deployed with higher controller gains naturally experienced more force, highlighting the role of the penalty in explicitly shaping force-aware behavior.
- **Gains Robustness (Q5)**: FORGE policies demonstrated robust success rates and consistently low contact forces across a wide range of controller gains (from 400k_p to 800k_p) at deployment. In contrast, policies without force observations or dynamics randomization showed less consistent performance and higher force spreads, often exceeding the desired force threshold at higher gains. IndustReal policies also showed increasing force with higher gains, confirming its dependence on careful gain tuning. This indicates that FORGE effectively enables the policy to modulate its actions based on force feedback, rather than relying on fixed controller gains.

#### 4.4. D. Success Prediction Analysis (Q6, Q7)

- **Sim-to-Real Transfer (Q6)**: The success prediction mechanism, trained solely in simulation, transferred effectively to the real world. FORGE policies achieved high Early Termination Precision (0.74-1.00) and Recall (0.74-1.00) across all tasks, and generally resulted in the shortest Delay Times (2.19s - 6.54s) compared to baselines and ablations, particularly when force observations were utilized. This demonstrates the utility of force feedback for sensing task completion.
- **Force Threshold Tuning (Q7)**: For the snap-fit task, which required 15N for insertion, the automatic force threshold tuning procedure successfully identified the necessary force. In 8 out of 10 complete tuning trials, successful insertion was achieved, with 9/10 instances succeeding on the third execution (when F_th exceeded 15N). The policy’s success prediction was correct in 27 out of 29 executions during this process. A limitation identified was that a single force threshold might be suboptimal for tasks requiring varying forces across different phases.

#### 4.5. E. Multi-Stage Assembly

- **Planetary Gearbox Assembly**: FORGE policies were successfully applied to the multi-stage assembly of a planetary gearbox, executing 8 contact-rich primitives. This included Ring Insertion (5/5 success), Small Gear Meshing (15/15), Large Gear Meshing (3/5), and M16 Nut Threading (15/15). The complete assembly succeeded in 3 out of 5 trials.
- **Efficiency**: Early terminations, powered by success prediction, saved an average of 65 seconds per trial compared to a fixed-duration execution.

#### 4.6. F. Generalization across Part Geometry (Appendix G)

- Simulated experiments showed that specialist FORGE policies, trained on a single part size for Peg Insertion, Gear Meshing, and Nut Threading, generally generalized well to other part sizes within the same task. Exceptions occurred when there were significant geometry differences (e.g., training on large gears and evaluating on a small gear with a much smaller base).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The FORGE framework presents several significant advancements with potential impacts across robotic manipulation and industrial automation:

- **Enhanced Robustness to Uncertainty**: By combining force threshold mechanisms with dynamics randomization, FORGE enables policies to perform robustly in contact-rich tasks despite significant levels of position estimation error (up to 5mm). This is crucial for real-world deployment where perfect perception is often unavailable. The ability to autonomously adapt to uncertainty reduces the need for high-precision vision systems or extensive calibration.
- **Force-Aware and Safe Interaction**: The explicit conditioning on a force threshold and the use of an excessive-force penalty in the reward function lead to policies that exhibit safer and gentler search behaviors. This prevents part slip, damage, and aggressive robot actions, which are common failure modes in contact-rich assembly. This directly addresses a critical safety concern in industrial robotics.
- **Simplified Sim-to-Real Transfer and Deployment**: FORGE reduces the complexity of sim-to-real transfer by making policies robust to variations in controller gains and robot dynamics through randomization. This shifts the deployment tuning from complex controller parameters to a single, interpretable scalar force-threshold, thereby lowering the barrier to entry for deploying learned manipulation skills in industrial settings.
- **Increased Efficiency in Task Execution**: The integration of success prediction allows for efficient, dynamic termination of policies, reducing overall cycle times by avoiding unnecessary waiting periods. The reported savings of up to 66% in delay times demonstrate a direct impact on industrial throughput and operational cost.
- **Autonomous Adaptation to Task Requirements**: The ability of FORGE policies to predict task success enables automated tuning of the force threshold for forceful insertion tasks where optimal forces are not known a priori. This adaptive capability allows robots to tackle a wider variety of assembly tasks, including those with unknown or changing material properties, without requiring human intervention for parameter adjustments.
- **Foundational for Complex Assembly**: The successful demonstration of multi-stage assembly of a planetary gearbox, requiring a diverse set of contact-rich skills (nut threading, insertion, gear meshing), highlights FORGE's potential as a foundational framework for tackling long-horizon, complex assembly sequences in unstructured or semi-structured environments.

In summary, FORGE contributes to the development of more intelligent, adaptable, and safe robotic systems for manufacturing and assembly, moving towards a future where robots can handle a broader range of complex, contact-rich manipulation tasks with minimal human oversight and configuration.
