# HaWoR: World-Space Hand Motion Reconstruction from Egocentric Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> HaWoR reconstructs 3D hand motion in world coordinates from egocentric video by decoupling camera-space hand pose estimation from world-space camera trajectory estimation, using an adaptive SLAM module and a motion-infiller network for out-of-frame hands, achieving state-of-the-art results on hand and camera trajectory benchmarks.

## Problem

- Prior 3D hand pose methods reconstruct only camera-frame poses per single image, ignoring world-space motion needed for egocentric video understanding.
- Standard SLAM systems degrade under egocentric conditions since large moving hands corrupt feature matching and bundle adjustment used for camera localization.
- Existing world-grounded body motion methods rely on heavy per-sequence optimization or third-person priors that do not transfer to hands, which frequently leave the view frustum.

## Method

- **HaWoR**'s ==transformer-based hand motion network== with ==Image Attention Module== and ==Pose Attention Module== adds ==temporal self-attention== over ==ViT features== and ==MANO pose tokens== for coherent camera-frame reconstruction.
- ==Adaptive egocentric SLAM== masks detected hand regions from ==DROID-SLAM=='s input images and confidence maps before ==dense bundle adjustment==, removing dynamic hands from camera trajectory estimation.
- ==Adaptive sampling module== combines ==Metric3D== depth predictions with a ==German-McClure robust loss== over a filtered, hand-excluded point set to recover metric-scale camera translation.

## Results

- On DexYCB under severe occlusion (75-100%), HaWoR reaches **5.07** PA-MPJPE versus **5.68** for WiLoR, the strongest single-image baseline.
- On HOT3D world-space evaluation, HaWoR achieves **33.20** W-MPJPE and **11.27** WA-MPJPE versus **119.41** and **39.46** for the best optimization baseline HMP-SLAM.
- HaWoR runs one forward pass at **40ms** per frame, a **75%** runtime reduction versus HMP-SLAM's **160ms** per frame optimization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling world motion into camera-space reconstruction plus a separately estimated camera trajectory sidesteps the need for hard-to-obtain joint world-space motion priors.
> - Masking the dynamic foreground subject before bundle adjustment is a general recipe for adapting SLAM to any video domain with large moving foreground objects.
> - A single feed-forward pass with a learned infiller can match or beat iterative test-time optimization while cutting inference cost by an order of magnitude.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Understanding human hand movements in three-dimensional space has become increasingly important for applications in augmented reality, robotics, and human behavior analysis. While existing methods excel at reconstructing hand poses relative to the camera's viewpoint, they fall short when it comes to understanding hand movements within the broader context of the real world.

HaWoR (Hand World-space motion Reconstruction) addresses this fundamental limitation by introducing the first method specifically designed to reconstruct 3D hand motion in world coordinates from monocular egocentric videos. Developed by researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University and Imperial College London, this work represents a significant advancement in computer vision's ability to understand hand movements in their true spatial context.

The research tackles a particularly challenging scenario: egocentric videos where both the camera (worn by the user) and hands are in constant motion, hands frequently move out of view, and traditional visual tracking methods struggle with dynamic foreground objects. The resulting system can reconstruct complete hand motion trajectories even when hands disappear from the camera's field of view, opening new possibilities for immersive technologies and behavioral analysis.

### 2. The Challenge of World-Space Hand Motion

Current 3D hand pose estimation methods primarily focus on camera-frame reconstruction, meaning they estimate hand poses relative to the camera's perspective. While this approach works well for static camera scenarios, it becomes problematic in egocentric settings where understanding the global motion of hands within the environment is crucial.

The transition from camera-space to world-space reconstruction presents several unique challenges:

**Scale Ambiguity**: Determining the actual size and distance of hand movements in the real world requires resolving the inherent scale ambiguity of monocular vision. Unlike full-body motion estimation, hand movements occur at much finer scales, making accurate metric estimation more difficult.

**Dynamic Co-motion**: In egocentric videos, both the camera and hands move simultaneously, creating complex relative motions that must be disentangled to understand each component's true trajectory in world space.

**Frequent Occlusions and Truncations**: Hands in egocentric videos often move outside the limited field of view or become severely occluded, creating gaps in the observable motion that must be intelligently filled to maintain temporal coherence.

**SLAM Challenges**: Traditional Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) methods assume static backgrounds for reliable camera tracking. The presence of dynamic hands can interfere with feature matching and bundle adjustment, leading to inaccurate camera trajectory estimation.

### 3. Methodology

HaWoR addresses these challenges through a decomposition strategy that breaks the complex problem into three interconnected modules, each designed to handle specific aspects of the world-space reconstruction challenge.

#### 3.1. Hand Motion Estimation Module

The first component focuses on robust camera-frame hand motion reconstruction. Building upon the WiLoR architecture, this module incorporates temporal reasoning to handle the challenging conditions typical of egocentric videos.

The module processes video frames through several stages:

1. **Feature Extraction**: A Vision Transformer (ViT) backbone extracts rich visual features from hand regions detected through off-the-shelf tracking methods.

2. **Image Attention Module (IAM)**: To address issues with truncated hands and improve feature robustness, IAM employs temporal self-attention to fuse appearance features across adjacent frames. This helps maintain consistent representations even when hands are partially visible.

3. **Pose Attention Module (PAM)**: This component applies temporal self-attention directly to MANO pose parameters, learning hand motion priors that improve the realism and temporal consistency of 3D reconstructions.

The module outputs MANO parameters including pose, shape, and camera-space translation and orientation, forming the foundation for subsequent world-space transformation.

#### 3.2. Camera Trajectory Estimation

Accurate camera trajectory estimation is crucial for transforming camera-frame hand motions to world coordinates. This module addresses two primary challenges: interference from dynamic hands and metric scale determination.

**Adaptive Egocentric SLAM**: The system employs a dual-masking strategy to make SLAM robust to dynamic hands. Using the reconstructed 3D hand motions, it creates 2D hand masks that exclude hand regions from both input images and confidence maps used by DROID-SLAM. This ensures that only static background features contribute to camera motion estimation.

**Metric Scale Recovery**: Since monocular SLAM produces trajectories with arbitrary scale, the system integrates Metric3D, a foundational metric depth estimation model, to recover real-world scale. An Adaptive Sampling Module (AdaSM) optimizes this process by:

```
α* = argmin Σ ρ(d_slam,i * α - d_metric3d,i)
```

where ρ represents the German-McClure robust loss function, and the sampling focuses on intermediate depth ranges while excluding hand regions to improve reliability.

#### 3.3. Hand Motion Infiller

The infiller module addresses the challenge of missing hand motion data when hands move outside the camera's field of view. This transformer-based architecture operates in a canonical space to simplify the learning task.

**Canonical Space Transformation**: Before processing, incomplete MANO sequences are transformed to a canonical coordinate system by aligning the first frame's hand rotation and translation to zero. This decouples hand motion from camera dynamics, making the completion task more tractable.

**Transformer Architecture**: The infiller uses a multi-layer transformer encoder with:
- Positional embeddings to encode temporal relationships
- Multi-head self-attention layers to capture long-range dependencies
- A fully-connected decoder to regress complete MANO sequences

Training involves randomly masking segments of complete sequences while providing start and end frames as context, teaching the network to generate plausible intermediate motions.

### 4. Key Technical Innovations

HaWoR introduces several technical innovations that distinguish it from existing approaches:

**Dual-Masking SLAM Strategy**: The use of reconstructed hand motions to create dynamic masks for SLAM represents a novel approach to handling moving foreground objects in egocentric scenarios. This creates a feedback loop where better hand tracking improves camera estimation and vice versa.

**Canonical Space Infilling**: By transforming incomplete sequences to a canonical coordinate system before infilling, the method significantly simplifies the learning task while maintaining the ability to generate realistic hand motions.

**Adaptive Depth Sampling**: The AdaSM module's intelligent sampling strategy for metric scale estimation demonstrates how domain knowledge can improve the reliability of foundational models in specific applications.

**Spatio-Temporal Attention**: The combination of image-level (IAM) and pose-level (PAM) temporal attention provides multiple levels of temporal reasoning, improving robustness to various types of degradation in egocentric videos.

### 5. Experimental Results

HaWoR demonstrates superior performance across multiple evaluation metrics and datasets, establishing new benchmarks for world-space hand motion reconstruction.

#### 5.1. Quantitative Results

**Camera-Frame Performance**: On the DexYCB dataset, HaWoR achieves robust performance across different occlusion levels, with particularly strong improvements in heavily occluded scenarios (75%-100% occlusion: 5.07 MPJPE vs. WiLoR's 5.68 MPJPE).

**World-Space Accuracy**: On the HOT3D dataset, HaWoR significantly outperforms baseline methods:
- World-space MPJPE: 33.20mm (vs. best baseline HMP-SLAM: 119.41mm)
- World-aligned MPJPE: 11.27mm (vs. HMP-SLAM: 39.46mm)
- Root Translation Error: 0.78% (demonstrating trajectory stability)
- Acceleration Error: 5.41 m/s² (indicating smooth, realistic motion)

**Camera Trajectory**: The adaptive SLAM approach achieves superior camera tracking with ATE-S of 14.61mm compared to DROID + Metric3DV2's 21.07mm, demonstrating the effectiveness of hand-aware trajectory estimation.

#### 5.2. Qualitative Analysis

Visual comparisons reveal HaWoR's ability to produce smooth, coherent trajectories that closely match ground truth, while baseline methods often exhibit jittery or inaccurate reconstructions. The method successfully handles complex scenarios including rapid hand movements, occlusions, and hands moving out of view.

#### 5.3. Ablation Studies

Systematic ablation studies confirm the importance of each component:
- Removing PAM increases W-MPJPE from 33.20 to 36.32mm
- The motion infiller achieves significantly lower Frechet Inception Distance (0.57) compared to naive interpolation methods (1.42)
- Each attention module contributes meaningfully to overall performance

### 6. Limitations and Future Work

Despite its achievements, HaWoR faces several limitations that point toward future research directions:

**Runtime Performance**: At 40ms per frame, the method is not yet suitable for real-time applications requiring immediate feedback, such as live AR/VR systems or responsive robotics applications.

**Tracking Dependencies**: The system's reliance on off-the-shelf hand detection and tracking makes it susceptible to upstream errors, particularly tracking identity failures that can confuse left and right hands.

**Hand Interaction Modeling**: The current approach models each hand independently without explicit constraints against self-penetration, which can lead to unnatural configurations during bimanual interactions.

**Scale Sensitivity**: While the adaptive sampling approach improves metric scale estimation, the system still depends on the accuracy of foundational depth models, which may struggle in certain environmental conditions.

Future work could address these limitations through end-to-end learning approaches, incorporation of physical constraints, and development of more efficient architectures suitable for real-time deployment.

### 7. Conclusion

HaWoR represents a significant advancement in 3D hand motion reconstruction, introducing the first method specifically designed for world-space estimation from egocentric videos. By decomposing the complex problem into manageable components and introducing novel technical solutions for each challenge, the work establishes new benchmarks for accuracy and robustness in this domain.

The method's ability to handle missing data through intelligent infilling, robust camera tracking in dynamic scenes, and accurate metric scale estimation makes it particularly valuable for real-world applications. The comprehensive evaluation demonstrates clear advantages over existing approaches, with substantial improvements in both quantitative metrics and qualitative coherence.

As applications in AR/VR, robotics, and human behavior analysis continue to demand more sophisticated understanding of human hand movements in world coordinates, HaWoR provides a foundation for future research and development in this critical area of computer vision. The work opens new possibilities for creating more immersive and responsive systems that can understand and respond to human hand movements in their full spatial context.
