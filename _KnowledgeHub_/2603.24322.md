# Heuristic Self-Paced Learning for Domain Adaptive Semantic Segmentation under Adverse Conditions

> [!summary] Summary
> A self-paced learning framework, Heuristic Semantic Class Mining (HeuSCM), was developed to enhance unsupervised domain adaptive semantic segmentation by autonomously learning a dynamic class curriculum. It achieved state-of-the-art mean Intersection-over-Union (mIoU) of 72.9% on the ACDC dataset for adverse weather conditions and demonstrated strong generalization in night scene and synthetic-to-real adaptation tasks.

## Problem

- Semantic segmentation models trained on ideal data perform poorly under adverse real-world conditions (e.g., fog, night) due to complex non-linear sensor degradation and feature shifts.
- Existing Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) methods rely on static, human-defined curriculum learning or hard class mining heuristics, which are suboptimal for dynamic, high-dimensional model learning states.
- Adverse conditions exacerbate class imbalance, particularly for safety-critical but rare semantic classes, leading to biased learning and reduced model robustness.

## Method

- **HeuSCM** reformulates UDA training as a ==sequential decision-making== problem, using a reinforcement learning-inspired agent to dynamically learn a class scheduling policy.
- High-dimensional Semantic State Extraction (HSSE), comprising a GM-VAE for low-dimensional state representation and SKFEN for key feature distillation, enables the agent to perceive the segmentation model's evolving learning status comprehensively.
- Categorical α-Fairness for Policy Gradients (CαPG) is introduced to optimize the agent's policy with a global fairness objective, counteracting class bias by ensuring equitable reward distribution and balanced improvement across all semantic categories.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art mIoU of **72.9%** on the ACDC test set and **72.7%** on the ACDC validation set for Cityscapes→ACDC domain adaptation, using the HRDA backbone.
- Demonstrated robust performance in night scenes, reaching **52.8%** mIoU on Dark Zurich-val and **59.3%** mIoU on Nighttime Driving test set with the HRDA backbone.
- The learned Heuristic Class Sampling Policy (HCSP) showed generalization by consistently improving existing methods (e.g., IAST, HIAST) in synthetic-to-real UDA (GTA5 → Cityscapes).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically learning a class curriculum through a reinforcement learning agent, rather than relying on static heuristics, yields superior adaptation performance under adverse conditions.
> - Comprehensive, high-dimensional state perception of the model's learning status is crucial for effective autonomous decision-making in curriculum generation.
> - Integrating an α-fairness objective into policy gradients effectively mitigates category bias, leading to more robust and balanced learning across all semantic classes, particularly critical for rare categories.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Semantic segmentation is a foundational technology for environmental perception in autonomous driving systems. However, models trained on datasets collected under controlled conditions often experience significant performance degradation when deployed in real-world scenarios, particularly under adverse weather conditions such as fog, nighttime, or heavy rain. This degradation is attributed to complex non-linear sensor data degradation and feature shifts between training and deployment environments.

Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) is a prevailing paradigm to mitigate these domain shift issues without requiring extensive target domain annotations. Within the context of semantic segmentation under adverse weather, UDA faces a compound challenge: (1) complex non-uniform domain shifts due to continuously varying adverse conditions, and (2) exacerbated class imbalance, as the observability of safety-critical but inherently rare semantic classes further diminishes in severe weather.

Existing UDA methods for semantic segmentation under adverse conditions typically employ mechanisms such as style transfer or image generation to reduce appearance discrepancies, or feature-level alignment to learn domain-invariant features. Concurrently, optimization strategies like Curriculum Learning (CL) and Hard Class Mining (HCM) are utilized to address class imbalance and guide the learning process. However, these CL and HCM approaches commonly rely on static, human-defined heuristics. The "difficulty" of the curriculum is quantified by fixed metrics (e.g., uncertainty or confidence), and the learning "path" is dictated by predefined rules (e.g., "easy-to-hard" or "all hard"). This "prescriptive paradigm" is considered suboptimal by the authors because a model's "cognitive state" during training is a high-dimensional, dynamic, and non-monotonic process, which cannot be optimally managed by static, one-dimensional, artificial scalars. This rigidity can lead to local optima, such as underfitting to noise or overfitting to majority classes.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged in UDA research, primarily for tasks like feature selection or feature-level alignment in image classification or segmentation. However, its application to dynamic class scheduling in semantic segmentation, particularly concerning the inherent class bias and fairness issues in adverse conditions, remains relatively unexplored. This work positions itself by proposing a paradigm shift: moving from manually designing curricula to autonomously learning them, leveraging RL principles to create a dynamic, adaptive, and class-fair learning strategy for UDA semantic segmentation under adverse conditions. This approach aims to overcome the limitations of static, human-defined curricula by enabling the model to adapt its learning path based on its evolving internal state.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel framework for Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) semantic segmentation that dynamically adjusts its learning strategy, particularly under challenging adverse weather conditions, by autonomously learning a curriculum rather than relying on fixed, human-defined heuristics.

The motivation for this objective stems from several key observations and limitations of existing approaches:

1. **Limitations of Traditional Curriculum Learning (CL) and Hard Class Mining (HCM):** Current CL and HCM methods in UDA for semantic segmentation employ static, predefined notions of "difficulty" and "learning paths." These definitions are typically based on simple metrics (e.g., prediction uncertainty, class frequency) and rigid rules (e.g., easy-to-hard progression). The authors argue that this "prescriptive paradigm" is fundamentally insufficient because the actual learning process of a deep learning model is a complex, high-dimensional, dynamic, and often non-monotonic process. A static, one-dimensional heuristic cannot effectively capture or adapt to the model's evolving internal state, leading to suboptimal learning outcomes, such as category bias where certain classes are learned poorly or overfit.

2. **Challenges of Adverse Weather Conditions:** Semantic segmentation under adverse conditions (fog, night, rain) presents a compounded challenge. Beyond the typical domain shift between source and target domains, these conditions introduce complex, non-uniform feature degradations and significantly exacerbate class imbalance, further reducing the visibility and representation of safety-critical semantic classes. Static learning strategies are ill-equipped to handle these dynamic and severe challenges, often leading to performance bottlenecks.

3. **Need for Adaptive and Dynamic Learning:** The authors propose that an optimal learning trajectory should not be predetermined by human assumptions but should be autonomously discovered by the model based on its real-time learning status. This adaptive approach is hypothesized to enable more effective knowledge transfer and robust performance, especially in highly variable environments.

To achieve this overarching objective, the research sets forth several specific technical goals:

- **Re-envisioning UDA Training as a Sequential Decision Problem:** By drawing inspiration from Reinforcement Learning (RL), the authors aim to recast the UDA training process as a sequence of decisions regarding which semantic classes to focus on at each learning stage.
- **Developing an Autonomous Class Scheduler (Agent):** This agent should be capable of dynamically generating a ranked list of semantic classes, guiding the segmentation model towards the most informative classes at any given point in training.
- **Implementing Autonomous State Perception:** The agent's decision-making should move beyond single, human-defined metrics. This requires designing a mechanism, termed High-dimensional Semantic State Extraction (HSSE), to comprehensively characterize the model's current learning progress from a high-dimensional state representation. This state should capture intricate relationships among features relevant to the domain adaptation process.
- **Ensuring Dynamic Policy Optimization with Fairness:** The agent's optimization objective should not merely maximize an aggregate reward, which can perpetuate class bias. Instead, a global fairness objective, termed Categorical α-Fairness for Policy Gradients (CαPG), is proposed. This objective aims to ensure equitable reward distribution and balanced improvement across all semantic classes, which is particularly critical for safety in autonomous driving.
- **Demonstrating Performance and Generalization:** The final objective is to validate the effectiveness of the proposed framework through extensive experiments on challenging adverse weather semantic segmentation benchmarks (e.g., ACDC, Dark Zurich, Nighttime Driving) and to confirm its generalization capability in synthetic-to-real semantic segmentation tasks (e.g., GTA5 → Cityscapes).

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces Heuristic Semantic Class Mining (HeuSCM), a self-paced curriculum framework conceptually inspired by reinforcement learning. HeuSCM dynamically perceives the learning progress of a semantic segmentation model from a high-dimensional, evolving state space, learning a policy-based class scheduler rather than adhering to a static curriculum.

#### 3.1. Segmentation Model and Mixed Domain Generation

The core task is UDA semantic segmentation from a source domain ($D_S$) to a target domain ($D_T$) under adverse weather. To bridge the domain gap, cross-domain mixed sampling is performed, generating a mixed domain ($D_M$). For each mixed image ($X^t_{Tmix}$) and label ($Y^t_{Rmix}$), the generation process combines a source image ($x^i_s$) and label ($y^i_s$) with a target image ($x^j_t$) and its pseudo-label ($g_\theta(x^j_t)$). A binary mask $H$ determines which pixels come from the source and which from the target. Crucially, the mask $H_{ijk}$ is defined by an indicator function $I$ that selects pixels belonging to specific semantic classes $k$ from a "Ranked Classes" list ($R$). This list $R$ is dynamically generated by the autonomous agent, making it the central control mechanism of HeuSCM. The segmentation model ($g_\theta$) is then optimized using a `SegLoss` that combines cross-entropy loss on both source images and the mixed image/label pairs.

#### 3.2. High-dimensional Semantic State Extraction (HSSE)

To enable the agent to comprehensively understand the model's learning status, HSSE processes high-dimensional segmentation states into key features.

- **Low-dimensional State Representation Learning (LSRL):** The initial high-dimensional state $s$ is processed by a Gaussian Mixture Variational Autoencoder (GM-VAE). The GM-VAE encoder ($Enc_\psi$) maps $s$ into a compact latent space, inferring both a discrete categorical distribution (representing learning mode) and a continuous latent state posterior. The GM-VAE is pre-trained by maximizing a variational lower bound, then its decoder is frozen, and the encoder is fine-tuned jointly with the policy network optimization. A reconstruction loss ($L_{recon}$) regularizes the encoder to preserve the structure of the original state manifold.

- **Semantic Key Feature Extraction Network (SKFEN):** This network addresses redundancy in the low-dimensional latent space ($z^s_t$) by distilling key features. SKFEN operates in two phases:
 1. **Initial Transformation and Grouping:** The latent state $z^s_t$ undergoes initial feature fusion and interaction modeling via 1x1 convolutions and a 5x5 depth-wise separable convolution. The channel dimension is then expanded, shuffled, and grouped into $G$ feature maps ($f_g$).
 2. **Group-wise Feature Aggregation:** For each group $f_g$, channel-wise max pooling and average pooling operations are performed in parallel to extract salient and statistical information. These pooled features are concatenated across all groups, then fused by a final 3x3 convolution. A residual connection adds the original $z^s_t$ to the refined output ($z_{out}$), ensuring the preservation of original information.

#### 3.3. Categorical α-Fairness for Policy Gradients (CαPG)

To counteract the inherent training bias favoring certain semantic classes and ensure balanced learning, CαPG introduces a fairness objective into the policy gradient optimization.

- **Class-Specific Value Function:** A value function $V^\pi_c(s)$ is defined for each semantic class $c$, representing the expected total discounted return the category can obtain following policy $\pi$ from state $s$. The reward signal $r_c(t)$ for each class $c$ at time $t$ is crucial for this.

- **Unsupervised Composite Reward Mechanism:** Given the absence of target labels, an unsupervised, composite reward $r_c(t)$ is computed. This reward quantifies two factors:
 1. **Transferability:** Measured by the cosine similarity between the average feature representations of class $c$ in the source domain ($A^S_c(t)$) and target domain ($A^T_c(t)$).
 2. **Discriminability:** Measured by the dissimilarity between the average feature representation of class $c$ and all other classes $k \neq c$ in the target domain ($1 - \text{cosine similarity}(A^T_c(t), A^T_k(t))$).
 The average feature representations ($A^S_c(t)$ and $A^T_c(t)$) are calculated by averaging the feature outputs of the segmentation network $g_\theta$ for pixels belonging to class $c$ in the source and target domains, respectively. A hyperparameter $\lambda$ balances these two objectives.

- **α-Fairness Objective:** Instead of maximizing a standard sum of class values ($J_{sum}(\pi)$), which is prone to bias, a global fairness objective $J_F(\pi)$ is maximized. This objective utilizes an α-fairness formulation, where $J_F(\pi) = \sum_{c=1}^C \frac{1}{1-\alpha} (V^\pi_c(s))^{1-\alpha}$. This formulation, with $\alpha$ controlling the degree of fairness, implicitly assigns higher weights to classes with lower current values, thus promoting balanced improvement.

- **Fairness-Weighted Policy Gradient:** The gradient of the policy $\pi_\theta$ is computed using a fairness-weighted aggregate advantage $\tilde{A}_\alpha$. This involves summing the advantage functions for each semantic class ($A_c(s_t, a_t)$) weighted by fairness weights $w_c(s_t) = V^\pi_c(s_t)^{-\alpha}$. By maximizing this objective, the policy is compelled to follow a more balanced learning trajectory.

The overall training procedure alternates between updating the segmentation model ($g_\theta$) based on mixed data guided by the agent's ranked classes, and updating the agent (GM-VAE encoder, SKFEN, and policy network ClassGen) using the fairness-weighted policy gradient derived from the observed rewards.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research demonstrates the effectiveness and generalization capabilities of the proposed HeuSCM framework through extensive experiments on various semantic segmentation benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Performance on ACDC Dataset (Adverse Weather UDA)

- **State-of-the-art Performance:** HeuSCM consistently outperformed existing UDA methods across different backbones (DeepLab-v2, DAFormer, HRDA) on both the ACDC test and validation sets for Cityscapes→ACDC domain adaptation.
 - With the HRDA backbone, HeuSCM achieved a mean Intersection-over-Union (mIoU) of **72.9%** on the ACDC test set and **72.7%** on the ACDC validation set, establishing new state-of-the-art results for this benchmark.
- **Qualitative Improvements:** Visual comparisons on the ACDC validation set showed that HeuSCM's predictions were visually closer to the ground truth compared to other state-of-the-art UDA methods under the same backbone, particularly with the HRDA backbone. This indicates more accurate and robust segmentation in adverse conditions.

#### 4.2. Performance on Dark Zurich and Nighttime Driving Datasets (Night Scene UDA)

- **Effectiveness in Night Scenes:** The method's effectiveness was further verified on night scene datasets for Cityscapes→DarkZurich domain adaptation.
 - Using the HRDA backbone, HeuSCM achieved **52.8%** mIoU on the Dark Zurich-val set, surpassing previous state-of-the-art methods.
 - For the Nighttime Driving test set, HeuSCM also demonstrated strong generalization, achieving **59.3%** mIoU with the HRDA backbone.
- **Qualitative Results:** Visualizations on Dark Zurich-val and Nighttime Driving test sets confirmed the method's strong performance in challenging low-light conditions.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study conducted on the ACDC validation set (using HRDA backbone and Refign as baseline) investigated the contribution of each core component of HeuSCM: Low-dimensional State Representation Learning (LSRL), Semantic Key Feature Extraction Network (SKFEN), and Categorical α-Fairness for Policy Gradients (CαPG).
- **Individual Contributions:**
 - Adding LSRL alone improved mIoU by 1.1 percentage points (from baseline 71.1% to 72.2%).
 - Adding SKFEN alone improved mIoU by 0.6 percentage points (to 71.7%).
 - Adding CαPG alone improved mIoU by 0.5 percentage points (to 71.6%).
- **Combined Contributions:**
 - Combining LSRL and SKFEN yielded a 1.2 percentage point improvement (to 72.3%).
 - Combining LSRL and CαPG yielded a 0.9 percentage point improvement (to 72.0%).
 - Combining SKFEN and CαPG yielded a 1.1 percentage point improvement (to 72.2%).
- **Full Integration:** The full integration of all three proposed designs achieved the highest performance of **72.7%** mIoU, confirming the collective effectiveness and indispensability of each module.

#### 4.4. Generalization Study (Synthetic-to-Real UDA)

To assess the generalization capability of the Heuristic Class Sampling Policy (HCSP), it was integrated into existing hard-class mining methods for synthetic-to-real semantic segmentation (GTA5 → Cityscapes).
- **Consistent Gains:** HCSP consistently yielded performance gains when applied to IAST (52.2% to 52.4% mIoU) and HIAST (56.3% to 56.5% mIoU) methods. These results indicate that the learned class sampling policy generalizes effectively beyond adverse weather conditions to other UDA scenarios, confirming its broad applicability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces HeuSCM, a novel framework that represents a methodological shift in unsupervised domain adaptation for semantic segmentation. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

#### 5.1. Paradigm Shift in Curriculum Learning

The work pioneers a paradigm shift from "human-defined heuristic" curriculum learning to an "autonomously learned strategy." By reframing UDA curriculum learning as a sequential decision problem within a reinforcement learning framework, the model can dynamically adjust its learning path based on its evolving internal state, moving beyond the limitations of static, one-dimensional heuristics. This approach offers a more adaptive and dynamic learning process, which is particularly beneficial in complex and variable real-world scenarios.

#### 5.2. Novel Technical Contributions

The framework's efficacy is attributed to two primary technical innovations:
- **High-dimensional Semantic State Extraction (HSSE):** This mechanism, comprising Low-dimensional State Representation Learning (LSRL) using a GM-VAE and the Semantic Key Feature Extraction Network (SKFEN), enables the agent to comprehensively perceive and distill the high-dimensional learning status of the segmentation model. This provides a rich, informative state representation for decision-making.
- **Categorical α-Fairness for Policy Gradients (CαPG):** This objective function explicitly addresses the critical issue of category bias inherent in domain adaptation, especially under adverse conditions where rare classes are further diminished. By ensuring equitable reward distribution and balanced improvement across all semantic classes, CαPG promotes robust performance for all categories, which is crucial for safety-critical applications.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Performance in Challenging Conditions

HeuSCM achieves state-of-the-art performance on highly challenging adverse weather semantic segmentation benchmarks, including ACDC, Dark Zurich, and Nighttime Driving. The quantitative improvements demonstrate its ability to facilitate more effective knowledge transfer and robust segmentation in conditions that traditionally severely degrade model performance. This directly contributes to advancing the reliability of autonomous systems operating in diverse environmental settings.

#### 5.4. Demonstrated Generalization Capability

Beyond adverse weather, the Heuristic Class Sampling Policy (HCSP) component of the framework exhibits strong generalization capability when integrated into existing methods for synthetic-to-real semantic segmentation. This suggests that the learned class scheduling principles are not limited to a specific domain adaptation challenge but hold broader applicability across different UDA contexts, indicating the fundamental soundness of the approach.

#### 5.5. Future Research Avenues

This work opens promising new avenues for research in UDA. It suggests that learned, strategic, and multi-objective scheduling policies can significantly outperform traditional fixed-heuristic methods in complex adaptation scenarios. Future research could explore more sophisticated state representations, alternative fairness objectives, or the application of similar RL-based curriculum learning to other computer vision tasks beyond semantic segmentation.

In conclusion, the HeuSCM framework represents a significant step forward in making semantic segmentation models more robust and adaptable to challenging real-world conditions by introducing an intelligent, self-paced learning mechanism. Its direct impact is improved perception capabilities for autonomous driving and other safety-critical applications operating under diverse and unpredictable environmental conditions.
