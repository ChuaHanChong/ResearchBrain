# Centralized Reward Agent for Knowledge Sharing and Transfer in Multi-Task Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A multi-task reinforcement learning framework called Centralized Reward Agent (CenRA) is introduced, utilizing a centralized agent to generate dense, informative auxiliary rewards for distributed policy agents. This system enhances knowledge sharing among existing tasks and facilitates efficient transfer to new, unseen tasks, demonstrating improved learning efficiency, faster convergence, and outperforming state-of-the-art baselines across various sparse-reward domains.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning agents often struggle with sparse and delayed rewards, which significantly hinder effective exploration and policy learning.
- Existing Multi-Task Reinforcement Learning (MTRL) methods for knowledge transfer face limitations in efficiently adapting to new tasks and generalizing across diverse tasks.
- MTRL frameworks can suffer from imbalanced learning progress across different tasks, where some tasks might dominate or lag, leading to suboptimal overall system performance.

## Method

- A ==Centralized Reward Agent== (CRA) learns to generate continuous "knowledge rewards" from state-action observations, augmenting the sparse environmental rewards for individual policy agents.
- Policy agents utilize these augmented reward signals (environmental + knowledge reward) to optimize their learning, allowing for the integration of various RL backbone algorithms.
- An information synchronization mechanism dynamically adjusts the sampling quantity for CRA training, prioritizing less similar tasks and tasks with lagging performance to ensure balanced knowledge distribution.

## Results

- CenRA consistently achieved higher episodic returns and faster convergence compared to state-of-the-art baselines across Meta-World, 2DMaze, 3DPickup, and MujocoCar sparse-reward environments.
- The framework demonstrated robust knowledge transfer, with the pre-trained CRA enabling rapid adaptation and superior performance on new, unseen tasks, even without further training.
- The information synchronization mechanism, combining similarity and performance weights, significantly improved learning stability and overall system performance, preventing unbalanced progress across tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A centralized reward agent can effectively distill environment-relevant knowledge into dense auxiliary reward signals, offering a direct approach to overcome sparse reward challenges.
> - Knowledge encoded within these learned reward signals serves as a robust and transferable medium, enabling rapid adaptation and superior performance for both familiar and new tasks.
> - Dynamic coordination of task contributions based on their similarity and individual learning progress is crucial for achieving stable and efficient multi-task learning outcomes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has demonstrated capabilities in domains such as robotics, gaming, and autonomous vehicles. A persistent challenge in RL is sparse and delayed rewards, where agents receive minimal feedback, hindering effective exploration and learning. Reward Shaping (RS) is a technique designed to address this by providing dense, informative auxiliary rewards. Concurrently, Multi-Task Reinforcement Learning (MTRL) is a growing area that aims to improve learning efficiency and robustness by enabling knowledge transfer across multiple tasks.

Existing MTRL approaches for knowledge transfer generally fall into several categories:
- **Policy distillation methods:** These approaches focus on identifying and combining commonalities across different policies, often by training a single policy to mimic multiple expert policies or distilling a centroid policy from multiple task-specific policies. Examples include Distral and Actor-Mimic.
- **Representation sharing methods:** These methods extract and share common features or gradients among agents by reusing network modules or learning foundational policies. Approaches like soft modularization and parameter compositional techniques fall into this category.
- **Parameter sharing methods:** These involve designing network architectures that allow for the reuse of parameters or layers across different tasks.
- **Single-policy generalization methods:** These aim to train a single policy capable of solving multiple tasks simultaneously or continuously, often leveraging meta-learning techniques or focusing on overcoming gradient interference.

Despite their potential, these established MTRL strategies can face limitations such as slow adaptation to new tasks, constrained generalization abilities when tasks are highly diverse or unseen, and the inability to fully capture the complexity of varied tasks. The integration of reward shaping into MTRL offers an alternative by directly modifying the reward function, which can be a more direct mechanism for distributing task-specific knowledge. Within reward shaping, the paper distinguishes between intrinsic-motivation-based rewards (task-agnostic, for exploration) and task-contextual rewards (automatically learned, encoding task-specific information). This work focuses on the latter, leveraging task-contextual rewards as a transferable knowledge medium within an MTRL framework.

The proposed Centralized Reward Agent (CenRA) framework positions itself within this landscape by combining task-contextual reward shaping with a centralized knowledge management system for MTRL. It aims to enhance knowledge sharing across established tasks and facilitate transfer to new tasks, specifically addressing the challenges of sparse rewards and inefficient knowledge utilization in existing MTRL paradigms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to:
1. Develop a novel multi-task reinforcement learning framework that integrates a centralized reward agent (CRA) with multiple distributed policy agents.
2. Enable the efficient distillation and distribution of knowledge across various tasks to improve learning efficiency and adaptability, particularly in sparse-reward environments.
3. Design a mechanism for the CRA to function as a knowledge pool, capable of transferring meaningful reward signals to both established and new, unseen tasks.
4. Introduce an information synchronization mechanism to balance knowledge distribution across tasks by considering factors such as task similarity and the learning progress of individual policy agents, thereby ensuring optimal system-wide performance.

The motivation for these objectives stems from several challenges in current reinforcement learning and multi-task learning:
- **Sparse and Delayed Rewards:** Many real-world RL environments provide sparse rewards, meaning agents receive feedback only upon successful completion of a task or very infrequently. This absence of immediate feedback significantly hinders an agent's ability to learn effective policies and leads to extensive, often aimless, exploration. Reward shaping has been identified as an effective strategy to mitigate this by providing dense, informative auxiliary rewards.
- **Inefficient Knowledge Transfer in MTRL:** While MTRL aims to leverage commonalities across tasks, existing knowledge transfer methods—such as policy distillation, representation sharing, or parameter sharing—often face difficulties. These can include slow adaptation to new tasks, limited utilization of transferred knowledge, or struggles to capture the complexity of highly diverse tasks, sometimes requiring substantial architectural modifications for new tasks.
- **Lack of a Direct Knowledge Sharing Mechanism:** The paper posits that task-specific information encoded within reward signals offers a direct and intuitive means for knowledge transfer in MTRL. Existing MTRL methods typically transfer knowledge through shared policies, representations, or parameters, which can be indirect.
- **Unbalanced Multi-Task Learning:** In an MTRL setting, different tasks may vary in difficulty or contribute differently to the overall learning process. Without a mechanism to balance these contributions, the system's performance could be compromised, as some tasks might dominate the learning or others might lag behind. A method to synchronize knowledge distribution is needed to maintain overall system equilibrium.

By addressing these motivations, the proposed framework aims to create a more robust, efficient, and adaptable multi-task reinforcement learning system capable of effectively tackling sparse-reward problems and enhancing generalization across diverse tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed **Centralized Reward Agent based MTRL fRAmework (CenRA)** is a dual-component system comprising a centralized reward agent (CRA) and multiple distributed policy agents. This framework is designed to operate in an MTRL setting with $N$ distinct tasks, {T1, T2..., TN}, where state and action spaces are consistent across tasks, although specific goals, transition dynamics, and reward functions may differ.

#### 3.1. Knowledge Distillation and Distribution

**Problem Formulation:**
Each policy agent, $A_{pol\_i}$, learns control behaviors for its task $T_i$ using an appropriate RL algorithm (e.g., DQN for discrete, SAC for continuous). The CRA, $A_{rwd}$, is responsible for extracting and distributing knowledge. The observation for the CRA is defined as $s_{rwd} = (s_i, a_i)$, combining the environmental state $s_i$ and the action $a_i$ taken by the policy agent.

**Centralized Reward Agent (CRA):**
The CRA functions as a self-contained RL agent, similar to the assistant reward agent in the ReLara framework, but extended to interact concurrently with multiple policy agents across various tasks.
- **Reward Generation:** The CRA's policy, $\pi_{rwd}$, generates continuous "knowledge rewards" ($r_{knw}$) by mapping the combined state and action $(S \times A)$ to a defined reward space $R = [R_{min}, R_{max}]$.
- **Experience Aggregation:** A concatenated replay buffer $D = \cup_{i=1}^N D_i$ is constructed, where $D_i$ is the replay buffer of policy agent $A_{pol\_i}$. Each transition stored in $D_i$ is augmented as $\tau = (s_{rwd_t}, r_{knw_t}, r_{env_t}, s_{rwd_{t+1}} | T_i)$, incorporating the environmental reward ($r_{env_t}$) and the CRA-generated knowledge reward ($r_{knw_t}$). This shared buffer design minimizes storage overhead and facilitates experience reuse.
- **Optimization:** The CRA is optimized using an off-policy actor-critic algorithm.
 - The **critic module** is updated using a squared TD-error objective: $J(V_{rwd}) = E_{\tau_t \sim D}[\delta_t^2]$, where $\delta_t = r_{env_t} + \gamma V_{rwd}(s_{rwd_{t+1}}) - V_{rwd}(s_{rwd_t})$.
 - The **actor module** is updated with an objective function that maximizes the expected log probability of the knowledge reward weighted by the TD-error: $J(\pi_{rwd}) = E_{\tau_t \sim D}[E_{r_{knw_t} \sim \pi_{rwd}(\cdot|s_{rwd_t})}[\log \pi_{rwd}(r_{knw_t}|s_{rwd_t}) \cdot \delta_t]]$.

**Policy Agents with Knowledge Rewards:**
Each policy agent $A_{pol\_i}$ receives two types of rewards: the environmental reward $r_{env_i}$ from its task $T_i$, and the knowledge reward $r_{knw}$ generated by the CRA.
- **Augmented Reward:** The effective reward for a policy agent is $r_{pol_i} = r_{env_i} + \lambda r_{knw}$, where $\lambda \in (0, 1]$ is a scaling weight.
- **Policy Optimization:** Each policy agent aims to learn an optimal policy $\pi_{pol_i}^*$ by maximizing its cumulative augmented reward. The environmental reward is typically retrieved from the replay buffer, while the knowledge reward is computed in real-time by the most recently updated CRA. This design allows for flexibility, as policy agents can employ any suitable RL backbone algorithm.

#### 3.2. Information Synchronization of Policy Agents

To address potential imbalances in knowledge extraction and distribution arising from varying task contributions, CenRA incorporates an information synchronization mechanism. This mechanism controls the sampling quantity from each task's replay buffer $D_i$ for CRA training using a sampling weight $w$, which considers two factors:

- **Similarity Weight ($w_{sim}$):** This weight is derived from the similarity among tasks, encouraging the CRA to focus more on outlier tasks.
 - Task features are represented by averaged hidden layers from policy agents' neural network encoders over the most recent $K$ steps ($H_i$).
 - A cross-attention mechanism calculates similarity $s_i$ between task $T_i$'s feature $H_i$ and the centroid $c$ of all task features: $s_i = (c^T \cdot H_i) / \sqrt{D}$, where $c = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{k=1}^N H_k$. ReLU activation ensures non-negative features and stable centroids.
 - The similarity weight is then defined as $w_{sim} = \text{Softmax}([1/s_1, 1/s_2, \ldots, 1/s_N])$, assigning higher weights to less similar tasks.

- **Performance Weight ($w_{per}$):** This weight is determined by the real-time learning performance of each policy agent, enabling the CRA to prioritize lagging tasks.
 - Recent learning trends are measured by averaging environmental rewards ($R_{tail_i}$) over the most recent $K$ steps.
 - The performance weight is defined as $w_{per} = \text{Softmax}([1/R_{tail_1}, 1/R_{tail_2}, \ldots, 1/R_{tail_N}])$, giving higher weights to tasks with lower recent performance.

- **Final Sampling Weight:** The combined sampling weight is $w = \alpha w_{sim} + (1 - \alpha) w_{per}$, where $\alpha$ is a hyperparameter balancing the two components. The CRA samples from the concatenated replay buffer $D$ according to these weights, ensuring a balanced and effective knowledge extraction.

#### 3.3. Overall Framework

The CenRA framework operates iteratively. Policy agents interact with their respective tasks, collecting transitions. The CRA then generates knowledge rewards for these transitions, which are stored in the agents' replay buffers. Policy agents are updated using their backbone RL algorithms and the augmented rewards. Subsequently, the sampling weights $w$ are calculated based on updated encoders and current learning performance. Finally, the CRA is updated by drawing samples from the concatenated replay buffer $D$ according to the calculated weights. This alternate and asynchronous update schedule allows for continuous adaptation. The learned CRA serves as a knowledge pool, facilitating transfer to new tasks by providing auxiliary reward signals, and can be further optimized in a continuous learning scheme.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated CenRA across four multi-task RL domains: Meta-World (ML10-sparse with 10 tasks and ML50-sparse with 50 tasks), 2DMaze, 3DPickup, and MujocoCar. All environments were configured to provide sparse environmental rewards. CenRA was benchmarked against several state-of-the-art baselines, including backbone RL algorithms (DQN/SAC), ReLara (a decentralized reward agent variant), TD-MPC2, CMTA, PiCor, MCAL, PaCo, SC, and SoftModule.

#### 4.1. Comparative Evaluation in MTRL

CenRA demonstrated consistent outperformance across all tasks and domains in multi-task learning environments.
- **Learning Efficiency and Convergence:** CenRA achieved the highest episodic returns and exhibited faster convergence compared to all baselines (Table 1, Figure 3). This was attributed to the auxiliary dense rewards generated by the CRA, which effectively guided learning in sparse-reward settings.
- **Stability and Robustness:** The framework displayed good stability, characterized by fewer fluctuations and oscillations in performance, particularly after convergence, relative to other models.
- **Balanced Performance:** Unlike some baselines (e.g., PiCor, MCAL) that showed uneven progress, CenRA maintained relatively consistent learning progress and minimal variability across task groups within a domain. This indicated an effective balance in knowledge distribution, preventing any single task from dominating or lagging.
- **Effectiveness of Centralized Knowledge Sharing:** CenRA's performance significantly surpassed that of ReLara, a decentralized approach where each policy agent has an independent reward agent without cross-task information sharing. This comparison highlighted the advantages of the CRA's integrated knowledge management and sharing capabilities.

#### 4.2. Knowledge Transfer to New Tasks

CenRA's ability to transfer previously learned knowledge to unseen tasks was evaluated on held-out test tasks. Two scenarios for CenRA were tested: one where the CRA continued to be optimized with the new policy agent ("CenRA w/ learning"), and another where the CRA remained fixed ("CenRA w/o learning").
- **Rapid Adaptation with Continued Learning:** "CenRA w/ learning" achieved rapid convergence and the highest episodic returns on new tasks (Table 2, Figure 4a). This indicated that the CRA successfully retained prior knowledge while adapting efficiently to novel task specifics through ongoing optimization.
- **Effective Transfer without Further Training:** Even when the CRA was not further trained ("CenRA w/o learning"), it still outperformed both ReLara (which required training a new reward agent) and the backbone RL algorithms. This finding demonstrated that the CRA's encoded, environment-relevant knowledge could be directly reused by new policy agents, providing a significant learning acceleration, particularly in sparse-reward scenarios.
- **Meaningful Reward Signals:** Visualizations in the 2DMaze environment (Figure 4b, Appendix C) showed that the CRA learned to provide semantically meaningful knowledge rewards. For example, it directed the agent towards a key before it was obtained and then towards the exit after the key was acquired. This indicated that the knowledge rewards provided concrete guidance, reducing the exploration burden for new tasks.

#### 4.3. Effect of Sampling Weight

Experiments ablating the information synchronization mechanism's components (similarity weight $w_{sim}$ and performance weight $w_{per}$) were conducted to assess their impact on task coordination and synchronization (Table 3).
- **Importance of Sampling Weights:** The absence of any sampling weight led to unbalanced learning outcomes and increased variance in episodic returns across different tasks. This suggested that uniform sampling from replay buffers was suboptimal for multi-task learning coordination.
- **Coordinated Optimization:** The full CenRA model, incorporating both $w_{sim}$ and $w_{per}$, achieved notably more stable learning and consistently better overall system performance compared to variants where one or both weights were removed. This highlighted the importance of jointly considering task similarity and learning progress.
- **Relative Impact of Weights:** While both weights contributed, the performance weight ($w_{per}$) had a more significant impact. It enabled the CRA to prioritize samples from underperforming or slower-progressing policy agents, thus ensuring a more balanced system-wide learning progress.
- **Flexibility of Balancing Factor $\alpha$:** The hyperparameter $\alpha$ allowed for adjusting the trade-off between emphasizing task similarity (larger $\alpha$) and compensating for lagging tasks (smaller $\alpha$), enhancing the framework's stability and adaptability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CenRA framework represents a contribution to multi-task reinforcement learning by integrating reward shaping as a core mechanism for knowledge sharing and transfer.

### 6. Significance

- **Addressing Sparse Rewards:** The framework provides a direct and effective strategy to overcome the fundamental challenge of sparse rewards in complex multi-task environments. By generating dense, informative knowledge rewards, CenRA enables agents to learn significantly faster and more reliably than traditional sparse-reward settings allow.
- **Novel Knowledge Transfer Mechanism:** CenRA introduces a distinct method for knowledge transfer in MTRL, leveraging learned task-contextual reward signals. This approach contrasts with, and complements, existing methods that rely on shared policies, representations, or parameters, offering a direct metric for encoding and distributing valuable insights.
- **Balanced Multi-Task Learning:** The proposed information synchronization mechanism is significant for managing the complexities of diverse tasks in MTRL. By dynamically adjusting the focus on different tasks based on their similarity and individual learning progress, CenRA ensures a more balanced and efficient overall system performance, preventing performance disparities among tasks.
- **Robust Transferability:** The demonstrated ability of the CRA to transfer knowledge effectively to new, unseen tasks, even without further training, highlights its robustness and potential for generalization in dynamic environments. This capability is crucial for scaling RL to real-world applications where agents frequently encounter novel situations.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Real-World RL Applications:** CenRA's ability to handle sparse rewards and transfer knowledge across tasks could significantly impact the development of more capable and adaptable autonomous agents in domains like robotics, autonomous driving, and complex industrial control. These fields often involve multiple related tasks and sparse performance feedback.
- **Reduced Training Time and Resources:** By accelerating learning and improving convergence speed, CenRA could lead to substantial reductions in the computational resources and time required to train multi-task RL agents, making advanced RL more accessible.
- **Foundation for Adaptive Systems:** The framework's flexibility in integrating various RL backbones and its adaptive information synchronization mechanism lay groundwork for building more sophisticated and self-adjusting RL systems.
- **Future Research Directions:** The identified limitations, such as the requirement for consistent state/action dimensions and the fixed trade-off in synchronization weights, suggest clear avenues for future research. This includes exploring preprocessing techniques for heterogeneous task structures and developing adaptive weight regulation mechanisms. Investigating how to maintain the performance ceiling of high-performing tasks while supporting lagging ones also presents an opportunity for further refinement.
