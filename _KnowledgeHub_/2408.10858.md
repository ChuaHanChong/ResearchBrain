# Centralized Reward Agent for Knowledge Sharing and Transfer in Multi-Task Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A multi-task reinforcement learning framework called Centralized Reward Agent (CenRA) is introduced, utilizing a centralized agent to generate dense, informative auxiliary rewards for distributed policy agents. This system enhances knowledge sharing among existing tasks and facilitates efficient transfer to new, unseen tasks, demonstrating improved learning efficiency, faster convergence, and outperforming state-of-the-art baselines across various sparse-reward domains.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning agents often struggle with sparse and delayed rewards, which significantly hinder effective exploration and policy learning.
- Existing Multi-Task Reinforcement Learning (MTRL) methods for knowledge transfer face limitations in efficiently adapting to new tasks and generalizing across diverse tasks.
- MTRL frameworks can suffer from imbalanced learning progress across different tasks, where some tasks might dominate or lag, leading to suboptimal overall system performance.

## Method

- A ==Centralized Reward Agent== (CRA) learns to generate continuous "knowledge rewards" from state-action observations, augmenting the sparse environmental rewards for individual policy agents.
- Policy agents utilize these augmented reward signals (environmental + knowledge reward) to optimize their learning, allowing for the integration of various RL backbone algorithms.
- An information synchronization mechanism dynamically adjusts the sampling quantity for CRA training, prioritizing less similar tasks and tasks with lagging performance to ensure balanced knowledge distribution.

## Results

- CenRA consistently achieved higher episodic returns and faster convergence compared to state-of-the-art baselines across Meta-World, 2DMaze, 3DPickup, and MujocoCar sparse-reward environments.
- The framework demonstrated robust knowledge transfer, with the pre-trained CRA enabling rapid adaptation and superior performance on new, unseen tasks, even without further training.
- The information synchronization mechanism, combining similarity and performance weights, significantly improved learning stability and overall system performance, preventing unbalanced progress across tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A centralized reward agent can effectively distill environment-relevant knowledge into dense auxiliary reward signals, offering a direct approach to overcome sparse reward challenges.
> - Knowledge encoded within these learned reward signals serves as a robust and transferable medium, enabling rapid adaptation and superior performance for both familiar and new tasks.
> - Dynamic coordination of task contributions based on their similarity and individual learning progress is crucial for achieving stable and efficient multi-task learning outcomes.
