# MobileManiBench: Simplifying Model Verification for Mobile Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> MobileManiBench introduces a simulation-first benchmark for mobile manipulation, generating a large-scale, multi-modal dataset from autonomously trained reinforcement learning policies for two distinct mobile robot embodiments. This platform enables scalable verification of Vision-Language-Action models, showing that richer sensory inputs enhance generalization while highlighting challenges in adapting to unseen objects and effectively utilizing dexterous hands.

## Problem

- Lack of diverse, scalable, and multi-modal datasets for training and verifying Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in complex mobile manipulation scenarios.
- Existing datasets are often limited to static, tabletop environments, simple grippers, and costly teleoperation, hindering generalization to real-world complexities.
- The high cost and inefficiency of real-world data collection and model verification for robots with mobile bases and dexterous hands.

## Method

- Developed **MobileManiBench**, a ==simulation-first framework== built on NVIDIA Isaac Sim, featuring two mobile-based robots with different end-effectors (parallel gripper and dexterous hand).
- Trained universal ==state-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies== (**MobileManiRL**) for autonomous trajectory generation across 630 objects and 5 skills.
- Automatically generated **MobileManiDataset** containing ==150K multi-modal trajectories== with synchronized language instructions, multi-view RGB-D-segmentation images, and robot/object states for VLA model training.

## Results

- MobileManiRL successfully generated **150K** trajectories with high success rates (G1: **89.6%**, XHand: **92.9%**), forming the diverse MobileManiDataset.
- MobileManiVLA achieved a **56.7%** success rate for G1 and **57.3%** for XHand on unseen objects and scenes, demonstrating improved generalization over previous VLA models by leveraging multi-view RGB-D and proprioceptive inputs.
- Unseen objects proved more challenging for VLA generalization than unseen scenes, and an initial real-world transfer experiment with the G1 robot achieved a **40%** success rate for opening a laptop.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Object structure and skill complexity significantly impact manipulation success, with generalizable VLA models facing greater challenges when learning from implicit sensory inputs for unseen objects/scenes compared to explicit state-based RL.
> - Rich multi-view RGB-D visual inputs and robot proprioceptive states are critical for improving VLA model generalization performance.
> - Dexterous hands offer potential advantages in specific pick tasks but introduce complexities like finger collisions for VLA models, resulting in varied performance compared to parallel grippers across different skills.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses challenges within the field of robotic manipulation, specifically concerning the development and verification of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Recent advancements in VLA models have enabled significant progress in robotic manipulation, including improved generalization to novel objects and diverse visual domains. However, this success is largely predicated on the availability of large-scale datasets. The prevailing datasets, such as the openX-Embodiment dataset, are primarily collected via teleoperation and are characterized by several limitations:
- They often feature static, tabletop environments.
- They typically involve a limited set of household objects.
- The data is predominantly collected from fixed, third-person, or head-mounted viewpoints, primarily using simple gripper-equipped robots.

These constraints present significant practical and research challenges. Modifying hardware configurations, such as integrating new sensors (e.g., depth or wrist-mounted cameras), extending capabilities to mobile platforms, or employing dexterous hands, necessitates costly and inefficient re-collection of data from scratch through teleoperation. Furthermore, teleoperation becomes increasingly complex and cumbersome when scaled to dexterous or mobile robotic platforms, thereby impeding rapid model development and iteration.

The MobileManiBench research positions itself by proposing a simulation-first framework designed to overcome these limitations. It seeks to provide a scalable and efficient alternative for verifying VLA architectures prior to real-world deployment. This approach contrasts with existing real-world benchmarks, which, despite their scale, often concentrate on fixed-base or gripper-equipped robots performing tabletop tasks, and whose data collection methods limit diversity and scalability for more complex mobile or dexterous manipulation scenarios. While some recent real-world efforts (e.g., DexGraspVLA, Humanoid Everyday, Mobile ALOHA) have expanded to dexterous hands and mobile manipulation, they still rely on human teleoperation, which restricts their diversity and scalability.

Compared to other simulation-based benchmarks, which alleviate cost and risk by simulating diverse manipulation tasks and collecting large-scale trajectories, MobileManiBench differentiates itself by specifically addressing the lack of large-scale, multi-modality trajectory datasets that support training universal models capable of generalizing across diverse object categories, manipulation skills, and realistic scenes, particularly for mobile manipulation with both parallel grippers and dexterous hands. By leveraging high-fidelity simulation and automated data generation via reinforcement learning, this work aims to fill a critical gap in VLA research, enabling systematic studies on the impact of different model structures and input modalities on mobile manipulation performance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this work stems from the limitations of current VLA model development, which is hindered by the high cost and impracticality of generating diverse, large-scale datasets for complex robotic setups. The authors identify two main issues:
- **Hardware and Sensor Configuration Rigidity:** Current teleoperation-based data collection methods are inflexible. Any alteration to robot hardware (e.g., adding new sensors like depth cameras, changing camera placements, extending to mobile bases, or replacing grippers with dexterous hands) requires extensive and costly data re-collection. This process is inefficient, particularly if the hardware modifications do not yield substantial performance improvements.
- **Scalability Challenges with Advanced Platforms:** While teleoperation pipelines are effective for simpler gripper-based robots, they become increasingly difficult to scale for dexterous or mobile robotic platforms. This operational burden impedes the rapid development, iteration, and verification of new VLA models tailored for these advanced systems.

To address these challenges, the overarching objective of this research is to propose and implement a "simulation-first" framework. This framework is designed to enable the scalable verification of VLA architectures within a simulated environment, thereby reducing the reliance on expensive and laborious real-world data collection.

The specific objectives of the MobileManiBench project are detailed as follows:
1. **Develop a Simulation-First Data Generation and Evaluation Pipeline:** Create an efficient pipeline for generating robotic manipulation data and evaluating VLA models entirely within a simulated environment, leveraging NVIDIA Isaac Sim.
2. **Introduce MobileManiBench:** Establish a large-scale benchmark specifically for mobile-based robotic manipulation, designed for embodied skill learning.
3. **Facilitate Diverse Robot Embodiments:** Incorporate two distinct mobile robot platforms: one equipped with a parallel gripper (G1 robot) and another with a dexterous anthropomorphic hand (XHand robot), allowing for comparative studies of end-effector capabilities.
4. **Support Mobile-Base Operation:** Ensure both robots are mounted on mobile bases, enabling the execution of spatially extended tasks that require coordinated navigation and manipulation.
5. **Enable Rich Multi-Modal Sensing:** Integrate two synchronized cameras (head-mounted and right wrist-mounted), each capable of capturing RGB, depth, and segmentation images, to facilitate multi-view and multi-sensory perception studies.
6. **Generate Comprehensive Multi-Modality Trajectories:** Automatically generate manipulation trajectories that include rich annotations: natural language instructions, multi-view visual data (RGB, depth, segmentation), and synchronized object/robot states and actions.
7. **Provide Diverse and Scalable Content:** Populate the benchmark with a substantial amount of content: 630 unique objects across 20 categories, 100 realistic and semi-structured scenes, and 5 mobile manipulation skills (open, close, pull, push, pick) encompassing over 100 tasks, ultimately yielding over 300,000 trajectories.
8. **Enable Controlled Experimentation:** Design the benchmark to serve as a flexible testbed for controlled studies of different robot configurations, sensory modalities, and model architectures, allowing researchers to efficiently evaluate improvements from new inputs or architectural designs.
9. **Accelerate Research on Data Efficiency and Generalization:** Provide a resource that can accelerate research in data-efficient learning and generalization for VLA models in embodied AI.
10. **Benchmark and Analyze VLA Models:** Systematically compare existing VLA models and the proposed MobileManiVLA model on the benchmark, offering empirical insights into perception, reasoning, and control in complex simulated environments.
11. **Ensure Open Science:** Publicly release all developed code, datasets, and models to foster reproducible research and collaborative innovation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The construction of MobileManiBench is structured into two main stages: MobileManiRL Training and MobileManiDataset Generation, followed by the training of the MobileManiVLA model. The entire framework leverages NVIDIA Isaac Sim as the simulation platform and employs reinforcement learning (RL) for autonomous data generation.

#### 3.1. MobileManiRL Training

Given the large number of robot-object-skill combinations (2 robots, 630 objects, 5 skills, 100+ tasks), manual policy design or teleoperation would be impractical. Therefore, the authors propose training a universal state-based reinforcement learning policy, termed MobileManiRL, for each specific robot-object-skill combination.

- **Robot Platforms:**
 - **G1 Robot:** Equipped with a parallel gripper, a 7-DOF right arm, and a 2-DOF mobile base. Its actions are 7-dimensional (6 for wrist pose displacement, 1 for gripper DOF).
 - **XHand Robot:** Equipped with a 12-DOF dexterous hand, a 7-DOF right arm, and a 2-DOF mobile base. Its actions are 18-dimensional (6 for wrist pose displacement, 12 for dexterous hand DOFs).
 Both robots are controlled by computing target joint angles from wrist pose displacements via inverse kinematics.
- **Objects, Skills, and Tasks:** The benchmark includes 630 articulated and holistic objects from 20 categories, sourced from PartNet-Mobility, UniDoorManip, and YCB datasets. Five mobile manipulation skills are defined: "open," "close," "pull," "push," and "pick," leading to over 100 distinct tasks.
- **Policy Parameterization:** MobileManiRL policies are parameterized using keypoint-based displacements, specifically focusing on the robot's gripper/hand points, the object's grasp point, and the task's goal point. This design aims for generalizability across diverse scenarios while maintaining task specificity.
- **Inputs:** The MobileManiRL policies receive five types of input:
 - **Time:** Encodes the timestep embedding.
 - **Object State:** Represents the states of the object's grasp point and goal point.
 - **Robot Proprioception:** Captures the robot's body and joint states.
 - **Robot-Object Distance:** Measures distances between robot gripper/hand points and the object grasp point.
 - **Previous Action:** Records the previously executed (6+D)-dimensional action.
- **Network Architecture:** Both the policy and value networks are implemented as 4-layer Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) with hidden dimensions {1024, 1024, 512, 512}. The policy network's output layer predicts the (6+D) dimensional action, while the value network outputs a single scalar.
- **Reward Function:** A comprehensive reward function combines:
 - **Global Distance Reward ($R_d$):** Penalizes distances between gripper/hand points and the object grasp point, and between the object grasp point and the goal point.
 - **Approach Action Reward ($R_a$):** Guides the gripper/hand towards the object grasp point before grasping.
 - **Grasp Reward ($R_g$):** Provides a bonus once grasping is achieved (determined by a distance threshold).
 - **Move Action Reward ($R_m$):** Encourages movement of the grasped object towards the goal point.
 - **Success Reward ($R_s$):** An additional bonus for reaching the goal point within a maximum of 300 steps.
- **Simplified Scene Training:** MobileManiRL policies are trained in 2 simplified scene settings (ground and tabletop) within Isaac Sim. Objects are initialized with randomized heights and orientations, and the robot's mobile base is initialized with random positional and rotational offsets relative to the object grasp point. This setup ensures policy robustness. Training was conducted for 1,182 robot-object-skill combinations, achieving mean success rates of 89.6% for the G1 robot and 92.9% for the XHand robot.

#### 3.2. MobileManiDataset Generation

Following the training of MobileManiRL policies, successful manipulation trajectories are collected in diverse realistic scenes.

- **Realistic Scene Rendering:** The 20 object categories are integrated into 5 realistic scene settings (space, wall, door, outdoor, tabletop), using digital assets from Isaac Sim and Genie Sim. 100 scene placements are manually annotated, with 80 designated for VLA training and 20 for testing (unseen scenes). These scenes include various everyday environments.
- **Dataset Analysis and Trajectory Details:** For each robot (G1 and XHand), the dataset combines 630 objects, 20 categories, 5 skills, 100+ tasks, and 100 scene placements. The dataset is split into training (506 objects, 80 scenes) and testing (124 objects, 20 scenes) subsets. For each of the 15,232 training combinations, 10 successful trajectories are generated, resulting in 150,000 training trajectories per robot.
 Each trajectory is recorded at 30 FPS, averaging 160 frames, and includes:
 - A natural language instruction (e.g., "open faucet").
 - Synchronized multi-view (head and wrist) RGB, depth, and segmentation images (520 × 520 resolution).
 - Corresponding object states.
 - Robot states.
 - Executed (6+D)-dimensional actions.
 All states and actions are recorded in the global world coordinate frame.

#### 3.3. MobileManiVLA Training

The aggregated MobileManiDataset is used to train a universal vision-language-action model, MobileManiVLA, for each robot. This model aims to generalize to unseen objects and scenes.

- **Model Architecture:** MobileManiVLA adopts a three-component structure inspired by CogACT:
 - **Vision Module:** Utilizes a pretrained SigLIP vision encoder from PaliGemma-2. It processes multi-view RGB and depth images (reshaped to 224 × 224 × 3) from both head and wrist cameras, generating dense visual embeddings.
 - **Language Module:** Employs a pretrained Gemma-2 language model from PaliGemma-2. It tokenizes and projects natural language instructions ("<skill> <object>") into language embeddings.
 - **Fusion and Cognition:** Vision and language embeddings are fused via a linear projection and combined with a learnable cognition token. These are then fed into the Gemma-2 language model to produce a cognition feature ($f_c_t$) that captures both perceptual and instructional context.
 - **Action Module:** Implemented as a diffusion-transformer (DiT). This module takes noisy action sequences, the cognition feature ($f_c_t$), and a state feature ($f_s_t$) (derived from encoding the 6-D robot wrist pose using an MLP) as inputs. The DiT iteratively refines its predictions to generate a sequence of clean actions (a_t to a_t+N) over multiple denoising steps.
- **Coordinate Frame Consistency:** All states and actions are expressed in the robot's mobile base coordinate frame to ensure consistency across different scenes.
- **Training Objective:** The model is trained end-to-end by minimizing the mean squared error (MSE) between the predicted Gaussian noise and the ground truth noise, as applied to the noisy action sequences.
- **Inference Strategy:** During inference, MobileManiVLA predicts a chunk of actions (N=16). An adaptive ensemble strategy with a window size of K=4 is employed to enhance trajectory smoothness and robustness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of MobileManiBench involved comprehensive experiments with MobileManiRL and MobileManiVLA, along with ablation studies and comparisons with existing VLA models.

#### 4.1. General Performance

- **MobileManiRL:** Achieved high mean success rates on seen objects in simplified training scenes (89.6% for G1 robot, 92.9% for XHand robot).
- **MobileManiVLA:** Showed moderate mean success rates when generalizing to unseen objects and scenes (56.7% for G1 robot, 57.3% for XHand robot). This decline indicates the inherent difficulty of generalization from explicit state-based learning to implicit, vision-language-based learning in novel environments.

**Takeaway 1: Object structure and skill complexity drive performance variance.**
- Object categories with simpler structures and motion patterns, such as toilet, laptop, and dishwasher (requiring lid-flipping), yielded higher success rates.
- More complex objects like car, door, and table, which demand precise handle localization and stable grasping, presented greater manipulation challenges.
- Manipulation skills requiring accurate and stable grasping (open, pull, pick) were generally more difficult to learn compared to simpler actions like close and push.

**Takeaway 2: Generalization remains challenging for VLA models with implicit inputs.**
- The performance difference between MobileManiRL (trained on seen objects with explicit state observations) and MobileManiVLA (trained as a universal model on unseen objects and scenes with implicit sensory inputs) highlights the generalization gap.
- MobileManiVLA's reliance on implicit inputs (language instruction, multi-view RGB-D images, wrist pose) rather than explicit world coordinates of keypoints introduces additional learning challenges but is necessary for real-world applicability and generalization to novel scenarios.

**Takeaway 3: Dexterous hands improve manipulation precision and versatility, but can be hindered by collisions.**
- With MobileManiRL, the XHand robot (92.9% success) outperformed the G1 robot (89.6%), particularly in open, pull, and pick skills, indicating advantages of dexterous manipulation.
- With MobileManiVLA, both robots showed comparable success rates (G1: 56.7%, XHand: 57.3%). The XHand robot performed worse on open and pull skills due to collisions between its dexterous fingers and object surfaces, which can disrupt stable grasps. However, the XHand demonstrated superior performance on the pick skill, benefiting from its ability to form force-closure grasps on holistic objects.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies and Model Comparisons

These experiments focused on a subset of challenging skills (open, pull, pick for the G1 robot) involving 6 object categories and 264 unseen test combinations.

**Takeaway 4: Multi-view and multi-modality visual inputs significantly enhance policy generalization.**
- Using only a head-view RGB image resulted in a 7.9% success rate, indicating its insufficiency for precise mobile manipulation.
- Adding either a head-view depth image or a wrist-view RGB image improved performance to 14.1% and 14.9%, respectively, demonstrating the value of additional geometric or visual cues.
- The combination of both RGB-D images from head-view and wrist-view cameras achieved the highest success rate of 28.2%, confirming the positive impact of multi-view and multi-modality visual inputs on manipulation accuracy and generalization.

**Takeaway 5: State inputs from robot proprioception or simple object detection further improve performance.**
- Incorporating the robot's wrist pose (proprioceptive information) increased the success rate from 22.4% (no state input) to 28.2%.
- Further adding pseudo-observations like the first-frame positions of the object grasp point and goal point (expressed in the robot's mobile base coordinate frame) led to an additional performance gain, reaching 36.6%. This indicates that even coarse spatial priors about objects can enhance policy grounding.

**Takeaway 6: Unseen objects present greater challenges than unseen scenes.**
- MobileManiVLA achieved 59.6% success when both objects and scenes were seen during testing.
- Performance dropped to 51.3% for seen objects in unseen scenes and 39.2% for unseen objects in seen scenes.
- The lowest success rate (28.2%) occurred when both objects and scenes were unseen, highlighting that generalizing to novel object structures is more challenging than adapting to new scene layouts.

**Takeaway 7: MobileManiBench provides a unified platform for training and evaluating VLA models.**
- Comparative benchmarking showed that OpenVLA (4.5%) and CogACT (6.8%), which primarily use head-view RGB images, had lower success rates.
- π_0 (11.2%) and π_0.5 (18.8%) performed better by processing both head-view and wrist-view RGB images along with wrist pose states. π_0.5's higher performance was attributed to pretraining on more mobile manipulation trajectories.
- MobileManiVLA achieved the highest success rate of 28.2%, attributed to its joint use of head-view and wrist-view RGB-D images alongside wrist pose states, underscoring the benefits of comprehensive multi-view and multi-modality inputs.

**Takeaway 8: Base mobility is crucial for effective manipulation beyond tabletop tasks.**
- Evaluating MobileManiRL with a fixed G1 robot base on a subset of tasks resulted in a success rate of 25.4%, a significant drop compared to the 82.8% achieved with a mobile base. This demonstrates the necessity of base mobility for spatial manipulation that extends beyond static tabletop interactions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MobileManiBench represents a contribution to the field of robotic manipulation and embodied AI by addressing several critical limitations of current research paradigms.

The primary significance lies in its **simulation-first framework for VLA model verification**. By establishing a large-scale, automated data generation pipeline within a high-fidelity simulator, the project provides an efficient and scalable alternative to expensive and cumbersome real-world data collection. This reduces the financial and logistical barriers to entry for researchers, enabling faster iteration and development cycles for VLA architectures.

The **diversity and scale of the MobileManiBench benchmark itself** are notable. It incorporates two distinct mobile robotic platforms (parallel-gripper and dexterous-hand), 630 objects across 20 categories, 5 mobile manipulation skills, and over 100 tasks executed in 100 realistic scenes, generating 300,000 multi-modal trajectories. This rich dataset, comprising language instructions, multi-view RGB-depth-segmentation images, and synchronized robot/object states and actions, offers a comprehensive resource for training and evaluating advanced VLA models.

The benchmark's design **facilitates controlled scientific studies**. Researchers can systematically investigate the impact of different robot embodiments, sensing modalities (e.g., single vs. multi-view, RGB vs. RGB-D), and policy architectures on manipulation performance and generalization abilities without the practical constraints of real-world experiments. This capability is expected to accelerate research on data efficiency and generalization, key challenges in embodied AI.

Furthermore, the **empirical insights derived from benchmarking representative VLA models and conducting ablation studies** on MobileManiBench contribute to a deeper understanding of perception, reasoning, and control in complex robotic environments. Findings regarding the challenges of generalizing to unseen objects versus unseen scenes, the benefits of multi-view and multi-modal sensory inputs, the trade-offs and advantages of dexterous hands, and the critical role of base mobility for non-tabletop tasks provide valuable guidance for future VLA model design.

Finally, the **public release of all code, datasets, and models** promotes open science, reproducible research, and collaborative innovation within the robotics and AI communities. By providing a standardized and flexible platform, MobileManiBench has the potential to foster the development of next-generation, general-purpose VLA models, ultimately paving the way for more capable and adaptive intelligent robots.
