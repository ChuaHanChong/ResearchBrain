# MobileManiBench: Simplifying Model Verification for Mobile Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> MobileManiBench introduces a simulation-first benchmark for mobile manipulation, generating a large-scale, multi-modal dataset from autonomously trained reinforcement learning policies for two distinct mobile robot embodiments. This platform enables scalable verification of Vision-Language-Action models, showing that richer sensory inputs enhance generalization while highlighting challenges in adapting to unseen objects and effectively utilizing dexterous hands.

## Problem

- Lack of diverse, scalable, and multi-modal datasets for training and verifying Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in complex mobile manipulation scenarios.
- Existing datasets are often limited to static, tabletop environments, simple grippers, and costly teleoperation, hindering generalization to real-world complexities.
- The high cost and inefficiency of real-world data collection and model verification for robots with mobile bases and dexterous hands.

## Method

- Developed **MobileManiBench**, a ==simulation-first framework== built on NVIDIA Isaac Sim, featuring two mobile-based robots with different end-effectors (parallel gripper and dexterous hand).
- Trained universal ==state-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies== (**MobileManiRL**) for autonomous trajectory generation across 630 objects and 5 skills.
- Automatically generated **MobileManiDataset** containing ==150K multi-modal trajectories== with synchronized language instructions, multi-view RGB-D-segmentation images, and robot/object states for VLA model training.

## Results

- MobileManiRL successfully generated **150K** trajectories with high success rates (G1: **89.6%**, XHand: **92.9%**), forming the diverse MobileManiDataset.
- MobileManiVLA achieved a **56.7%** success rate for G1 and **57.3%** for XHand on unseen objects and scenes, demonstrating improved generalization over previous VLA models by leveraging multi-view RGB-D and proprioceptive inputs.
- Unseen objects proved more challenging for VLA generalization than unseen scenes, and an initial real-world transfer experiment with the G1 robot achieved a **40%** success rate for opening a laptop.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Object structure and skill complexity significantly impact manipulation success, with generalizable VLA models facing greater challenges when learning from implicit sensory inputs for unseen objects/scenes compared to explicit state-based RL.
> - Rich multi-view RGB-D visual inputs and robot proprioceptive states are critical for improving VLA model generalization performance.
> - Dexterous hands offer potential advantages in specific pick tasks but introduce complexities like finger collisions for VLA models, resulting in varied performance compared to parallel grippers across different skills.
