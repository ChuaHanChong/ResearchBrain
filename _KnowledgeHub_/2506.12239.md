# ViTaSCOPE: Visuo-tactile Implicit Representation for In-hand Pose and Extrinsic Contact Estimation

> [!summary] Summary
> ViTaSCOPE presents a visuo-tactile implicit representation that simultaneously estimates in-hand object pose and localizes extrinsic contacts. The method fuses visual point clouds with high-resolution tactile shear fields, trained entirely in simulation, to achieve accurate pose and contact predictions on a robot with zero-shot transfer to real-world tasks.

## Problem

- Robot perception often struggles with partial observations and occlusions from vision alone, while tactile sensing is limited to intrinsic contacts, making simultaneous in-hand object pose and extrinsic contact estimation challenging.
- Many existing methods assume fixed, rigid grasps or known object poses, limiting their applicability to dynamic manipulation where objects can shift within the gripper.
- Collecting large-scale, diverse, and precisely labeled real-world data for contact-rich interactions is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming, necessitating effective sim-to-real transfer strategies.

## Method

- A unified neural implicit representation is developed, comprising an Object Module (==SDF== for geometry), a Tactile Module (==shear field prediction==), and a Contact Module (extrinsic contact probability).
- A two-stage training process is employed: pre-training the Object Module on object geometry, then jointly training the Tactile and Contact Modules using simulated visuo-tactile data.
- Inference involves an optimization-based approach to infer the in-hand SE(3) object pose and a ==latent trial code== that characterizes the extrinsic contact configuration, enabling subsequent contact prediction on the object's surface.

## Results

- ViTaSCOPE estimated in-hand object pose in real-world scenarios with average translational errors of **4.049 mm** and rotational errors of **0.907 degrees**, outperforming the ICP baseline (**43.0 mm** translational, **78.6 degrees** rotational error).
- The model accurately predicted extrinsic contact patches with an average Chamfer Distance of **0.0920 m²** in real-world experiments, and its predictions often appeared more realistic than simulation ground truth.
- The system operates at **0.3Hz** inference speed and demonstrated effective generalization to unseen object geometries beyond its training set.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fusing vision with high-resolution tactile shear fields is crucial for robust in-hand object pose and extrinsic contact estimation, outperforming tactile RGB images for sim-to-real transfer.
> - Neural implicit representations can effectively model dynamic in-hand object pose and extrinsic contact information simultaneously within a unified framework, leading to physically consistent predictions.
> - Training entirely in simulation using advanced tactile sensor simulation (TacSL) and leveraging robust shear field representations enables successful zero-shot transfer to real-world robotic systems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is rapidly advancing towards more autonomous and dexterous manipulation in unstructured environments. A fundamental challenge in achieving this is robust and accurate perception of objects and their interactions with the environment, especially under conditions of partial observability or physical contact. This paper addresses this challenge by focusing on in-hand object pose estimation and, critically, extrinsic contact localization, which are crucial for planning and executing complex manipulation tasks such as tool use, assembly, or re-grasping.

The work builds upon several key areas within robotics and computer vision:
- **Perception for Dexterous Manipulation:** Traditional approaches often rely solely on vision, which struggles with occlusions and provides limited information about contact forces. This paper highlights the necessity of multi-modal sensing.
- **Multimodal Sensing (Vision and Touch):** The integration of vision and touch has emerged as a powerful paradigm to overcome the limitations of single modalities. Vision provides global context and object geometry, while touch offers high-resolution, localized information about contact patches, forces, and material properties. ViTaSCOPE directly contributes to this by demonstrating a seamless fusion of these complementary cues.
- **Neural Implicit Representations:** Pioneered by works like DeepSDF for shape representation, implicit functions have gained traction for their ability to represent continuous 3D geometry at infinite resolution. This paper extends their application beyond static shape reconstruction to dynamic state estimation, incorporating contact information. Previous works, including those from Fazeli's group (VIRDO, NDCF), have explored implicit representations for deformable objects and contact, but often with assumptions like rigid grasps or known object poses.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** A significant hurdle in robot learning is the difficulty of collecting large, diverse real-world datasets for training. Simulation offers a scalable solution, but the "sim-to-real gap" often prevents direct transfer. ViTaSCOPE tackles this by training entirely in simulation using advanced tactile sensor simulation (TacSL) and demonstrating zero-shot transfer to the real world. This is a critical enabler for practical robotic systems.

ViTaSCOPE differentiates itself from prior art by addressing several key limitations:
- **Beyond Rigid Grasps and Known Poses:** Unlike many previous works that assume rigid grasps or fixed, known in-hand object poses (e.g., NCF, VIRDO), ViTaSCOPE explicitly infers the in-hand object pose in the world frame, making it more applicable to real-world, dynamic manipulation scenarios where objects might shift within the gripper.
- **Focus on Extrinsic Contact with Shear Fields:** While some works have explored extrinsic contact estimation (e.g., Im2Contact, NCF), they often rely on 2D visual cues, force/torque sensing, or tactile RGB images. ViTaSCOPE uniquely leverages high-resolution *tactile shear fields*, which are shown to be more robust for characterizing extrinsic contact and bridging the sim-to-real gap compared to tactile RGB images. This is a crucial distinction as shear information directly relates to forces and slip, providing richer contact dynamics.
- **Unified Framework for Simultaneous Estimation:** Instead of treating pose estimation and contact localization as separate problems, ViTaSCOPE proposes a single, unified implicit representation that jointly models object shape, in-hand pose, and extrinsic contact, allowing for physically consistent predictions (e.g., contacts lie on the object surface).
- **Scalable Data Generation:** The reliance on Isaac Gym and TacSL for synthetic data generation allows for creating large-scale datasets of diverse contact interactions, which would be prohibitively expensive to collect in the real world.

In summary, ViTaSCOPE pushes the boundaries of robotic perception by offering a robust, generalized, and data-efficient solution for simultaneous in-hand pose and extrinsic contact estimation, leveraging the strengths of visuo-tactile fusion and implicit neural representations for real-world deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of ViTaSCOPE is to enable precise and robust perception for dexterous, contact-rich object manipulation. Specifically, the authors aim to achieve **simultaneous in-hand object pose estimation and extrinsic contact localization** using a novel approach that fuses visual and high-resolution tactile feedback.

The core motivations driving this research stem from the inherent challenges in robot manipulation:
- **Ambiguity of Partial Observations:** Both vision and tactile sensing, when used in isolation, provide incomplete and often noisy information. Vision offers a global view but is susceptible to occlusions (e.g., by the robot's gripper or the object itself during extrinsic contact). Tactile sensing provides high-resolution, localized contact information but is inherently limited to areas of direct physical interaction (intrinsic contact) and cannot perceive contacts occurring on parts of the object not touching the gripper (extrinsic contacts). A unified framework is needed to leverage their complementary strengths.
- **Importance of Extrinsic Contacts:** For many complex manipulation tasks, such as using a tool to interact with the environment (e.g., writing, pivoting, or pushing), understanding *where* and *how* the held object makes contact with external surfaces is critical. This "extrinsic contact" information is distinct from the "intrinsic contact" between the gripper and the object.
- **Challenge of In-Hand Pose Variability:** Many existing methods for contact estimation or shape reconstruction assume a fixed, known pose of the object relative to the end-effector (a rigid grasp). In reality, objects can translate and rotate within the gripper due to sensor compliance, slip, or intentional re-grasping, requiring accurate in-hand pose inference in the world frame.
- **Need for Scalable Training Data:** Training data for contact-rich interactions, especially those involving precise tactile feedback and extrinsic contact labels, is scarce and difficult to acquire in the real world. This necessitates a robust sim-to-real transfer strategy.
- **Limitations of Prior Work:** As discussed in Section 2, existing methods often fall short by:
 - Assuming rigid grasps or fixed object poses.
 - Failing to fully exploit high-resolution tactile information for extrinsic contact.
 - Suffering from sim-to-real gaps when using less robust tactile representations (e.g., RGB images from tactile sensors).
 - Not providing infinite resolution or physically consistent predictions (e.g., contacts on the object surface).

By addressing these motivations, ViTaSCOPE aims to provide a foundational perceptual capability for robots to perform more complex, human-like dexterous manipulation tasks in dynamic and unstructured environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ViTaSCOPE proposes a unified neural implicit representation system designed to infer object geometry, in-hand pose, and extrinsic contact from visuo-tactile observations. The methodology is structured around three interconnected neural modules and a two-stage training and inference process, heavily relying on simulation for data generation.

**A. Core Architectural Components (Neural Implicit Modules):**
1. **Object Module (O):** Represents the object's 3D geometry as a continuous Signed Distance Function (SDF). Given a 3D query point `q` in the object's canonical frame, `O` predicts its signed distance `s`. This allows for high-resolution reconstruction of the object's surface as the zero-level set of the SDF.
2. **Tactile Module (T):** Models the tactile shear field induced by extrinsic contact interactions. This module takes a 2D query point `g` on the tactile sensor's plane and predicts the local shear displacement vector `[u, v]T`. Crucially, `T` is conditioned on a latent representation of the in-hand object pose (ξ) and a "trial code" (ψ) which characterizes the contact configuration. This conditioning allows the model to interpret shear feedback in the context of the object's pose. Separate tactile modules are instantiated for distributed sensors (e.g., left and right grippers).
3. **Contact Module (C):** Estimates the probability of extrinsic contact at a given 3D query point `q`. To ensure geometry-aware contact predictions, `C` is conditioned on the intermediate activations `zO` from the Object Module, as well as the inferred in-hand pose `ξ` and tactile trial code `ψ`. This design enforces that predicted contacts lie on the object's surface and are consistent with both geometry and tactile evidence.

**B. Data Generation and Sim-to-Real Strategy:**
Acknowledging the difficulty of real-world data collection, ViTaSCOPE adopts a sim-to-real approach:
- **Simulation Environment:** Isaac Gym, coupled with TacSL (a library for vision-based tactile sensor simulation), is used to generate a large-scale dataset.
- **Data Collection Process:** A Franka Emika Panda robot with simulated GelSight R1.5 sensors grasps 3D-printed tool geometries (6 different shapes, Fig. 4) at randomized pitch angles. The robot then pushes the grasped object onto a table at varying angles, creating diverse grasp poses and extrinsic contact modes (point, line, patch contacts).
- **Sensor Data Generation:**
 - **Visual:** Eight cameras are placed around the end-effector to capture partial point clouds of the segmented object.
 - **Tactile (Shear Field):** TacSL's penalty-based tactile simulation is used to collect shear displacement vectors on a uniform 2D grid for each tactile sensor. These vectors are normalized and augmented with Gaussian noise to enhance robustness and bridge the sim-to-real gap.
 - **Ground Truth Contact Labels:** Since Isaac Gym doesn't directly provide contact locations, scenes are replicated in Open3D, and contact points are estimated by thresholding object-table interpenetration (soft-contact points).
- **Dataset Size:** 500 contact interactions per tool (3000 total) for training, and 50 per tool (300 total) for simulated testing. Real-world test data consists of 10 interactions per geometry (60 total) using GelSlim 4.0 sensors, a Realsense D435 camera, and AprilTags for ground truth pose tracking. Grounded SAM v2 is used for real-world object segmentation.

**C. Training Process (Two Stages):**
1. **Pre-training Object Module (O):** `O` is initially trained in isolation to accurately represent the object's geometry as an SDF. This involves sampling query points (off-surface, on-surface, near-surface) from the normalized canonical mesh of the object and minimizing an SDF loss (`L_sdf`) that includes both distance error and normal alignment error. Once trained, the weights of `O` are frozen.
2. **Joint Training of ViTaSCOPE:** With `O` frozen, the Tactile (`T`) and Contact (`C`) modules, along with the object module's activations, are jointly trained. The training loss (`L_train`) comprises:
 - `L_shear`: L1 loss between predicted and ground truth shear displacement vectors.
 - `L_embedding`: L2 regularization on the trial code `ψ` to encourage compact latent representations.
 - `L_contact`: Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss for classifying contact probability at query points on the object surface.
 - `L_hyper`: L2 regularization on the weights predicted by the hypernetworks of `T` and `C` to prevent overfitting and encourage smoothness.

**D. Inference Process (Inference via Optimization):**
At inference time, given a partial visual point cloud, tactile point clouds, and observed shear displacement fields:
1. **Pose Estimation:** The in-hand SE(3) object pose is inferred by minimizing the SDF loss from the Object Module. This optimization takes the observed visuo-tactile surface points in the world frame and finds the object pose that aligns them with the object's canonical SDF. This is performed using gradient descent, often referred to as "inference via optimization."
2. **Extrinsic Contact Trial Code Inference:** Once the object pose is estimated, the trial code `ψ` is optimized. This optimization aims to find the `ψ` that best explains the observed shear field measurements through the Tactile Module, given the inferred object pose. This extracts latent information about the contact configuration.
3. **Contact Prediction:** With both the object pose and the trial code inferred, the full ViTaSCOPE model is evaluated at query points on the object surface. The Contact Module predicts the contact probability for each point. The final contact patch geometry is extracted as the intersection of points on the object's surface with a contact probability above a defined threshold.

The use of hypernetworks for conditioning `T` and `C` on latent codes for pose and contact configuration is a modern technique in neural implicit modeling, allowing for greater flexibility and expressiveness in the learned fields.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ViTaSCOPE was extensively evaluated in both simulated and real-world environments across six different tool geometries, assessing its performance on geometry reconstruction, in-hand pose estimation, and extrinsic contact prediction.

**A. Geometry Reconstruction:**
- The pretrained Object Module achieved a mean Chamfer Distance (CD) of 0.052 m² between the normalized SDF mesh reconstruction and the ground truth normalized object mesh. This indicates high fidelity in reconstructing the object's 3D geometry, which serves as a foundational capability for subsequent pose and contact estimation.

**B. In-hand Pose Estimation:**
- **Superiority over Baselines:** ViTaSCOPE demonstrated significantly higher accuracy in estimating in-hand object pose compared to the Open3D ICP baseline. In real-world experiments, ViTaSCOPE achieved average translational errors of 4.049 mm and rotational errors of 0.907 degrees, drastically outperforming ICP (average translational error 43.0 mm, rotational error 78.6 degrees). This is particularly impressive given that ViTaSCOPE does not have direct access to the ground truth object geometry during inference, relying on its learned implicit representation.
- **Complementary Visuo-Tactile Fusion:** Ablation studies (Table II) clearly show the benefit of fusing vision and tactile data. Vision-only or tactile-only pose estimation led to degraded accuracy, highlighting that vision provides global alignment while touch refines local pose with dense, lower-uncertainty contact points. The combined approach leverages the strengths of both modalities for robust estimation.
- **Effective Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The pose estimation performance remained consistent across simulation and real-world trials, indicating successful sim-to-real transfer of the learned model.

**C. Extrinsic Contact Estimation:**
- **Accurate Localization:** ViTaSCOPE accurately predicted extrinsic contact patches on the object surface, even in challenging scenarios with occlusions. The average Chamfer Distance for contact patches in the real world was 0.0920 m².
- **Realistic Contact Predictions:** The model's predicted contact patches often appeared more realistic (e.g., sharper point or line contacts for rigid objects) than the ground truth contact labels generated in simulation via soft-contact SDF thresholding. This suggests that ViTaSCOPE learns a more physically plausible representation of contact.
- **Robustness of Shear Field Representation:** A key finding from the ablation study (Table III) is the superiority of tactile shear field representation over RGB-based tactile information for sim-to-real transfer and accurate extrinsic contact prediction. Replacing the shear module with an RGB-based one led to a performance drop, underscoring the richer and more robust nature of shear cues for characterizing contact.
- **Failure of SOTA Baseline (NCF):** The SOTA Neural Contact Fields (NCF) baseline, when adapted for real-world scenarios by providing it with ViTaSCOPE's pose estimates, showed a significant drop in performance (average CD of 0.8904 m²), highlighting its sensitivity to pose estimation errors and the limitations of its tactile RGB image interpretation for sim-to-real.
- **Impact of Pose Accuracy on Contact:** Ablations showed that while ViTaSCOPE is robust to *moderate* pose errors (e.g., vision-only pose estimation still yielded comparable contact prediction), severe inaccuracies from tactile-only pose estimation significantly degraded contact prediction performance, reinforcing the need for accurate in-hand pose.

**D. Computational Performance and Generalizability:**
- **Inference Speed:** ViTaSCOPE operates at 0.3Hz (2.5s for pose estimation, 0.7s for trial code inference, 0.08s for contact prediction), which is practical for many robotic applications, with potential for further acceleration.
- **Generalization to Unseen Objects:** The work demonstrated ViTaSCOPE's ability to handle more complex geometries, including real-world predictions on a Stanford bunny, suggesting potential for generalization to novel objects beyond the training set, possibly through object codes or open-vocabulary detectors in future extensions.

Overall, the results strongly validate ViTaSCOPE's design, demonstrating its effectiveness in simultaneous, highly accurate, and robust visuo-tactile perception of in-hand objects and their extrinsic interactions, bridging the critical sim-to-real gap.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ViTaSCOPE represents a significant advancement in robot perception for dexterous manipulation, with substantial implications for future robotics research and applications.

**A. Foundational Capability for Dexterous Manipulation:**
By providing accurate and simultaneous estimates of in-hand object pose and extrinsic contact locations, ViTaSCOPE delivers a critical perceptual foundation for robots. These capabilities are indispensable for complex tasks that require precise interaction with the environment, such as:
- **Tool Use:** Enabling robots to effectively wield and manipulate tools (e.g., screwdrivers, spatulas, pens) by understanding where the tool interacts with the workspace.
- **Assembly and Insertion:** Guiding objects into precise locations or fittings, where contact forces and locations are crucial for successful completion.
- **Re-grasping and In-hand Manipulation:** Adapting grasps based on new contact information or deliberately re-positioning an object within the hand through environmental contact.
- **Contact-Rich Locomotion:** While focused on manipulation, the principles could potentially extend to robotic locomotion where understanding ground contact is vital.

**B. Paradigm Shift in Multimodal Perception:**
ViTaSCOPE's unified implicit representation framework for fusing vision and high-resolution tactile feedback sets a new standard. It moves beyond separate perception modules by intrinsically linking object geometry, pose, and contact information. This allows for:
- **Physically Consistent Predictions:** Ensuring that predicted contacts always lie on the object's surface, as the contact module is conditioned on the object's geometry, leading to more robust and reliable estimates.
- **Seamless Reasoning:** Enabling the model to reason holistically about visuo-tactile cues, exploiting their complementarity to overcome ambiguities inherent in single modalities.

**C. Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer and Scalability:**
The successful zero-shot transfer of a model trained entirely in simulation to the real world is a major breakthrough. This is achieved through:
- **Leveraging Advanced Simulation:** Utilizing Isaac Gym and TacSL for generating vast, diverse datasets of contact interactions and high-fidelity shear field data.
- **Robust Tactile Representation:** The finding that shear fields are more effective than RGB tactile images for sim-to-real transfer is a crucial insight, enabling more reliable deployment of contact-aware policies trained in simulation. This addresses one of the most persistent challenges in robot learning.
- **Scalable Data Acquisition:** The simulation-based training pipeline alleviates the immense challenge and cost of collecting real-world data for complex, contact-rich scenarios.

**D. Future Research Directions:**
ViTaSCOPE also opens up several exciting avenues for future research, as outlined by the authors:
- **Integration of Normal Force:** Incorporating normal force information alongside shear fields could provide a more complete understanding of contact pressures.
- **Handling In-Hand Slippage and Object Deformation:** While the current work infers object pose, explicitly modeling compliant grasps, slippage, and object deformation would further enhance realism and applicability.
- **Generalization to Unseen Objects:** Extending the framework to learn object-agnostic representations or quickly adapt to novel objects without requiring explicit mesh models during training is a critical next step for broader applicability.
- **Extension to Dexterous Multi-fingered Hands:** Applying the distributed tactile module concept to complex dexterous hands would unlock more sophisticated manipulation capabilities.
- **Real-time Control and Planning:** The ability to accurately estimate pose and contact in a unified manner paves the way for integrating these perceptual outputs directly into real-time feedback control loops for agile, contact-rich manipulation tasks.

In conclusion, ViTaSCOPE is a landmark contribution that significantly advances the state-of-the-art in robot perception. Its novel integration of visuo-tactile sensing, implicit representations, and robust sim-to-real methodologies provides foundational capabilities that will empower robots to perform more dexterous, intelligent, and reliable manipulation in complex, unstructured environments.
