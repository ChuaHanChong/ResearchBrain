# DexSinGrasp: Learning a Unified Policy for Dexterous Object Singulation and Grasping in Densely Cluttered Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> DexSinGrasp, a framework from the National University of Singapore, presents a unified policy for dexterous robotic hands to perform simultaneous object singulation and grasping in densely cluttered environments. This approach achieves up to 98% success in simulation and 61% in real-world practical clutter, leveraging finger dexterity to efficiently clear space and grasp objects.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation in densely cluttered environments struggles to efficiently grasp target objects, particularly with dexterous hands whose high degrees of freedom are underutilized for object singulation.
- Existing methods for cluttered grasping often require complete object isolation or rely on multi-stage approaches, which limit synergistic potential between singulation and grasping, leading to suboptimal performance.
- Transferring complex dexterous manipulation policies from simulation to real-world robots remains challenging due to reliance on privileged information and the difficulty of learning in high-dimensional action spaces from scratch.

## Method

- Developed **DexSinGrasp**, a unified ==reinforcement learning== policy that integrates dexterous object singulation and grasping into a single objective using a piece-wise reward function that continuously incentivizes increasing space around the target.
- Implemented a clutter arrangement curriculum learning strategy, starting with simple single-object grasping and progressing through increasingly complex dense and random clutter configurations, to enhance learning efficiency and generalization.
- Employed a teacher-student policy distillation method, training a vision-based student policy from state-based teacher policies to enable robust sim-to-real transfer and real-world deployment.

## Results

- In simulation, the DexSinGrasp teacher policy achieved a **98%** average success rate and 118 average steps in dense clutter, significantly outperforming baselines which reached **39%** (GraspReward-Only) and **72%** (Multi-Stage) success rates.
- The vision-based student policy achieved an **89%** average success rate in simulation and demonstrated strong generalization, achieving **81%** success on unseen irregular clutter (teacher) and **61%** success in zero-shot real-world practical clutter.
- Comparative analysis across different hand DoF confirmed that increasing finger dexterity consistently improved success rates and reduced average steps across all complex cluttered tasks, highlighting its critical role in effective singulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified policy, leveraging finger dexterity through a continuous singulation reward, significantly outperforms multi-stage or grasp-only approaches for cluttered object retrieval by enabling synergistic singulation and grasping.
> - Clutter arrangement curriculum learning, progressing from dense to random arrangements, is crucial for effectively training complex dexterous manipulation policies in high-dimensional, cluttered spaces, leading to high success rates and strong generalization.
> - High degrees of freedom in robotic fingers are empirically validated as essential for efficient and precise object singulation within tight, confined spaces, directly impacting task success and efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation in cluttered environments, specifically grasping target objects, presents a fundamental challenge in the field of robotics, with applications spanning production lines and assembly processes. Prior research has explored methods for two-fingered grippers, often focusing on learning synergies between pushing and grasping. For instance, approaches like that by Zeng et al. used two networks to coordinate pushing and grasping, though these methods often faced limitations such as low sample efficiency or the requirement for complete object isolation due to the mechanical constraints of grippers, making singulation (spatial separation of objects) inefficient. Subsequent work introduced adversarial training to enhance synergy learning efficiency.

With the advent of dexterous robotic hands, possessing high degrees of freedom (DoF), there is a recognized potential for complex manipulation tasks. However, effectively leveraging these capabilities for grasping in densely cluttered settings remains largely unexplored. Existing dexterous grasping approaches primarily target loosely cluttered environments by extracting scene information from segmented point clouds or images, without explicit training for singulation. These methods typically struggle in denser clutter where simply avoiding surrounding objects is insufficient for a successful grasp.

Some studies have attempted to address dense clutter with dexterous hands through task decomposition, such as the three-stage framework proposed by Jiang et al. which trains separate policies for isolating, grasping, and lifting. While decomposition simplifies learning, it can limit the synergistic potential between singulation and grasping, potentially leading to suboptimal transitions and longer execution times. Moreover, recent work exploring dexterous hands for object exposure has often emphasized palm-driven motion during singulation, rather than leveraging the finer dexterity of individual fingers.

This research paper positions itself by identifying a gap in the existing literature: the lack of a unified policy that effectively leverages the high DoF of dexterous hands for *efficient object singulation specifically to facilitate grasping* in *densely cluttered environments*, with a particular emphasis on finger dexterity. The authors argue that previous methods either lack the necessary singulation capability for dense clutter, or fail to fully exploit the inherent dexterity of multi-fingered hands within a unified, synergistic framework. The work aims to integrate singulation and grasping into a single learning objective, drawing on the successes of curriculum learning for complex dexterous tasks and policy distillation for real-world deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified policy, named DexSinGrasp, that enables a dexterous robotic hand to efficiently retrieve a target object from densely cluttered environments. This overarching goal is driven by several motivations and broken down into specific objectives:

- **Addressing the Challenge of Cluttered Grasping with Dexterous Hands:** The core motivation stems from the difficulty of grasping objects in environments where target objects are tightly surrounded by others, rendering direct grasping impossible. While dexterous hands offer significant potential due to their high DoF for complex manipulation, their capabilities have not been fully exploited for efficient singulation within cluttered contexts. Existing approaches either do not perform explicit singulation or rely on less efficient methods like palm-driven movements, which limit maneuverability in tight spaces.

- **Leveraging Finger Dexterity for Efficient Singulation:** The authors argue that the high DoF of dexterous fingers can be leveraged to create sufficient space for grasping more efficiently than two-fingered grippers or palm-dominant dexterous hand strategies. The flexibility of fingers allows for subtle movements to separate surrounding objects without requiring large movements of the end-effector (palm). This efficiency is a key driver for developing a policy that integrates singulation as an inherent part of the grasping process.

- **Developing a Unified Policy for Singulation and Grasping:** Instead of a multi-stage approach where singulation and grasping are performed by separate, sequentially executed policies, the research aims to learn a single, unified policy. The motivation here is to harness the synergy between singulation and grasping. Decomposed approaches can lead to longer execution times and suboptimal transitions because minimal singulation may often be sufficient for a dexterous grasp, a nuance that separate policies might not capture. A unified reward design is proposed to seamlessly integrate these two actions.

- **Enhancing Learning Efficiency and Generalization with Curriculum Learning:** Learning complex dexterous manipulation in densely cluttered, high-dimensional spaces from scratch is computationally challenging and sample-inefficient. To mitigate this, the authors are motivated to employ curriculum learning, progressively increasing the complexity of the clutter arrangements during training. This approach is intended to improve both the success rate and the generalization capabilities of the policy across diverse clutter conditions.

- **Enabling Real-World Deployment through Policy Distillation:** Sim-to-real transfer is a known challenge in robotics. Policies trained with privileged information (e.g., precise object poses, singulation distances, joint forces) from simulation often cannot be directly deployed on physical robots due to sensor limitations. The motivation is to train a vision-based student policy via distillation from the state-based teacher policy. This allows the learned skills to be transferred to a real robot using visual observations (e.g., point clouds), making the system deployable.

In summary, the key objectives are:
1. To develop a unified reinforcement learning (RL) policy capable of performing both dexterous object singulation and grasping to efficiently retrieve objects from tightly cluttered environments.
2. To implement a clutter arrangement curriculum learning strategy to progressively train this unified policy, enhancing its performance and generalization across varied clutter complexities.
3. To utilize teacher-student policy distillation to convert the state-based simulation policy into a vision-based policy suitable for real-world robotic deployment.
4. To rigorously evaluate the proposed method against baselines across a range of cluttered grasping tasks, analyzing its effectiveness, efficiency, generalization capabilities, and specifically the impact of finger dexterity.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The DexSinGrasp framework addresses the challenge of dexterous grasping in cluttered environments through a structured reinforcement learning approach, incorporating a unified reward design, curriculum learning, and policy distillation.

**Problem Formulation:**
The authors define a "Singulation-Necessary Clutter (SNC)" as a configuration where a target object is initially ungraspable and requires singulation before a successful grasp. Three conditions are imposed to create reproducible SNC scenes: the target is fully enclosed by non-target objects at the geometric center, horizontal distance to surrounding objects is less than fingertip size, and all objects have identical height. The study focuses on 2D cluttered scenes on a horizontal tabletop. Two types of SNCs are distinguished: Dense Arrangement (tightly packed objects with minimal variability) and Random Arrangement (loosely placed objects with more diverse configurations). Cuboid-shaped objects are primarily used during training for consistent geometric structure and to facilitate curriculum design, with generalization to unseen shapes explored later.

The problem is formulated as an RL task where a policy learns to singulate the target object from its surroundings, create space, grasp it, and lift it to a goal position.

**Observation Space (`s_t`):**
The observation space is 168-dimensional, comprising:
- **Proprioceptive robot state (`s_R_t`):** 72 dimensions, including wrist pose, joint positions, velocities, and forces for each finger and wrist dummy joints of a Leap Hand.
- **Previous action (`a_t-1`):** The action executed at the prior time step.
- **Object state (`s_O_t`):** 16 dimensions, providing the target object's position, quaternion, linear and angular velocity, and its position difference relative to the hand.
- **Hand-object distances (`d_HO_t`):** 21 dimensions, representing minimum distances between each hand link and points on the target object.
- **Time encoding (`T_t`):** 29 dimensions, encoding the current time with a sine-cosine embedding.
- **Singulation distance (`d_S_t`):** 8 dimensions, representing distances between the target object and surrounding objects, indicating enclosure level. Padded with zeros if fewer than 8 surrounding objects are present.

**Action Space (`a_t`):**
The action space is 22-dimensional:
- **Palm delta pose (`a_P_t`):** 6 dimensions for translation and rotation of the palm.
- **Linearly smoothed finger joint positions (`a_F_t`):** 16 dimensions for target joint positions of each finger, smoothed over time to ensure fluid movements.

**Unified Reward Design:**
A piece-wise reward function `r_t` is central to integrating singulation and grasping:
- **Approach Phase:** When the palm (`d_P_t`) or hand links (`d_J_t`) are sufficiently far from the target (e.g., `d_P_t >= 0.06` or `d_J_t >= 0.2`), the reward encourages the hand to approach the target (`r_P_t`), promotes joint contact (`r_J_t`), and includes a **singulation reward (`r_S_t`)**.
- **Grasping/Lifting Phase:** Once the palm and fingers are close enough (e.g., `d_P_t < 0.06` and `d_J_t < 0.2`), indicating contact for grasping, the reward function adds terms that encourage fingertip contact (`r_F_t`), lifting the object (`r_L_t`), guiding it to a goal position (`r_G_t`), and a bonus for success (`r_B_t`). The **singulation reward (`r_S_t`)** remains active in this phase.
The `r_S_t` term is defined as `50 * min(||p_target - p_i||^2)`, which directly incentivizes increasing the distance between the target object and its closest surrounding obstacles. This continuous singulation reward across both phases drives the synergistic learning.

**Clutter Arrangement Curriculum Learning:**
To handle the complexity of SNCs, a curriculum learning strategy is employed using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) in Isaac Gym.
1. **Clutter Generation:** A module creates diverse cuboid-based tasks with varying quantities (4 to 8) and shapes (1x1, 1x2, 1x3 cuboids). "Dense Arrangements" (D-n) involve tightly packed 1x1 cuboids, while "Random Arrangements" (R-n) feature looser placements and diverse shapes. D-8 and R-8 are the primary SNC tasks.
2. **Curriculum Progression:**
 - Training starts with a simple Single-Object (SO) grasping scenario.
 - It then progresses through dense arrangements: D-4, followed by D-6, and finally D-8.
 - The expert policy from D-8 training serves as a starting point for random arrangements: R-4, R-6, and R-8.
 - The final policies from D-8 and R-8 training are designated as "teacher policies."

**Teacher-Student Policy Distillation:**
To facilitate real-world deployment where privileged simulation information is unavailable, policy distillation is used to train a vision-based student policy.
1. **Data Collection:** 1000 episodes of observation-action data `(s_V_t, a_t)` are collected from the two trained teacher policies (dense-clutter and random-clutter) across D-4/6/8 and R-4/6/8 tasks. The dataset is balanced to represent varying complexities.
2. **Vision-Based Policy Behavior Cloning:** The student policy is trained using behavior cloning. Its visual observation `s_V_t` includes proprioceptive robot state, previous action, the center position of the scene point cloud (`s_O'_t`), hand-object distances, time encoding, and visual features (`v_t`). The `v_t` are encoded from the scene point cloud using a pre-trained point cloud encoder from UniGraspTransformer, with the encoder weights frozen during distillation. Unlike the teacher policy, the student policy does not use the explicit singulation distance (`d_S_t`) or full object state (`s_O_t`).

**Implementation Details:**
Experiments are conducted in Isaac Gym with 1000 simulated environments. PPO is used for teacher training over 10K iterations. The student policy is trained for 200 epochs via behavior cloning. Hand rotation is disabled to encourage finger dexterity for singulation and simplify sim-to-real transfer. Small inter-object gaps (1-1.5 cm) are maintained to enable horizontal contact forces. Domain randomization on hand joint damping, stiffness, friction, and table friction is applied during teacher retraining to further improve sim-to-real transfer.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of DexSinGrasp involved comprehensive experiments in both simulation and real-world scenarios, using "Success Rate (SR)" and "Average Steps (AS)" as key metrics. The method was compared against a "GraspReward-Only" baseline (pure grasping without singulation reward) and a "Multi-Stage" baseline (sequential singulation and grasping policies).

**Performance in Dense Arrangements (D-4/6/8):**
- **GraspReward-Only:** Exhibited low average SR (39%) and high AS (249 steps), performing particularly poorly in the densest D-8 task (10% SR). This indicates the insufficiency of direct grasping in dense clutter.
- **Multi-Stage:** Showed improved average SR (72%) and lower AS (215 steps) compared to GraspReward-Only, confirming the benefit of an explicit singulation phase.
- **Ours (Teacher):** Achieved an average SR of 98% and a substantially lower AS of 118 steps, significantly outperforming both baselines in both effectiveness and efficiency. This demonstrates the advantages of the unified policy in balancing singulation and grasping.
- **Ours (Student):** Maintained a high average SR of 89% and comparable AS of 135 steps, outperforming baselines despite the transition to a vision-based policy.

**Performance in Random Arrangements (R-4/6/8):**
- Baselines generally performed better in random arrangements than dense ones, suggesting that looser object packing allowed for more opportunities for direct grasps.
- **Ours (Teacher):** Achieved an average SR of 96% and an AS of 97 steps, demonstrating good spatial generalization to diverse random arrangements.
- **Ours (Student):** Achieved an average SR of 88% and an AS of 109 steps, again outperforming the baselines in effectiveness and efficiency.

**Learned Singulation Patterns:**
Qualitative analysis revealed that the policy learned various singulation patterns, including finger flickering and finger-palm vibration. These patterns were observed to effectively displace and nudge surrounding objects, indicating a reliance on the dexterity of the fingers for singulation.

**Clutter Arrangement Curriculum Learning Analysis:**
Three curriculum strategies were evaluated: training from scratch, random-to-dense, and dense-to-random (the proposed method).
- **Training from scratch:** Resulted in poor performance, especially in complex tasks like R-8 (0% SR).
- **Random-to-dense:** Performed better than training from scratch but showed limitations, particularly in dense tasks (D-8 SR 81%).
- **Dense-to-random (Ours):** Consistently yielded the highest average SR across both dense (96%) and random (95%) tasks. This finding indicates that starting with highly constrained dense environments and gradually progressing to more varied random arrangements is effective for enhancing policy accuracy and efficiency.

**Generalization to Irregular Clutters:**
The policy, fine-tuned from a D-8 expert, was tested on randomly generated, tightly packed irregular clutters (unseen geometries).
- **GraspReward-Only:** Achieved 26% SR.
- **Multi-Stage:** Achieved 53% SR.
- **Ours (Teacher):** Achieved 81% SR, demonstrating robust geometric generalization beyond cuboid shapes.
- **Ours (Student):** Achieved 60% SR, again outperforming baselines in this challenging scenario.

**Discussion of Dexterity:**
A comparative study using LEAP Hand variants with varying DoF (Low-DoF: 6 DoF, Mid-DoF: 13 DoF, Full-DoF: 16 DoF) was conducted.
- Performance (SR and AS) consistently improved with increased hand dexterity across all dense-clutter tasks and irregular clutters.
- The Full-DoF LEAP Hand (used in DexSinGrasp) achieved the highest success rates (e.g., 97% on D-8, 81% on irregular) and lowest average steps.
- Low-DoF and Mid-DoF variants experienced substantial drops in SR and increases in AS, especially in more complex environments. This empirically validates the hypothesis that high finger dexterity is crucial for complex contact interactions and efficient singulation in tight, singulation-necessary cluttered environments.

**Real-World Experiments:**
Real-world experiments were conducted using a uFactory xArm6 with a LEAP Hand and RGB-D cameras. The vision-based student policy, which was retrained with domain randomization, was evaluated on D-8, R-8, irregular, and "practical" clutters (unseen objects arranged randomly).
- **GraspReward-Only:** Performed poorly (e.g., 3/20 D-8, 0/20 irregular, 17/100 practical).
- **Multi-Stage:** Improved but showed sensitivity to sim-to-real discrepancies, leading to failures when singulation created novel configurations (e.g., 7/20 D-8, 3/20 irregular, 22/100 practical).
- **Ours (Student):** Achieved significantly higher success rates (e.g., 15/20 D-8, 12/20 irregular, 61/100 practical).
- The 61% success rate in zero-shot practical clutter, despite training solely on cuboids, demonstrates strong generalization to unseen shapes and spatial configurations in the real world. This suggests that the learned spatial reasoning for singulation is effective even when object geometries vary.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DexSinGrasp framework introduces a significant contribution to robotic manipulation in cluttered environments through its unified policy for dexterous object singulation and grasping. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Advancement in Dexterous Manipulation:** The work successfully integrates object singulation and grasping into a single reinforcement learning objective for dexterous hands. This unified approach, unlike traditional multi-stage methods, enables a more synergistic and efficient execution of complex tasks in Singulation-Necessary Clutters (SNCs). It demonstrates that finger dexterity can be actively leveraged for precise, subtle maneuvers that effectively clear space for grasping, which is an improvement over palm-driven or two-fingered gripper-based singulation.

- **Improved Performance and Efficiency in Clutter:** The experimental results demonstrate that DexSinGrasp achieves consistently higher grasp success rates and greater efficiency (fewer average steps) compared to baseline methods, particularly in densely cluttered environments. This represents a tangible improvement in the capability of robots to handle real-world scenarios that are often characterized by tight packing of objects.

- **Robust Generalization Capabilities:** The implementation of clutter arrangement curriculum learning is shown to be highly effective, not only boosting training success rates but also significantly enhancing the policy's generalization across diverse spatial arrangements (dense and random). Furthermore, the successful transfer of skills from policies trained on cuboid objects to irregular shapes and unseen "practical clutters" in zero-shot real-world settings highlights the robustness of the learned spatial relationships and dexterity, rather than merely specific object geometries.

- **Effective Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The methodology of teacher-student policy distillation, coupled with domain randomization during teacher retraining, provides a viable strategy for deploying complex dexterous manipulation skills learned in simulation to physical robotic systems. This addresses a critical challenge in robotics by enabling vision-based control without relying on privileged simulation information.

- **Empirical Validation of Finger Dexterity's Importance:** The comparative analysis across different degrees of freedom for the dexterous hand provides strong empirical evidence for the crucial role of high-DoF finger dexterity in effectively performing singulation within confined spaces. This finding informs future research and hardware design in dexterous manipulation, emphasizing the value of multi-articulated fingers.

- **Broader Applicability:** The ability to efficiently singulate and grasp objects in dense clutter has broad implications for various robotic applications. These include automated warehouses, industrial assembly lines, domestic service robots, and hazardous material handling, where precise and adaptive manipulation in unarranged environments is essential.

- **Contribution to RL Methodology:** The definition of Singulation-Necessary Clutter (SNC) and the structured curriculum design offer valuable contributions to the methodological toolkit for developing and evaluating complex robotic policies, particularly in tasks involving environment interaction and rearrangement.

Future work outlined by the authors suggests extensions to even more complex clutter configurations, a wider range of object shapes, and continued improvements in real-world robustness. Further exploration into how spatial diversity in curriculum design enhances generalization remains an area for continued investigation.
