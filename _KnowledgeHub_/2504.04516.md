# DexSinGrasp: Learning a Unified Policy for Dexterous Object Singulation and Grasping in Densely Cluttered Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> DexSinGrasp, a framework from the National University of Singapore, presents a unified policy for dexterous robotic hands to perform simultaneous object singulation and grasping in densely cluttered environments. This approach achieves up to 98% success in simulation and 61% in real-world practical clutter, leveraging finger dexterity to efficiently clear space and grasp objects.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation in densely cluttered environments struggles to efficiently grasp target objects, particularly with dexterous hands whose high degrees of freedom are underutilized for object singulation.
- Existing methods for cluttered grasping often require complete object isolation or rely on multi-stage approaches, which limit synergistic potential between singulation and grasping, leading to suboptimal performance.
- Transferring complex dexterous manipulation policies from simulation to real-world robots remains challenging due to reliance on privileged information and the difficulty of learning in high-dimensional action spaces from scratch.

## Method

- Developed **DexSinGrasp**, a unified ==reinforcement learning== policy that integrates dexterous object singulation and grasping into a single objective using a piece-wise reward function that continuously incentivizes increasing space around the target.
- Implemented a clutter arrangement curriculum learning strategy, starting with simple single-object grasping and progressing through increasingly complex dense and random clutter configurations, to enhance learning efficiency and generalization.
- Employed a teacher-student policy distillation method, training a vision-based student policy from state-based teacher policies to enable robust sim-to-real transfer and real-world deployment.

## Results

- In simulation, the DexSinGrasp teacher policy achieved a **98%** average success rate and 118 average steps in dense clutter, significantly outperforming baselines which reached **39%** (GraspReward-Only) and **72%** (Multi-Stage) success rates.
- The vision-based student policy achieved an **89%** average success rate in simulation and demonstrated strong generalization, achieving **81%** success on unseen irregular clutter (teacher) and **61%** success in zero-shot real-world practical clutter.
- Comparative analysis across different hand DoF confirmed that increasing finger dexterity consistently improved success rates and reduced average steps across all complex cluttered tasks, highlighting its critical role in effective singulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified policy, leveraging finger dexterity through a continuous singulation reward, significantly outperforms multi-stage or grasp-only approaches for cluttered object retrieval by enabling synergistic singulation and grasping.
> - Clutter arrangement curriculum learning, progressing from dense to random arrangements, is crucial for effectively training complex dexterous manipulation policies in high-dimensional, cluttered spaces, leading to high success rates and strong generalization.
> - High degrees of freedom in robotic fingers are empirically validated as essential for efficient and precise object singulation within tight, confined spaces, directly impacting task success and efficiency.
