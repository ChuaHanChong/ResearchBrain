# RRTrack: Robust and Recoverable Object 6D Pose Tracking for Dynamic Scenes

> [!summary] Summary
> RRTrack presents a unified framework for robust and recoverable 6D object pose tracking in dynamic and occluded scenes. The system improves pose accuracy and autonomously recovers from object disappearance, achieving an average 55.2 FPS on a new synthetic benchmark and high success rates on challenging real-world tasks without object-specific training.

## Problem

- Existing 6D pose tracking methods are fragile to fast object motion and abrupt viewpoint changes, leading to tracking failure when objects exit refinement convergence basins.
- Hybrid 2D-6D trackers suffer from gradual drift during prolonged occlusion or appearance changes due to unreliable 2D observations contaminating visual memory without geometric verification.
- Most trackers lack autonomous recovery mechanisms for complete target disappearance and subsequent reappearance, requiring manual re-initialization and disrupting robotic tasks.

## Method

- A ==closed-loop 2D–6D tracking== strategy integrates ==memory-based video object segmentation (VOS)== with ==CAD-model-based 6D pose refinement==, employing ==rendered-mask agreement== for geometric verification.
- A ==DINOv2==-based ==dual-bank recovery module==, utilizing both offline synthetic templates and dynamically updated online observation anchors, enables efficient and autonomous re-initialization after object loss.
- An ==adaptive threshold manager==, based on an ==exponential moving average–median absolute deviation (EMA–MAD)==, dynamically adjusts decision thresholds for state transitions and memory updates based on tracking quality.

## Results

- Achieved superior overall pose accuracy on a new synthetic RGB-D benchmark, with ADD AUC/AR of **31.7**/**55.1** and ADD-S AUC/AR of **49.2**/**73.5**, outperforming state-of-the-art methods like FoundationPose and RGBTrack.
- Demonstrated robust recovery from **72** lost–reappear events with a **72.2%** success rate and a median recovery delay of **2.0** frames, while maintaining an average inference speed of **55.2** FPS.
- Qualitatively validated robust 6D tracking and autonomous recovery in challenging real-world agile drone experiments, including noisy sensing and occlusions, operating at **49.3** FPS.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Tightly coupling 2D visual observations with 3D geometric consistency checks, through rendered-mask agreement, is crucial for preventing drift and maintaining robustness in dynamic environments.
> - A dual-bank feature retrieval system, combining diverse offline synthetic views with real-time online observations via foundation models, allows for efficient and training-free recovery from full object loss.
> - Adaptive thresholding, continuously updated from stable tracking data, provides a robust and responsive mechanism for managing tracker states and memory, improving recovery responsiveness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Accurate and real-time 6D object pose tracking is a critical capability for robotic systems, underpinning applications such as manipulation, visual servoing, and mobile navigation. Unlike single-frame pose estimation, tracking methods aim to provide continuous and stable pose estimates over time. Recent advancements in CAD-model-based pose estimation, exemplified by methods like FoundationPose and GigaPose, have improved generalization to unseen objects by leveraging render-and-compare refinement. These developments have led to the evolution of tracking approaches from pose-only systems to hybrid 2D–6D frameworks, which incorporate image-space tracking cues to enhance robustness under dynamic conditions, as seen in RGBTrack and DynamicPose.

Despite these advancements, existing 6D pose tracking methods face several limitations when operating in practical industrial environments characterized by rapid object motion, abrupt viewpoint changes, and temporary target disappearance (occlusion). Current pose-based trackers, relying on local optimization, can fail if an object moves outside their convergence basin. Moreover, 2D tracking components, while capable of maintaining object support under fast motion, often lack geometric verification, which can lead to gradual drift during occlusion or appearance changes if unreliable observations are integrated into memory updates or pose refinement. A significant gap also exists in the ability of most systems to recover from complete target disappearance and subsequent reappearance, requiring manual re-initialization.

RRTrack addresses these limitations by introducing a unified framework designed for robust long-term tracking and efficient recovery without manual intervention. It specifically targets the challenges of rapid inter-frame motion invalidating local pose refinement, prolonged occlusion contaminating visual memory, and the need for efficient pose re-initialization during disappearance–reappearance events. The work integrates a 2D–6D closed-loop strategy with a novel recovery mechanism, positioning it as an advancement in training-free, recoverable 6D pose tracking for dynamic and occluded scenes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust, recoverable, and efficient 6D object pose tracking framework capable of operating effectively in challenging dynamic and occluded scenes, particularly within industrial robotic contexts. The motivations for this work stem from several identified limitations in existing 6D pose tracking methodologies:

1. **Addressing Fragility to Fast Motion and Viewpoint Changes:** Conventional pose-based tracking methods often rely on local optimization routines that update an object's pose from the previous frame. While efficient for small inter-frame motions, these methods become unreliable when an object undergoes rapid displacement or significant viewpoint changes, causing it to exit the refinement algorithm's convergence basin. This leads to tracking failure and necessitates frequent re-initialization. The objective is to maintain tracking continuity even under such challenging dynamic conditions.

2. **Mitigating Drift During Occlusion and Appearance Changes:** Hybrid 2D–6D tracking approaches incorporate 2D image-space cues (e.g., object masks or bounding boxes) to maintain object localization when 6D pose estimation falters. However, without geometric verification, these 2D cues can gradually drift or become corrupted during prolonged partial or full occlusion, or when the object's appearance changes. If these unreliable 2D observations are continuously integrated into the system's memory or pose refinement, error accumulation can lead to irreversible tracking failure. A key objective is to introduce mechanisms that verify the geometric consistency of 2D observations before they influence the tracker's state.

3. **Enabling Autonomous Recovery from Disappearance–Reappearance Events:** A significant limitation of many existing trackers is their inability to autonomously recover when an object completely disappears from the camera's field of view and subsequently reappears with a potentially large displacement. Such events currently often require manual intervention to re-initialize the tracking process, disrupting robotic tasks. The motivation is to develop an efficient and automatic recovery mechanism that can re-establish the object's 6D pose after complete loss and reappearance without compromising real-time performance.

4. **Developing a Training-Free and Zero-Shot Solution:** Many advanced pose estimation techniques require object-specific training data or fine-tuning, limiting their "out-of-the-box" applicability to novel objects. The objective is to leverage foundation models and render-and-compare strategies to create a training-free framework that can track unseen objects based solely on their CAD models at test time.

To address these motivations, the authors identify three specific challenges:
- **Rapid inter-frame motion:** Invalidates local pose-conditioned refinement.
- **Prolonged occlusion:** Contaminates visual memory and induces long-term drift.
- **Disappearance–reappearance events:** Require efficient pose re-initialization without sacrificing real-time performance.

RRTrack aims to overcome these by tightly coupling visual memory, geometric verification, and retrieval-based recovery into a unified framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RRTrack is structured around two primary components: a closed-loop 2D–6D tracking strategy and a DINOv2-based dual-bank recovery module. An adaptive threshold manager, based on an exponential moving average–median absolute deviation (EMA–MAD), underpins decision-making across various modules.

#### 3.1. Closed-Loop 2D–6D Tracking

This component integrates memory-based video object segmentation (VOS) with CAD-model-based 6D pose refinement, designed to maintain object support in 2D and verify it geometrically in 3D.

- **2D Observation Stream (VOS):** A memory-based VOS module, specifically CUTIE, is employed to propagate an object-centric segmentation mask (M_t) over time. This module also provides a confidence signal (H_t) based on prediction entropy, indicating the visual reliability of the propagated mask. The 2D stream is crucial for maintaining target localization under rapid motion and short occlusions.

- **6D Pose Refinement:** A frozen FoundationPose refiner continuously updates the object's 6D pose (T_t) using current RGB-D observations and the propagated mask. FoundationPose is chosen for its strong pose estimation capabilities and render-and-compare refinement approach.

- **Rendered-Mask Agreement for Tracking Assessment:** To prevent drift, RRTrack introduces a mechanism to evaluate the consistency between the propagated 2D mask (M_t) and a mask rendered from the current 6D pose estimate (R_t). Two mask agreement scores are computed:
 - **P_t (Precision):** |M_t ∩ R_t| / |M_t|, measuring how well the rendered pose explains the propagated 2D support.
 - **S_t (Support):** |M_t ∩ R_t| / |R_t|, measuring how much of the rendered object is covered by the propagated mask.
 - These scores, along with the VOS confidence H_t, characterize the geometric consistency and visual reliability of the current frame, guiding state transitions and memory updates.

- **Snap Correction for Local Drift:** When the precision (P_t) falls below a tracking threshold (τ_track^P) but the propagated mask area (|M_t|) remains sufficiently large (≥ a_snap), a "snap correction" is triggered. This involves re-estimating the object's translation by back-projecting the 2D mask center (c(M_t)) and its median depth (z_t) into 3D camera coordinates. The new translation is combined with the previous rotation to form a refined pose proposal, which is then passed to the 6D refiner. This helps correct moderate drift before full tracking failure.

- **Quality-Gated Memory Injection:** The mask agreement scores (P_t, S_t) and VOS confidence (H_t) are used to regulate updates to both long-term and short-term memory, and the online template bank. This mechanism ensures that only geometrically consistent and visually reliable observations are stored, preventing the accumulation of errors:
 - **Long-term memory (I_long_mem):** Updates are stricter, requiring high P_t and S_t, typically for storing rendered pose masks.
 - **Short-term VOS memory (I_short_mem):** Updates are guided by P_t and H_t (low entropy), allowing the propagated mask M_t to update VOS memory.
 - **Online template bank (I_bank):** Updates require both high P_t and S_t, ensuring newly added anchors are visually plausible and pose-consistent.

#### 3.2. State-Aware Recovery

This module is activated when the tracker enters an unreliable state, providing a mechanism for re-initializing the pose after target loss.

- **State Logic:** An adaptive threshold controller manages transitions between normal tracking, correction, lost, and recovery states. Local refinement continues as long as P_t is above a floor threshold (τ_floor^P) and |M_t| is above a minimum area (a_lost).
 - **Lost State (I_lost):** Triggered if P_t < τ_floor^P or |M_t| < a_lost.
 - **Stagnation State (I_stag):** Triggered if the pose exhibits little change (∆θ_t, ∆d_t) over a window W_stag despite persistently low P_t (P_t < τ_stag^P). This identifies situations where the local optimizer is trapped.
 When either I_lost or I_stag is true, local refinement is disabled, and retrieval-based recovery is initiated.

- **Dual-Bank Feature Retrieval:** To enable robust re-initialization, two distinct template banks are maintained:
 - **Offline Bank (B_off):** Contains pre-rendered synthetic templates (features f_i^off, poses T_i^off) covering a broad range of viewpoints.
 - **Online Bank (B_on):** Dynamically populated with real observation anchors (features f_i^on, poses T_i^on) recorded during high-quality tracking frames, helping to bridge the domain gap.
 When recovery is triggered, the current masked observation (I_t ⊙ M_t) is processed by DINOv2 to extract an L2-normalized global CLS token (f_t). The top-K candidates (C_m^t) are independently retrieved from both banks based on cosine similarity with f_t. Distinct EMA-MAD validation thresholds are applied to prevent one bank from dominating and ensure fallback hypotheses.

- **Candidate Solve and Acceptance:** The retrieved coarse pose hypotheses from both banks are treated as initializations for FoundationPose's RoIGrid-based refinement. Each candidate is refined once and scored by the FoundationPose ranker. The refined hypothesis with the highest score (T_t^⋆) is then verified against the current 2D observation using its rendered-mask precision (P_t^⋆). Recovery is accepted (I_acc) if P_t^⋆ > τ_track^P. If no candidate meets the acceptance criteria, the tracker remains in the lost state, with a fallback to spherical pose search if no acceptable candidates are found.

#### 3.3. Adaptive Threshold Manager

An EMA–MAD adaptive threshold manager continuously updates thresholds (e.g., τ_track^P, τ_floor^P, τ_stag^P) for decision-making. It calculates EMA and MAD statistics only from stable tracking frames, providing robust running statistics that dynamically adjust thresholds to varying scene conditions and object appearances.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of RRTrack was evaluated through both synthetic and real-world experiments, focusing on pose accuracy, recovery behavior, and processing speed under challenging conditions.

#### 4.1. Experiment Settings

- **Implementation Details:** The system operates on 640x320 RGB-D streams, downsamples CUTIE to 1/4 resolution, and uses DINOv2 ViT-S/14 CLS-token descriptors. For recovery, Top-K=3 candidates are retrieved from both offline (256 rendered views) and online (up to 64 observation anchors) banks. The EMA-MAD manager uses α=0.05 and a 100-frame MAD buffer. All experiments run on a single RTX 4090 GPU.
- **Metrics:**
 - **Pose Accuracy:** ADD and ADD-S (for symmetric objects) with a normalized distance threshold of 0.1d. Metrics include Area Under accuracy-threshold Curve (AUC) and Average Recall (AR) at 0.1d.
 - **Recovery-focused Metrics:** Success rate for lost–reappear events (at least one frame with ADD-S AR > 0.9 within 10 frames of reappearance), and post-recovery ADD/ADD-S AUC/AR over the 10-frame window after successful recovery. Median successful recovery delay in frames.
 - **Efficiency:** Frames Per Second (FPS).

#### 4.2. Evaluation on Synthetic Benchmarks

A new synthetic RGB-D benchmark was introduced, comprising 3 robotic scenarios designed to include fast motion, full occlusion, and disappearance–reappearance events:
- **Franka-YCB:** Pick-and-place manipulation with a Franka arm and YCB objects, simulating partial and full occlusions from manipulator motion and orbiting camera viewpoint changes.
- **Agile Quadcopter:** High-speed drone flight through ring-gate courses, involving large inter-frame displacements, rapid scale/viewpoint changes, and out-of-view re-entry.
- **Dingo Factory:** Automated guided vehicle (AGV) navigation in factory scenes with obstacles causing repeated full occlusions and extended target absence.
The benchmark totals 38 video–object tracking episodes and 72 lost–reappear events across 22 RGB-D videos.

**Comparison with State-of-the-Art:** RRTrack was compared against FoundationPose (tracking mode), GigaPose (adapted for tracking efficiency), and RGBTrack.

- **Overall Performance:** RRTrack achieved the highest equal-subset mean accuracy across all four pose metrics: ADD AUC/AR of 31.7/55.1 and ADD-S AUC/AR of 49.2/73.5. It also maintained an inference speed of 55.2 FPS.
- **Scenario-Specific Gains:**
 - **Franka-YCB (manipulation-induced occlusion):** RRTrack demonstrated competitive performance, showing benefits beyond complete disappearance cases by improving robustness against partial occlusion and viewpoint changes that destabilize local pose updates.
 - **Agile Quadcopter (fast motion, out-of-view re-entry):** RRTrack showed significant gains in this high-dynamics setting. For `quad1`, RRTrack achieved 81.7 ADD-S AR, compared to 9.2 for FoundationPose, 9.1 for GigaPose, and 79.8 for RGBTrack. This highlights the value of coupling 2D target support with 6D pose refinement under agile motion.
 - **Dingo Factory (disappearance and delayed recovery):** RRTrack consistently outperformed other methods. For `black` object, RRTrack achieved 71.4 ADD-S AR, while FoundationPose was 40.8, GigaPose 0.2, and RGBTrack 7.9. This indicates the strong benefit of RRTrack's recovery-oriented design during object absence and reappearance.
- **Inference Speed:** The full RRTrack pipeline averaged 18.1 ms per frame (55.2 FPS). Normal tracking operations (VOS memory propagation and local 6D refinement) were efficient (7.46 ms and 6.50 ms respectively). Recovery frames, while taking longer (32.02 ms), remained within a 30 FPS budget, demonstrating that recovery capability was added without compromising real-time throughput.

#### 4.3. Real-World Drone Experiment

Qualitative evaluation on real-world agile-drone sequences (captured with a handheld Intel RealSense D435 camera) validated RRTrack's robustness under noisy sensing conditions (continuous shake, jitter, motion blur) and designed target-loss events (leaving camera view, flying behind obstacles). RRTrack maintained visually consistent 6D bounding-box overlays, whereas FoundationPose quickly drifted and lost track. The recovery module successfully re-established 6D poses after disappearance and reappearance, retrieving appropriate templates from memory. This demonstrated RRTrack's effectiveness in challenging physical environments without scene-specific tuning, running at 49.3 FPS.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

- **Effects of 2D Backbones:** Replacing CUTIE with SAM2 or OSTracker showed that CUTIE provided the best balance of accuracy and efficiency. OSTracker was faster but less accurate, while SAM2 preserved higher accuracy but was significantly slower, confirming the design choice of a memory-based VOS backbone.
- **Effects of Key Components:** Ablating recovery-related components on 72 lost–reappear events demonstrated their individual contributions:
 - **Full Model:** Achieved 0.722 success rate, 0.398 ADD, 0.671 ADD-S, and a median recovery delay of 2.0 frames.
 - **Without Dual-Bank Matcher:** Success rate dropped to 0.681, and ADD/ADD-S to 0.326/0.629, indicating the importance of the recovery branch for both frequency and geometric quality.
 - **Without Pose-Gated Memory Control:** Success rate dropped to 0.681, and ADD/ADD-S to 0.341/0.622, highlighting the role of geometric verification in preventing drift.
 - **Without Adaptive Threshold:** Success rate was 0.639, and ADD/ADD-S 0.379/0.573. Critically, the median recovery delay sharply increased to 9.0 frames, demonstrating that adaptive thresholding significantly improves recovery responsiveness and temporal consistency.
These ablations confirmed that the dual-bank recovery, geometry-gated memory control, and adaptive thresholding are all critical for stable and efficient recovery from target disappearance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of RRTrack represents a significant advancement in the field of 6D object pose tracking, particularly for applications in dynamic and occluded environments encountered in industrial robotics. The work's primary significance lies in its ability to address longstanding challenges that limit the practical deployment of autonomous robotic systems.

**Key areas of significance include:**

- **Enhanced Robustness in Dynamic Scenes:** By introducing a closed-loop 2D–6D tracking strategy, RRTrack integrates the stability of memory-based video object segmentation with the geometric precision of 6D pose refinement. The rendered-mask agreement mechanism is crucial for validating 2D observations with 3D geometry, preventing the accumulation of drift that often plagues hybrid trackers under fast motion, viewpoint changes, and partial occlusions. This robustness allows robotic systems to operate more reliably in unpredictable environments.
- **Autonomous Recovery from Disappearance:** The DINOv2-based dual-bank recovery module provides a novel solution to the problem of target disappearance and reappearance. By jointly leveraging offline synthetic templates and dynamically updated online observation anchors, RRTrack can efficiently re-initialize tracking after an object has been fully occluded or moved out of view. This eliminates the need for manual re-initialization, thereby increasing system autonomy and reducing operational downtime in industrial settings.
- **Real-time Efficiency:** Despite incorporating sophisticated validation and recovery mechanisms, RRTrack maintains real-time performance (55.2 FPS on the synthetic benchmark). This efficiency is critical for applications like robotic manipulation and visual servoing, where immediate pose feedback is required for control. The modular design, with recovery only triggered when needed, ensures that normal tracking operations remain fast.
- **Training-Free and Zero-Shot Capability:** Leveraging foundation models like DINOv2 and a render-and-compare refiner like FoundationPose, RRTrack operates without requiring object-specific training data. This "zero-shot" capability simplifies deployment for novel objects, making it highly adaptable for diverse industrial scenarios where new objects are frequently introduced.
- **Comprehensive Benchmark for Challenging Conditions:** The introduction of a new synthetic RGB-D benchmark explicitly designed to test high dynamics, full occlusion, and disappearance–reappearance events is a valuable contribution to the research community. This benchmark provides a standardized platform for evaluating tracking methods under conditions that are more representative of real-world robotic challenges than many existing datasets.

**Potential Impact:**

The capabilities demonstrated by RRTrack have the potential to significantly impact several domains:
- **Industrial Robotics:** Improving the reliability of object manipulation, assembly, and quality inspection tasks in factories where objects may move rapidly or be temporarily obscured.
- **Autonomous Navigation:** Enhancing the ability of mobile robots and drones to track dynamic targets or landmarks in complex, cluttered, and changing environments.
- **Augmented Reality and Human-Robot Interaction:** Providing more stable and continuous object tracking for AR applications and for robots interacting with objects in shared human workspaces.
- **General Computer Vision Research:** The closed-loop design, state-aware recovery logic, and adaptive threshold management offer new methodological directions for building robust and resilient perception systems.

Future work exploring symmetry-aware and probability-distribution-based pose estimation could further improve accuracy for challenging symmetric objects, extending the impact of RRTrack to even broader applications.
