# SafeFlow: Real-Time Text-Driven Humanoid Whole-Body Control via Physics-Guided Rectified Flow and Selective Safety Gating

> [!summary] Summary
> SafeFlow is a framework that enables real-time text-driven humanoid whole-body control by integrating physics-guided rectified flow motion generation with a multi-stage safety gating mechanism. The system reduced joint limit violations from 43.14% to 3.08% and improved tracking success rates from 80.6% to 98.5% on a Unitree G1 robot, effectively handling unsafe inputs in real-time.

## Problem

- Kinematics-only motion generators for humanoid robots frequently produce physically infeasible or unsafe trajectories (e.g., joint limit violations, self-collisions) that are challenging for real hardware to execute.
- The issue of physical infeasibility and potential safety risks is exacerbated when robots receive out-of-distribution (OOD) or ambiguous text commands, leading to distorted or unexecutable motions.
- Many current text-driven motion generation approaches lack explicit, real-time integration of physics-aware objectives and robust safety mechanisms during the generation process for real-world robotic deployment.

## Method

- A two-level framework combining a physics-guided rectified flow motion generator with a comprehensive, training-free 3-stage selective execution safety gate.
- The motion generator incorporates differentiable physics costs (joint limits, self-collision, smoothness, CoM stability) to steer latent space sampling, with this guidance then distilled via reflow for efficient, real-time (NFE=1) inference.
- A hierarchical safety gate proactively filters unsafe conditions: Stage 1 detects semantic OOD text inputs, Stage 2 identifies generative model instability, and Stage 3 performs deterministic hard kinematic screening on the output trajectory.

## Results

- Physical feasibility was substantially improved: Joint Limit Violation Rate reduced from **43.14%** (baseline TextOp) to **3.08%** and Self-Collision Rate from **11.05%** to **1.42%** using SafeFlow with guidance and reflow.
- System-level tracking fidelity on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot increased the success rate from **80.6%** to **98.5%**, with the mean position error (MPJPE) for successful motions reduced from **78.04** mm to **41.20** mm.
- Real-time performance was achieved with the reflow-distilled generator operating at **10.80** ms latency (**92.6** Hz), and the full guarded pipeline maintaining a **14.78** ms latency (**67.7** Hz), demonstrating successful rejection of a high-risk "double backflip" command on real hardware.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physics-guided sampling within a rectified flow model effectively directs motion generation towards physically executable regions, significantly reducing kinematic violations without requiring extensive model retraining.
> - Reflow distillation successfully internalizes complex physics constraints into the generator's weights, enabling real-time, low-latency generation (10.80 ms) of safe motions without online gradient computations.
> - A multi-stage, training-free safety gate provides robust deployment-time safety by detecting and rejecting unsafe inputs or unstable generations at semantic, model, and kinematic output levels, ensuring reliable real-robot operation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics has seen advancements in controlling humanoid robots, traditionally relying on task-specific commands, predefined reference trajectories, or teleoperation. More recently, the integration of large-scale motion datasets and generative models has enabled the translation of natural language instructions into robot motions. A significant development in this area is real-time interactive control, where robots respond to streaming text commands, exemplified by systems such as TextOp. This paradigm shift positions natural language as a dynamic control signal, moving beyond one-shot task specifications.

Despite progress in text-driven motion generation, a recurring challenge in deploying these systems on real hardware is the generation of physically infeasible or unsafe motions. Kinematics-only generators often produce trajectories that violate joint limits, induce self-collisions, or lead to unstable balance. While low-level motion tracking controllers can mitigate some of these issues, severe physical violations can degrade motion fidelity and compromise safety. These problems are particularly exacerbated when the robot receives out-of-distribution (OOD) or ambiguous user inputs, which can result in distorted or unexecutable motions.

Existing approaches to address physical feasibility include simulator-in-the-loop diffusion methods (e.g., PhysDiff) that incorporate physics during sampling, but these often incur substantial latency. Physics-based reinforcement learning (RL) methods (e.g., PhysHOI) are tailored for virtual characters rather than hardware-constrained robots. In robotics, methods like RobotMDM and Humanoid-R0 aim to bridge the kinematic-execution gap, but they are typically limited to offline generation, requiring pre-computation of full motion sequences, which makes them unsuitable for real-time streaming control. Furthermore, many interactive control frameworks, such as TextOp and LangWBC, treat the motion generator as a black box, relying on downstream controllers to handle potentially problematic references, without explicit runtime mechanisms to reject unsafe inputs or generations.

This work, SafeFlow, aims to advance the state of real-time text-driven humanoid control by explicitly addressing these gaps. It integrates physics-aware guidance into the motion generation process and introduces a deployment-time safety mechanism to detect and filter out unsafe behaviors proactively. By focusing on real-time execution and robust safety under unconstrained language inputs, SafeFlow attempts to move beyond purely semantic alignment to achieve physically executable and safe control on humanoid robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a real-time text-driven humanoid whole-body control framework that enhances physical executability and ensures deployment-time safety, particularly when faced with open-ended or out-of-distribution (OOD) text inputs. The motivation stems from several limitations observed in current text-driven motion generation systems for robots:

1. **Physical Infeasibility of Kinematics-Only Generations**: Current high-level motion generators, which often focus solely on kinematic plausibility and semantic alignment, frequently produce trajectories that are physically impossible or unsafe for a real robot to execute. These "physical hallucinations" include violations of joint limits, self-collisions between robot links, and unstable full-body configurations. Such issues place a heavy burden on downstream motion tracking controllers, which may struggle to track unfeasible references, leading to reduced motion fidelity or outright system failures.

2. **Increased Risks from Out-of-Distribution Inputs**: The problem of physical infeasibility becomes more pronounced under open-ended or OOD user commands. When confronted with ambiguous, novel, or extreme prompts, generative models can produce severely distorted or structurally unstable motions that are entirely unsuitable for direct execution. Existing frameworks often lack explicit mechanisms to detect and reject such unsafe references at runtime, leaving the robot vulnerable to executing hazardous behaviors.

3. **Lack of Physics-Aware Objectives for Real-Robot Execution**: Many motion generation approaches do not explicitly incorporate physics-aware objectives relevant to real-robot hardware constraints during the generation process. While some work has explored physics-guided sampling in character animation or offline settings, its application to improve real-robot executability in real-time text-driven control remains an underexplored area. This absence leads to a significant gap between generated kinematic trajectories and what is truly executable on physical hardware.

4. **Need for Real-Time Performance**: For interactive control, low-latency motion generation is crucial. Integrating sophisticated physics checks or complex guidance mechanisms directly into the online sampling process can introduce significant computational overhead, hindering real-time deployment. Therefore, there is a need for efficient methods that can internalize physics-aware guidance without compromising inference speed.

To address these motivations, SafeFlow sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:
- To develop a physics-guided motion generator that actively steers motion sampling towards physically executable regions, considering constraints such as joint limits, self-collision avoidance, and stability, without requiring retraining for each new constraint.
- To enable real-time deployment of this physics-guided generation by using distillation techniques to reduce the computational cost of incorporating physics, allowing for fast, single-step inference.
- To implement a hierarchical, training-free safety gating mechanism that proactively detects and rejects unsafe conditions at various stages: semantic input level, generative model level (instability), and kinematic output level (hard constraints).
- To demonstrate improved success rates, physical compliance, and inference speed on a real humanoid robot compared to prior diffusion-based methods, while maintaining expressive and diverse behaviors.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SafeFlow operates as a two-level framework designed for real-time, interactive text-driven humanoid control, integrating physics-aware motion generation with a multi-stage selective execution mechanism.

**A. Overview of SafeFlow**
The system receives streaming text commands and previous robot proprioceptive states. It first processes the text input through a Stage-1 Safety Gate. If accepted, a high-level motion generator produces a future reference motion sequence, conditioned on past motions and the current text. This generated reference then passes through Stage-2 and Stage-3 Safety Gates. If all gates are cleared, the reference is sent to a low-level motion tracking controller for execution. If any gate triggers a rejection, the system executes a safe fallback motion (e.g., standing) and awaits the next command.

**B. Physics-Guided Rectified Flow Motion Generation**
This component is responsible for generating physically executable reference motions with low latency.

1. **Latent-Space Motion Representation**: Motion is represented using DoF-based local incremental per-frame features. A Variational Autoencoder (VAE) is trained to learn a compact latent space, encoding motion from past and future frames into a latent vector `z` and reconstructing future frames from `z` and past frames.

2. **Text-Conditioned Rectified Flow Matching**: The distribution of future motion latents is modeled using rectified flow matching. A CLIP text encoder embeds the streaming text command. A velocity field `v_θ` is learned to define an Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) that transports a noise distribution to the data distribution in the latent space. During training, the model minimizes an objective `L_RFM` by learning to predict a constant target velocity between a noise latent and a ground truth motion latent (from the VAE encoder). At inference, a noise latent is integrated through the ODE to obtain the generated motion latent, which is then decoded into a kinematic reference.

3. **Physics-Guided Sampling**: To ensure physical feasibility without retraining the base model, physics-guided sampling is applied during inference.
 - **Differentiable Physics Cost (C)**: A differentiable cost function `C` quantifies executability violations. It is a weighted sum of four terms:
 - **Joint Limit (`C_lim`)**: Penalizes deviations from hardware-defined joint position limits using ReLU-squared barriers.
 - **Self-Collision (`C_col`)**: Penalizes penetrations between robot links using ReLU-squared barriers, considering collision sphere radii and a safety margin.
 - **Smoothness (`C_sm`)**: Regularizes high-order derivatives (velocity and acceleration) of joint configurations to generate jitter-free motions.
 - **CoM Stability (`C_stab`)**: Regularizes high-order derivatives (velocity and acceleration) of the Center of Mass (CoM) to promote stable postures and movements.
 - **Guided Velocity Field**: The gradient of `C` with respect to the motion latent `z` is computed (via backpropagation through the VAE decoder) and used to steer the velocity field `v_θ`, modifying it into `˜v_θ`. This effectively pushes the generated trajectory towards physically feasible regions during ODE integration.

4. **Physics-Aware Reflow**: To achieve real-time performance, the computationally expensive physics-guided integration is distilled. Synthetic pairs of initial noise latents and the resulting guided latents are generated. The model is then retrained using reflow to learn the straightened paths connecting these pairs. This process internalizes the physics constraints directly into the network weights, enabling generation of safe motions with a minimal number of function evaluations (NFE=1) during deployment, bypassing online gradient computations.

**C. Selective Execution via 3-Stage Safety Gate**
This training-free mechanism hierarchically filters unsafe inputs and generations to ensure robust deployment.

1. **Stage 1: Semantic OOD Filtering (Input Level)**:
 - **Mechanism**: Detects out-of-distribution (OOD) text prompts by calculating the Mahalanobis distance of the incoming CLIP text embedding from the mean of in-distribution training prompt embeddings, normalized by their covariance.
 - **Action**: If the distance exceeds a calibrated threshold, the prompt is rejected, triggering a safe fallback and preventing undefined motions from being generated.

2. **Stage 2: Generation Instability Filtering (Model Level)**:
 - **Mechanism**: Monitors the structural stability of the flow matching model during inference. It introduces a "directional sensitivity discrepancy" metric `R`, calculated as the standard deviation of the flow's response to small perturbations along multiple random directions in the latent space. This involves probing the Jacobian of the velocity field.
 - **Action**: A high `R` score (exceeding a threshold) indicates a chaotic or anisotropic vector field, signaling unreliable generation. This triggers an early rejection and safe fallback, preventing structurally unstable motions from being executed.

3. **Stage 3: Hard Kinematic Screening (Output Level)**:
 - **Mechanism**: Performs a deterministic check on the final kinematic trajectory (decoded from the latent space). It verifies strict adherence to hardware constraints, specifically joint position, velocity, and acceleration limits defined for the robot.
 - **Action**: Any violation triggers a rejection and safe fallback, serving as a final defense against immediate hardware damage.

**D. RL-Based Motion Tracking Controller**
The system adopts a goal-conditioned RL motion tracking controller, trained with PPO in Isaac Lab. This controller outputs residual joint corrections to the reference motions, enhancing robustness. Observations (e.g., velocities, root height, joint targets) are expressed in the body-local frame to improve generalization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of SafeFlow was conducted through extensive simulation studies and real-world deployment on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, addressing physical executability, deployment-time safety, computational efficiency, and practical performance.

**A. Physical Executability (Q1)**
The study assessed the physical feasibility of generated motions and the success rate of the downstream tracking controller.

- **Generator-Only Kinematic Feasibility**: Compared to the TextOp baseline, SafeFlow significantly reduced physical violations. The baseline exhibited a 43.14% Joint Limit Violation Rate (JV) and 11.05% Self-Collision Rate (SC). SafeFlow, even with its base flow matching formulation, reduced these to 12.75% JV and 7.25% SC. With physics-guided sampling, JV dropped to 6.32% and SC to 4.39%. The reflow-distilled model (SafeFlow + Guid. & Reflow), designed for real-time control (NFE=1), maintained low violations at 3.08% JV and 1.42% SC. The analysis of CoM velocity and joint acceleration plots indicated that SafeFlow stabilized trajectories and suppressed erratic spikes, resulting in kinematically feasible references.

- **System-Level Tracking Fidelity**: The integrated SafeFlow pipeline demonstrated improved performance in closed-loop control. The success rate (completing a sequence without falling) increased from 80.6% (TextOp) to 98.5% (SafeFlow + Guid. & Reflow). Tracking discrepancy metrics—MPJPE, Velocity Error, and Acceleration Error—were substantially reduced, indicating that the physically compliant references alleviated the burden on the tracker. For instance, the MPJPE (success-only) decreased from 78.04 mm to 41.20 mm. Torque and joint velocity plots confirmed that SafeFlow mitigated severe torque chattering and erratic velocity spikes observed in the baseline, contributing to hardware-safe tracking.

- **Diversity Preservation**: While the baseline showed a higher overall multimodality (1.40 rad) compared to SafeFlow (1.09 rad), this difference was largely attributed to unstable and physically implausible motions generated by the baseline. When comparing only successful and physically compliant motions, the diversity difference between SafeFlow and the baseline diminished, suggesting that SafeFlow retains meaningful behavioral variation while filtering out unexecutable diversity.

**B. Deployment-Time Safety and Robustness (Q2)**
The 3-Stage Safety Gate's ability to filter unsafe inputs and generative instabilities was evaluated.

- **Stage 1: Input-Level Semantic Filtering**: This stage utilized Mahalanobis distance to detect OOD prompts. For "Unseen Verbs" and "Extreme Dynamics" OOD prompts, Stage 1 achieved high AUROCs (0.9872 and 0.9715, respectively). It successfully rejected 93-95% of OOD inputs while maintaining a 90.56% acceptance rate for in-distribution prompts. This indicated its effectiveness in preventing the generation of motions for semantically anomalous commands.

- **Stage 2 & 3: Model- and Output-Level Filtering**:
 - **Generation Instability Score (R)**: The instability score `R` was found to correlate monotonically with downstream tracking error (MPJPE). Higher `R` values corresponded to increased tracking errors, indicating `R` is a predictor of physically unstable references. Even in-distribution prompts could produce high-`R` windows, demonstrating that semantic filtering alone is insufficient, and real-time monitoring of generative dynamics is necessary.
 - **Instability Score-Triggered Safe Fallback**: When `R` exceeded a threshold, Stage 2 triggered a safe fallback (injecting a "stand" prompt and interpolating to a nominal pose). This mechanism prevented robot collapse from unstable generations, allowing the robot to remain stable and await the next command, as demonstrated in comparative scenarios where the robot failed without Stage 2.
 - **Hard Kinematic Screening (Stage 3)**: This final fail-safe deterministically checked joint position, velocity, and acceleration limits, ensuring hardware-safe execution by rejecting any violating motion segment.

**C. Real-Time Performance (Q3)**
Latency measurements confirmed SafeFlow's real-time capabilities.

- Physics-guided sampling initially increased generator latency (172.03 ms). However, reflow distillation effectively reduced the generation latency to 10.80 ms, making it faster than the TextOp baseline (23.59 ms) and enabling a generation frequency of 92.6 Hz.
- The 3-Stage Safety Gate added minimal overhead, totaling approximately 3.98 ms for all three stages. This resulted in a total guarded generator latency of 14.78 ms, corresponding to an operational frequency of approximately 67.7 Hz for the complete high-level pipeline. When combined with the shared text encoder and motion tracking controller, SafeFlow met the requirements for real-time closed-loop control.

**D. Real-Robot Deployment (Q4)**
Deployment on the Unitree G1 humanoid demonstrated successful sim-to-real transferability and deployment-time safety.

- The robot executed continuous, long-horizon sequences of diverse behaviors, including upper-body gestures and demanding whole-body dynamics, with smooth transitions and without manual resets.
- Crucially, the 3-Stage Safety Gate successfully intervened when a high-risk prompt ("double backflip") was given. It identified and filtered the unsafe reference, preventing a potentially hazardous trajectory from reaching the controller. The system then triggered a safe fallback, allowing the robot to remain balanced and continue with subsequent commands, validating the practical value of the safety mechanisms on real hardware.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SafeFlow represents an advancement in the field of real-time text-driven humanoid robot control, addressing several critical challenges that hinder the deployment of generative motion models on physical hardware.

The primary significance of this work lies in its integration of physics-guided motion generation with a comprehensive, multi-stage safety gating mechanism. By actively steering the generation process towards physically executable motions and subsequently filtering out unsafe outputs at various levels (semantic input, model instability, kinematic output), SafeFlow enhances the reliability and safety of humanoid robot operation under diverse and open-ended text commands. This approach mitigates the "physical hallucinations" commonly observed in kinematics-only generative models, which can lead to hardware damage or unstable robot behavior.

The incorporation of rectified flow matching and reflow distillation is significant for enabling real-time performance. This allows the system to internalize complex physics constraints during training, rather than relying on computationally intensive online gradient computations. The resulting low-latency motion generation is critical for interactive applications where robots must respond promptly to human input.

The novel 3-Stage Safety Gate, particularly the Mahalanobis-based semantic OOD filtering and the directional sensitivity discrepancy metric for generation instability, offers a practical and efficient solution for robust deployment. These training-free mechanisms provide a proactive defense against unforeseen or adversarial prompts, preventing potentially hazardous situations before they impact the robot's physical state. The real-robot deployment on the Unitree G1, demonstrating successful execution of complex behaviors and effective intervention of the safety gate, validates the practical utility and robustness of the framework in a real-world setting.

Potential impacts of SafeFlow include:
- **Increased Robot Autonomy and Safety**: By providing a more reliable and safer foundation for text-driven control, SafeFlow could accelerate the development of autonomous humanoid robots capable of operating in unstructured environments with human-like expressiveness, while minimizing risks to hardware and nearby individuals.
- **Improved Human-Robot Interaction**: The ability to translate natural language into physically safe and executable motions in real-time could lead to more intuitive and accessible human-robot interfaces, broadening the scope of applications for humanoid robots in various domains such as assistance, logistics, and entertainment.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The principles of physics-guided generation and hierarchical safety gating could be extended to other areas of robot control involving generative models, complex motor skills, or dynamic human-robot collaboration. The work also highlights the importance of developing more sophisticated fallback behaviors, suggesting avenues for future research into task-aware recovery mechanisms.

Overall, SafeFlow contributes to bridging the gap between high-level language-based control and low-level physical execution, paving the way for more robust, safe, and deployable intelligent humanoid robots.
