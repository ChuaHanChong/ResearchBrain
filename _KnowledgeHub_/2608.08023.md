# 4D-WAM: Infusing Spatiotemporal Awareness into World Action Models through Trajectory Fields

> [!summary] Summary
> 4D-WAM is a model-agnostic training recipe that infuses 4D awareness into world action models by aligning intermediate DiT features with 3D trajectory fields from a frozen Trace Anything teacher. Two auxiliary losses, motion alignment on frame differences and source-to-destination distribution alignment, add no inference cost yet raise out-of-distribution LIBERO-Plus success from 62.21% to 71.01%.

## Problem

- Existing world action models represent futures in 2D pixel space, leaving a representation gap with the 3D space where robot actions are actually executed.
- Prior 3D approaches rely on per-frame depth or point maps, so they never explicitly encode how 3D structure continuously evolves over time.
- Predicting pixel-level depth pushes models toward low-level reconstruction detail instead of the high-level 4D understanding that accurate action prediction actually requires.

## Method

- **4D-WAM**'s ==motion alignment== projects WAM ==DiT== hidden states and frozen 4D teacher features into a shared space, then matches normalized consecutive-frame differences via token-wise cosine loss.
- ==Destination alignment== treats first-frame tokens as queries and final-frame teacher tokens as keys, minimizing ==KL divergence== between temperature-scaled source-to-destination similarity distributions for goal-directed supervision.
- Both auxiliary losses join the ==latent-diffusion== and action objectives with coefficients below 0.1, applied at DiT layer 20, with teacher trajectory features pre-cached.

## Results

- LIBERO-Plus out-of-distribution average rises from **62.21%** to **71.01%** (**+8.8** points), including **+17.26** under camera perturbation, while in-distribution LIBERO moves only **98.2%** to **98.6%**.
- RoboTwin 2.0 average reaches **91.34%** versus **90.58%** base; on Clean2Rand with a different backbone, **61.7%** average beats the strongest baseline's **57.3%**.
- Ablations: removing either loss drops LIBERO to **98.0%**; OOD video prediction improves PSNR **19.22** to **21.44** and SSIM **0.75** to **0.83**, costing one extra GPU-hour.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - When two representation spaces are incompatible, align their temporal differences instead of absolute features; relative motion structure transfers where direct feature matching causes optimization conflict.
> - Auxiliary alignment gains concentrate out-of-distribution; near-saturated in-distribution benchmarks hide robustness improvements roughly twenty times larger, so always evaluate under perturbations.
> - Teacher supervision can be training-only: caching frozen teacher features keeps overhead near 2% and leaves inference architecture, latency, and parameter count untouched.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of embodied artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on World Models (WMs) and World Action Models (WAMs). World Models aim to predict how physical environments evolve under interaction, serving as a foundation for intelligent agents. Recent advancements have led to the development of World Action Models, which extend this predictive capability from general video modeling to embodied robotic domains by jointly modeling video prediction and action generation.

A fundamental challenge in this area arises from the disparity between how WAMs typically represent visual information and the inherent nature of robotic actions. Most WAMs represent videos in 2D pixel space, which creates a representation gap with the 3D physical world where robots operate. While some prior research has attempted to integrate 3D information into WAMs by incorporating modalities such as depth maps, surface normals, or point clouds as inputs or auxiliary reconstruction targets, these approaches have shown limitations. They frequently operate on a per-frame basis, providing static 3D geometry at each timestep but often overlooking the continuous spatiotemporal evolution, or "4D nature," of dynamic scenes where 3D structure and its temporal changes are intrinsically linked. Furthermore, focusing solely on pixel-level 3D reconstruction details may divert the model from developing a higher-level understanding of 4D dynamics, which is more critical for effective action prediction in complex manipulation tasks. This paper positions itself to address these limitations by proposing a method to inject comprehensive spatiotemporal (4D) awareness into WAMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the spatiotemporal understanding of World Action Models (WAMs) by explicitly infusing knowledge about 3D trajectory fields into their learned representations. The motivation stems from two critical limitations observed in existing WAMs:

1. **Representation Gap with 3D Space:** Traditional WAMs often operate by modeling 2D pixels across video frames. However, robotic manipulation inherently occurs in a 3D physical environment, and videos are merely 2D projections of a dynamic 3D world. This discrepancy between the 2D visual input and the 3D action space creates a representation gap, potentially limiting the precision and effectiveness of action generation.
2. **Inadequate Exploitation of 4D Dynamics:** While some contemporary 3D WAMs incorporate depth maps, surface normals, or point clouds to introduce 3D information, they tend to represent 3D structures independently at each time step. This approach is confined to per-frame 3D representations and overlooks the continuous "4D" nature of dynamic scenes, where 3D spatial structure and its temporal evolution are intrinsically coupled. Such methods may fail to explicitly represent the continuous movement and transformation of objects and the robot over time. Additionally, focusing on pixel-level depth image reconstruction can cause models to prioritize low-level details rather than developing the high-level 4D understanding necessary for robust action prediction.

To overcome these limitations, the authors propose that effective spatiotemporal perception in WAMs requires:
- A compact and fundamental representation that captures both 3D structure and its temporal evolution.
- A simple yet scalable training methodology to integrate this representation.

The paper identifies 3D trajectory fields as a suitable 4D representation, capable of associating each pixel across frames with a parametric 3D trajectory, thereby directly capturing the motion of objects and robots as functions of time.

The key objectives of 4D-WAM are:
- To propose a model-agnostic training strategy that injects spatiotemporal knowledge from 3D trajectory fields into WAMs.
- To achieve this by aligning the intermediate visual features of a WAM with 4D features encoded by an external 4D foundation model (Trace Anything).
- To introduce two complementary alignment objectives to facilitate this knowledge transfer:
 1. **Motion Alignment:** This objective aims to build local 4D awareness by aligning temporal feature variations between adjacent frames, encouraging the WAM to learn consistent motion patterns.
 2. **Destination Alignment:** This objective guides the WAM to infer the final destination of manipulated objects from the source frame, promoting an understanding of long-horizon, goal-directed motion by minimizing the gap between attention-like similarity distributions.

Collectively, these objectives are designed to provide both local motion supervision and long-horizon goal guidance, enabling WAMs to learn trajectory-level spatiotemporal representations that are more suitable for robotic manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, 4D-WAM, is a model-agnostic training strategy designed to infuse spatiotemporal awareness into World Action Models (WAMs) by leveraging 3D trajectory fields. The core idea is to align the intermediate visual features of a WAM with the 4D features extracted from a specialized 4D foundation model, specifically Trace Anything (Liu et al. 2026), which provides parametric 3D trajectories for video pixels.

**Initial Representation Alignment Challenge:**
The authors first investigate directly aligning WAM representations with 4D features, similar to approaches used in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. However, they observe that WAM representations (e.g., from Lingbot-VA) show little to no correlation with 4D features across most layers, in contrast to the consistent positive similarity found in VLA models. This suggests a significant discrepancy between the feature spaces, making direct alignment problematic. Instead, the paper identifies that frame-wise *feature differences* between WAM and 4D foundation model representations exhibit substantially higher similarity, indicating that temporal dynamics provide a more suitable signal for alignment.

**4D-WAM's Two Complementary Alignment Objectives:**

1. **Motion Alignment via Token-wise Frame Differences:**
 - This objective aims to capture local motion dynamics by aligning the temporal variations of features across consecutive frames. It avoids direct alignment of absolute feature representations.
 - **Process:**
 - Intermediate DiT representations ($H_f \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d_h}$) from the WAM and 4D reconstruction representations ($Z_f \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d_z}$) from Trace Anything are obtained for each frame $f$, where $N$ is the number of visual tokens.
 - Two independent, two-layer Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) projection networks, $P_h$ and $P_z$, map these representations into a shared $d$-dimensional feature space. $P_z$ (for the teacher model, Trace Anything) is frozen during optimization to provide a stable target space.
 - The projected representations are normalized using $\ell_2$ norm.
 - Token-wise temporal motion is characterized by computing the difference between consecutive normalized projected frames: $\Delta h_{f,i} = h_{f+1,i} - h_{f,i}$ and $\Delta z_{f,i} = z_{f+1,i} - z_{f,i}$. These difference vectors capture motion while reducing reliance on the original feature spaces.
 - A token-wise cosine distance is used as the motion alignment loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{motion}}$) to align the motion directions in the WAM's DiT representations with those from the 4D reconstruction features. This loss encourages consistent temporal changes without forcing exact feature matching.

2. **Source-Conditioned Destination Constraint:**
 - While motion alignment handles local changes, it may not explicitly model the long-term goal of manipulation. This objective introduces goal-directed supervision by associating moving regions at the start of a trajectory with their expected destinations in future frames.
 - **Process:**
 - The first frame of an episode is typically selected as the source frame, and the final frame as the destination frame, representing the manipulation outcome.
 - Projected and normalized WAM and teacher representations from the source frame ($h_{\text{src}}, z_{\text{src}} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d}$) and teacher representations from the destination frame ($z_{\text{dest}} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d}$) are used.
 - Similarity scores ($s_h, s_z$) are computed between source tokens (queries) and destination tokens (keys), inspired by Transformer attention.
 - These similarity scores are converted into source-conditioned destination probability distributions ($p_h$ for the WAM, $q_z$ for the teacher) using a temperature-scaled softmax function.
 - The Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{dest}}$) is minimized between the WAM's predicted destination distribution ($p_h$) and the teacher's target distribution ($q_z$). This objective guides the WAM to learn the relational structure between source and destination frames, focusing on the target-oriented motion.

**Overall Training Objective:**
The full training objective combines the standard WAM objectives with the proposed alignment losses:
$\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{latent}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{action}} + \lambda_{\text{motion}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{motion}} + \lambda_{\text{dest}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{dest}}$
- $\mathcal{L}_{\text{latent}}$: Supervises the denoising process in the latent space (standard diffusion training).
- $\mathcal{L}_{\text{action}}$: Encourages the generation of actions consistent with the demonstrated sequence.
- $\lambda_{\text{motion}}$ and $\lambda_{\text{dest}}$: Balancing coefficients, recommended to be below 0.1 to prevent excessive interference with the primary video generation task.
The alignment losses act as auxiliary supervision to enhance the temporal consistency and goal awareness of the learned video representations.

**Computational Aspects:**
4D-WAM's trajectory supervision is applied only during training. The Trace Anything model is frozen, and its 4D trajectory representations can be pre-extracted and cached to reduce computational overhead. This "cached 4D-WAM" approach results in a minimal increase in training time and GPU memory usage compared to the baseline WAM. The alignment projectors add a small number of trainable parameters (56 MiB). Critically, after training, Trace Anything, the alignment projectors, and all alignment objectives are removed, meaning 4D-WAM introduces no additional inference latency or memory consumption during deployment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of 4D-WAM was evaluated through extensive experiments across various settings, including in-distribution, out-of-distribution (OOD), video prediction, ablation studies, and real-world scenarios.

#### 4.1. In-distribution Experiments

- **Benchmarks:** RoboTwin 2.0 and LIBERO.
- **Base Model:** FastWAM-Joint.
- **RoboTwin 2.0:** 4D-WAM achieved an average success rate of 91.34%, outperforming FastWAM-Joint (90.58%). Specific improvements were 1.08% in the clean setting and 0.44% in the randomized setting.
- **LIBERO:** In this benchmark, where baseline performance was already high, 4D-WAM further increased the average success rate from 98.2% (FastWAM-Joint) to 98.6%. Improvements were observed across the Object, Goal, and Long suites. These results indicate consistent benefits even for strong WAM baselines.

#### 4.2. Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Experiments

- **Benchmarks:** LIBERO-Plus and RoboTwin Clean2Rand, designed to assess generalization and robustness under unseen variations.
- **LIBERO-Plus (trained on LIBERO, evaluated on perturbations):** 4D-WAM demonstrated a substantial improvement in robustness, outperforming FastWAM-Joint across all seven perturbation categories. The average success rate increased by 8.8 percentage points (from 62.21% to 71.01%). Notably, a 17.26-point gain was achieved under camera perturbations, suggesting enhanced spatial understanding. Significant improvements were also observed under lighting, noise, and background variations.
- **RoboTwin Clean2Rand (trained on clean data, evaluated on randomized):** Using Lingbot-VA as the base model, 4D-WAM achieved an average success rate of 61.7%, surpassing Lingbot-VA (57.7%). It was the only model evaluated to achieve an average success rate above 60% across clean and randomized settings. This highlights improved generalization to unseen object configurations and scene variations, mitigating performance degradation common in OOD scenarios.

#### 4.3. Experiments on Video Prediction

- **Quantitative Results (LIBERO-Plus):** 4D-WAM enhanced video prediction quality. PSNR increased from 19.22 to 21.44, and SSIM increased from 0.75 to 0.83 compared to FastWAM-Joint. This indicates more accurate and structurally consistent future observations.
- **Qualitative Results:** Visual comparisons showed that 4D-WAM better preserved object motion, target regions, and temporal consistency in future frames. Examples included improved tracking of a robotic arm under camera blur, consistent tracking of a microwave door despite occlusion, and stable preservation of a book's position from its initial to destination frame, unlike the baseline.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

- **Alignment Layer (on LIBERO):** Aligning both motion and destination objectives at the 20th layer yielded the best performance (98.6% average success rate). Performance decreased when aligning at shallower layers (e.g., 16th layer: 97.9%) or deeper layers (e.g., 24th layer: 97.8%), suggesting that intermediate layers (around 20th) offer a more suitable representation space for alignment objectives.
- **Contribution of Objectives (on LIBERO):** Removing either Motion Alignment or Destination Alignment led to a performance drop (both variants achieved 98.0% average success rate), indicating that both objectives contribute complementarily to the overall performance of 4D-WAM.

#### 4.5. Real-world Experiments

- **Platform:** ARX LIFT2 bimanual manipulator.
- **Tasks:** Four long-horizon tasks, including cylinder insertion, towel flattening (deformable object manipulation), toolbox wrench localization (spatial reasoning), and rotating light pick-and-place (dynamics understanding).
- **Base Model:** Lingbot-VA (pretrained on large-scale robot data).
- **Results:** 4D-WAM consistently outperformed Lingbot-VA across all four tasks, achieving a total success rate of 5.5% compared to Lingbot-VA's 1.0%. It also improved sub-task progress by over 10% on average. These results suggest that the enhanced spatiotemporal understanding from 4D-WAM directly translates to improved prediction precision and long-horizon manipulation capabilities in real-world settings.

#### 4.6. Probing Experiments: Mechanism of 4D Awareness

- **Method:** Frozen WAM backbone, extracted visual-token representations from the 20th DiT block, and trained lightweight MLP probes for frame-to-frame position prediction and source-to-destination correspondence.
- **Results:** 4D-WAM consistently achieved lower KL divergence, lower cross-entropy, higher Top-k accuracy, and lower mean rank compared to FastWAM-Joint variants on both LIBERO and LIBERO-Plus for both probing tasks. This indicates that 4D-WAM learns representations that are more trajectory-aware, capable of better capturing local dynamics and predicting precise destinations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of 4D-WAM represents a notable advancement in the development of World Action Models for robotic manipulation. By explicitly addressing the limitations of 2D pixel-space modeling and per-frame 3D representations, this work contributes to more robust and capable embodied AI systems.

The significance of 4D-WAM lies in several key areas:
- **Enhanced Spatiotemporal Understanding:** The core contribution is enabling WAMs to acquire a deeper understanding of the 4D nature of dynamic environments. By aligning intermediate features with 3D trajectory fields through motion and destination alignment, the model learns to encode both local temporal dynamics and long-horizon goal-directed movements. This moves beyond mere pixel-level reconstruction to a more semantically rich representation of how objects and agents move in 3D space over time.
- **Improved Robotic Manipulation Performance:** The empirical results consistently demonstrate that this enhanced spatiotemporal awareness translates directly into improved downstream performance. 4D-WAM achieves higher success rates in various simulated benchmarks (RoboTwin 2.0, LIBERO) and, critically, in complex real-world manipulation tasks. The gains in execution precision and the ability to complete long-horizon tasks are particularly impactful for practical robotic applications.
- **Increased Robustness and Generalization:** The significant performance improvements observed in out-of-distribution (OOD) settings, such as LIBERO-Plus and RoboTwin Clean2Rand, highlight the model's enhanced robustness to unseen environmental variations, camera perturbations, and object configurations. This indicates that 4D-WAM learns more generalizable policies, a crucial factor for deploying robots in diverse and unstructured environments.
- **Model-Agnostic and Computationally Efficient Integration:** The proposed alignment strategy is designed to be model-agnostic, meaning it can be applied to various existing WAM architectures without requiring fundamental architectural changes. Furthermore, by pre-extracting and caching trajectory representations, 4D-WAM minimizes additional training overhead. Crucially, it introduces no extra parameters or latency during inference, making it a practical and efficient enhancement for real-world deployment.
- **Bridging the Representation Gap:** This work effectively bridges the inherent representation gap between the 2D visual data processed by WAMs and the 3D physical world in which robots operate. By injecting 4D knowledge, WAMs are better equipped to reason about the underlying physical reality, leading to more intelligent and effective decision-making.

The potential impact of 4D-WAM extends to the development of more intelligent and autonomous robotic systems. By fostering a richer understanding of spatiotemporal dynamics, this approach could contribute to advancements in complex manipulation, human-robot interaction, and adaptive control in dynamic environments. It suggests a promising direction for future research in embodied AI, emphasizing the importance of grounding visual representations in the continuous, four-dimensional reality of the physical world.
