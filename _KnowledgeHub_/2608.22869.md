# UniMem: Unifying Multimodal Memory and Control for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> UniMem equips a vision-language-action model with unified multimodal memory under one backbone. A lightweight event classifier reads the VLA's final-layer latent to detect task milestones, autoregressively appending textual event strings and caching keyframe hidden states that a temporally-attentive vision encoder ingests. It reaches 93.4% success in simulation and 80.0% on hardware, beating fixed-interval and hierarchical baselines while holding roughly 90 ms inference.

## Problem

- Reactive VLA policies condition only on the current frame, so perceptual aliasing makes visually similar phases of non-Markovian tasks demand different actions the policy cannot distinguish.
- Hierarchical memory systems bolt on a separate vision-language model producing subtask commands, siloing the low-level actor from raw visual history and fracturing the training pipeline.
- Feeding frames sampled at arbitrary fixed intervals injects task-irrelevant observations, inviting spurious correlations and causal confusion while inflating attention cost as horizon grows.

## Method

- **Event classifier**: an ==MLP head== on the backbone's final-layer latent predicts milestones from an event vocabulary, trained jointly so classification gradients shape the shared control representation.
- **Multimodal memory update**: a fired event appends a natural-language phrase to textual memory and stores multi-view keyframes, capped at four, giving both sequential progress and spatial scene history.
- **Keyframe encoder** plus caching: ==causal temporal self-attention== interleaved into ==SigLIP==, with pre-positional-embedding hidden states cached so only the current frame recomputes spatial attention.

## Results

- Simulation average **93.4%** over **five** tasks versus **68.2%** for the fixed-interval video-encoder baseline, **72.6%** keyframe-only, **65.4%** text-only, and **29.6%** with no memory.
- Hardware average **80.0%** versus **43.5%** for the hierarchical MemER baseline; TableClean rose **13%** to **80%** and TapScoopPour **7%** to **60%**.
- Ablations show modality complementarity: counting task UpDown3Times hits **93%** with text but **23%** with keyframes; caching keeps **16** keyframes across **four** streams within **25 ms** extra latency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sequential and spatial memory are separable failure modes: text compresses progress for counting, keyframes ground geometry, and neither alone suffices on long-horizon tasks.
> - Gating memory writes on learned event detection, rather than fixed sampling, filters distractors and doubles as an in-distribution confidence check on what gets stored.
> - Caching pre-attention hidden states makes visual history nearly free at inference, so context length stops being the deployment bottleneck; dataset coverage becomes the limit.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a notable area of progress in robotics, particularly in robot manipulation. These models leverage large-scale pretraining on internet data and subsequent task-specific fine-tuning to achieve capabilities in various tasks. Foundational VLA models such as RT-1, Octo, RT-2, and π0.5 have demonstrated semantic reasoning and generalization across different objects, scenes, and robot embodiments. However, their success has primarily been observed in short-duration, Markovian tasks, where the current observation is sufficient for determining the next action.

A significant limitation for these models is their performance in non-Markovian tasks that necessitate memory, a phenomenon the authors characterize as "perceptual aliasing." Perceptual aliasing occurs when different task states appear visually similar but require distinct actions based on past events or task progress. Addressing this requires equipping VLAs with architectural memory that can retain both sequential and spatial information about the task history.

Existing approaches to incorporate memory into robotic systems can be broadly categorized. Early methods utilized recurrent neural networks (RNNs) or state-space belief encoders for implicit hidden states, or transformer-based architectures to process temporal sequences of frames. While these methods are effective in low-dimensional or short-horizon domains, blindly ingesting dense observation streams can lead to computational overhead, context degradation, and a lack of selectivity for task-critical events.

More recently, specific strategies for VLA models have emerged. Some approaches employ hybrid systems, using a high-level Vision-Language Model (VLM) to manage long-term memory, often by generating textual summaries of completed milestones and issuing sub-task commands to a low-level VLA. Examples include MemER, which stores historically relevant keyframes but maintains a decoupled architecture, and MEM, which uses a VLM to generate textual summaries for the VLA. While these hierarchical methods can be effective in simpler tasks, they introduce several challenges. The fractured approach creates "artificial silos" that can isolate the VLA from the rich spatiotemporal context needed for fine-grained reasoning. Furthermore, they may discard essential spatiotemporal information inherent in past video frames, data that VLAs are designed to process. Combining sub-task commands with fixed-interval historical frames can also expose the VLA to visual distractions and spurious correlations, as irrelevant past observations might be considered by the policy.

UniMem positions itself within this landscape by addressing the limitations of existing memory-equipped VLA systems. It proposes a unified, single-model system that directly integrates multimodal memory (both textual and visual) with low-level control. In contrast to decoupled hierarchical systems, UniMem aims to provide memory within a shared backbone, thereby minimizing inference latency and simplifying the training pipeline. Its event-driven architecture directly addresses the problem of irrelevant historical context by selectively retaining task-critical information, distinguishing it from methods that rely on fixed-interval frame sampling. The work seeks to demonstrate that a tightly integrated, multimodal memory system can unlock robust performance in challenging non-Markovian tasks, thereby advancing the capabilities of VLA models beyond their current reactive limitations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this work arises from a fundamental limitation observed in contemporary Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: their struggle with non-Markovian tasks that require memory. Despite significant advancements in robot manipulation enabled by internet-scale pretraining and task-focused fine-tuning, state-of-the-art VLA policies typically operate reactively, relying solely on the immediate observation and current instruction. This reactive nature proves insufficient for tasks where the optimal action depends on past events or the sequence of steps taken, a scenario characterized by the authors as **perceptual aliasing**. In such cases, visually similar observations might demand entirely different actions based on prior context, necessitating both sequential memory to track task progress and spatial memory to retain pertinent information about past scene states.

The authors pose a central research question: "Can conditioning VLAs on multimodal memory directly unlock robust performance in non-Markovian tasks?" This question highlights the pursuit of a memory mechanism that is seamlessly integrated and leverages diverse modalities.

The paper identifies several shortcomings in existing approaches to incorporate memory into VLA systems, which serve as direct motivations for UniMem's design:

- **Fractured Architectures and Memory Bottlenecks:** Many existing methods incorporate memory via an additional VLM that generates textual summaries or sub-task commands. This factorized approach creates architectural silos, separating the VLA from direct access to the rich historical context. This decoupling introduces a memory bottleneck and results in a fractured training pipeline, increasing complexity and potentially hindering end-to-end learning.
- **Discarded Spatiotemporal Information:** Hierarchical approaches often distill complex historical context into textual summaries, thereby discarding essential spatiotemporal information encoded in past video frames. VLAs are inherently structured to process visual data, and neglecting this modality in memory limits their reasoning capabilities.
- **Context Degradation and Spurious Correlations:** Conditioning VLAs on long, continuous streams of past observations or arbitrarily chosen, fixed-interval historical frames can degrade performance. Irrelevant observations may introduce spurious correlations, causing the model to learn undesirable relationships between memory and action, rather than focusing on task-relevant information. This leads to computational overhead and reduced temporal efficiency.
- **Inference Latency:** Decoupled memory systems and the need to re-encode historical visual data at each step can introduce significant inference latency, making real-time control challenging.

To address these limitations, the key objectives for UniMem are:

- **Unify Multimodal Memory and Low-Level Control:** To integrate high-level textual events and dense visual context directly within a single VLA backbone, allowing memory and control loops to operate adjacent to each other. This aims to simplify the system architecture and training pipeline.
- **Implement an Event-Driven Memory Architecture:** To dynamically detect "task-critical milestones" and selectively retain only task-relevant textual and visual context. This design is intended to mitigate causal confusion, improve temporal efficiency, and provide a concentrated signal for memory representations.
- **Enable Real-Time Inference through Efficient Keyframe Management:** To incorporate a keyframe caching technique that minimizes computational overhead during policy rollouts. By avoiding redundant re-encoding of historical visual data, UniMem aims to preserve fast inference speeds despite conditioning on rich visual histories.

In summary, the motivation is to overcome the inherent memory limitations of current VLA models, specifically perceptual aliasing, by developing a unified, event-driven, multimodal memory system that integrates seamlessly with the VLA's control loop while maintaining real-time performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UniMem is designed as a unified framework that integrates multimodal memory and control within a single Vision-Language-Action (VLA) backbone. The system is implemented on top of π0.5, an open-source VLA that utilizes a PaliGemma backbone and a Gemma action expert. The core of UniMem's methodology involves an event-driven mechanism for dynamically updating textual and visual memory, coupled with efficient keyframe encoding and caching.

**Problem Formulation:** The work frames long-horizon, non-Markovian robotic manipulation as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP). The agent receives an observation (o_t) and a high-level task instruction (g) at each timestep. UniMem addresses perceptual aliasing by conditioning the policy on a history of past states, mapping a sequence of observations (o_{t-T:t}) to the correct low-level motor action (a_t).

**Event-Driven Multimodal Memory:**
UniMem maintains two complementary forms of history:
1. **Textual Memory (M_t):** Records discrete task events as natural language strings.
2. **Visual Keyframe Memory (H_t):** Retains key spatial information from past multi-view visual observations.

These memories are updated online via a lightweight **event classifier head (f_ϕ)**. This classifier is an MLP appended directly to the final layer latent representation (z_t) extracted from the VLA backbone. The latent representation z_t is typically used for autoregressive text generation in the underlying VLM. Instead, f_ϕ processes z_t to predict an event e_t from a predefined event vocabulary E_all (E ∪ {null}).
The predicted event e_t is chosen via the most likely class index from the probability distribution produced by f_ϕ. The event classifier loss is jointly optimized with the VLA's action generation loss, ensuring that the shared backbone learns latent features supporting both memory and control.

- **Memory Update Logic:**
 - If the predicted event e_t is a task-critical milestone (e_t ∈ E, i.e., e_t ≠ null), a memory update is triggered.
 - **Textual Update:** The event e_t is appended as natural language to the textual history M_t, modifying the language instruction for the subsequent step (M_{t+1} = [M_t || e_t]). M_t is initialized as empty at the start of a rollout.
 - **Visual Update:** The corresponding multi-view visual observation (I = {I_wrist, I_ext}) is stored as a keyframe in H_t. H_t is initialized with the initial scene image. The size of this keyframe history is capped (e.g., 3-4 past milestones) to manage computational resources.
- **Recursive Memory Loop:** The event classifier f_ϕ operates on the unified latent representation z_t, which itself is constructed via cross-attention over both textual memory M_t and visual keyframes H_t. This design creates a recursive memory loop where prior milestones directly influence the detection of subsequent events. The updated policy formulation conditions on H_t, M_t, proprioception (q_t), and the task instruction (g).

**Efficient Keyframe Encoding and Caching:**
To process the growing keyframe bank (H_t) without degrading inference speed, UniMem employs a modified vision encoder and a caching mechanism:

- **Keyframe Encoder:** The π0.5 vision encoder (SigLIP) is modified to include interleaved causal temporal self-attention layers within its standard spatial attention stack. These temporal attention layers reuse the query/key/value and layer-norm weights of their corresponding spatial layers. This encoder is trained end-to-end during fine-tuning. Unlike prior work, which often samples frames at fixed intervals, UniMem's event-driven keyframes provide a concentrated, task-relevant signal.
- **Keyframe Caching:** To prevent redundant re-encoding of historical keyframes at every timestep, their intermediate representations are cached in a feature cache (H_cache). Specifically, hidden states are cached prior to positional embeddings and layer-normalizations. When a new frame arrives, only its positional embedding is shifted, and the current image forms the query for temporal attention, avoiding costly spatial attention recomputation for cached frames. This preserves real-time control by minimizing the computational overhead as temporal context scales.

**Data and Training:**
Training UniMem requires a dataset of robot actions (a_t:t+H), observations (o_t), proprioception (q_t), and high-level task instructions (g), along with labels for events (e_t) and textual memory (M_t).

- **Automated Data Labeling:** The authors utilize a VLM (Claude Sonnet 5.0) to generate scripts that parse existing demonstration datasets. These scripts identify specific action signatures (e.g., gripper closing implies grasping) to label frames with corresponding events (e_t ∈ E_all). Frames outside these event windows are labeled with a "null" class. Human verification and refinement of these scripts ensure label quality.
- **Preventing Target Leakage:** During training, M_t is updated with an event only *after* the entire event window has passed, preventing the memory from prematurely revealing the target outcome.
- **Keyframe Sampling for Training:** For training H_t, a single frame is uniformly sampled from each past event window along with the current observation (I_t).
- **Loss Function:** The policy is trained end-to-end with a two-term objective:
 1. **Action-chunking loss (L_a):** The standard flow-matching objective from π0.5, where the action expert integrates the chunk a_t:t+H along a denoising velocity field conditioned on o_t.
 2. **Event classification loss (L_e):** A class-weighted cross-entropy loss supervises the event classifier head f_ϕ against the automatically generated event labels e_t. Null frames, which dominate the dataset, are downweighted (w_null = 0.02) rather than masked, to train the classifier to withhold predictions when no event occurs. This asymmetric weighting ensures that the model learns to selectively fire the event detector.
- The total loss is a weighted sum: L = L_a + λ L_e, with λ = 0.1. The classification gradient flows back through the shared VLA backbone, coupling memory learning with control. Fine-tuning uses LoRA.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of UniMem was conducted across nine memory tasks, comprising five simulation tasks and four real-world hardware tasks, designed to assess its performance in sequential and spatial memory settings.

**Overall Performance Summary:** UniMem consistently demonstrated improved performance compared to fixed-interval image sampling baselines in simulation and hierarchical memory baselines in hardware.
- **Simulation:** UniMem achieved an average success rate of 93.4% across five tasks, significantly outperforming the fixed-window video baseline (π0.5 +V.E.) which averaged 68.2%.
- **Hardware:** UniMem attained an average success rate of 80.0% across four tasks, exceeding the best-performing hierarchical baseline (MemER) which achieved 43.5%.

**Simulation Experiments and Ablations (Five Tasks):**
- **Task Design:** Tasks included UpDown (pick and place once), UpDown3Times (counting task), OccludedTap (spatial memory with occlusion), UpDownSpatial (continuous spatial recall), and PlateRecall (discrete spatial recall).
- **Performance Comparison:**
 - **UpDown and OccludedTap:** Basic non-Markovian tasks showed that history conditioning did not impede low-level control, with UniMem achieving 100% and 96% respectively.
 - **UpDown3Times (Counting):** Keyframe-only (K.O.) conditioning struggled (23% success), indicating limitations in temporal reach for pure visual memory. Text-only (T.O.) conditioning achieved 93%, demonstrating the value of textual memory for tracking discrete progress. UniMem, combining both, reached 96%.
 - **OccludedTap (Spatial):** K.O. performed 12% better than T.O., suggesting keyframes provided richer spatial memory.
 - **UpDownSpatial (Continuous Spatial Grounding):** T.O. significantly dropped to 30%, lacking geometric awareness. K.O. and the Video Encoder (V.E.) baseline performed better (52% and 49%), but UniMem (79%) resolved both failure modes by integrating both modalities.
 - **PlateRecall (Discrete Spatial Recall):** T.O. achieved 20% (random chance for 1-in-4 plates). K.O. and V.E. matched UniMem at 96%, demonstrating visual memory's efficacy for discrete spatial disambiguation when progress is inferred.
- **Ablation Insights:** The ablations confirmed the complementarity of textual and visual memory. Visual memory alone struggled with counting, while textual memory alone lacked fine-grained spatial awareness. UniMem's combined approach consistently addressed both.

**Real-world Experiments and Analyses (Four Tasks):**
The hardware experiments addressed three core research questions:
- **Q1: UniMem vs. Hierarchical Systems (MemER):**
 - **HammerMeasure:** Both UniMem and MemER achieved 87% success, indicating that high-level subtask commands are sufficient for simpler tasks where memory needs are minimal.
 - **BeanScoop (Counting & Sequential):** UniMem achieved 93% success, while MemER reached 67%. MemER's high-level planner sometimes output incorrect subtask commands after two scoops, leading to unrecoverable states. UniMem's continuous, direct memory access prevented such high-level misclassifications and allowed for continuous trajectory adjustment.
 - **TableClean (Continuous Spatial):** UniMem achieved 80% success, significantly outperforming MemER (13%). MemER's augmented commands (left, right, center) were insufficient for precise spatial recall over a large area. UniMem's combined keyframe and textual memory provided direct access to past visual states for accurate wiping.
 - **TapScoopPour (Discrete Spatial & Human Interaction):** UniMem achieved 60% success, while MemER managed only 7%. MemER's command vocabulary was insufficient to disambiguate eight cups. UniMem's direct visual history enabled better cup selection, with errors typically limited to adjacent cups in the keyframe-only ablation, and the full system achieving exact selection.
 - **Conclusion (Q1):** Unified, multimodal memory directly provided the VLA with spatial and sequential awareness, overcoming the limitations of hierarchical policies that rely on abstract subtask commands.

- **Q2: Contributions of Joint Textual and Visual Memory:**
 - Across all hardware tasks, neither the text-only nor keyframe-only ablations matched the performance of full UniMem.
 - **HammerMeasure:** No Memory (N.M.) 13%, T.O./K.O. 53%, Ours 87%.
 - **BeanScoop:** N.M. 0% (continuous scooping), T.O. 27% (undercounting), K.O. 20% (limited context). Ours 93%.
 - **TableClean:** N.M. 0% (aliased actions), T.O. (random wipes), K.O. 53% (infinite wipes/fail to place). Ours 80%.
 - **TapScoopPour:** N.M. 7% (requires multiple taps), T.O. 7% (random cup selection), K.O. 27% (adjacent cup errors). Ours 60%.
 - **Conclusion (Q2):** Textual and visual memories are complementary and both are essential for robust success in multimodal, non-Markovian tasks.

- **Q3: Inference Speed and Scalability via Keyframe Caching:**
 - Benchmarking demonstrated that UniMem's hidden-state caching mechanism for keyframes preserved near single-frame inference latencies.
 - Maintaining a 16-keyframe memory context across four camera streams added approximately 25 milliseconds of latency beyond the single-frame, two-camera stream base policy.
 - UniMem achieved a 6x speedup over hierarchical memory baselines (MemER).
 - **Conclusion (Q3):** The caching mechanism allows UniMem to scale to significantly longer context windows and different embodiments (e.g., bimanual manipulators with four camera streams) without sacrificing real-time inference speed.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The UniMem framework addresses a critical limitation in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: their struggle with non-Markovian tasks that demand sequential and spatial memory. The significance and potential impact of this work can be summarized as follows:

- **Enabling Robust Performance in Complex Non-Markovian Tasks:** By providing VLAs with unified multimodal memory, UniMem allows robots to overcome perceptual aliasing. This enables execution of tasks that require remembering past states, counting events, or recalling precise spatial locations, which are common in real-world scenarios but challenging for reactive policies. The demonstrated performance improvements in both simulation and hardware signify a step towards more capable and autonomous robotic systems.

- **Architectural Unification and Simplification:** UniMem unifies high-level memory and low-level control under a single backbone. This contrasts with previous hierarchical systems that often involve separate VLM components for memory management, leading to fractured training pipelines and increased complexity. The unified architecture simplifies the development and deployment process for memory-equipped VLAs.

- **Efficiency and Real-time Operation:** The event-driven memory architecture and keyframe caching mechanism are crucial for maintaining computational efficiency. By selectively storing only task-relevant information and avoiding redundant re-encoding of historical frames, UniMem achieves significantly faster inference speeds (e.g., 6x speedup over hierarchical baselines). This low-latency control is critical for practical, real-time robotic deployment and interaction.

- **Demonstrating Complementarity of Multimodal Memory:** The extensive ablation studies highlight that both textual and visual memory modalities are individually important and, more critically, complementary. Neither modality alone achieved the performance of the unified system, underscoring the necessity of integrating diverse forms of historical context for comprehensive task understanding and execution. This finding has implications for the design of future memory architectures in robotics and embodied AI.

- **Mitigation of Context Degradation and Causal Confusion:** The event-driven approach, which dynamically detects and stores task-critical milestones, mitigates the issues of spurious correlations and context degradation associated with fixed-interval or dense history sampling. This focused approach ensures that the VLA conditions on meaningful history, leading to more robust and temporally efficient decision-making.

- **Pathways for Future Research:** The paper identifies several promising directions for future work, including extending UniMem to handle longer execution horizons (tens of minutes or hours) through memory editing mechanisms (pruning, consolidation), developing unsupervised methods for autonomous event detection, and exploring distinctions between short-term and long-term memory for motor function improvement and learning from mistakes. These directions suggest that UniMem provides a strong foundation for advancing sophisticated, human-like memory capabilities in robotic agents.

In essence, UniMem contributes a principled and effective method for endowing VLA models with contextual memory, moving them beyond purely reactive control to enable more intelligent and adaptable behavior in complex, sequential tasks. Its unified architecture and efficient design have the potential to impact the scalability and practicality of advanced robotic systems.
