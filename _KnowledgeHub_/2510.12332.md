# Shape-Aware Whole-Body Control for Continuum Robots with Application in Endoluminal Surgical Robotics

> [!summary] Summary
> The framework developed by researchers at the University of Edinburgh and Playground Robotics integrates physics-informed modeling with neural residuals, a shape-aware whole-body Model Predictive Path Integral controller, and a task manager for continuum robots. This system reduced wall contacts by 69% and increased target access success rates from 67% to 100% in bronchoscopy phantom navigation compared to joystick-only control.

## Problem

- Controlling continuum robots is challenging due to their highly nonlinear and high-dimensional mechanics, hindering accurate shape estimation and robust control.
- Existing continuum robot control strategies often focus solely on tip tracking, which can lead to wall contact or inability to reach distal targets in constrained environments like endoluminal surgery.
- Physics-based models can be computationally intensive and degrade under unmodeled effects, while purely data-driven methods lack safety guarantees and generalization crucial for medical applications.

## Method

- Developed a Physics-informed Backbone Modeling approach that combines Cosserat rod theory with an Augmented Neural ODE (ANODE) to accurately capture full-body deformation and learn unmodeled dynamics.
- Designed a ==Model Predictive Path Integral== (MPPI) controller that performs shape-aware whole-body control, simultaneously optimizing tip tracking, full-body shape regulation, and dynamic obstacle avoidance.
- Implemented a high-level Task Manager module enabling real-time adjustment of control objectives, allowing operators to prioritize behaviors like wall clearance or direct advancement in tele-operated settings.

## Results

- Simulation studies showed millimeter-level accuracy in trajectory tracking (e.g., **5.2** mm RMSE for helical paths) and successful dynamic obstacle avoidance while maintaining shape control.
- Real-robot experiments in a bronchoscopy phantom reduced wall contacts by **69%** (from **7.4** to **2.3** per trial) and increased success rates in accessing distal targets from **67%** to **100%** compared to joystick-only control.
- The task manager module allowed operators to further reduce wall contacts by **30%** or decrease task completion times by approximately **10%** by dynamically adjusting control objective priorities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining a physics-informed model with neural residuals offers a balanced approach, providing both structural interpretability and adaptability to real-world complexities without extensive offline training.
> - A whole-body control strategy that explicitly considers full robot shape and dynamic obstacle avoidance significantly enhances safety and efficacy in navigating constrained, sensitive environments.
> - Dynamically adjustable control objectives via a task manager improve shared control capabilities and operator efficiency by allowing real-time prioritization of specific robot behaviors.
