# Shape-Aware Whole-Body Control for Continuum Robots with Application in Endoluminal Surgical Robotics

> [!summary] Summary
> The framework developed by researchers at the University of Edinburgh and Playground Robotics integrates physics-informed modeling with neural residuals, a shape-aware whole-body Model Predictive Path Integral controller, and a task manager for continuum robots. This system reduced wall contacts by 69% and increased target access success rates from 67% to 100% in bronchoscopy phantom navigation compared to joystick-only control.

## Problem

- Controlling continuum robots is challenging due to their highly nonlinear and high-dimensional mechanics, hindering accurate shape estimation and robust control.
- Existing continuum robot control strategies often focus solely on tip tracking, which can lead to wall contact or inability to reach distal targets in constrained environments like endoluminal surgery.
- Physics-based models can be computationally intensive and degrade under unmodeled effects, while purely data-driven methods lack safety guarantees and generalization crucial for medical applications.

## Method

- Developed a Physics-informed Backbone Modeling approach that combines Cosserat rod theory with an Augmented Neural ODE (ANODE) to accurately capture full-body deformation and learn unmodeled dynamics.
- Designed a ==Model Predictive Path Integral== (MPPI) controller that performs shape-aware whole-body control, simultaneously optimizing tip tracking, full-body shape regulation, and dynamic obstacle avoidance.
- Implemented a high-level Task Manager module enabling real-time adjustment of control objectives, allowing operators to prioritize behaviors like wall clearance or direct advancement in tele-operated settings.

## Results

- Simulation studies showed millimeter-level accuracy in trajectory tracking (e.g., **5.2** mm RMSE for helical paths) and successful dynamic obstacle avoidance while maintaining shape control.
- Real-robot experiments in a bronchoscopy phantom reduced wall contacts by **69%** (from **7.4** to **2.3** per trial) and increased success rates in accessing distal targets from **67%** to **100%** compared to joystick-only control.
- The task manager module allowed operators to further reduce wall contacts by **30%** or decrease task completion times by approximately **10%** by dynamically adjusting control objective priorities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining a physics-informed model with neural residuals offers a balanced approach, providing both structural interpretability and adaptability to real-world complexities without extensive offline training.
> - A whole-body control strategy that explicitly considers full robot shape and dynamic obstacle avoidance significantly enhances safety and efficacy in navigating constrained, sensitive environments.
> - Dynamically adjustable control objectives via a task manager improve shared control capabilities and operator efficiency by allowing real-time prioritization of specific robot behaviors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The control of continuum robots, characterized by their compliant materials and ability to navigate tortuous environments, presents a significant challenge in robotics. These robots are particularly suited for applications such as endoluminal surgery, search and rescue, and hazardous environment exploration due to their inherent adaptability to constrained spaces. However, their highly nonlinear, high-dimensional mechanics make accurate shape estimation and robust control difficult.

Current research in continuum robot control largely falls into two paradigms: physics-based modeling and data-driven learning. Physics-based methods, such as Piecewise Constant Curvature (PCC) and Cosserat rod models, offer interpretability but can be computationally intensive and susceptible to accuracy degradation under external interactions or unmodeled effects. Data-driven approaches, including reinforcement learning, aim to circumvent explicit modeling but often suffer from extensive data requirements, poor generalization, and a lack of safety guarantees, which are critical in sensitive applications like surgery. Hybrid strategies, which combine the strengths of both, are increasingly being explored to balance fidelity, tractability, and adaptability. Recent advancements also include online Jacobian error compensation, adaptive controllers for interaction, and spectral submanifold reduction for real-time model predictive control.

For control, while classical models provide abstractions, they struggle with full-body nonlinearities. Data-driven methods have shown promise but typically emphasize tip tracking and lack flexibility once deployed. Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control has emerged as an alternative, offering a sampling-based approach that handles nonlinear dynamics, requires no offline training, and provides transparency through an explicit cost function. A key advantage of MPPI is its ability to dynamically reweight objectives, offering adaptability in tele-operated scenarios.

This work addresses the limitations of existing approaches by proposing a unified framework that integrates a physics-informed backbone model with residual learning, combined with a shape-aware whole-body MPPI controller and a task manager for real-time objective adjustment. This approach aims to provide the interpretability and safety of physics-based models while incorporating the adaptability of learning-based methods, moving beyond conventional tip-centric control to enable precise whole-body coordination and obstacle avoidance, particularly in constrained surgical environments. It builds on the growing trend of hybrid modeling and leverages the flexibility of MPPI to achieve both accuracy and adaptability without extensive offline training.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the demanding requirements of endoluminal surgical procedures, such as bronchoscopy. In these applications, continuum robots must navigate through patient-specific, tortuous anatomical pathways. Conventional control strategies, which often focus solely on the robot's tip, are frequently insufficient. They can lead to unintended consequences such as wall contact, resulting in tissue trauma, or an inability to reach distal targets due compromising the safety or efficacy of the procedure. While the inherent compliance of continuum robots makes them suitable for conforming to complex anatomies and tolerating contact, this very compliance also introduces significant challenges for accurate shape modeling and control due to their nonlinear and high-dimensional mechanics. Reliable whole-body coordination in such constrained and delicate settings remains a significant hurdle.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the presented work are multi-faceted:

1. **Physics-informed Backbone Modeling:** To develop a backbone model that accurately captures the full-body deformation of the continuum robot. This involves integrating a structured, physics-based model (Cosserat rod theory) with learnable residuals (via an Augmented Neural ODE) to account for unmodeled dynamics, thereby achieving a representation that is both interpretable and adaptable. A critical goal is to provide access to the entire backbone configuration, which is necessary for whole-body control, unlike tip-only models.
2. **Shape-aware Whole-Body Control:** To design a control framework that simultaneously optimizes multiple objectives crucial for safe and effective navigation. Specifically, this includes precise tip tracking, regulation of the entire backbone shape, and dynamic obstacle avoidance, all while respecting the physical constraints of the robot's actuation system. The aim is to move beyond tip-centric control to enable comprehensive spatial regulation.
3. **Task Manager for Online Adaptation:** To create a high-level supervisory module that allows for real-time adjustment of control objectives. This module is intended to provide operators with the flexibility to prioritize different behaviors, such as wall clearance, directional bias, or selective segment freedom, in tele-operated settings, thereby enhancing the robot's adaptability to changing task demands and user intent.
4. **Open-Source Framework:** To make the developed framework publicly available, providing a reproducible platform for further research and development in continuum robot modeling and control.

Collectively, these objectives aim to advance continuum robot control beyond tip-centric methods, particularly for medical navigation, but also for other applications in confined and safety-critical environments. The overarching goal is to increase safety, reliability, and operator efficiency in minimally invasive endoluminal surgery.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework for shape-aware whole-body control of continuum robots consists of three interconnected modules: Physics-informed Backbone Modeling, Whole-Body Controller, and Task Manager.

**A. Physics-informed Backbone Modeling:**
The foundation of the backbone modeling is the Cosserat rod model, which describes the kinematics of a continuously deformable curve. The pose of a local frame along the robot's centerline is represented by a homogeneous transformation `T(s,t)`, where `s` is the arc-length and `t` is time. The evolution of this frame is governed by Cosserat rod equations, which relate the local body twist (encoding curvature) to changes along the arc-length. For multi-segment robots, segment continuity is maintained by propagating terminal states.

To address discrepancies between the nominal Cosserat model and real-world behavior (due to unmodeled effects like material nonlinearities, friction, or actuation imperfections), an Augmented Neural ODE (ANODE) framework is introduced. The nominal backbone evolution `p'(s,t) = f(p, u(t))` is augmented with a neural correction term `f_θ(p(t), u(t), t)`, parameterized as a time-dependent Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP). The full backbone configuration is then reconstructed by numerically integrating the augmented ODE along the arc length for each segment.

The neural network `f_θ` is trained to minimize the mismatch between its predicted states and ground-truth backbone points distributed along the entire arc length, rather than solely at the tip. This distributed loss function facilitates learning residual errors across the full deformation profile. To ensure consistent integration across samples with varying effective backbone lengths, a batch-wise calculation strategy is employed, where integration is performed up to the maximum length in the batch, and solutions for shorter robots are masked beyond their true length. This batch formulation also allows for efficient online Jacobian computation using central differences, accelerating gradient evaluation.

**B. Whole-Body Control:**
The control objective is formulated as a shape-aware whole-body problem, aiming to guide the robot's tip to a desired reference while simultaneously regulating its global backbone deformation. The robot's state `x` is represented by concatenating the coordinates of multiple equidistant points along the backbone, and the control input `q` is the actuation vector. The robot's dynamics are approximated by a first-order model `ẋ = J(q) q̇`, where the Jacobian `J(q)` is estimated online using batch central differences from the augmented backbone model (Eq. 6).

The control problem is solved using the Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) framework, a sampling-based variant of Model Predictive Control (MPC). MPPI optimizes a cost functional `J` over a finite prediction horizon `T`. This cost function includes several weighted terms:
- **Tip tracking error:** `w_tip ||x_tip - x_ref||^2`
- **Shape regulation:** `w_shape ||x - x_ref||^2`
- **Control smoothness:** `w_u ||q - q_prev||^2`
- **Terminal performance:** `w_term ||x_tip_T - x_ref_T||^2`
Additionally, obstacle avoidance `C_obs(x)` is incorporated by penalizing proximity between any backbone point and predefined obstacle surfaces using a barrier-like penalty function.

MPPI works by generating `K` candidate control sequences, predicting their trajectories using the approximate dynamics, and assigning importance weights based on the exponential transformation of the total cost. The optimal control input is then derived as a weighted average of these candidates. Actuation constraints (`q_min ≤ q ≤ q_max`) are enforced by clamping sampled inputs, ensuring physical realizability. This formulation enables the controller to be sensitive to both tip accuracy and full-body deformation while operating efficiently.

**C. Task Manager:**
A high-level Task Manager module is implemented to provide an intuitive interface for users to dynamically adjust control objectives based on their intent or contextual constraints. It modulates the relative importance of different terms in the MPPI cost function (Eq. 9-10) by tuning the weights (`w_tip`, `w_shape`, `w_u`, `w_term`) and actuation constraint thresholds (`q_min`, `q_max`) in real time.

This module enables "affordance-based behavior." For instance, an operator can prioritize wall clearance by increasing the corresponding penalty weight or enable greater freedom for a specific segment while stiffening others. Functionally, it operates as a policy layer that translates user commands or task descriptors into specific configurations of these adjustable parameters, enhancing the versatility and shared-control capabilities of the framework.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

**A. Simulation Studies:**
Extensive simulation studies were conducted to assess the framework's performance across various scenarios, using a cable-driven continuum robot with three segments.

1. **Trajectory Tracking:** The robot was tasked with following five different 3D paths (circular, curvy-edge circular, butterfly, elliptical, helical). Across 50 randomized trials per shape, the framework demonstrated consistent accuracy. Mean errors typically ranged from 2–3 mm, with RMSE values generally between 5–8 mm. The helical trajectory yielded the lowest RMSE (0.0052 ± 0.0007 m), attributed to its smooth curvature, while the butterfly trajectory, with its abrupt curvature changes, presented the most challenge (RMSE = 0.0078 ± 0.0005 m). This indicated millimeter-level tracking precision, with performance degradation observed primarily in trajectories requiring rapid curvature variations.
2. **Dynamic Obstacle Avoidance:** The robot followed a circular path while avoiding two oscillating obstacles. The controller successfully coordinated whole-body motion to maintain safe clearance. Tracking error remained in the millimeter range, with only minor spikes during avoidance maneuvers. The minimum distance to obstacles consistently stayed above the 0.02 m safety threshold. Control effort increased as the robot reshaped its body to ensure clearance, demonstrating effective dynamic adaptation of body shape while maintaining trajectory.
3. **Shape-constrained Reaching:** In this task, the robot was required to reach a target position while conforming its body to a desired reference shape. Over 50 trials, the controller simultaneously achieved both objectives. The final average tip error was 0.0084 ± 0.0024 m, and the backbone shape deviation was 0.0051 ± 0.0007 m. Settling times were approximately 1.9 seconds for the tip and 2.2 seconds for the shape, indicating effective balance between endpoint accuracy and global shape coordination.
4. **Tele-operated Shape Tracking:** Over 10 trials, the controller maintained consistent tracking performance under varying operator command complexity. The average final tip error was approximately 0.0097 m, and the shape deviation was 0.0060 m, demonstrating millimeter-level accuracy during tele-operation. An efficiency metric, inversely related to the product of final error and command complexity, revealed that performance varied significantly with the complexity of operator commands, with smoother motions yielding higher efficiency.

**B. Real-Robot Experiments:**
The framework was validated on a bronchoscopy phantom using a tendon-driven continuum robot with a tip-mounted camera, tele-operated via a joystick.

- **Comparison to Joystick-only Navigation:** Experiments involved navigating to 18 subsegmental pulmonary regions, with each region attempted using both conventional joystick-only control and the proposed framework.
 - **Wall Contacts:** Framework-assisted navigation reduced wall contacts to 2.3 ± 1.1 per trial, compared to 7.4 ± 2.0 with joystick-only navigation.
 - **Depth of Access/Success Rate:** The framework enabled successful access to all 18 targets (100%), including distal regions. Joystick-only control reached only 12 of 18 targets (67%).
 - **Task Completion Time:** Framework-assisted trials averaged 54 ± 6 seconds, a 12% speed-up compared to 61 ± 8 seconds for joystick-only operation (p < 0.05).
- **Task Manager Adaptability:** The task manager allowed operators to dynamically adjust behavior. Prioritizing wall clearance further reduced contacts by 30% (at the expense of slightly longer completion times), while prioritizing direct advancement shortened procedures by about 10% (with a modest increase in contacts).

**C. Comparison Study:**
The framework was compared against end-to-end models, Neural-ODE-based approaches, and PD-control, specifically against a method from that explicitly accounts for backbone shape.

- **Butterfly Trajectory Tracking:** All methods achieved broadly similar tip tracking accuracy, with and the proposed framework explicitly regulating backbone configuration. The proposed framework's integration of dynamic obstacle handling and a task manager offered advantages in tele-operated scenarios.
- **Shape-constrained Reaching:** While a re-trained version of (with added shape term) achieved faster settling times, it required extensive offline training data and parameter tuning. In contrast, the proposed framework achieved comparable accuracy without any offline training and maintained online adaptability via the task manager. This highlights the framework's efficiency and flexibility without substantial data requirements.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work introduces a unified framework for shape-aware whole-body control of continuum robots that addresses several critical limitations in the field, particularly for applications in constrained and safety-critical environments.

The integration of physics-informed backbone modeling with neural residuals provides an accurate and adaptable shape estimation capability, which is crucial for controlling the entire robot body. By augmenting the Cosserat rod model with a neural network, the framework leverages the interpretability and structural properties of physics-based models while compensating for real-world unmodeled dynamics without the need for extensive calibration or rigid assumptions.

The Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) controller, coupled with this detailed backbone representation, represents a significant advancement by enabling simultaneous optimization of tip tracking, full-body shape regulation, and dynamic obstacle avoidance. This moves beyond conventional tip-centric control paradigms, offering a more comprehensive approach to spatial control. The MPPI's sampling-based nature and its capacity for real-time objective reweighting provide a degree of adaptability not commonly found in purely data-driven or rigid model-based controllers.

The inclusion of a task manager further enhances the framework's utility by allowing operators to dynamically adjust control objectives based on evolving task demands or subjective preferences during tele-operation. This feature contributes to improved shared control capabilities, where the system intelligently assists the operator by prioritizing relevant behaviors such as wall clearance or direct advancement.

The simulation results, demonstrating millimeter-level accuracy across diverse and challenging trajectories, dynamic obstacle avoidance, and shape-constrained reaching, establish the framework's robust performance. Crucially, the real-robot experiments in a bronchoscopy phantom validate these capabilities in a clinically relevant scenario. The reported reductions in wall contact frequency (from 7.4 to 2.3 per trial), increased access to distal anatomical regions (from 67% to 100% success rate), and improved task completion times (12% speed-up) compared to joystick-only navigation and existing baselines suggest a tangible improvement in safety, reliability, and operator efficiency.

The significance extends beyond endoluminal surgical robotics. The principles of shape-aware whole-body control, combined with adaptable objective management, are broadly applicable to other domains involving confined space navigation, such as industrial inspection, search and rescue operations in debris-filled environments, or exploration of hazardous areas. The provision of an open-source framework is also a notable contribution, fostering reproducibility and potentially accelerating future research and development in continuum robotics. This work contributes to making continuum robots more autonomous, safer, and more effective tools for complex real-world tasks.
