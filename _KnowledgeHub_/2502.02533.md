# Multi-Agent Design: Optimizing Agents with Better Prompts and Topologies

> [!summary] Summary
> MASS (Multi-Agent System Search) automates the design of LLM multi-agent systems by interleaving prompt and topology optimization in three stages: block-level prompt tuning, influence-pruned workflow topology search, then workflow-level prompt tuning. It beats hand-built multi-agent systems and the automated designers ADAS and AFlow, reaching 78.8% average accuracy on Gemini 1.5 Pro.

## Problem

- Multi-agent systems rely on handcrafted prompts, and prompt sensitivity compounds when sensitive agents are cascaded. Applying automatic prompt optimization to them is hard because intermediate outputs have no ground truth.
- Prior automated designers such as ADAS and AFlow search topologies but neglect prompt optimization, and it was unclear which design factors (prompts, agent count, topology type) actually drive performance.
- The joint space of prompts and topologies is combinatorial, and most topology building blocks give little or negative gain, so unpruned search wastes budget on unhelpful components.

## Method

- Stage 1, ==block-level prompt optimization==: warm up a single predictor with instruction and ==exemplar optimization==, then optimize each ==topology block== (aggregate, reflect, debate, summarize, tool-use) at minimum agent count.
- Stage 2, ==workflow topology optimization==: compute each block's ==incremental influence==, convert it to selection probabilities with a softmax, prune weak dimensions by ==rejection sampling==, and search configurations in a fixed rule-based order under an agent budget.
- Stage 3, ==workflow-level prompt optimization==: re-optimize prompts jointly on the best-found topology to model dependence between agents. Stages 1 and 2 can run in parallel.

## Results

- Averaged over 8 tasks on Gemini 1.5 Pro, MASS scores **78.8%**, versus **63.5%** for the chain-of-thought start. On Gemini 1.5 Flash it scores **74.3%**. It also beats ADAS and AFlow, with consistent gains on Claude 3.5 Sonnet and Mistral Nemo.
- Stage ablation: average accuracy rises from **68.0%** for single-agent prompt optimization to **74.2%** after block-level tuning (about **6 points**), **77.3%** after topology search (about **3**), and **78.8%** after workflow-level tuning (about **2**).
- Topology analysis: on HotpotQA only debate helps (**+3%**), while other blocks give no gain or degrade. Removing pruning or the stage-1 prompt warm-up hurts search. Prompt-optimized agents also beat scaled self-consistency, reflection and debate at equal token cost.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimize individual agents' prompts before scaling or composing them. Better prompts are more token-efficient than adding more default-prompted agents, which saturate early.
> - Only a small fraction of topologies help, so measuring each block's marginal influence and pruning the search space matters as much as the search algorithm itself.
> - Interleave optimization from local to global, and tune prompts again at the workflow level to capture agent interdependence. This decomposition makes combinatorial multi-agent design tractable and parallelizable.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large language models (LLMs) has revolutionized AI, enabling sophisticated capabilities in understanding, reasoning, and generation. A significant evolution in this landscape is the move from single-turn LLM interactions to complex **agentic systems**, where LLMs autonomously perform multi-step tasks, interact with environments, and utilize tools. Multi-agent systems (MAS), which orchestrate multiple LLM-based agents, represent a further advancement, often outperforming single agents by leveraging diverse perspectives and collaborative reasoning (e.g., debate, self-reflection, aggregation).

However, designing effective MAS is profoundly challenging. This paper identifies two primary complexities:
1. **Prompt Sensitivity**: Individual LLM agents are highly sensitive to their prompts, meaning minor changes can drastically alter performance. This sensitivity is amplified in MAS where agents are cascaded, leading to compounding errors.
2. **Combinatorial Complexity of Design Space**: The design of MAS involves not only crafting effective prompts for each agent but also determining the optimal **topology** (how agents interact and are orchestrated) and the types/quantities of agents. This creates a vast, combinatorial search space that is intractable for manual, trial-and-error design.

Existing research has attempted to automate various aspects of agentic design:
- **Prompt Optimization**: Works like DSPy focus on automating prompt engineering for single agents or pipelines by optimizing exemplars and instructions.
- **Topology Optimization**: Other approaches explore scaling agents (e.g., majority voting), or using LLM-based meta-agents (ADAS) or search algorithms like Monte-Carlo Tree Search (AFlow) to find better interaction topologies.

The critical gap identified by Zhou et al. is the **lack of understanding and optimization of the *interplay* between prompt design and topology design within MAS.** Most prior works treat these as separate concerns, or only optimize one while keeping the other fixed (often to handcrafted defaults). This paper positions itself at the intersection of prompt engineering and neural architecture search (NAS), drawing parallels to the evolution of NAS where search space design proved as critical as search methods. By addressing the joint optimization of prompts and topologies, this work aims to provide a more holistic and effective solution for automated MAS design, going beyond the limitations of single-focus approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objectives of this research are multi-fold:

1. **Conduct an In-Depth Analysis of the MAS Design Space**: The authors aim to systematically investigate the factors that contribute to effective MAS design. This involves understanding the relative importance of optimizing individual agent prompts versus orchestrating agents in various topologies.
2. **Propose an Efficient MAS Optimization Framework (Mass)**: Based on the insights from their analysis, the paper seeks to develop an automated framework that can efficiently explore the complex MAS design space. This framework should integrate the optimization of both agent prompts and interaction topologies in an interleaved manner.
3. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance**: The proposed Mass framework is intended to produce MAS designs that significantly outperform existing manual and automated baselines across a diverse range of challenging tasks, including reasoning, multi-hop understanding, and code generation.
4. **Derive Generalizable Design Principles**: By analyzing the MAS architectures discovered by Mass, the authors aim to extract and articulate clear guidelines and principles for building effective multi-agent systems, thereby contributing to the broader understanding and future development of LLM-based agents.

The motivation for these objectives stems directly from the observed challenges in designing MAS: the inherent complexity of prompt sensitivity and the combinatorial explosion of the design space. Manual experimentation is costly, time-consuming, and often yields sub-optimal results. Automated methods focusing on only one aspect (prompts *or* topologies) miss the crucial interactions between them. The authors are motivated to create a more principled, automated, and effective approach that can unlock the full potential of multi-agent LLM systems. They argue that a proper understanding and joint optimization of these two intertwined components are essential for advancing the field.

### 3. Methodology and Approach: Multi-Agent System Search (Mass)

The core contribution of this paper is the **Multi-Agent System Search (Mass)** framework, a novel three-stage optimization algorithm designed to efficiently navigate the complex MAS design space by interleaving prompt and topology optimization. The methodology is built upon initial empirical analyses that highlight the importance of prompts and the sparsity of effective topologies.

**Initial Design Space Analysis:**
The authors first analyze the influence of prompt design versus scaling agents with default prompts. They demonstrate that equipping agents with optimized, informative prompts and few-shot exemplars (via a state-of-the-art prompt optimizer like MIPRO) yields significantly higher token-effectiveness compared to simply scaling agents with methods like self-consistency or self-refine. This empirical observation forms the basis for prioritizing prompt optimization. Furthermore, they analyze various standard topologies (Aggregate, Reflect, Debate) and task-specific agents (Summarize, Tool-use), revealing that not all topologies are beneficial, and influential ones represent a small fraction of the search space.

**Mass Framework Stages (Illustrated in Figure 1 and Algorithm 1):**

Mass tackles the combinatorial complexity by breaking down the optimization into three interleaved stages, moving from local to global and from prompts to topologies:

1. **Block-level Prompt Optimization (1PO: Local Prompt Warm-up)**:
 - **Objective**: Ensure individual agent types (building blocks) are highly effective in their specific roles *before* being composed into a larger system.
 - **Approach**: For each predefined agentic module (e.g., Predictor, Reflector, Debator, Summarizer, Executor), Mass applies an automatic prompt optimizer (MIPRO in this case) on a validation set. This optimizes both the instructional text and few-shot demonstrations for each agent type in isolation or in its minimal functional block (e.g., two predictors + one debater for the debate topology).
 - **Rationale**: This "warm-up" stage prevents compounding errors from sub-optimally prompted agents later in the workflow. The validation performance of each optimized block is stored to quantify its "incremental influence."

2. **Workflow Topology Optimization (2TO)**:
 - **Objective**: Discover the most effective arrangement and connectivity of the pre-optimized agent blocks to form the overall MAS workflow.
 - **Approach**:
 - **Search Space Pruning**: Based on the "incremental influence" (performance gain) quantified in Stage 1, Mass prunes the topology search space. Topologies that showed little or negative influence are less likely to be sampled (using a Softmax-weighted rejection sampling based on influence scores). This focuses the search on promising configurations.
 - **Rule-Based Workflow Construction**: Valid configurations are sampled from the pruned space, and a predefined rule-based sequence ([summarize, reflect, debate, aggregate]) is used to arrange the flow of agents, reducing ordering complexity.
 - **Evaluation**: Each sampled workflow (composed of agents with their Stage 1 optimized prompts) is evaluated on the validation set.
 - **Rationale**: This stage efficiently explores the architectural space by composing already high-performing components and focusing on potentially beneficial structures, rather than exhaustively searching an enormous, inefficient space.

3. **Workflow-level Prompt Optimization (3PO: Global Prompt Refinement)**:
 - **Objective**: Fine-tune the prompts of all agents within the *best-performing topology* found in Stage 2, adapting them for optimal collective performance and interdependencies.
 - **Approach**: The prompt optimizer is applied again, but this time to the entire best-found MAS as an integrated entity. This allows for joint optimization of prompts, considering how agents interact within the specific workflow.
 - **Rationale**: While Stage 1 optimizes individual agents, Stage 3 ensures that prompts are synergistically tuned for the full MAS, accounting for subtle interdependencies that might emerge in the complete system.

**Building Blocks and Search Space:**
Mass uses a configurable design space that includes common agentic building blocks:
- **Aggregate**: Parallel agents with an aggregation step (e.g., majority vote, self-consistency).
- **Reflect**: Agents providing feedback for iterative self-refinement (e.g., self-refine, Reflexion).
- **Debate**: Multiple agents debating to reach a consensus (e.g., multi-agent debate).
- **Custom Agents**: Task-specific agents like "Summarize" for long contexts.
- **Tool-use**: Binary decision to integrate external tools (e.g., code execution, RAG retriever).
Each block is parameterized (e.g., number of parallel agents, rounds of reflection/debate).

The entire framework is designed to be **plug-and-play**, allowing for integration with arbitrary prompt optimizers and customizable topology spaces, making it highly flexible and adaptable to future advancements.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that the Mass framework consistently yields optimized multi-agent systems that achieve state-of-the-art performance across a diverse and challenging set of benchmarks.

**State-of-the-Art Performance:**
- **Superiority over Baselines**: Mass-optimized systems significantly outperform a spectrum of existing alternatives. On Gemini 1.5 Pro, Mass achieved an average performance of 78.8%, compared to 65.3% for standard Chain-of-Thought (CoT), 68.2% for Self-Consistency, 66.9% for Self-Refine, and 70.3% for Multi-Agent Debate. Even against state-of-the-art automated agent design baselines like ADAS (69.7%) and AFlow (variable, often lower on certain tasks), Mass showed substantial improvements. Similar gains were observed with Gemini 1.5 Flash, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, and Mistral Nemo-12B, demonstrating strong generalization across different LLM backbones.
- **Diverse Task Coverage**: Mass showed consistent improvements across various task categories:
 - **Reasoning**: MATH, DROP.
 - **Multi-hop Long-context Understanding**: HotpotQA, MuSiQue, 2WikiMultiHopQA.
 - **Coding**: MBPP, HumanEval, LiveCodeBench (test output prediction).
 - Notably, for tasks like MATH and DROP, Mass achieved over 84% and 90% accuracy/F1 respectively on Gemini 1.5 Pro, showcasing its ability to tackle complex problem-solving.

**Ablation Studies and Incremental Gains (Figure 5):**
The stage-by-stage ablation study provides clear evidence for the effectiveness of each component of the Mass framework:
- **Prompt Optimization is Key (1PO)**: Optimizing individual agents at the block-level (1PO) alone yielded an average 6% gain over single-agent Automatic Prompt Optimization (APO), highlighting the substantial benefit of properly optimized agents within MAS building blocks.
- **Topology Optimization Adds Value (2TO)**: Composing these optimized agents into influential topologies (2TO) further improved performance by another 3% on average. Crucially, this stage benefits significantly from both the prior prompt optimization and the search space pruning, as demonstrated by the performance degradation when these elements are removed.
- **Global Refinement (3PO)**: A final workflow-level prompt optimization (3PO) for the entire best-found MAS provided an additional ~2% gain, confirming the importance of fine-tuning prompts to account for interdependencies within the complete system.

**Cost-Effectiveness (Table 7 & Figure 9):**
- Mass demonstrated comparable training costs to other automatic agent design baselines (ADAS, AFlow) while delivering significantly higher performance.
- Its optimization trajectory showed steady improvement, contrasting with the higher variance or stagnation observed in baselines.
- Mass can generate a Pareto-front of optimized designs, offering stronger token-effectiveness (performance per token cost) than baselines, allowing for selection of more cost-efficient workflows.

**Mass-Found Architectures and Design Principles (Figure 7 & 8):**
By analyzing the optimized MAS discovered by Mass, the authors propose three key design principles:
1. **Optimize Individual Agents Properly**: Ensuring each agent (block-level) is well-prompted for its role is foundational before combining them into a system.
2. **Compose Influential Topologies**: Identifying and integrating beneficial interaction patterns (topologies) is crucial for enhancing MAS performance. The paper observed task-specific patterns, e.g., debate for multi-hop QA, aggregation for reasoning, and reflection with tool-use for coding.
3. **Model Interdependence**: Optimizing prompts at the workflow level for the entire system accounts for the complex interdependencies between agents, leading to further performance gains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Multi-Agent Design: Optimizing Agents with Better Prompts and Topologies" paper makes several significant contributions with substantial potential impact on the field of LLM-based agentic systems:

**1. Principled Automation of MAS Design:**
The most significant impact is the introduction of a systematic and automated framework (Mass) for designing complex multi-agent LLM systems. This moves beyond manual, heuristic-based design, which is often time-consuming, prone to errors, and yields sub-optimal results. Mass provides a robust methodology that can efficiently discover high-performing MAS architectures.

**2. Highlighting the Interplay of Prompts and Topologies:**
The paper rigorously demonstrates that both prompt design and topology orchestration are critical for MAS performance and, crucially, that their optimization should be interleaved. This challenges previous work that often focused on one aspect in isolation. By showing that prompt optimization is highly token-effective and that influential topologies are sparse, the authors guide future research towards more efficient and effective design strategies.

**3. State-of-the-Art Performance Across Diverse Tasks:**
The substantial performance gains achieved by Mass across a wide array of reasoning, long-context, and coding tasks validate its effectiveness. This indicates that Mass-optimized systems can tackle real-world complex problems with higher accuracy and reliability, broadening the applicability of LLM-based agents.

**4. Generalizability and Flexibility:**
Mass is designed as a plug-and-play meta-framework, agnostic to the specific prompt optimizers or topology search spaces used. This modularity means it can readily incorporate future advancements in either prompt engineering or architectural search algorithms, ensuring its relevance and continued effectiveness. Its successful application across different commercial and open-source LLM backbones further underscores its generalizability.

**5. Practical Design Principles:**
The distilled design principles—optimizing individual agents, composing influential topologies, and modeling interdependence—provide valuable, actionable guidelines for researchers and practitioners. These principles offer a shortcut for building more effective MAS, bypassing extensive trial-and-error.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerated LLM Application Development**: By automating MAS design, Mass can significantly reduce the time and expertise required to deploy complex LLM applications, making advanced agentic systems more accessible.
- **More Robust and Intelligent Agents**: The ability to optimize both agents and their interactions will lead to more robust, reliable, and "intelligent" multi-agent systems capable of tackling increasingly complex tasks.
- **New Avenues for Research**: The work opens new research directions, such as exploring more advanced search algorithms (e.g., Bayesian optimization) for MAS design, integrating feedback-based prompt optimizers, and investigating sparser or more dynamic communication topologies.
- **Resource Efficiency**: The focus on cost-effectiveness and the generation of Pareto-fronts mean that Mass can help develop high-performing MAS that are also computationally efficient, a crucial factor for large-scale deployment.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations: the current topology search space, while comprehensive, may not include all possible effective designs (e.g., highly sparse communication patterns). More advanced search algorithms could further improve sample efficiency. Integrating text-based feedback from error logs into prompt optimizers is also a promising future direction. Despite these, Mass serves as a robust foundation and a guiding framework for continued innovation in automated multi-agent system design.
