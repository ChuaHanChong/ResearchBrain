# ASCII Art Turns LLMs into VLA Controllers

> [!summary] Summary
> By converting visual observations into ASCII art, text-only large language models can effectively serve as Vision-Language-Action (VLA) controllers for robot manipulation. This approach enabled simulated LLMs to achieve competitive task success rates, with models like Qwen3-4B reaching 89.2% macro success, and demonstrated successful transfer to a physical robotic arm.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) systems heavily rely on Vision-Language Models (VLMs), which are computationally intensive and demand large, multimodal datasets.
- The high computational and data requirements of VLM-based controllers create significant barriers for VLA research and deployment.
- Existing VLM systems often suffer from opaque visual embeddings, hindering the interpretability and debuggability of robot decision-making processes.

## Method

- Convert RGB camera frames into fixed-size, color-aware **ASCII** art rasters, preserving spatial layout and task-relevant visual cues.
- Adapt text-only Large Language Models (LLMs) to serve as VLA controllers by feeding them natural language instructions and the generated ASCII observations.
- Employ an iterative DAgger learning pipeline, bootstrapping with A* expert demonstrations, to train the LLMs for closed-loop 2D manipulation and unreachability detection.

## Results

- Iterative DAgger training improved macro success scores on simulated test episodes, with Qwen3-4B achieving an increase from **30.8%** to **89.2%**.
- Text-only LLMs exhibited competitive performance with VLM backbones of similar scale, sometimes marginally outperforming them (e.g., Qwen3-8B LLM achieved **80.4%** reachable success compared to Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct's **79.0%**).
- The ASCII-as-vision pipeline enabled successful sim-to-real transfer, allowing controllers trained in simulation to perform multi-step manipulation tasks on a physical robotic arm.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - ASCII art provides a lightweight and interpretable modality bridge, allowing text-only LLMs to effectively perform Vision-Language-Action control.
> - Iterative Data Aggregation (DAgger) substantially enhances the robustness and performance of LLM-based VLA controllers beyond initial expert supervision.
> - Higher-capacity LLM backbones generally yield better effectiveness and safety in manipulation tasks, including improved task success and reduced collision rates.
