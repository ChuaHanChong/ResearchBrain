# ASCII Art Turns LLMs into VLA Controllers

> [!summary] Summary
> By converting visual observations into ASCII art, text-only large language models can effectively serve as Vision-Language-Action (VLA) controllers for robot manipulation. This approach enabled simulated LLMs to achieve competitive task success rates, with models like Qwen3-4B reaching 89.2% macro success, and demonstrated successful transfer to a physical robotic arm.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) systems heavily rely on Vision-Language Models (VLMs), which are computationally intensive and demand large, multimodal datasets.
- The high computational and data requirements of VLM-based controllers create significant barriers for VLA research and deployment.
- Existing VLM systems often suffer from opaque visual embeddings, hindering the interpretability and debuggability of robot decision-making processes.

## Method

- Convert RGB camera frames into fixed-size, color-aware **ASCII** art rasters, preserving spatial layout and task-relevant visual cues.
- Adapt text-only Large Language Models (LLMs) to serve as VLA controllers by feeding them natural language instructions and the generated ASCII observations.
- Employ an iterative DAgger learning pipeline, bootstrapping with A* expert demonstrations, to train the LLMs for closed-loop 2D manipulation and unreachability detection.

## Results

- Iterative DAgger training improved macro success scores on simulated test episodes, with Qwen3-4B achieving an increase from **30.8%** to **89.2%**.
- Text-only LLMs exhibited competitive performance with VLM backbones of similar scale, sometimes marginally outperforming them (e.g., Qwen3-8B LLM achieved **80.4%** reachable success compared to Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct's **79.0%**).
- The ASCII-as-vision pipeline enabled successful sim-to-real transfer, allowing controllers trained in simulation to perform multi-step manipulation tasks on a physical robotic arm.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - ASCII art provides a lightweight and interpretable modality bridge, allowing text-only LLMs to effectively perform Vision-Language-Action control.
> - Iterative Data Aggregation (DAgger) substantially enhances the robustness and performance of LLM-based VLA controllers beyond initial expert supervision.
> - Higher-capacity LLM backbones generally yield better effectiveness and safety in manipulation tasks, including improved task success and reduced collision rates.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robot control, specifically addressing challenges related to computational resource intensity and data requirements often associated with current VLA systems.

**Current VLA Landscape:** Most contemporary VLA systems are built upon Vision-Language Models (VLMs), which are multimodal backbones that interpret visual observations conditioned on natural language and are further trained with action supervision. Notable examples include RT-1, RT-2, PaLM-E, OpenVLA, and Octo, which demonstrate the benefits of large-scale multimodal pretraining and imitation learning on diverse robot datasets. These approaches often involve substantial human teleoperation for data collection or rely on simulations that face sim-to-real discrepancies. Furthermore, the training and deployment of VLM-based controllers are typically compute-intensive, posing a barrier to entry for researchers.

**Alternative Approaches:** A complementary line of research utilizes Large Language Models (LLMs) as high-level planners or program synthesizers that invoke external perception and control tools, rather than directly learning end-to-end visuomotor policies. Examples include Code-as-Policies and VoxPoser, which leverage LLMs for high-level reasoning and tool orchestration. Other approaches simplify visual complexity by converting observations into structured or symbolic representations, such as 3D scene graphs in SayPlan or GRID, which aim to facilitate planning and grounding for LLMs.

**This Work's Contribution:** This research introduces a method that bridges the gap between text-only LLMs and visual control by converting visual observations into an ASCII art representation. This approach differentiates itself by enabling text-only LLMs to function as VLA-style controllers without requiring direct image inputs or a multimodal backbone. It focuses on a closed-loop policy interface where the LLM directly maps instruction and ASCII observation to a domain-specific action. This contrasts with tool-using LLM agents by embedding perception and action directly within the LLM's input/output structure. It also differs from other symbolic visual representation methods by using a deterministic, fixed-size, color-aware ASCII raster that preserves spatial layout, rather than extracting higher-level scene graphs or symbolic states. The paper suggests that ASCII rendering serves as a lightweight and interpretable modality bridge, potentially lowering the computational and data barriers to VLA research and deployment, while complementing existing VLA pipelines.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate whether a text-only Large Language Model (LLM) can be effectively adapted into a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) controller by converting visual observations into an ASCII representation. This core objective is driven by several motivations related to the current state and challenges of VLA model development:

- **Addressing Compute and Data Intensity of VLMs:** Current VLA systems predominantly rely on Vision-Language Models (VLMs), which require multimodal backbones and extensive data collection (often through large-scale human teleoperation or simulations susceptible to sim-to-real gaps). Training and deploying these VLM-based controllers are compute-intensive, raising the barriers to entry for research and iteration. The motivation is to explore a VLA solution that circumvents these large data and compute requirements by utilizing text-only LLMs.

- **Leveraging Existing LLM Infrastructure:** The proposed ASCII-as-vision interface allows the use of established training and deployment stacks for text-only LLMs. This is motivated by the potential for more efficient conditioning on visual states, following natural-language instructions, and producing constrained, executable actions without the overhead of adapting or training a multimodal architecture.

- **Improving Interpretability and Debuggability:** ASCII art is a human-readable representation. This property is motivated by the desire to make observations and decision-making processes more transparent, which can facilitate logging and debugging compared to opaque visual embeddings.

- **Enabling VLA Research with Text-Only Backbones:** The research is motivated by the possibility of opening new directions for VLA research by demonstrating that text-only LLMs can perform VLA-style control, potentially leading to more accessible and resource-efficient approaches.

- **Evaluation of Reachability and Obstacle Avoidance:** A specific objective is to test whether the resulting controllers can identify task-relevant entities, plan feasible action sequences, and assess task reachability (e.g., recognizing when a task is physically blocked or undefined due to missing entities) in the presence of obstacles.

In summary, the key objective is to demonstrate the viability of using ASCII art as a modality bridge to enable text-only LLMs to act as VLA controllers for manipulation tasks, thereby reducing computational demands, enhancing interpretability, and expanding the scope of VLA research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology involves converting visual observations into ASCII art to enable text-only LLMs to function as VLA controllers for 2D manipulation tasks. This process includes problem formulation, a specific observation interface, a constrained action space, and a staged learning pipeline.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The research addresses a VLA control problem where an agent performs multi-step manipulation actions based on visual observations and natural-language instructions.
- **Environment:** A top-down 2D workspace featuring a gripper, movable objects (distinguished by color and shape), static obstacles, and colored target markers.
- **Tasks:** Natural-language instructions specify sequences of goals (e.g., visiting targets) and pick-and-place operations (e.g., moving specific objects to specific targets).
- **Inputs:** At each timestep, the agent receives a visual observation ($o_t$) and an instruction ($I$).
- **Outputs:** The agent produces an action ($a_t$).
- **Infeasibility Detection:** The agent is also required to declare task infeasibility with specific labels:
 - **BLOCKED:** Obstacles physically obstruct all paths for a necessary subgoal.
 - **NARROW:** A path exists geometrically for a point gripper, but not for the real gripper due to size constraints.
 - **UNDEFINED:** The instruction refers to a non-existent entity.
- **Termination:** Episodes conclude upon successful instruction completion, collision, agent-declared infeasibility, or reaching a step budget.

#### 3.2. ASCII-as-Vision Observation Interface

This interface converts RGB camera frames into fixed-size, colored ASCII rasters, which serve as text inputs for the LLM.
- **Encoder Design:** The encoder prioritizes preserving task-relevant visual cues (objects, targets, spatial layout) and produces a deterministic, human-readable representation. Background pixels are mapped to whitespace.
- **Resolution:** A 96×54 ASCII resolution is used.
- **Color-Aware Discretization:** A fixed character palette is used for color mapping and border delimiters, encoded in HSV color space.
- **Simulation Environment:** Procedurally generated using pygame, scenes feature randomized appearance and geometry (colors, object sizes, rotations) to enhance dataset diversity and encoder robustness.
- **Real-World Pre-processing:** For physical robot experiments, a fixed overhead RGB camera feed undergoes a deterministic pipeline:
 1. Camera calibration with a checkerboard.
 2. Workspace definition using AprilTags, followed by cropping and resampling to 1920×1080 pixels.
 3. Color-based mask extraction and denoising for task-relevant entities.
 4. Gripper parallax correction: The gripper's image-coordinates are remapped to the tabletop reference frame using a calibrated pixel-to-pixel mapping model, compensating for its closer proximity to the camera.
 5. The corrected composite image is then rendered into ASCII.

#### 3.3. Action Space and Output Constraints

The policy outputs actions in a constrained Domain-Specific Language (DSL):
- **Motion:** `MOVE(x,y)` where `(x,y)` are discrete grid coordinates (192×108 grid for a 1920×1080 pixel workspace).
- **Grasping:** `GRIP(PICK)` and `GRIP(DROP)`.
- **Unreachability Declaration:** `UNREACHABLE(BLOCKED|NARROW|UNDEFINED)`.
- **Constraint:** The model must output exactly one valid DSL action per step; malformed outputs are considered failures.

#### 3.4. Learning Pipeline

Training is structured in stages:
- **Stage 0 (Expert Demonstrations):** Initial supervised training data is generated by an A* motion planner, serving as an expert teacher with access to geometric ground truth. This teacher follows instructions, plans paths, and applies deterministic grasping rules to produce step-wise actions.
- **Stage 1 Onward (DAgger):** Iterative Data Aggregation (DAgger) is employed to address compounding errors. The current policy interacts with the environment, and the teacher provides corrective labels for the states encountered by the policy.
- **Model Adaptation:** Base LLMs are adapted using Lightweight Fine-tuning (LoRA).
- **Dataset Composition:** Reachable episodes are categorized into Simple and Complex difficulty levels. A fixed per-stage data mixture includes 8000 Simple-Reachable episodes and 2000 episodes for each of Complex-Reachable, Unreachable-Blocked, Unreachable-Narrow, and Unreachable-Undefined regimes. DAgger data constitutes 25% of the training mixture (50% for Gemma models due to observed instabilities).
- **Sequence Length:** A maximum sequence length of 4096 tokens accommodates all training examples.

#### 3.5. Models and Training

A variety of LLMs and VLMs across different families and scales are fine-tuned (e.g., Qwen3, Llama-3.2, Gemma). For VLMs, RGB observations are downsampled to 96×54 pixels to match the ASCII raster resolution, ensuring a controlled comparison of modality interfaces under similar information budgets.

#### 3.6. Metrics

Performance is evaluated using:
- **Reachable Scenes:** Task success rate (completing instruction without collisions or false `UNREACHABLE` declarations), collision rate, and average steps.
- **Unreachable Scenes:** Unreachability detection rate (outputting `UNREACHABLE(*)` before collision/timeout) and subtype accuracy (exact match of subtype).
- **Safety:** False-positive unreachability rate in reachable scenes.
- **Horizon:** Episodes failing to complete within 30 steps are counted as failures.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the efficacy of ASCII-as-vision for VLA controllers.

#### 4.1. Overview of Findings

1. **Stage-wise Improvement:** Iterative data aggregation with DAgger resulted in performance gains beyond the initial expert demonstration bootstrapping, improving closed-loop robustness for most models and scenarios.
2. **Model Capacity Benefits:** Higher-capacity LLM backbones generally exhibited improved effectiveness and safety, achieving higher success rates in reachable tasks while concurrently reducing collisions and false-positive unreachability predictions.
3. **LLM-VLM Competitiveness:** When utilizing the same ASCII observation interface and action DSL, text-only LLMs demonstrated competitive performance with VLM backbones. At comparable scales, VLMs were not consistently superior, with some stronger text-only models approaching or matching the performance of VL checkpoints on several metrics.

#### 4.2. Training Results

Figure 5 illustrates consistent stage-wise improvements in success rates from Stage 0 to Stage 20 across various models and scenarios in simulation. For instance, on test episodes, the Qwen3-4B model improved from a 30.8% macro success score (unweighted mean of five scenario success rates) at Stage 0 to 89.2% at Stage 20. Similarly, Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct improved from 48.8% to 91.4%, and Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct improved from 32.7% to 85.3%. These improvements align with the objective of staged aggregation, where early stages establish basic behaviors and later stages enhance robustness in learner-visited states. In unreachable regimes, success was defined by the policy correctly emitting `UNREACHABLE` with the correct subtype.

#### 4.3. Evaluation Results (Stage 20 Test Performance)

Table II summarizes the Stage 20 test performance.
- **Reachable Performance:** The Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct model achieved the highest reachable success rate at 83.6%, accompanied by a collision rate of 8.6%. Among the text-only LLMs, Qwen3-14B demonstrated 81.2% reachable success. Lower-capacity models generally exhibited higher collision rates and more false-positive `UNREACHABLE` predictions.
- **Unreachability Detection:** Most later-stage checkpoints achieved high unreachability detection rates, generally in the mid-to-high 90% range. Subtype accuracy for unreachable scenes also reached mid-90% to high-90% for many models.
- **LLM vs. VLM Comparison:** Within the Qwen3 family, the performance difference between text-only models and VLMs of similar scale was relatively minor. In some cases, text-only LLMs performed comparably or even slightly better; for example, Qwen3-8B (text-only) marginally outperformed Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct on reachable success (80.4% vs 79.0%) and had a lower reachable false-positive unreachability rate (5.4% vs 6.9%). This outcome supports the premise that ASCII-as-vision can serve as an alternative to native image embeddings for VLA tasks.
- **Gemma Model Performance:** The Gemma models, both text-only and image-enabled, showed significantly lower performance compared to Qwen3 and Llama-3.2, with reachable success rates of 1.2% and 7.0% respectively, along with higher collision rates and false-positive unreachability. This indicates model-specific optimization challenges.

#### 4.4. Real-World Experimental Results

The learned controllers were successfully deployed on a Flexiv Rizon4s robotic arm using the same ASCII-as-vision interface.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The system demonstrated transferability from simulation to physical hardware. This involved a deterministic pre-processing pipeline for real camera inputs, including calibration, workspace cropping, masking, denoising, and a crucial gripper-to-tabletop viewpoint correction to align observations with the simulator's frame.
- **Qualitative Observations:** An example pick-and-place rollout (Fig. 6) illustrated the policy's ability to execute multi-step manipulation sequences (moving, grasping, transporting, releasing).
- **Remaining Challenges:** The paper noted that remaining errors in real-world deployment were primarily attributable to real-world perception and execution uncertainties not present in the idealized simulator, such as lighting variations, residual alignment errors, and grasp variability. These observations suggest areas for future robustness improvements within the existing ASCII observation framework.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research demonstrates that a text-only Large Language Model (LLM) can be adapted into an effective Vision-Language-Action (VLA) controller by employing an ASCII art representation of visual observations. This approach has several significant implications and potential impacts on the field of robotics and VLA research:

- **Lowering Barriers to VLA Research:** The primary significance lies in showing that VLA control can be achieved without relying on complex, compute-intensive multimodal backbones or extensive multimodal pretraining data. By leveraging existing text-only LLMs and their mature training/deployment stacks, this work offers a potentially more accessible pathway for researchers to explore VLA systems, reducing the computational and data requirements that currently limit entry.

- **Increased Interpretability and Debuggability:** ASCII art provides a human-readable representation of visual states. This enhances the interpretability of the model's perception and decision-making process, facilitating debugging and understanding of policy behavior, which can be challenging with opaque deep learning visual embeddings.

- **Complementing Conventional VLA Pipelines:** The ASCII-as-vision interface is presented not as a replacement but as a complement to existing VLA pipelines. It offers an alternative modality bridge that can be integrated where lightweight, interpretable perception is beneficial, or where resources for full VLM deployment are constrained.

- **Opening New Research Directions for Text-Only Backbones:** The findings suggest that LLMs, originally designed for text processing, possess sufficient capabilities to perform closed-loop control and abstract reasoning (like reachability assessment) when visual information is appropriately textualized. This encourages further investigation into leveraging and adapting text-only LLMs for embodied AI tasks, potentially extending to more diverse sensing modalities beyond RGB, such as ASCII-encoded RGBD data, without requiring specialized multimodal architectures.

- **Demonstrated Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The successful deployment of ASCII-trained controllers on a physical robot arm, with a relatively straightforward deterministic pre-processing pipeline, indicates the practical viability of this approach for real-world applications. This suggests that the information preserved in the ASCII representation is sufficient for fundamental manipulation tasks in a controlled 2D environment.

- **Challenges and Future Impact:** While significant, the work also highlights limitations. ASCII rendering is an information bottleneck, which might restrict performance in highly cluttered scenes or tasks demanding fine geometric precision. The varying performance across different LLM families (e.g., Gemma models showing instability) suggests that model-specific optimization remains a challenge. Addressing these limitations, for example, through higher-resolution or multi-scale ASCII encodings, more sophisticated action primitives, or hybrid approaches combining learned policies with classical planners, could further extend the impact and robustness of this methodology. The focus on local geometric reasoning also indicates that globally optimal planning in high-dimensional spaces remains outside the current scope, pointing to avenues for future development.
