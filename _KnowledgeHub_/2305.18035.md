# Physics-Informed Computer Vision: A Review and Perspectives

> [!summary] Summary
> A systematic review establishes a unified taxonomy for Physics-Informed Computer Vision (PICV), categorizing how physical knowledge is integrated into computer vision pipelines and tasks. The analysis of over 250 papers demonstrates that incorporating physical priors consistently improves model performance, robustness, and data efficiency across a wide range of CV applications.

## Problem

- Conventional, purely data-driven computer vision (CV) models often suffer from reliance on massive datasets, lack of robustness, and poor interpretability.
- These models frequently produce physically implausible or inconsistent results due to neglecting fundamental physical laws governing real-world phenomena.
- The complex spatial and temporal characteristics of visual data require domain-specific physics knowledge that current CV approaches often fail to incorporate.

## Method

- Integrating physical information directly into computer vision models and pipelines to ensure solutions adhere to physical consistency and improve generalization.
- Employing diverse strategies, categorized as observational, inductive, and learning biases, to embed physical laws and constraints at different stages of the CV workflow.
- Leveraging various types of physics priors, including governing equations, multi-modal data, and physical properties, to augment learning processes with fundamental knowledge.

## Results

- Physics-informed approaches consistently lead to enhanced performance metrics, including improved accuracy, robustness, data efficiency, and generalization, while ensuring physical plausibility in model outputs.
- Quantitative improvements are observed across various CV tasks, such as a 16.4% PSNR increase in video denoising, a reduction in RMSE for cardiac activation times from 3.92 ms to 1.53 ms, and a decrease in MPJPE for 3D human pose estimation from 123.9 to 96.1.
- The review identifies six distinct categories of physics priors successfully leveraged in PICV applications spanning computational imaging, medical imaging, robotics, and fluid mechanics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The proposed unified taxonomy effectively categorizes the 'what' (physics knowledge), 'how' (representation), and 'where' (integration strategies) of physics information within PICV, bringing clarity to a fragmented field.
> - Physics incorporation strategies (observational, inductive, learning biases) are systematically mapped to distinct stages of the computer vision pipeline, from data acquisition to model training.
> - PICV methodologies foster cross-domain innovation, yielding robust, data-efficient, and generalizable models particularly advantageous in scenarios with sparse data, complex phenomena, or high accuracy requirements.
