# Physics-Informed Computer Vision: A Review and Perspectives

> [!summary] Summary
> A systematic review establishes a unified taxonomy for Physics-Informed Computer Vision (PICV), categorizing how physical knowledge is integrated into computer vision pipelines and tasks. The analysis of over 250 papers demonstrates that incorporating physical priors consistently improves model performance, robustness, and data efficiency across a wide range of CV applications.

## Problem

- Conventional, purely data-driven computer vision (CV) models often suffer from reliance on massive datasets, lack of robustness, and poor interpretability.
- These models frequently produce physically implausible or inconsistent results due to neglecting fundamental physical laws governing real-world phenomena.
- The complex spatial and temporal characteristics of visual data require domain-specific physics knowledge that current CV approaches often fail to incorporate.

## Method

- Integrating physical information directly into computer vision models and pipelines to ensure solutions adhere to physical consistency and improve generalization.
- Employing diverse strategies, categorized as observational, inductive, and learning biases, to embed physical laws and constraints at different stages of the CV workflow.
- Leveraging various types of physics priors, including governing equations, multi-modal data, and physical properties, to augment learning processes with fundamental knowledge.

## Results

- Physics-informed approaches consistently lead to enhanced performance metrics, including improved accuracy, robustness, data efficiency, and generalization, while ensuring physical plausibility in model outputs.
- Quantitative improvements are observed across various CV tasks, such as a 16.4% PSNR increase in video denoising, a reduction in RMSE for cardiac activation times from 3.92 ms to 1.53 ms, and a decrease in MPJPE for 3D human pose estimation from 123.9 to 96.1.
- The review identifies six distinct categories of physics priors successfully leveraged in PICV applications spanning computational imaging, medical imaging, robotics, and fluid mechanics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The proposed unified taxonomy effectively categorizes the 'what' (physics knowledge), 'how' (representation), and 'where' (integration strategies) of physics information within PICV, bringing clarity to a fragmented field.
> - Physics incorporation strategies (observational, inductive, learning biases) are systematically mapped to distinct stages of the computer vision pipeline, from data acquisition to model training.
> - PICV methodologies foster cross-domain innovation, yielding robust, data-efficient, and generalizable models particularly advantageous in scenarios with sparse data, complex phenomena, or high accuracy requirements.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of physical information into machine learning frameworks, broadly known as Physics-Informed Machine Learning (PIML), has rapidly expanded, transforming various application domains by augmenting learning processes with fundamental knowledge and governing physical laws. This paradigm ensures solutions adhere to physical consistency, accelerates training, and improves generalization, particularly with sparse data.

Within this broader landscape, numerous surveys have emerged, focusing on PIML applications in specific fields such as cyber-physical systems, hydrology, fluid mechanics, climate modeling, medical imaging, and crowd analysis. This paper distinguishes itself by providing a dedicated, comprehensive review specifically for **Physics-Informed Computer Vision (PICV)**. While some existing reviews touch upon physics-informed methods within particular computer vision sub-domains (e.g., medical imaging), this work offers a holistic perspective across the entire spectrum of computer vision tasks. It aims to unify the diverse approaches of integrating physical principles into visual data interpretation and understanding, a distinction crucial given the unique spatial and temporal characteristics of visual data compared to other data types (e.g., 1D signals). The authors explicitly state their work "extends these efforts by offering a comprehensive view, identifying established areas, and underscoring emerging opportunities within physics-informed computer vision (PICV)." By systematically analyzing over 250 papers, the review solidifies PICV as a distinct and rapidly growing research trend, as evidenced by an exponentially increasing number of publications in the last eight years.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this systematic review stems from the recognized limitations of conventional, purely data-driven computer vision (CV) models. Despite significant advancements in tasks like image classification and object detection, these models often suffer from reliance on massive datasets, a lack of robustness, poor interpretability, and a fundamental misalignment with the physical laws governing real-world phenomena. Real-world events, such as human motion or fluid dynamics, are inherently constrained by physical principles (e.g., Navier-Stokes equations, anatomical limits), which humans instinctively apply for effective environmental interaction. Current CV approaches often neglect these crucial physical priors, leading to physically implausible or inconsistent results.

The authors highlight the proven benefits of PIML, including enhanced solution relevance and efficiency, accelerated neural network training, improved generalization with less data, and adherence to physical laws. Applying these benefits to computer vision tasks, where visual data's complex spatial and temporal characteristics demand domain-specific physics knowledge, is the core impetus.

The key objectives of this paper are summarized as follows:
- **Develop a Unified Taxonomy:** To provide a structured framework for understanding what physics knowledge/processes are modeled, how they are represented, and the strategies employed to incorporate them into computer vision models.
- **Conduct an In-depth Review of CV Tasks:** To delve into a wide array of computer vision tasks—including imaging, super-resolution, generation, forecasting, reconstruction, classification, object detection, segmentation, and human analysis—and analyze how physics information is integrated into specific algorithms for each category.
- **Analyze Integration Mechanisms:** To detail the physical processes modeled as priors, the network architectures or augmentations utilized for physics integration, and the context and datasets employed within these tasks.
- **Identify Challenges and Future Directions:** To synthesize perspectives on the current challenges, open research questions, and promising avenues for future research in physics-informed computer vision. This includes discussing practical issues such as selecting appropriate physics priors and establishing standardized benchmarking platforms.

By addressing these objectives, the authors aim to demystify PICV, highlight its current state, and inspire future innovations that lead to more physically plausible, accurate, data-efficient, and generalizable computer vision models for increasingly realistic applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors conducted a **systematic literature review** encompassing "more than 250 papers" to comprehensively survey the field of Physics-Informed Computer Vision (PICV). The data for this review was "systematically collected from major academic databases, including IEEE Xplore, ACM Digital Library, and others, emphasizing peer-reviewed journals and conference proceedings," ensuring a high standard of academic rigor and relevance.

The core of their methodological approach involved:

1. **Decomposition of the Computer Vision Pipeline:** The popular computer vision pipeline was broken down into five distinct stages: data acquisition, pre-processing, model design (further divided into feature extraction and architecture customization), training, and inference. This structured decomposition allowed for a granular analysis of how physics information is integrated at each specific point within the typical CV workflow.

2. **Taxonomy of Physics Incorporation Strategies:** The authors introduced a unified taxonomy to categorize how physics priors are incorporated into machine learning models, specifically adapting it for computer vision. These strategies, referred to as "biases," include:
 - **Observational Bias:** Involves modifying the input data by directly feeding physical observations, simulated data, or extracted physical data to the model. This occurs primarily during the data acquisition stage.
 - **Learning Bias:** Enforces prior knowledge or physics information through soft penalty constraints, typically by augmenting the loss function with terms based on physical laws (e.g., momentum, conservation of mass). This is prominent during the model training stage.
 - **Inductive Bias:** Incorporates physics knowledge through custom neural network architectures that embody "hard" constraints, respecting fundamental conservation laws or physical structures (e.g., Hamiltonian Neural Networks, Lagrangian Neural Networks). This is applied during the model design stage.

3. **Categorization of Physics Prior Types:** They classified physics information based on its source and nature into six categories:
 - Governing equations and constraints (e.g., PDEs, conservation laws, physics models/simulations).
 - Mixed, historical, and multi-modal data.
 - Visual representations (e.g., time-frequency signals, flood maps).
 - Physical/statistical properties (e.g., Shannon entropy, speckle correlation).
 - Physical variables (e.g., porosity, noise parameters).
 - Hybrid approaches combining the above.

4. **Task-Specific Analysis:** The reviewed papers were then systematically analyzed across a wide range of computer vision tasks, grouped into five primary categories: Imaging Inverse Problems (Imaging, Super-resolution, Reconstruction), Image Generation, Predictive Modeling, Image Analysis (Classification, Segmentation), and Human Analysis. For each task, the review detailed:
 - How physics information is integrated into specific algorithms.
 - What physical processes are modeled and incorporated.
 - What network architectures or augmentations are utilized.
 - The context and datasets employed.

This multi-faceted approach allowed the authors to provide a comprehensive, structured, and insightful overview of the PICV landscape, identifying commonalities, unique contributions, and crucial gaps across diverse applications.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The core finding of this review is the successful establishment of a **unified taxonomy** for Physics-Informed Computer Vision (PICV), systematically categorizing how physics knowledge is incorporated into computer vision pipelines and tasks. This taxonomy provides a coherent framework for understanding the diverse and rapidly evolving field.

**Key aspects of the taxonomy and findings include:**

- **Strategies for Physics Incorporation:** The review meticulously categorizes physics integration into three main "biases":
 - **Observational Bias:** Physics information directly influences input data during **data acquisition**, through simulations, multi-modal data, or extracted physical insights (e.g., incorporating PSF in lensless imaging).
 - **Inductive Bias:** Physics is embedded as "hard" constraints within **model design**, via customized network architectures that inherently respect physical laws (e.g., Hamiltonian NNs).
 - **Learning Bias:** Physics acts as "soft" constraints, typically through augmented **loss functions** during model training, incorporating governing equations (e.g., PINNs embedding PDEs) or conservation laws.

- **Categories of Physics Priors:** The authors identify six distinct types of physics information leveraged in PICV:
 - **Governing Equations and Constraints:** Direct use of differential equations (PDEs/ODEs), conservation laws, or complete physics models (simulators) as priors.
 - **Mixed, Historical, and Multi-modal Data:** Combining observed data with physics-generated data or leveraging historical data to derive physical insights.
 - **Visual Representations:** Encoding physics into specialized visual data formats like time-frequency signals or flood maps.
 - **Physical/Statistical Properties:** Utilizing inherent physical or statistical characteristics, such as Shannon entropy or speckle correlation.
 - **Physical Variables:** Incorporating physically relevant parameters either as additional network inputs or as components in the loss function.
 - **Hybrid Approaches:** Combinations of the above, often pairing simulated data with physics-informed loss functions.

- **Integration Points Across the CV Pipeline:** The review maps these incorporation strategies to different stages of the standard computer vision pipeline:
 - **Data Acquisition:** Primarily driven by observational bias, feeding physics-aware data directly.
 - **Pre-processing:** Learning bias is heavily applied here, using physics-guided methods for tasks like super-resolution or image synthesis.
 - **Model Design:** Inductive bias is key, with custom architectures encoding physical principles.
 - **Model Training:** Learning bias is predominant, integrating physics-based loss components.
 - **Inference:** While no new physics information is induced, the trained physics-informed models are deployed for physically consistent predictions.

- **Applications and Performance Enhancements:** The review details the widespread application of PICV across diverse domains, including computational imaging, medical imaging, robotics, surveillance, remote sensing, weather modeling, and fluid mechanics. A quantitative analysis demonstrates the significant performance enhancements achieved through physics integration:
 - **Imaging:** Robust generalization in lensless imaging, substantial PSNR and SSIM improvements in video denoising (e.g., 16.4% PSNR increase over FastDVDnet).
 - **Generation:** Improved IoU (0.553) and lower LPIPS (0.263) for flood visualization, superior semantic inpainting.
 - **Predictive Modeling:** Significant reduction in RMSE for cardiac activation times (e.g., 3.92 ms to 1.53 ms), high accuracy in EP parameter estimation.
 - **Classification:** High accuracy (98.7% for fault diagnosis, 85.38% for motor imagery classification with a 4.71% improvement).
 - **Segmentation:** Enhanced IoU for solar bursts (0.134-0.146 to 0.229-0.312), improved Dice scores for MRI brain segmentation.
 - **Human Analysis:** Reduced MPJPE in 3D human pose estimation (e.g., 123.9 to 96.1), and substantial reductions in other motion errors, leading to improved pose accuracy and realism.

- **Scenarios for PICV Adoption:** PICV approaches are particularly advantageous when data is sparse or limited, for complex physical phenomena, in applications requiring high accuracy and reliability, when data acquisition costs are high, and in environmental modeling and prediction tasks.

- **Cross-Domain Synergies:** The authors highlight that PICV methodologies foster cross-domain innovation, with shared insights and methods benefiting fields from medical imaging to fluid mechanics, leading to more robust, accurate, and efficient models.

Overall, the review comprehensively maps the current state of PICV, showcasing its systematic integration of physics to overcome limitations of purely data-driven models and achieve superior performance and physical consistency across a wide range of computer vision tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This systematic review holds significant value and potential impact for the scientific community, particularly in the fields of computer vision, machine learning, and scientific computing.

**Key Significance:**

- **Unified Framework:** The most substantial contribution is the proposed unified taxonomy, which clearly categorizes the "what," "how," and "where" of physics information integration into computer vision models and pipelines. This framework provides much-needed clarity and organization to a rapidly expanding, yet fragmented, research area. It allows researchers to better understand existing approaches, identify common patterns, and strategically explore new avenues.
- **Comprehensive Landscape Mapping:** By reviewing over 250 papers, the authors have meticulously mapped the current state-of-the-art in PICV, highlighting successful applications across a broad spectrum of computer vision tasks and scientific domains. This comprehensive overview serves as an invaluable resource for both newcomers and seasoned researchers.
- **Demonstrating Performance Benefits:** The quantitative analysis compellingly illustrates that incorporating physics consistently leads to improved performance metrics—such as accuracy, robustness, data efficiency, and generalization—while ensuring physical plausibility in model outputs. This evidence strongly advocates for wider adoption of PICV methodologies.
- **Identifying Gaps and Opportunities:** The review doesn't merely summarize; it critically assesses the field, pinpointing underexplored areas, open problems, and future research directions. This forward-looking perspective is crucial for guiding future research efforts and fostering innovation.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Development of Better CV Models:** The primary promise of PICV, as articulated by the authors, is the development of computer vision models that are more physically plausible, accurate, data-efficient, and generalizable in increasingly realistic and complex applications. This could revolutionize applications where physical consistency is paramount, such as autonomous driving, robotics, medical diagnosis, and climate modeling.
- **Enhanced Interpretability and Trustworthiness:** By grounding models in physical laws, PICV has the potential to make complex deep learning models more interpretable and trustworthy. Understanding the "why" behind a model's prediction, beyond mere data patterns, is crucial for high-stakes applications and for bridging the gap between AI and scientific discovery.
- **Addressing Data Scarcity and Robustness Issues:** In domains where obtaining large, labeled datasets is difficult or expensive (e.g., certain medical imaging, scientific simulations), PICV can leverage physical laws as a powerful form of regularization, enabling robust learning from limited data and improving out-of-distribution generalization.
- **Catalyzing Interdisciplinary Research:** The review acts as a bridge between the computer vision community and researchers in physics, engineering, and other scientific disciplines. By providing a common language and framework, it encourages more interdisciplinary collaborations, which are essential for tackling complex, real-world problems.

**Future Challenges and Directions:**

The authors clearly articulate several open problems and research gaps that need to be addressed to realize the full potential of PICV:
- **Balancing Physics and Data:** A crucial challenge lies in finding the optimal balance between physics-based constraints and data-driven learning, especially when formalized representations of intuitive physics are lacking.
- **Choice of Physics Prior:** Selecting the most appropriate and effective physics prior for a given task remains challenging, often requiring extensive domain expertise.
- **Benchmarking and Evaluation:** The absence of standardized benchmarking platforms hinders fair comparison and robust evaluation of new PICV methods across diverse applications.
- **Scalability, Complexity, and Integration:** Developing scalable models that can accurately predict complex physical phenomena in dynamic environments and seamlessly integrate disparate physics disciplines (e.g., mechanics, electromagnetism) into a unified CV framework is a major hurdle.
- **Uncertainty and Incompleteness:** Robustly managing uncertainties and addressing the incompleteness of physical knowledge within PICV models is critical for reliable predictions.
- **Interpretability and Explainability:** Further work is needed to enhance the interpretability of how physical constraints interact with learned features, making models transparent to experts and non-experts alike.

In conclusion, this paper serves as a vital compass for the rapidly expanding landscape of Physics-Informed Computer Vision. By providing a structured overview, demonstrating compelling advantages, and clearly outlining future research trajectories, it is poised to significantly influence and accelerate advancements in developing more intelligent, reliable, and scientifically consistent computer vision systems.
