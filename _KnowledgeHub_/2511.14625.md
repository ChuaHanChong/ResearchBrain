# Gallant: Voxel Grid-based Humanoid Locomotion and Local-navigation across 3D Constrained Terrains

> [!summary] Summary
> Gallant presents a system for humanoid locomotion and local navigation across complex 3D terrains, utilizing a voxel-grid-based perception and an efficient z-grouped 2D CNN to understand full 3D environments. This framework enables a single policy to generalize across diverse obstacles, achieving high success rates on a Unitree G1 humanoid in zero-shot real-world deployment.

## Problem

- Traditional perception methods like depth cameras and 2.5D elevation maps offer limited field of view or discard crucial multi-layer 3D structural information, preventing humanoids from fully understanding complex environments.
- Raw 3D LiDAR data is high-dimensional, sparse, and noisy, creating computational bottlenecks for real-time processing and efficient policy learning on onboard robotics hardware.
- Developing a single, generalized policy capable of robustly navigating diverse 3D constrained terrains (e.g., stairs, overhead obstacles, narrow passages) has been a challenge for humanoid robots.

## Method

- A high-fidelity LiDAR simulation pipeline, leveraging ==NVIDIA Warp==, was developed to accurately model dynamic object scanning, sensor noise, and latency, facilitating robust sim-to-real transfer.
- Raw LiDAR point clouds are converted into a robot-centric 32x32x40 binary occupancy voxel grid (0.05m resolution) to preserve complete multi-layer 3D environmental structure.
- A z-grouped 2D CNN efficiently processes the voxel grid by treating height slices as channels, applying 2D convolutions over the x-y plane to capture vertical context with reduced computational cost.

## Results

- The system achieved high success rates, including **97.1%** on ceiling terrains, by accurately simulating LiDAR returns from the robot's own body, which was essential for handling 3D constraints.
- A single policy trained with Gallant successfully generalized to diverse real-world 3D constrained terrains, such as ducking under ceilings, navigating doorways, and ascending/descending stairs, during zero-shot deployment on a Unitree G1 humanoid.
- The z-grouped 2D CNN demonstrated superior performance in balancing accuracy and computational efficiency compared to other 3D perception architectures, making real-time onboard inference viable.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Accurately simulating LiDAR returns from the robot's own dynamic body (self-scanning) is crucial for a policy to generalize to 3D constrained terrains and avoid out-of-distribution observations.
> - The z-grouped 2D CNN effectively captures full 3D spatial information from sparse voxel grids using efficient 2D convolutions, offering a favorable accuracy-compute trade-off over 3D CNNs for real-time robotic deployment.
> - An optimal voxel resolution of 0.05m balances geometric precision for fine interactions with sufficient field of view for tasks involving large vertical extents, crucial for robust navigation.
