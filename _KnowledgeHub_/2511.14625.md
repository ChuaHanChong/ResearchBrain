# Gallant: Voxel Grid-based Humanoid Locomotion and Local-navigation across 3D Constrained Terrains

> [!summary] Summary
> Gallant presents a system for humanoid locomotion and local navigation across complex 3D terrains, utilizing a voxel-grid-based perception and an efficient z-grouped 2D CNN to understand full 3D environments. This framework enables a single policy to generalize across diverse obstacles, achieving high success rates on a Unitree G1 humanoid in zero-shot real-world deployment.

## Problem

- Traditional perception methods like depth cameras and 2.5D elevation maps offer limited field of view or discard crucial multi-layer 3D structural information, preventing humanoids from fully understanding complex environments.
- Raw 3D LiDAR data is high-dimensional, sparse, and noisy, creating computational bottlenecks for real-time processing and efficient policy learning on onboard robotics hardware.
- Developing a single, generalized policy capable of robustly navigating diverse 3D constrained terrains (e.g., stairs, overhead obstacles, narrow passages) has been a challenge for humanoid robots.

## Method

- A high-fidelity LiDAR simulation pipeline, leveraging ==NVIDIA Warp==, was developed to accurately model dynamic object scanning, sensor noise, and latency, facilitating robust sim-to-real transfer.
- Raw LiDAR point clouds are converted into a robot-centric 32x32x40 binary occupancy voxel grid (0.05m resolution) to preserve complete multi-layer 3D environmental structure.
- A z-grouped 2D CNN efficiently processes the voxel grid by treating height slices as channels, applying 2D convolutions over the x-y plane to capture vertical context with reduced computational cost.

## Results

- The system achieved high success rates, including **97.1%** on ceiling terrains, by accurately simulating LiDAR returns from the robot's own body, which was essential for handling 3D constraints.
- A single policy trained with Gallant successfully generalized to diverse real-world 3D constrained terrains, such as ducking under ceilings, navigating doorways, and ascending/descending stairs, during zero-shot deployment on a Unitree G1 humanoid.
- The z-grouped 2D CNN demonstrated superior performance in balancing accuracy and computational efficiency compared to other 3D perception architectures, making real-time onboard inference viable.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Accurately simulating LiDAR returns from the robot's own dynamic body (self-scanning) is crucial for a policy to generalize to 3D constrained terrains and avoid out-of-distribution observations.
> - The z-grouped 2D CNN effectively captures full 3D spatial information from sparse voxel grids using efficient 2D convolutions, offering a favorable accuracy-compute trade-off over 3D CNNs for real-time robotic deployment.
> - An optimal voxel resolution of 0.05m balances geometric precision for fine interactions with sufficient field of view for tasks involving large vertical extents, crucial for robust navigation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robust humanoid locomotion in diverse and unstructured 3D environments remains a grand challenge in robotics. Historically, approaches to perceptive locomotion have relied on various sensor modalities and representations, each with inherent limitations.

**Existing Perception Modalities and Their Limitations:**
- **Depth Cameras:** Offer low-latency perception but suffer from a narrow field of view (FoV) and limited range, hindering the robot's ability to reason about complex, spatially extended environments and anticipate distant obstacles. While effective for some quadruped robots, their limitations become more pronounced for complex humanoid movements.
- **Elevation Maps (2.5D Height Fields):** These methods compress 3D LiDAR point clouds into a height estimate for each ground-plane cell. While useful for ground-level obstacles, they inherently discard crucial vertical and multi-layer structural information (e.g., overhangs, low ceilings, mezzanines, stair undersides). This loss of 3D context makes them unsuitable for navigating environments with overhead constraints or complex multi-level structures. Furthermore, the reconstruction stage can introduce algorithm-specific distortions and latency, decoupling perception from control.
- **Raw LiDAR Point Clouds:** While 3D LiDAR provides detailed scene geometry with a wide FoV, its raw point clouds are sparse and noisy. This high dimensionality and unstructured nature create a bottleneck for sample-efficient policy learning and real-time inference on onboard compute platforms.
- **3D CNNs:** While capable of processing full 3D data, traditional 3D convolutional neural networks (CNNs) are computationally expensive and memory-intensive, especially for sparse data, making them challenging for real-time humanoid deployment.

**Gallant's Contribution to the Landscape:**
Gallant addresses these limitations by introducing a **voxel-grid–based perceptual representation** combined with a novel **z-grouped 2D CNN architecture**. This approach positions Gallant as a significant step forward for humanoid locomotion:
1. **Preserving Full 3D Structure:** Unlike elevation maps, voxel grids explicitly retain the multi-layer spatial information critical for navigating environments with overhead and lateral constraints. This allows for a comprehensive understanding of the surrounding 3D geometry.
2. **Efficiency and Wide FoV:** By aggregating raw LiDAR points into a structured voxel grid, Gallant reduces dimensionality and smooths noise, yielding a lightweight tensor. The dual-LiDAR setup further ensures a wide, near-omnidirectional field of view (∼4.00π solid angles, compared to ∼1.97π for elevation maps and ∼0.43π for depth cameras, as per Table 1 in the paper).
3. **Computational Feasibility:** The z-grouped 2D CNN treats height slices as channels, applying efficient 2D convolutions over the x-y plane. This design choice leverages spatial context and channel mixing to capture vertical structure, achieving a favorable accuracy-compute trade-off compared to heavier 3D CNNs, making real-time onboard inference viable.
4. **End-to-End Learning and Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Gallant proposes a full-stack pipeline, including a high-fidelity LiDAR simulation with dynamic object scanning and domain randomization. This allows for scalable training in simulation and facilitates zero-shot transfer to real-world hardware, a crucial aspect for practical robotics.

By effectively bridging the gap between comprehensive 3D perception and efficient real-time control, Gallant enables a single policy to handle a broader range of complex 3D constrained terrains—from ground-level obstacles to lateral clutter and overhead constraints—which were previously beyond the reach of existing methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for Gallant stems from the need for **robust humanoid locomotion and local navigation in complex 3D environments** that go beyond simple ground-level traversal. The authors identify a critical gap in existing humanoid control systems: their perception modules provide only partial or flattened views of the environment, failing to capture the full 3D structural context.

The key objectives of this research are therefore:

1. **Develop a geometry-preserving perceptual representation:** To overcome the limitations of depth images and 2.5D elevation maps, the primary objective is to create a lightweight yet comprehensive 3D representation that can accurately capture multi-layer scene structure and a wide field of view. This led to the adoption of robot-centric voxel grids derived from LiDAR data.
2. **Design an efficient neural network architecture for 3D perception:** Given the computational constraints of real-time onboard deployment, a crucial objective is to process the 3D perceptual input efficiently. This motivated the development of the z-grouped 2D CNN, which leverages the sparsity and structured nature of voxel data to balance representation capacity with computational cost.
3. **Enable a single, generalized policy for diverse 3D terrains:** Current methods often struggle with different types of obstacles (ground-level, lateral, overhead). The goal is to train a single end-to-end policy that can generalize across a wide array of 3D constrained terrains, including ascending/descending stairs, passing doors, ducking under ceilings, stepping onto platforms, and crossing gaps.
4. **Achieve robust sim-to-real transfer:** To enable scalable training and practical deployment, a major objective is to narrow the simulation-to-reality gap. This involves developing a high-fidelity LiDAR simulation that accurately models sensor noise, latency, and the scanning of dynamic objects (including the robot's own body).
5. **Attain high success rates in challenging scenarios:** Specifically, the paper aims to demonstrate near-100% success rates in tasks like stair climbing and stepping onto elevated platforms, which have historically presented significant stability and collision risks for humanoids.

In essence, Gallant seeks to empower humanoids with a more holistic and accurate understanding of their 3D surroundings, translating this perception into versatile and robust locomotion behaviors through an efficient, end-to-end learning framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Gallant proposes a full-stack perception-learning framework that integrates a novel perceptual representation, an efficient neural network, and a realistic simulation pipeline for training.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

Humanoid perceptive locomotion is formulated as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP). An actor-critic policy is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). The observation space (`o_t`) is comprehensive, including:
- **Command:** Relative goal position (`P_t`), elapsed time (`T_elapse,t`), remaining time (`T_left,t`).
- **Action History:** Past actions (`a_t-4:t-1`).
- **Proprioception:** Root angular and linear velocities (`ω_t`, `v_t`), gravity vector in robot frame (`g_t`), joint positions and velocities (`q_t`, `˙q_t`)—all with temporal history.
- **Perception:** The core voxel grid (`Voxel Grid_t`).
- **Privileged Information (Critic-only):** Height Map (`Height Map_t`).

The reward structure primarily focuses on a goal-reaching reward (`r_reach`) augmented by three general-purpose, geometry-aware shaping terms: directional velocity reward (incorporating obstacle avoidance), head height reward (for ducking under ceilings), and foot clearance reward (for proactively lifting legs over steps). Curriculum-based training with increasing terrain difficulty further refines the learning process.

#### 3.2. Efficient LiDAR Simulation

Recognizing the limitations of existing GPU simulators for dynamic LiDAR scanning, the authors developed a custom, lightweight, and efficient raycast-voxelization pipeline using **NVIDIA Warp**. This pipeline addresses key challenges:
- **Dynamic Object Scanning:** It accounts for all relevant geometry, including static terrain and dynamic robot links. By precomputing Bounding Volume Hierarchies (BVHs) for each mesh in its local frame, raycasting performance is optimized even with frequent updates due to robot motion.
- **Domain Randomization:** To reduce the sim-to-real gap, various randomizations are applied:
 - LiDAR pose perturbations (position and orientation).
 - Randomization of hit positions to simulate noise.
 - Simulated LiDAR latency (100–200 ms delay at 10 Hz).
 - Random masking of 2% of voxels to model real-world dropout.
This high-fidelity simulation environment is crucial for scalable, LiDAR-based training.

#### 3.3. Voxel Representation and 2D CNN Perception

- **Voxel Grid Construction:** Raw point clouds from dual torso-mounted Hesai JT128 LiDARs (providing a wide 95°x360° FoV each) are transformed into a unified torso frame. The perception volume is a 1.6m × 1.6m × 2.0m cuboid, discretized at a **resolution of 0.05m**, yielding a 32 × 32 × 40 binary occupancy grid (`X ∈ {0, 1}^(C×H×W)` where C=40, H=W=32). This effectively aggregates noisy points and preserves multi-layer structure.
- **Z-grouped 2D CNN:** Instead of computationally expensive 3D convolutions, the architecture treats the z-axis (height slices) as the channel dimension. A series of **2D convolutions** are then applied over the x-y plane. This leverages the typically sparse and locally concentrated nature of voxel occupancy, where most columns have few occupied z-slices. This design efficiently captures vertical structure through channel mixing while exploiting highly optimized dense 2D operators, offering a significant computational advantage. Features from the CNN are fused with proprioceptive signals and fed into an MLP-based actor for whole-body control.

#### 3.4. Terrain Design

Eight diverse terrain families are designed for curriculum training: Plane, Ceiling, Forest, Door, Platform, Pile, Upstair, and Downstair. Each terrain type has a scalar difficulty parameter `s ∈`, interpolating between easy (`p_min`) and hard (`p_max`) settings. A curriculum-based training strategy promotes the robot to harder settings upon success and demotes it upon failure, ensuring progressive learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Gallant's effectiveness is rigorously evaluated through extensive simulation ablations and real-world deployment on a Unitree G1 humanoid.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

Evaluations were conducted on the most challenging terrain settings (`p_max`) using two metrics: **Success rate (E_succ)** and **Collision momentum (E_collision)**.

- **Necessity of LiDAR Returns from Dynamic Objects (Self-scan):** Gallant, which simulates LiDAR returns from the robot's own moving links, achieved significantly higher success rates across all tasks compared to the `w/o-Self-Scan` baseline (e.g., 97.1% vs. 28.4% on Ceiling). Omitting dynamic objects creates out-of-distribution (OOD) observations when the robot's body occludes the environment (e.g., crouching under a ceiling), leading to dramatic performance drops. This highlights the crucial role of realistic self-scanning in the LiDAR simulation.
- **Efficacy of z-grouped 2D CNN:** The proposed z-grouped 2D CNN outperformed other perceptual network variants, including `Sparse-3D-CNN`, `3D-CNN`, and `Sparse-2D-CNN`, in most terrains. While some variants showed marginal gains on specific tasks, Gallant consistently achieved better overall accuracy with markedly lower compute. The study found that for the given voxel density, sparse convolutions offered little advantage due to overhead, while full 3D CNNs introduced too many parameters and memory traffic, hindering optimization. The z-grouped 2D CNN provided the optimal balance of efficiency and representation capability.
- **Optimal Perceptual Interface (Voxel Grid + Height Map for Critic):** Gallant's asymmetric design (voxel grid for actor, voxel grid + height map for critic) yielded superior results compared to `Only-Height-Map` or `Only-Voxel-Grid`. `Only-Height-Map` failed dramatically on 3D structures like Ceiling (5.3% success) and Door (10.2% success) due to its 2.5D limitations. The `Only-Voxel-Grid` (critic without height map) performed reasonably well but was generally outperformed by Gallant, indicating that the privileged height map, even if not directly fed to the actor, provides a valuable signal for value estimation and credit assignment during training.
- **Optimal Voxel Resolution (5cm):** A 0.05m (5cm) voxel resolution was found to be the most suitable. A `10CM` resolution, while providing a larger FoV, led to coarser geometric fidelity and impaired fine interactions. Conversely, `2.5CM` offered higher precision but a reduced FoV, hindering perception of long vertical extents critical for tasks like Ceiling and Downstair.

#### 4.2. Real-world Experiments

A single Gallant-trained policy was deployed zero-shot onto a 29-DoF Unitree G1 humanoid without any fine-tuning.
- **Superior Generalization:** Gallant consistently outperformed baselines (`HeightMap` and `NoDR`) across diverse real-world terrains (flat, ceilings, doors, platforms with gaps, stepping stones, staircases).
- **Full 3D Obstacle Handling:** The `HeightMap` baseline failed on overhead (e.g., Ceiling) and lateral (e.g., Door) obstacles due to its limited representation. Gallant, however, demonstrated versatile capabilities, including crouching under ceilings, precise lateral navigation through doorways, robust platform stepping, gap crossing, and stable stair climbing/descent.
- **Importance of Domain Randomization:** The `NoDR` variant (trained without LiDAR domain randomization) performed poorly on ground-level terrains, often reacting too late due to misjudging its position relative to obstacles. This confirmed the critical role of simulating LiDAR latency and noise in bridging the sim-to-real gap.
- **Near-100% Success on Challenging Terrains:** Gallant achieved high success rates on previously unstable terrains like Platforms and Stairs by proactively adjusting foot trajectories.

#### 4.3. Further Analyses

A strong correlation between simulation and real-world success rates was observed, validating the use of large-scale simulated evaluation. Terrains like Ceiling and Door, previously difficult for height map-based methods, became the easiest with voxel grids, demonstrating their effectiveness for full-space perception. The main limitation identified was on the `Pile` terrain (around 80% success), where accurate foothold selection is critical, and real-world LiDAR latency (100-200ms at 10Hz) proved to be a bottleneck for pre-emptive action.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Gallant represents a significant advancement in humanoid locomotion and local navigation, offering a robust and practical solution for operating in complex 3D environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling True 3D Perceptive Locomotion:** By demonstrating the effectiveness of voxel grids and z-grouped 2D CNNs, Gallant moves beyond the limitations of 2.5D elevation maps and narrow-FoV depth cameras. It provides humanoids with a comprehensive understanding of their full 3D surroundings, including overhead and lateral constraints, which is crucial for real-world deployment in cluttered and multi-level spaces.
2. **Efficient and Scalable End-to-End Learning:** The framework's ability to train a single policy that generalizes across a wide range of diverse terrains, combined with an efficient perceptual network, makes it highly scalable. This reduces the need for task-specific tuning or hierarchical controllers, simplifying the development and deployment of complex humanoid behaviors.
3. **Advancing Sim-to-Real Transfer for LiDAR:** The development of a high-fidelity LiDAR simulation pipeline, including dynamic object scanning and comprehensive domain randomization for sensor noise and latency, is a major contribution. This significantly improves the reliability of zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, accelerating research and development in real-world robotics.
4. **Practical Deployment on Onboard Hardware:** The optimized z-grouped 2D CNN architecture and the overall efficiency of the pipeline allow for real-time inference on resource-constrained onboard compute platforms (e.g., NVIDIA Orin NX). This is a critical step towards autonomous and untethered humanoid operation.
5. **Setting New Benchmarks for Challenging Tasks:** Gallant achieves near-100% success rates in tasks like stair climbing and platform stepping, which have historically been sources of instability and failure for humanoids. This demonstrates a new level of robustness and capability in complex terrains.

**Potential Impact:**
The principles and methodologies introduced by Gallant could have a broad impact on various fields:
- **Humanoid Robotics:** It provides a solid foundation for future humanoid research, especially in developing more autonomous and versatile robots capable of operating in human-centric environments (e.g., disaster response, domestic assistance, industrial inspection).
- **Legged Robotics:** While focused on humanoids, the concepts of voxel-based perception and efficient CNN architectures are transferable to other legged robots (quadrupeds, bipeds), potentially improving their navigation capabilities in unstructured terrains.
- **Perception Systems:** The z-grouped 2D CNN offers a novel and efficient way to process sparse 3D data, which could inspire new architectures for various 3D perception tasks beyond locomotion.
- **Simulation Research:** The emphasis on high-fidelity dynamic LiDAR simulation will encourage further development in realistic sensor modeling for robotics, a key enabler for advanced AI training.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
Despite its successes, the paper acknowledges a primary limitation: **LiDAR latency**. The 10 Hz operation introduces a 100-200 ms delay, which restricts the robot's ability to act preemptively, particularly on fine-grained terrains like "Pile." Future work aims to address this by exploring lower-latency sensors or using Gallant as a geometry-aware teacher for policies trained with more reactive, lower-latency sensing modalities. Overcoming this latency challenge will be key to achieving near-perfect performance across all terrains.
