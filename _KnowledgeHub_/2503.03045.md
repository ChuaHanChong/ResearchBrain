# ArticuBot: Learning Universal Articulated Object Manipulation Policy via Large Scale Simulation

> [!summary] Summary
> ArticuBot presents a single, vision-based robotic policy capable of robustly manipulating diverse, unseen articulated objects in the real world. By training on a large-scale simulated dataset and employing a hierarchical policy architecture, it achieves effective zero-shot transfer for tasks like opening drawers and cabinets with standard parallel-jaw grippers.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation of articulated objects faces challenges in generalizing across diverse geometries, sizes, and articulation types due to vast variations.
- Prior methods often rely on simplifying assumptions like pre-existing knowledge of articulation parameters, object-specific policies, or specialized grippers, limiting their real-world scalability and universality.
- Bridging the simulation-to-real-world gap remains difficult for complex manipulation tasks, often requiring extensive real-world data or fine-tuning.

## Method

- The approach leverages large-scale, automated demonstration generation (42.3k trajectories on 322 objects) in a physics-based simulator (Pybullet) using a sampling-based grasping and motion planning pipeline.
- A vision-based hierarchical policy is learned via imitation: a high-level weighted displacement model predicts sub-goal end-effector poses, which then conditions a low-level 3D diffusion policy to generate delta end-effector actions.
- Zero-shot transfer to real robots is enabled by using point cloud observations (with real-world segmentation and filtering), end-effector space actions, and targeted sim augmentations to bridge the sim2real gap.

## Results

- ArticuBot's hierarchical policy achieved **0.7**-**0.75** normalized opening performance in simulation, significantly outperforming non-hierarchical baselines (below **0.25**).
- It demonstrated robust zero-shot sim2real transfer, achieving **0.63** normalized opening on a Franka arm and **0.54** on an X-Arm on 20 diverse, unseen articulated objects across multiple real-world environments.
- ArticuBot outperformed existing methods like FlowBot3D (**0.86** vs. **0.57** normalized opening after grasp) and a leading generalist robotic foundation model, OpenVLA (which failed to open any objects).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A hierarchical policy, decoupling 'where to go' from 'how to move', is crucial for achieving object-level generalization across diverse articulated objects.
> - The weighted displacement model in the high-level policy enables robust predictions by grounding them in the 3D scene structure and implicitly focusing on task-relevant regions like handles.
> - A learned low-level policy is essential for handling minor collisions and accurately following object kinematic constraints during real-world opening, outperforming traditional motion planning and IK controllers.
