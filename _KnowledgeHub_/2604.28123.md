# Beyond SFT-to-RL: Pre-alignment via Black-Box On-Policy Distillation for Multimodal RL

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduce PRISM, a three-stage pipeline for large multimodal models (LMMs) that inserts a pre-alignment stage between supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR). This intermediate stage employs black-box on-policy distillation with a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) discriminator to mitigate SFT-induced distributional drift and improve subsequent RLVR performance.

## Problem

- Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) often introduces distributional drift, causing LMMs to deviate from their target behavior and potentially compromise their inherent capabilities, particularly in multimodal reasoning.
- Multimodal errors are heterogeneous, encompassing distinct visual grounding and logical reasoning failures, which a single corrective objective cannot adequately address.
- Access to logits from proprietary, high-quality LMM teachers is typically unavailable, preventing standard divergence-based knowledge distillation for policy alignment.

## Method

- **PRISM** establishes a three-stage post-training pipeline: Cold-Start SFT, Distribution Alignment via On-Policy Distillation, and **RLVR**.
- The core Distribution Alignment stage utilizes a logit-free, black-box adversarial ==on-policy distillation== framework.
- A ==Mixture-of-Experts== (**MoE**) ==discriminator== is employed, featuring specialized perception and reasoning experts to provide disentangled corrective signals for heterogeneous multimodal errors.

## Results

- PRISM consistently boosted downstream RLVR performance by an average of +4.4 accuracy points on the 4B LMM and +6.0 points on the 8B LMM, compared to the standard SFT-to-RLVR baseline.
- The alignment stage itself primarily functions as a distributional corrector, with its benefits manifesting in the subsequent RLVR stage where all tested RL algorithms showed improved performance.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of the MoE discriminator, as replacing it with a single dense model led to an average -3.4 points performance degradation, especially on complex reasoning tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - SFT, while necessary for initial competence, can degrade a strong base model's performance by introducing distributional drift that needs explicit correction.
> - An intermediate distribution alignment stage, prior to RLVR, significantly improves the efficiency and stability of downstream reinforcement learning by providing a better-aligned initialization.
> - Disentangling multimodal errors (visual perception vs. logical reasoning) through specialized discriminator experts is essential for effective policy correction and robust multimodal reasoning.
