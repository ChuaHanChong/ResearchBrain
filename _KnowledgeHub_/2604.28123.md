# Beyond SFT-to-RL: Pre-alignment via Black-Box On-Policy Distillation for Multimodal RL

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduce PRISM, a three-stage pipeline for large multimodal models (LMMs) that inserts a pre-alignment stage between supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR). This intermediate stage employs black-box on-policy distillation with a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) discriminator to mitigate SFT-induced distributional drift and improve subsequent RLVR performance.

## Problem

- Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) often introduces distributional drift, causing LMMs to deviate from their target behavior and potentially compromise their inherent capabilities, particularly in multimodal reasoning.
- Multimodal errors are heterogeneous, encompassing distinct visual grounding and logical reasoning failures, which a single corrective objective cannot adequately address.
- Access to logits from proprietary, high-quality LMM teachers is typically unavailable, preventing standard divergence-based knowledge distillation for policy alignment.

## Method

- **PRISM** establishes a three-stage post-training pipeline: Cold-Start SFT, Distribution Alignment via On-Policy Distillation, and **RLVR**.
- The core Distribution Alignment stage utilizes a logit-free, black-box adversarial ==on-policy distillation== framework.
- A ==Mixture-of-Experts== (**MoE**) ==discriminator== is employed, featuring specialized perception and reasoning experts to provide disentangled corrective signals for heterogeneous multimodal errors.

## Results

- PRISM consistently boosted downstream RLVR performance by an average of +4.4 accuracy points on the 4B LMM and +6.0 points on the 8B LMM, compared to the standard SFT-to-RLVR baseline.
- The alignment stage itself primarily functions as a distributional corrector, with its benefits manifesting in the subsequent RLVR stage where all tested RL algorithms showed improved performance.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of the MoE discriminator, as replacing it with a single dense model led to an average -3.4 points performance degradation, especially on complex reasoning tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - SFT, while necessary for initial competence, can degrade a strong base model's performance by introducing distributional drift that needs explicit correction.
> - An intermediate distribution alignment stage, prior to RLVR, significantly improves the efficiency and stability of downstream reinforcement learning by providing a better-aligned initialization.
> - Disentangling multimodal errors (visual perception vs. logical reasoning) through specialized discriminator experts is essential for effective policy correction and robust multimodal reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large multimodal models (LMMs) has seen increasing success in instruction-following and reasoning, often building on the paradigms established by large language models (LLMs). A prevalent post-training methodology involves a two-stage pipeline: supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on curated demonstrations, followed by reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR). SFT aims to bootstrap the model's capabilities by anchoring it to high-quality supervision, while RLVR refines the policy towards specific task objectives using automatic verifiers.

However, recent studies have identified a challenge with SFT: it can introduce a "distributional drift" where the model neither perfectly matches the demonstration policy nor retains its original favorable distribution. This drift is particularly problematic for stronger base models, as token-level imitation during SFT can displace rather than supplement existing native strengths. In multimodal contexts, this problem is amplified because visual perception errors and reasoning failures exhibit distinct drift patterns, which can compound during subsequent RL. Existing work in RLVR has primarily focused on improving the stability and effectiveness of the RL stage itself, or on developing perception-aware reward signals, but these approaches generally do not address the distributional gap inherited from the preceding SFT stage.

This paper addresses this specific bottleneck by proposing an intermediate stage that explicitly mitigates SFT-induced distributional drift. It draws inspiration from advances in knowledge distillation, particularly on-policy distillation (OPD), which trains a student model on its own generations to mitigate exposure bias and encourage faithful policy refinement. While OPD has been explored for LLMs, most existing methods treat it as a terminal training objective, rely on teacher logits, or use a single, undifferentiated discriminator. This work repositions OPD as a pre-alignment stage, specifically tailored for the heterogeneous nature of multimodal drift.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method that mitigates the distributional drift introduced by Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) in large multimodal models (LMMs) before they undergo Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing SFT-induced Distributional Drift:** The authors observe that SFT, while providing an initial capability bootstrap, can lead to a "compromised state" where the model's policy distribution deviates from both its original strengths and the target supervision. This drift means the model might learn superficial patterns rather than robust reasoning capabilities.
2. **Mitigating Heterogeneous Multimodal Errors:** In multimodal reasoning, distributional drift is particularly complex because errors can stem from two distinct sources: failures in visual grounding (perception errors) and failures in logical deduction (reasoning errors). A single, undifferentiated corrective objective, common in existing SFT or RL methods, is insufficient to address these qualitatively different error modes. The goal is to provide disentangled corrective signals.
3. **Improving RLVR Initialization:** Directly feeding a drift-affected policy from SFT into RLVR forces the online optimization process to start from a distorted state. This can limit the effectiveness and stability of RLVR, preventing it from fully leveraging the model's potential or even recovering lost performance. The motivation is to provide a "more reliable initialization" for downstream RLVR, thereby maximizing its gains.
4. **Enabling Black-Box Alignment without Teacher Logits:** Many high-quality supervision sources, especially those distilled from powerful proprietary models (like Gemini 3 Flash), are "black-box" models whose internal logits are inaccessible. Standard divergence-based knowledge distillation methods, which rely on teacher logits, are thus inapplicable. The authors are motivated to develop a logit-free, response-level alignment mechanism.
5. **Curating High-Fidelity Multimodal Supervision Data:** Effective distribution alignment, particularly for complex multimodal reasoning tasks, demands high-fidelity supervision. Existing public datasets often lack the detailed visual grounding and step-by-step reasoning traces required. Therefore, a motivation is to curate a specialized dataset that targets hard, unsolved problems with dense annotations to serve as a strong reference for alignment.

In essence, the research aims to repair the "mismatch" between the post-SFT policy and the target supervision distribution by introducing an explicit, specialized alignment stage, thereby laying a stronger foundation for subsequent reinforcement learning in multimodal contexts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose **PRISM** (PRe-alignment via black-box on-policy dIStillation for Multimodal reinforcement learning), a three-stage post-training pipeline designed to mitigate distributional drift in LMMs. This pipeline extends the conventional SFT→RL recipe by inserting an explicit pre-alignment stage.

#### 3.1. Overall Pipeline Structure

1. **Cold-Start Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** Provides an initial reasoning-capable policy.
2. **Distribution Alignment via On-Policy Distillation (OPD):** Recalibrates the post-SFT policy distribution towards the supervision distribution using an adversarial framework. This is the core contribution of PRISM.
3. **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR):** Applies standard outcome-based RL to the aligned checkpoint for final policy improvement.

#### 3.2. Stage 1: Cold-Start Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

- **Purpose:** To equip the base model (Qwen3-VL-4B/8B) with initial multimodal reasoning capabilities and narrow the initial distribution gap sufficiently for adversarial training.
- **Data Curation:** A 113K multimodal reasoning corpus is curated from Gemini 3 Flash, focusing on problems with zero pass rates for contemporary models. This data features fine-grained visual grounding and step-by-step deductions, verified through multi-stage filtering (truncation, format, LLM-based correctness).
- **Combined Corpus:** The curated 113K samples are supplemented with 1.26M publicly available demonstrations from the same Gemini model family, totaling 1.37M samples for SFT. 107K of the curated samples are used for SFT, with the remaining 6K reserved for alignment and RL.
- **Training:** Standard full-parameter SFT is performed on the language model, with the vision tower and multimodal projector frozen, for 1 epoch. The system prompt enforces a structured output format: `<caption>`, `<think>`, `<answer>`.

#### 3.3. Stage 2: Distribution Alignment via On-Policy Distillation (OPD)

This is the central, novel component of PRISM. It addresses the SFT-induced drift and prepares a better initialization for RLVR.

- **Problem Formulation:** Alignment is cast as a black-box, response-level adversarial minimax game between the policy (generator, G) and a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) discriminator. This approach bypasses the need for teacher logits from black-box models.
- **Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) Discriminator:**
 - **Purpose:** To provide disentangled corrective signals for heterogeneous errors (visual grounding vs. logical reasoning).
 - **Architecture:** The discriminator is composed of two specialized experts:
 - **Perception Expert (D_v):** Evaluates the visual description (`c`) within a response (`y`) to measure grounding in the visual input (`x`).
 - **Reasoning Expert (D_r):** Evaluates the reasoning trace (`t`) within a response (`y`) to measure consistency and validity of deduction.
 - **Discriminator Score:** The overall reward `r(x, y)` is a weighted combination: `r(x, y) = α * D_v(x, c) + (1 - α) * D_r(x, t)`, where `α` (set to 0.5) balances feedback.
- **Initialization for Alignment:**
 - **Policy (G):** Initialized from the SFT checkpoint (`π_sft`).
 - **MoE Discriminator:** Both experts are initialized from a pretrained backbone (Qwen3-VL-MoE architecture, four Qwen3-VL-2B models as experts) and warm-started on preference pairs derived from visual descriptions and reasoning traces, respectively. An auxiliary load-balancing loss prevents expert collapse.
- **Adversarial On-Policy Distillation Process:**
 - The policy `G` is optimized to generate responses resembling high-quality reference demonstrations, while the discriminator is trained to distinguish policy rollouts from supervision.
 - **Discriminator Update:** For a given input `x`, the discriminator receives a reference response (`y+`) from the supervision data `T` and a policy rollout (`y-`) from `G(·|x)`. Each expert `D_k` (where `k ∈ {v, r}`) is trained to assign a higher score to `y+` and a lower score to `y-` by minimizing its Bradley-Terry loss: `L_D_k = -E log σ(D_k(x, y+_k) - D_k(x, y-_k))`. Both experts are optimized jointly with the policy, adapting to the evolving rollout distribution.
 - **Policy Update:** For each input `x`, `N` responses (`{y-_i}`) are sampled from `G`. Each response `y-_i` is evaluated by the MoE discriminator to obtain a reward `r_i`. These rewards are then converted into group-wise advantages using standardization. The policy `G` is updated via a GRPO-style policy gradient to maximize these advantages. Crucially, KL regularization (common in RL) is removed to allow the policy to freely shift its distribution.
 - **Iteration:** The process alternates between updating the discriminator and the policy for 500 steps.
- **Output:** The aligned policy `π_align`.

#### 3.4. Stage 3: Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)

- **Purpose:** To refine the policy for final task performance using outcome-based rewards.
- **Initialization:** The policy (`π`) is initialized with the `π_align` checkpoint from the previous stage.
- **Training Data:** A difficulty-filtered subset of the 6K reserved curated samples is used (approx. 2K instances). These problems have a pass rate between 0.2 and 0.8 under the aligned policy, ensuring they are challenging but not impossible.
- **Reward Function:** The reward switches from the learned MoE discriminator to a deterministic verifiable reward `r_v(x, y) = r_acc(x, y) + r_fmt(x, y)`, combining answer accuracy (`r_acc`) and format compliance (`r_fmt`).
- **Policy Optimization:** Policy optimization follows a GRPO-style objective. PRISM is designed to be agnostic to the specific RL algorithm used, and experiments are conducted with GRPO, DAPO, and GSPO. Training runs for 1500 steps.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

- **Models:** Qwen3-VL-4B and Qwen3-VL-8B as base models. Gemini 3 Flash for supervision data.
- **Discriminator:** Qwen3-VL-MoE architecture, using four Qwen3-VL-2B models as experts with top-2 routing.
- **Compute:** All stages are conducted on 8 NVIDIA H100-SXM5-80GB GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on Qwen3-VL-4B and Qwen3-VL-8B, across mathematical reasoning and general multimodal benchmarks, yielded several observations regarding PRISM's effectiveness.

#### 4.1. Consistent Improvement in Downstream RLVR Performance

- PRISM consistently improved downstream RLVR performance across multiple RL algorithms (GRPO, DAPO, GSPO).
- Specifically, PRISM+GRPO outperformed the standard SFT→GRPO baseline by an average of +4.4 percentage points on Qwen3-VL-4B and +6.0 points on Qwen3-VL-8B.
- The most substantial gains were observed on MathVision and WeMath benchmarks.
- Similar performance enhancements were recorded when integrating PRISM with DAPO and GSPO, indicating that the alignment stage provides a robust and algorithm-agnostic improvement in initialization for RLVR.
- Beyond accuracy, PRISM+GRPO also demonstrated improved token efficiency, achieving higher accuracy with fewer tokens per response compared to SFT+GRPO on benchmarks like MathVision, MathVerse, and MMMU-Pro. This suggests more concise and effective reasoning.

#### 4.2. Role of the Alignment Stage

- The checkpoint after the PRISM alignment stage (but before RLVR) showed accuracy comparable to the SFT-only checkpoint. This indicates that the alignment stage's immediate effect is not a direct increase in task accuracy, but rather a distributional correction.
- The benefit of this distributional shift is realized in the subsequent RLVR stage, where every tested RL algorithm showed improved performance due to the better-aligned initialization.

#### 4.3. SFT-Induced Drift is More Pronounced for Stronger Models

- Consistent with the paper's motivation, SFT alone led to an average degradation of performance from the base `Instruct` checkpoint across both model scales, with a larger drop observed for the 8B model.
- On the Qwen3-VL-8B model, the standard SFT→RLVR pipeline (with GRPO and GSPO) barely recovered the original `Instruct` performance. This indicates that for stronger base models with already capable reasoning priors, SFT can indeed introduce significant drift that RLVR alone struggles to fully compensate for.
- In contrast, PRISM+GRPO on the 8B model significantly exceeded the `Instruct` baseline by over 5 percentage points, demonstrating that the alignment stage effectively repaired this drift and unlocked further performance gains from RLVR.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study Insights (on Qwen3-VL-4B with GRPO)

- **MoE Discriminator:** Replacing the MoE discriminator with a single dense model of equivalent computational capacity resulted in a consistent average degradation of -3.4 points. Larger drops were observed on WeMath (-6.0) and MathVerse (-4.9). This supports the efficacy of the MoE design in providing disentangled feedback for distinct multimodal error modes.
- **Three-Stage Pipeline Necessity:**
 - Removing the alignment stage (reducing it to SFT→RLVR) led to a -4.4 average drop, confirming that explicit distribution correction is beneficial.
 - Removing SFT entirely (attempting alignment directly from the base model) resulted in a substantial -16.8 average drop. This indicates SFT provides critical cold-start initialization, narrowing the distribution gap sufficiently for adversarial training to be effective. Each stage was found to be indispensable.
- **Vision-Language Discriminator:** Using a text-only discriminator, which cannot "see" the image, caused performance degradation, particularly on tasks requiring faithful visual perception. This suggests that "parrot alignment" (matching textual patterns without visual grounding) is insufficient for meaningful multimodal distribution alignment.
- **SFT Data Scale:** Using only the 107K curated SFT samples (instead of the full 1.37M corpus) for initialization resulted in a -3.7 average drop in downstream RLVR performance. This highlights the complementary roles of broad SFT data (for initial competence) and the alignment stage (for fine-grained distribution correction).

#### 4.5. Analysis of Training Dynamics and Structural Proxies

- **Training Dynamics:** Tracking the reward gap (supervision minus policy) for both perception and reasoning experts during alignment showed distinct "rise-then-convergence" patterns. The perception expert converged rapidly, while the reasoning expert exhibited a more gradual rise and greater oscillation. This asynchronous convergence further supports the need for specialized experts.
- **Structural Proxies:** Analysis of reasoning complexity (number of steps in `think` traces) and perceptual grounding granularity (number of descriptive items in `caption`s) revealed that the base model deviates significantly from the supervision distribution. SFT moved the policy closer but left visible mismatches. The alignment stage substantially reduced these mismatches, and these improvements largely persisted through the subsequent RLVR stage. This indicates that alignment provides a better-shaped initialization that is preserved and further refined by RLVR.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PRISM framework introduces a methodological advancement in the post-training pipeline for large multimodal models (LMMs) by explicitly addressing the problem of SFT-induced distributional drift. This work has several areas of significance and potential impact:

1. **Improved LMM Training Paradigm:** By proposing a three-stage pipeline that inserts an alignment stage between SFT and RLVR, PRISM offers a refined paradigm for LMM optimization. This challenges the conventional two-stage approach and highlights the importance of distributional matching prior to outcome-based reinforcement learning.
2. **Mitigation of SFT Drawbacks:** The research demonstrates that SFT, while foundational, can inadvertently compromise model performance, especially for already capable base models. PRISM provides a mechanism to repair this drift, thereby unlocking the full potential of subsequent RLVR and potentially improving the scalability of post-training for stronger LMMs.
3. **Novel Multimodal Alignment Strategy:** The introduction of a black-box adversarial on-policy distillation framework with a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) discriminator is a significant contribution. This approach provides disentangled corrective signals for distinct multimodal error modes (visual perception and logical reasoning), which is crucial for the heterogeneous nature of multimodal understanding. The black-box nature also expands applicability to proprietary or inaccessible teacher models.
4. **Enhanced RLVR Effectiveness:** PRISM consistently improves the performance of various downstream RLVR algorithms. This suggests that a well-aligned policy serves as a superior initialization, allowing RL to achieve greater gains and more stable optimization, rather than expending resources to correct prior distributional errors.
5. **High-Quality Data Curation:** The curation of a 113K high-fidelity multimodal reasoning corpus, specifically designed to target challenging, unsolved problems with dense visual grounding and step-by-step reasoning, contributes valuable resources to the research community. This type of data is essential for driving progress in complex multimodal tasks.
6. **Insights into Model Behavior:** The detailed analysis of training dynamics and structural proxies provides deeper insights into how models learn and how their internal reasoning processes evolve across different training stages. The observation that perception and reasoning experts converge along distinct trajectories, and that structural alignment persists through RLVR, offers empirical support for the proposed architecture and methodology.

The potential impact extends to the development of more robust, accurate, and efficient LMMs for a wide range of applications requiring sophisticated multimodal reasoning. By focusing on fundamental distributional alignment, PRISM contributes to a more principled approach to LMM post-training, potentially influencing future research on foundation model instruction tuning and reinforcement learning. The authors also acknowledge limitations, such as increased training cost and dependency on structured response formats, which point to avenues for future work, including extending to broader task formats and developing more generalized alignment metrics.
