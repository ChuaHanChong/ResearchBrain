# Squeeze Evolve: Unified Multi-Model Orchestration for Verifier-Free Evolution

> [!summary] Summary
> SQUEEZE EVOLVE introduces a unified multi-model orchestration framework for verifier-free evolutionary inference, strategically allocating tasks to models with varying costs and capabilities. The framework achieves up to 3x API cost reduction and 10x throughput increases while matching or surpassing verifier-based methods on complex reasoning and discovery tasks.

## Problem

- Verifier-free evolutionary methods for LLMs often suffer from 'diversity collapse', where the solution population converges narrowly, limiting improvements.
- These methods are cost-inefficient due to the uniform use of expensive, high-capability models for all operations, making long iterative processes prohibitive.
- Traditional verifier-based evolution incurs substantial costs and latency from external feedback, or is impractical in domains lacking reliable verification.

## Method

- **SQUEEZE EVOLVE** unifies various ==test-time scaling== methods into a single ==evolutionary framework==, abstracting ==selection==, ==recombination==, and ==fitness estimation==.
- It employs a ==multi-model orchestration strategy== that dynamically assigns evolutionary operations (e.g., initialization, recombination) to models based on their cost-capability profiles.
- Model-intrinsic ==confidence signals== (log-probabilities) and ==diversity metrics== are utilized as lightweight, verifier-free proxies for fitness and adaptive routing decisions.

## Results

- Achieved up to **3x** API cost reduction and **10x** fixed-budget serving throughput improvements across various benchmarks.
- Matched or exceeded verifier-based evolutionary methods on discovery tasks like circle packing (**2.635896** score vs. AlphaEvolve's **2.635862**) without explicit verification.
- Reached **97.5%** accuracy on ARC-AGI-V2 at **$5.93**/task without code execution, representing a **4.9x** cost saving over prior state-of-the-art approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The quality of the initial candidate population (ancestor function) generated by the strongest model significantly correlates with final accuracy.
> - Cheaper, weaker models can effectively aggregate solutions when provided with a sufficiently strong set of input candidates.
> - Model-intrinsic self- and cross-model confidence signals, along with diversity metrics, serve as reliable indicators of candidate quality for fitness estimation and dynamic routing.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work on SQUEEZE EVOLVE is situated within the broader field of test-time scaling for large language models (LLMs). Test-time scaling involves expending additional computational resources during inference to enhance the quality of generated solutions, moving beyond single-shot inference. This paradigm includes methods like parallel sampling, sequential refinement, search-based techniques, and extended reasoning chains. A particularly relevant direction is self-evolution, where models iteratively improve candidates through processes inspired by biological evolution, such as selection, mutation, and recombination.

A significant challenge in self-evolution is the reliance on external verifiers to evaluate candidate solutions and guide the evolutionary process. While verifier-based evolution (e.g., AlphaEvolve, FunSearch) can achieve strong discovery capabilities, it often incurs substantial costs and latency due to the need for external feedback, or may be entirely impractical or unavailable in domains where verification is inherently expensive or impossible (e.g., certain scientific discovery tasks). This motivates the development of *verifier-free evolution*, where fitness signals are derived solely from the models' own outputs, such as log-probabilities or consensus frequency.

However, existing verifier-free evolutionary methods face two primary bottlenecks:
1. **Diversity Collapse**: Without external correction, repeated evolution tends to converge towards narrow solution modes, reducing the diversity of the candidate population and limiting the potential for further improvement.
2. **Cost Inefficiency**: Verifier-free evolution often requires generating a large number of tokens (e.g., 500-700x more than standard LLM inference), making the uniform use of high-cost, high-capability models economically prohibitive. There is a notable disparity in cost-capability trade-offs among models, with proprietary frontier models being significantly more expensive than open-weight alternatives.

SQUEEZE EVOLVE addresses these challenges by introducing a unified multi-model orchestration framework. It departs from the single-model paradigm common in many test-time scaling and self-aggregation methods by adaptively assigning different evolutionary operations to models with varying cost-capability profiles. This approach seeks to optimize the cost-capability frontier, enabling advanced capabilities under tight budget constraints. It builds upon previous work in test-time scaling, self-aggregation (e.g., RSA, Mixture-of-Agents), confidence signal utilization (e.g., DeepConf), LLM-driven evolutionary search, and model routing, extending them to a verifier-free, multi-model, and dynamically orchestrated evolutionary framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this work stems from the inherent limitations of current verifier-free evolutionary inference methods. The authors identify two primary issues: the susceptibility of these methods to diversity collapse without explicit external verification, and their substantial computational and economic cost, particularly when relying on expensive, high-capability models for all operations. This presents a critical tension, as scaling compute efficiently becomes paramount for practical applications. The research shifts the focus from simply increasing resources to achieve new capabilities to optimizing resource allocation to meet capability targets within budget constraints.

The key objectives of SQUEEZE EVOLVE are detailed as follows:

1. **Unify Existing Test-Time Scaling Methods**: The first objective is to demonstrate that a wide array of seemingly disparate test-time scaling methods can be expressed as instances of a single, unified evolutionary framework. By doing so, the authors aim to reveal a common design space that can be optimized comprehensively, providing a formal foundation for reasoning about test-time evolution.

2. **Address Diversity and Efficiency Bottlenecks**: The work seeks to overcome the observed diversity collapse in verifier-free evolution, where populations tend to narrow into limited solution modes, thereby hindering performance. Simultaneously, it aims to tackle the economic inefficiency arising from the uniform application of expensive models across all evolutionary stages, which makes prolonged iterative improvement impractical.

3. **Develop a Multi-Model Orchestration Strategy**: A core objective is to design a framework that intelligently allocates model capabilities based on marginal utility. This involves determining which model (among a set with varying costs and capabilities) should be assigned to specific operations within the evolutionary pipeline, such as initialization, generation, recombination, or fitness estimation. The goal is to reserve stronger, more expensive models for high-impact stages while delegating other stages to cheaper models.

4. **Maximize Capability per Unit Cost**: By strategically coordinating models across the pipeline, the research aims to maximize the overall capability achieved for a given computational budget. This involves minimizing orchestration overheads while ensuring that resource allocation decisions lead to a better cost-capability frontier than single-model approaches.

5. **Empirically Validate Model-Intrinsic Signals for Routing**: The authors aim to empirically analyze the effectiveness of model-intrinsic confidence signals (e.g., log-probabilities) and diversity metrics as reliable proxies for fitness estimation in a verifier-free context. These signals are proposed as a lightweight mechanism for routing decisions, allowing for adaptive model assignment without requiring external verifiers.

6. **Demonstrate Versatility Across Model Types and Benchmarks**: The objective includes demonstrating the framework's versatility by supporting open-source, closed-source, and mixed-model deployments across a diverse set of benchmarks, including math, coding, multimodal vision, visual reasoning, and scientific discovery tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SQUEEZE EVOLVE employs a multi-model orchestration strategy within a unified evolutionary framework. The methodology is structured around empirically identified insights into verifier-free evolution and a carefully designed algorithmic approach.

#### 3.1. Unified Evolutionary Framework

The authors abstract various test-time scaling methods into a single evolutionary framework. For a given query `Q`, an initial population `P^(0)` of candidate solutions is generated from a strong model. The core of the evolution is an iterated process defined by an evolutionary operator `Φ_f = recomb_f ◦ select_f`, where `recomb_f` performs recombination guided by a fitness signal `f`, and `select_f` chooses candidates for the next generation. The fitness signal `f` in verifier-free evolution is derived entirely from the models' outputs. This unified view categorizes existing methods based on their instantiation of selection, recombination, and fitness (e.g., majority voting as single-step, RSA as multi-step verifier-free, AlphaEvolve as verifier-based). SQUEEZE EVOLVE extends this by incorporating model-intrinsic confidence as a fitness signal and enabling adaptive per-group model assignment.

#### 3.2. Motivation Analysis for Verifier-Free Evolutionary Framework (Empirical Findings)

Prior to designing SQUEEZE EVOLVE, the authors conducted an empirical analysis that informed the framework's design principles:

1. **Inherent Pass@K Bottleneck of Verifier-Free Evolution**: Observations on GPQA-Diamond showed that simple verifier-free evolution leads to a rapid decline in semantic diversity and `pass@K` scores over loops. This "diversity collapse" occurs because the models tend to amplify solutions they already recognize, converging to narrow modes. This finding underscores the necessity of multi-model orchestration to introduce different priors and reasoning styles, thereby preserving diversity and maintaining search capacity.

2. **Ancestor Function Dominates Final Accuracy**: Experiments on HMMT 25 and AIME 2025 demonstrated that the quality of the initial population (`P^(0)`) generated by the "ancestor function" (initialization model) strongly correlates with the final accuracy. Using a strong model for initialization and a weaker one for subsequent recombination yielded significantly higher accuracy than the reverse. This informed the decision to use the most capable (and typically most expensive) model for initialization.

3. **Weak Models as Aggregators with Strong Candidate Sets**: It was observed that the accuracy of recombination depends heavily on the quality of the input candidate set. On AIME 2025 and HMMT 2025, aggregation accuracy rose from 0% when no correct candidate was present to 100% when all candidates were correct. This suggests that if sufficiently strong candidates are identified within a group, a cheaper model can effectively perform the aggregation.

4. **Self- and Cross-Model Confidence as Fitness Proxies**: The analysis revealed that both self-model (generated by the model itself) and cross-model (generated by one model, scored by another) confidence signals are strong indicators of candidate quality. High-confidence subsets were found to be more likely to contain correct trajectories and lead to successful aggregation. This motivated the use of these confidence signals for fitness estimation and routing in the verifier-free setting.

#### 3.3. SQUEEZE EVOLVE Algorithm

Building on these insights, SQUEEZE EVOLVE instantiates the evolutionary operator `Φ_f` with specific components:

1. **Initialization**: The initial population `P^(0)` of `N` candidates is sampled from the strongest and typically most expensive model (`M2`), aligning with the finding that initialization quality is critical.

2. **Fitness Signal**:
- **Group Confidence (GC)**: Calculated from the top-`K_l` token log-probabilities (Eq. 3) for each token in a trajectory, then averaged to candidate-level `C(τ)` and group-level `GC(g)` (Eq. 4). High GC indicates a peaked predictive distribution, suggesting certainty. Self-confidence (scoring by the generating model) is cost-free; cross-model confidence (scoring by a different model) requires a prefill-only pass.
- **Group Diversity (D)**: Used when log-probabilities are unavailable, `D(g)` is simply the number of unique final answers within a group (Eq. 5). Low GC and high D both indicate uncertainty or conflict, signaling the need for a stronger model.

3. **Selection**: At each loop `t ≥ 1`, `M` groups of size `K` are formed from the current population. This can be done via uniform sampling (random `K`-subsets, as in RSA) or fitness-weighted sampling, where candidates with higher fitness (e.g., higher confidence) are more likely to be selected.

4. **Recombination and Routing**: Based on the group's fitness `F(g)`, a routing function assigns each group to one of three recombination tiers:
- `B2`: Recombined by the more expensive, stronger Model 2 (`M2`).
- `B1`: Recombined by the cheaper, weaker Model 1 (`M1`).
- `Blite`: Aggregated via a lightweight, non-LLM method (e.g., majority vote or random sampling) for groups with high consensus or low difficulty.
 An adaptive threshold `θ_q` (calculated as the `p`-th percentile of the fitness distribution for the current problem `q`) determines which non-lite groups are "easy" and routed to `M1`, and which are "hard" and routed to `M2` (Eq. 6, 7). The parameter `p` controls the routing aggressiveness.

5. **Population Update**: The recombined trajectories from all tiers are merged. The `replace` rule discards the previous population, while `accumulate` retains it, preserving high-quality solutions across generations.

#### 3.4. System Implementation

The practical effectiveness of SQUEEZE EVOLVE is supported by system-level co-design:

1. **Latency-Matched Serving**: Model 1 and Model 2 are deployed in separate GPU pools. These pools are sized such that they complete their assigned work in approximately the same wall-clock time per loop, preventing idle resources or bottlenecks.

2. **Confidence Scoring Optimization**: Self-confidence is inherently free. Cross-model confidence, requiring a single forward pass, is optimized as a prefill-only operation, batching `N` scoring calls per loop to minimize latency.

3. **Custom Confidence Engine**: A custom prefill path in vLLM is implemented to compute the confidence statistic directly on the GPU and return only the scalar result (∼100 bytes), instead of materializing and transferring full token-level logprob tensors (∼13 MB). This significantly reduces scoring latency (4-10x speedup) and enables confidence scoring for very large models where the native path would run out of memory.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SQUEEZE EVOLVE consistently improved the cost-capability frontier across various benchmarks, achieving new state-of-the-art results on several tasks.

1. **Overall Performance Improvements**:
- API cost reduction: Up to ~3x.
- Fixed-budget serving throughput increase: Up to ~10x.
- It is reported as the first verifier-free evolutionary method to match, and in some cases exceed, the performance of verifier-based evolutionary methods on discovery tasks.

2. **Math and Coding Benchmarks (AIME 2025, HMMT 2025, GPQA-Diamond, LiveCodeBench V6)**:
- **Homogeneous Pairs (Open-source + Open-source)**: SQUEEZE EVOLVE achieved 1.4–2.1x lower costs while matching or exceeding the accuracy of Model 2 alone. For instance, on AIME25, Qwen3-30B Instruct→Thinking achieved 90.7% accuracy at $0.66/problem (1.4x savings) compared to 89.2% at $0.94 for Model 2 alone.
- **Heterogeneous Pairs (Open-source + Closed-source)**: When pairing open-source models (e.g., GPT-OSS-20B) with proprietary models (e.g., GPT-5 mini), SQUEEZE EVOLVE delivered 1.4–3.3x savings. On AIME25, GPT-OSS-20B paired with GPT-5 mini achieved 95.4% accuracy at $0.50/problem (1.8x savings), surpassing Model 2 alone (94.2% at $0.89). The routing percentile `p` served as a tunable parameter for trading accuracy for cost.

3. **Multimodal Vision Tasks (MMMU-Pro, BabyVision)**:
- **Homogeneous Pair (Kimi-2.5 Instant / Thinking)**: Matched Model 2 accuracy on MMMU-Pro with 1.9x savings.
- **Heterogeneous Pair (Qwen3.5-35B (text-only) → Kimi-2.5 Thinking)**: Surpassed Model 2 on MMMU-Pro (79.1% vs. 78.6%) with 2.7x savings, even though Model 1 processed no images after initialization. This highlights the importance of initialization quality and that subsequent aggregation can be delegated to cheaper, text-only models once visual grounding is established.

4. **ARC-AGI-V2**:
- SQUEEZE EVOLVE achieved 97.5% accuracy at $7.74/task without code execution, marking a substantial improvement in the cost-capability frontier for this benchmark (3.7x savings over RSA with Gemini 3.1 Pro at $28.85/task for 93.3% accuracy).
- Further optimization by incorporating Gemini 3.0 Flash as Model 1 led to the same 97.5% accuracy at $5.93/task (4.9x savings). This result is reported to be competitive with or surpass performance/cost of code-execution-based methods (e.g., Confluence Lab at 97.9% for $11.77/task).

5. **Circle Packing (Scientific Discovery)**:
- SQUEEZE EVOLVE (using GPT-OSS-120B + 20B) achieved a score of 2.635896, which is comparable to, and in some cases exceeded, that of verifier-based approaches like AlphaEvolve (2.635862) and ShinkaEvolve (2.635982). This was achieved without in-flight program execution or relying on ground-truth feedback, demonstrating that model-intrinsic confidence signals can serve as a practical surrogate for explicit verification in discovery settings.

6. **System Results**:
- **Routing Overhead**: The confidence scoring and model dispatch mechanism introduced minimal overhead, adding only 2.4–4.3% to the end-to-end latency on average. This confirmed that the prefill-only nature of confidence scoring and batching strategies effectively minimized computational cost.
- **System Throughput**: Under a fixed total GPU budget, SQUEEZE EVOLVE demonstrated significant throughput improvements. The Qwen3-30B/235B pair achieved 4–10x throughput speedup, while the GPT-OSS 20B/120B pair yielded 1.4–3.4x speedup, attributed to efficient resource partitioning and latency-matched serving.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SQUEEZE EVOLVE framework presents a notable advancement in the domain of efficient and capable large language model inference, particularly for complex, iterative reasoning and discovery tasks. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling Practical Verifier-Free Evolution**: By addressing the dual bottlenecks of diversity collapse and cost inefficiency, SQUEEZE EVOLVE makes verifier-free evolutionary inference a more practical and economically viable approach. This is crucial for domains where external verification is prohibitively expensive, slow, or simply unavailable, such as in certain scientific research, drug discovery, or complex problem-solving scenarios.

2. **Optimizing the Cost-Capability Frontier**: The work demonstrates that strategic multi-model orchestration can significantly shift the cost-capability frontier for LLMs. Instead of solely relying on increasingly large and expensive models, SQUEEZE EVOLVE provides a mechanism to achieve higher capabilities at lower costs by intelligently leveraging a spectrum of available models (open-source, closed-source, varying sizes/modes). This has implications for broader access to and deployment of advanced AI capabilities.

3. **Leveraging Diverse Model Ecosystems**: SQUEEZE EVOLVE naturally supports deployments involving heterogeneous model ecosystems. Its ability to integrate different models, including those with varying reasoning modes (e.g., "Instruct" vs. "Thinking") or even multimodal capabilities alongside text-only models, allows practitioners to select and combine the most suitable models for specific tasks, thereby enhancing flexibility and resource utilization.

4. **Advancing Scientific Discovery**: The performance on the circle packing problem, where SQUEEZE EVOLVE matched or exceeded verifier-based evolutionary methods without explicit verification, suggests a significant potential impact on scientific discovery tasks. The finding that model-intrinsic confidence can serve as an effective surrogate for external reward signals opens new avenues for using LLMs in problem-solving where immediate ground-truth feedback is not feasible.

5. **Robustness and Efficiency in Complex Reasoning**: The consistent improvements across diverse benchmarks (math, coding, multimodal vision, visual reasoning) indicate that SQUEEZE EVOLVE offers a more robust and efficient approach to complex, multi-step reasoning. The adaptive routing based on group confidence or diversity allows the system to dynamically allocate computational power where it has the highest marginal utility, leading to more intelligent resource consumption.

6. **System-Level Innovations for Deployment**: The co-design of the serving system, including latency-matched GPU pools and a custom confidence engine, is a practical contribution. These innovations ensure that the algorithmic benefits of multi-model orchestration translate into tangible gains in terms of reduced latency and increased throughput in real-world deployments, making the framework ready for practical adoption.

In summary, SQUEEZE EVOLVE provides a unified, efficient, and versatile framework that democratizes access to powerful evolutionary inference by making it significantly more cost-effective and applicable to a wider range of challenging problems, particularly in verifier-free settings.
