# Integrated Hierarchical Decision-Making in Inverse Kinematic Planning and Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced an optimization framework, Sparse Hierarchical Nonlinear Programming (SH-NLP), which integrates hierarchical decision-making directly into whole-body inverse kinematic planning and control for robots. Their S-SHQP solver, leveraging a continuous "ell"_0-norm approximation, demonstrates real-time performance and high accuracy across diverse tasks, enabling autonomous decision-making with kinematic feasibility.

## Problem

- Existing Mixed-Integer Nonlinear Programming (MINLP) methods for discrete decision-making are computationally intractable for real-time robotic applications due to high complexity.
- Approximating the "ell"_0-norm with the "ell"_1-norm often leads to inaccurate or suboptimal solutions and redundant resource allocation, especially for nonlinear decision functions.
- Separating decision-making from whole-body inverse kinematics risks selecting kinematically unreachable targets or underutilizing the robot's workspace due to reliance on approximate reachability.

## Method

- Developed **Sparse Hierarchical Nonlinear Programming (SH-NLP)** to tightly integrate hierarchical decision-making and whole-body inverse kinematics, using a continuous ==logarithmic surrogate function== to approximate the ℓ₀-norm for true sparsity in selection tasks.
- Introduced the ==Sequential Sparse Hierarchical Quadratic Programming (S-SHQP)== solver, an iterative method based on SQP principles, featuring a dedicated ==interior-point solver (NQP)== for efficient subproblem resolution.
- Implemented ==parallel hierarchical decision-making== with a modified weighting scheme to ensure unique allocation among multiple selection groups and avoid conflicts.

## Results

- S-SHQP achieved decision-making errors below **1e-5 m** for pick-and-place tasks with up to **100** objects, outperforming IPOPT (often failed or inaccurate), NLOPT (lower accuracy), and MINLP (timed out).
- For a humanoid robot selecting from **200** candidate locations, the NQP solver completed the task in **0.23 seconds**, outperforming hierarchical versions of PIQP (**0.33 s**) and MOSEK (**0.59 s**).
- The framework enabled real-time object tracking by a manipulator with an approximate **0.8 ms** control loop time and allowed a humanoid to successfully interact with **92 out of 100** descending objects, where ℓ₁/ℓ₂-norm formulations failed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly integrating decision-making with whole-body inverse kinematics using a continuous "ell"_0-norm approximation provides accurate, kinematically feasible solutions without relying on reachability approximations.
> - The proposed NQP interior-point solver, when integrated into S-SHQP, achieves linear scaling with the number of sparse constraints, enabling real-time performance for problems with many candidate choices.
> - Hierarchical processing, facilitated by nullspace projection, effectively reduces problem dimensionality at each priority level, enhancing computational efficiency while preserving higher-priority task solutions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics frequently involves decision-making processes integrated with inverse kinematics (IK), particularly in scenarios requiring sparse control or selection among multiple discrete options for end-effector placement. Existing approaches to these problems include Mixed-Integer Nonlinear Programming (MINLP), sparse optimization using the $\ell_1$-norm, and methods that separate decision-making from inverse kinematics.

MINLP techniques are capable of solving decision-making problems to global optimality, but they incur substantial computational costs, rendering them impractical for real-time robotic applications, especially with high dimensionality. Sparse optimization methods, such as those employing the $\ell_1$-norm, offer greater efficiency. However, the $\ell_1$-norm can introduce inaccuracies, lead to redundant resource allocation (e.g., selecting multiple end-effector locations when only one is required), and may not be versatile enough for complex nonlinear problem formulations. Furthermore, many $\ell_1$-norm approaches primarily address linear problems or rely on continuous surrogate functions for the $\ell_0$-norm.

Another common strategy involves approximating kinematic reachability, which decouples decision-making from the robot's whole-body IK. This separation can result in selected end-effector locations being unreachable if the approximations are not sufficiently conservative, or underutilizing the workspace if they are overly restrictive.

This work addresses these identified limitations by proposing a framework that integrates hierarchical decision-making directly into whole-body inverse kinematic planning and control. It aims to overcome the computational burden of MINLP, the inaccuracies and lack of versatility of $\ell_1$-norm methods, and the risks associated with separating decision-making from full kinematic reasoning. The authors specifically highlight their use of the $\ell_0$-norm, which is less commonly applied in robotics, to enable versatile and accurate decision-making in hierarchical nonlinear contexts.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient and versatile nonlinear programming framework that tightly integrates hierarchical decision-making with whole-body inverse kinematic planning and control. The authors aim to enable robots to make autonomous, real-time decisions in complex scenarios while maintaining kinematic feasibility and computational efficiency.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in current robotics research:

- **Computational Intractability of Existing Methods:** Problems involving discrete decision-making, such as selecting optimal end-effector locations or activating a minimal set of joints, are often formulated as MINLPs. While MINLP can yield globally optimal solutions, its computational complexity makes it unsuitable for real-time applications or problems with a large number of discrete choices. This significantly restricts its applicability in dynamic robotic environments.

- **Inaccuracies and Limitations of $\ell_1$-norm Approximations:** Many sparse optimization techniques approximate the $\ell_0$-norm (which counts non-zero elements, indicating true sparsity) with the $\ell_1$-norm. While the $\ell_1$-norm can be solved more efficiently, it often results in suboptimal or inaccurate solutions, and can lead to redundant allocations (e.g., a robot attempting to use multiple end-effectors for a single task). Furthermore, the $\ell_1$-norm is not always agnostic to the specific formulation of decision-functions (e.g., quadratic least-squares), limiting its versatility.

- **Decoupling of Decision-Making and Inverse Kinematics:** Current approaches often treat decision-making (e.g., selecting a target location) and inverse kinematics separately. This separation frequently relies on kinematic reachability approximations, which can be either too optimistic (leading to unreachable targets) or too conservative (underutilizing the robot's workspace). An integrated approach that simultaneously considers whole-body IK and decision-making could provide immediate certificates of kinematic reachability, avoiding these issues.

- **Absence of Hierarchical Nonlinear Decision-Making Frameworks:** The authors identify a gap in the literature regarding a unified framework that can address sparse hierarchical nonlinear decision-making problems, particularly those involving the $\ell_0$-norm, in a computationally efficient manner. Such a framework is necessary for complex robotic tasks where multiple tasks with varying priorities and discrete choices must be resolved concurrently.

To address these motivations, the work focuses on two specific use-cases:
- **Planning (SHIK-P):** Directly solving nonlinear sparse hierarchical IK and decision-making problems for offline trajectory generation or complex task planning.
- **Control (SHIK-C):** Solving sparse hierarchical IK and decision-making instantaneously for real-time, closed-loop robot control, allowing immediate reaction to environmental changes.

The core objectives include developing a solver that offers real-time performance even with a large number of candidate choices, ensures versatile application across different decision-function formulations, supports parallel decision-making while avoiding conflicts, and demonstrates these capabilities across various robotic platforms and tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology presented in this paper centers on a novel optimization framework called Sparse Hierarchical Nonlinear Programming (SH-NLP) and its associated solver, Sequential Sparse Hierarchical Quadratic Programming (S-SHQP).

#### 3.1. Sparse Hierarchical Nonlinear Programming (SH-NLP)

The SH-NLP framework is designed to minimize the $\ell_0$-norm of slack variables across multiple priority levels ($l = 1, \ldots, p$). The general form is:
$\min_{\chi,(\nu_i)_{i \in C_l}} \|\left(\nu_i\right)_{i \in C_l}\|_0, \quad l = 1, \ldots, p$
subject to constraints that include task functions $f_i(\chi) \geqq \nu_i$ for the current level $l$, and consistency constraints ($f_j(\chi) \ge 0$, $f_k(\chi) = \nu_k^*$) for inactive and active constraints from previous levels. The $\nu_i$ are slack variables that allow for controlled relaxation of constraints. The goal is to find an optimal point where as many $\nu_i$ as possible are zero, satisfying the corresponding constraints.

#### 3.2. $\ell_0$-norm Approximation

Since direct minimization of the $\ell_0$-norm is combinatorial and intractable for large problems, the authors approximate it using a continuous logarithmic surrogate function:
$\min_{\chi,(\nu_i),(\tau_i)} \sum_{i \in C_l} \log(\tau_i + \xi), \quad l = 1, \ldots, p$
subject to bounds on $\nu_i$ ($-\tau_i \le \nu_i \le \tau_i$) and the original task constraints. The auxiliary variable $\tau_i$ and the bounds approximate the discontinuous absolute value function, promoting sparsity. A small constant $\xi > 0$ ensures numerical stability.

#### 3.3. Sequential Sparse Hierarchical Quadratic Programming (S-SHQP) Solver

The SH-NLP is solved using S-SHQP, an iterative method based on sequential quadratic programming (SQP) principles. At each iteration $\iota$, S-SHQP approximates the nonlinear problem with a Sparse Hierarchical Quadratic Program (SHQP) subproblem around the current state.

#### 3.3.1. SHQP Subproblem Formulation

The SHQP subproblem results from applying Newton's method to the first-order conditions of the logarithmic approximation. It is formulated as:
$\min_{z_l,(v_i),(t_i)} \sum_{i \in C_l} \omega_i t_i + 0.5(x_{l-1}^* + N_{l-1} z_l)^T H_l (x_{l-1}^* + N_{l-1} z_l)$
subject to $ -t_i \le v_i \le t_i $, linearized task constraints ($\tilde{a}_i^T z_l - \breve{b}_i \geqq v_i$, etc.), and a trust-region constraint.
- **Weights $\omega_i$**: Defined as $1/(t_i + \xi)$, these weights guide the sparsity promotion.
- **Nullspace Projection**: The variable $x_l$ is represented as $x_{l-1}^* + N_{l-1} z_l$, where $x_{l-1}^*$ is the optimal primal from higher priority levels, and $N_{l-1}$ is a nullspace basis for active constraints from those levels. This projection reduces the problem dimensionality ($z_l \in \mathbb{R}^{n_r}$), effectively isolating the remaining variables for the current level.
- **Hierarchical Lagrangian Hessian $H_l$**: Captures second-order information of constraints, crucial for convergence, especially in singular tasks.

#### 3.3.2. Solver Operation

- **Planning (SHIK-P):** The S-SHQP uses a Hierarchical Step Filter (HSF) and a trust-region mechanism. The HSF evaluates both feasibility and optimality, accepting or rejecting steps. The trust-region radius is adapted based on step acceptance. Once converged, optimal slacks are stored, and sparse constraints with zero slacks are used to remove non-sparse ones from consideration in the current level.
- **Control (SHIK-C):** For real-time control, each step is accepted directly. A constant trust-region radius is used to maintain approximate feasibility.

#### 3.4. Parallel Hierarchical Decision-Making

To prevent multiple selection groups (e.g., different end-effectors) from converging to the same candidate location, a modified weighting scheme is introduced. The weights $(\tilde{\omega}_{s_c})$ are computed using masks $(\phi_{s_c})$ which represent the minimum value of constraints in each set $S_c$. These masks are computed sequentially, and indices already chosen by higher-priority or previously processed groups are set to zero in subsequent masks. This ensures unique allocation.

#### 3.5. Implications for Newton's Method and Hierarchical Decision-making

The framework leverages feasible entries within selection groups to avoid unnecessary activation of Newton's method (i.e., using the full Lagrangian Hessian). If a feasible constraint is found within a group, it is added to the active set, and other (infeasible) constraints are discarded. This strategy is based on the principle that once one constraint in a selection group is fixed, the values for others are implicitly determined.

#### 3.6. NQP: An Interior-Point Method for SHQP

A dedicated interior-point solver, NQP, is developed for SHQP subproblems.
- **Theorem 1**: This theorem demonstrates that for $\omega_i > 0$ and infeasible cases ($v_i \ne 0$), $v_i$ is strictly bounded by either $t_i$ or $-t_i$. This implies that only one bound of each auxiliary constraint pair ($-t_i \le v_i \le t_i$) can be active if $v_i \neq 0$. If $v_i = 0$, then $t_i = 0$. This allows for simplification: once $v_i^*$ is determined, $t_i^*$ can be set to $|v_i^*|$, making $\omega_i t_i^*$ a constant term in the cost function for lower priority levels.
- **Reformulation**: This observation allows each level's objective function (which includes the linear term $\sum \omega_i t_i$) to be reformulated into a least-squares problem for subsequent levels, similar to traditional Hierarchical Least-Squares Programs (HLSP).
- **KKT System**: The interior-point method solves the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions of the augmented Lagrangian, which includes log-barrier functions for inequality constraints and slack variables.
- **Computational Efficiency**: The Newton step in NQP requires $O(n_r^3)$ operations (e.g., for Cholesky decomposition), where $n_r$ is the number of projected variables. Crucially, the number of sparse constraints $|C_l|$ affects the computation only linearly ($O(n_r^2 |C_l|)$), rather than cubically ($O((n+|C_l|)^3)$) as in methods that don't eliminate auxiliary variables.

#### 3.7. Implementation and Benchmarking

The algorithms are implemented in C++ using the Eigen library, with SH-NLP and SHQP computations leveraging Pinocchio for rigid body dynamics. NQP is compared against state-of-the-art QP solvers like (H-)MOSEK and (H-)PIQP. All solvers benefit from Theorem 1 to avoid propagating auxiliary variables.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the S-SHQP framework across numerical test functions and robotic applications provides several key findings.

#### 4.1. SHIK-P of Manipulator for Pick-and-Place (Section VIII-A)

- **Accuracy and Iterations:** For a pick-and-place task with UFactory's xarm6, involving selection from 10 to 100 objects, S-SHQP reliably converged to a decision-making error below $1 \times 10^{-5}$ m, requiring 37 to 67 iterations.
- **Comparison with NLP/MINLP:** In contrast, IPOPT converged accurately for only 4 out of 10-100 object instances and often exceeded the maximum iteration count. NLOPT converged faster (5-15 iterations) but with significantly lower accuracy (3 mm to 1.2 cm error). MINLP solver SCIP timed out for 70 objects, demonstrating the computational advantage of S-SHQP for such problems.
- **$\ell_0$-norm Versatility:** The study indicated that $\ell_1$-norm and $\ell_2$-norm formulations yielded inaccurate results for this problem due to the quadratic nature of the decision function, highlighting the versatility and robustness of the $\ell_0$-norm.

#### 4.2. Hierarchical Decision-Making with Test Functions (Section VIII-B)

- S-SHQP with NQP successfully solved a hierarchy of 10 test functions with 10 variables.
- It correctly identified and selected zero constraints (feasible solutions) for both equality and inequality constraints across different levels.
- Infeasible selection constraints were effectively removed, preventing unnecessary Hessian activation and improving hierarchical decision-making for lower-priority levels.

#### 4.3. SHIK-P of Humanoid Robot with Many Candidate Locations (Section VIII-C)

- **Performance:** The Unitree G1 humanoid robot computed postures while autonomously selecting from 200 potential Cartesian locations for each end-effector. The problem was solved by NQP in 0.23 s over 89 S-SHQP iterations.
- **Solver Comparison:** NQP outperformed hierarchical versions of PIQP (0.33 s, 90 iterations) and MOSEK (0.59 s, 46 iterations).
- **Scalability:** The computation time exhibited a linear relationship with the number of sparse $\ell_0$-norm constraints, from 4 to 1604 (1 to 400 locations per end-effector). NQP consistently outperformed H-PIQP and H-MOSEK, which do not exploit this structured sparsity.
- **Robustness:** In 100 simulations, S-SHQP achieved a 95% success rate with decision-making errors below $1 \times 10^{-5}$ m for foot placement, demonstrating robustness. Failure cases were attributed to the local nature of the solver in certain initial configurations.

#### 4.4. SHIK-C of Manipulator for Object Tracking (Section VIII-D)

- **Real-time Control:** A UFactory xarm6 manipulator tracked one of two moving targets in real-time. The control loop time using NQP was approximately 0.8 ms.
- **Sparse Actuation:** An $\ell_0$-norm regularization term successfully induced sparse kinematic control, leading to inactive joints (e.g., $\chi_4, \chi_5, \chi_6$) when possible, confirming the parsimonious control objective. This came with a slight, expected decrease in tracking accuracy.

#### 4.5. SHIK-C of Humanoid Robot with Many Candidate Locations (Section VIII-E)

- **Dynamic Task:** For a G1 humanoid tasked with touching 100 descending objects, the $\ell_0$-norm formulation enabled the robot to successfully interact with 92 out of 100 objects.
- **$\ell_0$ vs. $\ell_1$/$\ell_2$:** Formulating the selection constraint with $\ell_1$ or $\ell_2$-norms resulted in failure to contact any objects, further demonstrating the effectiveness of the $\ell_0$-norm for true selection tasks.
- **Solver Efficiency:** The NQP solver processed each SHQP subproblem in 1.6 $\pm$ 0.3 ms, significantly faster than H-PIQP (2.2 $\pm$ 0.7 ms) and H-MOSEK (8.3 $\pm$ 2.7 ms).

#### 4.6. Potential Applications (Section VIII-F)

- **Multi-Manipulator Sorting:** The framework supports scenarios where multiple manipulators collaboratively clear objects from a conveyor, with selected objects being excluded from consideration for other robots, suitable for industrial sorting.
- **Bi-manual Grasping:** The method demonstrated a humanoid robot grasping a randomly rotated box, with the left and right hands simultaneously selecting opposite sides, formulated within a single $\ell_0$-norm constraint.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a framework for integrating hierarchical decision-making within model-based robotics, offering several significant contributions and potential impacts:

- **Integration of Decision-Making and Whole-Body Control:** The SH-NLP framework directly integrates autonomous, discrete decision-making (e.g., selecting from multiple end-effector locations) with whole-body inverse kinematic planning and control. This contrasts with approaches that separate these concerns, providing an immediate certificate of kinematic reachability and avoiding approximations.
- **Enhanced Computational Efficiency:** The proposed S-SHQP solver, particularly with the NQP interior-point method, demonstrates improved computational performance. Its linear scaling with respect to the number of sparse constraints allows for efficient real-time decision-making even when faced with a large number of candidate choices, which is crucial for complex robotic systems operating in dynamic environments.
- **Versatility and Accuracy through $\ell_0$-norm:** By leveraging a continuous approximation of the $\ell_0$-norm, the framework enables true sparse selection and is agnostic to the specific mathematical formulation of decision-functions (e.g., quadratic least-squares tasks). This versatility addresses a limitation of $\ell_1$-norm-based methods, which can be inaccurate or yield redundant allocations for certain nonlinear problems.
- **Support for Complex Robotic Behaviors:** The ability to handle prioritized tasks, parallel decision-making (e.g., multiple end-effectors selecting from the same set of candidates without double-allocation), and real-time control enables more sophisticated and autonomous robotic behaviors. This has direct applicability in areas such as collaborative manipulation, dynamic object interaction, and complex locomotion tasks.
- **Potential for Industrial and Service Robotics:** The demonstrated applications, including pick-and-place with many candidate objects, multi-manipulator sorting on a conveyor belt, and bi-manual grasping of arbitrarily oriented objects, suggest significant utility in industrial automation, logistics, and service robotics where rapid, accurate, and autonomous decision-making is essential.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework unifies model-based optimal control with discrete contact planning, opening avenues for further research. The differentiability of the $\ell_0$-norm approximation, unlike mixed-integer programming, is highlighted as a feature amenable to integration with reinforcement learning pipelines, potentially leading to more robust and intelligently trained robotic systems, for instance, through techniques like Sobolev training.
