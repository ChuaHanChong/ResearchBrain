# Adaptive Prompt Tuning: Vision Guided Prompt Tuning with Cross-Attention for Fine-Grained Few-Shot Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The Adaptive Prompt Tuning (APT) framework enhances fine-grained few-shot learning by dynamically refining text prompts with visual guidance through a cross-attention mechanism, built upon the CLIP model. It outperforms existing prompt tuning methods on challenging datasets and incorporates Monte-Carlo Dropout for efficient uncertainty quantification, providing reliable confidence estimates.

## Problem

- Existing prompt tuning methods, like CoOp and VPT, struggle with fine-grained few-shot classification due to their static nature, leading to poor generalization on tasks with subtle visual distinctions and high intra-class variance.
- Vision-Language Models often provide overconfident predictions without reliable measures of confidence, hindering their trustworthiness and applicability in real-world, high-stakes scenarios.
- Integrating robust uncertainty quantification into VLM-based few-shot learning, particularly with computationally efficient methods, remains a challenge.

## Method

- The ==Adaptive Prompt Tuning (APT)== framework introduces a learnable ==cross-attention module== that dynamically tunes initial text prompts based on real-time visual input from the frozen CLIP image encoder.
- Dropout layers are strategically placed within the cross-attention module and kept active during inference to implement ==Monte-Carlo Dropout (MCD)==, enabling the quantification of predictive uncertainty.
- The final classification is performed by computing cosine similarity between the image's `[CLS]` token feature and the dynamically tuned text features, followed by a softmax function.

## Results

- APT consistently achieved superior accuracy on fine-grained datasets, notably on FGVC Aircraft (e.g., **47%** at 16 shots, outperforming Zero-shot **17%**) and CUBirds (**77%** at 16 shots), surpassing state-of-the-art prompt tuning methods.
- The framework demonstrated improved generalization capabilities to new, unseen classes, achieving the highest F1-Mean scores on FGVC Aircraft (36.46) and Oxford Flowers (83.23) in base-to-new evaluation setups.
- Integration of Monte-Carlo Dropout successfully quantified uncertainty, with Expected Calibration Error (ECE) decreasing with more training data, and the model exhibited high uncertainty for out-of-distribution inputs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically adjusting text prompts with visual information via a cross-attention mechanism allows for better adaptation to subtle visual cues, crucial for fine-grained few-shot learning tasks.
> - Monte-Carlo Dropout offers an efficient and scalable approach to provide well-calibrated confidence estimates and quantify uncertainty in large pre-trained Vision-Language Models like CLIP.
> - Models equipped with effective uncertainty quantification can distinguish between in-distribution and out-of-distribution data, expressing higher uncertainty for unfamiliar inputs, thereby increasing system reliability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The present work is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields in artificial intelligence, primarily computer vision, natural language processing, few-shot learning, fine-grained image recognition, and uncertainty quantification.

**a. The Rise of Foundation Models and Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** In recent years, the AI landscape has been dramatically reshaped by large-scale pre-trained models, often termed "foundation models." These models, trained on vast datasets, demonstrate remarkable generalization capabilities. Among these, Contrastive Language-Image Pre-Training (CLIP) by OpenAI stands out as a highly influential Vision-Language Model (VLM). CLIP learns a joint embedding space for both textual and visual data, allowing it to perform tasks like zero-shot classification by comparing image features directly with text embeddings of class labels. This paper leverages CLIP as its foundational architecture, recognizing its power but also seeking to push its boundaries in challenging scenarios.

**b. Advancements in Few-Shot Learning:** A critical challenge in machine learning is the ability to learn effectively from limited labeled data, a problem known as few-shot learning. Traditional deep neural networks often overfit in such low-data regimes. Foundation models, including CLIP, offer a promising avenue for few-shot learning by adapting their pre-trained knowledge to new tasks. This paper contributes directly to this area by proposing a more effective adaptation strategy for CLIP.

**c. Evolution of Prompt Tuning:** A popular and efficient method for adapting foundation models like CLIP is "prompt tuning." Instead of full model fine-tuning, prompt tuning involves optimizing a small set of learnable parameters (prompts) to guide the pre-trained model for specific downstream tasks. The authors acknowledge pioneering works in this domain, such as Context Optimization (CoOp) and Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT). CoOp learns learnable context vectors for text prompts, while VPT introduces learnable visual tokens for the image encoder. This paper positions itself as an advancement beyond these methods, arguing that their static nature or reliance on visual tokens can limit generalization, especially in complex scenarios.

**d. The Niche of Fine-Grained Image Recognition:** Within computer vision, fine-grained image recognition is a particularly challenging sub-field. It requires distinguishing between highly similar subcategories (e.g., different bird species or aircraft models) where differences are often subtle and localized. Existing prompt tuning methods struggle with the high intra-class variance and low inter-class distinctions characteristic of fine-grained datasets. This research specifically targets this gap, aiming to develop a prompt tuning method that can dynamically adapt to these nuanced visual features.

**e. Growing Importance of Uncertainty Quantification (UQ):** Beyond mere accuracy, the trustworthiness and reliability of AI predictions are paramount, especially in critical applications like medicine or autonomous systems. Traditional neural networks often produce overconfident predictions, even when incorrect. Uncertainty Quantification (UQ) aims to provide a measure of confidence in a model's outputs. This paper integrates UQ into its proposed framework, addressing a relatively underexplored aspect within the domain of VLM-based few-shot learning. While other works, like Miao et al. (2024), have explored UQ in this context, they often rely on computationally intensive methods (e.g., Gaussian Processes). This paper offers a more efficient UQ approach using Monte-Carlo Dropout.

In essence, this work builds upon the success of CLIP and prompt tuning for few-shot learning, specifically innovating to tackle the difficulties of fine-grained classification. It further differentiates itself by integrating a robust and computationally efficient uncertainty quantification mechanism, contributing to the development of more reliable and trustworthy AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and underlying motivations of this research can be summarized as follows:

**a. Enhance Few-Shot Fine-Grained Classification Performance with Dynamic Prompt Tuning:**
- **Motivation:** Existing prompt tuning methods like CoOp and VPT, while effective, rely on static or fixed prompts once trained. This rigidity makes them prone to poor generalization, especially in fine-grained classification tasks where subtle visual distinctions require more adaptive interpretation. Datasets with high intra-class variance (e.g., different types of aircraft within the same model family) and low inter-class differences (e.g., visually similar bird species) demand that the model dynamically adjust its understanding of class definitions based on the specific visual input. Static prompts struggle to capture these nuances, potentially leading to overfitting on limited examples.
- **Objective:** To introduce a novel, dynamic prompt tuning mechanism, called Adaptive Prompt Tuning (APT), that leverages real-time visual inputs to refine text prompts. This mechanism aims to enable an image-specific alignment of textual features with visual information, thereby improving classification accuracy and generalization on fine-grained few-shot learning tasks.

**b. Improve the Trustworthiness and Reliability of Model Predictions through Uncertainty Quantification (UQ):**
- **Motivation:** Deep Neural Networks are notoriously overconfident, often making confident but incorrect predictions. In high-stakes applications (e.g., medical diagnosis, autonomous driving), knowing when a model's prediction can be trusted is as crucial as the prediction itself. While UQ is a recognized need, its integration into VLM-based few-shot learning, particularly with efficient methods, is not widespread. Previous UQ approaches for VLMs might be memory-intensive (e.g., Gaussian Processes), limiting their practical applicability.
- **Objective:** To integrate Monte-Carlo Dropout (MCD) into the APT framework to produce well-calibrated confidence estimates and quantify predictive uncertainty. This aims to provide valuable insights into the model’s predictive confidence, enabling users to identify when predictions are trustworthy and when additional verification or human oversight is necessary, without incurring significant computational overhead.

In summary, the authors are motivated to overcome the limitations of static prompt tuning in handling the complexity of fine-grained classification, simultaneously addressing the critical need for reliable uncertainty estimates in AI systems to foster greater trust and applicability in real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, Adaptive Prompt Tuning (APT), introduces a novel dynamic prompt tuning mechanism for CLIP, enhanced with Monte-Carlo Dropout for Uncertainty Quantification.

**a. Foundation: Contrastive Language-Image Pre-Training (CLIP)**
The method builds upon the CLIP model, which consists of two main components:
- **Image Encoder:** A Vision Transformer (ViT) that processes input images by dividing them into patches and encoding them into a high-dimensional feature space. It also extracts a `[CLS]` token representing the global image context.
- **Text Encoder:** A Transformer-based model that processes natural language text prompts (e.g., "A photo of a [CLASS]") into the same high-dimensional feature space.
CLIP is pre-trained using contrastive learning to align semantically related image-text pairs in a shared embedding space. For classification, CLIP typically uses cosine similarity between image features and static text prompt features.

**b. Adaptive Prompt Tuning (APT) Architecture (Figure 1)**
The core innovation of APT lies in its dynamic adjustment of text prompts based on visual input through a cross-attention mechanism:
- **Feature Extraction:**
 - An input image `x` is processed by the frozen CLIP image encoder ($f_\phi$) to produce image features `z` (including the sequence of patches and the `[CLS]` token).
 - Initial text prompts `Y` (e.g., "A photo of a [CLASS]") are processed by the frozen CLIP text encoder ($f_\psi$) to produce text features `W`.
- **Cross-Attention Module:** This is the learnable component of APT, designed to dynamically refine text features based on visual information.
 - **Queries (Q):** Created from the initial text features `W`.
 - **Keys (K) and Values (V):** Created from the image features `z` (specifically, the sequence of patches from the ViT).
 - The cross-attention mechanism allows the text encoder's representation (queries) to attend to the visual features (keys and values), effectively enabling the model to dynamically "tune" the text prompt for the specific image at hand. This dynamic interaction helps in focusing on relevant visual cues for fine-grained distinctions.
 - **Layer Structure:** The cross-attention module includes standard Transformer components: multi-head cross-attention, layer normalization, a feed-forward layer (for non-linearity), and skip connections.
 - **Output:** The module outputs a set of "tuned text features" `W'`, which are refined representations optimized for the downstream classification task.
- **Classification:** The final classification is performed by computing the cosine similarity between the image's `[CLS]` token feature and the `W'` tuned text features, followed by a softmax function to obtain class probabilities, similar to standard CLIP inference.

**c. Uncertainty Quantification (UQ) with Monte-Carlo Dropout (MCD)**
- **Integration:** Dropout layers are strategically placed within the cross-attention module. Crucially, these dropout layers are kept active *during inference time* to implement MCD.
- **Stochasticity:** By performing multiple forward passes (M samples) with dropout enabled for a single input image, APT generates a distribution of output probabilities for each class.
- **Prediction and Uncertainty Estimation:**
 - The mean probability for each class (`$\bar{p}_k$`) is computed across the M samples.
 - The predicted class (`$\hat{y}$`) is the one with the maximum mean probability.
 - Uncertainty is quantified using the **entropy (S)** of the mean probabilities (`$S = -\sum_k \bar{p}_k \log \bar{p}_k$`).
 - Confidence is derived by normalizing entropy: `conf = $1 - S / S_{max}$`.
- **Evaluation Metrics for UQ:**
 - **Reliability Plots:** Qualitatively assess calibration by plotting binned accuracy vs. confidence.
 - **Expected Calibration Error (ECE):** A quantitative metric that sums the absolute differences between accuracy and confidence across prediction bins. Lower ECE indicates better calibration.
 - **Confidence vs. Uncertainty Plots:** Visualize the relationship between model confidence and uncertainty for correct and incorrect predictions, aiming for low uncertainty at high confidence.
 - **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Detection:** Assesses the model's ability to express high uncertainty for unfamiliar (OOD) data, evaluated using Confidence vs. Entropy plots and qualitative analysis of prediction histograms.

**d. Datasets:**
The methodology is evaluated on:
- **Fine-Grained Datasets:** Caltech-UCSD Birds (CUBirds), Oxford Flowers, and FGVC Aircraft (known for high intra-class variance).
- **General Classification Dataset:** Caltech101 (also used for OOD detection).

**e. Implementation Details:**
- CLIP with a ViT-B/16 image encoder and 8-head multi-head attention.
- Dropout rate of 20% within APT's cross-attention module for MCD.
- Standard CLIP image preprocessing and data augmentation (random resized cropping, flipping).
- Training schedule adapted to few-shot settings (50-150 epochs depending on shots per class).
- SGD optimizer with a learning rate of 0.001 and cosine decay scheduler.
- Experiments repeated 3 times with different sampling seeds, and average performance reported.

This comprehensive approach allows APT to dynamically leverage visual context for prompt refinement, addressing the specific challenges of fine-grained classification while simultaneously providing robust uncertainty estimates for trustworthy predictions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings across few-shot learning performance, generalization capabilities, and the quality of uncertainty quantification.

**a. Few-Shot Learning Performance (Accuracy):**
- **Overall Superiority on Fine-Grained Tasks:** Adaptive Prompt Tuning (APT) consistently outperformed state-of-the-art prompt tuning methods like CoOp and VPT, especially on datasets characterized by high intra-class variance and subtle inter-class differences.
 - **FGVC Aircraft:** APT showed the most significant performance gains. Starting at 27% accuracy with one shot, it rose to 47% at 16 shots, marking a substantial improvement over the Zero-shot baseline (17%) and significantly outperforming CoOp and VPT. This highlights APT's effectiveness in leveraging visual cues for complex variations within classes.
 - **CUBirds:** APT demonstrated robust performance, achieving 56% accuracy at one shot and 77% at 16 shots, outperforming CoOp. Its adaptability across varying degrees of class variance was evident.
 - **Oxford Flowers:** On this dataset with more distinct inter-class features, APT achieved very high accuracy (84% at one shot, 97% at 16 shots), slightly leading CoOp. The high performance across all models suggests that dynamic tuning might offer marginal gains when class features are already well-separated.
- **Modest Gains on General Classification (Caltech101):** For the Caltech101 dataset, which features a wider variety of objects and backgrounds rather than fine-grained distinctions, APT showed modest improvements (93.8% at one shot to 95.8% at 16 shots) that were comparable to CoOp and VPT. The authors attribute this to the fact that dynamic prompt adjustment might offer less advantage when class boundaries are clearer, and background noise could potentially interfere with adaptive tuning.

**b. Generalization to New Classes (Base-to-New Setup):**
- **Improved Generalization:** When evaluated on base (seen during training) and new (unseen during training) classes, APT generally demonstrated better generalization compared to CoOp and CoCoOp.
 - **FGVC Aircraft:** APT achieved the highest F1-Mean score (36.46), significantly outperforming CoOp (28.75) and CoCoOp (27.74). This indicates a strong ability to generalize to novel aircraft types.
 - **Oxford Flowers:** APT again led with an F1-Mean of 83.23, surpassing CoOp (74.06) and CoCoOp (81.71), demonstrating robustness across flower categories.
 - **CUBirds:** APT showed competitive generalization (F1-Mean 57.02) compared to the CLIP baseline (58.06) and outperformed CoOp (48.60) and CoCoOp (14.40). While strong in base classes, generalization to new bird species remained a notable challenge, reflecting the inherent complexity of bird classification.

**c. Uncertainty Quantification (UQ) Analysis:**
- **Expected Calibration Error (ECE):**
 - For Oxford Flowers and CUBirds, ECE consistently decreased as the number of training samples increased, stabilizing at low values (around 0.01-0.02), indicating improved calibration with more data.
 - FGVC Aircraft showed a more gradual improvement in ECE, suggesting it poses a greater challenge for calibration due to its inherent complexity.
- **Reliability Plots:**
 - The plots revealed that APT was slightly overconfident on CUBirds and FGVC Aircraft, particularly in higher confidence bins.
 - For Oxford Flowers, the model showed a mixed profile, being well-calibrated in high-confidence bins but exhibiting under- or over-confidence in lower confidence ranges, likely due to the dataset's high overall accuracy.
- **Confidence vs. Uncertainty Plots:**
 - These plots consistently demonstrated an inverse relationship: high confidence correlated with low uncertainty, and vice-versa.
 - Correct predictions clustered in regions of high confidence and low uncertainty, while incorrect predictions were more dispersed with higher uncertainty.
 - The "cone shape" observed indicates the model's ability to differentiate between "easy" (high confidence, low uncertainty) and "difficult" (lower confidence, but still low uncertainty, implying awareness of difficulty) examples.
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Detection:**
 - When tested with OOD data (Caltech101 images after training on fine-grained datasets), the model exhibited consistently high uncertainty and clustered predictions around low confidence. This behavior, reflected in dispersed prediction histograms, confirms its ability to correctly identify unfamiliar inputs and express a lack of confidence, which is a crucial aspect of reliable AI.

In essence, APT successfully addresses the core problem of fine-grained few-shot classification by leveraging dynamic visual guidance. Furthermore, its integrated MCD mechanism provides meaningful uncertainty estimates, making its predictions more transparent and trustworthy, albeit with some remaining calibration challenges on certain datasets.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Adaptive Prompt Tuning (APT) framework represents a significant step forward in several critical areas of artificial intelligence, with notable potential impacts:

**a. Advancement in Few-Shot Fine-Grained Image Recognition:**
The most direct impact of this work is its contribution to few-shot fine-grained image classification. By demonstrating superior performance over existing methods like CoOp and VPT on challenging datasets (e.g., FGVC Aircraft, CUBirds), APT offers a robust solution for scenarios where data collection is expensive or impractical. This is particularly relevant for specialized domains such as biodiversity monitoring (identifying rare species), industrial inspection (differentiating subtle defects), or medical imaging (classifying nuanced disease subtypes), where the ability to learn from minimal examples is crucial. The dynamic, vision-guided nature of APT allows models to adapt more effectively to the subtle visual distinctions inherent in fine-grained categories, a task where static prompt tuning methods often fall short.

**b. Enhanced Trustworthiness and Reliability of AI Predictions:**
The integration of Monte-Carlo Dropout (MCD) for Uncertainty Quantification (UQ) is a crucial contribution. In many real-world applications, model accuracy alone is insufficient; users need to know *how confident* a model is in its predictions. APT's ability to provide well-calibrated confidence estimates and quantify uncertainty allows for:
- **Informed Decision-Making:** Identifying when predictions can be confidently acted upon and when they require human review or additional data. This is vital in high-stakes fields like healthcare, autonomous systems, and finance, where incorrect or overconfident predictions can have severe consequences.
- **Improved Model Debugging and Robustness:** High uncertainty on specific examples can signal data outliers, distribution shifts, or areas where the model is struggling, guiding further development and improving overall system robustness.
- **Responsible AI:** Contributing to the development of more transparent and accountable AI systems by providing explicit measures of predictive reliability.

**c. Practical and Efficient Uncertainty Quantification:**
The choice of MCD for UQ is pragmatic. Unlike more complex Bayesian Neural Networks or Gaussian Processes that can be memory-intensive and computationally demanding, MCD offers an efficient way to induce stochasticity and estimate uncertainty, leveraging existing dropout layers in neural networks. This makes UQ more accessible and scalable for large pre-trained models like CLIP, widening its potential adoption in resource-constrained environments or applications requiring fast inference. The paper's explicit comparison to GP-based UQ highlights the memory efficiency of their approach.

**d. Insights into Model Behavior and Dataset Characteristics:**
The detailed analysis of performance across various datasets (e.g., FGVC Aircraft vs. Oxford Flowers vs. Caltech101) provides valuable insights into the strengths and limitations of dynamic prompt tuning. The observation that APT benefits more from fine-grained datasets with high intra-class variance, while showing more modest gains on general classification tasks, helps researchers understand when and where such complex adaptive mechanisms are most impactful. The UQ analysis further elucidates model calibration issues and its ability to detect out-of-distribution data, guiding future research in model refinement.

**e. Foundation for Future Research:**
The work naturally opens several avenues for future research, as acknowledged by the authors. These include:
- Improving initial learning efficiency and calibration techniques.
- Exploring more advanced data augmentation and cross-attention configurations.
- Investigating other UQ methods (e.g., Deep Ensembles, Bayes-by-backprop) in conjunction with adaptive prompting.
These future directions underscore that APT is not merely an end-solution but a springboard for further innovation in adaptive learning and trustworthy AI.

In conclusion, APT offers a robust and adaptable framework that significantly pushes the boundaries of few-shot fine-grained classification by dynamically aligning visual and textual information. Coupled with its efficient uncertainty quantification capabilities, this research paves the way for more reliable, trustworthy, and practically applicable AI systems across a diverse range of specialized domains.
