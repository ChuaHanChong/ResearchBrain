# WebEvolver: Enhancing Web Agent Self-Improvement with Coevolving World Model

> [!summary] Summary
> The WebEvolver framework from Tencent AI Lab boosts self-improving web agents by co-evolving a world model that generates diverse synthetic training data, leading to a 10% performance gain over prior self-evolving baselines on WebVoyager and sustained learning without relying on proprietary models.

## Problem

- Self-improving web agents often plateau in performance due to limited exploration diversity during iterative training on real-world interactions.
- Current LLM-based web agents do not fully leverage pre-trained web knowledge for efficiently generating diverse and novel training scenarios.
- Existing world models in web environments are primarily used for short-term inference-time planning, not actively integrated into the agent's training process for data augmentation.

## Method

- **WebEvolver** introduces a co-learning framework that trains both an agent policy and a dedicated world model LLM, allowing them to evolve together.
- The world model synthesizes diverse multi-step web trajectories, acting as a virtual web server to generate augmented training data, thereby expanding the agent's learning exposure.
- During inference, the co-evolved world model enables ==multi-step look-ahead planning (WMLA)== to simulate future interactions and select optimal actions in the real environment.

## Results

- WebEvolver achieves a **10%** performance gain over the OpenWebVoyager baseline on WebVoyager and reaches up to a **51.37%** success rate when combined with WMLA at a depth of two.
- The framework also boosts success rates on Mind2Web-Live, improving from an **18.86%** baseline to **24.53%** with WMLA at a depth of two.
- Optimal multi-step look-ahead planning is observed at a depth of two, which correlates with the world model's declining plausibility in generating web observations at greater depths.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating a co-evolving world model for synthetic data generation effectively overcomes the performance stagnation typically seen in self-improving agents by enhancing exploration.
> - LLM-based world models can be actively utilized within an agent's training loop to create diverse learning experiences, extending their utility beyond passive inference-time planning.
> - Even partially 'hallucinated' yet plausible observations generated by the world model are beneficial for expanding training data diversity, fostering more robust agent policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

"WebEvolver" is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields within AI: autonomous agents, large language models (LLMs), agent self-improvement, and world modeling. Its core contribution addresses critical limitations in the development of sophisticated web agents.

**Autonomous Agents and Artificial General Intelligence (AGI):** The paper opens by framing web agents as pivotal for automating complex tasks and a stepping stone towards AGI. This aligns with the ambitious goals set by leading AI organizations (e.g., OpenAI, Monica.Im) that envision agents capable of operating autonomously in complex, open-ended environments. WebEvolver directly contributes to this vision by enhancing the adaptability and learning capacity of such agents.

**Web Agents:** The landscape of web agents has seen rapid progress, primarily driven by advancements in (multimodal) LLMs (e.g., Llama-3, GPT-4o, Claude 3.7). These LLMs serve as the "brain" for agents, enabling reasoning through frameworks like ReAct, MCP, and the "Cognitive Kernel" (developed by some of the authors). Evaluation benchmarks (WebShop, Mind2Web, WebVoyager, WebArena) have emerged to measure their capabilities in real-world web navigation. Beyond applying off-the-shelf LLMs, research efforts focus on data scaling (Explorer, NNetNav, InSTA) and inference-time optimizations like tree search (AgentTreeSearch, MCTS) and reflective learning (Reflexion). More recently, the focus has shifted towards developing "agent foundation models" and "deep research agents" (WebThinker, WebDancer, WebSailor, WebShaper, Cognitive Kernel-Pro), aiming for more generalist and robust agents. WebEvolver directly enhances these agent foundation models by improving their self-improvement mechanisms.

**Agent Self-Improvement:** This area explores how agents can autonomously improve their underlying LLMs by learning from their own interactions. Existing methods, such as those in BAGEL, OpenWebVoyager (a baseline directly built upon by this work, also from Tencent AI Lab), and Self-Improved Agents, involve iterative cycles of exploration, feedback, and fine-tuning on self-collected data. While promising for reducing reliance on human-labeled data, these methods often suffer from performance plateaus due to limited exploration diversity and an inability to efficiently generate novel training scenarios. WebEvolver directly tackles these bottlenecks by introducing a mechanism for generating diverse synthetic data.

**World Models:** The concept of world models originated in reinforcement learning (e.g., Ha and Schmidhuber, 2018), where a model learns to simulate the environment's dynamics. Recent work has extended this to LLMs, treating them as implicit world models for planning and reasoning (RAP, WKM). In web environments, approaches like WebDreamer and WMA use LLMs to predict action outcomes through natural language simulations. However, as the authors correctly point out, these LLM-based world models are typically used *only at inference time* as part of sophisticated prompting pipelines (e.g., with GPT-4o) and do not actively contribute to *improving the agent through training*. WebEvolver represents a significant departure by *co-learning a dedicated world model* that is integrated into the agent's *self-improvement training loop*, enabling genuine multi-step trajectory synthesis and significantly expanding its utility.

In essence, WebEvolver bridges a critical gap by providing a mechanism for LLM-based agents to not only learn from their own real-world experiences but also to *actively create* new, diverse learning experiences through a co-evolving world model, thereby accelerating and sustaining their self-improvement process.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of "WebEvolver" is to **enhance the self-improvement capabilities of web agents** by overcoming common limitations observed in existing iterative self-training paradigms.

The core motivations stem from two key bottlenecks in current agent self-improvement methods:

1. **Limited Exploration Diversity:** As an agent repetitively trains on self-sampled trajectories, it tends to overfit to familiar paths, leading to a decline in exploration diversity. This prevents the agent from discovering novel states or more optimal strategies, causing performance to stagnate. Existing inference-time exploration methods often require costly real-world interactions for only marginal gains.
2. **Under-utilization of Pre-trained Web Knowledge and Limited Multi-step Planning:** While LLMs are pre-trained on vast web content, this inherent understanding of web dynamics, user intents, and task workflows is not fully leveraged for *generating diverse training data*. Additionally, existing simulation or imagination-based approaches typically offer only one or two steps of look-ahead during inference, lacking the capability for coherent, multi-step rollouts that could genuinely guide action selection. Furthermore, these world models are rarely integrated into the agent's *training process*.

To address these issues, the authors propose a novel framework that introduces a **co-evolving World Model LLM**. This world model is designed with dual roles, directly targeting the aforementioned limitations:

1. **As a Virtual Web Server for Training Data Generation:** The world model generates diverse, self-instructed *synthetic training trajectories* by simulating interactions with unseen web environments. This mitigates exploration bottlenecks by exposing the agent to a wider range of scenarios than real interactions alone, without incurring the high cost of real-world interactions. The key insight here is that while the world model might produce some "hallucinated" states, this noise can still be beneficial for learning flexible action prediction during training.
2. **As an Imagination Engine for Inference-time Planning:** During inference, the world model performs multi-step look-ahead simulations. This allows the agent to evaluate multiple possible action sequences and their predicted outcomes *without costly real-world trials*, enabling more informed and optimal action selection.

Ultimately, the motivation is to achieve sustained adaptability and improved performance for web agents in open-domain environments, with minimal human supervision and, importantly, without resorting to distillation from more powerful, closed-source models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WebEvolver introduces a **co-learning framework** for an Agent Policy model and a World Model. The overall process combines iterative supervised fine-tuning (SFT) of the agent with the dynamic integration of a self-evolving world model.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The web agent task is framed as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP). The agent receives a natural language query `q` and interacts with a web environment represented by an observation `o` (an accessibility tree of visible elements). The agent’s policy `π` generates actions `a` (click, type, goback, scroll, stop) and thoughts `h`. Trajectories `τ` consist of sequences of observations, thoughts, and actions, with a final reward `r` (success/failure) determined by an automatic self-assessment. The backbone agent framework used is **Cognitive Kernel** (from the same lab), and observations are accessibility trees provided by Playwright.

#### 3.2. Agent Self-Improvement (Baseline)

The self-improvement process, adapted from existing methods like OpenWebVoyager, operates as follows:
- **Trajectories Collection:** An initial backbone LLM (Llama-3.3-70b) samples actions based on queries, generating web navigation trajectories. The history of observations is clipped to `k` steps to manage context length, while thoughts and actions are retained.
- **Rejection Sampling:** Trajectories are filtered, retaining only those that successfully complete the task, as evaluated by an automatic evaluation method (`r(τ, q)`). Critically, for true *self-improvement*, Llama-3.3-70b itself is used as the evaluator, avoiding reliance on stronger external models like GPT-4o for this internal loop.
- **Iterative Optimization:** The successful trajectories form a dataset `D_i` which is used to fine-tune the agent policy LLM (M) via supervised fine-tuning (SFT), optimizing the likelihood of generating the observed actions and thoughts. This process is iterated, with new successful trajectories augmenting `D_i` for subsequent training rounds.

#### 3.3. WebEvolver: Co-learning World Model

This is the core contribution, introducing a dedicated world model (`M_w`) and leveraging it for both training data augmentation and inference-time planning.
- **Co-learning World Model:** A separate LLM (also Llama-3.3-70b) is fine-tuned to serve as the world model. Its objective is to predict the *next observation* (`o_t`) given the *current observation* (`o_t-1`) and the *scheduled action* (`a_t-1`). This world model is trained on the same real-world trajectories collected during the agent's self-improvement cycles, allowing it to "co-evolve" with the agent's understanding of web dynamics. Distilled rationales (`h_w_t`) from the base LLM about transition logic are also incorporated to aid generation.
- **Trajectory Synthesis (for Training Data Augmentation):**
 - The trained world model (`M_w`) acts as a "virtual web server," replacing the real environment's transition function.
 - For a given query, the agent policy (`M_i`) generates an action, and then the world model predicts the *synthetic next observation*. This cycle repeats for a fixed number of steps (e.g., T=7), generating entirely *synthetic multi-step trajectories*.
 - These synthetic trajectories are then subjected to rejection sampling (using Llama-3.3-70b as evaluator) to filter for plausible and successful ones.
 - The successful synthetic trajectories are combined with the real trajectories (`D_i`) to form an augmented training dataset. This expanded dataset is then used to fine-tune a new agent policy model, resulting in **WebEvolver**. This addresses the exploration bottleneck by exposing the agent to a wider, artificially generated range of scenarios.
- **Inference-time Look-ahead (WMLA - World Model Look-Ahead):**
 - During inference, to enhance planning, WebEvolver employs WMLA. For a given current observation, the agent policy generates a small number (`k`, e.g., 3) of candidate actions.
 - For each candidate action, the world model and agent policy collaboratively simulate `d`-step (e.g., d=1, 2, or 3) rollouts of future interactions.
 - An external LLM-based evaluator (GPT-4o in experiments) scores each simulated rollout (e.g., 0, 0.5, 1.0) based on its likelihood of task completion.
 - The action that yields the highest expected progress across its simulated rollout is chosen for execution in the real environment.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Foundation Model:** Llama-3.3-70b is used as the backbone LLM for both the agent policy and the world model.
- **Evaluation:** Success rate is measured on real-world, live web navigation benchmarks: **WebVoyager** and **Mind2Web-Live**. A filtered subset of websites is used due to accessibility issues. For generalizability, **GAIA-web** and **SimpleQA** are also used. Task completion is assessed end-to-end by GPT-4o for robust, objective evaluation (allowing for diverse successful paths).
- **World Model Intrinsic Evaluation:** The generated accessibility trees are assessed using three metrics: Structural Correctness (STR), Similarity (Sim.), and Overall Assessment (O/A), all automatically evaluated by GPT-4o and normalized 0-1.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that WebEvolver significantly enhances the performance and adaptability of self-improving web agents.

1. **Impact of Self-Improvement and Synthetic Trajectories:**
 - Initial self-improvement iterations (fine-tuning Llama-3.3-70b on self-collected real data) show a performance gain, e.g., ~6% increase in success rate on WebVoyager over the zero-shot baseline, mainly due to improved format compliance and task familiarity.
 - However, performance plateaus after a few iterations with only real data, confirming the stagnation bottleneck.
 - **Crucially, incorporating world-model-synthesized data into the training process (WebEvolver) yields a further 4% improvement**, leading to a total performance gain of approximately **10% over the existing self-evolving baseline (OpenWebVoyager)** on WebVoyager. This demonstrates the efficacy of synthetic data in overcoming exploration limitations and sustaining self-improvement.

2. **Effectiveness of World Model Look-Ahead (WMLA):**
 - Inference-time WMLA, using the co-evolved world model, consistently provides additional performance gains.
 - Optimal performance is observed at a look-ahead depth of `d=2`, indicating a balance between predictive accuracy and computational overhead. Increasing the depth to `d=3` shows diminishing returns, aligning with the intrinsic evaluation of the world model's predictive capabilities.
 - WebEvolver combined with WMLA achieves the **highest overall success rates** on both WebVoyager (e.g., 51.37% with WMLA d=2, compared to 32.98% for Llama-3.3-70B self-improve (2) baseline) and Mind2Web-Live (24.53% with WMLA d=2, compared to 18.86% for Llama-3.3-70B self-improve (2) baseline).

3. **World Model Intrinsic Evaluation:**
 - The world model's ability to generate plausible next web pages (measured by Structural Correctness, Similarity, and Overall Assessment) shows a sharp decline for generation depths greater than `d=2`. This directly correlates with the observed diminishing returns of WMLA beyond this depth, providing empirical support for the optimal look-ahead horizon.
 - The analysis highlights that the LLM-based world model can leverage inherent commonsense knowledge about web structures to generate diverse and plausible (even if sometimes "hallucinated") observations, which is beneficial for expanding training data diversity.

4. **Out-of-domain Generalization:**
 - WebEvolver demonstrates consistent improvements on **GAIA-web Level 1** and **SimpleQA** tasks, reinforcing its generalizability beyond the primary training benchmarks. This is particularly notable as it performs well on `bing.com` queries, despite the training data not including trajectories from this specific search engine.
 - However, performance gains on **GAIA-web Level 2** queries, which require deeper reasoning and extended multi-step interactions, are limited. This indicates that while WebEvolver enhances action generation, the deeper, complex reasoning capabilities required for such tasks remain a challenge beyond its current scope.

5. **Ablations:**
 - Ablation studies on the branching factor (`k`) for WMLA show that performance plateaus around `k=3-5`, suggesting that `k=3` (as used in the paper) is an efficient choice, as the action space rarely offers more than 5 substantially different options per state.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "WebEvolver" framework represents a significant step forward in the development of autonomous AI agents, particularly those operating in dynamic web environments.

1. **Overcoming Self-Improvement Stagnation:** The most critical impact is WebEvolver's ability to break through the performance plateaus commonly encountered in agent self-improvement. By introducing a co-evolving world model for generating diverse synthetic training data, it addresses the fundamental limitation of insufficient exploration and enables sustained learning, making iterative self-training a more robust and scalable paradigm.

2. **Transformative Role of World Models:** This work redefines the utility of LLM-based world models. Instead of merely being inference-time tools for chain-of-thought prompting, WebEvolver integrates them directly into the agent's *training loop*. This shift from passive imagination to active data generation is a paradigm shift, enabling agents to actively construct their learning environment and accelerate their development without constant human intervention.

3. **Reduced Cost and Increased Scalability:** Generating diverse training trajectories in real-world web environments is notoriously expensive, time-consuming, and susceptible to website changes. WebEvolver's ability to synthesize high-quality training data significantly reduces this reliance on costly real-world interactions, making agent development more efficient, scalable, and environmentally friendly.

4. **Enhanced Adaptability and Robustness:** By exposing the agent to a wider variety of scenarios, including novel or even slightly "hallucinated" ones generated by the world model, the agent learns more flexible and robust action policies. This improved adaptability is crucial for agents operating in the ever-changing and unpredictable landscape of the internet.

5. **Advancing Open-Source AI:** The demonstration of significant performance gains using an open-source model (Llama-3.3-70b) without relying on distillation from proprietary, more powerful LLMs is a substantial contribution. It highlights a viable path for developing advanced, self-improving agents within the open-source ecosystem, fostering greater accessibility and collaboration in AI research.

6. **Progress Towards Generalist Agents:** By improving the self-learning mechanisms of web agents, WebEvolver contributes to the broader long-term goal of developing generalist AI agents capable of autonomously performing a wide range of complex tasks in diverse environments, bringing the field closer to the aspiration of AGI.

While the paper acknowledges limitations regarding the current lack of a separate planning module and the challenges of web environment reproducibility, "WebEvolver" provides a strong foundation for future research, particularly in integrating more sophisticated reasoning capabilities with its innovative self-improvement framework.
