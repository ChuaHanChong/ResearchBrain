# The Cityscapes Dataset for Semantic Urban Scene Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> The Cityscapes dataset, a collaboration between Daimler AG R&D, MPI Informatics, and TU Darmstadt, provides 5,000 densely annotated and 20,000 coarsely annotated urban street scene images. This resource offers pixel-level and instance-level semantic labels to advance robust perception systems for autonomous driving by capturing real-world complexity and diversity.

## Problem

- Existing datasets for urban scene understanding lacked sufficient scale, diversity, and complexity to train deep learning models for robust semantic segmentation and instance-level understanding in real-world inner-city environments.
- Autonomous driving applications require precise instance-level identification and segmentation of individual objects (e.g., cars, pedestrians), a detail often missing or insufficiently captured in prior datasets.
- The limited size and variability of previous datasets hindered the generalization capabilities of algorithms, especially in diverse and challenging urban traffic scenarios.

## Method

- A large-scale dataset, **Cityscapes**, was developed consisting of 5,000 finely annotated images and 20,000 coarsely annotated images, captured across 50 diverse cities and various seasons.
- The dataset provides high-quality ==pixel-level semantic labels== for 30 classes (19 for evaluation) and precise ==instance-level segmentation masks== with unique IDs for humans and vehicles.
- A comprehensive benchmark suite was established with specific evaluation metrics, including a novel ==instance-level IoU (iIoU)== and ==Average Precision (AP)== for instance segmentation, to assess performance on both pixel and instance-level tasks.

## Results

- State-of-the-art pixel-level semantic segmentation baselines achieved an IoU_class_ of **67.1%** on Cityscapes, lower than on less complex datasets, confirming the benchmark's inherent difficulty.
- The instance-level semantic labeling task proved particularly challenging, with baseline Fast R-CNN models achieving a mean Average Precision (AP) of only **4.6%** using existing region proposal methods.
- Models trained on Cityscapes demonstrated strong transferability, performing comparably to or better than models specifically trained on other datasets like CamVid and KITTI when evaluated on those benchmarks, indicating the dataset's generalizability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The Cityscapes dataset's high scene complexity, with a greater density of traffic participants and a wider range of object distances, significantly challenges existing state-of-the-art semantic segmentation and instance-level methods.
> - Pixel-level semantic segmentation performance is highly sensitive to input image downscaling, especially for small objects, indicating a need for algorithms optimized for high-resolution inputs and multi-scale processing.
> - For instance-level semantic labeling, the object proposal generation component was identified as a critical bottleneck for baseline methods, suggesting current approaches struggle with precise object localization and segmentation in cluttered urban scenes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a recognized challenge in computer vision: visual understanding of complex urban street scenes, which is crucial for applications such as autonomous driving. The authors note that while object detection has benefited significantly from large-scale datasets, particularly in the context of deep learning (e.g., ImageNet, PASCAL VOC, Microsoft COCO), datasets for semantic urban scene understanding have not adequately captured the complexity and variability of real-world scenarios.

Existing datasets focused on urban or traffic scenes, such as KITTI, CamVid, Leuven, and Daimler Urban Segmentation (DUS), are generally smaller in scale compared to generic scene understanding datasets. The authors argue that these existing datasets also lack sufficient variability and complexity to drive further progress in understanding inner-city traffic scenes. For instance, KITTI primarily focuses on less busy suburban traffic. A recent dataset offers 3D semantic instance annotations for suburban scenes, which the authors consider complementary to Cityscapes due to differences in annotation methodology and scene types (suburban vs. inner-city).

The Cityscapes dataset is introduced to fill this gap by providing a larger, more diverse, and richly annotated dataset specifically tailored for complex urban environments. It aims to complement existing general and specialized datasets by offering a more challenging benchmark that considers both pixel-level and instance-level semantic labeling, as well as depth information through stereo vision. The initiative aligns with the broader trend in computer vision where the availability of large, high-quality datasets has been a major enabler for the success of deep learning methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to introduce Cityscapes, a benchmark suite and a large-scale dataset specifically designed to train and test approaches for pixel-level and instance-level semantic labeling in urban environments. The motivation stems from several limitations observed in the existing research landscape:

1. **Inadequate Complexity and Variability:** Current datasets for semantic urban scene understanding, particularly those relevant to autonomous driving, do not fully capture the complexity and diversity of real-world inner-city traffic scenes. This limits the ability of algorithms to generalize to the wide range of conditions encountered in urban environments. The authors emphasize the need for a dataset that reflects the challenges of dense inner-city traffic, varied road layouts, and diverse object scales.

2. **Dataset Size Limitations:** Previous urban scene datasets are often much smaller in scale than those used for general object recognition or scene understanding tasks. The resurgence of deep learning has demonstrated that larger datasets are crucial for deep neural networks to achieve their full potential, enabling them to learn more robust and generalizable features.

3. **Advancing Autonomous Driving Systems:** Visual understanding of complex urban street scenes is identified as an enabling factor for a wide range of applications, with self-driving cars being a particularly demanding and challenging application. Progress in this domain requires high-performance and reliable vision systems, which, in turn, depend on comprehensive training and evaluation data.

4. **Enabling Instance-Level Understanding:** Beyond simple pixel-level semantic categories, the ability to identify and segment individual object instances (e.g., distinguishing between multiple cars or pedestrians) is critical for advanced scene understanding and decision-making in autonomous systems. Existing datasets often lack this level of detail or provide it only through bounding boxes rather than precise pixel-level masks.

5. **Facilitating Research on Weakly-Labeled Data:** The high cost and effort associated with dense pixel-level annotations limit the scale of finely labeled datasets. The authors aim to provide a large volume of coarsely annotated images to enable and encourage research into methods that can leverage weakly-labeled data to augment training.

To address these motivations, Cityscapes was developed with the specific goals of:
- Creating a large, diverse set of stereo video sequences recorded in 50 different cities.
- Providing high-quality pixel-level semantic annotations for 5,000 images, along with instance-level labels for relevant objects like humans and vehicles.
- Offering an additional 20,000 images with coarse annotations to support weakly-supervised learning.
- Exceeding previous efforts in terms of dataset size, annotation richness, scene variability, and complexity.
- Establishing a robust benchmark with dedicated evaluation metrics for both pixel-level and instance-level semantic labeling.
- Providing supplementary information such as depth data (from stereo), vehicle odometry, outside temperature, and GPS tracks to support multi-modal research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The development of the Cityscapes dataset involved careful design choices regarding data acquisition, annotation protocols, and data splitting to ensure it met the stated objectives.

**Data Acquisition and Specifications:**
- **Recording Environment:** Data was collected from a moving vehicle over several months, covering spring, summer, and fall in 50 different cities, predominantly in Germany and neighboring countries. Adverse weather conditions (heavy rain, snow) were deliberately excluded to focus on a primary set of challenges.
- **Camera System:** An automotive-grade stereo camera system with a 22 cm baseline, 1/3-inch CMOS 2 MP rolling shutter sensors, and a frame rate of 17 Hz was used. The system yielded 16-bit linear High Dynamic Range (HDR) images.
- **Calibration:** Extrinsic and intrinsic calibrations were performed using established methods, with on-site recalibration before each recording session to maintain accuracy.
- **Image Formats:** The dataset provides rectified 16-bit HDR stereo image pairs. For compatibility and annotation quality, 8-bit Low Dynamic Range (LDR) RGB images were derived by applying a logarithmic compression curve and separate tone mapping to each image, making them visually more pleasing for annotators.
- **Contextual Data:** In addition to imagery, the dataset includes vehicle odometry from in-vehicle sensors, outside temperature, and GPS tracks.

**Annotation Process and Classes:**
- **Annotation Selection:** 5,000 images were meticulously selected from 27 cities for dense pixel-level annotation, aiming for high diversity across foreground objects, background, and overall scene layouts. These were the 20th frame of 30-frame video snippets. Another 20,000 images from the remaining 23 cities were selected for coarse annotation (one image every 20 seconds or 20 meters driven).
- **Fine Annotations:** These involved layered polygons, created in-house, with an average annotation time exceeding 1.5 hours per image. Annotators were instructed to label objects from back to front, implicitly providing depth ordering and ensuring consistent object boundaries.
- **Coarse Annotations:** For these, accuracy on object boundaries was traded for speed, with a target of less than 7 minutes per image. Annotators used coarse polygons, ensuring each polygon belonged to a single object class.
- **Annotation Quality:** The quality was assessed through two experiments:
 - 30 images were finely annotated twice by different annotators, showing 96% pixel agreement (98% when excluding void labels).
 - All fine annotations were additionally coarsely annotated, revealing 97% class agreement between fine and coarse labels.
- **Class Definitions:** 30 visual classes were defined and grouped into eight categories (flat, construction, nature, vehicle, sky, object, human, void). 19 classes, chosen based on frequency, application relevance, and compatibility with other datasets, are used for evaluation. Instance-level labels are provided for humans and vehicles.

**Dataset Splits:**
- The 5,000 densely annotated images were split into 2,975 for training, 500 for validation, and 1,525 for testing (with withheld annotations for benchmarking). The 20,000 coarsely annotated images are used solely as additional training data.
- Splitting was non-random and performed at the city level to ensure each split is representative of real-world variability, balancing geographic location, city population size, and time of year.
- A representativeness assessment using an FCN-8s model indicated homogeneous performance across most split characteristics, with minor variations (e.g., better performance in cloudy conditions at year-end, which was attributed to softer lighting).

**Statistical Analysis and Comparison:**
- Cityscapes was compared against other autonomous driving-focused datasets (CamVid, DUS, KITTI) and general datasets (Caltech Pedestrian, MS COCO, PASCAL VOC) based on:
 - **Annotation Volume and Density:** Cityscapes provides significantly more annotated pixels (up to two orders of magnitude) and higher density.
 - **Class Distribution:** Differences were observed, for instance, KITTI has fewer flat ground structures and humans due to its suburban focus, while Cityscapes reflects dense inner-city traffic.
 - **Scene Complexity:** Measured by the density and scale of traffic participants. Cityscapes contains substantially more object instance annotations than KITTI, and its images cover a greater variety of scene complexity, with a higher proportion of highly complex scenes and a larger range of object distances due to higher-resolution imagery and detailed annotation.

**Semantic Labeling Task and Metrics:**
- **Task:** Pixel-level semantic labeling.
- **Metrics:** Standard Jaccard Index (Intersection-over-Union, IoU) computed for both class (IoU_class) and category (IoU_category) granularities. A novel instance-level IoU (iIoU) was introduced to address bias towards large objects, weighting pixel contributions based on instance size relative to the average class instance size.
- **Control Experiments:** Included static predictions (most frequent label per pixel), re-classification of ground truth segments using a trained FCN-8s, performance upper bounds by subsampling ground truth annotations, and nearest training neighbor predictions. These experiments provided context for baseline performance and highlighted the dataset's diversity and demand for generalization.
- **Baselines:** The authors performed experiments using Fully Convolutional Networks (FCNs) (FCN-32s, FCN-16s, FCN-8s) and evaluated several third-party state-of-the-art methods (SegNet, DPN, CRF as RNN, DeepLab, Adelaide, Dilated10). Training details, including ImageNet pre-training, PASCAL-Context setup, learning rates, epochs, image splitting, and downscaling factors, were provided.

**Instance-Level Semantic Labeling Task and Metrics:**
- **Task:** Simultaneous detection and segmentation of individual traffic participant instances.
- **Metrics:** Average Precision (AP) for region-level segmentation, averaged across 10 Intersection-over-Union (IoU) thresholds (0.5 to 0.95). Additional scores included AP_50%_ (for 50% overlap), AP_100m_, and AP_50m_ (restricting evaluation to objects within 100m and 50m, respectively).
- **Baselines and Oracles:** Fast R-CNN (FRCN) was trained using either MCG (Multiscale Combinatorial Grouping) object proposals or ground truth segments/bounding boxes as oracle inputs, combined with the FRCN classifier.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings concerning the characteristics of the Cityscapes dataset and the performance of current state-of-the-art methods on its benchmarks.

**Dataset Characteristics:**
- **Scale and Diversity:** Cityscapes represents a substantial increase in scale and diversity compared to previous urban scene datasets. It offers 5,000 fine-annotated images and 20,000 coarse-annotated images from 50 different cities, encompassing diverse geographic locations, city sizes, and seasonal variations.
- **Annotation Richness:** The dataset provides pixel-level and instance-level annotations, with specific instance IDs for humans and vehicles. This detail is crucial for advanced perception tasks in autonomous systems.
- **Scene Complexity:** Statistical analysis confirmed that Cityscapes captures significantly higher scene complexity. It features a greater number of traffic participant instances per image and a wider range of object distances compared to datasets like KITTI (Table 2, Figures 3 and 4). This complexity poses a challenge for algorithms that need to handle objects across various scales and levels of occlusion.
- **Annotation Quality:** High inter-annotator agreement (96-98%) and consistency between fine and coarse annotations (97%) were reported, indicating robust ground truth.

**Semantic Labeling Task (Pixel-Level):**
- **Challenge Level:** Control experiments demonstrated the dataset's inherent difficulty. Static predictors (using the most frequent label) achieved only around 10% IoU_class_, highlighting the scene diversity. Even highly downsampled ground truth (e.g., by a factor of 64) performs comparably to or better than some state-of-the-art baselines.
- **Baseline Performance:** The best performing baseline for pixel-level semantic segmentation (Dilated10) achieved an IoU_class_ of 67.1%. This is notably lower than the reported state-of-the-art on less complex datasets like PASCAL VOC (77.9%) and KITTI (72.5%), indicating that Cityscapes presents a significant new challenge for the field.
- **Impact of Downscaling:** Experiments with FCNs revealed a strong negative correlation between the degree of input image downscaling and performance, particularly for the iIoU metric which is sensitive to small objects. This suggests that algorithms need to effectively handle large scale variations and high-resolution inputs to perform well.
- **iIoU Metric Insights:** The novel instance-level IoU (iIoU) metric provided distinct insights. Methods employing Conditional Random Fields (CRFs) for regularization (e.g., DPN, CRF as RNN) often showed a larger drop from IoU to iIoU compared to other methods, suggesting potential over-smoothing of small objects. Adelaide was an exception, indicating its specific FCN-derived pairwise terms allowed for more selective regularization.
- **Coarse Annotations Utility:** Training FCN-8s on the 20,000 coarse annotations yielded comparable IoU performance to training on only 500 fine annotations, suggesting their value for training, especially in scenarios where fine annotations are sparse. However, training on the full set of 3,475 fine annotations resulted in significantly better performance.

**Cross-Dataset Evaluation:**
- An FCN model trained on Cityscapes (half-resolution) was tested on CamVid and KITTI. Without specific training or fine-tuning for these datasets, the Cityscapes-trained model achieved performance on par with or, in some cases, outperforming methods specifically trained on those benchmarks (Table 5). This demonstrates the transferability and generalizability of models trained on Cityscapes and the dataset's compatibility with existing benchmarks.

**Instance-Level Semantic Labeling Task:**
- **Challenging Task:** The instance-level task proved particularly difficult for the baselines. The Fast R-CNN (FRCN) using MCG (Multiscale Combinatorial Grouping) proposals achieved a low mean Average Precision (AP) of 4.6%.
- **Proposal Bottleneck:** Oracle experiments, replacing either the classifier or the proposals with ground truth, revealed that the object localization and proposal generation component was the primary bottleneck. When perfect ground truth regions were used as proposals, the FRCN classifier achieved a significantly higher AP of 41.3% (Table 6). This indicated that current region proposal methods, particularly MCG, are inadequate for the cluttered and complex street scenes in Cityscapes and struggle with the requirement for precise instance segmentation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Cityscapes dataset represents a notable contribution to the computer vision and autonomous driving research communities due to several key aspects:

1. **Addressing a Critical Gap:** The dataset directly addresses the long-standing need for a large-scale, diverse, and highly complex dataset for semantic urban scene understanding. It specifically targets the demanding requirements of autonomous driving by focusing on inner-city traffic scenarios that are more complex than those captured by previous datasets.

2. **Driving Deep Learning Advancements:** By providing a substantial volume of high-quality pixel-level and instance-level annotations, alongside a large corpus of weakly-labeled data, Cityscapes is poised to enable the development and training of more robust and generalizable deep learning models. Its scale is expected to allow deep networks to fully leverage their capabilities, similar to how ImageNet and PASCAL VOC fueled advancements in image classification and object detection.

3. **Establishing a Challenging Benchmark:** The low baseline performance of state-of-the-art methods on Cityscapes (e.g., 67.1% IoU_class_ for pixel-level segmentation, 4.6% AP for instance-level) underscores its difficulty and highlights significant room for future research. This challenge is crucial for stimulating innovation and pushing the boundaries of what is achievable in urban scene understanding.

4. **Novel Insights into Method Limitations:** The detailed empirical study and the introduction of the iIoU metric have already provided valuable insights, such as the sensitivity of performance to input image resolution and the varying capabilities of different methods (e.g., CRF-based regularization) in handling small objects. These findings can guide the design of future algorithms.

5. **Facilitating Research on Weak Supervision and Scale Variation:** The inclusion of 20,000 coarsely annotated images encourages research into weakly-supervised and semi-supervised learning techniques, which are critical for scaling annotation efforts. The dataset's wide range of object scales and distances also necessitates the development of multi-scale and high-resolution perception algorithms.

6. **Enabling Cross-Dataset Research:** The demonstrated compatibility of Cityscapes with existing datasets like CamVid and KITTI, allowing models trained on Cityscapes to perform well on them, indicates its potential as a foundational dataset that can foster broader research and lead to more universally applicable models.

In conclusion, Cityscapes provides a crucial resource for advancing the understanding of complex urban environments, with direct implications for the safety and reliability of autonomous vehicles and other intelligent systems operating in cities. Its comprehensive nature and inherent challenges are expected to drive the next generation of research in semantic scene understanding.
