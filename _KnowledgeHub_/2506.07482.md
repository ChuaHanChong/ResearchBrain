# Information-guided optimization of image-based sensorless adaptive optics methods

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces a Fisher information-based analytical framework for objectively optimizing image-based sensorless adaptive optics methods. The framework quantifies information encoding in images to guide the systematic design of phase modulation strategies, demonstrating improved aberration correction accuracy and efficiency across diverse imaging scenarios.

## Problem

- Current sensorless adaptive optics (AO) methods often rely on empirical or heuristic design for crucial parameters like phase modulation strategies, leading to suboptimal performance.
- Inefficient data acquisition in sensorless AO results from input data that are not optimally conditioned for phase information extraction, necessitating more images and prolonged iterative processes.
- Objectively assessing and determining optimal phase modulation strategies is challenging due to the complex interplay of practical factors such as illumination power, sample brightness, noise, and out-of-focus structures.

## Method

- Developed a ==Fisher information==-based analytical framework to objectively quantify the amount of information carried by image measurements about unknown aberration parameters.
- Utilized the ==Cramér-Rao Lower Bound (CRLB)==, derived from the Fisher information matrix, to set a theoretical minimum variance for unbiased aberration estimators, serving as an optimization objective.
- Implemented a numerical model of a multiphoton fluorescence adaptive microscope to simulate image generation and optimize bias aberration strategies (magnitude, modal shape, and number) using ==gradient descent==.

## Results

- Optimized bias amplitudes, derived by minimizing the **CRLB**, closely corresponded to the lowest residual errors for intensity-based parabolic fitting methods in both two-photon and three-photon microscopy simulations.
- A machine learning-based sensorless AO algorithm using the optimized set of bias modes consistently achieved lower residual errors compared to using individual Zernike-mode biases, with optimal biases varying for point-like versus volumetric objects.
- Across different photon budgets, the sensorless AO algorithm utilizing **2N** optimized biases demonstrated the best performance (lowest residual error), with the **CRLB** analysis accurately predicting these performance trends.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fisher information provides an objective statistical measure to quantify phase information in images, enabling systematic optimization of sensorless AO method settings for improved accuracy and efficiency.
> - The optimal phase modulation strategy (bias amplitudes, modal shapes) is highly dependent on specific imaging conditions, including the imaging modality (e.g., 2-P vs. 3-P microscopy) and the nature of the object (point-like vs. volumetric).
> - Modal ambiguities can arise for certain Zernike modes (e.g., odd-row modes) when used as biases in 2D in-focus imaging, but these can be resolved for volumetric samples where out-of-focus planes provide additional distinguishable information.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Optical systems such as microscopes, telescopes, and ophthalmoscopes are susceptible to wavefront distortions, or aberrations, which can degrade image quality and obscure scientific information. Adaptive Optics (AO) technology addresses this issue by employing reconfigurable optical elements to actively compensate for these aberrations. Within the field of AO, two main approaches exist: wavefront sensor-based AO, which directly measures the wavefront, and sensorless AO, which infers aberration information indirectly from acquired images.

Sensorless AO methods have gained prominence, particularly in microscopy, due to their flexibility and versatility, circumventing some challenges associated with implementing traditional wavefront sensors. These methods typically involve introducing known phase modulations (often called "bias aberrations") into the system and observing the resulting changes in image properties. The wavefront correction is then deduced from these image variations.

A significant limitation in the current landscape of sensorless AO methods is that the design of critical parameters, such as the magnitude and shape of phase modulations, has largely relied on empirical experience and heuristic approaches. This often results in suboptimal method performance, leading to prolonged iterative correction processes, increased image acquisition requirements, and inefficient data conditioning. Factors such as illumination power, sample brightness, noise, background levels, and out-of-focus structures further complicate the objective determination of optimal phase modulation strategies.

This research addresses this gap by introducing a systematic and objective framework for optimizing sensorless AO methods. By leveraging Fisher information analysis, the work aims to move beyond empirical design towards a principled, information-guided approach. This positions the paper as a contribution to enhancing the accuracy and efficiency of existing and emerging sensorless AO techniques, particularly those utilizing machine learning, by optimizing the data acquisition process itself.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce and demonstrate a Fisher information-based analytical framework for the systematic optimization of image-based sensorless adaptive optics methods. This framework aims to provide objective guidance for designing method settings, specifically focusing on the phase modulation strategies, to improve the accuracy and efficiency of aberration correction.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in current sensorless AO practices:

- **Suboptimal Method Design:** Many sensorless AO methods, despite their effectiveness and flexibility, are designed based on empirical experience. This often leads to suboptimal choices for crucial parameters, such as the magnitude and shape of introduced phase modulations.
- **Inefficient Data Acquisition:** Heuristically designed method settings can result in input data that are not optimally conditioned for phase information extraction. This necessitates more image acquisitions and prolonged iterative processes, which are undesirable in many practical applications due to time constraints, phototoxicity, and photobleaching.
- **Difficulty in Objective Assessment:** The relationship between introduced phase modulations and encoded phase information in images is complex, being influenced by practical factors like illumination power, sample brightness, noise, background levels, and out-of-focus structures. This complexity makes it challenging to objectively determine how phase modulations should be introduced to best condition the detected data.
- **Need for a Generalizable Framework:** There is a need for a systematic optimization approach that is not specific to any particular AO method or imaging modality, thus providing broad applicability across different scenarios and advancing the foundational understanding of information encoding in sensorless AO.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives outlined by the authors include:

- **Quantifying Phase Information:** To objectively assess how various interdependent factors jointly contribute to the extraction of phase information from microscope measurements, using Fisher information as a statistical measure.
- **Establishing a Lower Bound for Estimation Variance:** To compute the Cramér-Rao Lower Bound (CRLB) from the Fisher information, thereby setting the theoretical limit for the highest possible accuracy of an unbiased aberration estimator, independent of the specific estimation algorithm.
- **Optimizing Phase Modulation Strategy:** To utilize this information-guided analysis to derive optimized bias aberrations (magnitude, shape, and number) that lead to statistically more accurate estimation of aberration coefficients.
- **Demonstrating Practical Applicability:** To show through numerical simulations that method settings optimized with this Fisher information analysis lead to improved correction accuracy under various simulated imaging conditions and for different sensorless AO algorithms (e.g., parabolic fitting, machine learning-based methods).
- **Ensuring Framework Versatility:** To develop a framework that is adaptable and can provide insights into AO method design across a wide range of imaging scenarios and for different types of objects (point-like vs. volumetric).

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to provide a robust, data-driven foundation for the design of sensorless AO systems, thereby enhancing their performance and expanding their utility in scientific imaging.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the methodology is a Fisher information-based analysis used to quantify the amount of information carried by image measurements about unknown aberration parameters. This forms the basis for an optimization framework to determine optimal phase modulation strategies.

### 4. Fisher Information and Cramér–Rao Lower Bound (CRLB)

- **Fisher Information ($I_X(\Theta)$):** Defined as a statistical measure of the information an observable variable $X$ carries about an unknown parameter $\Theta$. In the context of sensorless AO, $X$ represents image measurements, and $\Theta$ represents unknown phase aberrations. A larger Fisher information indicates that $X$ is more sensitive to changes in $\Theta$, meaning $X$ carries more information about $\Theta$. For multi-dimensional parameter spaces and multiple independent observations (e.g., pixel readings within an image or multiple images), a generalized Fisher information matrix is calculated by summing the contributions from each independent observation.
- **Cramér–Rao Lower Bound (CRLB):** The CRLB for an unbiased estimator $\hat{\theta}_k$ of a parameter $\theta_k$ is calculated as the inverse of the Fisher information matrix. It sets the theoretical minimum variance, and thus the maximum possible accuracy, achievable by any unbiased estimator. By minimizing the CRLB, the optimization aims for the highest possible estimation precision.

### 5. Adaptive Microscope Numerical Simulation

To demonstrate and evaluate the framework, a numerical model of a multiphoton fluorescence adaptive microscope was developed, based on a scalar Fourier optics model.

- **Wavefront and Aberrations:** The total phase $\phi$ in the illumination pupil for each acquired biased image is modeled as a sum of system aberration coefficients ($\theta_k$) and introduced bias aberration coefficients ($a_k$) for a set of Zernike modes ($Z_k$). The number of Zernike modes considered varied in different analyses (e.g., five or seven modes).
- **Point Spread Function (PSF):** The multiphoton PSF is calculated using the Fourier transform of the pupil function incorporating the total phase $\phi$, followed by a nonlinearity factor $\beta$ (e.g., $\beta=2$ for two-photon, $\beta=3$ for three-photon).
- **Image Generation:** The observed image $X$ of an object $O$ is modeled as an incoherent imaging process. It follows a Poisson distribution, derived from the convolution of the PSF with the object, scaled by an effective brightness $\alpha$, and with added background $b$. This model accounts for factors like sample brightness, illumination power, and detector noise.
- **3D Object Approximation:** For extended volumetric objects, the focal volume is sampled at five equally spaced axial planes. Each 2D plane of the 3D PSF is convolved with the corresponding 2D plane of the 3D object, and the resulting five 2D images are summed axially to compute a single 2D image.
- **Input Measurements:** The definition of $X$ varied depending on the sensorless AO method being analyzed. For example, $X$ could be the total intensity of a point-like object image (for parabolic fitting) or full images of point-like or volumetric objects (for machine learning-based methods). All pixels in $N$ images (obtained from $N$ different biases) were treated as independent observations and summed for Fisher information calculation.

### 6. Optimization Process

The objective of the optimization is to determine the optimal set of bias aberrations ($a_k$) that minimize the CRLB for the estimated aberration coefficients ($\theta_k$).

- **Loss Function:** A loss function was defined as the square root of the sum of CRLBs for the parameters to be estimated.
- **Optimizer:** The Adam gradient descent optimizer was employed for minimization.
- **Initialization:** The $N$ bias aberrations were initialized randomly, following a uniform distribution within a predefined range.
- **Dataset Generation:** A large dataset (e.g., >8,000 samples) of simulated examples with randomly generated system aberrations (following a uniform distribution in an n-sphere) was created to ensure stable convergence and sufficient coverage of the analysis range.
- **Mini-batch Optimization:** To manage memory usage, mini-batch optimization was used, where optimization steps were performed on randomly selected subsets of the dataset.
- **Implementation:** The entire framework was implemented using Tensorflow in Python and executed on a GPU (Nvidia GeForce RTX 3070). Hyperparameters of the optimization process (e.g., learning rate, batch size) were empirically adjusted.

### 7. Main Findings and Results

The Fisher information-based analysis framework was applied to optimize sensorless AO methods across three distinct application scenarios, demonstrating its versatility and effectiveness.

### 8. Optimized Bias Amplitude for Intensity-Based Parabolic Fitting Methods

This section investigated the optimization of bias amplitude for a common sensorless AO technique: the 2N+1 parabolic fitting method, which uses total image intensity as feedback. Two imaging modalities, two-photon (2-P) and three-photon (3-P) microscopy, were simulated. The initial aberrations consisted of five Zernike modes (astigmatism: Z$_5$, Z$_6$; coma: Z$_7$, Z$_8$; spherical: Z$_{11}$) with RMS values not exceeding 0.5 rad or 1.0 rad.

- **CRLB and Residual Error Correspondence:** For 2-P microscopy (both 0.5 rad and 1.0 rad RMS aberrations) and 3-P microscopy (0.5 rad RMS aberrations), the mean CRLB loss curves generally tracked the simulated mean residual errors after correction.
- **Optimal Bias Amplitudes:** The bias amplitudes derived from minimizing the CRLB closely corresponded to the minima of the simulated residual error curves.
- **Mode-Specific Behavior:** Astigmatism modes (Z$_5$, Z$_6$) and coma modes (Z$_7$, Z$_8$) exhibited similar optimal bias amplitudes within their respective pairs. The spherical mode (Z$_{11}$) generally required the smallest optimized bias amplitude for 2-P microscopy.
- **Aberration Range Dependence:** The optimized bias amplitudes increased when correcting larger RMS aberrations, aligning with prior research.
- **3-P Microscopy Deviation:** For 3-P microscopy with 1.0 rad RMS aberrations, the CRLB loss curves started to deviate from the computed residual errors. However, the residual error curve was notably flat, indicating that the parabolic fitting algorithm's validity range is narrower for 3-P systems due to higher nonlinearity. The CRLB-derived optimum still fell within this flat, near-optimal range.

### 9. Optimized Bias Modes for Better-Conditioned Phase Information

This analysis aimed to derive optimal bias modal shapes for sensorless AO, particularly relevant for machine learning-based methods. Seven Zernike modes (astigmatism: Z$_5$, Z$_6$; coma: Z$_7$, Z$_8$; trefoil: Z$_9$, Z$_{10}$; spherical: Z$_{11}$) with RMS values below 3 rad were considered. The study compared two imaging scenarios: point-like objects and extended volumetric objects in a 2-P microscope.

- **Scenario-Specific Optimal Biases:**
 - For point-like objects, the two derived optimal bias aberrations closely resembled the defocus mode.
 - For extended volumetric objects, the derived optimal biases were similar to astigmatism. (Defocus was excluded from analysis for volumetric objects to avoid ambiguity with axial sample shifts.)
- **Performance Improvement:** When using a machine learning-based sensorless AO algorithm, the optimized set of bias modes consistently resulted in lower residual errors compared to individual Zernike-mode biases. The CRLB loss similarly indicated superior performance for the optimized modes.
- **Modal Ambiguity for Odd-Row Modes:** Coma (Z$_7$, Z$_8$) and trefoil (Z$_9$, Z$_{10}$) modes performed less effectively as biases for point-like objects compared to volumetric objects. This was attributed to an ambiguity: for ideal, in-focus 2D planes, PSF deformations caused by opposite-signed odd-row Zernike modes are not distinguishable. For volumetric samples, however, these opposite-signed modes produce distinct PSF deformations in out-of-focus planes, resolving the ambiguity and improving their utility as biases.

### 10. Optimized Bias Strategy for Fixed Image Exposure

This section investigated the impact of the number of bias images (M) on sensorless AO accuracy under practical constraints, specifically a fixed total photon budget. Three levels of photon budget (high, medium, low) were analyzed in a 2-P microscope using a machine learning-based algorithm. The initial aberration comprised seven Zernike modes (RMS < 3 rad). Three biasing strategies were compared: 2 biases, N+1 biases, and 2N biases (where N is the number of corrected modes).

- **2N Biases Superiority:** Across all three photon budget levels, the sensorless AO algorithm utilizing 2N optimized biases consistently achieved the best performance (lowest residual error).
- **Diminishing Returns:** While increasing the number of biases generally improved performance, the relative benefit became marginal as the number of measurements increased significantly.
- **CRLB Predictive Power:** The CRLB analysis accurately predicted the performance trends of the different sensorless AO configurations across varying photon budgets and numbers of biases, indicating its utility in guiding method design.
- **Bias Characteristics (Supplementary Information):**
 - The RMS values of the derived optimized biases generally increased with the number of biases used in the correction cycle.
 - For the 2-bias strategy, astigmatism was the dominant modal component. As the number of biases increased, the trefoil component became more significant. For the 2N-bias strategy, both coma and spherical modes showed significant contributions, and there was no single dominant modal shape.
 - The optimized bias modes were predominantly orthogonal, as shown by the diagonal dominance of their inner product matrices. This suggests an even distribution of information spanning the aberration coefficient space.

### 11. Significance and Potential Impact

This work introduces a Fisher information-based framework that offers a systematic and objective approach to optimizing image-based sensorless adaptive optics methods. This represents a significant shift from the predominantly empirical and heuristic design procedures currently employed in the field.

The potential impacts of this research are multi-faceted:

- **Enhanced Accuracy and Efficiency:** By objectively determining optimal method settings, such as bias amplitudes and modal shapes, the framework can lead to more accurate aberration correction and improved efficiency in data acquisition. This translates to fewer required image acquisitions and faster iterative correction processes, which are critical for practical applications constrained by time, phototoxicity, or photobleaching.
- **Improved Data Conditioning:** The optimization ensures that input data for sensorless AO algorithms are optimally conditioned, maximizing the extractable phase information. This is particularly relevant for advanced algorithms, including those based on machine learning, where the quality of input data directly influences performance.
- **Guidance for Algorithm Design:** The framework provides valuable insights into the design of new sensorless AO schemes, offering a statistical and reliable overview of method performance even when direct experimental comparisons are impractical. This can accelerate the development and refinement of novel AO techniques.
- **Versatility and Broad Applicability:** The demonstrated flexibility of the Fisher information analysis across different AO methods (e.g., parabolic fitting, machine learning), imaging modalities (2-P, 3-P), and object types (point-like, volumetric) suggests its potential to benefit a wide range of optical imaging applications beyond the specific examples presented.
- **Fundamental Understanding:** The analysis contributes to a deeper understanding of how phase information is encoded in images under various practical conditions, including different aberration types, photon budgets, and sample structures. This includes insights into modal ambiguities for specific Zernike modes depending on the imaging scenario.
- **Complementary to Empirical Approaches:** While acknowledging that practical algorithms may not always be unbiased, the framework provides a powerful, information-guided complement to empirical method design. It sets an upper bound for obtainable information, offering a theoretical benchmark for actual algorithm performance.

Future developments, such as variations to compute more targeted aberration-related information, could further refine the framework for method-specific analyses. Ultimately, this information-guided approach has the potential to elevate the design of sensorless AO systems from a largely experiential endeavor to a scientifically optimized process, thereby advancing the capabilities of high-resolution optical imaging.
