# Modality Bias in LVLMs: Analyzing and Mitigating Object Hallucination via Attention Lens

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University and ShanghaiTech University identified "modality bias" as a mechanism behind object hallucination in large vision-language models (LVLMs), where models fail to balance attention between visual and textual inputs. They developed TVAI, a training-free inference method that manipulates attention weights, reducing sentence-level hallucination on LLaVA-1.5 from 47.6% to 22.4% on the CHAIR benchmark and improving VQA accuracy on POPE.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) frequently suffer from "object hallucination," generating content inconsistent with the visual input, which hinders real-world deployment.
- Previous explanations for hallucination, such as fine-grained visual underrepresentation or overwhelming linguistic prior, were identified as incomplete.
- Existing hallucination mitigation strategies often incur high computational costs, requiring extensive re-training, significant data annotation, or substantial inference overhead.

## Method

- Empirically analyzed attention distributions during hallucination using ==Textual Attention Ratio (TAR)== and ==Visual Attention Ratio (VAR)== to characterize modality bias.
- Developed ==Textual and Visual Attention Intervention (TVAI)==, a training-free inference method that dynamically augments attention weights for text and image tokens within the deeper decoder layers of LVLMs.
- Integrated a ==contrastive decoding strategy== with TVAI to further reduce the model's overreliance on internal parametric knowledge and drive responses toward visual grounding.

## Results

- TVAI significantly reduced sentence-level hallucination (C_S) for LLaVA-1.5 from **47.6%** to **22.4%** and instance-level hallucination (C_I) from **13.7%** to **6.3%** on the CHAIR benchmark.
- The method improved visual question answering accuracy on the POPE benchmark for LLaVA-1.5 from **79.3%** to **81.2%** and for MiniGPT-4 from **70.6%** to **71.7%**, demonstrating enhanced visual grounding.
- TVAI preserved or slightly improved the general capabilities (accuracy, precision, recall, F1) of LVLMs across various architectures on the MMBench benchmark, indicating no compromise to overall performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - "Modality bias" is a primary driver of object hallucination, where LVLMs struggle to simultaneously attend to and integrate information from both visual and textual modalities.
> - Generative hallucination (describing non-existent objects) is often associated with models predominantly focusing on visual information while neglecting text; discriminative hallucination (incorrect "yes/no" answers) shows a bias towards textual information.
> - Balanced attention distribution between visual and textual modalities is crucial for accurate, non-hallucinated responses, indicating that fragmented understanding contributes significantly to errors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) represent a significant leap in multimodal AI, demonstrating impressive capabilities in understanding and reasoning across visual and textual data. Models like LLaVA, MiniGPT-4, Qwen-VL, and Shikra have showcased their potential in diverse applications, from autonomous driving to healthcare. However, a critical limitation impeding their real-world deployment is "object hallucination," where models generate descriptions or answers inconsistent with the provided visual information. This phenomenon poses substantial risks, particularly in sensitive domains requiring high fidelity and factual grounding.

Previous research into object hallucination primarily attributed it to two main causes:
1. **Fine-grained underrepresentation:** The inability of cross-modal alignment techniques to capture nuanced semantic associations, limiting LVLMs' accuracy in representing object properties.
2. **Linguistic prior/Language bias:** The disproportionately larger parameter size of Large Language Models (LLMs) compared to visual encoders often leads to an over-reliance on learned linguistic knowledge and internal parametric memory, overriding visual evidence.

In response to these identified causes, existing mitigation strategies have emerged across different phases:
- **Post-processing:** Applying corrections to generated outputs (e.g., Zhou et al. 2023). While direct, these incur significant inference costs.
- **Training-phase interventions:** Re-training or fine-tuning LVLMs with additional curated, high-quality annotated data (e.g., Li et al. 2024b). These approaches are effective but demand substantial time, computational resources, and data annotation efforts.
- **Inference-stage interventions:** Employing diverse decoding strategies to penalize language bias (e.g., Liu et al. 2024c, VCD Leng et al. 2024). These methods also add to inference time and computational overhead. PAI (Liu, Zheng, and Chen 2024) is a notable prior work that, similar to this paper, intervenes at the inference stage by augmenting visual attention weights, based on the observation that image tokens often receive low attention.

This paper distinguishes itself by challenging the prevailing notion that hallucination is solely due to insufficient visual information or an overwhelming linguistic prior. It introduces a novel empirical observation: **modality bias**. The authors demonstrate that LVLMs not only ignore visual information but can also overlook textual instructions during hallucination, suggesting a fragmented understanding where the model struggles to simultaneously attend to both modalities. This new perspective reframes the problem of hallucination, moving beyond a simple "language bias" vs. "visual insufficiency" dichotomy to propose a more fundamental issue of imbalanced cross-modal attention integration.

By proposing a **training-free** method that manipulates attention weights to balance textual and visual information, coupled with contrastive decoding, this work aims to provide a highly practical and generalizable solution. It directly addresses the limitations of costly and domain-specific retraining, significant inference overhead, or reliance on external tools, offering a more efficient and broadly applicable approach within the current research landscape.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to thoroughly investigate the underlying mechanisms of object hallucination in Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) and, based on these insights, develop an effective and efficient mitigation strategy.

The motivation for this study stems from several critical observations and challenges:
- **Pervasiveness and Impact of Object Hallucination:** Despite their advanced multimodal reasoning capabilities, current LVLMs frequently generate object descriptions or make claims that contradict the visual content of an image. This "hallucination" severely undermines their reliability and trustworthiness, hindering their deployment in real-world applications where factual accuracy is paramount, such as autonomous driving or medical diagnosis.
- **Limitations of Prior Hallucination Explanations:** Previous research largely attributed hallucination to either a "linguistic prior" (over-reliance on textual knowledge due to the LLM's scale) or "fine-grained visual underrepresentation." While these factors contribute, the authors hypothesized a deeper, less-explored mechanism.
- **Empirical Discovery of "Modality Bias":** Through in-depth empirical investigation, the authors uncovered a novel phenomenon termed "modality bias." They observed that during hallucination, LVLMs do not just underutilize visual information but can also disregard textual instructions. This indicates that models struggle to simultaneously attend to and integrate information from both visual and textual modalities, leading to a fragmented understanding of user intent.
 - Specifically, they identify two patterns: **generative hallucination** (e.g., describing non-existent objects) where the model might overly focus on visual representations while neglecting textual nuance, and **discriminative hallucination** (e.g., incorrect "yes/no" answers) where the model might over-rely on textual queries while ignoring visual cues.
- **Demand for Cost-Effective Mitigation:** Existing hallucination mitigation techniques often involve significant computational costs, such as extensive re-training, large-scale data annotation, or substantial inference overhead. There is a strong motivation to develop practical solutions that can enhance LVLM reliability without incurring these prohibitive costs.

Therefore, the key objectives are to:
1. Empirically validate and characterize the "modality bias" phenomenon as a primary driver of object hallucination in LVLMs.
2. Propose a simple, effective, and **training-free** method, called TVAI (Textual and Visual Attention Intervention), to mitigate object hallucination by balancing the model's attention to both textual and visual modalities during inference.
3. Enhance TVAI's efficacy by integrating a contrastive decoding strategy to further reduce the model's overreliance on internal parametric knowledge.
4. Demonstrate the generalizability and superior performance of the proposed method across various open-source LVLMs and standard hallucination benchmarks, while ensuring the preservation of the models' general capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this paper is two-pronged: first, an empirical investigation to characterize the newly identified "modality bias," and second, the development of a training-free intervention mechanism.

#### 3.1. Empirical Investigation of Modality Bias

To understand how LVLMs distribute attention during hallucination, the authors define two metrics:
- **Textual Attention Ratio (TAR):** Quantifies the cumulative attention assigned by a newly generated token to the input text tokens from the instruction.
- **Visual Attention Ratio (VAR):** Quantifies the cumulative attention assigned by a newly generated token to the input image tokens from the instruction.

The authors conducted experiments using prominent open-source LVLMs (LLaVA-1.5 and Qwen-VL-Chat) on the COCO dataset. They computed TAR and VAR for hallucinated tokens during both generative (e.g., describing an object not in the image) and discriminative (e.g., incorrectly answering "yes/no" about an object's presence) hallucination events. The empirical findings, illustrated in Figure 1 and Figure 3, revealed distinct attention patterns:
- For **generative hallucination**, models predominantly focused on visual representation (higher VAR) while neglecting textual information (lower TAR).
- For **discriminative hallucination**, models exhibited a bias toward textual information (higher TAR) while ignoring visual cues (lower VAR).
- In contrast, **non-hallucinated tokens** showed more balanced TAR and VAR, indicating a more integrated understanding.

This analysis empirically confirmed the existence of "modality bias," where LVLMs struggle to allocate balanced and modality-aware attention, processing modalities independently rather than integratively.

#### 3.2. Textual and Visual Attention Intervention (TVAI)

Inspired by the empirical observation of modality bias, the authors proposed TVAI, a training-free method designed to adjust TAR and VAR during the inference phase to mitigate unimodal bias.

- **Mechanism of Intervention:** TVAI intervenes in the self-attention mechanisms within the decoding layers of LVLMs. For each attention head `h` in layer `l`, the attention weights `A_l,h` are manipulated before the softmax operation.
 - For **text tokens**, their attention weights `A_l,h(k, i)` (where `k` is the current token being generated, and `i` is an input text token) are augmented: `A'_l,h(k, i) = A_l,h(k, i) + α|A_l,h(k, i)|`. The hyperparameter `α` controls the excitation strength for textual attention.
 - For **image tokens**, their attention weights `A_l,h(k, j)` (where `j` is an input image token) are augmented: `A'_l,h(k, j) = A_l,h(k, j) + β|A_l,h(k, j)|`. The hyperparameter `β` controls the excitation strength for visual attention.
- **Optimal Excitation Location:** The intervention is specifically applied in the deeper decoder layers, where the "attention sink" phenomenon (attentional redundancy) typically emerges and hidden states stabilize. This ensures that the manipulation effectively enhances the influence of textual and visual tokens during generation.
- **Rationale:** The approach is grounded in the belief that pretrained visual encoders and language decoders already possess robust comprehension. By directly augmenting attention weights along their original directions, the model can be guided towards more reliable, instruction-following responses without requiring complex learned activation shifts.

#### 3.3. Contrastive Decoding

To further reduce the LVLM's overreliance on its internal parametric knowledge, which can still lead to hallucination despite balanced attention, TVAI integrates a contrastive decoding strategy:
- **Mechanism:** The final conditional probability `p_final` for generating the `k`-th token is a weighted combination of the token probability derived from the attention-intervened map `p'(y_k|y_<k)` and the original probability from the vanilla attention weights `p(y_k|y_<k)`.
 - `p_final = γ ⋅ p'(y_k|y_<k) + (1 - γ) ⋅ p(y_k|y_<k)`
- **`γ` Parameter:** The hyperparameter `γ` (where `γ > 1`) controls the strength of the contrastive decoding. A `γ` greater than 1 emphasizes the `p'` distribution, which is guided by the TVAI-modified attention, effectively pushing the model away from its default, potentially hallucination-prone parametric knowledge.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

The efficacy and generalizability of TVAI were evaluated across four popular open-source LVLMs: LLaVA-1.5, Qwen-VL-Chat, MiniGPT-4, and Shikra. Baselines included vanilla LVLMs and state-of-the-art hallucination mitigation methods like OPERA, VCD, and PAI.
- **Benchmarks:**
 - **CHAIR:** For long-sequence image captioning tasks, assessing generative hallucination via instance-level (C_I) and sentence-level (C_S) hallucination rates.
 - **POPE:** For visual question answering (VQA) tasks (specifically discriminative object recognition), measuring accuracy and F1 score, particularly in the challenging adversarial setting.
 - **MMBench:** For general capability evaluation, using overall average accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 across English and Chinese subsets.
- **Training-free nature:** A crucial aspect is that TVAI does not require re-training or fine-tuning, operating purely at the inference stage.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted by Zheng and Zhang yielded compelling evidence supporting their hypothesis of modality bias and the effectiveness of their proposed TVAI method.

#### 4.1. Widespread Modality Bias in LVLMs

The empirical analysis, using Textual Attention Ratio (TAR) and Visual Attention Ratio (VAR), robustly confirmed the presence of modality bias across different LVLMs (LLaVA-1.5 and Qwen-VL-Chat). As depicted in Figure 1, the attention patterns for hallucinated tokens were distinctly unbalanced:
- **Generative Hallucination:** Tokens generated inconsistently with the image displayed a predominant focus on visual representation (high VAR), often neglecting textual instructions.
- **Discriminative Hallucination:** Tokens generated as incorrect "yes/no" responses showed a stronger reliance on textual information (high TAR), disregarding visual cues.
- **Balanced Attention for Non-Hallucinated Tokens:** In contrast, tokens that were correctly generated exhibited a more balanced distribution of attention between visual and textual modalities (Figure 3), indicating that effective cross-modal integration is crucial for accurate responses. This finding is a foundational contribution, shifting the understanding of hallucination mechanisms.

#### 4.2. Superior Hallucination Mitigation

**Long-sequence Generation Task (CHAIR Benchmark):**
TVAI demonstrated remarkable success in mitigating generative hallucination, outperforming vanilla LVLMs and existing state-of-the-art methods (OPERA, VCD, PAI) across LLaVA-1.5, MiniGPT-4, and Shikra.
- For **LLaVA-1.5**, TVAI reduced the sentence-level hallucination (C_S) from 47.6% (Vanilla) to 22.4%, and instance-level hallucination (C_I) from 13.7% to 6.3%.
- Similar significant reductions were observed for MiniGPT-4 (C_S from 29.6% to 15.4%, C_I from 10.0% to 5.2%) and Shikra (C_S from 46.0% to 35.0%, C_I from 14.2% to 9.4%).
- Ablation studies confirmed the synergistic effect of jointly manipulating textual and visual attention (TVAI performed better than TAI or VAI alone), underscoring the importance of balanced multimodal intervention.

**Visual Question Answering Task (POPE Benchmark):**
In the VQA task, particularly under the challenging adversarial setting designed to exploit parametric knowledge biases, TVAI consistently achieved superior accuracy and F1 scores.
- For **LLaVA-1.5**, accuracy improved from 79.3% (Vanilla) to 81.2% and F1 from 81.1% to 81.8%.
- MiniGPT-4 saw an increase in accuracy from 70.6% to 71.7% and F1 from 71.1% to 71.5%.
- Shikra improved accuracy from 76.2% to 78.7% and F1 from 78.4% to 79.8%.
These results highlight TVAI's ability to ground models' responses more effectively in visual evidence, overcoming reliance on prior knowledge when answering discriminative questions.

#### 4.3. Preservation and Enhancement of General Capabilities

Crucially, TVAI did not compromise the general capabilities of the LVLMs. On the **MMBench benchmark**, TVAI either preserved or slightly improved the models' performance (accuracy, precision, recall, F1) across both English and Chinese subsets (Table 3). This indicates that mitigating modality bias not only reduces hallucination but also enhances the overall perceptual and instruction-following abilities of LVLMs, suggesting that modality bias inherently impairs the model's comprehensive understanding.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies and Hyperparameter Sensitivity

Ablation studies provided insights into the method's components:
- The comparison between TVAI and its simplified variants (TAI for textual intervention only, VAI for visual intervention only) confirmed the synergistic benefits of a dual-modality approach.
- The impact of manipulation strengths (`α`, `β`) and contrast decoding intensity (`γ`) was explored (Table 4). Increasing `α` and `γ` generally improved hallucination reduction. However, excessively high `γ` could lead to overcorrection, disrupting precision-recall balance and reducing output reliability, emphasizing the need for careful hyperparameter tuning. The optimal hyperparameters were found to vary across different LVLM architectures, reflecting differences in their internal mechanisms and tokenization.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Examples

Visualizations (Figure 5) provided qualitative evidence that TVAI-augmented outputs were factually consistent with the images, avoided spurious details, and, importantly, often provided more fine-grained and accurate descriptions (e.g., identifying a "Giants jersey" or "sunglasses"), demonstrating an enhanced perceptual capability.

In summary, the main findings strongly support that modality bias is a significant, widespread contributor to hallucination in LVLMs, and TVAI offers a robust, efficient, and generalizable solution to address this issue effectively across diverse tasks and models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Modality Bias in LVLMs: Analyzing and Mitigating Object Hallucination via Attention Lens" carries significant implications for the development and deployment of Large Vision-Language Models, with potential impacts across several key areas:

#### 5.1. Novel Understanding of Hallucination Mechanisms

The most profound significance of this work lies in its empirical identification and characterization of **"modality bias."** By demonstrating that LVLMs struggle to simultaneously attend to and integrate both visual and textual information, even ignoring instructions, the paper provides a crucial new perspective beyond the widely accepted "language prior" or "visual insufficiency" explanations. This finding reshapes the theoretical understanding of hallucination, opening new avenues for fundamental research into the architectural designs, training paradigms, and attention mechanisms required for true cross-modal integration. It suggests that a model's "understanding" can be fragmented, leading to errors not just from lack of information but from a failure to synthesize available information.

#### 5.2. Practical and Cost-Effective Hallucination Mitigation

The proposed **TVAI** method is training-free, meaning it does not require expensive retraining, fine-tuning, or extensive data annotation. This significantly lowers the barrier to entry for hallucination mitigation, making it highly practical for immediate application to existing and future LVLMs. For researchers and developers, this translates into substantial savings in computational resources and development time. The generalizability across various LVLM architectures and decoding strategies further enhances its practical utility, promoting widespread adoption and enabling a more rapid improvement in model reliability across the ecosystem.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Trustworthiness and Reliability of LVLMs

Object hallucination is a major impediment to the safe and trustworthy deployment of LVLMs in real-world, high-stakes applications. By substantially reducing both generative and discriminative hallucinations, TVAI directly contributes to making LVLMs more dependable. This is critical for applications such as:
- **Autonomous Driving:** Where misinterpreting visual cues or fabricating non-existent objects could have catastrophic safety implications.
- **Healthcare:** Where accurate descriptions of medical images and factual responses are vital for diagnostic assistance and patient safety.
- **Educational Tools:** Ensuring that generated content is factually correct and aligned with visual aids.
The enhanced factual consistency leads to greater user confidence and broader societal acceptance of LVLM technologies.

#### 5.4. Improved Perceptual and Multimodal Reasoning Capabilities

Beyond simply preventing errors, the findings from MMBench and qualitative examples demonstrate that mitigating modality bias can actually **enhance the LVLM's overall perceptual ability and fine-grained understanding.** By forcing models to more effectively integrate all available modal information, TVAI enables them to produce more comprehensive, nuanced, and accurate descriptions. This suggests that modality bias is not just a source of error but also a bottleneck for achieving deeper, more grounded multimodal reasoning. The method pushes LVLMs beyond superficial representations towards more holistic comprehension.

#### 5.5. Guiding Future Research Directions

The identification of modality bias provides clear directions for future research. The authors themselves suggest exploring **dynamic attention distribution manipulation** during inference, which could adapt in real-time to the current token generation context, offering even greater flexibility and robustness. Furthermore, the findings highlight the need for improved multimodal data preparation pipelines and training paradigms that inherently foster balanced cross-modal attention, moving beyond ad-hoc interventions. This research serves as a foundational step, inspiring the AI community to rethink how LVLMs are designed and trained to fundamentally resolve issues related to fragmented multimodal understanding.

In conclusion, "Modality Bias in LVLMs" is a timely and impactful contribution that not only offers a novel diagnostic for a critical LVLM flaw but also provides an immediate, efficient, and broadly applicable solution. Its significance extends from advancing the theoretical understanding of multimodal AI to enabling more trustworthy and capable next-generation vision-language technologies.
