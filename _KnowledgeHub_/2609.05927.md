# GIF: Agentic Generation of Interactive and Functional Object Compositions for Robot Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> GIF is an agentic framework that generates simulation-ready two-object compositions with functionally correct contact. It recasts the problem as disentangled reconstruction plus relative-pose recovery: CoGen produces instance-disentangled meshes via paired direct and erase-and-regenerate branches, GPRM refines pose through ICP, render-based, collision, and simulation-evolutionary stages, and a VLM verifier selects candidates. Across eight contact-geometry classes it beats baselines with collision rate below 1%.

## Problem

- Automated scene generators target coarse room- or table-scale layouts, specifying approximate placements, so they miss the object-level functional relations manipulation requires.
- Language-prompted methods ask vision-language models to emit positions and rotations directly, but these models lack the precise local 3D geometry understanding contact tasks demand.
- Visual-prompted pipelines depend on pose estimators that fail out of distribution, and mutual occlusion at contact interfaces makes reconstruction fuse instances or hallucinate hidden surfaces.

## Method

- **CoGen** builds instance-disentangled meshes by pairing direct image-to-3D lifting with an ==erase-and-regenerate== branch that removes the partner object and amodally completes the target, keeping both candidates.
- **GPRM** chains coarse-to-fine ==ICP==, render-based silhouette and depth optimization, collision-avoidance programming, and ==CMA-ES== evolutionary search under physics feedback, with a VLM verifier inspecting multi-view rollouts.
- Verified scenes become terminal configurations for demonstration synthesis: randomized poses, VLM-annotated symmetry equivalence classes, ray-traced rendering with ==domain randomization==, then a vision-language-action policy trained purely in simulation.

## Results

- On the 120-scene eight-class benchmark GIF leads relationship matching in every class, averaging **7.82** versus **6.33** for the strongest baseline and **1.48** for the weakest.
- Real-robot success scales with generated asset diversity: sausage serving rises **0.20** to **0.90** and pot covering **0.00** to **0.75** as variants grow from two to forty.
- Ablations confirm both modules: dropping complementary reconstruction cuts capping relationship matching **9.00** to **2.80**, and human raters prefer GIF in **95.9-100%** of pairwise relation comparisons.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Contact-precise placement is a reconstruction problem first: if meshes fuse or truncate at the occluded interface, no pose optimizer recovers the functional relation.
> - Image-space alignment alone is insufficient; only closing the loop through simulation feedback catches relations that stay collision-free yet collapse under gravity.
> - Asset diversity, not just demonstration count, drives sim-to-real transfer: forty generated variants turned a zero-success real-robot task into a usable policy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The field of robot learning and embodied AI increasingly relies on simulation for scalable data generation and evaluation of foundation models. Existing work on automated scene generation has made progress in creating diverse simulated environments. However, much of this prior research has primarily focused on generating coarse-grained scene layouts, such as arranging objects on tables, furniture, or within rooms, for applications like navigation and scene-level reasoning.

Robot manipulation tasks, in contrast, often demand fine-grained functional relationships between objects, governed by precise local geometric compatibility, collision avoidance, and physical stability. Tasks such as capping, insertion, hanging, and containment require accurate relative 6-Degrees of Freedom (6-DoF) poses that align with human intention and physical constraints. While advancements in 3D asset generation provide high-fidelity objects, they frequently encounter challenges in accurately recovering these precise relative poses, especially in contact-rich scenarios where mutual occlusion occurs at the interaction interfaces. Language-prompted methods often lack detailed 3D geometry understanding, and visual-prompted methods can suffer from out-of-distribution issues or numerical optimization difficulties.

This work addresses this identified gap by introducing a framework specifically designed for generating interactive and functional object compositions with contact-precise relative poses. It moves beyond coarse scene layouts to focus on the intricate local interactions crucial for robotic manipulation, aiming to bridge the "pose gap" in automated scene generation for robot learning.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an agentic framework, named GIF (Agentic Generation of Interactive and Functional Object Compositions), capable of generating simulation-ready object compositions that exhibit diverse, interactive, and functional relationships with precise relative poses. This addresses the challenge of scalable evaluation and data generation pipelines required for robot manipulation foundation models.

The motivation for GIF stems from several key observations and limitations in existing approaches:

1. **Demand for scalable data:** Robot manipulation foundation models necessitate vast amounts of diverse and realistic data for training and evaluation. Simulation offers a cost-effective and parallelizable environment for this purpose.
2. **Limitations of existing scene generators:** Prior automated scene generation systems primarily focus on broad scene layouts, specifying approximate placements. These systems typically do not account for the fine-grained geometric and physical constraints essential for functional interactions between objects.
3. **Gap in functional object compositions:** Tasks like capping, hooking, insertion, and containment require objects to be placed in specific relative 6-DoF poses that ensure functional contact, avoid interpenetration, and maintain physical stability. Existing methods struggle to reliably generate such "contact-precise" compositions.
4. **Challenges in asset and pose recovery:** While 3D generative models can produce high-fidelity assets, recovering precise relative poses for interacting objects is difficult. Language-prompted methods often lack the geometric understanding for accurate placements, while visual-prompted methods face issues with generalization and robust initialization for pose estimation. Specifically, in functional object pairs, mutual occlusion at contact interfaces can lead to instance fusion, partner geometry leakage, or incomplete object reconstruction when using direct image-to-3D lifting.
5. **Need for robust simulation-ready assets:** For robot learning, generated scenes must be ready for physics simulation, meaning objects must be independently manipulable and possess accurate collision geometry in addition to visual plausibility.

By addressing these motivations, GIF aims to provide a robust and automated solution for generating diverse and physically plausible functional object compositions, thereby facilitating the development and evaluation of more generalizable robot manipulation policies.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The GIF framework recasts the problem of generating interactive and functional object compositions as a two-stage process: disentangled reconstruction of individual object assets, followed by refined relative pose recovery. The process is guided by a multimodal agent and verified by a Vision-Language Model (VLM).

The methodology comprises five main components:

1. **Multimodal Input Canonicalization (Sec. 3.1):**
 - A multimodal agent converts various input forms (language instructions, reference images, or combinations) into a unified specification: a foreground-only reference image (`I_ref`) of the desired object pair and a structured JSON record (`J_scene`) detailing object identities, attributes, and the target relation.
 - This agent operates in an analyze-act-verify loop. It first identifies objects and their relations, then uses image generation or editing tools to produce `I_ref`. A VLM verifier checks the candidate `I_ref` against criteria such as background simplicity, object count, visibility, and contact plausibility.
 - Simulation-ready assets are prepared by performing convex decomposition on the visual meshes, separating visual and collision geometries.

2. **CoGen: Complementary 2D-3D Generative Asset Construction (Sec. 3.2):**
 - CoGen addresses the challenge of obtaining instance-disentangled meshes and coarse initial poses, especially in contact-rich scenarios where mutual occlusion can lead to fused or incomplete reconstructions.
 - It generates two mesh-pose candidates for each object:
 - **Direct Reconstruction:** Uses an off-the-shelf image-to-3D model (SAM3D) conditioned on `I_ref` and a binary instance mask (`b_ref_i`).
 - **Erase-and-Regenerate Branch:** Exploits 2D generative models' stronger object priors and larger training corpora. It erases the partner object from `I_ref` and then completes the target object, generating an occlusion-free image (`I_erase_i`). This image is then lifted to 3D by SAM3D.
 - Both candidate mesh-pose pairs (`m_ref_i`, `p_ref_i`) and (`m_erase_i`, `p_erase_i`) are retained for downstream refinement. Mask preparation incorporates a synonym-driven retry mechanism, and the erase-and-regenerate process is a closed-loop agent with VLM-based verification to ensure pose preservation.

3. **GPRM: Geometry- and Physics-Guided Pose Refinement Modules (Sec. 3.3):**
 - GPRM iteratively refines CoGen’s coarse mesh–pose candidates through a sequence of modules. For each object pair, four initial pairings (from `ref` and `erase` candidates) are considered.
 - **Geometry-Guided Refinement I: Coarse-to-Fine ICP:** Iterative Closest Point (ICP) aligns the 3D model to observed geometry. It samples a source cloud from the candidate mesh and a target cloud from back-projected reference depth (from a monocular geometry estimator), progressively tightening ICP rounds.
 - **Geometry-Guided Refinement II: Render-Based Optimization (RenderOpt):** This 2D refinement stage rasterizes each candidate mesh and updates its pose and scale to match the reference image's silhouette and depth. It uses per-object recall and precision terms to prevent leakage and a depth term for occlusion order, with regularization to the ICP initialization.
 - **Physics-Guided Refinement I: Collision-Avoidance Optimization (ColliOpt):** To ensure physical validity, this constrained optimization finds the nearest collision-free pose to the RenderOpt output. It enforces separation between convex-decomposition collision pieces of different objects and ground clearance, using Drake and SNOPT.
 - **Physics-Guided Refinement II: Simulation-Aware Evolutionary Optimization (EvoOpt):** When earlier refinements fail to yield a stable relation, MuJoCo is treated as a black-box objective. CMA-ES searches the pose neighborhood, with a fitness function penalizing dynamic drift, residual penetration, residual velocity, and deviation from the input pose, to find poses that remain stable under gravity.

4. **VLM-based Scene Selection (Sec. 3.4):**
 - For each candidate scene (initial or refined), it is loaded into MuJoCo and simulated until convergence. Multi-view, multi-timestep renders are tiled into a single image.
 - A VLM verifier inspects this image to determine if the target relation holds initially and is preserved in the final gravity-settled state.
 - Among scenes that pass verification, a second VLM ranks the candidates to select the one that best matches the `J_scene` specification.

5. **Policy Data Synthesis and VLA Training (Sec. 3.5):**
 - VLM-verified target scenes (`S_b`) define the terminal configurations for robot manipulation tasks.
 - Demonstrations are synthesized by randomizing initial object poses, scene illumination, and camera parameters.
 - A motion planner generates collision-free trajectories to achieve the target relative pose.
 - Symmetry-aware target sampling (using an LLM-based symmetry analyzer) defines target poses over equivalence classes to avoid redundant supervision.
 - High-fidelity ray-traced rendering with domain randomization is used to narrow the sim-to-real gap.
 - A Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model (`π_0.5`) is trained purely on these synthesized trajectories for direct real-robot deployment.

### 4. Main findings and results

The research presents several findings regarding GIF's performance in generating functional object compositions and its utility for robot learning.

1. **Benchmark Construction and Metrics:** The authors developed a benchmark suite spanning eight contact-geometry classes (Containment, Capping, Resting, Leaning, Hooking, Slotting, Spanning, Pegging), derived from ego-centric manipulation data. This suite comprises 24 tasks, each with five semantic variants, totaling 120 scenes. Evaluation metrics include Object Matching (O.M.), Relationship Matching (R.M.), and Collision Rate (C.R.).

2. **Superior Performance over Baselines:**
 - **Relationship Matching (R.M.):** GIF consistently achieved the highest R.M. scores across all eight relation classes, for both language-prompted and image-prompted inputs (Table 1). For language prompts, GIF averaged 7.82, outperforming the strongest baseline, SceneSmith, which averaged 6.33. For image prompts, GIF maintained a similar lead. This indicates GIF's ability to generate scenes with precise functional contact.
 - **Object Matching (O.M.):** GIF demonstrated strong asset fidelity, exceeding SceneSmith by an average of 0.50 in O.M. scores for language prompts, confirming that CoGen preserves asset quality while enhancing pose accuracy.
 - **Collision Rate (C.R.):** GIF achieved a near-zero collision rate (below 1%) across all tested scenarios, attributable to its physics-guided refinement modules (Table 1). This is a substantial improvement over baselines, some of which had C.R.s as high as 55.8%.

3. **Human Preference Study:** Human raters corroborated the VLM-based quantitative findings. In pairwise comparisons against four baselines, scenes generated by GIF were preferred with large Cohen's h effect sizes and tight Wilson confidence intervals (Table 2). The advantage was particularly pronounced in R.M., with GIF winning 95.9% against SceneSmith and 100% against GenieSim.

4. **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Effectiveness of CoGen:** Disabling complementary reconstruction (erase-and-regenerate branch) within CoGen resulted in a consistent drop in both O.M. and R.M. across all classes (Table 3, Figure 5). This highlights the importance of disentangled asset reconstruction for mitigating issues arising from occlusion and object interference.
 - **Effectiveness of GPRM:** A cumulative ablation of the GPRM stages (ICP, RenderOpt, ColliOpt, EvoOpt) showed consistent gains in R.M. with each successive module (Table 4). EvoOpt, which incorporates simulation feedback, provided the largest gains in classes where stability is highly sensitive to small errors (e.g., Hooking), affirming the necessity of physical simulation feedback for generating stable functional scenes.

5. **Policy Learning with GIF-Generated Tasks:** GIF-generated compositions were evaluated as tasks for policy learning. Training a VLA model (`π_0.5`) purely on simulated trajectories synthesized from GIF-generated asset variants demonstrated improved real-robot success with increased asset diversity (Figure 6). For "serve sausage with a paper box," real-robot success rose from 0.20 with two assets to 0.90 with forty. Similarly, for "cover pot with a lid," success increased from 0.00 to 0.75. This indicates that GIF assets and poses provide executable policy-learning goals and that diversity enhances real-world transfer.

6. **Preliminary Multi-Object Extension:** The paper presents a preliminary iterative extension of the pipeline to multi-object scenes, where new objects are successively inserted into an existing scene, and GIF's modules are rerun on the newcomer (Figure 7).

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The GIF framework represents a development in automated scene generation for robot learning, particularly in its focus on interactive and functional object compositions. Its significance and potential impact include:

1. **Enabling Scalable Robot Learning:** By automating the generation of contact-precise, simulation-ready functional object compositions, GIF directly addresses a bottleneck in acquiring diverse and high-quality data for training and evaluating robot manipulation foundation models. This scalability can accelerate research in generalist robotic capabilities.
2. **Bridging the "Pose Gap":** GIF's disentangled asset reconstruction (CoGen) and multi-stage pose refinement (GPRM), incorporating both geometric and physics-guided optimization, provide a robust solution for achieving precise 6-DoF relative poses. This is crucial for manipulation tasks that demand intricate object interactions.
3. **Enhanced Realism and Physical Plausibility:** The integration of physics-guided refinement and VLM-based verification ensures that generated scenes are not only visually plausible but also physically stable and free from interpenetration, making them suitable for reliable simulation and real-world transfer.
4. **Reduced Manual Effort:** Traditionally, creating complex, functionally interacting scenes for robot simulation requires extensive manual design by domain experts. GIF automates this process, significantly reducing the labor and expertise required, thereby making advanced simulation more accessible.
5. **Improved Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The empirical results demonstrating that increased diversity of GIF-generated assets improves real-robot policy success suggest that the framework can contribute to better generalization and transferability of policies from simulation to the real world.
6. **Foundation for Embodied AI:** The ability to generate complex, interactive environments with specific functional relations can serve as a foundational component for developing and testing more advanced embodied AI agents capable of understanding and executing human-like manipulation tasks.
7. **New Research Directions:** While GIF currently focuses on rigid object pairs, its agentic architecture and modular design suggest potential for future extensions to articulated and deformable objects, or hierarchical scene generation, which could further broaden its applicability in complex environments.

In summary, GIF offers a novel approach to generating high-fidelity, physically consistent interactive object compositions, which has the potential to significantly advance the capabilities and scalability of robot learning and embodied AI research.
