# ActLoc: Learning to Localize on the Move via Active Viewpoint Selection

> [!summary] Summary
> ActLoc presents a framework enabling robotic systems to improve visual localization by actively selecting optimal camera viewpoints. This approach learns to predict a dense distribution of localization performance across multiple yaw and pitch directions in a single inference pass, achieving higher localization success rates (e.g., 92.11% at 0.1m/1° on HM3D) with significantly reduced computation time compared to prior methods.

## Problem

- Visual localization systems often perform unreliably due to the passive nature of viewpoint selection and narrow camera field of view, especially in unmapped or ambiguous environments.
- Existing learning-based active localization methods are computationally expensive, requiring individual evaluation of viewpoints, which hinders real-time robotic applications.
- Prior learning-based approaches often struggle with generalization to diverse environments due to limited training datasets and frequently decouple viewpoint selection from overall path planning.

## Method

- An ==attention-based== model was developed to predict a '==LocMap==,' a 2D grid representing expected localization performance across various yaw and pitch directions in a single forward pass, leveraging prior ==Structure-from-Motion (SfM)== scene knowledge.
- The predicted LocMap is integrated into an active localization planner (**ActLoc**) that uses a ==mixed cost map== to balance localization accuracy with motion smoothness for online viewpoint selection and trajectory planning.
- A large-scale simulated dataset derived from 100 single-floor HM3D scenes was used to train the model, improving its generalization capabilities to unseen environments.

## Results

- ActLoc achieved superior localization success rates (e.g., **92.11%** at 0.1m/1° and 1° thresholds on HM3D) and lower average translation/rotation errors compared to baselines LWL and FIF in both single-viewpoint and full-trajectory planning scenarios.
- The framework demonstrated significant computational efficiency, processing viewpoints **2-3 orders of magnitude** faster than LWL (**110 ms** per waypoint vs. **8230 ms**), making it suitable for real-time robotic applications.
- Ablation studies confirmed that combining camera poses and 3D landmarks yields optimal performance, and ActLoc showed robustness to sparse map conditions and strong scalability with increasing LocMap resolution and scene size.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Predicting the distribution of localization performance for multiple viewpoints simultaneously in a single pass (LocMap) dramatically enhances computational efficiency compared to sequential viewpoint evaluations.
> - Fusing complementary information from 3D landmarks and camera poses derived from SfM provides more robust and accurate viewpoint selection than using either modality in isolation.
> - Integrating localization performance predictions directly into path planning, via a mixed cost map, enables robots to actively choose trajectories that jointly optimize both localization reliability and kinematic feasibility.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Visual localization is a fundamental capability for autonomous robots, enabling tasks such as navigation, inspection, and human-robot collaboration. Its advantages include the affordability of cameras compared to LiDAR, wider applicability to various devices (e.g., handheld, head-mounted), and the potential for multi-agent spatial alignment through shared visual maps.

Despite these benefits, visual localization presents challenges, often performing less reliably than LiDAR-based methods, particularly due to the narrow field of view (FoV) of cameras. Standard visual localization systems are inherently "passive," meaning they infer camera pose solely from a captured view without considering how viewpoint selection might enhance reliability. This passivity can lead to unreliable localization when observing unmapped, ambiguous, or uninformative regions within an environment.

To address these limitations, the field of active vision has explored strategies for actively guiding sensor orientations and robot motion to maximize observation informativeness. Within active localization, early approaches utilized analytical, geometry-based metrics (e.g., Fisher information) to identify informative viewpoints. While interpretable, these methods can be computationally intensive, especially for dense viewpoint sampling in large environments, and are often environment-specific.

More recent work has transitioned to learning-based formulations for active localization. These methods generally aim to improve accuracy but often suffer from computational expense, requiring significant resources to evaluate localization quality for each candidate viewpoint. This characteristic poses a challenge for real-time robotic systems. Furthermore, many learning-based approaches are trained on limited datasets, which can restrict their generalization capabilities. A common limitation is that they often assess viewpoints independently, leading to sparse sampling strategies and a decoupling of viewpoint selection from the overall path planning process.

ActLoc positions itself within this landscape by addressing the inefficiencies and generalization limitations of existing learning-based active localization methods. It proposes a more efficient and scalable approach that can predict localization quality across multiple viewpoints simultaneously and integrate these predictions directly into a real-time path planning framework, thereby moving beyond independent viewpoint evaluation and small-scale training.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an active viewpoint-aware planning framework, named ActLoc, that enhances the accuracy and robustness of visual localization for general robot navigation tasks. The motivation for ActLoc stems from several limitations observed in existing visual localization and active vision systems:

1. **Addressing the Assumption of Uniform Viewpoint Informativeness:** Most current visual localization systems implicitly assume that all viewing directions at a given location are equally informative. In practice, localization becomes unreliable when a robot's camera observes areas that are unmapped, visually ambiguous, or simply lack distinctive features. ActLoc is motivated by the need to explicitly account for and predict the varying informativeness of different viewpoints.

2. **Improving Computational Efficiency for Real-time Applications:** Existing learning-based active localization methods often require substantial computational resources to evaluate the localization quality of individual viewpoints. This "per-viewpoint" evaluation makes them computationally expensive and challenging to integrate into real-time robotic systems where rapid decision-making is essential. ActLoc aims to overcome this by predicting localization performance across multiple yaw and pitch directions in a single forward pass, thereby enabling faster decision-making.

3. **Enhancing Generalization Beyond Limited Datasets:** Many prior learning-based approaches are trained and evaluated on relatively small datasets, which can limit their ability to generalize effectively to diverse and unseen environments. ActLoc seeks to develop a scalable approach, trained on large simulated datasets, to improve generalization.

4. **Integrating Viewpoint Selection with Trajectory-level Planning:** Traditional methods frequently assess viewpoints independently, leading to sparse sampling and often decoupling viewpoint selection from the broader path planning process. This separation can hinder the robot's ability to plan trajectories that jointly optimize task objectives and localization robustness. ActLoc is motivated to integrate viewpoint selection directly into path planning, allowing for trajectory optimization that considers both localization accuracy and motion constraints.

In summary, the key contributions of this work are:
- The development of a learning-based, attention-based model trained on visual localization tasks. This model predicts a distribution of localization accuracy across multiple yaw and pitch directions in a single inference pass.
- The creation of ActLoc, an active localization planner that incorporates this predictive model into a comprehensive navigation system for online viewpoint selection and trajectory planning.
- The experimental validation of ActLoc, demonstrating its performance in both single-viewpoint selection scenarios and long-horizon path planning tasks, where it consistently performs comparably to or better than established baselines and heuristic methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ActLoc framework comprises two principal components: an attention-based model for predicting localization performance, and a path planning pipeline that integrates these predictions.

**System Overview:**
The system is designed for active localization, where a robot selects viewpoints along a sequence of waypoints to achieve accurate visual localization while following a specified trajectory. It assumes the environment has been pre-mapped, which is standard for visual localization.

**Predicting Localization Performance (LocMap):**
At the core of ActLoc is a model that encodes prior scene knowledge and predicts expected localization performance across possible viewing directions at any 3D waypoint.
- **LocMap Representation:** The model outputs a "LocMap," which is a two-dimensional grid (H × W). Each cell in this grid represents the predicted localization performance for a specific candidate viewpoint, defined by its yaw (horizontal axis) and pitch (vertical axis) angles. The system excludes extreme pitch angles and in-plane (roll) rotations, as these are empirically found to offer negligible benefit or degrade localization performance.
- **Single-Pass Prediction:** Unlike methods that evaluate viewpoints individually, ActLoc's model predicts the entire distribution of viewpoint performance in a single forward pass. This design is intended to capture spatial correlations between viewpoints and reduce redundant computation.
- **Inputs:** The model utilizes raw inputs derived from a Structure-from-Motion (SfM) pipeline: the 3D landmarks (P ∈ R^(3×N)) from the metric map and the camera poses (T ∈ SE(3)^M) used during map construction. For a given query waypoint x ∈ R³, the model computes C_x^(H×W) = f_θ(P, T, x), where C_x is the predicted distribution of localization performance. Each cell of C_x stores the likelihood that the localization error for the corresponding yaw-pitch direction falls below a predefined threshold.

**Model Architecture:**
The model is an attention-based network designed to process multi-modal inputs (landmarks and camera poses).
- **Preprocessing:**
 1. Uncertain 3D landmarks are filtered and removed from P.
 2. An egocentric transformation is applied to T and the filtered P, shifting their origins to the local frame of waypoint x while preserving rotations. This implicitly incorporates the waypoint's location.
 3. The filtered P is cropped within a bounding box centered at x, enforcing locality and normalizing input scale.
- **Feature Construction:**
 1. Camera features are constructed from T by extracting their Euclidean representation (7D: 3D coordinates of camera center and a unit quaternion for orientation).
 2. Landmark features are built from the filtered P, with each landmark represented as a 6D vector (3D coordinates and normalized RGB values).
- **Embedding and Encoding:** Both feature types are independently projected into a shared high-dimensional embedding space using linear layers. Transformer encoder blocks are then applied to each modality, generating context-aware representations.
- **Fusion:** A multi-layer bidirectional cross-attention block integrates the modalities, allowing camera pose embeddings to attend to landmark embeddings and vice versa. This facilitates information flow and produces fused features.
- **Output Layer:** Masked mean pooling aggregates the features into fixed-size vectors, which are concatenated and passed through a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) to produce the final LocMap logits.
- **Efficiency:** The architecture incorporates FlashAttentionV2, an optimized attention mechanism, to reduce memory complexity and improve processing speed for long sequences.

**Training Data Creation:**
The model is trained using a large-scale simulated dataset derived from 100 single-floor scenes of the HM3D dataset (90 for training/validation, 10 for testing).
- **Prior Scene Map Construction:** Free-space points are sampled at typical operation heights (1.5-2.0 meters). At each point, a camera performs a 360° sweep (36° azimuth increments), capturing images at randomly sampled elevation angles ([-15°, 15°)). Structure-from-Motion (SfM) is then run on these images using SuperPoint features and the Hierarchical Localization (hloc) framework to reconstruct 3D landmarks (the metric map).
- **Ground-Truth Label Generation:** Waypoints are independently sampled within each scene (0.4-2.0 meters height), without grid-based assumptions. At each waypoint, the camera virtually rotates across a dense grid of yaw (20° intervals, [-180°, 180°)) and pitch (20° intervals, [-60°, 60°)) angles. For each such viewpoint, hloc and COLMAP are used to localize the captured image against the pre-constructed metric map, providing ground-truth localization performance (error metrics). This process generates large-scale, viewpoint-labeled data for training ActLoc.

**Path Planning with Localization Awareness:**
ActLoc integrates the predicted LocMap into a path planning pipeline to make viewpoint selections along a trajectory.
- **Mixed Cost Map:** To balance localization accuracy with motion constraints, a mixed cost map M_x is defined for each waypoint x: M_x = C_x + λ ⋅ D_x.
 - C_x: The predicted localization performance from the LocMap.
 - D_x: The Mahalanobis distance from each candidate viewpoint to the previously selected viewpoint, serving as a proxy for motion smoothness (e.g., limiting rotations between consecutive frames).
 - λ: A tunable parameter that controls the trade-off between localization accuracy and motion smoothness.
- **Viewpoint Selection:** As the robot moves along a sequence of waypoints, it computes M_x at each step and selects the viewpoint (yaw and pitch) that minimizes this combined cost. This strategy aims to ensure both reliable localization and kinematically feasible robot motion.

### 4. Main findings and results

The experiments evaluated ActLoc's performance in single-viewpoint selection and full-trajectory planning, along with ablation studies and scalability analyses.

**Single-viewpoint Selection:**
- **Performance:** ActLoc demonstrated superior performance compared to two baselines, Learning-Where-to-Look (LWL) and Fisher Information Field (FIF). On the HM3D dataset, ActLoc achieved success rates of 92.11%, 92.48%, 92.86%, and 93.23% for thresholds of 0.1m/1°, 0.25m/2°, 0.5m/5°, and 5m/10° respectively, outperforming LWL's 90.04%, 90.98%, 91.17%, 91.92% and FIF's significantly lower scores. Similar trends were observed on the ScanNet dataset, where ActLoc also achieved higher success rates. These results suggest ActLoc's ability to capture global spatial structure from SfM inputs and generalize to unseen scenes.
- **Efficiency:** ActLoc exhibited a significant advantage in processing efficiency. The average processing time per waypoint was 110 ms for ActLoc, while LWL required 8230 ms in batch mode. This difference indicates a speed-up of two to three orders of magnitude, supporting ActLoc's suitability for real-time applications.
- **Robustness to Sparse Reconstruction:** Experiments showed that ActLoc maintained slightly higher localization success rates than LWL across various levels of reconstructed model sparsification (ranging from 0% to 90% removal of camera poses and associated 3D landmarks), indicating its robustness in degraded mapping conditions.
- **Effect of In-plane Rotation:** Evaluations confirmed that large in-plane (roll) rotations (e.g., ±90° and 180°) resulted in extremely poor localization success rates (e.g., 0.14% at -90° compared to 84.17% at 0°), validating the decision to exclude these from the LocMap representation.

**Path Planning Evaluation:**
- **Trajectory-level Performance:** When integrated into a planning pipeline for full-trajectory execution, ActLoc achieved the highest success rates and, in most cases, the lowest average translation and rotation errors compared to LWL and a heuristic forward-facing planner across five HM3D test scenes. For instance, in HM3D scene 00005, ActLoc achieved a 52.75% success rate with 0.07m translation error and 1.09° rotation error, surpassing both baselines.
- **Motion Smoothness:** The use of a mixed cost map (combining localization performance with motion constraints) enabled ActLoc to not only improve localization accuracy but also maintain smooth rotations between consecutive frames, adhering to typical robot kinematic constraints.
- **LWL under Constraints:** Under equalized processing time constraints, LWL's performance degraded due to its inability to sample a sufficient number of candidates within the given timeframe, further highlighting ActLoc's efficiency advantage in practical planning scenarios.

**Ablation Study:**
- An ablation study investigated the contribution of the two input modalities: camera poses and the metric map (point clouds). Three variants were tested: ActLoc-Cam (camera poses only), ActLoc-Map (point clouds only), and ActLoc-Full (both modalities).
- ActLoc-Full consistently outperformed both single-modality variants across all localization thresholds, confirming that camera poses and 3D landmarks provide complementary information crucial for optimal viewpoint selection.

**Scalability:**
- **LocMap Resolution:** ActLoc's inference time remained nearly constant (around 108 ms) irrespective of the LocMap output resolution (e.g., 6×18, 12×36, 24×72). In contrast, LWL's runtime increased significantly with resolution, from approximately 8 seconds to over 100 seconds, demonstrating ActLoc's superior scalability to higher-resolution predictions.
- **Scene Size:** ActLoc maintained efficiency even in larger scenes (measured by the number of SfM landmarks), with its runtime growing considerably slower than LWL's, indicating strong scalability for deployment in expansive environments.

**Cross-domain Generalization (Limitations):**
- An experiment replacing the default SuperPoint features with SIFT showed that ActLoc did not outperform LWL in this configuration. This suggests a trade-off where processing raw inputs for efficiency might reduce robustness to different underlying feature types, indicating a potential area for future improvement.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The ActLoc framework introduces a methodological advancement in visual localization for robotic systems, addressing critical limitations of current approaches. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Reliability of Robot Navigation:** By actively predicting and selecting optimal camera viewpoints, ActLoc directly addresses the challenge of unreliable localization in visually ambiguous or uninformative environments. This capability can lead to more robust and dependable navigation for robots in real-world scenarios, reducing the likelihood of localization failures that could impede task completion or compromise safety.

- **Enabling Real-time Active Localization:** The framework's design, particularly its ability to predict localization performance across a dense grid of viewpoints in a single forward pass and its efficient model architecture (e.g., using FlashAttentionV2), significantly reduces computational overhead. This efficiency makes ActLoc suitable for real-time deployment in robotic platforms, allowing for dynamic viewpoint adjustment during operation without introducing substantial latency.

- **Integrated Perception and Planning:** ActLoc provides a unified approach where localization awareness is directly incorporated into the robot's path planning process. By using a mixed cost map that balances localization accuracy with motion constraints, robots can plan trajectories that are not only task-efficient but also ensure continuous, reliable self-localization. This tight coupling between perception and planning represents a more holistic approach to autonomous navigation.

- **Broad Applicability Across Robotic Tasks:** The modular design of ActLoc suggests its adaptability to a diverse range of robotic applications beyond general navigation. This includes inspection tasks where observing specific features is crucial, monitoring operations requiring consistent spatial awareness, and other scenarios where robust visual localization directly impacts performance.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** While ActLoc demonstrates strong performance, the paper identifies areas for further exploration. The observed trade-off in cross-domain generalization (e.g., with different feature sets like SIFT) suggests future work could focus on input normalization, data augmentation, or self-supervised learning to enhance robustness. Moreover, extending ActLoc to multi-modal sensor fusion or adapting it for dynamic environments could broaden its applicability to more complex, real-world robotics challenges. The generation of global score heatmaps from the multi-class variant also indicates potential for providing higher-level planning cues.

In essence, ActLoc contributes to making visual localization more adaptive, reliable, and computationally feasible for the next generation of autonomous robotic systems operating in complex and varied environments.
