# The Majority is not always right: RL training for solution aggregation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at FAIR at Meta introduce AggLM, a method that fine-tunes a language model using Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to synthesize a single solution from multiple LLM-generated candidates. AggLM consistently surpasses majority voting and reward-model-based selection on math reasoning benchmarks, demonstrating improved accuracy, token efficiency, and generalization across diverse solution models.

## Problem

- Standard majority voting methods for LLM-generated solutions often fail when the correct answer is a minority view among candidates, leading to overlooked valuable insights.
- Existing model-based selection approaches (e.g., reward models) only pick the best candidate and cannot synthesize a novel, correct solution from partially accurate or complementary components.
- Fixed heuristics for aggregating solutions lack the adaptive reasoning required to intelligently reconcile and combine diverse outputs from LLMs.

## Method

- ==AggLM (Aggregation Language Model)== is trained using Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to take a problem and multiple candidate solutions as input, then output a single, synthesized solution.
- A carefully ==balanced training dataset== is constructed by mixing 'hard' examples (where the majority candidate is incorrect) with 'easy' examples (where it's correct), enabling AggLM to learn both selection and synthesis skills.
- AggLM is optimized using ==Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== with a binary reward signal for exact match to the ground-truth solution.

## Results

- AggLM-1.7B consistently outperforms majority voting and reward-model baselines by **3 to 7** percentage points on all four MathArena benchmarks.
- The method demonstrates strong generalization, maintaining top performance when aggregating solutions from a stronger Qwen3-8B model or from non-thinking mode solutions.
- AggLM achieves higher accuracy with superior token efficiency, using roughly **one-third** fewer tokens per inference compared to baselines relying on generating a larger number of samples for majority voting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aggregation can be learned as an explicit reasoning skill through RLVR, moving beyond fixed heuristics to intelligently review, reconcile, and synthesize information from multiple sources.
> - Balancing easy and hard examples in the training data is crucial for the aggregation model to effectively learn to recover minority-but-correct answers while also handling straightforward cases.
> - The learned aggregation skill generalizes robustly across different base models (weaker or stronger) and solution generation modes, indicating broad applicability.
