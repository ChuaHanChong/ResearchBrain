# TRACED: Transition-aware Regret Approximation with Co-learnability for Environment Design

> [!summary] Summary
> TRACED is a framework for Unsupervised Environment Design (UED) that enhances reinforcement learning agent generalization to novel environments. It refines task difficulty approximation by combining value-function loss with a novel transition-prediction error and incorporates a "Co-Learnability" metric to capture cross-task transfer. This approach achieves superior zero-shot generalization and significantly faster training convergence compared to existing UED methods on MiniGrid and BipedalWalker environments.

## Problem

- Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) agents frequently overfit to training environments, leading to poor zero-shot generalization to novel, unseen tasks.
- Current Unsupervised Environment Design (UED) frameworks approximate task difficulty (regret) mainly using value-function loss, which is incomplete and does not account for the agent's understanding of environmental dynamics.
- Existing UED approaches often select training tasks independently, failing to explicitly model or leverage the transfer effects and relationships between different tasks.

## Method

- Introduces a "Transition-aware ==Regret Approximation==" (ATPL) that combines traditional Positive Value Loss with a new transition-prediction error from a learned dynamics model.
- Develops a lightweight "Co-Learnability" metric to quantify the observed reduction in difficulty of other tasks after training on a specific task, capturing positive transfer effects.
- Integrates these components into a unified "Task Priority" score, which guides the UED curriculum to select tasks that are both challenging and offer high transfer potential.

## Results

- Achieved superior zero-shot generalization on MiniGrid and BipedalWalker environments, outperforming strong baselines like ACCEL and CENIE, often matching their performance in approximately half the training time.
- Generated curricula that ramped up task complexity significantly faster than baselines, with TRACED introducing challenging BipedalWalker levels (**14.6%**) where ACCEL generated none.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the transition-prediction error (ATPL) and the Co-Learnability metric are essential contributors to the framework's improved performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Accurate regret approximation in DRL requires considering not only the agent's value estimation errors but also its ability to correctly model the environment's transition dynamics.
> - Explicitly quantifying the "Co-Learnability" between tasks, representing how training on one task impacts the learning or performance on others, can lead to more efficient and generalizable curricula.
> - Combining task-specific difficulty (incorporating dynamics error) with inter-task transfer potential provides a more principled and effective criterion for curriculum generation in UED.
