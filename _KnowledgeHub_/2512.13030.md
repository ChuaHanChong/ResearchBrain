# Motus: A Unified Latent Action World Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Motus, a unified latent action world model from Tsinghua University, Peking University, and Horizon Robotics, integrates five embodied AI capabilities into a single generative framework. It leverages latent actions from optical flow to learn from diverse, unlabeled visual data, achieving an 88.66% success rate in clean simulation and demonstrating strong generalization on real-world dual-arm robots.

## Problem

- Current embodied AI systems are fragmented, modeling cognitive functions like perception, imagination, and action generation in isolated components, hindering holistic learning.
- Difficulty in leveraging vast, heterogeneous datasets (internet videos, human demos) for robot learning due to varying action spaces across embodiments and scarcity of explicit action labels.
- Existing unified models often lack comprehensive integration of all key distributions or robust vision-language and physical interaction knowledge from pre-trained foundation models.

## Method

- A ==Mixture-of-Transformer (MoT)== architecture unifies ==Vision-Language Understanding (VLM)==, ==Video Generation (VGM)==, and an ==Action Expert== through ==Tri-model Joint Attention==, allowing joint modeling of five key embodied AI distributions.
- Introduces ==latent actions==, derived from ==optical flow== and compressed by a ==VAE==, to represent general motion patterns, enabling learning from large-scale, heterogeneous, and often unlabeled video data.
- Employs a three-phase ==pretraining-finetuning== pipeline on a ==six-layer data pyramid== to progressively learn visual dynamics, action representations with latent actions, and specialize for target robotic embodiments.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **88.66%** in clean simulation and **87.02%** in randomized simulation on RoboTwin 2.0, surpassing baselines like π0.5 by over **45%** and X-VLA by over **15%**.
- Demonstrated robust generalization on real-world dual-arm robots (AC-One and Agilex-Aloha-2), with average partial success rates of **63.22%** and **59.30%**, respectively, representing improvements of **+48.43%** and **+10.7%** over the π0.5 baseline.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the full multi-stage pretraining with latent actions contributed a **10.02%** increase in success rate over training from scratch in randomized simulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Tri-model Joint Attention within a Mixture-of-Transformer architecture effectively integrates specialized generative and understanding experts while preventing task interference.
> - Latent actions, derived from optical flow, provide a universal and embodiment-agnostic representation for motion, allowing scalable pretraining of action experts from unlabeled visual data.
> - A progressive, multi-stage training pipeline, building upon pre-trained foundation models and leveraging a six-layer data pyramid, enables robust generalization across diverse robotic tasks and embodiments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions Motus within the rapidly evolving landscape of embodied AI, particularly focusing on the development of generalist agents capable of performing a wide range of tasks. The authors identify a critical challenge: the **fragmentation of current embodied AI systems**. Traditionally, different cognitive functions of an embodied agent—such as understanding scenes and instructions, imagining future states, predicting consequences, and generating actions—are modeled in isolation using specialized approaches like Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, World Models (WMs), Inverse Dynamics Models (IDMs), Video Generation Models (VGMs), and Video-Action Joint Prediction Models. This modular, rather than unified, approach hinders holistic learning and the efficient utilization of diverse, large-scale datasets.

Motus directly addresses this fragmentation by proposing a **unified latent action world model**. This aligns with the broader trend in AI towards "foundation models" – large, pre-trained models capable of handling multiple modalities and tasks. In robotics, this translates to developing "embodied foundation models" that can integrate perception, reasoning, and action generation within a single framework.

Previous attempts at unification, such as F1 and Unified World Models (UWMs), are acknowledged but also critiqued for their limitations. F1 combined VLAs and IDMs but excluded world models and video generation. UWMs, while a theoretical prototype for unifying the five distributions, were often trained from scratch or lacked the robust vision-language understanding of pre-trained VLMs or the rich physical interaction knowledge of VGMs. Motus aims to overcome these limitations by:

1. **Leveraging Existing Pre-trained Models:** Instead of training from scratch, Motus integrates existing powerful pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Video Generation Models (VGMs). This allows it to inherit robust prior knowledge for vision-language understanding and physical interactions, a crucial departure from less informed unified models.
2. **Addressing Data Heterogeneity:** Embodied intelligence requires learning from vast, heterogeneous datasets, including internet videos, human demonstrations, and multi-robot trajectories. A major challenge is that action spaces vary widely across embodiments, and much video data lacks explicit action labels. Motus's introduction of **latent actions derived from optical flow** is a significant contribution to this problem. It allows the model to learn general motion patterns from unlabeled visual data, thereby facilitating large-scale pretraining of the action expert, a capability often missing in prior VLA or world model approaches that rely heavily on labeled robot trajectories.
3. **Cross-embodiment Transfer:** By learning universal motion priors through latent actions and employing a structured training pipeline, Motus seeks to enable better generalization across different robotic embodiments. This is a key goal in robotics, as it reduces the need for extensive, embodiment-specific data collection for every new robot or task.

In essence, Motus represents a significant step towards truly generalist embodied agents by creating a unified system that intelligently combines state-of-the-art generative capabilities and scalable learning from diverse data sources, moving beyond isolated task-specific models towards a more comprehensive and robust AI for robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The development of Motus is motivated by two fundamental challenges in embodied AI that prevent the creation of truly general and unified embodied agents:

**Challenge 1: Unifying Multimodal Generative Capabilities.**
- **Motivation:** A capable embodied agent needs to seamlessly integrate a spectrum of cognitive functions, including scene understanding, instruction interpretation, future imagination (video generation), consequence prediction (world modeling), and action generation. Current methods, however, address these capabilities in isolation. This fragmentation results in disparate models for Vision-Language-Action (VLA), World Modeling (WM), Inverse Dynamics Modeling (IDM), Video Generation Modeling (VGM), and Video-Action Joint Prediction.
- **Objective:** The primary objective is to develop a single, unified framework that can jointly model these five key distributions ($p(a|o, \ell)$, $p(o|o, a)$, $p(a|o)$, $p(o|o, \ell)$, and $p(o, a|o, \ell)$) without compromising general multimodal priors. This unification aims to provide a human-like cognitive capacity, allowing the agent to perform comprehensive reasoning and action generation within one system, leveraging robust knowledge from pre-trained foundation models.

**Challenge 2: Utilization of Heterogeneous Data.**
- **Motivation:** Embodied intelligence demands the ability to learn from large-scale, heterogeneous data, including internet videos, egocentric human demonstrations, and diverse robot trajectories. A significant hurdle is the wide variability in action spaces across different robotic embodiments and the scarcity of action labels in most video data. This makes it challenging to pretrain an "action expert" with general motion and interaction priors that can transfer across different robots. Existing methods often rely heavily on labeled robotic trajectories or employ restrictive approaches that limit the integration of vast unlabeled visual data.
- **Objective:** To enable effective utilization of large-scale heterogeneous data by introducing a mechanism that can extract generalizable action patterns directly from visual dynamics, even without explicit action labels. This involves learning cross-embodiment transferable motion knowledge to pretrain the action expert, thereby overcoming the limitations posed by varying action spaces and data sparsity.

In summary, Motus aims to build a **unified, scalable, and generalizable embodied foundation model** by intelligently integrating diverse multimodal generative capabilities and effectively leveraging heterogeneous, large-scale data through novel latent action representations and a structured training pipeline.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Motus proposes a novel methodology to address the challenges of unifying multimodal generative capabilities and leveraging heterogeneous data. Its core approach revolves around a unified architecture, a unique action representation, and a multi-stage training strategy.

#### 3.1. Motus Architecture

- **Mixture-of-Transformer (MoT):** Motus employs a MoT architecture to integrate three distinct "experts": a **Video Generation Model (VGM)** (generative expert), a **Vision-Language Understanding Model (VLM)** (understanding expert), and an **Action Expert**. Unlike models that simply concatenate tokens, Motus uses **Tri-model Joint Attention**, where each expert maintains its individual Transformer module, but their multi-head self-attention layers are concatenated. This design allows specialized functionalities to be preserved for each expert while enabling effective cross-modal feature fusion, preventing task interference, and encouraging complementary knowledge sharing.
- **UniDiffuser-like Scheduler:** To coordinate multimodal generation and allow flexible switching between different modeling modes (VLA, WM, IDM, VGM, Video-Action Joint Prediction Model), Motus incorporates a UniDiffuser-like scheduler. This scheduler allocates distinct timesteps and noise scales to different modalities (e.g., videos and actions). This diffusion-based approach enables simultaneous modeling of marginal, conditional, and joint distributions across observations, actions, and language instructions.
- **Action-Dense Video-Sparse Prediction:** To improve training and inference efficiency and balance token counts in the Tri-model Joint Attention, Motus uses an "Action-Dense Video-Sparse Prediction" strategy. Given that action chunking often requires predicting many actions per few video frames, the model downsamples video frames (e.g., video frame rate one-sixth of action frame rate) to balance the number of video and action tokens. This prevents the model from overfitting to video prediction at the expense of action prediction.
- **Expert Details:**
 - **Generative Expert:** Uses Wan 2.2 5B as the video foundation model, extended with self-attention context for cross-modal interaction.
 - **Understanding Expert:** Leverages Qwen3-VL-2B for its 3D grounding, spatial understanding, and object localization capabilities, crucial for robotics. Its input comes from the VLM's last-layer tokens.
 - **Action Expert:** A Transformer block of the same depth as Wan, incorporating AdaLN for rectified flow timesteps, an FFN, and Tri-model Joint Attention.

#### 3.2. Latent Actions

To address the challenge of heterogeneous data and learn from unlabeled videos, Motus introduces **latent actions**:
- **Optical Flow Based Representation:** Latent actions encode motion patterns derived from optical flow, which captures pixel-level displacements between consecutive frames. Optical flows are computed using DPFlow, converted to RGB images, and then compressed by a **deep convolutional variational autoencoder (DC-AE)** into low-dimensional latent tokens (four 512-dimensional tokens). A lightweight encoder further projects these into a 14-dimensional vector, aligning with typical robot action spaces. This "pixel-level delta action" representation bridges visual dynamics and control signals.
- **Training and Distribution Alignment:** The VAE is trained to reconstruct optical flow while its encoded latents are supervised with a small amount of action labels (both task-related and task-agnostic) alongside unlabeled data. This weak action supervision, coupled with task-agnostic data (collected by randomly sampling robot action spaces), helps align the latent space to realistic action distributions, ensuring that motion priors learned from videos can map to executable controls. The total loss combines reconstruction error, action alignment loss, and KL regularization.

#### 3.3. Model Training and Data

Motus employs a structured **three-phase pretraining-finetuning pipeline** on a **six-layer data pyramid**:

- **Six-Layer Data Pyramid:** Data is organized hierarchically by richness and policy relevance:
 1. Web Data (large-scale, indirect)
 2. Egocentric Human Videos
 3. Synthetic Data
 4. Task-agnostic Data
 5. Multi-Robot Task Trajectory Data
 6. Target-Robot Task Trajectory Data (smallest, most relevant)

- **Three-Phase Training:**
 1. **Stage 1: Learning Visual Dynamics (Video Generation):** Adapts the pre-trained VGM using multi-robot trajectories and human videos to generate plausible future video sequences from language and an initial image.
 2. **Stage 2: Learning Action Representations (Unified Training with Latent Actions):** Pretrains the entire Motus model (with frozen VLM) on videos, language, and the learned latent actions. This stage initializes the action expert by embedding motion and interaction knowledge into the latent action space, leveraging the heterogeneous data pyramid (Levels 2-5).
 3. **Stage 3: Specializing for the Target Robot (SFT):** Fine-tunes the full Motus model (using real actions) on target-robot-specific data (Level 6), adapting the acquired priors to the specific embodiment's dynamics and kinematics.

This comprehensive methodology allows Motus to progressively learn from diverse data, integrate robust pre-trained priors, and unify various embodied AI capabilities into a single, highly generalized model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Motus demonstrates superior performance and significant advancements in embodied AI across both simulation and real-world robotic environments.

#### 4.1. Evaluation in Simulation Environment (RoboTwin 2.0)

- Motus was evaluated on 50 representative manipulation tasks from the RoboTwin 2.0 benchmark in both clean and heavily randomized scenes. All models (Motus, π0.5, X-VLA) were trained on a multi-task setting with 2500 clean and 25000 randomized demonstrations, then finetuned for 40k steps.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** Motus achieved state-of-the-art performance, with an **average success rate of 88.66% in clean scenes and 87.02% in randomized scenes**.
- **Quantitative Improvement:**
 - Compared to π0.5, Motus showed over a **+45% absolute improvement** (e.g., π0.5 average 42.98% clean, 43.84% rand.).
 - Compared to X-VLA, Motus achieved over a **+15% improvement** (e.g., X-VLA average 72.8% clean, 72.84% rand.).
- These results validate Motus's success in unifying vision, language, and action generation (Challenge 1) and effectively leveraging both labeled and large-scale unlabeled data via latent actions for generalization (Challenge 2).

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

- Motus was evaluated on two distinct dual-arm robotic platforms: **AC-One** and **Agilex-Aloha-2**, across a comprehensive set of non-trivial tasks (e.g., fold towel, brew coffee, grind coffee, get water, place cube, touch keyboard, put bread into oven).
- **Strong Generalization and Robustness:** Using only 100 trajectories per task for training, Motus significantly **outperformed the baseline π0.5 across all tasks on both robotic arms**.
- **Quantitative Improvement (Partial Success Rate):**
 - On **AC-One**, Motus achieved an average partial success rate of **63.22%**, compared to π0.5's 14.79% and a "w/o Pretrain" variant's 25.86%. This represents an improvement of **+48.43%** over π0.5.
 - On **Agilex-Aloha-2**, Motus achieved an average partial success rate of **59.30%**, compared to π0.5's 48.60% and a "w/o Pretrain" variant's 26.60%. This is an **+10.7%** improvement over π0.5.
- Specific task examples show dramatic improvements: e.g., Brew Coffee using Coffee Maker (AC-One): 0% (π0.5) -> 62% (Motus); Grind Coffee Beans (AC-One): 8% (π0.5) -> 92% (Motus); Get Water from Dispenser (Agilex-Aloha-2): 62% (π0.5) -> 96% (Motus).
- Qualitative results (Figure 5 and 8 in the paper) further illustrate Motus's ability to execute complex, long-horizon tasks, demonstrating effective spatial understanding, deformable object manipulation, precision fluid control, and visual understanding.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- Ablation studies confirmed the contribution of Motus's training stages.
- Comparing "w/o pretrain" (Motus trained from scratch), "Stage 1 pretrain" (VGM adaptation only), and "Stage 2 pretrain" (full Motus with latent actions), the results show a clear progressive improvement.
- In RoboTwin 2.0 randomized multi-task setting:
 - "w/o pretrain" achieved 77.00% success.
 - "Stage 1 pretrain" improved to 81.86% (+4.86%).
 - "Stage 2 pretrain" (Motus) further improved to 87.02% (+5.16% from Stage 1, +10.02% from w/o pretrain).
- Similar trends were observed in clean scenes and real-world settings (Table 3), highlighting the critical role of pretraining, especially with latent actions, in enhancing generalization.

#### 4.4. Other Benchmarks (from Supplementary Material)

- **LIBERO-Long:** Motus achieved an average success score of **97.6%**, matching the best reported performance of X-VLA and thus reaching state-of-the-art on this benchmark for long-horizon, multi-stage manipulation tasks.
- **VLABench:** Motus showed competitive performance on the VLABench universal language-conditioned manipulation tasks.
 - In-Distribution: Motus average success rate of **0.48**, slightly outperforming π0.5 (0.43).
 - Cross Category: Motus average success rate of **0.25**, slightly outperforming π0.5 (0.22).

#### 4.5. Unified Model's Generative Capabilities (from Supplementary Material)

- **VGM Mode:** Motus produced high-quality video generation results across both robotic embodiments.
- **World Model Mode:** Showed high-quality future video prediction capabilities on real-world robot data, achieving strong metrics (e.g., average FID of 11.209, SSIM of 0.8661).
- **IDM Mode:** Achieved a lower action Mean Squared Error (0.014) compared to specialized IDM baselines (ResNet18+MLP: 0.044, DINOv2+MLP: 0.122), demonstrating its effectiveness in inverse dynamics modeling.
- **VLA Mode:** Demonstrated competitive performance (83.90% success rate on RoboTwin 2.0 randomized data) compared to the video-action joint prediction mode (87.02%).
- **Video-Action Joint Prediction Model Mode:** Demonstrated strong capabilities in simultaneously generating high-quality videos and precise actions.

In conclusion, the experimental results rigorously demonstrate that Motus successfully achieves its objectives of unifying multimodal generative capabilities and leveraging heterogeneous data, leading to state-of-the-art performance and robust generalization in complex robotic manipulation tasks across diverse settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Motus marks a significant advancement in the field of embodied artificial intelligence and robotics, with several far-reaching implications and potential impacts:

1. **Towards Generalist Embodied Agents:** Motus's unified architecture, capable of integrating five distinct embodied AI paradigms (WMs, IDMs, VLAs, VGMs, and Video-Action Joint Prediction Models) into a single generative framework, is a crucial step towards creating truly generalist robots. This unification simplifies the cognitive architecture of embodied agents, potentially leading to more coherent reasoning and decision-making by eliminating the fragmentation inherent in current modular systems.
2. **Enhanced Learning from Heterogeneous Data:** The concept of **latent actions derived from optical flow** is a key innovation. It provides a universal, embodiment-agnostic representation of motion, enabling the action expert to learn from vast quantities of unlabeled internet videos and human demonstrations. This dramatically expands the data sources available for robotic pretraining, overcoming the bottleneck of requiring explicit action labels for every robot and every task. This ability to leverage "pixel-level delta action" information is pivotal for scalable robot learning.
3. **Improved Generalization and Transferability:** By pretraining on a six-layer data pyramid and aligning cross-embodiment behaviors through motion space described by optical flows, Motus demonstrates significantly improved generalization performance in both simulation and real-world scenarios. This enhanced transferability of learned priors across different embodiments and tasks reduces the need for extensive, task-specific data collection for every new robot or manipulation challenge, accelerating the deployment of robotic systems.
4. **Foundation Model for Robotics:** Motus positions itself as an embodied foundation model, similar to large language models or vision models that serve as general-purpose backbones. By effectively fusing large-scale general multimodal priors from vision-language models and rich physical interaction knowledge from video generation models, Motus offers a robust base model that can be fine-tuned for a wide array of downstream robotic tasks. This paradigm shift could streamline robotic development by providing a powerful, pre-trained starting point.
5. **New Research Directions:** The work inspires future research in several areas:
 - **Unified Architectures:** Further exploration of more advanced and efficient unified model architectures that can seamlessly integrate even more modalities (e.g., haptics, audio) and cognitive functions.
 - **Motion-Centric Representation Learning:** Deeper investigation into universal motion priors and alternative latent action representations that are robust, highly transferable, and perhaps even more expressive than optical flow.
 - **Large-Scale Embodied Pretraining:** Scaling up the pretraining process using even larger, internet-scale general videos for learning latent actions, pushing the boundaries of what embodied agents can learn through observation.

**Limitations:** The paper does not explicitly detail major limitations, but common challenges for such models often include:
- **Computational Cost:** Training and deploying such a large, unified model can be computationally expensive, requiring significant GPU resources.
- **Real-world Robustness:** While showing strong real-world results, generalizing to entirely novel, highly unstructured environments or extreme out-of-distribution scenarios remains a persistent challenge for any robotic system.
- **Interpretability:** As a complex neural network, understanding the precise mechanisms behind its decision-making and action generation can be challenging.

Despite these inherent complexities, Motus represents a substantial leap forward in the quest for intelligent, versatile, and general-purpose robots, laying a strong foundation for future advancements in embodied AI.
