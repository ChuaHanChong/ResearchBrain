# SIA: Self Improving AI with Harness & Weight Updates

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Hexo Labs and the University of Oxford developed SIA, a system that autonomously improves AI performance by iteratively updating both its operational scaffold and underlying model weights. SIA consistently surpassed harness-only approaches and prior state-of-the-art across diverse tasks, achieving gains such as a 20.1 percentage point accuracy increase in Chinese legal classification and a 12.4% faster runtime for GPU kernel optimization.

## Problem

- Human involvement in AI development, from model design to tuning, acts as a significant bottleneck in AI progress.
- Existing self-improving AI research is split into two isolated approaches: harness/scaffold improvement (fixed weights, external changes) and test-time post-training (weight updates, fixed harness), failing to combine their benefits.
- Neither approach alone can fully adapt both the operational structure and the internal knowledge of an AI agent to a given task.

## Method

- **SIA** introduces a unified self-improving loop where a Feedback-Agent dynamically chooses between updating the task-specific agent's operational scaffold (harness) or its model weights.
- The Task-Specific Agent uses an LLM (gpt-oss-120b) with Low-Rank Adaptation (==LoRA==) for weight updates, while the Meta-Agent and Feedback-Agent employ Claude Sonnet 4.6.
- The Feedback-Agent adaptively selects appropriate ==reinforcement learning== algorithms (e.g., PPO, ==GRPO==, REINFORCE) based on task type and observed reward dynamics.

## Results

- SIA-W+H achieved **70.1%** accuracy on LawBench (Chinese legal charge classification), a 20.1 percentage point gain over SIA-H and **25.1%** over prior SOTA.
- For AlphaEvolve TriMul (CUDA kernel optimization), SIA-W+H resulted in a **14.02x** speedup over the baseline, performing **12.4%** faster than the previous SOTA.
- In MAGIC scRNA-seq Denoising, SIA-W+H reached 0.289 mse norm, representing a **20%** gain over SIA-H and **20.4%** over prior SOTA, notably introducing a critical `np.clip + np.rint` post-processing step via weight updates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining both harness and model weight updates in a unified feedback loop (SIA-W+H) consistently yields superior performance compared to scaffold iteration alone (SIA-H) across diverse application domains.
> - Harness updates primarily drive 'externalized changes' such as tool refinement, parsing logic, and search procedures, affecting how the agent operates.
> - Weight updates facilitate 'internalized knowledge,' enabling the model to encode domain-specific patterns and structural invariants directly into its parameters, which are often inaccessible through scaffold edits alone.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses the long-standing goal of developing artificial intelligence that can improve itself without constant human intervention, identifying human involvement in model design, post-training, scaffolding, prompting, debugging, and tuning as a bottleneck in current AI progress.

The existing research landscape for self-improving AI is characterized as having largely bifurcated into two distinct, isolated approaches:
- **Harness/Scaffold Self-Improvement:** This approach involves a meta-agent iteratively rewriting the "scaffold" (e.g., system prompts, tool-dispatch logic, retry policies, search procedures) of a task-specific agent, while the underlying language model weights remain fixed. Previous work in this area includes the Darwin Gödel Machine (Zhang et al., 2025), Meta-Harness (Lee et al., 2026), and Hyperagents (Zhang et al., 2026). The paper notes that empirical observations from this silo often show scaffold edits concentrating on software engineering aspects like parsing and retries, rather than delivering domain-specific reasoning capabilities.
- **Test-Time Post-Training:** This approach focuses on updating the model's own weights using hand-written reinforcement learning (RL) pipelines based on task feedback at test time. In this paradigm, the agent's harness (e.g., prompt and grader template) is typically held constant. Representatives of this research line include TTRL (Zuo et al., 2025) and the Discover line of test-time training (Yuksekgonul et al., 2026). The paper indicates that while this approach yields gains through internal policy change, the underlying training pipeline is human-engineered and does not adapt to the task-specific structures that a scaffolded agent might expose.

The work presented in this paper, SIA (Self Improving AI with Harness & Weight Updates), positions itself by addressing a recognized gap between these two silos. It aims to combine both levers—harness updates and model weight updates—within a single, integrated self-improving loop. The paper states that, to the authors' knowledge, SIA is the first system to update both the scaffold and model weights in a unified feedback loop, thus extending the capabilities of self-improving agents beyond the limitations of either isolated approach.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that human involvement currently limits the pace and scope of AI development and improvement. The paper posits that achieving true self-imimprovement requires systems that can autonomously adapt and refine both their operational structure and their internal knowledge.

The key objectives of the SIA project are:

- **Develop a unified self-improvement system:** The overarching goal is to create an AI system that, given only a task specification and a verifier, can autonomously improve both its operational scaffold and its model weights without requiring further human intervention. This represents a concrete step toward the broader vision of an AI that can figure out how to improve itself.

- **Evaluate the efficacy of combined updates (RQ1):** The first research question (RQ1) investigates whether iteratively updating both the harness and the model weights in a single, interleaved loop can achieve superior performance compared to scaffold iteration alone. This objective includes assessing the robustness of this combined approach across diverse and contrasting application domains.

- **Differentiate the impact of each improvement lever (RQ2):** The second research question (RQ2) aims to characterize the distinct contributions of harness updates versus weight updates. Specifically, it seeks to determine if weight updates can surface domain knowledge that is inaccessible through scaffold edits alone, and conversely, if harness iteration primarily leads to external infrastructure changes distinct from internal knowledge acquisition. This objective seeks to understand the mechanisms by which each type of update contributes to overall performance improvement.

By pursuing these objectives, the research aims to demonstrate that self-improving systems can benefit from a holistic approach that integrates both structural (harness) and parametric (weight) modifications, thereby overcoming the limitations observed when these two aspects are treated in isolation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SIA is designed as a configurable self-improving loop driven by three core LLM-based components: a Meta-Agent, a Task-Specific Agent, and a Feedback-Agent. The system is described as task-agnostic, requiring only a task specification and a verifier as input.

#### 3.1. Agent Components

- **LLM:** The base model used for the Task-Specific Agent throughout the experiments is `openai/gpt-oss-120b`. For weight updates, Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA; Hu et al., 2022) with a rank of 32 is applied to this base model.
- **Scaffold (Harness):** This comprises the fixed, non-weight components of the Task-Specific Agent. It includes the system prompt (text prepended to model calls), tool-dispatch logic (Python code for parsing model outputs and routing them to handlers for I/O, code execution, dataset lookup, or grader calls), and answer extraction code (to format model responses into benchmark predictions).
- **Grader:** A deterministic verifier invoked by the orchestrator to compute per-instance rewards.

#### 3.2. Meta-Agents

- **Meta-Agent (M):** Responsible for generating the initial scaffold (A1) for the task-specific agent based on a task specification (U) and any provided reference implementations (R). For all experiments, Claude Sonnet 4.6 is used as the Meta-Agent.
- **Feedback-Agent (F):** This is the orchestrating component of the SIA loop. It observes the previous generation's scaffold (Ag), its execution trajectory (τg), and performance metrics (Eg). Based on this analysis, the Feedback-Agent synthesizes an improved scaffold (Ag+1) or triggers a weight update. Claude Sonnet 4.6 is also used as the Feedback-Agent.

#### 3.3. Improvement Loop Protocol

Each generation (g) within the SIA loop follows a three-phase protocol:
1. **Execution:** The current Task-Specific Agent (Ag) is run on the evaluation dataset (D) within a sandboxed environment. The complete structured execution log, including prompts, model responses, tool calls, tool results, and extracted answers for each task instance, is captured as the trajectory (τg).
2. **Analysis:** The Feedback-Agent receives the source code of Ag, the captured trajectory τg, performance metrics Eg, and optionally, sample task descriptions to prevent overfitting to a single instance.
3. **Improvement:** The Feedback-Agent generates an improvement report (prose analysis and proposed changes) and either an improved scaffold (Ag+1) or initiates a weight update.

#### 3.4. Two Levers of Improvement

The Feedback-Agent dynamically selects between two complementary action types:
1. **Harness Update Phase:** When selected, the Feedback-Agent modifies the scaffold (Ag). The model weights (θ) of the Task-Specific Agent are held fixed during this phase. This involves one iteration of the Execution, Analysis, and Improvement protocol, leading to A(g+1). Sample-task regularization is employed to mitigate overfitting the initial scaffold.
2. **Weight Update Phase:** When selected, the Feedback-Agent triggers a reinforcement learning (RL) training algorithm to update the LoRA weights (θ) of the Task-Specific Agent. During this phase, the scaffold is held fixed. Weight updates are executed on H100 GPUs using Modal, Hexo Labs' RL training platform. The learning rate for LoRA updates is set to 4 × 10−5.

#### 3.5. Dynamic RL Algorithm Selection

A key aspect of SIA's methodology is the Feedback-Agent's ability to dynamically select the appropriate RL training algorithm based on the task type and observed reward dynamics. The paper outlines several observed patterns:
- **PPO with GAE (Proximal Policy Optimization with Generalized Advantage Estimation):** Applied when step-level rewards are dense and training stability is a primary concern (e.g., multi-step tool-use or long code generation).
- **GRPO (Gradient Reweighted Policy Optimization):** Used when rollouts are inexpensive to sample and the verifier triggers at episode end (e.g., classification, short-answer, or unit-test tasks).
- **Entropic Advantage Weighting:** Employed when the reward distribution is heavily right-skewed, meaning correct solutions are rare but highly informative (e.g., hard mathematical proofs or low-pass-rate code synthesis).
- **REINFORCE + KL-to-base:** Selected for dense rewards where capability regression is a risk, and the base model is already proficient, making large parameter shifts undesirable (e.g., fine-grained domain adaptation).
- **Best-of-N behavioural cloning:** Utilized as a cold-start strategy when reward sparsity is extreme, distilling top-k performing rollouts into the model via cross-entropy loss to establish a viable baseline.
- **DPO (Direct Preference Optimization):** Applied when the verifier can rank outputs by quality but cannot assign absolute scores, leveraging ordinal signal.

The Feedback-Agent's decision to switch between harness and weight updates, and its choice of specific RL algorithm, is conditioned on trajectory observations and observed reward dynamics rather than a fixed, predetermined schedule. This allows for an adaptive approach to self-improvement.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The SIA system was evaluated across three distinct domains: Chinese legal charge classification (LawBench), low-level GPU kernel optimization (AlphaEvolve TriMul), and single-cell RNA denoising (MAGIC scRNA-seq Denoising). The evaluation compared an initial baseline (gpt-oss-120b with the Meta-Agent's first scaffold), SIA-H (harness updates only), SIA-W+H (combined harness and weight updates), and the reported prior state-of-the-art (SOTA).

#### 4.1. Overall Thesis (RQ1: Combined vs. Harness-Only)

The empirical results consistently demonstrated that SIA-W+H (combined harness and weight updates) strictly outperformed SIA-H (harness updates only) across all three tasks. This supports the paper's primary research question that running both levers together pushes performance beyond the ceiling of harness-only iteration.

#### 4.2. Per-Task Results

- **LawBench: 191-Class Chinese Criminal Charge Classification**
 - **Task:** Identify the correct criminal charge from 191 categories based on factual case summaries.
 - **Metric:** Top-1 accuracy.
 - **Initial Baseline:** 13.5%.
 - **Previous SOTA:** 45.0%.
 - **SIA-H (Harness Updates Only):** Reached 50.0% accuracy. Early iterations established a classification pipeline, and subsequent generations refined it into a TF-IDF + LinearSVC pipeline with tuned n-gram ranges and regularizer parameter (C). Progress stalled at this point, leading the Feedback-Agent to initiate weight updates.
 - **SIA-W+H (Harness + Weight Updates):** Achieved 70.1% accuracy. The Feedback-Agent applied PPO with GAE for weight updates, utilizing the clean outcome-based scalar reward signal. This resulted in an additional 20.1 percentage point gain over SIA-H, representing a 25.1% improvement over prior SOTA. Weight updates sharpened the model's ability to disambiguate fine-grained legal distinctions.

- **AlphaEvolve TriMul: CUDA Kernel Optimisation for Protein Structure Prediction**
 - **Task:** Write a custom CUDA kernel for the triangular multiplicative update (TriMul) operation on an H100 GPU, focusing on runtime optimization for a memory-bandwidth-limited operation.
 - **Metric:** Score = 1500/runtime (higher score indicates faster kernel).
 - **Initial Baseline:** 0.105.
 - **Previous SOTA:** 1.292.
 - **SIA-H (Harness Updates Only):** Achieved a score of 0.120 (1.14x speedup). The agent iteratively built and refined CUDA kernels, with scaffold changes including memory layout hints, compilation flags, and retry logic.
 - **SIA-W+H (Harness + Weight Updates):** Achieved a score of 1.475, corresponding to a runtime of 1,017 μs (a 14.02x speedup and 91.9% reduction from the harness-only peak). This is 12.4% faster than prior SOTA (1,017 vs 1,161 μs). The Feedback-Agent used entropic advantage weighting due to the sparse, outcome-heavy reward structure of kernel optimization. Weight updates enabled the model to internalize H100-specific design patterns, such as shared-memory tiling, fp32 register accumulation, and block-size selection.

- **MAGIC scRNA-seq Denoising: Single-Cell RNA Imputation**
 - **Task:** Tune hyperparameters (k, t, α) and preprocessing choices for the MAGIC algorithm to denoise pancreas scRNA-seq data.
 - **Metric:** mse norm (normalized reconstruction quality, higher is better).
 - **Initial Baseline:** 0.048 mse norm.
 - **Previous SOTA:** 0.240 mse norm.
 - **SIA-H (Harness Updates Only):** Reached 0.241 mse norm. The agent systematically explored and tuned MAGIC's coupled hyperparameters, but further scaffold iterations yielded no significant improvement.
 - **SIA-W+H (Harness + Weight Updates):** Achieved 0.289 mse norm. The Feedback-Agent applied GRPO for weight updates. Notably, the first weight-update checkpoint introduced a structural transformation (a `np.clip + np.rint` post-processing step) to round imputed counts to non-negative integers, enforcing a biological invariant that the scaffold-only loop had not generated. This provided a 20% gain over the harness-only best, and a 20.4% improvement over prior SOTA.

#### 4.3. Ablation Summary

A direct comparison in Table 3 confirms that SIA-W+H consistently yielded substantial gains over SIA-H across all benchmarks. For example, LawBench saw a +20.1 percentage point gain, TriMul experienced a 91.9% runtime reduction, and denoising achieved a 20% improvement. This indicates that the two levers address distinct aspects of problem-solving, and their combined application is more effective than either in isolation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in this paper, SIA, contributes to the field of artificial intelligence by addressing the challenge of autonomous AI improvement. Its primary significance lies in demonstrating a functional, combined approach to self-improvement that integrates both structural and parametric modifications.

#### 5.1. Advancement in Self-Improving AI

By proposing a system where a Feedback-Agent dynamically updates both the operational scaffold and the underlying model weights, SIA takes a step towards alleviating the "human bottleneck" in AI development. The consistent performance gains observed across diverse domains (law, systems, biology) indicate that this integrated approach is more effective than relying on either harness iteration or weight updates in isolation. This suggests a potential for more capable and adaptive AI agents that can refine their own performance given only a task specification and a verifier.

#### 5.2. Distinct Contributions of Each Lever (RQ2)

The research provides empirical evidence differentiating the roles of harness updates and weight updates:
- **Harness updates** are shown to drive "externalized changes." These typically involve software engineering improvements such as the creation of new tools, refinement of output parsers, implementation of more effective search procedures, adjustment of retry policies, and structuring of prompts. These modifications primarily influence *how* the agent interacts with its environment and mediates its reasoning process.
- **Weight updates**, in contrast, facilitate "internalized knowledge." They enable the model to encode domain-specific patterns directly into its parameters, which the authors argue cannot be instilled through prompt or scaffold modifications alone. Examples include the model's improved disambiguation of legal categories in LawBench, its acquisition of H100-specific kernel design patterns in AlphaEvolve TriMul, and the introduction of a biologically relevant post-processing step in scRNA-seq denoising. These changes modify *what* the model knows, enhancing its intrinsic domain intuition.

The finding that weight updates can introduce structural invariants or specialized domain knowledge not conceived by scaffold iterations highlights the complementary nature of these two improvement mechanisms.

#### 5.3. Adaptive Learning and Generalizability

The Feedback-Agent's ability to dynamically select appropriate reinforcement learning algorithms based on observed task type and reward dynamics signifies an adaptive meta-learning capability. This flexibility suggests that the SIA framework could be robust across a wider variety of tasks, adjusting its learning strategy to suit the specific characteristics of the problem, ranging from dense to extremely sparse reward signals.

#### 5.4. Limitations

A recognized limitation is the "coupled co-evolutionary Goodhart" problem. Since both the harness search and the RL weight updates optimize against the same fixed verifier, there is a risk that the coupled system could converge to a Nash equilibrium that performs strongly on the training verifier but is fragile under perturbations or when faced with out-of-distribution scenarios. This points to potential challenges in ensuring broad generalizability beyond the specific evaluation context.

#### 5.5. Future Work

The authors propose several directions for future research:
- **Meta-RL over the action-selection policy:** Investigating a more principled approach where the Feedback-Agent's action-selection policy (between harness and weight updates) is itself learned via reinforcement learning over a distribution of tasks. This could lead to a truly recursive, self-improving system where the improvement mechanism itself adapts and refines.
- **Finer-grained interleaving:** Exploring more granular and dynamic scheduling of harness and weight updates. The current approach alternates in discrete rounds; a finer-grained schedule could reduce lag in adaptation and potentially unlock novel improvement trajectories.

In summary, SIA represents a step in integrating two previously distinct paradigms of AI self-improvement, offering a framework for agents to autonomously enhance both their operational structure and their internal knowledge, with potential implications for developing more autonomous and adaptive AI systems.
