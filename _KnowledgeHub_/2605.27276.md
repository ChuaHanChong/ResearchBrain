# SIA: Self Improving AI with Harness & Weight Updates

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Hexo Labs and the University of Oxford developed SIA, a system that autonomously improves AI performance by iteratively updating both its operational scaffold and underlying model weights. SIA consistently surpassed harness-only approaches and prior state-of-the-art across diverse tasks, achieving gains such as a 20.1 percentage point accuracy increase in Chinese legal classification and a 12.4% faster runtime for GPU kernel optimization.

## Problem

- Human involvement in AI development, from model design to tuning, acts as a significant bottleneck in AI progress.
- Existing self-improving AI research is split into two isolated approaches: harness/scaffold improvement (fixed weights, external changes) and test-time post-training (weight updates, fixed harness), failing to combine their benefits.
- Neither approach alone can fully adapt both the operational structure and the internal knowledge of an AI agent to a given task.

## Method

- **SIA** introduces a unified self-improving loop where a Feedback-Agent dynamically chooses between updating the task-specific agent's operational scaffold (harness) or its model weights.
- The Task-Specific Agent uses an LLM (gpt-oss-120b) with Low-Rank Adaptation (==LoRA==) for weight updates, while the Meta-Agent and Feedback-Agent employ Claude Sonnet 4.6.
- The Feedback-Agent adaptively selects appropriate ==reinforcement learning== algorithms (e.g., PPO, ==GRPO==, REINFORCE) based on task type and observed reward dynamics.

## Results

- SIA-W+H achieved **70.1%** accuracy on LawBench (Chinese legal charge classification), a 20.1 percentage point gain over SIA-H and **25.1%** over prior SOTA.
- For AlphaEvolve TriMul (CUDA kernel optimization), SIA-W+H resulted in a **14.02x** speedup over the baseline, performing **12.4%** faster than the previous SOTA.
- In MAGIC scRNA-seq Denoising, SIA-W+H reached 0.289 mse norm, representing a **20%** gain over SIA-H and **20.4%** over prior SOTA, notably introducing a critical `np.clip + np.rint` post-processing step via weight updates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining both harness and model weight updates in a unified feedback loop (SIA-W+H) consistently yields superior performance compared to scaffold iteration alone (SIA-H) across diverse application domains.
> - Harness updates primarily drive 'externalized changes' such as tool refinement, parsing logic, and search procedures, affecting how the agent operates.
> - Weight updates facilitate 'internalized knowledge,' enabling the model to encode domain-specific patterns and structural invariants directly into its parameters, which are often inaccessible through scaffold edits alone.
