# RenderWorld: World Model with Self-Supervised 3D Label

> [!summary] Summary
> RenderWorld is a vision-only autonomous driving framework that self-generates 3D occupancy labels via a Gaussian-Splatting Img2Occ module, encodes air and non-air voxels separately with AM-VAE, then feeds discrete tokens to an autoregressive world model for 4D occupancy forecasting and motion planning, reaching near-LiDAR-input performance.

## Problem

- 3D occupancy ground truth needed for supervision is expensive and hard to annotate, limiting scalable purely-vision occupancy training pipelines.
- NeRF-based self-supervised occupancy methods rely on continuous implicit fields, causing high GPU memory cost during ray sampling.
- Existing world models like OccWorld are less effective on pure 2D camera input, losing information during encoding and predicting future scenes poorly.

## Method

- **Img2Occ Module**: anchor-based ==Gaussian Splatting== projects multi-camera features into 3D Gaussians, rendered to 2D depth/semantic maps for self-supervised 3D occupancy label generation.
- **Air Mask Variational Autoencoder (AM-VAE)**: trains two separate ==VQ-VAE codebooks== to encode air versus non-air voxels independently for finer-grained scene tokenization.
- Autoregressive world model: ==temporal generative transformer== with ==masked temporal attention== over ==hierarchical scene tokens== predicts future occupancy and ego displacement in two training phases.

## Results

- On Occ3D-nuScenes, RenderWorld reaches **27.87** mIoU, ranking second overall among ten methods and best among all 2D-input approaches.
- 4D forecasting with 3D-occ input achieves **20.80** average mIoU versus OccWorld's **17.14**, while cutting memory from **13500M** to **13000M**.
- Ablation shows Air-Mask plus separate VAE lifts reconstruction mIoU from **35.13** to **40.25**, and Gaussian representation cuts memory to **14400M** versus **79000M** for OccNeRF.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Separating air from non-air voxels into independent latent codebooks is a cheap way to sharpen scene tokenization without added architecture complexity.
> - Gaussian Splatting's explicit, sparse representation trades some memory efficiency for retained semantic granularity versus more compact but coarser methods.
> - Self-supervised 2D-to-3D label generation can substitute for costly 3D occupancy ground truth without collapsing downstream forecasting quality.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

RenderWorld addresses several critical and rapidly evolving areas within autonomous driving (AD) and computer vision:

**a. The Shift Towards Vision-Only End-to-End Autonomous Driving:**
Historically, AD systems relied heavily on costly and complex LiDAR-camera fusion for robust 3D perception. However, there is a growing interest in vision-only systems due to their cost-effectiveness and potential for higher reliability in certain scenarios, mirroring approaches taken by leading industry players like Tesla. RenderWorld directly contributes to this paradigm by proposing a purely visual end-to-end framework, aiming to simplify the sensor suite and processing pipeline. The "end-to-end" nature further represents a significant trend in AD research, integrating perception, forecasting, and planning into a single, cohesive model, moving away from cascaded, modular designs that can suffer from error propagation.

**b. Advancements in 3D Occupancy Prediction:**
3D occupancy prediction is emerging as a powerful alternative to traditional 3D object detection, offering a more comprehensive and fine-grained understanding of the environment. Unlike bounding boxes, occupancy maps describe the presence or absence of objects (and their semantics) at a voxel level, crucial for nuanced decision-making and safe navigation, especially in complex urban scenarios. The challenge, however, lies in obtaining high-quality 3D ground truth labels, which are notoriously difficult and expensive to annotate.
- **NeRF-based approaches:** Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) have shown promise in generating 3D scene representations from 2D images. However, their high memory consumption and computational demands due to ray sampling make them less suitable for real-time AD applications.
- **Gaussian Splatting (GS):** Recently, 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) has revolutionized real-time radiance field rendering, offering significantly improved efficiency and quality compared to NeRFs. RenderWorld innovatively adapts GS for 3D occupancy prediction, positioning itself at the forefront of leveraging this new rendering technology for AD perception.
- **Self-Supervised Learning:** By training its Img2Occ module with self-supervised 2D depth and semantic labels (derived from projected LiDAR point clouds), RenderWorld bypasses the need for explicit 3D occupancy ground truth annotation, addressing a major bottleneck in the field.

**c. World Models for Autonomous Driving:**
World models, originally from reinforcement learning, are gaining traction in AD for their ability to learn predictive models of the environment and simulate future states, which can then be used for robust forecasting and planning. However, existing world models often struggle with efficiently encoding complex 3D scenes, especially when relying solely on 2D camera inputs, and can lose critical information during the encoding process. RenderWorld addresses this by providing a superior 3D occupancy representation as input and introducing the Air Mask Variational Autoencoder (AM-VAE) to improve the granularity and efficiency of scene encoding for the world model. This enhances the world model's capacity to understand and predict dynamic driving scenarios.

In summary, RenderWorld integrates state-of-the-art techniques from 3D vision (Gaussian Splatting, NeRF-inspired self-supervision), representation learning (VQ-VAE), and predictive modeling (world models) to tackle the grand challenge of robust, real-time, and cost-effective end-to-end autonomous driving with vision-only input.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of RenderWorld is to develop a robust and economical purely visual end-to-end autonomous driving system. This central goal is driven by several specific motivations and translated into distinct research objectives:

**a. Reduce Cost and Enhance Robustness of AD Systems:**
- **Motivation:** Traditional AD systems reliant on LiDAR-vision fusion incur high hardware costs and computational overhead. Additionally, the lack of fine-grained environmental information from typical 3D target detection methods can lead to non-robust planning, impacting system safety.
- **Objective:** Propose a vision-only framework to reduce hardware costs and improve real-time performance and robustness compared to multimodal fusion approaches.

**b. Generate High-Quality 3D Occupancy Labels from 2D Inputs:**
- **Motivation:** 3D occupancy ground truth is crucial for training advanced perception and prediction models but is exceedingly difficult and expensive to annotate manually. Prior NeRF-based methods for generating 3D occupancy from 2D labels suffered from high memory consumption, while other Gaussian-based methods often sacrificed accuracy.
- **Objective:** Develop a self-supervised Gaussian-based Img2Occ module that generates accurate 3D occupancy labels from standard 2D multi-view depth and semantic images. This module specifically aims to leverage Gaussian Splatting to improve segmentation accuracy and reduce GPU memory compared to NeRF-based counterparts.

**c. Improve Fine-grained Scene Representation for World Models:**
- **Motivation:** Existing Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) and world models struggle to encode the distinct features of various scene elements, particularly differentiating free space ("air") from obstacles ("non-air") effectively. This limits the model's ability to represent scenes at a fine-grained level and impacts the accuracy of future predictions and planning.
- **Objective:** Introduce the Air Mask Variational Autoencoder (AM-VAE) to separately encode air and non-air voxels. This aims to achieve a more fine-grained scene element representation, enhance data compression efficiency, and improve the world model's inference capabilities.

**d. Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance in 4D Occupancy Forecasting and Motion Planning:**
- **Motivation:** The ultimate goal of an end-to-end AD system is to accurately predict future scenarios (4D occupancy) and generate safe, efficient motion plans. Current world model approaches, especially with pure 2D input, still have limitations in these aspects due to information loss during encoding or insufficient scene understanding.
- **Objective:** Integrate the Img2Occ module and AM-VAE with an autoregressive world model to achieve state-of-the-art performance in both 4D occupancy forecasting and ego-vehicle motion planning, demonstrating the overall effectiveness and synergistic benefits of the proposed framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RenderWorld proposes a comprehensive end-to-end autonomous driving framework composed of three main interconnected modules: a Gaussian-based Img2Occ module for 3D occupancy label generation, an Air Mask Variational Autoencoder (AM-VAE) for optimized scene representation, and a World Model for 4D occupancy forecasting and motion planning.

**a. 3D Occupancy Prediction with Multi-frame 2D Labels (Img2Occ Module):**
This module is responsible for converting 2D multi-camera inputs into 3D semantic occupancy labels, serving as ground truth for the subsequent world model.
1. **Feature Extraction:** Multi-camera 2D images are fed into a pretrained BEVStereo4D backbone and Swin Transformer to extract 2D image features.
2. **3D Feature Volume Creation:** Using known intrinsic and extrinsic camera parameters, these 2D features are lifted and interpolated into a 3D volume.
3. **Gaussian Splatting for 3D Scene Representation:** Inspired by recent advances in real-time rendering, RenderWorld employs Gaussian Splatting. Anchor points are initialized at the center of each voxel with a learnable scale. These anchors form a set of 3D Gaussians, each characterized by a center position ($\mu$), a full 3D covariance matrix ($\Sigma$), and semantic labels. To ensure the validity of $\Sigma$, it is decomposed into a scaling matrix ($S$) and a rotation matrix ($R$).
4. **2D Rendering and Self-Supervised Training:** The 3D Gaussians are then projected back to 2D camera space to render semantic and depth maps for each camera view using alpha blending.
 - **Loss Function:** The module is trained in a self-supervised manner using readily available 2D ground truth depth and semantic images (derived by projecting LiDAR point clouds with 3D segmentation labels). The loss function combines a Pearson correlation loss for depth supervision ($L_{dep}$) and a cross-entropy loss for semantic segmentation ($L_{sem}$).
5. **Output:** Once trained, this module generates dense 3D occupancy labels, including semantic information, which are then used as input for the AM-VAE.

**b. Air Mask Variational Autoencoder (AM-VAE):**
The AM-VAE addresses the challenge of efficiently encoding 3D occupancy data, particularly by distinguishing between "air" (free space) and "non-air" (obstacles/objects) voxels to improve scene representation granularity and compression.
1. **Separate Encoding:** Instead of a single VAE, AM-VAE utilizes two distinct Vector Quantized Variational Autoencoders (VQ-VAEs) operating in parallel: one for air voxels ($o_{Air}$) and another for non-air voxels ($o_{N-Air}$).
2. **Encoding and Quantization:** A 3D convolutional neural network encodes the input occupancy into a continuous latent space ($f$). This is then mapped to separate latent variables for air ($s_{Air}$) and non-air ($s_{N-Air}$). Each latent variable is quantized by finding the most similar entry in its respective learnable codebook ($C_{Air}$ or $C_{N-Air}$), yielding discrete tokens ($s'_{Air}$, $s'_{N-Air}$).
3. **Decoding and Reconstruction:** Separate decoders reconstruct the air ($\hat{o}_{Air}$) and non-air ($\hat{o}_{N-Air}$) occupancy from these quantized tokens.
4. **Mask-based Fusion:** A non-air indicator function ($I_M(o)$) and a mask derived from the reconstructed air occupancy ($\text{mask} = (\hat{o}_{Air} \neq 0)$) are used to combine the air and non-air components into a complete reconstructed occupancy ($\hat{o}$). This intelligent fusion ensures that non-air elements are prioritized where present, while air defines the free space.
5. **Loss Function:** The AM-VAE is trained with a reconstruction loss ($L_{Recon}$) and a commitment loss ($L_{Com}$) to ensure accurate reconstruction and effective codebook learning.

**c. World Model:**
The world model takes the compressed, fine-grained scene tokens from the AM-VAE and the ego-vehicle's spatial position to forecast future 4D occupancy and plan vehicle trajectories.
1. **Input:** High-level scene tokens ($T$) from the AM-VAE and a vehicle token ($z_0$) representing the ego-vehicle's position.
2. **Temporal Generative Transformer:** An autoregressive temporal generative transformer architecture is employed. It processes historical scene tokens through spatial aggregation and downsampling to create a hierarchical set of tokens.
3. **Multi-Scale Prediction:** Multiple sub-world models are used to predict future tokens at different spatial scales, leveraging masked temporal attention to capture scene evolution.
4. **Decoding for Prediction:**
 - A scene decoder ($d$) decodes the predicted scene tokens ($\hat{z}_{T+1}$) back into future 3D occupancy ($\hat{y}_{T+1}$).
 - A vehicle decoder ($d_{ego}$) predicts the ego-vehicle's displacement relative to the current frame ($\hat{p}_{T+1}$), which is used for motion planning.
5. **Two-Stage Training:**
 - **Phase 1:** Train the scene tokenizer ($e$) and scene decoder ($d$) using a 3D occupancy loss (softmax and Lovasz-softmax losses).
 - **Phase 2:** Train the world model ($w$) and vehicle decoder ($d_{ego}$). This involves minimizing the softmax loss between predicted tokens and ground truth tokens (from the learned tokenizer) and an L2 loss for the predicted vehicle displacement.

The overall methodology of RenderWorld represents a holistic approach to end-to-end autonomous driving, meticulously designed to leverage the strengths of modern computer vision techniques while addressing the practical challenges of cost, memory, and fine-grained scene understanding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

RenderWorld demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across 3D semantic occupancy prediction, 4D occupancy forecasting, and motion planning, particularly excelling in vision-only settings. The experiments were conducted on the NuScenes dataset.

**a. 3D Semantic Occupancy Prediction (Table I):**
- RenderWorld (2D input) achieved an mIoU of 27.87%, ranking second overall among all compared methods. It was only marginally surpassed by CTF-Occ (28.53%), which relies on costly 3D occupancy ground truth (GT) for supervision, unlike RenderWorld's 2D supervision.
- Crucially, RenderWorld significantly outperformed all other methods that exclusively use 2D input for training (e.g., RenderOcc 23.93%, GaussianFormer 19.10%, GaussianOcc 9.94%).
- The model demonstrated exceptional performance in segmenting various categories, including vehicles (trailers, construction vehicles, trucks) and environmental terrains (vegetation, sidewalk, manmade structures), indicating its strong ability to capture diverse scene elements. This superior performance is attributed to the 3D Gaussian representation's effectiveness in leveraging scene sparsity and object diversity.

**b. 4D Occupancy Forecasting (Table II):**
- When provided with 3D occupancy input, RenderWorld achieved an average mIoU of 20.80% for 4D occupancy forecasting (across 1s, 2s, 3s horizons), outperforming OccWorld (17.14%) and Copy&Paste (11.33%). This confirms its ability to learn underlying scene evolution effectively.
- In the more challenging scenario of pure camera (2D) input without auxiliary supervision, RenderWorld achieved an average mIoU of 2.58%. While numerically lower than methods leveraging LiDAR (e.g., TPVFormer+Lidar+OccWorld-T at 3.56%), it significantly surpassed other camera-only baselines like TPVFormer+SelfOcc+OccWorld-S (0.26%), demonstrating its superior capability in generating "non-trivial" future 3D occupancy from purely visual data.

**c. Motion Planning (Table III):**
- With 3D occupancy as input, RenderWorld achieved an average L2 error of 1.03 meters in motion planning, outperforming OccWorld (1.17m). Its collision rate (0.61%) was also competitive with OccWorld (0.60%).
- When using only camera (2D) input without auxiliary support, RenderWorld achieved an average L2 error of 1.48 meters. This result is highly competitive, matching LiDAR-based methods like NMP (1.48m) and significantly outperforming other camera-only approaches like OccWorld-S (1.83m). The collision rate (0.97%) was also favorable compared to OccWorld-S (2.02%).

**d. Ablation Studies:**
- **Efficiency Comparisons (Table V):** The 3D Gaussian representation used in RenderWorld demonstrated significantly reduced GPU memory usage (14400MB) compared to NeRF-based methods (e.g., OccNeRF 79000MB, RenderOcc 23000MB) and even other common BEV-based approaches (e.g., BEVFormer 25100MB). While GaussianFormer used less memory (6229MB), RenderWorld achieved higher segmentation accuracy by employing denser Gaussians, indicating a better balance between efficiency and detail.
- **Scene Tokenizer Analysis (Table IV):** The study on hyperparameters for the scene tokenizer (latent spatial resolution, channel dimension, codebook size) identified an optimal configuration (50^2, 128, 512). It revealed that excessively large resolutions or codebook sizes could lead to overfitting or reduced prediction capabilities, while smaller ones compromised scene representation accuracy.
- **AM-VAE Module Effectiveness (Table VI):**
 - The introduction of the Air-Mask module alone improved mIoU for reconstruction from 35.13% to 37.68%.
 - The VAE separation operation (dividing latent space into air and non-air) also contributed positively, achieving 35.42% mIoU on its own.
 - Combining both the Air-Mask module and VAE separation yielded the best performance, boosting mIoU to 40.25%. This strong synergistic effect confirms that the proposed air separation strategy significantly enhances reconstruction accuracy and reduces positional errors, validating the core design of the AM-VAE.

In summary, RenderWorld successfully demonstrates that a vision-only, end-to-end autonomous driving framework can achieve highly competitive, and in many cases, state-of-the-art results in 3D perception, 4D forecasting, and motion planning. Its innovations in self-supervised 3D occupancy generation using Gaussian Splatting and the specialized AM-VAE are key to its efficiency and performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RenderWorld presents several significant contributions with far-reaching potential impacts on the field of autonomous driving and broader computer vision applications:

**a. Democratization of Autonomous Driving:**
By achieving state-of-the-art performance using only camera inputs, RenderWorld significantly reduces the reliance on expensive LiDAR sensors. This cost reduction is crucial for the widespread adoption and commercial viability of autonomous vehicles, making AD technology more accessible and deployable on a larger scale.

**b. Enhanced Safety and Robustness:**
The framework's ability to generate fine-grained 3D occupancy predictions and distinguish clearly between "air" and "non-air" elements provides a more nuanced understanding of the driving environment. This detailed perception directly translates to more robust forecasting of future scenarios and more precise, safer motion planning, which are paramount for accident prevention in complex real-world conditions.

**c. Advancement in 3D Scene Understanding from 2D Inputs:**
The Img2Occ module's innovative use of Gaussian Splatting for self-supervised 3D occupancy label generation from 2D images is a significant methodological advance. It overcomes the memory constraints of NeRF-based methods while maintaining high accuracy, effectively bridging the gap between readily available 2D labels and the critical need for 3D ground truth in AD. This approach could inspire new paradigms for 3D reconstruction and understanding in various domains.

**d. More Efficient and Effective World Models:**
The Air Mask Variational Autoencoder (AM-VAE) specifically addresses a critical limitation of existing world models by enabling more granular and efficient encoding of 3D scenes. By consciously separating and representing free space from obstacles, the AM-VAE allows the world model to learn more meaningful and compressed representations, leading to improved predictive capabilities and better decision-making for motion planning. This innovation strengthens the foundation of end-to-end learning in AD.

**e. Real-time Capabilities:**
The meticulous focus on GPU memory reduction, particularly through the adoption of Gaussian Splatting over NeRF and the efficiency gains from AM-VAE, suggests that RenderWorld is designed with real-time performance in mind. Real-time processing is non-negotiable for autonomous driving systems, and this work moves closer to achieving that goal with a vision-only setup.

**f. Broader Applications in Robotics and XR:**
The techniques developed in RenderWorld for efficient 3D scene representation, perception, and forecasting are not limited to autonomous driving. They could be highly valuable for other robotic applications requiring robust environmental understanding (e.g., mobile manipulation, drone navigation), as well as in Extended Reality (XR) for real-time 3D reconstruction and rendering of dynamic environments.

In conclusion, RenderWorld makes a compelling case for the viability and superiority of vision-only end-to-end autonomous driving. Its synergistic combination of self-supervised 3D occupancy generation, an innovative scene representation learning module, and a powerful world model represents a substantial leap forward, promising safer, more economical, and more widely accessible autonomous technologies.
