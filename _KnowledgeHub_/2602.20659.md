# Recursive Belief Vision Language Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> RB-VLA is a belief-centric VLA that replaces dense VLM re-querying with a fixed-size, action-conditioned recursive belief state (RSSM-style world model) tracking task progress under partial observability. A VLM is queried once for intent; belief plus intent condition a diffusion policy. Beats pi-0 by 52.5% and 37.5% success on multi-stage pick-place and stacking, with 5x lower latency.

## Problem

- Existing VLAs condition actions on short observation windows, failing under occlusion, perceptual aliasing, and long multi-stage tasks needing persistent context.
- Scaling context via token accumulation or repeated VLM queries causes high inference latency and memory that grows unboundedly with episode length.
- Prior long-horizon methods rely on explicit stage or subgoal supervision, which is task-specific and fails to generalize across smoothly evolving manipulation dynamics.

## Method

- **Recursive belief estimator**: an ==RSSM-style== GRU updates a fixed-size latent belief from prior belief, evidence tokens, and a stochastic latent, trained via ==ELBO== with EMA-targeted one-step and five-step future prediction.
- ==Action-grounded belief training==: temporal contrastive frame encoder warm-start plus an ==inverse dynamics loss== that forces the belief to encode controllable, action-relevant state rather than appearance.
- Decoupled episodic intent: a frozen VLM is queried once via ==attention pooling== over its fused hidden states to produce a static intent embedding conditioning a ==DiT diffusion== action-chunk policy.

## Results

- RB-VLA reaches **82.5%**/**77.5%**/**85.0%**/**80.0%** success on single/multi pick-place and single/multi stacking, exceeding pi-0 by up to **52.5** points and OpenVLA which fails entirely.
- Inference latency drops over **5x** versus stateless OpenVLA/RT1-X and over **3x** versus multi-frame GR00T-N1, while memory stays constant versus superlinear growth (up to **7.12x** at 8 frames) for baselines.
- Ablations show belief-policy conditioning is the primary driver: success falls from **77.5%** to **32.5%** when belief is removed, with frame-encoder targets and stochastic latent each contributing incrementally.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling episodic semantic grounding (query VLM once) from continuous causal state tracking (recursive belief) can cut both latency and memory scaling without sacrificing accuracy.
> - Action-conditioned, dynamics-grounded belief representations generalize better than discrete stage labels for smoothly evolving multi-stage manipulation.
> - Belief-space proximity predicting future action and observation similarity (Pearson r of 0.87 and 0.78) suggests compact learned states can substitute for costly rollout-based world-model planning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is progressing towards Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which aim to enable autonomous agents to execute complex tasks by integrating language understanding with visual perception to directly produce robot actions. These models offer the potential for improved scalability compared to classical control systems. However, current VLA approaches frequently operate under an implicit Markovian assumption, conditioning actions primarily on the immediate observation or a very short history window. This reactive reasoning paradigm presents several limitations in practical applications, particularly for long-horizon, multi-stage tasks under partial observability.

Existing VLA models often suffer from a loss of task progress, action repetition due to perceptual aliasing (where visually similar situations are treated identically regardless of underlying state), and high inference latency. These issues arise because models may rely on repeated, computationally expensive queries to large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) or scale memory by accumulating observation tokens, leading to increasing computational and memory costs over time. Furthermore, these models often exhibit limited temporal and physical reasoning capabilities, which hinders their effectiveness in multi-stage control scenarios that require understanding action consequences and persistent state tracking.

To address some of these challenges, prior work has explored various avenues. Some methods attempt to define multi-stage dynamics with explicit task-stage supervision, but this approach can be task-specific and may not generalize well, as manipulation is often driven by smoothly evolving, action-grounded internal dynamics rather than discrete labels. World models represent another line of research, aiming to model agent-environment dynamics to enable future-aware planning. While VLMs are proficient in semantic inference, they typically lack explicit grounding in physical dynamics and state evolution. World models bridge this gap, but existing implementations often rely on predicting future observations and computationally intensive dynamic rollouts for online correction, which can introduce significant overhead.

In terms of memory and context, previous attempts to incorporate history involve recurrent features or expanding memory tokens over time. However, these mechanisms may not explicitly ground memory in action-conditioned dynamics or may primarily serve as post-hoc modulators of policy outputs rather than internal states that actively shape perception and encode action-induced transitions. Classical belief-space planners, while addressing partial observability, traditionally depend on explicit models and extensive forward simulation, which also incurs substantial computational overhead.

The work presented in "Recursive Belief Vision Language Action Models" positions itself by introducing a belief-centric VLA architecture, RB-VLA, designed to maintain a compact, latent state representation (a "belief") that explicitly encodes task-relevant history, dynamics, and object interactions. This approach aims to provide persistent state awareness, enable causal reasoning under partial observability, and decouple the computationally intensive semantic grounding provided by VLMs from the real-time, low-level control loop. By doing so, RB-VLA seeks to overcome the limitations of observation-driven reactive policies, reduce inference latency, and achieve robust, long-horizon control without the need for extensive memory growth or continuous VLM re-inference.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model capable of executing long-horizon, multi-stage manipulation tasks robustly under conditions of partial observability. This objective is motivated by several identified shortcomings in existing VLA frameworks:

1. **Addressing Partial Observability:** Many real-world robotic tasks involve scenarios where crucial information is not always fully visible or consistently present in the immediate observations. Objects may become occluded, leave the camera's field of view, or appear visually identical to other objects, leading to perceptual aliasing. Existing observation-driven VLAs often struggle in such conditions, losing track of task progress or repeating actions. The motivation is to enable agents to maintain a consistent understanding of the environment and task state even when visual evidence is incomplete or ambiguous.

2. **Enabling Long-Horizon and Multi-Stage Control:** Current VLAs typically operate with short context windows, making it difficult to reason over extended interactions or to sequence multiple sub-tasks required for long-horizon objectives. This leads to a loss of task progress and an inability to adapt to the evolving state of a multi-stage task. A key motivation is to equip the VLA with a persistent memory mechanism that summarizes relevant interaction history and internal dynamics, allowing for sustained, goal-directed task progression over long durations.

3. **Improving Computational Efficiency and Reducing Latency:** The prevalent practice of repeatedly querying large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) at every timestep for action prediction is computationally expensive, leading to high inference latency and increased energy consumption. Furthermore, memory often scales with interaction history through token accumulation, which is unsustainable for very long tasks. The researchers are motivated to reduce this computational burden by decoupling the high-level semantic reasoning (where VLMs excel) from the low-level, high-frequency control execution. This aims to enable real-time deployment of VLA agents without incurring prohibitive computational costs.

4. **Developing Action-Conditioned State Representations:** Existing VLAs often lack an explicit internal state that continuously tracks task progress and the consequences of past actions. Semantic grounding alone, while crucial, does not fully capture the physical dynamics and evolving state of a manipulation task. The motivation is to introduce a compact, latent state representation (a "belief") that recursively integrates multimodal observations (visuals, proprioception) and actions to form a coherent, dynamics-consistent internal model of the task, thereby providing a basis for causal reasoning and phase-aware control.

5. **Enhancing Robustness and Consistency:** The absence of a persistent internal state can lead to inconsistent decision-making, such as re-attempting already completed sub-tasks or exhibiting erratic behavior under minor visual variations. The objective is to imbue the VLA with a robust internal representation that filters observation noise, adapts to environmental variability, and ensures a more consistent and reliable execution of manipulation tasks.

In summary, the core motivation behind RB-VLA is to move beyond purely reactive, observation-driven VLA paradigms by introducing a dynamics-grounded, recursive belief state. This belief state aims to provide persistent task awareness, facilitate causal reasoning, and enable computationally efficient, robust, and consistent execution of long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks under real-world partial observability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Recursive Belief VLA (RB-VLA) framework addresses long-horizon manipulation as a partially observable control problem by introducing a learned belief estimator that links perception to underlying motion dynamics. The system architecture comprises three main components: a vision-language reasoning module, a belief estimator, and a diffusion-based controller.

**A. Vision-Language Reasoning Module (High-Level Intent Prediction)**
The VLM component is designed to be queried sparsely. At the beginning of a task (t=0), a pre-trained VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, with its language generation head removed and backbone frozen) processes the natural language instruction and the initial visual observation. Its final multimodal hidden states are then pooled using a learned goal query vector and single-query attention mechanism. This process selectively aggregates visual and linguistic tokens into a single grounded representation (`h_task`). A lightweight Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) then projects `h_task` into a compact, continuous intent embedding (`I_t`). This `I_t` remains static throughout the task execution, as it represents the fixed semantic goal, decoupling semantic grounding from step-wise control. This episodic semantic grounding strategy aims to reduce computational overhead.

**B. Belief Estimator**
The belief estimator is central to RB-VLA, maintaining a fixed-size, action-conditioned recursive belief memory (`b_t`) that captures task-relevant interaction history and system dynamics. This belief (`b_t`) is a latent state, recursively updated at each timestep, which provides information not available from a single observation.

1. **Input Processing:** At each timestep, the belief estimator takes the previous belief (`b_{t-1}`), a fixed-length window of tokenized observations (`o_{t-K:t}`), past actions (`a_{t-K:t}`), and proprioceptive states (`x_{t-K:t}`) as input. Visual observations are encoded into patch-level features using a pre-trained DINOv2 backbone, while proprioceptive signals (end-effector pose, velocity, acceleration, force-torque) are embedded into a shared latent space.
2. **Frame Encoder:** These multimodal tokens are fused by a transformer-based frame encoder. This encoder uses multi-head self-attention and pooling via learned attention to produce a task-relevant frame summary (`f_t`). A temporal contrastive objective warm-starts this encoder, aligning nearby interaction states and separating dynamically inconsistent ones, thereby encouraging `f_t` to reflect action-conditioned dynamics rather than mere appearance similarity.
3. **Temporal Integration (Belief Transformer):** A transformer processes a recent fixed-length window (K=5 frames) of the frame encoder representations. Crucially, the previous belief state (`b_{t-1}`) is prepended as a token, allowing the model to condition new evidence on accumulated dynamics-relevant history. This belief token actively biases temporal attention. The output of this stage is an `evidence representation (e_t)`, summarizing new observations conditioned on the prior belief.
4. **Stochastic Latent Variable:** To account for uncertainty and branching futures, a stochastic latent variable (`z_t`) is introduced. The model learns a prior distribution `p(z_t | b_{t-1})` (predicted uncertainty) and a posterior distribution `q(z_t | b_{t-1}, e_t)` (incorporating new evidence). Both are Gaussian distributions.
5. **Belief Update:** The deterministic belief (`b_t`) is then updated using a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) as `b_t = GRU(b_{t-1}, [e_t, z_t])`.

**C. Belief Training Objectives:** The belief estimator is trained as an RSSM-style world model using several self-supervised objectives:
- **ELBO Objective:** A standard Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) objective is applied to predict future latent observations (`x_{t+1}`). The target for `x_{t+1}` is derived from an Exponential Moving Average (EMA)-updated frame encoder for stability. An auxiliary decoder also predicts `x_{t+5}`, encouraging the belief to encode longer-horizon dynamical evolution. The objective includes a KL divergence term `KL(q(z_t) || p(z_t))` with a scheduled weight, promoting exploration and structured belief refinement.
- **Inverse Dynamics Loss:** An L2 loss, `L_inv = ||g(b_t, b_{t+1}) - a_t||^2_2`, encourages the belief to encode controllable state information relevant for predicting executed actions.
The belief model operates at 10-20 Hz during inference.

**D. Low-Level Action Generation (Diffusion Policy)**
Low-level control is handled by a diffusion transformer policy. This policy operates in latent space and is conditioned on the current belief (`b_t`), the static intent (`I_t`), and a fused state representation (`s_t`). `s_t` is obtained from a state encoder that combines robot proprioception and DINOv2 image embeddings, decoupling multimodal perception from belief- and intent-level reasoning. The policy samples action chunks (`a_{t:t+H}`) via iterative denoising, warm-started from the previously predicted action sequence with added low-variance Gaussian noise for efficiency and temporal coherence. Actions are executed in a receding-horizon manner at a high frequency (50 Hz).

**E. Training Details**
A staged training pipeline is employed. First, the belief transformer is trained with its self-supervised world-model objectives. Subsequently, the intent extraction MLP layers and the diffusion policy are trained jointly. The VLM backbone (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) is kept frozen during training, with only the lightweight intent extraction module adapting its high-dimensional features to the control space. The training uses 40,000 simulated manipulation trajectories across 10 tasks in environments like RoboSuite, LIBERO-Long, and a UR5 MuJoCo environment, and is further fine-tuned on real-world data. RGB observations are augmented to enhance robustness. The belief model has 150M parameters, and the diffusion controller has 120M parameters.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The RB-VLA framework was evaluated extensively in both simulated and real-world environments, with a focus on long-horizon tasks, computational efficiency, belief representation quality, and component ablation.

**A. Long-Horizon Tasks:**
RB-VLA demonstrated substantial performance improvements on long-horizon, multi-stage manipulation tasks, including multi-stage pick-and-place under occlusion and stacking tasks, in RoboSuite and LIBERO-Long environments. Across 40 trials per task, RB-VLA achieved 77.5% success on multi-stage pick-and-place, outperforming baseline models like π0 (25.0%) by 52.5% and GROOT-N1 (20.0%) by 57.5%. OpenVLA was unable to complete the task in this setting. For multi-stage stacking, RB-VLA achieved 80.0% success, surpassing π0 (42.5%) and GROOT-N1 (32.5%). This indicated that the belief-based state estimation mechanism effectively maintained task progress and object awareness, particularly when objects became occluded or visually similar, a limitation for observation-driven baselines relying on repeated VLM re-inference.

**B. Latency and Memory Analysis:**
RB-VLA exhibited significantly reduced computational overhead. Its average episode inference latency was observed to be over five times lower than stateless models like OpenVLA and RT1-X, and more than three times faster than multi-frame GROOT-N1. This efficiency gain is attributed to querying the VLM only once at the beginning of the task to obtain the intent embedding, rather than at every step.
Regarding memory usage, RB-VLA maintained a constant memory footprint irrespective of the episode length. In contrast, large VLM backbones (Qwen2-VL-7B) and end-to-end VLA systems (GR00T-N1) showed superlinear memory growth as the number of observed frames increased, confirming the advantage of RB-VLA's fixed-size recursive belief state for scalable long-horizon control.

**C. Belief Representation Analysis:**
Analysis of the belief space revealed a strong correlation between belief similarity and future dynamics. A high Pearson correlation coefficient (r = 0.87) was observed between belief cosine similarity and the L2 distance between future action sequences. Similarly, belief cosine similarity strongly correlated with future visual feature similarity (r = 0.78). This indicated that proximity in the learned belief space predicted consistency in future perception and control behavior, suggesting the belief acts as a robust, action-conditioned dynamical state.
Investigation into the stochastic latent variable `z_t` showed that sampling different `z_t` from a fixed belief state `b_t` produced diverse yet structured future predictions. This suggested that while `b_t` encoded sharp underlying dynamics, `z_t` effectively captured uncertainty and multimodality over plausible future evolutions, preventing collapse to a single averaged future. Visualizations of attention weights confirmed that the belief adaptively attended to its recursive state and causally informative past frames, retaining relevant dynamical history, particularly under occlusion.

**D. Ablation Study:**
An ablation study quantitatively assessed the contribution of key components. Removing the belief-to-policy conditioning (effectively using a standard Diffusion Transformer policy) resulted in the lowest average success rate of 32.5% on the two-object pick-and-place task. Performance incrementally improved with the reintroduction of stochastic latent variables (`z_t`) (57.5% success), followed by using frame-encoder targets for belief training (62.5% success), with the full RB-VLA achieving 77.5% success. This confirmed that the dynamics-grounded belief state is the primary driver of performance for long-horizon, multi-stage behavior.

**E. Real-World Deployment:**
RB-VLA successfully transferred to a physical UR5 manipulator from a simulated UR5 MuJoCo setup without architectural modifications. After fine-tuning on 100 real-world trajectories to adapt to sensor noise and unmodeled dynamics, the system achieved a 68.0% success rate over 25 real-world trials on multi-object pick-and-place tasks under partial observability. This demonstrated reliable long-horizon execution with low inference latency and stable closed-loop control despite real-world visual noise and actuation variability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces RB-VLA, a belief-centric Vision-Language-Action framework that represents a step in addressing critical limitations of existing VLA models, particularly in the context of long-horizon, multi-stage robotic manipulation under partial observability.

The primary significance of RB-VLA lies in its approach to **maintaining persistent task awareness without incurring escalating computational or memory costs.** By leveraging a compact, fixed-size recursive belief state, the system effectively tracks task progress, interaction history, and underlying dynamics. This contrasts with common VLA strategies that repeatedly re-query large VLMs or accumulate raw observations, which are often inefficient and unscalable for extended tasks. The empirical results demonstrate a substantial reduction in inference latency (up to 5x faster than baselines) and constant memory usage, which are crucial for real-time deployment and long-duration autonomous operation of robotic systems.

RB-VLA's ability to **decouple semantic grounding from low-level control** is a notable architectural contribution. By querying the VLM only once per task for high-level intent, the framework capitalizes on the semantic understanding capabilities of VLMs while offloading the high-frequency, dynamics-aware control to a more efficient, belief-conditioned diffusion policy. This hierarchical approach enhances robustness by allowing the agent to infer task progression implicitly through the belief state's evolution, even when visual evidence is ambiguous or occluded. The demonstrated success rates on multi-stage tasks under partial observability, significantly outperforming prior VLAs, highlight this enhanced robustness.

The learned action-conditioned belief state represents an advancement in **internal state representation for embodied agents.** Unlike reactive policies that rely solely on immediate observations, the belief provides a causally grounded, anticipatory state sufficient for predicting future observations and guiding action selection without computationally intensive forward simulations. The analysis of belief space and stochasticity further suggests that this representation effectively filters perceptual noise, preserves control-relevant structure, and encodes multi-modal future possibilities.

The **successful sim-to-real transfer** of RB-VLA to a physical UR5 manipulator, with fine-tuning, indicates the potential for practical deployment. This suggests that the learned belief captures fundamental control-relevant dynamics that are generalizable enough to cope with real-world sensor noise and unmodeled dynamics. This capability is vital for bridging the gap between simulated environments and real-world robotic applications.

In summary, RB-VLA offers a **scalable, efficient, and robust solution** for long-horizon robotic manipulation, addressing key challenges in partial observability, computational expense, and persistent state tracking. Its contributions could impact the development of more capable and autonomous robotic systems that can operate reliably in complex, real-world environments over extended periods. Future work extending this framework to dynamic environments and integrating reinforcement learning could further amplify its utility and influence in the field of embodied AI and robotics.
