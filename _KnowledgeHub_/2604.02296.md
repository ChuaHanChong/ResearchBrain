# VOID: Video Object and Interaction Deletion

> [!summary] Summary
> The VOID framework enables video object removal that generates physically plausible counterfactual scenarios by modeling how a scene would dynamically evolve in the absence of a target object and its interactions. It achieved 64.8% human preference and consistently high VLM-as-a-judge scores, particularly for interaction and physics realism, surpassing existing baselines.

## Problem

- Existing video object removal methods primarily focus on inpainting visual gaps and correcting appearance-level artifacts, failing to account for dynamic physical consequences.
- Current models produce physically implausible or inconsistent results when removed objects play dynamic roles (e.g., collisions, support), due to a lack of causal reasoning.
- Ambiguities in prior conditioning mechanisms (e.g., trimasks) hinder precise understanding of affected regions and complex object overlaps in dynamic scenes.

## Method

- **VOID** introduced a novel ==counterfactual video dataset==, generated from Kubric (rigid-body dynamics) and HUMOTO (articulated interactions), to train models on dynamic scene re-simulation.
- The framework developed an ==interaction-aware quadmask== conditioning mechanism that precisely defines removed, affected, overlapping, and unaffected regions, resolving prior ambiguities.
- A ==two-pass generation strategy== was implemented, combining initial counterfactual trajectory synthesis with an optional ==flow-warped noise stabilization== pass to mitigate structural deformation in newly synthesized motions.

## Results

- **VOID** achieved superior human preference, being selected as the best option in **64.8%** of cases, significantly outperforming competitors like Runway (**18.4%**) and Generative Omnimatte (**11.2%**).
- The model consistently scored highest across VLM-as-a-judge evaluations, with the most notable improvements observed in the "Interaction & Physics" criterion, affirming its capability in causal scene updates.
- **VOID** demonstrated strong generalization to unforeseen physical effects (e.g., balloons floating, blenders not activating) suggesting it learned intuitive physics principles beyond its explicit training data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training on diverse datasets, including both rigid-body and articulated interactions, is crucial for a model to learn generalized intuitive physics for dynamic object removal.
> - Precise, interaction-aware conditioning signals like the quadmask are critical for guiding diffusion models to generate accurate and physically plausible counterfactuals.
> - VLMs can effectively bridge the gap between user intent and pixel-level conditioning, translating high-level causal reasoning into concrete guidance for complex video generation tasks.
