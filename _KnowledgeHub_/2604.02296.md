# VOID: Video Object and Interaction Deletion

> [!summary] Summary
> The VOID framework enables video object removal that generates physically plausible counterfactual scenarios by modeling how a scene would dynamically evolve in the absence of a target object and its interactions. It achieved 64.8% human preference and consistently high VLM-as-a-judge scores, particularly for interaction and physics realism, surpassing existing baselines.

## Problem

- Existing video object removal methods primarily focus on inpainting visual gaps and correcting appearance-level artifacts, failing to account for dynamic physical consequences.
- Current models produce physically implausible or inconsistent results when removed objects play dynamic roles (e.g., collisions, support), due to a lack of causal reasoning.
- Ambiguities in prior conditioning mechanisms (e.g., trimasks) hinder precise understanding of affected regions and complex object overlaps in dynamic scenes.

## Method

- **VOID** introduced a novel ==counterfactual video dataset==, generated from Kubric (rigid-body dynamics) and HUMOTO (articulated interactions), to train models on dynamic scene re-simulation.
- The framework developed an ==interaction-aware quadmask== conditioning mechanism that precisely defines removed, affected, overlapping, and unaffected regions, resolving prior ambiguities.
- A ==two-pass generation strategy== was implemented, combining initial counterfactual trajectory synthesis with an optional ==flow-warped noise stabilization== pass to mitigate structural deformation in newly synthesized motions.

## Results

- **VOID** achieved superior human preference, being selected as the best option in **64.8%** of cases, significantly outperforming competitors like Runway (**18.4%**) and Generative Omnimatte (**11.2%**).
- The model consistently scored highest across VLM-as-a-judge evaluations, with the most notable improvements observed in the "Interaction & Physics" criterion, affirming its capability in causal scene updates.
- **VOID** demonstrated strong generalization to unforeseen physical effects (e.g., balloons floating, blenders not activating) suggesting it learned intuitive physics principles beyond its explicit training data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training on diverse datasets, including both rigid-body and articulated interactions, is crucial for a model to learn generalized intuitive physics for dynamic object removal.
> - Precise, interaction-aware conditioning signals like the quadmask are critical for guiding diffusion models to generate accurate and physically plausible counterfactuals.
> - VLMs can effectively bridge the gap between user intent and pixel-level conditioning, translating high-level causal reasoning into concrete guidance for complex video generation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of video editing and generation has seen advancements, particularly with the emergence of diffusion and flow-matching methods. Existing video object removal techniques primarily focus on "inpainting" content behind the removed object and correcting appearance-level artifacts, such as shadows and reflections. Examples include methods for video decomposition (e.g., Omnimatte, OmnimatteRF, Generative Omnimatte) and video inpainting (e.g., Propainter, DiffuEraser, AVID, ROSE, Object-Wiper). These methods excel at disentangling object effects like shadows or reflections, but they generally treat the background as static or only address photometric alterations.

However, a significant limitation arises when the removed object plays a dynamic role, such as causing collisions, supporting other objects, or initiating a chain of events. In such scenarios, current models often fail to account for the physical consequences of the object's removal, leading to physically implausible or inconsistent results. For instance, if a model removes middle domino tiles from a falling chain, it might incorrectly depict the subsequent tiles still falling, ignoring the physical dependency. Similarly, text-guided video editing models, while versatile, frequently lack the inherent physical intuition required to accurately predict and synthesize counterfactual outcomes in dynamic scenes. These models learn from unstructured data, which can lead to pixel-perfect but physically unsound generations when complex reasoning is required.

The work presented in "VOID" aims to address this gap by extending video object removal to scenarios demanding "high-level causal reasoning" and "physically-plausible inpainting." Instead of merely removing an object and filling the void, VOID endeavors to model how the scene *would have evolved* had the object never been present, thereby altering downstream physical interactions and ensuring dynamic consistency. This positions VOID as a development that seeks to bridge the gap between visual fidelity in video editing and the simulation of intuitive physics, moving beyond purely reconstructive or appearance-based approaches to a more sophisticated form of world modeling within video generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a video object removal framework, named VOID, capable of performing physically-plausible inpainting in complex scenarios where the removed object has significant physical interactions with other elements in the scene. This goes beyond merely filling in the visual space left by a removed object and instead focuses on generating a *counterfactual video* that reflects how the scene would have dynamically unfolded in the absence of the target object and its interactions.

The motivation behind this objective stems from several limitations observed in existing video editing and generation technologies:

- **Failure of Current Methods in Dynamic Scenarios:** Current video object removal techniques are adept at handling photometric effects (like shadows and reflections) and background inpainting. However, they frequently fail when the removed object is involved in more profound physical interactions, such as collisions, support structures, or causal chains (e.g., a domino effect). These failures result in visually jarring and physically implausible outcomes, undermining the realism of the edited video.
- **Need for High-Level Causal Reasoning:** The task of removing an object with significant interactions necessitates a model that can perform high-level causal reasoning. It requires the model to "imagine" a counterfactual scenario – "What would happen if this object was removed?" – and then synthesize a realistic video based on that causal understanding. This is a departure from relying solely on low-level visual features or local pixel-filling.
- **Ambiguities in Existing Conditioning Mechanisms:** Prior works, such as Generative Omnimatte, utilize trimasks to guide object removal, distinguishing between the object, affected areas, and unaffected areas. However, these trimasks can present ambiguities, particularly when the 'affected' region is broadly defined or when the object to be removed overlaps with the area where dynamic effects occur. Such ambiguities can hinder the model's ability to precisely understand and execute the desired counterfactual changes.
- **Enhancing Realism in Video Editing and Visual Effects:** The ability to realistically remove objects and their dynamic consequences has substantial implications for film visual effects, content creation, and making advanced video editing tools accessible to non-experts. Producing edited videos that maintain physical plausibility is crucial for believability and high-quality production.
- **Advancing World Modeling in Generative AI:** By tackling complex physical interactions, this research aims to contribute to the broader goal of making video editing models better "simulators of the world." This involves endowing generative models with a more robust understanding of physics and causality, which is a fundamental challenge in artificial intelligence.

In summary, the authors are motivated to move beyond purely visual synthesis in video editing towards models that can infer and apply intuitive physics and causal relationships, generating more coherent and plausible counterfactual realities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VOID is designed as a video object removal framework that integrates data construction, a specialized training strategy, and an inference-time optimization using vision-language models (VLMs). The framework builds upon a diffusion transformer backbone and aims to elicit high-level causal reasoning for physically-plausible counterfactual video generation.

#### 3.1. Counterfactual Dataset Supervision

To address the lack of training data for dynamic counterfactual object removals, the authors generated a new paired dataset:
- **Rigid-body dynamics (Kubric):** Approximately 1900 video pairs were created using the Kubric simulation and rendering engine. This dataset focuses on simulating interactions like collisions, falling, and structural dependencies among diverse objects. For each video (V) with an object (O), a counterfactual video ($\hat{V}$) is generated by re-simulating the scene with O removed, while keeping initial conditions for other objects constant. This process yields physically consistent alternative interactions. Camera trajectories and focal zoom are randomized during rendering to aid disentanglement.
- **Articulated interactions (HUMOTO):** Approximately 4500 video pairs were generated using HUMOTO, a 4D motion-capture dataset of human-object interactions. Here, the human is designated as object O. V and $\hat{V}$ are created by simulating and rendering the scene with and without the human, respectively. This teaches the model about object removal in scenarios involving dynamic manipulations. Textures of objects, backgrounds, and humans are randomized.

#### 3.2. Interaction-Aware Quadmask Conditioning

This work extends the "trimask" concept used in prior research (e.g., Generative Omnimatte) to a "quadmask" ($M_q$) to provide more precise guidance. The quadmask addresses two ambiguities of the trimask:
- **Refined "Affected Region":** Instead of highlighting almost the entire scene as potentially affected, the quadmask focuses the "light gray" region more closely on where effects actually take place. During data generation, rendering engines precisely determine these regions, which are then gridified for inference.
- **Overlap Resolution:** A new "dark gray" region is introduced to denote pixels where the object to be removed (black) overlaps with other parts of the scene that are affected (light gray). This resolves ambiguity when, for example, a ball is about to be caught by a person being removed; the dark gray indicates the area where the person's body and the ball's new trajectory intersect.

The four regions of the quadmask are:
- **Black:** Object(s) to be removed.
- **Light Gray:** Regions affected by the removal, but not overlapping with the removed object.
- **Dark Gray:** Regions where the removed object overlaps with affected areas.
- **White:** Unaffected regions that should remain unchanged.

#### 3.3. Backbone Initialization and Counterfactual Generation

VOID is built upon the CogVideoX diffusion transformer backbone, initialized with weights from Generative Omnimatte. This initialization provides a prior for layered object-effect disentanglement. The model is then finetuned using the newly created counterfactual video pairs and the quadmask conditioning. This process aims to transition the model from layered removal to dynamic counterfactual rewriting, enabling it to synthesize physically plausible trajectories.

#### 3.4. Two-Pass Generation Strategy

The framework employs a two-pass generation strategy:

- **Pass 1: Counterfactual Trajectory Synthesis ($\hat{V}_{p1}$):**
 - In the first pass, VOID generates an initial counterfactual video prediction using Gaussian diffusion noise (z), the input video (V), and the quadmask ($M_q$).
 - This pass primarily focuses on capturing broadly correct hypotheses regarding motion, such as objects entering free-fall or continuing motion after an obstruction is removed.
 - However, objects undergoing newly synthesized motion may exhibit structural deformation (e.g., bending, stretching, or structural drift), a common artifact in lightweight video diffusion models when generating complex motion without strong motion guidance.

- **Pass 2: Flow-Warped Noise Stabilization ($\hat{V}$):**
 - To mitigate structural deformation, a second, optional pass is employed, inspired by the "Go-with-the-Flow" technique.
 - Optical flow is computed from the first-pass output ($\hat{V}_{p1}$) to derive "warped noise" ($z_{warp}$).
 - This flow-aligned noise is then used as input to a second pass of a warped-noise variant of VOID (VOID$_{warp}$). This encourages the diffusion model to denoise consistently along the predicted motion trajectories, thereby better preserving object rigidity.
 - **Conditional Triggering:** This second pass is only triggered when a VLM determines that the object removal is predicted to cause "substantial dynamic reconfiguration" and significant object motion (e.g., free-fall or trajectory change).

#### 3.5. VLM-Guided Quadmask Generation at Inference Time

For real-world inference where ground-truth quadmasks are unavailable, a VLM-based pipeline generates $M_q$ from user-provided binary object masks ($M_o$):
1. **Affected Object Identification:** The input video (V) and binary mask ($M_o$) are fed to a VLM (e.g., Gemini 3 Pro). The VLM generates a list of descriptions of objects it predicts will be affected by the removal of object O.
2. **Original Affected Mask Generation:** Segment Anything 3 (SAM 3) is used to generate a mask ($M_{orig\_a}$) covering all objects identified by the VLM in their *original positions*.
3. **Counterfactual Position Prediction:** The VLM is further used to predict the *counterfactual positions* of these affected objects. This is done by overlaying a coarse spatial grid on the video and asking the VLM to identify cells in each frame that may contain effects, resulting in a block-structured mask ($M_{count\_a}$).
4. **Combined Affected Mask:** The final affected area mask ($M_a$) is computed as the union of $M_{orig\_a}$ and $M_{count\_a}$ ($M_a := M_{orig\_a} \lor M_{count\_a}$).
5. **Final Quadmask Construction:** The quadmask ($M_q$) is then computed:
 - Black for pixels only in $M_o$.
 - Dark Gray for pixels in both $M_o$ and $M_a$.
 - Light Gray for pixels only in $M_a$.
 - White for all other pixels.

This VLM-guided approach leverages the VLM's world knowledge to expand a simple object mask into richer, pixel-space guidance for the diffusion model, enabling robust inference on unseen scenarios.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated VOID against various baselines (traditional inpainting, effect removal inpainting, and text-guided video editing models) on both synthetic and real-world datasets, employing human preference studies, VLM-as-a-judge evaluations, qualitative comparisons, and quantitative metrics.

#### 4.1. Real-World Counterfactual Comparisons

- **Human Preference Study:** A user study involving 25 participants evaluating 125 scenarios demonstrated VOID's superior performance. VOID was selected as the best option in 64.8% of cases, significantly outperforming competitors like Runway (18.4%) and Generative Omnimatte (11.2%). Traditional inpainting models received negligible selections, indicating their inability to perform interaction-aware synthesis. Participants judged based on visual quality, temporal consistency, blending, realism of scene evolution, and absence of artifacts.
- **VLM-as-a-judge Evaluation:** Three different VLMs (Gemini 3 Pro, GPT-5.2, and Qwen 3.5-32B) were used as automated judges across six fine-grained criteria: "Interaction & Physics," "Object Removal," "Background & Artifacts," "Temporal Consistency," "Preservation," and "Sharpness." VOID consistently achieved the highest total scores across all three judges. The most significant and consistent improvements for VOID were observed in the "Interaction & Physics" criterion, directly affirming its capability in causal scene updates. The VLM rankings largely aligned with the human preference study, with VOID ranking first, followed by Runway and Generative Omnimatte.
- **Qualitative Comparisons:** Visual examples illustrated VOID's ability to maintain object structure and produce plausible motion over time. Baselines frequently exhibited failures such as object deformation (e.g., a kettlebell indenting a pillow even after removal), incomplete object removal, or generation of physically implausible outcomes (e.g., paint appearing without a roller). VOID demonstrated high generalization and artifact-free, physically plausible results.
- **Generalizations to Unseen Effects:** VOID exhibited capabilities to extrapolate to novel physical interactions not present in its explicit training data. Examples included a balloon floating upwards after its holder was removed (despite no floating objects in training), a blender not activating when the person pressing it disappeared (no blenders or electrical devices in finetuning data), and accurate disentanglement of complex interactions like Jenga towers or bowling balls. This suggests VOID leverages the intuitive physics reasoning of the underlying VLM and video diffusion model in a general manner.

#### 4.2. Synthetic Dataset Comparisons

- **Benchmarking:** Evaluations were conducted on a synthetic benchmark comprising 10 videos for object/shadow/reflection removal (from prior work) and 30 new dynamic counterfactual cases from Kubric and HUMOTO.
- **Metrics:** VOID achieved the strongest performance across most quantitative metrics, including PSNR, DreamSim, DINOv2, and FVD. The largest performance margins over baselines were observed in FVD (Fréchet Video Distance) and the VLM-judge metrics, both comprehensive video-level assessments. VOID's LPIPS (Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity) score was comparable but not always superior, which the authors attributed to LPIPS's sensitivity to local translations and potential penalties for accurate counterfactual effects generated in slightly different spatial regions.
- **Fidelity to Ground Truth:** VLM-judge evaluations on the synthetic dataset, where ground-truth counterfactuals were available, confirmed VOID's ability to produce physically plausible and semantically coherent videos that align closely with the true counterfactual outcomes.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Data Composition:** Ablations demonstrated that training on a mix of Kubric and HUMOTO datasets yielded better performance than training on either dataset alone, even with constant total sample size (1200 videos). This indicates the benefit of data diversity in capturing various interaction types.
- **Masking Strategy:** An ablation using less detailed "trimasks" (similar to Generative Omnimatte, without VLM-guided generation) significantly degraded performance across all evaluation categories. This confirmed the critical importance of VOID's detailed quadmasks and its VLM-guided mask generation pipeline for effective guidance.
- **Second-Pass Refinement:** For samples requiring substantial dynamic reconfiguration (as flagged by the VLM), the optional second pass significantly improved "Interaction Physics" and "Object Removal Quality" scores, leading to higher overall scores compared to Pass 1 results alone. This validated the effectiveness of flow-warped noise stabilization in preserving object rigidity during complex new motions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VOID framework represents a notable advancement in the domain of video object removal and editing, addressing a critical limitation of existing methods: the inability to generate physically plausible counterfactual scenarios when removed objects are involved in complex dynamic interactions.

The key significance and potential impact of this work include:

- **Enhanced Realism in Video Editing:** By enabling models to reason about and synthesize the physical consequences of object removal, VOID can produce significantly more realistic and believable edited videos. This is particularly valuable for professional applications in film visual effects, content creation, and post-production, where maintaining physical consistency is paramount.
- **Advancement in Causal Reasoning for Generative Models:** The framework pushes video generation beyond purely visual inpainting to incorporate high-level causal reasoning and intuitive physics. This contributes to the broader field of AI by demonstrating a method for endowing generative models with a deeper understanding of world dynamics, moving closer to models that can act as "simulators of the world."
- **Novel Data Creation Methodology:** The introduction of new paired datasets derived from Kubric and HUMOTO simulations for counterfactual object removal fills a significant gap in training data availability. This methodology for generating physics-informed training data can inspire future work in creating more robust datasets for complex video understanding and generation tasks.
- **Innovation in Conditioning Mechanisms:** The development of the "quadmask" and the VLM-guided mask generation pipeline provides a more precise and intelligent way to guide video diffusion models. This approach leverages the world knowledge embedded in VLMs to interpret user intent and generate contextually rich conditioning signals, which can be adapted for other complex video editing tasks.
- **Improved Temporal Coherence and Object Consistency:** The two-pass generation strategy, particularly the second pass with flow-warped noise stabilization, addresses the challenge of maintaining object rigidity and temporal coherence when generating novel object motions. This improves the visual quality and believability of dynamic elements in edited videos.
- **Generalization Capabilities:** The demonstrated ability of VOID to extrapolate to unforeseen physical interactions (e.g., balloons floating, blenders not turning on) suggests that the model is learning generalized principles of physics rather than merely memorizing training data. This indicates a promising direction for developing more adaptable and robust video generation models.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper identifies limitations such as domain gaps, video length, and resolution. These points serve as clear directions for future research, including the exploration of even better training datasets beyond rendering engines and advancements in model architectures for longer and higher-resolution video generation.

In conclusion, VOID represents a step towards making video editing models more intelligent and capable of simulating complex physical realities, thus paving the way for more sophisticated and intuitive content creation tools.
