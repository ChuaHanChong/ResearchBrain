# Wiki-R1: Incentivizing Multimodal Reasoning for Knowledge-based VQA via Data and Sampling Curriculum

> [!summary] Summary
> WIKI-R1 introduces a curriculum reinforcement learning framework to enhance multimodal reasoning in large multimodal models for Knowledge-Based Visual Question Answering by systematically managing training data distribution and sampling. The framework achieved new state-of-the-art overall accuracies of 37.1% on Encyclopedic VQA and 44.1% on InfoSeek, demonstrating robust performance with reduced data requirements.

## Problem

- Knowledge-Based Visual Question Answering (KB-VQA) tasks require integrating visual and external encyclopedic knowledge, creating a distributional gap from MLLM pretraining data.
- Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) in KB-VQA often suffers from noisy retrieval, forcing models to reason over imperfect or irrelevant information.
- Reinforcement learning (RL) for KB-VQA experiences severe sparse reward problems and low training efficiency due to this distributional gap and retrieval noise, leading to many unproductive training samples.

## Method

- WIKI-R1, a ==curriculum== reinforcement learning framework, generates training distributions of progressively increasing difficulty by dynamically modifying the retrieval system based on model accuracy.
- Controllable curriculum data generation manipulates the number of retrieved candidates and ground-truth inclusion to vary the 'gap level' from easy (ground-truth only) to hard (inference-time distribution).
- Curriculum sampling with observation propagation addresses sparse rewards by prioritizing challenging but solvable samples and using label propagation to estimate the difficulty and expected rewards of unobserved samples.

## Results

- WIKI-R1 achieved new state-of-the-art overall accuracies of **37.1%** on Encyclopedic VQA and **44.1%** on InfoSeek, surpassing previous baselines by **1.6%** and **4.0%** respectively.
- The framework demonstrated strong generalization, obtaining a zero-shot F1 score of 55.6 on ViQuAE, outperforming existing MLLM baselines and even semi-oracle configurations.
- Ablation studies confirmed that curriculum data generation and observation-propagated sampling significantly improved training efficiency and stability, enabling high performance with substantially fewer training samples (40k total) compared to baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Systematically controlling data difficulty by manipulating the retrieval system is effective for bridging the distributional gap and improving MLLM adaptation in KB-VQA.
> - Observation propagation, which disseminates reward information across similar samples, can significantly mitigate the sparse reward problem in RL for complex tasks like KB-VQA.
> - Gradually increasing training data difficulty based on model performance, combined with intelligent sampling, leads to more stable and efficient RL optimization for multimodal reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Knowledge-Based Visual Question Answering (KB-VQA) represents a challenging domain within multimodal AI research, requiring models to integrate visual information from an image with external, often encyclopedic, knowledge to answer questions. This task differs significantly from standard VQA by demanding reasoning beyond what is visually present in an image, relying instead on a vast, structured knowledge base. The integration of external knowledge often occurs within a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) framework, where a retriever first fetches relevant information, and a generator then synthesizes an answer.

A central challenge in KB-VQA stems from the characteristics of the external knowledge base, which typically contains structured, encyclopedic information. This domain often presents a significant distributional gap from the data used to pretrain large multimodal language models (MLLMs), making effective reasoning and domain adaptation difficult in post-training stages. Furthermore, retrieval systems are inherently prone to noise, meaning models frequently need to reason over imperfect or irrelevant evidence.

Prior research in KB-VQA has primarily followed two directions:
1. **Improving Retrieval Quality**: Efforts have focused on enhancing the accuracy and relevance of retrieved knowledge passages to provide better context for answer generation. However, due to the scale and complexity of knowledge bases, retrieval noise often remains an unavoidable factor.
2. **Enhancing Reasoning for Noisy Retrieval**: This line of work aims to make models more robust to imperfect retrieval, enabling them to comprehend encyclopedic passages, extract relevant information, and filter out noise. Early methods in this category largely relied on supervised fine-tuning (SFT), while more recent advancements have explored reinforcement learning (RL) techniques, such as those building on GRPO, for general RAG settings.

Despite these advances, the application and effectiveness of RL-based approaches in tasks demanding both multimodal reasoning and cross-domain adaptation, specifically KB-VQA, have remained less explored. Preliminary investigations, as cited by the authors using the DAPO algorithm, revealed a severe sparse reward problem and low training efficiency in KB-VQA, largely attributed to the distributional gap and retrieval noise. This work positions itself by addressing these specific limitations, aiming to develop a systematic RL framework that can effectively incentivize multimodal reasoning in MLLMs for KB-VQA by managing data distribution and sampling strategies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the reasoning capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for Knowledge-Based Visual Question Answering (KB-VQA) by addressing core challenges associated with the task, particularly the sparse reward problem and the distributional gap from MLLM pretraining.

The motivation for this work stems from several observed difficulties in applying existing methods, particularly reinforcement learning (RL), to KB-VQA:
1. **Nature of KB-VQA Task**: The task requires integrating information from an image with external, often structured and encyclopedic, knowledge. This inherently demands robust reasoning to synthesize answers from diverse and complex data sources.
2. **Noisy Retrieval**: Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) frameworks, commonly used for KB-VQA, often suffer from noisy retrieval. The retriever may fetch irrelevant or partially relevant information, requiring the MLLM to discern and utilize only the pertinent facts, while filtering out noise.
3. **Distributional Gap**: The structured and encyclopedic nature of knowledge bases for KB-VQA creates a significant distributional shift compared to the broad, general-purpose data MLLMs are exposed to during their pretraining phase. This gap hinders effective domain adaptation and robust reasoning in the post-training stage.
4. **Sparse Reward Problem in RL**: Initial experiments conducted by the authors using a popular RL algorithm, DAPO (Yu et al., 2025), on KB-VQA revealed substantial challenges. Specifically, over 80% of training samples yielded "zero advantages," meaning they provided no meaningful gradient signal for learning. This led to low overall training accuracy (around 10%). This sparse reward signal severely impedes stable and effective RL optimization.
5. **Impact of Retrieval Noise on RL**: Further investigation using ground-truth retrieval, which minimizes retrieval noise, showed an alleviation of both the zero-gradient prevalence and low training accuracy. This observation directly implicated retrieval noise as a significant contributing factor to the sparse reward problem and inefficient RL training in KB-VQA.

To address these challenges, the authors propose **Wiki-R1**, a data-generation-based curriculum reinforcement learning framework. The key objectives of Wiki-R1 are:
- **Systematically Incentivize Reasoning**: Develop a mechanism to foster the reasoning ability of MLLMs in the KB-VQA context.
- **Bridge the Distributional Gap**: Construct a sequence of training distributions that gradually align with the MLLM’s evolving capabilities, progressively narrowing the gap between its pretraining data and the target KB-VQA domain.
- **Mitigate Sparse Rewards**: Improve the efficiency and stability of RL optimization by ensuring more informative training signals. This involves generating data at appropriate difficulty levels and selectively sampling instances likely to yield non-zero advantages.

This approach aims to guide the model through progressively challenging examples, ensuring that it learns effectively from meaningful signals, thereby enabling more robust multimodal reasoning for KB-VQA.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Wiki-R1 is presented as a data-generation-based curriculum reinforcement learning framework. It operates by constructing a sequence of training distributions designed to align with the MLLM's evolving capabilities, thereby bridging the gap between pretraining and the KB-VQA target distribution. The framework comprises two main, interconnected components: controllable curriculum data generation and curriculum sampling with observation propagation.

#### 3.1. Task Definition

The task of KB-VQA is defined as generating an answer `y` to a textual query `q` given an image `I_q`, by reasoning over the input and relevant knowledge retrieved from an external knowledge base `B`. The knowledge base `B` is a collection of textual articles `P_i` and corresponding visual content `I_i` for various entities. A retriever, with a non-parametric, rule-based modification function `ϕ`, selects a subset `S_ϕ` of relevant multimodal documents from `B`. The MLLM then models the conditional distribution `p_θ(y | I_q, q, S_ϕ)`.

#### 3.2. Training Objective

The training objective is to adapt a pretrained MLLM to the KB-VQA task using reinforcement learning. The model is optimized to maximize the expected reward over KB-VQA data. The policy gradient for the model `π_θ` under a sampling schedule `μ` and retrieval modification function `ϕ` is given by:
`∇_θ J(π_θ, μ, ϕ) = E_(q,I_q,y)∼μ [ E_ŷ∼π_θ(·|q,I_q,S_ϕ) [ ∇_θ log π_θ(ŷ | q, I_q, S_ϕ) r(ŷ, y) ] ]`
where `ŷ` is the sampled answer and `y` is the ground-truth answer. The framework explicitly integrates curriculum-aware sampling (`μ`) and controllable retrieval modifications (`ϕ`) to shape the learning signal and training distribution, addressing the sparse reward problem and distributional gap.

#### 3.3. Curriculum Data Generation

This component manipulates the retrieval system to generate training samples of progressively increasing difficulty.
- **Controllable Data Generation**: The core idea is that retrieving more candidates increases the likelihood of including useful information but also introduces more noise. A discrete "gap level" `g ∈ {0, 1. . . , G}` is defined, representing the degree of distribution shift from the generated samples to the true KB-VQA target distribution. For each `g`, a retrieval modification function `ϕ_g(k, γ)` is instantiated:
 - **Easiest level (g = 0)**: `k = 1`, `γ = 1`. Only the ground-truth snippet is retrieved, resembling the pretraining distribution.
 - **Intermediate levels (1 < g < G)**: `k = g`, `γ = 1`. Noisy candidates are introduced alongside the ground truth.
 - **Hardest level (g = G)**: `γ = 0`, `k = G − 1`. The retrieval system no longer guarantees ground-truth inclusion, fully aligning with the inference-time distribution.
- **Gap-Level Schedule**: The gap level `g` is dynamically adjusted based on the model’s observed training accuracy. A sliding window `w` of recent samples is used to compute average training accuracy. When this moving average exceeds an upgrade threshold `τ`, the gap level `g` is incremented, and the window is reset. This ensures that the model progresses to harder distributions only after mastering the current level.

#### 3.4. Curriculum Sampling with Observation Propagation

This component addresses the challenge that generated data might not always match the intended difficulty and that observed rewards are sparse.
- **Sampling Schedule**: During training, data is sampled using a Gaussian distribution centered at a historical mean training accuracy of 0.5. This aims to prioritize samples that are challenging yet solvable, maximizing learning efficiency.
- **Difficulty Estimation via Observation Propagation**:
 - To overcome sparse reward signals, an observation propagation mechanism is introduced. It estimates the difficulty of *unobserved* samples by propagating reward information from *observed* samples.
 - A label propagation graph `K` is constructed where VQA samples are nodes. Edge weights between samples reflect the similarity of their associated knowledge base articles. The paper mentions using TF-IDF for this purpose, with Sentence Transformer as an alternative.
 - Label propagation is applied to disseminate observed accuracies (rewards) from training samples to unobserved ones, yielding approximate sample-wise expected accuracies that guide curriculum sampling.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

- **Training Data**: A balanced training set of 40k examples (20k from Encyclopedic VQA and 20k from InfoSeek), sampled to ensure proportional entity representation. This is noted as significantly smaller than baselines.
- **RL Framework**: The VERL framework is used, with DAPO as the underlying RL algorithm.
- **Reward Function**: A binary signal (1 for exact match, 0 otherwise).
- **Hyperparameters**: Learning rate of 1e-6, 4 rollouts per sample.
- **Base MLLMs**: Qwen2.5-VL models (3B and 7B variants).
- **Curriculum Parameters**: Window size `w=300`, gap threshold `τ=0.55`, maximum gap `G=6`.
- **Computational Resources**: 9 hours (3B) / 12 hours (7B) on 4 A100 GPUs.
- **Retrieval System**: Combines EVA-CLIP 8B (visual similarity) and ColBERT V2 (textual relevance from 256-chunk article passages). Scores are fused using a weighted sum (`λ`) tuned on training sets (e.g., `λ=0.985` for EVQA, `λ=0.997` for InfoSeek).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of Wiki-R1 was conducted on two KB-VQA benchmarks, Encyclopedic VQA (EVQA) and InfoSeek, along with a generalization study on ViQuAE. Ablation studies investigated the contribution of each component.

#### 4.1. Performance Comparison with State-of-the-Art

(Table 1 in paper)
- **Overall Performance**: Wiki-R1 achieved new state-of-the-art results on both primary benchmarks.
 - On **Encyclopedic VQA (EVQA)**, Wiki-R1 (7B) reached an overall accuracy of 37.1%, surpassing the previous best of 35.5%. The 3B variant also achieved 35.9% overall. Notably, on the "Single-hop" split of EVQA, the 7B variant achieved 41.0%, outperforming the previous best (ReflectiVA with visual retrieval) of 35.5%.
 - On **InfoSeek**, Wiki-R1 (7B) attained an overall accuracy of 44.1%, improving upon the prior state-of-the-art of 40.1% (ReflectiVA with textual retrieval). On the challenging "Unseen-Question" split of InfoSeek, the 7B model achieved 47.8%, indicating generalization capability to novel queries.
- **Robustness Across Retrieval Modes**: The framework demonstrated robustness across benchmarks using a single visual+textual retrieval system, contrasting with methods like ReflectiVA and EchoSight, which showed significant performance sensitivity to the choice of retrieval mode (e.g., ReflectiVA's performance difference on EVQA vs. InfoSeek based on retrieval mode).

#### 4.2. Inference with Oracle Documents

(Table 3 in paper)
- In an oracle setting where the ground-truth Wikipedia entity was provided (eliminating entity-level retrieval noise), Wiki-R1 demonstrated strong performance. The 7B variant achieved 69.2% on EVQA (Single-hop) and 68.2% on InfoSeek (Overall), indicating its ability to effectively leverage correct retrieval results when available and reason within a relevant article even with passage-level noise.

#### 4.3. Generalization to ViQuAE

(Table 2 in paper)
- For zero-shot transfer evaluation on the ViQuAE benchmark, Wiki-R1 (7B) achieved 55.6 F1 and 50.3 Exact Match scores. This significantly outperformed existing MLLM baselines like ReflectiVA and even surpassed the RC semi-oracle configuration, suggesting strong cross-dataset generalization to unseen knowledge sources and question distributions.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

(Table 4 in paper)
- **Effectiveness of Curriculum Data Generation**: Incorporating the data curriculum strategy into DAPO significantly improved performance over vanilla DAPO, particularly on EVQA, highlighting its role in handling noisy retrieval. Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) alone showed limited gains.
- **Effectiveness of Curriculum Sampling**: Directly applying curriculum sampling without observation propagation led to degraded performance. This was attributed to observation sparsity. However, when combined with observation propagation, curriculum sampling became effective, improving both training efficiency and overall performance.
- **Efficiency of Observation Propagation**: Observation propagation significantly reduced the number of ignored trajectories (samples yielding zero advantage) during RL training, thereby enhancing optimization efficiency (Figure 3, Left). This also contributed to improved overall training effectiveness.
- **Training Dynamics**: Wiki-R1 exhibited stable performance improvements across training iterations, with its best performance achieved when the curriculum reached the highest difficulty level. In contrast, vanilla DAPO showed rapid early improvement but then degraded on the EVQA benchmark, possibly due to overfitting on the easier InfoSeek dataset (Figure 3, Right).
- **Similarity Measures for Label Propagation**: The framework demonstrated generalization across different similarity metrics (TF-IDF vs. Sentence Transformer) for label propagation, with semantic embeddings providing a slight benefit (Table 5).

#### 4.5. Training Efficiency

(Table 7 and 8 in paper)
- Wiki-R1 achieved its superior performance using substantially fewer training samples (40k total) compared to baseline methods that utilized hundreds of thousands or millions of samples (Table 7). The training costs were competitive or lower than estimated baselines (Table 8), indicating resource efficiency.

#### 4.6. Experimental Stability

(Figure 5 in paper)
- The authors conducted experiments three times with independent runs. While convergence speed varied slightly, the final performance levels were consistently high, indicating the stability and reliability of the reported results.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "WIKI-R1: INCENTIVIZING MULTIMODAL REASONING FOR KNOWLEDGE-BASED VQA VIA DATA AND SAMPLING CURRICULUM" offers several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of multimodal AI and knowledge-based reasoning:

- **Addressing Fundamental Challenges in KB-VQA**: The research directly confronts the core issues of sparse rewards and the distributional gap that hinder the effective application of reinforcement learning to Knowledge-Based Visual Question Answering. By providing a systematic solution, Wiki-R1 improves the viability of MLLMs for complex tasks requiring external knowledge.

- **Novel Curriculum Reinforcement Learning Framework**: Wiki-R1 introduces a principled approach to curriculum learning by integrating controllable data generation with adaptive sampling. The ability to manipulate the retriever to generate data at varying difficulty levels, combined with a sampling strategy guided by propagated reward signals, represents a methodological advance in how models adapt to challenging, knowledge-intensive domains. This moves beyond selection-based curricula to proactive data construction.

- **State-of-the-Art Performance**: The consistent achievement of new state-of-the-art results on two prominent KB-VQA benchmarks (Encyclopedic VQA and InfoSeek) validates the effectiveness of the proposed framework. The particular improvement on "Unseen-Question" splits and zero-shot transfer to ViQuAE highlights the model's enhanced generalization capabilities.

- **Improved Training Efficiency and Robustness**: Wiki-R1 demonstrates that high performance can be achieved with significantly less training data compared to prior methods. The ablation studies confirm that components like curriculum data generation, curriculum sampling, and especially observation propagation, contribute to more stable and efficient RL optimization by reducing the occurrence of zero-advantage samples and preventing premature overfitting. This efficiency is crucial for deploying large models in resource-constrained environments.

- **Insights into Domain Adaptation**: The methodology provides insights into how to effectively bridge distributional gaps between pretraining and target domains, particularly when dealing with noisy and structured external knowledge. This could inform strategies for other retrieval-augmented tasks where MLLMs struggle with domain-specific adaptation.

- **Future Research Directions**: The identified limitation regarding the "partial means of controlling the gap" suggests a clear path for future research in developing more fully controllable data generation processes, which could further refine curriculum design for KB-VQA and related tasks. The framework serves as a foundation for exploring more sophisticated methods for intelligent data synthesis and difficulty estimation.

- **Broader Applicability**: The principles of data-generation-based curriculum learning and observation propagation for sparse reward settings could be applied to other complex multimodal or language tasks that require robust reasoning, external knowledge integration, and adaptation to challenging data distributions. This includes areas like complex reasoning, fact verification, and scientific question answering.

In conclusion, Wiki-R1 advances the field by providing a robust and efficient mechanism to train MLLMs for KB-VQA, offering both superior performance and a clear direction for future research in adaptable, knowledge-driven AI systems.
