# Human2Humanoid: Physics-Aware Cross-Morphology Motion Retargeting for Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces Human2Humanoid, an unsupervised motion retargeting framework that transfers human movements to humanoid robots, effectively addressing significant morphological differences and data scarcity. The system generates physically plausible robot motions with high downstream controllability, achieving superior tracking error and success rates compared to existing optimization-based and industrial baselines on a Unitree G1 robot.

## Problem

- Existing motion retargeting methods struggle with semantic preservation under severe morphological mismatches between humans and diverse humanoid robots.
- Unpaired learning approaches often produce visually plausible but physically infeasible motions for robots, leading to issues like foot skating or unstable poses.
- Traditional data-driven methods require costly and difficult-to-acquire paired human-robot motion data, limiting scalability across different robot embodiments.

## Method

- A CycleGAN-style unsupervised learning framework is developed, using Skeleton-Aware Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) in its generators to model the hierarchical structure of skeletal data.
- A morphology-invariant end-effector consistency loss aligns end-effector trajectories by normalizing displacements relative to embodiment-specific body scales, preserving motion semantics despite drastic limb proportion differences.
- Physics-aware feasibility constraints (foot contact, foot height, and joint limits) are incorporated directly into the training to ensure generated robot motions are physically stable and executable, suppressing issues like foot skating and ground penetration.

## Results

- Human2Humanoid achieved the lowest average Tracking Error (0.12) and the highest average Success Rate (**88.5%**) on the Unitree G1, indicating superior motion trackability and task completion compared to PHC, GMR, and Unitree Retarget baselines.
- The framework demonstrated superior physical feasibility, exhibiting the lowest average Ground Penetration (0.05 cm) and competitive Foot Skating (**4.7%**) among evaluated methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical roles of the morphology-invariant end-effector consistency loss for semantic preservation and the physics-aware constraints for improving contact-related feasibility and mitigating ground penetration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Semantic preservation in cross-morphology motion transfer can be achieved by aligning morphology-invariant end-effector trajectories, rather than absolute Cartesian coordinates.
> - Integrating explicit physics-based constraints directly into an unsupervised generative model can produce motions that are both semantically faithful and practically executable on physical robots, bypassing the need for paired data.
> - Skeleton-Aware GCNs provide an effective way to handle varied skeletal topologies and degrees of freedom in a cross-domain generative model, maintaining spatial and temporal coherence.
