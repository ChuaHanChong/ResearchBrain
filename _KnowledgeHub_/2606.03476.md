# Human2Humanoid: Physics-Aware Cross-Morphology Motion Retargeting for Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces Human2Humanoid, an unsupervised motion retargeting framework that transfers human movements to humanoid robots, effectively addressing significant morphological differences and data scarcity. The system generates physically plausible robot motions with high downstream controllability, achieving superior tracking error and success rates compared to existing optimization-based and industrial baselines on a Unitree G1 robot.

## Problem

- Existing motion retargeting methods struggle with semantic preservation under severe morphological mismatches between humans and diverse humanoid robots.
- Unpaired learning approaches often produce visually plausible but physically infeasible motions for robots, leading to issues like foot skating or unstable poses.
- Traditional data-driven methods require costly and difficult-to-acquire paired human-robot motion data, limiting scalability across different robot embodiments.

## Method

- A CycleGAN-style unsupervised learning framework is developed, using Skeleton-Aware Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) in its generators to model the hierarchical structure of skeletal data.
- A morphology-invariant end-effector consistency loss aligns end-effector trajectories by normalizing displacements relative to embodiment-specific body scales, preserving motion semantics despite drastic limb proportion differences.
- Physics-aware feasibility constraints (foot contact, foot height, and joint limits) are incorporated directly into the training to ensure generated robot motions are physically stable and executable, suppressing issues like foot skating and ground penetration.

## Results

- Human2Humanoid achieved the lowest average Tracking Error (0.12) and the highest average Success Rate (**88.5%**) on the Unitree G1, indicating superior motion trackability and task completion compared to PHC, GMR, and Unitree Retarget baselines.
- The framework demonstrated superior physical feasibility, exhibiting the lowest average Ground Penetration (0.05 cm) and competitive Foot Skating (**4.7%**) among evaluated methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical roles of the morphology-invariant end-effector consistency loss for semantic preservation and the physics-aware constraints for improving contact-related feasibility and mitigating ground penetration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Semantic preservation in cross-morphology motion transfer can be achieved by aligning morphology-invariant end-effector trajectories, rather than absolute Cartesian coordinates.
> - Integrating explicit physics-based constraints directly into an unsupervised generative model can produce motions that are both semantically faithful and practically executable on physical robots, bypassing the need for paired data.
> - Skeleton-Aware GCNs provide an effective way to handle varied skeletal topologies and degrees of freedom in a cross-domain generative model, maintaining spatial and temporal coherence.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "Human2Humanoid" addresses the fundamental problem of motion retargeting in robotics, specifically for humanoid platforms. This area is critical for applications such as teleoperation, imitation learning, and human-robot interaction, where transferring human motion priors to executable robot behaviors is essential.

Historically, motion retargeting for humanoid robots has largely relied on traditional methods involving inverse kinematics and constrained optimization. These approaches, exemplified by works from Ayusawa and Yoshida, and Penco et al., enforce robot-specific feasibility constraints like joint limits, balance, collision avoidance, and contact conditions. While interpretable and capable of directly imposing physical constraints, these methods suffer from sensitivity to initial conditions, objective weights, and embodiment mismatches. They can also lead to semantically distorted or contact-inconsistent robot motions, as highlighted by Araujo et al. with their General Motion Retargeting (GMR) method.

More recently, data-driven methods have emerged to reduce the engineering burden associated with optimization-based approaches. However, a significant challenge for supervised learning methods is the requirement for paired human-robot motion data, which is costly and difficult to acquire and scale across different robot embodiments. Efforts such as S3LE have attempted self-supervised generation but still depend on paired human poses and robot configurations. Neural Motion Retargeting (NMR) has further improved feasibility by constructing physically refined supervision through expert-policy rollouts in simulation. Nevertheless, this approach often necessitates morphology-specific data construction pipelines and may produce mappings biased towards dynamically trackable motions, potentially sacrificing the full diversity of human motion. Generative models, like G-DReaM, offer an alternative by representing embodiments with graph structures and using diffusion models, improving scalability but still requiring robust semantic preservation, contact consistency, and kinematic feasibility for practical robot deployment.

The field of motion retargeting in computer graphics and animation provides another relevant context, focusing on reusing motion assets across characters with varying body shapes or skeletons. Early graphics work, like Gleicher's spacetime constraints, shares conceptual similarities with robotic optimization methods. Learning-based retargeting in animation, including Neural Kinematic Networks, PMnet, Skeleton-Aware Networks, and Pose-to-Motion, has demonstrated the feasibility of unsupervised motion transfer using structural priors and cycle-consistency objectives. Recent graphics methods, such as R2ET, ReConForM, and MoReFlow, also consider geometry, contact, and generative modeling explicitly. However, a key distinction is that animation methods primarily prioritize visual plausibility and often assume consistent topological structures or homeomorphic skeletons, which do not align with the substantial morphological and kinematic differences between humans and humanoid robots. They typically do not account for robot-specific joint limits, whole-body stability, ground clearance, or slip suppression.

Against this backdrop, "Human2Humanoid" positions itself by addressing two coupled challenges that existing methods often struggle with: semantic preservation under severe morphology mismatches and physical plausibility under unpaired learning. It seeks to bridge the gap between computer graphics techniques for unpaired motion transfer and the stringent requirements for executability on real humanoid robots. The paper aims to develop an unpaired learning framework that eliminates the need for paired supervision while ensuring both cross-morphology semantic fidelity and contact-related physical plausibility, a critical step toward more robust and scalable humanoid robot control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an unsupervised motion retargeting framework, named Human2Humanoid, capable of transferring human motions to humanoid robot behaviors with high fidelity, specifically addressing the challenges arising from substantial morphological discrepancies and the scarcity of paired human-robot motion data.

The motivation for this work stems from two main, tightly coupled challenges inherent in human-to-humanoid motion retargeting:

1. **Semantic Preservation under Severe Morphology Mismatches:** Humans and humanoid robots often possess significant differences in skeletal topology, limb proportions, and degrees of freedom (DoFs). Directly translating human poses to robot configurations through simple geometric alignment can lead to semantic distortion. For instance, a human's arm movement might translate into an unnatural or unreachable pose for a robot with different limb lengths, or a human's stride length might be impossible for a robot to replicate directly without losing the intended "walking" semantics. The paper notes that the same action semantics may correspond to vastly different absolute coordinates and reachable configurations across embodiments. Therefore, a key motivation is to preserve the *meaning* or *intent* of the human motion, rather than merely its absolute geometric representation, despite these large physical disparities.

2. **Physical Plausibility under Unpaired Learning:** While unsupervised or unpaired learning approaches offer a way to overcome data scarcity, they often produce motions that are visually plausible but physically infeasible for a real robot. Traditional data-driven methods, especially those based on adversarial or cycle-consistency objectives from computer graphics, typically focus on visual realism rather than practical executability. This can result in contact-related artifacts such as foot skating, ground penetration, or unstable body poses, which are unacceptable for deployment on physical robots. The motivation here is to ensure that the generated robot motions are not only semantically consistent with the human input but also adhere to the physical laws and mechanical constraints of the target robot, thereby guaranteeing their executability and stability.

The authors highlight that these two challenges are tightly coupled: methods focusing solely on physical feasibility might inadvertently distort the original motion's semantics, while those focusing on appearance or distribution matching might produce physically unreliable behaviors. Therefore, the core motivation is to achieve a robust balance, simultaneously ensuring that the retargeted robot motions preserve the action semantics of the human source while being physically stable and executable on the target humanoid robot, all without relying on expensive paired motion datasets. This aims to reduce the dependence on robot-specific paired datasets and make the learning process more compatible with downstream control tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Human2Humanoid framework is an unsupervised motion retargeting system designed to transfer human motions to humanoid robot behaviors. It adopts a CycleGAN-style architecture to perform unpaired cross-domain transfer, enhanced with specific components to address morphological discrepancies and ensure physical feasibility.

**Overall Framework:**
The system comprises two generators, $G_{B \to A}$ and $G_{A \to B}$, and two discriminators, $D_A$ and $D_B$. $G_{B \to A}$ translates motion from the human domain (B) to the robot domain (A), and $G_{A \to B}$ performs the reverse translation. Training is conducted using unpaired collections of human motion ($Q_B$) and robot motion ($Q_A$), meaning there is no temporal or frame-wise correspondence between the datasets. Fixed-length temporal windows are sampled from these collections for adversarial and cycle learning.

**Network Architecture:**

1. **Skeleton-Aware Generator:**
 - **Structure:** Each generator consists of an encoder, a latent space, and a decoder.
 - **SkeletonConv Layers:** Unlike methods that treat poses as flat vectors, Human2Humanoid uses Skeleton-Aware Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs). These layers model the native hierarchical structure of skeletal data by explicitly leveraging the adjacency matrix of the skeleton. For a joint $j$, features $f_{in}(k)$ from itself and its neighbors $N(j)$ are aggregated using learnable weights $W_k$ and a bias term $b$, resulting in $f_{out}(j)$.
 - **Topology-Based Pooling/Unpooling:** High-dimensional skeletal features are compressed into a low-dimensional latent space using topology-based pooling strategies, and then recovered to the target topology via unpooling operations.
 - **Temporal Coherence:** While SkeletonConv and pooling/unpooling operate on the spatial skeleton graph at each time step, temporal coherence is achieved by applying the generator in a sliding window over motion sequences, ensuring smooth frame-to-frame transitions.
 - **Cross-Domain Coupling:** The architecture does not require a per-joint correspondence between embodiments. Instead, skeleton-aware layers operate on each domain's native kinematic graph, and cross-domain coupling is achieved through the cycle objective and a shared set of semantic end-effectors for consistency constraints.

2. **Frame-wise Pose Discriminator:**
 - **Operation:** The discriminator evaluates the realism of individual pose frames.
 - **Feature Extraction:** An input pose sequence (represented by per-joint rotation features) is processed by a joint-shared embedding module (1x1 convolutions and LeakyReLU activations) to extract latent joint features.
 - **Branches:** The discriminator has two complementary branches:
 - **Joint-wise Pose Branch ($D_{each}$):** Predicts realism scores for each joint independently using joint-specific linear heads.
 - **Full-body Pose Branch ($D_{all}$):** Flattens the full-body latent pose feature to predict a pose-level coherence score via a Multi-Layer Perceptron.
 - **Combined Output:** The full-body score is broadcast across the joint dimension, and the final joint-wise discriminator output $D$ is a weighted sum of $D_{each}$ and the broadcasted $D_{all}$.
 - **Loss Application:** The Least Squares GAN (LSGAN) loss is applied element-wise to these joint-wise scores and averaged over all joints and frames.

**Losses and Constraints:**

1. **Morphology-Invariant End-Effector Consistency Loss ($L_{EE}$):**
 - **Motivation:** To address significant limb length and proportion discrepancies between humans and robots, which make direct absolute position alignment ineffective.
 - **Mechanism:** Instead of matching absolute Cartesian coordinates, this loss aligns end-effector trajectories in a morphology-invariant space. It computes the displacement of shared semantic end-effectors (hands and feet) from their respective T-pose configurations for both the source human motion ($q_B$) and the generated robot motion ($\hat{q}_A$). These displacements are then normalized by an embodiment-specific body scale ($S_B$ for human, $S_A$ for robot) derived from their T-poses. The squared $L_2$ difference between these normalized, relative displacements is minimized.

2. **Physics-Aware Feasibility Constraints:** Applied primarily to the generated robot motion ($\hat{q}_A$) to enhance deployability.
 - **Foot Contact Constraint ($L_{con}$):**
 - **Motivation:** To suppress foot skating during ground contact.
 - **Mechanism:** A binary contact indicator $c^{(m)}_B(t)$ is inferred from the source human foot velocity (below a threshold $\tau$). When the human foot is in contact, the loss penalizes the horizontal velocity of the corresponding robot foot, forcing it to remain still.
 - **Foot Height Constraint ($L_{hei}$):**
 - **Motivation:** To penalize foot hovering (floating) during stance phases.
 - **Mechanism:** A stance-weight mask $w^{(m)}(t)$ is created using the source human's contact indicator and foot height relative to its T-pose. For frames identified as stance, a hinge penalty (using ReLU) is applied to the robot's foot height, encouraging it to remain close to its nominal grounded height ($h_{ref_{A,m}}$).
 - **Joint Limit Constraints ($L_{lim}$):**
 - **Motivation:** To prevent hardware damage or emergency stops caused by exceeding mechanical ranges of motion.
 - **Mechanism:** This loss applies a squared ReLU penalty to robot joint angles that fall outside the predefined minimum ($q_{min}$) and maximum ($q_{max}$) limits for each joint.

**Full Objective Function:**
The training is driven by a composite objective that includes:
- **Adversarial Losses ($L_{D_{LSGAN}}$, $L_{G_{LSGAN}}$):** Standard LSGAN objectives applied element-wise (per-joint, per-frame) to match data distributions.
- **Cycle-Consistency Loss ($L_{cyc}$):** An $L_1$ norm that ensures motion reversibility (i.e., translating from B to A and back to B should reconstruct the original B motion).
- **Identity Loss ($L_{idt}$):** An $L_1$ norm that regularizes the generators to produce the input motion if the input already belongs to the target domain.
- **Proposed Losses:** $L_{EE}$, $L_{con}$, $L_{hei}$, and $L_{lim}$.
These losses are combined with scalar weights ($\lambda$ terms) for balancing. Generators minimize $L_G$, while discriminators minimize $L_{D_{LSGAN}}$.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Human Data:** Motion-X dataset, providing 3D full-body motion annotations in SMPL-X format.
- **Robot Data:** Unitree G1 subset of the PHUMA (Physically-Grounded Humanoid Locomotion Dataset), which offers physics-based filtered motions for executability.
- **Preprocessing:** Both datasets are resampled to 30 Hz, segmented into 64-frame clips, and converted to joint rotation matrices. Root linear velocity (for translation invariance) and global root rotation are used. No temporal synchronization or semantic correspondence is assumed.
- **Baselines:** Performance is compared against PHC (optimization-based SMPL-to-humanoid retargeting), GMR (two-stage constrained optimization), and Unitree Retarget (an industrial-quality reference).
- **Evaluation:** Retargeted motions are evaluated in a simulation environment using a fixed Unitree G1 tracking policy. Metrics include Downstream Controllability (Success Rate, Tracking Error) and Physical Feasibility (Foot Skating, Ground Penetration).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the Unitree G1 platform, comparing Human2Humanoid against optimization-based baselines (PHC, GMR) and an industrial reference (Unitree Retarget), yielded several key findings:

1. **Overall Performance in Downstream Controllability and Physical Feasibility (Table I):**
 - **Tracking Error (TE):** Human2Humanoid achieved the lowest average Tracking Error (0.12), indicating that its generated reference motions are generally easier for a fixed tracking policy to follow compared to all other methods. The Unitree Retarget, while an industrial reference, showed a higher average TE (0.19).
 - **Success Rate (SR):** Human2Humanoid demonstrated the highest average Success Rate (88.5%), surpassing GMR (86.9%), Unitree Retarget (71.2%), and PHC (32.7%). This suggests its generated motions lead to more stable and complete task executions in simulation.
 - **Ground Penetration (GP):** Human2Humanoid exhibited the lowest average Ground Penetration (0.05 cm), indicating superior suppression of foot-ground penetration, a critical aspect of physical plausibility.
 - **Foot Skating (FS):** Human2Humanoid achieved a low average Foot Skating rate (4.7%), which was competitive, outperforming GMR (6.8%) and Unitree Retarget (11.1%), though PHC achieved the lowest (1.4%). However, PHC's low FS came at the cost of a significantly lower SR.
 - **Summary:** These quantitative results indicate that Human2Humanoid provides a more trackable and physically plausible reference motion distribution compared to the evaluated baselines.

2. **Failure Cases of Optimization-Based Baselines (Fig. 2):**
 - The study observed that traditional optimization-based methods, despite explicit constraints, could produce catastrophic artifacts, especially when motions approach joint limits or under large morphology mismatches.
 - PHC was noted to exhibit joint-limit induced discontinuities or "snapping," where the solver might switch to a different local solution branch.
 - GMR occasionally showed noticeable jitter, often requiring non-trivial per-clip parameter tuning to mitigate.
 - In contrast, the proposed Human2Humanoid method demonstrated a reduction in these types of catastrophic failures.

3. **Qualitative Comparison (Fig. 3):**
 - Qualitative comparisons further supported the quantitative findings. Human2Humanoid produced visually more stable contacts and fewer penetration artifacts, particularly evident when dealing with large embodiment mismatches between human and robot. This visual stability is crucial for real-world robot deployment.

4. **Ablation Study (Table II):**
 - An ablation study was conducted to ascertain the contribution of key components:
 - **Removal of Morphology-Invariant End-Effector Consistency Loss (w/o $L_{EE}$):** Removing this loss led to a decrease in average Success Rate (from 92.8% to 85.7%) and an increase in average Tracking Error (from 0.099 to 0.104). This confirms that $L_{EE}$ is important for preserving trackable motion semantics, enabling the robot to better execute the intended actions.
 - **Removal of Physics-Aware Contact and Height Constraints (w/o $L_{con}$, $L_{hei}$):** Removing these constraints resulted in an increase in average Foot Skating (from 6.89% to 7.02%) and a more significant increase in average Ground Penetration (from 0.264 cm to 0.326 cm). A slight worsening of Tracking Error (from 0.099 to 0.106) was also observed. This validates the role of physics-aware objectives in improving contact-related feasibility, especially in mitigating ground penetration, which is critical for stable robot locomotion.

In summary, Human2Humanoid demonstrated a robust capability to retarget human motion to a humanoid robot in an unsupervised manner, achieving superior performance in terms of motion trackability and physical realism, and effectively addressing the challenges of morphological discrepancies and data scarcity. The ablation studies confirmed the individual contributions of the proposed morphology-invariant end-effector consistency loss and physics-aware feasibility constraints.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Human2Humanoid framework addresses a significant challenge in robotics by enabling robust and high-fidelity motion retargeting from humans to humanoid robots despite substantial morphological differences and the inherent scarcity of paired human-robot motion data. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Reduced Dependence on Paired Data:** A primary impact is the elimination of the need for expensive and labor-intensive paired human-robot motion datasets. By adopting an unsupervised, CycleGAN-style learning architecture, Human2Humanoid significantly lowers the barrier to entry for deploying human-like skills on diverse humanoid robot platforms. This enhances scalability, as new robot embodiments no longer require a complete rebuilding of robot-specific data collection and refinement pipelines.

2. **Enhanced Semantic Preservation:** The introduction of the morphology-invariant end-effector consistency loss is crucial. This mechanism allows the framework to preserve the core semantics of human motion (e.g., hand reaching, foot placement) even when there are drastic differences in limb proportions and overall scale between human and robot. This ensures that the robot's actions are meaningfully derived from human demonstrations, preventing semantic distortion often seen in direct geometric translations.

3. **Improved Physical Plausibility and Executability:** The integration of explicit physics-aware feasibility constraints (foot contact, foot height, and joint limits) directly into the generative training process is a key contribution. This directly tackles critical deployment challenges such as foot skating, base floating, and ground penetration. By generating motions that inherently respect the robot's mechanical limits and physical interaction with the environment, the framework produces robot behaviors that are not only visually plausible but also reliably executable and stable on real hardware, which is a major concern for robot control.

4. **Superior Performance over Baselines:** The experimental results demonstrate that Human2Humanoid consistently outperforms existing optimization-based and even an industrial-quality reference method in terms of downstream controllability (higher success rate, lower tracking error) and physical feasibility (lower ground penetration, competitive foot skating). This indicates a more robust and reliable solution for generating robot reference motions.

5. **Facilitating Advanced Human-Robot Interaction and Learning:** By providing high-quality, physically plausible robot behaviors from human demonstrations, Human2Humanoid can significantly advance:
 - **Teleoperation:** Enables more intuitive and natural remote control of humanoid robots.
 - **Imitation Learning:** Allows robots to learn complex skills directly from human examples without requiring extensive robot-specific programming or fine-tuning.
 - **Human-Robot Interaction:** Facilitates more natural and fluid interactions by allowing robots to mimic human movements more accurately and realistically.

6. **Broader Applicability:** While demonstrated on the Unitree G1, the methodology's reliance on general skeletal topologies, normalized end-effector trajectories, and explicit physical constraints suggests potential for generalization to other heterogeneous humanoid robots. This could streamline the development of versatile humanoid robots capable of performing a wide array of human-centric tasks.

In conclusion, "Human2Humanoid" represents a notable advancement in motion retargeting for humanoid robots, offering a more efficient, robust, and physically grounded approach to transferring human motor skills. Its impact lies in reducing development costs, improving the fidelity and executability of robot motions, and ultimately accelerating the integration of humanoid robots into complex real-world applications. Future work on generalizing to non-humanoid robots and tighter coupling with downstream control policies suggests further potential for expanding its scope and robustness.
