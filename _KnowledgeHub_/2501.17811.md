# Janus-Pro: Unified Multimodal Understanding and Generation with Data and Model Scaling

> [!summary] Summary
> DeepSeek-AI's Janus-Pro is a unified multimodal model that enhances both visual understanding and generation by scaling its decoupled visual encoding architecture to 7 billion parameters, optimizing its multi-stage training, and incorporating large-scale synthetic data. The model achieves an 80% accuracy on GenEval for instruction-following image generation and an average of 79.2 on MMBench for multimodal understanding, often surpassing larger state-of-the-art unified and specialized models.

## Problem

- Unified multimodal models often face a conflict between representations needed for understanding and generation tasks, leading to performance compromises.
- Previous unified models, including Janus, struggled to achieve optimal performance in both visual understanding and stable, high-quality image generation, particularly with short prompts.
- Limitations of earlier models were attributed to insufficient model capacity and limited, noisy training data.

## Method

- Employs a ==decoupled visual encoding architecture==, using a SigLIP encoder for understanding and a VQ tokenizer for generation, both feeding into a unified autoregressive DeepSeek-LLM.
- Optimized its ==three-stage training strategy== by increasing initial training steps, exclusively using dense text-to-image data in pretraining, and refining data ratios in fine-tuning.
- Scaled up training data significantly, adding 90 million multimodal understanding samples and 72 million high-quality ==synthetic aesthetic data==, while increasing model capacity by scaling the base language model to 7 billion parameters.

## Results

- Janus-Pro-7B achieved an average of **79.2** on MMBench for multimodal understanding, surpassing its predecessor Janus (**69.4**) and other unified models like TokenFlow-XL (**68.9**).
- For visual generation, it scored **80%** accuracy on GenEval and **84.19** on DPG-Bench, outperforming commercial models such as DALL-E 3 (**67%** GenEval, **83.50** DPG-Bench) and Stable Diffusion 3 Medium (**74%** GenEval, **84.08** DPG-Bench).
- The model demonstrated improved stability, visual quality, and instruction-following for generated images, including better performance on short prompts and complex compositional instructions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The decoupled visual encoding architecture effectively mitigates the conflict between understanding and generation tasks, proving scalable and robust with increased model capacity and data.
> - Optimized training strategies, such as focusing pretraining solely on dense text-to-image data and carefully adjusting supervised fine-tuning data ratios, significantly enhance both computational efficiency and overall model performance.
> - Integrating large volumes of high-quality synthetic aesthetic data is crucial for accelerating convergence and producing more stable and aesthetically superior text-to-image outputs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen rapid advancements in multimodal models, which aim to process and understand information from multiple modalities, such as text and images. Initially, models were often specialized, either for multimodal understanding (e.g., visual question answering) or for generative tasks (e.g., text-to-image synthesis). However, a significant trend has emerged towards developing **unified multimodal understanding and generation models**. This paradigm seeks to create a single model capable of both interpreting complex visual inputs and generating high-quality images based on textual instructions.

The motivation behind unified models is multifaceted: they can enhance instruction-following capabilities in visual generation, reduce model redundancy by sharing a common backbone, and potentially unlock new emergent abilities by fostering a deeper, more integrated understanding across modalities. However, this unification presents a key challenge: the representations required for understanding tasks (which often need fine-grained semantic features) can conflict with those needed for generation tasks (which might prioritize broader compositional attributes). Many existing unified models, such as Emu3-Chat, Show-o, TokenFlow-XL, and VILA-U, often struggle to achieve optimal performance in both understanding and generation simultaneously, frequently compromising one for the other or relying on complex architectures.

The previous work, **Janus**, addressed this conflict by proposing a novel approach of **decoupling visual encoding**. Instead of using a single visual encoder for both tasks, Janus employs separate encoders: one optimized for understanding (e.g., SigLIP for high-dimensional semantic features) and another for generation (e.g., VQ tokenizer for discrete image IDs). These distinct features are then fed into a unified autoregressive transformer. This decoupling was a pioneering step, demonstrating that it could alleviate the conflict and achieve strong performance in both domains, albeit validated at a smaller 1B parameter scale.

**Janus-Pro** enters this landscape as an advanced iteration of Janus, aiming to overcome the limitations of its predecessor, particularly its suboptimal performance on short-prompt image generation and occasional instability due to limited data and model capacity. By focusing on optimized training strategies, expanded data, and model scaling, Janus-Pro directly competes with leading models in both understanding-only categories (like LLaVA and MobileVLM) and unified models (like Emu3, D-DiT, ILLUME, TokenFlow-XL, Chameleon, and even commercial offerings like DALL-E 3 and Stable Diffusion 3 Medium). Its success in surpassing many of these models highlights a significant step forward in building truly versatile and high-performing unified multimodal AI systems, demonstrating the effectiveness and scalability of the decoupled visual encoding paradigm.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of Janus-Pro are clearly articulated:

1. **Address Shortcomings of Janus:** The previous Janus model, while pioneering in its decoupled visual encoding, suffered from certain limitations. These included suboptimal performance when generating images from short prompts and overall instability in text-to-image generation quality. These issues were attributed to its relatively small model capacity (1B parameters) and limited training data. Janus-Pro was specifically designed to overcome these deficiencies.
2. **Achieve Significant Advancements in Multimodal Understanding and Generation:** The core motivation was to push the boundaries of performance in both key areas. For multimodal understanding, the goal was to achieve higher accuracy on diverse benchmarks, demonstrating deeper comprehension abilities. For visual generation, the aim was to improve instruction-following capabilities, leading to more accurate, higher-quality, and stable image outputs.
3. **Enhance Stability of Text-to-Image Generation:** A critical specific goal was to improve the reliability and aesthetic quality of generated images, especially for various types of prompts, including short ones, which were a weakness of the original Janus.
4. **Demonstrate Scalability of the Decoupled Visual Encoding Method:** By scaling the model to a larger parameter count (from 1.5B to 7B), the authors sought to validate that the fundamental architectural principle of decoupling visual encoding remains effective and benefits from increased model capacity and data. This demonstrates the robustness of their approach.
5. **Inspire Further Exploration:** By publicly releasing the code and models, DeepSeek-AI aims to contribute to the broader research community, providing a strong baseline and encouraging further innovation in unified multimodal AI.

In essence, Janus-Pro is an ambitious effort to build a more robust, capable, and scalable unified multimodal model by systematically optimizing its training, data, and architecture based on insights gained from its predecessor.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Janus-Pro's methodology builds upon the core architectural innovation of its predecessor, Janus, while introducing significant enhancements in training, data, and model scale.

#### 3.1. Architectural Design: Decoupled Visual Encoding

The fundamental architecture of Janus-Pro, identical to Janus, centers on the principle of **decoupling visual encoding** for understanding and generation tasks. This addresses the inherent conflict between the distinct representation needs of these two modalities.
- **Understanding Encoder (Und. Encoder):** For multimodal understanding tasks (e.g., visual question answering, image captioning), Janus-Pro uses a **SigLIP encoder (specifically SigLIP-Large-Patch16-384)**. SigLIP is chosen for its ability to extract high-dimensional semantic features from images. These 2-D image features are flattened into a 1-D sequence and then mapped into the input space of the LLM via an "understanding adaptor" (a two-layer MLP).
- **Generation Encoder (Gen. Encoder) / VQ Tokenizer:** For visual generation tasks (text-to-image), images are converted into discrete IDs using a **VQ tokenizer from**. This process essentially compresses the image into a sequence of tokens, which is a common approach in autoregressive image generation. The ID sequence is flattened into 1-D, and its corresponding codebook embeddings are mapped into the LLM's input space using a "generation adaptor" (also a two-layer MLP).
- **Unified Autoregressive Transformer:** The encoded visual features (from either the understanding or generation pathway) are concatenated with the language instruction tokens to form a multimodal feature sequence. This sequence is then fed into a **unified autoregressive transformer (DeepSeek-LLM)**. This LLM processes the combined sequence, generating either a language response (for understanding tasks) or a sequence of image prediction tokens (for generation tasks) via a randomly initialized prediction head. The entire model operates within an autoregressive framework, predicting the next token in the sequence.

#### 3.2. Optimized Training Strategy

Janus-Pro refines the three-stage training process of Janus to improve efficiency and performance:

1. **Stage I (Adaptor and Image Head Training):** This stage focuses on training the adaptors (understanding and generation) and the image prediction head. In Janus-Pro, the authors significantly **increase the training steps for Stage I** compared to Janus. This extended training, specifically on the ImageNet dataset, allows the model to effectively learn pixel dependence and generate reasonable images based on category names, even with the LLM parameters fixed.
2. **Stage II (Unified Pretraining):** This stage trains all components except the understanding and generation encoders. A key modification here is the **removal of ImageNet data** from this stage. The original Janus divided text-to-image training into two parts, with a significant portion allocated to ImageNet data for pixel dependence modeling. Janus-Pro's experiments found this suboptimal. Instead, Stage II now directly and solely utilizes **normal text-to-image data** to train the model to generate images based on dense descriptions. This "focused training" approach is found to be more computationally efficient and leads to better overall performance.
3. **Stage III (Supervised Fine-Tuning - SFT):** This final stage further unlocks parameters, including the understanding encoder. Janus-Pro **adjusts the data ratios** in this stage. The proportion of multimodal data, pure text data, and text-to-image data is changed from 7:3:10 to **5:1:4**. A slight reduction in text-to-image data proportion is observed to maintain strong visual generation capabilities while simultaneously improving multimodal understanding performance.

#### 3.3. Data Scaling Initiatives

Janus-Pro significantly expands its training data in both modalities:

- **Multimodal Understanding Data:** For Stage II pretraining, approximately **90 million additional samples** from DeepSeek-VL2 are incorporated. These include diverse datasets such as image captioning (e.g., YFCC) and specialized data for table, chart, and document understanding (e.g., Docmatix). For Stage III SFT, further datasets from DeepSeek-VL2 are added, including MEME understanding, Chinese conversational data, and dialogue-enhancing datasets. This broadens the model's understanding capabilities and conversational proficiency.
- **Visual Generation Data:** Recognizing noise and aesthetic issues in previous real-world data, Janus-Pro integrates approximately **72 million samples of synthetic aesthetic data**. This brings the ratio of real to synthetic data to **1:1** during the unified pretraining stage. The prompts for this synthetic data are publicly available (e.g., from Midjourney prompts dataset). Training on this high-quality synthetic data is shown to accelerate convergence and yield more stable and aesthetically superior text-to-image outputs.

#### 3.4. Model Scaling Efforts

The effectiveness of the decoupled visual encoding approach was initially validated with a 1.5B LLM in Janus. Janus-Pro scales this up significantly by utilizing **DeepSeek-LLM with 7 billion parameters**. The paper provides detailed architectural configurations (Table 1), including increased embedding size, attention heads, and layers for the 7B model. This model scaling is crucial for testing the scalability of the proposed architecture and leveraging the benefits of larger model capacity, which the authors observe leads to faster convergence for both understanding and generation losses. The Janus-Pro series includes both 1B and 7B variants, demonstrating the scalability of their method.

The training process itself leverages **HAI-LLM**, an efficient distributed training framework built on PyTorch, indicating sophisticated engineering support for handling large-scale model training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Janus-Pro demonstrates substantial improvements across both multimodal understanding and visual generation tasks, often outperforming state-of-the-art models, including larger unified models and specialized task-specific models.

#### 4.1. Multimodal Understanding Performance

Evaluated on widely recognized benchmarks (POPE, MME-Perception, MMBench, SEED, GQA, MMMU, MM-Vet), Janus-Pro-7B consistently achieves top-tier performance:
- **Overall Superiority:** Janus-Pro-7B achieved an average score of **79.2 on MMBench**, significantly surpassing its predecessor Janus (69.4) and other state-of-the-art unified models like TokenFlow-XL (68.9) and MetaMorph (75.2).
- **Competitive with Larger Models:** Despite being a 7B parameter model, Janus-Pro-7B often outperforms even larger models like TokenFlow-XL (13B) on most benchmarks (e.g., POPE, MME-P, MMB, SEED, MMMU, MM-Vet), indicating its efficiency and the effectiveness of its architecture.
- **Comparison to Understanding-Only Models:** While designed for unification, Janus-Pro-7B also maintains highly competitive scores against understanding-only models like LLaVA-v1.5-7B across several benchmarks, showcasing its strong analytical capabilities. For instance, on MME-Perception, Janus-Pro-7B scores 1567.1 compared to LLaVA-v1.5-7B's 1510.7.
- **Performance Across Benchmarks:** Janus-Pro-7B shows notable improvements across diverse understanding tasks:
 - **POPE (Hallucination):** 87.4 (Janus-Pro-7B) vs. 87.0 (Janus)
 - **MME-Perception:** 1567.1 (Janus-Pro-7B) vs. 1338.0 (Janus)
 - **MMB:** 79.2 (Janus-Pro-7B) vs. 69.4 (Janus)
 - **MMMU (Multi-discipline):** 41.0 (Janus-Pro-7B) vs. 30.5 (Janus)
 - **MM-Vet (Integrated capabilities):** 50.0 (Janus-Pro-7B) vs. 34.3 (Janus)

#### 4.2. Visual Generation Performance

Janus-Pro's visual generation capabilities are evaluated on GenEval and DPG-Bench, both challenging benchmarks for instruction-following.
- **GenEval (Instruction-Following Compositionality):** Janus-Pro-7B achieves an impressive **80% overall accuracy**, significantly outperforming both its predecessor Janus (61%) and leading commercial models such as DALL-E 3 (67%) and Stable Diffusion 3 Medium (74%). This demonstrates superior ability to accurately follow complex, compositional instructions. It shows particular strengths in "Position" and "Color Attribute" sub-categories, which are often challenging for text-to-image models.
- **DPG-Bench (Dense Prompt Graph Benchmark):** Janus-Pro-7B scores **84.19**, surpassing all other methods in the comparison, including DALL-E 3 (83.50), SD3-Medium (84.08), and PixArt-Σ (80.54). This highlights Janus-Pro's exceptional capability in semantic alignment and adherence to lengthy, dense prompts, even compared to dedicated generation models.
- **Qualitative Results:** Visual examples (Figure 2 and Figure 4) show that Janus-Pro-7B generates images with significantly improved visual quality, richer details, and enhanced stability, especially for short prompts. It also demonstrates the ability to generate simple text within images, a common challenge for many T2I models. The generated images are highly realistic and semantically accurate, capturing the essence of imaginative and creative scenes.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Observations

Beyond quantitative metrics, the qualitative results highlight several key strengths:
- **Improved Stability and Aesthetic Quality:** The synthetic aesthetic data and optimized training lead to more consistent and visually pleasing outputs.
- **Detail and Realism:** Generated images, even at 384x384 resolution, show remarkable detail and realism for general scenes.
- **Semantic Accuracy:** The model accurately captures and translates semantic information from prompts, leading to coherent and well-reasoned images.
- **Versatile Understanding:** Examples like detailed scene descriptions, landmark recognition, and text recognition showcase robust comprehension across diverse visual contexts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Janus-Pro represents a significant step forward in the development of unified multimodal AI models, demonstrating that it is possible to achieve high performance in both understanding and generation tasks within a single, coherent framework.

**Contributions to the Field:**
- **Validation of Decoupled Encoding at Scale:** The work strongly re-validates the effectiveness of "decoupled visual encoding" at larger model scales (7B parameters), proving its scalability and robustness as a core architectural principle for unified multimodal models. This addresses a critical challenge in the field, where conflicts between understanding and generation objectives often lead to suboptimal performance.
- **Optimized Training and Data Strategies:** The specific optimizations in the three-stage training process and the strategic incorporation of high-quality synthetic data for generation significantly contribute to the model's superior performance and stability. These insights provide valuable guidance for future multimodal model development.
- **Setting New Benchmarks:** By outperforming many state-of-the-art models, including larger and more established systems like DALL-E 3 and SD3-Medium, Janus-Pro sets new competitive benchmarks for both multimodal understanding and instruction-following text-to-image generation.
- **Efficiency and Practicality:** The demonstration of strong performance with a 7B LLM (compared to 13B LLMs for some competitors) suggests a more efficient and potentially more accessible model for deployment and further research, especially given the costs associated with training and running large models.
- **Open Science Contribution:** The public release of code and models by DeepSeek-AI is a substantial contribution, accelerating research and development across the AI community by providing a powerful, transparent, and reproducible foundation for future work.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Human-AI Interaction:** A model capable of both deeply understanding visual inputs and generating highly accurate visual content based on instructions could power more intuitive and versatile AI assistants, creative tools, and educational applications.
- **Advanced Content Creation:** For creative industries, Janus-Pro's robust instruction-following and aesthetic generation capabilities could revolutionize tasks like concept art, advertising, and digital media production, allowing for more precise control over AI-generated visuals.
- **Improved Research Baseline:** Researchers can leverage Janus-Pro as a strong baseline for exploring new multimodal architectures, training techniques, and applications, potentially leading to faster advancements in the field.
- **Foundation for More Complex AI Systems:** Unified models like Janus-Pro lay the groundwork for developing even more sophisticated AI systems that can seamlessly interact with the real world, understand complex scenes, and generate relevant visual responses.

**Acknowledged Limitations and Future Directions:**
The paper openly discusses several limitations, which also serve as clear directions for future research:
- **Input Resolution:** The current input resolution of 384x384 pixels is a bottleneck. It affects performance in fine-grained understanding tasks (e.g., OCR, where small text might be illegible) and limits the detail in generated images (e.g., small facial regions may lack fidelity).
- **Reconstruction Losses:** The use of a vision tokenizer introduces reconstruction losses, which, despite semantic accuracy, can lead to a lack of fine details in the generated images.

The authors explicitly state that "Increasing the image resolution could mitigate these issues," indicating that future work will likely focus on scaling up the resolution for both understanding and generation, potentially through techniques like cascaded diffusion or higher-resolution vision tokenizers. Addressing these limitations would further solidify Janus-Pro's position as a leading unified multimodal AI model.
