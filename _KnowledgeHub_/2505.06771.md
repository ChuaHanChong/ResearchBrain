# JaxRobotarium: Training and Deploying Multi-Robot Policies in 10 Minutes

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Georgia Institute of Technology present JaxRobotarium, an end-to-end platform for accelerated multi-robot reinforcement learning that addresses sim2real challenges. It integrates a high-throughput Jax-based simulator with the physical Robotarium testbed, achieving up to 20x faster policy training and enabling reliable hardware deployment with built-in safety features.

## Problem

- Existing multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) platforms often abstract away realistic robot dynamics and safety, creating a significant "sim2real" gap for real-world deployment.
- Prior robotics-focused multi-robot learning (MRRL) platforms, such as MARBLER, suffered from slow training speeds due to a lack of parallelization and GPU/TPU support, limiting comprehensive algorithmic evaluation.
- There is a need for standardized, efficient, and open platforms to facilitate reproducible research and fair benchmarking of MRRL algorithms.

## Method

- Developed **JaxRobotarium**, an open-source platform leveraging Jax for a highly efficient, parallelized simulator (**Jax-RPS**) that models realistic robot dynamics and incorporates ==control barrier functions== for collision avoidance.
- Integrated the platform with ==JaxMARL== to provide a comprehensive learning interface for training multi-robot policies across eight standardized benchmark scenarios, including four novel tasks.
- Established a direct deployment pipeline for trained policies to the remote-access Robotarium hardware testbed, enabling seamless sim2real evaluation and hardware-based experimentation.

## Results

- Jax-RPS accurately replicated robot trajectories with a **0.00 ± 0.00** trajectory error compared to the original Robotarium Python Simulator, while achieving **140-150x** faster simulation on a single CPU.
- The platform demonstrated up to a **20x** speedup in training and allowed **20x** more timesteps within the same duration compared to MARBLER across benchmark scenarios, leading to higher episodic returns.
- Real-world deployments on the Robotarium showed that policies trained in simulation with domain randomization improved real-world performance (e.g., PQN prey tags from **1.4** to **4.7**) while consistently maintaining zero collisions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging Jax for simulation and training dramatically improves computational efficiency, providing speedups of up to 150x in simulation and 20x in training over previous robotics-focused platforms.
> - The integration of control barrier functions ensures collision-free operation in both simulated environments and real-world robot deployments, crucial for practical multi-robot systems.
> - Simple domain randomization techniques, like action noise, can effectively mitigate identified sim2real gaps, improving the performance of trained policies when transferred to physical robots.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) is a growing area for developing complex coordination behaviors in multi-robot systems. Traditional multi-robot coordination methods often require explicit specification of behaviors, which can be challenging in complex or unstructured environments. Learning-based techniques like multi-robot reinforcement learning (MRRL) offer a more generalizable approach for diverse applications such as warehouse automation and autonomous driving.

However, a significant challenge in MRRL is the gap between theoretical algorithmic development and practical hardware deployment. While the broader MARL community benefits from standardized simulation and learning environments (e.g., Multi-Agent Particle Environment (MPE), StarCraft Multi-Agent Challenge (SMAC), JaxMARL), these environments typically abstract away crucial robotics-relevant characteristics such as realistic robot dynamics, safety constraints, and collision avoidance. This abstraction leads to a considerable "sim2real" gap, making it difficult to transfer policies trained in these environments to physical robots. Consequently, researchers often develop bespoke environments and hardware testbeds, which limits reproducibility and standardized benchmarking of MRRL algorithms.

A notable step towards addressing this issue was the Multi-Agent RL Benchmark and Learning Environment for the Robotarium (MARBLER). MARBLER aimed to bridge existing MARL software infrastructure with the Robotarium, an open and remotely-accessible multi-robot testbed. While MARBLER provided a robotics-relevant platform with sim2real capabilities, it lacked support for parallelization and GPU/TPU execution. This limitation resulted in slow training speeds, hindering its widespread adoption and the thorough evaluation of modern MARL algorithms that rely on high-throughput simulation.

JaxRobotarium positions itself within this landscape by building upon the foundation laid by MARBLER. It aims to overcome MARBLER's computational limitations by leveraging Jax, a high-performance numerical computing library, to enable parallelization and hardware acceleration (GPU/TPU). By doing so, JaxRobotarium seeks to provide an end-to-end platform for simulation, learning, deployment, and benchmarking that offers the efficiency of modern MARL environments while retaining robotics relevance, realistic dynamics, safety constraints, and a direct pipeline to hardware deployment on the Robotarium. This work contributes to making MRRL research more accessible, efficient, and reproducible by offering a standardized and accelerated platform for sim2real evaluation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop and validate JaxRobotarium, an end-to-end open-source platform designed to significantly accelerate the training and deployment of multi-robot reinforcement learning (MRRL) algorithms. This acceleration is intended to facilitate more comprehensive evaluation and faster iteration in MRRL research, while maintaining ease-of-use and providing open access to hardware-based evaluations.

The motivation for developing JaxRobotarium stems from several limitations observed in the existing multi-robot learning and multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) landscape:

1. **Lack of Robotics-Relevant and Deployable MARL Platforms:** Established MARL platforms, such as SMAC and MPE, are designed for general multi-agent learning tasks and often abstract away physical robot dynamics and safety considerations. This abstraction creates a significant sim2real gap, making policies trained in these environments difficult to transfer to real-world multi-robot systems. The robotics community therefore frequently resorts to developing custom, often closed, environments and testbeds, which impedes reproducibility and benchmarking.

2. **Computational Inefficiency of Existing Robotics-Focused Platforms:** While platforms like MARBLER attempted to integrate MARL with a realistic multi-robot testbed (the Robotarium), they suffered from a lack of parallelization and GPU/TPU support. This deficiency led to prohibitively slow training speeds, rendering them impractical for the extensive data collection and computation required by modern deep reinforcement learning algorithms. The slow speed limited the scope of experiments and hindered broader adoption by researchers accustomed to faster MARL environments.

3. **Need for Standardized Benchmarking and Reproducibility:** The absence of a standardized, open, and efficient platform for multi-robot learning makes it difficult to compare different MRRL algorithms fairly and reproduce research findings. A unified benchmark that combines realistic simulation with hardware evaluation capabilities is necessary to foster robust development in the field.

4. **Democratization of Multi-Robot Learning Research:** Access to physical multi-robot testbeds is often limited due to cost, logistical complexities, and specialized expertise requirements. The Robotarium provides a unique, publicly available, and remotely accessible hardware platform. Integrating this testbed with an efficient learning pipeline could democratize access to hardware evaluation for MRRL algorithms, enabling a broader range of researchers to conduct sim2real experiments without needing to build their own robot fleets.

By addressing these motivations, JaxRobotarium aims to become a valuable tool for the multi-robot systems community, accelerating the development, benchmarking, and sim2real transfer of learning algorithms by offering rapid training, realistic simulation, safety constraints, a standardized benchmark, and an open-access deployment pipeline.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

JaxRobotarium is designed as an end-to-end platform for multi-robot reinforcement learning, encompassing simulation, policy learning, and hardware deployment. The methodological approach centers on leveraging the Jax library for computational efficiency and integrating it with the Robotarium testbed for physical realism and deployment capabilities.

The platform consists of four main components:

1. **Jax-based Robotarium Simulator (Jax-RPS):**
 - **Design:** Jax-RPS is a new simulator developed from the ground up to faithfully model the behavior of the Robotarium's GRITSBots using a unicycle dynamics model. It implements both single integrator and unicycle controllers to guide robots to desired positions or poses.
 - **Safety Constraints:** To ensure safe operation, especially in real-world deployment, Jax-RPS incorporates control barrier certificates for collision avoidance. These certificates solve a quadratic program to modify robot control inputs to prevent collisions, as described in prior work (e.g.,).
 - **Optimization:** The core functionality of Jax-RPS is designed to be compatible with Jax's advanced features: `jit` (just-in-time compilation) for accelerated execution of computations and `vmap` for efficient parallelization across multiple environments. This design choice aims to optimize the simulator for data-driven learning paradigms like MRRL.
 - **Evaluation:** The fidelity and optimization of Jax-RPS were validated using a "Random Waypoint Navigation" scenario with four robots. Fidelity was measured by comparing robot trajectories between Jax-RPS and the original Robotarium Python Simulator (RPS) over 10,000 timesteps, reporting trajectory mismatch. Optimization performance was assessed by simulating 100,000 timesteps across multiple parallel environments and measuring wall time, comparing against a multi-processing modified RPS.

2. **Platform for Multi-Robot RL and Learning Interface:**
 - **Integration with JaxMARL:** JaxRobotarium is integrated with JaxMARL, a state-of-the-art Jax-based MARL library, to provide access to several common MARL algorithms and facilitate policy training. The generalizable learning interface ensures compatibility with other Jax multi-agent learning frameworks.
 - **Learning Interface Components:**
 - `RobotariumEnv`: The primary interface for input/output with Jax-RPS, maintaining a generalized environment state and visualization utilities.
 - `HetManager`: Facilitates the injection of heterogeneity into multi-robot environments, supporting various representations like robot IDs, class IDs, and capability vectors, which can be included in the observation space.
 - `ControllerManager`: Translates actions from policies into robot commands. It processes waypoints into unicycle velocity commands and applies modifications from barrier functions for collision avoidance if enabled.
 - `Scenario interface`: Provides a standardized structure for defining new multi-robot tasks within JaxRobotarium, aligning with other learning frameworks.
 - **Training Evaluation:** The utility of the framework was evaluated by comparing its training performance and efficiency against MARBLER on four re-implemented MARBLER scenarios (Arctic Transport, Discovery, Material Transport, Warehouse). Experiments measured episodic returns and simulated timesteps against wall time, using both single and parallelized (8 environments) JaxRobotarium configurations.

3. **Benchmark Scenarios:**
 - JaxRobotarium includes eight standardized multi-robot coordination scenarios.
 - **Re-implemented MARBLER Tasks (4):** Arctic Transport, Discovery (renamed from Predator Capture Prey), Material Transport, and Warehouse. These tasks were re-implemented in Jax.
 - **Novel Tasks (4):** Navigation (Multi-Agent Path Finding - MAPF), Foraging (inspired by Level-Based Foraging), Continuous-RWARE (inspired by RWARE), and Predator-Prey (based on a classic variant with a heuristic-controlled prey). These novel tasks bring established MARL benchmarks into a realistic robotics setting.
 - Detailed descriptions of robot observations, heterogeneity injection, reward structures, and specific parameters (e.g., number of robots, max steps, training timesteps) are provided for each scenario.

4. **Direct Deployment Pipeline for Sim2Real Evaluation:**
 - `Deploy` Module: This module streamlines the process of deploying trained policies to the physical Robotarium testbed. It compiles all necessary files for an experiment and handles model conversion and file sanitation to ensure compatibility with the Robotarium's hardware, which does not natively support Jax libraries.
 - **Sim2Real Evaluation:** The platform's sim2real transfer capabilities were studied by deploying policies, trained entirely in simulation, onto the physical Robotarium hardware. Over 200 real-world deployments were conducted across various task-algorithm combinations. This evaluation compared simulated performance metrics (rewards, task-specific metrics, collisions) with real-world results.
 - **Domain Randomization:** To mitigate identified sim2real gaps, simple domain randomization techniques, specifically action noise, were applied and evaluated on a challenging task (Predator Prey).

Throughout these components, the methodology emphasizes open-source availability, realistic robot dynamics, safety guarantees via control barrier functions, and extensive empirical validation of both computational efficiency and sim2real transferability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the efficiency, fidelity, and utility of JaxRobotarium, as well as insights into multi-robot learning algorithms and sim2real transfer.

#### 4.1. Simulator Fidelity and Efficiency (Jax-RPS Evaluation)

- **Fidelity:** Jax-RPS demonstrates high fidelity, simulating robot trajectories identical to the original Robotarium Python Simulator (RPS) with a computed trajectory error of 0.00 ± 0.00. This indicates that the Jax-based simulator accurately replicates the behavior of the GRITSBots.
- **Efficiency (Single-Environment):** When run on a CPU, Jax-RPS achieved a significant speedup of approximately 140-150x over RPS for simulating trajectories (e.g., 9.18 ms vs. 1425.28 ms for position control).
- **Efficiency (Parallelization):** Jax-RPS maintains high efficiency under increased parallelization, demonstrating a monotonically decreasing wall time as the number of parallel environments increases. It retained a 100x or more speed advantage over RPS even with multiple environments. In contrast, RPS's performance suffered slightly at higher degrees of parallelization due to process management overhead.

#### 4.2. Training Efficiency (JaxRobotarium vs. MARBLER)

- JaxRobotarium demonstrated substantial improvements in training efficiency compared to MARBLER across all four re-implemented MARBLER scenarios (Arctic Transport, Discovery, Material Transport, Warehouse).
- It achieved up to a **20x speedup in training** and could simulate up to **20x more timesteps** within the same training duration, consistently resulting in higher episodic returns. This efficiency gain is attributed to its support for GPU execution, parallelization, and Jax's just-in-time compilation.

#### 4.3. Algorithm Benchmarking (Simulation)

- Four state-of-the-art MARL algorithms (PQN, QMIX, MAPPO, IPPO) were benchmarked across the eight scenarios.
- **No Single Best Algorithm:** No single algorithm consistently outperformed all others across all scenarios.
- **PQN Performance:** PQN (a Q-Learning approach without a replay buffer) performed comparably to or, in some cases, better than QMIX (a Q-Learning method using a hypernetwork-generated centralized critic), with Material Transport being an exception where QMIX showed better performance. This suggests PQN as a promising and efficient alternative, particularly in multi-robot settings where large replay buffers might be intractable.
- **IPPO vs. MAPPO:** Independent learning with IPPO (decentralized policies and critics) often performed better or comparably to MAPPO (decentralized policies with a centralized critic). This finding is attributed to the limited partial observability configured in most benchmark scenarios, suggesting that centralized critics may offer less advantage in such settings.
- **Safety:** The control barrier functions ensured collision-free operation across all simulated training and benchmark experiments, with reported collisions of 0.0 ± 0.00.

#### 4.4. Sim2Real Transfer and Domain Randomization

- **Sim2Real Gap:** Reasonable performance gaps between simulated and real-world deployments were observed for most task-algorithm combinations.
- **Task-Dependent Gaps:** Larger sim2real gaps were identified in the novel tasks, particularly Predator-Prey and Continuous-RWARE, especially for QMIX and PQN. Qualitative analysis suggested that these tasks contain "inflection points" where small differences in robot states or environment dynamics could cascade into qualitatively different outcomes between simulation and reality. This highlights the importance of hardware evaluation for scenarios with complex interaction dynamics.
- **Domain Randomization Effectiveness:** Simple domain randomization, implemented through action noise during training, was shown to reduce the sim2real gap and improve real-world performance for PQN and QMIX in the Predator-Prey scenario. For instance, PQN's real-world prey tags improved from 1.4 ± 0.54 to 4.7 ± 0.96 with domain randomization.
- **Safety (Real-World):** Consistent with simulation, all real-world deployments also reported zero collisions, demonstrating the effectiveness of the integrated control barrier functions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

JaxRobotarium contributes to the multi-robot learning community by addressing critical challenges related to efficiency, realism, and accessibility in research and development. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Accelerating MRRL Research:** By providing dramatic speedups (up to 150x in simulation and 20x in training) through Jax, GPU/TPU execution, and parallelization, JaxRobotarium enables researchers to conduct more extensive experiments, iterate faster, and explore a wider range of algorithms and hyperparameters. This acceleration can lead to more rapid advancements in multi-robot coordination behaviors.

2. **Bridging the Sim2Real Gap for Robotics:** JaxRobotarium offers an end-to-end pipeline from simulation to real-world deployment on the Robotarium testbed. By integrating realistic robot dynamics and safety constraints (via barrier certificates) directly into the simulator, it aims to reduce the sim2real gap. The platform also provides tools to study and mitigate this gap, as demonstrated by the effectiveness of domain randomization, which is crucial for developing deployable multi-robot policies.

3. **Democratizing Access to Hardware Evaluation:** The Robotarium is a publicly available and free-to-use multi-robot hardware testbed. By streamlining the deployment process and integrating with this accessible platform, JaxRobotarium democratizes access to real-world multi-robot experiments. This lowers the barrier to entry for researchers who may not have access to their own physical robot fleets, fostering broader participation and diverse contributions to MRRL research.

4. **Standardized Benchmarking for Reproducibility:** The platform provides eight standardized multi-robot coordination scenarios, including both re-implementations of existing tasks and novel additions that bring established MARL benchmarks to a robotics context. This standardized set of tasks, coupled with an open-source framework and an intuitive learning interface (compatible with libraries like JaxMARL), promotes reproducibility, facilitates fair comparison of MRRL algorithms, and allows for consistent evaluation of algorithmic contributions.

5. **Insights into Algorithm Performance:** The comprehensive benchmarking of various MARL algorithms across diverse multi-robot tasks provides valuable insights into their relative strengths and weaknesses. Findings, such as the surprising effectiveness of independent learning (IPPO) in scenarios with limited partial observability and the competitive performance of PQN without a replay buffer, can guide future algorithmic development and selection for specific multi-robot applications.

6. **Foundation for Future Research:** JaxRobotarium serves as a robust foundation for future work in several areas, including exploring more complex policy architectures (e.g., GNNs, Hyper-Networks), investigating stronger partial observability settings, and developing advanced sim2real transfer techniques. The open-source nature of the platform encourages community contributions and extensions.

In summary, JaxRobotarium is positioned to be a significant resource for the multi-robot systems community, fostering more efficient, reproducible, and practically relevant research in multi-robot reinforcement learning.
