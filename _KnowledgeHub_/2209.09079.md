# MSVIPER: Improved Policy Distillation for Reinforcement-Learning-Based Robot Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Maryland and NIST developed MSVIPER, a framework that distills black-box deep reinforcement learning policies into interpretable decision trees for robot navigation, using multi-scenario sampling to produce smaller, more robust policies. The approach enables post-training direct modification of these decision trees to address navigation issues like freezing, oscillation, and vibrations on real robots, achieving up to a 95% reduction in freezing behaviors.

## Problem

- Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) policies for robot navigation are typically neural networks, which are opaque and difficult to analyze, verify, or debug.
- Existing DRL policies often exhibit suboptimal behaviors or errors (e.g., freezing, oscillation, vibration) that are costly and time-consuming to correct, requiring full retraining.
- The lack of mechanisms for post-training policy modification prevents efficient adaptation to new errors or changing conditions without extensive re-training.

## Method

- The **MSVIPER** framework comprises a three-stage pipeline involving training an expert DRL policy, distilling it into an interpretable decision tree, and then directly modifying the tree.
- The expert neural network policy is distilled into a decision tree using multi-scenario trajectory generation, which samples from diverse simulated environments of varying complexities to create a broader dataset.
- Algorithms are applied for direct decision tree modification to identify and alter specific nodes to correct undesirable behaviors like robot freezing, oscillation, and vibrations without requiring retraining of the original expert policy.

## Results

- MSVIPER generated decision trees were significantly smaller (e.g., 411 nodes vs. 2651 for SSVIPER) and achieved higher average rewards than previous distillation methods, closely mimicking the expert policy.
- On real robots, tree modifications reduced freezing behaviors by up to **95%** (from **100%** to **5%**) and oscillations by up to **90%** (from **96%** to **6%**) in indoor navigation tasks.
- For outdoor navigation, the approach reduced robot vibrations (e.g., from **0.53** to **0.48**, an approximate **17%** improvement) and oscillations, leading to smoother and more stable movement.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Distilling DRL policies into decision trees using diverse, multi-scenario sampling improves the robustness and interpretability of the resulting policy while yielding smaller, more efficient tree structures.
> - Direct, post-training modification of interpretable decision tree policies can effectively resolve specific navigation errors and sub-optimal behaviors in robots, eliminating the need for computationally expensive retraining.
> - Targeted structural changes to decision trees, such as modifying actions in specific leaf nodes or adjusting branch thresholds, can significantly enhance robot navigation performance and stability in real-world scenarios.
