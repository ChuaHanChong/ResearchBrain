# Are Multimodal Large Language Models Ready for Omnidirectional Spatial Reasoning?

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduce OSR-Bench, the first benchmark to evaluate multimodal large language models' spatial reasoning in 360-degree panoramic images. Evaluation of eight state-of-the-art MLLMs revealed limited capabilities in omnidirectional spatial understanding and high sensitivity to hallucination.

## Problem

- Most existing MLLMs are trained and evaluated primarily on traditional 2D pinhole camera images, which only capture a limited field of view.
- There was no comprehensive benchmark to systematically evaluate MLLMs' spatial reasoning capabilities in 360-degree omnidirectional (panoramic) environments.
- Understanding spatial relationships in full 360-degree contexts is crucial for applications in embodied AI, augmented/virtual reality, and robotics.

## Method

- Introduced ==OSR-Bench (Omnidirectional Spatial Reasoning Benchmark)==, the first comprehensive benchmark specifically designed to evaluate MLLMs' spatial reasoning in panoramic images.
- Constructed the dataset from ReplicaPano and DeepPanoContext, comprising ==1,526 panoramic scenes== across ==65 object categories==.
- Defined three spatial reasoning categories: object counting, relative distance, and relative direction, supported by ==omni-cognitive maps== and a negative sampling strategy for robustness.
- Developed a two-stage evaluation framework assessing cognitive map generation via Hungarian matching and spatial reasoning tasks using rule-based and LLM-based evaluators.

## Results

- All **eight** state-of-the-art MLLMs evaluated achieved only modest success rates, indicating limited omnidirectional spatial understanding.
- Proprietary models (GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro) consistently outperformed open-source models across most tasks, showing a performance gap.
- Models frequently hallucinate, providing answers about non-existent objects when distractor objects are present, indicating limited robustness in visual verification.
- Structured reasoning prompts ("think mode") generally improved performance across tasks, with the most significant gains observed in relative direction reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current multimodal large language models (MLLMs) demonstrate limited spatial reasoning capabilities when processing panoramic images.
> - Proprietary models consistently outperform open-source alternatives in omnidirectional spatial understanding tasks, particularly in instruction following and cognitive map generation.
> - All evaluated models show significant vulnerability to hallucination, frequently generating responses about non-existent objects in panoramic scenes.
> - Utilizing chain-of-thought reasoning ("think mode") generally improves MLLM performance, especially for complex relative direction tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have demonstrated impressive capabilities in understanding and reasoning about visual content. However, most existing models are trained and evaluated primarily on traditional 2D pinhole camera images, which capture only a limited field of view. As 360-degree cameras become increasingly prevalent in applications such as augmented reality (AR), virtual reality (VR), and robotics, there is a growing need to understand how well these models can process and reason about omnidirectional images.

This research addresses a fundamental question: are current MLLMs capable of performing spatial reasoning tasks when presented with panoramic images that capture the full 360-degree environment? The ability to understand spatial relationships in omnidirectional contexts is crucial for applications in embodied AI, where agents must navigate and interact with complete 3D environments.

The authors introduce OSR-Bench (Omnidirectional Spatial Reasoning Benchmark), the first comprehensive benchmark specifically designed to evaluate MLLMs' spatial reasoning capabilities in panoramic settings. This work fills a critical gap in the evaluation landscape by providing a systematic framework for assessing how well models can understand spatial relationships when presented with the complete visual context that 360-degree images provide.

### 2. The OSR-Bench Dataset

OSR-Bench is built upon two existing datasets: ReplicaPano and DeepPanoContext, which provide photorealistic indoor panoramic scenes with comprehensive layout annotations. The dataset comprises 1,526 panoramic scenes covering 65 different object categories commonly found in indoor environments.

The dataset construction involves several key components:

**Omni-Cognitive Maps**: These are top-down view representations extracted from the 3D layout annotations. They serve as spatial abstractions that capture the relative positions and orientations of objects within each scene. The cognitive maps provide a bird's-eye view of the environment, allowing for systematic evaluation of spatial understanding.

**Three Spatial Reasoning Categories**:
1. **Object Counting**: Tests the model's ability to accurately count instances of specific objects within the panoramic scene
2. **Relative Distance**: Evaluates understanding of which objects are closer or farther from reference points
3. **Relative Direction**: Assesses the model's capability to determine directional relationships between objects (front, back, left, right, etc.)

**Negative Sampling Strategy**: To test robustness against hallucination, the researchers introduce distractor objects that do not actually appear in the scenes. This approach helps evaluate whether models can accurately distinguish between objects that are present versus those that are not, providing insights into the reliability of their visual understanding.

**Template-Based Question Generation**: The dataset uses a scalable template system to generate question-answer pairs, ensuring consistency and comprehensive coverage of spatial reasoning scenarios across all scenes.

### 3. Methodology and Evaluation Framework

The evaluation framework consists of two complementary stages designed to assess different aspects of spatial reasoning:

**Stage 1: Cognitive Map Generation**
Models are asked to generate omni-cognitive maps from panoramic images by identifying and spatially arranging objects in a top-down view format. The evaluation uses:
- Hungarian matching algorithm to align predicted object positions with ground truth
- Rotation-invariant F1 scores to account for different orientational perspectives
- Distance thresholds to determine whether object placements are spatially accurate

**Stage 2: Spatial Reasoning Tasks**
Models answer questions about object counting, relative distances, and directional relationships. The evaluation employs:
- Rule-based evaluators for precise answer matching
- LLM-based evaluators for more nuanced response assessment
- CHAIR metrics (CHAIR_S, CHAIR_I, CHAIR_instance) to measure hallucination sensitivity

**Model Evaluation**
The researchers evaluated eight state-of-the-art MLLMs under strict zero-shot conditions:
- **Proprietary models**: GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro
- **Open-source models**: InternVL2.5, LLaVA-v1.5, LLaMA3.2-Vision, Qwen2.5-VL, DeepSeek-VL2, Janus-Pro

The evaluation includes both "vanilla mode" (direct answers) and "think mode" (chain-of-thought reasoning) to understand how structured reasoning affects performance.

### 4. Key Findings and Results

The evaluation reveals several important findings about the current state of MLLMs in omnidirectional spatial reasoning:

**Limited Spatial Reasoning Capabilities**
All evaluated models demonstrate limited spatial reasoning abilities in panoramic settings. Even the best-performing models achieve only modest success rates, indicating that omnidirectional spatial understanding remains a significant challenge for current MLLMs.

**Proprietary Model Advantage**
Proprietary models (GPT-4o and Gemini 1.5 Pro) consistently outperform open-source alternatives across most tasks. This performance gap is particularly pronounced in:
- Instruction following and JSON format adherence
- Cognitive map generation accuracy
- Robustness to negative sampling

**Cognitive Map Utility**
The relationship between cognitive map quality and downstream reasoning performance is complex:
- Cognitive maps generally improve object counting accuracy
- Their impact on relative distance and direction reasoning varies significantly across models
- Some models perform better on reasoning tasks without explicitly generating cognitive maps

**Hallucination Sensitivity**
All models show substantial vulnerability to hallucination when presented with distractor objects:
- Models frequently provide answers about non-existent objects
- CHAIR metrics reveal high rates of hallucinated object mentions
- This indicates limited robustness in visual verification capabilities

**Chain-of-Thought Benefits**
Structured reasoning prompts ("think mode") generally improve performance:
- Most significant improvements observed in relative direction tasks
- Enhanced reasoning is particularly beneficial for complex spatial relationships
- The explicit reasoning process helps models organize their spatial understanding

### 5. Implications and Future Directions

The findings from this research have significant implications for the development of more capable MLLMs and their applications in spatial reasoning tasks:

**Technical Improvements Needed**
The results highlight several areas where current MLLMs require enhancement:
- Better spatial encoding mechanisms for panoramic images
- Improved geometric understanding of 360-degree projections
- More robust visual grounding to reduce hallucination
- Enhanced integration of spatial and semantic information

**Applications and Use Cases**
The limitations identified have direct implications for real-world applications:
- **Embodied AI**: Current models may struggle with navigation and spatial planning tasks
- **AR/VR**: Limited spatial understanding could affect virtual object placement and interaction
- **Robotics**: Robots relying on these models may have difficulty with spatial reasoning in complex environments

**Future Research Directions**
The work opens several promising research avenues:
1. **Architecture Improvements**: Developing specialized architectures for omnidirectional image processing
2. **Training Strategies**: Creating more comprehensive training datasets with panoramic images and spatial annotations
3. **Multimodal Integration**: Better fusion of visual, spatial, and linguistic information
4. **Geometric Reasoning**: Incorporating explicit geometric reasoning capabilities into model architectures

**Benchmark Contributions**
OSR-Bench provides the research community with:
- A standardized evaluation framework for omnidirectional spatial reasoning
- Comprehensive baseline results across multiple state-of-the-art models
- A foundation for tracking progress in this important area
- Insights into the specific challenges that need to be addressed

The research demonstrates that while current MLLMs have made significant progress in visual understanding, omnidirectional spatial reasoning remains a challenging frontier. The systematic evaluation provided by OSR-Bench reveals both the limitations of existing approaches and the potential for future improvements. As applications requiring spatial reasoning in 360-degree environments become more prevalent, addressing these limitations will be crucial for developing more capable and reliable multimodal AI systems.

This work establishes an important baseline for evaluating progress in omnidirectional spatial reasoning and provides clear directions for future research in this emerging area of multimodal AI.
