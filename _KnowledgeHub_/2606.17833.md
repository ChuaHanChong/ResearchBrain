# HumanoidArena: Benchmarking Egocentric Hierarchical Whole-body Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> HUMANOIDARENA is a new simulation benchmark that evaluates egocentric hierarchical whole-body learning for humanoid robots, emphasizing complex "leg-critical" interaction tasks. It reveals that learned policies struggle with cross-General Motion Tracker (GMT) transfer and are sensitive to visual perturbations, experiencing significant performance drops in deployment across different low-level execution backends.

## Problem

- Developing scalable policies for humanoid robots that seamlessly integrate high-level task decision-making with low-level whole-body dynamic execution, especially when the lower body is structurally involved.
- Existing benchmarks often evaluate end-to-end control stacks or fixed action representations, hindering the isolation of high-level policy learning from low-level execution and the assessment of the policy-tracker interface.
- A lack of controlled settings to study and benchmark 'leg-critical' interactions that demand precise foot placement, balance maintenance, and whole-body reorientation, beyond simple locomotion or manipulation.

## Method

- Introduced HUMANOIDARENA, a simulation-first benchmark built on Isaac Lab, which explicitly decouples high-level, perception-conditioned policies from low-level ==General Motion Trackers (GMTs)==.
- Developed seven 'leg-critical' egocentric Human-Object Interaction (HOI) and Human-Scene Interaction (HSI) tasks, requiring coordinated whole-body movements and collected via VR-based teleoperation into a canonical 40D intermediate action space.
- Defined controlled evaluation protocols for robustness to visual, semantic, and execution perturbations, and for cross-GMT transferability, using metrics like Success Rate, Average Fall Rate, and transfer retention.

## Results

- In-GMT evaluation showed Flow Matching (FM) achieved the best average success rates on HOI (**36.11%**) and HSI (**58.75%**) with TWIST2, while Diffusion Policy (DP) performed best with SONIC (**52.22%** on HOI, **65.83%** on HSI).
- Perturbation-conditioned evaluations revealed performance drops across all baselines, with visual perturbations causing consistent decreases, for example, ACT's HOI success rate with TWIST2 dropping from **28.33%** to **14.44%**.
- Cross-GMT tests demonstrated average success rate drops of **39.9%** when policies trained with TWIST2 were deployed with SONIC, and **36.0%** for the reverse, with relative transfer retention often reaching **0.0%** for many tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The performance of hierarchical whole-body policies is significantly a property of the policy-tracker pair, with certain GMTs enabling higher success rates than others (e.g., SONIC generally outperformed TWIST2).
> - Policies exhibit varied robustness; visual perturbations consistently caused the most substantial performance degradation, highlighting sensitivity to egocentric appearance shifts and the need for robust perception.
> - There are significant and asymmetric failures in cross-GMT transfer, indicating that high-level policies tend to overfit to the motion priors and dynamics of the GMT used during training, rather than learning truly tracker-invariant intermediate actions.
