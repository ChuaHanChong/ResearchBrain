# GE-Sim 2.0: A Roadmap Towards Comprehensive Closed-loop Video World Simulators for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> GE-Sim 2.0 introduces a comprehensive closed-loop video world simulator for robotic manipulation, integrating visual simulation, proprioceptive state prediction, and automated reward assessment. The system achieved top scores on the WorldArena leaderboard and demonstrated improved real-robot policy success rates by 15% through simulator-based filtered behavior cloning.

## Problem

- Evaluating complex robot manipulation policies on real robots is resource-intensive, slow, and difficult to reproduce, creating a bottleneck as models scale.
- Existing robotic benchmarks and simulators often inaccurately model contact dynamics, deformable objects, and fine-grained visual appearance, failing to capture real-world complexities.
- Prior action-conditioned video world simulators primarily predict future visual states, lack proprioceptive state for policy feedback, do not provide machine-verifiable scoring, and have insufficient rendering throughput for scalable use.

## Method

- **GE-Sim 2.0** enhances an ==action-conditioned multi-view diffusion== model (vision expert) by retraining it on thousands of hours of diverse real-world robot data, incorporating memory-frame augmentation for robustness.
- It introduces a Proprioceptive State Expert, running in parallel with the vision expert, to predict joint-space states by consuming visual context from the vision model's intermediate features.
- A World Judge module, based on a vision-language model, provides machine-verifiable success signals and rewards by scoring generated rollouts frame-by-frame against task instructions.
- An acceleration framework, combining step distillation and random-stride training, enables high-throughput rollout generation, achieving 100-frame rollouts in ~2.3 seconds on a single H100 GPU.

## Results

- GE-Sim 2.0 achieved the top overall score on the public WorldArena leaderboard and demonstrated superior visual quality (e.g., +3.96 dB PSNR over Ctrl-World) and long-horizon temporal stability across six complex manipulation tasks.
- The simulator exhibited high closed-loop consistency, with simulated success rates closely aligning with real-robot outcomes and achieving an average episode-level success prediction accuracy of **0.81** and recall of **0.82** with state conditioning.
- Using GE-Sim 2.0 for filtered behavior cloning improved real-robot policy success rates by an average of **15%** (from **0.417** to **0.567**) across representative tasks, demonstrating its utility for policy learning beyond evaluation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Extensive training on diverse, action-grounded real-world robot data significantly improves the visual fidelity, action-following accuracy, and long-horizon temporal robustness of video world simulators, allowing them to outperform general video generators.
> - Integrating a dedicated proprioceptive state expert is crucial for maintaining closed-loop policy consistency and preventing policy drift, particularly in tasks requiring accurate state tracking across multiple action chunks.
> - A specialized, task-aware 'world judge' is more effective than general-purpose multimodal LLMs for robustly assessing task success and providing reliable reward signals in complex robotic manipulation scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning is undergoing a period of scaling, characterized by larger models, extensive datasets, and advanced vision-language-action (VLA) policies. These policies are enabling robots to perform complex manipulation tasks that extend beyond rigid-body pick-and-place to include long-horizon, contact-rich, and deformable-object interactions. However, the evaluation of these increasingly capable policies has emerged as a significant bottleneck. Real-robot benchmarking is resource-intensive, slow, and challenging to reproduce. Existing robotic benchmarks and simulators frequently exhibit limitations in accurately modeling contact dynamics, deformable objects, fine-grained visual appearance, and even the nuances of robot actuation, often abstracting away critical physical effects. This discrepancy between what can be trained and what can be reliably evaluated is widening.

Against this background, recent advancements in generative video modeling, which can synthesize photorealistic footage across diverse scenes and interactions, offer an alternative approach. This has motivated the development of neural world simulators for manipulation. In this paradigm, a model generates a video of a robot executing a specified behavior, given an initial observation and an action trajectory. By replacing hand-built physics and rendering engines with data-driven generative processes, such simulators aim to capture the variety of real-world appearances and interactions that traditional engines often miss. This approach holds the potential for scalable evaluation and closed-loop policy learning, including reinforcement learning, for modern manipulation policies.

Previous research in video-based world simulators for manipulation, including the authors' prior work GE-Sim, typically re-purposes pre-trained text-image-to-video (TI2V) generators into action-image-to-video simulators by substituting text conditions with action conditions. Much of the effort has focused on ensuring fidelity in action-following. However, existing systems still exhibit limitations in simulating deformable objects and out-of-distribution or failure trajectories. More fundamentally, these systems primarily predict future visual states, often lacking the proprioceptive state necessary for modern policy models. They also render rollouts without providing machine-verifiable scoring, which is crucial for scalable evaluation and reward-driven learning. Furthermore, their rendering throughput has often been insufficient for the demands of chunk-wise, parallel rollout across numerous tasks. GE-Sim 2.0 is positioned to address these three gaps, aiming to advance the capabilities of action-conditioned video models toward a comprehensive closed-loop world simulator for manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of GE-Sim 2.0 is to establish a practical and scalable platform for the evaluation and closed-loop learning of manipulation policies. This objective is motivated by several critical challenges in contemporary robot learning:

1. **Addressing Evaluation Bottlenecks**: As robot learning models grow in complexity and scope, the traditional methods of policy evaluation become increasingly impractical. Real-robot experiments are inherently slow, difficult to replicate, and costly. Existing simulation environments, while useful, often fail to accurately capture the complexities of real-world physics, such as deformable object interactions, intricate contact dynamics, and the subtle visual details of environments, which are crucial for assessing advanced manipulation policies.
2. **Leveraging Generative AI for Simulation**: The emergence of highly capable generative video models offers a new pathway to overcome the limitations of handcrafted simulators. These models, trained on vast visual datasets, can synthesize photorealistic and diverse visual sequences, potentially covering the "long tail" of real-world variations and interactions that are difficult to programmatically specify in traditional physics engines. The motivation is to harness this capability to create a simulator that learns the visual world directly from data.
3. **Enhancing Action-Following Fidelity**: Existing action-conditioned video simulators, including earlier iterations of GE-Sim, have shown promise in generating visually plausible rollouts. However, their fidelity, particularly in scenarios involving deformable objects, out-of-distribution actions, or failure conditions, requires substantial improvement to be reliable for policy development. A key motivation is to re-train the foundational video generation model on extensive real-world robot data to achieve more accurate and robust action-following.
4. **Closing the Policy-Interaction Loop**: A significant gap in current video simulators is their inability to provide policy models with the complete range of information required for iterative decision-making. Policies often rely on proprioceptive state (e.g., joint angles, gripper states) in addition to visual observations for predicting subsequent actions. A core objective is to integrate a mechanism to decode and provide this critical proprioceptive state from the simulated visual latents.
5. **Enabling Automated Evaluation and Reward Signals**: For scalable evaluation and reward-driven learning paradigms like reinforcement learning (RL) or filtered behavior cloning (BC), simulators must provide machine-verifiable success signals and rewards. Current video simulators typically only render visual outcomes without inherent judgment capabilities. A key motivation is to develop a "world judge" that can automatically score generated rollouts against task instructions, transforming raw video output into actionable feedback.
6. **Achieving High Throughput**: The utility of a simulator for large-scale policy training and evaluation depends heavily on its speed. The multi-step inference processes typical of diffusion models can be computationally expensive. Therefore, a practical objective is to develop acceleration techniques to significantly increase the throughput of video rollout generation, enabling efficient chunk-wise and parallel simulation.

In summary, GE-Sim 2.0 aims to transform video generation models from passive visual predictors into active, closed-loop simulation environments that can support the full lifecycle of modern robotic manipulation policy development, from scalable evaluation to iterative learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GE-Sim 2.0 builds upon the Genie Envisioner platform, specifically its GE-Base world foundation model and GE-Sim action-conditioned simulator, and introduces three new modules to achieve closed-loop capabilities and improved performance.

**Core Foundation: Action-Conditioned Video Generation (Vision Expert)**
The visual backbone of GE-Sim 2.0 is an action-conditioned multi-view diffusion model, referred to as the vision expert. It retains the chunk-wise autoregressive generation, sparse memory mechanism, and multi-view diffusion transformer architecture of GE-Base.

- **Training Data**: The vision expert is re-trained on thousands of hours of real-world robot data, encompassing large-scale teleoperation episodes, contact-rich arm-object interaction sequences, and rollouts collected during policy deployment on physical robots. This data diversity aims to improve action-following accuracy, visual fidelity of object deformation and contact, and coverage of diverse trajectories (successes, failures, varied tasks/scenes).
- **Conditioning Interface**: The model operates in the latent space of a video VAE. All conditions are integrated via channel-wise concatenation. The input `z_cond` includes:
 - `z_noisy`: The 16-channel noisy video latent.
 - `R_ray`: A 6-channel per-pixel ray map derived from camera intrinsics and extrinsics, encoding the camera's viewpoint motion (origin and unit direction of each pixel's ray in the world frame).
 - `M_pose`: A 3-channel End-Effector (EE) pose map. This is generated by rendering the dual-arm future EE trajectory into the image space of each camera view. It encodes EE position (projected origin and axis keypoints), orientation, and gripper openness (via a continuous color map), with distinct color schemes for each arm. This decouples viewpoint changes from EE motion.
 - `m_cond`: A 1-channel binary mask indicating memory frames versus predicted frames.
- **Training Objective**: A latent flow-matching objective, `L_video`, is used, computed only on frames to be predicted.
- **Memory-Frame Augmentation**: To mitigate distribution shift when using self-generated memory frames during closed-loop inference, a randomized error-simulation mechanism is applied during training. This involves progressive noise mixing, local Gaussian blur, and multi-view-synchronized color jitter to memory frame latents.

**Proprioceptive State Expert**
This module addresses the need for proprioceptive state information, which is critical for many policy models. It runs in parallel with the vision expert.

- **Function**: Predicts the joint-space state sequence (`s_hat_t`) for the corresponding time interval, consuming intermediate features from the vision expert as visual context.
- **Proprioceptive State Representation**: A 16-dimensional vector per frame, concatenating the seven joint angles and gripper openness for both the left and right arms (`θ_L_t, g_L_t, θ_R_t, g_R_t`).
- **Visual Context**: The vision expert's intermediate layer outputs (`h_video_l`) are aggregated into a single fused visual feature (`H_fuse`) using learnable scalar weights and a LayerNorm. This `H_fuse` serves as the key and value for cross-attention in the proprioceptive state expert, attending to features from all camera views.
- **Architecture**: A stack of lightweight transformer blocks. Each block includes self-attention (with rotary positional embedding on the time axis), cross-attention to `H_fuse`, and a feed-forward layer. Diffusion timestep is injected via AdaLN-single.
- **Training**: Trained with a flow-matching objective, `L_proprio_state`, on top of a frozen vision expert. Only the future proprioceptive state tokens are noised.
- **History-State Augmentation**: To improve robustness against noisy history states during closed-loop inference, two perturbations are applied to the history segment during training: temporal index perturbation (simulating misalignment) and history-trajectory resampling (mimicking temporal stretching/compression).

**World Judge**
This module provides machine-verifiable success signals and rewards for evaluation and learning.

- **Function**: Scores generated rollouts frame by frame against task instructions, producing a task-completion success signal (`r_hat_t`).
- **Backbone**: Uses a vision-language model (VLM). The vision encoder is frozen, while the language model and a downstream prediction head are trained.
- **Input**: Each frame of the rollout is fed individually as a separate image. A dedicated per-frame token's hidden state forms the representation (`f_i`) for that frame. The textual condition is a sub-task caption.
- **Success Head and Supervision**: A lightweight MLP predicts a binary success logit (`s_hat_i`) per frame. Supervision comes from manual success annotations (`i_succ`) for each trajectory, defining success for `i >= i_succ` and failure otherwise. A class-balanced binary cross-entropy loss `L_judge` is used to address data imbalance.
- **Closed-Loop Integration**: The world judge provides a success curve over the rollout, serving as an automated decision for policy evaluation and dense feedback for reward-driven learning.

**Acceleration Framework**
To enable scalable policy evaluation, the system incorporates two acceleration techniques:

- **Step Distillation**: The multi-step diffusion world model is distilled into a few-step student using the DMD2 distribution-matching framework. The student is trained to generate high-quality samples in 1 to 4 inference steps, significantly reducing generation time.
- **Temporal Acceleration via Random-Stride Training**: During world model training, frames within each chunk are sampled with a random temporal stride. This allows the model to learn from trajectories of varying temporal densities. At inference, this enables frame skipping (up to 4x) to cover longer time spans with fewer frames and autoregressive chunks, without noticeable loss in consistency.

Combining these techniques, GE-Sim 2.0 can generate a 100-frame rollout in approximately 2.3 seconds on a single H100 GPU with four inference steps.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of GE-Sim 2.0 focused on five key questions: visual fidelity, closed-loop faithfulness, verifiable rewards, policy improvement capabilities, and individual component contributions. Experiments were conducted on six long-horizon dual-arm manipulation tasks, chosen to challenge video world models with liquid handling, deformable objects, fine-force interactions, open-flame ignition, language-grounded selection, and surface contact under appearance variation. Comparisons were made against leading open robotic world models (Ctrl-World, DreamDojo) and closed-source general video generators (Sora, Veo), as well as a general-purpose multimodal LLM (Qwen3.5-122B) for reward judging.

#### 4.1. Video Simulation Quality

- **WorldArena Leaderboard**: GE-Sim 2.0, utilizing a 2B-parameter backbone, achieved the top overall score on the public WorldArena leaderboard. This performance surpassed both dedicated robotic world models (e.g., Ctrl-World, DreamDojo, GigaWorld, ABot) and substantially larger closed-source general video generators (e.g., Sora, Veo). This suggests the importance of domain-specific training on diverse, action-grounded robot data.
- **Per-Task Replay Metrics**: Across six manipulation tasks, GE-Sim 2.0 demonstrated superior performance on all five visual quality metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FID, FVD) in both single-view (head camera) and multi-view configurations. For instance, on the head view, it improved PSNR by +3.96 dB over Ctrl-World and +5.67 dB over DreamDojo, halved FID, and reduced FVD by more than a factor of two. In the multi-view setting, GE-Sim 2.0 showed even more pronounced improvements, such as -31.9 FID and -2.5× FVD relative to Ctrl-World.
- **Long-Horizon Temporal Robustness**: GE-Sim 2.0 maintained visual stability over long-horizon rollouts. When videos were divided into 10-second segments, GE-Sim 2.0 exhibited a PSNR degradation of under 4 dB across a 50-second rollout, flattening after the first segment. In contrast, baselines showed sharper declines, often dropping by nearly 5 dB more than GE-Sim 2.0. This stability is attributed to large-scale real-robot retraining, memory-frame augmentation, and random-stride training.

#### 4.2. Closed-Loop Policy Consistency

- **Task-Level Success-Rate Alignment**: When policies were run inside GE-Sim 2.0, the simulated success rates closely aligned with real-robot outcomes across tasks. The fitted trend line for GE-Sim 2.0 with state conditioning had a slope close to one with a small negative offset, indicating better preservation of real-robot task difficulty. Removing state conditioning weakened this correspondence, and Ctrl-World showed a larger mismatch.
- **Episode-Level Outcome Agreement**: GE-Sim 2.0 with state conditioning achieved the best average accuracy (0.81) and recall (0.82) for predicting episode-level success or failure, compared to Ctrl-World (accuracy 0.63, recall 0.25) and GE-Sim 2.0 without state conditioning (accuracy 0.74, recall 0.67). This indicates that the simulator is effective at preserving successful episodes and aligning with real-world policy behavior.

#### 4.3. World Judge: Reward Quality

- **Comparison with General VLM**: The GE-Sim 2.0 world judge was evaluated against Qwen3.5-122B, a strong general-purpose multimodal LLM. On world-model (WM) rollouts, the GE-Sim 2.0 world judge achieved 79% accuracy (vs. 60% for Qwen) and a reduced event distance (28.2 frames vs. 57.8 frames). Similar advantages were observed on ground-truth (GT) videos. Qwen struggled particularly with tasks involving subtle visual cues or specific success criteria (e.g., "Clean mirror stains").

#### 4.4. WM-Based Filtered Behavior Cloning

- **Policy Improvement**: As a proof-of-concept, GE-Sim 2.0 was used to generate synthetic trajectories, which were then scored by the world judge, filtered for high-reward episodes, and mixed with original behavior cloning data for policy training. This "WM-filtered BC" consistently improved real-robot success rates across three representative tasks. The average success rate increased from 0.417 to 0.567, representing an absolute gain of 0.150. This demonstrates the simulator's utility beyond evaluation, contributing to policy learning.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Proprioceptive State Expert**: Removing the proprioceptive state expert reduced average episode-level accuracy from 0.81 to 0.74 and recall from 0.82 to 0.67. This component was found to be particularly beneficial for tasks requiring accurate state tracking across multiple action chunks or involving fine-grained arm motion and contact maintenance, preventing policy drift during autoregressive rollouts.
- **Specialist vs. General-Purpose Reward Model**: Replacing the specialist world judge with Qwen3.5-122B reduced WM-mode accuracy by 19 percentage points and increased event distance by 30 frames. This confirms that a task-aware specialist reward model is more effective than a general VLM for robust success judgment in robotic manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GE-Sim 2.0 represents an advancement in the development of video world simulators for robotic manipulation, moving beyond "view-only" video generation to a more comprehensive, closed-loop system.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Comprehensive Simulation Platform**: It integrates visual simulation, proprioceptive state prediction, and reward assessment into a single, efficient framework. This addresses critical gaps identified in previous video world models, particularly the lack of proprioceptive state and machine-verifiable reward signals.
- **Enhanced Fidelity and Robustness**: Through extensive retraining on diverse real-world robot data, GE-Sim 2.0 demonstrates superior visual fidelity, action-following accuracy, and long-horizon temporal stability compared to existing robotic world models and even larger general video generators. Its top performance on the WorldArena leaderboard underscores the efficacy of domain-specific data and architectural design.
- **Practical for Policy Development**: The system's ability to provide accurate visual and proprioceptive state, combined with a reliable world judge for success evaluation, makes it a practical tool. The demonstrated improvements in real-robot policy performance through WM-filtered behavior cloning highlight its potential to actively drive policy learning, not merely serve as a passive replay mechanism.
- **Efficiency**: The acceleration framework, leveraging step distillation and random-stride training, allows for high-throughput rollout generation, addressing the computational bottlenecks that hinder large-scale policy evaluation and training.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Scalable Robot Learning**: By providing a robust, high-fidelity, and efficient closed-loop simulation environment, GE-Sim 2.0 can significantly accelerate the development and evaluation of robotic manipulation policies. This is particularly crucial for complex, long-horizon, and contact-rich tasks where real-robot experimentation is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming.
- **Advanced Policy Training**: The integration of proprioceptive state and verifiable reward signals lays the groundwork for more sophisticated closed-loop policy learning paradigms, including online reinforcement learning. This could enable robots to learn new skills and adapt to novel environments more efficiently by leveraging synthetic data and iterative feedback.
- **Democratization of Robot Learning**: A highly capable and accessible simulator could lower the barrier to entry for robot learning research, allowing more researchers to experiment with complex policies without extensive access to physical robot hardware.
- **Foundational Step for Embodied AI**: GE-Sim 2.0 defines a set of components for an "omni world simulator" for robotic manipulation. It provides a roadmap for future work towards general embodied AI, where world models can simulate not just visual observations but also internal robot states and task outcomes.

#### 5.3. Looking Forward

The authors acknowledge several future directions, including scaling the data and models to include cross-embodiment and ego-view data, unifying state decoding and success judgment into a single world model, transitioning from offline filtered behavior cloning to online closed-loop reinforcement learning within the simulator, and validating the system across a broader range of VLA policy families. These steps aim to further enhance the generality and utility of video world simulators for embodied AI.
