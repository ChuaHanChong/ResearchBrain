# AR-VLA: True Autoregressive Action Expert for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> AR-VLA introduces an autoregressive action expert for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, generating continuous, kinematically consistent robot trajectories by maintaining persistent temporal context. The framework achieved an average 61.5% success rate on SimplerEnv tasks, surpassing existing generalist policies, and demonstrated superior history awareness on multi-stage manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models predominantly operate reactively, predicting actions in isolated chunks and suffering from "Markovian amnesia" by discarding temporal context.
- This reactive approach leads to disjointed, jittery action trajectories and hinders the execution of long-horizon tasks requiring continuous intent and memory of past states.
- A fundamental frequency mismatch exists between the slow, high-level semantic reasoning of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and the fast, high-frequency requirements of robotic motor control.

## Method

- **AR-VLA** proposes a standalone ==Autoregressive (AR) Action Expert== that generates actions as a continuous, causal sequence, maintaining its own long-lived memory through a ==Hybrid Key-Value (HKV) cache==.
- It structurally decouples high-level semantic reasoning from high-frequency motor control, allowing the action expert to condition on asynchronously updated VLM prefixes without interrupting its continuous action stream.
- A ==Dynamic Temporal Re-anchoring (DTR)== mechanism, leveraging ==Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE)==, is introduced to mathematically account for and bridge the temporal misalignment between asynchronous visual-language conditions and the action stream.

## Results

- Achieved an average **61.5%** success rate on SimplerEnv tasks, outperforming state-of-the-art generalist policies like CogACT (**52.1%**) and Pi-0.5 (**51.0%**) with the same VLM backbone.
- Demonstrated superior history awareness on non-Markovian tasks, achieving **81.2%** completion on PushT2 (vs. **43.8%** for Action Chunking) and **44.0%** completion on Stack3 (vs. **24.0%** for Diffusion Policy).
- Produced smoother trajectories with the lowest maximum (**39.83 rad/s^3**) and average (**7.89 rad/s^3**) jerk, while maintaining a stable **29ms** per action control frequency despite a **70ms** VLM perception rate.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Maintaining a persistent, causal history of past actions and states within a dedicated autoregressive expert significantly enhances temporal consistency and kinematic smoothness in robot trajectories.
> - Decoupling the high-frequency action generation from slower VLM perception enables a stable control frequency and allows the system to robustly handle the inherent latency differences.
> - Effective integration of asynchronous visual-language context into a continuous action stream requires explicit temporal modeling of staleness, which DTR with RoPE addresses by encoding relative distances.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic control has seen increasing adoption of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which leverage large-scale pretraining from Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to enable robots to understand and execute tasks based on visual observations and linguistic instructions. This paradigm, inspired by the success of "next-token prediction" in Large Language Models (LLMs), aims to equip robots with broad semantic understanding for physical control.

However, the paper identifies a key limitation in current VLA architectures (e.g., OpenVLA, RT-2, Diffusion Policies, π0-FAST). While often termed "autoregressive," these models typically operate in a "reactive" manner. They predict actions in "chunks" or static blocks, often resetting their temporal context with each new observation. This "action chunking" approach, while sometimes offering short-horizon smoothness, leads to what the authors refer to as "Markovian amnesia," where the model discards its internal state and temporal continuity between observation steps. Consequently, control decisions are based solely on immediate snapshots, resulting in disjointed, jittery action trajectories and a lack of consistent long-horizon intent.

This work proposes to address this fundamental structural limitation by introducing a truly autoregressive Action Expert. It seeks to integrate a continuous, causal sequence modeling approach into robot control, drawing a parallel to how LLMs predict the next word based on a flowing conversation. By maintaining a persistent internal state and explicit temporal awareness, this approach aims to bridge the frequency mismatch between slow, high-level semantic reasoning (from VLMs) and fast, low-level motor control, thereby enhancing the fluidity, consistency, and long-horizon capabilities of robotic policies. The paper positions itself as improving upon existing VLA and diffusion policy frameworks by providing a robust structural foundation for action generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, despite being labeled "autoregressive," do not consistently maintain temporal context across sequential actions. This architectural limitation results in policies that are fundamentally reactive and lack persistent internal memory of past actions and states, a phenomenon termed "Markovian amnesia." This leads to several issues in robotic manipulation:

- **Temporal Incoherence:** Actions are generated as a series of isolated events or chunks, rather than a continuous stream. This can cause jittery, inconsistent robot motions.
- **Loss of Context:** At each new perception step, the model re-encodes the visual context and generates actions as if "waking up" for the first time, losing awareness of "what has already occurred and how the end-effector is accelerating." This hinders performance on long-horizon tasks requiring memory of unobservable past states.
- **Frequency Mismatch:** Vision-Language Models (VLMs) operate at a slower frequency, performing complex semantic reasoning. Robotic control, however, requires high-frequency, continuous action generation. The current reactive VLA paradigm often couples these frequencies, forcing the control loop to wait for VLM updates or discarding valuable temporal information.

To address these motivations, the paper sets forth several key objectives:

- **Develop a standalone Autoregressive (AR) Action Expert:** This expert should generate actions as a continuous, causal sequence, maintaining its own history through a long-lived memory.
- **Decouple high-level semantic reasoning from high-frequency motor control:** This involves conditioning the Action Expert on refreshable, asynchronous vision-language prefixes from a VLM backbone without interrupting the action stream.
- **Ensure spatio-temporally consistent action generation:** By maintaining context, the expert should produce smoother, more kinematically consistent action trajectories compared to reactive baselines.
- **Facilitate independent pretraining of kinematic syntax:** Allow the action expert to learn the "language of motion"—dynamics, joint constraints, and physical causality—on action-only data, separate from visual alignment.
- **Introduce a synchronization mechanism:** Develop a method to mathematically account for and bridge the temporal misalignment (staleness) between asynchronously updated visual-language conditions and the continuous action stream during both training and inference.
- **Demonstrate superior history awareness and task success:** Validate the proposed AR-VLA framework on both specialist and generalist robotic manipulation tasks, showing improved performance, particularly on tasks requiring memory of past states.
- **Provide a scalable and robust structural foundation:** Offer an architecture that supports effective robotic policies by inherently managing temporal context.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The AR-VLA framework is designed to bridge the temporal gap between high-latency semantic perception (from VLMs) and high-frequency motor control. It employs a stateful, two-stage architecture that integrates a standalone autoregressive action expert.

**Problem Formulation:**
The paper formalizes robot trajectory generation as a sequence prediction problem.
- **Reactive Actor (Standard VLAs):** Predicts the current action ($a_t$) based solely on the current observation ($o_t$), which includes visual frames ($v_t$), language instructions ($l$), and proprioceptive states ($s_t$). Its context memory is reset at each step, leading to $P_{react}(\tau) = \prod_{t=1}^T P(a_t | \Phi(v_t, l), s_t)$, where $\Phi(\cdot)$ is the perception encoder.
- **Autoregressive (AR) Actor (AR-VLA):** Predicts the current action ($a_t$) based on a continuous kinematic history ($a_{<t}, s_{<t}$) and the most recently available visual-language prefix ($\Phi(v_i, l)$), where $i \le t$ is the index of the most recent visual frame. This is formulated as $P_{ar}(\tau) = \prod_{t=1}^T P(a_t | \Phi(v_i, l), a_{<t}, s_{<t})$. This explicit dependency on continuous history is intended to ensure kinematic smoothness and robustness to VL latency.

**Model Structure:**
AR-VLA is instantiated as a unified Transformer decoder designed to handle a hybrid stream of continuous proprioceptive data and high-dimensional VLM embeddings.

- **Continuous Action Representation:** Robot actions ($a_t \in \mathbb{R}^d$) are typically target end-effector pose deltas or joint velocities. These continuous vectors are projected to the model's hidden dimension via a linear layer, treating each timestep's vector as a single token. The model's output is then regressed to $a_{t+1}$ using a deterministic prediction head.
- **Unified Decoder with Hybrid Key-Value (HKV) Cache:** The architecture leverages a specialized HKV Cache to manage two distinct memory streams with different update rules, enabling structural decoupling:
 - **Proprioceptive Stream (KV_X):** A rolling First-In-First-Out (FIFO) buffer that stores the Key-Value (KV) pairs of the robot’s past states and actions. This buffer maintains a longer history compared to reactive VLAs, capturing the momentum of the trajectory.
 - **Visual-Language Stream (KV_VL):** A single-slot buffer that stores KV pairs projected from the VLM backbone. This stream functions as a refreshable semantic prefix, replaced entirely whenever a new visual frame is processed.
- **Dynamic Temporal Re-anchoring (DTR):** This mechanism addresses the temporal alignment challenge between asynchronous streams by using Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE). RoPE encodes the relative distance between the high-frequency action stream and the inherently atemporal VL context.
 - For action tokens ($k_X$), RoPE assigns sequential indices ($n$) corresponding to their execution timestep.
 - For VL tokens ($k_{VL}$), RoPE assigns a fixed index ($n$) corresponding to the timestep when the image was captured.
 - This formulation ensures that the attention score ($Score(q_m, k_{VL_n})$) between a query at time $m$ and a VL key at time $n$ depends only on the relative distance ($m-n$), which represents the "data staleness." Crucially, RoPE's shift-invariance property means that $Score(q_{m+T}, k_{VL_{n+T}}) = Score(q_m, k_{VL_n})$. This allows the model to generalize across varying absolute timesteps, ensuring that the same visual grounding logic applies regardless of when a visual frame was captured relative to the current action.

**Training Details:**
A two-phase training protocol is employed:

- **Phase 1: Action-Only Pretraining:** The Action Expert is initially trained on large-scale kinematic trajectories as a standalone autoregressive sequence generator. Using a causal mask and sequential RoPE indices, it optimizes a sequence modeling objective $L_{Phase1} = \sum_{t=1}^T L(x_t | x_{<t})$. This phase aims to teach the expert the "syntax of motion"—robot dynamics, joint constraints, and common movement patterns—independently of visual or linguistic data.
- **Phase 2: VL-Action Alignment:** The VLM backbone is connected to the pretrained Action Expert via DTR. Given a training sample (past actions $x_{past}$, visual observation $v$, language instruction $l$, future actions $x_{fut}$), the process involves:
 - **Priming:** Past actions ($x_{past}$) are fed into the actor with indices $\{0, \dots, H-1\}$.
 - **Anchoring:** VL features from $(v, l)$ are assigned a fixed index $H$, anchoring the perception to the history-future junction.
 - **Stochastic Supervision with Historical Dropout:** The model predicts a horizon of $M$ future actions ($x_{fut}$). To prevent over-reliance on a perfect historical context and simulate execution noise, a random binary mask ($M_k$) is applied to the historical context for each future token. This encourages the model to attend to the VL prefix even when historical information is incomplete or corrupted. The final loss is $L_{Phase2} = \sum_{k=0}^{M-1} L(x_{H+k} | M_k \odot x_{past}, \Phi(v_H, l_H), x_{H:H+k-1})$.

**Inference Details:**
During inference, AR-VLA functions as a conditional next-token predictor $P(x_t | x_{<t}, \Phi(v_i, l_i))$.

- **Hybrid KV Cache Maintenance:** The `KV_X` (action domain) cache operates as a persistent FIFO buffer. The `KV_VL` (visual-language) cache is a refreshable, single-slot FIFO buffer. When a new visual embedding becomes available (captured at time $i$), DTR is explicitly applied to its keys, and the `KV_VL` content is replaced, "snapping" the perception to the new timestamp.
- **Decoupled Dual-Thread Architecture:** This design allows for asynchronous execution:
 - **Action Thread:** Operates at a high control frequency, autoregressively generating actions, updating the `KV_X` cache, and incrementing the global time index.
 - **Perception Thread:** Operates at the VLM's native (slower) frequency, processing the latest visual frame and asynchronously pushing updates to the `KV_VL` buffer.
This decoupling provides advantages in terms of lower latency per step and a consistent control frequency, as action prediction is not blocked by VLM inference. The model can condition on its internal kinematic model and the latest valid VL prefix until a new one is available, ensuring smooth, uninterrupted actuation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation aimed to assess AR-VLA's performance across specialist and generalist robot policies, focusing on task success, inference efficiency, trajectory quality, and history awareness.

**A. Generalist and Specialist Policy Performance**

- **SimplerEnv Simulation (BridgeV2 Pretraining):** AR-VLA achieved an average success rate of 61.5%, outperforming other state-of-the-art generalist policies. It surpassed CogACT by 9.4% (52.1%) and also exceeded Pi-0-Fast (49.0%) and Pi-0.5 (51.0%), despite sharing an identical VLM backbone with the latter two. Notable performance gains were observed on tasks requiring precise manipulation, such as the "carrot task" (54.2% success rate, significantly higher than Pi-0-Fast at 29.2% and Pi-0.5 at 33.3%).
- **Real-world WidowX Evaluation (Zero-Shot):** In zero-shot real-world deployment, AR-VLA achieved an average success rate of 89%, with 100% success on tasks like "cup on plate" and "Lobster." The model demonstrated robustness, gracefully recovering from initial failures by re-attempting grasps, in contrast to baselines that exhibited erratic motions and unrecoverable states after failures.
- **Specialist Policy Performance:** AR Actor showed strong performance on specialist tasks:
 - **ALOHA Cube Transfer:** 97.33% scripted success and 67.33% human demonstration success, surpassing ACT (86.0%/50.0%) and Diffusion Policy (33.33%/10.0%).
 - **ALOHA Peg Insertion:** 54.67% scripted success compared to ACT's 32.0%.
 - **PushT:** Achieved a competitive 60.40% success rate and 0.920 Max IoU, close to Diffusion Policy's 65.20%.
 The AR Actor consistently performed well across diverse task types, suggesting its architectural simplicity and consistent objective contribute to task-agnostic performance.

**B. Efficiency and Smoothness Analysis**

- **Jerk and Latency:** AR-VLA exhibited the lowest maximum (39.83 rad/s$^3$) and average (7.89 rad/s$^3$) jerk during task execution, indicating smoother and more kinematically consistent trajectories. This was attributed to its ability to maintain spatial and temporal action consistency.
- **Control Frequency:** The asynchronous execution framework enabled AR-VLA to maintain a stable 29ms per action control frequency, even when the VLM perception backbone operated at a slower 70ms per frame. The overall effective latency per single action was 46.25ms, lower than chunk-based models.

**C. History-Awareness Evaluation**

- **PushT2 (Simulation) and Stack3 (Real-world):** Two tasks specifically designed to require memory of unobservable past states were used.
 - **PushT2:** Requires pushing a T-block to two goals sequentially, with the first goal becoming unobservable midway. AR-VLA (with history length H=40) achieved 81.2% completion, significantly higher than Action Chunking (43.8%) and Flow-matching (56.3%).
 - **Stack3:** Involves covering a battery with one cup and stacking another, where the battery's location becomes unobservable once covered. AR-VLA (H=40) achieved 44.0% completion, outperforming Flow-matching (34.0%) and Diffusion Policy (24.0%).
- These results indicated that AR-VLA's autoregressive generation and persistent KV cache allowed it to maintain task intent and kinematic momentum, leading to superior success rates on these non-Markovian, multi-stage manipulation sequences, where reactive policies suffered from "temporal amnesia."

**D. Ablations on Design Decisions**

- **Causal Pretraining (Phase 1):** Removing this phase increased Phase 2 convergence time by 2x and resulted in inferior final performance (37.5% success vs. 61.5%), demonstrating the importance of learning kinematic syntax prior to visual alignment.
- **RoPE Re-anchoring:** Replacing Dynamic Temporal Re-anchoring (DTR) with fixed or no positional embedding led to substantial performance drops (e.g., a 52% drop without any positional embedding), confirming the criticality of proper temporal position encoding for robust autoregressive action generation and generalization to varying temporal offsets.
- **Stochastic History Masking:** A mask rate of 0.6 achieved optimal performance (61.5%). A 0.0 mask rate, despite leading to the lowest validation error, resulted in 0% task success, indicating that the model over-relied on its internal history and failed during rollouts when predictions deviated from training data. This highlights the importance of robustness to prediction errors.
- **Context Length:** Increasing the action KV cache length from 1 to 20 steps resulted in a clear upward trend in success rate (from 36.5% to 61.5%), directly validating that extended historical context correlates with improved performance for autoregressive action experts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in this paper introduces a structural shift in how Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models generate robotic control. By formulating a standalone Autoregressive (AR) Action Expert, the research moves beyond reactive, snapshot-based control to a paradigm of continuous streaming action sequences.

**Significance:**

- **Enhanced Temporal Consistency and Smoothness:** The AR-VLA framework directly addresses the temporal inconsistencies prevalent in existing VLA architectures. By maintaining a persistent causal history of its own actions, the model ensures spatio-temporally consistent action generation, resulting in significantly smoother trajectories and reduced execution latency compared to reactive baselines.
- **Improved History Awareness for Long-Horizon Tasks:** The proposed architecture demonstrates superior robustness in long-horizon tasks that explicitly require maintaining historical context, such as PushT2 and Stack3. This capability allows the robot to infer unobservable states and maintain task intent over extended periods, which is a common limitation for memoryless reactive policies.
- **Decoupling of Perception and Control:** AR-VLA structurally decouples high-level semantic reasoning (from VLMs) from high-frequency motor control. This enables the motor thread to run at a consistent, high frequency independently of the VLM's latency, thereby addressing a fundamental frequency mismatch in robotics.
- **Modular and Scalable Training:** The two-phase training protocol, including action-only pretraining, allows the model to master the kinematic "syntax of motion" independently. This modularity facilitates efficient learning of robot dynamics and enables asynchronous integration of perception and control, offering a scalable framework for embodied learning.
- **Robustness to Perception Staleness:** The Dynamic Temporal Re-anchoring (DTR) mechanism provides a mathematical solution to account for the "staleness" of visual-language features, ensuring that the attention mechanism generalizes robustly to varying temporal offsets between perception updates and action generation.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Foundation for More Capable Robotic Policies:** The AR-VLA architecture provides a robust structural foundation for training effective robotic policies that can perform more fluid, adaptive, and intelligent manipulation in complex, real-world environments.
- **Advancements in Generalist and Specialist Robot Control:** The demonstrated improvements across both generalist VLA policies and specialist task-specific scenarios suggest that this autoregressive approach can enhance the capabilities of a wide range of robotic applications.
- **Improved Human-Robot Interaction:** By enabling robots to execute smoother, more predictable, and context-aware movements, AR-VLA could contribute to more natural and intuitive human-robot interaction in collaborative and assistive settings.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** The paper identifies several open challenges and future directions, including robust handling of Out-of-Distribution (OOD) trajectories, synergistic joint training objectives for VLMs and action experts, and the potential for fully integrated or "streaming" VLMs that maintain continuous memory across both visual and linguistic modalities. This indicates that the work could serve as a stepping stone for further innovation in embodied AI and robotic learning.
