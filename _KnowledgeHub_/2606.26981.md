# In-Context Model Predictive Generation: Open-Vocabulary Motion Synthesis from Language Models to Physics

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Sun Yat-sen University and Peng Cheng Laboratory developed In-Context Model Predictive Generation (ICMPG), a framework that synthesizes human motion from open-vocabulary text descriptions with both semantic fidelity and physical realism. The system leverages large language models for high-level semantic planning and uses a Model Predictive Control-inspired, closed-loop refinement process with physics and semantic rewards, achieving competitive semantic alignment and physical plausibility on diverse benchmarks, including an 82.9% success rate and 3.704 Phys-Err on BABEL.

## Problem

- Existing text-to-motion synthesis methods often face a trade-off between semantic fidelity (interpreting diverse language) and physical realism (adhering to physical laws).
- Large Language Model (LLM)-based approaches produce semantically rich motions but frequently violate physical constraints, leading to implausible dynamics.
- Physics-aware models generate realistic motions but struggle with complex, open-vocabulary instructions due to semantic brittleness and often require expensive task-specific policy training.

## Method

- The In-Context Model Predictive Generation (**ICMPG**) framework combines a Context-Aware Motion Generation (CAMG) module for initial LLM-based planning with a ==Model Predictive Generation (MPG)== module for inference-time physical and semantic refinement.
- CAMG uses a VQ-VAE to discretize motion into tokens, an LLM Planner to decompose text commands, and a SemAligner to retrieve relevant in-context examples for the LLM Generator.
- MPG applies a Model Predictive Control-like iterative process, using pre-trained physics policies and semantic encoders to evaluate candidate motion sequences and select the physically and semantically optimal segment for refinement in a closed-loop.

## Results

- ICMPG with LoRA fine-tuning achieved the best CLIP Score (**29.32**) and R-Precision (Top-1: **0.316**) on the open-vocabulary BABEL dataset, outperforming physics-based and LLM-based baselines in semantic alignment.
- The framework demonstrated high physical realism with an **82.9%** success rate and a Phys-Err of **3.704** on BABEL, comparable to dedicated physics-based methods without task-specific reinforcement learning.
- On challenging benchmarks, ICMPG achieved a higher MM-Hit (**76.3%**) than MotionCraft (**74.1%**) while maintaining a significantly lower Mod-Cost (**1.85** vs. **7.92**), indicating a better balance between semantic fidelity and physical reliability for complex instructions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reformulating motion synthesis as a Model Predictive Control (MPC) problem allows for dynamic, closed-loop refinement of motions at inference time, bridging the semantic-physical gap without extensive task-specific policy retraining.
> - Leveraging LLMs for high-level semantic planning and contextual understanding, combined with a discrete motion token representation, enables robust open-vocabulary motion generation.
> - A dual reward system, combining physical simulation feedback and semantic alignment scores, effectively guides the generative process to produce both realistic and semantically faithful motions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of human motion synthesis from textual descriptions is integral to the development of immersive digital applications such as gaming, virtual reality, and cinematic production. Traditional text-to-motion methods have shown progress but often struggle with generalizing to diverse, open-vocabulary instructions due to their reliance on large-scale, pre-labeled motion capture datasets.

The broader research landscape for text-to-motion synthesis can be divided into two main paradigms:
- **LLM-based approaches**: These methods leverage the semantic understanding and autoregressive capabilities of large language models (LLMs) to interpret open-vocabulary instructions and generate high-level action plans. While effective at semantic interpretation, they frequently produce motions that violate fundamental physical constraints, leading to physically implausible dynamics. Examples include Prompt, Plan, Perform and other LLM-based generators.
- **Physics-aware models**: These approaches integrate physical constraints, often through simulation or reinforcement learning (RL), to generate motions that are physically realistic and interact plausibly with environments. However, they typically exhibit semantic brittleness, struggling with complex, fine-grained, or novel open-vocabulary instructions due to insufficient capture of semantic nuance. Examples include PhysDiff and AnySkill, which usually require task-specific policy training. Other control-based methods like PADL depend on offline skill libraries, limiting diversity.

Existing solutions often face a trade-off: methods excelling in semantic fidelity tend to lack physical realism, and vice-versa. Open-loop generation in many LLM-based models further exacerbates this issue by separating semantic planning from physical execution. This paper positions itself at the intersection of these two paradigms, aiming to bridge the gap by integrating high-level semantic planning from LLMs with real-time physical feedback, particularly within an open-vocabulary context. It proposes a closed-loop, inference-time optimization strategy to reconcile semantic understanding with physical plausibility without necessitating extensive task-specific policy retraining.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework capable of synthesizing human motion from open-vocabulary textual descriptions that are both semantically faithful and physically realistic. The authors identify a critical gap in existing methods: the persistent trade-off between semantic fidelity (the ability to interpret diverse language) and physical realism (adherence to physical laws).

The key motivations driving this work are:
- **Addressing the semantic-physical gap:** Current LLM-based methods can interpret complex language but generate physically implausible motions. Physics-aware methods achieve realism but struggle with semantic complexity and generalization to novel or fine-grained instructions. The paper aims to reconcile these two aspects within a single framework.
- **Enabling open-vocabulary motion generation:** Many existing text-to-motion methods are limited by their dependence on large-scale, pre-labeled motion capture datasets, which restricts their ability to generalize to unseen textual descriptions. The goal is to create a system that can handle a broader range of textual inputs, including novel concepts and complex prompts.
- **Improving physical plausibility without extensive retraining:** Reinforcement learning-based physics-aware methods typically require training task-specific policies from scratch, which is computationally expensive and limits zero-shot adaptability. The authors aim for a solution that incorporates physical realism without the need for offline policy retraining for each new task.
- **Leveraging LLM capabilities for planning:** Recognizing the strength of LLMs in contextual understanding and decomposing complex instructions, the motivation is to utilize them as high-level planners to generate semantically grounded action sequences.
- **Introducing closed-loop refinement:** Unlike open-loop generation where errors can accumulate, the authors are motivated to implement a closed-loop feedback mechanism, inspired by Model Predictive Control (MPC), to continuously refine generated motions based on both physical and semantic evaluations at inference time. This iterative refinement is intended to improve stability and realism.
- **Maintaining architectural flexibility:** The framework is designed to be flexible enough to incorporate different LLM backbones and adjustable training configurations, allowing it to benefit from future advances in LLM technology.

In essence, the research aims to create a robust and adaptable text-driven motion synthesis system that simultaneously achieves high semantic fidelity and strong physical realism for diverse, open-vocabulary commands, while minimizing the need for task-specific training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, In-Context Model Predictive Generation (ICMPG), reformulates motion synthesis as a Model Predictive Control (MPC)-like process with two main modules: Context-Aware Motion Generation (CAMG) and Model Predictive Generation (MPG).

**A. Context-Aware Motion Generation (CAMG)**
The CAMG module is responsible for generating initial open-vocabulary motion candidates using an LLM.

1. **VQ-VAE (Vector Quantized Variational AutoEncoder):** Human motions are first encoded into discrete motion tokens (atomic motion primitives, e.g., 8-frame clips) using a VQ-VAE. This representation transforms continuous motion data into a discrete format amenable to LLM processing. The VQ-VAE decoder handles global decoding to ensure smooth transitions.

2. **LLM Planner:** A pre-trained, frozen LLM (e.g., LLaMA-3.1-70B-Instruct) serves as the Planner. It receives the input textual command and, guided by a specific prompt, decomposes it into a structured, motion-focused description (target prompt `t_p`). This involves temporal decomposition of complex instructions into time-ordered atomic descriptions, simplifying the task for subsequent token generation. It uses a set of atomic action demonstrations (`A_dem`) to understand the correspondence between simple motion tokens and natural language.

3. **SemAligner:** To provide relevant in-context examples for the LLM Generator, SemAligner identifies semantically similar examples from a labeled motion dataset. It is trained with a contrastive loss to match feature embeddings of text-motion pairs. This module encodes all textual data into semantic vectors and retrieves the most relevant data pairs corresponding to `t_p`, forming a set of semantic demonstrations (`S_dem`). This narrows the LLM's learning space and improves the effectiveness of in-context learning.

4. **LLM Generator:** This module (sharing parameters with the LLM Planner but guided by a different prompt) learns motion-token representations through in-context learning. It takes the target prompt `t_p` from the LLM Planner, semantic demonstrations `S_dem` from SemAligner, and the current motion history `o` (initially empty) as input. It then generates a set of candidate motion-token sequences (`S_m`) that can be decoded by the VQ-VAE decoder.

**B. Model Predictive Generation (MPG)**
The MPG module refines the motion candidates generated by CAMG using inference-time physical and semantic feedback, inspired by MPC.

1. **Control Sequence Design (Online Motion Refinement):** MPG reformulates motion generation as a stepwise refinement process. At each iteration, the LLM Generator produces candidate motion sequences based on the current motion prefix `o`. These candidates are evaluated for physical and semantic rewards. The short-term optimal motion sequence (`o_i`) is selected and appended to `o`, which then guides the generation in the next iteration. The length of `o_i` (prediction horizon `H`) is flexible.

2. **Reward Function Design:**
 - **Physical Reward (`R_phy`):** A pre-trained physics policy (e.g., PHC) is used to simulate candidate motion sequences in an Isaac Gym environment. `R_phy` quantifies how well a motion segment conforms to physical constraints. It is calculated as a discounted sum of normalized rewards `R` (from the RL objective of the policy) over a prediction horizon `H`.
 - **Semantic Reward (`R_sem`):** Candidate motion sequences (`m`) are rendered into textureless SMPL meshes. Key frames from these rendered motions are fed into an external image encoder (`C_im`). A text encoder (`C_text`) computes the semantic vector of the target prompt `t_p`. `R_sem` is then calculated as the cosine similarity between the averaged image embeddings and the text embedding, quantifying semantic alignment.

3. **Online Motion Refinement (MPC Analogy):** The MPG module combines `R_phy` and `R_sem` to compute a total reward (`R_total = R_phy + βR_sem`), where `β` balances the two rewards. The candidate motion sequence (`q`) with the highest `R_total` from the top-K physically plausible sequences (`S_q`) is selected as `o_i`. If `R_sem` of the selected `o_i` falls below a threshold `ϵ`, the generation step is re-triggered to mitigate hallucinations. This process is an iterative loop:
 - **State Measurement:** The current generated motion sequence `o`.
 - **Prediction and Optimization:** The LLM Generator (predictive model) extends `o` with candidate segments. These are evaluated using `R_phy` and `R_sem` to find the optimal segment.
 - **Control Action:** The optimal segment is appended to `o`.
 - **Repeat:** The cycle continues until the full motion is generated.

**C. LoRA Fine-Tuning Variant:**
A variant is explored using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) fine-tuning. In this approach, CAMG generates a single motion-token sequence per iteration, and a differentiable world model (approximating the PHC simulator) is trained. This allows gradients from a synthesized-motion reconstruction loss (`L_MSE`), `L_phy` (negative log of physical reward), and `L_sem` (1 - semantic reward) to backpropagate to upstream components, improving efficiency during training by reducing computational overhead and enabling the LLM Generator to learn a basic representation of motion tokens.

**Implementation Details:**
- VQ-VAE uses 8-frame segments. SemAligner uses a BERT-Large-Uncased backbone fine-tuned on HumanML3D.
- LLM backbone for non-fine-tuned ICMPG is LLaMA-3.1-70B-Instruct; for LoRA, it's LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct.
- Physical policy for simulation is PHC in Isaac Gym. Semantic evaluator is ActionCLIP.
- Prediction horizon `H` is 4 motion tokens, 10 candidates generated per step, top-K retained. Discount factor `γ` = 0.95. Temperature 0.6, top-p 0.9 for diversity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates ICMPG across standard and open-vocabulary text-to-motion generation tasks using HumanML3D, KIT-ML, BABEL, MotionMillion, and PP-Motion datasets.

**A. Evaluation on Standard Textual Descriptions (HumanML3D, KIT-ML)**
- **VQ-VAE Performance:** The VQ-VAE module demonstrates strong reconstruction quality, achieving low FID and competitive R-Precision and physical metrics, indicating its ability to preserve essential motion features.
- **ICMPG Performance (In-Context Learning):** Under its baseline in-context learning configuration, ICMPG's Top-1 and Top-3 R-Precision scores were within 0.026-0.025 (HumanML3D) and 0.018-0.024 (KIT-ML) of the best-performing methods, suggesting competitive semantic alignment.
- **ICMPG Performance (LoRA Fine-tuning):** With LoRA fine-tuning, ICMPG achieves competitive FID scores, indicating strong motion generation quality. It outperforms non-physics-aware baselines in physical realism, achieving the best Phys-Err performance.

**B. Evaluation on Open-Vocabulary Textual Descriptions (BABEL)**
- **Semantic Alignment:** Baseline ICMPG outperforms AvatarCLIP and MotionCLIP in FID, CLIP Score, and R-Precision. The LoRA-fine-tuned variant further surpasses AnySkill across these semantic metrics, achieving the best CLIP Score (29.32) and R-Precision (Top-1: 0.316, Top-3: 0.603).
- **Physical Realism:** ICMPG achieves success rates (77.2% for baseline, 82.9% for LoRA) and Phys-Err scores (4.583 for baseline, 3.704 for LoRA) comparable to AnySkill, a physics-based method, without task-specific reinforcement learning.
- **Challenging Benchmarks (MotionMillion, PP-Motion):** ICMPG achieves a higher MM-Hit (76.3%) than MotionCraft (74.1%) and UniPhys (58.7%), indicating better semantic generalization. Simultaneously, its Mod-Cost of 1.85 is substantially lower than generative baselines (MotionGPT: 10.21, MotionCraft: 7.92), reflecting improved physical reliability. This demonstrates a balance between semantic fidelity and physical realism.

**C. Qualitative Comparison**
- **Open-Vocabulary Inputs:** ICMPG generates motions that semantically align with complex open-vocabulary descriptions (e.g., "Ballroom dance in pattern series," "Hands up high jump"), capturing both explicit and implicit meanings, unlike MotionCLIP and AnySkill which struggle.
- **Physically Constrained Realism:** In tasks requiring balance and physical adherence (e.g., "perform a squat," "sprint backwards"), ICMPG generates physically plausible motions with stable foot-ground contact and appropriate posture during simulation, while MotionCLIP and AnySkill exhibit implausible behaviors like floating or limb entanglement.
- **Long-Sequence Generation:** ICMPG demonstrates the ability to generate coherent and smooth long motion sequences with complex semantic transitions (e.g., "Squat in frog pose, stand up," "Walking casually, single-leg standing"), maintaining physical realism throughout.

**D. Ablation Studies**
- **Codebook Design:** A codebook width (w) of 512 with a length (l) of 512 showed the best semantic consistency. Increasing `w` beyond 512 yielded marginal FID improvements but decreased R-Precision, possibly due to redundancy hindering fine-grained semantic distinctions by the LLM. t-SNE visualization indicated that out-of-distribution motions reuse fundamental primitives, supporting compositional generalization.
- **LLM Variants:** LLaMA-3.1-70B-Instruct yielded superior performance (CLIP Score 26.25, Phys-Err 4.583) compared to LLaMA-2-70B-Instruct (CLIP Score 16.94, Phys-Err 14.933) and Phi-4-14B (CLIP Score 24.47, Phys-Err 4.782), highlighting the importance of LLM reasoning capabilities. The framework's model-agnostic design allows it to benefit from future LLM advancements.
- **LLM Planner:** The LLM Planner significantly improved performance for the default LLaMA-3.1-70B backbone (success rate increased from 53.6% to 74.9%, FID decreased from 0.932 to 0.614 on complex BABEL prompts). Its contribution diminished with more capable LLMs like Gemini 3 Pro, indicating it provides crucial reasoning support for current mainstream models while demonstrating ICMPG's compatibility with stronger foundation models.
- **Reward Function Design:** The MPG module consistently improved performance over a baseline without MPG. Varying the hyperparameter `β` showed a trade-off: increasing `β` improved R-Precision (semantic alignment) but led to a non-monotonic trend in Phys-Err, where excessive semantic emphasis could weaken adherence to physical constraints. `β=1` was selected for prioritizing semantic alignment with acceptable physical error, while `β=0.75` yielded the lowest Phys-Err. ActionCLIP as the semantic encoder resulted in the best overall performance, likely due to its fine-tuning on human motion data. The MPG mechanism demonstrated robustness to noise.

**E. Computational Efficiency:**
ICMPG (LoRA) achieves training and inference overhead comparable to other LLM-based methods (e.g., ~0.6s inference latency). While the standard inference-time ICMPG incurs higher latency (~10s per generation) due to physics simulation and closed-loop refinement, it offers zero-shot generalization without task-specific retraining, mitigating the "cold start" bottleneck of RL-based methods and accelerating synthesis for out-of-distribution tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The In-Context Model Predictive Generation (ICMPG) framework represents a significant advancement in text-to-motion synthesis by effectively integrating the semantic understanding capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) with the adherence to physical constraints provided by simulation and control.

**Significance:**
- **Bridging the Semantic-Physical Gap:** ICMPG directly addresses a long-standing challenge in the field, demonstrating a viable method for generating human motions that are both semantically faithful to open-vocabulary textual descriptions and physically plausible. This overcomes the limitations of prior LLM-centric methods (lacking physical realism) and physics-aware methods (lacking semantic generalization).
- **Novel Application of MPC Principles:** The reformulation of motion generation as a receding-horizon optimization problem, inspired by Model Predictive Control, is a novel application within generative AI. This closed-loop, inference-time optimization paradigm allows for dynamic refinement of motions without the need for extensive offline policy retraining, which is a major bottleneck for reinforcement learning-based approaches.
- **Enhanced Open-Vocabulary Generalization:** By leveraging LLMs for semantic planning and in-context learning, coupled with the SemAligner for relevant demonstration retrieval, ICMPG exhibits robust generalization to diverse, unseen, and complex textual descriptions, as evidenced by its performance on open-vocabulary benchmarks like BABEL and MotionMillion.
- **Modular and Flexible Architecture:** The framework's modular design, allowing for the integration of different LLM backbones and semantic encoders, ensures its adaptability and potential to benefit from future advancements in these underlying technologies.
- **Computational Efficiency for Zero-Shot Tasks:** While the inference-time optimization introduces latency compared to purely generative models, the LoRA-tuned variant offers competitive efficiency. More importantly, for zero-shot tasks, ICMPG's ability to avoid task-specific retraining offers a significant advantage in terms of development time and resource allocation.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Immersive Digital Environments:** The ability to generate realistic and semantically precise human motions from natural language commands has direct and substantial applications in gaming, virtual reality, augmented reality, and cinematic production. It could enable more dynamic, interactive, and personalized character behaviors and narratives.
- **Robotics and Embodied AI:** Generating physically plausible motions from high-level linguistic instructions is critical for embodied AI agents and robots. ICMPG could serve as a foundation for more intelligent and adaptable robotic control, allowing robots to interpret complex human commands and execute them with physical fidelity.
- **Content Creation and Animation:** Animators and content creators could benefit from more intuitive and efficient tools for motion generation, reducing manual effort and expanding creative possibilities.
- **Human-Computer Interaction:** More natural and expressive motion synthesis could lead to advancements in human-computer interaction, allowing for richer communication and collaboration with virtual characters or AI assistants.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework provides a strong foundation for future research in controllable multimodal human motion generation, particularly in exploring how to further leverage the reasoning and interaction capabilities of LLMs within physically constrained environments.

In conclusion, ICMPG represents a step towards synthesizing human motions that are highly aligned with complex linguistic semantics while adhering to the laws of physics, paving the way for more sophisticated and versatile applications across various digital domains.
