# Real-is-Sim: Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap with a Dynamic Digital Twin

> [!summary] Summary
> The `real-is-sim` framework unifies robotic policy training, evaluation, and deployment by using a dynamic digital twin, powered by the Embodied Gaussians simulator, as the policy's sole interface. This approach continuously synchronizes the simulator with the real world using visual feedback, effectively eliminating the sim-to-real gap for the learning policy and achieving an 80% success rate on the PushT task with augmented data.

## Problem

- The persistent "sim-to-real gap" complicates transferring policies learned in simulation to physical robots, leading to inconsistent performance.
- Current robotic policy development struggles with the safety and scalability of real-world testing and the inefficiency of direct hardware evaluation.
- Traditional methods require complex domain adaptation or randomization, increasing engineering effort without guaranteeing robust real-world generalization.

## Method

![[2504.03597-method.png]]
*The six-step pipeline showing how the system operates at 60Hz, from policy decisions through physics simulation to real-world synchronization.*

- A dynamic digital twin, built on the Embodied Gaussians simulator, acts as the central interface for all policy interactions, continuously correcting its state using real-world RGB feedback at 60Hz.
- The learned policy, trained with Conditional Flow Matching, consistently controls the simulated robot and consumes representations derived from the simulator's state, never directly from real-world sensors.
- The physical robot operates as a "follower," mirroring the simulated robot's joint states, which decouples the policy from direct hardware control and provides a short-horizon state preview.

## Results

- Policy evaluation in offline mode demonstrated a strong correlation with real-world performance on the PushT task, preserving the relative ordering of checkpoints and enabling faster iteration.
- Augmenting 30 real-world demonstrations with 30 simulated ones increased a state-based policy's success rate from 57% to 80%, matching the performance achieved with 60 purely real-world demonstrations.
- Policies using a gripper-mounted virtual camera, leveraging the simulator's flexible representation capabilities, achieved the highest success rate of 82% on the PushT task, exhibiting emergent search behaviors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The framework effectively eliminates the sim-to-real gap for the policy by making the continuously corrected simulator its sole operational interface, shifting the synchronization burden to the digital twin.
> - Rapid offline policy evaluations within the virtualized environment strongly correlate with real-world performance, allowing for efficient hyperparameter tuning and checkpoint selection.
> - Leveraging the synchronized simulator enables cost-effective data augmentation with simulated demonstrations and facilitates exploration of diverse visuomotor representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics research, particularly in manipulation, has long grappled with the "sim-to-real gap"—the challenge of transferring policies learned in simulation to real-world robots effectively. Simulation offers immense advantages like parallelized data collection, full state access, and safe testing, but achieving consistent performance between simulation and reality remains elusive.

Traditional approaches to bridge the sim-to-real gap generally fall into several categories:
- **Domain Randomization:** Training policies on a wide variety of simulated environments to make them robust to variations encountered in the real world.
- **Domain Adaptation:** Techniques that align representations or dynamics between simulation and reality.
- **Real-to-sim-to-real (R2S2R) approaches:** Using real-world data to refine a simulator or adapt policies before transferring back to reality.
- **World Models:** Learning predictive models of the environment to enable planning and control.

This paper, "Real-is-Sim," introduces a paradigm shift that fundamentally redefines the relationship between simulation and reality in the context of behavior cloning for robotics. Instead of attempting to *bridge* a gap that separates simulation and reality for the policy, `real-is-sim` effectively *eliminates* this gap by making the continuously corrected simulator the *sole interface* for the policy, even during real-world deployment. The responsibility for crossing the sim-to-real gap is shifted from the policy itself to the digital twin's synchronization mechanisms.

The core enabler for this paradigm is the **Embodied Gaussians** simulator, developed by some of the same authors. This simulator provides real-time synchronization with the physical environment by using RGB-based sensor feedback to continuously correct its state through "fictitious visual forces" applied to simulated physical particles. This live alignment means the policy always operates within a simulated environment that closely mirrors the real world.

By proposing that "real-is-sim," the authors challenge the conventional view of simulation as a separate domain. They unify training, evaluation, and deployment within a single, consistent simulation-driven environment. This approach is distinct from prior art because it doesn't try to make a policy robust to sim-real differences; rather, it ensures that the policy *never directly experiences* those differences by interposing a constantly updated digital twin. This framework inherently addresses the limitations of purely real-world evaluation (time-consuming, unsafe) and the distribution mismatch issues common in traditional sim-to-real transfer.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the `real-is-sim` framework is to introduce a novel approach to integrating simulation into behavior cloning pipelines that unifies policy training, evaluation, and deployment, thereby eliminating the conventional "sim-to-real gap" for the learning policy.

The key motivations driving this research are rooted in overcoming significant limitations inherent in current robotic manipulation policy development:

1. **Safety and Scalability of Real-World Policy Testing:** Real-only methods, while providing ground truth, suffer from the inability to safely test policies before deployment. They also lack the scalability for extensive, parallelized rollouts, crucial for efficient policy learning and evaluation.
2. **Complexity of Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Traditional sim-to-real methods require complex domain adaptation or randomization techniques to ensure policies trained in simulation generalize to the real world. This often involves significant engineering effort and can still lead to unpredictable performance.
3. **Inefficient Policy Evaluation:** Evaluating robotic policies directly on real hardware is labor-intensive, time-consuming, and resource-intensive, making efficient checkpoint selection during training challenging. Researchers often resort to suboptimal practices like using final training checkpoints due to evaluation costs.
4. **Lack of Unified Framework:** A disconnect often exists between simulation environments (used for training) and real-world deployment, leading to inconsistencies in observation and control spaces.
5. **Desire for Flexible Representation Learning:** Leveraging privileged information (like precise object poses) or novel sensor configurations (like virtual cameras) is difficult in real-world setups but highly beneficial for policy learning.

`Real-is-sim` aims to address these motivations by ensuring policies can seamlessly switch between running on real hardware and in parallelized virtual environments. By continuously synchronizing a dynamic digital twin with the real world, the framework shifts the burden of managing sim-to-real discrepancies to the simulator itself, allowing the policy to operate consistently within a familiar simulated domain. This promises to enable safer, faster, more scalable, and more flexible development and deployment of robotic manipulation policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The `real-is-sim` framework operates on the principle that a continuously synchronized digital twin acts as the central interface for all policy interactions, unifying training, evaluation, and deployment.

**Core Framework Components:**
1. **The Simulator (Embodied Gaussians):** At the heart of `real-is-sim` is the Embodied Gaussians simulator. This simulator represents the environment using two tightly coupled components:
 - **Oriented Particles:** Capture physical properties (position, rotation, mass, velocity) and are simulated using a particle-based physics system.
 - **3D Gaussians:** Model visual appearance (position, rotation, scale, color, opacity) and are rendered using Gaussian splatting.
 - **Real-time Synchronization:** The simulator continuously corrects itself at 60Hz using RGB-based feedback from real-world cameras. By comparing real images with rendered views, it computes "fictitious visual forces" that adjust the physical particles to align the simulated environment with reality. This ensures the digital twin is a dynamic, accurate representation of the physical world.
2. **The Learned Policy (Behavior Cloning with Conditional Flow Matching):** The policy, parameterized by $\theta$, maps observations to action trajectories.
 - It uses **Conditional Flow Matching (CFM)**, a generative modeling method, to learn a continuous trajectory distribution over future actions. This allows for smoother and more consistent control over an action horizon $H$.
 - Crucially, the policy **always controls the simulated robot** and consumes representations derived *from the simulator's state*, never directly from the real-world sensors (except indirectly via the simulator's correction mechanism).
3. **The Real Robot (Follower Configuration):** When connected to the real world, the physical robot is configured as a "follower." It simply mirrors the joint states of the simulated robot (`c_t = q_t`), decoupling the policy from direct physical hardware control. This design choice simplifies the software architecture and provides the system with a short-horizon preview of future states, as the real robot slightly lags the simulated one.

**Operational Modes:**
The framework distinguishes between two operational modes, both using the same underlying system architecture:
- **Online Mode (Real-World Deployment):** The simulator is actively connected to the real-world system, receiving RGB images for continuous state correction, and the real robot follows the simulated robot.
- **Offline Mode (Virtual Deployment/Evaluation):** The simulator runs independently, without real-world camera input or corrective force calculations. This mode enables rapid, parallelized evaluations and data augmentation, achieving speedups of 5x to 8x with 20 concurrent environments.

**Pipeline Steps:**
1. **Scene Setup:** Objects are generated with collision geometry (fixed-radius spheres) and visual appearance (Gaussians optimized via Gaussian Splatting) from multiple viewpoints. The robot and ground are manually specified.
2. **Demonstration Collection:** Demonstrations are collected with the Embodied Gaussians simulator continuously correcting its state using live camera observations (online mode). This crucial step allows the demonstrator to implicitly learn behaviors that maintain simulation-reality alignment, minimizing occlusions and adapting movements to ensure synchronization. The full sequence of simulation states is recorded.
3. **Policy Training:** The policy is trained to output an action trajectory (desired joint configuration and a scalar progress value). It can be conditioned on various representations derived from the simulator state:
 - **State-based:** Object pose (position and quaternion) and robot joint configuration.
 - **Image-based:** Encoded rendered images (e.g., from a static virtual camera or a gripper-mounted virtual camera) and robot joint configuration.
4. **Policy Deployment:** During real-world deployment, the simulator runs in online mode, constantly synchronized with the real world. The trained policy processes the simulator's state and outputs actions for the simulated robot. The real robot then directly follows the simulated robot's joint positions.
5. **Policy Evaluation:** Policies are evaluated in offline mode, deactivating real-world coupling and corrections. This allows for fast, parallelized assessment of success rates (e.g., using the policy's reported progress value) in a virtual environment, which has been shown to correlate strongly with real-world performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The `real-is-sim` framework was evaluated on the **PushT task**, a long-horizon manipulation task requiring a robot to push a T-shaped object into a predefined target location. The experimental setup used a Franka Emika robot, three RealSense D455 cameras, and T-block geometry generated via Embodied Gaussians.

The main findings highlight the framework's flexibility and effectiveness across training, evaluation, and deployment:

1. **Offline vs. Online Policy Evaluation Consistency (Figure 7):**
 - The experiments demonstrated a strong correlation between policy performance evaluated in the virtual-only offline mode and performance on the real robot in online mode.
 - While absolute success rates might differ, the *relative ordering* of policy checkpoints across training steps was preserved. This means that an optimal checkpoint identified through rapid offline evaluation (taking minutes) would also be optimal for real-world deployment (saving hours of real-robot testing).
 - This finding validates the offline mode as a reliable and practical proxy for real-world policy assessment, enabling efficient hyperparameter tuning and early stopping.

2. **Offline Data Collection and Augmentation (Figure 8a):**
 - A state-based policy trained on 30 real-world demonstrations achieved approximately 57% success.
 - Augmenting this dataset with an additional 30 simulated demonstrations (collected by identifying failure states in offline mode and providing new expert trajectories for those specific challenging configurations) significantly improved the policy's success rate to **80%**. This demonstrated the cost-effectiveness of using the simulator to augment data.
 - Interestingly, a policy trained solely on 30 simulated demonstrations achieved a similar success rate (~57%) to the policy trained on 30 real-world demonstrations. This suggests that for kinematically-driven tasks like PushT, where successful rollouts depend primarily on poses, the Embodied Gaussians simulator's strong correction capabilities make purely simulated data highly effective.
 - A baseline of 60 real-world demonstrations also yielded an 80% success rate, indicating that the simulated data augmentation was as effective as collecting double the real-world data.

3. **Representation Flexibility (Figure 8b):**
 - The `real-is-sim` framework allows for flexible extraction of diverse policy representations from the simulator's state, including privileged information.
 - Policies conditioned on different representations—state-based object poses, images from a static virtual camera, and images from a gripper-mounted virtual camera—all successfully solved the PushT task.
 - Notably, the policy using the **gripper-mounted virtual camera achieved the highest performance (82%)**. This policy also exhibited emergent behaviors such as actively searching for the T-block when it left the camera’s field of view, aligning with prior work on the benefits of wrist-mounted cameras.
 - These results demonstrate the framework's ability to systematically evaluate and identify optimal representations offline using the same set of demonstrations, before seamless deployment in online execution.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The `real-is-sim` framework represents a significant advancement in robotic policy learning, offering a novel paradigm that profoundly impacts how roboticists approach training, evaluation, and deployment.

**Significance:**
- **Paradigm Shift:** By positioning the continuously corrected simulator as the persistent, real-time interface for the policy, `real-is-sim` eliminates the conventional sim-to-real gap *from the policy's perspective*. The challenge of domain alignment is shifted to the underlying digital twin's synchronization mechanisms, which is handled by the Embodied Gaussians.
- **Unified Workflow:** It provides a single, consistent framework for the entire policy lifecycle. This coherence simplifies development, reduces engineering overhead, and minimizes inconsistencies between training and deployment environments.
- **Enhanced Efficiency and Scalability:** The ability to perform rapid, parallelized offline evaluations that strongly correlate with real-world performance drastically accelerates policy iteration, checkpoint selection, and hyperparameter tuning. This translates to faster research cycles and more efficient resource utilization.
- **Data Augmentation and Cost Reduction:** The framework enables the cost-effective augmentation of real-world datasets with simulated data, particularly for addressing specific failure states. This can reduce the reliance on expensive and time-consuming real-world data collection.
- **Flexible Representation Learning:** It unlocks the potential to leverage privileged information (like precise object poses) and explore diverse visuomotor representations (e.g., various virtual camera configurations) that are difficult or impossible to acquire directly from the real world. This flexibility can lead to more robust and higher-performing policies.
- **Foundation for Future Algorithms:** `Real-is-sim` establishes a robust foundation for developing next-generation real-world learning algorithms that tightly integrate simulation and reality.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerated Robotic Development:** By streamlining the development and evaluation process, `real-is-sim` can significantly accelerate the pace of research in robotic manipulation and other control tasks.
- **Increased Accessibility:** Lowering the barriers to entry for complex robot tasks, as less specialized hardware and extensive real-world experimentation are needed for initial policy development.
- **Improved Safety and Reliability:** Enabling thorough virtual testing reduces the risk associated with deploying nascent policies on physical robots, leading to safer and more reliable robotic systems.
- **Broader Task Applicability:** The framework's ability to adapt to different representations and leverage robust simulation could extend its applicability to a wider range of complex manipulation tasks, potentially including multi-object rearrangement, deformable object manipulation (with simulator improvements), and dynamic interactions.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations, providing clear directions for future research:
- **Simulator Fidelity:** The framework's applicability is limited by the underlying physics simulator's accuracy. Future work could improve this through adaptive parameter estimation (e.g., friction, mass) or learning residual physics networks.
- **Reliance on Visual Forces:** The current visual correction mechanism restricts applicability to scenarios with frequent object visibility, making prolonged occlusions or manipulation without observation challenging. Future work could integrate semantic cues (e.g., TAPIR, segmentation masks) for more robust alignment.
- **Correction-Physics Conflict:** The assumption that visual corrections align with physics predictions may break down. Incorporating more sophisticated correction signals could address this.
- **Scene Initialization:** The current simple initialization scheme is sensitive. Advances in learned priors for geometry initialization could enhance this process.
- **Task Scope:** While validated on PushT, extending to more complex tasks like multi-object rearrangement or cloth manipulation will require adapting simulator dynamics and corrective policies.
- **Further Enhancements:** Future directions include backpropagating into physics parameters, reinforcement learning-based fine-tuning, online planning during deployment for real-time adaptability, and encoding higher-level features (like DINO embeddings) from Embodied Gaussians.

In conclusion, `real-is-sim` presents a compelling vision for future robotics, where the distinction between simulation and reality blurs, leading to more efficient, scalable, and robust development of robotic intelligence.
