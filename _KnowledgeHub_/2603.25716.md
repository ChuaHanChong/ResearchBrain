# Out of Sight but Not Out of Mind: Hybrid Memory for Dynamic Video World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called Hybrid Memory enables video world models to maintain spatiotemporal consistency for dynamic subjects moving out of and back into the camera's view. This work from Huazhong University of Science and Technology and Kuaishou Technology introduces the HM-World dataset and the HyDRA architecture, which achieved superior dynamic subject consistency and overall generation quality compared to baselines on the new dataset and outperformed a commercial model in zero-shot evaluation.

## Problem

- Existing video world models struggle to maintain the appearance and motion consistency of dynamic subjects when they move out of view and subsequently re-enter the frame.
- Current memory mechanisms primarily treat environments as static, leading to distortions, freezing, or vanishing of dynamic entities in complex scenarios.
- A lack of specialized video datasets with rigorously captured dynamic subject exit-and-re-entry events hinders the systematic evaluation of hybrid memory capabilities.

## Method

- A ==Hybrid Memory== paradigm is defined, requiring models to simultaneously preserve static background consistency and track dynamic subject motion/appearance across out-of-view intervals.
- The HM-World dataset, generated in Unreal Engine 5, was constructed with 59,225 high-fidelity video clips featuring diverse scenes, subjects, and camera trajectories specifically designed to induce exit-and-re-entry events.
- The HyDRA (Hybrid Dynamic Retrieval Attention) architecture is proposed, utilizing a ==3D-convolution-based Memory Tokenizer== to create motion-aware tokens and a ==Dynamic Retrieval Attention== mechanism for spatiotemporal-informed selection of relevant memory cues.

## Results

- HyDRA achieved superior quantitative performance on the HM-World dataset, including a PSNR of **20.357**, SSIM of **0.606**, LPIPS of **0.289**, and a Dynamic Subject Consistency (DSC_GT) of **0.849**, outperforming baseline models.
- In zero-shot evaluation against the commercial model WorldPlay, HyDRA demonstrated higher overall quality (PSNR **20.357** vs. 14.855) and better dynamic subject consistency (DSC_GT **0.849** vs. 0.832).
- Ablation studies confirmed that the temporal dimension of the Memory Tokenizer's kernel size is critical for performance, and dynamic affinity retrieval consistently outperformed FOV overlap-based methods by adaptively selecting relevant motion cues.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Achieving consistent video generation in dynamic environments necessitates explicit spatiotemporal decoupling of camera ego-motion from the independent trajectories of dynamic subjects.
> - Memory tokenization through 3D convolutions with a temporal receptive field is crucial for capturing long-duration motion information, producing motion-aware representations for effective retrieval.
> - Dynamic retrieval attention based on fine-grained, feature-level spatiotemporal affinity significantly outperforms methods relying solely on static geometric overlap (e.g., FOV overlap) for tracking out-of-view subjects.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Video world models, which simulate the physical world through video generation, represent a growing area of research due to their potential applications in fields such as autonomous driving and embodied intelligence. Recent advancements in video generation have laid a foundation for these models. A critical aspect of developing robust world models is the implementation of effective memory mechanisms, which are essential for maintaining spatiotemporal consistency in generated content, especially over extended durations or during viewpoint changes.

Prior research in memory for video generation and world models has primarily focused on enhancing memory capacity through techniques such as advanced retrieval mechanisms (e.g., Vmem, Context-as-Memory, Worldmem) and compression strategies (e.g., MemoryPack, RELIC). These methods have demonstrated capabilities in memorizing and reconstructing static environments or handling relatively simple dynamic scenarios. However, a limitation of these existing approaches is their tendency to treat the environment as a predominantly static canvas. They often struggle when dynamic subjects (e.g., people, animals) move out of the camera's field of view and subsequently re-enter. In such instances, current models may render returning subjects inconsistently, exhibiting issues like freezing, distortion, or complete vanishing.

This paper addresses this specific limitation by introducing a "Hybrid Memory" paradigm, which extends the capabilities of memory mechanisms beyond static scene consistency to encompass the continuous tracking and prediction of dynamic subjects' motion and appearance, even when they are out of sight. This work positions itself at the intersection of video world modeling and advanced memory mechanisms, aiming to bridge the gap in handling complex dynamic scenes where subjects interact with the camera's field of view. By proposing a new dataset and a specialized architecture, the research contributes to making world models more capable of simulating realistic, dynamic environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable video world models to maintain consistent representations of dynamic subjects, particularly when these subjects move out of and then re-enter the camera's field of view. The paper identifies a significant challenge in existing world models: while they can manage static scene consistency, they often fail to preserve the identity, appearance, and motion continuity of dynamic entities during out-of-view intervals. This failure manifests as distorted, frozen, or vanishing subjects upon re-entry into the frame.

The motivation for introducing "Hybrid Memory" stems from the understanding that the physical world is dynamic, containing both static backgrounds and independently moving subjects. To simulate such a world realistically, a world model needs a memory mechanism that can concurrently:
1. **Act as a precise archivist for static backgrounds:** This involves accurately remembering and reconstructing immobile elements of the environment.
2. **Act as a vigilant tracker for dynamic subjects:** This requires not only retaining the subject's visual appearance but also implicitly predicting its unseen trajectory and motion state, ensuring seamless re-entry into the frame with motion and appearance consistency.

The authors detail several specific challenges that motivate this new paradigm:
- **Spatiotemporal Decoupling:** Unlike static memory, which primarily maps camera poses to a fixed 3D space, hybrid memory necessitates decoupling the camera's ego-motion from the independent trajectories of dynamic subjects.
- **Out-of-View Extrapolation:** When a subject leaves the frame, direct visual evidence is lost. The model must then implicitly simulate the subject's movement in a latent space, extrapolating its trajectory without explicit input.
- **Feature Entanglement:** In standard latent representations, static background features and dynamic subject features are often coupled. Retrieving historical context without disentangling these dynamic cues can lead to subjects freezing into the background or distorting unnaturally.

To facilitate research and evaluation in this direction, the paper is motivated to:
- **Construct a specialized dataset (HM-World):** Existing datasets are deemed insufficient because they lack natural, well-annotated instances of dynamic subjects exiting and re-entering the frame. A purpose-built dataset with meticulously designed exit-entry events and diverse scenarios is required to rigorously test hybrid coherence.
- **Design a corresponding memory mechanism (HyDRA):** A novel architectural approach is needed that can specifically address the challenges of spatiotemporal decoupling, out-of-view extrapolation, and feature entanglement to preserve the identity and motion of hidden subjects.

Ultimately, the overarching motivation is to advance the capabilities of video world models towards simulating more complex and realistic dynamic environments, moving beyond their current limitations in handling mobile entities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research addresses the challenges of Hybrid Memory through the introduction of a new dataset, HM-World, and a specialized memory architecture, HyDRA (Hybrid Dynamic Retrieval Attention).

#### 3.1. Hybrid Memory Definition and Challenges

The paper defines Hybrid Memory as the model's capacity to simultaneously maintain consistency for static background elements and motion continuity for dynamic subjects, even during intervals when subjects are out of view. This goes beyond static memory, which only focuses on immobile elements. The key challenges identified are:
- **Spatiotemporal Decoupling:** The model must independently process the camera's movement and the subject's motion.
- **Out-of-View Extrapolation:** Without direct visual input, the model needs to implicitly predict a subject's trajectory in a latent space.
- **Feature Entanglement:** Distinguishing and preserving dynamic subject features from static background features within latent representations is crucial.

#### 3.2. HM-World Dataset Construction

Recognizing the scarcity of natural videos with unoccluded subject exit-and-re-entry events, the authors constructed HM-World, a large-scale synthetic video dataset. The construction pipeline utilizes Unreal Engine 5 and is structured along four dimensions:
1. **Scenes:** 17 stylistically diverse 3D scenes serve as backgrounds.
2. **Subjects:** 49 distinct subjects, including various human appearances and animal species, are combined into groups of 1 to 3 and placed within scenes. Each subject has its own motion animation.
3. **Subject Trajectories:** Subjects follow randomly selected paths from a set of 10 predefined trajectories.
4. **Camera Trajectories:** 28 distinct camera trajectories were meticulously designed to induce hide-and-reappear dynamics. These trajectories involve deliberate back-and-forth movements (e.g., panning left then right) to ensure subjects frequently leave and re-enter the frame. Multiple initial positions for each camera movement further enhance diversity.

The final dataset comprises 59,225 high-fidelity video clips. Each sample is comprehensively annotated with:
- The rendered video.
- A descriptive caption generated by MiniCPM-V.
- Corresponding camera poses.
- Per-frame 3D positions of all subjects.
- Precise timestamps marking each subject's exit from and entry into the frame.

This dataset provides a rigorous benchmark for evaluating spatiotemporal coherence in dynamic environments, uniquely featuring dynamic subjects, exit-entry events, subject pose annotations, and movable cameras, distinguishing it from existing datasets like Context-as-Memory or 360°-Motion.

#### 3.3. HyDRA (Hybrid Dynamic Retrieval Attention) Architecture

HyDRA is a specialized memory architecture designed to decouple and preserve consistency for dynamic subjects within a full-sequence video diffusion model.

##### 3.3.1. Base Architecture and Camera Injection

- **Overall Architecture:** HyDRA is built upon a causal 3D Variational Autoencoder (VAE) and a Diffusion Transformer (DiT). The 3D VAE encodes video frames into compressed latents (z_0), capturing both temporal and spatial dimensions. The DiT blocks, modulated by diffusion timestep embeddings, learn to predict the ground-truth velocity (v_t) via Flow Matching.
- **Camera Injection:** To provide explicit spatial control, camera trajectories (rotation matrices and translation vectors) are flattened and encoded by a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP). These encoded camera features are then spatially broadcast and added element-wise to the latent features within the DiT blocks.

##### 3.3.2. Memory Tokenization for Retrieval

- **Problem:** Directly injecting all raw encoded memory latents (Z_mem) is computationally inefficient and can introduce irrelevant noise, degrading consistency. A retrieval mechanism is necessary to filter relevant information.
- **Solution:** A 3D-convolution-based Memory Tokenizer (T_mem) processes the encoded memory latents (Z_mem) into compact memory tokens (M). The use of 3D convolutions allows the tokenizer to expand its spatiotemporal receptive field, capturing richer, motion-aware representations from both spatial and temporal dimensions. This compression into dense, spatiotemporally-aware tokens filters out irrelevant context while preserving essential motion and appearance cues.

##### 3.3.3. Dynamic Retrieval Attention

- This mechanism replaces standard 3D self-attention layers within the DiT blocks to optimize information flow from memory.
- **Query, Key, Value Generation:** Denoising target latents (Z_tgt) are projected into queries (Q), while memory tokens (M) are projected into keys (K_mem) and values (V_mem).
- **Spatiotemporal Affinity Computation:** For each target latent query (q_i), it is spatially pooled (downsampled) to match the resolution of memory tokens. A spatiotemporal affinity metric (S_i,j) is then computed between the downsampled query (˜q_i) and each temporal slice of the memory key (k_mem,j) using an element-wise product across spatial dimensions. This metric quantifies the spatiotemporal correspondence.
- **Top-K Selection:** Based on the computed affinities, a Top-K selection strategy is applied to identify the K most relevant memory tokens (K_sel, V_sel) that exhibit the strongest correlation with the current target query.
- **Local Context Integration:** To maintain local denoising stability and structural integrity, keys (K_loc) and values (V_loc) derived from adjacent latents within a local temporal window around the target frame are forcibly included.
- **Final Attention Calculation:** The selected memory features (K_sel, V_sel) and local context features (K_loc, V_loc) are concatenated to form final keys (K'_i) and values (V'_i). Standard attention is then computed using the target query (q_i) and these combined keys and values.

This iterative process enables the model to selectively attend to pertinent motion and appearance cues of out-of-sight subjects, aiming to preserve spatiotemporal consistency while reducing computational burden.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated HyDRA against a baseline and state-of-the-art methods on the HM-World dataset, using a comprehensive set of metrics.

#### 4.1. Experiment Setup

- **Model:** The base model used was Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B, equipped with a camera encoder.
- **Context:** 77 context frames were encoded and temporally downsampled by a factor of 4.
- **HyDRA Modules:** Memory Tokenizer used a 3D convolution with a 2x4x4 kernel. Dynamic Retrieval Attention had a retrieval token length of 10 and a local window of 5.
- **Training:** 10,000 iterations on HM-World using 32 GPUs with a batch size of 32.
- **Evaluation:** A test set of 1,000 unseen video samples from HM-World.
- **Competitors:** Baseline (Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B with camera encoder), DFoT, Context-as-Memory (all trained on HM-World), and WorldPlay (zero-shot evaluation).
- **Metrics:**
 - **General Memory Capacity:** PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS (pixel-wise reconstruction fidelity).
 - **Frame-level Consistency:** Subject Consistency and Background Consistency (from Vbench).
 - **Dynamic Subject Consistency (DSC):** A novel metric proposed by the authors. It utilizes YOLOv11 for bounding box detection and CLIP for semantic feature extraction from cropped subject regions. DSC_GT measures fidelity against ground truth, and DSC_ctx measures fidelity against historical context.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Comparison

- **Overall Performance (Table 2):** HyDRA consistently outperformed the baseline, DFoT, and Context-as-Memory across all metrics.
 - PSNR: Improved from 18.696 (baseline) to 20.357.
 - SSIM: Increased from 0.517 (baseline) to 0.606.
 - LPIPS: Reduced from 0.356 (baseline) to 0.289.
 - **Dynamic Subject Consistency:** Achieved the highest DSC_ctx (0.827) and DSC_GT (0.849), demonstrating robust subject tracking and maintenance of appearance and motion.
 - **Frame-level Consistency:** Scored 0.926 for Subject Consistency and 0.932 for Background Consistency, indicating success in anchoring static elements while preserving overall visual coherence.
- **Comparison with Commercial Model (Table 3):** HyDRA also surpassed the zero-shot performance of WorldPlay, a commercial model known for consistency.
 - HyDRA's PSNR (20.357) was substantially higher than WorldPlay's (14.855).
 - While WorldPlay showed lower performance on GT-referenced metrics due to domain gaps, its DSC_ctx of 0.822 indicated robustness on context-referenced metrics, which the authors used to validate the rationality of their DSC metrics.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Comparison

- **Visual Results (Fig. 6):** For complex exit-and-re-entry events:
 - Baseline and Context-as-Memory exhibited severe subject distortion and motion incoherence.
 - DFoT sometimes failed to maintain subject integrity, leading to vanishing subjects.
 - WorldPlay preserved appearance but suffered from stuttering and unnatural movements.
 - HyDRA successfully maintained hybrid consistency, preserving both subject identity and motion coherence after re-entry.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Kernel Size of Memory Tokenizer (Table 4, Fig. 5):**
 - The model showed robustness to spatial kernel size variations (2x2, 4x4, 8x8), with marginal performance differences.
 - Reducing the temporal dimension to 1 (no temporal interaction) resulted in a significant performance drop (e.g., 1.281 PSNR decrease compared to 2x4x4), highlighting the necessity of temporal interaction for capturing long-term dynamic information.
- **Number of Retrieved Tokens (Table 5, Fig. 6):**
 - Retrieving only 5 tokens led to suboptimal performance (PSNR 19.309) and severe artifacts (e.g., hallucinating extra subjects), indicating information loss.
 - 10 and 15 tokens yielded better and more stable results, with minimal differences, suggesting that a moderate number is sufficient without introducing redundant noise.
- **Token Retrieval Approaches (Table 6, Fig. 3, Fig. 4):**
 - Dynamic Affinity retrieval consistently outperformed FOV overlap-based retrieval across all metrics (e.g., Subject Consistency improved from 0.908 to 0.926).
 - Qualitative analysis showed dynamic affinity selected keyframes with rich subject details, maintaining consistency, while FOV overlap often selected empty shots.
 - Dynamic Affinity computes feature-level correlations and adaptively selects tokens across timesteps and DiT layers, providing a broader and more flexible memory receptive field compared to the static 3D geometric calculations of FOV overlap.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a "Hybrid Memory" paradigm, which represents an advancement in video world modeling by explicitly addressing the challenge of maintaining spatiotemporal consistency for dynamic subjects, even when they are temporarily out of view. This paradigm shifts the focus from treating environments as static canvases to simulating dynamic worlds with independently moving entities.

The key contributions and their potential impact include:
- **Novel Research Direction:** By defining Hybrid Memory and outlining its unique challenges (spatiotemporal decoupling, out-of-view extrapolation, feature entanglement), the paper opens a new avenue for research in world models.
- **Dedicated Benchmark Dataset (HM-World):** The creation of HM-World is a significant contribution. As the first large-scale video dataset explicitly designed for hybrid memory research, it provides a standardized and rigorous benchmark. This dataset, with its decoupled camera and subject trajectories, diverse scenarios, and detailed annotations (including exit-entry events and 3D subject poses), enables systematic training and evaluation of models in complex dynamic environments, which was previously difficult with existing datasets.
- **Specialized Architectural Solution (HyDRA):** The proposed HyDRA architecture, with its Memory Tokenizer and Dynamic Retrieval Attention, provides a concrete and effective method for extracting and retrieving motion-aware cues. Its ability to process spatial and temporal information jointly and perform relevance-driven retrieval for out-of-sight subjects demonstrates improved capabilities over existing state-of-the-art approaches.
- **Advancement in World Model Realism:** By enabling models to track dynamic subjects consistently, HyDRA contributes to developing more realistic and coherent video world models. This improved fidelity is crucial for generating convincing simulations that reflect the complexities of the physical world.
- **Implications for Downstream Applications:** The ability of world models to handle dynamic subject consistency has implications for various downstream applications. More robust world models could lead to advancements in:
 - **Autonomous Driving:** More accurate prediction of pedestrian or vehicle movements, even when partially or temporarily obscured.
 - **Embodied Intelligence:** Agents operating in simulated environments could better understand and interact with dynamic elements, improving their perception and decision-making.
 - **Video Generation and Editing:** The generation of longer, more consistent videos with complex character interactions and camera movements could become more feasible.

While the paper acknowledges limitations regarding highly complex scenes with multiple subjects or severe occlusions, the establishment of the Hybrid Memory paradigm, the HM-World dataset, and the HyDRA framework provides a foundational contribution for future research aimed at building more comprehensive and robust video world models capable of simulating dynamic reality.
