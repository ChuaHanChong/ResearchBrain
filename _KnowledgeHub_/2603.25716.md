# Out of Sight but Not Out of Mind: Hybrid Memory for Dynamic Video World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called Hybrid Memory enables video world models to maintain spatiotemporal consistency for dynamic subjects moving out of and back into the camera's view. This work from Huazhong University of Science and Technology and Kuaishou Technology introduces the HM-World dataset and the HyDRA architecture, which achieved superior dynamic subject consistency and overall generation quality compared to baselines on the new dataset and outperformed a commercial model in zero-shot evaluation.

## Problem

- Existing video world models struggle to maintain the appearance and motion consistency of dynamic subjects when they move out of view and subsequently re-enter the frame.
- Current memory mechanisms primarily treat environments as static, leading to distortions, freezing, or vanishing of dynamic entities in complex scenarios.
- A lack of specialized video datasets with rigorously captured dynamic subject exit-and-re-entry events hinders the systematic evaluation of hybrid memory capabilities.

## Method

- A ==Hybrid Memory== paradigm is defined, requiring models to simultaneously preserve static background consistency and track dynamic subject motion/appearance across out-of-view intervals.
- The HM-World dataset, generated in Unreal Engine 5, was constructed with 59,225 high-fidelity video clips featuring diverse scenes, subjects, and camera trajectories specifically designed to induce exit-and-re-entry events.
- The HyDRA (Hybrid Dynamic Retrieval Attention) architecture is proposed, utilizing a ==3D-convolution-based Memory Tokenizer== to create motion-aware tokens and a ==Dynamic Retrieval Attention== mechanism for spatiotemporal-informed selection of relevant memory cues.

## Results

- HyDRA achieved superior quantitative performance on the HM-World dataset, including a PSNR of **20.357**, SSIM of **0.606**, LPIPS of **0.289**, and a Dynamic Subject Consistency (DSC_GT) of **0.849**, outperforming baseline models.
- In zero-shot evaluation against the commercial model WorldPlay, HyDRA demonstrated higher overall quality (PSNR **20.357** vs. 14.855) and better dynamic subject consistency (DSC_GT **0.849** vs. 0.832).
- Ablation studies confirmed that the temporal dimension of the Memory Tokenizer's kernel size is critical for performance, and dynamic affinity retrieval consistently outperformed FOV overlap-based methods by adaptively selecting relevant motion cues.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Achieving consistent video generation in dynamic environments necessitates explicit spatiotemporal decoupling of camera ego-motion from the independent trajectories of dynamic subjects.
> - Memory tokenization through 3D convolutions with a temporal receptive field is crucial for capturing long-duration motion information, producing motion-aware representations for effective retrieval.
> - Dynamic retrieval attention based on fine-grained, feature-level spatiotemporal affinity significantly outperforms methods relying solely on static geometric overlap (e.g., FOV overlap) for tracking out-of-view subjects.
