# Consolidating Reinforcement Learning for Multimodal Discrete Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> MaskGRPO introduces a systematic framework for multimodal reinforcement learning in discrete diffusion models (DDMs), addressing the challenges of importance sampling and rollout generation in non-autoregressive settings. The method yields over 5% absolute improvement in mathematical reasoning and coding accuracy and enhances text-to-image generation quality, aligning with human preferences.

## Problem

- Applying reinforcement learning (RL), specifically Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), to discrete diffusion models (DDMs) is challenging due to their non-autoregressive nature.
- Effective rollout generation for exploration in DDMs is complex because of parallel token generation at arbitrary positions, unlike sequential autoregressive models.
- Importance estimation, crucial for policy optimization, becomes intractable in DDMs due to the lack of a factorized likelihood function for gradient updates.

## Method

- **MaskGRPO** clarifies the theoretical foundation for DDMs to build an ==importance estimator==, approximating log-likelihood differences for newly unmasked tokens.
- It introduces modality-specific importance estimators: a fading-out masking estimator for language and a random reversing strategy with high truncation for vision.
- Rollout samplers are adapted to each modality, employing a semi-autoregressive sampler for text and a probability-based emerging sample strategy for images.

## Results

- Achieves over **5%** absolute improvement in solution accuracy on mathematical reasoning (GSM8K, MATH500) and coding (MBPP) benchmarks, nearly doubling prior RL gains for DDMs.
- Successfully applies GRPO to visual DDMs, showing consistent improvements in compositional generation metrics on GenEval (reaching **0.80**, comparable to leading discrete models) and enhanced alignment with human preferences.
- Qualitatively demonstrates substantial improvements in artistic style, photographic details, and overall atmosphere in generated images compared to base DDM models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective policy optimization in discrete diffusion models requires modality-aware samplers and estimators, as a generic approach is suboptimal across language and vision.
> - The timestep truncation hyperparameter (γ\gammaγ) is critical and modality-dependent (e.g., γ=0.6\gamma=0.6γ=0.6 for language, γ=0.8\gamma=0.8γ=0.8 for vision) for training stability and avoiding policy divergence.
> - The probability-based 'emerge sampler' for visual generation, despite initial instabilities, facilitates broader exploration during policy optimization, leading to stronger convergence and superior quality post-RL training.
