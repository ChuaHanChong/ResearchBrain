# GEPA: Reflective Prompt Evolution Can Outperform Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> GEPA is a novel prompt optimizer for large language model (LLM) systems that employs reflective prompt evolution and Pareto-based candidate selection. It achieves superior performance and up to 35 times greater sample efficiency compared to reinforcement learning methods for adapting complex, modular LLM workflows.

## Problem

- Prevailing reinforcement learning methods for adapting Large Language Models (LLMs) to complex tasks are highly sample-inefficient, requiring tens to hundreds of thousands of expensive 'rollouts'.
- Scalar rewards provide sparse learning signals for LLMs, limiting their ability to diagnose and correct errors effectively in complex, modular AI systems.
- Current LLM adaptation methods often struggle with robust generalization or incur high computational costs due to lengthy prompts.

## Method

- GEPA employs an ==iterative genetic optimization loop== that manages a pool of candidate prompt sets for modular AI systems.
- It leverages ==natural language reflection==, where a meta-LLM analyzes detailed execution traces and textual feedback to propose refined instructions for module prompts.
- A ==Pareto-based candidate selection== strategy maintains a diverse set of high-performing prompt configurations, balancing exploration and exploitation across individual task instances.

## Results

- GEPA outperforms Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) by an average of **+10%** and achieves up to **35x** greater sample efficiency across diverse tasks.
- GEPA surpasses MIPROv2, a joint instruction and few-shot optimizer, with aggregate gains of **+14.29%** for GPT-4.1 Mini and **+12.44%** for Qwen3 8B, while generating prompts up to **9.2x** shorter.
- Pareto-based candidate selection yields an aggregate **+6.4%** improvement over naive selection strategies, promoting more robust generalization and higher final performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Natural language reflection, using detailed execution traces and textual feedback, provides a more effective and sample-efficient learning signal for LLMs than sparse scalar rewards.
> - Pareto-based candidate selection is crucial for robust optimization, enabling exploration of diverse high-performing strategies and preventing convergence to local optima.
> - Instruction-only prompt optimization, when paired with strong reflective capabilities, can outperform methods that jointly optimize instructions and few-shot examples, leading to shorter and more efficient prompts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancement of large language models (LLMs) has led to their widespread adoption in complex "compound AI systems" that interleave natural language reasoning with external tool calls (e.g., retrieval, code execution). A central challenge in this landscape is effectively adapting these LLMs to specific downstream tasks for optimal performance.

Traditionally, reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) methods, such as Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), have been a popular approach for optimizing LLM behavior. These methods treat LLMs as policies that generate actions (e.g., text, tool calls) and learn by observing scalar rewards at the end of task rollouts. However, this paper identifies a critical limitation of RLVR: its notorious sample inefficiency, often requiring tens to hundreds of thousands of expensive rollouts to learn new tasks. This bottleneck is exacerbated by the high computational and monetary costs of LLM inference and external tool calls, making RLVR impractical for many real-world applications or when dealing with proprietary LLMs whose weights cannot be fine-tuned.

"GEPA" (Genetic-Pareto) directly addresses this inefficiency by proposing a fundamental shift in how LLMs learn from their experiences. The authors argue that unlike typical RL agents operating in abstract state spaces, LLMs produce highly interpretable "traces" of natural (and formal) language during their operation. These traces encompass everything from internal reasoning chains and tool calls to tool outputs and even detailed error messages from reward functions (e.g., compiler errors). GEPA posits that these rich, language-based signals offer a far more potent learning medium than sparse scalar rewards.

The work positions itself against both weight-space optimization (like GRPO, which adjusts model weights) and existing prompt optimization techniques (like MIPROv2, which optimizes both instructions and few-shot examples). While prior work in prompt optimization has explored using LLMs to generate prompts (e.g., PromptBreeder, EvoPrompt), GEPA distinguishes itself by:
1. **Leveraging explicit textual feedback:** It systematically incorporates natural language feedback from system execution and evaluation, going beyond simple success/failure signals.
2. **Employing a Pareto-based evolutionary search:** This multi-objective optimization strategy allows GEPA to explore a diverse set of promising prompt candidates, avoiding local optima and encouraging robust generalization.
3. **Focusing on compound AI systems:** It explicitly addresses the modular nature of these systems, optimizing individual LLM modules within a larger workflow.

In essence, GEPA challenges the dominance of sample-inefficient RL by demonstrating that "reflective prompt evolution" using language-rich feedback can lead to superior performance with significantly fewer resources. It also pushes the frontier of prompt engineering, suggesting that advanced instruction optimization alone can outperform methods that jointly optimize instructions and few-shot examples, a notable shift attributed to the improving instruction-following and self-reflective capabilities of modern LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind GEPA stems from the critical limitations of current state-of-the-art methods for adapting Large Language Models (LLMs) to downstream tasks, particularly within complex "compound AI systems." While Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) algorithms, such as Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), have demonstrated effectiveness, they suffer from severe sample inefficiency. These RL methods typically necessitate thousands to hundreds of thousands of rollouts—complete executions of the AI system on a task instance—to learn new behaviors. This demand for extensive rollouts translates into significant computational costs, monetary expenses (especially with proprietary LLMs), and considerable time, making them impractical for many real-world, resource-constrained applications.

The authors hypothesize that this sample inefficiency arises from the sparse nature of the learning signal provided by scalar rewards in traditional RL. Instead of these abstract numerical signals, they argue that the rich, interpretable natural language traces generated during an LLM's execution (e.g., reasoning steps, tool calls, tool outputs, and even internal error messages) contain a much denser and more diagnostic learning medium. Modern LLMs possess an inherent ability to understand and reason with natural language, making these traces a potentially powerful source of learning.

Given this motivation, the key objectives of the GEPA project are manifold:

1. **To introduce GEPA (Genetic-Pareto):** A novel prompt optimizer designed for compound AI systems that fundamentally incorporates natural language reflection to learn high-level rules from trial and error.
2. **To demonstrate superior sample efficiency:** A core objective is to show that GEPA can achieve significant performance gains with substantially fewer rollouts compared to leading reinforcement learning algorithms like GRPO.
3. **To outperform existing prompt optimizers:** GEPA aims to surpass the performance of established prompt optimizers, such as MIPROv2, which jointly optimize instructions and few-shot demonstrations, across various tasks and LLMs.
4. **To validate robust generalization:** The work seeks to prove that GEPA’s optimized prompts generalize effectively across diverse tasks (multi-hop reasoning, instruction following, privacy-aware delegation, retrieval-augmented verification) and different LLMs (open-source and proprietary).
5. **To explore GEPA's utility beyond adaptation:** A secondary objective is to investigate GEPA's potential as an inference-time search strategy, particularly for challenging code optimization tasks, showcasing its versatility in leveraging language-based feedback.

Ultimately, the overarching motivation is to develop a more efficient, human-interpretable, and robust method for optimizing complex LLM-based AI systems, thereby making advanced AI capabilities more accessible and practical in resource-limited environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GEPA (Genetic-Pareto) is a novel reflective prompt optimizer for compound AI systems, designed to maximize learning from limited data by merging textual reflection with multi-objective evolutionary search. Its methodology is anchored by three core principles: genetic prompt evolution, reflection using natural language feedback, and Pareto-based candidate selection.

#### 3.1. Compound AI System Definition

The paper defines a compound AI system (Φ) as a modular architecture comprising one or more LLM invocations, possibly interleaved with external tool calls, orchestrated by arbitrary control flow. Each module Mᵢ within the system has a system prompt (πᵢ) and underlying model weights (θᵢ). The optimization problem aims to find the optimal set of prompts (Π) and weights (Θ) that maximize a task-specific evaluation metric (μ) over a task distribution (T), subject to a strict rollout budget (B).

#### 3.2. Genetic Optimization Loop

GEPA initiates with a candidate pool (P) containing the base system's initial parameters. It then operates in an iterative loop until the rollout budget is exhausted. In each iteration:
1. **Candidate Selection:** GEPA strategically selects existing candidates from the pool to evolve. This is where the Pareto-based selection (detailed below) plays a crucial role.
2. **Prompt Proposal (Mutation or Crossover):** A new candidate is proposed, typically by "mutating" a prompt within a selected module based on reflective feedback, or by performing a "crossover" operation between two candidates (GEPA+Merge variant).
3. **Minibatch Evaluation:** The newly proposed candidate is evaluated on a small minibatch of tasks from the training dataset.
4. **Candidate Addition:** If the new candidate demonstrates improved performance relative to its parent(s) on this minibatch, it is added to the candidate pool (P), its ancestry is tracked, and it undergoes a full evaluation on a dedicated validation set (D_pareto).
Upon budget depletion, GEPA returns the candidate with the best aggregate performance on D_pareto. This iterative, evolutionary process allows GEPA to accumulate "lessons" along genetic trees, with new candidates inheriting and building upon previous successful strategies.

#### 3.3. Reflective Prompt Mutation

This is a cornerstone of GEPA’s sample efficiency. Rather than relying on scalar rewards, GEPA leverages the rich natural language traces generated during a compound AI system's execution. These traces include LLM reasoning chains, tool calls, tool outputs, and the success/failure outcomes.
1. **Trace Collection:** For a selected candidate and a target module, GEPA generates a few rollouts over a minibatch, meticulously recording their execution traces and outcomes.
2. **LLM Reflection:** An LLM is then prompted to reflect on this information. Using a carefully designed meta-prompt (Appendix B), the LLM examines the module’s inputs, outputs, reasoning, and the final system outcome. It performs implicit credit assignment, diagnosing problems or attributing successes to specific elements (or omissions) in the module’s current prompt.
3. **Prompt Update Proposal:** Based on this reflection, the LLM proposes a new, refined instruction for the target module. This new prompt aims to incorporate the lessons learned from the trial-and-error process.
4. **Feedback Function (μ_f):** GEPA enhances the standard evaluation metric (μ) with a specialized `feedback_function` (μ_f). This function extracts highly diagnostic textual traces generated during the evaluation process (e.g., compiler error messages from code execution environments, module-specific feedback in multi-hop systems) and provides them alongside the scalar score. This rich textual feedback significantly boosts the LLM's ability to reflect and propose targeted prompt updates.

#### 3.4. Pareto-Based Candidate Selection

To prevent the optimizer from getting stuck in local optima by solely pursuing the single best-performing candidate, GEPA employs a Pareto-based "illumination" strategy (Algorithm 2).
1. **Pareto Frontier Construction:** GEPA identifies the highest score achieved for each individual training instance across all candidates in the pool, thereby constructing a "Pareto frontier" of optimization progress.
2. **Non-Dominated Candidate Identification:** It compiles a list of candidates that achieve the best score on at least one training instance.
3. **Pruning Dominated Candidates:** Candidates that are strictly dominated (i.e., another candidate performs equally well on all tasks and better on at least one task) are pruned.
4. **Stochastic Sampling:** From this pruned set of non-dominated candidates, GEPA stochastically samples one for the next mutation step. Candidates that achieve the best score across more training instances are given a higher selection probability. This strategy ensures a balance between exploiting known good strategies and exploring diverse promising avenues, leading to more robust generalization.

#### 3.5. System Aware Merge (Crossover Strategy)

In the `GEPA+Merge` variant, a genetic crossover strategy is introduced (Appendix F, Algorithm 4). This strategy identifies two distinct candidate lineages that have evolved complementary strengths (e.g., one optimized Module A well, another optimized Module B well). It then combines the best-performing modules from these distinct lineages into a new candidate system, aiming to synthesize disparate valuable insights into a single, highly optimized prompt set.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of GEPA across four diverse tasks (HotpotQA, IFBench, PUPA, HoVer) and two LLMs (Qwen3 8B, GPT-4.1 Mini) yields several compelling observations that underscore its effectiveness and efficiency.

#### 4.1. Superior Sample Efficiency and Outperformance of Reinforcement Learning (GRPO)

GEPA consistently demonstrated rapid adaptation and robust generalization, significantly outperforming Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), a state-of-the-art RL algorithm.
- **Performance Gap:** On Qwen3 8B, GEPA achieved an average improvement of +10% over GRPO, with gains as high as +19% on tasks like HotpotQA. The `GEPA+Merge` variant further widened this margin, showing a +21% improvement.
- **Rollout Reduction:** GEPA required dramatically fewer rollouts. For instance, on IFBench, it used up to **35 times fewer rollouts** than GRPO (678 vs. 24,000) while still outperforming it. Across all tasks, GEPA typically used hundreds to a few thousand rollouts, compared to GRPO's fixed 24,000.
- **Training Efficiency:** When only considering rollouts used for actual learning (training set rollouts), GEPA's efficiency is even more pronounced, matching GRPO's best validation scores with as few as 6 training rollouts for HoVer and 32 for IFBench, showcasing up to **78 times greater sample efficiency**. This highlights that the rich natural language feedback provides a much stronger learning signal than sparse scalar rewards.

#### 4.2. Outperformance of Leading Prompt Optimizers (MIPROv2)

GEPA also consistently surpassed MIPROv2, a prominent joint instruction and few-shot optimizer, on every benchmark and model combination.
- **Aggregate Gains:** GEPA achieved aggregate optimization gains of +14.29% (GPT-4.1 Mini) and +12.44% (Qwen3 8B) over the baseline, more than doubling the average gains achieved by MIPROv2 (+7.04%).
- **Instruction vs. Few-Shot Optimization:** This finding is particularly significant because GEPA primarily focuses on optimizing instructions, while MIPROv2 optimizes both instructions and few-shot examples. The results suggest that with modern LLMs' enhanced instruction-following and self-reflective capabilities, reflectively evolved instructions alone can be more effective than relying on a combination of instructions and examples.
- **Generalization Gap:** GEPA's reflectively evolved instructions demonstrated a lower generalization gap (difference between validation and test set performance) compared to MIPROv2, indicating more robust adaptation to unseen data.

#### 4.3. Impact of Pareto-Based Candidate Selection

A key design choice, the Pareto-based candidate selection strategy, was critical for GEPA's success. Ablation studies confirmed that this strategy significantly outperformed a naive "SelectBestCandidate" approach.
- **Avoiding Local Optima:** The "SelectBestCandidate" strategy often led to the optimizer getting stuck in local optima, making marginal improvements before stalling. In contrast, Pareto-based sampling enabled a more diverse exploration of the prompt search space.
- **Performance Improvement:** GEPA with Pareto-based sampling maintained an aggregate margin of +6.4% over the "SelectBestCandidate" ablation, demonstrating its role in achieving higher-performing and more generalizable solutions within the same budget.

#### 4.4. Efficiency of Optimized Prompts

Beyond performance, GEPA-optimized prompts offered practical advantages in terms of length.
- **Shorter Prompts:** GEPA and `GEPA+Merge` produced prompts that were up to **9.2 times shorter** than those generated by MIPROv2. This is because MIPROv2's few-shot examples often add substantial token count, whereas GEPA's instructions are more compact.
- **Reduced Cost and Latency:** Shorter prompts directly translate to lower inference costs (fewer input tokens), reduced latency, and improved overall efficiency for LLM serving systems, making GEPA's output more deployable in production environments.

#### 4.5. System Aware Crossover Strategies (Merge)

The `GEPA+Merge` variant, incorporating a system-aware crossover strategy, showed additional gains, particularly with GPT-4.1 Mini (up to +5% over GEPA, +2% aggregate). This suggests that merging complementary, module-specific improvements from different optimization lineages can further enhance overall system performance. However, its effectiveness varied with the underlying LLM (less consistent with Qwen3 8B), indicating potential sensitivity to the timing and budget allocation for crossover operations.

#### 4.6. Promising Results as an Inference-Time Search Strategy

Preliminary experiments demonstrated GEPA's potential as an inference-time search technique for complex code generation tasks (NPU kernels, CUDA kernels).
- **NPU Kernel Optimization:** For AMD NPUs, GEPA boosted GPT-4o's mean vector utilization from a baseline of 4.25% to an impressive 30.52% (Pareto) or 26.85% (single best prompt), significantly outperforming even RAG+MIPROv2 (19.03%). This was achieved by leveraging the feedback function to inject domain-specific knowledge (e.g., compiler error messages) into the reflection process.
- **CUDA Kernel Optimization:** On KernelBench, GEPA iteratively refined CUDA kernel code, boosting GPT-4o's `fast_p` score (fraction of tasks executing faster than p times baseline) to over 20%.

These results collectively highlight GEPA's ability to effectively leverage natural language reflection and evolutionary search to achieve state-of-the-art performance with unprecedented sample efficiency, challenging existing paradigms for LLM adaptation and opening new avenues for optimization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GEPA research introduces a highly significant advancement in the field of Large Language Model (LLM) optimization, with profound potential implications across academic research and real-world AI applications.

#### 5.1. Shifting the Paradigm of LLM Learning

GEPA fundamentally challenges the prevailing reliance on reinforcement learning with sparse, scalar rewards for LLM adaptation. By demonstrating that "reflective prompt evolution" leveraging rich, interpretable natural language feedback can significantly outperform RL (like GRPO) in terms of sample efficiency and often absolute performance, the paper proposes a paradigm shift. It highlights that LLMs' inherent linguistic capabilities make them uniquely suited to learn from detailed textual diagnostics and self-reflection, moving towards a more human-like, language-driven learning process. This opens new theoretical avenues for understanding how LLMs acquire and refine complex behaviors.

#### 5.2. Enabling Practical and Cost-Effective LLM Deployment

The most immediate and impactful contribution of GEPA is its unparalleled sample efficiency. The ability to achieve state-of-the-art performance with up to 35 times fewer rollouts than RL methods (and even fewer training rollouts) is a game-changer for practical LLM deployment.
- **Reduced Costs:** Expensive LLM inference calls and tool invocations (e.g., API calls, heavy computations) are a major bottleneck. GEPA's efficiency drastically lowers the monetary and computational costs of adapting LLM-based systems, making advanced AI capabilities accessible to organizations with limited budgets or compute resources.
- **Faster Development Cycles:** The reduced need for rollouts accelerates the iterative development and refinement of compound AI systems, allowing researchers and developers to deploy and update systems more rapidly.
- **Scalability for Complex Systems:** This efficiency makes it feasible to optimize multi-module, compound AI systems where each component might require individual tuning, something prohibitively expensive with sample-inefficient methods.

#### 5.3. Enhancing Prompt Engineering and LLM Generalization

GEPA's success in instruction optimization, even outperforming methods that jointly optimize instructions and few-shot examples (MIPROv2), is highly significant. It suggests that modern LLMs are becoming increasingly proficient at interpreting and adhering to detailed instructions.
- **Focus on Instructions:** This finding could lead to a renewed focus on "feedback engineering" and designing sophisticated meta-prompts for LLM self-improvement, rather than solely relying on costly few-shot demonstrations.
- **Improved Generalization:** The demonstrated lower generalization gap for GEPA's reflectively evolved instructions suggests that this approach leads to more robust and adaptable prompts that perform well on unseen data, which is crucial for real-world reliability.
- **Efficient Prompt Design:** The fact that GEPA generates significantly shorter prompts (up to 9.2 times shorter than MIPROv2) further enhances efficiency, reducing token usage during inference and improving system latency.

#### 5.4. Broader Applicability and Future Research Directions

The preliminary results showcasing GEPA's promise as an inference-time search strategy for code optimization (NPU and CUDA kernels) indicate a broader applicability beyond traditional task adaptation. This opens exciting possibilities for:
- **Automated Code Optimization:** Using LLMs to dynamically generate and refine high-performance code for specialized hardware, guided by detailed textual feedback (e.g., compiler errors, profiling data).
- **Domain Adaptation:** Applying GEPA's principles to rapidly adapt LLMs to highly specialized or scientific domains where traditional data collection and fine-tuning are challenging.
- **Unifying Prompt and Weight-Based Learning:** The authors envision future work integrating GEPA's language-based insights with weight-space adaptation, potentially leading to hybrid optimization strategies that combine the best of both worlds.
- **Feedback Engineering:** Further research into designing optimal "feedback functions" to extract the most diagnostic and valuable learning signals from system traces is a promising direction.

In conclusion, GEPA offers a compelling alternative to current LLM optimization paradigms. Its emphasis on language-based reflection and robust evolutionary search promises to make LLM adaptation more accessible, efficient, and effective, thereby accelerating the development and deployment of sophisticated AI systems across a multitude of applications.
