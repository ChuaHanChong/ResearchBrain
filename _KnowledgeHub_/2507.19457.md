# GEPA: Reflective Prompt Evolution Can Outperform Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> GEPA is a novel prompt optimizer for large language model (LLM) systems that employs reflective prompt evolution and Pareto-based candidate selection. It achieves superior performance and up to 35 times greater sample efficiency compared to reinforcement learning methods for adapting complex, modular LLM workflows.

## Problem

- Prevailing reinforcement learning methods for adapting Large Language Models (LLMs) to complex tasks are highly sample-inefficient, requiring tens to hundreds of thousands of expensive 'rollouts'.
- Scalar rewards provide sparse learning signals for LLMs, limiting their ability to diagnose and correct errors effectively in complex, modular AI systems.
- Current LLM adaptation methods often struggle with robust generalization or incur high computational costs due to lengthy prompts.

## Method

- GEPA employs an ==iterative genetic optimization loop== that manages a pool of candidate prompt sets for modular AI systems.
- It leverages ==natural language reflection==, where a meta-LLM analyzes detailed execution traces and textual feedback to propose refined instructions for module prompts.
- A ==Pareto-based candidate selection== strategy maintains a diverse set of high-performing prompt configurations, balancing exploration and exploitation across individual task instances.

## Results

- GEPA outperforms Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) by an average of **+10%** and achieves up to **35x** greater sample efficiency across diverse tasks.
- GEPA surpasses MIPROv2, a joint instruction and few-shot optimizer, with aggregate gains of **+14.29%** for GPT-4.1 Mini and **+12.44%** for Qwen3 8B, while generating prompts up to **9.2x** shorter.
- Pareto-based candidate selection yields an aggregate **+6.4%** improvement over naive selection strategies, promoting more robust generalization and higher final performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Natural language reflection, using detailed execution traces and textual feedback, provides a more effective and sample-efficient learning signal for LLMs than sparse scalar rewards.
> - Pareto-based candidate selection is crucial for robust optimization, enabling exploration of diverse high-performing strategies and preventing convergence to local optima.
> - Instruction-only prompt optimization, when paired with strong reflective capabilities, can outperform methods that jointly optimize instructions and few-shot examples, leading to shorter and more efficient prompts.
