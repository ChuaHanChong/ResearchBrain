# Lemon: A Unified and Scalable 3D Multimodal Model for Universal Spatial Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> LEMON, a unified transformer architecture, directly processes 3D point cloud patches and language tokens as a single sequence to achieve universal spatial understanding. This model sets new state-of-the-art results in 3D embodied interaction and scene spatial awareness benchmarks, outperforming existing 3D multimodal models and demonstrating competitive performance with 2D vision-language models.

## Problem

- Existing 3D Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) use fragmented architectures with separate, modality-specific encoders, leading to architectural imbalances and representational bottlenecks.
- 3D encoders typically suffer from limited pretraining on smaller datasets, hindering generalization and end-to-end optimization of 3D representations.
- The inherent irregularity, sparsity, and unordered nature of 3D point cloud data complicates its effective integration into deep learning models and large language models.

## Method

- A ==unified transformer architecture== processes both 3D point cloud patches and natural language tokens as a single, concatenated sequence, integrating 3D spatial information directly within the language model framework.
- A ==hierarchical spatial partitioning scheme== dynamically divides point clouds along Z, Y, and X axes into patches, which are then tokenized with specialized separator tokens to preserve spatial context and structure.
- A ==progressive three-stage training curriculum== systematically builds 3D spatial understanding, starting with object recognition, moving to object captioning, and culminating in scene spatial question answering.

## Results

- LEMON-7B achieved the highest GPT-4 score of **57.22** on 3D MM-Vet for embodied object QA and established new state-of-the-art on 3D-GRAND (**74.32%** binary accuracy), substantially outperforming previous 3D LMMs.
- The model demonstrated strong 3D object generative recognition (**59.20%** accuracy, comparable to GPT-4V) and significantly surpassed other 3D multimodal models in object captioning using semantic metrics like Sentence-BERT and SimCSE.
- Ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of the unified architecture, the three-stage training curriculum, and the Z-Y-X spatial tokenization, with the model also showing robustness to sparse/noisy data and superior computational efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unified transformer architecture consistently outperforms modular designs, demonstrating that separate 3D encoders often create a representational bottleneck and can even degrade performance.
> - LEMON provides the first systematic evidence of power-law scaling behavior in 3D LMMs, indicating predictable performance gains with increased model size and training data.
> - The proposed Z-Y-X hierarchical spatial ordering of patches with explicit separator tokens is crucial for effective scene-level spatial reasoning and significantly outperforms other tokenization strategies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) has seen remarkable progress, particularly in the 2D vision-language domain with models like Flamingo, GPT-4V, and numerous open-source alternatives. These models have demonstrated impressive capabilities in tasks such as image captioning, visual question answering, and embodied interaction within 2D contexts. However, extending these capabilities to the 3D physical world presents unique and substantial challenges.

The paper highlights several inherent difficulties with 3D data, particularly point clouds:
1. **Irregularity and Sparsity**: Unlike structured 2D images, point clouds are sparse, unordered, and irregular, making direct application of standard convolutional or transformer architectures challenging.
2. **Fragmented Architectures**: Most existing 3D LMMs adopt modular designs. They typically rely on separate, modality-specific encoders (e.g., PointNet++ for 3D geometry and a language model for text), followed by complex cross-modal alignment mechanisms. This approach leads to several issues:
 - **Limited Pretraining**: 3D encoders are often pretrained on smaller, task-specific datasets, limiting their generalizability to diverse spatial reasoning tasks.
 - **Data Scarcity**: The scale of available 3D-language paired data is significantly smaller than for 2D counterparts, hindering the development of high-quality 3D representations.
 - **Representational Bottleneck**: The architectural imbalance, where smaller 3D encoders feed into much larger language models, creates a bottleneck, restricting the overall spatial understanding capabilities.
 - **Lack of End-to-End Optimization**: Reliance on frozen, pretrained encoders prevents holistic, end-to-end optimization, impeding generalization to novel 3D structures.

LEMON directly addresses these limitations by proposing a *unified transformer architecture* that jointly processes 3D point cloud patches and language tokens as a single sequence. This represents a significant departure from the prevalent "separate encoder then align" paradigm. By enabling early spatial-linguistic fusion, Lemon aims to overcome the architectural imbalances and bottlenecks, allowing for more scalable and end-to-end learning of 3D representations deeply aligned with language. This approach parallels successful unified architectures in the 2D domain, such as VisualBERT and Fuyu-8B, but tackles the additional complexities introduced by 3D data. The work seeks to provide a fundamental, scalable solution for universal 3D spatial understanding, a crucial step for applications in robotics, AR/VR, and embodied AI, where a comprehensive understanding of the physical environment is paramount.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of the LEMON project is to develop a unified, scalable 3D multimodal model capable of universal spatial understanding. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and aims:

1. **Overcome 3D Data Challenges**: The project aims to directly tackle the inherent difficulties of 3D point cloud data, specifically its sparse, irregular, and high-dimensional nature, which complicates its integration into large multimodal models.
2. **Develop a Unified Architecture**: A primary motivation is to move beyond fragmented, modality-specific encoders and cross-modal alignment modules common in existing 3D LMMs. Lemon seeks to introduce a single transformer architecture that processes both 3D geometry and natural language tokens jointly, facilitating early and deeper spatial-linguistic fusion.
3. **Improve Scalability and Parameter Efficiency**: By eliminating redundant, separate encoders and enabling unified processing, the authors aim to create a more parameter-efficient model. This design is intended to improve model scalability, allowing 3D representation capabilities to grow more effectively with increasing training data and model size, akin to the scaling observed in 2D LMMs.
4. **Enhance End-to-End Optimization**: The unified architecture is motivated by the desire to enable end-to-end optimization of 3D representations alongside language understanding, thereby improving generalization to diverse and novel 3D structures.
5. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance across Diverse 3D Tasks**: The researchers are motivated to demonstrate that this unified approach can establish new state-of-the-art performance across a comprehensive suite of 3D understanding and reasoning tasks, ranging from basic object recognition and captioning to complex scene-level spatial reasoning and embodied interaction.
6. **Provide a Foundational Model for Spatial AI**: Ultimately, the work aims to provide a robust and open-source foundation for advancing 3D spatial intelligence, supporting real-world applications in embodied AI, robotics, and other domains requiring sophisticated understanding of physical environments. The motivation includes addressing limitations like "spatial hallucinations" often seen in 2D LMMs when dealing with 3D contexts (as illustrated in Figure 3).

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LEMON introduces a novel methodology characterized by a unified transformer architecture, a structured patchification and tokenization scheme for 3D data, and a progressive three-stage training curriculum.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

Unlike traditional LMMs that use separate encoders for each modality, Lemon employs a **unified transformer architecture** that directly integrates 3D spatial information within the language model framework.
- **Unified Processing**: Both 3D point cloud patches and natural language tokens are treated as a single, concatenated sequence and processed by a single transformer.
- **Linear Projector**: Each 3D patch is mapped into the language model's embedding space via a learnable linear projector. This eliminates the need for complex, heavy 3D encoders (like PointNet++) and cross-modal alignment modules.
- **Specialized Tokens**: To encode the 3D modality and its structure, Lemon introduces specialized tokens:
 - `<pointcloud>` and `</pointcloud>`: Mark the beginning and end of a 3D point cloud sequence.
 - `<point_patch>`: Denotes an individual point cloud patch.
 - `<layer_sep>` and `<row_sep>`: Maintain spatial hierarchy by separating different Z-layers and Y-rows, respectively, within the flattened 1D token sequence.

#### 3.2. Hierarchical Spatial Partitioning and Tokenization

To handle the irregular nature of point clouds and preserve spatial context, Lemon introduces a dynamic patchification and tokenization strategy:
- **Hierarchical Division**: Point clouds are partitioned into patches using a recursive 3D spatial scheme that divides the space sequentially along the Z, then Y, then X axes. This ensures uniform patch sizes while respecting geometric structure.
- **Adaptive Splitting**: The number of splits for each axis (Z-layers, Y-rows, X-patches) is determined adaptively based on the point distribution within the cloud, balancing spatial coverage and patch density. A target number of points per patch ($M=512$) and maximum splits per axis ($K=5$) are empirical choices ensuring compatibility with transformer dimensions.
- **Patch Standardization**: To ensure uniform patch size, insufficient patches are augmented via point replication, while oversized patches are downsampled using Farthest Point Sampling (FPS). This balances detail retention with computational efficiency, with FPS applied locally rather than globally.
- **Spatial Token Organization**: Patches are sorted by their (z, y, x) coordinates, and the specialized separator tokens (`<layer_sep>`, `<row_sep>`, `<point_patch>`) are inserted to explicitly encode the hierarchical spatial relationships within the 1D token sequence fed to the transformer. This ordering, mirroring a grid-based approach common in 2D, aligns with human perception of indoor scenes (e.g., floor to object).

#### 3.3. Training Paradigm

A progressive, three-stage training curriculum is designed to systematically build 3D spatial understanding capabilities:
- **Stage 1: Object Recognition**: The initial stage focuses on learning fundamental 3D object representations. Lemon is trained on large-scale 3D object classification tasks using diverse datasets like Objaverse, ProcThor, ScanNet, and ShapeNet. This stage is crucial for aligning the learned 3D patch embeddings with the language model's semantic space.
- **Stage 2: Object Captioning and Grounding**: Building on object recognition, this stage trains the model to generate natural language descriptions of individual 3D objects and their properties. High-quality caption annotations from Cap3D and object grounding data from GAPartNet are used to bridge geometric understanding with language generation.
- **Stage 3: Scene Spatial Question Answering**: The final stage elevates the model's capabilities to comprehensive scene-level spatial reasoning. Lemon is fine-tuned on complex question-answering tasks from 3D-GRAND, requiring an understanding of spatial relationships, object interactions, and overall scene context. A portion of object-level data is maintained in this stage to prevent catastrophic forgetting.

This curriculum follows a progressive learning paradigm (object-to-scene) and a complexity-driven approach (recognition to reasoning), ensuring stable and scalable learning. The model is implemented using LLaMA-Factory with modifications for 3D inputs and trained on an 8xH100 cluster.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

LEMON demonstrates significant advancements across various 3D understanding and reasoning tasks, consistently outperforming existing state-of-the-art 3D LMMs and often matching or exceeding strong 2D VLMs.

#### 4.1. Performance on Embodied Interaction and Scene Spatial Awareness

- **Embodied Object QA (3D MM-Vet)**: Lemon-7B achieves the highest performance among all 3D LMMs (57.22 GPT-4 score), substantially outperforming ShapeLLM-13B (53.15) and PointLLM-13B (46.63), indicating superior parameter efficiency. Its performance is competitive with top 2D VLMs, with only a minimal gap to GPT-4V (63.40 with multi-view inputs).
- **Scene Spatial Awareness QA (3D-GRAND)**: Lemon sets new state-of-the-art results, with 74.32% binary accuracy and 53.45 GPT-4 score for non-binary QA. This represents significant gains (8.9% and 7.7% respectively) over the next best 3D baseline. Importantly, Lemon outperforms GPT-4V when using single-view images, highlighting the critical advantage of native 3D input for understanding depth, occlusion, and spatial relationships (Figure 3).

#### 4.2. 3D Object Generative Recognition and Captioning

- **Object Recognition**: Lemon achieves performance comparable to GPT-4V (59.20% accuracy vs. 61.33%) and significantly surpasses all other 3D LMMs (e.g., PointLLM-13B at 54.32%), demonstrating robust understanding of 3D object properties.
- **Object Captioning**: Lemon substantially outperforms other 3D multimodal models across Sentence-BERT (52.23), SimCSE (53.59), and GPT-4 (50.76) metrics (Table 3). This highlights its ability to generate detailed, semantically accurate textual descriptions. Even on traditional lexical metrics (BLEU-1, ROUGE-L), Lemon maintains competitive performance while demonstrating superior semantic understanding (Table 9).

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Architectural Insights

- **Scaling Laws**: Lemon provides the first systematic analysis of scaling laws in 3D LMMs, demonstrating clear power-law scaling behavior for both Lemon-3B and Lemon-7B with increasing training data (from 0.5M to 1.87M samples). This suggests predictable performance gains with larger models and datasets.
- **Architectural Benefits**: Controlled experiments showed that Lemon's unified transformer architecture consistently outperforms modular designs (like ShapeLLM) even when using the same LLM backbone and training data (Table 4). This confirms that removing the 3D encoder bottleneck is a primary driver of performance.
- **Necessity of 3D Encoders**: A crucial finding is that introducing dedicated, pre-trained 3D encoders (like PointNet++) actually *degrades* performance compared to Lemon's unified approach (Figure 5c, Table 6). This is attributed to the limited pretraining and representational bottleneck of these external encoders, validating Lemon's design choice to directly integrate 3D processing.
- **Training Curriculum**: The three-stage progressive training curriculum is validated as highly effective, with each stage contributing significantly to overall performance. Skipping Stage 1 or using mixed training approaches resulted in substantial performance drops (Figure 5b).
- **Tokenization and Spatial Ordering**: The Z→Y→X hierarchical spatial ordering of patches with separator tokens significantly outperforms FPS-based sampling (spatially discontinuous) and space-filling curves (Hilbert/Z-order), suggesting it provides a more interpretable and semantically aligned structure for the LLM (Table 7). The spatial separator tokens are critical for scene-level spatial reasoning (Table 8).
- **Robustness and Generalization**: Lemon demonstrates robustness to sparse and noisy point clouds (Table 11) and achieves state-of-the-art results on other spatial reasoning benchmarks like ScanQA and SQA3D, as well as competitive performance in 3D visual grounding on ScanRefer, without specific pre-training for these tasks (Table 10, Table 12).
- **Computational Efficiency**: Lemon exhibits superior inference latency compared to other 2D and 3D LMMs due to its encoder-free design (0.052s per token generation step on H100) and efficient training (78 hours on 8xH100 GPUs for all stages).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of LEMON represents a significant leap forward in the field of 3D multimodal learning, addressing long-standing challenges and offering a new paradigm for building spatial intelligence in AI.

**Key Significance:**

1. **Unified Architecture as a New Paradigm**: Lemon's most profound contribution is its pioneering unified transformer architecture that jointly processes 3D point cloud patches and language tokens as a single sequence. This departure from fragmented, modality-specific encoder-decoder designs simplifies the architecture, improves parameter efficiency, and enables early and deeper spatial-linguistic fusion. This paradigm shift could inspire future research to re-evaluate the necessity of complex 3D encoders, potentially leading to more streamlined and powerful 3D LMMs.
2. **State-of-the-Art Performance Across Diverse Tasks**: By consistently outperforming existing 3D LMMs and often matching or exceeding highly capable 2D VLMs on 3D-specific tasks, Lemon establishes a new benchmark for universal 3D spatial understanding. Its ability to accurately reason about embodied interactions and complex scene-level spatial relationships, while mitigating spatial hallucinations common in 2D models, is critical for real-world applications.
3. **Demonstration of 3D LMM Scaling Laws**: The work provides the first systematic analysis demonstrating clear power-law scaling behavior in 3D multimodal language models. This empirical evidence offers valuable insights for guiding future research, resource allocation, and development strategies in scaling 3D LMMs, predicting performance gains with larger models and datasets.
4. **Effective Handling of Intrinsic 3D Challenges**: The dynamic patchification, hierarchical spatial tokenization (Z→Y→X ordering with separator tokens), and progressive three-stage training curriculum effectively address the irregularity, sparsity, and data scarcity inherent in 3D point clouds. These methodical approaches ensure that the unified transformer can extract meaningful 3D representations and learn complex spatial semantics.
5. **Open-Source Contribution**: The commitment to release all training datasets, code, and model weights as open-source resources is a major contribution to the research community. This will accelerate future research and development in 3D multimodal learning, enabling broader adoption, benchmarking, and innovation.

**Potential Impact:**

1. **Advancement in Embodied AI and Robotics**: Lemon's robust spatial understanding capabilities, particularly in embodied interaction and scene-level reasoning, are directly applicable to enhancing the intelligence of robots and embodied agents. It can enable more natural human-robot interaction, safer navigation, precise manipulation, and more effective task planning in complex 3D environments.
2. **Improved AR/VR and Digital Twin Applications**: The ability to semantically understand and reason about 3D environments will be invaluable for creating more immersive and intelligent augmented reality (AR), virtual reality (VR), and digital twin applications, where precise spatial context and object interaction are crucial.
3. **Foundation for General-Purpose Spatial AI**: Lemon lays a solid foundation for the development of truly general-purpose spatial AI systems that can understand, interpret, and interact with the physical world in a holistic manner, moving beyond limited 2D perceptions.
4. **New Research Directions**: The findings, particularly regarding the non-necessity and even detriment of traditional 3D encoders, will likely spark new research into alternative, more deeply integrated approaches for multimodal processing across various modalities. Future work might explore even more fine-grained 3D grounding, novel cross-modal alignment techniques, and methods to further reduce computational requirements.

In conclusion, Lemon represents a pivotal step towards achieving universal 3D spatial understanding, offering a powerful, scalable, and unified framework that has the potential to significantly impact the future of AI in physical environments.
