# Joint Model-based Model-free Diffusion for Planning with Constraints

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Georgia Institute of Technology developed Joint Model-based Model-free Diffusion (JM2D), a framework that integrates model-free diffusion planners with model-based optimization modules for constrained robot motion planning. This approach enables the generation of mutually compatible and safe robot trajectories, outperforming existing methods in task success and constraint adherence in both simulated and real-world environments.

## Problem

- Integrating powerful model-free diffusion planners with model-based safety and constraint enforcement modules poses challenges, as model-free outputs may be unsafe or infeasible, while model-based corrections can degrade performance.
- Existing methods, such as sequential post-processing, often lead to module contradiction and inefficiencies, while constraint embedding struggles with non-differentiable or hard constraints, potentially generating out-of-distribution samples.
- There is a critical gap in frameworks that can simultaneously align the outputs of model-free and model-based components to achieve mutual compatibility and ensure robust, safe robot operation.

## Method

- The **Joint Model-based Model-free Diffusion (JM2D)** framework formulates module integration as a joint sampling problem from a distribution combining model-free outputs, model-based priors, and an ==interaction potential== quantifying compatibility.
- It extends standard ==diffusion models== to a ==joint diffusion process== over concatenated plan and control variables, iteratively refining both through a joint score function.
- A gradient-free ==Monte Carlo estimator== approximates the joint score using ==importance sampling==, allowing the incorporation of non-differentiable objectives and hard constraints from the model-based module without explicit differentiation or additional training.

## Results

- **JM2D** achieved significantly higher **Safe Success Rates** and lower **Intervention Rates** compared to sequential and Gibbs sampling baselines in the D4RL-PointMaze environment, demonstrating improved safety and efficiency.
- On a real Franka robot, the framework enabled successful, collision-free mug-pickup tasks in cluttered environments, leveraging multimodal planning to select safer trajectories.
- Experiments in constrained toy domains and complex D3IL-Avoiding tasks showed **JM2D** generates samples that are both feasible and in-distribution with **dramatically fewer constraint violations**, outperforming other constrained diffusion planners.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Formulating module integration as a joint sampling problem over plan and control variables enables simultaneous exploration and refinement, fostering mutual compatibility throughout the generative process.
> - The interaction potential `V(x, k)` effectively quantifies the coherence and feasibility between model-free and model-based outputs, allowing for robust guidance without requiring differentiability or retraining.
> - Monte Carlo approximation of the joint score provides a practical, gradient-free mechanism to incorporate complex, non-differentiable model-based objectives and constraints into the diffusion process.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research paper addresses a critical and evolving challenge at the intersection of generative AI, particularly diffusion models, and robotics: the robust integration of model-free learning with model-based safety and constraint enforcement.

**Diffusion Models in Robotics:**
Diffusion models have rapidly emerged as powerful generative tools, excelling in tasks like image and audio synthesis. Their ability to capture complex, multimodal data distributions has led to their increasing adoption in robotics for tasks such as motion planning, control, and imitation learning. These model-free approaches learn directly from data, enabling robots to generate diverse and high-quality trajectories in scenarios where explicit models are difficult to formulate.

**The Core Problem: Bridging Model-Free and Model-Based Paradigms:**
Despite the success of diffusion models, practical robotic systems demand strict guarantees regarding stability, safety, and constraint satisfaction. These requirements are typically more amenable to traditional *model-based optimization modules*, such as trajectory optimizers, feedback controllers, and safety filters (e.g., those based on reachability analysis or control barrier functions). The fundamental challenge lies in effectively *integrating* these two disparate paradigms. Model-free diffusion models, while powerful, inherently generate stochastic outputs and may propose unsafe or infeasible actions, while model-based modules provide rigorous but often restrictive corrections. This misalignment can lead to sub-optimal performance, loss of generative diversity, or even system failure.

**Limitations of Existing Integration Strategies:**
The paper identifies two main strategies in existing literature for integrating learning-based planning with safety constraints, along with their respective shortcomings:
1. **Sequential Post-processing (Filtering):** Methods like RAIL first generate trajectories using a model-free planner and then minimally correct them using a model-based safety filter. This approach often leads to "module contradiction," where the safety filter overrides the planner's output, potentially negating task objectives, causing out-of-distribution actions, or significantly degrading efficiency. It lacks reciprocal information sharing, making the safety module reactive rather than proactive.
2. **Constraint Embedding (Guidance):** These methods attempt to incorporate constraints directly into the diffusion denoising process.
 - **Soft-constraint guidance** (e.g., classifier-based or gradient steering) often requires differentiable loss functions and provides only soft guarantees, failing in safety-critical applications requiring strict feasibility.
 - **Hard-constraint guidance** (e.g., projection-based methods or reflected SDEs) can enforce strict feasibility but are often limited to convex constraint domains, can be computationally prohibitive, and may generate "out-of-distribution" samples by projecting noisy inputs onto regions inconsistent with the learned data manifold.

**The Identified Gap:**
The paper highlights that both sequential and embedding strategies suffer from being one-directional and often rely solely on diffusion for task objectives. The key gap is the lack of a framework that can *simultaneously align* model-free diffusion, model-based optimization, and external objectives, achieving "mutual compatibility" between their outputs. This implies a need for a joint reasoning process that allows both components to "be aware" of each other's capabilities and limitations during generation.

**Contribution of this Work:**
"Joint Model-based Model-free Diffusion (JM2D)" directly addresses this gap. It proposes a novel generative modeling framework that formulates module integration as a *joint sampling problem*. By defining an "interaction potential" that quantifies compatibility, JM2D guides the diffusion process to generate mutually compatible outputs without requiring additional training or differentiable objectives from the model-based side. This represents a significant step towards enabling the practical, safe, and efficient deployment of advanced learning-based robotic systems in real-world scenarios, particularly where hard constraints are paramount.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel and robust framework for integrating model-free diffusion planners with model-based optimization modules, specifically to enforce constraints in robotic motion planning. The authors aim to overcome the inherent challenges of aligning these fundamentally different types of modules to achieve "mutual compatibility," where outputs from both components are coherent and effective.

The key motivations driving this work are:

1. **Enabling Practical Deployment of Diffusion Planners in Safety-Critical Robotics:** While model-free diffusion planners show great promise for generating diverse and high-quality robot trajectories, they often lack the strict stability and safety guarantees demanded by real-world robotic applications. These guarantees are typically provided by model-based optimization modules. The inability to seamlessly integrate these two types of modules hinders the practical adoption of diffusion models in safety-critical domains.
2. **Addressing Compatibility Challenges:** Naive integration of model-free diffusion outputs with model-based optimization often leads to incompatibility. For instance, a diffusion planner might generate an unsafe trajectory, which then leaves the model-based safety filter with no feasible corrective action, or the correction might severely degrade the planner's intended performance. The goal is to avoid such adversarial interactions and ensure outputs are aligned.
3. **Handling Non-Differentiable Objectives and Hard Constraints:** Model-based optimization modules frequently involve non-differentiable objectives or hard, non-convex constraints (e.g., collision avoidance, reachability conditions). Existing diffusion guidance methods, particularly gradient-based ones, struggle with such non-differentiability, limiting their applicability. The authors seek a method that can incorporate these types of constraints without requiring gradient information or additional training.
4. **Preserving Generative Diversity and Data Fidelity:** Many constraint enforcement methods, especially projection-based ones, can severely perturb the learned distribution of the model-free planner, leading to out-of-distribution, dynamically inconsistent, or semantically incorrect trajectories. The objective is to enforce constraints while preserving the generative diversity and fidelity to the learned data distribution, ensuring that the robot's motions remain natural and effective.
5. **Achieving "Mutual Compatibility":** The core challenge is to simultaneously align the model-free diffusion output, the model-based optimization output, and an external objective (like task completion). This mutual compatibility ensures that the diffusion planner proposes trajectories that are "aware" of the model-based module's capabilities, and the model-based module's corrections are anticipated by the planner, leading to a synergistic rather than adversarial relationship.

By addressing these objectives, the research aims to pave the way for more reliable, safe, and performant robotic systems that leverage the best of both data-driven learning and formal control methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology is **Joint Model-based Model-free Diffusion (JM2D)**, a novel generative modeling framework designed to jointly sample from a model-free diffusion planner and a model-based optimization module to ensure mutual compatibility.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation: Joint Model-free Model-based Generation (JM2G)

The authors first formally define the problem as Joint Model-free Model-based Generation (JM2G). Given a model-free generative distribution `p_θ(x)` (e.g., a diffusion planner for robot plans `x`) and a model-based prior `p(k)` (e.g., representing an optimization module's output `k`), along with an "interaction potential" `V(x, k)`, the goal is to sample efficiently from the joint distribution `p(x, k) ∝ p_θ(x)p(k)V(x, k)`.
In robotics, `x` is typically a sequence of actions or states (plan), and `k` is an output from an optimization module (e.g., a low-level control or safety correction) obtained by solving `min J(k | x) s.t. g(k | x) ≤ 0`. The interaction potential `V(x, k)` is then defined as `exp(-λ⁻¹J(k | x)) · 1(g(k | x) ≤ 0)`, which quantifies compatibility, optimality, and hard constraint satisfaction. Crucially, `p(k)` and `V(x, k)` are only available at inference time, and `V` can be non-differentiable due to hard constraints or non-convex optimization.

#### 3.2. Joint Diffusion Process

To solve the JM2G problem, JM2D extends standard diffusion models to a joint space. Instead of sequential sampling (first `x` then `k`), which can lead to incompatible samples and wasted computation, JM2D proposes a *joint diffusion process* over the concatenated variable `y = [x, k]`. A pseudo-forward diffusion process `p_αy_i(y_i | y_0)` is defined, with separate noise schedules `α_x_i` and `α_k_i` for `x` and `k`, respectively.
The key idea is to iteratively apply a reverse diffusion process (similar to DDIM) to denoise `y_i` to `y_i-1`, guided by a *joint score function* `∇_y_i log p_i(y_i)`. This joint score enables the simultaneous exploration and refinement of `x` and `k`, ensuring mutual compatibility throughout the denoising process.

#### 3.3. Monte Carlo Joint Score Approximation

The main technical challenge is estimating the joint score `∇_y_i log p_i(y_i)` efficiently and practically, especially given the non-differentiability of `V(x, k)`, its dependence on clean (fully denoised) samples, and the need to minimally perturb the pre-trained model-free score.
JM2D addresses this with a gradient-free **Monte Carlo (MC) estimator** using importance sampling:
- The joint score is derived as an expectation of the Tweedie score `s_αy_i(y_0 | y_i)` over the posterior `p_0|i(y_0 | y_i)` (Eq. 4).
- Since `p_0|i(y_0 | y_i)` is intractable, it's approximated using self-normalized importance sampling with a tractable proposal distribution `q(y_0 | y_i)`.
- The proposal `q(y_0 | y_i)` is defined as `p_x_0|i(x_0 | x_i) · q_k(k_0 | k_i)`, where `p_x_0|i` is the model-free reverse diffusion and `q_k` is a Gaussian approximation for `k`.
- The crucial result (Theorem 2) states that the joint score can be approximated as an importance-weighted combination of the Tweedie scores of individually estimated clean joint samples `(x_0, k_0)`, where the weights are determined by the interaction potential `V(x_0, k_0)` and the model-based prior `p_k_0(k_0)` (Eq. 7). This means `V` only needs to be *evaluated*, not differentiated.

#### 3.4. Algorithm 1: Joint Model-free Model-based Diffusion (JM2D)

The algorithm iteratively refines a noisy joint sample `y_i = [x_i; k_i]`. At each step `i`:
1. **Monte Carlo Sample Construction (Algorithm 2):** It generates `N` clean model-free samples `ˆx_0` by running reverse diffusion steps from `x_i` (potentially `u`-steps or full denoising). It also samples `N_K` clean model-based parameters `ˆk_0` from the proposal `q_k(k_0 | k_i)`.
2. **Joint Score Estimation:** The MC samples `(ˆx_0, ˆk_0)` are used to estimate the joint score `∇_y_i log p_i(y_i)` according to Theorem 2.
3. **Joint Denoising:** The estimated joint score is then used in a DDIM-like reverse step to denoise `y_i` to `y_i-1`.
This process continues until a clean joint sample `y_0 = [x_0; k_0]` is recovered.

#### 3.5. Post-processing for Hard Guarantees

While JM2D encourages feasibility, it doesn't strictly guarantee `g(k | x) ≤ 0` for every final sample `(x_0, k_0)` because the interaction potential only *guides* the sampling process. Therefore, in practice, infeasible joint samples are post-processed with a *single model-based optimization step* to ensure hard constraint satisfaction. The efficacy of JM2D lies in producing samples already well-aligned with the optimization, minimizing the need for severe post-correction.

#### 3.6. Special Cases and Unification

The paper demonstrates that Conditional Generation and Model-Based Diffusion (MBD) are special cases of JM2D under specific conditions (e.g., factored interaction potential `V(x, k) = V_x(x)V_k(k)` and uniform `p(k)`), thus providing a unifying framework.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents extensive experimental evaluations across various settings, demonstrating JM2D's effectiveness in integrating model-free diffusion with model-based constraints.

#### 4.1. JM2D for Robot Motion Planning and Safety Filtering (2D D4RL-PointMaze & Real Franka Robot)

- **Improved Safety and Performance in D4RL-PointMaze:**
 - **Outperformance against Baselines:** JM2D significantly outperforms sequential sampling (RAIL) and Gibbs sampling strategies in the D4RL-PointMaze environment, especially when faced with increasing inference-time wall padding (novel obstacles).
 - **Key Metrics:** JM2D achieves a higher "Safe Success Rate," a significantly lower "Intervention Rate" (percentage of timesteps requiring safety filter override), and a shorter "Task Horizon" (time to completion). This indicates that JM2D not only maintains strict safety but also enables more efficient task execution by proactively aligning the planner and safety module.
 - **Mutual Compatibility:** The lower intervention rate of JM2D highlights its ability to generate plans that are inherently compatible with the safety module, reducing the need for costly external overrides.
- **Ablation on Monte Carlo Sample Size:** Increasing the number of Monte Carlo samples (`N` and `N_K`) for score estimation leads to a further decrease in the intervention rate, confirming that a more accurate estimation of the joint score improves mutual compatibility, albeit at a computational cost.
- **Robustness to Backup Planner Quality:** JM2D demonstrates remarkable resilience to variations in the quality of the model-based backup planner. Even with constrained or low-quality backup policies, JM2D maintains higher safe success rates and lower intervention frequencies compared to RAIL. This suggests that JM2D's joint sampling process helps the diffusion planner anticipate the limitations of the backup module, leading to more robust performance.
- **Real-world Validation on Franka Robot:**
 - **Task Success in Cluttered Environments:** In a mug-pickup task with novel obstacles, a vanilla Diffusion Policy fails due to collisions. JM2D, by integrating with a reachability-based safety filter, successfully completes the task without collisions, demonstrating robust navigation and safety guarantees in a real-world, unseen cluttered environment.
 - **Guided Multimodality:** Visualizations show that JM2D can leverage the multimodal nature of diffusion models to actively select safer modes among potential trajectories, implicitly rejecting unsafe paths during the denoising process.

#### 4.2. JM2D for Constrained Generation (Donut Toy Domain & D3IL-Avoiding)

- **Feasible, In-Distribution Samples (Donut Toy Domain):**
 - JM2D effectively generates samples that are both feasible (satisfying tight constraints) and *in-distribution* (aligned with the training data manifold) without explicitly projecting onto the constraint set.
 - **Comparison to Projection:** In contrast, projection-based methods, while guaranteeing feasibility, often produce *out-of-distribution* samples. This highlights a key advantage of JM2D in maintaining data fidelity alongside constraint satisfaction, which is crucial for natural and effective robot behavior.
- **Improved Alignment in Robot Motion Plans (D3IL-Avoiding):**
 - **Outperformance against Constrained Diffusion Baselines:** JM2D significantly outperforms other constrained diffusion planners like SafeDiffuser, DPCC (projection-based), and MPD (gradient-based) in the D3IL-Avoiding task (7DOF manipulator navigating narrow passages with novel obstacles).
 - **Higher Success, Fewer Violations:** JM2D achieves consistently higher "Safe Success Rates" and dramatically fewer "Constraint Violations" across various challenging scenarios (Top Left, Top Right, Both, Cluttered obstacles).
 - **Addressing Baseline Limitations:** The paper explains that SafeDiffuser suffers from "trap" behaviors due to per-state projection, while DPCC's reliance on linearized dynamics and conservative uncertainty padding makes it struggle in cluttered environments. MPD's gradient-based guidance can lead to noisy, out-of-distribution trajectories. JM2D, by integrating constraints into denoising, yields dynamically feasible trajectories that respect the learned data manifold.
- **Ablation on Clean Sample Estimation Quality:**
 - The quality of clean sample estimation (how many denoising steps `u` are taken before evaluating the interaction potential) directly impacts JM2D's performance.
 - Increasing `u` monotonically improves "Data Fidelity," "Objective Alignment" (safe success rate), and reduces "Constraint Adherence" (violation count). This confirms that accurate clean sample estimates are critical for effective guidance, aligning samples with the true support of the target distribution.

In summary, the experimental results consistently demonstrate that JM2D provides a superior approach to integrating model-free and model-based components, leading to safer, more robust, and more performant robotic systems compared to existing methods, particularly when dealing with non-differentiable or complex inference-time constraints.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Joint Model-based Model-free Diffusion (JM2D) framework represents a significant advancement in the field of robotics and generative AI, offering a robust solution to a long-standing challenge of integrating learning-based methods with safety-critical control.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**

1. **Unified Framework for Module Integration:** JM2D provides a novel and theoretically grounded framework for explicitly *jointly sampling* from model-free generative distributions (like diffusion planners) and model-based optimization modules. This moves beyond sequential processing or simple constraint embedding, fostering a deeper "mutual compatibility" between diverse robotic subsystems.
2. **Handling Non-Differentiable Constraints:** A major strength of JM2D is its ability to incorporate arbitrary, possibly non-differentiable constraints and objectives from model-based modules without requiring additional training or gradient information. This is crucial for real-world robotics where safety constraints are often expressed as hard, non-convex, or non-smooth functions.
3. **Enhanced Safety without Performance Sacrifice:** The experiments consistently show that JM2D significantly improves safety (e.g., lower intervention rates, higher safe success) while simultaneously boosting task performance (e.g., shorter task horizons). This addresses a critical trade-off in robotics, demonstrating that safety need not come at the cost of efficiency or task success.
4. **Preservation of Generative Diversity and Data Fidelity:** Unlike many projection-based or gradient-guided methods that can force trajectories out of the learned data distribution, JM2D effectively guides the generative process towards feasible and *in-distribution* samples. This ensures that robot motions remain smooth, dynamically consistent, and semantically plausible, leveraging the full expressive power of the diffusion model.
5. **Robustness to Imperfect Model-based Modules:** The ablation studies highlight JM2D's resilience to variations in the quality of the model-based backup planner. This increased robustness is vital for real-world deployments where perfect model-based modules may not always be available or precisely characterized.
6. **Unification of Existing Paradigms:** By demonstrating that conditional generation and model-based diffusion are special cases, JM2D offers a broader conceptual understanding and a common platform for comparing and developing new constrained generative approaches in robotics.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Accelerated Deployment of AI in Safety-Critical Domains:** JM2D provides a critical piece of the puzzle for safely deploying advanced learning-based robotic systems (e.g., autonomous vehicles, medical robots, industrial manipulators) in environments where failures can have severe consequences. By effectively combining the flexibility of learning with the guarantees of model-based control, it can build trust in autonomous systems.
- **Improved Robot Autonomy and Capabilities:** Robots equipped with JM2D can autonomously plan and execute complex tasks in dynamic and uncertain environments, proactively adapting to novel constraints while maintaining safety. This can unlock new levels of autonomy and enable robots to perform tasks that were previously too risky or computationally intensive.
- **New Avenues for Human-Robot Interaction:** The framework's ability to seamlessly integrate external objectives and constraints could pave the way for more intuitive human-robot interaction, where human safety preferences or high-level commands can be naturally incorporated into the robot's planning process.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** JM2D's approach to joint sampling and its Monte Carlo score approximation open up new research directions for integrating multiple heterogeneous modules in robotics, beyond just planners and safety filters. Future work could explore aligning generative planners with model-based tracking controllers, combining multiple perception modules, or other complex multi-module interactions.

**Limitations Acknowledged by Authors:**
Despite its strengths, the authors acknowledge several limitations:
1. **Computational Overhead:** The reliance on Monte Carlo sampling and potential for full denoising steps for clean sample estimation introduces increased computational cost compared to simpler methods.
2. **Support Overlap Assumption:** The framework assumes that the generative model's support sufficiently overlaps with the constraint-feasible region. Its effectiveness may diminish if the learned policy is not multimodal or if the feasible region lies entirely outside the learned distribution.
3. **"Soft" Constraint Satisfaction:** While JM2D guides towards compatibility, it does not strictly enforce hard constraints per sample, necessitating a separate "backup" optimization module for guaranteed satisfaction.

In conclusion, JM2D represents a significant step towards enabling the practical, safe, and efficient deployment of advanced learning-based robotic systems by offering a principled and robust method for integrating model-free learning with model-based safety and constraint enforcement. Its ability to handle non-differentiable objectives and preserve data fidelity makes it a highly promising framework for the future of robotic autonomy.
