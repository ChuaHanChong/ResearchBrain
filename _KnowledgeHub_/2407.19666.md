# Take A Step Back: Rethinking the Two Stages in Visual Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University propose a two-stage framework for visual reasoning, disentangling task-specific symbolization from a shared, generalizable reasoner. This approach allows a simple MLP reasoner to achieve performance comparable to state-of-the-art ad-hoc models across diverse visual reasoning benchmarks, demonstrating improved generalization on out-of-domain tasks when trained multi-domain.

## Problem

- Current end-to-end visual reasoning models entangle recognition and reasoning, leading to poor generalization and susceptibility to data biases.
- Existing methods struggle with efficiency and often remain confined to specific datasets, failing to achieve broad cross-domain generalization.
- Multi-Modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) perform well on text but still struggle with complex visual reasoning tasks requiring precise logical operations.

## Method

- A two-stage framework is proposed, explicitly separating ==task-specific symbolization== (via dedicated encoders) from a ==shared logical reasoning component==.
- The framework uses separate encoders for different data modalities and tasks, feeding their symbolic outputs into a single, unified ==MLP reasoner==.
- Comprehensive experiments systematically evaluate optimal symbolization depth, suitable reasoner architectures, and the generalization capabilities of the shared reasoner via ==multi-domain training==.

## Results

- The proposed "Shared-Reasoner-Only" architecture consistently outperformed other design paradigms, validating the core hypothesis of separating symbolization and sharing reasoning.
- A simple Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) reasoner, when combined with task-specific symbol encoders, achieved performance comparable to or surpassing "lite" ad-hoc state-of-the-art models across various visual reasoning benchmarks.
- Training the shared reasoner on an increasing number of diverse datasets consistently improved its generalization performance on unseen, out-of-domain tasks, confirming the "approximation principle."
- While powerful, MiniGPT-4 struggled with precise logical reasoning in complex visual puzzles compared to the MLP reasoner, highlighting limitations of current MLLMs in this specific area.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Disentangling symbolization (perception) from logical reasoning leads to superior generalization capabilities in visual reasoning systems.
> - The optimal depth and design for the symbolization stage vary significantly across different visual tasks, necessitating task-specific encoders.
> - Surprisingly, a simple Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) can serve as an effective and efficient shared reasoner when provided with well-formed symbolic inputs.
> - Training a shared reasoner on diverse, cross-domain datasets significantly enhances its ability to generalize to unseen, out-of-domain tasks.
