# Search-TTA: A Multimodal Test-Time Adaptation Framework for Visual Search in the Wild

> [!summary] Summary
> Search-TTA introduces a multimodal test-time adaptation framework that dynamically refines Vision-Language Model predictions for autonomous visual search in outdoor environments. It significantly improves target detection, particularly when initial VLM priors are inaccurate, by using an uncertainty-weighted gradient update mechanism inspired by Spatial Poisson Point Processes.

## Problem

- Scarcity of annotated in-the-wild datasets for satellite images where targets are not directly visible, hindering robust model training.
- Limitations of conventional vision models in generalized reasoning and the inaccuracy (hallucinations) of VLMs when presented with out-of-domain inputs, lacking online error correction.
- Inefficiency of existing informative path planning (IPP) methods due to operating without reliable priors or relying on rigid end-to-end architectures that require full retraining for component changes.

## Method

- **Search-TTA** proposes a modular multimodal test-time adaptation framework for autonomously refining VLM predictions online in complex outdoor visual search.
- ==AVS-Bench==, a new internet-scale ecological dataset, was curated by aligning Sentinel-2 images with unseen iNat-2021 taxonomic targets and generating refined taxonomic score maps using a combination of GSNet and GPT4o.
- An SPPP-based online adaptation feedback loop employs uncertainty-weighted gradient updates on a fine-tuned satellite image encoder to dynamically correct VLM priors during the search process.

## Results

- Search-TTA consistently improved autonomous visual search performance, showing up to a **20.0%** increase in targets found for the bottom **2%** of initial VLM prediction quality and decreasing RMSE by up to **8.5%** in score map accuracy.
- The CLIP-based framework with TTA outperformed larger state-of-the-art VLMs and remote sensing AVS baselines in target detection while maintaining significantly faster inference times suitable for onboard deployment on edge devices (e.g., 0.14s on Orin AGX).
- Hardware-in-the-loop experiments with a simulated drone searching for bears demonstrated that TTA enabled the discovery of 5 targets compared to 3 without adaptation, by correcting an initially inaccurate probability distribution.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aligning a pre-trained CLIP satellite image encoder with a VLM (BioCLIP) through patch-level contrastive learning enables effective multimodal score map generation across image, text, and sound queries.
> - An uncertainty-driven weighting scheme within the SPPP-based loss function is crucial for robust online adaptation, preventing premature collapse of probability distributions in sparse target environments.
> - The framework demonstrates zero-shot generalization to unseen modalities (text and sound) with minimal performance degradation, indicating successful emergent alignment within the shared embedding space.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research integrates and extends concepts from three primary areas: visual navigation, multimodal learning, and online adaptation, specifically test-time adaptation (TTA).

**Visual Navigation:** Recent advancements in visual navigation leverage either purely vision-based approaches [3–6] or vision-language foundation models (VLMs) [7–9]. While VLM-based approaches have achieved strong performance and generalization in indoor Object Goal Navigation (ObjectNav) [10–12], Autonomous Visual Search (AVS) in outdoor, natural environments remains relatively less explored. Prior outdoor AVS methods often work with closed-set targets or employ end-to-end architectures that necessitate retraining when the vision backbone or planner changes. This work aims to contribute a modular framework that connects pre-trained VLMs to off-the-shelf search planners, addressing the need for more flexible and generalizable outdoor AVS solutions. It also confronts the challenge of limited battery life and sensor field-of-view in outdoor robotic search by utilizing satellite imagery for high-level priors. Many existing informative path planning (IPP) methods either operate without priors or do not account for their acquisition, a gap this work addresses.

**Multimodal Learning:** The emergence of powerful VLMs [24–28] has propelled progress in training language foundation models with diverse modalities such as audio [44–46] and point clouds. In remote sensing, there is growing interest in using language models with satellite images for tasks like semantic segmentation and environmental monitoring. However, collecting aligned data across multiple modalities remains resource-intensive. Contemporary efforts focus on aligning different modalities to a shared representation space [31–33] to achieve zero-shot generalization. This research explores this concept by applying it to visual search, enabling the system to be prompted by various input modalities, thereby expanding the utility of multimodal learning in robotic tasks.

**Online Adaptation (Test-Time Adaptation - TTA):** TTA is crucial for foundation models operating under out-of-domain data distributions and has roots in continual learning [59–61]. In robotics, TTA is associated with meta-learning and adapting to dynamic disturbances. While online adaptation related to Large Language Models (LLMs) has explored replanning through Chain-of-Thought prompting [65–69] or direct backpropagation to modify prompts or model weights [30, 70–75], its application with foundation models on satellite images is less explored. This paper contributes to this area by developing a TTA mechanism for visual search that leverages detection measurements to perform weight updates on a vision model, a distinct approach from prior prompt-based adaptation methods.

The work identifies several challenges within this landscape: the scarcity of annotated in-the-wild datasets for satellite images where targets are not directly visible, the limited reasoning capacity of conventional vision models, and the inaccuracies (hallucinations) of VLMs when presented with out-of-domain inputs, coupled with their lack of mechanisms for online error correction during search. Search-TTA is proposed as a solution to these issues, offering a modular framework for refining VLM predictions online in outdoor visual search.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the challenges associated with Autonomous Visual Search (AVS) in complex outdoor environments, particularly for tasks such as environmental monitoring and search and rescue. Robots undertaking these missions often operate under constraints such as limited battery life and sensor field-of-view (FOV). While satellite imagery can provide high-level visual priors to inform search strategies, enabling robots to direct their exploration towards likely target areas, several issues impede the effectiveness of current approaches.

The key objectives and motivations are:

1. **Address the scarcity of suitable datasets:** Existing research on visual search, especially in outdoor settings, is hampered by a lack of diverse, in-the-wild datasets that contain satellite images with annotated targets not directly visible from orbit. This limits the ability to train robust models capable of reasoning about indirect visual cues.
2. **Overcome limitations of conventional vision models:** Traditional vision models, trained on narrow sets of classes, often lack the capacity for generalized reasoning beyond directly observable features. This prevents them from effectively inferring the presence of targets based on environmental semantics in satellite images.
3. **Mitigate VLM inaccuracies and "hallucinations":** While Vision-Language Models (VLMs) [24–28] offer advanced reasoning and generalization abilities due to large-scale pretraining, they can still generate inaccurate or "hallucinated" visual outputs. This is particularly problematic when encountering out-of-domain inputs, such as satellite images or specific taxonomic data. The motivation is to provide a mechanism for VLMs to correct these errors dynamically during the search process, rather than having persistent inaccurate predictions.
4. **Enhance informative path planning (IPP) efficiency:** Many existing IPP or search-based approaches either operate without initial prior information or utilize priors without considering their origin or potential unreliability [16–18]. Furthermore, current VLM-based AVS frameworks often adopt end-to-end architectures that require complete retraining if the vision backbone or planner components are altered. The objective is to develop a modular framework that can flexibly integrate with various planning methods and modalities, making the search process more efficient and adaptable.
5. **Develop a dynamic online adaptation mechanism:** The core motivation is to introduce a feedback loop where an agent's real-time measurements collected during AVS can refine and improve the VLM's initial probability predictions. This online adaptation should be robust to sparse target detections and prevent premature collapse of probability distributions, especially in challenging environments.

To achieve these objectives, the research proposes Search-TTA, a multimodal test-time adaptation framework. It aims to:
- Pretrain a satellite image encoder aligned with a VLM (CLIP) to generate initial probability distributions of target presence.
- Implement a novel TTA feedback mechanism that refines these predictions using uncertainty-weighted gradient updates, drawing inspiration from Spatial Poisson Point Processes (SPPP).
- Curate a new dataset, AVS-Bench, to facilitate the training and evaluation of Search-TTA in ecologically relevant scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on developing Search-TTA, a multimodal test-time adaptation framework, and evaluating its performance in an autonomous visual search (AVS) context.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The AVS problem is framed as an informative path planning (IPP) task. A robot searches for multiple targets within a given satellite image, represented as an n x n grid map M, under a time budget. Target locations (M_t) are initially unknown. The framework accepts search queries (G) in various modalities (ground image G_i, text G_t, or sound G_s). A vision model processes the query (G) and satellite image (S) to generate visual priors p(T | G, S), typically as predicted target probability distributions. The robot then uses these priors to sequentially explore grid cells (M) to locate targets. The detection sensor is modeled to cover only the robot's current cell (ψ_r). The objective is to find an optimal trajectory ψ* that maximizes information gain (I(ψ), measured by targets found) within an execution cost (C(ψ)) budget (B).

#### 3.2. AVS-Bench Ecological Dataset

To address the lack of suitable datasets, the authors curated AVS-Bench, an internet-scale ecological dataset.
- **Composition:** It comprises Sentinel-2 level 2A satellite images paired with unseen taxonomic targets from the iNat-2021 dataset. Each target includes a ground-level image and a taxonomic label, with some also having sound data. The hierarchical structure of taxonomic labels allows for evaluation across various levels of specificity.
- **Dataset Generation:** Starting from the iSatNat dataset (2.7M satellite images matched with ground images), a filtering process yielded 441k non-overlapping images. A subset with ≥3 distinct landmarks and 3-20 counts of the same taxonomy was selected. Taxonomies were split into "in-domain" and "out-domain" categories. This resulted in 80k training images, 4k in-domain validation images, and 4k out-domain validation images.
- **Taxonomic Score Maps:** Since the initial dataset provides only point locations, converting them to segmentation masks with likelihood scores was crucial for VLM training.
 - **Initial Segmentation:** GSNet, an open-vocabulary semantic segmentation model, was used to generate label maps for low-resolution Sentinel-2 images based on broad landmarks (Urban, Water, Vegetation, Barren). GSNet was fine-tuned with the FLAIR dataset to improve performance on low-resolution imagery.
 - **Refinement and Scoring:** GPT4o, augmented with human-labeled examples, was employed to correct mislabeled regions from GSNet and then to score the refined masks based on the likelihood of finding specified taxonomies. This process generated 80k score maps for taxonomic-image pairs, along with rationale explanations.
- **Training Data Usage:** The full 380k in-domain images (from the original 441k, excluding validation sets) were used to fine-tune the satellite image encoder. The 80k score maps were used to train VLM baselines.

#### 3.3. Search-TTA Framework

Search-TTA is designed to generate and dynamically update probability distributions during the search process.

- **Multimodal Score Map Generation:**
 - **Alignment:** To handle diverse query modalities, encoder outputs are aligned to a common representation space. BioCLIP (ViT-B/16) embedding space, pre-trained on the TreeOfLife dataset, was chosen.
 - **Satellite Image Encoder Training:** A CLIP (ViT-L/14@336px) image encoder was fine-tuned to align with BioCLIP's embedding space. This was achieved using a patch-level contrastive loss objective, a modification of InfoNCE, which aligns features of ground images with corresponding patches on the satellite map. BioCLIP weights remained frozen during this process.
 - **Inference:** During inference, the fine-tuned satellite image encoder produces per-patch feature vectors. The 24x24 probability distribution (score map) is generated by computing the cosine similarity between the query input features (from BioCLIP's ground image/text encoder or a fine-tuned sound encoder) and all satellite image patch features.
- **Search Planners:** The framework is compatible with various planners, including conventional methods like Information Surfing (IS) and Deep Reinforcement Learning (RL) approaches. The paper details the use of an Attention-based RL planner trained with the soft actor-critic (SAC) algorithm and IS.
- **Test-Time Adaptation Feedback Loop:** This core component refines the vision model's probability predictions using collected measurements.
 - **K-means Clustering:** Before each search episode, k-means clustering is applied to the per-patch satellite image features (z_i[p]) to group semantically similar regions. The optimal 'k' is determined by combining silhouette and elbow criteria.
 - **SPPP-based Online Adaptation:** The framework adapts the Negative Log-Likelihood loss function from inhomogeneous Spatial Poisson Point Processes (SPPP) to perform gradient updates on the satellite image encoder.
 - **Loss Function:** L(λ) = Σ (α_pos,i) log λ(x_i) - Σ (α_neg,j) λ(x_j) dx, where P ≈ λ.
 - **Uncertainty-driven weighting scheme:** To prevent mode collapse in sparse target scenarios, an uncertainty-driven weighting scheme acts as a regularizer. Positive measurements (p) are weighted by a constant α_pos,i (set to 4). Negative measurements (n) are scaled by α_neg,j = min (β (O_r / L_r)^γ, 1), where O_r is the number of observed patches in region r, L_r is the total patches in that region, and γ (set to 2) introduces an exponent to reduce the weight of negative measurements in largely unexplored regions. β (set to 1/9) is a relative weighting factor.
 - **Update Mechanism:** The satellite encoder weights are reset to their base values before TTA updates. A learning rate schedule (gradually increasing from 1e-6 to 1e-5) is employed to adjust gradient updates as more measurements are collected.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to assess Search-TTA's effectiveness in enhancing AVS performance, its comparability with other VLMs, and its generalization to unseen modalities.

#### 4.1. Effectiveness of TTA on Different Planners

Search-TTA was evaluated with an Attention-based RL planner and a greedy Information Surfing (IS) planner, using Lawnmower as a no-priors baseline. Experiments were conducted on 4k out-domain validation images, discretizing the search space into 24x24 grids.
- **Overall Performance Improvement:** Search-TTA consistently improved performance across all metrics: percentage of targets found, Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) between CLIP predictions and ground truth, and steps taken to reach the first target.
- **Impact on Poor Predictions:** The most significant improvements were observed in scenarios with poor initial CLIP predictions. For the bottom 2% of CLIP prediction quality, Search-TTA achieved up to a 20.0% increase in targets found with the RL planner (B=384). This indicates its ability to effectively correct inaccurate initial score maps.
- **Score Map Accuracy:** RMSE showed a decreasing trend of up to 7.2% (out-domain) and 8.5% (in-domain), indicating that the predicted score maps became more accurate with TTA iterations.

#### 4.2. Comparison with Baselines

The performance of Search-TTA's CLIP vision backbone was compared against other state-of-the-art VLMs and existing AVS frameworks.
- **VLM Comparison:** Search-TTA, utilizing CLIP, generally outperformed larger VLMs such as LISA, LLM-Seg, Qwen2+GroundedSAM, and LLaVA+GroundedSAM in terms of targets found. Notably, CLIP with TTA performed comparably or better than these larger models, especially for out-of-domain data.
- **Inference Time:** CLIP with TTA exhibited the fastest inference time on an A5000 GPU (0.11s for initial inference, 0.15s for TTA updates every 20 steps). On an Orin AGX edge device, these times were 0.14s and 0.37s respectively, highlighting its suitability for onboard deployment. Fully decoupled baselines (Qwen2+GroundedSAM, LLaVA+GroundedSAM) showed significantly longer inference times.
- **AVS Framework Comparison:** Against remote sensing AVS baselines (VAS and PSVAS), CLIP+RL with TTA substantially outperformed them by up to 13.5% in targets found.

#### 4.3. Multimodal Inputs (Zero-shot Generalization)

Search-TTA demonstrated zero-shot generalization capabilities to previously unseen input modalities.
- **Text and Sound Modalities:** When using full taxonomic names as text input or fine-tuned sound embeddings, the performance gap compared to ground-image input was minimal (at most 0.9% for text, 2.4% for sound). This indicates successful emergent alignment to text and sound modalities without additional fine-tuning of the satellite image encoder with these specific data types.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Scaling of Training Dataset:** Models trained on smaller datasets benefited more significantly from TTA. For example, with the 80k dataset, TTA yielded up to a 30.0% increase in targets found (bottom 2% percentile). This suggests Search-TTA effectively improves poorer score maps generated by models with limited training data.
- **SPPP Loss Coefficient:** The uncertainty-driven weighting scheme (γ=2) and relative weighting factor (β=1/9) in the SPPP loss function were found to be critical. Removing these components resulted in poorer performance (up to 8.1% decrease), affirming their role in preventing premature collapse of probability distribution modes.
- **Varying TTA Methodology:** Comparison with prompt learning indicated that SPPP-based weight updates achieved better average performance. Against text-based TTA, the SPPP-based approach demonstrated more consistent score map improvements throughout the search process.

#### 4.5. Evaluation on Hardware

Hardware-in-the-loop experiments were conducted using a Crazyflie 2.1 drone simulated in Yosemite Valley with onboard perception (YOLO11x for bear detection).
- **Real-world Performance:** In a simulated search for black bears, 5 targets were found with TTA enabled, compared to 3 targets without TTA. This outcome was attributed to TTA's ability to correct an inaccurate initial score map (which underestimated densely forested areas). Upon detecting the first bear, TTA refined the probability distribution, guiding the robot to explore denser forested regions and locate additional targets.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Search-TTA as a framework that addresses several challenges in autonomous outdoor visual search, offering implications for both academic research and practical applications.

**Enhanced Autonomous Visual Search (AVS) Performance:** The primary significance lies in the demonstrated improvement of planner performance in AVS, particularly in scenarios where initial VLM predictions are inaccurate due to domain mismatch or limited training data. By refining these predictions online, Search-TTA contributes to more efficient and robust robotic search missions in complex "in the wild" environments. The up to 30.0% improvement in planner performance, especially when initial VLM predictions are poor, indicates a practical advancement for field robotics.

**Modular and Flexible Framework:** Search-TTA's design as a plug-and-play interface compatible with various input modalities (image, text, sound) and planning methods (e.g., RL-based) enhances its versatility. This modularity reduces the need for extensive retraining when system components are changed, fostering adaptability and broader applicability across diverse robotic platforms and search objectives.

**Improved Vision-Language Model (VLM) Reliability:** The introduction of a novel test-time adaptation mechanism, inspired by Spatial Poisson Point Processes and featuring uncertainty-weighted gradient updates, directly addresses the issue of VLM "hallucinations" and inaccuracies. By dynamically correcting VLM-generated priors with real-time sensor measurements, Search-TTA improves the trustworthiness and utility of these powerful models in mission-critical robotic tasks.

**Demonstration of Zero-Shot Generalization:** The work demonstrates emergent alignment to unseen modalities (text and sound) without additional fine-tuning. This zero-shot generalization capability suggests that search systems can be prompted and guided by a wider array of information sources, expanding their operational scope and potential for human-robot interaction.

**Resource Efficiency and Onboard Deployment:** Achieving performance comparable to larger, state-of-the-art VLMs while maintaining faster inference times, and validating its operation on an edge device (Orin AGX), highlights its practical feasibility for resource-constrained robotic systems. This makes Search-TTA a viable solution for real-world deployment on UAVs and other autonomous platforms with limited computational and power budgets.

**Contribution of a New Dataset:** The curation of AVS-Bench, an internet-scale ecological dataset featuring unseen taxonomic targets not directly visible from satellite imagery, provides a valuable resource for future research. This dataset can stimulate further development in outdoor visual search, multimodal learning, and test-time adaptation, particularly in domains like environmental science and conservation.

**Potential Impact on Real-World Applications:** The hardware-in-the-loop evaluation, simulating a UAV searching for black bears, underscores the framework's potential in ecological conservation and environmental monitoring. Beyond this, it has implications for search and rescue operations, infrastructure inspection, and other applications requiring autonomous exploration and target localization in challenging outdoor terrains.

**Future Research Directions:** The paper also identifies several areas for future work, including extending TTA to handle more complex and realistic sensor models (e.g., detection uncertainty, false positives/negatives), integrating other sensor modalities (e.g., thermal cameras) and robot types, expanding VLM reasoning beyond visual semantics to incorporate deeper ecological factors, and developing continual learning methodologies to address multi-target search and catastrophic forgetting during online adaptation. These proposed directions indicate avenues for further scientific inquiry and technological advancement based on the foundation laid by Search-TTA.
