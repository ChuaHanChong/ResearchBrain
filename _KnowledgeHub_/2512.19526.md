# QuantiPhy: A Quantitative Benchmark Evaluating Physical Reasoning Abilities of Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Stanford University introduce QuantiPhy, a benchmark designed to quantitatively evaluate Vision-Language Models' (VLMs) physical reasoning in videos. The benchmark reveals that state-of-the-art VLMs struggle with input faithfulness, often relying on memorized world knowledge about object properties rather than precisely extracting information from visual cues or adhering to explicit numerical priors.

## Problem

- Existing physical reasoning benchmarks for Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are predominantly qualitative and VQA-based, limiting insight into models' ability to infer precise numerical physical properties.
- Generalist AI agents and embodied systems require a robust, quantitative understanding of physical laws (e.g., object size, velocity, acceleration) for real-world applications like autonomous driving and robotics.
- Current evaluation methods fail to assess if VLMs can leverage implicit physical knowledge from training data to perform end-to-end quantitative reasoning from visual observations.

## Method

- **QuantiPhy** is introduced as the first benchmark to quantitatively evaluate VLM physical reasoning on moving objects through kinematic inference tasks (determining size, velocity, acceleration in world space).
- The benchmark categorizes tasks along two axes: dimensionality (2D vs. 3D motion) and physical prior type (static size vs. dynamic velocity/acceleration), resulting in four core task categories.
- It utilizes a diverse dataset of over 3.3K video-text instances from Blender simulations, multi-view lab captures, and internet videos, employing Mean Relative Accuracy (MRA) as the primary evaluation metric across various tolerance thresholds.

## Results

- Current state-of-the-art VLMs consistently struggle with quantitative kinematic inference, achieving significantly lower Mean Relative Accuracy (MRA) scores (best at **53.1%** for ChatGPT-5.1) compared to human performance (**55.6%** MRA).
- Scaling model parameters generally leads to MRA improvements, particularly in dynamic categories, but these gains show diminishing returns and do not close the gap to top proprietary models or human accuracy.
- VLMs perform slightly better in scenes with multiple objects and visually complex backgrounds (compared to simple textures), suggesting these offer more implicit reference cues for scale and motion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLMs exhibit a fundamental lack of "input faithfulness," frequently relying on pre-trained world knowledge about object properties rather than accurately utilizing visual observations or explicit numerical priors.
> - Models show only a modest performance gain from video input compared to a "prior-only" setting, indicating they often act as sophisticated guessers conditioned on textual hints.
> - Counterfactual physical priors (e.g., a car length given as 5670m) drastically impair VLM performance, as models tend to revert to plausible magnitudes from memorized knowledge, disregarding the explicit numerical input.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and their integration into "generalist AI agents" or "embodied AI systems" has underscored the critical need for these models to understand and reason about the physical world. Existing research has explored VLM physical understanding for over a decade, with numerous benchmarks developed to assess qualitative physical reasoning abilities. These benchmarks typically frame tasks as Visual Question Answering (VQA) problems, where models are asked to provide multiple-choice answers or descriptive explanations about physical events (e.g., collisions, object relationships, scene understanding). Examples include benchmarks focused on kinematics, dynamics, and more comprehensive reasoning tasks like PhysBench and STAR.

However, the authors identify a crucial gap in this landscape: almost all existing evaluations are **qualitative** and **VQA-based**. This paradigm limits the depth of insight into whether VLMs can truly infer *quantitative* physical properties. For instance, in a VQA setting, an answer of "3.1 meters" for a 3-meter object is treated the same as "31 meters"—both are simply "wrong." This qualitative approach fails to capture the numerical accuracy and fine-grained understanding essential for real-world applications such as embodied AI, augmented/virtual reality (AR/VR), and autonomous driving, where precise measurements of object size, velocity, and acceleration are paramount. While some nascent efforts, like VSI-Bench, have touched upon basic spatial understanding with numerical metrics, they are often limited to static objects, focusing more on perception than emergent reasoning.

**QuantiPhy directly addresses this critical void by introducing the first benchmark specifically designed to quantitatively measure a VLM’s physical reasoning ability in videos.** It shifts the focus from "what is plausible" to "what is numerically correct," thereby pushing the research frontier toward a more rigorous and application-oriented evaluation of VLM capabilities in understanding dynamic physical environments. This work posits that for AI to truly navigate and interact with the physical world, it must move beyond verbal plausibility to a numerically grounded understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of QuantiPhy is to provide a **rigorous and scalable testbed** for evaluating the quantitative physical reasoning abilities of Vision-Language Models. The authors are motivated by several key challenges and limitations in the current state of VLM evaluation:

1. **Bridging the Qualitative-Quantitative Gap**: The prevailing VQA-based evaluation methods for physical reasoning offer limited insights into a VLM's capacity to infer precise kinematic quantities. The paper aims to move beyond these qualitative assessments to determine if models can achieve numerical accuracy in understanding physical properties.
2. **Necessity for Generalist AI**: For generalist AI agents and embodied systems to operate effectively in the real world, they must possess a robust, quantitative understanding of physical laws and object properties. Applications like autonomous driving, AR/VR, and robotic manipulation critically depend on accurate estimation of object size, velocity, and acceleration.
3. **Evaluating Implicit Physical Knowledge**: VLMs are trained on vast amounts of real-world data that implicitly contain physical principles. A key motivation is to assess whether these models can leverage this implicit knowledge to perform end-to-end reasoning about precise kinematic and geometric properties from visual observations.
4. **Assessing Generative Model Realism**: A robust evaluation of VLM reasoning also has the potential to help assess and improve the physical realism of videos created by generative models, which often struggle with basic physical consistency.
5. **Standardized and Fair Comparison**: To enable fair comparisons across different VLM architectures and training paradigms, the benchmark standardizes prompts, provides clear numerical ground truths, and employs a robust scoring metric.

In essence, QuantiPhy seeks to answer whether state-of-the-art VLMs can transition from qualitatively describing physical events to quantitatively predicting kinematic properties, which is a fundamental step towards truly intelligent and interactive AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

QuantiPhy is meticulously designed as the first benchmark to quantitatively evaluate VLM physical reasoning on moving objects. Its methodology encompasses a well-defined task, diverse data collection, precise annotation, and a robust evaluation metric.

#### 3.1. The Task of Kinematic Inference

The core task is to evaluate a VLM's ability to infer an object's kinematic properties (size, velocity, acceleration) in **world space** (e.g., meters, m/s, m/s²) from a video, given a single physical prior as textual input. The model must convert pixel-space measurements, derived from video observations, into real-world units. This conversion relies on an unknown scalar scale factor (γ), which is determined by the provided world-space prior and its corresponding pixel-space quantity. Once γ is established, other kinematic properties can be calculated by rescaling their pixel-space counterparts.

#### 3.2. Benchmark Setup: Categorization Axes

QuantiPhy categorizes video-question pairs along two primary axes to ensure comprehensive evaluation:
- **Dimensionality**:
 - **2D**: Object movement is strictly in a plane parallel to the camera (no depth change).
 - **3D**: Object movement includes changes in depth relative to the camera, inherently more challenging.
- **Physical Prior**:
 - **Static**: The provided prior is an object's size (S_world), which is constant.
 - **Dynamic**: The provided prior is an object's velocity (V_world_t) or acceleration (A_world_t) at a given timestamp.
These two axes define **four core task categories**: 2D-Static, 2D-Dynamic, 3D-Static, and 3D-Dynamic.

#### 3.3. Data Collection

The benchmark comprises over 3.3K video-text instances, derived from 569 unique videos, to ensure diversity and controllability. Data is sourced from three main types:
- **Blender Simulation**: Provides complete control over the environment and guarantees precise ground-truth annotations. It enables testing 2D/3D motion, static/dynamic priors, and robustness to varying backgrounds/visual noise. Scenes range from microscopic to astronomical.
- **Lab Capturing**: Utilizes a multi-view stereo setup (four Orbbec Femto Mega cameras) to reconstruct 4D (3D space + time) object motion in real-world scenarios. This source mainly contributes to 3D tasks, featuring diverse motions like free fall, sliding, and bouncing.
- **Internet Scraping**: Leverages "in-the-wild" monocular videos, filtered to include consistent moving objects captured by a static camera with discernible reference objects. Due to the lack of calibrated depth information, internet data is restricted to 2D tasks.
Additionally, **segmented data** is created by applying SAM to isolate moving objects against solid backgrounds, allowing for controlled analysis of background complexity.

#### 3.4. Data Annotation and Question Design

- **Blender Simulations**: Ground-truth sizes, velocities, and accelerations are directly extracted via automation scripts.
- **Lab Captures**: Metric depth from depth cameras combined with multi-view stereo enables 4D scene reconstruction. Object movements are computed in world coordinates, with manual annotation of object centers across frames.
- **Internet Data**: Manual annotation of pixel-space sizes and displacements, with reference objects and approximate scale factors used to convert to world scale.
Prompts are standardized, explicitly stating the physical unit, timestamp, and target quantity. Output constraints ("output only the numerical answer and unit") are included to facilitate automated evaluation.

#### 3.5. Evaluation Metric

The primary metric is **Mean Relative Accuracy (MRA)**, adapted from VSI-Bench. MRA measures the proximity between model predictions (ˆy) and ground truth (y) by calculating the average accuracy across a set of tolerance thresholds (C = {0.1, 0.2..., 0.95}). This metric is preferred over exact match accuracy or continuous error for its robustness to measurement noise and its ability to provide a calibrated sense of "accurate enough" for physical reasoning. The overall score for a model is the unweighted mean of its MRA scores across the four kinematic categories. Models are queried up to five times, with failure to produce a parseable numerical answer resulting in a zero MRA for that instance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of 21 state-of-the-art VLMs on QuantiPhy reveals several significant findings regarding their quantitative physical reasoning abilities:

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Gap to Human Baseline

Current VLMs consistently struggle with quantitative kinematic inference. Even the best systems do not achieve human-level performance, despite theoretically having access to more precise pixel-level information.
- **Human Baseline**: Human annotators achieve an average MRA of **55.6%** across all categories, with scores ranging from 50.0% (2D-Static) to 59.1% (2D-Dynamic). This indicates that the tasks are solvable with reasonable accuracy through visual estimation and intuition.
- **Proprietary Models**: **ChatGPT-5.1** leads with **53.1% MRA**, followed closely by Gemini-2.5 Pro at 49.6%. ChatGPT-5.1 slightly surpasses humans in the 2D-Dynamic category but falls short of the overall human average. Other closed models like GPT-5 and Claude Sonnet 4.5 are significantly weaker (around 32.6% and 22.8% MRA, respectively).
- **Open-Weight Models**: The best open-weight system, Qwen3-VL-Instruct-32B, achieves **46.0% MRA**, comparable to mid-tier proprietary models but still below the top performers. A wide performance spread exists, with smaller models like Phi-4 Multimodal and SmolVLM-256M showing non-trivial but much lower MRAs.

#### 4.2. Scaling Effects and Super-Human Potential

Scaling up model parameters (e.g., within the Qwen3-VL and InternVL families) generally leads to improved MRA, especially in dynamic categories, suggesting better temporal information integration. However, these gains exhibit diminishing returns and do not close the gap to top proprietary models or human performance. The fact that VLMs, with access to pixel-accurate video tensors, do not significantly outperform humans (who rely on coarse visual estimation) suggests they fundamentally **under-utilize visual precision and physical priors**, indicating that scale alone is insufficient for faithful physical reasoning.

#### 4.3. Dissecting Quantitative Reasoning in VLMs

- **Effect of Scene Context**:
 - **Background Complexity**: Background complexity has only a mild effect. Models perform slightly better with SAM-denoised (plain) backgrounds, implying reduced distractions. Interestingly, performance in visually complex scenes (C) is often *better* than simple-textured (S) backgrounds, possibly because complex scenes offer more implicit reference cues (e.g., tiles, road markings) for scale and motion inference.
 - **Number of Objects**: Scenes with multiple objects consistently yield higher MRA than single-object scenes. Additional objects provide extra reference targets for size and speed comparisons.

- **Faithfulness to Inputs: VLMs Rely on Memorized World Knowledge, Not Just Visuals/Priors**: This is a critical finding.
 - **Prior-Only vs. Video+Prior**: When the video is removed (prior-only setting), many models exhibit only modest performance drops compared to the video+prior setting. This indicates that models can achieve reasonable scores by relying on their internal prior knowledge about typical object sizes and speeds, with the actual video frames adding limited value. They act more like "powerful guessers" conditioned on textual hints.
 - **Counterfactual Priors**: When the provided physical prior is counterfactually altered (e.g., a car length given as 5670m), VLM performance drops dramatically (by 70-80%). Instead of faithfully scaling their predictions based on the altered prior, models tend to revert to plausible magnitudes derived from **pre-trained world knowledge** about typical object sizes, ignoring the explicit numerical input. This suggests models "memorize" rather than "reason" faithfully from inputs.

- **Effect of Structured Prompts (Chain-of-Thought)**:
 - Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, which decomposes questions into step-by-step pixel measurement, scale estimation, and rescaling, was largely ineffective. Only three of 21 models showed improvement, with most exhibiting worse performance than direct zero-shot prompting. This implies that VLMs struggle to reliably solve intermediate numerical subproblems, and decomposing the task often amplifies and propagates errors.

In summary, VLMs struggle to establish a reliable, input-faithful link between visual observations and quantitative physical facts. Their quantitative kinematic inferences are often dominated by internal, pre-trained world knowledge, with visual evidence and explicit numerical priors acting as soft hints rather than hard constraints.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The QuantiPhy benchmark represents a significant advancement in the evaluation of Vision-Language Models, moving beyond qualitative assessments to demand precise numerical reasoning about the physical world. Its potential impact is multi-faceted:

#### 5.1. Highlighting a Critical Gap in VLM Capabilities

The most immediate significance is QuantiPhy's revelation of a fundamental weakness in current state-of-the-art VLMs: a lack of "input faithfulness" and an over-reliance on memorized world knowledge. This disconnect between visual processing, explicit numerical priors, and quantitative output limits their reliability for real-world applications where precise physical understanding is essential. This diagnostic insight is crucial for the research community, as it pinpoints *why* current VLMs fall short in physical reasoning, not just *that* they do.

#### 5.2. Driving Future Research and Development

QuantiPhy provides a clear roadmap for future VLM development. The identified gaps necessitate:
- **Development of Physics-Informed VLMs**: New architectures and training methodologies that explicitly encourage models to extract and utilize pixel-level information and adhere to numerical priors, rather than defaulting to semantic guesses. This could involve physics-informed objectives, specialized pre-training on physics-rich data, or novel reasoning modules.
- **Richer Datasets for Diverse Physics**: The benchmark's limitations (focus on translational motion, rigid objects, fixed camera) highlight the need for more comprehensive datasets incorporating rotational dynamics, deformable objects, varied camera perspectives, and complex multi-object interactions. Such datasets would further challenge models to generalize their physical understanding.
- **Improved Interpretability and Debugging**: QuantiPhy's diagnostic capabilities can help researchers better understand *how* models are reasoning, allowing for targeted improvements when they fail to be input-faithful or to leverage structured prompts effectively.

#### 5.3. Advancing Embodied AI and Real-World Applications

The ultimate goal of this research is to advance the development of generalist embodied AI agents capable of sophisticated reasoning and interaction with the physical world. By pushing VLMs towards numerically grounded physical understanding, QuantiPhy contributes directly to the foundational capabilities required for:
- **Autonomous Systems**: More accurate perception of object states (size, speed, acceleration) is vital for safer and more efficient autonomous vehicles and robots.
- **Augmented/Virtual Reality**: Realistic and physically consistent interactions in AR/VR environments depend on precise estimation of real-world objects and their dynamics.
- **Robotics**: Robots need accurate physical models to manipulate objects, predict outcomes of actions, and navigate complex environments.

In conclusion, QuantiPhy is not merely a new dataset; it is a conceptual shift in how we evaluate AI's understanding of physics. By demanding quantitative accuracy, it sets a higher bar for VLM capabilities and offers critical diagnostic insights that will guide the next generation of intelligent systems towards a truly grounded and robust understanding of our physical reality.
