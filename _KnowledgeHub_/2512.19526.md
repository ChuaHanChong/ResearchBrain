# QuantiPhy: A Quantitative Benchmark Evaluating Physical Reasoning Abilities of Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Stanford University introduce QuantiPhy, a benchmark designed to quantitatively evaluate Vision-Language Models' (VLMs) physical reasoning in videos. The benchmark reveals that state-of-the-art VLMs struggle with input faithfulness, often relying on memorized world knowledge about object properties rather than precisely extracting information from visual cues or adhering to explicit numerical priors.

## Problem

- Existing physical reasoning benchmarks for Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are predominantly qualitative and VQA-based, limiting insight into models' ability to infer precise numerical physical properties.
- Generalist AI agents and embodied systems require a robust, quantitative understanding of physical laws (e.g., object size, velocity, acceleration) for real-world applications like autonomous driving and robotics.
- Current evaluation methods fail to assess if VLMs can leverage implicit physical knowledge from training data to perform end-to-end quantitative reasoning from visual observations.

## Method

- **QuantiPhy** is introduced as the first benchmark to quantitatively evaluate VLM physical reasoning on moving objects through kinematic inference tasks (determining size, velocity, acceleration in world space).
- The benchmark categorizes tasks along two axes: dimensionality (2D vs. 3D motion) and physical prior type (static size vs. dynamic velocity/acceleration), resulting in four core task categories.
- It utilizes a diverse dataset of over 3.3K video-text instances from Blender simulations, multi-view lab captures, and internet videos, employing Mean Relative Accuracy (MRA) as the primary evaluation metric across various tolerance thresholds.

## Results

- Current state-of-the-art VLMs consistently struggle with quantitative kinematic inference, achieving significantly lower Mean Relative Accuracy (MRA) scores (best at **53.1%** for ChatGPT-5.1) compared to human performance (**55.6%** MRA).
- Scaling model parameters generally leads to MRA improvements, particularly in dynamic categories, but these gains show diminishing returns and do not close the gap to top proprietary models or human accuracy.
- VLMs perform slightly better in scenes with multiple objects and visually complex backgrounds (compared to simple textures), suggesting these offer more implicit reference cues for scale and motion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLMs exhibit a fundamental lack of "input faithfulness," frequently relying on pre-trained world knowledge about object properties rather than accurately utilizing visual observations or explicit numerical priors.
> - Models show only a modest performance gain from video input compared to a "prior-only" setting, indicating they often act as sophisticated guessers conditioned on textual hints.
> - Counterfactual physical priors (e.g., a car length given as 5670m) drastically impair VLM performance, as models tend to revert to plausible magnitudes from memorized knowledge, disregarding the explicit numerical input.
