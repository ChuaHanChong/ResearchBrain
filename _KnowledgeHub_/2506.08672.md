# RuleReasoner: Reinforced Rule-based Reasoning via Domain-aware Dynamic Sampling

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the State Key Laboratory of General Artificial Intelligence, BIGAI, developed RULEREASONER, a framework that enhances rule-based reasoning in language models through a novel domain-aware dynamic sampling strategy. It achieved an 84.0% pass@1 on in-distribution tasks and a 10.4% improvement over frontier Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) on out-of-distribution benchmarks while reducing training steps by 1.4x.

## Problem

- Current Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) struggle with diverse rule formats, scalability in long contexts, and precise instruction following for rule application.
- A significant generalization gap exists, where models overfit to seen tasks and fail to adapt to unseen reasoning tasks, forms, or difficulties, hindering real-world deployment.
- Conventional Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) approaches suffer from sample inefficiency and rely on static, manually engineered data mixing strategies.

## Method

- Introduces ==RULECOLLECTION==-32K, a novel, diverse dataset spanning eight rule-based reasoning tasks with varying depths (0-7 hops), formats, and inference rules to support curriculum learning.
- Leverages an on-policy Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) framework, adapting the reward function for rule-based exact matching and strategically discarding the KL loss term for better exploration and generalization.
- Develops Domain-aware Dynamic Sampling (DADS), an online scheduler that dynamically adjusts sampling probabilities for different domains within each training batch based on historical performance, prioritizing under-optimized tasks.

## Results

- RULEREASONER-**8B** achieved an average pass@1 of **84.0%** across eight in-distribution tasks, outperforming frontier LRMs (e.g., **4.1%** over OpenAI-o1, **4.8%** over DAPO).
- Demonstrated strong out-of-distribution generalization, with RULEREASONER-**8B** surpassing frontier LRMs by **10.4%** on challenging OOD benchmarks and achieving a **56.0%** gain from the baseline Qwen3-**8B** model.
- Achieved comparable OOD performance with approximately 72 fewer training steps (**1.4x** acceleration) compared to existing RLVR methods like DAPO, indicating improved computational and sample efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically adjusting data sampling via DADS effectively balances training across diverse domains, improving sample efficiency and preventing over-optimization on easy tasks while under-fitting on complex ones.
> - Discarding the KL divergence term from policy optimization and the entropy bonus encourages broader exploration of valid reasoning steps, leading to better generalization to unseen rules and tasks.
> - Improved out-of-distribution performance is attributed to the emergence of "meta introspection" in the model, enabling self-simulation, self-verification, and correction of logical flaws during reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Rule-based reasoning, the ability to derive conclusions from a set of facts and predefined logical rules, is a cornerstone of intelligent behavior, mimicking structured deductive processes essential in fields like law, mathematics, and medical diagnostics. As Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) and Large Language Models (LLMs) continue to demonstrate impressive capabilities, particularly with techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting and Reinforcement Learning (RL), the challenge of robust and generalizable rule-based reasoning remains significant.

Current frontier LRMs face several limitations:
- **Variations in Rules:** Real-world applications encounter diverse rule formats, types, and complexities, which existing models struggle to handle adaptively.
- **Scalability Challenges:** While model scaling often improves performance, it also leads to issues such as "lost in the middle," where models fail to attend to relevant information in long contexts.
- **Weak Instruction Following:** LLMs can exhibit difficulty in precisely understanding and applying rules and facts provided in context.
- **Generalization Gap:** Many methods tend to overfit to seen tasks and struggle to generalize to unseen reasoning tasks, forms, or difficulties, a critical barrier for real-world deployment.
- **Reinforcement Learning Efficiency:** Conventional RL approaches, including RL with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), often suffer from sample inefficiency and reliance on static data mixing strategies that are manually engineered and not adaptive to the model's learning progress across diverse domains.

RULEREASONER enters this landscape by specifically addressing these limitations. It builds upon recent advancements in RLVR, which have shown promise in enhancing mathematical reasoning and code generation, but aims to overcome their data efficiency issues and extend their applicability to the more diverse and dynamic domain of rule-based reasoning. The paper differentiates itself from prior rule-based reasoners (which might be tied to specific symbolic forms or limited task types), frontier LLMs (which, despite their size, still struggle with consistent rule application and OOD generalization), and existing RLVR and curriculum learning methods (by introducing a novel, self-adaptive dynamic sampling strategy). The work positions itself as a step towards more controllable and adaptable reasoning capabilities, essential for AI systems operating under ever-changing circumstances and requiring transparency and explainability.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of the RULEREASONER project are clearly articulated:
1. **Enhance Rule-Based Reasoning Capability:** To significantly improve the ability of language models to perform rule-based reasoning, explicitly considering variations in rule formats, types, and complexity.
2. **Ensure Generalization to Unseen Tasks:** To validate that the improved reasoning capability can generalize effectively to unseen reasoning tasks, forms, and difficulties (Out-of-Distribution, OOD scenarios), moving beyond mere memorization.
3. **Mitigate RL Limitations:** To address the sample inefficiency inherent in on-policy RL and overcome the challenges of applying rigid rules in dynamic and multi-domain contexts, specifically by obviating the need for human-engineered static mix-training.
4. **Improve Computational Efficiency:** To achieve higher reasoning performance with notably fewer training steps and computational costs compared to prior methods.

The motivation stems from the recognition that while LRMs possess remarkable reasoning potential, their practical application in rule-based domains is hindered by their struggles with rule variations, instruction following, and generalization. The authors aim to investigate whether a carefully designed RLVR framework, coupled with an adaptive data sampling strategy and a comprehensive rule-centric dataset, can effectively tackle these challenges, leading to more robust, adaptable, and efficient rule-based reasoners.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RULEREASONER introduces an effective method for rule-based reasoning via a curated collection of tasks and a novel domain-aware dynamic sampling approach within an RLVR framework.

#### 3.1. Novel Rule-centric Dataset: RULECOLLECTION-32K

A core component of RULEREASONER is the creation of a large and diverse dataset, RULECOLLECTION-32K, spanning eight distinct rule-based reasoning tasks. The curation principles emphasize:
- **Varying Depths:** Including 0-7 hop reasoning data to support curriculum learning across different complexity levels and reasoning forms (deductive, inductive, analytical).
- **Different Formats:** Incorporating explicit or implicit rules as premises or constraints, fostering flexibility in rule recognition and application.
- **Multiple Inference Rules:** Focusing on diversity of inference rules to facilitate learning dynamic rule employment and length generalization.
- **Context Dependency:** Requiring adaptive application of contextual rules, moving beyond memorization or common sense.
- **Robust Evaluation:** Prioritizing boolean and multiple-choice questions for precise outcome rewards and evaluation.
This dataset is critical for training and ID/OOD evaluation, preventing models from merely memorizing specific rules or datasets.

#### 3.2. RLVR for Rule-based Reasoning

RULEREASONER leverages an on-policy Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) framework, adapted for rule-based reasoning:
- **Reward Shaping:** A rule-based Exact Match (EM) reward function is designed to evaluate responses based on the final answer's correctness and adherence to format, returning 1 for equivalence and -1 otherwise.
- **Policy Optimization:** The framework adopts a basic form of GRPO (Gradient Policy Optimization) but strategically discards the KL loss term. This encourages exploration of valid reasoning steps rather than strict imitation, improving generalization, and saving memory/computation.
- **On-Policy Training:** Strict on-policy training is followed, with only one gradient update after a group of rollouts, to ensure stable RL training and prevent entropy collapse.

#### 3.3. Domain-aware Dynamic Sampling (DADS)

This is the central algorithmic innovation. DADS dynamically adjusts the probability of sampling data from different domains within each training batch, addressing domain imbalance and sample inefficiency:
- **Domain-aware Rewards:** At each training step, the algebraic mean of historical EM rewards (¯r_d_i,s) is calculated for each domain d_i. An "under-optimization estimation" (1 - er_s,d_i) is then derived by comparing the estimated reward to a target reward (r_target = 1).
- **Decaying Importance Sampling:** Exponentially Weighted Moving Average (EWMA) is applied to historical estimated rewards, incorporating a smoothing factor (α) to provide a more stable, long-term estimate of domain performance.
- **Domain Re-weighting:** A softmax function, incorporating hyperparameters τ (temperature for priority strength) and ϵ (minimum sampling weight), normalizes these under-optimization estimations into domain sampling weights (W_s). This ensures a minimum sampling probability for all domains while prioritizing under-optimized ones.
- **Iterative Process:** The updated weights (W_s) are used to construct the next training batch (B_s), and this process iterates throughout training, serving as an online scheduler.

#### 3.4. Training Regularization

To prevent memorization and improve generalization, RULEREASONER incorporates several regularization techniques during RL training:
- **Disabling Entropy Bonus:** Removes a common RL component to avoid entropy explosion without cold-start bootstrapping.
- **Discarding KL Divergence:** Eliminates the KL term from policy optimization, arguing that the rule-based reward function already addresses distributional shifts, thus saving resources and encouraging more exploration.
- **Rules Order Shuffling:** Randomly shuffles the order of contextual logical rules for each training sample to prevent models from memorizing rule sequences.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

RULEREASONER-4B and RULEREASONER-8B, using Qwen3 base models, demonstrate significant performance improvements and efficiencies:

#### 4.1. In-Distribution (ID) Performance

- **Superiority over Baselines:** RULEREASONER-8B achieved an average pass@1 of 84.0% across eight ID tasks, outperforming frontier LRMs (e.g., ∆4.1% over OpenAI-o1, ∆4.5% over Claude-3.7-Sonnet), strong prior rule-based reasoners (e.g., HtT, Chain-of-Logic), behavioral cloning methods, and advanced RLVRs (e.g., ∆4.8% over DAPO).
- **Balanced Domain Performance:** RULEREASONER exhibited lower intra-task performance variance (3.1% compared to DAPO's 3.6%), indicating balanced improvement across diverse domains without significant tradeoffs.

#### 4.2. Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Performance

- **Strong OOD Generalization:** RULEREASONER-8B significantly surpassed frontier LRMs on challenging OOD benchmarks (BigBench Hard, BigBench Extra Hard, ProverQA), achieving a remarkable ∆10.4% improvement over OpenAI-o1. It demonstrated the highest average performance gains (∆56.0% from baseline Qwen3-8B) across these OOD tasks.
- **Effectiveness of Smaller Models:** RULEREASONER-4B also showed strong OOD performance, achieving an average pass@1 of 78.3% (∆7.3% over OpenAI-o1), suggesting that effective rule learning is possible even with smaller model sizes.
- **Contrast with SFT:** Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) showed comparable ID performance but significantly lagged in OOD performance (34.4% vs. 54.5% for RULEREASONER), underscoring RLVR's advantage in generalization to unseen rules.
- **Extrapolation to Unseen Reasoning Tasks:** RULEREASONER-8B, trained only on rule-based tasks, showed substantial improvements on other general verifiable reasoning tasks like AIME 2025 (+20.0%), GPQA (Diamond) (+28.3%), and Coin Flip (+50.4%) compared to the base model, demonstrating cross-domain generalization.

#### 4.3. Computational Efficiency

- **Faster Training Convergence:** RULEREASONER achieved comparable OOD performance with approximately 72 fewer training steps (1.4× acceleration) compared to existing RLVR methods like DAPO. This indicates improved sample efficiency due to dynamic batch-level data curricula.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Analyses and Generalization Mechanism

- **DADS Advantages:** DADS successfully balanced training across domains, actively increasing weights for underperformed tasks like AR-LSAT without negatively impacting other domains. It prevented over-optimization on easy tasks and under-fitting on complex ones.
- **Impact of Task Mixing:** Incremental task mixing strategies consistently improved OOD performance, especially with larger models, validating the benefit of diverse training data curation.
- **Improved Rule Application Quality:** Case studies (Table 4, 15) demonstrated that RULEREASONER significantly improved the model's ability to derive valid, logically rigorous reasoning chains, overcoming issues like poor reasoning logic or distraction by noisy rules observed in untrained models.
- **Meta Introspection for Generalization:** The OOD performance gains are attributed to the emergence of "meta introspection" – self-simulation and self-verification abilities during training. The trained model explores candidate paths, verifies logical consistency against inferred rules, and corrects flawed steps, enabling generalization beyond seen tasks by eliminating cognitive biases and logical fallacies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RULEREASONER presents a significant advancement in enhancing the rule-based reasoning capabilities of large language models, offering both improved effectiveness and efficiency.

**Significance:**
- **Bridging the Generalization Gap:** By effectively generalizing to unseen rule formats, complexities, and out-of-distribution tasks, RULEREASONER addresses a critical limitation of current LLMs, which often struggle beyond their training distribution.
- **Advancing RLVR:** The novel Domain-aware Dynamic Sampling (DADS) algorithm significantly improves the efficiency and stability of multi-domain RLVR training. It moves beyond static, human-engineered data mixing strategies, offering a self-adaptive online curriculum that balances learning across diverse tasks without requiring external difficulty estimation or excessive computational overhead.
- **Practical Applicability:** The method's ability to imbue models with more rigorous and verifiable reasoning trajectories makes it highly relevant for applications requiring transparency, explainability, and adherence to domain constraints, such as legal tech, medical diagnostics, and scientific discovery.
- **Efficiency for Smaller Models:** Demonstrating strong performance with 4B and 8B parameter models suggests that high-quality rule-based reasoning is achievable without relying solely on ever-larger models, potentially democratizing access to advanced reasoning capabilities.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Foundation for Trustworthy AI:** By enabling models to reason more explicitly and verifiably according to rules, RULEREASONER contributes to the development of more trustworthy and accountable AI systems.
- **Next-Generation AI Assistants:** The enhanced adaptability and generalization could lead to more intelligent AI assistants capable of navigating complex rule sets in real-world, dynamic environments.
- **Data-Centric AI:** The work highlights the power of data-centric approaches, not just in dataset curation but also in dynamic data scheduling during training, as a potent lever for improving AI model performance and efficiency.
- **Future Research Directions:** RULEREASONER opens avenues for further exploration into self-adaptive curriculum learning, meta-reasoning, and the development of even more diverse and challenging rule-based reasoning benchmarks. The identified limitations regarding scalability to very large models and robustness to noisy rules also chart important paths for future research.

**Limitations and Ethical Considerations (as stated by authors):**
The authors acknowledge limitations including the scarce and imbalanced nature of rule-based reasoning data, which might not cover all real-world complexities. The method's constraint by the quality of rule filtering, especially with noisy or redundant rules, is another challenge. While effective for smaller models, scalability to very large-scale modeling remains unverified. Ethically, the authors emphasize responsible development, transparency for interpretability, rigorous validation to avoid biases in manually crafted rules, and optimizing for computational efficiency to mitigate environmental impacts.

In conclusion, RULEREASONER represents a significant step forward in making language models more robust, adaptable, and efficient at rule-based reasoning, with broad implications for the development of more capable and trustworthy AI.
