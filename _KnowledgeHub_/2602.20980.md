# CrystaL: Spontaneous Emergence of Visual Latents in MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> CrystaL is a self-supervised, single-stage framework for Multimodal Large Language Models that cultivates robust latent reasoning by enforcing consistency across dual processing paths, leading to the spontaneous emergence of task-relevant visual semantics. The framework achieves state-of-the-art performance on perception-intensive benchmarks with a significant reduction in training data.

## Problem

- Existing latent Chain-of-Thought (CoT) methods for MLLMs suffer from insufficient supervision signals for intermediate latent states, hindering genuine visual reasoning.
- Prior approaches often depend on external modules, auxiliary human annotations, or complex multi-stage training, increasing complexity and cost.
- The reliance on indirect supervision can lead to MLLMs performing superficial reasoning or struggling with fine-grained visual information in complex perception tasks.

## Method

- A single-stage, self-supervised framework employs a ==dual-path architecture== to process high-fidelity and stochastically corrupted visual inputs concurrently.
- ==Visual latent tokens== from the intact path are copied to the corrupted path, creating an ==information bottleneck== that compels these latents to encode essential visual semantics.
- Alignment objectives, including predictive distribution consistency and mechanistic consistency, enforce similarity between the two paths' outputs and internal reasoning patterns.

## Results

- CrystaL achieved a state-of-the-art average score of **75.4%** across diverse perception-intensive benchmarks, surpassing strong baselines.
- The framework demonstrated enhanced performance in high-resolution perception, improving scores on HRBench 4K by **4.8%** and HRBench 8K by **6.2%** over Qwen2.5-VL-7B.
- It exhibited high data efficiency, achieving top accuracy with only **16,000** training samples, a more than **6x** reduction compared to some previous methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-supervised consistency training can compel MLLMs to spontaneously distill task-relevant visual semantics into their internal latent representations.
> - Stochastic image corruption acts as an effective information bottleneck, forcing the model to rely on visual latent tokens and ensuring their functional indispensability.
> - Aligning both the output predictive distributions and the internal attention mechanisms provides strong supervisory signals for effective latent visual reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) represent a significant advancement in Artificial Intelligence, integrating powerful language models with large-scale visual encoders to achieve general-purpose vision-language understanding. A key area of development within MLLMs is the cultivation of complex reasoning capabilities, often inspired by Chain-of-Thought (CoT) methods. These methods decompose intricate queries into intermediate inferential steps.

The broader landscape of multimodal CoT reasoning can be categorized into three main paradigms:
- **Textual CoT**: Converts visual inputs into discrete natural language rationales. While effective for certain problems, this approach can suffer from limited expressiveness for continuous visual details, information loss, and potential hallucination, as models may rely on self-generated text rather than raw visual inputs.
- **Tool-augmented CoT**: Extends reasoning by invoking external vision models to handle perception tasks. These pipelines are often non-differentiable and their performance is contingent upon the quality of the off-the-shelf tools utilized.
- **Latent CoT**: Performs reasoning directly within continuous hidden states of the model. This paradigm offers advantages such as preserving visual information, avoiding quantization loss inherent in discrete representations, and enabling seamless integration of vision and language, leading to faster inference.

Despite the advantages of latent CoT, existing methods face a limitation. The primary supervision signal, often the standard next-token prediction objective, primarily constrains the final output rather than providing explicit guidance for how intermediate latent states should encode or preserve critical visual evidence. To address this, prior works have resorted to auxiliary supervision:
- Using features from external vision models (e.g., CoVT leveraging SAM or DINO).
- Employing human-annotated images that describe reasoning trajectories (e.g., Monet).
- Enforcing visual bottlenecks through attention masking, often requiring multi-stage training (e.g., LIVR).

These existing solutions frequently entail external modules, additional data, heuristic supervision signals that may be task-dependent, or complex multi-stage training pipelines. This often prevents the latent space from maintaining semantically grounded visual information effectively aligned with the final generative objective.

CrystaL positions itself within this landscape by addressing the inadequacy of current supervision paradigms for latent reasoning. It aims to develop a principled, self-supervised, and single-stage framework that intrinsically guides latent representations to "crystallize" into task-relevant visual semantics, thereby overcoming the limitations of previous approaches by eliminating the dependence on external priors or manual annotations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for CrystaL stems from a recognized deficiency in existing latent Chain-of-Thought (CoT) methods within Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). While latent CoT offers benefits such as preserving visual information and seamless vision-language integration by operating in continuous hidden states, the mechanisms for supervising these intermediate latent states are often indirect and insufficient.

**The core problem is the misalignment between existing supervision signals and the functional requirements of latent reasoning.** Specifically:
- **Indirect Supervision**: The standard next-token prediction objective typically used in MLLMs primarily supervises the final output. It provides limited explicit guidance for how intermediate latent states should encode or preserve critical visual information that is essential for complex reasoning steps. This can lead to situations where models generate answers without genuinely engaging or relying on the latent visual reasoning path.
- **Reliance on Heuristic or External Supervision**: To compensate for this lack of intrinsic guidance, prior latent CoT approaches have introduced various forms of auxiliary supervision. These include:
 - **External Vision Models**: Methods like CoVT leverage pre-defined features from external vision models (e.g., SAM, DINO). This makes the reasoning process reliant on specific external tools, which can be non-differentiable and dependent on the quality of these tools.
 - **Human-Annotated Data**: Approaches such as Monet introduce specialized datasets with human annotations of reasoning trajectories or bounding box supervision to guide intermediate steps. This is resource-intensive and often task-dependent.
 - **Multi-stage Training**: Some methods, like LIVR, attempt to create visual bottlenecks through attention masking but often require complex multi-stage training pipelines, adding to the complexity and computational cost.

These auxiliary supervision methods are often heuristic, task-dependent, and do not inherently guarantee that the latent tokens are functionally aligned with the final generation objective. They enforce similarity to predefined visual features rather than directly supervising the fidelity of the reasoning process itself. Consequently, the latent space may not consistently maintain semantically grounded visual information that is truly indispensable for robust multimodal reasoning.

**CrystaL's key objectives are to address these limitations by:**
1. **Enabling Spontaneous Emergence of Visual Latents**: To design a mechanism that guides latent representations to autonomously capture and "crystallize" into task-relevant visual semantics without relying on auxiliary annotations or external modules.
2. **Providing Self-Supervision**: To introduce a consistency-driven training paradigm that offers task-aligned self-supervision for latent representations, thereby eliminating the need for external priors or manual labels.
3. **Developing a Single-Stage Framework**: To simplify the training process by integrating all objectives into a unified, single-stage framework, in contrast to the multi-stage pipelines often used by previous methods.
4. **Enhancing Reasoning Robustness and Fine-Grained Understanding**: To improve the model's performance on perception-intensive benchmarks, demonstrating superior fine-grained visual understanding while maintaining robust reasoning capabilities, especially under visually degraded conditions. The motivation is to ensure that latent inferential steps remain tightly coupled with visual evidence, fostering more reliable multimodal intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CrystaL (Crystallized Latent Reasoning) is a single-stage framework designed to instill robust latent reasoning into Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) through a process termed Latent Crystallization. This process anchors targeted semantics into dedicated latent tokens by employing a dual-path architecture and consistency-driven objectives.

#### 3.1. Reasoning with Chain of Thoughts

CrystaL operates within the Latent CoT paradigm. Instead of discrete textual tokens, reasoning steps are represented as continuous latent variables within the model’s hidden space. The framework explicitly supervises these latent representations during training to ensure they preserve high-dimensional semantic nuances. The model `M` generates an output sequence `Y` given a visual input `I` and a textual query `Q`, maximizing the likelihood of the ground-truth answer and Chain-of-Thought `P_M(Ans, CoT | I, Q)`.

#### 3.2. Visual Corruption as Information Bottleneck (Stochastic Image Corruption - SIC)

A central challenge in training latent reasoning models is ensuring that visual latent tokens are actively utilized rather than bypassed when raw visual tokens alone suffice for an answer. To compel the model to engage the latent reasoning path, CrystaL introduces the Stochastic Image Corruption (SIC) module. This module applies a generalized operator `C` to transform a high-fidelity image `I` into a degraded observation `I_cor`. The corruption is parameterized by a mode `S` and a stochastic intensity `η`.
The library of corruption primitives `S` includes:
- **Spectral Decay (G)**: Gaussian-based low-pass filtering to suppress high-frequency textures while retaining global topology.
- **Spatial Discontinuity (K)**: Random cropping, masking, or Jigsaw shuffling to disrupt geometric coherence.
- **Chromatic Distortion (V)**: Jittering color distributions to decouple semantic shape from pixel-level intensity.
- **Stochastic Noise (N)**: Additive Gaussian or impulse noise.

#### 3.3. Dual-Path Latent Reasoning Framework

The core of CrystaL's training involves two parallel processing streams during a single forward pass, as illustrated in Figure 2:

- **Intact Path**:
 - Receives the original, high-fidelity image `I_int = I`.
 - The input sequence is `S_int = {I_int, Q, CoT, Ans}`.
 - Acts as a "teacher" stream, utilizing complete visual evidence to compute the latent states of visual tokens, denoted `T_lat = {T_1, T_2..., T_l}` (where `l` is the number of layers in the language model). These latents provide raw information from the intact sample.
 - Generates output logits `P_int`, which serve as a reference for the corrupted path and for computing a standard cross-entropy loss.

- **Corrupted Path**:
 - Operates on the perturbed image `I_cor = C(I)`.
 - The corresponding input sequence is `S_cor = {I_cor, Q, CoT, Ans}`.
 - A crucial step is enforced: at each decoder layer, the latent representations corresponding to the visual tokens in the corrupted path are *replaced* by the `T_lat` derived from the intact path (Figure 3).
 - The model then generates output logits `P_cor` for this perturbed sequence.

This dual-path design bypasses the need for manual data labeling to supervise visual tokens. By forcing the corrupted path to "borrow" complementary information from the intact path via `T_lat`, it ensures that these visual latent tokens become indispensable for successful reasoning, even when the direct visual input is compromised.

#### 3.4. Alignment Objectives

To further guide `T_lat` to absorb robust image features, two alignment objectives are introduced:

- **Predictive Distribution Consistency (L_kl)**:
 - This objective anchors the reasoning process by ensuring the corrupted path's final predictions align with those of the high-fidelity intact path.
 - It minimizes the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the output probability distributions `P_int` and `P_cor`, specifically for the tokens corresponding to the ground-truth answer `A`.
 - `L_kl = (1/M) * sum_{i in Ω_A} D_KL(P(y_hat_i | S_int) || P(y_hat_i | S_cor))`, where `Ω_A` are answer token indices. This compels the model to recover targeted semantics from `T_lat`.

- **Mechanistic Consistency (L_attn)**:
 - While `L_kl` aligns outputs, `L_attn` ensures consistent inferential patterns internally.
 - It constrains the attention weights assigned by the answer tokens to the visual latent tokens. Let `A` be the aggregated attention maps.
 - `L_attn = E_{l in L_sub} ||A^(l)_int - A^(l)_cor||^2_F`, minimizing the squared Frobenius norm of the discrepancy between attention maps across a subset of decoder layers `L_sub`. This ensures reasoning behavior remains grounded in semantic representations encoded by `T_lat`, even with visual degradation.

#### 3.5. Overall Training Objective

CrystaL integrates these objectives into a unified, single-stage optimization framework:

- **Cross-Entropy Losses**:
 - `L_int_ce`: Standard next-token prediction loss on the intact path, preserving instruction-following and generative capabilities.
 - `L_cor_ce`: Standard next-token prediction loss on the corrupted path, encouraging the model to extract information from the visual latent tokens (`T_lat`) when the direct visual input `I_cor` is degraded.

- **Total Loss**: `L_total = L_int_ce + L_cor_ce + L_kl + L_attn`

This combined objective incentivizes the model to not only produce coherent textual responses but also actively encode task-essential visual semantics into `T_lat`. Consequently, `T_lat` acts as a repository of semantic invariants that can sustain complex reasoning under varied perceptual conditions, ensuring a latent reasoning path that is consistent and grounded in visual logic.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of CrystaL involved fine-tuning a Qwen2.5-VL-7B base model using LoRA adapters on a workstation equipped with 4 NVIDIA A40 GPUs. Performance was assessed using the VLMEvalKit framework across a suite of perception-intensive benchmarks, including CVBench (2D, 3D), HRBench (4K, 8K), RealWorldQA (RWQA), BLINK, V\*Bench, and POPE (for hallucination robustness). CrystaL was compared against open-source baselines such as Qwen2.5-VL-7B, CoVT, LVR, Vision-R1, LIVR, and SKILA, with some closed-source models also provided for broader context.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Analysis

- **Overall Performance**: CrystaL achieved a leading average score of 75.4% across all evaluated benchmarks, establishing a new state-of-the-art among the compared open-source models. This represented a substantial average gain of 4.3% over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline.
- **Perception-Intensive Tasks**: CrystaL demonstrated particular strengths in high-resolution perception, outperforming Qwen2.5-VL-7B by 4.8% on HRBench 4K and 6.2% on HRBench 8K. It also showed significant gains on CVBench-3D (+11.1%) and V\*Bench (+6.3%) over the baseline.
- **Comparison with Latent CoT Baselines**: CrystaL surpassed other advanced latent CoT methods like CoVT (73.8% average) and LIVR (74.0% average) by 1.6% and 1.4% respectively, demonstrating improved capabilities. While Vision-R1 showed lower performance in some perception tasks (e.g., 8.0% on CVBench-2D), CrystaL maintained a robust and balanced performance profile across diverse benchmarks.
- **Robustness to Hallucination**: On the POPE benchmark, designed to diagnose hallucination, CrystaL achieved 88.7%, an improvement of 2.2% over Qwen2.5-VL-7B.

#### 4.2. Data Efficiency

CrystaL exhibited notable data efficiency. It achieved state-of-the-art accuracy using only 16,000 training samples. This contrasts with methods like SKILA, which required 100,000 samples, indicating that CrystaL can achieve superior multimodal understanding with over six times fewer training data, highlighting its scalability in data-scarce scenarios.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Impact of Latent Token Configuration (Quantity and Type)**:
 - Employing "diverse" latent tokens consistently outperformed using "identical" tokens, suggesting that semantic variety within the latent space is crucial for capturing complex multimodal dependencies.
 - A non-monotonic relationship was observed between performance and token count, with 8 tokens yielding the optimal balance (72.0% average with diverse tokens), outperforming both 4 and 16 tokens.
- **Importance of Alignment Strategy**:
 - The study demonstrated that each component of the alignment objective (cross-entropy loss on corrupted path `L_cor_ce`, predictive distribution consistency `L_kl`, and mechanistic consistency `L_attn`) contributes positively to performance. The full configuration (`L_cor_ce + L_kl + L_attn`) achieved the highest average score (72.0%).
 - Specifically, `L_kl` and `L_attn` were critical.
 - For `L_attn`, applying KL-type alignment specifically from *answer tokens to latent tokens* ("Answer to Latents (KL)") yielded the best results (72.0% average). Aligning the entire attention map ("Answer to All (KL)") or using MSE-based alignment for latent attention ("Answer to Latents (MSE)") resulted in lower performance, indicating that targeted alignment of reasoning behavior to visual latents is key.
- **Effectiveness of Corruption Strategy**:
 - Among the tested Stochastic Image Corruption (SIC) methods (Gaussian blur, random mask, jigsaw shuffling, Gaussian noise, colour distortion), **Gaussian blur consistently achieved the best performance** (72.0% average).
 - Random Mask also proved useful (71.0% average), while other strategies yielded inferior results. This suggests that Gaussian blur strikes an effective balance between suppressing information and preserving essential semantic context, which facilitates the learning of robust visual latents.
- **Comparison with Direct Fine-tuning**:
 - CrystaL consistently outperformed a direct fine-tuning baseline across all eight evaluated benchmarks. For instance, it achieved absolute gains of 2.7% on CVBench-2D and 4.2% on V\*Bench over direct fine-tuning. This highlights that the explicit latent reasoning mechanism introduced by CrystaL is more effective than standard supervised weight updates for complex multimodal tasks.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Analysis

A qualitative case study on the CVBench-2D dataset illustrated CrystaL's enhanced perceptual capabilities. In a scenario requiring spatial-relational reasoning between objects, CrystaL provided the correct answer, whereas CoVT (even when augmented with external models like SAM, DINO, and Depth Anything for structural alignment) exhibited difficulties. This supports the claim that CrystaL's latent crystallization process leads to more accurate and visually grounded reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of CrystaL represents a notable advancement in the field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly concerning the development of robust and efficient latent reasoning capabilities.

### 6. Significance of the Work

1. **Addresses a Core Challenge in Latent CoT**: CrystaL directly confronts the long-standing problem of inadequate supervision for intermediate latent states in MLLMs. By devising a method for the spontaneous emergence of task-relevant visual latents, it eliminates the need for external, often heuristic, supervision signals that have constrained previous approaches.
2. **Novel Self-Supervised Paradigm**: The dual-path architecture combined with stochastic image corruption and cross-path consistency objectives provides a principled, fully self-supervised mechanism for latent representation learning. This self-supervision is task-aligned, ensuring that the learned visual latents are functionally indispensable for the reasoning process.
3. **Efficiency and Scalability**: CrystaL operates within a single-stage training framework, which simplifies the development pipeline compared to multi-stage methods. Furthermore, its demonstrated data efficiency (achieving state-of-the-art performance with significantly fewer training samples) enhances its practical applicability and scalability in real-world scenarios where extensive annotated data may be scarce.
4. **Enhanced Performance and Robustness**: The consistent outperformance across a diverse set of perception-intensive benchmarks, including those requiring fine-grained visual understanding and high-resolution processing, indicates that CrystaL genuinely improves MLLMs' reasoning capabilities. The method's effectiveness under visually degraded conditions, enforced by the corruption strategy, suggests increased robustness.
5. **Mechanistic Insight**: The detailed ablation studies illuminate the specific contributions of each component of the CrystaL framework, particularly highlighting the importance of diverse latent tokens, specific alignment strategies (predictive and mechanistic consistency), and the appropriate choice of image corruption. This provides valuable insights into how to effectively guide latent space learning in MLLMs.

**Potential Impact:**

1. **Improved Multimodal AI Systems**: By enabling MLLMs to anchor their reasoning more firmly in visual evidence, CrystaL can contribute to the development of more reliable, accurate, and less hallucinating multimodal AI systems. This is particularly relevant for applications requiring precise visual understanding and complex inference.
2. **Reduced Training Costs and Data Dependency**: The self-supervised nature and high data efficiency of CrystaL could reduce the computational and human annotation costs associated with training advanced MLLMs. This democratizes access to powerful multimodal models by lowering the barriers to entry for researchers and practitioners with limited resources.
3. **Foundation for Future Research**: CrystaL provides a robust framework and a new perspective on latent reasoning. Future research could explore extending this approach to other modalities, incorporating more sophisticated corruption strategies, or applying these principles to even more complex, real-world reasoning challenges. The concept of "crystallizing" latent representations may inspire novel architectural designs for multimodal foundation models.
4. **Applications in Vision-Language Tasks**: The demonstrated improvements in fine-grained visual understanding and reasoning capabilities are directly beneficial for a wide array of vision-language tasks, including complex Visual Question Answering (VQA), visual dialogue systems, and multimodal content generation, potentially leading to more nuanced and contextually aware AI assistants.

In summary, CrystaL offers a scalable, efficient, and semantically grounded solution to a fundamental challenge in MLLM development, paving the way for more capable and reliable multimodal intelligence.
