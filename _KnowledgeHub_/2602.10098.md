# VLA-JEPA: Enhancing Vision-Language-Action Model with Latent World Model

> [!summary] Summary
> VLA-JEPA enhances Vision-Language-Action models for robotic control by integrating a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) and a latent world model. This framework learns robust dynamics abstractions from human videos and robot data, achieving state-of-the-art performance across simulation and real-world manipulation benchmarks with improved generalization and robustness to out-of-distribution conditions.

## Problem

- Existing latent action learning in VLA models is often biased by pixel-level variations rather than action-relevant state changes, and is vulnerable to noisy motion and irrelevant background changes in real-world videos.
- Information leakage from future observations during training can lead to latent actions that are semantically empty and ineffective for control.
- Many prior VLA pipelines rely on complex, multi-stage training procedures, increasing their overall complexity and fragility.

## Method

![[2602.10098-method.png]]
*VLA-JEPA supports cross-domain training on both human videos and robot data, where human videos are trained using an alignment loss under the latent world modeling objective, while robot data are trained with a joint objective consisting of an alignment loss and a robot action prediction loss.*

- **VLA-JEPA** integrates a ==JEPA-style latent world model== to learn robust dynamics abstractions by predicting future ==latent representations== instead of pixels, using a ==leakage-free state prediction== mechanism where future frames are used only for supervision, so ==latent actions== capture true transitional dynamics without shortcuts.
- The framework introduces ==learnable tokens== for state transitions and embodied actions, conditioned by a ==Qwen3-VL== backbone and utilizing a ==flow-matching action head== for trajectory generation.
- A unified ==two-stage pretraining== pipeline combines action-free human videos and action-labeled robot data, simplifying the training process compared to multi-stage approaches.

## Results

- Achieved SOTA performance on in-distribution (LIBERO, **97.2%** average success) and out-of-distribution (LIBERO-Plus, **79.5%** average success) benchmarks, plus the highest average success rate (**65.2%**) on Google Robot tasks in SimplerEnv real-to-sim transfer.
- Attained SOTA performance in real-world table-top manipulation tasks, producing more stable execution and exhibiting emergent skills like repeated grasping, attributed to exposure to human videos.
- Ablation studies confirmed substantial performance gains from human video pretraining and the unified training paradigm, with attention visualizations validating the leakage-free design's focus on relevant regions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging JEPA principles for latent state prediction, via a carefully designed leakage-free world model, allows the model to learn semantic abstractions of dynamics that are robust to pixel-level noise and irrelevant visual disturbances, while encoding meaningful latent actions that reflect genuine state transitions rather than merely future observations.
> - Pretraining on a combination of diverse human videos and robot data significantly enhances policy robustness, generalization, and can enable the emergence of useful real-world skills.
> - A unified, joint optimization of world modeling and action prediction objectives streamlines the training process and improves the quality of learned action representations by focusing on operation-relevant regions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper is situated within the rapidly evolving field of **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models** for robotic control. The core idea in this domain is to leverage the vast amounts of readily available internet-scale video data, often paired with natural language, to pretrain robust robot policies. This approach is highly appealing compared to relying solely on costly and narrow robot-interaction data.

Current VLA research heavily explores **latent action learning**—where models first learn representations and transition structures from videos and then adapt them for downstream control. This method seeks to extract meaningful "actions" from visual observations that capture how an underlying state evolves under interaction. However, the authors argue that many existing latent-action pretraining pipelines for VLA models suffer from fundamental issues:
1. **Pixel-level bias**: Objectives often focus on predicting pixel variations (e.g., direct future pixel prediction or compressed frame differences), leading to representations dominated by high-variance, low-control factors like texture, illumination, or background clutter, rather than action-relevant state changes.
2. **Vulnerability to noisy motion**: Real-world videos contain significant camera motion and irrelevant background changes, which frame-difference-based objectives mistakenly encode as "action," hindering the learning of meaningful dynamics.
3. **Information leakage**: When future observations influence the learned latent action during training, it creates a shortcut where the "action" simply encodes the future directly, becoming semantically empty for control.
4. **Complex multi-stage pipelines**: Many methods rely on intricate, multi-stage training (e.g., representation pretraining, latent-action learning, policy learning), increasing complexity and fragility.

VLA-JEPA directly addresses these challenges by integrating **Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA)**, a paradigm from self-supervised learning, into the VLA framework. JEPA is known for learning semantic abstractions by predicting latent representations instead of pixels, making it inherently robust to low-level noise. By adapting JEPA's principles, VLA-JEPA aims to learn dynamics abstractions that are robust, leakage-free, and directly relevant to action-centric state transitions, simplifying the overall training pipeline. This positions VLA-JEPA as a significant step towards more effective and robust self-supervised pretraining for generalist robotic policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of VLA-JEPA is to develop a more effective and robust pretraining framework for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies that can leverage internet-scale video data. The authors are specifically motivated to overcome four critical limitations they identify in existing "latent action from video" objectives:

1. **To learn action-relevant state transitions, not just pixel differences**: The goal is to move beyond representations biased by appearance or nuisance motion and instead focus on controllable, underlying state evolution.
2. **To eliminate information leakage**: Prevent the model from taking "shortcuts" by encoding future information directly into the latent action, ensuring the latent action captures true transitional dynamics.
3. **To achieve semantic robustness**: Ensure the learned dynamics abstractions are robust to common real-world noise such as camera motion and irrelevant background changes.
4. **To simplify the training pipeline**: Reduce the complexity and fragility associated with multi-stage training procedures prevalent in prior latent-action pipelines.

By achieving these objectives, VLA-JEPA aims to enable VLA models to learn more meaningful, generalizable, and robust robot policies from diverse, unlabeled video data, bridging the gap between abundant raw video and effective robotic control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VLA-JEPA introduces a novel JEPA-style pretraining framework tailored for VLA policies, characterized by its "leakage-free state prediction" mechanism and unified training approach.

#### 3.1. Model Backbone

The core of VLA-JEPA is built upon the **Qwen3-VL** large vision-language model (VLM), which uses **SigLIP-2** as its vision encoder. This choice leverages the extensive world knowledge and image understanding capabilities acquired by such VLMs during large-scale pretraining. To distill continuous latent action representations that encode state transition information, VLA-JEPA introduces special learnable tokens: `⟨latent_i⟩` for state transitions and `⟨action⟩` for embodied actions.

#### 3.2. Learning from Human Videos

The framework is designed to learn robust environment dynamics from action-free human videos.
1. **World State Encoder**: Unlike single-view approaches, VLA-JEPA creates a *unified world state representation* `s_t` by aggregating representations from multiple viewpoints. This is achieved by using a self-supervised **V-JEPA2 encoder** for single-view video state representation and then concatenating these representations across views. The V-JEPA2 encoder is kept frozen during pretraining to act as a stable target.
2. **Latent Action Pretraining through World Modeling**: To instill state transition dynamics into the `⟨latent_i⟩` tokens, an auto-regressive transformer-based world model is employed. The VLM takes initial multi-view observations and a language instruction as input, mapping `⟨latent_i⟩` tokens into latent representations `z_t`. These `z_t` representations then condition the world model to predict future chunks of world states `ˆs_t+1`.
 - **Leakage-Free Design**: Crucially, future frames are *never* provided as inputs to the VLM backbone or the latent action pathway. Instead, they are *only* used by the target encoder (frozen V-JEPA2) to construct the supervision targets (`s_t+1`). This prevents information leakage, ensuring the latent action truly captures how states transition rather than simply encoding the future.
 - **JEPA Alignment Loss**: The objective maximizes the predictive log-likelihood in semantic space, effectively a reconstruction loss in the latent space: `L_WM = E(ˆs_t - s_t)`. The frozen V-JEPA2 encoder serves as the target encoder with a stop-gradient, aligning predictions from the online predictor (world model). This latent-space prediction makes the model robust to pixel-level noise.

#### 3.3. Action Prediction with Joint Optimization Objectives

For robot data, VLA-JEPA integrates action prediction with the learned latent action representations.
1. **Action Token Conditioning**: Learnable embodied action tokens `⟨action⟩` are appended after the `⟨latent_i⟩` tokens. The VLM, leveraging its causal attention mechanism, captures dependencies among these tokens, initial visual observations, and language instructions to produce a global action-conditioning representation `z_a`.
2. **Conditional Flow-Matching Action Head**: A **flow-matching-based Transformer architecture (DiT-B)** serves as the action head. It models a distribution over continuous action trajectories, conditioned on `z_a`. The flow-matching objective `L_FM` trains the model to predict the velocity field that transforms Gaussian noise into the ground-truth action sequence.
3. **Joint Optimization**: The overall training objective for action-labeled robot data combines the flow-matching loss with the world modeling loss: `L = L_FM + βL_WM`. This enables end-to-end fusion, leveraging the learned state-transition dynamics for precise end-effector trajectory generation.

The training follows a streamlined two-stage recipe:
1. **Pretraining**: Jointly on action-free human videos (Something-Something-v2) and action-labeled robot data (Droid), optimizing both `L_WM` and `L_FM`.
2. **Fine-tuning**: On specific robot datasets (LIBERO, SimplerEnv, real-world data) with the combined objective. This unified pretraining pipeline avoids the multi-stage complexity of prior methods.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

VLA-JEPA demonstrates consistent and significant gains in generalization and robustness across various simulation and real-world robotic manipulation benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Simulation Benchmarks

- **LIBERO (In-distribution)**: VLA-JEPA achieves state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance on 2 out of 4 task suites, securing the highest average success rate of 97.2%. It notably outperforms other top models that rely on more extensive robot datasets, indicating superior data efficiency. Prior latent-action VLAs like UniVLA and LAPA consistently underperform VLA-JEPA.
- **SimplerEnv (Real-to-sim gap OOD)**: VLA-JEPA achieves the best performance on 2 out of 4 tasks for both Google Robot and WidowX, attaining the highest average success rate (65.2%) on the Google Robot and the second highest (57.3%) on the WidowX Robot. This highlights VLA-JEPA's ability to bridge the visual appearance gap between real and simulated environments with less training data compared to methods like villa-X.
- **LIBERO-Plus (OOD Perturbations)**: VLA-JEPA achieves SOTA performance on 5 out of 7 perturbation dimensions, demonstrating a substantial advantage in robustness with an average success rate of 79.5%. It particularly excels under Language, Light, Background, and Layout perturbations, verifying that its learned latent actions effectively handle task-agnostic disturbances, leading to a more robust and generalized policy.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

On real-world table-top manipulation tasks using a Franka Research 3 arm, VLA-JEPA achieves SOTA performance under both in-distribution (ID) and object layout out-of-distribution (OOD) settings. In task OOD scenarios, it achieves the second-best result. Critically, while other models like π0.5 might more accurately follow instructions, VLA-JEPA produces significantly more stable and reliable execution trajectories, rarely breaching safety boundaries. A notable emergent skill is **repeated grasping** (reopening the gripper after a failure), attributed to exposure to similar behaviors in human videos, a skill rarely present in robot-only demonstration datasets.

#### 4.3. Further Analysis and Ablation Study

- **Impact of Human Videos**: Ablation studies show that pretraining *with* human videos provides substantial performance gains, especially for robustness on LIBERO-Plus. While not always critical for ID or real-to-sim OOD scenarios (where high-quality expert robot demos are key), human videos primarily enhance the *robustness and stability* of existing skills (e.g., repeated grasping) rather than introducing entirely new action execution capabilities. Increased human video data consistently improves policy robustness.
- **Impact of Unified Pretraining**: The unified pretraining method consistently outperforms previous two-stage paradigms. Visualization of attention weights reveals that VLA-JEPA's latent actions focus more precisely on operation-relevant regions (robot arm, hand, target objects) compared to LAPA (overly dense visual information due to leakage) and UniVLA (overemphasis on semantics, attending to irrelevant background). This validates the effectiveness of the leakage-free design in capturing true action semantics.
- **Future Video Horizon (T)**: Experiments show that the model performs best when the future video horizon `T` aligns with the action horizon. Too small `T` leads to insufficient encoded information, while excessively large `T` introduces redundancy, hindering fine-grained manipulation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VLA-JEPA represents a significant advancement in the field of robot learning, particularly for scaling generalist robot policies using diverse, internet-scale video data. Its primary significance lies in:

1. **Addressing Core Limitations of Latent Action Learning**: By directly tackling pixel-level bias, noisy motion, information leakage, and multi-stage complexity, VLA-JEPA provides a more principled and effective approach to learning action-relevant state transitions. This makes robot policies more robust and less prone to brittle behavior.
2. **Leveraging JEPA for Embodied AI**: The successful integration of JEPA-style latent predictive alignment demonstrates its power in learning robust dynamics abstractions for embodied agents. This shifts the focus from pixel-reconstruction to semantic-space prediction, inherently improving robustness to visual noise.
3. **Streamlined and Unified Training**: The two-stage, unified pretraining pipeline simplifies the development and deployment of VLA models, making them more accessible and easier to manage compared to complex, fragile multi-stage systems.
4. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness**: The consistent SOTA performance across diverse benchmarks (in-distribution, real-to-sim OOD, perturbed OOD) and real-world settings highlights VLA-JEPA's ability to generalize to novel scenarios and withstand various disturbances. The emergent skill of repeated grasping from human videos is a compelling demonstration of improved real-world adaptability.
5. **Scalability and Future Directions**: The authors emphasize the scalability of the human-video pretraining paradigm, suggesting that VLA-JEPA can be further extended by incorporating more robot data and text-based reasoning to enhance generalization and robustness. This work paves the way for more intelligent, autonomous robotic systems that can learn complex skills from observation.

In essence, VLA-JEPA offers a robust foundation for building more capable and reliable VLA models, pushing the boundaries of what robots can learn from readily available human and robot video data in an increasingly complex and unstructured world.
