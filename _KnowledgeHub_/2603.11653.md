# Simple Recipe Works: Vision-Language-Action Models are Natural Continual Learners with Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Challenging established beliefs, a study on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models using Reinforcement Learning and Low-Rank Adaptation found that simple Sequential Fine-Tuning exhibits high plasticity, minimal catastrophic forgetting, and preserved zero-shot generalization, often surpassing more complex continual learning strategies.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, despite initial generalization, lack robustness and adaptability in evolving or out-of-distribution environments.
- Conventional continual learning approaches, particularly sequential fine-tuning, suffer from catastrophic forgetting, where new learning degrades performance on past tasks.
- Complex continual learning methods often face a stability-plasticity dilemma and are computationally intensive for large VLA models, making their application with Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) challenging.

## Method

- Conducted a systematic empirical study of Continual Reinforcement Learning on large pre-trained VLA models, challenging conventional wisdom regarding catastrophic forgetting.
- Evaluated ==Sequential Fine-Tuning== (Seq. FT) using ==Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)== with ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== as the on-policy RL algorithm.
- Compared Seq. FT against a comprehensive set of existing CRL methods, including regularization, replay, and parameter-isolation techniques, on five lifelong RL benchmarks.

## Results

- Sequential Fine-Tuning (Seq. FT) consistently delivered high average success rates and minimal catastrophic forgetting (less than **2% Negative Backward Transfer**) across diverse benchmarks.
- The method preserved and often enhanced zero-shot generalization, with its final policy frequently outperforming the multi-task oracle on unseen tasks.
- Seq. FT's performance and properties remained robust under environmental perturbations, across different VLA models (OpenVLA, Pi-0), and with varied task orders.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Simple Sequential Fine-Tuning with LoRA and on-policy RL is surprisingly effective for continual learning in large VLA models, maintaining high plasticity and minimal forgetting.
> - The effectiveness stems from a synergy between implicit regularization from on-policy RL, the "blessing of dimensionality" in large pre-trained models, and constrained updates from LoRA.
> - Large VLA models benefit from gradients for new tasks acting nearly orthogonally to pre-trained knowledge, while LoRA ensures controlled, low-rank structural changes across layers.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work is situated within the field of Continual Reinforcement Learning (CRL) applied to Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. VLA models represent a paradigm for creating general-purpose embodied agents by fine-tuning Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for decision-making. These models exhibit generalization capabilities across various scenarios. However, their reliability in dynamic or out-of-distribution environments necessitating continuous adaptation remains a challenge.

Continual Reinforcement Learning addresses the requirement for agents to learn incrementally from non-stationary streams of tasks and experiences while retaining previously acquired knowledge. A common challenge in CRL is catastrophic forgetting, where an agent's performance on prior tasks degrades significantly as it adapts to new ones. Traditional CRL research has developed various strategies to counteract this, including regularization-based methods (e.g., Elastic Weight Consolidation), replay-based methods (e.g., Expert Replay, Dark Experience Replay), and parameter-isolation methods (e.g., Dynamic Weight Expansion). These methods often involve trade-offs between retaining old knowledge (stability) and adapting to new tasks (plasticity), a dilemma known as the stability–plasticity trade-off.

The emergence of large-scale VLA models, often with billions of parameters, introduces additional complexities. Fine-tuning these models is computationally intensive, leading to the adoption of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods, such as Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA). The interaction between PEFT and CRL strategies, as well as the preservation of strong zero-shot generalization capabilities inherent in pretrained VLAs, represents a developing area of research. Prior CRL work primarily focused on smaller models trained from scratch, leaving the unique properties of large pretrained VLA models in a continual learning context largely unexplored. This paper aims to investigate these interactions and properties.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to conduct a systematic empirical study of Continual Reinforcement Learning (CRL) methods when applied to large pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Specifically, the authors sought to answer several questions:
- How do existing CRL methods perform when applied to VLA models?
- Does the interplay between large pretrained VLAs, parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods like LoRA, and reinforcement learning introduce new technical challenges or opportunities?
- What is the effectiveness of simple Sequential Fine-Tuning (Seq. FT) in this context, particularly concerning catastrophic forgetting, plasticity, and zero-shot generalization?

The motivation for this study stems from the observed brittleness of current VLA models in evolving environments, despite their initial broad competence. Enabling these models to incrementally refine and extend their capabilities through ongoing interaction is crucial for transforming initial generalization into sustained, lifelong competence. Prior work in continual learning has consistently identified Sequential Fine-Tuning as highly susceptible to catastrophic forgetting, necessitating complex strategies. However, the unique characteristics of large pretrained VLA models—their vast pretrained knowledge, high parameter count, and the use of PEFT—suggest that conventional assumptions about continual learning might not directly apply.

The authors were particularly motivated to investigate whether these large models, when subjected to parameter-efficient reinforcement learning, might intrinsically handle continual learning challenges differently. They aimed to determine if the perceived necessity for complex CRL strategies holds true in this new paradigm, or if a simpler approach could prove effective, thereby offering a more scalable path toward lifelong adaptation for embodied agents. The study also intended to provide a mechanistic understanding of any observed effectiveness, particularly if Sequential Fine-Tuning exhibited behaviors contrary to established continual learning wisdom.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employed a systematic empirical evaluation of various Continual Reinforcement Learning (CRL) algorithms on large pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models across multiple lifelong RL benchmarks.

**Problem Formulation:**
Each task in VLA post-training is formulated as a finite-horizon, language-conditioned Markov Decision Process (MDP) with a sparse reward function. The VLA policy, conditioned on natural language instructions, aims to maximize cumulative reward. In the continual setting, the agent learns sequentially over a fixed order of tasks, optimizing the average return over all tasks seen so far without access to data or environments from previous tasks.

**Evaluation Metrics:**
Standard continual learning metrics were used, alongside a novel metric for VLA models:
- **Average Success (AVG):** Overall performance across all learned tasks at the end of training.
- **Negative Backward Transfer (NBT):** Measures forgetting by quantifying the degradation in performance on previous tasks relative to their peak performance. Lower values indicate less forgetting.
- **Forward Transfer (FWT):** Quantifies improvement on future tasks before training, relative to initial zero-shot performance.
- **Zero-Shot Success (ZS):** Measures the ability to retain and potentially enhance pretrained capabilities on a set of held-out tasks not encountered during training.

**VLA Models and Reinforcement Learning Algorithm:**
The primary VLA model used was OpenVLA-OFT, which employs autoregressive action generation. For robustness checks, Pi-0 (a flow-matching VLA based on PaliGemma) and OpenVLA (an autoregressive VLA based on Llama 2) were also evaluated.
The core reinforcement learning algorithm adopted for post-training was Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), a stable policy-gradient method. For VLAs with continuous flow/diffusion action heads, GRPO was applied using a Flow-SDE formulation to introduce stochasticity for policy gradient computation.

**Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT):**
Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) was used as the primary PEFT method. LoRA adapts pretrained models by adding low-rank matrices to weight updates while keeping original weights frozen, significantly reducing trainable parameters.

**CRL Algorithms Evaluated:**
The study compared eight algorithms, including two reference points and six representative CRL methods:
- **Sequential Fine-Tuning (Seq. FT):** Baseline where the model is fine-tuned sequentially on new tasks without explicit forgetting prevention.
- **Multi-Task Training (Oracle):** Upper-bound baseline, where a single model is trained simultaneously on all tasks with access to all data.
- **Regularization-based:** Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) was used to penalize updates important to previous tasks.
- **Replay-based:** Expert Replay (ER), which stores and replays expert demonstrations, and Dark Experience Replay (DER), which replays previous model logits, were included.
- **Parameter-isolation:** Dynamic Weight Expansion (DWE) was implemented by allocating task-specific LoRA adapters.
- **Large pretrained model adaptation methods:** SLCA (Layered LR), which applies layerwise learning-rate decoupling, and RETAIN (Weight Merging), which uses discounted weight merging, were also evaluated.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Benchmarks:** Experiments were conducted on `libero-object`, `libero-spatial`, and `libero-long-horizon` benchmarks, consisting of challenging robot manipulation tasks. Additional robustness tests were performed on `RoboCasa` and `Maniskill`.
- **Training Protocol:** A consistent protocol was maintained, inheriting hyperparameters from a standard VLA RL post-training setup. Method-specific hyperparameters were tuned within a limited range.
- **Ablation Studies:** To understand the source of Seq. FT's effectiveness, ablations were conducted by removing key components:
 1. Replacing on-policy RL with supervised fine-tuning.
 2. Replacing the large VLA model with a smaller neural network (12M parameters).
 3. Removing LoRA (i.e., full fine-tuning).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings concerning the application of CRL methods to large pretrained VLA models.

#### 4.1. Performance of Continual Reinforcement Learning Methods

- **Sequential Fine-Tuning (Seq. FT) Performance:** Contrary to conventional expectations in continual learning, simple Sequential Fine-Tuning with LoRA consistently demonstrated competitive performance. It achieved high average success (AVG), exhibited minimal to no catastrophic forgetting (low NBT), and maintained or enhanced strong zero-shot generalization (high ZS) across the three primary benchmarks (`libero-object`, `libero-spatial`, `libero-long-horizon`).
 - On AVG, Seq. FT performed comparably to replay-based and parameter isolation methods and surpassed other CRL approaches. The gap between Seq. FT and the multi-task oracle was generally small.
 - For NBT, Seq. FT consistently showed less than 2% (and sometimes negative) forgetting, indicating robust retention of previously learned tasks. This contrasts significantly with prior work suggesting severe forgetting for Seq. FT.
 - Seq. FT preserved strong zero-shot generalization capabilities, frequently outperforming the multi-task oracle on the ZS metric, suggesting it did not degrade and often enhanced the pretrained model's generalization.

- **Other CRL Methods:** The inclusion of more complex CRL techniques generally did not provide significant benefits and, in some cases, degraded performance.
 - Regularization-based (EWC), layered learning rate (SLCA), and weight merging (RETAIN) methods often suffered from reduced plasticity, leading to lower average success rates due to imposed constraints.
 - Parameter isolation (DWE) did not benefit from positive transfer between tasks.
 - Replay-based methods (ER, DER), despite requiring access to previous data or model outputs and additional storage, did not consistently improve performance over Seq. FT.

#### 4.2. Robustness Under Controlled Perturbations

The favorable properties of Seq. FT (minimal forgetting, good plasticity, preserved zero-shot generalization) persisted under various controlled perturbations:
- **Environmental Perturbations:** Seq. FT maintained strong performance when visual (camera angle, lighting) and robot state conditions were altered across tasks, indicating its robustness beyond fixed environment parameters.
- **Domain and Model Variations:** The effectiveness of Seq. FT extended to different VLA models (Pi-0, OpenVLA) and benchmarks (RoboCasa, Maniskill), demonstrating that the phenomenon is not specific to a particular VLA architecture or simulation environment.
- **Task Order Sensitivity:** Seq. FT's performance remained consistent across different permutations of task order within the continual sequence, suggesting it is not highly sensitive to task presentation order, unlike many classical continual learning methods.

#### 4.3. Additional Experiments and Discussions

- **Closing the Training Gap:** The small performance gap between Seq. FT and the multi-task oracle on average success could be closed by simply extending the training episodes for lower-performing tasks in Seq. FT. This indicated that Seq. FT did not converge to sub-optimal solutions but might be less sample-efficient than multi-task training.
- **VLAs with Continuous Diffusion Heads:** While the "simple recipe" worked for VLAs with diffusion heads (e.g., Pi-0), they empirically showed a higher sensitivity to constraints (e.g., requiring lower LoRA ranks), possibly due to the continuous denoising objective and high expressivity.
- **Longer Task Sequences:** Seq. FT demonstrated maintained knowledge retention even when the number of sequential tasks scaled to 30.

#### 4.4. Analysis of Effectiveness (Why Simple Sequential Fine-Tuning Works)

The analysis indicated that the robustness of Seq. FT arises from a synergy between three components:
- **Effect of On-Policy RL:** On-policy reinforcement learning inherently mitigates catastrophic forgetting. Unlike supervised fine-tuning, which can arbitrarily shift probability mass, policy gradient updates reweight probability mass only where the policy already has support. This creates an implicit regularization effect that minimizes KL divergence drift from the base policy, correlating with reduced forgetting. However, on-policy RL alone was not sufficient.
- **Effect of Large Pretrained Models:** Large, overparameterized VLA models provide a "Null Space" where most gradient updates minimally affect pretrained knowledge. Empirical analysis using the Fisher Information Matrix (FIM) showed very low Fisher energy for task gradients in large VLA models (0.02), indicating minimal interference with pretrained knowledge, compared to smaller models (0.16).
- **Effect of Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA):** LoRA constrains updates to a low-rank subspace, limiting the degrees of freedom and preventing substantial structural changes to the model. While LoRA reduces the effective rank of updates, analysis showed that its benefit goes beyond simple rank reduction. LoRA prevented a subset of layers from undergoing disproportionately large, high-rank structural modifications that were observed in full fine-tuning, thereby preserving the network's geometry and previously acquired knowledge.

In summary, the combined effect of on-policy RL (objective), LoRA (constraints), and the large VLA model (capacity) synergistically alleviates catastrophic forgetting, with the removal of any single component leading to a significant increase in forgetting.

#### 4.5. Good Plasticity

The study found that Seq. FT maintained good plasticity even with LoRA. This was attributed to the low-capacity requirements of policy gradient RL, especially with sparse rewards. Unlike supervised learning, where each episode contains rich, high-dimensional information, sparse reward RL typically provides O(1) bits of information per episode, which can be effectively absorbed by LoRA's constrained parameter updates without sacrificing adaptation capabilities.

#### 4.6. Good Zero-Shot Generalization

Seq. FT consistently preserved and sometimes enhanced zero-shot generalization, occasionally outperforming multi-task oracle training. This could be partly explained by the mechanisms preventing forgetting (Sec. 5.1). The slight edge over the oracle was hypothesized to be due to task sequencing acting as an implicit regularization, encouraging more robust representations by exposing the model to a shifting objective over time rather than overfitting to an aggregated multi-task objective.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The findings of this research challenge conventional wisdom in continual learning by demonstrating that a simple Sequential Fine-Tuning approach, when combined with Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) and applied to large pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models with on-policy Reinforcement Learning, achieves competitive plasticity, minimal catastrophic forgetting, and preserved or enhanced zero-shot generalization. This outcome contrasts with established beliefs that Seq. FT typically leads to severe forgetting and necessitates complex CRL strategies.

The significance of this work lies in several areas:
- **Simplified Continual Learning for Large Models:** The research provides a "simple but scalable recipe" for continual post-training of large VLA models. This suggests that the complexity previously thought necessary for CRL might be reduced in the era of large, pretrained models, potentially streamlining the development of self-improving embodied agents.
- **Re-evaluation of Stability-Plasticity Trade-off:** The study offers new insights into how the interaction between large pretrained models, parameter-efficient adaptation, and on-policy RL fundamentally reshapes the stability-plasticity dilemma. It proposes that these components collectively make continual adaptation both stable and scalable, alleviating the typical trade-off.
- **Mechanistic Understanding:** The detailed analysis of why this simple recipe works—attributing stability to the complementary effects of on-policy RL (objective), large model capacity (null space), and LoRA (update constraints)—advances the theoretical understanding of continual learning in large model contexts.
- **Practical Implications for Robotics:** The results have direct implications for physical robotic systems. A simpler, yet effective, continual learning approach can facilitate the deployment of VLA models in real-world, evolving environments where agents need to adapt continuously without prohibitive computational costs or storage requirements. This could accelerate progress in lifelong embodied intelligence, moving towards agents that can learn and improve over their operational lifespan.
- **Shift in Research Focus:** The paper suggests that as pretrained models become larger and more capable, the traditional focus on mitigating catastrophic forgetting might become less critical. Instead, future CRL research could shift emphasis towards designing algorithms that prioritize efficient adaptation and further improve zero-shot generalization capabilities, building upon the inherent robustness demonstrated by large VLA models.

The release of the open-source implementation alongside the paper is also significant, providing a principled starting point for the broader research community to further investigate and build upon these findings for more capable and adaptable lifelong embodied agents.
