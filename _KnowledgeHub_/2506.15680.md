# Particle-Grid Neural Dynamics for Learning Deformable Object Models from RGB-D Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Columbia University and UIUC developed Particle-Grid Neural Dynamics (PGND), a hybrid Lagrangian-Eulerian framework that learns deformable object models directly from real-world RGB-D video. PGND achieves superior dynamics prediction accuracy, robustness to partial observations, and strong generalization to unseen objects, leading to improved action-conditioned video prediction and robot manipulation planning.

## Problem

- Physics-based simulators for deformable objects struggle with a "sim-to-real gap" due to the difficulty of identifying precise material parameters from real-world data.
- Existing learning-based models, particularly Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), are often sensitive to graph connectivity, computationally expensive for dense particle sets, and vulnerable to partial observations.
- There is a need for robust, accurate, and generalizable dynamics models that can learn directly from real-world data to enable advanced robotic manipulation of deformable objects.

## Method

- A Particle-Grid Neural Dynamics (PGND) framework was introduced, combining Lagrangian particle representations with a fixed Eulerian spatial grid to capture both detailed local motion and global spatial continuity.
- An automated data annotation pipeline leverages foundational vision models (Segment-Anything, CoTracker) to extract persistent, dense 3D particle tracks from multi-view RGB-D videos as ground truth for training.
- The dynamics function utilizes a PointNet encoder for global information, an MLP-based neural velocity field, Grid Velocity Editing for physical constraints (e.g., ground contact, grasping), and B-spline kernel-based Grid-to-Particle integration.

## Results

- PGND consistently outperformed physics-based (MPM) and learning-based (GBND) baselines in dynamics rollout accuracy across diverse deformable objects, quantified by lower Mean Distance Error, Chamfer Distance, and Earth Mover’s Distance.
- The framework demonstrated superior robustness to partial observations (e.g., from a single camera view) and strong generalization capabilities to unseen object instances within a category, achieving significantly lower prediction errors than baselines.
- Integrated with Model Predictive Control, PGND enabled more effective robot manipulation, achieving lower terminal errors and higher error reduction rates in tasks like cloth lifting and rope bending, and produced higher-quality 3D action-conditioned video predictions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The hybrid Lagrangian-Eulerian particle-grid representation effectively combines the strengths of both paradigms, providing fine-grained particle motion details while maintaining spatial continuity and robustness to partial observations through the grid.
> - Leveraging state-of-the-art foundational vision models for perception and tracking significantly reduces the burden of data annotation and enables direct learning from real-world RGB-D input, bypassing the sim-to-real gap.
> - The integration of learned dynamics with 3D Gaussian Splatting facilitates the creation of high-fidelity learning-based digital twins capable of realistic action-conditioned video rendering and improved model-based planning for robot manipulation.
