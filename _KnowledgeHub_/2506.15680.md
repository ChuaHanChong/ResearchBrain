# Particle-Grid Neural Dynamics for Learning Deformable Object Models from RGB-D Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Columbia University and UIUC developed Particle-Grid Neural Dynamics (PGND), a hybrid Lagrangian-Eulerian framework that learns deformable object models directly from real-world RGB-D video. PGND achieves superior dynamics prediction accuracy, robustness to partial observations, and strong generalization to unseen objects, leading to improved action-conditioned video prediction and robot manipulation planning.

## Problem

- Physics-based simulators for deformable objects struggle with a "sim-to-real gap" due to the difficulty of identifying precise material parameters from real-world data.
- Existing learning-based models, particularly Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), are often sensitive to graph connectivity, computationally expensive for dense particle sets, and vulnerable to partial observations.
- There is a need for robust, accurate, and generalizable dynamics models that can learn directly from real-world data to enable advanced robotic manipulation of deformable objects.

## Method

- A Particle-Grid Neural Dynamics (PGND) framework was introduced, combining Lagrangian particle representations with a fixed Eulerian spatial grid to capture both detailed local motion and global spatial continuity.
- An automated data annotation pipeline leverages foundational vision models (Segment-Anything, CoTracker) to extract persistent, dense 3D particle tracks from multi-view RGB-D videos as ground truth for training.
- The dynamics function utilizes a PointNet encoder for global information, an MLP-based neural velocity field, Grid Velocity Editing for physical constraints (e.g., ground contact, grasping), and B-spline kernel-based Grid-to-Particle integration.

## Results

- PGND consistently outperformed physics-based (MPM) and learning-based (GBND) baselines in dynamics rollout accuracy across diverse deformable objects, quantified by lower Mean Distance Error, Chamfer Distance, and Earth Mover’s Distance.
- The framework demonstrated superior robustness to partial observations (e.g., from a single camera view) and strong generalization capabilities to unseen object instances within a category, achieving significantly lower prediction errors than baselines.
- Integrated with Model Predictive Control, PGND enabled more effective robot manipulation, achieving lower terminal errors and higher error reduction rates in tasks like cloth lifting and rope bending, and produced higher-quality 3D action-conditioned video predictions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The hybrid Lagrangian-Eulerian particle-grid representation effectively combines the strengths of both paradigms, providing fine-grained particle motion details while maintaining spatial continuity and robustness to partial observations through the grid.
> - Leveraging state-of-the-art foundational vision models for perception and tracking significantly reduces the burden of data annotation and enables direct learning from real-world RGB-D input, bypassing the sim-to-real gap.
> - The integration of learned dynamics with 3D Gaussian Splatting facilitates the creation of high-fidelity learning-based digital twins capable of realistic action-conditioned video rendering and improved model-based planning for robot manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The modeling and manipulation of deformable objects represent a grand challenge in robotics, crucial for tasks ranging from industrial assembly and medical procedures to everyday household chores. This paper directly addresses the limitations of existing approaches in this complex domain, carving out a significant niche for its novel framework.

Traditionally, deformable object modeling has pursued two main avenues:

1. **Physics-based simulators:** Methods like the Mass-Spring system, Finite Element Method (FEM), Discrete Elastic Rods (DER), Position-Based Dynamics (PBD), and Material Point Method (MPM) offer high fidelity under ideal conditions. However, they suffer from a "sim-to-real gap," making it difficult to accurately transfer models trained in simulation to the real world. This gap arises from the challenges of precisely identifying real-world material properties (e.g., Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio) and estimating the full physical state from limited visual information.
2. **Learning-based predictive models:** These leverage deep neural networks to learn dynamics directly from data. Early video-based models, while promising, often struggle with computational expense, lack true 3D spatial understanding, and are sensitive to viewpoint and appearance changes, limiting their utility for precise manipulation.

More recently, **Graph Neural Networks (GNNs)** have emerged as a leading approach in learning 3D dynamics for particles, explicitly modeling spatial relationships. GNN-based methods (often referred to as Graph-Based Neural Dynamics or GBND) have shown promise across various materials and scenarios. However, they face several critical limitations that this paper seeks to overcome:
- **Sensitivity to graph connectivity:** Their performance is heavily dependent on how particle graphs are constructed and connected, making them vulnerable to partial observations common in real-world settings.
- **Inability to capture global information:** Insufficient message-passing steps can lead to a lack of global context, while excessive steps may result in overly smoothed predictions, losing fine details.
- **Computational burden:** Handling dense particle sets with GNNs can be computationally expensive.
- **Sparse representations:** Many GNN methods operate on spatially sparse graph vertices, which might not capture the rich geometry and deformation of dense objects.

This paper positions its "Particle-Grid Neural Dynamics" as a **hybrid Lagrangian-Eulerian approach** that bridges the gap between particle-based (Lagrangian) and grid-based (Eulerian) representations, drawing inspiration from physics-based methods like MPM. By doing so, it directly tackles the aforementioned limitations:
- It improves robustness to partial observations by using a global point cloud encoder.
- It ensures spatial continuity and enhances learning efficiency through the grid representation.
- It allows for denser particle sets, capturing finer geometric details than sparse GNNs.
- It learns directly from real-world RGB-D observations, circumventing the sim-to-real gap and complex material property identification inherent in physics-based models.

Furthermore, the work leverages recent advancements in **foundational vision models** (Segment-Anything, CoTracker) for dense 3D tracking and **3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)** for realistic rendering. This integration of state-of-the-art perception and rendering technologies with a novel dynamics model places the work at the forefront of learning accurate and visually rich digital twins for deformable objects. It pushes the boundaries of how effectively robots can perceive, predict, and interact with the physical world, especially with challenging non-rigid materials.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust, accurate, and generalizable neural dynamics framework for deformable objects that can learn directly from real-world RGB-D video observations of robot-object interactions. The motivation stems from the inherent difficulties of existing approaches:

1. **Addressing the "Sim-to-Real Gap" and System Identification Challenges:** Physics-based simulators, while powerful, often require precise material parameters (e.g., Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio) that are hard to identify for real-world objects. Their performance degrades significantly when these parameters are unknown or vary, leading to a discrepancy between simulated and real-world behavior. The authors aim to bypass this by learning dynamics empirically from real data.
2. **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Learning-Based Models:**
 - **Computational Expense and 3D Understanding:** Video-based predictive models often operate in 2D image space, making them computationally intensive for 3D tasks and sensitive to viewpoint changes. The goal is to develop a model with intrinsic 3D spatial understanding.
 - **GNN Vulnerabilities:** Current state-of-the-art GNN-based methods (GBND) struggle with sparse observations, require careful definition of graph connectivity, and can either smooth out fine details or fail to capture global effects. The authors seek a dynamics model that is robust to incomplete observations, can capture both local and global deformations, and handles dense particle representations effectively.
3. **Enabling Advanced Robotic Applications:** An accurate and generalizable dynamics model is crucial for downstream robotic tasks such as:
 - **Model-based planning:** Allowing robots to predict future object states and plan actions to achieve desired outcomes.
 - **Policy evaluation:** Assessing the effectiveness of manipulation strategies.
 - **Real-to-sim asset generation:** Creating realistic digital twins for use in simulated environments.

Specifically, the paper sets out to answer several key questions through its experiments:
- How well can the particle-grid model learn the dynamics of diverse deformable objects (ropes, cloth, plush toys, bags, boxes, bread)?
- Can the model perform effectively even under limited visual observation (e.g., sparse camera views)?
- Can a unified model be trained for multiple instances within an object category, and how well does it generalize to unseen instances?
- Can the learned model improve the quality of 3D action-conditioned video prediction and the effectiveness of model-based planning for manipulation tasks?

By addressing these objectives, the authors aim to provide a more practical and high-fidelity solution for deformable object modeling, directly contributing to the advancement of autonomous manipulation capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work is the **Particle-Grid Neural Dynamics (PGND)** framework, a novel approach that combines object particles with a fixed spatial grid in a hybrid representation. This hybrid nature allows the model to capture both the detailed, Lagrangian motion of individual particles and the global, Eulerian spatial continuity of the object's deformation.

#### 3.1. State and Action Representation

- **State Representation:** The deformable object is represented as a collection of **particles** (X_t ∈ R^(3×n)) with their positions and velocities (V_t ∈ R^(3×n)) at time `t`. Additionally, a **uniformly distributed spatial grid** (G) discretizes the 3D space, acting as a regularizer and computational efficiency enhancer.
- **Action Representation:** Robot actions (A_t) are defined as a tuple including an action type label (grasped or non-prehensile), the end-effector pose (T_t), its time derivative (˙T_t), and the gripper open distance (o_t).

#### 3.2. Particle-Grid Dynamics Function (f_θ)

The dynamics function `f_θ` predicts the future particle velocities (ˆV_(t+∆t)) based on current and historical particle states (X, V) and the robot's action (A). It utilizes a neural network parameterized by `θ` and performs forward Euler time integration to update particle positions. The function `f_θ` is a composition of four main components:
- `f_feature_φ` (Point Encoder)
- `f_field_ψ` (Neural Velocity Field)
- `g_grid` (Grid Velocity Editing - GVE)
- `h_G2P` (Grid-to-Particle Integration)

#### 3.3. Model Components

1. **Point Encoder (`f_feature_φ`):** This component takes the historical particle positions and velocities as input and extracts per-particle latent features (Z_t). The authors use a **PointNet** architecture for its efficiency and capability to capture global information from the entire particle set. This is crucial for robustness against incomplete observations, as the encoder can infer object shape and dynamic state even from partially visible data.
2. **Neural Velocity Field (`f_field_ψ`):** An MLP (Multi-Layer Perceptron) acts as a neural implicit function to predict a **spatial velocity field** at each grid point (g ∈ G). It takes the grid location's sinusoidal positional encoding (`γ(x_g)`) and a "locality-aware feature" (`z_g,t`) as inputs. The locality-aware feature is computed by averaging particle features (Z_t) within a specified radius `r` of the grid point, ensuring that grid velocities depend on local geometry.
3. **Grid Velocity Editing (GVE, `g_grid`):** Inspired by MPM, this component modifies the predicted grid velocities to enforce physical constraints.
 - **Ground Contact:** Velocities are projected back from the contact surface, incorporating friction.
 - **Grasped Interactions:** For rigidly grasped points, grid points within a certain distance of the grasp center are assigned velocities matching the gripper's linear and angular motion.
4. **Grid-to-Particle Integration (G2P, `h_G2P`):** After the grid velocities are determined and edited, they are transferred back to the particles. A continuous **B-spline kernel** is used to interpolate grid velocities (`ˆv_g,t`) into individual particle velocities (`ˆv_p,t`). This smooth interpolation ensures realistic particle motion based on the grid's velocity field.

#### 3.4. Controlling Deformation

The paper details two methods for integrating robot actions:
- **Grid Velocity Editing (GVE):** Used for **grasped interactions** and object-ground contact, where velocities of relevant grid points are directly modified to match physical constraints or gripper motion.
- **Robot Particles (RP):** For **non-prehensile actions** (e.g., pushing), the robot gripper is represented by additional particles that are fused with the object's point cloud. The PGND then operates on this augmented point cloud, allowing the interaction to implicitly influence particle features and dynamics without directly prescribing velocities.

#### 3.5. Data Collection and Training

- **Data Source:** Multi-view RGB-D videos of random robot-object interactions are collected via teleoperation using a bimanual xArm setup.
- **Automatic Annotation Pipeline:** Foundational vision models are central to data processing:
 - **Segment-Anything (SAM):** Extracts persistent object masks from each camera view.
 - **CoTracker:** Tracks 2D pixel trajectories within segmented regions.
 - **3D Fusion:** Depth information is used to inverse-project 2D tracks into persistent, dense 3D particle tracks (X, V), which serve as ground truth for training.
- **Training Objective:** The model is trained to minimize the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between predicted and actual particle positions over a K-step future horizon, using iterative dynamics model rollouts. The entire dynamics function `f_θ` is fully differentiable, enabling end-to-end optimization with gradient descent.

#### 3.6. Integration with 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) for Rendering

For realistic visual rendering of predictions, the learned particle motion is integrated with 3DGS. The 6-DoF transformations (position and rotation) of the Gaussian kernels are interpolated using **Linear Blend Skinning (LBS)**, where the predicted particle motions serve as control points to deform the Gaussian reconstruction of the object.

#### 3.7. Integration with Model Predictive Control (MPC) for Planning

The learned PGND model can be embedded within an MPC framework (specifically, MPPI). The dynamics model predicts future states given sampled robot actions. The MPC then optimizes these actions to minimize a cost function (e.g., Chamfer Distance to a target object state) in a closed-loop fashion, enabling goal-conditioned object manipulation.

This comprehensive methodology allows the framework to leverage the strengths of various computational paradigms—neural networks for learning complex functions, hybrid Lagrangian-Eulerian representations for physical realism, and modern vision models for robust data acquisition and rendering—to achieve advanced deformable object modeling.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the superior performance and versatility of the Particle-Grid Neural Dynamics (PGND) framework across a wide array of deformable objects and tasks.

#### 4.1. Dynamics Learning and Prediction (Table I, Fig. 3)

- **Superior Accuracy:** PGND consistently outperforms all baselines—Material Point Method (MPM), Graph-Based Neural Dynamics (GBND), and a particle-only baseline (ours w/o grid)—in terms of dynamics rollout accuracy. This is quantified by lower Mean Distance Error (MDE), Chamfer Distance (CD), and Earth Mover’s Distance (EMD) across diverse objects like cloth, rope, plush toys, boxes, paper bags, and bread.
- **Qualitative Fidelity:** Visualizations show that PGND's particle predictions align more closely with ground truth, exhibiting higher resolution and fewer artifacts compared to baselines. GBND, for instance, often predicts inadequate motions or generates unrealistic artifacts. MPM struggles with scattering particles and gravity effects, especially with insufficient internal point density. The particle-only baseline shows improvements over GBND but still produces artifacts, particularly near the gripper, highlighting the benefits of the grid representation.

#### 4.2. Sparse-View Dynamics Prediction (Fig. 4, Fig. 11)

- **Robustness to Partial Observations:** PGND maintains lower prediction errors than GBND even when the number of input camera views is reduced (from 4 down to 1). Crucially, the rate of performance degradation for PGND is less significant compared to GBND, especially for objects like cloth, demonstrating its robustness to incomplete visual information. Qualitative examples further illustrate PGND's ability to make accurate predictions from highly incomplete particle data.

#### 4.3. Category-Level Generalization (Fig. 5, Fig. 12)

- **Generalization to Unseen Instances:** The model demonstrates strong generalization capabilities. When trained on a diverse set of instances within a category (e.g., various ropes or cloths) and tested on unseen instances, PGND achieves significantly lower prediction errors compared to GBND. GBND shows a notable performance drop on unseen instances, whereas PGND maintains a relatively low error, proving its ability to generalize to novel objects with distinct physical properties and shapes. Qualitative video predictions for unseen cloth and rope instances further confirm the generation of realistic deformations.

#### 4.4. Action-Conditioned Video Prediction (Table II, Fig. 6, Fig. 7)

- **Realistic Rendering:** PGND, when integrated with 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), achieves the best overall performance in generating 3D action-conditioned videos. This is reflected in higher mask similarity scores (J-Score/IoU, F-Score) and better appearance-wise similarity (lower LPIPS score) compared to baselines.
- **High Visual Fidelity:** Qualitative comparisons show that PGND produces higher-quality renderings and better alignment with ground truth. It can also be applied to high-quality phone-scanned 3DGS reconstructions, yielding even greater visual fidelity for simulations from static scenes.

#### 4.5. Planning (Fig. 8, Fig. 9)

- **Enhanced Manipulation Performance:** Integrated with Model Predictive Control (MPC) using the MPPI algorithm, PGND significantly improves planning for deformable object manipulation. Across four diverse tasks (cloth lifting, box closing, rope manipulation, plush toy relocating), PGND consistently achieves lower terminal errors and a higher error reduction rate compared to the GBND baseline.
- **Effective Task Execution:** Qualitative planning results show that PGND enables more effective and accurate manipulation, producing outcomes visually closer to the target states. For example, it successfully lifts both sides of a box and accurately bends a rope, tasks where the GBND baseline often struggles or fails to predict correct actions.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies and Runtime Analysis (Table III, IV, V)

- **Deformation Control:** The ablation study confirms that Grid Velocity Editing (GVE) is more effective for prehensile tasks (e.g., cloth), while Robot Particles (RP) perform better for non-prehensile interactions (e.g., boxes), validating the design choice of context-specific control methods.
- **Grid Size and Encoder:** While finer grid sizes or advanced encoders like PTv3 can achieve comparable or slightly better performance, the chosen 2cm grid and PointNet offer a good balance of accuracy and computational cost.
- **Efficiency:** The model demonstrates efficient inference, running at 4.8 ms per forward pass for a batch size of 1, and 11.9 ms for a batch size of 50, supporting real-time applications and planning.

In summary, the main findings highlight that the hybrid particle-grid representation and its robust learning from real-world RGB-D data enable PGND to be a highly accurate, generalizable, and efficient dynamics model for a wide range of challenging deformable objects, significantly advancing capabilities for robotic manipulation and visually realistic simulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Particle-Grid Neural Dynamics framework represents a significant step forward in the field of deformable object modeling and robotic manipulation, with profound implications across several domains.

#### 5.1. Advancing the State-of-the-Art in Deformable Object Modeling

The most immediate significance is the substantial improvement over existing learning-based and physics-based dynamics models. By effectively combining Lagrangian particle representations with Eulerian grid representations, the authors have engineered a system that inherently addresses critical limitations:
- **Robustness:** The use of a global point cloud encoder and locality-aware features makes the model robust to partial observations, a pervasive challenge in real-world robotics.
- **Accuracy and Density:** PGND's ability to predict dense particle motions with high fidelity, preventing artifacts and maintaining spatial continuity, significantly surpasses prior graph-based methods that often suffer from sparse representations or overly smoothed predictions.
- **Generalizability:** The framework's demonstrated ability to generalize to unseen object instances within a category, even with diverse physical properties, is a crucial breakthrough for practical deployment in varied environments.

#### 5.2. Enabling More Capable Robotic Manipulation

The improved accuracy and robustness of the dynamics model directly translate into more effective robot control. Its seamless integration with Model Predictive Control (MPC) frameworks for planning means:
- **Complex Task Execution:** Robots can now plan and execute more intricate manipulation tasks involving highly deformable objects (e.g., folding cloth, precise rope knotting, delicate food handling) with greater success rates and precision.
- **Reduced Sim-to-Real Gap for Planning:** By learning dynamics directly from real-world data, the models produced by PGND are inherently more aligned with reality, minimizing the need for extensive domain randomization or complex parameter tuning often required when using physics simulators.

#### 5.3. Creation of High-Fidelity Digital Twins for Robotics and Beyond

The coupling of PGND with 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) is particularly impactful, leading to the creation of "learning-based digital twins" of real-world deformable objects. These digital twins offer:
- **Realistic Visual Simulation:** The ability to generate high-quality, action-conditioned videos that accurately reflect both geometric deformation and appearance changes provides an invaluable tool for:
 - **Simulator Development:** Creating more realistic environments for training and testing robotic policies.
 - **Human-Robot Interaction:** Allowing humans to preview and understand robot actions with high visual fidelity.
 - **Virtual Prototyping:** Simulating the behavior of new deformable products before physical manufacturing.
- **Data Generation:** The accurate dynamics model can generate synthetic data for further training of other vision or control systems, especially in scenarios where real-world data collection is expensive or hazardous.

#### 5.4. Potential Future Directions and Limitations

The authors openly acknowledge several limitations, which also point to promising avenues for future research:
- **Fixed Particle Count:** The current framework assumes a fixed number of particles, limiting its applicability to scenarios where particles appear or disappear (e.g., cutting, tearing, pouring). Addressing this could involve dynamic particle management or more advanced state correction mechanisms.
- **Implicit Physical Properties:** While sufficient for single instances or distinct categories, implicitly inferring physical properties from point clouds could benefit from a more systematic approach to explicit property identification. This could lead to more interpretable models and better adaptation to unknown materials at test time, potentially through parameter-conditioned neural dynamics.
- **Dependency on Foundational Vision Models:** The accuracy of the entire pipeline relies heavily on the performance of underlying vision models like Segment-Anything and CoTracker for object segmentation and tracking, and 3DGS for reconstruction. Failures or inaccuracies in these upstream components could propagate errors, highlighting the need for more robust, possibly end-to-end, perception-to-dynamics systems.

Despite these limitations, the Particle-Grid Neural Dynamics framework offers a compelling solution to a long-standing challenge in robotics. Its contributions pave the way for more autonomous, adaptable, and precise robotic interaction with the ubiquitous and complex world of deformable objects.
