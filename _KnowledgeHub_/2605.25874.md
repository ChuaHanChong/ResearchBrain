# WBench: A Comprehensive Multi-turn Benchmark for Interactive Video World Model Evaluation

> [!summary] Summary
> WBENCH provides a comprehensive multi-turn benchmark for evaluating interactive video world models, assessing 20 diverse models across five dimensions including video quality, interaction adherence, and physical plausibility. This benchmark identifies current limitations such as rapid degradation in multi-turn navigation and low performance in perspective switching, while demonstrating competitive results from open-source models in specific capabilities.

## Problem

- Existing evaluation of interactive video world models is fragmented, focusing on specific competencies or particular interactive capabilities, hindering comprehensive assessment.
- There is an absence of a unified standard to systematically evaluate interactive world models across all five essential roles: Renderer, Director, Controller, Memory, and Engine.
- Fair comparison and detailed diagnosis of failure modes across crucial dimensions like visual quality, controllability, memory, and physical adherence are difficult with current methods.

## Method

- Introduces WBENCH, a multi-turn benchmark dataset comprising 289 cases and 1,058 interaction turns, supporting diverse open-domain scenes and both first- and third-person perspectives.
- Develops a comprehensive evaluation suite with 22 fine-grained sub-metrics categorized into five dimensions: Video Quality, Setting Adherence, Interaction Adherence, Consistency, and Physical Adherence.
- Evaluates 20 contemporary models, covering text-driven, camera-controlled, and action-conditioned paradigms, using an iterative image-to-video protocol for fair comparison.

## Results

- Models with native control interfaces (camera-controlled, action-conditioned) outperformed text-driven models in navigation by approximately 10 points.
- Perspective switching proved the most challenging task for text-driven models, achieving an average score of only 30.7.
- Navigation performance degraded significantly faster than other interaction types, with a 33-point drop from Turn 1 to Turn 4+, highlighting difficulties in maintaining a spatial reference frame over long horizons.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - No single model or paradigm consistently dominates all five evaluation dimensions, indicating a multi-faceted challenge in interactive world model development.
> - Navigation performance is largely decoupled from video quality, consistency, and physical compliance, suggesting distinct underlying mechanisms.
> - Physical plausibility correlates strongly with video quality and consistency (r=0.84 and r=0.72 respectively), implying reliance on rich generative priors rather than specialized control capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of video generation has experienced rapid advancements, moving from early diffusion models to scalable Diffusion Transformers capable of producing longer, higher-resolution, and temporally coherent video outputs. This progress has enabled the development of "interactive video world models," which are designed to synthesize subsequent frames in response to user actions, creating closed-loop simulations akin to game engines. These models find applications in areas such as game environments, autonomous driving, embodied interaction, and open-domain content generation.

Despite these developments, the evaluation of interactive world models remains fragmented. Existing benchmarks often focus on specific competencies, such as perceptual quality (e.g., VBench, EvalCrafter), or particular interactive capabilities like navigation (e.g., WorldMark, MIND), causal interaction (e.g., Omni-WorldBench), or domain-specific tasks like autonomous driving (e.g., WorldLens). Many evaluations rely on selected demonstrations or task-specific protocols, which can hinder fair comparison, comprehensive assessment of capabilities, and detailed diagnosis of failure modes across crucial dimensions such as visual quality, controllability, memory, and physical adherence.

WBENCH addresses this gap by aiming to provide a unified and comprehensive evaluation standard. It seeks to establish a standardized protocol that can systematically assess a model's ability to act as a "Renderer" (for visually convincing video), a "Director" (for correct world initialization), a "Controller" (for faithful interaction execution), a "Memory" (for preserving world state across turns), and an "Engine" (for physically compliant world evolution). This benchmark differentiates itself by combining diverse open-domain scenes, support for both first- and third-person perspectives, a broad taxonomy of interaction types, and multi-turn closed-loop evaluation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of WBENCH is to introduce a comprehensive multi-turn benchmark for the systematic evaluation of interactive video world models. The motivations for developing such a benchmark are detailed as follows:

- **Fragmented Evaluation Landscape**: Existing benchmarks for video generation and world models cover only a subset of the required competencies. For instance, general video generation benchmarks prioritize perceptual quality but lack interactive control, while many interactive world model benchmarks are limited in scope, focusing on navigation or memory without considering semantic interactions, supporting only a single perspective, or being restricted to specific domains like autonomous driving. This fragmentation makes it challenging to conduct unified, systematic evaluations and draw comprehensive conclusions about a model's strengths and weaknesses.
- **Need for a Unified Standard**: There is an absence of a unified standard to systematically evaluate interactive world models across their full range of competencies. A capable interactive world model needs to function akin to a game engine, encompassing high-fidelity rendering, accurate world initialization, faithful execution of interactions, consistent memory of world state, and adherence to physical laws. Current evaluation methods do not collectively assess these "five complementary roles."
- **Difficulty in Fair Comparison and Failure Diagnosis**: Without a standardized and comprehensive evaluation framework, comparing different interactive world models fairly becomes difficult. Models often excel in specific aspects while performing poorly in others, and the underlying reasons for failures (e.g., poor rendering, inability to follow instructions, loss of consistency, or physics violations) are hard to diagnose systematically.
- **Multi-turn and Diverse Interactions**: Many existing benchmarks are single-turn or limited in interaction types. Real-world interactive scenarios, however, often involve multi-turn sequences with diverse interactions, such as navigating, performing actions with a subject, editing environmental events, or switching perspectives. Evaluating long-horizon consistency and the ability to compose various interaction types is crucial for assessing practical utility.
- **Support for Diverse Control Paradigms**: Interactive world models may accept different input interfaces for control, such as natural language text, 6-DoF camera poses, or discrete action commands. A comprehensive benchmark needs to accommodate these varied paradigms to enable fair comparison.
- **Actionable Diagnostic Insights**: The aim is to move beyond aggregate scores and provide fine-grained diagnostic insights into the characteristic strengths, weaknesses, and open challenges of current models, thereby guiding future research and development in the field.

By addressing these motivations, WBENCH aims to provide a robust framework for assessing the progress of interactive world models and identifying key areas for improvement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of WBENCH involves constructing a dataset of multi-turn interaction cases, defining a comprehensive evaluation suite with automated metrics, and applying this suite to a range of contemporary models.

#### 3.1. Dataset Construction

The dataset is designed to systematically evaluate interactive world models by decomposing inputs into a **World Setting (W)** defining the initial state and an **Interaction sequence (I)** specifying user control signals over multiple turns.

- **World Settings**: Each case is defined by four attributes:
 - **Scene**: Environment type, spatial layout, and dynamics (e.g., terrain, buildings, offscreen elements).
 - **Style**: Rendering appearance (e.g., realistic, cartoon, anime, oil painting).
 - **Perspective**: First-person (disembodied or embodied) or third-person.
 - **Subject**: The primary entity (e.g., human, animal, vehicle, robot, or object) in third-person cases or visible in embodied first-person.
 These attributes form environment and subject prompts, combined with an initial frame from generative models or collected images, manually verified for quality.
- **Interactions**: Multi-turn interaction sequences are composed from four types:
 - **Navigation**: Governs camera or ego-agent motion using W/S/A/D for translation and arrow keys for rotation. The interpretation is perspective-dependent (e.g., W moves the camera in FPP, the subject in TPP). Trajectories include six path topologies (round-trip, progressive, repeat, L-shape, loop, zigzag).
 - **Subject Action**: Actions performed by the primary subject (e.g., manipulation, locomotion, tool use, combat, gesture).
 - **Event Editing**: Externally imposed environmental changes (e.g., weather, time-of-day, object appearance).
 - **Perspective Switching**: Transitions between first- and third-person views, including same-subject, multi-subject, and scope mode changes.
- **Case Construction**: Cases follow a "setting-first" principle, where interactions are physically executable and semantically coherent within the chosen world setting. Stratified sampling ensures diverse coverage across attributes, and all cases undergo manual review.
- **Dataset Statistics**: WBENCH contains 289 cases and 1,058 interaction turns. First-person cases constitute 62%, and third-person 38%. Navigation is the most common interaction (57%), followed by subject action (20%), event editing (17%), and perspective switching (6%). Scenes are diverse (nature, urban, indoor, etc.), subjects are varied (humans, animals, robots, vehicles, objects), and styles include photorealistic and stylized renderings. Cases span 2-9 turns, averaging 3.7.

#### 3.2. WBENCH Evaluation Suite

Evaluation is structured across five dimensions, each comprising multiple fine-grained sub-metrics, totaling 22. All sub-metrics are rescaled to.

- **Video Quality**: Measures perceptual quality irrespective of conditioning signals. Includes aesthetic quality (CLIP + LAION Aesthetic head), imaging quality (MUSIQ), temporal flickering (pixel MAE), dynamic degree (RAFT optical flow), motion smoothness (AMT-S interpolation), and HPSv3-Norm (human-preference reward score).
- **Setting Adherence**: Assesses if the video reflects the specified world setting (W).
 - **Scene Adherence**: Evaluates environment maintenance (visible elements and style consistency) and offscreen content appearance using a VLM (Doubao-Seed-2.0-lite).
 - **Subject Adherence**: For third-person, checks subject appearance maintenance and action match using a VLM.
- **Interaction Adherence**: Evaluates execution of requested interactions (I).
 - **NavScore**: Compares MegaSaM-estimated camera poses against synthetic ground-truth trajectories (adaptive, arc-length resampled) for translation and rotation accuracy (normalized ATE) and consistency across repeated actions.
 - **Event Editing Adherence & Subject Action Adherence**: Both use a unified VLM protocol with five binary checks per turn (e.g., change detection, event occurrence, completion, detail accuracy, anomaly absence), yielding a score per turn.
 - **Perspective Switching Adherence**: Uses a categorical VLM protocol with three binary criteria per turn (transition visibility, target-type consistency, structural compliance). A turn is successful only if all criteria are met.
- **Consistency**: Measures stability of geometry, appearance, and perspective across turns.
 - **Spatial Consistency**: For roundtrip trajectories, compares the return frame with the initial frame using DreamSim, with a motion gate.
 - **Segment Continuity**: Detects unexpected hard cuts within videos using TransNetV2.
 - **Perspective Consistency**: Tracks subject centroid stability with SAM2, weighted by subject presence.
 - **Geometric Consistency**: Uses Depth Anything 3 for depth and pose estimation, then reprojects pixels across views to measure 3D structural coherence via reprojection displacement.
 - **Photometric Consistency**: Measures appearance stability via PSNR between reprojected frame pairs.
 - **Subject Consistency**: Uses SAM2 to isolate subjects, then combines DINOv2 for local continuity and CLIP for global drift.
 - **Background Consistency**: Measures mean pairwise CLIP cosine similarity between consecutive background frames.
- **Physical**: Assesses adherence to physical rules.
 - **Causal Fidelity**: Uses a two-stage VLM protocol (Doubao-Seed-2.0-lite) with three-point grading. Stage 1 assesses global plausibility (rendering physics, causal consistency). Stage 2 averages scores over seven case-specific physics dimensions (e.g., fluid, collision, deformation, human motion), selected by a text-only VLM and manually verified.
 - **Visual Plausibility**: A fine-tuned Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B model automatically scores low-level physical artifacts (geometric distortion, object penetration, unnatural deformation) on a scale.

#### 3.3. Evaluated Models and Protocol

- **Models**: 20 models are evaluated, categorized into three paradigms:
 - **Text-driven (9 models)**: e.g., Seedance 1.5, Wan 2.7, Kling 3.0, YUME 1.5. These accept all four interaction types and are evaluated on the full 289-case test set using an iterative image-to-video (I2V) protocol (last frame forwarding).
 - **Camera-controlled (5 models)**: e.g., LingBot-World, HY-World 1.5. These natively accept 6DoF camera parameters and are evaluated on a 158-case navigation subset.
 - **Action-conditioned (6 models)**: e.g., Genie 3, Matrix-Game 3.0. These natively accept discrete action signals (e.g., keyboard commands) and are evaluated on the navigation subset.
- **Fair Comparison**: For navigation, actions are mapped to natural language, 6-DoF poses, or discrete commands as appropriate for each paradigm. Semantic interactions are text-only, limiting them to text-driven models.
- **Web-based Evaluation**: Models accessible only via web interfaces (Genie 3, Happy Oyster) are evaluated using an automated browser-use agent to perform interactions and download videos.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on 20 models across the WBENCH evaluation suite revealed several insights:

- **No Single Model Dominates All Dimensions**: No individual model or paradigm consistently outperformed others across all five evaluation dimensions. This indicates a multi-faceted challenge in interactive world model development, where different architectures and training strategies excel in specific areas.
- **Video Quality**: This dimension is relatively mature across models, with many sub-metrics approaching saturation. Text-driven models like Seedance 1.5 and Wan 2.7 showed high scores (around 81-82), but camera-controlled models such as LingBot-World and Happy Oyster also achieved competitive quality (around 77-79) without compromising control capabilities. This suggests that raw video quality is no longer the primary differentiator.
- **Setting Adherence**: Text-driven models demonstrated a significant lead in setting adherence. Wan 2.7 (91.4) and Kling 3.0 (91.0) substantially surpassed the best world models (Happy Oyster 74.2, LingBot-World 72.6). This gap was more pronounced in scene adherence than subject adherence, suggesting that world model training may prioritize navigation fidelity over the broader scene-following capabilities of text-driven models.
- **Interaction Adherence**:
 - **Navigation**: Models with native control interfaces (camera-controlled, action-conditioned) outperformed text-driven models by approximately 10 points (76.0 and 77.7 vs. 67.6). YUME 1.5, a text-driven model, achieved the highest navigation score within its paradigm (72.0), potentially due to navigation-focused fine-tuning.
 - **Semantic Interactions (Event Editing, Subject Action, Perspective Switching)**: These are exclusive to text-driven models. Kling 3.0 and Wan 2.7 led in event editing and subject action. However, perspective switching proved to be the most challenging task, with models achieving an average score of only 30.7.
- **Consistency**: Consistency is a multi-faceted capability. LingBot-World achieved the highest overall consistency (89.9). Camera-controlled models showed superior geometric consistency (93.1 vs. 88.2 for text-driven models), attributable to explicit pose supervision. However, they underperformed in perspective consistency (averaging 67.1 vs. 74.7 for text-driven models), indicating that accurate camera control does not guarantee coherent subject motion, particularly in third-person views. A negative correlation (r=−0.56) between dynamic degree and consistency suggested that some high consistency scores in certain models might be inflated by scene stasis rather than robust consistency during active motion.
- **Physical Adherence**: Text-driven models (67.0) generally outperformed camera-controlled (64.2) and action-conditioned models (61.7). This suggests that broad generative priors contribute more to physical correctness than specialized control training. Wan 2.7 notably led in causal fidelity (83.3), potentially benefiting from diverse physical interaction data.
- **Cross-Dimension Analysis**:
 - **Decoupled Navigation**: Navigation scores showed near-zero correlation with video quality, consistency, and physical compliance, implying that strong performance in rendering, memory, or physics does not directly translate to controllable movement.
 - **Physical vs. Rendering**: Physical scores correlated strongly with video quality (r=0.84) and consistency (r=0.72) but not navigation (r=−0.15), suggesting physical plausibility derives more from rich generative priors than from control capabilities.
 - **Semantic vs. Navigation**: Semantic interactions correlated more with setting adherence (r=0.76) than navigation (r=0.29), indicating that event editing and subject action rely on instruction grounding, while navigation requires distinct spatial-state representation.
- **Structured Difficulty by World Settings**: First-person perspective eased navigation due to direct camera mapping, while third-person introduced complexity from joint subject-camera control. Sports/game scenes and animal subjects were found to be most challenging for navigation due to fast dynamics and complex non-rigid motion, whereas workspace scenes and robot subjects were easier due to static geometry and rigid-body motion.
- **Performance Degradation Over Turns**: Navigation performance degraded significantly faster than other interaction types (33-point drop from Turn 1 to Turn 4+), indicating that maintaining a spatial reference frame across compounded pose errors is a major challenge. Event editing and subject action degraded moderately, while perspective switching remained consistently low-performing across turns.
- **Open-Source Model Competitiveness**: Open-source models demonstrated strong performance in specific dimensions, with HY-World 1.5 leading in navigation (87.5) and LingBot-World in consistency (89.9), suggesting that open-source systems can compete with closed-source alternatives given appropriate design choices.
- **Human Preference Alignment**: All ten evaluated aspects demonstrated high Spearman rank correlation (ρ ≥ 0.94, with four reaching ρ = 1.00) between per-model human win rates and WBENCH automated scores, confirming the reliability of the benchmark's metrics in reflecting human judgment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

WBENCH makes contributions to the evaluation of interactive world models through its design and empirical findings.

- **Unified and Comprehensive Evaluation**: By introducing a benchmark that spans five complementary evaluation dimensions and 22 fine-grained sub-metrics, WBENCH offers a more holistic assessment capability compared to previous fragmented approaches. This allows for a comprehensive understanding of model performance across critical aspects of visual quality, adherence to settings and interactions, temporal and spatial consistency, and physical plausibility.
- **Multi-turn Interaction Dataset**: The dataset's multi-turn structure, covering both first- and third-person perspectives and four distinct interaction types (navigation, subject action, event editing, and perspective switching), provides a challenging environment for evaluating long-horizon coherence and the accumulation of errors. The unified navigation interface further enables fair comparisons across different control paradigms.
- **Diagnostic Baselines and Actionable Insights**: The application of WBENCH to 20 diverse models establishes diagnostic baselines for the current state of interactive world models. The identified findings—such as the decoupling of navigation from other capabilities, the distinction between camera control and perspective consistency, and the correlation of physical correctness with rendering quality rather than control—provide specific insights into current limitations. These insights can guide future research by highlighting areas where current models struggle, such as reliable controllability across multiple turns, consistent subject motion in third-person, and robust physical interactions.
- **Guidance for Future Development**: The benchmark's ability to expose fine-grained failure modes in physics, controllability, and consistency is intended to direct the development of more reliable and robust world models. This could have downstream benefits for various applications requiring interactive simulations, including robotics, gaming, education, and virtual environments, by informing the design of architectures and training methodologies that address identified shortcomings.
- **Advancing the Field**: By offering a standardized framework, WBENCH aims to facilitate more rigorous and comparable research, enabling the community to track progress more effectively and collaboratively tackle the complex challenges of building truly capable interactive world models that unify high-fidelity rendering with reliable control, consistency, and physical understanding.
