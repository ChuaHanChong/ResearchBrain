# WBench: A Comprehensive Multi-turn Benchmark for Interactive Video World Model Evaluation

> [!summary] Summary
> WBENCH provides a comprehensive multi-turn benchmark for evaluating interactive video world models, assessing 20 diverse models across five dimensions including video quality, interaction adherence, and physical plausibility. This benchmark identifies current limitations such as rapid degradation in multi-turn navigation and low performance in perspective switching, while demonstrating competitive results from open-source models in specific capabilities.

## Problem

- Existing evaluation of interactive video world models is fragmented, focusing on specific competencies or particular interactive capabilities, hindering comprehensive assessment.
- There is an absence of a unified standard to systematically evaluate interactive world models across all five essential roles: Renderer, Director, Controller, Memory, and Engine.
- Fair comparison and detailed diagnosis of failure modes across crucial dimensions like visual quality, controllability, memory, and physical adherence are difficult with current methods.

## Method

- Introduces WBENCH, a multi-turn benchmark dataset comprising 289 cases and 1,058 interaction turns, supporting diverse open-domain scenes and both first- and third-person perspectives.
- Develops a comprehensive evaluation suite with 22 fine-grained sub-metrics categorized into five dimensions: Video Quality, Setting Adherence, Interaction Adherence, Consistency, and Physical Adherence.
- Evaluates 20 contemporary models, covering text-driven, camera-controlled, and action-conditioned paradigms, using an iterative image-to-video protocol for fair comparison.

## Results

- Models with native control interfaces (camera-controlled, action-conditioned) outperformed text-driven models in navigation by approximately 10 points.
- Perspective switching proved the most challenging task for text-driven models, achieving an average score of only 30.7.
- Navigation performance degraded significantly faster than other interaction types, with a 33-point drop from Turn 1 to Turn 4+, highlighting difficulties in maintaining a spatial reference frame over long horizons.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - No single model or paradigm consistently dominates all five evaluation dimensions, indicating a multi-faceted challenge in interactive world model development.
> - Navigation performance is largely decoupled from video quality, consistency, and physical compliance, suggesting distinct underlying mechanisms.
> - Physical plausibility correlates strongly with video quality and consistency (r=0.84 and r=0.72 respectively), implying reliance on rich generative priors rather than specialized control capabilities.
