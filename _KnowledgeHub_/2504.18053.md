# DREAM: Disentangling Risks to Enhance Safety Alignment in Multimodal Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> DREAM, developed by Alibaba Group, Zhejiang University, and partners, introduces a two-stage training framework to enhance Multimodal Large Language Models' (MLLMs) safety alignment by enabling them to disentangle complex multimodal risks. This approach significantly reduces both attack success rates on unsafe inputs and over-refusal rates on benign content, achieving up to a 16.17% improvement in "safe & effective" scores compared to GPT-4V.

## Problem

- MLLMs face complex safety challenges due to the intricate interplay between visual and textual data, creating new attack surfaces and subtle risk combinations.
- Existing safety alignment methods often result in either 'unsafety' (failing to block harmful content) or 'oversafety' (erroneously refusing benign queries, also known as 'alignment tax').
- Prior approaches generally lack explicit, step-by-step reasoning about multimodal risk combinations, assuming MLLMs inherently possess the ability to identify them.

## Method

- DREAM utilizes ==Multimodal Risk Disentanglement (MRD)== to systematically analyze multimodal inputs by breaking them into constituent parts and identifying specific risk categories, forming the foundation for training.
- The first stage, ==Risk-aware Fine-tuning==, employs a powerful 'teacher' model to generate detailed, disentangled risk observations and corresponding safe, helpful responses for supervised fine-tuning of a student model.
- The second stage, ==Risk-aware Preference Optimization==, refines the student model's safety alignment through iterative Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback (RLAIF) using teacher-generated observation-wise and global scores to guide Direct Preference Optimization (DPO).

## Results

- The preliminary Multimodal Risk Disentanglement (MRD) strategy, even as an inference-time method, outperformed existing baselines by achieving a substantially lower average Attack Success Rate (ASR) / Refusal Rate (RR) score (e.g., **9.37%** for InternVL2-26B vs. **33.34%** vanilla).
- DREAM demonstrated superior robustness to oversafety, maintaining very low refusal rates on benign benchmarks (e.g., **0%** for InternVL2-8B), in contrast to **100%** oversafety rates exhibited by some previous training-time methods like VLGuard.
- DREAM achieved a significant **16.17%** improvement in the 'safe & effective' score on the SIUO benchmark compared to GPT-4V (InternVL2-26B at **39.52%** vs. GPT-4V at **23.35%**), while effectively maintaining the model's general helpfulness and utility.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit, step-by-step disentanglement of multimodal risks significantly improves MLLMs' risk awareness, leading to better detection of unsafety and mitigation of oversafety.
> - Internalizing this risk disentanglement capability through a two-stage training process (fine-tuning and iterative preference optimization) allows MLLMs to inherently understand and respond to complex safety challenges without explicit prompting.
> - Combining fine-grained, observation-wise feedback with global safety and helpfulness scores during preference optimization effectively balances safety alignment with the model's general utility and helpfulness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancement of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), such as GPT-4V, LLaVA, and Qwen-VL, has enabled them to perform exceptionally well across a wide array of vision-language tasks. However, this impressive capability comes with significant safety challenges that are arguably more complex than those faced by their text-only counterparts, Large Language Models (LLMs).

**Challenges of MLLM Safety:** MLLMs integrate visual and textual data, creating new attack surfaces and complex risk combinations. Malicious intent can be subtly hidden within images, amplified by images, or emerge from seemingly benign combinations of image and text. For instance, a harmless text query paired with a dangerous image, or vice versa, can lead to harmful model outputs. Existing defense strategies generally fall into two categories:
1. **Training-time methods:** These approaches focus on model architecture optimization, loss function design, or constructing safety-aligned instruction data for Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF). Examples include VLGuard and SPA-VL.
2. **Inference-time methods:** These involve deploying external detectors to identify risks in inputs or outputs, regenerating responses if necessary. Examples include ECSO and AdaShield.

**Limitations of Prior Work:** The authors argue that a critical limitation of existing methods is their assumption that MLLMs inherently possess the ability to identify complex risks. Most approaches lack explicit, step-by-step reasoning about multimodal risk combinations. This often leads to unsatisfactory outcomes: either *unsafety* (failing to detect and mitigate harmful content) or *oversafety* (erroneously refusing benign queries, also known as "alignment tax").

**DREAM's Position:** This paper directly addresses this gap by proposing a novel approach that emphasizes **disentangling multimodal risks**. By enabling MLLMs to systematically analyze and understand the individual components of risk within complex multimodal inputs, DREAM aims to fundamentally enhance their "risk awareness." This work thus represents a significant step towards developing more robust, reliable, and user-friendly MLLMs by equipping them with a deeper understanding of safety implications, moving beyond superficial pattern matching to a more nuanced, reasoned approach to safety alignment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of this research is to **enhance the safety alignment of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs)** by improving their ability to identify and respond appropriately to complex multimodal risks, without compromising their general helpfulness.

The key motivations driving this work are:
- **Addressing Complex Multimodal Risks:** MLLMs face unique safety challenges due to the intricate interplay between visual and textual inputs. Malicious intent can be amplified, hidden, or dispersed across modalities, creating scenarios that current MLLMs often fail to navigate effectively.
- **Mitigating Unsatisfactory Safety Outcomes:** Existing safety alignment methods often result in either "unsafety" (failing to block harmful content) or "oversafety" (incorrectly refusing benign queries). The authors are motivated to develop a method that achieves a better balance, ensuring safety without excessive caution.
- **Internalizing Risk Awareness:** The initial observation that explicit, step-by-step reasoning (Multimodal Risk Disentanglement, MRD) significantly boosts risk detection leads to the motivation of internalizing this capability within the MLLM itself. This would remove the need for explicit prompts during inference, making the models inherently safer and more efficient.
- **Developing a Comprehensive Training-time Solution:** While inference-time methods can provide immediate safeguards, the goal is to imbue the model with intrinsic safety intelligence during training, allowing it to generalize better to novel threats and operate more autonomously.
- **Reducing "Alignment Tax":** The paper aims to reduce the performance degradation (or "alignment tax") often associated with safety alignment efforts, ensuring that MLLMs remain highly capable and helpful on benign tasks while robustly handling risky ones.

In essence, DREAM seeks to move MLLM safety alignment from a reactive, pattern-matching approach to a proactive, reasoning-based understanding of multimodal risks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DREAM (Disentangling Risks to Enhance Safety Alignment in MLLMs) is a novel training-time approach built upon a preliminary analysis of multimodal risk disentanglement. It comprises two main components: **Risk-aware Fine-tuning** and **Risk-aware Preference Optimization**.

#### 3.1. Preliminary Analysis: Multimodal Risk Disentanglement (MRD)

The paper begins by meticulously analyzing risk combinations and introducing a concept of Multimodal Risk Disentanglement (MRD).
- **Risk Combination Analysis:** The authors categorize multimodal input risks into four types: Unsafe-Unsafe (both image and text are harmful), Unsafe-Safe (image harmful, text harmless), Safe-Safe (both harmless but harmful when combined), and Safe-Unsafe (image harmless, text harmful). This taxonomy highlights the complexity MLLMs must handle.
- **Risk Disentanglement Mechanism:** MRD involves designing detailed prompts to guide MLLMs to systematically analyze multimodal inputs. This process focuses on breaking down the input into its constituent parts—text instruction, text content, image content, and text within the image—and identifying potential risks for each. For each identified risk, the model outputs an observation tuple `(source, category, risk_content)`. Nine risk categories (e.g., illegal activities, violence, discrimination, sexual content) are compiled from existing policies (VLGuard, OpenAI Usage Policies) to standardize this process. The evaluation in Section 3.3 confirms that MRD significantly improves MLLMs' risk awareness and ability to mitigate oversafety compared to simpler prompting or external detectors.

#### 3.2. DREAM Framework

Motivated by the success of MRD in preliminary analysis, DREAM aims to internalize this risk disentanglement capability within MLLMs through a two-stage training process:

##### 3.2.1. Risk-aware Fine-tuning

- **Objective:** To enable the student model (S) to perform risk disentanglement and generate safe, effective responses without requiring explicit MRD prompts during inference.
- **Process:**
 1. **Data Synthesis:** A powerful "teacher" model (T) (e.g., InternVL2-26B) is given a multimodal instruction (x).
 2. The teacher model first applies the MRD prompt to generate detailed, risk-disentangled observations (o).
 3. Based on these observations, the teacher model then produces a final response (y). This response includes a natural language explanation of the identified risks, followed by a harmless and helpful answer to the original question.
 4. **Model Training:** The student model (S) is then fine-tuned (SFT) on these (x, y) pairs. By learning from the teacher's reasoned, safety-aligned responses, the student model internalizes the ability to perform risk disentanglement and respond safely.

##### 3.2.2. Risk-aware Preference Optimization (Iterative RLAIF)

- **Objective:** To further refine the student model's safety alignment by leveraging MRD as an effective feedback signal in an Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback (RLAIF) manner.
- **Feedback Collection:**
 1. For each multimodal instruction (x) in the training dataset, the student model (S) samples N candidate responses.
 2. The teacher model (T), equipped with MRD, evaluates these N responses using two proposed scoring methods:
 - **Observation-wise Score:** Measures how accurately the student's response recognizes *each individual risk* identified by the teacher's MRD (0-10 scale). This ensures fine-grained risk recognition is preserved during distillation.
 - **Global Score:** The teacher assigns an overall quality score (0-10) to each student response, evaluating both safety (based on disentangled observations) and adherence to the original instruction's helpfulness requirements. This helps mitigate oversafety.
 3. The final score for each response is the sum of its observation-wise and global scores.
- **Iterative DPO (Direct Preference Optimization):**
 1. From the N sampled responses, the response with the highest final score is chosen as `y_w` (preferred), and the one with the lowest score as `y_l` (rejected), forming preference pairs `D = {(x, y_w, y_l)}`.
 2. The student model (S) is then optimized using DPO to prefer `y_w` over `y_l`.
 3. To counter distribution shift issues that can arise with fixed preference datasets, an *iterative DPO* strategy is employed. After each training iteration (k), the trained student model `S_k` resamples new responses, and new preference pairs `D_{k+1}` are generated based on the updated scoring. This iterative process ensures the model continually learns from relevant, high-quality feedback.

By combining the explicit reasoning of MRD with a robust two-stage training strategy, DREAM aims to create MLLMs that are not only capable but also inherently safe and reliable.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate DREAM's significant advancements in MLLM safety alignment:

#### 4.1. Preliminary Analysis Results (MRD Effectiveness)

- **Superior Risk Awareness without Oversafety:** The Multimodal Risk Disentanglement (MRD) strategy, even when applied as an inference-time prompting method, significantly outperforms existing inference-time baselines (ECSO, AdaShield) and vanilla MLLMs in balancing safety and avoiding oversafety.
- **Quantitative Improvement:** MRD consistently achieves lower Attack Success Rates (ASR) on unsafe benchmarks (VLGuard, FigStep-Unsafe, SIUO) while maintaining low Refusal Rates (RR) on benign benchmarks (FigStep-Benign, MOSSBench). For instance, InternVL2-26B with MRD achieved an average ASR/RR score of 9.37%, a substantial improvement over its vanilla performance (33.34%) and better than ECSO (32.75%) and AdaShield (36.93%). GPT-4o with MRD also showed superior performance (9.66%).
- **Reasoning Capability:** These results confirm that explicit, step-by-step disentanglement enables MLLMs to better reason about complex multimodal risks, leading to more accurate detection of unsafety and mitigation of oversafety.

#### 4.2. DREAM Framework Results (Training-time Method)

- **Robustness to Oversafety:** DREAM demonstrates greater robustness to oversafety challenges compared to previous training-time methods like VLGuard. For example, on the FigStep-Benign benchmark, VLGuard exhibited a 100% oversafety rate, erroneously refusing safe instructions, whereas DREAM maintained very low refusal rates (e.g., 4% for LLaVA1.5-7B, 0% for InternVL2-8B). This highlights DREAM's ability to discern true risks from benign ones.
- **Significant Improvement in Safe & Effective Rate:** DREAM achieved a substantial improvement in the "safe & effective" rate on the SIUO benchmark across various MLLMs. For InternVL2-26B, DREAM improved the safe&effective score from 17.96% (vanilla) to 39.52%. Critically, DREAM achieved a 16.17% improvement in the SIUO safe&effective score compared to GPT-4V (DREAM with InternVL2-26B at 39.52% vs. GPT-4V at 23.35%). This is attributed to its comprehensive and fine-grained feedback collection strategy.
- **Maintained Helpfulness and Utility:** Unlike some safety alignment methods that incur a significant "alignment tax," DREAM enhances helpfulness (evaluated on VLGuard safe-safe test set) while maintaining most utility capabilities on general multimodal benchmarks (MME, MM-Vet, MMBench). VLGuard, in contrast, showed notable performance degradation on MM-Vet, suggesting that DREAM effectively reduces the trade-off between safety and utility.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Further Analysis

- **Importance of Components:** Ablation studies confirm that both Risk-aware Fine-tuning and Risk-aware Preference Optimization are crucial for DREAM's performance. Removing either component or individual scoring methods (observation-wise or global score) leads to performance degradation. The global score notably improves safety, while the observation-wise score helps maintain effectiveness by preventing the model from ignoring risk-related details.
- **Scalability:** Experiments on larger datasets (SPA-VL) show that DREAM's risk-aware capabilities increase with data size, demonstrating its effectiveness and scalability. It also outperforms standard DPO and PPO methods with similar data scales.

In summary, the findings strongly support that disentangling multimodal risks and leveraging this understanding for both fine-tuning and iterative preference optimization leads to significantly safer MLLMs that maintain high utility and avoid common pitfalls like oversafety.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "DREAM: Disentangling Risks to Enhance Safety Alignment in Multimodal Large Language Models" carries significant implications for the development and deployment of safe and responsible AI.

#### 5.1. Foundational Contribution to MLLM Safety

- **Novelty of Risk Disentanglement:** The paper is pioneering in its thorough and meticulous disentanglement of multimodal risk factors. By treating multimodal risks not as a monolithic problem but as a combination of distinct visual and textual components, it offers a more nuanced and effective approach to understanding and mitigating them. This shift from holistic detection to component-wise reasoning is a crucial conceptual advancement.
- **Enhanced Intrinsic Risk Awareness:** DREAM's methodology allows MLLMs to internalize sophisticated risk reasoning during training, making them inherently more "risk-aware" rather than relying on external, often brittle, safety filters or simplistic pattern matching. This intrinsic capability is more robust and generalizable to novel threats.

#### 5.2. Practical Improvements and Broader Adoption

- **Superior Safety-Utility Trade-off:** A critical achievement of DREAM is its ability to significantly boost safety alignment without compromising the model's performance on normal, benign tasks. By reducing "oversafety" and the "alignment tax," DREAM makes MLLMs more reliable and practical for real-world applications. This ensures that safety mechanisms do not hinder the helpfulness and utility that users expect from these powerful models.
- **Improved User Experience:** Models aligned with DREAM are more likely to provide safe, helpful, and relevant responses, avoiding both dangerous outputs and unwarranted refusals. This improves user trust and the overall experience with MLLM-powered applications.
- **Scalability and Efficiency:** The iterative RLAIF framework, coupled with automated scoring methods derived from MRD, offers a scalable and data-efficient approach to generating high-quality safety alignment data. This is crucial for training large models where manual annotation is prohibitively expensive.
- **Benchmark Advancement:** The paper's contribution of detailed analysis and a new framework implicitly contributes to the refinement of safety benchmarks, pushing the community to develop more comprehensive evaluation metrics that account for nuanced multimodal interactions.

#### 5.3. Future Research Directions

- **Beyond Image and Text:** The identified limitation of focusing primarily on image and text modalities opens avenues for extending the disentanglement framework to other modalities like video and audio, which are increasingly relevant for next-generation MLLMs.
- **Refining Response Generation:** The observation that DREAM-aligned MLLMs can generate verbose responses points to future work on incorporating explicit, customized safety rules. This could allow models to learn to generate concise, human-preferred safe responses while retaining their deep risk understanding.
- **Data-Efficient and Automated Reasoning:** The mentioned future work on data-efficient and automatic reinforcement reasoning algorithms suggests continuous improvement in the efficiency and autonomy of safety alignment processes, reducing human oversight requirements over time.

In conclusion, DREAM represents a significant step forward in making MLLMs safer and more trustworthy. By providing MLLMs with a granular understanding of multimodal risks, the work paves the way for their more responsible and widespread deployment across diverse industries and applications, from content moderation to educational tools and beyond.
