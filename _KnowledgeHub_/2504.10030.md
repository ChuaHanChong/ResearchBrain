# EmbodiedAgent: A Scalable Hierarchical Approach to Overcome Practical Challenge in Multi-Robot Control

> [!summary] Summary
> EmbodiedAgent, developed by researchers at CUHK Shenzhen and SIAR, introduces a scalable hierarchical framework for multi-robot control that specifically addresses and mitigates LLM "hallucination" and practical infeasibility in real-world tasks. It achieves a 71.85% RPAS score by fine-tuning Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct on a novel MultiPlan+ dataset which includes explicit impractical cases.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) used in robotics often generate "hallucinated" plans that are impractical, violate physical constraints, or require non-existent objects, leading to critical failures in real-world scenarios.
- Traditional multi-robot control struggles with the dynamic complexities and diverse capabilities of heterogeneous robot teams, lacking scalability and adaptability for complex, long-horizon tasks.
- Existing LLM-driven planners primarily validate in simulators, with limited real-world deployment and an absence of comprehensive evaluation metrics that capture practical feasibility and error diagnosis.

## Method

- A hierarchical control framework featuring an LLM-based high-level planner and a low-level execution module, designed for iterative next-action prediction and tool invocation, including explicit error interrupts for impractical cases.
- ==The MultiPlan==+ dataset, an extension of MultiPlan, comprising over 18,000 data entries from 3,400+ tasks, crucially augmented with explicit impractical cases and categorized error signals (e.g., Lack of Ability, Lack of Object) for supervised fine-tuning.
- A Robot Planning Assessment Schema (RPAS) for comprehensive evaluation, combining ASR_top-k (alignment with reference plans) and LLM-aided Expert Grading, alongside a Multi-Robot Planning Error Diagnosis (MRED) taxonomy.

## Results

- EmbodiedAgent achieved a leading **71.85%** RPAS score and **74.01%** ASR_top-k, significantly outperforming proprietary LLMs like GPT-4o (**20.14%** RPAS) and other open-source models, demonstrating superior planning quality and practical feasibility.
- The system exhibited a **0.00%** Ending Error (EE), indicating robust handling of task termination and prevention of both premature halting and redundant continuation, attributed to specific training on termination and impractical error signals.
- Successful real-world deployment demonstrated the framework's ability to coordinate a heterogeneous team (robotic arm and quadrupedal robot) for complex office service tasks, validating its scalability and robustness in unstructured environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly training LLMs on impractical scenarios and error signals (e.g., through the MultiPlan+ dataset) is crucial for mitigating hallucination and enabling planners to recognize and signal when a task is infeasible.
> - Domain-adaptive fine-tuning of a smaller LLM (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) with specialized robot planning data significantly outperforms larger, general-purpose proprietary and open-weight LLMs not fine-tuned for the domain.
> - A comprehensive evaluation framework like RPAS, which includes both quantitative alignment and qualitative expert grading with detailed error diagnosis, is essential for robustly assessing multi-robot planning systems beyond simple success rates.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) into robotics has emerged as a transformative paradigm, shifting how complex objectives are translated into robot actions. Traditional multi-robot control, often reliant on rigid consensus algorithms or solving Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equations, struggles with the dynamic complexities and diverse capabilities of heterogeneous robot teams. LLMs, with their high-level reasoning capabilities, offer a promising solution by enabling human operators to specify broad objectives through natural language commands, which are then decomposed into executable robotic skills.

Recent advancements in LLM-driven planners have focused on converting mission objectives into structured descriptive frameworks, often using language-only or language-vision modalities. Projects like PARTNER, Smart-LLM, COHERENT, and EMMA exemplify this trend. However, a significant limitation of many existing frameworks is their heavy reliance on integrated simulators for validation, with limited or no extensive validation in real-world environments. More critically, these planners frequently fail when confronted with *impractical or counterfactual scenarios*. This "hallucination" by LLMs—generating plans that violate physical constraints, operational limitations, or rely on non-existent objects—is a major barrier to real-world deployment, particularly in safety-critical industrial or domestic applications. While some prior works have attempted to mitigate hallucination through fine-tuning or feedback loops (e.g., MultiPlan, ProgPrompt, Adaplanner), they have not fully resolved the issue of *practical infeasibility*.

"EmbodiedAgent" directly addresses these critical limitations. By focusing on mitigating hallucination caused by impractical tasks and enhancing real-world adaptability, this work positions itself at the forefront of robust and deployable LLM-driven multi-robot systems. It extends previous efforts like MultiPlan by augmenting the planning data with explicit impractical cases and introducing structured error signals, thereby teaching the agent not just *what to do*, but *what not to do* or *when a task is impossible*. This explicit practical grounding, combined with a hierarchical architecture and comprehensive evaluation (RPAS), pushes the boundaries of embodied AI towards more reliable and trustworthy autonomous systems in diverse, unstructured environments. The paper contributes to the growing body of research that seeks to bridge the gap between theoretical LLM reasoning and practical robotic execution.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind EmbodiedAgent stems from the critical need for robust and reliable planning in heterogeneous multi-robot systems, particularly in real-world scenarios. While Large Language Models (LLMs) have shown immense promise in high-level reasoning and task decomposition for robotics, they suffer from significant drawbacks, mainly "hallucination" and limited adaptability when faced with impractical or counterfactual situations. Such errors, where a planner generates actions that violate physical constraints, require non-existent objects, or exceed robot capabilities, can lead to critical failures in practical applications.

In response to these challenges, the key objectives of EmbodiedAgent are:

1. **To overcome practical infeasibility and hallucination:** The core objective is to develop an LLM-based planner capable of discerning and signaling impractical tasks, thereby preventing hallucinated plans and counterfactual executions that are detrimental in real-world deployments. This involves explicitly training the system to recognize and respond to scenarios where a task cannot be completed due to environmental constraints or robot limitations.
2. **To establish a scalable hierarchical control framework:** The system aims to provide a robust and generalizable architecture for heterogeneous multi-robot control, allowing for the dynamic composition of diverse robot skills and efficient task decomposition for long-horizon objectives.
3. **To enhance planner robustness and generalizability:** Through supervised fine-tuning on a specialized dataset, the goal is to make the planner more resilient to unforeseen variations in tasks and environments, improving its performance beyond integrated simulators to real-world deployment.
4. **To develop a comprehensive evaluation schema:** There is a need for a more nuanced assessment of multi-robot planning systems that goes beyond simple success rates. Thus, an objective is to introduce a systematic framework that captures various aspects of planning quality, including practical feasibility and error diagnosis.
5. **To validate real-world applicability:** The ultimate motivation is to demonstrate the practical utility and effectiveness of the proposed framework in coordinating heterogeneous robot teams for complex service tasks in unstructured, real-world environments.

In essence, EmbodiedAgent seeks to bridge the gap between abstract task planning by LLMs and the grounded, constraint-aware execution required for safe and effective multi-robot operations in the physical world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EmbodiedAgent introduces a hierarchical control framework comprising a high-level planner and a low-level execution module. This architecture is designed to enable robust coordination of heterogeneous multi-robot systems, with a particular focus on addressing practical infeasibility and hallucination.

**A. EmbodiedAgent Architecture:**
The system operates through an iterative next-action prediction paradigm.
- **High-Level Planner:** This core component is an LLM-based reasoning engine. It interprets task goals and generates sequential actions by invoking "tools" from a dedicated library. These tools encapsulate fundamental robot skills, task completion signals, and, critically, explicit error interrupts for impractical cases. To maintain context, an embedded memory module stores structured records of prior actions and environmental states, iteratively updating the LLM's prompt. An environment interface validates generated actions against physical constraints (e.g., collision avoidance) before dispatching them. The agent generates one action and its arguments per inference, terminating upon an `endPlanning` signal or an impractical error signal.
- **Low-Level Execution Module:** This layer translates the high-level plans into actionable commands for individual robots. It utilizes specialized Software Development Kits (SDKs) or single-task policies, which are often trained using reinforcement or imitation learning (e.g., Action Chunk Transformer (ACT) for robotic arms). This ensures precise, real-time control, adapting to sensor feedback and dynamic environments.

**B. MultiPlan+ Dataset:**
A central component of EmbodiedAgent's robust design is the MultiPlan+ dataset, an extension of the previous MultiPlan work.
- **Scale and Diversity:** It comprises over 18,000 data entries from more than 3,400 unique tasks across 100 diverse scenarios (e.g., office, domestic, urban).
- **Next-Action Prediction Format:** The dataset is structured for supervised fine-tuning in a next-action prediction format, where each entry includes task specifications, environmental states, planning memory, and a ground-truth next action.
- **Impractical Cases:** A crucial innovation is the explicit inclusion of a subset of *impractical cases*. This is designed to mitigate hallucination by training the LLM to recognize and signal when a task is not feasible. Impractical error signals are categorized into four types:
 - **Lack of Ability (LoA):** System lacks fundamental robot capabilities.
 - **Lack of Skill (LoS):** A required skill is unregistered.
 - **Load Over Limit (LoL):** Task exceeds a robot's load capacity.
 - **Lack of Object (LoO):** Essential objects are unavailable.
- **Tool-based Annotations:** Annotations are structured as callable tools (robot skills, end planning signals, error signals), facilitating direct training for the agent's function-calling capabilities.

**C. Robot Planning Assessment Schema (RPAS):**
To provide a comprehensive evaluation, the authors propose RPAS, which moves beyond simplistic success rates.
- **Combined Metric:** RPAS is calculated as the average of the top-k Average Success Rate (ASR_top-k) and LLM-aided Expert Grading, scaled to a percentage.
- **ASR_top-k:** Measures the alignment of the predicted planning sequence with the reference sequence for the first `k` steps, rewarding partially correct plans.
- **LLM-aided Expert Grading:** Utilizes an LLM to assess logical consistency, feasibility, efficiency, and robustness of planning sequences on a 0-100 scale, capturing nuanced differences in quality.
- **Multi-Robot Planning Error Diagnosis (MRED):** An extended error taxonomy for root-cause analysis, categorizing failures into: Unregistered Error (UE), Position Error (PoE), Planning Error (PlE), Skill Error (SE), and a newly added **Ending Error (EE)**, which detects improper termination or continuation of planning.

**D. Implementation Details:**
The core LLM for EmbodiedAgent is Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, which is fine-tuned on the MultiPlan+ dataset using 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs over three epochs. For real-world validation, a robotic arm's low-level control utilizes ACT trained on teleoperated demonstrations, while a quadrupedal robot uses its integrated SDK.

This multi-faceted approach, combining a hierarchical LLM agent with a specially curated dataset addressing practical challenges and a comprehensive evaluation framework, forms the backbone of EmbodiedAgent's methodology.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental validation of EmbodiedAgent involved both quantitative benchmarks against state-of-the-art LLMs and a real-world deployment scenario.

**A. Heterogeneous Multi-Robot Task Planning Performance:**
EmbodiedAgent's performance was rigorously evaluated using the proposed RPAS metrics on an unseen test dataset of 32 tasks, including impractical instances.
- **Superior Performance:** EmbodiedAgent achieved a remarkable **74.01% ASR_top-k** and a leading **71.85% RPAS score**, outperforming all other proprietary and open-source LLMs benchmarked. This demonstrates its superior ability to generate correct initial planning steps and overall high-quality plans.
- **Comparative Analysis with Proprietary LLMs:** Proprietary models like GPT-4o, OpenAI-o1, and Claude-3.5-Sonnet exhibited significantly lower RPAS scores (20.14%, 20.56%, and 34.85% respectively). These models struggled with skill errors and planning errors, indicating that while powerful, they lack the specific domain adaptation necessary for robust multi-robot control in complex, constrained environments without dedicated fine-tuning. For instance, GPT-4o showed a 93.75% Skill Error (SE) rate.
- **Comparative Analysis with Open-Weight LLMs:** Other open-weight LLMs, even larger ones like LLaMA-3.1-70B, also performed considerably worse than EmbodiedAgent. For example, LLaMA-3.1-70B achieved only 34.22% RPAS, suffering from high Planning Error (PlE) and Skill Error (SE) rates.
- **Impact of Domain-Adaptive Training:** The results highlighted the critical importance of task-specific optimization. The fine-tuned MAP-Neo-7B-Multiplan, despite its smaller parameter count, achieved a competitive 45.31% RPAS, outperforming several larger, general-purpose models. This reinforces EmbodiedAgent's core premise that specialized fine-tuning with robot-planning specific data, particularly data augmented with impractical cases, is more effective than simply scaling model parameters.
- **Error Diagnosis (MRED):** EmbodiedAgent exhibited relatively low error rates across most MRED categories. Notably, it achieved **0.00% Ending Error (EE)**, suggesting its robust handling of task termination and prevention of both premature halting and redundant continuation. This directly reflects its training on explicit termination signals and impractical error signals within the MultiPlan+ dataset.

**B. Multi-Robot System for Office Service in Real-World:**
To demonstrate practical applicability, EmbodiedAgent was deployed in a real-world office service task.
- **Heterogeneous Collaboration:** The experiment successfully orchestrated a robotic arm and a quadrupedal robot. The robotic arm was tasked with lifting a cup and wiping spilled coffee, utilizing tissues delivered by the quadrupedal robot.
- **Low-Level Control Integration:** The robotic arm leveraged an Action Chunking Transformer (ACT) for precise manipulation, while the quadruped used its native SDK for locomotion.
- **Task Success:** The system successfully executed the complex, long-horizon task, validating EmbodiedAgent’s ability to coordinate diverse robots effectively in an unstructured environment. This practical demonstration underscores the framework's scalability, robustness, and potential for real-world deployment in service robotics.

In summary, the findings robustly establish EmbodiedAgent as a state-of-the-art solution for heterogeneous multi-robot control, particularly in its ability to mitigate practical challenges like hallucination and ensure robust task completion in both simulated and real-world settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EmbodiedAgent represents a significant stride in the field of multi-robot systems and embodied AI, offering solutions to critical challenges that hinder the widespread adoption of LLM-driven robotics in practical applications. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Mitigation of LLM Hallucination in Robotics:** The most impactful contribution is its direct and effective approach to overcoming "impractical errors" and hallucination. By explicitly training with a dataset (MultiPlan+) containing impractical scenarios and incorporating specific error signals (LoA, LoS, LoL, LoO), EmbodiedAgent equips LLM-based planners with the crucial ability to understand and communicate task infeasibility. This capability is paramount for real-world deployment, where executing a hallucinated plan could lead to physical damage, safety hazards, or task failure.
2. **Enhanced Robustness and Generalizability:** The hierarchical architecture, combined with supervised fine-tuning on a diverse and comprehensive dataset, significantly enhances the planner's robustness and generalizability. This allows EmbodiedAgent to not only perform well in structured tasks but also adapt to unstructured and dynamic environments, a key requirement for pervasive robotic systems.
3. **Scalable and Unified Framework for Heterogeneous Robots:** The system provides a unified and scalable framework for coordinating diverse robotic platforms. This is critical as heterogeneous robot teams offer superior performance for complex tasks by leveraging specialized capabilities. EmbodiedAgent's ability to seamlessly integrate different robot types and their skills for long-horizon objectives advances the state of multi-robot collaboration.
4. **Advancement in Evaluation Methodologies (RPAS):** The introduction of the Robot Planning Assessment Schema (RPAS) is a notable contribution to the broader embodied AI community. By combining automated metrics with LLM-aided expert grading and a detailed error diagnosis (MRED), RPAS offers a more comprehensive and nuanced evaluation of planning performance, moving beyond simplistic success rates. This will serve as an essential benchmarking tool for future developments, promoting more rigorous and realistic assessments of robot intelligence.
5. **Validation in Real-World Scenarios:** The successful real-world deployment in an office service task, involving a robotic arm and a quadrupedal robot, underscores the practical viability of EmbodiedAgent. This demonstration bridges the gap between theoretical advancements and tangible applications, showcasing its potential in various service, industrial, and domestic settings where autonomous, collaborative robot teams can significantly enhance efficiency and capability.
6. **Open-Sourced Resources:** The commitment to open-sourcing the MultiPlan+ dataset and code will greatly benefit the research community. These resources provide a valuable foundation for other researchers to build upon, benchmark new methods, and accelerate advancements in multi-robot planning, particularly in addressing the practical feasibility challenge.

In conclusion, EmbodiedAgent addresses a critical bottleneck in LLM-driven robotics by making these systems more reliable and grounded in physical reality. Its contributions to robust planning, comprehensive evaluation, and practical deployment pave the way for more intelligent, trustworthy, and widely applicable multi-robot systems in the future.
