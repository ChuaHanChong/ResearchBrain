# MoWM: Mixture-of-World-Models for Embodied Planning via Latent-to-Pixel Feature Modulation

> [!summary] Summary
> MOWM introduces a framework for embodied action planning that integrates pixel-space and latent-space world models through latent-to-pixel feature modulation, creating a hybrid visual representation. It achieves state-of-the-art task success rates on the CALVIN benchmark and demonstrates effectiveness in real-world complex manipulation tasks, such as cloth folding.

## Problem

- Pixel-space world models, while preserving detail, often contain significant visual redundancy (e.g., static backgrounds), which can obscure critical motion cues and hinder generalization for action planning.
- Latent-space world models achieve efficiency by operating in a compressed feature space, but this compression can suppress fine-grained visual details essential for precise manipulation tasks.
- Existing embodied action planning methods struggle to effectively combine the complementary strengths of detailed pixel representations and motion-aware latent representations in a single framework.

## Method

- **MoWM** trains two instruction-conditioned world models: a ==pixel-space model== built on Stable Video Diffusion for fine-grained details and a ==latent-space model== using a ViT-g transformer for motion-aware representations.
- It extracts multi-scale visual features from the pixel world model and motion-aware features directly from the latent world model's predictions.
- These aligned pixel and latent features are fused via ==concatenation and a residual mechanism== to create a robust hybrid representation, which then conditions a ==Diffusion Policy== action decoder to generate robot actions.

## Results

- MoWM achieved state-of-the-art task success rates on the CALVIN benchmark, demonstrating a **5.7%** improvement over the best VLA-based baseline (Pi0) and a **13.4%** improvement over the best world model baseline (VPP).
- The framework successfully executed a challenging, long-horizon cloth-folding task on a real-world AgileX robotic platform, with an average inference latency of **110 ms** for 50 steps.
- Ablation studies confirmed that fusing pixel-level and latent representations improved average task success by **11.2%** compared to using only pixel features, validating the benefit of hybrid visual features.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A hybrid visual representation, combining fine-grained pixel details with motion-aware latent features, significantly enhances performance in embodied action planning compared to relying on either representation alone.
> - Latent-space world models exhibit an inductive bias towards action-centric information, effectively encoding dynamic motion and geometric structures while potentially abstracting away task-irrelevant visual attributes like exact chromatic details.
> - Simple concatenation-based feature fusion with a residual integration mechanism effectively leverages the complementary strengths of pixel and latent representations, outperforming more complex methods like cross-attention for this specific task.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied action planning is a fundamental challenge in robotics, requiring systems to interpret visual observations and language instructions to generate precise robot actions. The field has evolved through several research paradigms, each with distinct advantages and limitations.

Early approaches primarily relied on imitation learning (IL), where robots learn from expert demonstration trajectories. While effective in specific scenarios, IL-based methods, such as RT-1 (Brohan et al., 2022) and Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2023), frequently exhibit limited generalization capabilities and struggle to adapt to novel environments or tasks unseen during training. They also face challenges in collecting diverse, high-quality demonstration data.

More recently, Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, exemplified by systems like Pi0 (Black et al., 2024), Octo (Team et al., 2024), and OpenVLA (Kim et al., 2024), have emerged. These models leverage large language models, vision encoders, and action decoders to improve complex task understanding and action prediction. Despite their advancements in understanding instructions and visual context, VLA models fundamentally retain an imitation learning paradigm. Consequently, they still depend on extensive, high-quality demonstration data and may encounter difficulties in achieving broad generalization, performing counterfactual reasoning, or handling tasks requiring elaborate planning.

A parallel and increasingly active research direction involves video-based world models (WMs) for action planning. This paradigm aims to pre-train models on large-scale video datasets to learn general physical dynamics, then map visual observations to robot actions. This approach offers advantages in data efficiency and holds potential for cross-domain generalization by learning rich video dynamics. Existing world model-based methods, such as VPP (Hu et al., 2024), Uni-Pi (Du et al., 2023), and GR-1 (Wu et al., 2023), typically involve two main strategies: either mapping predicted future state sequences to actions via an inverse dynamics model or using an action-conditioned world model to sample and evaluate trajectories.

However, current world model-based approaches face limitations primarily concerning their visual representation learning. Many methods, particularly those leveraging diffusion-based video generation models (e.g., Stable Video Diffusion), operate in pixel space. While these "pixel world models" are adept at preserving fine-grained appearance details, their representations often contain substantial redundant and action-irrelevant information (e.g., static backgrounds), which can obscure crucial motion cues and impede action decoding and generalization. In contrast, "latent world models" (e.g., Assran et al., 2025; Zhou et al., 2024) learn state transitions in a compressed feature space. These models typically emphasize motion-aware and temporally structured representations, aligning well with action planning by reducing redundancy. However, operating in a highly compressed space can suppress fine-grained visual details, limiting performance in tasks demanding precise spatial reasoning or intricate object interactions.

This work, MoWM, positions itself within the world model-based paradigm, directly addressing the limitations of existing visual representation learning strategies. It aims to bridge the gap between the detailed but redundant pixel-level representations and the motion-aware but potentially coarse latent-level representations. By proposing a hybrid framework, MoWM seeks to combine the complementary strengths of both types of world models, thereby enhancing the relevance of visual features for precise embodied action planning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of this research is to advance embodied action planning by enabling robots to generate precise executable actions from visual observations and language instructions more effectively than existing methods. The paper identifies a key bottleneck in current world model-based approaches: the limitations of their visual representation learning.

The motivation for proposing the Mixture-of-World-Models (MoWM) framework stems from a detailed analysis of the trade-offs inherent in current world model designs:

1. **Redundancy in Pixel-Space World Models:**
 - **Problem:** Many existing world models, particularly those based on video diffusion models, primarily operate in pixel space, aiming for pixel-level reconstruction. While this preserves fine-grained appearance details, the representations generated often contain significant visual redundancies. These include static background elements and other visual information that is irrelevant to the action planning task.
 - **Consequence:** This redundant information can obscure the motion cues that are critical for accurate action decoding. By treating all pixels uniformly, irrespective of their relevance to control, these models may struggle to generalize effectively to new scenarios where irrelevant background details change.

2. **Loss of Detail in Latent-Space World Models:**
 - **Problem:** An alternative approach involves latent world models, which learn state transitions within a highly compressed feature space. These models are generally more efficient and tend to capture motion-aware, temporally structured representations.
 - **Consequence:** The high level of compression in latent space can inadvertently suppress or discard fine-grained visual details. For tasks requiring precise manipulation, such as grasping small objects or navigating cluttered environments, these minute details are often critical for successful execution. The lack of such detail can limit the precision and overall performance of the robot.

Given these complementary limitations, the primary motivation for MoWM is to synergistically leverage the strengths of both pixel-space and latent-space representations. The goal is to develop a visual feature that simultaneously incorporates the motion awareness characteristic of latent models and the spatial precision and fine-grained details provided by pixel-based models. By fusing these hybrid representations, the authors aim to construct a feature space that emphasizes action-relevant visual details while mitigating the impact of visual redundancies.

Specifically, the key objectives articulated in the paper are:
- To propose a hybrid world model architecture, MoWM, that integrates motion-aware advantages of a latent world model with the low-level detail generation capabilities of a pixel-space world model.
- To explore and analyze effective fusion mechanisms for visual features derived from both pixel and latent space world models.
- To demonstrate, through extensive experimentation, the superiority of MoWM in terms of task success rate and generalization to unseen scenarios in embodied action planning.

The underlying motivation is to move beyond the limitations of single-modality visual representations in world models, creating a more robust and adaptable framework for embodied intelligence that can generate precise actions in complex, dynamic environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MoWM framework is structured as a two-stage process: first, the independent training of hybrid world models, and second, their integration into an end-to-end action planning module.

**Stage 1: Instruction-Conditioned Training of Hybrid World Models**
The initial stage focuses on training two complementary world models, both conditioned on natural language instructions and an initial observation, to predict future states in their respective representation spaces.

1. **Pixel-Space World Model:**
 - **Base Model:** The pixel world model is built upon Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) (Blattmann et al., 2023), which is a widely used video generation diffusion model.
 - **Training:** This base model is initially pre-trained on generic video datasets and subsequently fine-tuned on embodied domain datasets specific to robot manipulation.
 - **Text Conditioning:** Language instructions (l) are injected into the generation process via cross-attention mechanisms, guiding the model to generate future video sequences {x_t} given an initial frame x_0 and the instruction.
 - **Mechanism:** The model iteratively denoises a sequence of noisy latents {z_t}, learning a denoising function ϵ_θ that predicts the noise added to the latent representation at each time step.
 - **Loss Function:** The training objective minimizes an L2 loss: L_Pixel-WM = E_{t,x_0,l,ϵ}[||ϵ - ϵ_θ(z_t, x_0, l, t)||₂²].

2. **Latent-Space World Model:**
 - **Encoder:** A pre-trained visual encoder, specifically ViT-g from V-JEPA 2 (Assran et al., 2025), is utilized to tokenize each input video frame x_i into a sequence of visual tokens s_i.
 - **Model Architecture:** A transformer-based network, F(·), is designed to forecast future states within this compressed latent space. This model consists of multiple transformer blocks, each comprising an attention layer and a SwiGLU-activated feed-forward network.
 - **Input:** The model takes as input encoded text instruction tokens (c) and a sequence of state tokens from current and past frames ({s_j}).
 - **Prediction:** The model processes these inputs to predict the next state token, s_k+1.
 - **Training Strategy:** A teacher-forcing strategy is employed during training.
 - **Loss Function:** The training objective minimizes an L1 loss: L_Latent-WM = E_{k,c,s}[||F(c, {s_j}_{j≤k}) - s_k+1||₁].
 - **Training Alignment:** Both world models are trained on the same embodied datasets, ensuring identical temporal sampling intervals and action frequencies for aligned learning.

**Stage 2: End-to-End Action Planning via Mixture-of-World Models**
In the second stage, the trained world models are frozen, and their predicted features are combined to guide an action decoding module.

1. **Feature Extraction and Aggregation:**
 - **Pixel Features (Φ_pixel):** The pixel-space world model G(·) performs a single forward pass with an initial frame x_0 and language instruction l. Following VPP (Hu et al., 2024), it efficiently generates a multi-scale visual representation. Features {V_i} are extracted from multiple upsampling layers of the U-Net at different scales. These features are then bilinearly upsampled to a unified spatial dimension (h, w) and concatenated channel-wise to form Φ_pixel.
 - **Latent Features (Φ_latent):** The latent-space world model F(·) directly outputs its predicted feature sequence, Φ_latent, which represents the motion-aware information.

2. **Feature Alignment and Fusion:**
 - **Alignment:** Both Φ_pixel and Φ_latent are mapped to a shared embedding dimension (D) using distinct learned linear projection layers (W_pixel and W_latent), resulting in Φ'_pixel and Φ'_latent.
 - **Fusion Mechanism:** The aligned latent features (Φ'_latent) and pixel features (Φ'_pixel) are concatenated. This concatenated representation is then passed through another linear projection layer to yield the fused feature representation, Φ_fused.
 - **Residual Integration:** A learnable residual mechanism further integrates Φ_fused with Φ'_pixel to produce the final output visual feature, Φ_final = W_gate * Φ_fused + Φ'_pixel, where W_gate is a learnable gating matrix. This design aims to preserve fine-grained details while incorporating the higher-level semantic context from the fused representation.

3. **Action Decoding:**
 - **Module:** A Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2023) serves as the action decoding module.
 - **Conditioning:** The fused feature Φ_final acts as the condition, guiding the denoising process.
 - **Mechanism:** The denoiser ϵ_θ progressively refines an initially noisy action vector a_t (sampled from a normal distribution) over multiple steps to produce the final predicted action.
 - **Loss Function:** The denoising loss function is an L2 loss: L_denoise(ψ) = E_{a_0,ϵ,k}[||ϵ - ϵ_θ(a_t, Φ_final, t)||₂²].

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Datasets:** Evaluated on the CALVIN simulator (using the ABC→D split for generalization assessment) and a real-world AgileX platform for a challenging cloth-folding task (trained on 600 trajectories).
- **Baselines:** Compared against a range of methods including imitation learning-based (RT-1, Diffusion Policy, 3D Diffusor Actor), VLA-based (RoboFlamingo, 3D-VLA, OpenVLA, Pi0), and other world model-based approaches (Susie, Uni-Pi, GR-1, Vidman, VPP).
- **Implementation Details:** The latent world model used a 24-layer transformer (~400M parameters) based on ViT-g, trained on 4 H20 GPUs. The pixel world model was the SVD model from VPP. The end-to-end action planning module was trained on 4 NVIDIA H20 GPUs. A separate CNN decoder was trained to visualize latent states into RGB images for analysis.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the performance and characteristics of the MoWM framework:

**Quantitative Performance on CALVIN Benchmark:**
- **State-of-the-Art (SOTA) Performance:** MoWM achieved the highest task success rates on the CALVIN benchmark compared to various imitation learning, VLA-based, and world model-based baselines (Table 1).
- **Improvement Over Baselines:** MoWM demonstrated a 5.7% improvement in average task success rate compared to the most competitive VLA-based baseline (Pi0) and a 13.4% improvement compared to the most competitive world model-based baseline (VPP).
- **Long-Horizon Task Efficacy:** The method showed a notable advantage in longer-horizon tasks, achieving a 12.7% improvement in the 5th task success rate over the next best baseline. This suggests that the hybrid world model approach facilitates more effective long-range planning by mitigating the tendency of other methods to become trapped in local optima due to over-reliance on immediate observations.
- **Comparison of Paradigms:** Both VLA-based and world model-based approaches generally outperformed imitation learning methods, supporting the benefit of incorporating complex reasoning or future state prediction. While VLA and existing world model methods showed broadly comparable performance, the paper suggests that existing world models can be limited by the quality of their generated images. MoWM’s approach, which enhances visual features beyond pixel-level reconstruction, contributes to more accurate action planning.

**Qualitative Analysis of World Model Predictions:**
- **Plausible Future States:** Visualizations of future state predictions (Figure 3) from both the pixel-space and latent-space world models indicated their ability to generate plausible future states conditioned on text instructions. These predictions contained cues considered relevant for action decoding.
- **Feature Space Characteristics:** The reconstructed frames from the latent-space model, while faithfully preserving geometric structures and dynamic trajectories, exhibited chromatic deviations (e.g., altered table colors). This observation implies that the latent-space world model prioritizes the encoding of action-centric information and dynamic motion, potentially discarding task-irrelevant visual attributes or fine-grained chromatic details, which aligns with its motion-aware design. In contrast, the pixel world model preserves more scene details, but sometimes produces static frames lacking dynamic movement (Figure 5).

**Real-World Robot Manipulation Validation:**
- **AgileX Platform Performance:** MoWM was validated on a real-world AgileX platform for a challenging, long-horizon cloth-folding task (Figure 4).
- **Task Success:** The model successfully executed the instructed cloth-folding task, demonstrating its effectiveness in handling complex manipulation in real-world environments.
- **Inference Efficiency:** The system achieved an average inference latency of 110 ms for 50 steps, indicating practical applicability for robotic control.

**Ablation Study on Feature Fusion:**
- **Benefit of Hybrid Representations:** The ablation study (Table 2) confirmed that fusing pixel-level and latent representations consistently led to better performance than relying solely on pixel features. This supports the hypothesis that hybrid representations are beneficial for embodied action planning.
- **Fusion Mechanism Comparison:**
 - **Concatenation-based Fusion:** This approach, as implemented in MoWM, achieved the best overall performance, outperforming the pixel-feature-only baseline by an average of 11.2% in task success rate across all five stages. This suggests that direct concatenation is an effective method for integrating the complementary information from the two feature spaces.
 - **Cross-Attention-based Fusion:** This alternative fusion method performed worse than concatenation. The paper attributes this to two potential reasons: cross-attention may introduce additional optimization difficulties due to the need for fine-grained alignment between fundamentally different feature spaces, and aggressive remapping through attention might be unnecessary when pixel features already encode strong spatial structure.
- **Complementary Inductive Biases:** The analysis further reinforced that the pixel-space world model excels in preserving fine-grained spatial and appearance details, but introduces redundancy. The latent-space world model, conversely, emphasizes temporal dynamics and motion patterns. The fusion mechanism effectively highlights action-relevant regions and suppresses irrelevant content, resulting in representations that are both spatially precise and dynamically informative.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MoWM framework represents a notable contribution to the field of embodied action planning, primarily by addressing a fundamental challenge in visual representation learning for world models.

**Addressing Representation Limitations:**
The work highlights the inherent trade-offs in existing world model representations: pixel-based models offer detail but suffer from redundancy, while latent-based models provide motion awareness but can lack fine-grained precision. MoWM's core significance lies in demonstrating an effective method for combining these complementary strengths. By modulating pixel-space features with latent-space motion-aware representations, the framework creates a visual representation that is more targeted and informative for action decoding. This approach improves upon prior methods that rely solely on one type of representation.

**Advancing Embodied Action Planning Performance:**
The empirical results, particularly the achievement of state-of-the-art task success rates on the CALVIN benchmark and improved generalization capabilities, signify MoWM's practical effectiveness. Its enhanced performance in long-horizon tasks suggests a greater capacity for complex sequential reasoning and planning, which is a critical aspect of generalist robotics. The successful deployment on a real-world robot platform for a complex task like cloth folding further underscores the method's robustness and applicability beyond simulated environments.

**Providing Insights into Feature Spaces:**
The detailed analysis of the strengths and weaknesses of pixel-level and latent-level features, including the observation that latent features prioritize action-centric information by exhibiting chromatic deviations in reconstructed images, offers valuable insights for future research. This understanding can guide the design of subsequent visual representation learning architectures for embodied AI, potentially leading to more efficient and specialized models.

**Potential for Broader Impact:**
- **More Robust Robot Policies:** By providing a mechanism to generate more precise and relevant visual features, MoWM contributes to the development of more robust and reliable robot policies capable of operating in diverse and dynamic environments.
- **Enhanced Generalization:** The improved generalization to unseen scenarios is crucial for deploying robots in real-world settings where complete training data coverage is impractical.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework provides a foundation for exploring more sophisticated multi-modal representation learning. Future work could build upon this by investigating adaptive fusion strategies that dynamically weigh feature contributions based on task context or by scaling the framework to even larger and more diverse datasets for broader zero-shot transfer capabilities, as suggested by the authors. This could pave the way for more generalist robot policies.

In summary, MoWM offers a principled and empirically validated approach to integrate hybrid visual representations, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable in embodied action planning and providing a direction for the development of more capable and adaptable robotic systems.
