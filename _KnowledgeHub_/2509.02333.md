# DCPO: Dynamic Clipping Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Baichuan.inc developed Dynamic Clipping Policy Optimization (DCPO), a method to enhance Large Language Models using Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). DCPO achieved state-of-the-art mathematical reasoning performance across various Qwen2.5 models, improving average scores on benchmarks by up to 6.5 points compared to previous methods, while doubling training efficiency and significantly reducing token clipping.

## Problem

- Existing RLVR methods like GRPO suffer from 'zero gradients' and 'entropy collapse' when all responses in a batch have identical rewards, halting learning and limiting exploration.
- Prior approaches inefficiently utilize generated responses, discarding over half of them in some cases (e.g., GRPO), leading to prolonged training times and wasted computational resources.
- Fixed or uniformly asymmetric clipping bounds for token-level probability ratios (in GRPO, DAPO) are suboptimal, as they restrict exploration for low-probability, high-entropy tokens crucial for complex reasoning.

## Method

- Introduces ==Dynamic-Adaptive Clipping Bounds (DAC)== that vary with the old token probability, granting wider clipping for low-probability tokens to encourage greater policy exploration.
- Employs ==Smooth Advantage Standardization (SAS)== by computing advantage statistics cumulatively across all responses for a prompt, preventing zero advantages and reducing fluctuations.
- Proposes ==Only Token Mean (OTM) Loss==, which averages advantage-weighted token losses only within each individual response, maintaining relative advantage structure and avoiding dilution from batch-level averaging.

## Results

- DCPO achieved superior mathematical reasoning performance, improving average scores by up to **6.5** Avg@1 and **7.7** Avg@32 points on Qwen2.5-14B models over GRPO across various benchmarks.
- The method reduced the Token Clipping Ratio (TCR) by an order of magnitude compared to GRPO and DAPO, discarding fewer tokens and allowing more complete response sequences to contribute to updates.
- DCPO significantly improved the Response Utilization Ratio (RUR) to approximately **70%** (a **28%** absolute increase over GRPO), doubling training efficiency compared to DAPO by requiring **3 to 5 times** fewer generated responses for the same number of parameter updates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adaptive clipping, where bounds are dynamically adjusted based on token probabilities, is crucial for balanced exploration, allowing models to leverage rare but informative tokens without destabilizing updates.
> - Aggregating reward statistics cumulatively across training steps, rather than just within the current batch, prevents premature entropy collapse and ensures more consistent gradient signals for policy updates.
> - The way loss is aggregated (per token within a response) significantly impacts training stability and efficiency, with response-centric averaging preserving the relative importance of individual response advantages.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the rapidly evolving field of enhancing Large Language Models (LLMs) through Reinforcement Learning (RL), specifically focusing on **Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)**. RLVR is a powerful paradigm that leverages rule-based outcome rewards to fine-tune LLMs, particularly for tasks requiring precise reasoning, such as mathematics and coding. Unlike Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), which relies on human preferences, RLVR benefits from objective, verifiable criteria, making it highly suitable for domains where correctness can be programmatically checked.

The broader landscape of RL for LLMs has seen the emergence of several key algorithms, notably the **GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization)** family (DeepSeek-AI et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025; Yuan et al., 2025; Zheng et al., 2025). GRPO, while robust and widely validated, has known limitations that subsequent research has aimed to address. These limitations include "entropy collapse," where advantage values decay to zero, leading to ineffective gradient updates and a restricted exploration of diverse response spaces. The fixed clipping bounds for token-level probability ratios in GRPO can discard valuable learning signals, especially from rare but informative tokens, thereby limiting the model's ability to explore novel reasoning paths.

In response to these challenges, **DAPO (Dynamic sAmpling Policy Optimization)** (Yu et al., 2025) was introduced. DAPO attempts to mitigate entropy collapse by relaxing the upper clipping bound (Clip-Higher strategy) and employing "Dynamic Sampling" to filter out samples with uniform rewards, ensuring that each batch contributes valid gradients. However, DAPO itself introduces new inefficiencies, such as reduced sampling efficiency due to discarding responses with identical rewards, leading to slower training convergence. Moreover, its fixed asymmetric clipping bounds still fail to account for the token-specific optimal clipping needs, leading to a "token clipping deficit." Another recent method, **GSPO (Group Sequence Policy Optimization)** (Zheng et al., 2025), shifts from token-wise to sequence-wise clipping, aiming for greater stability but potentially wasting more informative tokens by discarding entire sequences.

DCPO enters this landscape by building upon the strengths of these predecessors while explicitly addressing their identified shortcomings. It aims to provide a more efficient, stable, and explorative RLVR framework. By introducing dynamic clipping and smooth advantage standardization, DCPO seeks to overcome the zero-gradient problem and the inefficiencies of fixed or response-level-discarding clipping strategies. This positions DCPO as an evolution in the RLVR space, striving to achieve state-of-the-art performance by optimizing the core mechanisms of policy update and reward signal utilization, thereby allowing LLMs to achieve superior reasoning capabilities with improved data and training efficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of the "DCPO: Dynamic Clipping Policy Optimization" paper are to overcome critical limitations in existing Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods for large language models, specifically GRPO, DAPO, and GSPO. The motivation stems from observed inefficiencies and performance plateaus in these prior approaches.

The core problems DCPO aims to address are:

1. **Zero Gradients and Entropy Collapse:** Existing methods, particularly GRPO, frequently suffer from "zero gradients" when all generated responses for a given prompt in a batch yield identical rewards. This leads to ineffective parameter updates, halting learning for those prompts, and contributing to "entropy collapse," where the policy loses its ability to explore diverse solutions.
2. **Ineffective Utilization of Generated Responses:** The problem of zero gradients directly results in the underutilization of generated responses. Methods like DAPO attempt to address this by dynamically sampling or discarding responses, which, while ensuring non-zero gradients, leads to significant sampling inefficiency and prolonged training times.
3. **Fixed Clipping Bounds for Token-Level Probability Ratios:** Previous approaches (e.g., GRPO, DAPO) use fixed or uniformly asymmetric clipping bounds for token-level probability ratios. The paper argues that these fixed bounds are suboptimal because the ideal clipping range varies significantly across tokens based on their prior probabilities. This fixed approach can overly restrict exploration for low-probability, high-entropy tokens, which are crucial for developing complex reasoning capabilities, while potentially being too permissive for high-probability tokens.
4. **Batch-Level Averaging Issues:** Standard loss functions (e.g., Sequence-Level Mean in GRPO, Token-Level Mean in DAPO) can dilute the relative advantage structure among responses to the same prompt or distort the influence of responses based on their length, leading to suboptimal or unstable updates.

To counter these issues, DCPO is motivated by the need for:

- **Enhanced Token-Level Exploration:** By dynamically adjusting clipping bounds, DCPO aims to provide more flexibility for low-probability tokens to explore novel sequences, which are often key to emergent reasoning abilities.
- **Improved Response-Level Data Utilization:** Through a more robust advantage standardization mechanism, DCPO seeks to ensure that a higher proportion of generated responses contribute meaningfully to policy updates, reducing waste and improving training efficiency.
- **Greater Training Stability and Efficiency:** By addressing the root causes of zero gradients and suboptimal clipping, DCPO aims to create a more stable and faster convergence trajectory for RLVR training, reducing GPU hours and accelerating LLM capability development.

In essence, DCPO's objectives are to develop a more adaptive and efficient policy optimization framework that leads to superior reasoning capabilities in LLMs by intelligently managing exploration, exploitation, and data utilization within the RLVR paradigm.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DCPO introduces three key innovations: **Dynamic-Adaptive Clipping Bounds (DAC)**, **Smooth Advantage Standardization (SAS)**, and an **Only Token Mean (OTM) Loss** function, designed to address the limitations of previous RLVR methods.

#### 3.1. Dynamic-Adaptive Clipping Bounds (DAC)

The paper identifies that fixed clipping bounds in methods like GRPO (e.g., $|r(x) - 1| \leq \epsilon$) are suboptimal. While DAPO introduces asymmetric fixed bounds, the core issue of a non-adaptive constraint persists. DCPO proposes **Dynamic-Adaptive Clipping (DAC)** by introducing probability-dependent constraints. Instead of directly clipping the ratio $r(x) = p(x)/q(x)$, DCPO constrains the term $|(r(x) - 1)p(x)| \leq \epsilon$. By substituting $p(x) = r(x)q(x)$ and solving for $r(x)$, the method derives dynamic-adaptive clipping bounds that vary with the old token probability $q(x)$ and hyperparameters $\epsilon_{\text{low}}$ and $\epsilon_{\text{high}}$:

$0.5 + \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\max \left(1 - \frac{4\epsilon_{\text{low}}}{q(x)}, 0\right)} \leq r(x) \leq 0.5 + \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1 + \frac{4\epsilon_{\text{high}}}{q(x)}}$

This formulation ensures that tokens with lower prior probabilities ($q(x)$) are granted wider clipping bounds, thereby allowing greater policy exploration in these low-probability regions. This is crucial because low-probability, high-entropy tokens are often associated with emergent reasoning capabilities. To prevent excessive and destabilizing clipping bounds, especially for extremely low $q(x)$, DCPO also incorporates a dual clipping mechanism, capping the maximum ratio for both positive and negative advantages at 10, similar to dual clipping methods (Ye et al., 2020). The hyperparameters $\epsilon_{\text{low}}$ and $\epsilon_{\text{high}}$ are calibrated to align with traditional fixed clipping at specific boundary points, making the adaptation smooth and grounded.

#### 3.2. Smooth Advantage Standardization (SAS)

Existing RLVR methods (GRPO, DAPO, GSPO) compute advantages by standardizing rewards $R^i_j$ solely against the mean ($\mu^i_{\text{new}}$) and standard deviation ($\sigma^i_{\text{new}}$) of rewards within the *current batch* of generated responses for a given prompt. This "step-specific standardization" leads to zero advantages if all rewards in a batch are identical, preventing parameter updates despite potentially valuable reasoning trajectories. It also causes high fluctuations due to small sample sizes.

DCPO addresses this with **Smooth Advantage Standardization (SAS)**, motivated by the idea that rewards for a prompt are drawn from a broader global distribution over training. SAS computes advantage statistics not just from the current step but **cumulatively across all responses generated so far for the same prompt**. This cumulative mean ($\mu^i_{\text{total}}$) and standard deviation ($\sigma^i_{\text{total}}$) prevent advantages from zeroing out when within-batch rewards are uniform.

Furthermore, SAS introduces two smoothing functions, $\widehat{SA}^i_{\text{new},j}$ and $\widehat{SA}^i_{\text{total},j}$, which are weighted averages between the step-specific and cumulative standardized advantages. These weights dynamically change with the training step $i$. The final advantage $\widehat{A}^i_j$ is then defined as the smoothed advantage with the *smaller absolute value* (Equation 7), which helps to reduce fluctuations and improve training stability by balancing adaptation to recent policy outputs with the overall cumulative reward distribution. This ensures that even responses with identical rewards in a given step can contribute to model updates, utilizing useful learning signals more efficiently.

#### 3.3. Only Token Mean (OTM) Loss

DCPO also re-evaluates the loss function. GRPO uses a Sequence-Level Mean (SLM) loss (Equation 12) which averages over responses, potentially weakening the impact of standardized advantages. DAPO uses a Token-Level Mean (TLM) loss (Equation 13) which averages over all tokens in the batch, leading to disproportionate influence from longer responses despite their individual advantage values.

DCPO proposes **Only Token Mean (OTM) loss** (Equation 8). Instead of averaging over an entire batch or normalizing by total token count across the batch, OTM averages the advantage-weighted token losses *only within each individual response* ($1/|o_i| \sum_{t=1}^{|o_i|} \dots$). This approach maintains the relative advantage structure among responses to the same prompt, avoids the dilution effect of batch-level averaging, and distributes importance equally to each token within its respective response. Importantly, DCPO's loss function does not include a KL divergence regularization term, aligning with DAPO and GSPO.

By combining DAC for flexible exploration, SAS for stable and efficient advantage calculation, and OTM for preserving per-response importance, DCPO constructs a novel and robust RLVR pipeline for enhancing LLM reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DCPO demonstrates state-of-the-art performance and significant improvements in efficiency and stability across various metrics and models.

#### 4.1. Main Performance Results (Table 1)

DCPO consistently achieves superior or comparable performance across four mathematical reasoning benchmarks (MATH500, AMC23, AIME24, AIME25) and four different Qwen2.5 models (1.5B, 3B, 7B, 14B).
- **Overall Superiority:** DCPO achieved the highest average scores across models. For instance, on Qwen2.5-Math-7B, DCPO scored (57.1/45.2) Avg@1/Avg@32, surpassing GRPO (+4.0/+3.6), DAPO (+3.3/+2.8), and GSPO (+1.9/+1.9). On the largest Qwen2.5-14B model, DCPO delivered (53.2/39.0), outperforming GRPO (+6.5/+7.7), DAPO (+1.3/+0.1), and GSPO (+4.2/+5.5).
- **Robustness on AIME (Avg@32):** DCPO showed particularly strong gains on the challenging AIME benchmarks under 32-time sampling (Avg@32), indicating improved robustness and reliability of correct generations. For example, DCPO-7B scored 38.8 on AIME24-Avg@32, significantly higher than GRPO (32.1), DAPO (31.6), and GSPO (34.9). DCPO-14B reached 19.0 on AIME25-Avg@32, surpassing GRPO (10.5), DAPO (15.3), and GSPO (9.9). These results highlight DCPO's ability to optimize both reward fidelity and exploration under high-entropy decoding.

#### 4.2. Token Clipping Ratio (TCR) Analysis (Figure 1)

TCR measures the proportion of tokens excluded from policy updates due to clipping.
- **Order-of-Magnitude Reduction:** DCPO maintained a remarkably stable and an order of magnitude lower TCR compared to GRPO and DAPO across all models and training stages. While GRPO and DAPO exhibited significant fluctuations and upward trends in TCR (especially for DAPO), DCPO's TCR remained consistently low.
- **Enhanced Exploration:** This low and stable TCR indicates that DCPO discards fewer tokens, allowing a greater portion of complete response sequences to contribute to updates. This frees up more space for the model to explore the diversity of rare, high-entropy tokens, which are crucial for developing reasoning capabilities.

#### 4.3. Response Utilization Ratio (RUR) Analysis (Table 2, Figure 2)

RUR is the percentage of generated responses with non-zero advantages that participate in policy updates.
- **Substantial Improvement:** GRPO's average RUR was only 44.6%, meaning more than half of generated responses were discarded. In contrast, DCPO achieved an average RUR of approximately 70% after the first epoch, representing a 28% absolute increase and 64% relative improvement over GRPO.
- **Stable and High Utilization:** While GRPO's RUR sharply decreased during training (e.g., to 30% for Qwen2.5-Math-7B), DCPO consistently maintained a high RUR around 70%. This demonstrates DCPO's superior data efficiency and its ability to fully utilize sampled data to provide more exploration space.
- **Training Efficiency:** DCPO doubled training efficiency compared to DAPO, requiring 3 to 5 times fewer generated responses to achieve the same number of parameter updates, leading to significantly reduced GPU hours. DAPO's data utilization decreased with model size, sometimes dropping below 30% for Qwen2.5-14B.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies (Figure 3)

Ablation studies on Qwen2.5-Math-7B, using Avg@32, confirmed the positive contribution of each DCPO component:
- **Only-Token-Mean (OTM) Loss:** Outperformed baselines when GRPO aggregated loss within a single response.
- **Smoothed Advantage Standardization (SAS):** Significantly improved GRPO's performance, demonstrating the benefits of aggregating reward statistics across training steps.
- **Dynamic Adaptive Clipping (DAC):** Yielded significant improvements over GRPO, DAPO, and GSPO, exhibiting greater stability.
- **Full DCPO (OTM + SAS + DAC):** The combination achieved the best and most stable overall results, significantly outperforming all baselines and showing continued improvement in later training stages where baselines plateaued.

#### 4.5. Entropy Trend (Figure 7)

- GRPO exhibited sharp entropy decay, indicating a rapid loss of policy randomness and limiting exploration.
- DAPO maintained high entropy with large fluctuations, compromising stability.
- DCPO consistently maintained a moderate and stable entropy level, promoting both convergence and sufficient exploration, which is particularly beneficial for larger models. This balanced entropy regulation mechanism contributes to DCPO's superior inference performance and robust policy learning.

In summary, DCPO's innovations lead to a more efficient, stable, and powerful RLVR framework, enabling LLMs to achieve higher reasoning capabilities by effectively leveraging generated data and managing policy exploration.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Dynamic Clipping Policy Optimization (DCPO) represents a significant advancement in the field of Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for Large Language Models (LLMs), with substantial potential impact on the development and capabilities of AI reasoning systems.

**1. Enhanced LLM Reasoning Capabilities:** The most direct impact of DCPO is its demonstrated ability to push the state-of-the-art in LLM mathematical reasoning. By achieving superior performance on challenging benchmarks like AIME, particularly with high-entropy sampling (Avg@32), DCPO shows that it can effectively train LLMs to generate more robust, diverse, and accurate solutions to complex problems. This directly translates to LLMs that are more reliable and intelligent in domains requiring logical inference and problem-solving, such such as mathematics, coding, and potentially scientific discovery.

**2. Improved Data and Training Efficiency:** DCPO's innovations directly address critical inefficiencies in prior RLVR methods.
 - The **Smooth Advantage Standardization (SAS)** mechanism drastically improves the **Response Utilization Ratio (RUR)**, ensuring that a much higher percentage of generated responses contribute to policy updates. This reduces wasted computation and makes the learning process more sample-efficient.
 - The paper explicitly states that DCPO **doubled the training efficiency over DAPO**, requiring significantly fewer generated responses and GPU hours for the same number of parameter updates. This is a crucial factor for industrial applications and academic research alike, as training LLMs is notoriously resource-intensive. Faster and more efficient training cycles allow for quicker iteration, experimentation, and deployment of more capable models.

**3. More Stable and Principled Policy Optimization:** The **Dynamic-Adaptive Clipping (DAC)** strategy and the overall design of DCPO lead to a more stable training process.
 - The order-of-magnitude reduction in the **Token Clipping Ratio (TCR)** signifies that DCPO is more effective at leveraging informative tokens, particularly those with low probabilities that are key to emergent reasoning. By adaptively expanding the update interval for high-entropy tokens, DCPO encourages broader and more meaningful exploration without destabilizing updates.
 - The sustained, moderate entropy levels observed in DCPO's training, contrasted with the entropy collapse of GRPO or high fluctuations of DAPO, indicate a better balance between exploration and exploitation. This stability is critical for reliably scaling RL to very large models.

**4. Broader Applicability and Future Research Directions:** The principles introduced by DCPO—dynamic clipping based on token probabilities and cumulative-smooth advantage standardization—are generalizable. The paper concludes by mentioning future work on extending DCPO to other domains like code generation and semantic reasoning. This suggests that the core innovations could benefit a wider range of LLM applications where verifiable rewards are available, potentially leading to a new generation of more capable and domain-specific AI assistants.

In essence, DCPO offers a more sophisticated and effective approach to fine-tuning LLMs with verifiable rewards. Its impact will likely be seen in the development of more powerful, efficient, and robust reasoning LLMs, accelerating progress in AI systems that can tackle complex intellectual challenges with greater accuracy and less computational overhead.
