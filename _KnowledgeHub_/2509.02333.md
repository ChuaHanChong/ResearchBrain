# DCPO: Dynamic Clipping Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Baichuan.inc developed Dynamic Clipping Policy Optimization (DCPO), a method to enhance Large Language Models using Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). DCPO achieved state-of-the-art mathematical reasoning performance across various Qwen2.5 models, improving average scores on benchmarks by up to 6.5 points compared to previous methods, while doubling training efficiency and significantly reducing token clipping.

## Problem

- Existing RLVR methods like GRPO suffer from 'zero gradients' and 'entropy collapse' when all responses in a batch have identical rewards, halting learning and limiting exploration.
- Prior approaches inefficiently utilize generated responses, discarding over half of them in some cases (e.g., GRPO), leading to prolonged training times and wasted computational resources.
- Fixed or uniformly asymmetric clipping bounds for token-level probability ratios (in GRPO, DAPO) are suboptimal, as they restrict exploration for low-probability, high-entropy tokens crucial for complex reasoning.

## Method

- Introduces ==Dynamic-Adaptive Clipping Bounds (DAC)== that vary with the old token probability, granting wider clipping for low-probability tokens to encourage greater policy exploration.
- Employs ==Smooth Advantage Standardization (SAS)== by computing advantage statistics cumulatively across all responses for a prompt, preventing zero advantages and reducing fluctuations.
- Proposes ==Only Token Mean (OTM) Loss==, which averages advantage-weighted token losses only within each individual response, maintaining relative advantage structure and avoiding dilution from batch-level averaging.

## Results

- DCPO achieved superior mathematical reasoning performance, improving average scores by up to **6.5** Avg@1 and **7.7** Avg@32 points on Qwen2.5-14B models over GRPO across various benchmarks.
- The method reduced the Token Clipping Ratio (TCR) by an order of magnitude compared to GRPO and DAPO, discarding fewer tokens and allowing more complete response sequences to contribute to updates.
- DCPO significantly improved the Response Utilization Ratio (RUR) to approximately **70%** (a **28%** absolute increase over GRPO), doubling training efficiency compared to DAPO by requiring **3 to 5 times** fewer generated responses for the same number of parameter updates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adaptive clipping, where bounds are dynamically adjusted based on token probabilities, is crucial for balanced exploration, allowing models to leverage rare but informative tokens without destabilizing updates.
> - Aggregating reward statistics cumulatively across training steps, rather than just within the current batch, prevents premature entropy collapse and ensures more consistent gradient signals for policy updates.
> - The way loss is aggregated (per token within a response) significantly impacts training stability and efficiency, with response-centric averaging preserving the relative importance of individual response advantages.
