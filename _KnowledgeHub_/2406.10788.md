# Physically Embodied Gaussian Splatting: A Realtime Correctable World Model for Robotics

> [!summary] Summary
> Physically Embodied Gaussian Splatting develops a real-time, self-correcting world model for robotics by coupling 3D Gaussians for visual representation with particles for physics simulation. This approach tracks and predicts dynamic scenes at 30 Hz, achieving lower tracking error than physics-only or visual-only baselines in diverse manipulation and interaction scenarios.

## Problem

- Traditional robotics world models struggle to bridge the gap between accurate visual representation and physics-based reasoning, often lacking real-time self-correction.
- Existing neural scene representations (e.g., NeRF, D3DGS) are either computationally intensive or lack predictive physical understanding.
- Physics-only simulations accumulate error over time without visual feedback, while visual tracking alone struggles with occlusions or rapid movements.

## Method

- A **Gaussian-Particle** representation is introduced, coupling ==3D Gaussians== for visual rendering with ==particles== for ==physics simulation==.
- A continuous ==prediction-correction loop== is employed: ==Position-Based Dynamics (PBD)== predicts particle movement, followed by ==visual optimization== to correct Gaussian positions based on camera observations.
- ==Visual forces== are computed from ==photometric error== during visual optimization and applied back to the particles, driving corrections into the physics simulation.

## Results

- The system operates at **30 Hz** using three cameras, enabling real-time world modeling for dynamic scenarios.
- Demonstrates significantly lower tracking error compared to physics-only simulations, Dynamic 3D Gaussians (D3DGS), and CoTracker baselines across various simulated and real-world tasks.
- Successfully handles diverse dynamic scenarios including object manipulation, falling, picking up, and deformable objects like ropes in both simulated and real environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The dual Gaussian-Particle representation allows leveraging both visual accuracy of 3D Gaussians and the predictive power of particle-based physics.
> - Real-time visual feedback via "visual forces" enables continuous correction of physics simulations, ensuring the world model remains synchronized with the actual environment.
> - Combining real-time Gaussian Splatting with efficient Position-Based Dynamics facilitates practical, high-frequency world modeling for dynamic scenes.
