# Deep Reinforcement Learning-based Large-scale Robot Exploration

> [!summary] Summary
> A DRL-based reactive planner from NUS efficiently solves large-scale autonomous robot exploration problems by implicitly predicting unknown areas. This approach achieves 12% better distance efficiency and 60% faster planning times compared to state-of-the-art conventional planners like TARE in large simulated environments, and successfully explores real-world indoor spaces without additional training.

## Problem

- Existing conventional planners, even hierarchical ones, make myopic decisions based solely on partial maps, struggling to predict unknown environmental structures for long-term efficiency.
- Learning-based (DRL) exploration methods were largely confined to small-scale environments and often underperformed compared to advanced conventional planners in large settings.
- Explicitly modeling the stochastic transition of unknown areas in Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) formulations for traditional exploration is highly challenging and unstable.

## Method

- Frames the exploration problem as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) on a dense collision-free graph, enhanced with utility and guidepost features for informative input.
- Employs an attention-based neural network as the policy, trained with the Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) algorithm, leveraging privileged learning where the critic network accesses ground truth information during training for stable learning.
- Introduces a graph rarefaction algorithm to generate sparse, informative graphs, enabling the model trained on small environments to scale and generalize to large-scale exploration tasks.

## Results

- Achieved **11%** better exploration efficiency (path length) in small-scale environments compared to TARE Local and Utility baselines, with privileged learning improving performance by **7%** over a vanilla DRL model.
- Outperformed state-of-the-art TARE in large-scale Gazebo simulations, showing **12%** better distance efficiency and **6%** better time efficiency, while being **60%** faster in per-step planning time.
- Demonstrated successful and efficient real-world exploration in a cluttered indoor laboratory using a physical robot, without additional training, validating robust transferability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Providing the critic with ground truth information during DRL training (privileged learning) significantly stabilizes value estimation, leading to more robust and efficient policy learning for exploration.
> - Attention mechanisms within a DRL framework can enable implicit prediction of unknown environmental structures from partial observations, leading to more non-myopic exploration paths.
> - Intelligent graph sparsification (rarefaction) is effective for scaling DRL models, trained on smaller datasets, to vastly larger and more complex environments without retraining.
