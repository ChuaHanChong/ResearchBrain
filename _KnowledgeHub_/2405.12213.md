# Octo: An Open-Source Generalist Robot Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> Octo introduces an open-source, transformer-based generalist robot policy that achieves strong zero-shot control and state-of-the-art data-efficient finetuning across diverse robot embodiments and tasks. It is trained on the largest available robot manipulation dataset (800k trajectories from Open X-Embodiment), supporting flexible task and observation definitions.

## Problem

- Existing generalist robot policies often lack flexibility in input observations, constraining users to predefined sensory inputs.
- Many prior generalist models offer ineffective finetuning support, limiting their adaptation to entirely new domains or observation/action spaces.
- The most capable generalist robot policies are typically proprietary and not publicly accessible, hindering widespread research and deployment.

## Method

- **Octo** employs a modular, ==transformer-first architecture== with a ==conditional denoising diffusion process== for action prediction, enabling flexible input observations (language, goal images, various cameras) and action spaces.
- It is pretrained on **800,000** trajectories from the ==Open X-Embodiment dataset==, carefully curated and augmented for diversity and scale.
- The entire framework, including pretrained model checkpoints (Octo-Small, Octo-Base), training pipelines, and data loaders, is released as open-source.

## Results

- Octo (**93M parameters**) achieved a **29%** higher average success rate than RT-1-X on language-conditioned tasks across multiple robot platforms, performing competitively with the much larger RT-2-X (**55 billion parameters**).
- It significantly outperformed training from scratch and state-of-the-art visual representation finetuning (VC-1) by an average of **52%** across six diverse finetuning setups.
- Finetuning to new domains (e.g., force-torque inputs, joint position control, bimanual robots) required only **~100** in-domain demonstrations and less than **5 hours** on a single NVIDIA A5000 GPU.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A "transformer-first" ViT architecture substantially outperforms ResNet-based models when trained on large, diverse robot data mixtures.
> - Performance of generalist policies directly scales with the diversity and quantity of the training data, with broader data leading to better generalization.
> - Conditional diffusion decoding is superior to MSE or discretized action prediction for continuous control, effectively modeling multi-modal action distributions.
