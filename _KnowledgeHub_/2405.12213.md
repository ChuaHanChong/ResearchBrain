# Octo: An Open-Source Generalist Robot Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> Octo introduces an open-source, transformer-based generalist robot policy that achieves strong zero-shot control and state-of-the-art data-efficient finetuning across diverse robot embodiments and tasks. It is trained on the largest available robot manipulation dataset (800k trajectories from Open X-Embodiment), supporting flexible task and observation definitions.

## Problem

- Existing generalist robot policies often lack flexibility in input observations, constraining users to predefined sensory inputs.
- Many prior generalist models offer ineffective finetuning support, limiting their adaptation to entirely new domains or observation/action spaces.
- The most capable generalist robot policies are typically proprietary and not publicly accessible, hindering widespread research and deployment.

## Method

- **Octo** employs a modular, ==transformer-first architecture== with a ==conditional denoising diffusion process== for action prediction, enabling flexible input observations (language, goal images, various cameras) and action spaces.
- It is pretrained on **800,000** trajectories from the ==Open X-Embodiment dataset==, carefully curated and augmented for diversity and scale.
- The entire framework, including pretrained model checkpoints (Octo-Small, Octo-Base), training pipelines, and data loaders, is released as open-source.

## Results

- Octo (**93M parameters**) achieved a **29%** higher average success rate than RT-1-X on language-conditioned tasks across multiple robot platforms, performing competitively with the much larger RT-2-X (**55 billion parameters**).
- It significantly outperformed training from scratch and state-of-the-art visual representation finetuning (VC-1) by an average of **52%** across six diverse finetuning setups.
- Finetuning to new domains (e.g., force-torque inputs, joint position control, bimanual robots) required only **~100** in-domain demonstrations and less than **5 hours** on a single NVIDIA A5000 GPU.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A "transformer-first" ViT architecture substantially outperforms ResNet-based models when trained on large, diverse robot data mixtures.
> - Performance of generalist policies directly scales with the diversity and quantity of the training data, with broader data leading to better generalization.
> - Conditional diffusion decoding is superior to MSE or discretized action prediction for continuous control, effectively modeling multi-modal action distributions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning has traditionally relied on training policies from scratch for specific robots and tasks. This approach is data-intensive, requires substantial effort for each new task, and often results in policies with narrow generalization capabilities. The broader research landscape, especially in areas like natural language processing (NLP) and computer vision (CV), has seen a paradigm shift towards "foundation models" – large, pretrained models that can be finetuned for various downstream tasks with minimal data. This success has inspired a drive to develop analogous "general-purpose robot models."

This paper explicitly positions itself within the emerging domain of "generalist robot policies" (GRPs) or "robotic foundation models," which aim to map robot observations directly to actions and exhibit zero-shot or few-shot generalization. Prior works, such as GNM for navigation, RoboCat for goal-conditioned tasks, and RT-X/RT-1/RT-2 for language-conditioned manipulation, have made significant strides in this direction. RT-X, for instance, used a subset of the Open X-Embodiment dataset for language-conditioned manipulation across five robot embodiments. RT-2-X further pushed the boundaries by incorporating vision-language models for web-knowledge transfer.

However, the authors identify several critical limitations of existing GRPs that Octo aims to address:
1. **Lack of Flexibility in Input Observations:** Many previous models constrain users to a predefined, often restrictive, set of sensory inputs (e.g., a single camera stream), making them less adaptable to diverse real-world robot setups.
2. **Ineffective Finetuning Support:** Existing GRPs often lack robust mechanisms for efficient adaptation or finetuning to entirely new domains, observation spaces, or action spaces. This limits their applicability beyond the pretraining distribution.
3. **Accessibility Issues:** The largest and most capable GRPs (e.g., RT-2-X) are typically not available to the general public, hindering widespread research, reproducibility, and deployment.

Octo directly tackles these limitations by introducing a novel, highly flexible architecture designed for efficient finetuning and by making the entire model, training pipeline, and data open-source. It builds upon foundational concepts like transformer architectures, denoising diffusion objectives for action decoding, and action chunking, but critically integrates them into a unique combination optimized for cross-embodiment generalization and adaptability. Furthermore, by training on an even larger and more diverse dataset (800k trajectories from Open X-Embodiment, compared to 350k for RT-X), Octo pushes the boundaries of data scale in robot manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind Octo is to overcome the limitations of traditional robotic learning by creating a flexible and widely applicable generalist robot policy. Inspired by the success of foundation models in other AI domains, the authors aim to transform robotic learning from training policies from scratch to efficiently finetuning a powerful base model.

The key objectives of the Octo project are multifaceted:

1. **Develop an Open-Source Generalist Robot Policy:** The primary objective is to create and release a transformer-based policy, named Octo, that is fully open-source. This includes the model checkpoints, the entire training pipeline, and compatible data loaders. This addresses the critical need for accessible foundation models in robotics.
2. **Achieve Versatility in Task and Observation Definitions:** Octo is designed to support flexible task definitions, allowing instructions via language commands or goal images. It also aims to accommodate flexible observation spaces, including various camera configurations (e.g., wrist and third-person cameras) and proprioceptive feedback.
3. **Enable Efficient Finetuning to New Domains:** A core objective is to ensure that Octo can be quickly and effectively finetuned to new robot setups with novel sensory inputs, action spaces, or even different robot morphologies, using only a small amount of in-domain data and accessible compute. This is crucial for its "generalist" claim.
4. **Leverage Large-Scale, Diverse Data:** The policy is motivated by the hypothesis that large-scale pretraining on diverse robot data can lead to robust generalization. Thus, a key objective is to train Octo on the largest and most diverse robot manipulation dataset available.
5. **Serve as a Versatile Policy Initialization:** The goal is for Octo to act as a strong initialization for learning new tasks and controlling new robots, significantly reducing the data and computational resources typically required.
6. **Guide Future Research:** Through detailed ablations of design choices, the authors aim to provide empirical insights into what factors (architecture, data distribution, policy formulation) are most important for building effective generalist robot models, thereby guiding future research efforts.

In essence, Octo seeks to be the "launchpad" for robotics researchers and practitioners, offering a powerful, adaptable, and accessible foundation model to accelerate advancements in robot manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Octo model is a transformer-based policy designed for flexibility and scalability, comprising three main components: input tokenizers, a transformer backbone, and readout heads.

**A. Architecture:**
- **Input Tokenizers:**
 - **Language Inputs:** Utilizes a pretrained `t5-base` (111M parameters) transformer model to tokenize language instructions into a sequence of language embedding tokens.
 - **Image Observations and Goals:** Employs a shallow convolutional neural network (CNN) stack, followed by splitting into sequences of flattened patches (16x16 pixels). This "transformer-first" approach places most of the model's parameters and computational load on the transformer backbone, unlike prior designs that rely on large ResNet encoders.
 - **Proprioception:** While explored, initial findings on proprioceptive inputs suggested "causal confusion," leading to its exclusion from the main design, though the architecture could accommodate it.
- **Transformer Backbone:**
 - Processes a unified sequence of task and observation tokens.
 - Uses a block-wise masked attention pattern, allowing observation tokens to attend causally to past tokens and task tokens. Non-existent tokens (e.g., no language instruction) are masked out.
 - This modular design is critical for flexibility, enabling the addition or removal of observation types, task specifications, and even action spaces during finetuning without modifying pretrained parameters. This is achieved by adding new positional embeddings, lightweight encoders, or specific heads as needed.
- **Readout Heads and Action Prediction:**
 - Learned "readout tokens" are inserted into the transformer sequence, serving as compact vector embeddings of the processed input.
 - A lightweight "action head" is applied to these readout tokens. This head implements a conditional denoising diffusion process to predict a "chunk" of several consecutive actions, allowing for continuous and multi-modal action distributions. This contrasts with simpler MSE loss or discretized action prediction methods.

**B. Training Data:**
- Octo is pretrained on a curated subset of **800,000 robot trajectories** from the **Open X-Embodiment dataset**, making it the largest robot manipulation dataset used for pretraining to date.
- This dataset comprises 25 distinct datasets, encompassing a variety of tasks, robot embodiments, and scenes.
- **Data Curation and Augmentation:**
 - Datasets without image streams or those not using delta end-effector control were removed.
 - Repetitive, low-resolution, or excessively niche datasets were also filtered.
 - Diverse datasets were up-weighted, and some repetitive ones were down-weighted to balance content.
 - Missing camera channels were zero-padded, and gripper action spaces were standardized.
 - **Hindsight Goal Relabeling** was used for goal image conditioning.
 - Standard image data augmentations were applied.
 - Language instructions or goal images were randomly zeroed out per example to enable conditioning on either modality, helping the model learn from self-supervised visual observations.

**C. Training Objective:**
- The primary training objective is a **conditional diffusion decoding** (DDPM) head for predicting continuous, multi-modal action distributions. This objective allows for a single forward pass of the transformer backbone, with multi-step denoising performed efficiently within the small diffusion head.

**D. Training Details:**
- **Model Variants:** Two sizes were trained: Octo-Small (27M parameters, similar to ViT-S) and Octo-Base (93M parameters, similar to ViT-B).
- **Optimizer and Schedule:** AdamW optimizer, inverse square root decay learning rate schedule with linear warm-up.
- **Computational Resources:** Octo-Base was trained for 300k steps with a batch size of 2048 on a TPU v4-128 pod, taking 14 hours. Finetuning takes approximately 5 hours on a single NVIDIA A5000 GPU.
- **Observation History:** Uses 2 frames of observation history.

**E. Open-Source Release:**
- A fundamental part of the methodology is the commitment to open-sourcing all components: pretrained checkpoints (Octo-Small, Octo-Base), finetuning scripts (in JAX), the complete pretraining pipeline (in JAX), and standalone data loaders compatible with JAX and PyTorch.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments comprehensively evaluate Octo's capabilities across nine robotic platforms at four institutions, focusing on zero-shot control, data-efficient finetuning, and design decision ablations.

**A. Octo Controls Multiple Robots Out-of-the-Box (Zero-Shot Evaluation):**
- **Superior Performance over RT-1-X:** Octo (93M parameters) achieved a 29% higher average success rate than RT-1-X (35M parameters), the best openly available generalist robot policy, on language-conditioned tasks across WidowX, UR5, and RT-1 robot platforms.
- **Competitive with RT-2-X:** Octo performed similarly to the significantly larger RT-2-X (55 billion parameters) on the tested WidowX and RT-1 Robot tasks.
- **Goal Image Conditioning:** Octo supports goal image conditioning, achieving a 25% higher success rate than language conditioning on WidowX tasks, indicating the value of richer visual task specifications.
- **Generalization Analysis:** While Octo demonstrated high success on novel objects and good generalization to novel environments within its pretraining domains, its zero-shot performance degraded significantly for entirely novel skills (e.g., flipping a cup or precise insertion) not observed in the WidowX embodiment.

**B. Octo Enables Data-Efficient Learning in New Domains (Finetuning Evaluation):**
- **State-of-the-Art Finetuning:** Octo significantly outperformed training from scratch (ResNet+Transformer Scratch) and finetuning with state-of-the-art pretrained visual representations (VC-1) by an average of 52% across six diverse finetuning setups.
- **Adaptability to New Paradigms:**
 - **New Observations:** Successfully adapted to force-torque inputs for precise contact-rich tasks (e.g., "Berkeley Insertion").
 - **New Action Spaces:** Successfully adapted to joint position control (e.g., "Berkeley Pick-Up," "Berkeley Bimanual").
 - **New Robot Embodiments:** Demonstrated generalization to completely unseen robot platforms (e.g., ViperX for "Berkeley Coke," ALOHA bimanual robot for "Berkeley Bimanual"). This includes adapting to bimanual control despite pretraining only on single-arm data.
- **Efficiency:** Finetuning required only around 100 in-domain demonstrations and less than 5 hours on a single NVIDIA A5000 GPU, using consistent hyperparameters across all tasks.

**C. Design Decisions for Generalist Robot Policy Training (Ablation Studies):**
- **Model Architecture (Transformer-First vs. ResNet):** The "transformer-first" ViT architecture used in Octo substantially outperformed ResNet-based architectures when trained on the full Open X-Embodiment data mix. This highlights the scalability benefits of ViTs for large, diverse datasets.
- **Training Data Distribution:** Performance directly scaled with the diversity and quantity of the training data. Models trained on the widest data mixtures (25 datasets) showed the best performance, suggesting further improvements with even broader data.
- **Training Objective (Diffusion vs. MSE/Discrete):** Octo's conditional diffusion decoding objective significantly out-performed both simple MSE loss (which led to "hedging" or slow policies) and cross-entropy loss on discretized actions (which lacked precision), by effectively modeling multi-modal action distributions with continuous control.
- **Model Scale:** Zero-shot performance consistently improved with increasing model size (Octo-Tiny < Octo-Small < Octo-Base), indicating the benefits of scale for robustness and visual scene perception.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Octo represents a significant advancement in the field of robot learning, particularly in the development of generalist robot policies, with far-reaching potential impacts:

1. **Democratization of Advanced Robotics:** By open-sourcing the entire Octo framework—including pretrained models, training code, and data loaders—the authors have dramatically lowered the barrier to entry for researchers and practitioners. This will enable a broader community to experiment with and build upon large-scale robot models, accelerating research and development beyond institutions with vast proprietary resources.
2. **Paradigm Shift in Robot Learning:** Octo validates the foundation model paradigm for robotics. Instead of extensive data collection and training from scratch for every new task or robot, Octo provides a powerful, versatile initialization. This can significantly reduce development time and resources for real-world robotic applications.
3. **Enhanced Adaptability and Flexibility:** Octo's architectural design, which allows for efficient finetuning to new observation types, action spaces, and robot embodiments, makes it an exceptionally versatile tool. This flexibility is crucial for deploying robots in diverse and unpredictable environments, moving beyond rigid, single-task agents.
4. **Guidance for Future Research:** The detailed ablation studies offer invaluable empirical insights into critical design choices for generalist robot policies. Findings regarding model architecture (transformer-first), data mixtures, and the efficacy of diffusion-based action prediction provide a strong foundation and clear directions for future research.
5. **Accelerating Embodied AI:** Octo provides a robust generalist policy for manipulation, which can serve as a building block for more complex embodied AI systems. Future work can extend this foundation to areas like mobile manipulation, navigation, and human-robot interaction, potentially leading to more capable and autonomous robots.
6. **Addressing Key Challenges:** While acknowledging current limitations (e.g., wrist camera processing, language conditioning, reliance on optimal demos), Octo sets a clear agenda for the community. The open-source nature facilitates collaborative efforts to address these challenges, pushing the boundaries of what generalist robot policies can achieve.

In summary, Octo not only demonstrates state-of-the-art performance in zero-shot control and data-efficient finetuning but also champions an open-source ethos that is poised to catalyze innovation and collaboration across the robotics research landscape. It brings the vision of widely applicable, adaptable, and accessible generalist robots closer to reality.
