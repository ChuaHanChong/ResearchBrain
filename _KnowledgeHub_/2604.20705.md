# SSL-R1: Self-Supervised Visual Reinforcement Post-Training for Multimodal Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> SSL-R1 introduces a self-supervised visual reinforcement post-training framework for multimodal large language models (MLLMs) that derives intrinsic, verifiable rewards from five distinct visual tasks. The method achieves average performance gains of +3.44% and +3.84% on Qwen2.5-VL-3B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B, respectively, across 13 vision-centric benchmarks, surpassing existing supervised and self-supervised RL post-training approaches.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for MLLMs largely relies on language-centric rewards, which limits fine-grained visual understanding.
- The training data for current RLVR solutions typically requires expensive manual annotations, creating scalability and sustainability challenges.
- Prior self-supervised MLLM post-training methods often involve a limited set of SSL tasks, require multi-stage training, or exhibit restricted transferability.

## Method

- **SSL-R1** is a ==self-supervised RLVR== framework that derives verifiable rewards directly from visual inputs, fostering intrinsic visual understanding.
- The framework integrates ==five diverse self-supervised visual tasks==: Rotation Prediction, Visual Similarity, Region Inpainting, Patch Ordering, and Geometric Correspondence.
- ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== is used for policy optimization, eliminating the need for a separate critic model by comparing candidate responses directly.

## Results

- SSL-R1 achieved average performance gains of **+3.44%** for Qwen2.5-VL-3B and **+3.84%** for Qwen2.5-VL-7B across **13** vision-centric benchmarks compared to their baselines.
- The self-supervised framework outperformed prior reasoning models relying on supervised RL post-training (e.g., ThinkLite-VL) and concurrent self-supervised methods (e.g., Visual Jigsaw).
- Each of the five individual self-supervised tasks positively contributed to vision-centric perception and understanding, with **Geometric Correspondence** yielding the highest average gain (**+2.13%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-supervised visual tasks can effectively serve as intrinsic, verifiable puzzles for MLLM post-training, enhancing vision-centric understanding without human annotations.
> - Combining diverse self-supervised visual tasks leads to synergistic performance improvements, indicating that different tasks contribute to distinct visual reasoning abilities.
> - Increasing the difficulty of self-supervised tasks generally results in greater performance gains, suggesting a stronger supervision signal for downstream reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large language models (LLMs) and, subsequently, multimodal large language models (MLLMs) has been significantly advanced by reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR). RLVR has shown potential in enhancing the reasoning capabilities of these models. Prior works in this domain, particularly for MLLMs, have largely relied on rewards that are primarily language-centric. These approaches typically leverage dense visual information only to extract sparse cues for text-based reasoning, often resulting in limitations in fine-grained visual understanding. Furthermore, the training data for these existing RLVR solutions frequently necessitate expensive manual annotations, which restricts their scalability and sustainability given the increasing complexity and volume of real-world tasks.

Self-supervised learning (SSL) has been established as a fundamental paradigm in representation learning, deriving supervision from raw data by exploiting inherent priors or structures through pretext tasks. While SSL has been extensively applied in the pre-training stages for visual, textual, and multimodal domains, its application in the post-training stage, especially for MLLMs, has received less exploration. Recent and concurrent works, such as Jigsaw-R1, Visual Jigsaw, SSL4RL, and Spatial-SSRL, have begun to investigate self-supervised approaches for MLLM post-training. However, these methods often focus on a limited set of SSL tasks, require multi-stage training, or exhibit restricted transferability to diverse downstream tasks.

This work positions itself within this landscape by addressing the identified challenges of language-centric rewards and costly manual annotations in MLLM post-training. It proposes a generic, self-supervised RLVR framework that aims to derive verifiable rewards directly from visual inputs, thereby promoting intrinsic visual understanding and scalable reward design. The authors contribute a framework that encompasses multiple diverse self-supervised tasks, supports one-time and one-stage training, and demonstrates transferability across a broad range of downstream tasks, distinguishing it from prior and concurrent self-supervised RL post-training methodologies.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a scalable and cost-effective post-training framework for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) that enhances their intrinsic visual understanding and reasoning capabilities. This objective is motivated by two key challenges observed in existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) solutions for MLLMs:

1. **Reliance on Language-Centric Rewards:** Current RLVR approaches for MLLMs often design rewards that are heavily biased towards linguistic interpretations and reasoning. While they process visual information, it is frequently used to extract minimal cues that support text-based reasoning rather than fostering a deep, fine-grained visual understanding. This orientation can lead to systemic weaknesses in MLLMs' abilities related to detailed visual perception and reasoning tasks, where precise visual grounding is crucial. The motivation is to shift this paradigm by creating a mechanism that derives rewards directly from visual signals, thereby encouraging a more vision-centric approach to learning and reinforcing MLLMs’ visual understanding without relying on language-centric priors.

2. **High Cost and Lack of Scalability of Manual Annotations:** The training data for many existing RLVR methods typically requires extensive manual annotations. Obtaining high-quality, language-centric multimodal data with human annotations is a labor-intensive and expensive process. This cost becomes a significant barrier to scalability, particularly as the complexity and quantity of real-world multimodal tasks increase. The authors are motivated to overcome this limitation by introducing a self-supervised framework that eliminates the need for human or external model supervision. By deriving verifiable rewards intrinsically from the input images, the framework aims to provide a scalable and sustainable method for MLLM post-training, making it feasible to apply reinforcement learning on a larger scale without incurring prohibitive annotation costs.

In summary, the motivation is to bridge the gap between the potential of RLVR for MLLMs and the practical limitations imposed by language-centric rewards and the reliance on expensive human annotations. The research aims to achieve this by re-purposing self-supervised learning principles from visual domains to formulate intrinsic, verifiable visual puzzles for RL post-training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SSL-R1 framework is designed as a generic self-supervised reinforcement learning post-training approach. It leverages a suite of five self-supervised visual tasks to derive intrinsic, verifiable rewards directly from images.

#### 3.1. Preliminary: Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)

The methodology is founded on RLVR, which differs from Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) by determining reward scores directly through pre-defined rules, rather than relying on a separate reward model trained on preference data. This approach is intended to simplify the reward mechanism while maintaining alignment with objective correctness criteria. The reward function $R(x, y)$ is typically binary, assigning 1 for a correct response $y$ to an input prompt $x$, and 0 otherwise.

The policy optimization is performed using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). GRPO eliminates the requirement for a separate critic model by comparing a group of candidate responses directly. For a given prompt $x$ and $L$ sampled responses $\{y_1..., y_L\}$, GRPO obtains rewards $\{r_1..., r_L\}$ and calculates an advantage score $A_i$ for each response: $A_i = (r_i - \text{mean}(\{r_1..., r_L\})) / \text{std}(\{r_1..., r_L\})$. This approach measures relative performance within the sampled group, encouraging the model to favor responses with higher reward values.

#### 3.2. SSL-R1 Framework

The core of SSL-R1 involves designing self-supervised tasks that serve as verifiable visual puzzles. These tasks cover different aspects of visual information, contributing to comprehensive vision-centric reasoning capabilities.

#### 3.2.1. Task Design

Five distinct self-supervised tasks, adapted from the vision literature, are re-purposed for the RLVR framework:

1. **Rotation Prediction:** An input image is rotated by a random angle from a predefined set (e.g., multiples of 90 degrees). The model is then prompted to predict the specific rotation angle. This task targets understanding global image orientation.

2. **Visual Similarity:** Two augmented views (reference and positive) are randomly cropped from an input image. Three additional views from other images serve as negative samples. The model is presented with the reference image and four candidate images (one positive, three negatives) and must select the most visually similar image. This task aims to improve the model's ability to discern semantic similarities despite variations in appearance.

3. **Region Inpainting:** A random region of an input image is masked out. The model is given the masked image and four candidate regions (one ground-truth, three random) in a multiple-choice format, and is required to select the region that best fits the missing part. This task focuses on contextual understanding and pixel-level detail.

4. **Patch Ordering:** An input image is partitioned into an $M \times N$ grid of patches, which are then randomly shuffled. The model receives the shuffled patch sequence and is prompted to predict the original patch order that reconstructs the image. This task challenges the model's understanding of local patch relationships and global spatial layout.

5. **Geometric Correspondence:** $K$ points are randomly selected on an original image and indexed. The image and points undergo a random geometric transformation (e.g., affine). The transformed image shows the points in a shuffled order. The model is prompted to find the corresponding point on the transformed image for each point on the original image, predicting the correct point ordering. This task requires the model to leverage 3D-aware spatial cues for fine-grained geometric reasoning.

#### 3.2.2. Reward Design

The total reward for each task is a sum of two components:

1. **Accuracy Reward ($R_{acc}$):**
 - For single-value answers (Rotation Prediction, Visual Similarity, Region Inpainting), $R_{acc}$ is binary (1 if exact match, 0 otherwise).
 - For sequence-based answers (Patch Ordering, Geometric Correspondence), $R_{acc}$ is a graded fractional value between 0 and 1, proportional to the number of correctly placed indices.

2. **Format Reward ($R_{format}$):** This reward assesses adherence to a prescribed response format. The model is required to enclose its reasoning process within `<think> </think>` tags and the final answer within `\boxed{}` tags, with each tag appearing exactly once and in the correct sequence (reasoning before answer). A value of 0.2 is awarded for strict adherence, and 0 otherwise.

#### 3.3. Implementation Details

- **SSL Task Setup:** Specific configurations were used for each task (e.g., 4 rotation angles, 0.5 masking ratio for inpainting, 3x3 grid for patch ordering, 6 points for geometric correspondence). Standard image augmentations were applied for Visual Similarity.
- **Training Data:** 118K raw images from COCO were used to generate 591K self-supervised question-answer pairs (SSL-R1-591K), with an equal balance across all five tasks and diverse question formats.
- **Baselines:** Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B-Instruct models were used as base MLLMs. Comparisons were also made against reasoning models post-trained with supervised RL (ThinkLite-VL, VL-Cogito, LLaVA-Critic-R1) and concurrent self-supervised RL post-training methods (Visual Jigsaw).
- **Training Details:** Models were optimized using GRPO, with KL regularization and entropy loss removed. 16 responses were sampled per prompt with a temperature of 1.0. Training involved a global batch size of 256 and a learning rate of 1x10^-6 for either 1000 steps (single-task) or 2000 steps (multi-task).
- **Benchmarks:** Evaluations were conducted on 13 vision-centric multimodal benchmarks categorized into fine-grained perception and understanding, monocular spatial understanding, and compositional visual understanding.

### 4. Main findings and results

The research presents several findings regarding the effectiveness of SSL-R1 for enhancing MLLMs.

#### 4.1. Single-Task Post-Training Performance

Experiments with individual SSL tasks demonstrated that all five tasks contributed positively to vision-centric perception and understanding compared to the baseline Qwen2.5-VL-3B model. Geometric Correspondence yielded the highest average gain (+2.13%) across 13 benchmarks, followed by Patch Ordering (+1.92%), Rotation Prediction (+1.80%), Visual Similarity (+1.60%), and Region Inpainting (+1.46%).

Specific task strengths were observed:
- **Geometric Correspondence:** Excelled in fine-grained and high-resolution perception (e.g., MMStar, HR-Bench-8K) and spatial understanding (e.g., DA-2K, OmniSpatial), with a +6.28% improvement on DA-2K for monocular depth estimation. This suggests its utility for pixel-level geometric reasoning.
- **Patch Ordering:** Showed balanced improvements, ranking second on more than half of the benchmarks, indicating its benefit for inferring local patch relations and global spatial layout.
- **Rotation Prediction:** Achieved the largest improvement on MMVP (+9.34%), implying its effectiveness in understanding image orientations and CLIP-blind visual patterns.
- **Visual Similarity:** Demonstrated strong performance in general visual perception and understanding, leading in the fine-grained perception category, likely due to its demand for multi-object semantic reasoning.
- **Region Inpainting:** Yielded a +5.24% gain on fine-grained visual search (V*), indicating its contribution to contextual understanding and pixel-level detail recognition.

#### 4.2. Multi-Task Post-Training Performance

Combining multiple SSL tasks generally led to further performance gains. Combining the two best-performing tasks (Geometric Correspondence and Patch Ordering) improved upon individual task performance. Combining all five tasks resulted in the best overall average performance, outperforming the best single task by an additional 1.31% on average. This indicates a task synergy where diverse SSL objectives complement each other to acquire different visual reasoning abilities. However, the study also noted that combining tasks did not universally lead to improvements on every benchmark, and sometimes the optimal performance was achieved with a subset of tasks, suggesting the potential for task conflicts or negative transfer in certain scenarios (e.g., LISA-Grounding, OmniSpatial).

#### 4.3. Effect of Task Difficulty

Varying the difficulty of SSL tasks (e.g., reducing sampled points for Geometric Correspondence, increasing rotation interval) generally showed that harder tasks led to greater performance gains, suggesting a stronger supervision signal for downstream reasoning. An exception was noted for Rotation Prediction, where an overly fine-grained task (45° interval) led to a performance degradation, possibly due to overfitting to the specific SSL objective.

#### 4.4. Training Dynamics

Reward curves during training indicated a gradual increase in rewards for all tasks. Visual Similarity and Rotation Prediction showed rapid early increases and converged to high accuracies. Region Inpainting, Geometric Correspondence, and Patch Ordering exhibited slower initial growth but accelerated in later stages. Multi-task training generally resulted in increased rewards, supporting the idea of task synergy.

#### 4.5. Comparison with Previous Methods

SSL-R1 demonstrated consistent improvements across all 13 vision-centric benchmarks for both Qwen2.5-VL-3B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B models.
- **Qwen2.5-VL-3B:** SSL-R1-3B achieved an average gain of +3.44% over the baseline, with notable improvements on MMVP (+11.34%), DA-2K (+9.81%), and MMStar (+6.18%).
- **Qwen2.5-VL-7B:** SSL-R1-7B further improved performance, yielding an average gain of +3.84% over its baseline.

The self-supervised 7B model outperformed prior reasoning models (ThinkLite-VL, VL-Cogito, LLaVA-Critic-R1) that rely on supervised, reasoning-intensive RL post-training. SSL-R1 also surpassed a concurrent self-supervised RL post-training method, Visual Jigsaw, by a clear margin (e.g., +1.59% for 3B and +0.96% for 7B on average).

#### 4.6. Further Analysis

- **MLLM Performance on SSL Tasks:** Initially, Qwen2.5-VL achieved near-random accuracy on the raw SSL tasks. Post-training significantly improved performance on the corresponding tasks. Cross-task transfer effects were observed; for instance, training on other tasks often benefited Visual Similarity. However, some tasks like Geometric Correspondence and Patch Ordering, initially very difficult for MLLMs, showed marginal improvements from training on easier SSL tasks. Negative transfer was also noted, where Visual Similarity training degraded Region Inpainting accuracy, potentially due to intensive augmentations interfering with pixel-level detail recognition. Multi-task training, however, consistently improved accuracy across all SSL tasks, often surpassing single-task training performance.
- **Performance on Reasoning Models:** When applied to a strong, reasoning-intensive baseline like ThinkLite-VL-7B, SSL-R1 still provided an average gain of +2.06% across a combined set of vision-centric and reasoning-centric benchmarks (including MathVista, MathVision, MathVerse, MMMU, EMMA). Notably, SSL-R1 maintained or improved performance on reasoning-heavy benchmarks where Visual Jigsaw showed degradation.
- **Performance on Other Base Models:** SSL-R1 demonstrated general effectiveness by significantly improving performance on another base model, MiMo-VL-7B-SFT, with an average gain of +2.88% on vision-centric benchmarks, also outperforming Visual Jigsaw on this architecture.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The introduction of SSL-R1 signifies a shift in the approach to post-training Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly in how visual understanding and reasoning capabilities are enhanced.

1. **Scalability and Cost-Effectiveness:** By deriving verifiable rewards intrinsically from input images and eliminating the need for human or external model supervision, SSL-R1 addresses a significant bottleneck in current RLVR methodologies: the high cost and limited scalability of manual annotations. This self-supervised paradigm suggests a pathway for more widespread and sustainable application of reinforcement learning to MLLMs, potentially accelerating their development and deployment in diverse real-world scenarios without prohibitive annotation budgets.

2. **Enhanced Vision-Centric Understanding:** The framework's emphasis on vision-centric self-supervised tasks, rather than language-centric priors, is designed to foster a more robust and intrinsic visual understanding within MLLMs. The empirical results demonstrate consistent improvements across a wide range of fine-grained perception, spatial understanding, and compositional understanding benchmarks. This indicates that MLLMs post-trained with SSL-R1 may exhibit improved capabilities in tasks requiring detailed visual grounding, object localization, and complex scene analysis.

3. **Diverse Visual Reasoning Abilities:** The design of five distinct self-supervised visual puzzles (Rotation Prediction, Visual Similarity, Region Inpainting, Patch Ordering, Geometric Correspondence) contributes to a comprehensive empirical study of what constitutes effective self-supervised tasks for RL-based post-training. The finding that these tasks encourage different visual reasoning abilities and can exhibit synergy suggests a flexible framework that can be adapted to specific downstream requirements or combined for holistic improvement. This diversity could lead to more versatile and robust MLLMs capable of handling a broader spectrum of visual challenges.

4. **Generality and Transferability:** The consistent performance gains observed across different MLLM scales (3B and 7B), on various base architectures (Qwen2.5-VL and MiMo-VL), and even when applied to already reasoning-intensive models (ThinkLite-VL), indicates the generality and transferability of the SSL-R1 framework. This suggests that the proposed method is not confined to a specific model or domain but can serve as a widely applicable post-training mechanism to fortify MLLMs' visual perception and reasoning.

The work aims to provide experience in devising effective self-supervised verifiable rewards. Its potential impact lies in enabling RLVR at scale, thereby contributing to the development of MLLMs with more sophisticated and intrinsically visual understanding capabilities, without the traditional reliance on labor-intensive supervision.
