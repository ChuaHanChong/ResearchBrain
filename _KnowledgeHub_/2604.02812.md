# Learning Structured Robot Policies from Vision-Language Models via Synthetic Neuro-Symbolic Supervision

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces a neuro-symbolic framework enabling Vision-Language Models to generate structured, interpretable, and reactive Behavior Tree policies for robots. It achieves zero-shot transfer of these policies from entirely synthetic multimodal supervision to physical robotic platforms like the Franka Emika Panda and UR5e.

## Problem

- End-to-end visuomotor policies from foundation models often operate as opaque "black boxes," hindering interpretation, analysis, and verification in safety-critical robotic domains.
- Linear action sequences generated by many VLM outputs lack reactivity, preventing autonomous recovery from unexpected environmental perturbations or task failures.
- Training robust VLM-based robot policies typically requires extensive, costly, and labor-intensive real-world datasets and manual annotation.

## Method

- A ==neuro-symbolic framework== is developed to enable a compact VLM to synthesize executable ==Behavior Trees== (BTs) from multimodal inputs (RGB observations, natural language instructions, and structured system specifications).
- An automated, fully ==synthetic dataset generation pipeline==, powered by a large foundation model (Gemini 3 Flash), creates 10,000 instruction-BT pairs from procedurally generated MuJoCo scenes.
- ==Structural priors and constraints==, such as ==reactive guarding==, ==spatial offsetting==, and a flat hierarchy, are enforced during BT generation to ensure valid, robust, and safe robotic execution.

## Results

- Policies learned from synthetic MuJoCo data successfully transferred to physical Franka Emika Panda and Universal Robots UR5e systems without any real-world fine-tuning.
- The fine-tuned **Pixtral-12B** model achieved **100%** Task Success Rate, JSON Validity Rate, and Schema Compliance in a zero-shot setting, matching or exceeding the performance of significantly larger state-of-the-art foundation models.
- The model demonstrated visual referential grounding by correctly identifying target objects based on relative spatial position but showed limitations in compositional generalization for unseen semantic tasks and out-of-vocabulary primitives.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLMs can learn robust symbolic grounding and spatial reasoning from purely synthetic multimodal supervision, enabling zero-shot logic transfer from simulation to real-world robot systems.
> - Carefully configured LoRA adaptation and sufficient context window sizes are crucial for VLMs to internalize complex robotic grammar and accurately map visual-spatial relationships to symbolic categories.
> - Automated synthetic supervision offers a scalable and cost-effective method for training structured robot policies, significantly reducing the reliance on expensive real-world data collection and manual annotation.
